Changes in the epidemiological burden of melanoma in China from 1990 to 2019 based on the Global Burden of Disease
Authors: Qiong Tian, Li Hu, Ruimin Bai, Rui Ge, Jian Zhang, Lishao Guo
Published: 2024

To the Editor: The incidence of melanoma, a highly aggressive malignancy, is associated with the level of social development, with a higher incidence being documented in developed than in developing countries.[1] With a large population, China has experienced substantial economic growth and urbanization over the past 30 years, with increasing severity of population aging.

The Global Burden of Disease Study (GBD) 2019, published on the Global Health Data Exchange (https://ghdx.healthdata.org/), covers 369 diseases and injuries in 204 countries and territories during 1990–2019.[2] The socio-demographic index (SDI) is a composite indicator developed by GBD researchers to measure a region’s social, economic, demographic, and health outcomes. The SDI is the geometric mean (range: 0–1) of the following parameters: total fertility rate under the age of 25 (TFU25), the average educational attainment of the population aged 15 years and over (EDU15+), and the lagging distribution of per capita income. A lower SDI indicates a lower level of health-related development in the region.[3] The specific method for calculating the SDI has been described previously,[4] and SDI values of individual countries during 1950–2019 are available on the the Institute for Health Metrics and Evaluation (IHME) website (https://ghdx.healthdata.org/record/ihme-data/gbd-2019-socio-demographic-index-sdi-1950-2019).

In this context, we retrieved data regarding the total number of melanoma cases of all ages and the age-standardized rate (ASR) in China, focusing on the following indicators: (1) incident/incidence, (2) prevalent/prevalence, (3) death/mortality, and (4) disability-adjusted life years (DALYs)/DALY. The 95% uncertainty intervals (UIs) of these measures were also searched. The distributions of these measures were described in terms of time, age, and sex. Incidence and mortality were analyzed by period and birth cohort, and the relationship between SDI and the incidence, mortality, and DALY rates of melanoma in China was analyzed. Data were sorted using Word Processing System form (version 12.1.0.15712), and figures and tables were plotted using the R software (version 4.0.3, https://www.r-project.org/).

According to GBD 2019, in Asia, China ranked 12th, 15th, and 27th for these rates among 48 countries, and 3rd among East Asian countries [Supplementary Figure 1, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C75]. In 2019, the number of incident cases and incidence ASR in China increased by 356.80% (95% UI: 157.35–525.59%) and 129.85% (95% UI: 31.13–209.49%) compared with the respective 1990 values. The mortality ASR decreased from 0.33/100,000 (95% UI: 0.23–0.48) in 1990 to 0.28/100,000 (95% UI: 0.18–0.35) in 2019, suggesting a 14.63% decrease (95% UI: −49.23% to 12.45%) [Supplementary Table 1, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C75].

From 1990 to 2019, the number of incident cases, prevalent cases, and deaths, as well as the incidence and prevalence ASR of melanoma in China, tended to increase annually; however, the mortality ASR decreased annually [Supplementary Figure 2A, B and Supplementary Figure 3, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C75]. Both men and women exhibited comparable patterns on the aforementioned measures [Supplementary Figure 4, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C75].

In 2019, the number of incident cases in China peaked among male patients aged 55–59 years (1082 [95% UI: 555–1556]) and female patients aged 50–54 years (961 [95% UI: 477–1367]). The incidence ASR showed an upward trend with increasing age, particularly in individuals aged ≥60 years. The prevalence ASR exhibited a similar trend. The number of deaths peaked among male patients aged 50–59 years (348 [95% UI: 180–491]) and female patients aged 75–79 years (316 [95% UI: 166–436]), corresponding to the variation trends in the number of incident and prevalent cases. With advancing age, the mortality ASR increased consistently, dramatically increasing in those aged ≥60 years [Supplementary Figure 5, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C75].

According to period and birth cohort trends of melanoma in China, the incidence ASR of melanoma in those aged ≥20 years tended to increase annually with age. From 2003 onward, the incidence and mortality ASRs of melanoma in those aged ≥70 years notably increased, and the mortality ASR in those aged ≥75 years increased during 2006–2012, decreasing after 2013. The incidence ASR of melanoma increased significantly with age and increasing birth year within the same age group; however, the mortality ASR remained unaltered in patients born in different periods in the population aged <80 years [Supplementary Figure 6, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C75].

During the 1990–2019 period, the SDI in China tended to increase continuously (from 0.433 in 1990 to 0.686 in 2019) at a significantly faster rate than the global average (from 0.511 in 1990 to 0.651 in 2019). The incidence ASR of melanoma in China was lower than the global average. As the SDI increased, the global ASR of incidence of melanoma increased initially and then decreased, peaking in 2010 (global SDI 0.607). However, the incidence ASR of melanoma in China increased consistently during the same period [Supplementary Figure 2C, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C75]. With the increase in SDI, the global melanoma ASR of mortality increased from 0.85/100,000 (95% UI: 0.72–1.10) in 1990 to 0.88/100,000 (95% UI: 0.73–1.10) in 1995, subsequently declining annually to 0.79/100,000 (95% UI: 0.58–0.89) in 2019; however, the melanoma ASR of mortality [Supplementary Figure 2D, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C75] and DALY [Supplementary Figure 7, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C75] in China decreased during 1990–2019, decreasing at a lower rate than the global average.

Given the huge population base, incident cases of melanoma in China reached 16,938 (95% UI: 10,456–21,524) in 2019, ranking third worldwide. Furthermore, the incidence has risen rapidly in the past 30 years. Conversely, mortality is declining at a lower rate than the growing incidence. The social development level positively correlates with melanoma incidence.[5] Our research revealed that with the increase in China’s SDI, melanoma incidence is also continuously growing. Both the incidence and mortality of melanoma among individuals aged ≥50 years are rapidly increasing in China. The ratio of the number of incident to prevalent cases in 2019 was higher than that in 1990, and the analysis of birth cohorts revealed that the melanoma incidence within the same age group increased with increasing years of birth, potentially due to an improved diagnostic level. However, this study had certain limitations. Data from the GBD 2019 database, generated by statistical modeling of data derived from various disease surveillance sites, are not obtained directly by epidemiological investigation. Consequently, all data pertaining to incidence, prevalence, and mortality were estimates. Additionally, the GBD 2019 database did not have detailed data on Chinese provinces, leading to inaccurate representation of melanoma burden differences in these regions.

In conclusion, our results suggest that melanoma incidence in China increased during 1990–2019, with the burden of premature melanoma deaths on the socio-economic and healthcare systems in China surpassing the global average. Preventing risk factors of melanoma, improving the early diagnostic rate, and promoting the application of novel therapeutics in China are necessary.

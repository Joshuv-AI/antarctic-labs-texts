Streamlining elective surgical pathways using early digital screening with complexity grading: feasibility study of a co-designed innovation
Authors: Tom Langford, Daveena Meeks, Deirdre Anderson, Lepalele Sepa, Stephanie Fraser, Pearl Okeke, Joseph Low, Polly Sinclair, Amanda Begley, Ramai Santhirapala
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesTo evaluate the feasibility, usability and validity of a co-designed digital questionnaire for early complexity screening in elective surgery patients.MethodsA mixed-methods feasibility study was conducted using operational data, patient interviews and surveys.ResultsThe average time to preoperative screening decreased from 3–4 weeks to 3.4 days. Of 244 patients, 44% were eligible for fast-tracking to low-complexity surgical hubs. A nurse-led validation process correctly identified 100% of medium-complexity and high-complexity cases. Most patients (97%) completed the questionnaire without assistance.DiscussionThe tool proved feasible and user-friendly, enabling earlier identification of complexity and streamlining patient triage. It also holds promise for reducing cancellations and facilitating timely prehabilitation.ConclusionsThis feasibility study supports the tool’s feasibility, usability and validity. Further research will assess its clinical, operational and health economic impacts within redesigned surgical pathways.

Introduction

Background

The elective care backlog remains a critical challenge, with late cancellations compounding the problem.1 2 Addressing this challenge requires innovative approaches that fast-track low-complexity patients to high-volume, low-complexity (HVLC) elective surgery hubs3 while reducing avoidable cancellations.1 2 4 Early identification of patient complexity within the perioperative pathway is essential for improving resource utilisation and patient outcomes.1 5–7

The current perioperative assessment process is inefficient with patients typically not pre-assessed until after their surgical outpatient appointment.1 5 This delay contributes to increased waiting times, higher cancellation rates and missed opportunities for earlier intervention and optimisation.1 5 7 As the elective backlog continues to grow, a solution is urgently needed to streamline complexity screening in order to maximise surgical hub utilisation. This approach is supported by causal evidence that hubs increase elective care activity,4 and it aligns with the NHS Long Term Plan, which prioritises more timely, person-centred and efficient elective care delivery.8

Traditionally, perioperative risk tools assess surgical and medical complexity, but few incorporate anaesthetic or social considerations (such as care arrangements at home following surgery). This digital screening tool is novel in its inclusion of the development and inclusion of these additional domains, such as social support needs and anaesthetic complexity, and was co-designed with patients and clinicians to maximise usability and accessibility. Our working definition of complexity, which is novel to this tool, includes any medical, anaesthetic or social need that would warrant further optimisation prior to surgery. Patients scoring low complexity across all domains can be rapidly identified as suitable for bypassing secondary care preoperative assessment and may be safely expedited to surgery.

This study evaluated the feasibility, usability and validity of a co-developed digital complexity tool (see online supplemental appendix A). Piloted across four surgical specialties, the tool aimed to identify complex patients earlier and support more efficient, person-centred perioperative pathways. The tool has the potential to support future improvements in shared decision-making and prehabilitation. It also has the potential to reduce same-day cancellations and optimise surgical hub utilisation, thereby aligning with the national priorities to address the elective backlog.

Objectives

To assess the feasibility, usability and validity of the digital complexity tool and to gather insights to inform further development and support implementation.

Methods

Participants

The study involved 284 consecutive patients referred for elective surgery at Guy’s and St Thomas’ NHS Foundation Trust between September and December 2023 across four specialties: Gastrointestinal Surgery, Urology, Gynaecology and Orthopaedics. There were no exclusion criteria. Of the participants, 55% were aged over 50 and 48% identified as women. Further demographic details are provided in online supplemental appendix B.

Study design and data collection

This mixed-methods feasibility study assessed the digital screening tool’s feasibility, usability and validity. The digital screening tool is available in online supplemental file 1online supplemental file 1.

Feasibility was measured by reviewing completion rates of the digital questionnaire, validation times and through qualitative feedback. Usability was evaluated through patient surveys and interviews with patients and staff. Focus areas included clarity, satisfaction and accessibility. Survey results were summarised descriptively, and interview data were analysed thematically. Validity was confirmed by anaesthetists through independent clinical validation of all participant data. Additionally, analysis of demographics, completion rates and qualitative themes supported the tool’s accuracy in representing patient complexity.

Quantitative data collected included operational system and service data. Qualitative data were collected via 10 semistructured patient interviews, 8 staff interviews (clinical and administrative) and a patient experience survey.

The patient experience survey was developed by the Health Innovation Network—South London to capture user perspectives on usability, clarity and satisfaction with the digital tool. Patients were involved in the co-design of the tool and the associated pathway as well as in reviewing the study findings. Additional detail on data collection tools and procedures is provided in online supplemental appendix C.

Data analysis

Quantitative data were analysed descriptively to assess completion rates, validation times and demographic patterns. Survey results were summarised using descriptive statistics, and open responses were thematically coded. Interview data were analysed using thematic analysis, with coding aligned to the evaluation’s core domains.

Discussion

This pilot study of a digital complexity questionnaire provides important early insights into the feasibility, usability and validity of a tool designed to support early complexity screening in perioperative care. The digital tool substantially reduced preoperative triage time from an estimated 3–4 weeks to a mean of 3.4 days, based on current pathway data for comparable low-complexity patients classified as American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) Physical Status 1 or 2 (ASA1/ASA2) from the central preoperative assessment team. The pre-implementation estimate reflects routinely observed pathway timelines obtained from operational dashboard data; more precise historical metrics were not retrospectively available. Of the 284 patients, 44% were identified as suitable for fast-tracking to surgery hubs. Staff feedback was positive (see online supplemental appendix D), particularly regarding ease of use and ability to streamline triage for low-complexity patients. Interestingly, patients aged 65 and over were more likely to complete the questionnaire, suggesting that digital exclusion in older adults may be less pronounced in this context than typically assumed.

These findings suggest the tool may have the potential to improve perioperative pathways and better allocate resources. This can be achieved by reducing unnecessary appointments, identifying complexity early, reducing same-day cancellations, enabling informed decision making and offering prehabilitation opportunities, though further evaluation is needed.

Where the Clinical Nurse Specialist (CNS) was required to alter the digital outcome, alignment was found in all medium-complexity and high-complexity cases, suggesting promising safety in patient categorisation (see online supplemental appendix D). The CNS also played a key role in patient engagement, communication and coordination particularly for medium-complexity and high-complexity patients. This underscores the importance of maintaining a clinical support function in future implementations.

Limitations

This was a feasibility study, which focused on clinical safety without clinical pathway disruption, and not powered to assess clinical or operational outcomes. Participation in the pilot was optional, and 25% of invited patients did not choose to take part; reasons for non-participation were not captured, although participation required additional time and had no impact on the care received. The next phase of the project will require mandatory participation to enhance demographic representativeness.

Although digital complexity questionnaire completion rates were high (86%), the patient experience survey had a lower response rate (34%), which limits the generalisability of usability and experience findings and may have introduced response bias.

Lack of integration between the digital complexity tool and the electronic health record system increased the amount of time spent by the CNS in validating the complexity scores. Similarly, delays caused by the need to manually obtain access to GP (General Practitioner) records were specific to the pilot design. These have since been addressed in the revised version of the tool.

Future work

Revisions to the digital tool and associated processes have been made based on this evaluation. These include content refinement and administrative simplifications. An implementation study launched in December 2024 to assess the impact on clinical outcomes, operational efficiency and health economics.

Conclusions

This pilot demonstrated the feasibility, usability and initial validity of a co-developed digital complexity screening tool for elective surgeries. It is the first known use of a novel complexity model for preoperative screening which incorporates medical, anaesthetic, social and surgical complexity. Early evidence suggests the potential to support earlier complexity identification, improve patient flow and optimise the use of HVLC surgical hubs. However, further evaluation is needed to assess long-term impact, clinical outcomes and broader applicability across surgical services.

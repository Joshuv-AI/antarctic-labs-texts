Preclinical characterization of IBD0333, a novel CD24×4-1BB bispecific antibody with tumor-dependent agonistic activity for cancer immunotherapy
Authors: Dong-Cheng Jiang, Dan-Dan Zhou, Xiao-Rui Xu, Xiao-Ling Jiang, Liu-Song Yin, Cai-Yun Zhong, Qing-Fang Miao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe immunosuppressive nature of tumor microenvironment (TME) limits the efficacy of current immunotherapies. Bispecific antibodies (bsAbs) offer a promising strategy to overcome this challenge by enabling localized immune activation. Specifically, simultaneously targeting the “don’t eat me” signal CD24 on tumor cells and the co-stimulatory receptor 4-1BB on immune cells may provide potent anti-tumor effects while mitigating the systemic toxicity typically associated with conventional 4-1BB agonists. Here, we present the preclinical evaluation of IBD0333, a novel bsAb targeting CD24 and 4-1BB.MethodsThe binding affinity of IBD0333 to CD24 and 4-1BB was evaluated, and its pharmacodynamic effects were assessed through multiple in vitro assays. Anti-tumor efficacy of IBD0333 was evaluated using C57BL/6J-h4-1BB humanized mouse models in vivo, while pharmacokinetic and safety profiles were further investigated in cynomolgus monkeys.ResultsIBD0333 exhibited high-binding affinity to both human and cynomolgus monkey CD24 and 4-1BB proteins. Its agonistic activity toward 4-1BB was strictly dependent on CD24, as evidenced by potent NF-κB activation and cytokine induction only in the presence of CD24-positive tumor cells. IBD0333 also demonstrated appropriate FcγR binding and no-detectable antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity/complement-dependent cytotoxicity activity. In a syngeneic tumor model, complete tumor regression was achieved at doses of 1 and 3 mg/kg. Furthermore, IBD0333 was well tolerated in cynomolgus monkeys with no observed immunotoxicity or hepatotoxicity, and the no-observed-adverse-effect level following repeated administration was established as 120 mg/kg.ConclusionIBD0333 is a tumor-conditionally activated anti-CD24×4-1BB bsAb that demonstrates potent antitumor efficacy along with a favorable safety and pharmacokinetic profile. These findings support its further clinical development.Keywords: bispecific antibody, CD24, 4-1BB, cancer immunotherapy

Introduction

Cancer remains one of the most formidable diseases worldwide. Before the 21st century, the main treatments for cancer were surgical resection, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy [1]. In recent years, cancer immunotherapies have captured the attention of the scientific and lay communities alike. However, its application in the treatment of solid tumors continues to face multiple challenges, among which the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment (TME) and the resulting primary and acquired resistance represent critical obstacles [2]. Although immune checkpoint inhibitors, particularly those targeting the PD-1/PD-L1 axis, have significantly advanced cancer therapy, most patients did not show durable remission, and some cancers have been completely insensitive to response with checkpoint blockade [3]. To bridge this therapeutic gap, research focus has gradually shifted toward other immunotherapeutic approaches, such as bispecific antibodies (bsAbs).

BsAbs are engineered molecules designed to simultaneously target two distinct antigenic epitopes or antigens. Unlike conventional immunotherapies and targeted therapies, bsAbs possess the capability to engage multiple antigens. Their unique structural configurations and versatile design formats enable multiple mechanisms of action, such as redirecting immune cells to the TME or blocking key oncogenic signaling pathways [4]. To date, 11 bispecific antibodies have received regulatory approval worldwide for the treatment of cancer [5]. However, only four of them are indicated for solid tumors, highlighting the continued need to develop bsAbs specifically targeting solid malignancies.

CD24 (cluster of differentiation 24) is a glycosylphosphatidylinositol-anchored protein. It is normally expressed on B and T lymphocytes, monocytes, and granulocytes as well as epithelial, neural, and muscle cells [6]. Over time, growing evidence has shown that CD24 is involved in a variety of inflammatory, immune, and neurological diseases. A recent study further revealed that CD24 functions as an anti-phagocytic cell-surface protein by interacting with the inhibitory receptor Siglec-10 on immune cells such as macrophages and dendritic cells, thereby transmitting a “don’t eat me” signal that helps tumor cells evade immune surveillance [7, 8]. This mechanism establishes CD24 as a key mediator of tumor immune escape. Furthermore, CD24 also contributes to the malignant transformation of tumor cells by promoting proliferation and metastasis. In this regard, the overexpression of CD24 has been documented in several malignancies, including breast, lung, colorectal, and pancreatic cancer [9, 10]. Therefore, targeting CD24 not only allows for specific tumor cell recognition but also blocks this important immunosuppressive pathway.

4-1BB (CD137, TNFRSF9), belonging to the tumor necrosis factor (TNF) receptor superfamily, is a critical co-stimulatory receptor. It plays a crucial role in the immune system and is primarily expressed on activated T cells, activated natural killer (NK) cells, Foxp3+ regulatory T cells (Tregs), and other immune cell surfaces. Its specific functional ligand, 4-1BBL/CD137L, is primarily expressed on professional antigen-presenting cells such as activated macrophages, B cells, and dendritic cells [11]. The 4-1BB/4-1BBL signaling pathway activates NF-κB and MAPK pathways through TNF receptor-associated factors, promoting the release of various cytokines [interleukin-2 (IL-2), IL-6, IL-12, TNF, and interferon-γ (IFN-γ)] [12]. This enhances the proliferation, differentiation, survival, and cytotoxicity of CD8+ T cells, while also facilitating NK cell proliferation and IFN-γ production. Urelumab is the first agonistic anti-4-1BB antibody developed for clinical trials. However, patients treated with urelumab experienced severe hepatotoxicity [13]. Utomilumab is another anti-4-1BB antibody, which has milder hepatotoxicity but less effective [14]. Therefore, multiple strategies, including bispecific antibody or combination therapies, are being explored to activate 4-1BB signaling specifically within the TME while minimizing hepatotoxicity.

IBD0333, developed by SunHo (China) BioPharmaceutical Co., Ltd., is a bispecific antibody based on an IgG4 backbone that simultaneously targets CD24 and 4-1BB. In this study, we conducted a comprehensive series of preclinical studies, including assessments of its pharmacology, pharmacodynamics, pharmacokinetics (PKs), and toxicology, to characterize its potential as a new therapeutic agent for patients with malignant solid tumors.

Materials and methods

Binding of IBD0333 to 4-1BB from different species

IBD0333 is an IgG4-based bispecific antibody targeting CD24 and 4-1BB. It was developed and manufactured by SunHo (China) BioPharmaceutical Co., Ltd., and its sequence was disclosed in the previously filed patent WO2023093744A1. Recombinant 4-1BB proteins from human (4IB-H52Hc), cynomolgus macaque (4IB-C52H4), mouse (4IB-M52H7), and rat (4IB-R52H3) (ACRO Biosystems) were diluted and immobilized onto 96-well plates. Subsequently, after the plates were blocked, serial dilutions of IBD0333 were added to the plates, followed by incubation at 37°C for 1 h. After washing, secondary antibody was added and incubated for 45 min. The plates were washed, developed with 3,3′,5,5′-Tetramethylbenzidine (TMB) peroxidase substrate, and the reaction was terminated with 1 M hydrochloric acid. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader (Tecan).

Binding activity of IBD0333 to cells overexpressing human and cynomolgus macaque CD24

CHOK1 cells stably overexpressing human CD24 (CHOK1-hCD24) and 293 cells overexpressing cynomolgus macaque CD24 (293-CynoCD24) were harvested. Aliquots of 2.5 × 106 cells per tube were incubated with graded concentrations of IBD0333, an anti-CD24 antibody, or an isotype control antibody at 4°C for 1 h. Cells were then washed and stained with a fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC)-conjugated secondary antibody (Abcam, diluted 1:500) at 4°C for 1 h. After a final wash, cell-associated fluorescence intensity was measured by flow cytometry using an ACEA NovoCyte instrument (Agilent).

Binding of IBD0333 to tumor cells

Breast cancer cell line MCF-7 and ovarian cancer cell line SKOV3 (both from the Chinese Academy of Sciences Cell Bank), known for high endogenous CD24 expression, were used. Cells were seeded into 96-well plates (1.0 × 105 cells/well). Experimental groups included: control, Human IgG4 isotype (HG4K, Sino Biological Inc.), anti-CD24 antibody (SunHo BioPharmaceutical Co., Ltd), and IBD0333 (SunHo BioPharmaceutical Co., Ltd) group. The anti-CD24 mAb and IBD0333 underwent three-fold serial dilutions from a starting concentration of 300 nM (8 gradients total). Cells were incubated with the antibodies for 1 h, washed, and then incubated with an FITC-conjugated secondary antibody (BioLegend, 1:500) for 30 min. Following a final wash, cells were analyzed by flow cytometry.

Dual-target simultaneous binding assay

MCF-7 cells (which express high levels of CD24) were seeded into a 96-well plate at 1.0 × 105 cells/well. Groups included: blank control, negative control, experimental group, single-target control groups, and isotype control groups. For all groups except the blank and negative controls, recombinant Human 4-1BB protein (His-tagged) was pre-mixed with the corresponding antibody prior to addition to the cells. The cell-antibody-protein mixtures were incubated at 4°C for 1 h. Cells were then washed and incubated with an FITC-labeled anti-His tag secondary antibody for 30 min in the dark. Following a final wash, FITC fluorescence on the cell surface was detected by flow cytometry.

Target-dependent activation of HEK-293/NF-κB-Luc/4-1BB cells by IBD0333

HEK-293 cells stably expressing human 4-1BB and an NF-κB-driven luciferase reporter (HEK-293/NF-κB-Luc/4-1BB, HanKeMab Biotech) served as effector cells. These cells were co-cultured with target cells: CHOK1-hCD24 (CD24-overexpressing), parental CHOK1 (CD24-negative), and MCF-7 (endogenously high). Serial dilutions of IBD0333, anti-4-1BB mAb (in collaboration with HanKeMab Biotech), anti-CD24 mAb, and isotype controls were added into a 96-well black plate. Then, effector cells (3 × 104/well) and target cells (1 × 104/well) were added. The plates were incubated at 37°C for 18 h. Luciferase activity was measured using a firefly luciferase substrate, and luminescence was recorded on a microplate reader (TECAN).

Activation of IL-2 secretion in PBMCs by IBD0333

A 96-well plate was coated with an anti-human CD3 antibody (BioGems International, 2 μg/ml) overnight at 4°C and washed. Test antibodies (IBD0333, anti-4-1BB mAb (in collaboration with HanKeMab Biotech), anti-CD24 mAb, Human IgG isotype controls) were serially diluted and added to the coated plate. Human peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) (Oricells) (1 × 104 cells) and tumor cells (1 × 104 cells) were added to each well. The plate was incubated at 37°C, 5% CO₂ for 72 h. Supernatants were collected, and IL-2 concentrations were quantified using a Human IL-2 ELISA kit (BioLegend) according to the manufacturer’s protocol.

Activation of IFN-γ secretion in PBMCs by IBD0333

Following plate coating procedure described in section 2.6, serial dilutions of IBD0333, anti-4-1BB mAb (in collaboration with HanKeMab Biotech), anti-CD24 mAb, and isotype controls were added to the anti-CD3-coated plate. PBMCs (3 × 104 cells per well) and corresponding tumor cells (MCF-7, MDA-MB-231 or MDA-MB-231-CD24, 1 × 104 cells/per well) were then added. After 72 h of incubation at 37°C in 5% CO₂, supernatants were harvested and IFN-γ secretion was quantified using a Human IFN-γ ELISA kit (BioLegend).

Binding affinity of IBD0333 to Fc gamma receptors and Fc receptor

Binding kinetics were assessed using bio-layer interferometry (BLI) on an Octet RED96 system (ForteBio). Streptavidin (SA) biosensors were pre-wet in phosphate-buffered saline with 0.05% Tween 20 for at least 20 min. Biotinylated human Fcγ receptors (FcγRI/CD64, FcγRIIA/CD32a, FcγRIIB/CD32b, FcγRIIIA/CD16a, FcγRIIIB/CD16b) and human FcRn/B2M heterodimer (ACRO Biosystems) were diluted to 5 μg/ml and loaded onto the sensors as ligands. Gradient-diluted IBD0333 served as the analyte. Real-time association and dissociation were monitored for 60 s each. Data were analyzed using the Octet Data Analysis Software.

Antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity activity of IBD0333

CHOK1-4-1BB or MCF-7 cells were used as target cells. FcR-TANK cells (HankeMab Therapeutics) were used as effector cells. Target cells (2 × 105/well) were seeded into a 96-well U-bottom plate. Serial dilutions of antibodies (Human IgG isotype control, anti-4-1BB mAb (in collaboration with HanKeMab Biotech), anti-CD24 mAb, and IBD0333, starting at 120 nM) were added, followed by effector cells at an effector-to-target (E:T) ratio of 30:1 (6 × 103 effector cells/well). The plates were incubated at 37°C, 5% CO₂ for 4 h. For lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) detection, 20 μl of lysis solution was added prior to the end of incubation. Supernatants were collected, and LDH release was measured using the LDH kit (Promega, G1781) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Absorbance at 490 nm was recorded.

Complement-dependent cytotoxicity activity of IBD0333

CHOK1-4-1BB or MCF-7 cells (3 × 104/well) were seeded into 96-well plates. Serial dilutions of antibodies [Human IgG4 isotype, anti-4-1BB mAb (in collaboration with HanKeMab Biotech), IBD0333, starting concentration 240 nM] were added. After a 15 min pre-incubation at 37°C, rabbit complement (Per Freez) was diluted to 20% and added to the 96-well plates except the cell-only control. Plates were incubated for 1 h at 37°C. Cell viability was then assessed using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (Dojindo) by adding 20 μl of reagent per well, incubating for 4 h, and measuring absorbance at 450 nm.

Binding of IBD0333 to immune cell subsets in PBMCs

Human PBMCs were initially characterized by surface staining using fluorescent antibodies against CD19 (B cells), CD56 (NK cells), CD14 (monocytes), CD4 (helper T cells), and CD8 (cytotoxic T cells). For the binding assay, PBMCs were pre-treated with an Fc receptor blocking reagent for 20 min and then seeded into 96-well U-bottom plates at 2 × 105 cells/well. Serially diluted biotinylated antibodies (Biotin-IBD0333, Biotin-anti-CD24, Biotin-anti-4-1BB, Biotin-Human IgG1, and Biotin-Human IgG4) were added to the cells, with eight concentrations ranging from 500 nM. After incubation for 1 h at room temperature, cells were washed and stained with the corresponding fluorescent-labeled secondary antibodies for 30 min. Following a final wash, binding to each immune subset was analyzed by flow cytometry (ACEA NovoCyte).

B cell-dependent activation of HEK-293/NF-κB-Luc/4-1BB cells

Following isolation from PBMCs with the EasySep™ Human B Cell Isolation Kit (STEMCELL Technologies) according to the manufacturer’s instructions, human B cells were resuspended in assay buffer. These cells (1 × 105/well) were then co-cultured with HEK-293/NF-κB-Luc/4-1BB effector cells (3 × 104/well) in 96-well black plates in the presence of serially diluted test antibodies (IBD0333, anti-4-1BB mAb, anti-CD24 mAb, Human IgG control). After 18 h of incubation at 37°C with 5% CO₂, luciferase activity was measured as described in section 2.5.

B cell-dependent PBMC activation assay

B cells were isolated from PBMCs as described above and adjusted to 2 × 106 cells/ml. Antibodies (IBD0333, anti-4-1BB mAb, anti-CD24 mAb, and Human IgG control) were serially diluted in ten-fold steps to final concentrations of 20, 2, and 0.2 nM. In a 96-well plate, PBMC cells (3 × 104/well), B cells (5 × 103/well), and 100 μl of diluted antibody were added to each well. Plates were incubated at 37°C with 5% CO₂ for 72 h. Supernatants were then collected, and IFN-γ levels were quantified using a Human IFN-γ ELISA kit (BioLegend).

Animal husbandry

C57BL/6J-h4-1BB transgenic mice were housed in individually ventilated cages under controlled conditions [temperature: 20–26°C; relative humidity: 40%–70%) (WuXi AppTec (Shanghai) Co., Ltd]. Cynomolgus monkeys (Macaca fascicularis), weighing 2.3–3.6 kg, were used for PK (Boji Medical Technology Co., Ltd) and toxicity studies (Medicilon Biopharmaceutical Technology (Shanghai) Co., Ltd). They were housed in stainless steel cages in a controlled environment (temperature: 18–26°C; relative humidity: 40%–70%) with a 12-h light/dark cycle. All animal procedures were reviewed and approved by the respective Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee.

In vivo anti-tumor activity in a tumor xenograft model

A cell line-derived xenograft model was established by subcutaneously inoculating ~1 × 106 MC38 cells expressing human CD24 (MC38-hCD24) into the right flank of C57BL/6 J-h4-1BB mice. When the mean tumor volume reached ~100 mm3, mice (n = 8 per group) were randomized and administered intraperitoneal injections of IBD0333 at 0.3, 1, or 3 mg/kg, or phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) as a vehicle control, twice weekly for 3 weeks. Tumor dimensions were measured twice weekly using calipers, and tumor volume was calculated using the formula: volume = (length × width2)/2.The tumor growth inhibition (TGI) rate was calculated using the following formula: TGI (%) = (1 − ΔVtreated/ΔVcontrol) × 100, where ΔVtreated = Vtreated,final − Vtreated,initial; ΔVcontrol = Vcontrol,final − Vcontrol,initial represent the mean changes in tumor volume from the start to the end of treatment in the treatment and vehicle control groups, respectively.

Single-dose pharmacokinetic study

Eighteen male/female monkeys (Boji Medical Technology Co., Ltd) were assigned to three dose groups (n = 6/group). Animals received a single intravenous bolus administration of IBD0333 at doses of 10, 30, or 60 mg/kg. Blood samples were collected pre-dose (0 h) and at 5 min, 4, 10, 24, 48, 72, 120, 168, 336, 504, and 672 h post-dose. Serum was separated and stored at −80°C until analysis.

Repeated-dose: 5-week toxicity and pharmacodynamic study

Forty cynomolgus monkeys (20 males, 20 females) were randomly assigned to four groups (n = 10/group). Animals received weekly IV injections of IBD0333 at doses of 0 (vehicle control), 30, 60, or 120 mg/kg for five consecutive weeks. All animals underwent a comprehensive set of evaluations during the dosing and a subsequent 5-week recovery period. Recovery animals were necropsied at the end of the recovery period to assess the reversibility of any findings. Assessment criteria included clinical observations, body weight, food consumption, clinical pathology (hematology, serum chemistry, coagulation, urinalysis), immunophenotyping, toxicokinetics, organ weights, and histopathology.

PK assay methods

Concentrations of IBD0333 in cynomolgus monkey serum were determined using an indirect antigen-capture enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). Briefly, 96-well plates were coated with recombinant human 4-1BB protein (with His tag, ACRO Biosystems) overnight at 4°C. After blocking, diluted serum samples and standards were added and incubated. Captured IBD0333 was detected using an HRP-conjugated goat anti-human IgG antibody (Southern Biotech). The reaction was developed with TMB substrate, stopped with acid, and absorbance was read at 450 nm. A standard curve was used for quantification.

Tissue cross-reactivity

The tissue cross-reactivity profile of IBD0333 was evaluated by immunohistochemistry (IHC) on frozen tissue sections. A full panel of 31 human and 32 cynomolgus monkey tissue types (each from three individual donors) was used. Human tissues were commercially obtained (National Disease Research Interchange/Asterand Bioscience/BioIVT). Cynomolgus monkey tissues were collected from naïve animals (Hainan Jingang Biotech Co., Ltd.), and embedded. Sections were stained with biotinylated IBD0333 or biotinylated human IgG4 isotype control, followed by appropriate detection systems. MC38-hCD24 cells served as a positive control. PBS was used as a reagent control. The adequacy of all tissue samples was verified by IHC staining for the endothelial marker CD31.

Statistical analysis

The data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism 7 software, and the results are expressed as mean ± standard deviation. P value of <.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

In vitro binding activity of IBD0333 to target proteins

IBD0333 is a bispecific antibody of the IgG4 subtype targeting CD24 and 4-1BB. Its structure is designed based on an anti-CD24 IgG4 backbone, with an anti-4-1BB single-chain variable fragment (scFv) fused to the C-terminus of the heavy chain, and incorporates the S225P mutation to enhance molecular stability (Fig. 1A). This design enables the N-terminal regions of the antibody to recognize CD24, while the C-terminal scFv binds 4-1BB, thereby achieving synergistic dual-target functionality. To characterize its target-binding capabilities, we first evaluated its binding affinity to 4-1BB and CD24 proteins from different species. IBD0333 exhibited high-affinity binding to both human and cynomolgus monkey 4-1BB proteins. No binding to mouse or rat 4-1BB was detected, confirming its specificity for primate 4-1BB (Fig. 1B).

(B) Binding activity fitting curves of IBD0333 to 4-1BB proteins from different species. (C and D) The binding fluorescence signal (C) and positive cell rate (D) of IBD0333 to CHOK1-hCD24 cells. (E and F) The binding fluorescence signal (E) and positive cell rate (F) of IBD0333 to 293-CynoCD24 cells.

Due to the highly glycosylated nature of CD24, its authentic conformation is difficult to mimic with recombinant proteins in vitro. To investigate IBD0333’s binding to CD24, we engineered cells overexpressing human or monkey CD24. Flow cytometry analysis using CHOK1-hCD24 (human CD24), with a CD24 monoclonal antibody as a control, demonstrated that the binding fluorescence signal and positive cell rate continuously increased with escalating antibody concentrations, indicating that the binding of IBD0333 to CHOK1-hCD24 cells is concentration-dependent (Fig. 1C and D). Similarly, the binding fluorescence signal and positive cell rate of IBD0333 to 293-CynoCD24 cells is concentration-dependent (Fig. 1E and F).

Binding of IBD0333 to tumor cells and dual-target engagement capability

Subsequently, we performed quantitative analysis of CD24 expression levels on various tumor cell lines. MCF-7 and SKOV3 cells were identified as CD24-high (Fig. 2A), consistent with literature reports identifying them as CD24 positive cell lines [15, 16]. Based on this, we evaluated the binding ability of IBD0333 to these tumor cells. The results demonstrated that both the binding fluorescence intensity and the percentage of positive cells for IBD0333 with MCF-7 cells progressively increased with rising antibody concentrations (Fig. 2B and C), indicating that the binding of IBD0333 to MCF-7 cells is concentration-dependent. Similarly, the binding fluorescence signal and positive cell rate of IBD0333 to SKOV3 cells is concentration-dependent (Fig. 2D and E). A key dual-binding experiment confirmed that only the bispecific antibody IBD0333 produced a detectable binding signal, whereas anti-CD24 antibody, anti-4-1BB antibody, and the isotype control groups all failed to show any binding signal (Fig. 2F). This result indicates that the binding of IBD0333 to its target CD24 does not interfere with the subsequent binding of its 4-1BB arm to Human 4-1BB protein.

(B and C) The binding fluorescence signal (B) and positive cell rate (C) of IBD0333 to MCF-7 cells. (D and E) The binding fluorescence signal (D) and positive cell rate (E) of IBD0333 to SKOV3 cells. (F) Detection of IBD0333 dual-target binding activity.

Target-dependent activation of NF-κB signaling pathway and PBMCs by IBD0333

The anti-4-1BB antibody moiety (4-1BB arm) of IBD0333 acts as an agonistic antibody. Its mechanism of action mimics that of the natural 4-1BB ligand (4-1BBL), which promotes cross-linking of 4-1BB receptors and induces the formation of a “trimer-like” activating complex. This complex transmits a co-stimulatory signal that enhances T-cell activation and proliferation by triggering downstream signaling pathways, including the secretion of IL-2 and IFN-γ [17, 18]. To validate the targeting specificity of this agonistic activity, we employed a HEK-293/NF-κB-Luc/4-1BB reporter system stably expressing both human 4-1BB and an NF-κB-driven luciferase gene. Results showed that IBD0333 induced strong, dose-dependent NF-κB activation only in the presence of CD24-positive cells (CHOK1-CD24 or MCF-7), with half-maximal effective concentrations (EC50) of 0.26 and 0.57 nM, respectively (Fig. 3A and B). No significant activation was detected when CD24-negative CHO cells were used, confirming the strict CD24-dependent mechanism of its 4-1BB agonism.

IBD0333 acts as a 4-1BB agonist. (A) Luciferase expression in HEK-293/NF-κB-Luci/4-1BB cells activated by IBD0333 in the presence of CHOK1-CD24 target cells. (B) Luciferase expression in HEK-293/NF-κB-Luci/4-1BB cells activated by IBD0333 in the presence of MCF-7 target cells. (C and D) IFN-γ (C) and IL-2 (D) secretion from PBMCs stimulated by IBD0333 in co-culture with MCF-7 cells. (E) IL-2 secretion from PBMCs stimulated by IBD0333 in co-culture with MDA-MB-231 or MDA-MB-231-CD24 cells.

To evaluate the CD24-dependent immunostimulatory activity of IBD0333, we employed target cell models with differential CD24 expression levels: MCF-7 (naturally high), MDA-MB-231 (naturally low), and engineered MDA-MB-231-CD24 (overexpressing). The specific activity of IBD0333 on PBMCs was systematically assessed under these conditions. Results showed that in the presence of PBMCs, co-culture with MCF-7 cells induced significantly, dose-dependent secretion of IFN-γ and IL-2 (Fig. 3C and D). In contrast, no IL-2 release was detected upon co-culture with MDA-MB-231 cells, whereas dose-dependent IL-2 secretion was observed when co-cultured with MDA-MB-231-CD24 cells (Fig. 3E). No PBMC activation was observed in either the isotype control or monoclonal antibody control groups (Fig. 3C–E).These findings indicate that IBD0333-mediated activation of PBMC lymphocytes depends on CD24 expression on target cells, with the activation strength correlating positively with CD24 abundance, demonstrating a target-specific and expression-level-dependent mechanism of action.

Evaluation of Fc receptor interaction and effector function of IBD0333

The binding of IBD0333 (IgG4 subtype) to Fc gamma receptors and the neonatal Fc receptor was evaluated by BLI. Results showed that IBD0333 binds to human FcγRI/CD64 with a KD value of 0.2 nM, to human FcγRIIA/CD32a with a KD of 7.3 μM, and to human FcγRIIB/CD32b with a KD of 4.5 μM. It binds to human FcγRIIIA/CD16a with a KD of 5 μM, does not bind to human FcγRIIIB/CD16b, and exhibits a KD of 1.7 μM for human FcRn. These data indicated that IBD0333 displayed appropriate binding affinity for FcRn, while its binding to human FcγRIIIA [which mediates antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) activity] was attenuated a characteristic attributable to its IgG4 backbone.

The Fc region of IBD0333 is of the IgG4 subclass, which exhibits weaker binding to Fc receptors compared to the IgG1 one. The ADCC activity of IBD0333 was evaluated against two target cell lines: CD24-high MCF-7 tumor cells and 4-1BB-overexpressing engineered CHOK1-4-1BB cells, using FcγRIIIa (CD16a)-expressing NK92MI cells as effectors. Results showed that the CD24 monoclonal antibody with IgG1 subclass elicited dose-dependent ADCC against MCF-7 cells (Fig. 4A). Similarly, a 4-1BB antibody with IgG1 subclass (ISH0899-M) displayed dose-dependent ADCC activity against CHOK1-4-1BB cells (Fig. 4B). In contrast, a 4-1BB monoclonal antibody with IgG4 subclass showed no detectable ADCC (Fig. 4B). Notably, IBD0333 did not mediate NK cell-driven ADCC against either target cell line at any concentration tested. This lack of ADCC activity is likely attributable to the IgG4 isotype chosen for IBD0333.

Assessment of effector functions of IBD0333. (A and B) IBD0333-mediated ADCC activity of NK cells against CD24-high MCF-7 (A) and CHOK1-4-1BB cells (B). (C and D) IBD0333-mediated CDC activity of NK cells against MCF-7 (C) and CHOK1-4-1BB cells (D).

Furthermore, the complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC) activity of IBD0333 was assessed in the same target cell models. The results showed that an IgG1-format CD24 monoclonal antibody exhibited CDC activity against CD24-expressing MCF-7 cells (Fig. 4C), and an IgG4-format 4-1BB monoclonal antibody showed CDC activity against 4-1BB-expressing CHOK1-4-1BB cells (Fig. 4D). However, IBD0333 did not demonstrate any complement-dependent cytotoxicity against either of these cell lines.

Binding of IBD0333 to PBMC subpopulations and its B cell-dependent 4-1BB activation

The binding of IBD0333 to B cells, NK cells, and monocytes was detected using biotin-labeled IBD0333 and FITC-conjugated SA, while its binding to CD4+ T cells and CD8+ T cells was assessed using a commercial FITC-labeled F(ab’)₂goat anti-human IgG Fcγ secondary antibody, with analysis by flow cytometry. Results showed IBD0333 exhibited strong, concentration-dependent binding to B cells, with a binding strength comparable to that of a CD24 monoclonal antibody, whereas no binding signals were detected in the Human IgG1, Human IgG4, or anti-4-1BB antibody control groups (Fig. 5A). In addition, no significant binding of IBD0333 to CD4+ T cells, CD8+ T cells, NK cells, or monocytes in normal human peripheral blood was observed (Fig. 5B–E). This binding profile is consistent with previously reported expression patterns of CD24 on B cells; however, the binding activity of IBD0333 to B cells was substantially lower than its binding to CD24 expressed on tumor cells (Fig. 5A and 2B).

Binding of IBD0333 to PBMC subpopulations and its B cell-dependent 4-1BB activation. (A) Binding of IBD0333 to B cells. (B and C) Binding of IBD0333 to CD4+ T cell (B) and CD8+ T cells (C). (D and E) Binding of IBD0333 to NK (D) cells and monocyte (E). (F) B cell-dependent activation of 4-1BB signaling by IBD0333 in HEK-293/NF-κB-Luc/4-1BB reporter cells. (G) B cell-dependent induction of IFN-γ secretion from PBMCs by IBD0333.

Given the expression of CD24 on B cells and the binding activity of IBD0333 to peripheral B cells, we further investigated whether CD24 on B cells could mediate the activation of 4-1BB. Using B cells isolated from PBMCs as target cells, we evaluated the effect of IBD0333 on downstream signaling in HEK-293/NF-κB-Luci/4-1BB reporter cells. Results showed that when B cells were used as target cells, neither IBD0333, 4-1BB mAb, CD24 mAb, nor human IgG induced significant activation of luciferase expression in the reporter cells (Fig. 5F).

Furthermore, we investigated whether IBD0333, upon co-incubation with B cells, could cross-link with CD24-expressing B cells to activate 4-1BB-expressing cells in PBMCs to secrete IFNγ and subsequently exert cytotoxicity toward B cells. The results showed that when IBD0333 was co-incubated with CD24-expressing B cells, no activation of PBMCs was observed, and no IFNγ secretion was detected (Fig. 5G). These results are consistent with the B cell-dependent 4-1BB reporter gene activation assay, indicating that although CD24 is expressed on B cells, its expression level may be too low or the small size of B cells may prevent effective cross-linking with the 4-1BB-targeting arm of the antibody. This dataset supports the safety profile of IBD0333 in peripheral blood.

In vivo anti-tumor efficacy and safety of IBD0333

The antitumor efficacy of IBD0333 was evaluated in C57BL/6J-h4-1BB humanized mice bearing syngeneic MC38-hCD24 colon adenocarcinoma tumors. The results demonstrated that IBD0333 treatment resulted in dose-dependent, potent TGI. Complete tumor suppression was achieved at doses of 1 and 3 mg/kg, with TGI rates of 106.46% and 107.47%, respectively. In contrast, a lower dose of 0.3 mg/kg resulted in a 53.79% inhibition rate (Fig. 6A). No significant body weight loss, mortality, or other adverse events were observed (Fig. 6B), indicating all regimens were well tolerated.

Pharmacokinetic profile of IBD0333

A single-dose PK study of IBD0333 was conducted in cynomolgus monkeys following an IV infusion at 10, 30, and 60 mg/kg, respectively (Table 1). The results revealed that the time to peak concentration (Tmax) for the low, medium, and high dose groups was approximately 0.083 h after infusion, with an elimination half-life (T1/2) ranging from 61.5 ± 48.2 to 131 ± 84 h. The serum clearance (CL) values were 0.740 ± 0.186, 0.747 ± 0.144, and 0.750 ± 0.082 ml/h/kg, respectively, and the area under the serum concentration-time curve from time zero to the last measurable point (AUC0–last) was 23 877 715 ± 7 590 576, 86 699 132 ± 28 660 320, and 197 606 003 ± 35 231 892 ng·h/ml, respectively. Systemic exposure, as indicated by both AUC0–last and Cmax, increased in proportion to the administered dose. No marked differences were observed in serum CL between dose groups, and no sex-related differences were detected.

Tissue cross-reactivity of IBD0333

A tissue cross-reactivity study was conducted on paraffin-embedded sections from 31 human and 32 cynomolgus monkey tissue types using IHC. A total of nine identical tissues were identified in both humans and cynomolgus monkeys that exhibit binding to IBD0333, including the pituitary gland, lungs, blood vessels, stomach, ovaries, eyeballs, pancreas, parotid gland, and fallopian tubes (Table 3). This finding supports the relevance of the cynomolgus monkey as a translational species. Furthermore, in all shared tissues where binding was observed, the localization was consistently detected in the cytoplasm, suggesting that such binding is likely devoid of biological significance.

Discussion

IBD0333 is an IgG4-based bispecific antibody that simultaneously targets CD24 and 4-1BB. The antibody is constructed by fusing an anti-4-1BB scFv to the C-terminus of the heavy chain of an anti-CD24 IgG4 antibody. The molecule consists of two identical heavy chains and two identical light chains, with an S225P mutation introduced into the IgG4 Fc region to reduce half-antibody formation and improve stability. The N-terminal variable domains of the heavy and light chains mediate specific binding to CD24, whereas the C-terminal scFv domain confers specific binding to 4-1BB.

CD24 serves as a marker for cancer stem cells in multiple tumor types, including ovarian, liver, gastric, and pancreatic cancers. 4-1BB is primarily induced on activated immune cells such as NK cells, NKT cells, dendritic cells, and T cells. When 4-1BB-expressing cells engage with its natural ligand 4-1BBL to form a “trimeric” complex, they become activated and release tumor-killing cytokines, including IL-2 and IFN-γ. The bispecific antibody IBD0333 targets both CD24 and 4-1BB, thereby exerting synergistic antitumor activity through complementary mechanisms. By binding to CD24, IBD0333 anchors to tumor cells, focusing 4-1BB-mediated costimulatory signaling within the TME to enhance effector cell function while minimizing systemic immune activation. Moreover, CD24 engagement may partially disrupt the CD24-Siglec-10 immune checkpoint axis, further contributing to antitumor efficacy. Mechanistically, the anti-CD24 module of IBD0333 specifically localizes the antibody to CD24-expressing tumor cells. The anti-4-1BB module functions as an agonist, mimicking 4-1BBL to induce trimerization and activate tumor-infiltrating T cells and NK cells, which in turn secrete IL-2 and IFN-γ to mediate targeted tumor killing. As an IgG4-based bispecific antibody, the 4-1BB-dependent immune stimulation by IBD0333 requires cross-linking by CD24-high tumor cells, thus tightly controlled by the level and specificity of CD24 expression on target cells.

Our results identified that IBD0333 effectively activated the 4-1BB downstream NF-κB signaling pathway and potently induced IL-2 and IFN-γ secretion from PBMCs, but only in the presence of tumor cells with high CD24 expression (Fig. 3). This activity was strictly dependent on CD24 expression levels: cells with low CD24 expression elicited a weaker response, while CD24-negative cells failed to induce any activation (Fig. 3E). Furthermore, we found that although peripheral blood B cells express CD24 and can bind IBD0333, this interaction does not lead to 4-1BB pathway activation or significant cytokine release (Fig. 5F and G). This is likely because the relatively low expression level of CD24 on B cells, compared to that on tumor cells, or their relatively small size, is insufficient to mediate the crosslinking required for IBD0333’s agonistic activity. Collectively, these findings confirm that the “dual-target bridging” mechanism successfully localizes 4-1BB activation to the tumor site, thereby minimizing the risk of systemic immune toxicity.

Given the limited capacity of IgG4 to elicit effector immune responses and the stabilizing effect of the S225P mutation [19], we selected the IgG4 subtype carrying the S225P mutation as the antibody framework for IBD0333. Experimental results confirmed that IBD0333 exhibits appropriate binding to FcRn while showing no binding to FcγRIIIA (also known as CD16a), which mediates ADCC activity (Fig. 4), indicating favorable PK and safety profiles.

In vivo pharmacodynamic studies demonstrated that IBD0333 exhibited potent and dose-dependent antitumor efficacy: at 0.3 mg/kg, TGI was 53.79%, while doses of 1 and 3 mg/kg achieved complete tumor inhibition, with TGI rates of 106.46% and 107.47%, respectively. Safety pharmacology studies showed that no notable effects on the cardiovascular or respiratory systems were observed in cynomolgus monkeys following administration of IBD0333 within the dose range of 30–120 mg/kg.

Furthermore, while the present study has validated the potent antitumor activity and favorable safety profile of IBD0333, several limitations should be acknowledged. For one, our mechanistic studies focused exclusively on 4-1BB-mediated T and NK cell activation, and did not explore whether the CD24-targeting arm of IBD0333 could contribute to antitumor immunity by promoting macrophage-mediated phagocytosis of tumor cells—a process that may represent an additional key mechanism underlying its therapeutic efficacy. In addition, tissue cross-reactivity analyses revealed detectable binding to multiple normal tissues, including the pituitary, lung, pancreas, and other organs. Although such cytoplasmic staining may arise from technical artifacts, the possibility of bona fide low-level specific binding cannot be completely excluded. Encouragingly, our B-cell studies demonstrated that low-level CD24 engagement is likely insufficient to elicit robust 4-1BB agonistic activity. Even so, the potential risk of “on-target, off-tumor” toxicity cannot be fully ruled out. Accordingly, close monitoring for potential adverse effects remains strongly warranted in subsequent clinical development.

Conclusion

IBD0333 is an innovative bispecific antibody that combines tumor targeting through CD24 with conditional 4-1BB activation, achieving both potent antitumor activity and a favorable safety profile in preclinical models. These results strongly support its clinical translation and a clinical trial to evaluate the effect of IBD0333 in patients with advanced malignant tumors is on-going (NCT06292208).

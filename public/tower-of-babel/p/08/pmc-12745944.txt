Editorial for the special issue “Pancreatic imaging: Recent advancements and new frontiers”
Authors: Piero Boraschi
Published: 2025

The pancreas has long posed a diagnostic challenge due to its retroperitoneal location, complex anatomy, and the often-subtle early manifestations of pancreatic diseases. Over the past decade, however, the field of pancreatic imaging has undergone remarkable advancements, driven by technological innovation, multidisciplinary collaboration, and an increasing demand for early and accurate diagnosis [1].

Advances in imaging modalities – particularly high-resolution MRI, multiphasic CT, endoscopic ultrasound, and functional imaging – have markedly improved the visualization of pancreatic lesions, ductal changes, and subtle parenchymal alterations [2], [3].

This Special Issue, titled “Pancreatic Imaging: Recent Advancements and New Frontiers”, presents a collection of high-quality articles that reflect the current state of the art, showcasing the rapid progress and expanding frontiers in this field. It highlights how imaging techniques are enhancing our ability to detect and characterize pancreatic pathology with greater precision and confidence.

Of particular note is the increasing integration of artificial intelligence (AI) and radiomics into pancreatic imaging [4]. This issue underscores the growing importance of radiomics and quantitative imaging biomarkers in the assessment of pancreatic diseases. By extracting high-dimensional data from medical images, radiomics provides a non-invasive method to capture tumor heterogeneity, predict treatment outcomes, and personalize therapy.

In addition to neoplastic conditions, the issue provides an illustrative and comprehensive overview of rare cystic neoplasms, supporting differential diagnosis in complex cases where imaging often plays a decisive role [5]. It also explores atypical imaging features and discusses the clinical implications of current management algorithms for intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasms (IPMNs) [6].

Furthermore, this issue emphasizes the essential collaboration between imaging and surgical teams, underlining the importance of effective communication in interpreting post-operative anatomy and complications – an area of abdominal imaging that is often underexplored but crucial [7].

Collectively, the contributions in this Special Issue not only document the rapid advancement of pancreatic imaging technologies but also demonstrate the increasingly central role of imaging in multidisciplinary boards. From sophisticated AI applications to essential updates in oncologic and post-operative imaging, this issue captures the main features of ongoing innovation.

I am deeply grateful to all contributing authors and peer reviewers, whose expertise and dedication made this Special Issue possible. I also thank the Editorial Board of the European Journal of Radiology Open for their continued support in disseminating cutting-edge research to the radiology community.

As the field continues to evolve, the insights and innovations presented in this issue reinforce the critical role of imaging in the multidisciplinary care of pancreatic diseases. I hope these contributions will inform clinical practice, inspire future research, and ultimately improve patient outcomes.

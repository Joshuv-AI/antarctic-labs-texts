The expression of prostate-specific membrane antigen in intraductal carcinoma of the prostate
Authors: Zheng Han, Binyu Wang, Shan Peng, Yao Fu, Fengjiao Yang, Shuyue Ai, Feng Wang, Junlong Zhuang, Hongqian Guo, Xuefeng Qiu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo investigate the immunohistochemical expression of prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA) and radiological parameters of [68Ga]Ga-PSMA-11-PET/CT of intraductal carcinoma of the prostate (IDC-P). Seventy men with prostate cancer who had preoperative [68Ga]Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT scan and following radical prostatectomy were retrospectively included. Radiological parameters including maximal tumor diameter, mean standard uptake value (SUVmean), and maximal standard uptake value (SUVmax) were derived from PET/CT images. Immunohistochemistry was used to determine the PSMA expression of IDC-P. The Mann-Whitney U test was performed for continuous variables and the chi-squared test for categorical variables. Logistic regression model was used for univariate analysis to compare the correlation between IDC-P and different preoperative variables. A total of 93 lesions were identified in 70 patients. Of these lesions, 21 (22.6%) in 20 patients (28.6%) were found to accompany IDC-P. The immune response score (IRS) of IDC-P lesions was significantly lower than that of prostate adenocarcinoma (P = 0.004).In multivariate analysis, SUVmean was found to be significantly lower in patients/lesions with IDC-P (P = 0.011, OR: 0.797 [95% CI 0.669–0.948] in the per-patient analysis and P = 0.028, OR: 0.829 [95% CI0.701-0.980] in the per-lesion analysis, respectively). SUVmean showed considerable AUC value of 0.71 (95% CI 0.589–0.839) in the per-patient analysis. Our results indicated lower histopathological PSMA expression and reduced [68Ga]Ga-PSMA-11 uptake in IDC-P compared to conventional prostate adenocarcinoma, suggesting that IDC-P may exhibit distinct biological features and could offer new insights for the radiological detection of intraductal carcinoma of the prostate in clinical practice.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-19283-1.Keywords: Intraductal carcinoma of the prostate, PSMA PET/CT, HistologySubject terms: Prostate cancer, Cancer imaging

Introduction

Prostate cancer (PCa) is the second most frequently diagnosed cancer and the fifth leading cause of cancer death among men worldwide1. As one of the special pathologic types of PCa, intraductal carcinoma of prostate (IDC-P), characerised by architecturally or cytologically malignant-appearing prostatic glandular epithelium confined to prostatic ducts, has been reported to be detected in roughly 15–30% of radical prostatectomy(RP) cases, as well as in 14% of needle biopsies in prostate cancer patients2,3. The presence of IDC-P has been found to be correlated with aggressive disease4–7 and poor outcomes6,8. Recently, some evidence indicated that patients with IDC-P had unfavorable treatment response to androgen deprivation therapy (ADT)9 and docetaxel-based chemotherapy10. Therefore, it is crucial to accurately report and distinguish IDC-P from other histologic types. However, the current diagnosis of IDC-P mainly relies on histopathology.

Prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA) is a type II transmembrane glycoprotein upregulated on almost all PCa cells compared with its sparse distribution in benign prostate tissue11. PSMA ligand positron emission tomography/computed tomography (PSMA PET/CT) has been shown to be an ideal tool for the primary staging12 and restaging13 of recurrent PCa due to its good sensitivity and specificity. The effectiveness of PSMA PET/CT in detecting primary tumor lesions and evaluating treatment response has also been demonstrated14–16. However, the expression of PSMA of IDC-P has been rarely investigated. Also, the diagnostic performance of PSMA PET/CT for detecting IDC-P is totally unknown.

Therefore, this retrospective study was designed to assess the expression of PSMA of IDC-P. Patients who underwent PSMA PET/CT examination prior to RP were included. The expression of PSMA of IDC-P, acinar adenocarcinoma, and prostatic tissue was investigated by histology and PSMA PET/CT.

Materials and methods

Patients

Between January 2018 and August 2022, a total of 244 consecutive patients with localized PCa who had [68Ga]Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT scans prior to RP were retrospectively identified. The excluded criteria was set as: (a) any treatments to prostate prior to RP (hormone therapy, n = 49; TURP, n = 26);(b) without tumor specimen or with incomplete postoperative pathological sections (n = 36); (c) RP pathology Gleason score ≤ 3 + 4 (n = 35); (d) PSMA PET/CT was performed more than 2 month prior to RP (n = 28). Finally, a total of 70 patients were eligible for analysis. Our study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Drum Tower Hospital affiliated to Medical School of Nanjing University(approval 2017-147-01).

[68Ga]Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT examination

All patients were injected intravenously with [68Ga]Ga-PSMA-11 (ABX Corporation, Radeberge, Germany) at a median dose 131.72 MBq (range 130.6–177.6 MBq)60 min before scanning. Patients were required to void urine before the scanning, and no catheters or diuretics were applied. PET-CT was performed in a µMI 780 PET-CT scanner (United Imaging Healthcare (UIH), Shanghai, China). A CT scan (130 keV, 80 mAs) and static emission scans, corrected for dead time, scatter, and decay, were conducted from vertex to proximal legs. Images were acquired with a median scan time of 18 min. After the above examinations, gathered images were iteratively reconstructed and a CT-based attenuation correction was obtained by OSEM algorithm.

Histological analysis

All whole-mount prostate specimens were fixed in 10% formalin after RP, sliced at 3-mm intervals. The slices were embedded in paraffin and stained with hematoxylin-eosin and were then digitized at × 200 magnification using a whole-slide scanner (NanoZoomer S60; Hamamatsu, Japan). All digital slices were reviewed in consensus by two urological pathologists (Y. F. and S. P. with over 10 years’ experience) according to the 2014 ISUP modified criteria for PCa17. PCa Lesions on slices were delineated for further radiologic registration. IDC-P was defined as malignant epithelial cells filling large acini and prostatic ducts, with preservation of basal cells forming either: (1) solid or dense cribriform patterns or; (2) loose cribriform or micropapillary patterns with either marked nuclear atypia (nuclear size 6 x normal or larger) or comedonecrosis18. For specimens in which it was difficult to ascertain the presence or quantity of IDC-P on hematoxylin and eosin by the morphologic identification of basal cells, immunohistochemistry for p63 and high–molecular weight cytokeratin were used to assist in the determination19. For lesions containing IDC-P, the area of IDC-P were delineated for further analysis.

PET/CT imaging evaluation

All [68Ga]Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT scans were independently reviewed and evaluated with consensus by two nuclear physicians (Dr. F. W. and Dr. S. A. with more than 5 years of experiences), who were blinded to the final pathological results from RP specimens, with use of local processing workstation (United Imaging Healthcare, Shanghai). The two nuclear medicine physicians established a consensus on the PSMA PET/CT assessment criteria for prostate cancer20, with any discrepancies being resolved through collaborative discussion. Each patient was discussed in a multidisciplinary team including 2 pathologists and 2 nuclear medicine physicians with the selected pathology slide available alongside with the PET/CT data. Afterward, each case was reviewed to match each lesion outlined on the pathological slices with the corresponding PET/CT images by comparing the specific location, slide number and identifiable anatomic landmarks (e.g. urethra, ejaculatory ducts and benign prostatic hyperplasia) according to the previously described protocols21.Histologically confirmed tumor corresponding to a previously identified lesion on PET/CT was considered a visible lesion (Fig. 1). For each tumor lesion, the maximal standard uptake value (SUVmax), mean standard uptake value (SUVmean) and index tumor diameter were determined on continuous PET/CT fusion images by RadiAnt DICOM viewer, 4.2.1 (Medixant, Poznan, Poland). Tumor boundaries were automatically delineated using a fixed SUV threshold of > 3 to define the region of interest (ROI). The mean standardized uptake value (SUVmean) was then calculated across the entire threshold-based tumor volume.

Immunohistochemistry

To compare the actual pathological PSMA expression between IDC-P and adenocarcinoma of PCa, 21 lesions containing IDC-P were selected. The IDC-P area, as well as the prostate adenocarcinoma area were identified and delineated on routine hematoxylin eosinophil stained sections. One representative slide from the RP specimen harboring the largest area of IDC-P and also prostate adenocarcinoma area was chosen for further investigation. Wax block which the slide derive from was selected for re-section and then immunohistochemistry was performed. We used immunohistochemistry (IHC) with monoclonal anti-PSMA antibody (clone 1D6, 1:100, ZM-0476 of ZSGB-BIO) in blank formalin-fixed, paraffin embedded tumor sections according to the protocol from the manufacturer and finally analyzed. Staining intensity and percentage in IDC-P and prostate adenocarcinoma area were recorded respectively. The IHC results analyzed by two independent pathologists were reported as immunoreactive score (IRS) and a modified four-point IRS classification (Supplementary Table 1).

Statistical analysis

The Mann-Whitney U test was performed for continuous variables and the chi-squared test for categorical variables. Logistic regression model was used for univariate analysis to compare the correlation between IDC-P and different preoperative variables. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis was used to determine the effectiveness of imaging parameters to differentiate IDC-P and prostate adenocarcinoma. The software SPSS 25.0(IBM SPSS Statistics 25.0, https://www.ibm.com/support/pages/spss-statistics-250) was employed for statistical analysis, of which all tests were two-sided with statistical significance set at p < 0.05.

IHC was used to identify PSMA expression in all lesions containing IDC-P. The expression of PSMA between IDC-P and adjacent prostate adenocarcinoma was also compared. In each lesion containing IDC-P, the IRS score of the IDC-P area was lower than that of the prostatic adenocarcinoma area (Supplementary Table 2). Diagnostic performance of PSMA PET/CT in detecting IDC-P

Logistic regression was used to identify the potential risk factors associated with IDC-P. As shown in Table 3, SUVmean was found to be the only significant predictor of IDC-P (P = 0.011 in the per-patient analysis and P = 0.028 in the per- lesion analysis), with odds ratios (OR) of 0.797 (95% CI 0.669–0.948) and 0.829 (95% CI 0.934–1.020) in the per-patient analysis and per-lesion analysis, respectively. ROC curve was used to evaluate the diagnostic effectiveness of SUVmean in differentiating the presence IDC-P. SUVmean showed considerable AUC value of 0.71 (95% CI 0.589–0.839) in the per-patient analysis(Table 4).

Discussion

In the present study, we investigated the expression of PSMA of IDC-P using histology and [68Ga]Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT. We found IDC-P had significantly lower expression of PSMA compared with adenocarcinoma, which was further confirmed by the lower PSMA uptake on [68Ga]Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT. In addition, PSMA PET/CT showed good efficacy in detecting IDC-P, with AUC of 0.71 in per-patient analysis. To the best of our knowledge, our study is the first to assess the expression of PSMA of IDC-P, using both histology and PSMA PET/CT.

The molecular genetics of IDC-P strongly support that the vast majority of IDC-P lesions occur at an advanced stage of tumor progression, where high-grade invasive prostate cancer spreads into (colonizes) pre-existing benign prostatic ducts or acini22,23. In fact, IDC-P has been found to be present together with a concomitant Gleason pattern 4 or 5 PCa18, which is similar to the results of our study. IDC-P was found only 1 of 31 patients with Gleason score ≤ 3 + 4 according to the final pathology. Therefore, patients with Gleason score ≤ 3 + 4 were excluded from the current analysis.

PSMA is a type II transmembrane glycoprotein upregulated in almost all prostate adenocarcinoma cells (97.6%) compared with its sparse distribution in benign prostate tissue11. Increased pathological PSMA expression has been found to be associated with higher tumor grade, pathological stage, and biochemical recurrence and an independent predictor for PCa progression24–26. High-grade tumor subtypes of prostate cancer, such as those with an aggressive cribriform morphology in PCa, generally exhibit elevated PSMA expression21. However, IDC-P, as a highly aggressive PCa, showed lower histological expression of PSMA in the present study (Fig. 2), which is contrary to our initial expectation. This was further confirmed by the PSMA PET/CT imaging. Our results indicated that SUVmean of the lesions containing IDC-P was significantly lower than those lesions without IDC-P. SUVmax showed similar trend, although the difference did not reach significance. To our knowledge, no prior studies have investigated the diagnostic utility of PSMA PET for intraductal carcinoma of the prostate (IDC-P). Our study represents the first identification of an association between SUVmean values and this unique histological subtype. This observed relationship may potentially be attributed to the distinct histological architecture characteristic of IDC-P. It has been suggested that the infiltrative growth pattern of IDC-P was associated with a lower [68Ga]Ga-PSMA-11 uptake27. IDC-P is characterized by two morphological hallmarks: a dense cribriform and/or solid architecture formed by dilated growth of atypical cells, and partial preservation of basal cells around these glands28. The retention of the basal cells, which have very low expression of PSMA compared with PCa cells, might be the other reason why IDC-P shows lower expression of PSMA, as well as the lower SUVmean. However, the role of lower PSMA expression in the biology of IDC-P remains unclear, which needs to be further investigated in basic research.

The current diagnosis of IDC-P mainly relies on histopathology. However, IDC-P was only found in 2.8% of prospectively collected biopsy series29. Needle biopsies do not always accurately pick up the tissue containing IDC-P, which have led to false negative pathological diagnosis of IDC-P. Although the pathological diagnosis of prostate specimens is accurate, it is only applicable to patients undergoing RP. Multi-parameter magnetic resonance imaging (mpMRI), currently considered to be the standard imaging method for noninvasive identification and characterization of PCa30, has performed poorly in identifying IDC-P31. In the present study, we found that PSMA PET/CT-derived SUVmean could identify IDC-P with considerable AUC, which suggested that [68Ga]Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT might be applicable for the auxiliary identification of IDC-P as a non-invasive imaging tool. However, lower SUV values are not only observed in IDC-P but are also more frequently associated with low-grade prostate cancer. PSMA PET alone lacks sufficient accuracy and reliability for definitively identifying IDC-P. Therefore, suspicion of IDC-P based solely on low SUVmean values remains premature and requires comprehensive integration with clinical characteristics (elevated PSA levels, advanced clinical stage) and additional imaging features (particularly MRI findings) for accurate diagnosis. Notably, patients with a high ISUP grade but low SUVmean should not be underestimated—the possibility of IDC-P must be considered, warranting more aggressive therapeutic strategies. Given the characteristic low PSMA uptake in IDC-P, future studies should explore alternative imaging modalities, such as integrated PET/MRI, to improve diagnostic accuracy. This hybrid approach may synergize MRI’s superior soft-tissue resolution with novel PET tracers’ molecular targeting capabilities. Also, it is expected that an effective predictive model harboring PSMA/PET imaging parameters could be established for predicting IDC-P.

This study has several limitations. First, the data were retrospectively collected and radical prostatectomy specimens were used as reference standards. Therefore, there might be selection bias. Nevertheless, the final surgical specimen is the most accurate gold standard for determining pathological features. Second, our results may not apply well to patients with Gleason scores ≤ 3 + 4 because these patients were excluded from our cohort. However, the incidence of IDC-P in patients with Gleason score of ≤ 3 + 4 was found to be very low. Third, our sample size was relatively small, to compensate, we included every lesion on all images for analysis. The small cohort size precluded meaningful internal and external validation of our findings. However, our study is a preliminary study, further studies with larger sample sizes and multi-centers are necessary for verification. Fourth, Our findings demonstrate significantly lower SUVmean in IDC-P compared to control group. Nevertheless, further validation is warranted to assess its clinical utility, particularly regarding potential limitations such as false negatives. Moreover, the development of more robust biomarkers remains crucial for accurate diagnosis.

Conclusion

Our findings reveal that IDC-P shows both decreased histopathological PSMA expression and lower [68Ga]Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT uptake relative to conventional prostatic adenocarcinoma. These results suggest that IDC-P may possess distinct biological features and could provide new insights for the radiological detection of IDC-P in clinical practice.

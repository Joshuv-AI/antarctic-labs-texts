Efficacy and Safety of Zabedosertib, an Interleukin-1 Receptor-Associated Kinase 4 Inhibitor, in Patients with Moderate-to-Severe Atopic Dermatitis: A Phase II Randomized Study
Authors: Stefan J Jodl, Margitta Worm, Frauke Friedrichs, Ulrike Heinzel-Pleines, Andrea Wagenfeld, Maximilian Feldmüller, Stefan Klein, Ruiping Zhang, Kaweh Shakery, Ruth D Holzmann, Beate Rohde, Vidya Perera
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionInterleukin-1 receptor-associated kinase 4 (IRAK4) is expressed in various immune cells and regulates proinflammatory cytokine production. Its inhibition represents a novel, promising therapeutic option in the treatment of atopic dermatitis (AD). Zabedosertib (BAY1834845) is a potent, selective IRAK4 inhibitor that suppresses markers of local and systemic immune responses. This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of zabedosertib in adults with moderate-to-severe AD.MethodsDAMASK was a randomized, double-blind, 12-week, placebo-controlled, phase 2a, proof-of-concept study. Patients were randomized 2:1 to receive oral zabedosertib 120 mg twice daily or placebo. The primary efficacy endpoint was a composite of 75% reduction from baseline on the Eczema Area and Severity Index (EASI-75), no discontinuation of study medication for lack of efficacy, no rescue medication during the 4 weeks before Day 84, and no initiation of systemic AD treatment. Other efficacy assessments included validated Investigator’s Global Assessment for Atopic Dermatitis (vIGA-AD), Peak Pruritus numerical rating scale score, and affected body surface area (BSA); for safety, it included frequency and severity of treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs).ResultsOf 77 randomized patients, 69 were included in the primary efficacy analysis (zabedosertib, n = 47; placebo, n = 22); 55 patients completed treatment. At Week 12, there was no significant difference between zabedosertib and placebo in the primary efficacy endpoint (32.3% vs. 37.4%) or percentage change in EASI from baseline (− 44.6% vs. − 55.9%). There were also no significant differences between zabedosertib and placebo at Week 12 in vIGA-AD response (15.9% vs. 28.5%), Peak Pruritus response (16.4% vs. 25.0%), percentage change in Peak Pruritus (− 20.7% vs. − 27.3%), or percentage change in BSA affected by AD (− 13.3% vs. − 20.3%). No severe or serious TEAEs were reported throughout the study.ConclusionsZabedosertib was safe and well tolerated in adults with moderate-to-severe AD but showed no evidence of efficacy in reducing disease severity or pruritus in this placebo-controlled study.Trial RegistrationClinicalTrials.gov identifier, NCT05656911.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s13555-025-01505-z.Keywords: Atopic dermatitis, Controlled trial, Interleukin-1 receptor-associated kinase 4 inhibitor, IRAK4, Kinase inhibition, Randomized

Key Summary Points

Why carry out the study? Zabedosertib is a potent and selective interleukin-1 receptor-associated kinase 4 (IRAK4) inhibitor with proven anti-inflammatory effect in humans.DAMASK was a phase 2a proof-of-concept study that evaluated the efficacy and safety of zabedosertib in adults with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis (AD). What was learned from the study? Zabedosertib was safe and well tolerated but showed no evidence of efficacy in reducing disease severity and pruritus.Other immune-mediated diseases with different molecular involvement of the innate immune system (specifically IRAK4) than AD may still benefit from zabedosertib.

Introduction

Interleukin (IL)-1 receptor-associated kinase 4 (IRAK4) is the key isoform of a four-kinase family regulating the production of proinflammatory cytokines. IRAK4 is the first of the four kinases that mediates the intracellular signal transduction pathway used by innate immune cell receptors, including IL-1 receptor (IL-1R), IL-18R, IL-33R, and Toll-like receptors (TLRs) except TLR3 [1]. When these receptors are bound with multiple exogenous ligands, endogenous stimuli, or proinflammatory cytokines, they initiate the first wave of inflammatory signals, with innate immune responses propagating and amplifying these signals [1–3]. These receptors are constitutionally expressed in a variety of innate immune cells and other cell types including macrophages, fibroblasts, monocytes, and endothelial cells. Inhibition of IRAK4, a key component of TLR/IL-1R signaling, may lead to suppression of the inflammatory process and amelioration of eczema symptoms and pruritus in atopic dermatitis (AD) [4], as shown in a previous study that reported the effect of IRAK4 inhibition on attenuating skin inflammatory processes in a variety of AD-like animal models [4]. This, combined with the potential for a low rate of infectious skin complications, makes IRAK4 inhibition a promising therapeutic option for AD management [1, 2, 4].

Zabedosertib (BAY1834845) is a potent, selective IRAK4 inhibitor with high oral bioavailability and favorable oral pharmacokinetic profiles across species in preclinical studies [1, 5]. A comprehensive phase 1 program for zabedosertib has been conducted, including a single ascending dose study (NCT03054402), a multiple ascending dose study (NCT03493269), a microtracer study (NCT03244462), and a drug-drug interaction study (EudraCT number 2020-005080-31), confirming its favorable safety and pharmacokinetic profiles [6]. In a mechanistic study of healthy male volunteers, zabedosertib 120 mg twice daily (BID) (the same dose as applied in the present study) significantly suppressed markers of local and systemic immune responses, including tumor necrosis factor alpha (TNF-α), IL-6, C-reactive protein, procalcitonin, and IL-8, after imiquimod-induced skin inflammation and systemic lipopolysaccharide (LPS) challenge [5]. In both challenges, the positive effect of zabedosertib on local and systemic immune response was similar or better than for prednisolone. These promising data support the anti-inflammatory effect of IRAK4 inhibition by zabedosertib in humans and, together with further preclinical data [data on file], suggest that IRAK4 is an attractive therapeutic target for immune-mediated diseases, including AD.

Despite existing and emerging therapeutic options, the treatment of moderate-to-severe AD remains challenging, and novel, efficacious, safe, and targeted treatment alternatives are required. Conventional systemic immunosuppressants, such as cyclosporin, methotrexate, and azathioprine, are associated with relapse after treatment withdrawal and a range of toxicities, including nephrotoxicity, myelosuppression, and gastrointestinal symptoms [7]. Biologic agents targeting IL-4 and/or IL-13 require parenteral administration and are associated with conjunctivitis and limited efficacy in AD, with a significant number of patients failing to achieve 75% reduction from baseline on the Eczema Area and Severity score (EASI-75) [8–10]. Inhibitors of Janus kinase signaling are associated with infrequent but important side effects, notably an increased risk of severe infections, non-melanoma skin cancer, and thromboembolism [11–14]. Moreover, according to routine patient management, these agents require continuous blood monitoring for hepatic transaminases, neutrophil and lymphocyte count, and lipids before treatment initiation and thereafter; there is also a risk of drug-drug interactions.

To evaluate the efficacy and safety of zabedosertib in AD, we conducted a phase 2 study in adults with moderate-to-severe AD who were inadequately controlled by topical corticosteroids (TCS) or for whom TCS were not medically advisable.

Methods

Study Design

The DAMASK study (NCT05656911) was a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, phase 2a, proof-of concept study consisting of a screening period of up to 28 days, a 12-week intervention period, and a 4-week follow-up period (Fig. 1). This study was conducted in compliance with the International Council for Harmonisation Good Clinical Practice, including the archiving of essential documents. Informed consent was obtained before initiation of any study-related procedures. The study was conducted from December 21, 2022, to January 31, 2024, at 18 sites in 6 countries in Europe (Germany, France, Italy, Poland, Czech Republic, and the UK) and at 3 sites in the US.

At least 7 days before randomization, patients were required to use a stable amount of topical emollient to moisturize the skin, applied to the whole body BID and continued during the study. Emollients used by the patients before entering the study were to be continued with the same frequency and intensity. During the same 7-day period, patients recorded the daily intensity of peak pruritus using a 0–10 numerical rating scale (NRS) on an electronic handheld device.

Patients were then randomized in a 2:1 ratio to receive oral zabedosertib 120 mg BID or matching placebo. The selected dose of 120 mg BID was the dose that provided a maximum plasma exposure (higher doses of zabedosertib tested in phase 1 did not provide higher exposure levels [6]; it had shown distinct efficacy in human in vivo systemic and topical skin challenges, achieving a skin exposure of approximately 50% of plasma exposure levels [5]. This dose also covered the anticipated therapeutic exposure in various preclinical inflammation models [1, data on file]. Zabedosertib was also effective in reducing the Canine Atopic Dermatitis Extent and Severity Index and itch in two field studies in dogs with canine atopic dermatitis (CAD), a relevant model for AD due to its similarities to its human counterpart [15, data on file].

In the event of a disease flare or AD symptoms, predefined topical rescue medication could be used for several days at the discretion of the investigator. Disease flare was defined as a validated Investigator’s Global Assessment for Atopic Dermatitis (vIGA-AD) score ≥ 3 requiring escalation of treatment confirmed by an investigator, which could not be tolerated by the patient. Before rescue medication was initiated, increasing the frequency of emollient use was encouraged to try to control AD symptoms, and efficacy assessments were performed. The preferred rescue medication included was methylprednisolone aceponate cream or an alternative TCS with similar potency if methylprednisolone aceponate was not available in the patient’s country. If TCS were not tolerated or their use was not advisable, a topical calcineurin inhibitor (pimecrolimus 1% cream or tacrolimus 0.1% or 0.03% ointment) was also allowed. Rescue medication could be used for a maximum of 14 days, and the product, dose, frequency, and duration of therapy were recorded. Patients who received systemic corticosteroids or non-steroidal systemic immunosuppressive or biologic drugs for AD had to discontinue study treatment.

Patients

Patients eligible for the study were adults (age 18–65 years) with a diagnosis of AD for at least 1 year at the screening visit. Patients were required to have moderate-to-severe AD at randomization, defined as Eczema Area and Severity Index (EASI) score ≥ 16, affected body surface area (BSA) ≥ 10%, vIGA-AD score ≥ 3, and average Peak Pruritus NRS ≥ 4 in the 7 days before randomization. Patients had to have a documented history of inadequate response to treatment with TCS or were unsuitable for TCS treatment (e.g., because of important side effects or safety or tolerability considerations).

Patients were excluded if they had received: topical or systemic antihistamines or topical treatments for AD within 7 days before randomization, systemic immunosuppressive or immunomodulating therapy within 4 weeks before randomization, or biologic drugs within five half-lives or 12 weeks before randomization, whichever was longer. Full inclusion and exclusion criteria are provided in Supplementary Tables S2 and S3.

Assessments

The primary efficacy endpoint was a composite of EASI-75, no discontinuation of study medication for reasons related to lack of efficacy, no rescue medication use during the 4 weeks before Day 84, and no use of systemic AD treatment. Secondary endpoints included: vIGA-AD response at Week 12, defined as vIGA 0 or 1 with an improvement of ≥ 2 points from baseline; weekly average Peak Pruritus NRS response at Week 12, defined as an improvement (reduction) from baseline of ≥ 4 points in the weekly average Peak Pruritus NRS score, recorded daily by the patient on a handheld device; percentage change in EASI score and weekly average Peak Pruritus NRS percentage change from baseline at Week 12; and absolute change in BSA affected by AD. Blood samples for pharmacokinetic assessment of zabedosertib and its pharmacologically inactive metabolite (BAY 2822815) were taken at each study visit during the treatment phase and at end of treatment.

Safety assessments included recording of adverse events (AEs), clinical laboratory values, vital signs, and electrocardiogram parameters. Confirmed or suspected severe invasive bacterial infections, systemic hypersensitivity reactions, non-invasive infections of the skin, and unexplained cases of rhabdomyolysis were prespecified as AEs of special interest (AESIs) based on the known mode of action of zabedosertib and findings from other IRAK4 inhibitors under development [16].

Sample Size Calculation and Statistical Analysis

Assuming an EASI-75 responder rate in zabedosertib-treated patients of 49% and in placebo-treated patients of 16%, evaluable data from 57 patients (zabedosertib, n = 38; placebo, n = 19) would provide ≥ 90% power to establish superiority of zabedosertib. Based on these assumptions, planned enrollment was 72 patients to obtain 57 evaluable patients overall.

Patients were analyzed according to the treatment they actually received. The main efficacy analysis was performed on the per-protocol set (PPS). These analyses assessed efficacy based on ≥ 80% compliance with study intervention and ≥ 8 weeks length of the treatment phase. Use of rescue medication in the PPS was considered an intercurrent event indicating lack of efficacy after Day 56 to account for a potential late onset of treatment effect. For supplementary analyses on the full analysis set (FAS), use of rescue medication was considered an intercurrent event starting from Day 1 onwards; these analyses were also more conservative regarding the effect size (see Supplementary Statistical Methods section for the exact definitions of PPS and FAS).

The main estimand for the primary and secondary responder endpoints (PPS) was the estimated difference in the proportion of responders between 120 mg zabedosertib and placebo. Use of topical rescue medication from Day 56 onwards, use of systemic standard of care for AD, and discontinuation of treatment due to lack of efficacy were handled as non-responses (composite strategy). Please refer to Supplementary Table S1 for handling of further intercurrent events. Bayesian inference was used to quantify the difference in responder frequencies.

The main analysis for the continuous secondary efficacy endpoints was performed on the PPS using the same strategies to address intercurrent events as described for the primary and secondary binary endpoints above. The mean difference between the treatment arms was estimated using analysis of covariance models. Please see Supplementary Statistical Methods for details.

Safety endpoints were analyzed in the safety analysis set (SAS), which included all patients who received at least one dose of zabedosertib or placebo.

Ethical Approval

The study was performed in accordance with the Helsinki Declaration of 1964 and its later amendments. All participating study sites received approval from an institutional review board (Germany: AM-2022-017, Ethik-Kommission bei der Landesärztekammer Baden-Württemberg, Liebknechtstraße 33, 70565 Stuttgart, Germany). All subjects provided informed consent to participate in the study.

Results

Patient Disposition and Baseline Characteristics

In total, 77 patients were randomized to treatment (zabedosertib, n = 52; placebo, n = 25), of whom 69 were eligible for efficacy analyses on the PPS (zabedosertib, n = 47; placebo, n = 22) and 74 on the FAS (zabedosertib, n = 51; placebo, n = 23) (Supplementary Figure S1). Baseline demographics were generally well balanced between the treatment arms (Table 1), as were AD severity and other AD-related baseline characteristics (Table 2). At baseline (PPS), ongoing medical history findings were present in 91.3% of patients, most often in the system organ class of respiratory, thoracic, and mediastinal disorders (68.1%; allergic rhinitis [47.8%], asthma [46.4%]) and immune system disorders (53.6%; mostly allergies). All patients had used medication for the treatment of AD within 6 months before the start of study drug intervention, including 20 patients (29.0%) who used systemic medication, mostly glucocorticoids (n = 13; 18.8%) and calcineurin inhibitors (n = 5; 7.2%). Nearly all patients (95.7%) had applied topical medication before treatment. Among them, TCS of different strengths (weak to very potent) were used alone or in combination with antiseptics, antibiotics, or other topical medications, and 14 patients (20.3%) had applied topical calcineurin inhibitors.

In total, 36 patients (69.2%) receiving zabedosertib and 19 receiving placebo (76.0%) completed the treatment phase (Fig. 2). Overall treatment duration was similar between the zabedosertib and placebo arms (PPS), with ~ 75% of patients taking the study intervention for ≥ 78 days. Based on electronic daily patient diaries, compliance with study drug intake was very high (mean > 98%) in both treatment arms, with no decrease in compliance observed during treatment and in the 4 weeks before end of treatment.

Patient disposition

Efficacy

At Week 12, there was no significant difference between zabedosertib and placebo in EASI-75 response (32.3% vs. 37.4%) (Fig. 3) or percentage change in EASI from baseline (− 44.6% vs. − 55.9%) (Supplementary Figure S2). There were also no significant differences between zabedosertib and placebo in vIGA-AD response (15.9% vs. 28.5%) (Fig. 4), Peak Pruritus NRS response (16.4% vs. 25.0%) (Fig. 5), percentage change in Peak Pruritus response (− 20.7% vs. − 27.3%) (Supplementary Figure S3), or change in BSA affected by AD (− 13.3% vs. − 20.3%) (Supplementary Figure S4) at Week 12.

Overall, at the end of the treatment phase, the cumulative rates of intercurrent events indicative for treatment non-response (use of rescue medication at or after Day 56, use of systemic standard of care for AD, or discontinuation of study intervention due to lack of efficacy) were 0.236 for zabedosertib and 0.136 for placebo (Supplementary Figure S5).

Biomarkers

Biomarkers from plasma were evaluated in samples collected before and at the end of treatment to determine the impact of study intervention. A variety of general markers of inflammation and AD-specific markers from different signaling pathways were analyzed, e.g., IL-31/33, IL-4, IL-6, TNFα, chemokine (C-X-C motif) ligand 17, and thymic stromal lymphopoietin. No relevant difference in the ratio to baseline between zabedosertib and placebo was observed.

Within an optional substudy, a small subset of skin biopsies from lesional skin was obtained from patients in the zabedosertib arm (n = 4). Differential gene expression analysis (customized Host Response Panel; Nanostring Technologies) provided no significantly different gene expression in lesional skin at baseline or end of treatment.

Safety

Zabedosertib was safe and well tolerated, with no severe or serious treatment-emergent AEs (TEAEs) reported during the study in any treatment arm (Table 3). In addition, there were no relevant differences between zabedosertib and placebo in the percentage of patients with TEAEs assessed by the investigator as related to study intervention, AE intensity, or AEs leading to discontinuation of study drug. The most frequently reported TEAEs were in the System Organ Classes (SOCs) of infections and infestations and gastrointestinal disorders, while the most frequently reported study drug-related TEAEs were in the SOC of infections and infestations (Table 4). Four patients discontinued study treatment because of TEAEs: one in the placebo arm (hypersensitivity) and three in the zabedosertib arm (one case each of hordeolum, eczema, and palatal swelling). Three treatment-emergent AESIs were reported: one case of non-invasive skin infection in each treatment arm (both MedDRA preferred terms: pustule) and one case of systemic hypersensitivity reaction in the placebo arm. No clinically relevant differences in the change of vital signs, weight/body mass index, electrocardiogram parameters, and laboratory values were observed between zabedosertib and placebo.

One female patient reported a positive pregnancy test 28 days after last intake of study intervention, which was confirmed in an unscheduled visit 6 days later. One male patient reported the pregnancy of his partner after having received study intervention for 87 days. Both cases concerned patients in the placebo arm of the study.

Discussion

The DAMASK study was the first placebo-controlled and adequately powered trial assessing zabedosertib, an IRAK4 inhibitor, in patients with moderate-to-severe AD. In this phase 2a, proof-of concept trial, there was no significant difference between zabedosertib and placebo in the primary endpoint of EASI-75 response at Week 12. Consistent with this result, vIGA-AD response and Peak Pruritus NRS response also showed no statistical difference compared with placebo. In line with the primary analysis, all sensitivity and supplementary analyses showed no effect of zabedosertib in the analyzed endpoints, emphasizing the robustness of the analysis result.

Also, descriptive subgroup analyses considering disease severity, demographics, and previous systemic treatments did not reveal any hints for a treatment response. Response rates in the placebo arm were high in the present study, with 37.4% of patients in the placebo arm achieving an EASI-75 response and 28.5% achieving a vIGA-AD response. In line with our findings, fluctuating placebo response rates were also observed in other recent AD studies, underlining the changeable nature of the disease [17, 18]. The possibility to use provided topical rescue medication (if required) and the standardized use of emollients with daily reminders via a handheld device may have contributed to this observation in the current study. However, the high placebo response rate only had a limited impact on the overall interpretation of the main study endpoint, given the low proportion of responders in the active arm.

Despite the numerically higher number of TEAEs in the treatment arm, no severe or serious TEAEs were reported in any arm. The incidence of study drug-related AEs assessed by the investigator was comparable between the two arms. Compared to the data from a pooled analysis of the safety of the approved IL-4 receptor blocker dupilumab in AD [19], the present study demonstrated a lower overall incidence of AEs. Therefore, zabedosertib appears to be safe and well tolerated.

A potential explanation of the negative result of the DAMASK study is that the plasma exposure levels of zabedosertib achieved in patients with AD were not high enough to inhibit IRAK4 in a clinically meaningful manner. In contrast to the drug exposure in the CAD studies, human zabedosertib exposure in plasma after oral administration showed a ceiling effect, and maximum reachable systemic levels were lower than those reported in the CAD studies (data on file). However, it is of note that preclinical modeling for the human dose of 120 mg zabedosertib BID showed a target coverage of approximately 80–90% based on in vitro data (data on file). This was also demonstrated by a proof-of-mechanism study with zabedosertib in human healthy subjects using the same dose regimen, which showed a clear and significant signal of anti-inflammatory pharmacodynamics after a systemic LPS challenge, with 80% reduction of TNF-α and 73% reduction of IL-6 compared with placebo and distinct reduction of other pro-inflammatory markers such as C-reactive protein and procalcitonin [5]. In addition, the same study also reported relevant skin exposure measured in skin suction blisters, with ~ 50% availability of zabedosertib in the skin compartment vs. plasma exposure and statistically significant anti-inflammatory treatment effects after a local imiquimod skin challenge on skin erythema and skin microperfusion [5]. Therefore, we consider that insufficient human exposure levels were unlikely to explain the negative outcomes of this study.

Another possible explanation is that the inhibition of IRAK4 is not relevant enough to produce clinically meaningful anti-inflammatory effects in the pathophysiology of AD. AD is characterized by T2-dominated cytokine responses, including IL-4, IL-13, and IL-31, driving skin barrier dysfunction, inflammation, and pruritus [20, 21]. The lack of a direct effect on these adaptive immune pathways may therefore explain the limited clinical efficacy of zabedosertib in AD, despite earlier reports of polymorphisms in the IRAK4 gene in atopic conditions [22] and reversed pathogenic molecular signatures in AD biopsies after IRAK4 inhibition [4].

Considering the limitations of the mode of action in addressing AD pathophysiology but positive study data in rheumatoid arthritis with another IRAK4-inhibitor [23], targeting other conditions where MyD88-dependent TLR/IL-1R signaling is more prominent, such as cutaneous lupus or pustular psoriasis, may hold promise by influencing this pathway. In vivo studies using murine models have demonstrated the therapeutic potential of IRAK4 inhibition for the treatment of lupus [24, data on file]. Moreover, hidradenitis suppurativa (HS) is found to be associated with activation of the TLR/IL-1R pathway and an overexpression of IRAK4 [24–27]. After mixed data in two clinical studies investigating IRAK4 as a target in HS [28, 29], a phase 2 study with an IRAK4 degrader was conducted to explore the role of IRAK4 degradation in this condition [30], with no results published so far.

In addition, the activation of counterregulatory pro-inflammatory pathways could provide an alternative explanation of this negative result. Although there were clear effects of zabedosertib reported in two uncontrolled field studies in CAD (data on file), and although several similarities between human AD and CAD have been described [15], the cross-species translation of IRAK4 inhibition to human AD remains uncertain.

Lastly, the binding mode of zabedosertib to IRAK4 may theoretically have contributed to the negative results. While zabedosertib solely inhibits the kinase activity of IRAK4, it does not affect its scaffolding function, unlike IRAK4 degraders, which are expected to inhibit both activities. At the time of writing this manuscript, studies with IRAK4 degraders in several immune-mediated indications including AD are ongoing. They will provide further insights about the precise role of IRAK4 in AD and show whether IRAK4 degradation is able to promote clinical efficacy in AD, which may in turn provide explanation for the suboptimal efficacy of IRAK4 inhibition by zabedosertib in the present study.

Conclusion

Zabedosertib was safe and well tolerated in adults with moderate-to-severe AD but showed no evidence of efficacy in terms of disease severity and pruritus in the DAMASK study. Considering the overall clinical and preclinical data on zabedosertib and its good safety and tolerability profile, other immune-mediated diseases with a different pathophysiological impact of the innate immune system (specifically IRAK4), including other inflammatory skin diseases, may still benefit from the oral IRAK4 inhibitor zabedosertib.

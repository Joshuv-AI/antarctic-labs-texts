Diagnostic value of the perfusion mismatch ratio in identifying intracranial atherosclerotic disease related occlusion
Authors: Tingyu Yi, Zhi-nan Pan, Lan Hong, Weifeng Huang, Ding-lai Lin, Zhijiao He, Shuyi Liu, Shujuan Gan, Fenglong Lang, Yi-ning Yang, Jinhua Ye, Huanghuang Chen, Dapeng Sun, Xin Cheng, Zhongrong Miao, Wen-huo Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe ability to differentiate intracranial atherosclerotic disease (ICAD) related large vessel occlusion (LVO) from embolism is critical for stroke management. We hypothesized that the mismatch ratio derived from the automated computed tomography perfusion (CTP) could predict underlying ICAD.MethodsPatients with acute ischemic stroke (AIS) and LVO from prospective registry databases who underwent CTP were included in the derivation cohort (n=1100). The mismatch ratio, calculated as the ratio of the hypo-perfused volume to the infarct core volume by software, was defined. Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) analysis was performed to assess the predictive performance of the mismatch ratio for ICAD, and logistic regression analysis was used to identify independent predictors of LVO associated with underlying ICAD. External validation was conducted using cohorts from two other stroke centers (n=385).ResultsIn the derivation cohort, 390 patients were classified as ICAD and 720 as embolism. The ICAD group had a higher mismatch ratio (9.8 vs 3.6, P<0.001). The mismatch ratio outperformed age and National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score in predicting ICAD (area under the curve (AUC), 0.77 vs 0.36 vs 0.28, P<0.001). The ROC curve had a best cut-off of 7.1 for predicting ICAD, which was an independent predictor of ICAD-related occlusion (adjusted odds ratio (aOR) 5.43, 95% CI 3.68 to 8.03), with 68% sensitivity and 76% specificity. These results were validated in an external cohort (AUC=0.78; 95% CI, 0.73 to 0.83).ConclusionThe perfusion mismatch ratio may be an optimal and simple predictor of anterior circulation ICAD- related LVO before endovascular treatment (EVT). When this ratio was ≥7.1, ICAD was five times more likely than embolism.

Introduction

Endovascular treatment (EVT) is the standard treatment for acute ischemic stroke (AIS) caused by intracranial large vessel occlusion (LVO).1 The two main causes of LVO are cardiac embolism and intracranial atherosclerotic disease (ICAD),2 3 and EVT strategies differ for these etiologies. Aspiration thrombectomy as a first-line strategy is suitable for embolic LVO but not for ICAD related LVO,4 emergent angioplasty as a first-line strategy is suitable for ICAD related LVO but not for embolic LVO5 . The BASIS technique is effective and safe as a first-line strategy for ICAD related LVO,6 administration of intravenous or intraarterial tirofiban7 is often needed in cases with ICAS related LVO. Therefore, recognizing ICAD-related LVO before EVT is crucial but clinically challenging.

On the other hand, computed tomography perfusion (CTP) is increasingly used in AIS patients, and quantitative CTP processed by software such as MISTAR8 or RAPID, can identify the infarct core and hypo-perfused area. ICAD patients often have better collateral circulation than embolic patients due to chronic cerebral ischemia.9 As a result, ICAD patients may present with a large hypoperfusion area and a small infarct core, leading to a higher ratio of hypoperfused volume to core volume (mismatch ratio) compared with embolic patients. We hypothesized that the mismatch ratio could predict underlying ICAD. This study aimed to test this hypothesis and determine the optimal mismatch ratio for predicting ICAD.

Methods

Patient population and clinical data collection

This study is based on a prospective, hospital-based registry of consecutive LVO patients treated with EVTs. Data on patient characteristics, baseline CTP, medical and endovascular interventions, periprocedural management, and clinical outcomes were collected in an electronic database after hospital admission. The study protocol was approved by the local hospital’s institutional review board. Written informed consent for EVT and registry enrollment was obtained from relatives, but the requirement was waived for this study due to its retrospective and observational nature. The ethics committees of Zhangzhou Municipal hospital approved the study, the ID was 2022KYB066.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria for the study were: (1) AIS patients with occlusion of the intracranial internal carotid artery or M1 segment of the middle cerebral artery; (2) Patients who underwent EVT; and (3) Patients who underwent CTP before EVT. Exclusion criteria included: (1) Tandem occlusion (simultaneous extracranial and distal intracranial artery occlusion); (2) Occlusion caused by factors other than large artery atherosclerosis (LAA) and embolic; or (3) Onset-to-presentation time exceeding 24 hours.

Definitions of ICAD

ICAD was determined based on the following criteria: a significant fixed focal stenosis at the site of occlusion observed on final angiography or during EVT, with exclusion of dissection and vasospasm as causative factors; the Trial of ORG 10172 in Acute Stroke Treatment (TOAST) subtype was classified as LAA10; ICAD was independently reviewed and determined by two experienced neurointerventionists (Dr. Lin and Z. Pan), who were blinded to the mismatch ratio results. In cases of disagreement, the findings were re-examined, and disputes were resolved through consensus.

Imaging

Perfusion maps and ischaemic core volumes were processed using an automated image postprocessing system (MIStar software, Apollo Medical Imaging, Inc., Melbourne, VIC, Australia). The delayed time (DT>3 s) was defined as the amount of hypoperfused brain tissue. The ischemic core volume was defined as the volume with relative cerebral blood flow (rCBF)<30%. The mismatch ratio was calculated by MIStar software8 as the volume of brain tissue with DT>3 s divided by the volume with rCBF<30%. When the infarct core was zero, the mismatch ratio was considered infinite. For statistical analysis, infinite values were counted as 1000.

Clinical and radiological assessment

The National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) was used to assess patients’ neurological functional deficits. The extended Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction (eTICI) scale was used to evaluate reperfusion status, with an eTICI score of 2b-3 indicating successful reperfusion.11 The modified Rankin scale (mRS) score was utilized to assess clinical outcomes at 90 days, where a score of ≤2 was considered a good outcome. Symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH) was defined as any hemorrhage associated with an increase in the NIHSS score of ≥4 points.12 The occlusion site was categorized as either the intracranial intracerebral artery (ICA) or the middle cerebral artery (MCA).

Statistical analysis

Quantitative variables are presented as the mean± standard deviation (SD) or median (interquartile range (IQR)), while categorical variables are expressed as a number (N (%)). Patients were divided into ICAD and embolic groups, and study variables were compared between the two groups. Differences in continuous variables were assessed using Student’s t-test or the Mann-Whitney U test, as appropriate. Differences in categorical variables were evaluated via the Chi-squared test (χ2) test. The relationship between the mismatch ratio and ICAD diagnosis was analyzed using regression modeling. Variables with P values <0.1 in univariate analysis were entered into a multivariate stepwise regression model for ICAD diagnosis.

To evaluate the diagnostic capacity of different features for distinguishing ICAD from embolic, the area under the curve (AUC) for each feature was calculated. The DeLong test was used to compare the AUC of the mismatch ratio with other parameters. Based on current statistical consensus, an AUC of 0.8 to 0.9 is considered very good, 0.7 to 0.8 as adequate, and below 0.7 as poor. The external validity of the optimal mismatch ratio for diagnostic value was assessed in patient cohorts with LVO undergoing EVT at two stroke centers ((Fujian Union Hospital and Huashan Hospital). These retrospective analyses were conducted on prospectively collected data from each site. The predictive performance of the mismatch ratio in all external cohorts was similarly assessed by AUC using the aforementioned criteria. All statistical inferences were two-sided, and P<0.05 was defined as significant. SPSS (version 15.0; SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) was used for all the statistical calculations.

Results

Patient characteristics in derivation cohort

Patients were retrospectively screened and selected by reviewing a prospectively designed hospital-based electronic database (Nalong 2.0, Xiamen, Fujian, China). From September 2018 to June 2023, the electronic medical records of 2236 patients with acute LVO in the anterior circulation who underwent emergent EVT were consecutively screened (figure 1). After excluding patients for the following reasons: (1) Onset-to-presentation time exceeding 24 hours (n=294); (2) Non-LAA or embolic etiology (n=349); (3) Tandem occlusion (n=226); (4) Distal medium vessel occlusion including M2 and M3 (n=101); (5) One or more chronic LVO (n=97); and (6) Acute multiple distal vessel occlusion (n=59), a total of 1110 patients (mean age 69±12 years; M:F 623:487) were included.

The performance of the mismatch ratio in predicting ICAD related occlusion in derivation cohort and external cohort

The baseline mismatch ratio demonstrated superior predictive performance compared with age and the NIHSS score (AUC, 0.77 vs 0.36 vs 0.28, P<0.001). Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis identified a cut-off value of 7.1 for predicting ICAD, with 76% sensitivity and 68% specificity (figure 2). A mismatch ratio ≤1.8 yielded 97% specificity for predicting embolic related LVO (only a 3% possibility of ICAD related occlusion) (figure 3), while a ratio ≤2 achieved 95% specificity for predicting embolic related LVO (only a 5% possibility of ICAD related occlusion) (figure 3). As the mismatch ratio increased, the likelihood of ICAD also increased (figure 4). When the mismatch ratio reached 39.5, 95% specificity for predicting ICAD related occlusion was achieved (only a 5% likelihood of embolic related LVO) (figure 3). After adjustment for age, sex, hypertension, diabetes, atrial fibrillation, rheumatic heart disease, smoking, and baseline NIHSS score, a mismatch ratio ≥7.1 was identified as a significant predictor of ICAD related occlusion (adjusted odds ratio (aOR) 5.43, 95% CI 3.68 to 8.03) (table 2).

Discussion

The primary finding of our study is that the perfusion mismatch ratio, defined as the ratio of hypoperfused tissue volume to infarct core volume, is strongly associated with the TOAST subtype of LVO. Specifically, a higher mismatch ratio suggests a greater likelihood of ICAD-related occlusion.

ICAD-related occlusions often involve a small infarct core and large mismatch volume due to relatively good collateral resulting from stimulation by chronic stenosis.9 Consequently, a higher mismatch ratio was associated with a greater likelihood of ICAD related occlusion, as confirmed in our study. Our study showed that when the mismatch ratio was ≥40, the possibility of ICAD related LVO exceeded 90%. Conversely, when the mismatch ratio was ≤2, the possibility of ICAD-related LVO was less than 10%. Moreover, ICAD-related LVO was five times more likely than embolic when the mismatch ratio was ≥7.1.

There are several methods to differentiate ICAD from embolic, including: (a) Composite scale such as ABC2D score13; (b) Preprocedural CT or magnetic resonance (MR) image, such as hyperdense artery sign (HAS) on plain CT, susceptibility vessel sign (SVS)14 15 on MRI; (c) Preprocedural computed tomography angiography (CTA) image, such as leptomeningeal collaterals derived from CTA,16 thrombus enhancement on thin-slab maximum-intensity projection of CTA17; (d) Preprocedural CTP image: such as Tmax > 4 s divided by Tmax >6 s ≥2,18 Tmax >10 s divided by Tmax >6 s,19 severe hypoperfusion growth rate Tmax >10 s divided by onset-to-imaging time;20 (e) Digital subtraction angiography (DSA) image, such as occlusive clot signs21 on DSA, the microcatheter “first pass effect”,22 and the stent unsheathing effect23 during the procedure.

Compared with the predictive value of the mismatch ratio, the parameters mentioned above have some limitations. First, the correct interpretation of certain signs, such as the HAS on plain CT, thrombus enhancement, and occlusive clot signs on DSA, is highly dependent on the reader’s experience. Second, the SVS sign can only be obtained from MRI scans, which are not routinely performed in most stroke centers. Moreover, SVS can be observed in ICAD related occlusions with distal thrombus formation, which is not uncommon in clinical settings in Asian countries. Third, some parameters, such as leptomeningeal collaterals, are not objective and their grading depends on the reader’s subjective judgment. Fourth, signs like the microcatheter “first pass effect” and the stent unsheathing effect can only be observed during the procedure, and by that time most endovascular devices have already been used, potentially leading to incorrect treatment direction. Fifth, the ABC2D score includes five parameters. The NIHSS score is not objective and can vary by one or two points when assessed by different evaluators; the detection of HAS is dependent on the reader’s experience and the slice of the CT scan; and paroxysmal atrial fibrillation may be missed at admission. All of these factors can affect the predictive value of the score.

Three previous studies have explored the use of CTP derived parameters to predict.18 19 Diogo C first reported that Tmax>4 s/Tmax>6 s could predict ICAD, with Tmax>4 s/Tmax>6 s ≥2 showing 73% specificity and 52% sensitivity. Dapeng Sun et al20 noted that severe hypoperfusion volume growth rate, defined as the volume with Tmax>10 s divided by onset-to-imagine time, could predict ICAD, with AUC 0.86, the optimal cut-off value was ≤11.2 mL/h (sensitivity, 78.95%; specificity, 77.06%; accuracy 77.94%). Takeshi et al19 found that a mismatch ratio of Tmax>10 s/>6 s was the best predictor of ICAD. Compared with these three studies, our study offers several advantages. First, the simplicity of the parameter we used is a readily available value from automated CT perfusion, whereas Dapeng Sun’s study required data on the time from onset to imaging. Second, in terms of applicability, the mismatch ratio is available in all automated software. Third, our study demonstrated better diagnostic ability than those reported by Diogo C and Takeshi. Fourth, our study had a larger sample size. Our study included approximately 1110 patients, while the three previous studies each had fewer than 300 patients. The larger sample size helps to minimize selection bias and increases the accuracy of the findings.

Our study has several limitations that warrant consideration. First, the retrospective nature of the study may introduce uncontrolled bias. However, the use of data from prospective registry databases and the large sample size may help to minimize this bias. Second, our study only evaluated the diagnostic ability of the mismatch ratio calculated by MIStar software for predicting ICAD-related occlusion. Therefore, caution should be exercised when interpreting the diagnostic ability of the mismatch ratio calculated by other CTP software. However, the algorithms of different perfusion software are similar, which may minimize this influence. Third, the cut-off value of the mismatch ratio for predicting ICAD had moderate sensitivity and specificity, which limits the accuracy of diagnoses based solely on this value. Fourth, our study included only patients who underwent EVT, meaning that some patients with an extremely low ratio, who were highly likely to have embolic occlusions, were not included. This may have affected the study results. Finally, our study was limited to the Asian population and may not be generalizable to other ethnic populations. Therefore, larger studies for external validation are needed.

In conclusion, our study demonstrated that the mismatch ratio is an optimal and simple parameter for predicting ICAD related LVO before EVT and can assist neurointerventionists in making EVT strategies. The likelihood of ICAD decreases as the ratio decreases and increases as the ratio increases. Specifically, when the mismatch ratio was ≥7.1, patients were five times more likely to have ICAD than an embolic etiology.

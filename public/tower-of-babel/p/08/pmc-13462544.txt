A novel mineral-organic bone adhesive promotes calvarial bone regeneration in microgravity conditions
Authors: Roberta Di Carlo, Uzma Mhate, Luigi Mancinelli, Sourabh Boruah, Taiana C. Leite, Xuehui Geng, Michael C. Brown, Afton K. Limberg, Douglas W. Van Citters, Brian J. Hess, George W. Kay, Giuseppe Intini
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Microgravity significantly impacts astronaut physiology, causing accelerated bone loss and impairing bone regeneration. As human space exploration expands, effective and minimally invasive bone repair treatments are crucial. In this study, we investigated the regenerative potential of a novel mineral-organic bone adhesive, Tetranite® (TN), compared to the osteoinductive rhBMP2 (Infuse®). We used a critical-size calvarial bone defect model in mice, with half launched on a 60-day mission to the International Space Station and the other half serving as ground controls. Histological and quantitative micro-computed tomography (MicroCT) analyses confirmed that both TN and rhBMP2 promoted bone regeneration in both spaceflight and ground conditions. While both biomaterials were effective, TN’s regenerative effect was more localized to the defect site. Our findings demonstrate that TN implantation effectively promotes calvarial bone regeneration under both microgravity and terrestrial conditions. This suggests its potential as a minimally invasive clinical solution for treating bone fractures during future space missions and on Earth.

Introduction

It is well documented that exposure to conditions of reduced gravity, or microgravity, can lead to different complications in normal physiological functions, including bone and muscle atrophy1, cardiovascular dysfunction2, and reduced immune function3. Under normal circumstances, bone is a highly dynamic organ in which osteoclasts and osteoblasts play an important role in balancing the process of resorption and bone formation, respectively4. The equilibrium between bone formation and resorption is necessary for normal bone homeostasis, regeneration, as well as for skeletal adaptation to mechanical loading and calcium metabolism. On the other hand, an imbalance of bone resorption or the reduction of bone formation results in several bone diseases5. For example, excessive resorption by osteoclasts without the corresponding amount of newly formed bone by osteoblasts contributes to bone loss and osteoporosis6.

Microgravity can alter the musculoskeletal physiology of the astronauts, leading to accelerated bone loss. In fact, according to NASA, a bone loss of 1% to 1.5% of has been recorded per months, equivalent to roughly 6% to 10% of bone loss over a 6-months stay in space1. Latest reports indicate that such bone loss is not fully recovered even one year after return from a long duration spaceflight, with poorer recovery associated with longer space missions7. As a consequence of this significant reduction of bone mass, microgravity may lead to bone fractures8,9. Since recent data also shows that healing of bone fractures in space is significantly impaired9, long duration space travelers that incur bone fractures may have a limited ability to recover, thus compromising the success of long-term missions. All together this evidence indicates that a solution to bone loss and to the inability to regenerate bone must be found before starting long-term space missions, such as those that will require long periods of residence on the Moon or on Mars.

RevBio Inc. (USA) has developed a potential solution for repair of bone fractures and regeneration of bone defects, based on a novel injectable mineral-organic bone adhesive (Tetranite®, RevBio Inc, Lowell, Massachusetts, USA) (TN). TN is a unique combination of multiphasic calcium phosphates and phosphoserine (O-phospho-L-serine, or OPLS), an organic phosphorylated amino acid residue10–14, which offers the opportunity to perform minimally invasive bone regenerative procedures while providing mechanical stability by means of its persistent adhesive bond strength. Therefore, we tested the ability of this novel bone adhesive to foster bone regeneration in conditions of microgravity. Since mechanical stability significantly sustains bone regeneration15, we theorized that a bone biomaterial conducive to bone formation and with adhesive qualities might be highly effective in sustaining bone regeneration is space.

Mice represent an excellent animal model to study bone regeneration16 and to study the effects of bone loss in space8,9; therefore, we relied on a mouse calvarial bone regeneration assay to test the TN bone adhesive in space. Thus, by means of a CASIS-sponsored research program (Rodent Research 25, launched with SpaceX CRS-26 in November 2022), we completed a series of investigations onboard the International Space Station (ISS), testing bone regeneration in a mouse calvarial critical size bone defect (full thickness bone defect of 2 mm in diameter, a defect unable to spontaneously regenerate17)(Spaceflight cohort). The earthbound experimental counterparts allowed us to test the bone regenerative efficacy in condition of normal gravity on Earth (House Ground Control cohort).

To test the efficacy of the TN bone adhesive, we compared its ability to sustain bone regeneration to that of a well-known osteoinductive biomaterial based on the combination of recombinant human BMP2 (rhBMP2) with a collagen membrane (Infuse®, Medtronic Inc. USA) (rhBMP2), which served as a positive control treatment of the 2 mm calvarial bone defects. Defects left empty (unfilled) served as negative control (NC). The experiments conducted in space were replicated on Earth to allow for comparison of the efficacy of the TN bone adhesive and of rhBMP2 to regenerate bone in conditions of both microgravity and normal gravity.

Methods

Animal acquisition and animal handling

Pre-flight and post-flight animal procedures, including surgical procedures and tissue collection, were performed at the Roskamp Institute (Sarasota, FL) with the approval of the Roskamp Institute IACUC (protocol number RR-25) and the Kennedy Space Center IACUC (contingency support, protocol number FLT-22-155). In-flight animal procedures on the International Space Station were conducted with the approval of the NASA IACUC (protocol number RR-25).

Four-week-old female C57BL/6 mice were purchased from Jackson Laboratories (Bar Harbor, ME). Prior to delivery, all mice were individually labeled with RFID identification chips implanted in the dorsal region (lateral to right scapula) (BMDS, IMI-500, 1.4 mm diameter × 8 mm long). Animals were shipped to the Roskamp Institute (Sarasota, FL) at ~4 weeks (L-27) before launch to allow for post-shipment recovery and rest (Fig. 1). Upon arrival, all animals were checked by a registered veterinarian technician (RVT) and housed in standard vivarium cages (5 animals per cage). Standard chow and sterilized deionized water via standard sipper tubes were provided ad libitum. After a rest period of 1 week (from L-27 to L-20) (Fig. 1), the animals were acclimated to the NASA Rodent Nutrient Food Bars and water bottles outfitted with water dispensary systems that facilitate water intake in conditions of microgravity (Lixit Inc., USA) (from L-20 to L-7) (Fig. 1). During the acclimation time, mice were housed in cages with raised mesh floors, to emulate the raised mesh floor of the spaceflight hardware (i.e., the “Transporter” which houses the mice while they are on the SpaceX Dragon, and the “Habitat” which houses the mice while they are on the International Space Station or ISS). Seven days before launch (L-7) (Fig. 1), mice were placed in double density housing, to get acclimated to the double dense housing conditions of the spaceflight hardware. At 3–5 days prior to launch (from L-5 to L-4) (Fig. 1), preflight surgeries occurred, establishing the experimental conditions. For surgical procedures, mice were anesthetized with 100 mg/kg of ketamine and 10 mg/kg of xylazine by intraperitoneal injection (IP). During the procedures, the respiratory rate and the temperature of each mouse was monitored, and ophthalmic ointment (Paralube Vet Ointment, Dechra Inc., USA) was applied to avoid corneal drying and eyes’ trauma. Ketamine/xylazine was the preferred method of anesthesia since it provides enough duration and adequate analgesia, while sustaining reduced muscle movements and minimal respiratory motions, critical for the success of cranial surgical procedures. Upon surgery completion, mice returned to their double density cages.

Following a post-surgical health check by the Animal Care Facility (ACF) attending veterinarians, 24 spaceflight mice (Spaceflight cohort) were loaded into the flight Transporter. The Transporter was turned over to the Space X Vehicle at 24 h before launch. Soon after the arrival to the International Space Station (ISS) (⁓48 h after launch), mice were transferred by the astronauts from the Transporter units into the Rodent Habitat units of the ISS, where the mice remained for the study duration (L + 60) (Fig. 1) (for further details about Transporter units and Habitat units, see https://www.nasa.gov/ames/space-biosciences/rodent-research-hardware-system/). Sixty days after surgery, mice were euthanized with IP injections of 150 mg/kg of ketamine and 45 mg/kg of xylazine followed by cervical dislocation. Then, calvarial tissue samples were collected.

To match the spaceflight conditions, 3 days after surgery of the Spaceflight cohort mice, asynchronous Earth mice were subjected to identical procedures and surgeries (House Ground Control cohort) (Fig. 1). Then, 3 days after launch (L + 3) (Fig.1), the House Grund Control cohort mice were housed into Transporter hardware and, after 2 days, were transferred into the Rodent Habitat units (identical to the hardware on board of the ISS), for the study duration of 60 days. Euthanasia and tissue collection followed the procedures described above for the Spaceflight cohort.

Study design

Mice were 10/11-week-old at the time of the surgeries. Briefly, 48 mice were randomly divided into two cohorts (24 mice per cohort): 1) the Spaceflight cohort that spent 60 days on the ISS, and 2) the House Ground Control cohort that spent the same amount of time on Earth, in the same housing conditions (Fig. 1). Upon creation of a critical size calvarial bone defect, mice were randomly distributed into three different treatment groups of 8 mice each: 1) the negative control group, with bone defects left empty (NC); 2) the test group with bone defects implanted with the TN bone adhesive (TN); 3) the positive control group with bone defects implanted with rhBMP2 (rhBMP2) (Fig. 1).

Surgical procedure

Mice were anesthetized with 100 mg/kg of ketamine and 10 mg/kg of xylazine by intraperitoneal injection. During procedures, the temperature of each mouse was monitored, and ophthalmic ointment was applied (Paralube, Dechra) to avoid corneal drying and trauma.

The calvaria were shaved and the surgical site was prepared with a triple application of betadine disinfectant alternating with 70% alcohol. Using a sterile blade, a 15 mm midline incision was made over the calvaria, to expose the parietal bone, then, a critical size defect of 2 mm in diameter was created in the right parietal bone using dental burs with increasing diameters (Brasseler, USA), by hand, carefully avoiding injuries to the dura mater. The defect was created at 3 mm from both the sagittal and the lambdoid sutures.

The rhBMP2 implantation: Infuse® is packed as a kit containing an absorbable collagen sponge, a vial with the lyophilized rhBMP2, and a vial with the sterile water for reconstituting the rhBMP2. Discs 2 mm in diameter were crafted from the collagen sponge and 10 µL of reconstituted rhBMP2 were absorbed onto each disc 15 min before implantation, to deliver a total of 2.5 µg of rhBMP2 into each calvarial defect.

TN bone adhesive implantation: The TN formulation utilized in this study was provided by RevBio, Inc. It is composed of multiphasic calcium phosphates (primarily tetracalcium phosphate with a minor amount of α-tricalcium phosphate) and O-phospho-L-serine combined as powders, with the addition of calcium carbonate to generate porosity and of sodium hydroxide to reduce acidity. It is packaged pre-dosed and sterilized in two-compartment triturator capsules fitted with a cannula applicator tip. The activation of the capsules was achieved by trituration for 12 s while still contained in the original sterile pouch. Immediately following activation, the capsules were inserted into an applicator device allowing for extrusion of the material onto a sterile tray from which a small amount, ~1.5 mm3, was picked up with a spatula and applied to the target calvarial site. The material was then trimmed to the contour of the defect.

After the defects were created, left untreated (NC) or treated (TN or rhBMP2), the incisions were closed with four interrupted sterile resorbable sutures (Coated Vicryl Suture 5-0, Ethicon, NJ) and with skin glue (3 M™ Vetbond™ Tissue Adhesives, USA). Supplemental heating (e.g., electric heating pad) was provided during the surgical procedure to maintain stable mouse body temperature.

After surgery, mice were treated with analgesics (Extended-release buprenorphine, Ethiqa XR, Fidelis Pharmaceuticals, NJ, dosage: 3.25 mg/kg).

Tissue sample collection

Prior to flying to the ISS, astronauts were trained to collect and fix tissue samples to standardize procedures with those performed on ground. Briefly, after 60 days in orbit and/or on Earth, mice were euthanized via intraperitoneal (IP) injection of Ketamine/Xylazine (150/45 mg/kg) followed by cervical dislocation. The cranium was dissected and placed in 4% methanol-free paraformaldehyde in Tissue Preservative Kit bags (TPKs, provided by Dr. Louis Stodieck, University of Colorado), for 48 h at 4 °C. Then, samples were washed twice with PBS for 15 min and stored in PBS + 0.1% sodium azide at 4 °C until returned to the PI’s laboratory at the University of Pittsburgh.

Histology

Briefly, after washing for 5 min in PBS, the calvarial samples were embedded in OCT compound (62550-01, Tissue-Tek® O.C.T Compound, Electron Microscopy Sciences, USA) and placed on dry ice before the cutting process. Using a cryostat and gold disposable blades (D554G50, C.L. Sturkey, Inc.), approximately 150 coronal plane sections of 7 µm of thickness were obtained from each embedded sample (cutting throughout the diameter of the hole). The sections included both the treated and contralateral sides of the calvaria.

A specialized staining protocol was developed for the staining of the delicate and easily breakable undecalcified calvarial bone sections. This method begins by placing slides on a flat, clean, non-absorbent surface. Next, distilled water is gently added to uniformly cover the tissue sections, which helps remove residual OCT compound and prepares the tissue for staining. Then, Hematoxylin (HT110116, Sigma-Aldrich) is gently applied to uniformly cover the sections and slides are incubate for 3 min at room temperature. After discarding the reagent and carefully removing residual droplets with a vacuum system, distilled water is added, twice for 20 s, to remove excess stain. Subsequently, the Bluing buffer (HT110116, Sigma-Aldrich) is gently added and incubated for 1 min at room temperature. Following this, distilled water is added twice for 20 s and then removed with vacuum. Next, Eosin (HT110116, Sigma-Aldrich) is gently added, incubated for 2 min at room temperature, and removed. Distilled water washes are repeated to remove excess Eosin. To prepare for imaging, Glycerol (49781, Sigma-Aldrich Inc., USA) is gently added on the slide, and a coverslip (16004-322, VWR Inc., USA) is applied. Imaging occurs immediately after the cover slip is applied to avoid artifacts that may arise from the glycerol clouding up.

For Von Kossa’s staining, tissue sections were covered with a 5% silver nitrate solution, resulting in a transient yellow coloration. Then, the bound silver was reduced to black metallic silver by means of UV light exposure for 10 min. Hematoxylin was utilized as counter stain to visualize unmineralized bone and bright field pictures were acquired.

Four calvarial samples for each group were processed for the histological analyses, totaling 24 samples (12/mouse cohort).

Micro-computed tomography analyses

Micro-computed tomography (microCT) scanning of ex vivo mouse calvaria was performed using x-ray microscopy (Zeiss XRadia Versa 610) at isotropic resolution of 8 microns using 60 kV 2 W energy. MicroCT data was first down-sampled to 32 µm before analysis using Slicer 3D to determine thickness and volume. The segmentation threshold for all specimens was set to 2000 Hounsfield Units, this allowed visualization of only the porous mineralized bone volume in the segment. “Wrap Solidify” operation was executed on this segment to fill the pores and obtain the total volume in the segment using the segment editor module in Slicer3D18. This method has been used previously to track growth of pediatric calvaria in 3D19.

Measurements were made in three adjacent concentric cylindrical and toroidal zones mutually exclusive of each other - the Defect zone, the Surrounding zone, and the Peripheral zone (Fig. 2). These were built by intersecting concentric zones aligned perpendicular to the calvarium with the mid-point at the center of the defect as visualized in the 2D and 3D reconstructions of the MicroCT. The Defect zone has a diameter of 2 mm while the Surrounding zone and the Peripheral zone have a wall of 1 mm each. A zone equivalent to the totality of the Defect zone, the Surrounding zone, and the Peripheral zone was mirrored on the sagittal plane and identified at the non-surgical left parietal bone (Contralateral side) (Fig. 2). Bone volume (BV), total volumes (TV), and the volume fraction (BV/TV) were measured within each of the three zones, and changes were compared across Spaceflight vs. House Ground Control and treatment types (NC, rhBMP2, and TN).Fig. 2Measurement zones in and around the calvarial defect.Defect zone, Surrounding zone, and Peripheral zone of the right parietal bone were identified with zone diameters of 2.0-, 3.0-, and 4.0-mm. Statistical analyses

Linear mixed model analyses (NCSS 2023) of the three outcome measures were conducted to test for mean differences in the combinations of treatment (NC, rhBMP2, and TN) and condition (Spaceflight and House Ground Control). A diagonal and heterogenous covariance matrix accounted for differing variances among treatments and conditions, and the level of significance was 0.05. Analyses were conducted separately for each outcome measure (BV = mineralized Bone Volume, TV = Total Volume, and BV/TV), to look at the effect of treatment and condition for every combination of zones (Defect, Surrounding, and Periphery) and the contralateral side.

All statistical comparisons are presented in Supplementary Table 1. For each parameter (BV, TV, and BV/TV), the Reference Group serves as the baseline, and the fold increase or decrease in these parameters of the Compared Group is expressed relative to that baseline, with corresponding p-values indicating statistical significance.

Inclusion criteria for histological and microcomputed tomography analyses

In space, mice live in double density conditions (the number of mice per cage is double respect to the usual condition of living animal facilities) because accommodation on board of the ISS is limited. Therefore, on Earth, mice were hosted in equal double density conditions, using replicas of the space habitats. Consequently, trauma and collisions among animals may have been more frequent than normal, reflecting on the inability of the surgically created bone defects to properly heal, as observed in some instances. In fact, in some instances, we noticed an inexplicable absence of regeneration/biomaterial in sites where the TN bone adhesive was implanted (Supplementary Figs. 1, 2). The adhesive property of the TN bone adhesive may have also been responsible for these findings, since its adhesion to the soft tissues covering the bone defect and the ongoing trauma of the constrained living could have contributed to the displacement of the biomaterial to an adjacent site, outside of the created bone defect. In fact, at least on one occasion, a displaced TN bone adhesive material of the size of the bone defect was found anteriorly to the original surgical site of implantation (Supplementary Fig. 2). Thus, we excluded from the analysis all mice in the TN group that presented as completely empty/non-regenerated. More specifically, two mice were excluded from the TN bone adhesive group of the Spaceflight cohort (Supplementary Fig. 1) and three mice were excluded from the TN bone adhesive group of the House Ground Control cohort (Supplementary Fig. 2), downsizing the spaceflight group from 8 to 6 mice and the ground group from 8 to 5.

Results

TN bone adhesive promotes bone regeneration of a mouse critical size calvarial bone defect in space and on Earth.

First, to test whether the TN bone adhesive is as effective in space as it is on Earth in sustaining bone regeneration, we performed microCT quantitative analyses of samples treated with the TN bone adhesive or rhBMP2 in space (Spaceflight cohort) or on Earth (House Ground Control cohort). The BV, TV, and BV/TV of the Defect zone were measured for each cohort. In reference to the TN bone adhesive samples, for all measurements performed, we found no statistically significant differences (p > 0.05) between the two cohorts, indicating that the TN bone adhesive is equally effective in microgravity as it is on Earth (Fig. 3). After demonstrating that the TN bone adhesive exhibited consistent regenerative capabilities in Space and on Earth, our next step involved a comparison of TN treated samples with the negative control (NC) and the positive control (rhBMP2), under each condition. To do so, we performed two different qualitative histological analyses of the treated and untreated calvarial critical size bone defects. First, to evaluate the morphology and structural architecture of the regenerated bone, we stained non-demineralized coronal plane sections of the calvaria defects with Hematoxylin and Eosin (H&E). Then, we used Von Kossa staining to test whether the newly regenerated bone was mineralized.

Starting with the analyses in conditions of microgravity, the H&E indicates that the TN bone adhesive sustains regeneration of bone (Fig. 4). More specifically, we found that all analyzed bone defects were partially or fully filled with newly regenerated mineralized bone, with bone defects bridged with newly regenerated bone in 4 out of 6 samples (Supplementary Fig. 1B). In all examined cases, the newly formed bone tissue exhibited a microstructural phenotype characteristic of woven bone (TN in Fig. 4A, B). As depicted in Fig. 4A and more in detail in Fig. 4B, the TN bone adhesive regenerated bone displayed a non-lamellar structure with a high cellular density. The presence of a non-lamellar pattern was a consistent finding. The Von Kossa staining indicated that the newly regenerated bone, although woven and therefore immature, is mineralized (TN in Fig. 4C). Differently, the defects of the mice treated with rhBMP2 (positive control) showed complete, but exuberant, regeneration of bone, with bone bridging in all samples (Supplementary Figs. 3, 4). In fact, the H&E staining of the rhBMP2 treated defects shows the newly regenerated bone to be lamellar in nature, with voluminous marrow spaces across the sections (rhBMP2 in Fig. 4A, B). The newly formed lamellar bone presents with a distinct, layered arrangement of collagen fibers and a lower cell density. In all analyzed cases treated with rhBMP2, we observed that the anatomical contour of the regenerated bone extended beyond the natural contour of the parietal bone, creating a bulging volume of newly formed bone (Fig. 4 and Supplementary Figs. 3, 4). Similar to what we observed with TN treatments, the Von Kossa staining of the rhBMP2 treated defects shows that the newly regenerated bone is fully mineralized (rhBMP2 in Fig. 4C). As per the negative control treatments (NC), as expected, the H&E and the Von Kossa analyses indicated that limited bone formation, mainly at the extreme margins of the created defects, was found in the NC defects (NC in Fig. 4A–C). In summary, in conditions of microgravity, both TN bone adhesive and the rhBMP2 sustain bone regeneration, while the NC control showed a limited amount of bone regeneration only at the margins of the defects. The quantitative data indicates that under spaceflight conditions, both TN bone adhesive and rhBMP2 achieved comparable levels of regenerated mineralized bone volume (BV) within the Defect zone (Fig. 6A and Supplementary Table 1). Although rhBMP2 demonstrated a 1.2-fold increase in BV compared to TN, the difference between the two treatments was not statistically significant (p > 0.05). In comparison to the NC group, TN and rhBMP2 showed a 3.8-fold (p < 0.001) and 4.7-fold (p < 0.0001) increase in BV, respectively. However, due to substantial volumetric expansion of marrow spaces within the rhBMP2-sustained regenerated bone structures (mean volume increase of 7.68 mm³ relative to TN), this treatment exhibited a total volume (TV) that was 2.7 times (p < 0.0001, statistically significant) and 9.9 times (p < 0.0001, statistically significant) greater than that of the TN bone adhesive and NC cohorts respectively (Fig. 6A and Supplementary Table 1). Consequently, there was a 2.2-fold increase (p < 0.0001, statistically significant) in the BV/TV between defects treated with the TN bone adhesive relative to those treated with rhBMP2, highlighting a measurable difference in the density of regenerated bone between the two treatments due to the presences of the marrow spaces. As expected, the NC group showed minimal bone regeneration, although with the BV/TV values elevated by 2.2 (p < 0.0001, statistically significant) and 1.0 times (p > 0.05, not statistically significant) compared to both the rhBMP2 and TN bone adhesive groups, respectively (Fig. 6A).

When we quantified the bone within the Surrounding zone, we observed a different response between treatments. In fact, the data indicated that the BV, TV, and BV/TV of the surrounding zone of the rhBMP2 treated defects were higher and significantly different from those of both the NC defects and TN bone adhesive treated defects (Fig. 6B and Supplementary Table 1). Specifically, defects treated with rhBMP2 showed a 1.8-fold increase over NC (p < 0.0001) and a 1.5-fold increase over TN bone adhesive (p < 0.0001). Similarly, rhBMP2 showed a TV that was 3.3-fold (p < 0.0001) and 2.7-fold (p < 0.0001) higher in comparison to both NC and TN bone adhesive, respectively. Consequently, rhBMP2-treated defects exhibited a BV/TV that was 1.8 times (p < 0.0001) and 1.7 times (p < 0.0001) lower than NC and TN bone adhesive, respectively. The comparison of TN bone adhesive with the NC showed a 1.2-fold increase for both BV and TV (p < 0.05), consequently a decrease of 1.0 time (p < 0.05) in terms of BV/TV. This data indicates that the effect of treatment with TN on the surrounding bone was similar to the NC, and not as excessive as those observed in samples treated with rhBMP2.

As we moved our analyses even further from the Defect zone to the Peripheral zone, the rhBMP2 treatment still induced significant differences in terms of BV, TV, and BV/TV in comparison to TN bone adhesive and NC (Fig. 6C and Supplementary Table 1). In particular, a 1.7-fold (p < 0.0001) increase was recorded for the BV when we compared rhBMP2 to both NC and TN bone adhesive. Likewise, rhBMP2 exhibited a 2.5-fold increase compared to NC and a 2.4-fold increase compared to the TN bone adhesive, both statistically significant (p < 0.001). As a result, rhBMP2 showed a BV/TV lower of 1.5 times (p < 0.001) than both NC and TN bone adhesive. In contrast, no significant difference was recorded between TN bone adhesive and NC (p > 0.05) (Fig. 6C).

We conclude that, in microgravity, both TN bone adhesive and rhBMP2 supported bone regeneration within the treated Defect zone. However, it was observed that the osteoinductive effects of the rhBMP2 reached bone beyond the treated defect, while the TN bone adhesive had a more predictable effect, mostly limited to the treated zone. In addition, the bone regenerated by rhBMP2 presented with altered morphology characterized by large bone marrow cavities and not a phenocopy of the compacted microarchitecture of the calvaria with sparse marrow volume (as also shown in Fig. 4).

The results obtained in conditions of normal microgravity (House Ground Control cohort) (Fig. 6D–F) recapitulated those observed in space, indicating that TN bone adhesive and rhBMP2 behaved similarly in space and on Earth.

Discussion

The significant impact of microgravity on the musculoskeletal system, particularly accelerated bone loss, is a well-documented consequence of human spaceflight1,7. This condition, which increases the risk of fractures, is further complicated by evidence from animal studies indicating that bone regeneration is also impaired in microgravity8,9. As human space exploration expands toward long-duration missions, the development of effective, minimally invasive treatments for bone fractures becomes critical. Therefore, we investigated the regenerative potential of TN, a novel mineral-organic bone adhesive that, being injectable, offers the opportunity to perform minimally invasive bone regenerative procedures. As shown above, our data indicate that the TN bone adhesive can promote bone regeneration in space as well as on Earth. Importantly, our data also confirm the ability of rhBMP2 to promote bone regeneration in space and on Earth. However, our studies indicate that the TN bone adhesive exhibited a localized bone regeneration effect confined to the treatment area, while the rhBMP2-treated defects showed bone growth bulging above the normal anatomical profile of the calvarial bone.

Previous research has established that mechanical unloading in microgravity leads to systemic bone loss and suppresses the differentiation of bone marrow stem cells into various lineages, including osteoblasts20,21. These findings led to the prevailing expectation that bone regeneration would be severely impaired in the microgravity environment. In fact, previous studies have shown that healing of the fractures of long bones is significantly impaired in space9. This phenomena may be due to the fact that while the “stemness” or quiescence of the skeletal stem cells is preserved, their ability to differentiate is inhibited22. However, our study challenges this premise by demonstrating that, with appropriate stimulation, calvarial bone regeneration can occur effectively in microgravity. In support of our studies, previous research has shown that proliferation of human fetal osteoblastic (hFOB) cells is not impaired in microgravity, indicating that the effects of microgravity could be cell-specific, not impairing the function of certain pre-osteoblastic cells23. In addition, the biological effects of microgravity on the skeletal stem cells may lead to a differential impact on bone homeostasis and regeneration between weight-bearing and non-weight-bearing bones, such as the calvaria. In fact, human data indicate that weight-bearing bones like calcaneus and tibia experience significant bone loss, whereas non-weight-bearing bones such as the radius are less affected24. Alternatively, considering that TN, thanks to its adhesive qualities, offers a significant mechanical stability, one may speculate that such stability offers the opportunity to the quiescent stem cells to overcome the differentiation blockage that they normally experience in space. Mechanical stability may not be required when a strong osteoinductive agent, such as the rhBMP2, is utilized to induce bone regeneration. This site-specific response highlights the complex interplay between mechanical loading, bone homeostasis, and bone regeneration during space missions, particularly in non-load-bearing regions like the calvaria. One final important consideration should be also made regarding the age of the animals utilized for these studies (11 weeks old at the time of launch). Since mice at this age are not skeletally mature25–27, the possibility exists that the observed difference between treated groups would not stand in 24 weeks old mice, when skeletal maturity is fully achieved and when bone homeostasis reaches a stable equilibrium25–27. Additional studies are required to ascertain the efficacy of TN and rhBMP2 in bone regeneration of long bones during spaceflight and at different ages.

Our results also highlight a significant difference in terms of quality and quantity of the bone regenerated by the TN bone adhesive versus rhBMP2. In fact, we observed, both in space and on Earth, that the TN bone adhesive promotes bone regeneration within the limits defined by the anatomical structure, while rhBMP2, perhaps because of its strong osteoinductive activity28, stimulates an excessive amount of bone formation, bulging above the normal anatomical profile of the calvarial bone and with excessive marrow spaces. On the basis of this difference, one may speculate that TN might be less effective than rhBMP2 in promoting bone regeneration; however, this reduced potency, which translates into a more predictable bone regeneration, may be a quality that clinician would appreciate since TN appears to have an effect limited to the treated defect. Whether such lower potency can be due to a mere osteoconductive capacity of TN or to a less significant osteoinductive capacity (compared to rhBMP2), needs to be tested in additional studies. For instance, if no bone is formed upon heterotopic transplantation of TN into the muscle or the subcutaneous tissues one could conclude that TN is solely osteoconductive. As mentioned above, we speculate that an important role in the observed postoperative differences between TN and rhBMP2 may be played by the mechanical stability provided by the TN bone adhesive. In fact, mechanical stability has been reported as conducive to bone regeneration15 and therefore we theorize that, by providing a stable regenerative micro environment to the osteogenic cells, the TN bone adhesive can be more efficient than rhBMP2 in sustaining localized bone regeneration.

Space explorations will require reliable and effective curative technologies to face the emergencies that may occur during the long-term absence from the Earth. Thus, important for future space explorations is the fact that TN bone adhesive is applied as an adhesive and cohesive viscous material within the bone defect, offering the opportunity to perform minimally invasive bone regeneration procedures when standard operative surgical procedures are limited. However, this is also a quality that can potentially provide significant advantages for bone regeneration procedures on Earth, since the bone regeneration sustained by the TN bone adhesive is comparable in both Spaceflight and Ground conditions. Thus, TN could be effectively used to treat patients on Earth, including those suffering from osteoporosis, since their bone regenerative potential is limited29.

In conclusion, this investigation demonstrates that the tested TN bone adhesive treatment can effectively lead to bone regeneration in a mouse calvaria critical size bone defect and can do so more predictably than rhBMP2, both in space and on Earth. If further studies in humans confirm the results shown in this animal studies, a novel biomaterial able to controllably regenerate bone may become available to humanity, both for future space explorations and for better health on Earth.

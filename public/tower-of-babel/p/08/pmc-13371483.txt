Effects of intraoperative low-dose remimazolam maintenance on emergence agitation and emergence time in patients undergoing oral surgery: protocol for a randomized controlled trial
Authors: Xiaoxuan Hu, Chong Fu, Guorong Bi, Qiyun Tan, Yaoyu Ying, Weiming Zhao, Xuechun Cai, Nan Yin, Xin Wang, Hong Liu, Lingzhong Meng, Fu-hai Ji, Qiang Sun, Baojian Zhao, Huayue Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionEmergence agitation (EA) is a clinically significant complication during early recovery from general anesthesia. This protocol describes a randomized controlled trial designed to determine whether intraoperative low-dose remimazolam maintenance reduces the incidence of EA without prolonging emergence time in patients undergoing oral surgery.Patients and MethodsThis multicenter, randomized, controlled clinical trial will enroll 388 patients scheduled for oral surgery under general anesthesia. Patients will be randomly allocated in a 1:1 ratio to either the remimazolam group, receiving a low-dose intraoperative infusion of remimazolam, or the control group, receiving an equivalent volume of normal saline. The primary outcome is the incidence of emergence agitation; the key secondary outcome is emergence time. Emergence time will be evaluated within a non-inferiority framework, whereas the incidence of emergence agitation will be tested for superiority. Additional endpoints comprise pain scores, analgesic use, the rate of postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV), recourse to rescue antiemetics, the occurrence of postoperative sleep disturbance (PSD), time spent in the post-anesthesia care unit (PACU), postoperative hospital length of stay, overall recovery quality, the frequency of perioperative adverse events, and patient satisfaction.DiscussionThis trial will investigate the efficacy and safety of intraoperative low-dose remimazolam maintenance in patients undergoing oral surgery. The findings may provide evidence supporting a practical pharmacological intervention to reduce EA and enhance postoperative recovery.Trial registrationChinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2600116544).

Introduction

Emergence agitation (EA) is a pathological behavioral state arising during early recovery from general anesthesia and characterized by psychomotor agitation, disorientation, and perceptual disturbance [1]. Its reported incidence varies widely, from 0.25% to 90.5% across surgical populations [1–4]. EA can precipitate adverse events such as patient falls, surgical-site bleeding, and respiratory compromise, increasing medical costs and posing risks to healthcare staff [1]. Recognized risk factors include patient age, postoperative pain, preoperative anxiety, surgical type, and anesthetic agents [5]. The incidence is especially high after oral surgery, owing to facial and airway manipulation, interindividual variability, perioperative medication, and nociceptive stimulation at the surgical site during emergence [5].

Remimazolam is a novel ultra-short-acting benzodiazepine that acts through modulation of γ-aminobutyric acid type A (GABAA) receptors, promoting chloride-channel opening and chloride influx. The resulting hyperpolarization of the neuronal membrane reduces neuronal excitability, producing sedative, hypnotic, anxiolytic, and anterograde-amnesic effects. Owing to its rapid onset, short emergence time, hepatic- and renal-independent metabolism, inactive metabolites, minimal accumulation during continuous infusion, limited respiratory and circulatory effects, and the availability of a specific antagonist, it is widely used in clinical anesthesia [6,7].

Recent studies suggest that remimazolam-based total intravenous anesthesia (TIVA) may lower the incidence of EA [8]. Remimazolam has also been associated with greater hemodynamic stability during general anesthesia [9], and low-dose remimazolam infusion has been reported to shorten extubation time and speed postoperative recovery [10–12]. Nonetheless, the evidence is not uniform: two recent meta-analyses found that, although remimazolam substantially reduces postoperative delirium and EA versus saline or placebo, it offers no clear advantage over propofol or dexmedetomidine in head-to-head comparisons, indicating that the magnitude of any benefit is context- and comparator-dependent [13,14].

However, clinical evidence on the effect of low-dose intraoperative remimazolam infusion on EA remains limited. Importantly, the present trial differs from remimazolam-based TIVA in that remimazolam is used here only as a low-dose adjunct to inhalational (sevoflurane) anesthesia rather than as the primary hypnotic agent in patients undergoing oral surgery, thereby providing evidence to inform perioperative pharmacological strategies.

Patients and methods

Study design

This multicenter, randomized, controlled clinical trial will enroll 388 patients across three institutions: the First Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University, Suzhou, China; Nanjing Stomatological Hospital, Affiliated Hospital of Medical School, Institute of Stomatology, Nanjing University, Nanjing, China; and the Affiliated Stomatological Hospital of Nanjing Medical University, Nanjing, China. The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the First Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University on January 7, 2026 (approval No. 20251197) and prospectively registered with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry on January 12, 2026 (ChiCTR2600116544; https://www.chictr.org.cn/showproj.html?proj=299814). This trial will be conducted from January 2026 to December 2027.

Following anesthesia induction, patients scheduled for oral surgery will be randomly allocated in a 1:1 ratio to the remimazolam group or the control group. In keeping with the Declaration of Helsinki, all participants will provide written informed consent before enrollment.

Inclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria are as follows: (1) patients aged 18–65 years scheduled to undergo oral surgery under general anesthesia; (2) American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) physical status I to III.

Exclusion criteria

The exclusion criteria are as follows: (1) a coexisting neuropsychiatric or mental-health disorder; (2) habitual psychoactive medication use or recent drug misuse; (3) severe respiratory failure, bronchial asthma, markedly elevated blood pressure, impaired hepatic or renal function (Child-Pugh class C or on renal replacement therapy), or severe cardiovascular disease; (4) participation in another clinical trial within the previous 4 weeks; (5) known hypersensitivity or other contraindication to any study drug; or (6) a planned tracheostomy or anticipated postoperative endotracheal-tube retention.

Withdrawal criteria

The withdrawal criteria are as follows: (1) consent is rescinded at any point, or the participant explicitly asks to stop taking part; (2) a serious adverse event arises that leads the investigator to halt participation on safety grounds; or (3) any other situation that makes ongoing participation untenable, such as a substantial protocol breach, an intervening illness, or loss to follow-up.

Termination criteria

The termination criteria are as follows: (1) the blind is broken before schedule; (2) serious flaws in the protocol come to light that undermine the study’s scientific integrity; or (3) recruitment stalls for an extended period or major deviations from the protocol recur frequently.

Randomization and blinding

Group assignment will use a center-stratified, permuted-block scheme. An independent statistician with no role in recruitment, data handling, or analysis will generate the allocation list via a web-based service (https://www.sealedenvelope.com/simple-randomiser/v1/lists), using a 1:1 ratio, block lengths of 2 and 4, and stratification by site. To conceal the sequence, allocations will be sealed in consecutively numbered, opaque envelopes.

Once anesthesia has been induced, a designated anesthesia nurse not involved in recruitment, intraoperative anesthetic care, or outcome assessment will open the next envelope in sequence, assign the patient to the indicated arm, and prepare the trial infusion. The remimazolam arm will receive a low-dose intraoperative remimazolam infusion, and the control arm an identical volume of normal saline. Because both are clear and colorless and the filled syringes look identical, the attending anesthesiologists, surgeons, and other care providers will remain adequately blinded.

Blinding will cover patients, surgeons, ward nurses, the anesthesia team, the outcome assessors, and the trial statistician throughout the study. Participants will learn their assigned arm only after all scheduled follow-up assessments are complete. Emergency unblinding will be permitted only when the principal investigator or attending anesthesiologist judges that knowledge of allocation is essential for patient safety; each event will be recorded with its rationale, date, and personnel involved, and reported to the institutional review board.

Anaesthetic and study interventions

Following current guidelines, participants will fast for at least 6 h from solids and 2 h from clear fluids before surgery. On arrival in the operating room, baseline monitoring will be established, including continuous pulse oximetry (SpO2), electrocardiography, and non-invasive blood pressure measurement.

All patients will receive a standardized general anesthesia induction protocol. Induction will use intravenous sufentanil 0.2–0.4 µg/kg followed by ciprofol 0.4 mg/kg. Once the Modified Observer’s Alertness/Sedation Scale (MOAA/S) score reaches 0 [16], rocuronium 0.6 mg/kg and dexamethasone 8 mg will be given to facilitate endotracheal intubation. Correct endotracheal tube placement will be confirmed by bilateral auscultation and continuous end-tidal carbon dioxide (EtCO₂) monitoring. Anesthesia will be maintained with sevoflurane (about 1.5–2.5%, titrated to a minimum alveolar concentration [MAC] of 0.7). The MAC target will be strictly maintained at 0.7 (permissible range 0.6–0.8) as a protocolized goal common to all three centers rather than being left to individual clinician discretion. Bispectral index (BIS) monitoring will also be standardized and kept between 40 and 60 to maintain consistent hypnotic depth as end-tidal sevoflurane is titrated toward the prespecified MAC. Intermittent sufentanil boluses of 0.1–0.2 µg/kg and a remifentanil infusion of 0.05–0.1 µg/kg/min will be given and adjusted according to surgical stimulation and hemodynamic changes. Deviations from the BIS 40–60 and MAC 0.6–0.8 targets will be permitted only for predefined clinical indications, such as inadequate anesthesia, hypotension, or hypertension, and all deviations will be recorded. Additional rocuronium will be given as needed to maintain adequate neuromuscular blockade. Ventilation will deliver a tidal volume of 6–8 mL/kg predicted body weight and a respiratory rate of 12–16 breaths/min to keep end-tidal CO₂ at 35–45 mmHg, with positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEP) of 5–10 cmH₂O. Nasopharyngeal temperature will be maintained at 36–37 °C with forced-air warming blankets [17].

The remimazolam group will receive a continuous infusion of remimazolam (25 mg/vial, Hubei Yichang Renfu Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd.) at 0.2 mg/kg/h. This low rate was selected on the basis of pharmacokinetic–pharmacodynamic data showing that full anesthetic maintenance of remimazolam requires approximately 1 mg/kg/h [18]; thus, 0.2 mg/kg/h is about one-fifth of the anesthetic dose and is intended to provide light, sub-hypnotic sedation that attenuates emergence agitation without delaying awakening. Comparable low-dose infusions of 0.1–0.2 mg/kg/h have been reported to shorten extubation time and post-anesthesia care unit stay and to reduce postoperative delirium and sleep disturbance [19]. For preparation, 25 mg is diluted to a total volume of 50 ml, giving an infusion rate of 0.4 ml/kg/h. The control group will receive normal saline at the same rate of 0.4 ml/kg/h. The remimazolam infusion will be discontinued at the start of wound closure or mucosal suturing. For procedures without a formal closure step, it will be stopped at the completion of the final major surgical manipulation, as wound irrigation or hemostasis begins. To minimize time-varying exposure bias, the actual infusion duration of remimazolam (or saline) and the interval from infusion cessation to the end of surgery will be recorded for each patient and included as covariates in prespecified sensitivity analyses of emergence time and emergence agitation. In addition, flurbiprofen axetil 50 mg will be given intravenously before the end of surgery.

Tracheal extubation, in either the operating room or the PACU, will be performed once the patient is fully awake, able to follow verbal commands, and breathing spontaneously and adequately with protective airway reflexes restored. After extubation, oxygen will be delivered by nasal cannula at 3 L/min. Transfer from the PACU to the surgical ward will be permitted once the patient reaches a modified Aldrete score of ≥9 [20].

During surgery and in the PACU, hemodynamic and respiratory disturbances will be managed as follows: hypertension (systolic pressure ≥160 mmHg or 20% above baseline), intravenous nicardipine 0.2 mg; hypotension (systolic pressure ≤90 mmHg or 20% below baseline), intravenous ephedrine 6 mg or phenylephrine 50–100 µg; bradycardia (heart rate <50 beats/min), intravenous atropine 0.25–0.5 mg; tachycardia (heart rate >100 beats/min), intravenous esmolol 20 mg; and oxygen desaturation (SpO2 < 92%) after extubation, supplemental oxygen at 5–10 L/min by nasal cannula or, if clinically indicated, mask ventilation.

Outcome measures

The primary outcome is the incidence of emergence agitation; the key secondary outcome is emergence time, defined as the interval from cessation of all anesthetic agents to the point at which the patient spontaneously opens their eyes or responds to verbal stimulation [21,22]. Emergence agitation is defined as a Ricker Sedation-Agitation Scale (SAS) score of ≥5. It will be assessed at emergence and repeatedly during early PACU recovery, from tracheal extubation until the patient reaches a modified Aldrete score ≥9 or for up to 30 min after extubation, whichever comes first; the highest SAS score within this window will determine the presence of EA. The SAS is a 7-point ordinal scale: 1 = unarousable (minimal or no response to noxious stimulus); 2 = very sedated (arouses to physical stimulation but unable to follow commands); 3 = sedated (difficult to arouse, able to follow simple commands); 4 = calm and cooperative (follows commands readily); 5 = agitated (attempts to sit up, calms with verbal redirection); 6 = very agitated (unable to be calmed, requires physical restraint, bites endotracheal tube); 7 = dangerously agitated (pulls at endotracheal tube, attempts to assault staff, climbs over bedrails)[23,24].

The secondary outcomes include the following: (1) rest and cough pain in the PACU and during the first 24 h after surgery, rated on the Numerical Rating Scale (NRS) [25]; (2) analgesic consumption in the PACU and during the first 24 h; (3) incidence of PONV in the PACU and during the first 24 h, defined as at least one episode of nausea or vomiting after surgery; (4) use of rescue antiemetics in the PACU and during the first 24 h, given for two or more episodes of vomiting or retching, nausea lasting more than 30 min, severe nausea, or at the patient’s request; (5) occurrence of PSD within the first 24 h, assessed with the Athens Insomnia Scale (AIS), an eight-item measure of night-time awakenings, sleep-onset latency, early-morning awakening, total sleep duration, sleep quality, sense of well-being, daytime functioning, and daytime sleepiness—with a total of ≥6 indicating clinically meaningful sleep impairment [26]; (6) duration of PACU stay; (7) length of postoperative hospital stay; (8) postoperative recovery quality during the first 24 h, measured with the 15-item Quality of Recovery-15 (QoR-15) questionnaire, on which each item scores 0 to 10 for a maximum of 150, with higher scores indicating better recovery [27]; (9) incidence of perioperative adverse events; (10) patient satisfaction at 24 h after surgery, rated on a five-point Likert scale (5 = highly satisfied, 4 = satisfied, 3 = neutral, 2 = dissatisfied, 1 = very dissatisfied); and (11) time to extubation, recorded separately from emergence time and defined as the interval from cessation of all anesthetic agents to removal of the tracheal tube.

Safety surveillance will capture intraoperative and postoperative adverse events for every participant. The adverse events of interest include hypertension, hypotension, bradycardia, tachycardia, oxygen desaturation, postoperative shivering, allergic reactions, hypothermia, severe ventricular arrhythmias, and cardiac arrest. Because this trial targets transient emergence agitation rather than postoperative delirium, persistent disorientation or fluctuating mental status will also be recorded as adverse events when clinically relevant. Full definitions are provided in Supplemental File S2. Each adverse event will be logged across the anesthesia, PACU, and ward phases. Any serious adverse event judged to be intervention-related must be reported to the Data Monitoring Committee (DMC) within 24 h using a standardized Adverse Event Form. For a serious adverse event, the attending anesthesiologist may unblind the allocation, modify the treatment plan, or withdraw the participant to ensure patient safety.

Data management

All patients will provide written informed consent, approved by the hospital ethics committee, prior to enrollment. Baseline demographic and clinical data will be collected by an investigator not involved in patient care or outcome assessment, including age, sex, body mass index (BMI), ASA physical status, coexisting conditions, smoking status (current, former, or never), weekly alcohol intake (standard drinks), preoperative vital signs, and relevant laboratory results.

Baseline anxiety and depression will be assessed with the preoperative Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS), alongside the AIS. The HADS comprises an anxiety and a depression subscale, each with seven items scored 0–3; a subscale total of ≥8 indicates clinically significant anxiety or depression [28]. Intraoperative variables will include the anesthetic drugs and doses, the procedure performed, the anesthetic interval (induction to emergence), the surgical interval (incision to closure), perioperative fluid data (crystalloids, colloids, blood products, estimated blood loss, and urine output), and any intraoperative adverse events.

Postoperative data will include pathology results and all outcome measures as specified in the Outcome Measures section. All relevant data will be recorded on standardized case report forms and entered into a secure electronic database with access restricted to authorized study personnel. An independent Data Monitoring Committee (DMC) will provide ongoing oversight of trial conduct and data integrity.

Sample size estimation

The sample size was estimated for both the primary outcome (incidence of emergence agitation, tested for superiority) and the key secondary outcome (emergence time, tested for non-inferiority), and the larger of the two estimates was adopted to ensure adequate power for each. For the primary outcome, pilot data showed an EA incidence of 25% with low-dose remimazolam versus 40% in the control group. At a two-sided α of 0.05 with 80% power, 152 patients per group are required; allowing for 10% dropout, this yields 336 patients (168 per group). For the key secondary outcome, the sample size was calculated to demonstrate non-inferiority of the remimazolam group to the control group for emergence time. Based on prior clinical trials and pilot data, the mean emergence time in the control group is estimated at 15 min (standard deviation 5 min). The non-inferiority margin was set at 10% of the control mean; that is, the upper limit of mean emergence time in the remimazolam group will not exceed 16.5 min. This margin of approximately 1.5 min was judged clinically acceptable: in a high-volume oral-surgery setting, a difference of this magnitude does not meaningfully delay recovery of protective airway reflexes, readiness for tracheal extubation, post-anesthesia care unit throughput, or operating-room turnover, and it is consistent with the margins used in previous non-inferiority trials of emergence from remimazolam-based anesthesia [22]. Thus, any reduction in emergence agitation would not be offset by a clinically important prolongation of emergence. At a one-sided α of 0.025 with 80% power, 175 patients per arm are required; allowing for 10% attrition, this yields 388 patients (194 per arm).

Because the trial must be adequately powered for both outcomes, the final sample size is the larger of the two estimates (388 vs. 336): 388 patients (194 per group). All sample-size calculations were performed in R (version 4.5.1; http://www.R-project.org).

Statistical analysis

Treatment effectiveness will be assessed primarily under the intention-to-treat (ITT) framework, with the per-protocol (PP) analysis as a sensitivity check. The ITT set will comprise all randomized patients, analyzed according to the group to which they were assigned and regardless of the intervention actually received. The PP set will be limited to patients who complete the protocol without major violations. Patients given non-protocol anesthetic drugs (e.g. dexmedetomidine or esketamine) will be excluded from the PP analysis.

Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics will be summarized descriptively. For each baseline variable, the absolute standardized difference (ASD) will be calculated, with an ASD above 0.1 regarded as clinically relevant imbalance requiring adjustment in the primary analysis. Normality of continuous variables will be checked with the Shapiro-Wilk test: normally distributed variables will be reported as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and compared with the independent-samples t-test, and non-normal variables as median (interquartile range, IQR) and compared with the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables will be reported as counts (percentages) and compared with the chi-squared test or, where appropriate, Fisher’s exact test.

The primary outcome, the incidence of emergence agitation, will be analyzed with the chi-squared test under a superiority hypothesis (two-sided α = 0.05), and the key secondary outcome, emergence time, will be assessed for non-inferiority using a log-binomial regression model (one-sided α = 0.025); both analyses will yield risk ratios (RR), absolute risk differences (RD), and 95% confidence intervals (CI). Because only a single primary outcome is prespecified, no alpha split across co-primary outcomes is required; instead, the primary superiority analysis and the key secondary non-inferiority analysis will be interpreted confirmatorily within a fixed-sequence (hierarchical) testing procedure, thereby controlling the family-wise error rate. If a baseline variable shows an ASD >0.1, a multivariable logistic-regression model will adjust for confounding, reporting adjusted odds ratios (aOR) with 95% CI.

Other secondary endpoints will be compared using tests matched to the data distribution: the independent-samples t-test or Mann-Whitney U test for continuous endpoints and the chi-squared test or Fisher’s exact test for categorical endpoints. To limit inflation from multiple testing, the Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) procedure will be applied at a threshold of q < 0.025. Continuous results will be summarized as mean differences (MD) with 95% CI, and categorical results as RR, RD, and 95% CI.

Subgroup analyses of the primary outcome will be performed by sex (male vs. female), age, surgical type, site, and surgery duration using interaction tests. Unless otherwise specified, tests will be two-sided with p < 0.05 defining statistical significance, and no interim analysis is planned. Consistent with the intention-to-treat principle, the primary and key secondary analyses will include all randomized patients, with missing outcome values estimated by multiple imputation; the target sample size was inflated by 10% to preserve power against the anticipated dropout, so that random loss to follow-up at or below this rate should not compromise power. Missingness will be minimized through real-time case-report-form checks and follow-up verification, and the reasons for dropout and missingness will be summarized by treatment group to assess potential non-random missingness. Complete-case and worst-/best-case analyses, together with the per-protocol analysis, will serve as sensitivity analyses. All analyses will be performed in R (version 4.5.1).

Discussion

This protocol describes a multicenter, randomized, controlled trial evaluating the effect of intraoperative low-dose remimazolam maintenance on emergence agitation and emergence time in 388 patients undergoing oral surgery. The primary objective is to determine whether this intervention produces a clinically meaningful reduction in EA incidence without delaying emergence; accordingly, EA incidence is the primary outcome (tested for superiority) and emergence time the key secondary outcome (tested for non-inferiority), with the trial powered for both. Secondary objectives include postoperative pain scores (at rest and on coughing), analgesic consumption, PONV incidence, rescue antiemetic use, PSD incidence, PACU and hospital length of stay, quality of recovery (QoR-15), and patient satisfaction. The trial will be conducted and reported in accordance with the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) 2025 statement [29].

Remimazolam has gained considerable traction in clinical anesthesia. In a randomized controlled trial, Jang et al. found that remimazolam-based TIVA significantly reduced EA incidence in adults undergoing nasal surgery under general anesthesia [8]. A non-inferiority trial reported that an intravenous remimazolam infusion at 0.5 mg/kg/h effectively prevented EA in elderly patients while improving hemodynamic stability [30]. Another trial showed that a 2.5 mg dose of remimazolam achieved an optimal balance between efficacy and safety, whereas a 5.0 mg dose conferred additional benefit in patients with dangerous agitation [31]. However, no study to date has specifically examined the effect of low-dose remimazolam infusion on EA after oral surgery. Accordingly, this trial will investigate whether a low-dose remimazolam infusion can significantly reduce EA incidence without prolonging emergence time.

Several limitations of this study warrant consideration. First, the study population was restricted to patients aged 18–65 years, which may limit generalizability to older adults. This restriction was applied to reduce confounding from age-related susceptibility to postoperative delirium, frailty, and altered benzodiazepine pharmacodynamics [19,30]; nonetheless, the applicability of the findings to elderly patients remains uncertain and should be interpreted with caution. Second, the remimazolam infusion rate was chosen from available prior evidence, which may not be the optimal dosing strategy, and dose–response relationships were not examined here. Third, the trial was conducted exclusively in oral-surgery patients, which may limit external validity to other surgical populations; whether the effects generalize to other procedures with a high incidence of emergence agitation, such as nasal, otolaryngological, ophthalmic, or thyroid surgery, remains speculative and requires confirmation in future studies.

Despite these limitations, this randomized clinical trial is designed to identify a feasible low-dose remimazolam regimen for reducing EA incidence. We anticipate that intraoperative low-dose remimazolam will produce a clinically meaningful reduction in EA and support a smoother postoperative recovery after oral surgery.

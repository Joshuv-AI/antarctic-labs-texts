SPEACx: a screening pathway-based evaluation and audit framework of cervical cancer diagnostic routes in a population-based programme
Authors: David Del Valle, Edurne Arenaza Lamo, Antonia Dávila Expósito, Ángel Gómez Amorín, Elena Rodríguez Camacho, Andrea Requena Menéndez, Jose Cruz Quílez Conde, Laia Bruni Coccoz, Jhon William Comba Miranda, Silvia de Sanjosé

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCervical cancer audits are widely used to evaluate organized screening programmes, yet existing approaches often fail to clearly attribute cancer diagnoses to specific stages of the screening pathway. This limits the ability to translate audit findings into actionable public health improvements. We developed the Screening Pathway-based Evaluation and Audit of Cervical Cancer (SPEACx) framework to attribute cervical cancer diagnoses across the screening pathway and improve programme evaluation.MethodsThe SPEACx framework was developed through an iterative expert consensus process involving screening programme coordinators, gynecologists, and epidemiologists. Cancer cases are attributed to operational stages of screening, including invitation, participation, test performance, follow-up, and post-treatment surveillance. As an applied example, all cervical cancer cases diagnosed within the population-based screening programme of the Basque Country (Spain) between 2018 and 2024 were analysed. Diagnostic pathways and FIGO stage were determined through clinical record review, and stage distributions were compared across major diagnostic pathways.ResultsAmong 678 confirmed cervical cancer cases, 34.8% were diagnosed through the screening programme in asymptomatic women, 6.1% were interval cancers, and 55.9% were diagnosed outside the organized programme. Programme-detected cancers were predominantly diagnosed at early stages, whereas interval cancers and cancers diagnosed outside the programme showed substantially higher proportions of advanced disease. Aggregation of SPEACx categories into operational domains enabled identification of key system vulnerabilities related to participation gaps, eligibility criteria, test performance, and follow-up pathways, supporting the development of targeted quality improvement interventions.ConclusionsSPEACx provides a structured and adaptable framework for attributing cervical cancer diagnoses to specific stages of screening delivery. By enabling attribution of cancer diagnoses to screening processes, SPEACx supports targeted quality improvement and provides a practical tool for public health evaluation of organized cervical cancer screening programmes.Trial registrationNot applicable.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13690-026-02009-8.Keywords: Cervical Cancer Screening, Mass Screening, Quality Assurance, Health Care, Program Evaluation, Early Detection of Cancer, Public Health Surveillance

Background

Cervical cancer (CC) screening is a well-established public health intervention that can substantially reduce incidence and mortality when implemented as an organized, population-based program [1, 2]. Its effectiveness depends on multiple steps working well together and requires consistent quality assurance. Cancer audits review the entire screening pathway to identify potentially preventable cases, delays in diagnosis, and missed opportunities for earlier detection, with the aim of improving program performance, safety, and equity [3, 4]. These audits focus on system-level processes rather than individual clinical decisions, supporting population-level learning and public health action rather than assigning blame. By examining where and how cancers arise along the screening pathway, audits can highlight structural bottlenecks and pathway vulnerabilities that may not be visible through overall outcome data alone. This system-focused approach helps guide targeted quality improvements and inform policy decisions in population-based cervical cancer screening programs.

To support such evaluations, the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) and the World Health Organization (WHO) have proposed a framework that classifies cervical cancer cases into three broad categories: cancers diagnosed in non-participants or inadequately screened women, cancers diagnosed following an abnormal screening result, and cancers diagnosed after a normal screening result [3]. Within this framework, the measurement of interval cervical cancer (ICC) rates—particularly those occurring after a negative screening test—has been widely adopted as a key process indicator of program performance, reflecting the effectiveness of screening, triage, and treatment pathways.

Building on this approach, several screening programs have proposed additional audit indicators. In 2020, CervicalCheck, the Irish national cervical screening program, introduced the annual ICC rate as a formal key performance indicator and issued detailed recommendations for the audit of ICCs [5]. Other authors have highlighted the importance of distinguishing specific diagnostic mechanisms, such as colposcopy sensitivity limitations, post-treatment risk, and algorithm-related gap, to better understand how cancers arise within organized screening systems [6].

In addition, discrepancies remain in the operational definition of interval cervical cancer (ICC). ICCs have been defined as cancers diagnosed between screening rounds following a negative screening test or after an abnormal screening result with subsequent negative triage or diagnostic assessment beyond a defined time interval [3]. Other definitions exclude cancers diagnosed following minor abnormalities that led to colposcopy [5]. These differences underscore the need for a framework that can accommodate diverse diagnostic pathways while preserving clarity and interpretability.

However, audits may identify cancer cases without clearly indicating which stages of the screening pathway were associated with the diagnosis, limiting the ability to translate findings into targeted public health interventions. Existing frameworks provide international comparability but do not systematically attribute cancers to specific stages of program delivery.

In this manuscript, we present the SPEACx framework, which maps each cancer case to specific steps within the screening pathway—from invitation and participation to post-test management and follow-up. The framework can be applied in a simplified manner when full categorization is not feasible. We illustrate its application using data from the Basque Country population-based cervical cancer screening program, the first region in Spain to achieve full invitation coverage, as a proof of concept to demonstrate the framework’s potential to inform system-level quality improvement.

Methods

Development of the SPEACx framework

The SPEACx framework was developed through an iterative expert consensus process conducted between June 2023 and May 2024. A multidisciplinary working group of ten experts—including representatives from four regional cervical screening programmes (Basque Country, Galicia, Valencia, and Catalonia), gynecologists specialized in cervical pathology, epidemiologists representing the Spanish Association for Cervical Pathology and Colposcopy (AEPCC), and a pathologist from the Basque Country screening programme—participated throughout its development.

The process was coordinated by the Basque Country cervical cancer screening program team, which presented an initial proposal based on international guidelines, existing audit frameworks, and practical experience in population-based screening. Following a literature review, the proposal was circulated to the expert group and refined through four meetings. Consensus was achieved through discussion and reference to international guidelines and published evidence. The final framework was subsequently presented to the national network of cervical screening programme coordinators, with representatives from all Spanish autonomous communities.

In line with IARC recommendations, the group standardized key definitions (including interval cervical cancer), developed a diagnostic pathway algorithm for cervical cancer (the SPEACx framework), and prepared practical recommendations for program evaluation. Based on existing international evidence and expert consensus, ICC_TEST was defined as cervical cancer diagnosed after the onset of signs or symptoms following a negative screening test. To account for the expected duration of protection associated with each screening modality, ICC_TEST cases were considered up to 3 years and 6 months after a negative cytology result and up to 5 years and 11 months after a negative HPV test. Beyond these intervals, women who were invited to a subsequent screening round but did not participate were no longer considered interval cancers and were classified as cancers diagnosed in invited non-participants (CC_OS_I). Detailed operational definitions are provided in Supplementary Table 1S. Although the framework includes multiple subcategories, it was designed to allow simplified application when full categorization is not feasible, facilitating its use across different programmatic settings. The final framework was validated and endorsed by consensus with the national network of cervical screening program coordinators and representatives of the AEPCC.

Application of the framework: Basque Country screening program

To illustrate the application of the SPEACx framework, all cervical cancer cases diagnosed in the Basque Country between 2018 and 2024 were analyzed. The Basque Country was the first region in Spain to implement an organized population-based cervical cancer screening program and achieved full invitation coverage of the eligible population by December 2024.

In this region, cervical cancer screening is centralized in a single laboratory located at Donostia University Hospital. During the study period, the screening program was implemented according to the national recommendations, using cytology as the primary screening test for women aged 25 to 34 years, with a three-year screening interval, and high-risk human papillomavirus (hrHPV) testing for women aged 35 to 65 years, with a five-year screening interval, in accordance with national guidelines [7, 8]. Cotesting was reserved for immunocompromised women or those with a previous history of premalignant cervical lesions.

Annual test coverage was defined as the proportion of eligible women undergoing cervical screening within a given calendar year, regardless of whether testing occurred within the organized screening program or opportunistically. Participation rate refers to attendance within the organized screening program following invitation.

Case review, variables, and statistical analysis

All cervical cancer cases were subjected to a comprehensive clinical review using the regional electronic medical records system by members of the cervical cancer screening program team responsible for programmatic evaluation. For cases diagnosed between 2018 and 2020, classification was independently reviewed by three investigators, with discordant cases discussed until consensus was reached. Challenging cases identified during this process contributed to refinement of the final SPEACx framework. For cases diagnosed between 2021 and 2024, classification was reviewed by two investigators and consensus was reached through discussion when required. For each case, histological type (categorized as squamous cell carcinoma, adenocarcinoma, or other less common types), detection pathway (screening-detected, symptom-detected, or diagnosed outside the organized programme), and FIGO 2018 stage were recorded [9, 10].

Interval cancers underwent additional review by the lead pathologist of the Cervical Screening Unit of the Basque Country, including retrospective cytological reassessment. All cases identified were assessed for their adherence to recommended diagnostic and follow-up protocols.

Categorical variables were compared using the chi-squared test. The distribution of FIGO stage (early vs advanced) was compared across major diagnostic pathways, using cancers detected directly through the screening program as the reference group. Statistical significance was defined as a two-sided p-value < 0.05. All analyses were performed using SPSS version 29 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). For interpretative purposes, SPEACx subcategories were subsequently grouped into broader operational domains of screening performance.

Results

Study population and screening context

During the study period, the population-based cervical cancer screening program in the Basque Country targeted 602,982 women. A total of 697 cervical cancer cases were initially identified. After clinical review, two cases were excluded due to uncertain tumor origin and 17 due to non-invasive premalignant pathology, resulting in 678 confirmed cervical cancer cases included in the analysis.

Participation in the organized screening program increased progressively from 49.0% in 2018 to 66.5% in 2024. When opportunistic screening was also considered, annual test coverage in 2024 reached 75.5%.

Distribution of cervical cancers across major diagnostic pathways

Based on the SPEACx framework, 236 cases (34.8%) were diagnosed through the screening program in asymptomatic women, after an abnormal screening test or in their follow-up. Ten of the 236 programme-diagnosed cervical cancers (4.2%) occurred in invited women who were subsequently reclassified as non-eligible due to the presence of symptoms at the screening consultation. Interval cervical cancers accounted for 6.1% of all diagnoses and were detected after symptom onset following either a negative screening test or an abnormal result before the recommended follow-up. The remaining 55.9% of cancers were diagnosed outside the organized screening program. These cases corresponded to diagnoses not linked to a screening test or follow-up pathway within the organized programme.

Within the screening program, 163 of 236 cancers (69.1%) were detected directly following an abnormal screening test, whereas 63 (26.7%) arose during the established follow-up pathway.

Cancers diagnosed outside the screening program derived from invited but non-participating women, eligible but non-invited women, and non-eligible women. Most non-eligible cancers (175/188; 93.1%) occurred in women older than 65 years, while the remainder corresponded to women who had been registered in the Basque Country for less than one year.

Diagnostic pathways associated with more advanced stage at diagnosis

To assess the association between diagnostic pathways and disease severity, FIGO stage at diagnosis was compared across groups, using cancers detected directly after an abnormal screening test as the reference category. While 76.7% of programme-diagnosed cancers were detected at an early stage (181/236), advanced-stage disease predominated among interval cancers (29/41, 70.7%) and cancers diagnosed outside the programme (304/379, 80.2%). This pattern was even more pronounced among cancers diagnosed in invited non-participants (85.5%) and non-eligible women (84.0%).

Evolution of diagnostic pathways over time

The relative contribution of different diagnostic pathways changed over the study period as the screening program matured (Fig. 3). These temporal trends should be interpreted in the context of the progressive implementation of the organised programme, which achieved full invitation coverage of the target population by the end of 2024. Participation also increased during the later years of the study period, reaching 66.5% in 2024. As invitation coverage expanded, the proportion of cancers diagnosed in eligible but non-invited women decreased sharply, from 31.2% to 2.1% (p < 0.001). In contrast, cancers diagnosed in invited but non-participating women increased from 0% to 21.9% (p < 0.001), becoming an increasingly prominent source of diagnoses outside the program.

Discussion

Principal findings and programmatic attribution

From a public health perspective, the principal contribution of the SPEACx framework is its ability to attribute cervical cancer diagnoses to specific stages of the screening pathway, including invitation, participation, test performance, follow-up, and post-treatment surveillance. This approach allows identification of pathway stages associated with delayed diagnosis and helps prioritize targeted quality improvement actions.

Most importantly, FIGO stages were strongly associated with early detection, as expected, in those cases identified within the program while those arising outside structured screening pathways were more frequently diagnosed at an advanced stage.

Implications for screening performance evaluation

To facilitate programmatic interpretation, diagnostic pathways were grouped into broader operational domains of screening performance (Table 3). This aggregation linked cancers to specific program areas, including invitation coverage, participation, test sensitivity, follow-up management, colposcopy performance, and treatment adequacy, translating audit findings into actionable quality improvement targets.

Cancer audit processes are widely recognized as essential components of screening quality assurance [3, 4], although their implementation and methodological approaches remain heterogeneous across countries. The IARC classification provides a valuable framework for international comparison by grouping cancers according to screening exposure. Most published evaluations and audits of cervical screening performance originate from European population-based screening programs, although practices differ in scope, methodology, and operationalization [11, 12]. SPEACx builds upon this model by further subdividing diagnostic pathways, enabling more granular program evaluation while preserving international comparability. In this sense, both approaches should be viewed as complementary. The IARC framework provides a broadly applicable audit structure that supports international comparisons and programme evaluation across diverse healthcare settings. In contrast, SPEACx was designed for organised screening programmes with access to more detailed programme data, allowing reconstruction of diagnostic pathways and identification of specific opportunities for quality improvement. Therefore, rather than replacing existing audit models, SPEACx should be considered a complementary framework that provides additional programme-level information when the necessary data infrastructure is available. The choice between frameworks is determined not only by the objectives of the audit but also by the level of programme organisation and availability of longitudinal screening, follow-up, treatment, and cancer registry data. The SPEACx framework has also been incorporated into the 2025 Spanish national cervical cancer screening guidelines published by the AEPCC, supporting its implementation in routine programme evaluation [13] Comparison with previous cervical cancer audit reports is challenging because published evaluations differ in programme organisation, screening technologies, age eligibility criteria, and operational definitions of interval cancer [3, 4]. Nevertheless, several findings from our study are consistent with observations from other organised screening programmes. In particular, the substantial proportion of cancers diagnosed outside organised screening pathways is in line with previous studies showing that non-participation and insufficient screening remain major contributors to cervical cancer occurrence despite the availability of organised screening programmes [6, 14, 15]. Likewise, the predominance of early-stage disease among programme-diagnosed cancers and the higher proportion of advanced-stage disease among interval cancers and cancers diagnosed outside screening pathways are consistent with previous reports demonstrating the benefits of organised screening for earlier cancer detection [6, 12].

Differences in attribution arise particularly for cancers diagnosed during structured follow-up after abnormal screening tests. While broader international definitions may classify these as interval cancers, SPEACx considers them events occurring within surveillance pathways. Their stage distribution, comparable to that of screen-detected cancers, supports attribution to program performance rather than screening limitations.

Programmatic implications and derived interventions

The principal vulnerabilities identified through SPEACx also suggest specific programme improvement actions, illustrating how audit findings can be translated into targeted interventions within organised screening programmes. The temporal trends observed across SPEACx categories reflect the progressive implementation of the organised screening programme during the study period. As invitation coverage expanded to the entire target population, cancers diagnosed in eligible but non-invited women became increasingly uncommon, while invited non-participants emerged as a more prominent diagnostic pathway. These findings illustrate how SPEACx can be used not only to identify vulnerabilities in screening pathways but also to monitor the impact of programme development over time. Programmatic analysis informed targeted public health interventions. A substantial proportion of cancers occurred beyond the upper screening age limit, particularly among women over 65 years (175 cases). This finding identifies an important area for improvement and illustrates how SPEACx can help detect programmatic vulnerabilities associated with advanced-stage disease and poorer survival. Different screening programmes may address this challenge through different strategies. Some countries have opted to extend routine screening beyond age 65 years, whereas others have focused on ensuring adequate screening before programme exit and implementing targeted strategies for insufficiently screened older women. Given the high negative predictive value of HPV testing, women who complete screening according to recommended exit criteria have a very low residual risk of cervical cancer. Therefore, in addition to potential age-extension strategies, targeted efforts to improve screening completion among women approaching the upper age limit and catch-up HPV testing among insufficiently screened women aged 66–75 years may represent efficient approaches to reducing the burden of cervical cancer in this population.

Following the achievement of full invitation coverage, cancers increasingly concentrated among invited but non-participating women. Ongoing initiatives include population-based surveys exploring barriers to attendance and the implementation of alternative access strategies, including self-sampling.

Quality assurance audits of interval cancers, including cytological reassessment, identified under-detection of glandular abnormalities. Among the 34 interval cancers diagnosed after a negative screening test, cytological reassessment was feasible only in four cases, as these corresponded to samples processed by the centralized laboratory. Three of these four initially negative cytology results were reclassified as glandular cell atypia, including one suggestive of adenocarcinoma in situ. Although limited by small sample sizes, glandular lesions are easily missed by cytology but are more likely to be identified when HPV testing is used. These findings support further research to improve early detection of glandular disease.

Beyond the interventions described above, SPEACx may also support evaluation of future changes in cervical screening programmes. Several countries are currently implementing risk-based management strategies that assign different follow-up pathways according to individual risk. In this context, SPEACx may help monitor the safety of these approaches by assessing cancers diagnosed during surveillance pathways, particularly those classified as CC_FU_IR, and evaluating their stage at diagnosis over time.

Furthermore, now that invitation coverage has reached the entire target population in our setting, cancers occurring in eligible but non-invited women (CC_OS_NI) are expected to become increasingly uncommon. Detailed review of these cases may help identify residual organisational barriers, registration issues, or invitation pathway limitations, providing opportunities for further programme improvement.

Strengths, limitations, and transferability

Key strengths of this study include its population-based design, complete invitation coverage, integration of screening databases with cancer registry and clinical records, and comprehensive clinical review of all cervical cancer cases, enabling robust attribution of cancers to diagnostic pathways.

This study also has limitations. The SPEACx framework is most readily applicable to screening programs with comprehensive digital infrastructures capable of integrating screening, diagnostic, and cancer registry data. Programs with fragmented information systems may face challenges in fully operationalizing pathway attribution. To enhance accessibility across diverse healthcare settings, a simplified operational version of the SPEACx classification has been developed (Figure S1). The full framework is intended for organised screening programmes with access to integrated screening, clinical, follow-up, treatment, and cancer registry data, enabling detailed reconstruction of diagnostic pathways and attribution of cancers to specific stages of programme delivery. In contrast, the simplified version was designed for settings with more limited data infrastructures and requires fewer data elements. At a minimum, implementation requires information on eligibility and invitation status, whether diagnosis originated from an abnormal screening test or follow-up pathway, and whether cancer was diagnosed after symptom onset following a negative assessment or after interruption of the recommended management pathway.

Although the simplified framework provides less detailed attribution than the full SPEACx classification, it retains the ability to identify the principal programme domains associated with cervical cancer occurrence and advanced-stage diagnosis. In our setting, the simplified and full versions highlighted the same major areas for improvement, suggesting that the simplified approach may provide meaningful programme evaluation even when detailed pathway reconstruction is not feasible.

Although the framework was developed using Basque Country data, its definitions were established through a national expert consensus process involving multiple regional screening programmes. Further studies should evaluate its reproducibility and applicability in other healthcare settings and organised screening programmes. Several Spanish regions are currently completing implementation of organised population-based cervical screening programmes. As these programmes mature, application of the SPEACx framework in future cervical cancer audits will provide an opportunity for external validation across different organisational settings.

In conclusion, SPEACx provides a structured and adaptable framework for evaluating cervical cancer screening performance by linking cancer diagnoses to programmatic stages. Its implementation may support targeted quality improvement, facilitate benchmarking across regions, and strengthen the public health impact of organized cervical cancer screening programs.

Conclusions

The SPEACx framework provides a structured, pathway-based approach to evaluating cervical cancer diagnoses within organized screening programs. By attributing each cancer case to specific stages of program delivery—such as invitation, participation, test performance, follow-up, and post-treatment surveillance—it enables a more actionable understanding of which stages of the screening pathway are associated with cancer diagnosis and may benefit from targeted quality improvement interventions.

Applied to the population-based cervical cancer screening program of the Basque Country, the framework demonstrated its capacity to identify diagnostic pathways associated with delayed diagnosis and advanced stage at detection, while also highlighting areas of program effectiveness. SPEACx is designed to complement existing international audit models by preserving comparability while enhancing program-level interpretability through a hierarchical and adaptable structure.

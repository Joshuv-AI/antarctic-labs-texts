Phase-TOF: single shot interferometric pulse laser ranging with nanometer accuracy
Authors: Xilun Gao, Peipei Jiang, Leilei Lin, Fengfan Cai, Tao Chen, Yonghang Shen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPulse ‘time of flight’ and interferometric measurement have long been two major approaches of laser ranging, with the former featuring simple and robust, suitable for long distance absolute ranging in a relatively poor accuracy and the latter behaving highly precise but complicated, suitable for follow-up measurement. Here we show these two approaches can be combined together to realize highly accurate and dynamic interferometric pulse laser ranging. By incorporating four frequency stabilized lasers to constitute a set of cascaded synthetic wavelengths, interferometric phase information of laser pulses can be effectively utilized to determine the ranging distance at up-to MHz pulse repetition rate with nano-meter accuracy. Pulse averaging was applied in the preliminary experiments while single shot ranging can be expected with improved apparatus. The approach can be used broadly to improve the performance of space-based telescope array, lithography and high precision three-dimensional survey Light Detection and Ranging (LiDAR).Subject terms: Optics and photonics, Physics

Introduction

Distance measurement has long been the important subject in science and engineering1–5. Laser ranging greatly facilitates the measurement with speed and accuracy. Since the laser was invented in 1960s, there have long been two categories of laser ranging techniques, with one called ‘time of flight (TOF)’ measurement1,6 and another using traditional interferometry7. The technique of TOF normally uses laser pulse directing to the object and measures the time interval, , between the emitting pulse and the received scattering light from the object to get the range information. In this way, the absolute distance between the laser emitting point and the object can be readily measured. It is thus suitable for quick and absolute long distance measurement with comparatively simple structure, and has been widely applied in practical applications8,9. Due to the accuracy limitation of time interval measurement, about tens of ps, however, TOF can only achieve ranging precision up to sub-mm level currently6,10. Many researches have been carried out and their works reported to improve the time measurement precision11–15. As a pity, the best results achieved were still limited to micron resolution. Recent advancements of TOF techniques in frequency modulated continuous wave16(FMCW) greatly enhance the anti-interference capability of laser ranging by providing coherent gain with local light, but the ranging precision is still limited.

Different from TOF, interferometry has long been considered as a highly accurate but relative distance measurement technique17–20. The traditional Michelson interferometer21,22 is still an important tool today for accurate optical measurement. It can achieve high distance precision up to sub-nanometer. The drawback of the technique mainly involves in its ‘follow-up’ dependence. That means the distance measurement can only be fulfilled by continuously tracking the amplitude and phase change of the interferometric signal when the ranging distance is greater than half wavelength of the laser. Such requirement makes the interferometric laser ranging system time-consuming, complicate and vulnerable to ambient interference, such as environmental shocking and temperature variation. It is not normally considered as suitable for long distance measurement. To overcome the above mentioned long range measurement limitation, dual-frequency interferometer23,24 and multi-wavelength interferometer (MWI)25 have been presented and successfully demonstrated. MWI achieves long unambiguous range by synthesizing much longer synthetic wavelengths from two or more distinct physical wavelengths. The key focus in MWI research is the synthetic wavelength technique26 and its corresponding wavelength selection methods27,28, which determine the optimal trade-off between the system’s unambiguous range and ultimate precision. It has been reported that the multi-wavelength interferometer can realize accurate distance measurement with range up to hundreds of meters and accuracy up to micrometer29–32. As a result, it has been constructed onto satellites to monitor the distance deviance of satellite array.

It should also be mentioned that as a type of specific interferometer, the advanced optical coherence tomography (OCT) system33–35 can realize micrometer distance ranging precision at rates of hundred kilo-Hertz, but the dynamic distance range is normally limited to tens of cent-meters36. Current mainstream OCT technologies are divided into spectral interferometry-based Spectral Domain OCT (SD-OCT)37,38 and Swept-Source OCT (SS-OCT) utilizing swept-frequency interferometry. Spectral interferometry typically employs a broadband, low-coherence light source in conjunction with a spectrometer for data acquisition. This technique operates by measuring the spectral modulation information resulting from the superposition of light beams within the wavelength domain. It requires only a single broadband source and a spectral analyzer, yielding a compact and structurally simple system. Spectral interferometry is highly effective at resolving the 2π ambiguity, thus achieving absolute distance or directional metrology. However, the ultimate measurement precision is inherently bounded by the resolution and calibration accuracy of the spectrometer. While swept-wavelength laser interferometry39 extended the ranging distance with comparatively high precision, its primary challenges stem from stringent hardware requirements and the non-linearity of the frequency sweep, which degrades the axial resolution.

The advent of optical frequency comb (OFC) provides a breakthrough technique for accurate and absolute long distance laser ranging40–43. Using two frequency slightly offset OFCs44–46(dual combs), accurate long distance measurement can be realized with accuracy of nanometers over measurement time around 60 ms44. Thus, the dual comb related laser ranging techniques have emerged as important and prospective laser ranging approaches, which have been extensively studied and practical applications strongly expected. The shortcoming of the dual-comb laser ranging is mainly resulted from its high complexity. Recent works in integrated dual OFCs may alleviate this problem to some extent47,48 so that the technique may be extended to applications of wider fields in the future. Another drawback is the multi-pulse requirement for the measurement and then the time required to determine the distance. Milliseconds, or even longer time, are normally required for achieving high ranging precision. Aiming to relieve this limitation, the so-called electro-optic sampling time detection49,50(EOS-TD) and multi-method combination51,52 were presented to achieve quick and highly dynamic long distance measurement, but the system was somewhat complicated.

As we have analyzed above, ‘TOF’ is a direct and simple method to measure the absolute distance. The difficulty deterring normal ‘TOF’ from realizing higher accuracy is mainly involved in the time precision between laser pulses. Now in this article we present a novel technique we call phase-TOF (Ф-TOF) to realize highly accurate and rapid interferometric pulse laser ranging, which holds the potential to achieve nanometer-level precision measurement over hundred meters range using single pulse.

Principle of Ф-TOF

The innovation associated with Ф-TOF is mainly at converting the time measurement to the phase difference interrogation, by using laser frequency and the associated phase change between the laser pulses reflected back from the reference and the object, respectively. It is well known that light of micron wavelength, such as visible and near infrared light, has very high frequency up to 1014 or 1015 Hz. The laser frequency-associated phase variation between the pulses will reflect the distance change. Thus, phase measurement precision of 1% light cycle is equivalent to 10− 16 or 10− 17 second, corresponding to nano-meter distance precision. The principle is illustrated in Fig. 1 with (a) showing the basic structure of Ф-TOF, (b) presenting the pulse characteristic comparison between normal TOF and Ф-TOF, and (c) the pulse evolution with phase variation in Ф-TOF.

Though the phase change of high frequency light signal as shown in Fig. 1 cannot be directly detected, it can be interrogated through heterodyne detection, in which a local continuous wave (CW) light is applied to interfere with the return pulse signal. Such local light should be controlled slightly different from the pulse signal in frequency, normally a radio-frequency value such as 200 MHz or so. As we have all known, the key point in the heterodyne detection is that the heterodyne interferometric signal will inherit all the phase change of the pulse signal light, but in much lower radio-frequency band, in which the interferometric signal can be readily detected using traditional wide band photodetector and sampling circuit. As shown in Fig. 1(a), a CW single frequency laser of frequency with good coherence is utilized as the light source and divided into two parts. One of them is utilized as the local light and another is shaped into pulses through an acousto-optic modulator (AOM). The pulses are directed to the object through a collimator with a reference reflection surface. The pulses reflected back from the reference and the object are combined to mix with the local CW light. As a result, there will form time-variant heterodyne interferometric fringes within the pulse envelopes, which can be used to resolve the frequency and the phase in addition to the intensity peak information. Ranging experiments with Ф-TOF

Preliminary experiments have been conducted to demonstrate the technical feasibility of Ф-TOF. Highly accurate and dynamic interferometric pulse laser ranging has been realized through four laser-combined coherent pulse demodulation, achieving distance precision of 30 nm at pulse repetition rate up to 100 kHz for an object up to 30 m away. A simplified Ф-TOF ranging system consisting of only two lasers has also been incorporated into a two-dimensional scanning galvanometer to realize fast three dimensional object shape survey, with ranging precision of 0.18 mm at laser pulse rate of 100 kHz.

The systematic diagram of the constructed Ф-TOF laser ranging system is illustrated in Fig. 3. Four wavelength-stabilized single frequency lasers are utilized as the CW laser sources, in which their wavelengths of = 1562.4435 nm, = 1562.3764 nm, = 1561.3049 nm and = 1535.2942 nm are specifically chosen to meet the requirement of the cascading rulers. Each laser is all divided into two parts, with one acting as the local light and another being combined together via fiber-optic couplers. The combined CW lasers are shaped into one single pulse when transmitting through an AOM, featuring pulse duration of about 20 ns. This pulse, composing of multi-wavelengths, is amplified in an Erbium doped fiber amplifier (EDFA) and then directed to a mirror through a three-port circulator and then a fiberized collimator. The mirror acts as the distant object to be measured, which is placed in a translation stage with nano-meter resolution. A Herriott multi-path gas cell with gas pressure of 0.07 atm is included in the optic-path so that the length exposed to air is only shorter than one meter, which is highly beneficial to eliminating the effect of air fluctuation during experiment.

During the laser ranging experiment, the laser pulses were continually emitted at repetition rate of 100 kHz, experiencing AOM frequency shift of 170 MHz. The pulse output from the circulator experienced minor reflection on the surface of the collimator, which served as an initial position reference to the distance measurement. The pulses reflecting back from the object were collected via the collimator and sent back to a fiber-coupler-constructed beam splitter through the circulator. The pulses coming back from the reference surface and the object were all divided into four parts, each interfering with a CW local light of specific wavelength to form the heterodyne interferometric pulses. All of light signals after interference were detected by four balanced photo-detectors (BPD) separately and recorded continuously in a four-channel broadband oscilloscope (Agilent DSO9254A) for analysis and ranging computation. As the AOM worked at designated modulation frequency, the interference signal between the pulses and local CW light would generate pulse envelopes, composing of interference fringe of the same frequency when the object to be measured was static.

One typical recorded waveform composing of a pulse train is shown in Fig. 3b. The typical interference fringe within the pulse envelope can be distinctly observed as shown in Fig. 3c. Each pair contains two pulses with the former coming back from the reference surface and the latter from the mirror. The phase differences between two pulses in all the channels were demodulated separately and used together to determine the distance between the reference and the object.

In Fig. 3d, the four ruler-corresponded phase differences at a fixed distance are demonstrated for all 150 times of measurement together with the accompanied TOF results. The precision of TOF is about 0.1 ns, corresponding to ranging precision of 15 mm, which can be used to determine the initial of the first synthesis wavelength. As to the pulse phases, the channel of single wavelength was directly demodulated while other three synthetic channels were computed from the differences of wavelength channels. A Ф-TOF –based imaging Light Detection and Ranging(LiDAR) system for three dimensional shape measurement of objects was also constructed. As shown in Fig. 4(a), the LiDAR consists of a simplified Ф-TOF ranging system and a two-dimensional galvanometric scanner. Its ranging precision is designed to be 0.2 mm, high enough for most surveying LiDAR. Two lasers, instead of four lasers, around 1562 nm with stable frequency difference of about 8.2460 GHz, are employed here. By independently processing the phase information carried in each wavelength and exploiting the phase relationship between two wavelength channels, a synthesized wavelength of 36.38 mm was achieved. The pulses composing of two wavelengths were launched at pulse repetition rate of 100 kHz while the two-dimensional galvanometric scanner directed the laser pulses toward the object to be measured. Ф-TOF –based ranging was realized for an array of 1932⨯1960, covering angles of 0.530°⨯0.537°. A three-dimensional image of the measured object, an eagle statute (see the photo in Fig. 4(b)) placed about 8.1 m away from the galvanometric scanner, is shown in Fig. 4(c). For this measurement, only one pulse is applied for every scanning point but the final ranging value was smoothly averaged with its neighbor points, resulting in a ranging resolution of 0.18 mm. In this setup, the lateral resolution is primarily constrained by the beam diameter, which is adjusted to less than 1 mm within the experimental working distance through manual focusing. Integration of active focusing module in the system will allow for dynamic compensation of the wavefront in the future, ensuring high-resolution 3D reconstruction over an extended volume.

Discussion and conclusion

Briefly in summary, we have presented a highly accurate and dynamic laser ranging approach, named ‘Ф-TOF’, which features simplicity of TOF ranging but with precision of interferometric measurement. We have theoretically deduced that the ranging precision can be improved from relatively rough of millimeters to final of nanometers by constructing a set of synthetic wavelengths to transit the accuracy step by step. The requirement of frequency stability is also given out, which is found being dependent on the distance to be measured. By combining four lasers with specific selected wavelengths, coherent pulse ranging experiment with 100 kHz pulse repetition rate has been successfully demonstrated with precision better than 30 nm under distance up to 30 m. A three dimensional imaging LiDAR has also been constructed by incorporating an simplified ranging system with only two lasers into a two-dimensional galvanometric scanner, realizing ranging precision of 0.18 mm, which can be further improved by using more lasers upon requirement.

The main advantage of the presented ‘Ф-TOF’ is its potential capability to reach nano-meter ranging precision in single shot pulse. Only one pulse will be required to emit for the highly precise distance measurement as the laser wavelengths involved in the whole measurement are all included in the same pulse, which is much different from the dual-comb-based ranging. The pulse phase information of different wavelengths for the heterodyne interferometry is collected from the identical time point when the pulse strikes the reference and the object. Because of the short pulse interval between the pulses reflecting back from the reference and the object, the lasers can stably maintain their statues during such short a time interval. The ambient disturbance, such as temperature variation and mechanical vibration, would not severely affect the local optic-path used for heterodyne interferometry. As a result, the local lights, detectors and the associated fiber devices can all be housed in simple compact structure, totally different from the traditional interferometer. Though the present results still utilized multi-pulse averaging to improve the precision and fulfill the step by step ruler accuracy translation, this situation will be greatly improved when better frequency locking of the lasers be accomplished. With better frequency locking, the phase demodulation results with different lasers would be stable enough. As a result, the phase demodulation precision from single probing pulse would be high enough to complete the precision transition among the synthetic rulers.

The pulse repetition rate for the distance measurement can be easily extended to MHz while the ranging precision be further improved to less than one nanometer by using highly stable single frequency lasers. The phase information within the interferometric pulse envelopes can be processed in real time via Field-Programmable Gate Array (FPGA), which favors the development of compact precise ranging instrument or three dimensional imaging LiDAR.

The frequency stability of the lasers are essential to ensure the Ф-TOF -based ranging precision. Though many factors, such as electronic noise, time jitter and pulse shape deviation, may also affect the ranging precision, our simulations reveal their effect was negligible under the preliminary experimental condition. The signal to noise ratio of the received signal pulse was much higher than 20 dB, as illustrated in Fig. 3. The time jitter of the sampling oscilloscope was a small value of 500 fs, resulting in very low phase fluctuation of less than rad. In the preliminary experiments, the received echo signal, as illustrated in Fig. 3(c), was observed to be near-ideal in shape by carefully using manual focusing to keep the laser spot size below 1 mm around the target location. In the future, auto-focus design of emitting telescope, together with laser power compensation in the whole system, will be implanted, which would be important to assure the precision. For the precision transition of synthetic wavelengths, only the relative frequency differences are concerned, which is comparatively easy to keep them stable. However, the requirement for the single frequency laser is much higher when the final precision of nanometer is concerned. Phase precision of 1% or better is necessary to achieve the ranging precision of better than 10 nm, which sets a rigid criterion for the laser frequency stability. Frequency locking to high finesse F-P or the frequency comb would be feasible to assure such stability.

The obtained distance ranging value is only compared with amount of translation stage movement for the time being. Further comparison with the long range follow-up interferometer will be carried out after the real-time pulse phase demodulation is realized in the FPGA in the future. Based on the simple ranging principle, the data obtained is credible if only the laser wavelengths are stable and the precision is assured. High accuracy wavelength-meter is suitable for such wavelength measurement when the distance range is within 100 m. For longer distance measurement, however, calibration onto highly stable OFC is required. With the development of Ф-TOF technique, we believe it can be widely applied for position control of highly precise translation stage in lithography, satellite base alignment of spaced–based telescope array and system construction of compact imaging LiDAR, and will be highly prospective in dynamic interferometry.

For a comprehensive assessment of the Ф-TOF performance, a qualitative comparison is provided in Table 3 with dual-comb, FMCW, traditional MWI and traditional TOF in terms of operating range, achievable precision, and speed. Compared with conventional multi-wavelength interferometry, the proposed Ф-TOF technique offers distinct advantages for high-precision, long-range metrology. By employing an AOM-based pulse frequency-shifting scheme, the system adopts a quasi-common-path Fizeau configuration, which greatly suppresses the measurement error induced by environmental vibrations—a common challenge in normal Michelson interferometer. Furthermore, the combination of interferometric measurement with normal TOF enables the Ф-TOF to cover large ranging distance with nano-meter high precision. In the current stage, however, the pulse interferometric information process is somewhat complicated, which needs substantial improvement to realize real-time signal processing. The final pulse phase resolution in the Ф-TOF may be slightly poorer than normal multi-wavelength interferometer because of the frequency expansion within the laser pulse envelope. It is also important to avoid the pulse overlapping between the reference and the object pulses so that the ‘so-called’ dead-zone can be avoided.

Simultaneously frequency locking of laser lines

In the experiment, four laser lines with suitable wavelengths have been used. Two of them are obtained by locking two single-frequency semiconductor lasers simultaneously to an ultra-stable F-P cavity. Frequency locking of 1535.2942 nm and 1562.4435 nm lasers are realized by Pound-Drever-Hall (PDH) locking technique, achieving relative frequency variation of at equivalent frequency of about 3.3952758 THz. The other two laser lines of 1562.3764 nm and 1561.3049 nm are obtained through EO modulation. A single-sideband EO modulator working at around 8.2460 GHz is used for 1562.3764 nm line generation after direct modulation of 1562.4435 nm laser. Another line of 1561.3049 nm is obtained through the cascaded EO modulation (including one intensity modulator and two phase modulators) to the original 1562.4435 nm laser and spectral filtering from the frequency extended multi-line comb source. A modulation frequency of 20 GHz is applied for the cascaded EO modulation.

These four frequency-stable laser wavelengths are deliberately selected as they form a set of synthetic rulers to ensure smooth transfers of precision from the coarsest TOF measurement down to the final nanometer-level single-wavelength interferometry. By combining these four wavelengths in pairs, we generate three distinct synthetic frequencies: 8.2460 GHz, 140.0204 GHz, and 3.3952758 THz.

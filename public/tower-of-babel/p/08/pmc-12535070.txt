Prevalence characteristics of human papillomavirus among women in Henan Province of China
Authors: Xiaoying Zhu, Shenfeng Li, Chunbo Zhuang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe aim of this study is to analyze the prevalence and genotype distribution of HPV among the female populations in Henan Province, which can provide an evidence for the local governments to make develop strategies for cervical cancer prevention and vaccination.MethodsThe study was designed to analyze the results of cervical swab HPV test results from female patients who visited the First Affiliated Hospital of Zhengzhou University from January 2020 to May 2023. The collected cervical specimens were processed using specialized nucleic acid extraction reagents to obtain purified nucleic acid extracts. Genotype identification was then performed via real-time fluorescence PCR technology with two commercial detection kits. The assay detects 23 HPV genotypes (17 high-risk types: 16, 18, 31, 33, 35, 39, 45, 51, 52, 53, 56, 58, 59, 66, 68, 73, 82; and 6 low-risk types: 6, 11, 42, 43, 44, 81). The HPV prevalence was calculated using GraphPad Prism 8.0.ResultsThe overall HPV prevalence was 21.31% (12,950/60,765) among the 60,765 female patients, of which the single, double, and multiple HPV infections accounted for 14.81% (9,001/60,765), 4.25% (2,584/60,765), and 2.25% (1,365/60,765) of the total cases. 17.82% (10,836/60,765) of the total samples were infected with high-risk HPV (HR-HPV), accounting for 83.67% (10,836/12,950) of the HPV positive women. The most common infection pattern is HPV single infection, accounting for 69.51% of the positive women. The top four most commonly detected types were HPV-52 (4.30% 2,612/60,765), HPV-58 (2.81% 1,710/60,765), HPV-16 (2.81% 1,709/60,765), and HPV-53 (2.25% 1,367/60,765). The positive rate of HPV among different age groups was statistically significant (p<0.05).ConclusionThis study provides baseline data on the epidemiological characteristics of HPV. The prevalence of HPV among women in Henan Province is high. The highest prevalence is among women less than 20 years. The predominant genotypes identified were HPV 52, 58, 16, and 53. This genotype distribution suggests that the Gardasil 9 vaccine, which covers HPV 52, 58, and 16, could potentially address a significant proportion of high-risk HPV infections in this population. The high prevalence of HPV 53, a genotype not targeted by current vaccines, warrants further investigation into its oncogenic role and epidemiological significance.Keyword: Human papillomavirus, Genotype, Cervical cancer

Introduction

Cervical cancer is one of the most common malignant tumors in women, which may initially manifest as abnormal hyperplasia of the cervical epithelium. The development of cervical cancer usually takes many years and gradually evolves into cancer [1]. Cervical cancer remains a significant global health issue, with substantial variations among different countries [2]. In China alone, there are approximately 98,900 new cervical cancer cases and 30,500 new deaths each year [3]. The incidence rate of cervical cancer is influenced by various factors, among which high-risk Human Papillomavirus (HPV) infection plays a pivotal role, particularly when left untreated for an extended period. Additional risk factors encompass smoking, multiple sexual partners, compromised immune function, socioeconomic status, and educational attainment [4]. HPV is a small, non-enveloped DNA virus that can easily induce abnormal growth of squamous epithelial cells in both human skin and mucous membranes. Currently, more than 200 genotypes of HPV have been discovered, with different types affecting various parts of the body and resulting in different clinical symptoms [5, 6]. HPV can be transmitted through skin-to-mucous membrane contact, particularly through sexual contact. HPV infection can lead to changes in cervical epithelial cells, potentially evolving into cervical cancer. Nevertheless, the majority infections caused by HPV can be cleared by the body immune system, and only a small portion would develop into cervical cancer [7, 8].. Based on their oncogenic potential, HPV genotypes are classified by the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC). Twelve HPV genotypes (16, 18, 31, 33, 35, 39, 45, 51, 52, 56, 58, and 59) are classified as Group 1 carcinogens, meaning they are definitively carcinogenic to humans. Additionally, HPV 68 is classified as probably carcinogenic (Group 2 A), and genotypes including HPV53, 66, 73, and 82 are classified as possibly carcinogenic (Group 2B)[9].

Many reports have shown that persistent infection of high-risk HPV was a key factor for the development of cervical cancer [10]. Despite the relatively high incidence of cervical cancer, especially the ascending incidence in younger women, the mortality rate has significantly decreased due to improvements in early screening and treatment methods [11]. Early detection plays a crucial role in identifying abnormalities before cervical cancer advances to advanced stages, thereby offering improved treatment opportunities. This emphasizes the importance of prevention and early screening measures in controlling cervical cancer [12]. Prophylactic HPV vaccines have been developed to target the most prevalent HPV types associated with cervical cancer and other diseases. The bivalent vaccine targets the two major high-risk types, HPV-16 and HPV-18, which are responsible for a large proportion of cervical cancer cases globally [13, 14]. The quadrivalent vaccine provides protection against these same two high-risk types (HPV-16, 18) as well as two low-risk types (HPV-6, 11) that cause the majority of genital warts [15]. The nonavalent vaccine targets an extended spectrum of seven high-risk types (HPV-16, 18, 31, 33, 45, 52, 58), which are associated with cervical cancers, in addition to the same two low-risk types (HPV-6, 11) responsible for genital warts [16]. By immunizing individuals against these high-risk HPV types, the likelihood of developing cervical cancer can be significantly reduced. The administration of HPV vaccines is typically recommended for adolescents and young adult females to ensure protection before potential exposure to the virus. However, because the prevalence and genotypes of HPV infections associated with cervical cancer vary among different countries, regions, and populations, it is essential to gather specific data on HPV in respective areas to guide vaccine administration and early screening efforts in clinical practice.

While previous studies have established the general prevalence of HPV in China, there is a pressing need for large-scale, contemporary data from Central China that utilizes precise genotyping methods aligned with the latest IARC classifications. Specifically, recent trends in genotype distribution and their implications for vaccination strategy in the Henan population, remain underexplored. Therefore, the purpose of this study was to investigate the prevalence and genotype distribution of HPV, with a specific focus on high-risk and potentially carcinogenic types as defined by IARC. Meanwhile, we also want to find the potential shift in the ranking of predominant genotypes. We aim to find the potential shift in the ranking of predominant genotypes and evaluate the theoretical coverage of current HPV vaccines against the circulating genotypes in this population.

Material and methods

Study population

The cervical swabs were collected from female patients who voluntarily visited the First Affiliated Hospital of Zhengzhou University from January 2020 to May 2023. The participants sought professional instruction and treatment at the hospital for various reasons, including physical examinations, abnormal uterine bleeding, fibroids, cervical lesions, menstrual disorders, vaginitis, cervical intraepithelial neoplasia, and genital warts. Participants were categorized into two groups based on the context of their visit: the Health Examination Group and Outpatient/Inpatient Group. The Health Examination Group consisted of asymptomatic women presenting for routine preventive gynecological screening. The Outpatient/Inpatient Group consisted of women seeking medical care for various gynecological or non-gynecological health concerns. A total of 60,765 female participants aged over 15 years and attending cervical screening for the first time were included in the study. Participants who had undergone subsequent rounds of screening were excluded from the study. To ensure adequate representation across different age groups, the participants were divided into six categories: ≤20 years, 21–30 years, 31–40 years, 41–50 years, 51–60 years, and >60 years.

Specimen collection

The specimens were collected by professional gynecologists using specialized tools such as a vaginal speculum to expose the patients cervix. They used a cotton swab to collect samples of cervical mucus and secretions. Subsequently, slowly inserted the cervical brush into the cervix and rotated it 3–4 times to obtain sufficient cervical epithelial cell samples. After collection, the cervical brush was placed into an HPV sample tube (ThinPrep PreservCyt Solution, Hologic Inc, USA) for preservation, and then sent to the laboratory for testing.

HPV DNA extraction, PCR amplification, detection and genotyping

HPV DNA was extracted by the steps in specification using a nucleic acid extraction reagent kit (Suzhou Bacme biotech co. LTD., Jiangsu, China). Crude HPV DNA was captured using magnetic glass particles, followed by cell lysis and purification using an MSS-32 automated nucleic acid extraction instrument. (Suzhou Bacme biotech Co. LTD., Jiangsu, China). HPV detection and genotyping were performed using two commercial real-time PCR assays: the Medium-High-risk Human PapillomavirusTyping Real-Time PCR kit (Suzhou Bacme biotech co., LTD, Suzhou, China) and the Low-risk Human PapillomavirusTyping Real-Time PCR kit (Suzhou Bacme biotech co., LTD, Suzhou, China). The Medium High risk Human PapillomavirusTyping Real-Time PCR kit was used to detect 17 HR-HPV types (HPV 16, 18, 31, 33, 35, 39, 45, 51, 52, 53, 56, 58, 59, 66, 68, 73, 82), while the Low risk Human PapillomavirusTyping Real-Time PCR kit was used to identify genotypes 6, 11, 42, 43, 44, 81. The experiments were conducted strictly according to the manufacturers protocols. The experiment used real-time fluorescence quantitative PCR technology to design specific primers and fluorescent probes for the conserved regions of various genotypes of nucleic acid. Four fluorescent groups labeled with different colors were used to independently detect and distinguish multiple HPV genotypes at different wavelengths. There were seven types of HR-HPV amplification reaction solutions: A, B1, B2, B3, B4, B5, and B6, while the LR-HPV amplification reaction solutions including A, B1, and B2. Class B reagents feature specific primers and fluorescent probe reagents. Before the experiments, a mixture of 10uL of A reagent and 10uL of B series reagent was prepared as an amplification reaction solution (ABn). The detection processes consisted of primary screening and specialized types identification. In the first step, 20μL of nucleic acids extract was mixed with 20μL of HR-HPV reagents AB1, AB2, and the LR-HPV AB1 amplification reaction system separately. The detection process employed four fluorescent channels: FAM, HEX/VIC, ROX, and CY5. The HR-HPV reagent AB1 could detect four types: HPV-16, internal control (IC), HPV-52, and HPV-58. The AB2 could detect HPV-18, and HPV-31. Besides, if the FAM channel showed abnormal signals during the AB2 detection, AB3 was used to detect types HPV-33 and HPV-45. If the ROX detection channel was abnormal, AB4 (HPV-59, HPV-51, HPV-68, HPV-35), AB5 (HPV-73, HPV-56, HPV-82), and AB6 (HPV-53, HPV-66, HPV-39) were employed for retesting the nucleic acids extract to complete the HPV type-specific detection with the same process. The LR-HPV AB1 could detect the types HPV-6, IC, and HPV-11. If the RXO channel was abnormal, AB2 (HPV-42, HPV-43, HPV-44, HPV-81) was used to detect the remaining LR-HPV types. The SLAN-96P Real-time PCR System assay (Hongshi Medical Technology Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) was used for gene amplification. The amplification parameters were set as follows: 50 °C for 2 min, 94 °C for 5 min, followed by 45 cycles of 10 s at 94 °C, 35 s at 55°C. During the experiment, besides the positive control and negative control, an internal reference system was used to identify human cell housekeeping genes (β-globin gene), which could monitor nucleic acids extract detection throughout the entire process.

Statistical analysis

The data were entered into Microsoft Excel 2019 v16.0 (Microsoft, Redmond, WA, USA) and analyzed using GraphPad Prism software version 8.0 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA). A descriptive analysis was performed to generate summary statistics, including the mean, median, standard deviation, and 95% confidence intervals, for the baseline demographic and behavioral attributes. HPV infection was defined using different criteria: any HPV infection, infection with low-risk HPV types, infection with high-risk HPV types, and the presence of specific HPV types targeted by vaccines such as Cervarix® (HPV-16 and HPV-18), Gardasil®4 (HPV-6, HPV-11, HPV-16, HPV-18), and Gardasil®9 (HPV-6, HPV-11, HPV-16, HPV-18, HPV-31, HPV-33, HPV-45, HPV-52, HPV-58).

Results

Overall prevalence of HPV infection

A total of 60,765 women aged 15–93 years were enrolled in the study, with an average age of 39.5 ± 11.7 years. Among these participants, 12,950 cervical swab samples tested positive for HPV, resulting in an overall prevalence rate of 21.31% (12,950/60,765) (Table 1). Analysis by group revealed a positivity rate of 14.73% in the Health Examination Group and 22.73% in the Outpatient/Inpatient Group (Table 2). Among the HPV positive cases, the rate of women infected with only low-risk HPV (LR-HPV) was 3.48% (2,114/60,765), accounting for 16.32% (2,114/12,950) of the total positive cases. On the other hand, the rate of women infected with only high-risk HPV (HR-HPV) was 14.91% (9,064/60,765), accounting for 69.99% (9,064/12,950) of the total positive cases. Additionally, the rate of HR-HPV and LR-HPV mixed infections was 2.91% (1,772/60,765), accounting for 13.68% (1,772/12,950) of the total positive cases. Based on the above data, it can be concluded that 83.67% (10,836/12,950) of the HPV infections were caused by one or more HR-HPV genotypes.

Distribution of HPV genotype

Among the positive cases of HPV infection, all 17 high-risk HPV types and 6 low-risk HPV types were detected. The detection rate for each specific HPV genotype was calculated as the number of samples testing positive for that specific genotype (as a single infection or part of a multiple infection) divided by the total number of samples in the study cohort, multiplied by 100. The most commonly detected HPV types were HPV-52 (4.30% 2,612/60,765), HPV-58 (2.81% 1,710/60,765), HPV-16 (2.81% 1,709/60,765), and HPV-53 (2.25% 1,367/60,765), all of which were high-risk HPV types. These four HPV types accounted for the highest prevalence among the positive cases. Regarding the low-risk HPV types, the three most commonly detected types were HPV-42 (1.84% 1,117/60,765), HPV-81 (1.18% 717/60,765), and HPV-6 (1.14% 694/60,765), as shown in Figure 1.

Prevalence and genotypes distribution of HPV among 60,765 women

HPV infection rates with different genotypes

Among the 12,950 infected individuals, the infection rates with different genotypes were as follows: 69.51% (9,001/12,950) for single HPV genotype; 19.95% (2,584/12,950) for double HPV genotypes; 6.50% (842/12,950) for triple HPV genotypes; 2.49% (323/12,950) for four HPV genotypes; 0.93% (121/12,950) for five HPV genotypes; and 0.61% (79/12,950) for more than five HPV genotypes. The highest number of genotypes detected in a single individual was nine (Table 2). It is worth noting that single HPV genotypes were found to be the most common pattern among individuals with detected HPV.

Distribution of infection types in different age groups

The enrolled participants were divided into six age groups, and the overall HPV prevalence rates were as follows: ≤20 years (34.02% 395/1,161), 21–30 years (21.22% 2,892/13,627), 31–40 years (19.63% 3,904/19,886), 41–50 years (19.55% 2,775/14,191), 51–60 years (24.54% 2,305/9,391), and >60 years (27.06% 679/2,509). The age group of ≤20 years had the highest prevalence rate, followed by a decrease in prevalence rate until the 41–50 years age group. Subsequently, the prevalence rate showed an increase in the 51–60 age group and >60 age group (Table 3). The Pearsons Chi-squared test revealed a significant difference in infection among the age groups (χ2=279.5, p<0.001). Similar trends were observed for the prevalence of high-risk HPV (Figure 2). Regarding the prevalence of different infection patterns in the age groups, a single HPV infection was found to be the most common pattern. The highest rate of single HPV infection occurred in the ≤20 years age group (17.92% 208/1,161), followed by the 51–60 years (16.48% 1,548/9,391), >60 years (15.98% 401/2,509), 31–40 years (14.50% 2,883/19,886), 41–50 years (14.41% 2,045/14,191), and 21–30 years (14.06% 1,916/13,627). For double HPV infections, the highest infection rate was found in the ≤20 age group (7.06% 82/1,161), while the lowest was observed in the 41–50 years age group (3.59% 509/14,191). Furthermore, among individuals with multiple HPV infections, the highest infection rate was still found in the ≤20 age group (9.04% 105/1,161). Notably, the prevalence of the ≤20 age group was the only one that exceeded the double infection rate (Table 3).

Discussion

Cervical carcinoma is one of the most common malignancies among women worldwide, which has been becoming a serious public health problem all over the world. Various researches had confirmed that the high-risk type HPV persistent infection was the main factor causing cervical carcinoma and precancerous lesions in women. In China, the prevalence of HPV was greatly varies between provinces [17]. However, the researches on the prevalence of HPV infection in Henan Province were rare. In our study, we provide evidence for the prevalence and genotype data in Henan Province.

In this study, among the enrolled participants, the overall prevalence of HPV was 21.31% (12,950/60,765), which is consistent with the reported rates of 21.0% in the Beijing area [18] and 22.41% in the Hangzhou [19]. It was lower than the rates in Heilongjiang at 27.1% [12] and Shandong at 28.4% [20], but higher than the reported rates in Hubei at 16.09% [21] and Xiamen at 17.13% [22]. In the distribution within the Henan region, it is higher than the reported rate of 19.7% in northern Henan [23]. These differences may be attributed to variations in population composition, education levels, and economic status among different regions [24]. These data suggest that the HPV infection level in Henan remains relatively high. We should strengthen education and vaccination efforts in the region to reduce the HPV infection rate.

Most studies have been only used the outpatient samples for the HPV epidemiology. Since the outpatients usually visit the hospital for further treatment, the HPV prevalence may be different from the whole population. Our study cohort comprised a large sample of women from both a routine health screening setting (n=10,750) and a hospital-based clinical care setting (n=50,015). It is important to note that the combined cohort is not intended to be a perfect representation of the general population of Henan Province, as hospital-based studies are susceptible to selection bias (e.g., the Outpatient/Inpatient Group may have a higher burden of health issues). However, the immense size of our dataset provides a powerful snapshot of the circulating HPV genotypes within a major tertiary hospital catchment area. The Health Examination Group sub-cohort, while also subject to the biases of a self-selected screening population, offers a better estimate of prevalence in asymptomatic women. The primary value of our data lies in the detailed analysis of genotype distribution, which is less likely to be severely skewed by the sampling framework than the absolute prevalence estimate. The results showed that the HPV prevalence in the Health Examination Group was only 14.73% (1,584/10,750), which is significantly lower than that of 21.31% (12,950/60,765) and the prevalence of Outpatient/Inpatient Group at 22.73% (11,366/50,015). Among the positive patients, HR-HPV infections accounted for 83.67% (10,836/12,950).

In genotype infections, single-type infections accounted for 69.51% (9,001/12,950), double infections accounted for 19.95% (2,584/12,950), and multiple infections accounted for 10.52% (1,365/12,950). Single-type infections were the predominant infection type, consistent with reports from most regions across the country. In this study, the highest number of genotypes observed in a single infection reached up to 9-fold infections.

As widely acknowledged, the distribution of HPV genotypes plays a crucial role in shaping vaccination strategies and providing essential data support. Among the high-risk HPV genotypes, HPV 16, 18, 31, 58, and 52 were globally prevalent, with particular emphasis on HPV 16 and 18, which were detected in approximately 70% of cervical cancer cases [25]. In various regions of China, the most frequently observed high-risk HPV genotypes are HPV 16, 52, and 58 [24, 26]. Our research findings indicate that the top five high-risk HPV genotypes, namely 52, 16, 58, 53, and 39, are closely associated with the occurrence of cervical cancer. Notably, HPV 52 exhibits the highest prevalence rate, accounting for 4.30% (2,612/60,765), higher than the 16 and 18 types with higher prevalence worldwide. In addition, we also analyzed the distribution of LR-HPV, and its top three genotypes were HPV42, 81, 6. These results indicate that in addition to considering the traditional HR-HPV 16 and 18 genotypes, the 52, 58, 53 genotypes with higher prevalence also should be included in our vaccination plan in Henan Province.

As a result of this study, we found that the age group under 20 had the highest HPV infection rate, reaching 34.02% (395/1,161). On the other hand, the age group over 60 had the second highest infection rate, reaching 27.06% (679/2,509). Among individuals aged 21–60, the HPV infection rate exhibits a trend of initially decreasing and then increasing, with the lowest HPV infection rate observed in the 41–50 age group, followed by a continuous increase.

The distribution of infections across various age groups exhibits a "U" shaped pattern, akin to investigations conducted on the female populace in China [24] and internationally [27]. Females under the age of 20 demonstrate a higher prevalence of HPV infection, potentially attributed to more frequent engagement in sexual activity without sufficient protective measures, as well as an incompletely developed immune system [28]. It has been documented that the infections in the majority of young women are transient [29].

After the age of 50, there was an increase in the HPV infection rate, possibly due to changes of women in the perimenopausal period, which may experience hormonal fluctuations and a decline in physical function, leading to a weakened immune system [10, 30]. This can result in reduced ability to clear viral infections, leading to sustained viral infections and disease progression. We observed that women in the ≤20 and >60 age groups, the overall infection rate, single infection rate, dual infection rate, and multiple infection rate were all very high. Therefore, we should simultaneously strengthen vaccination efforts for teenagers and HPV screening for the elderly regularly [31, 32]. This approach may help reduce HPV infections and the incidence of cervical cancer. In addition, among the distribution of infected individuals, the age of 21–60 years old has the highest number of infections. While we focus on the infection rate, it is also important to consider the size of the infected population. This can better guide us in formulating prevention and treatment strategies for HPV.

This research has certain limitations that should be considered. The primary limitation is the lack of detailed clinical data, such as cytology or histology results, which prevents us from determining the actual disease status (e.g., normal, CIN1, CIN2+, or cancer) of the HPV-positive individuals. Therefore, our results depict the circulation of HPV genotypes in the population but cannot directly assess their association with cervical disease progression. Secondly, as a single-center study, although conducted at a major referral hospital, our participant cohort may not be fully representative of the entire population of Henan Province, potentially limiting the generalizability of our findings. The patient population at a large tertiary hospital might differ from that in community screening centers. Future multi-center studies that combine HPV genotyping with clinical outcomes are essential to confirm the oncogenic impact of the most prevalent genotypes and to provide more robust data on the HPV prevalence landscape for the region.

Conclusion

In conclusion, our study provides critical, up-to-date evidence on the HPV genotype distribution in a large population from Henan Province. The key novel findings are the potential shift in the ranking of predominant genotypes and the significant prevalence of HPV-53 (a non-vaccine targeted type). The predominant genotypes were the HPV52, 58, 16, and 53. In terms of age distribution, relatively higher infection rates were observed among individuals aged ≤20 and those over 60. These results underscore the importance of nonavalent vaccine inoculation and highlight the need for preventing the HPV-53 genotype.

ABSTRACT
The exploration of non-Hermitian quantum systems, particularly those governed by parity–time (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathscr {P}\mathscr {T}$$\end{document})-symmetry, has revealed a rich landscape of unconventional phase transitions and localization phenomena. Spin chains with quasiperiodic potentials and competing Heisenberg interactions provide a versatile framework for probing these effects. In this study, we investigate the dynamical and spectral signatures of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathscr {P}\mathscr {T}$$\end{document}-symmetry breaking and localization-delocalization transitions in spin chains subjected to a non-Hermitian Aubry-André potential and tunable Heisenberg couplings across nearest (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$J_1$$\end{document}) and next-nearest neighbors (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$J_2$$\end{document}). Our analysis reveals that increasing \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$J_1$$\end{document} monotonically shifts the critical point \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda _C$$\end{document}, while \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$J_2$$\end{document} induces a non-linear response, generating a distinct minimum in \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda _C$$\end{document}. Notably, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$J_2$$\end{document} leads to the emergence of partially imaginary eigenvalues beyond \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda _C$$\end{document}, decoupling the onset of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathscr {P}\mathscr {T}$$\end{document}-symmetry breaking from the localization transition. Using inverse and normalized participation ratios, we identify three distinct phases: extended, localized, and an intermediate hybrid regime characterized by a mobility edge. To capture the dynamical fingerprints of these phases, we employ time-dependent density distribution, long-time survival probability \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathrm {P(r)}$$\end{document}, analysis. Extended phases exhibit ballistic spreading and algebraic decay, while localized regimes show spatial confinement and exponential decay. These observables serve as robust indicators of non-Hermitian phase transitions, offering insights beyond static spectral measures. Overall, our findings underscore the critical role of dynamical metrics in characterizing phase structure in non-Hermitian spin systems. As interest in non-equilibrium quantum dynamics continues to grow, such models provide a compelling platform for understanding the interplay between \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathscr{P}\mathscr{T}$$\end{document}-symmetry, localization, and quantum coherence in complex many-body systems.

Introduction

Non-Hermitian quantum systems, characterized by complex energy spectra and the emergence of exceptional points, offer a transformative framework that extends beyond traditional Hermitian mechanics1–3. Unlike closed systems, these frameworks effectively describe open quantum dynamics where gain and loss mechanisms lead to unconventional phase transitions4–6. A primary platform for exploring such physics is the one-dimensional quasicrystal, which possesses aperiodic long-range order. Among these, the Aubry-André (AA) model has remained a cornerstone for studying the localization-delocalization transition in quasi-periodic lattices7–9.

The introduction of non-Hermiticity into the AA model has revealed a rich landscape of dynamics, particularly through the lens of Parity-Time (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathcal{P}\mathcal{T}$$\end{document}) symmetry10,11. In standard \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathcal{P}\mathcal{T}$$\end{document}-symmetric systems, a purely real energy spectrum can be maintained as long as the loss and gain are balanced. Once a critical threshold is crossed, the symmetry “breaks”, and eigenvalues become complex, a transition typically marked by drastic changes in transport properties12,13. However, recent theoretical advances have shown that explicit site-centered \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathcal{P}\mathcal{T}$$\end{document} symmetry is not a strict prerequisite for real-to-complex spectral transitions. In non-\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathcal{P}\mathcal{T}$$\end{document} symmetric models, the ergodic nature of an irrational frequency \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha$$\end{document} can ensure the stability of a real phase even in the absence of traditional balanced gain and loss10.

While many non-Hermitian studies are linked to topological phase transitions and edge states14,15, the present work focuses on a different frontier: the stability of the real energy spectrum and localization phenomena within the bulk of a Heisenberg spin chain. Spin systems provide a versatile laboratory for such investigations16–18, especially when short-range nearest-neighbor (NN) couplings (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$J_1$$\end{document}) are extended to include next-nearest-neighbor (NNN) interactions (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$J_2$$\end{document}). While NN interactions capture basic correlations, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$J_2$$\end{document} couplings are essential for describing complex magnetic orders and the emergence of intermediate regimes19,20. Furthermore, incorporating modulated interactions can significantly enrich the system’s capacity for quantum state transfer and entanglement propagation21–23.

Building upon our previous findings24, this study demonstrates that the misalignment (decoupling) between the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathcal{P}\mathcal{T}$$\end{document}-symmetry breaking point and the localization transition is not merely a consequence of the interaction type, but is fundamentally driven by the presence of NNN interactions.This decoupling creates a unique intermediate regime, a hybrid phase where localized and extended states coexist, characterized by a mobility edge. By rigorously analyzing eigenvalue spectra, participation ratios, and long-time dynamical evolution, we uncover how NNN couplings disrupt the conventional one-to-one correlation between spectral and spatial transitions.

The system under investigation is a non-Hermitian Heisenberg spin chain characterized by both NN and NNN interactions. The lattice is subjected to a complex quasiperiodic potential, which varies across the sites with an incommensurate frequency. In non-Hermitian systems, the presence of a real energy spectrum is typically tied to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathcal{P}\mathcal{T}$$\end{document} symmetry, a balanced condition between parity (spatial reflection) and time-reversal. In standard models, this symmetry is usually “site-centered,” meaning the system remains invariant when reflected across the middle of the chain. While our model utilizes a complex exponential potential that lacks this conventional mirror symmetry, it remains fundamentally governed by its ergodic nature. Because the potential is incommensurate with the lattice, it samples all possible values across the chain, making its global spectral properties independent of any specific starting point or local shift. Consequently, the system still undergoes a robust transition from a real to a complex spectrum, effectively replacing local, site-centered symmetry with a global, structural order. The appearance of the intermediate phase in our model can be understood as a consequence of broken self-duality. In the standard non-Hermitian Aubry-André model, as discussed by Longhi29, the system maintains a form of self-duality that leads to a sharp metal-insulator transition. However, by introducing the second-neighbor coupling (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$J_2$$\end{document}), we explicitly break this dual symmetry. This deviation from the self-dual point, a mechanism also noted in generalized AA models to induce mobility edges, allows for a regime where extended and localized states coexist. Thus, the intermediate phase in our results signifies the interplay between the non-Hermitian potential and the non-self-dual hopping structure, leading to a more complex phase diagram than the one reported in29.

Conclusion

The exploration of non-Hermitian quantum systems has unveiled a new frontier in condensed matter physics, where the delicate balance of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathscr {P}\mathscr {T}$$\end{document}-symmetry governs unconventional phase transitions. In this work, we have demonstrated that the interplay between quasiperiodic potentials and long-range Heisenberg interactions in spin chains provides a remarkably rich platform for controlling quantum localization and spectral stability. Our investigation reveals that the introduction of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$J_2$$\end{document} fundamentally reshapes the system’s phase diagram, moving beyond the well-known behaviors of standard NN models. We established that while \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$J_1$$\end{document} monotonically shifts the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathscr {P}\mathscr {T}$$\end{document}-breaking threshold, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$J_2$$\end{document} introduces a compelling non-linear competition, manifesting as a distinct minimum in the critical potential amplitude \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda _C$$\end{document}. Most significantly, the NNN configuration catalyzes the emergence of a robust intermediate regime characterized by a mobility edge. This regime marks a sophisticated decoupling of eigenstates, where the real-to-complex spectral transition and spatial confinement no longer coincide, offering a degree of freedom for state manipulation that is absent in simpler chains. By bridging the gap between steady-state properties (IPR/NPR) and temporal evolution, we have shown that the system’s dynamical behavior serves as a high-fidelity indicator of its phases. Our analysis of the spatiotemporal density distribution and the long-time survival probability \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathrm {P(r)}$$\end{document} confirms that the localized phase acts as a quantum memory-like state, preserving the initial configuration, whereas the extended phase leads to a complete, ballistic-like erasure of initial information. The transition of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathrm {P(r)}$$\end{document} from a linear scaling to a saturating unity provides a powerful, experimentally accessible tool for identifying the onset of localization. Ultimately, these findings underscore that the synergy between non-Hermiticity and extended interactions does not merely modify existing phases but gives rise to entirely new physical landscapes. As we move toward the realization of non-Hermitian physics in synthetic quantum matter and optical lattices, the ability to engineer mobility edges and spectral transitions via \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$J_2$$\end{document} couplings, as demonstrated here, opens promising avenues for designing robust quantum devices and exploring the limits of quantum transport.

Leveraging state-of-the-art quantum simulation platforms, such as ultracold atoms in optical lattices, one can implement the complex quasiperiodic potential by introducing controlled atom loss to emulate non-Hermiticity, as discussed in29.

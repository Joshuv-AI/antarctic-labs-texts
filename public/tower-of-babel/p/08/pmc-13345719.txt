Comparison of Clinical Characterization and Therapeutic Strategies between Homozygous Familial Hypercholesterolemia and Heterozygous Familial Hypercholesterolemia
Authors: Daisuke Shishikura, Mariko Harada-Shiba, Masahito Michikura, Kenta Sakaguchi, Hirofumi Kusumoto, Shimpei Fujioka, Tomohiro Fujisaka, Yumiko Kanzaki, Hideaki Morita
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Aims: Homozygous Familial hypercholesterolemia (HoFH) is a rare genetic disease characterized by very high levels of low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C). Owing to LDL receptor activity being completely or nearly all lost in HoFH, LDL-C levels greatly exceed normal levels, and are even higher than in heterozygous (HeFH), which can cause fatal cardiovascular disease even in infancy. The current study updates differences in clinical characterization, therapeutic strategies and cardiovascular outcomes between HoFH and HeFH. Methods: A total of 157 patients who were genetically or clinically diagnosed with FH (HoFH: 15, HeFH: 142) were retrospectively analyzed. Clinical characteristics, lipid profiles and atherosclerotic prognosis were evaluated between HoFH and HeFH patients. Results: Age and sex were similar between the two groups. Untreated LDL-C in HoFH was about double that in HeFH (498.4±164.3 vs. 232.0±60.5 mg/dL,p＜0.001), while on-treatment LDL-C with lipid lowering therapies did not differ significantly (83.8±59.1 vs. 127.1±59.6 mg/dL,p = 0.15). There was wide diversity in lipid lowering therapies between the two groups and a significantly higher prevalence of coronary artery and valvular disease in HoFH. Consequently, HoFH patients were more likely to receive percutaneous and surgical interventions at a younger age compared to HeFH patients. Conclusions: The findings of this observational study show the clinical relevance of FH. Although both HeFH and HoFH are inherited disorders of lipoprotein metabolism, HoFH should be treated with a different, stricter therapeutic strategy to prevent premature ASCVD.

Introduction

Familial hypercholesterolemia (FH), an inherited disorder of lipoprotein metabolism caused by mutations of the genes associated with low-density lipoprotein (LDL) metabolism, in particular those of the LDL receptor (LDLR), apolipoprotein B (APOB), and protein convertase subtilisin/kexin 9 (PCSK9), is characterized by the clinical triad of primary hyper LDL cholesterolemia, tendon xanthomas and premature coronary artery disease (CAD) 1 , 2) . Notably, homozygous FH (HoFH) patients, who carry the mutated gene in both alleles, exhibit extremely high LDL-C levels, while those with heterozygous FH (HeFH) carry the mutated gene in a single allele. Having mutations in both alleles, patients with HoFH are likely to develop atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease even in the first decade of life and most of them may die of ASCVD before 30 years old if untreated 3 , 4) . Although the prevalence of HeFH had been estimated as 1 in 500 in the general population and that of HoFH as 1 in 1,000,000 5) in the past, more recent studies have revealed that FH is more common than previously expected. HeFH is now estimated as 1 in 200-300 and HoFH as 1 in 170,000-300,000 in various countries 5 - 8) . Given that the LDL receptor is severely impaired, most HoFH is refractory to lipid lowering therapies, in particular statin therapy. Most HoFH patients develop significant ASCVD in their teens and early adulthood 9 - 11) . Therefore, early diagnosis and appropriate interventions are essential to improving their prognosis. Owing to advances in medical care, surviving HoFH patients ＞50 years patients are now frequently encountered 12) . Although both HeFH and HoFH are an inherited disorder of lipoprotein metabolism, HoFH should be clinically distinguished from HeFH. The current study updates the differences in clinical characterization, therapeutic strategies and cardiovascular outcomes between HoFH and HeFH.

Methods

Study Population

A total of 157 patients who were genetically or clinically diagnosed with FH at Osaka Medical and Pharmaceutical University Hospital were retrospectively analyzed in the present study. Patients with definite HoFH were those in whom LDLR pathway gene mutations were found or LDLR activity was used to identify HoFH. Patients with probable HoFH were those having total cholesterol levels greater than 450 mg/dL (11.64 mmol/L) or LDL cholesterol levels greater than 370 mg/dL (9.57 mmol/L) in the fasting steady state or the presence of clinical manifestations suggesting severe hypercholesterolemia, such as the presence of percutaneous xanthoma from their childhood and being refractory to medications 13) . HeFH was diagnosed based on the Japan Atherosclerosis Society guidelines as follows 14) : Subjects who fulfill at least 2 of the clinical characteristic criteria among (1) untreated LDL-C level ≥ 180 mg/dL, (2) tendon xanthoma (tendon xanthoma on the dorsal hands, elbows, and knees, or Achilles tendon thickening) or nodular xanthoma on the skin, and (3) a history of familial hypercholesterolemia or premature CAD within first degree relatives for adults. For children, after ruling out other primary and secondary hyper-HDL cholesterolemia 14) , subjects who fulfill the clinical characteristic criteria of (1) and (2) are diagnosed with FH. Subjects who fulfill the clinical characteristic criteria of (1) and (3) are diagnosed with probable FH and if an individual’s LDL-C is 180 mg/dL or higher, a diagnosis of FH is made. Even if only 1 criterion is fulfilled, ≥ 250 mg/dL is diagnosed as FH and ≥ 180 mg/dL as probable FH. The clinical characteristics for children are [1] Hyper-LDL cholesterolemia (untreated LDL-C level ≥ 140 mg/dL, confirmed multiple times), [2] Family history of FH (Parents or siblings), [3] Parental LDL-C ≥ 180 mg/dL or family history of premature coronary artery disease (Grandparent or parent). These diagnostic criteria for FH in the Japanese guidelines apply to both HeFH and HoFH 14) . Xanthoma was defined as Achilles tendon thickening of ≥ 8 mm for males and ≥ 7.5 mm for females on radiography 15) or ≥ 6 mm for males and ≥ 5.5 mm for females on ultrasonography 16) . Achilles tendon thickness (ATT) was evaluated by radiography and ultrasonography. For radiographic ATT assessment, standard lateral ankle radiographs were obtained with the ankle in a neutral position. The radiographical ATT was defined as the maximal antero-posterior tendon thickness. The ultrasonographic ATT assessment was performed using an ultrasound system (5500CV; Koninklijke Philips N.V., the Netherlands) with a linear transducer (L12-5; 12–5 MHz). Participants were examined in the prone position with the ankle in neutral. The transducer was positioned perpendicular to the Achilles tendon to obtain maximally magnified, transversely oriented cross-sectional images. At the time of measuring ATT, carotid intima-media thickness (IMT) was also measured following the “Standardized Method for Evaluation of Carotid Artery Lesions by Ultrasound” outlined by the Japanese Society of Ultrasound Medicine 17) . Patients with systolic blood pressure of ≥ 140 mmHg, and/or diastolic blood pressure ≥ 90 mmHg, or the use of antihypertensive medications are considered to have hypertension. Diabetes was defined based on the Japan Diabetes Society’s definition of diabetes 18) , or the use of diabetes medication. Current smoking habit assessed by medical interview was considered as smoking status. Patients who have at least 1 segment with ＞75% diameter stenosis in right and/or left coronary arteries and/or ＞50% diameter stenosis in left main coronary artery by coronary angiography or multi-detector computed tomography 19) were considered as CAD. At least rosuvastatin of 10 mg, atorvastatin of 20 mg or pitavastatin of 4 mg were considered as high intensity statin. This study was approved by the Ethics Review Board of Osaka Medical and Pharmaceutical University (2023-066). Written informed consent was not mandatory for this observational and retrospective study. However, informed consent for genetic analyses was obtained from all patients with FH included in the present study. All procedures were conducted in accordance with the ethical standards of the responsible committee on human experimentation (institutional and national) and the 1975 Declaration of Helsinki, revised in 2008.

Lipid Profiles and Genetic Variant Annotation

Blood samples were drawn after overnight fasting. Serum levels of total cholesterol, triglycerides, and high-density lipoprotein cholesterol were measured with enzymatic assays (Sekisui Medical, Tokyo, Japan) using an automated analyzer (Hitachi Labospect 008; Hitachi-Hitec, Tokyo, Japan). Low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) levels were calculated using the Friedewald formula in patients with triglyceride levels ＜400 mg/dL; otherwise, LDL-C levels were determined enzymatically 20) . The following equation was adopted to calculate cumulative lifelong LDL-C exposure: LDL-C max x [age at diagnosis/statin initiation] + LDL-C at inclusion x [age at inclusion – age at diagnosis/statin initiation] using the LDL-C exposure simulation application on the Japan Atherosclerotic Society website (https://www.fh-ldl-c-app.com). Two classes of genetic variants (LDLR and PCSK9) were aggregated with respect to association with FH only if informed consent was obtained. The APOB variant was not detected and patients carrying LDLR mutations and PCSK9 V4I were diagnosed with HoFH in the present study 21) .

Statistical Analysis

Categorical data were expressed as frequencies and percentages and evaluated by Pearson χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Continuous variables were presented as mean±standard deviation if variables show normal distribution, whereas median with interquartile range (IQR) are reported for non-normally distributed variables. Mean values for continuous variables were compared using Student’s t-test for independent data, and median values were compared using the nonparametric Wilcoxon Mann-Whitney rank-sum test or the chi-square test for categorical variables with Fisher’s post hoc test. Uni- and multivariable logistic analyses were performed to identify predictors for atherosclerotic vascular disease. Two multivariable logistic models were performed controlling simultaneously for potential confounders to calculate odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs). The models included risk factors that were associated with atherosclerotic vascular disease in univariable analysis. All statistically significant variables in univariable analysis were considered for inclusion in each model and a backwards selection method at alpha = 0.05 was used to determine whether to keep variables in the final multivariable models. All statistical analyses were conducted using the JMP 16 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA).

Results

Patient Characteristics and Lipid Profiles

In total, 142 subjects were clinically diagnosed with heterozygous FH and 15 with homozygous FH in the analysis. The clinical characteristics of the study participants are shown in Table 1 . The mean age was 46 years old, and 64.3% were female. LDLR pathogenic variants were dominant in the genetic variant characteristics of the current study subjects (LDLR: 46.4%, PCSK9: 5.7%, respectively). While there were no significant differences in age and sex between the two patient groups, HoFH patients had been diagnosed with FH at a younger age compared to HeFH patients. Lipid parameters and therapies received are shown in Table 2 . Untreated LDL-C and total cholesterol levels were significantly higher in subjects with HoFH than HeFH (LDL-C: 498.4±164.3 mg/dL vs. 232.0±60.5 md/dL, p＜0.001, total cholesterol: 579.7±170.2 mg/dL vs. 317.8±69.6 mg/dL, p＜0.001, respectively). There were no significant differences in on-treatment lipid profiles (LDL-C: 83.8±59.1 mg/dL vs. 127.1±59.6 mg/dL, p = 0.15; total cholesterol: 200.8±101.7 mg/dL vs. 209.0±64.5 mg/dL, p = 0.70, respectively). Cumulative LDL-C exposure was significantly higher in HoFH than HeFH (20744 mg-year/dL (9000 – 24624.5) vs. 9550 mg-year/dL (7200 – 11742), p＜0.001). Regarding lipid lowering therapies, there were no significant differences in the percentages receiving statin, ezetimibe or resin between the two groups, whereas the percentages receiving high intensity statin (HIS), probucol, eicosapentaenoic acid, a PCSK9 inhibitor, lipoprotein apheresis, evinacumab or lomitapide were significantly higher in HoFH than HeFH (HIS: 80.0% vs. 47.9%, p = 0.02, probucol: 13.3% vs. 1.4%, p = 0.01, eicosapentaenoic acid: 20.0% vs. 3.5%, p = 0.01, PCSK9 inhibitor: 60.0% vs. 16.2%, p＜0.001; lipoprotein apheresis: 40.0% vs 1.4%, p＜0.001; evinacumab: 26.7% vs. 0, p＜0.001; lomitapide: 13.3% vs. 0, p = 0.002, respectively).

Cardiovascular Outcomes

Table 5 summarizes all clinical outcomes and includes a comparison between the HoFH and HeFH groups. There were 28 (17.8%) cardiovascular events (major adverse cardiovascular events; MACE) (6 in HoFH and 22 in HeFH group). The rates for coronary artery disease (CAD), PCI, and CABG, aortic supravalvular stenosis, aortic valve replacement and aortic dissection, and cardiovascular death were 16.6%, 9.0%, 5.1%, 1.3%, 1.9%, 0.6% and 1.3%, respectively. The incidences of coronary artery disease (CAD), PCI, CABG, aortic supravalvular stenosis, aortic valve replacement, aortic dissection, and cardiovascular death were significantly higher in the HoFH group than in the HoFH group (40.0% vs. 14.1%, p = 0.01 for CAD; 26.7% vs. 7.0%, p = 0.01 for PCI; 26.7% vs. 2.1%, p＜0.001 for CABG; 13.3% vs. 0%, p＜0.001 for aortic supravalvular stenosis; 13.3% vs. 0.7%, p＜0.001 for aortic valve replacement; 6.7% vs. 0%, p = 0.03 for aortic dissection; 13.3% vs. 0%, p＜0.001 for cardiovascular death, respectively). Factors Associated with Atherosclerotic Cardiovascular Disease

Univariate analysis identified age (odds ratio (OR): 1.06, 95% CI: 1.03 to 1.09, p＜0.0001), hypertension (OR: 2.97, 95% CI: 1.06 to 8.33, p = 0.04), and HoFH (OR: 3.64, 95% CI: 1.18 to 11.24, p = 0.03) as associated with CAD risk. In one of the models in multivariate analysis - after adjusting for age, sex, smoking, hypertension and diabetes - HoFH was associated with a 4-fold increase in ASCVD (OR: 4.49, 95% CI: 1.18 to 17.24, p = 0.03). ( Table 6 , Model 1). The other multivariable model ( Table 6 , Model 2) revealed that cumulative LDL-C exposure was independently associated with ASCVD (OR: 1.20, 95% CI: 1.08 to 1.34, p＜0.001).

Discussion

Our findings revealed that untreated LDL-C level was significantly higher in HoFH than HeFH and there was wide diversity in therapeutic strategies for lowering LDL-C levels between HoFH and HeFH. Specifically, evinacumab and lomitapide were commenced only in HoFH because their approval is limited to it, and high intensity lipid lowering therapies, in particular PCSK9 inhibitors and lipoprotein apheresis, were more likely to be used in HoFH than HeFH, which resulted in similar control of on-treatment LDL-C levels. Nevertheless, cardiovascular events were more likely to occur in HoFH and at a younger age compared to HeFH. Cumulative LDL-C exposure was an independent predictor associated with ASCVD. Therefore, early diagnosis and initiation of lipid lowering therapy are essential to prevent cardiovascular events. Although both HeFH and HoFH are an inherited disorder of lipoprotein metabolism, their clinical characterizations are substantially different. Even in HoFH, diverse characteristics were identified in genetic pathogenic variants, which leads to various clinical manifestations.

HoFH is a rare genetic disorder in which LDL-C metabolism is greatly affected. Extremely elevated LDL-C levels cause cholesterol build-up in the skin and tendons from early infancy and cholesterol deposition in the intima of the arterial wall, which leads to the cardiovascular events. To decrease the burden of this disorder on the cardiovascular system, lipid lowering therapy is essential throughout the patient’s lifetime to improve life expectancy. Given that the effectiveness of statins, ezetimibe and PCSK9 inhibitors all depend on residual LDL receptor activity, many HoFH patients are refractory to these drugs. Therefore, therapeutic options are limited for HoFH and differ greatly from those for HeFH. Specifically, lipoprotein apheresis, which involves direct removal of LDL from plasma through selective absorption or filtration of LDL using an extra-corporal circulation system, is effective in lowering LDL-C in FH. In the last several decades, lipoprotein apheresis had been the only practical method of controlling LDL-C levels in HoFH and contributed to improvement of prognoses. A new option indicated only for HoFH is the MTP inhibitor, lomitapide 22 , 23) , an oral drug in a therapeutic class that is independent of LDL receptor activity. Another is evinacumab 24) , an anti-ANGPTL3 antibody in a new therapeutic class that is also independent of LDL receptor activity. In the current study, various therapeutic options were adopted according to genetic pathogenic variants in HoFH. For instance, lipoprotein apheresis, lomitapide and evinacumab are mainly used in true HoFH and combination of high intensity statin and PCSK9 inhibitor is effective in compound and double heterozygous. These individual specialized treatments can lead to appropriate LDL-C lowering to prevent cardiovascular events. Although it has been reported that most HoFH patients may die of ASCVD before 30 years old if untreated 3 , 4) , the prognosis of HoFH could be improved with these specific lipid lowering treatments. Indeed, most of the subjects with HoFH in the present study have successfully survived due to appropriate lipid lowering treatments and interventions for cardiovascular events. Considering that genetic diagnosis indicates the potential efficacy of drug treatment in therapeutic strategies, genetic testing is recommended for suspected FH cases. However, it should be noted that FH is underdiagnosed and undertreated in the general population 2) . It was reported that the frequency of carrying the APOB variant in Japan is extremely low 25) and the additional PCSK9 V4I variant was reported to be linked to a significant increase in CAD in accord with elevation of the LDL-C level 21) . Therefore, patients carrying FH pathogenic variants (LDLR mutations and PCSK9 V4I) were diagnosed with HoFH in the present study 21) . The existence of FH-related genes that are still unknown or limitations of analytic technologies may be reasons that disease-causing mutations cannot be identified in 20-40% of clinically diagnosed FH. In this regard, variants of uncertain significance to LDLRs have been associated with high LDL-C levels and poor clinical outcomes in FH 26) . Furthermore, a clinical diagnosis of typical HoFH cannot be excluded even if LDLR mutations and PCSK9 V4I are not identified. That is, patients with severe HeFH who are not diagnosed HoFH do not receive adequate treatments. In particular, lipoprotein apheresis, lomitapide and evinacumab are not subsidized even in severe HeFH in Japan. A substantial portion of FH cases without known FH-gene mutations could be polygenic hypercholesterolemia 27) or oligogenic FH 28 , 29) . Further investigations are therefore warranted to comprehend the pathogenicity of genetic variants associated with FH in order to improve the prognosis.

The prognosis for untreated HoFH is extremely poor. In the present study, we demonstrated that the prevalence of cardiovascular events remained higher in HoFH than in HeFH despite LDL-C being similarly controlled. Before, supravalvular aortic stenosis (SVAS) had been one of the most specific forms of aortic calcification in HoFH. However, a previous study reported that the aortic valve phenotype had shifted from SVAS towards calcific aortic stenosis (AS) during the long period of statin use and that the prevalence of early AS had decreased, suggesting that statins and multi-modal lipid lowering therapies may delay the onset of ASCVD 30) . Furthermore, the newly introduced drug options of lomitapide and evinacumab may have even greater benefit for the cardiovascular prognosis.

As atherosclerotic plaques grow over time, total atherosclerotic plaque burden is determined from both the concentrations of circulating LDL-C and other apo B-containing lipoproteins and the total duration of exposure to these lipoproteins 31) . Therefore, total atherosclerotic plaque burden is proportional to cumulative exposure to LDL and other apo B-containing lipoproteins. This concept of cumulative exposure to LDL-C being a robust predictor of cardiovascular events in FH 32 , 33) and the general population 34 , 35) was validated previously. In the HoFH subjects in our cohort, cumulative LDL-C exposure was demonstrated to be an independent predictor of ASCVD. Accordingly, this concept may be applicable to HoFH and there are indications that early diagnosis and interventions could decrease the burden of LDL accumulation and mitigate the risk of cardiovascular events.

Limitations

A number of caveats should be noted with regard to the current study. Firstly, the number of subjects is relatively small and this is an observational, retrospective analysis conducted at a single center. Secondly, strategy of lipid-lowering therapies was decided depending on the discretion of each physician. Therefore, potential selection bias could not be excluded. Thirdly, maximum dose of high-intensity statin therapy between Japan and Western countries is different, which may be bias affecting our findings. Fourth, only Japanese subjects who were diagnosed FH by the Japan Atherosclerosis Society guidelines of FH diagnosis were included in the current study. It has yet to be elucidated whether the analysis can be translated to non-Japanese patients with FH. Therefore, further investigations are warranted.

Conclusion

In our cohort, we updated the clinical characterization, therapeutic strategies and clinical adverse outcomes between HoFH and HeFH. Although both should be treated with appropriate lipid lowering strategies, compared to HeFH, early identification of HoFH and initiation of treatment is more crucial because of its poorer prognosis.

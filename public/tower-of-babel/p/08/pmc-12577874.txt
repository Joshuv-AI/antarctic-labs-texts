From past to future: a comprehensive scientometric perspective of influential studies on pterygium
Authors: Zhimin Gu, Liang Lv, Ruo Qi, Lei Shi, Like Guan, Xin Liu, Lixiao Zhou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Pterygium is a relatively common ocular surface disease. Although treatment methods have been studied for a long time, pathogenesis remains unclear. We aimed to explore the research activity and future trends of influential studies from a scientometric perspective.Methods:We performed the search in Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) for accessible articles on pterygium from 1945 to 2024. The top 100 cited publications were identified, and the following characteristics for each article were analyzed: total citations, published year, authorship, journal, impact factor (IF), impact index (II), country, institution, study design, and evidence level. VOSviewer was utilized to present visualization network analysis.Results:A total of 2,633 studies on pterygium from 1945 to 2024 were retrieved from the WoSCC. The top 100 cited publications ranged from 536 to 69 citations, with the publication period spanning between 1960 and 2020. The leading country was the USA, followed by Australia and Singapore. Retrospective study was the most common study design while articles with evidence level III and IV were the most common. Recurrent pterygium, surgery and excision were vital keywords in co-occurrence network for current research status. While fibrin glue, mitomycin-c and amniotic membrane transplantation are likely to become the potential hotspots in the future.Conclusions:The present study identified the 100 most cited articles on pterygium comprehensively. Fibrin glue, mitomycin-c, and amniotic membrane transplantation might become future focus. However, more studies with strong evidence level and enhanced global collaboration are still required for pterygium research.

Introduction

Pterygium (also called as surfer’s eye) is an ocular surface disease characterized mostly by fibrovascular degenerations of the conjunctiva that advances across the cornea over time[1]. The main risk factors for pterygium are exposure to ultraviolet light, increasing age and male gender, with the overall prevalence in China is about 9.84%, aligning closely with the worldwide prevalence of 12%[1,2]. Owing to reconstructions in local ocular surface homeostasis, the major components of pterygium are proliferative clusters of limbal stem cells, epithelial metaplasia, active fibrovascular tissue, inflammation, and disruption of Bowman’s layer along the invading apex of the pterygium[3]. Clinical presentation and treatment methods of pterygium remain several uncertainties although many studies have been published[4]. Therefore, it is necessary for researchers to have a comprehensive understanding in published pterygium literature and research focus, which is helpful to their future scientific work. Additionally, due to many various publications related to pterygium, it is difficult for an individual researcher to screen all of them to obtain the newest hotspots timely. Scientometric study is a statistical analysis and presents an assessment of the published studies, and information according to visualized network analysis including bibliographic coupling and co-occurrence data of keywords[5]. It has been broadly applied in diverse subjects to evaluate the impact of published studies or identify research trends[6–8]. Only one similar bibliometric study has been conducted on pterygium in 2021 so far[9]. Nonetheless, it did not address the study design and evidence level of influential articles, limiting its’ usefulness to the domain. Yet, investigating the scientometric characteristics of impactful publications on pterygium would inform on the development of pterygium research in different fields such as clinical or basic science and distinguish current focus as well as future trends. In the present study, we aim to analyze the most influential studies to provide clinicians and researchers with useful information on the current status and future frontiers of pterygium research via scientometrics. Furthermore, our study encompasses a more extensive temporal span than previous investigation. This study adheres to the TITAN Guidelines 2025[10].

Methods

Ethics statement

Ethical approval was not required for this study as no human or animal subjects were included.

Data source and search strategy

The Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) (Clarivate Analytics, Boston, MA, USA) was used to search literature and citations from inception (January 1945) to December 2024 on pterygium on 2 January 2025. Up to date, WoSCC is considered as one of the most widely used databases globally. The “title” instead of “topic” searching strategy was chosen to make sure related items only[11–13]. The search terms were as follows: Title = pterygium OR surfer’s eye OR pterygia NOT pterygium-syndrome AND publishing year = (1945.01.01–2024.12.31) AND Language = (English) AND Document types = (Article OR Review). Searching results were ranked according to total citations. All records were saved and kept in plain text and for the purpose of drawing and analyzing the scientific characteristics. The detailed selection criteria and inclusion procedure of this study were shown as Figure 1A. Characteristics including titles, journals, authorships, total citations, institutions, countries, keywords, evidence level, study design, impact factor (IF), and impact index (II) were recorded and analyzed. Evidence levels were classified according to Murphy et al. from high to low: meta-analyses (Level I), randomized controlled trials (RCTs) (Level I), prospective studies (Level II), retrospective studies (Level III), Case control studies (Level III), experimental research (Level IV), review (Level IV), and case reports (Level IV)[14]. The impact index (II) is obtained by dividing the time duration (unit: year) since publication by total cited times and then multiplied by 100. The low II represents a robust scientific recognition[15]. Scientometric and statistical analysis

GraphPad Prism v. 10.2.2 and Microsoft Excel (version 2020) were applied to perform statistical analysis. The Spearman’s test was utilized to determine correlations among selected variables. Comparison in different mean values was based on one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). The unpaired t-test was chosen to make the comparison in two separate groups. The P-value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Visualized analysis was conducted via VOSviewer 1.6.18. Collaboration networks among institutions, countries as well as authors were evaluated. Current research focus and future trends were identified by co-occurrence keyword analysis. The line width and magnitude of dots illustrated the total link intensity and frequency of documents.

Discussion

Considering the accelerated growth in research across diverse disciplines, it has become imperative for researchers to comprehend the prevailing advancements within their respective domains. In contrast to meta-analysis and systematic review methodologies, scientometric analysis offers a more objective and accessible approach to validating and assessing extant literature[19]. In this study, we applied scientometric methods to visualize and analyze impactful publications in the field of pterygium and we sought to provide valuable insights into this domain.

The 100 most cited papers were cited 536 to 69 times, which was lower than that in other ophthalmology subspecialties such as glaucoma[20]. The hypothesis is put forward that there is a discrepancy in the citation frequencies of different subspecialties. This variation may be attributed to the size of the research community and selection of various subspecialties[21]. The II evaluation process can provide an objective assessment of the publication in question. It is important to note that lower II indices are indicative of a higher impact, irrespective of the year of publication[11]. The study with the highest citation number (n = 536) was published in the year 1992 by Rubinfeld RS et al. in Ophthalmology, which reported a series of 10 patients who experienced serious, vision-threatening complications associated with the use of this drug after pterygium surgery[16]. The authors strongly advised extreme caution with mitomycin. If used, the lowest possible concentration for the shortest possible duration was vital to avoid complications and a prospective multicenter study of usage in the eye was essential. While for the article with the lowest II was entitled “Expression of SARS-CoV-2 receptor ACE2 and TMPRSS2 in human primary conjunctival and pterygium cell lines and in mouse cornea” and it was published in the year 2020 by Ma D et al. in Eye[17]. The authors aimed to ascertain the expression levels of the angiotensin-converting enzyme 2 and type II transmembrane serine protease genes in ocular cells from both humans and mice. A comparative analysis of these gene expression levels across different tissue types was also be conducted. Concluding that the conjunctiva was less susceptible to infection by SARS-CoV-2, while pterygium exhibited a higher probability of infection.

Twenty-seven countries contributed to the top 100 cited articles. The USA was the most productive, as expected, as found in previous studies of glaucoma and cataract (Fig. 1C) [5,22]. This is due to ideal conditions for basic science and clinical trials, including advanced technology, professional researchers, and sufficient funds. Research has also noted that authors from the USA preferred to publish and cite local articles[23]. The British Journal of Ophthalmology was responsible for about a third of the articles in the top 100 list. This journal is of particular interest to the ophthalmological community as it publishes comprehensive research on ophthalmological diseases and external ocular structures. Its research on pterygium is both profound and influential.

The visualized analysis showed the cooperation network pertaining to institutions, countries and authors. The USA led the way in pterygium research, as shown by its high number of publications and collaboration with others. Singapore and Australia were also making a significant impact. Some European countries, like France, Sweden, and Spain, had a relatively low number of publications but a high frequency of links with other countries, showing their core status (Fig. 5A). The institution from Singapore has made a significant contribution to the area, as have other institutions and authors, including Coroneo MT, which has held the leading position based on global collaboration and authorship (Figs. 4A, 4B). The distribution of institutions in terms of publications and collaboration indicated their research power and influence. Singaporean and Australian institutions are prominent, suggesting they are prolific and excel in pterygium research. The analyzed results provide valuable information for scholars interested in pterygium research. Conversely, the collaboration among institutions is still lacking and it is necessary to enhance of inter-institution cooperation and the undertaking of more collaborative research endeavors focused on pterygium. Co-occurrence analysis was utilized to examine the keywords, thereby suggesting that recurrent pterygium, surgery, and excision persisted as pivotal concepts (Fig. 5B). Moreover, the novel research trends as identified by the time-visualized analysis of keywords included fibrin glue, mitomycin-C and amniotic membrane transplantation (Fig. 5C). The management of pterygium encompasses a wide spectrum of approaches, ranging from conservative lubrication and the utilization of protective eyewear to surgical removal and grafting. The selection of treatment modality is contingent upon the presence of symptoms and the patient’s aesthetic preferences[4]. However, the procedure has several limitations. It may not provide sufficient coverage for large pterygia. In such cases, preserving conjunctival tissue may be more useful in future glaucoma surgery. Recently, surgical techniques like amniotic membrane transplantation have been used instead of conjunctival autografting to reduce recurrence because they result in normal epithelial growth. Besides, using fibrin glue in lieu of traditional suturing has reduced surgical time, leading to faster recovery, less pain and fewer infections[24].

In the top 100 list, studies classified as evidence level III and IV were at the top (Fig. 2D). Nonetheless, publications with high evidence levels were insufficient in our analysis, as seen in other similar studies[25,26]. This suggests that evidence level is not closely associated with citations, which aligns with our results, which may be due to original ideas and techniques are usually published as observational studies and initially garner attention from researchers over time[27,28]. Retrospective study was the most common study design in our analysis, which was comparable to other bibliometric studies such as in orthopedic surgery (Fig. 2C) [29]. This approach is imperative in the investigation of rare diseases, manifestations and outcomes. Nevertheless, their scientific value is constrained by several factors. First, the absence of certain information can compromise the integrity. Second, selection and recall biases have the potential to influence the results, leading to an inaccurate representation of the original data. Third, the underlying reasons for observed differences in treatment or loss of follow-ups frequently remain unidentified, hindering the ability to draw definitive conclusions[30]. Therefore, to obtain a profound comprehension of pterygium, prospective trials and RCTs, as the gold standard of effective research, are required.

Our study is not without its limitations. Firstly, although the WoSCC database is one of the most comprehensive and widely used databases, it might not consist of specific manuscripts that appeared in other sources such as Google Scholar or PubMed. Secondly, we only focused on the 100 most cited articles, which may have led to outdated conclusions about research trends. Citation number analysis cannot reflect the influence of one publication totally, even if it has a wide range of applications; thus, outstanding and notable articles with fewer citation times were excluded, which may cause result bias. Thirdly, the objective was to identify only relevant publications on pterygium; consequently, the “title” searching strategy was utilized, which might have led to the exclusion of some relevant publications, though this is unlikely to have had a significant impact on the results. Finally, the presence of self-citation bias in our analysis may lead to an exaggeration of an individual’s citation count. Despite these limitations, the present study provided valuable insights into the characteristics of research activity and development on pterygium.

Conclusions

This scientometric study identified the top 100 cited publications on pterygium. The USA was the dominant country regarding publication numbers and global collaboration. Future frontiers might include fibrin glue, mitomycin-C, and amniotic membrane transplantation. However, scientific research with a high level of evidence and enhanced global collaboration are still required. This study may present valuable insights into pterygium research and provide help for researchers who want to obtain vital information on influential publications.

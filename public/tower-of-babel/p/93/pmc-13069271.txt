Signature ECG part 6: Palpitations in a young male with a positive family history for Sudden cardiac death
Authors: Arthur AM. Wilde, Priya Chockalingam
Published: 2026

Image 1

A young 21-year-old male presented at the emergency unit with palpitations. He had experienced it already several times in the past but it had always self-terminated, except this time. There were no specific triggers. There were no other complaints and physical examination was unremarkable. His family history reports a sudden death: his father died suddenly and unexpectedly at age 42.

The baseline ECG shows a typical (counterclockwise) atrial flutter. However, there are several unexpected findings. First of all, the ventricular rate is unusually slow (a little over 60 bpm); in a young male one would expect a ventricular rate of 150 bpm. The rate of the atrial flutter itself is also slower than expected. A typical atrial flutter rate is 300 cycles per minute, but in this case, it is only 260 cycles per minute (which with a 4:1 block leads to a ventricular rate of ≥60 bpm). Finally, the QRS width exceeds 120 msec.

Upon electrical cardioversion the ECG showed a marked first-degree AV block, a wide P-wave and the same wide QRS complex (not shown). Together with the family history this ECG should immediately raise suspicion on the involvement of a loss-of-function sodium channel variant. As the sodium channel is expressed throughout the heart, conduction parameters throughout the heart are affected leading to the ECG shown (during atrial flutter) and the ECG described (not shown).

Genetic testing is warranted in such cases. A pathogenic SCN5A variant was identified in this patient and family screening was advised.

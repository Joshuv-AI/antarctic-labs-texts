Effects of administration of a non-specific immunostimulant prior to mating on pregnancy establishment and fetal and placental development in mice
Authors: Whitney Brown, Michaela Kuzniar, Sarah Retherford, Brooke Mitrisin, Allison Ingols, Jezaniah-Kira Tena, Susan C. Sucheta, Jeremy Block
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Early embryonic mortality occurs in many mammalian species, including mice, humans and several livestock species. Previous work in mice has demonstrated a role for pro-inflammatory cytokines and immune cells in stimulating ovarian function and uterine receptivity during the peri-conception period. The objective was to determine if administration of an immunostimulant could improve pregnancy establishment and fetal survival. At proestrus/estrus, female mice (CB6F1) were randomly assigned to treatment with 0, 20 or 40 μg of mycobacterium cell wall fraction (MCWF; n = 73/treatment). Treatments were administered intraperitoneally prior to overnight exposure with males (BALB/c). A subset of females (n = 21/treatment) were sacrificed at gestational day (gd) 4.5 for assessment of spleen and liver weights. The remaining females were sacrificed at gd 17.5 to determine the number of viable fetuses and resorbing implantation sites, and fetal and placental weights. Treatment with MCWF increased spleen weight at gd 4.5 for both the 20 and 40 μg dose levels. However, there was no effect of MCWF treatment on the number of viable fetuses and resorbing implantation sites, nor fetal and placental weights on gd 17.5. Administration of MCWF during the periovulatory period induced an inflammatory response, but did not impact pregnancy establishment, fetal survival or placental development. Results are somewhat inconsistent with those observed in other species and may be impacted by the timing and route of administration.

Introduction

Early embryonic mortality occurs in many mammalian species, including humans, mice and various livestock species [[1], [2], [3], [4]]. In women, it is estimated that 10–40 % of pregnancies fail prior to implantation [4] and in livestock species such as cattle, embryonic mortality in the first month of gestation accounts for 60–80 % of observed pregnancy losses [5,6]. Successful pregnancy establishment is regulated in part by a coordinated interplay between the endocrine, immune, and reproductive systems [7,8]. During the peri-conception period, estrogen and progesterone stimulate the secretion of cytokines and chemokines in the preovulatory follicle and endometrium leading to recruitment of various immune cells [[9], [10], [11]]. Importantly, this coordinated immunoregulation plays a critical role in directing final follicle differentiation and luteinization, ovulation, and initial priming of endometrial receptivity for implantation [9,12].

Given the important role that immune cells play in regulating key reproductive events in the peri-conception period, one approach to improving early embryo survival could be therapeutic administration of an immunostimulant. Previous reports have indicated that administration of the cytokine granulocyte colony stimulating factor (G-CSF) to women with infertility or recurrent implantation failure can improve pregnancy outcomes following in vitro fertilization (IVF) [13,14]. Intrauterine application of activated, autologous peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMC) prior to embryo transfer has been reported to improve pregnancy outcomes in women undergoing IVF [15,16] and a similar approach also improved pregnancy establishment following embryo transfer in cattle [17]. Although approaches involving administration of G-CSF and PBMCs have demonstrated some efficacy, results have been inconsistent and may depend upon the extent of infertility [14,16,18,19].

In the present study, it was hypothesized that administration of a fraction of cell wall derived from the soil borne, non-pathogenic Mycobacterium phlei (MCWF) prior to mating could improve pregnancy establishment and fetal and placental development in mice. The cell wall fraction from M. phlei is an effective simulator of the innate immune system. Specifically, components of MCWF, including glycolipid lipoarabinomannan and phosphatidylinositol mannosides, can be recognized by toll-like receptor-2 and -4 [20,21], and MCWF stimulates the production of various cytokines, such as interleukin-1β (IL-1β), IL-6, IL-12, interferon-γ (IFN-γ) and tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) [[21], [22], [23]]. Moreover, previous research suggests that MCWF can enhance pregnancy establishment and fetal survival. In particular, administration of MCWF to mink prior to mating resulted in an increase in litter size [24] and treatment of dairy heifers with MCWF at the time of estrus increased the proportion of pregnancies obtained following embryo transfer [25].

Materials and methods

Unless specified otherwise, all materials were purchased from Millipore-Sigma (Burlington, MA, USA) or Fisher Scientific (Fairlawn, NJ, USA).

Animals and experimental design

Animal use and experimental procedures were approved by the University of Wyoming Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (Protocol #2022-0104). BALB/c x C57BL/6 (CB6F1) females and BALB/c males were purchased from Charles River Laboratories (Wilmington, MA, USA). Mice were housed in barrier conditions on a 12 h:12 h light-dark cycle and given standard rodent chow (LabDiet 5001, Purina Mills, Inc., Richmond, IN, USA) and water ad libitum. Following arrival, mice were allowed to acclimate for seven days prior to the initiation of experimental procedures.

CB6F1 females (6–8 weeks old) were obtained in five separate cohorts (n = 52 per cohort). Prior to arrival of each cohort, BALB/c male mice (n = 18; 10–27 weeks old) were assigned to experimental blocks consisting of up to two female mice. Following acclimation, females were submitted to daily vaginal lavage between 07:00 to 09:00 h to determine estrous cycle stage based on vaginal cytology as described previously [26]. Females determined to be in proestrus, or estrus were enrolled into the study daily and assigned to an experimental block based on the order of enrollment. Upon enrollment, females within a block were randomly assigned to one of three treatment groups: 0 (sterile normal saline), 20 or 40 μg of mycobacterium cell wall fraction (MCWF) obtained from Mycobacterium phlei (Amplimune, NovaVive, Inc., Napanee, ON, Canada). Assigned treatments were administered on the day of proestrus/estrus between 16:00 to 18:00 h via intraperitoneal injection. Following treatment, females, either in pairs (n = 202) or as singles (n = 17), were placed with their assigned BALB/c male overnight for mating. The following morning, designated gestational day (gd) 0.5, females were checked for the presence of a copulatory plug. During the experiment, 219 females were enrolled in the study, including 73 each for the 0, 20 and 40 μg MCWF treatment groups. Mice that did not have a copulatory plug after mating or that did not become pregnant were not re-enrolled in the study.

Assessment of liver and spleen weights

Within each cohort, at the time of enrollment, a subset of females (n = 12 total; n = 4 per treatment group) was randomly assigned to assess the effect of treatment on liver and spleen weight. Females were euthanized at 8:00 to 11:00 h on gd 4.5 and the liver and spleen were removed following dissection and weighed.

Pregnancy and fetal development parameters

Females enrolled in the study but not allocated for assessment of liver and spleen weight, were euthanized at 8:00 to 11:00 h on gd 17.5. Viable and resorbing implantation sites were counted, and fetuses and placentas were weighed.

Statistical analysis

All analyses were performed using SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA). Analysis of the proportion of females with a copulatory plug following mating and the proportion of females pregnant on gd 17.5 was performed using the Proc Glimmix procedure using binomial distribution with logit link. The model included treatment as fixed effect and cohort and block within cohort as random effects. The number of viable fetuses, resorbing implantation sites, fetal weight, placental weight, fetal:placental weight ratio, spleen weight, and liver weight were analyzed using the Proc Mixed procedure. The model included treatment as fixed effect, and cohort and block within cohort as random effects. All data are presented as least-squares means ± SEM. Significance level was set at 0.05 (2-sided).

Discussion

Early embryonic loss is a problem for many mammalian species, including women and various livestock species [3,4,6]. Several important reproductive events occur during the peri-conception period that are regulated by a coordinated crosstalk between the endocrine, immune, and reproductive systems, including follicle differentiation and luteinization, ovulation, and endometrial receptivity [9,12]. Prior research, primarily in women undergoing treatment for infertility, have evaluated immunomodulatory approaches for improving reproductive outcomes, including administration of G-CSF and PBMCs, but the efficacy of such treatments has been inconsistent or inconclusive [19]. In the present study, it was hypothesized that administration of an immunostimulant composed of MCWF during the peri-ovulatory period could improve pregnancy establishment and fetal development. However, results indicate that treatment of mice with MCWF prior to mating had no effect on pregnancy rate or parameters of fetal and placental development measured in this study.

Although studies evaluating the effects of MCWF on reproductive outcomes are limited, results of the present work contrast with some previous reports in cattle and mink. In two prior studies with dairy heifers, treatment with MCWF during the peri-ovulatory period or at the time of estrus resulted in a greater proportion of blastocyst stage embryos recovered following superovulation [27] and an increased percentage of pregnant animals following embryo transfer [25], respectively. Moreover, in mink, treatment with MCWF prior to mating increased subsequent litter size [24]. It is unclear why MCWF treatment in the current experiment did not affect pregnancy establishment or fetal survival, but the lack of an effect is likely not due to MCWF's capacity to induce an inflammatory response. The present work utilized a similar commercially marketed MCWF product as prior studies in cattle that observed positive effects on reproductive outcomes [25,27]. Moreover, results related to spleen weight indicate that both dose levels induced inflammation and several prior studies in various species, including mice, have demonstrated the effectiveness of MCWF in promoting proinflammatory cytokine secretion and inflammation [22,23,28]. Nevertheless, it should be noted that the present work did not directly assess effects of MCWF treatment on systemic or local cytokine levels or immune cell populations.

One possible explanation for the lack of an effect could be that the timing of administration of MCWF in the present experiment was not optimal for observing positive impacts on pregnancy establishment and fetal survival in mice. Timing of administration herein was based on a prior study in cattle where MCWF was administered at the time of detected estrus [25], or near the occurrence of the luteinizing hormone (LH) surge [29,30]. Treatment with MCWF in this study increased corpus luteum diameter, suggesting a potential role for MCWF in affecting ovarian function. Whether this timing is optimal in a mouse model assessing reproductive outcomes and fetal survival is unclear. Given that the time of ovulation relative to the LH surge is shorter in mice (10–12 h [31];) than in cattle (28–30 h; [32,33]) it is possible that earlier administration of MCWF, such as at the onset of proestrus, may have been more effective. Support for earlier administration also comes from the study in mink where positive effects on fetal survival were observed when MCWF was administered 10 h prior to mating [24], which given the delayed ovulation that occurs in this species would correspond to approximately 43–82 h prior to ovulation [34]. Earlier timing of administration of MCWF is also supported by a separate study in cattle where there was no effect on pregnancy rates following embryo transfer when MCWF was administered 12–24 h after detected estrus [35]. In addition to timing, it is also possible that differing routes of administration of MCWF, intraperitoneal in the present study versus intramuscular in the mink and cattle studies [24,25,27], could contribute to the conflicting results observed. Intraperitoneal administration of substances generally results in faster absorption and allows for first-pass metabolism in the liver, whereas absorption is typically slower following intramuscular administration and this route avoids first-pass metabolism [36].

An additional consideration in evaluating the results of the present work is that the study was conducted at an elevation of more than 2000 m (Laramie, WY, USA) and mice used in the study were not adapted to high altitude conditions. One of the adaptive responses to exposure to hypoxia is increased production of adenosine, which triggers an increase in the anti-inflammatory cytokine IL-10 [37,38]. Thus, it is possible that the lack of an effect of MCWF may have been due to a dampening of inflammation caused by exposure of mice to moderately hypoxic conditions. Recent work in mice has demonstrated that acute exposure to hypoxia can dampen inflammation through enhanced adenosine release and increased IL-10 production [39] Effects of exposure to high altitude in the present study were observed for gd 17.5 fetal weights, which were lower for all treatment groups than those reported previously for similar strains of mice [40,41]. This result is consistent with prior research where exposure to high altitude has been reported to result in fetal growth restriction and lower birth weights in several mammalian species, including mice [42,43].

Overall, pregnancy establishment and fetal survival were not affected by administration of MCWF, a non-specific immunostimulant, during the periovulatory period in mice. These results are in contrast to a previous study in cattle where administration of MCWF at detected estrus increased pregnancy rate follow embryo transfer [25] and a study in mink where administration of MCWF prior to natural mating increased litter size [24]. Possible reasons for the conflicting results include the timing of MCWF administration, differing routes of administration, and exposure of mice in the present work to moderate hypoxia. Future research should explore more extensively changes in cytokine levels and immune cell populations in ovarian and uterine tissues following MCWF administration and impacts on reproductive outcomes.

Genebody specific epigenomic reprogramming of transcriptome promotes immune evasion in oropharyngeal cancer patients in North-Eastern India
Authors: Sahana Ghosh, Indranil Bagchi, Lastborn Marthong, Subrata Patra, Neizekhotuo Brian Shunyu, Srimoyee Ghosh, Arindam Maitra
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSquamous cell carcinoma of oropharynx (OPSCC), a head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) subtype, exhibits a remarkably high incidence rate in the North-Eastern regions of India. The development of OPSCC is associated with the exposure to smokeless tobacco with or without consumption of alcohol and smoking tobacco. Despite advanced treatment modalities, OPSCC patients still face a dismal prognosis, necessitating a deeper exploration of the underlying molecular characteristics of the disease. While promoter CpG methylation-driven gene expression alterations in OPSCC have been studied, DNA methylation within gene bodies and its biological significance in this cancer subtype remain largely uncharted. This study represents the first endeavour to investigate gene-body specific DNA methylation-driven transcriptome alterations leading to immune response modulation on a genome-wide scale in OPSCC.ResultsThe genome-wide assay of DNA methylation and RNA-sequencing in paired tumour and adjacent normal tissues, employing high-throughput platforms (Illumina), identified gene-body specific somatic alterations within the DNA methylome that led to transcriptomic changes in OPSCC patients from Meghalaya. Integrative analysis of gene-body specific methylation and transcriptome data unveiled 98 epigenetically repressed and 39 epigenetically overexpressed genes. Major discoveries emerged from this study include deregulation of Tryptophan (Trp) metabolism pathway by the gene-body driven epigenetically modulated (TDO2, KYNU and TPH2) genes along with the conjoint impact of the upregulated IDO1, IDO2. The IFN-γ mediated PD-L1/PD-1, PD-L2/PD-1 interactions and dysregulation of Trp metabolism pathway collectively contributed to the depletion of cytotoxic T cells in OPSCC tissues. Upregulation of multiple chemokines—CCL2, CXCL1, CXCL2 promoting abundant infiltration of mast cell and neutrophils having pro-tumour phenotype in tumour microenvironment were observed.ConclusionsThese key alterations within tumour cells might effectively modulate the immune dynamics, leading to immune evasion by oropharyngeal malignant cells. The findings offer deep understanding of OPSCC and suggests novel immunotherapeutic targets, for the treatment of this challenging disease.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13148-026-02088-y.

Background

Squamous cell carcinoma of oropharynx (OPSCC) is a head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) subtype which has a very high incidence rate in North-Eastern region (NER) of India [1]. The anatomical site of OPSCC include the base of the tongue, palatine tonsils and tonsillar pillars, soft palate and posterior pharyngeal wall. The development of OPSCC is associated with the exposure to tobacco with or without consumption of alcohol. Additionally, the dietary habits of chewing areca nut (areca catechu) and betel-quid with or without tobacco are considered to be a major risk factor [2–4]. India, particularly the NER, stands as a major contributor to this disease, accounting for 21.8% (N = 23,174) of the global cases (Globocan 2022; Global Cancer Observatory, International Agency for Research on Cancer) [5]. The substantial prevalence of OPSCC in northeast India [6, 7] correlates strongly with their specific dietary and lifestyle habits, particularly the consumption of areca nut and tobacco [3, 4].

Large scale perturbations in the cellular epigenomic repertoire are the hallmark of cancer methylome. Alterations in the promoter CpG island methylations in various cancer types are well investigated and considered as highly stable molecular marks which have considerable potential as predictive and prognostic biomarkers. But DNA methylation of gene-bodies and their biological role in cancer remain largely unexplored until recently. Gene-bodies are typically CpG-deprived and heavily methylated and harbour multiple repetitive DNA elements and transposons. The existence of CpG islands within gene-bodies and gene deserts has been confirmed [8], though predominantly they reside in gene promoters. Intragenic CpG islands in cancer are susceptible to de novo methylation, yet the methylated CGIs within genebodies do not impede transcription elongation. Emerging findings further suggest that genebody DNA methylation affects intragenic promoters, repetitive DNA elements and culminates in the formation of ordered architecture within the transcriptional unit, ultimately impacting gene expression [9, 10].

We investigated paired tumour and adjacent normal tissues from a cohort of OPSCC patients from Meghalaya, northeast India, and earlier identified promoter DNA methylation driven alterations in expression of 435 genes. These genes are known to dysregulate cancer and immunomodulatory pathways [1]. In the present study, we have extended the knowledge of the regulatory effect of tumour specific alterations of the DNA methylation on transcriptome beyond promoter methylation in this cancer type and investigated the gene body methylation mediated transcriptional alterations in the paired OPSCC and normal tissues in this cohort. Our results indicate that such epigenetic alterations impact on the immune cell landscape through transcriptome reprogramming.

Material and methods

Study participants and sample acquisition

Twenty-six treatment-naive oropharyngeal squamous cell carcinoma patients, belonging to the NER population, were recruited at the Department of ENT, North Eastern Indira Gandhi Regional Institute of Health and Medical Sciences (NEIGRIHMS), Shillong, Meghalaya, India during the period of August 2015 to June 2017. Paired tumour and adjacent normal tissue samples were collected with voluntary informed consent of the study participants during the biopsy procedure by trained professionals from 16 patients (discovery set). The selection of the discovery set patients were performed through random sampling. Only tumour tissue samples were collected from rest of 10 independent patients and considered as validation set (Supplementary Fig. S1, Supplementary Table S1). The samples were stored in RNAlater™ Stabilization Solution (Thermo Fisher Scientific) in − 80 °C until further processing. The research protocol was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committees (in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki) of North Eastern Indira Gandhi Regional Institute of Health and Medical Sciences, Shillong, Meghalaya (NEIGR/IEC/2015/0039 dated 21 April 2016), North Eastern Hill University, Shillong, Meghalaya (IECHSP/2015–16 dated 23 June 2017) and National Institute of Biomedical Genomics, Kalyani (Dated 12th June 2015).

DNA methylation assay by Infinium 450K Beadchip Array and data analysis

Genomic DNA from oropharyngeal tumour and adjacent normal tissues were isolated and subjected to sodium bisulfite conversion (detailed laboratory procedure has been described in the Supplementary Sect. 1.1). Then the DNA samples were assayed using Illumina Infinium HumanMethylation450 BeadChip. DNA methylation data analysis was performed using R package “minfi” [11] where raw IDAT files were processed and detection p-value and beta (β) value for each of the ∼485,000 CpG sites were calculated [12].

CpG probes that satisfied following criteria were removed from analysis: (a) detection p-value > = 0.01 present in > = 50% of samples; (b) masked with “NA”- values; (c) polymorphic (MAF > 0.01) SNPs (dbSNP build 150) located within 10 bp of the interrogated site; (d) any non-CpG loci; (e) mapped to sex chromosome to minimize the false positive inferences [13]. From the discovery subset 2 pairs of samples were discarded as their respective tumour tissues were identified as misclassified samples by unsupervised hierarchical clustering and multidimensional scaling plot considering 1,000 of the most variable CpG sites. Intra-array normalization was performed using Subset quantile within array normalization (SWAN) [14] that removed the bias due to usage of dual Infinium chemistry followed by Quantile normalization which removes inter sample random variations [1]. Principal component analysis was performed on normalized β values to detect batch effect and none observed (Supplementary Fig. S4).

Wilcoxon signed rank test (tumour vs adjacent normal) was performed along with p-value correction by Benjamini–Hochberg method to identify significantly differentially methylated CpG site (DMP). A CpG site was considered as DMP if it exhibited corrected p-value < 0.05 and average |Δβ|≥ 0.2. DMPs having average Δβ ≥ 0.2 or Δβ ≤ − 0.2 were regarded as hypermethylated or hypomethylated. Differentially methylated regions (DMR) were identified by mapping the DMPs to multiple regions of the genes. Within gene boundaries the regions considered were promoter, gene body and 3′UTR. In particular, a genebody region was considered as an intermediate region spanning between 5’UTR to 3’UTR, excluding the both. This region consists of the 1st exon and gene body as mentioned in Illumina Infinium Human Methylation array annotations. Significantly differentially methylated regions (DMRs) were defined as the genomic regions harbouring at least one DMP (in case of > 1 DMPs they should be unidirectional). Quantification of the methylation level of DMRs were performed by averaging the β values of DMPs mapped to it [1, 13].

Whole transcriptome sequence data generation and analysis

Extracted RNA from OPSCC and normal tissues (details of laboratory procedure were provided in the Supplementary Sect. 1.2) were sequenced in Illumina HiSeq–2500 to generate 2 × 100 bp reads. FastQC, TopHat2 (v. 2.1.1) [15], Cufflinks [16] and Cuffmerge packages were used for QC, alignment, transcript assembly and normalized gene expression estimation respectively. Cuffdiff was used to identify differentially expressed genes between tumour and adjacent normal pairs. A gene was denoted as significantly differentially expressed (DEG) if it exhibited corrected p-value < 0.05 and |log2 (fold-change)|> = 1.

Pathway enrichment analysis

Pathway enrichment analysis was performed using the genes with positive correlation between genebody specific epigenetic alterations and gene expression changes in tumour compared to adjacent normal tissues. These genebody mediated epigenetically dregulated genes were imported in Cytoscape [17] with ClueGo V2.5.1 [18] and Cluepedia V1.5.1 [19] plugin. ClueGo was used to build the pathway networks in functionally categorized fashion whereas the visualization of the associated genes within the network were performed by Cluepedia. KEGG pathways and Gene Ontology (GO) biological processes (BP) terms were analyzed for enrichment, performing right-sided hypergeometric tests and the p values were corrected for multiple-testing using the Benjamini–Hochberg method. Only the pathways/GO BP terms with p-value < 0.05 were considered significant.

Gene set enrichment analysis

Significantly differentially expressed genes in oropharyngeal tumour samples compared to adjacent normal samples and all of which had non-zero normalized expression value (FPKM > 0) in all the samples were considered for gene set enrichment analysis using GSEA software (v. 4.0.3) [20] to identify enriched gene sets in oropharyngeal tumour tissues. The DEGs were ranked in descending order according to their average log2 (fold change) values. GSEA pre-ranked analysis was performed setting the number of permutations at 1000 whereas all other parameters were set to default. Under the comprehensive resources of gene sets MSigDB (Molecular Signatures Database) v 7.141 [21], Hallmark & KEGG gene sets were considered for enrichment analysis. A gene set was regarded as significantly enriched which satisfied the criteria (a) |normalized enrichment scores (NES) |> 1, (b) nominal P-value (NP) < 0.05, and (c) false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.25.

Quantification of tumour-infiltrating immune cells from whole transcriptome data

The abundance of tumour-infiltrating immune cells was evaluated using CIBERSORT [22] (http://cibersort.stanford.edu/) web tool. Normalized gene expression (FPKM) values of paired tumour and adjacent normal samples were imported to the web tool and the default gene signature matrix—LM22 was selected which contains expression information of 547 genes to distinguish 22 human hematopoietic cell phenotypes. The deconvolution algorithm was run, setting the permutation value at 100 which estimated the relative proportion of 22 types of infiltrating immune cells present in oropharyngeal tumour and respective adjacent normal samples. Significant alterations in the abundance of infiltrating immune cells in tumour compared to adjacent normal samples were evaluated by paired-sample t-test on log2 transformed proportions, where the p-value < 0.05 considered significant.

Results

Epigenomic dysregulations in genebodies of oropharyngeal tumours

Global methylome profile of oropharyngeal tumour and paired adjacent normal samples from 14 patients revealed 25,494 significantly differentially methylated CpG loci (DMP), with average |Δβ|≥ 0.2 and adjusted P-value < 0.05. Power analysis using a simulation-based beta-distribution model demonstrated that our paired cohort (N = 14) achieved 100% empirical power to detect methylation differences of |Δβ|≥ 0.2, the threshold applied in downstream analyses. Even small methylation changes (|Δβ|≥ 0.05) could be identified with > 80% power, confirming that the study design is sufficiently robust for detecting biologically meaningful CpG alterations (Supplementary Sect. 1.3, Supplementary Fig. S3). About, 42% (10,786 of 25,494) of the DMPs were found to map to genebody regions, out of which ~ 95% (N = 10,214) were found to be enriched in genebodies of protein-coding genes. Substantial variation was observed in the proportions of the genebody DMPs (gDMPs) across chromosomes. All the chromosomes were found to harbour significantly higher proportion of hypermethylated gDMPs than hypomethylated ones. Chromosome 11 was found to harbour maximal proportion of methylation changes whereas the same was minimal in chromosome 18. Chromosome 22 was observed to be heavily hypermethylated (4.3%) followed by Chromosome 14 (3.4%) and 17 (3.2%) respectively (Fig. 1A). Within the gene-bodies the pattern of DNA methylation changes in CGIs, shelf and shore regions were distinctively different compared to non-CGI open sea regions. Approximately 54% (N = 5556) of genebody DMPs mapped to non-CGI open sea regions. Open sea regions of genebodies were found to harbour twice of hypomethylation (~ 22.2%) as compared to the CGI and associated shelf and shore (~ 11.8%) regions, while both the regions had comparable amounts of hypermethylation (~ 34% in CGI and ~ 32% in open sea regions respectively) (Fig. 1B). The methylome landscape of oropharyngeal carcinoma tissues revealed that the genebodies of 3,689 protein coding genes were differentially methylated, of which 2,417 were hypermethylated and 1,272 were hypomethylated. These differentially methylated genebodies harboured 6,641 DMPs. These results were further validated on tumour tissues from 10 additional tumour samples from oropharyngeal cancer patients (validation set). Significantly strong correlation was observed between the β values of these unidirectional genebody DMPs in the tumours of the discovery set and the same the in tumours of the validation set (Spearman rho = 0.86 and p-value < 2.2e-16) (Supplementary Fig. S5). Out of 3,689 genes that had DNA methylation dysregulation in genebodies, ~ 20% of these genes (N = 730) also exhibited differential methylation in their promoters. Inverse relationship between differential methylation of promoter and gene body was observed in ~ 16% of these 730 genes. Unsupervised hierarchical clustering (along with heatmap) on Δβ values of differentially methylated genebody regions classified 14 OPSCC patients into two major clusters, further cluster 2 consisted of two sub clusters. The ranges of average Δβ values clearly elucidated that DNA methylation alternations found in Cluster 1: severe, Cluster 2A: mild, Cluster 2B: moderate respectively (Fig. 1C–E). Gene body specific DNA methylation driven alterations in gene expression

Significant gene expression alterations were observed in 1,549 protein-coding genes by analysing paired tumour and adjacent normal RNA-Seq data from the 14 patients in which DNA methylation analysis was performed. All of these significantly differentially expressed genes (DEGs) showed at least two-fold expression changes (mean of |log2 (fold change) |> = 1) with Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted p-value < 0.05. Upregulation in expression was observed in 781 genes, whereas 768 genes exhibited downregulation. Additionally, the quantitation of HPV (Human papillomavirus) reads from the RNA-Seq data revealed that none of the OPSCC samples harboured any sequence reads that mapped to HPV genomic sequences. The HPV negative status of all OPSCC samples were validated using a nested multiplex PCR (NMPCR) assay that detect and genotype HPV targeting the E6/E7 gene region (Supplementary Sect. 1.4, Supplementary Fig. S2, Supplementary Table S2). Further, integration of genebody DNA methylation and gene expression data of these patients led to the identification of 454 genes which showed significant differential methylation in genebodies as well as differential gene expression (Fig. 2A, B). Among these, methylation dysregulation in gene promoters with negatively correlated gene expression were found in 117 genes (Supplementary Sect. 1.7, Supplementary Table S9) previously [1], and were not considered in the present analyses. DNA methylation alterations in genebody that were positively correlated with gene expression (hyper/hypomethylation in genebody and upregulated/downregulated gene expression respectively), were observed in 137 genes (~ 41%), of which epigenetic upregulation and downregulation was identified in 98 genes and 39 genes respectively (Fig. 2C–F). Significant positive correlation (Spearman’s ρ > = 0.4, P-value < 0.05) were found in 116 genes (Supplementary Table S3). An overall resemblance was observed in the methylation and expression status of the DMPs & DEGs identified here with that of publicly available OPSCC data from TCGA-HNSCC cohort. Notably, one of the pathways identified in our analysis was Tryptophan metabolism [hsa00380]. The two genes (TDO2, KYNU) which were largely contributes to the dysregulation of Trp metabolism pathway showed significant differences in their methylation and expression levels while compared with that of OPSCC samples from TCGA-HNSCC cohort irrespective of their HPV status (Supplementary Fig. S8). Gene sets enrichment in oropharyngeal tumour samples

Transcriptome wide differentially expressed protein coding genes (DEG) in OPSCC tissues compared to adjacent normal were considered for Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) as genebody driven epigenetic modulation of Tryptophan metabolism pathway possibly triggers a cascade of tumour promoting immunomodulatory responses through major transcriptome reprograming. Twelve Hallmark gene sets were found to be significantly enriched (Supplementary Table S5) out of which nine were known to be associated with cancer. The enriched hallmark gene sets which play pivotal roles in carcinogenesis triggering pro-tumour immune responses were IL6/JAK/STAT3 signalling, IL2 STAT5 signalling and interferon alpha and gamma response. Tumour proliferation, migration, invasion and metastasis were facilitated through the alterations in tumour necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) signalling via nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB), epithelial mesenchymal transition (EMT) and KRAS signalling pathway. Twelve significantly enriched KEGG pathway gene sets (Supplementary Table S6) were identified by GSEA, considering the same set of DEGs, all of which drive tumour development and progression. Among them, the five most significantly enriched KEGG pathways—Cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction, Chemokine signalling pathway, JAK STAT signalling pathway, NOD and TOLL like receptor signalling pathway are known to be involved in immune modulatory processes promoting aggressive cancer.

Infiltrating immune cells signature in oropharyngeal carcinoma patients

Comprehensive analysis of the tumour infiltrating immune cells present in OPSCC tissues was performed using our RNA-Seq data, where the fractions of 22 immune cell populations were evaluated in these 14 OPSCC patients. Ten infiltrating immune cell populations showed significant differences (p-value < 0.05) in their proportions between tumour and adjacent normal tissues (Fig. 5, Supplementary Table S7). The proportion of CD8+ T cells (cytotoxic T cells) was found to be significantly reduced in oropharyngeal tumour indicating their inability to infiltrate and sustain in the tumour microenvironment (TME). Upregulation of IDO1, TDO2 and IDO2 observed by us might lead to inhibition of T cell proliferation, additionally confirming the depletion of cytotoxic T cells. Resting natural killer (NK) cells were found abound in these tumour tissues which are reflective of the reduced presence of activated NK cells. Significantly elevated proportions of CD4+ memory activated T cells were also found in tumours compared to adjacent normal tissues. This is also supported by the observed two-fold upregulation of CTLA4 in all except in one patient and four-fold upregulation in 64% of patients. The proportion of activated mast cells were higher in tumour tissues whereas, the proportion of resting mast cell was lower compared to adjacent normal. Highly proinflammatory, activated mast cells found in oropharyngeal tumour might recruit neutrophils in the tumour environment which is evident from the presence of high proportions of neutrophils in tumour tissues. Upregulation of multiple chemokines viz. CCL2, CXCL1, CXCL2 that are known to be released from mast cells are confirmatory evidence of the abundance of activated mast cells and neutrophils in the tumour environment. Inter tumour heterogeneity in the proportion of the infiltrating immune cells were also observed. Unlike rest of the patients, the CD8+ T cells proportion was elevated in tumour tissues in patients 18 and 22. Interestingly, the proportion of the activated dendritic cells and neutrophils were lower in the tumour tissue of patient 21, 27 and patient 8, 23 respectively, compared to other patients. Simple linear regression analysis revealed that the patients’ age has a significant effect on the differential proportions of cytotoxic CD8+ T cells (intercept: 0.1594, slope: − 0.004484 and p value = 0.02564) and neutrophils (intercept: − 0.179874, slope: 0.005015 and p value = 0.02558) in tumour tissues respectively (Supplementary Fig. S6). Discussion

In a recent study we had reported that DNA methylation changes in promoters modulated gene expressions in oropharyngeal cancer patients from Meghalaya, in North East India. DNA methylome profiling of HPV negative OPSCC tissues compared to paired adjacent normal samples had resulted in identification of 25,494 significantly differentially methylated CpG loci (DMP) [1]. In our present analysis on the same cohort, we have found that genebody regions harbour ~ 42% (N = 10,786) of the total identified DMPs. These genebody specific DMPs enriches 3,689 protein coding genes among which 2,417 were hypermethylated and 1,272 were hypomethylated. Contrary to promoter methylation driven gene expression changes, genebody methylation is reported to exhibit positive correlation with gene expression. In this study, positive regulatory effects of genebody specific DNA methylation on gene expression was observed in 137 genes, out of which ~ 72% were epigenetically upregulated and remaining were epigenetically downregulated.

Among the top most 10% of genes (N = 14) with significant high positive correlation between genebody methylation and gene expression, 13 genes were epigenetically upregulated (APBA2, C1QTNF1, GRAMD1B, CCDC136, DOK4, ADAM8, PANK1, PDE3B, MOV10L1, DGKG, CLDN14, HOXB7 and ADAMTS2) and only UACA showed epigenetic downregulation. Majority of these genes are known to be involved in tumourigenesis except ADAMTS2 which is reported to exhibit anti-tumour activity by impeding angiogenesis [23]. Upregulation of APBA2 and PDE3B have been previously implicated in HNSCC [24, 25]. A recent study found APBA2 upregulation in oral cavity and esophagus of rats, after administration of (S)-N’-Nitrosonornicotine—a major constituent of tobacco products and potent carcinogen [26], whereas the knockdown of PDE3 along with other PDEs inhibit tumour cell growth and angiogenesis [27]. The upregulation of HOXB7 that induces proliferative activity further increasing the tumour mass, is also reported in oral squamous cell carcinomas and associated with poor disease outcome [28]. High CLDN14 expression in the tumour tissues observed in this study might be indicative of activated PI3K/AKT/mTOR signalling driven tumour growth which has been previously reported in colorectal cancers to be associated with poor survival rate [29]. Loss of cytotoxic potential of T lymphocytes in OPSCC tissues can additionally be explained by activation of DGKG which limits DAG-dependent functions in T lymphocytes. DGKG inactivation reinstalls the T cell attack in tumours, decreasing the tumour cell growth [30]. The downregulation of UACA in oropharyngeal tumour tissues could promote genomic instability and inhibit apoptosis through the UACA deficiency mediated Apaf-1 nuclear entry in malignant cells as seen in non-small-cell lung cancer. However, there has been previous reports of high expression of UACA in squamous cell carcinomas and hence this finding needs to be investigated further [31]. Thus, our results indicate that genebody methylation mediated epigenetically regulated genes with high correlation scores (spearman’s rho > = 0.75) might be driving aggressive cancer.

Gene-body driven epigenetically modulated genes (N = 137) were found to significantly enrich Tryptophan metabolism and p53 signalling pathways. A major finding of this study is enrichment of Tryptophan metabolism pathway through genebody specific epigenetic upregulation of TDO2, KYNU and downregulation of TPH2 in oropharyngeal tumour tissues. Additionally, IDO1 and IDO2 which are important players of this pathway were found to be upregulated, of which IDO1 showed promoter hypomethylation driven upregulation. Tryptophan (Trp) is an essential amino acid and its metabolism via Trp metabolism pathway, releases metabolites which are crucial in neurotransmission and regulation of immune responses [32]. Enzymatic degradation of Trp is initiated by indoleamine-2,3-dioxygenase 1 (IDO1), IDO2 and tryptophan-2,3-dioxygenase (TDO2) and convert it to kynurenine (KYN). Conversion of KYN requires kynureninase (KYNU) and follows subsequent intermediate steps that complete the metabolism of the Trp and synthesise the vital energy cofactor, nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) as end product. Depletion of local Trp is known to inhibit T cell proliferation in tumour tissues [33]. Trp metabolites have the potential to promote cancer cell motility leading to metastasis. In-vitro studies on glioblastoma and breast cancer cells have reported that TDO2 expression promotes tumour cell migration and invasion. Similarly, IDO1 activation promotes migration of lung cancer cells, whereas its silencing reduces migration [34]. The genes upregulated in this pathway additionally facilitate the suppression of natural killer cells [35]. Overexpression of TDO2, KYNU along with IDO1 and IDO2 might result in immune modulation leading to the depletion of TRP in tumour microenvironment (TME), thus promoting immune evasion in oropharyngeal tumour.

Significant upregulation of DNMT1 (log2 fold-change = 1, p value = 0.0002) was noted in tumour samples. DNMT1 maintains the DNA methylation homeostasis in cells by restoring the specific methylation pattern on the daughter strand in accordance with that of the parental DNA, during replication [36]. The inhibition of DNMT1 has shown positive anticancer effects on various types of squamous cell carcinomas [37]. The TCGA HNSCC samples (N = 520) showed significantly high expression of DNMT1 as compared to normal tissues (N = 44, p < 0.001) [38]. A recent study on oral cancer reported that DNMT1 expression facilitates oral malignant transformation in human samples, and its inhibition considerably minimized the tumorigenicity in vitro and as well as in xenografted OSCC model [37].

Enrichment of interferon alpha (IFN-α), interferon gamma (IFN-γ), IL6/JAK/STAT3 and IL2/STAT5 signalling along with significant upregulation of IFNG, JAK3, STAT4, IL6 and IL2RA1 in OPSCC tissues, observed in this study might be indicative of major immunomodulation. IFN-γ shows pro-tumourigenic activity by inducing the expression of PD-L1 and IDO in the TME. In OPSCC tissue, IFN-γ mediated IDO activity modulates Trp metabolism pathway whereas, significant upregulation of PD-L1 and PD-L2 leads to IFN-γ mediated PD-L1/PD-1, PD-L2/PD-1 interactions in the tumour microenvironment, both of which promotes immunosuppression by inhibiting the function of cytotoxic T cells [13, 39, 40]. Elevated level of IL-6 in TME activates STAT3 signalling [41, 42] further leading to aberrant hyperactivity of cytokine-driven (IL-6) JAK/STAT3 pathway that promotes pro-tumour immune response through proliferation, survival, invasiveness and metastasis of malignant cells in OPSCC tissues [43, 44]. Significant enrichment of KEGG pathway gene sets viz. cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction, chemokine signalling and JAK STAT signalling pathways exhibited concordance with that of enriched hallmark gene sets which further strengthens the immune suppressive activity. Additionally, TOLL and NOD like signalling pathways were found significantly enriched in OPSCC tissues. Toll-like receptors (TLRs) trigger the expressions of multiple cytokines that play a pivotal role in cancer pathogenesis through the activation of multiple signalling pathways which includes NF-κB, MAPKs and interferon-regulatory factors (IRFs). In the present study, TLR4, TLR8 upregulation were observed in malignant tissues which contributes to aggressive tumourigenesis and associated with fatal outcome ascertained from the previous reports on lung cancer, colorectal cancer, thyroid carcinomas and pancreatic cancer [45–47]. In addition, Nod-like receptors (NLRs) inflammasome also triggers the expression of multiple cytokines and chemokines such as IL-1β, IL-6, IL-18, Gro-α/CXCL1 and CCL2/MCP-1 found in this study and in different cancer types [48]. Together, the activated TLR signalling cascade along with alterations in multiple TLR genes and the network of cytokines, chemokines and NLR-induced inflammatory factors possibly orchestrate a multitude of pro-neoplastic activity in the TME of OPSCC tissues promoting proliferation, invasion and migration of malignant cells.

Cytotoxic CD8+ T cells predominantly eradicate pathogens and neoplastic cells and its co-occurrence with CD4+ T cells prevent its exhaustion along with maintaining its cytotoxic responses. Thus, depletion of cytotoxic CD8+ T cells in oropharyngeal tumour tissue fails to provide anticancer immunity and might permit cancer cell growth [49, 50]. Pro-carcinogens present in tobacco influence the activation of mast cells (MC) in oral tissue, reported in oral squamous cell carcinomatous conditions [51] that explains the abundance of MC in OPSCC tissues as the study population were largely exposed to tobacco. Activated MC perturbs the TME promoting cell proliferation and survival, angiogenesis, invasiveness, and metastasis [52]. Mast cells were reported to be positively correlated with extracellular matrix degradation through the biosynthesis and release of MMP which possibly facilitates degradation of collagen tissue [51]. The upregulation of chemokines viz. CCL2, CXCL1, CXCL2 in OPSCC tissues indicative of their release from MC and facilitate early recruitment of neutrophil to sites of inflammation/TME [53]. Mast cells function as immunosuppressant in TME by impairing T cell immunity thus promoting tumour progression [54]. Activated CXCL1, CXCL2 and abundance of neutrophil in OPSCC tissues illustrates the pro-tumour phenotype of neutrophils in TME that could trigger high pro-angiogenic, pro-metastatic and immunosuppressive activity [55].

We acknowledge the multiple limitations of our study. The restricted site of oropharynx, difficulty in accessing the site and the small size of tumours compel us to include only a limited number of patients from whom both tumour and adjacent normal tissue samples in sufficient quantities could be obtained for both DNA methylation array and RNA-Seq assays. For the same reason, we were unable to carry out the validatory experiments. While the functional rescue experiments were beyond the present scope, we will be undertaken CRISPR-based perturbation and pharmacological inhibition followed by immune co-culture assays to mechanistically validate these targets as future work subsequently. The survival information in our cohort was inconsistent, with several patients lost to follow-up. Therefore, we were unable to perform reliable survival analyses otherwise which could lead to evaluation of the prognostic relevance of gene body methylation and associated expression of the key genes further strengthening the clinical translational value of the study. Despite the limitations, our study not only reveals interesting findings but is also the first study to explore the genebody methylation driven transcriptome and immune repose alteration at genome wide level in oropharyngeal cancer. The integrative approach of methylation–transcriptomic analysis of paired patient samples provides a robust, clinically anchored basis for implicating the IDO/TDO2 axis and other immune-metabolic regulators in OPSCC. The findings warrant their validation in an independent cohort of patients located in this region.

Conclusion

This is the first report that elucidates genome-wide genebody methylation driven gene expression alterations and changes in the abundance of immune cells in tumour microenvironment in oropharyngeal cancer. The novel findings of this study include dysregulation of Tryptophan (Trp) metabolism pathway by the genebody driven epigenetically modulated genes and IFN-γ mediated PD-L1/PD-1, PD-L2/PD-1 interactions which causes the depletion of cytotoxic T cells in OPSCC tissues. Upregulation of multiple chemokines—CCL2, CXCL1, CXCL2 lead to the abundance of mast cell and neutrophils having pro-tumour phenotype in tumour microenvironment. Such key alterations in the tumour cells modulate the immune responses promoting immune evasion of oropharyngeal malignant cells. Our results suggest potential immuno-therapeutic targets for the treatment of oropharyngeal squamous cell carcinoma.

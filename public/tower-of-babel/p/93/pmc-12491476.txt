Synchronized fertilization based on crop nutrient uptake and fertilizer nutrient release characteristics increases nutrient use efficiency in banana
Authors: Xiaolong Zhang, Li Zhao, Qilong Huang, Bowen Pang, Zhuoyi Zhou, Ying Lu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractProper fertilization plays a dual role in maintaining high banana yield while protecting the environment. This study investigates the effects of a fertilization scheme designed based on banana nutrient absorption characteristics and fertilizer nutrient release dynamics, aiming to improve nutrient use efficiency and yield while promoting the green and sustainable development of the banana industry. The experiment utilized Zhongjiao No. 9 as the test banana variety. Initially, the total nutrient accumulation in mature banana plants was measured, and a fertilizer formula (with optimized total nutrient amounts and proportions) was developed. Subsequently, the ratio of conventional fertilizer (providing readily available nutrients) to controlled-release fertilizer (providing time-released nutrients) was adjusted to align the nutrient release patterns of the fertilizer with the nutrient uptake patterns of banana plants, resulting in a synchronized fertilization. Field experiments were conducted to evaluate the performance of this fertilization scheme, focusing on plant growth, nutrient accumulation, and yield. The results demonstrated that, compared to conventional fertilizer treatment, the synchronized fertilization significantly increased nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P2O5), and potassium (K2O) accumulation by 8.66, 11.54, and 19.55%, respectively. nutrient use efficiency was also significantly enhanced, with improvements of 35.72, 94.85, and 29.41% for N, P2O5, and K2O, respectively. Additionally, the fresh weight and dry weight of banana plants increased by 20.93 and 56.14%, respectively, while banana yield increased by 7.03%. These findings indicate that the synchronized fertilization, tailored to the nutrient absorption characteristics of banana plants and the release dynamics of fertilizers, significantly enhances nutrient utilization efficiency, improves plant nutrient status, and boosts yield. Therefore, this fertilization strategy represents an economically viable and environmentally sustainable solution, offering a promising approach for the green and sustainable development of the banana industry.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-17602-0.Keywords: Synchronized fertilization, Nutrient absorption and utilization, ProductionSubject terms: Ecology, Environmental sciences, Plant physiology

Introduction

Banana is globally recognized as one of the most significant fruit and food crops, with an annual production of approximately 155 million tons. As the world’s second-largest banana producer, China achieved a total output of 11.7768 million tons in 2022, cultivated across an area of 3,366.67 km². The cultivation of banana is extensively distributed throughout tropical and subtropical regions in China1. Banana plants are tall, produce high yields, and have significant economic benefits, and have become a crucial agricultural commodity. However, current cultivation practices reveal that farmers often prioritize yield maximization and economic returns without scientific guidance, resulting in prevalent issues such as excessive fertilizer application, imbalanced nutrient inputs, and low nutrient use efficiency within the banana industry2. These extensive fertilization management practices are fundamentally incompatible with the principles of green and sustainable agriculture in the modern era. Consequently, the development of fertilization strategies that simultaneously ensure optimal yield and minimize environmental impacts has emerged as a critical scientific challenge in promoting the sustainable development of the banana industry.

The main approaches to improving fertilizer use efficiency in agriculture currently include precision fertilization technologies, optimization of fertilizer types, and improvement of application methods, among others3–6. Among them, formulated fertilization technology is a scientific fertilization method based on the law of crop fertilizer requirement, aiming to achieve efficient fertilizer utilization, reduce waste, and improve crop yield and quality through precise proportioning of nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, and medium and trace elements7. This technology mainly formulates personalized scientific fertilization schemes based on the nutrient absorption characteristics of crops, so as to avoid excessive or insufficient fertilization8,9. At present, there have been many studies on formulated fertilizers. It has been found that under the premise of reducing fertilizer nutrient input by 10%-25%, crop yield and quality can still be guaranteed, and the utilization rate of nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium can also be effectively improved10. Jiang et al.11 found that tobacco formulated fertilizer did not affect the growth and agronomic traits of flue-cured tobacco under the premise of 25% reduction, and could maintain yield and ensure the quality of flue-cured tobacco. In addition, other field experiments have confirmed that formulated fertilizer can increase winter wheat yield by 22–77% and nitrogen use efficiency by 15.0–17.0%7; it can increase rice yield by 9%-14% and nitrogen use efficiency by 15.0–19.0%12. The corn formulated fertilizer designed by Wang et al.12 can increase the yield of spring corn by 2.9% and 16.1% in black soil and chernozem areas, respectively, while the nitrogen recovery efficiency is increased by 5.4 and 23.9%, respectively. To sum up, the formulated fertilization technology designed according to the nutrient absorption characteristics of crops has achieved good results in the application of tobacco, wheat, rice, and corn, showing good efficiency, precision, and environmental protection. Therefore, it is theoretically feasible to design banana formulated fertilizer with reference to this principle. At present, the design of banana fertilization schemes is mostly based on field experience and field experiments, which are cumbersome and complex, and the final results may not be completely correct. However, the banana formulated fertilizer designed with reference to nutrient absorption characteristics can well solve the coupling problem of scientific, convenient, and environmental protection of banana nutrient input, which may be a good solution. However, there are few reports on banana formulated fertilizer at present, and whether this design scheme can succeed in bananas needs to be verified.

Although banana formula fertilizer can theoretically solve the scientific problem of total nutrient input and proportion, it cannot completely address the challenge of sustained nutrient supply throughout the entire growth period of bananas13. To meet the fertilizer requirement characteristics of bananas at various stages as much as possible, this study aims to further upgrade the nutrient composition characteristics of banana formula fertilizer based on the nutrient release properties of fertilizers, on the basis of designing banana formula fertilizer according to the nutrient absorption characteristics of bananas, and to achieve a better balance between nutrient supply and demand of bananas by combining split fertilization14. Currently, in the field of fertilizers, nutrients can be divided into quick-acting nutrients and controlled-release nutrients according to their release characteristics15. Each has distinct advantages and disadvantages: quick-acting nutrients have the advantages of strong fertilizer supply capacity and quick effect, but their disadvantages include easy leaching and loss, insufficient long-term nutrient supply, and adverse effects on the ecological environment. Slow-release nutrients have the advantages of strong sustained fertilizer supply capacity and high nutrient utilization rate; however, their high cost and limited short-term fertilizer supply capacity are their main drawbacks16. In order to give full play to the advantages of both types of fertilizers, this study intends to formulate banana formula fertilizer by scientifically proportioning quick-acting nutrients and controlled-release nutrients, aiming to fully exert the synergistic effect of the two types of fertilizers and meet the nutrient demands of bananas at each growth stage17. This fertilization scheme designed based on the nutrient absorption characteristics of bananas and the nutrient release characteristics of fertilizers is named “banana synchronized fertilization” in this paper. At present, research on banana formula synchronized nutrient fertilizer is still blank, and relevant experimental hypotheses and questions need to be verified and answered urgently18,19.

In summary, to comprehensively address the coupling challenges of scientific, ecological, and practical nutrient input in banana cultivation, this study adopted a systematic approach. First, the total nutrient accumulation of banana plants throughout their growth cycle was measured to determine nutrient absorption characteristics. Based on these characteristics, a banana-specific formula fertilizer was designed. Subsequently, an advanced synchronized fertilization was developed by optimizing the ratio of readily available nutrients (conventional fertilizer) and slow-release nutrients (controlled-release fertilizer). The efficacy of this formulation was validated through field trials, with a focus on its impact on banana growth, nutrient uptake, and yield. The results provide empirical evidence on the performance of synchronized fertilization under field conditions, offering a theoretical foundation for advancing green and sustainable practices in the banana industry.

Materials and methods

Test materials

Banana field is located in Longmen County, Huizhou City, banana demonstration station (East 114.25 ; north latitude 23.73). It has a subtropical monsoon climate with an average annual temperature of 20.8 °C and an average annual rainfall of 2150 mm. The soil type of banana garden was red soil, and the soil texture was clay. The soil pH, organic matter, total nitrogen, available phosphorus and available potassium were 5.09, 21.89 g kg− 1, 105 mg kg−1, 4.95 mg kg−1 and 229.71 mg kg− 1, respectively.

Synchronized fertilization design

Experimental design

In the preliminary phase, mature banana plants under conventional fertilization and management practices were sampled to analyze their nutrient characteristics. Five representative plants were selected and dissected into roots, stems, leaves, and fruits for sampling. The collected samples were transported to the laboratory, where they underwent washing, fixation, drying, and subsequent analysis to determine the total nutrient content and proportion absorbed by the entire plant. The current range of seasonal nutrient use efficiency of banana industry is about-N (15–30%), P₂O₅ (15–25%) and K₂O (20–50%). The maximum values in these ranges were specified as the ideal utilization efficiency, that is, the ideal utilization efficiencies of N, P₂O₅ and K₂O were 30%、25% and 50%, respectively, in order to maximize the fertilizer utilization efficiency. The total nutrient content and proportion of the synchronized fertilization were obtained by dividing the measured nutrient absorption by their respective ideal utilization efficiency20. Meanwhile, aiming to optimize the nutrient supply-demand balance in the banana-soil system and in light of the previous research findings of our research group—which indicated that a 30% proportion of controlled-release nitrogen is optimal21—30% of the application rates of N and K₂O were set as controlled-release nutrients, with the remaining 70% as quick-acting nutrients. These two types of nutrients were derived from controlled-release fertilizers and conventional fertilizers, respectively. Due to its low efficiency and susceptibility to fixation, P₂O₅ was not included in the controlled-release nutrient formulation. Detailed nutrient quantities are presented in Table S120.

Synchronized fertilization field application test

A field experiment was conducted to verify the fertilizer effect on banana. The tested banana variety was Zhongjiao No.9, the planting density was about 1800 plants hm2 the row spacing was 2.5 m, and the plant spacing was 2.25 m. A total of three treatments were set up, which were blank treatment (CK), conventional fertilizer treatment (CF) and synchronized fertilization treatment (FSF). Each treatment had about 120 plants and an area of 666.67 m2. The blank treatment was not fertilized, and the conventional fertilizer was designed according to the fertilization habits of local farmers. Each plant was applied with N 240 g, P2O5 300 g, K2O 360 g, and the ratio was 1:1.25:1.5, that is, each plant was applied with 1000 g 15-15-15 and 1000 g 9-15-21 (N:P2O5:K2O) conventional compound fertilizer. Synchronized fertilization was developed according to the nutrient demand law of banana and the nutrient release characteristics of fertilizer. Each plant was applied with N 195 g, P2O5 58.5 g, K2O 450 g, and the ratio was 1:0.3:2.31. 30% of the amount of N and K2O was provided by controlled release fertilizer, and the rest was provided by conventional fertilizer. The properties of controlled release fertilizer and conventional fertilizer are shown in table S2. (The Envelope thickness in the table is the mass ratio of the coating material to the fertilizer core ; the Fertilizer efficiency is the time when 80% of the nutrients in the controlled-release fertilizer are completely released in constant temperature water at 25 °C). That is, the application of conventional urea per plant is 169.57 g, vegetable oil coated controlled release urea is 133.56 g, conventional potassium chloride is 427.5 g, vegetable oil coated potassium chloride is 238.52 g, and conventional compound fertilizer is 390 g (15-15-15). In order to meet the nutrient requirements of banana at seedling, vegetative growth, flower bud differentiation and young fruit stages, the conventional fertilizer was applied once at the seedling stage, flower bud differentiation stage and young fruit stage respectively, and the vegetative growth stage was set twice due to the long span of time. Therefore, a total of 5 times of conventional fertilizer fertilization. To explore whether the synchronized fertilization has fertilizer efficiency advantages and economic advantages in reducing the number of fertilization times and labor costs, only one fertilization was set at the seedling stage, vegetative growth stage and flower bud differentiation stage, a total of three fertilizations. The fertilizer was evenly spread around the trunk 10 ~ 50 cm and covered with soil. The specific fertilization time and dosage are shown in Table S3.

Sample collection and determination

The plant height, stem diameter (stem circumference at 20 cm from the ground) and leaf number were measured at the seedling stage (G1), vegetative growth stage (G2), flower bud differentiation stage and mature stage (G3) and mature stage (G4) of banana plants, and destructive sampling of banana plants in each period was carried out. Five representative plants were taken from each treatment. Each plant was dissected and collected in four parts:root, stem, leaf and fruit, and the fresh weight of each part of the plant was measured. Then the sub-samples were collected by quartering method and weighed, and the sub-samples were taken back to the laboratory for washing and drying. The sub-samples were killed at 105 °C for 30 min, dried to constant weight at 75 °C, and then the samples were crushed and prepared. The contents of nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium were determined. The nitrogen content was determined by Futura continuous flow analyzer, the phosphorus content was determined by SmartChem automatic intermittent chemical analyzer, and the potassium content was determined by flame photometer22.

Data analysis and statistical methods

Nutrient (N, P, K) accumulation of banana plant = ∑ dry matter weight of each organ × element content of each organ.

Nutrient (nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium) use efficiency (%) = (nutrient accumulation of whole plant in fertilization treatment-nutrient accumulation of whole plant in control treatment)/nutrient application rate × 100;

Nutrient (nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium) harvest index = fruit nutrient accumulation/aboveground nutrient accumulation;

Nutrient (N, P, K) partial factor productivity (kg/kg) = fertilization treatment banana yield/nutrient application rate.

Excel 2019 software was used for data statistics。Before data analysis, Shapiro and Bartlett (Shapiro & Wilk, 1965) methods were used to test data normality and homogeneity of variance. Then, SPSS 16.0 software was used for one-way analysis of variance, and Origin 9.0 software was used for principal component analysis, correlation analysis and drawing. The error bars in the figure are all standard error (SE).

Results

Effects of synchronized fertilization on growth index of banana

Figure S1 showed that there was no significant difference in plant height, stem diameter and leaf number among synchronized fertilization (FSF), conventional fertilizer (CF) and CK treatment at banana seedling stage (P > 0.05). However, at the vegetative growth stage, flower bud differentiation stage and mature stage, the plant height, stem diameter and leaf number of synchronized fertilization and conventional fertilizer were significantly higher than those of CK (P < 0.05), but there was no significant difference between synchronized fertilization and conventional fertilizer (except for leaf number at flower bud differentiation stage).

Effects of synchronized fertilization on nutrient accumulation and absorption of banana

The accumulation of nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium in banana plants at different stages of different fertilization treatments is shown in Fig. S2. The nitrogen accumulation of banana plants was significantly higher than that of CK in conventional fertilizer and synchronized fertilization treatment at all stages of banana growth and development (P < 0.05). Compared with conventional fertilizer and synchronized fertilization treatment, the conventional fertilizer was significantly higher in seedling stage (P < 0.05), and there was no significant difference in vegetative growth period (P > 0.05). In the flower bud differentiation stage and maturity stage, the synchronized fertilization was significantly higher (P < 0.05), followed by conventional fertilizer, and CK was significantly the lowest (P < 0.05). Except that there was no significant difference between conventional fertilizer treatment and CK at mature stage, the phosphorus accumulation of conventional fertilizer and synchronized fertilization was significantly higher than that of CK at other stages (P < 0.05). However, there was no significant difference between conventional fertilizer and synchronized fertilization treatment at seedling stage and vegetative growth stage. At flower bud differentiation stage and mature stage, synchronized fertilization treatment was significantly higher than conventional fertilizer (P < 0.05). The potassium accumulation of banana plants was also significantly higher than that of CK at all stages of banana growth and development (P < 0.05), but there was no significant difference between conventional fertilizer and synchronized fertilization treatment at seedling stage and flower bud differentiation stage. The conventional fertilizer was significantly higher in the vegetative growth period (P < 0.05), and the synchronized fertilization was significantly higher in the mature period (P < 0.05). In summary, the accumulation of nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium in banana plants treated with conventional fertilizer and synchronized fertilization was significantly higher than that of CK in each period (P < 0.05). Compared with the synchronized fertilization treatment, the accumulation of nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium in the middle and early stages of banana growth and development (seedling stage and vegetative growth stage) was mostly no significant difference between the two, and a small amount of conventional fertilizer treatment was significantly higher (P < 0.05). In the middle and late stages of banana growth and development (flower bud differentiation stage and mature stage), the synchronized fertilization treatment was significantly higher than the conventional fertilizer treatment (P < 0.05).

Effects of synchronized fertilization on nutrient uptake and utilization efficiency of banana

The Nutrient use efficiency, nutrient partial productivity and nutrient harvest index of banana maturity were shown in Fig. S3. The nutrient use efficiency of nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium in synchronized fertilization treatment was significantly higher than that of conventional fertilizer (P < 0.05). For the nutrient partial productivity, the nitrogen and phosphorus nutrient partial productivity of the synchronized fertilization treatment was significantly higher (P < 0.05), but the potassium nutrient partial productivity was significantly lower (P < 0.05). The harvest index of nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium showed no significant difference between conventional fertilizer and synchronized fertilization treatment.

Effects of synchronized fertilization on fresh and dry matter accumulation of banana

As shown in Fig. S4. There was no significant difference in the fresh and dry weight of banana plants treated with synchronized fertilization and conventional fertilizer at seedling stage, vegetative growth stage and flower bud differentiation stage, but both were significantly higher than CK (P < 0.05). At the mature stage, the fresh and dry weights of banana plants treated with synchronized fertilization were 74.63 kg plant− 1 and 5486.59 g plant− 1, respectively, which were significantly increased by 20.93%, 56.14% and 24.90%, 69.05%, respectively (P < 0.05), compared with conventional fertilizer and CK treatment.

Effect of synchronized fertilization on banana yield

Figure S5 is the box diagram of banana yield of each treatment. The results showed that the average banana yield of synchronized fertilization and conventional fertilizer was 12.66 kg plant− 1 and 11.77 kg plant− 1, respectively. The synchronized fertilization was significantly increased by 7.03% compared with conventional fertilizer (P < 0.05).

Correlation between banana yield and various indicators

Principal component analysis of banana yield and 17 physiological and biochemical indexes showed that the contribution rate of the first principal component was 62.0%, the contribution rate of the second principal component was 11.4%, and the cumulative contribution rate of the first two principal components was 73.4%, which basically explained most of the information of the data (Fig. S6a). In the first principal component (PC1), yield (CL), fresh weight (XZ), dry weight (GZ), nitrogen accumulation (N1), phosphorus accumulation (P1), potassium accumulation (K1), nitrogen absorption efficiency (N2), phosphorus absorption efficiency (P2), potassium absorption efficiency (K2), nitrogen partial factor productivity (N3) and phosphorus partial factor productivity (P3) had a greater load. Therefore, PC1 mainly represented the above indicators, and PC1 showed a strong positive correlation. In the second principal component (PC2), plant height (ZC), stem diameter (JC), nitrogen harvest index (N4), and potassium harvest index (K4) had greater load, so PC2 mainly represented the above indicators. Among them, plant height (ZC), stem diameter (JC), and nitrogen harvest index (N4) were positively correlated with PC2, and potassium harvest index (K4) was negatively correlated with PC2. At the same time, because the synchronized fertilization treatment is mainly distributed on the positive axis of the PC1 axis, it is positively correlated with PC1, and the first principal component is positively correlated with yield (CL), fresh weight (XZ), dry weight (GZ), nitrogen accumulation (N1), phosphorus accumulation (P1), potassium accumulation (K1), nitrogen absorption efficiency (N2), phosphorus absorption efficiency (P2), potassium absorption efficiency (K2), nitrogen partial productivity (N3) and phosphorus partial productivity (P3). Therefore, the synchronized fertilization treatment can effectively improve the yield (CL), fresh weight (XZ), dry weight (GZ), nitrogen accumulation (N1), phosphorus accumulation (P1), potassium accumulation (K1), nitrogen absorption efficiency (N2), phosphorus absorption efficiency (P2), potassium absorption efficiency (K2), nitrogen partial productivity (N3) and phosphorus partial productivity (P3) of banana.

Correlation analysis of Fig. S6b heatmap shows. banana yield (CL) was significantly positively correlated with plant fresh weight (XZ) dry weight (GZ), nitrogen accumulation (N1), phosphorus accumulation (P1), potassium accumulation (K1), nitrogen absorption efficiency (N2), phosphorus absorption efficiency (P2), potassium absorption efficiency (K2), nitrogen partial productivity (N3), phosphorus partial productivity (P3)and significantly negatively correlated with phosphorus harvest index (P4) (P < 0.05), but not significantly correlated with plant height (ZG), stem diameter (JC), leaf number (YP), phosphorus partial productivity (P3), nitrogen harvest index (N4)and potassium harvest index (K4).

Discussion

Banana is a typical fertilizer-consuming crop, and fertilization is of great significance to increase its yield23. Nactual agricultural production, banana farmers generally maintain high banana yields by applying large amounts of fertilizer24. However, they are unaware that unreasonable fertilization can lead to problems such as resource waste and environmental pollution25,26. This is highly detrimental to maintaining the green and sustainable development of the banana industry.At present, fertilizer formulas designed according to crop nutrient characteristics have shown good practical effects on corn, millet, rice and other crops27,28. Therefore, in this study, the fertilizer formula (total nutrient dosage and proportion) of banana was designed by referring to the previous research methods. The nutrient absorption characteristics of banana plants at mature stage under the condition of banana farmers ' habitual fertilization management were obtained by pre-determination, that is, the accumulation of N 68.25 g, P2O5 14.56 g and K2O 270.00 g in the whole growth period of each banana plant. Then, the ideal utilization efficiency of N, P2O5 and K2O nutrients in the current season was used as the calculation coefficient based on the nutrient accumulation. Finally, the amount of N, P2O5 and K2O in banana formula fertilizer was 195.00 g plant− 1, 58.50 g plant− 1 and 450.00 g plant− 1, respectively. To further meet the dynamic supply and demand balance of nutrients in banana-soil system, 70% of the amount of N and K2O in formula fertilizer was set as available nutrients and 30% as controlled-release nutrients to ensure the continuous supply capacity of fertilizer nutrients during the whole growth period of banana15,29. The purpose of conventional fertilizer is to provide nutrients quickly and meet the short-term growth and development of banana. Controlled-release fertilizer aims to continuously provide nutrients, reduce nutrient loss, and meet the long-term growth and development of bananas. It aims to maximize the consistency of fertilizer nutrient release characteristics and banana nutrient absorption characteristics in the time dimension through reasonable ratio, in order to maximize fertilizer utilization efficiency30–32. In this study, this fertilization scheme based on banana nutrient absorption characteristics and fertilizer nutrient release characteristics, which can meet the dynamic balance of banana nutrient supply and demand, is called synchronized fertilization (Fig. S7).

The field application effect test of synchronized fertilization showed that the accumulation of N, P2O5 and K2O in the treatment of synchronized fertilization was not significantly different from that of conventional fertilizer in the middle and early stages of banana growth and development (seedling stage and vegetative growth stage), and a small amount or even significantly lower than that of conventional fertilizer treatment. However, in the middle and late stages of banana growth and development (flower bud differentiation stage and mature stage), the accumulation of N, P2O5 and K2O in the synchronized fertilization treatment was significantly higher than that in the conventional fertilizer treatment. Analysis reveals two reasons for the initially low but subsequently high nutrient absorption in the synchronized fertilization treatment. First, conventional fertilizers consist entirely of readily available nutrients, which rapidly increase the soil’s available nutrient content upon application, enabling banana plants to absorb nutrients quickly and produce short-term growth effects. However, as the growth period extends, conventional readily available nutrients are more prone to loss compared to controlled-release nutrients. In contrast, the controlled-release nutrients in the synchronized fertilization effectively mitigate this drawback, as their slow-release mechanism provides sustained nutrition to bananas over an extended period. This results in the synchronized fertilization treatment surpassing conventional fertilizer in nutrient accumulation during the mid-to-late growth stages, ultimately achieving significantly higher levels33–35. These results are consistent with current findings on controlled-release fertilizers. Research indicates that controlled-release fertilizers significantly enhance crop nutrient uptake efficiency and yield by increasing soil available nutrient content and improving soil pH, as evidenced in maize, rice, and wheat36–38. Secondly, although conventional fertilizers have higher total nutrient content, their nutrient distribution is imbalanced, with disproportionately high ratios of N and P2O5 and a notably low ratio of K2O. This likely results in insufficient potassium supply for bananas. Current studies confirm that bananas are potassium-demanding crops with high K2O requirements39. Therefore, an unscientific nutrient ratio may induce the “barrel effect,” impairing nutrient absorption and accumulation, ultimately reducing nutrient use efficiency, yield, and quality40. Zang et al.6 research on the scientific ratio of nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium also proved that the scientific ratio can significantly improve the yield of banana. Furthermore, Gao Liu’s research revealed that biogas slurry-based formulated fertilizer not only improves banana yield and quality but also significantly increases soil alkali-hydrolyzable nitrogen, available phosphorus, and rapid-acting potassium content, achieving dual benefits of boosting banana production and enhancing soil properties in banana plantations41. In summary, the synchronized fertilization developed in this study, which integrates banana nutrient requirements and fertilizer nutrient release characteristics, maximizes the fulfillment of banana nutrient demands. While reducing labor input for fertilization, it significantly improves nutrient uptake efficiency, indirectly minimizing fertilizer waste and offering substantial environmental benefits by mitigating the negative effects of fertilization42,43,.

The formation of banana yield is strongly influenced by the nutritional status of plants. Generally, the better the nutritional status of plants, the more vigorous the growth and development of bananas, the stronger their photosynthesis, and the higher the yield44. For example, potassium can enhance photosynthesis in bananas and promote the formation and accumulation of sugars and vitamin C. Phosphorus is an important component of various intracellular functional substances such as nucleic acids and phospholipids, participates in plant photosynthesis and respiration, and is a key medium element for energy conversion in plants. Nitrogen is a basic constituent of biological macromolecules such as proteins, nucleic acids, chlorophyll, plant hormones, and vitamins, and can directly participate in the synthesis of nutrients, exerting a significant effect on the accumulation of nutrients and quality in bananas45. A large number of studies have shown that an appropriate nutrient ratio and adequate nutrient supply can improve crop yield and quality46. As a fertilization scheme with both scientific nutrient ratio and sustained nutrient supply, synchronized fertilization can maximize the dynamic balance of nutrients in the fertilizer-soil-crop system through its scientific proportioning and slow nutrient release characteristics, providing a rich nutrient pool for root absorption through osmosis and active uptake47. The quick-acting nutrients applied to the soil can rapidly increase the nutrient content in the soil, timely supplementing nutrients for banana growth and development. However, during the long growth period, soil nutrients are gradually consumed, leading to a significant reduction in the content of nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium available for crop absorption. At this time, the slow-release nutrients in controlled-release fertilizers can be slowly released through pores in the special membrane materials wrapping them, continuously replenishing the soil and maintaining soil nutrients at a relatively high level, thus ensuring the nutrient demand for banana growth and development48. Previous studies have shown that abundant soil nutrients can promote stronger plant growth, enhance photosynthesis, and increase organic matter accumulation49,50. This is basically consistent with the results of this study. Correlation analysis revealed that banana yield and biomass were significantly positively correlated with nutrient accumulation and absorption efficiency. This indicates that synchronized fertilization can significantly improve the nutritional status of banana plants; the improved nutritional status further increases the production and accumulation of nutrients, ultimately leading to a significant increase in banana yield and biomass, thereby achieving higher yields45.

Conclusion

The synchronized fertilization, designed based on banana nutrient uptake characteristics and fertilizer nutrient release properties, effectively balances banana nutrient supply and demand, enhances nutrient uptake efficiency, reduces fertilizer waste, and significantly improves the nutrient status of banana plants, leading to higher yields and mitigating the environmental impact of fertilization. In practical applications, its high efficiency and long-lasting effects reduce the frequency and quantity of fertilization, significantly saving labor, time, and economic costs, thereby enhancing the productivity and economic benefits for banana farmers. In summary, the synchronized fertilization is an excellent fertilization method with dual economic and environmental benefits, offering promising prospects for the green and sustainable development of the banana industry.

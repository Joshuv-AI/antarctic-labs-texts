Risk factors and prediction model for massive transfusion during cesarean section in singleton pregnancies with anterior placenta previa, prior caesarean and prenatal suspicion of placenta accreta spectrum: a retrospective case-control study
Authors: Pin Cao, Lu Ji, Shuo Zhang, Chong Qiao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSevere postpartum hemorrhage is frequently encountered during cesarean sections (CSs) in individuals with placenta accreta spectrum (PAS). The prompt and adequate administration of blood transfusions has emerged as a critical intervention in managing severe postpartum hemorrhage. Considering that anterior placenta previa (APP) and a prior CS constitute key risk factors for PAS, this investigation aims to investigate these risk factors and develop a prediction model for massive transfusion (MT) during CS in cases of singleton pregnancies with APP, a prior CS, and prenatal suspicion of PAS, utilizing the placenta accreta spectrum ultrasound scoring system (PASUSS).MethodsA cohort of 430 individuals with APP, having undergone prior CS, and with prenatal suspicion of PAS, as determined by PASUSS, were retrospectively examined at Shengjing Hospital, affiliated with China Medical University, between January 2018 and December 2021. These patients were divided into cohorts of MT (168 cases) and non-MT (262 cases) according to the volume of packed red blood cells transfused intraoperatively. The cohort was arbitrarily subdivided into training and validation cohorts in a 7:3 proportion. LASSO and multivariate logistic regression analyses were employed to ascertain independent risk factors for MT. A prediction model was developed, and its predictive efficacy was evaluated through the use of receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, calibration curves, and decision curve analysis (DCA).ResultsOut of the 430 patients, 168 underwent MT during CS, reflecting an incidence rate of 39.07%. Independent risk factors for MT in cases of singleton pregnancies with APP, prior CS, and prenatal suspicion of PAS included emergency surgery, PASUSS score, preoperative hemoglobin level, clinical staging of PAS, and one-step conservative surgery. A nomogram was subsequently developed utilizing these identified factors. The areas under the ROC curves for the training and validation cohorts were 0.908 (0.875–0.940) and 0.925 (0.882–0.968), respectively. Both calibration curves and DCA demonstrated that this nomogram possessed a strong predictive value.ConclusionsIndependent risk factors for MT included emergency surgery, PASUSS score, preoperative hemoglobin level, clinical staging of PAS, and one-step conservative surgery. The nomogram constructed from these variables serves as an effective prediction model for identifying MT in singleton pregnancies characterized by APP, prior CS, and prenatal suspicion of PAS.

Background

The rising rate of cesarean deliveries, along with the implementation of China’s three-child policy, has led to an annual elevate in the occurrence of placenta accreta spectrum (PAS). Individuals affected by PAS frequently experience severe postpartum hemorrhage [1] with blood loss reaching as much as 10,000 ml [2] and severe postpartum hemorrhage remains the primary cause of maternal mortality [3]. In instances of postpartum hemorrhage, achieving prompt and effective hemostasis is essential. Moreover, the rapid and sufficient transfusion of packed red blood cells serves a pivotal function in enhancing tissue oxygenation, addressing or reversing coagulation disorders, and ensuring hemodynamic stability. Therefore, timely and adequate blood transfusion has emerged as a critical intervention in managing severe postpartum hemorrhage [4].

Patients with PAS are classified as being at high risk for massive transfusion (MT) [5]. Since the diagnosis of PAS depends on intraoperative and postoperative pathology, any misjudgment may result in inadequate preoperative blood preparation or delays in timely blood transfusion during surgery, potentially leading to adverse pregnancy outcomes or even maternal death. Anterior placenta previa (APP) and a history of cesarean section (CS) are recognized as the main risk factors for PAS [6] while the preoperative diagnosis of PAS predominantly relies on ultrasound examination. The placenta accreta spectrum ultrasound scoring system (PASUSS) is the most commonly used tool in our hospital and across China for the preoperative evaluation of placental accreta. Therefore, this study focuses on singleton pregnancies with APP, prior CS, and prenatal suspicion of PAS based on the PASUSS score. The aim is to investigate the risk factors for MT during CS by analyzing demographic characteristics, ultrasound findings, and intraoperative conditions. It is also to develop a nomogram to guide clinical practice and reduce the incidence of adverse outcomes.

Method

Study subjects

Patient data for those diagnosed with APP, prior CS, and prenatal suspicion of PAS based on PASUSS scores, who delivered at Shengjing Hospital, affiliated with China Medical University, between January 2018 and December 2021, were retrospectively collected. The inclusion criteria encompassed a preoperative ultrasound diagnosis of APP, prior CS, a PASUSS score of ≥ 3 points, gestational week at delivery of ≥ 28 weeks, singleton pregnancy, CS delivery, and live birth. The exclusion criteria included twin or multiple pregnancies, intrauterine fetal death, non-delivery, cesarean deliveries performed at other hospitals, pregnancies complicated by hematologic or autoimmune diseases, preoperative fever, maternal organ dysfunction, malignant tumors, uterine fibroids or ovarian cysts, or missing PASUSS scores or incomplete data. Following the application of these rigorous inclusion and exclusion standards, a final cohort of 430 participants was identified. The process of patient selection is illustrated in Fig. 1.

Research indicators

The medical center’s electronic records system was employed to gather patients’ demographic and clinical data. The demographic data included age, gravidity, parity, CS count, history of myomectomy, and count of intrauterine procedures. Regarding pregnancy characteristics, the study encompassed factors like conception approach, occurrence of bleeding during pregnancy, hypertensive disorders during pregnancy, diabetes. Activated partial thromboplastin time, prothrombin time, fibrinogen, D-dimer, and hemoglobin levels were recorded within 72 h pre-surgery; the amniotic fluid index and PASUSS score were recorded within one week before surgery; all of the above data were derived from the latest preoperative assessments. Delivery-related characteristics included fetal presentation, gestational week at termination, anesthesia method, surgery timing, operation duration, PAS clinical staging, abdominal aortic balloon placement, uterine artery ligation, uterine tamponade, one-step conservative surgery, hysterectomy, perioperative packed red blood cell transfusion, and neonatal birth weight. Maternal outcomes assessed were blood loss within 24 h post-operation, postoperative body temperature, ICU admission, duration of postoperative hospitalization, and hospitalization costs.

Related definitions

MT is characterized as the administration of ≥ 3 units of packed red blood cells within one hour or ≥ 10 units within a 24-hour period [5, 7]. Given the relatively brief duration of CS surgeries and the fact that transfusions predominantly occur during the procedure in PAS cases, MT during CSs in this study is characterized as the administration of ≥ 3 units of packed red blood cells within one hour. PAS clinical staging follows the FIGO criteria [8]. In this research, non-PAS and Grade 1 are grouped as non-invasive PAS, whereas Grade 2 and Grade 3 are classified under invasive PAS. PASUSS, a preoperative ultrasound scoring system developed by Peking University Third Hospital in 2016, encompasses 9 criteria, each scored from 0 to 2 (Table 1). The cumulative score reflects the PAS severity, with a total of 3 or more points indicating accreta, increta, or percreta [9]. The one-step conservative surgery technique involves excision of the uterine segment affected by placental invasion without removal of the placenta itself, followed by anatomical reconstruction [10]. Severe postpartum hemorrhage, as per ROCG guidelines, is defined by blood loss exceeding 2,000 ml within 24 h post-surgery [11]; postpartum hemorrhage is defined by blood loss ≥ 1,000 ml within 24 h post-surgery. Preoperative anemia is classified according to WHO guidelines, with hemoglobin levels below 110 g/L preoperatively considered as anemia [12].

Perioperative management

When conditions permit, hemoglobin levels should be increased to 80 g/L as much as possible before surgery. Consequently, blood transfusions were administered to two patients preoperatively to correct anemia. However, in this study, five patients still had preoperative hemoglobin levels below 80 g/L. Among these, three patients had a preoperative hemoglobin level of 78 g/L, one patient was admitted due to massive hemorrhage and underwent emergency surgery upon admission, and another patient did not receive a blood transfusion preoperatively due to other factors, with a hemoglobin level of 68 g/L. In our study, preoperative hemoglobin was the hemoglobin level of the last preoperative examination rather than admission hemoglobin levels and categorized as follows: ≥110 g/L, 100–109 g/L, 90–99 g/L, and ≤ 89 g/L. A multidisciplinary team, led by senior obstetricians, formulates surgical plans, and performs the operations. The surgeons were experienced in perioperative PAS management and capable of efficiently managing intraoperative emergencies. Efforts were made to preserve the uterus; however, in cases of uncontrollable hemorrhage, a hysterectomy was performed without delay.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was executed utilizing R software (version 4.2.2). The dataset was randomly split into training and validation cohorts at a 7:3 ratio for comparison. Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range), while categorical data are shown as percentages. For categorical variables, chi-square tests were conducted, and t-tests or rank-sum tests were utilized for continuous variables. Independent risk factors in the training cohort were identified through LASSO and multivariate logistic regression, which were also employed to construct a nomogram prediction model for MT. The nomogram’s efficacy was evaluated through receiver operating characteristic (ROC) and calibration curves, with decision curve analysis (DCA) applied to evaluate the net clinical utility of the prediction model. A P-value below 0.05 was deemed statistical significance.

Results

Impact of MT in singleton pregnancies with APP, prior CS and prenatal suspicion of PAS

A sum of 430 individuals fulfilled the stringent inclusion and exclusion standards and were incorporated into the investigation, with 168 requiring MT during CS, resulting in an incidence rate of 39.07%. Among these patients, 48 received 8 or more units of packed red blood cells, while 23 were transfused with 10 or more units, with the maximum transfusion reaching 19.5 units during surgery. Notably, there were no cases of secondary laparotomy or in situ placental retention. Furthermore, 26 patients underwent abdominal aortic balloon placement, and 9 underwent hysterectomy, all of which occurred within the MT cohort. The MT cohort exhibited markedly higher rates of intrauterine tamponade, uterine artery ligation, one-step conservative surgery, invasive PAS, postpartum hemorrhage, severe postpartum hemorrhage, longer operation duration, ICU admission, postoperative fever (temperature ≥ 38 °C), extended postoperative hospitalization, and increased maternal hospitalization costs compared to the non-MT cohort (Table 2).

Selection of risk factors for MT during CS

All candidate factors were incorporated into the study. In the training cohort, eight potential predictive factors were identified through LASSO regression analysis, with the LASSO coefficient path and cross-validation plots displayed in Fig. 2. Subsequent multivariate logistic analyses determined that emergency surgery, PASUSS score, preoperative hemoglobin level, clinical staging of PAS, and one-step conservative surgery were independent risk factors for MT in singleton pregnancies involving APP, prior CS, and prenatal suspicion of PAS (Table 4). ROC curve analysis was conducted for the final five factors, with the PASUSS score showing the largest area under the curve (0.834) (Fig. 3).

Construction of a nomogram for MT during CS

A nomogram model was developed utilizing the findings from the regression analysis, incorporating five key variables: emergency surgery, PASUSS score, preoperative hemoglobin level, clinical staging of PAS, and one-step conservative surgery. Each variable was assigned a score, which was then summed to evaluate the likelihood of MT during CS in cases of singleton pregnancies with APP, prior CS, and prenatal suspicion of PAS (Fig. 4).

Diagnostic performance analysis of the nomogram model

The areas under the ROC curve (AUC) for the training and validation cohorts were 0.908 (0.875–0.940) and 0.925 (0.882–0.968), respectively (Fig. 5). Calibration curves (Fig. 6) and DCA plots (Fig. 7) were generated for both cohorts, indicating that the predicted probabilities from the nomogram closely corresponded to the observed outcomes. The developed model exhibited strong reliability and yielded significant net benefits for clinical practice.

Discussion

The incidence of MT during CS was 39.07% in cases of singleton pregnancies with APP, previous CS, and prenatal suspicion of PAS based on the PASUSS score. Further statistical analyses identified emergency surgery, PASUSS score, preoperative hemoglobin level, clinical staging of PAS, and one-step conservative surgery as independent risk factors for MT during CS. The nomogram developed based on these factors allows clinicians to recognize patients at high-risk of MT, enabling proper blood preparation and timely transfusions to reduce the likelihood of adverse outcomes.

Ultrasonography has emerged as the preferred approach for the preoperative diagnosis of PAS. In China, the most commonly employed ultrasound scoring system for PAS is PASUSS, introduced by Peking University Third Hospital in 2016. This system assesses the invasion of trophoblast cells, vascular remodeling, and the extent of involvement of adjacent organs [13]. Previous research has shown that elevated PASUSS scores are linked to a greater risk of placental accreta, massive hemorrhage, MT, and hysterectomy [9, 14]. Nevertheless, PASUSS scores may be affected by individual variations and operator-related factors, resulting in a certain level of subjectivity. Relying solely on PASUSS can introduce bias, particularly in differentiating invasive PAS from simple scar dehiscence, potentially leading to unnecessary resource use or interventions. Hence, it is crucial to construct a more dependable predictive model. This study integrates basic demographic information, ultrasound findings, laboratory results, and intraoperative details, quantifying these factors to establish a nomogram model. The accuracy of predicting MT during CS is markedly improved when compared to relying solely on five independent factors, thereby demonstrating strong diagnostic performance (Fig. 3).

Anemia is recognized as the most common nutritional deficiency among pregnant women [15]. Preoperative hemoglobin level has been identified as an independent risk factor for MT during CS in singleton pregnancies complicated by APP, previous CS, and prenatal suspicion of PAS. Research indicates that in anemic patients, placental volume and angiogenesis increase proportionally to maintain sufficient oxygen and nutrient supply to the fetus, ensuring its growth and development [16]. Additionally, anemia reduces the availability of hemoglobin and oxygen to the uterine muscles, impairing uterine contractions and potentially contributing to postpartum hemorrhage [4, 17]. Correcting anemia has been shown to diminish the occurrence of postpartum hemorrhage [18]. Due to the increased angiogenesis in anemic individuals with APP and a history of CS, as well as the placental attachment to the lower uterine segment, the muscle strength of the lower segment is relatively weakened. This weakness may result in greater intraoperative blood loss and an elevated risk of MT during CS. And severity of anemia is closely linked to perioperative bleeding and the necessity for blood transfusion [19]. However, we found that when the preoperative hemoglobin level was 90–99 g/L, the risk of MT was lower than that at 100–109 g/L. This might be attributed to the fact that as preoperative hemoglobin levels decreased, greater attention was given to intraoperative bleeding, and more aggressive hemostatic measures were implemented.

For patients with stable PAS and placenta previa, elective surgery between 34 and 37 weeks is recommended [20]. In this study, 33 patients required emergency surgery, primarily due to antepartum hemorrhage, onset of labor, or suspected fetal distress. It was observed that emergency surgery, when compared to elective surgery, markedly heightened the risk of MT during CS. However, pregnancy termination before 34 weeks did not correlate with a higher incidence of MT, which is consistent with findings from previous studies [21]. Previous research centered on PAS patients who underwent hysterectomy, defining MT as the transfusion of ≥ 10 units of packed red blood cells within a 24-hour period. Our study encompassed a broader cohort and emphasized the rate of transfusion. At our hospital, surgical management of prenatally suspected PAS cases is conducted by a multidisciplinary team led by senior obstetricians, ensuring comprehensive evaluation and preoperative planning, which markedly lowers the risk of MT. Nevertheless, emergency surgeries, preoperative hemorrhage, and insufficient preoperative preparation can contribute to greater intraoperative blood loss, thus elevating the likelihood of MT.

It was observed in this study that both abdominal aortic balloon placement and hysterectomy were carried out in the MT cohort. Patients who underwent abdominal aortic balloon placement at our hospital had been preoperatively assessed as being at exceptionally high risk for significant bleeding. Due to the ongoing debate surrounding the effectiveness of abdominal aortic balloon placement in minimizing intraoperative bleeding [22, 23] its clinical use at our hospital has been progressively reduced in recent years. Nonetheless, hysterectomy remains the ultimate hemostatic intervention to preserve life in cases of severe hemorrhage. The clinical staging of invasive PAS was found to be a risk factor for MT during CS, consistent with previous research [24]. Invasive PAS is frequently associated with extensive vascular hyperplasia in the periplacental tissue, where abnormal blood vessel proliferation contributes to increased intraoperative bleeding, thereby escalating the risk of MT.

This study revealed that intraoperative uterine artery ligation and one-step conservative surgery were primarily employed for invasive PAS cases. Clinically, rapid hemorrhage related to invasive PAS typically occurs in two phases: initially, during uterine incision for fetal delivery, which is frequently accompanied by rupture of blood vessels at the site of placenta accreta, resulting in MT prior to the implementation of hemostatic measures. The second phase occurs during placental dissection, where a considerable number of blood vessels in the uterine wall at the site of placenta accreta become irritated, leading to significant bleeding. To minimize blood loss, uterine artery ligation was performed before placental dissection. This study consequently confirmed that intraoperative uterine artery ligation did not correlate with the incidence of MT. Moreover, one-step conservative surgery was ascertained as an independent risk factor for MT in our findings. This procedure involves directly excising the uterine wall at the site of the placenta accreta without removing the placenta itself. A 2022 study from Argentina reported that this method reduced intraoperative bleeding and lowered hysterectomy rates in patients with invasive PAS [25] seemingly contradicting our findings. The discrepancy likely arises from differences in outcome measures between the studies, as well as the yet-unexplored relationship between one-step conservative surgery and specific blood transfusion volumes under comparable accreta conditions. Future research will focus on refining grouping methods and further exploring the relationship between one-step conservative surgery and transfusion volume.

Strengths and limitations

A notable advantage of this research is its characterization of MT, which is based on transfusion speed. This approach more accurately captures the rate of bleeding and the immediate transfusion needs compared to relying solely on the total transfusion volume over 24 h. This study is a single-center study; however, as it utilized data from a large tertiary hospital—where most individuals with APP, prior CSs, and prenatal suspicion of PAS in northeast China delivered—we believe the findings have a reasonable degree of generalizability. The PASUSS scores are performed by two ultrasound physicians who are skilled in this technique in our hospital; which can help reduce the bias caused by human factors. Nevertheless, some limitations must be acknowledged. First, as a retrospective analysis, the clinical staging of PAS may have been influenced by human judgment, potentially introducing bias. Second, the PASUSS score was analyzed in aggregate rather than examining its individual components in detail, and not all elements of the score may have a direct causal relationship with the incidence of MT.

Conclusions

Emergency surgery, PASUSS score, preoperative hemoglobin levels, clinical staging of PAS, and one-step conservative surgery were ascertained as independent risk factors for MT during CS in singleton pregnancies complicated by APP, previous CS, and prenatal suspicion of PAS. The nomogram, developed based on demographic characteristics, ultrasound findings, and intraoperative conditions, can aid clinicians in promptly recognizing high-risk MT cohorts during CS. This enables timely administration of packed red blood cell transfusions during surgery, thereby reducing the risk of adverse outcomes.

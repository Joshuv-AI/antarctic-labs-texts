AJPC 2025 in review: A year of growth & clearer mandate for actionable prevention
Authors: Khurram Nasir, Izza Shahid
Published: 2026

For the American Journal of Preventive Cardiology (AJPC), 2025 was a year of continued scientific progress, impact, and reaffirmation of the journal’s core mission. As the official journal of the American Society for Preventive Cardiology (ASPC), we are committed to the advancing rigorous and clinically interpretable prevention science, striving to move cardiovascular care upstream from the current reactive management of advanced disease to prevention of cardiovascular risk among diverse populations across the globe.

Against this backdrop of momentum, 2025 also marked an important transition in the journal’s leadership profile. At the conclusion of their transformative tenures, our outgoing Co-Editors-in-Chief, Dr. Erin D. Michos, and Dr. Nathan D. Wong, formally passed on the baton [1] Their strategic vision and commitment has significantly strengthened AJPC’s foundation and positioned the journal for its next phase. It is a distinct honor for us to build upon that foundation, as AJPC continues to elevate prevention scholarship even further with global relevance and real-world impact.

The scientific community’s trust in AJPC is reflected in our rapidly accelerating metrics. In 2025, we published 252 articles, spanning rigorous original research, invited commentaries, and state-of-the-art reviews. This represents an 90% increase over 2024, a trajectory that appears to be gaining even more momentum into 2026. The quality and clinical importance of the science we publish are further evidenced by our rising Journal Impact Factor of 5.9. Our international footprint has expanded commensurately; currently, 43% of our submissions originate from outside North America. Together, these indicators underscore AJPC’s trajectory toward becoming a leading global destination for high-volume, open-access preventive cardiology research.

The breadth of the articles published in AJPC in 2025 reflects a multi-disciplinary evolution in our field [2] Among our last year portfolio, few themes were most noticeable. In 2025, perhaps the most consistent theme published was the consolidation of cardio-kidney-metabolic (CKM) as the central organizing framework for preventive cardiology, emphasizing in how we should reframe prevention pathways so that obesity, glycemic disorders, CKD, and metabolic liver disease are addressed as upstream drivers of lifetime ASCVD risk [[3], [4], [5], [6], [7], [8]] This is a major shift as historically preventive cardiology has been heavily lipid-centric. Our 2025 lipid portfolio also showed evolution, where now the predominate emphasis was on highlighting the barriers and optimizing care pathways, improving detection, intensifying strategies beyond selection and adherence, considering wealth of therapeutic options [[9], [10], [11], [12], [13], [14], [15]]

Beyond this, as prevention community is transitioning to the new AHA PREVENT equation and Life essential 8, AJPC provided the much rigorous, independent validation of the new risk calculator and its comparison against traditional models and imaging [[16], [17], [18], [19], [20], [21], [22]] Also, equally central to our 2025 mission was recognition that innovation without equity is a failure of our mandate and climate crisis is a cardiovascular crisis. Important insights childhood adverse conditions, psychological stressors, and climate fluctuations are independent, primary drivers of CV morbidity and mortality [[23], [24], [25], [26], [27]] This is a sobering reminder that achieving true global health requires overcoming these systemic barriers to reach the most vulnerable populations. AJPC in 2025 also continued to strengthen women’s CV prevention as a distinct, life-course domain. The unifying prevention message is that women’s cardiovascular risk is not episodic; it is cumulative and shaped by reproductive transitions and pregnancy-related exposures that can serve as early signals of future risk [[28], [29], [30], [31]]

In 2025, we also started seeing more digital health and AI papers, reflecting a pragmatic evolution that this field is moving beyond theoretical hype, but there appears to a genuine enthusiasm on how they can realize real-world benefit [32,33] Finally, our 2025 focus on implementation science and health economics showed us the way that just having transformative therapies is insufficient if systemic bottlenecks prevent their delivery. By raising issues such as payer rejections, economic modeling the of preventive care pathways, and championing nontraditional care models, AJPC is actively participating in shaping the future of where science is successfully translated into practice [[34], [35], [36], [37], [38]]

Among the most widely read and cited contributions of 2025 were articles that captured emerging areas in cardiometabolic prevention and translated evolving science into clinically actionable frameworks. Two of these high-impact publications highlighted the growing importance of lipoprotein(a) as a major residual risk factor, offering practical guidance on screening, risk stratification, and management strategies at a time when targeted therapies are approaching clinical translation, while also advancing understanding of sex-specific risk of Lp(a) trajectories across the life course in women, emphasizing how hormonal transitions and life-stage biology may influence risk detection and preventive strategies [39,40] Beyond lipid biology, highly accessed articles also addressed the broader cardiometabolic ecosystem, from meta-analyses demonstrating the cardiovascular and renal benefits of GLP-1 receptor agonists across diverse patient populations to top risk factors of cardiovascular disease, and clinical frameworks outlining comprehensive prevention strategies for high-risk groups such as South Asians [[41], [42], [43]] In addition to readership performance, the most cited contributions in 2025 included the ASPC clinical practice statement on ASCVD risk assessment, which provided a contemporary framework for integrating global risk scores, risk enhancers, sex- and ethnicity-specific considerations, and subclinical atherosclerosis into individualized prevention strategies [44] This was followed by a comprehensive review on glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists synthesizing emerging evidence on their cardiovascular and renal protective effects in patients with diabetes and chronic kidney disease [45] Complementing these therapeutic advances, a widely cited population analysis using the AHA Life’s Essential 8 framework highlighted persistently suboptimal cardiovascular health among young adults and important disparities by sex and race/ethnicity [46]

In short, these contributions underscore that prevention research is becoming a more multidimensional enterprise that seeks to unite biology and behavior, therapeutics and delivery science, and risk profile and the circumstances that create and perpetuate risk. In this regard, AJPC’s special contribution is not only to curate this breadth of science, but also ensuring clarity of purpose, and highlighting work that seeks to close the evidence to implementation gap.

Reflecting on this amazing progress in 2025, I feel this is the product of a broad community effort. For this, I would like to express my gratitude to the ASPC leadership for their unwavering support in aligning operations and resources for our joint mission. We are also very pleased to welcome our new Associate Editors, who bring unprecedented knowledge and commitment to the journal. I am equally appreciative of our Elsevier managing, who work behind the scenes ensure that our publication is smooth and efficient, enabling us to produce top-tier content in a timely fashion. Most importantly, we recognize the investigators worldwide who have entrusted us at AJPC with their best science, and the millions study participants who made all this research possible; their collective commitment is the foundation of every advance the field achieves.

Building upon AJPC’s 2025 success, we hope to sharpen the journal’s scientific and clinical value in ways that should become increasingly visible in 2026. A central priority is to reinforce emphasis on actionability and practical implications. While discovery science, risk assessment, phenotype definitions, emerging interventions remain essential; our mission and credibility also require deep articulation of interpretive value, implications of the novelty, and the potential to impact practice. Our goal is that the future publications, will not only be methodologically strong, but also clinically interpretable and attentive to translation. I am sure if we can apply this discipline consistently, it will not only enhance our current standards of publications but can also support prevention practice and policy in ways that can meaningfully improve cardiovascular health on a global scale.(Table).

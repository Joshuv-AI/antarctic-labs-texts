Clinical, procedural and patient-reported outcomes of the Ultimaster Nagomi sirolimus-eluting coronary stent in complex percutaneous coronary intervention: the NAGOMI COMPLEX prospective cohort study protocol
Authors: Mamas Mamas, Goran Stankovic, Robert Albert Byrne, Bernard Chevalier, Adel Aminian
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPercutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) has evolved significantly over the past decade with approximately 30% of patients presenting with complex coronary artery disease, posing heightened procedural and long-term risks. Complexity in PCI is increasingly recognised as multifactorial, encompassing lesion, procedural and patient-related characteristics. The Ultimaster Nagomi sirolimus-eluting coronary stent system is designed to address the challenges of complex PCI. The NAGOMI COMPLEX study aims to assess its safety and clinical performance in a comprehensively defined complex PCI population, reflecting routine clinical practice.Methods and analysisThis is a prospective, multicentre, single-arm cohort study enrolling 3000 subjects undergoing complex PCI across 52 European sites. Eligible patients must meet at least one complex PCI criterion, such as multivessel PCI, bifurcation lesions, chronic total occlusions or long total stent length implantation (>60 mm). Patients will undergo PCI using the Ultimaster Nagomi sirolimus-eluting coronary stent system, with follow-up assessments conducted at 30 days, 6 months, 1 year and 2 years. Secondary endpoints include device success, lesion success, procedure success, rates of stent thrombosis, bleeding complications (Bleeding Academic Research Consortium (BARC 3–5)), patient-reported outcomes (EQ-5D-5L, Seattle Angina Questionnaire (SAQ-7)) and resource utilisation for health-economic analysis. Subgroup analyses will explore outcomes across clinical and procedural complexity criteria. Clinical events will be adjudicated by an independent Clinical Events Committee, while a Data Monitoring Committee will oversee safety.Ethics and disseminationThe study complies with the Declaration of Helsinki, EU MDR 2017/745, ISO 14155:2020, EU Regulation 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation), GCP and applicable national regulations. Ethics Committee approval has been obtained at all participating sites. Written informed consent is required from all subjects. Results will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications and scientific conferences.Trial registration numberNCT05705973.Keywords: Coronary heart disease, Coronary intervention, Follow-Up Studies, Patient Reported Outcome Measures, Treatment Outcome

Introduction

Percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) is the most common means of revascularisation of coronary artery disease in both the acute and elective setting.1 Over the past decade, advancements in techniques, imaging modalities, stent design, and adjunctive pharmacological management have significantly expanded patient eligibility and improved both acute and long-term clinical outcomes.2,5 This has meant that patients undergoing PCI are increasingly elderly, multimorbid and increasingly complex lesions are being treated. Around 30% of patients undergoing PCI present with complex coronary artery disease, which is associated with increased peri-procedural risks in the short and medium term.6 Complex PCI is defined by challenging anatomical and procedural features, although the definition of ‘complex PCI’ lacks universal consensus.7 Feldman et al define complex PCI by lesion-specific features, including bifurcations, left main coronary artery intervention, calcified lesions, In-Stent Restenosis (ISR), graft interventions and Chronic Total Occlusions (CTO).8 9 Other authors also consider procedural aspects to define complex PCI, such as the number of stents implanted and total stent length.10 11 These complex scenarios pose greater technical difficulties for interventional cardiologists and are associated with a higher risk of adverse ischaemic events, including periprocedural Myocardial Infarction (MI),11 and poorer long-term prognoses compared with interventions for simpler lesions.6 10 12 13

While lesion and procedural characteristics have traditionally defined complexity in PCI, it is increasingly recognised that true complexity extends beyond the anatomy and the lesion. Patient-specific factors significantly influence both procedural success and long-term outcomes in PCI.14,20 Demographic factors, such as advanced age and female sex,14 16 17 and comorbidities, such as chronic kidney disease,14 17 18 diabetes,17 malignancy19 20 and heart failure14 17 all contribute to elevated risk profiles. These factors not only increase susceptibility to ischaemic events but also increase bleeding risk, particularly in the context of antithrombotic therapy.21 22 The challenge becomes even more pronounced in High Bleeding Risk (HBR) patients, where the balance between preventing ischaemic risk and minimising haemorrhagic events is especially delicate.23

The Ultimaster Nagomi bioresorbable polymer sirolimus-eluting coronary stent system is an iteration of the established Ultimaster and Ultimaster Tansei sirolimus-eluting coronary stent systems. The Ultimaster Nagomi stent incorporates design enhancements to improve flexibility and deliverability, with an extended range of sizes and overexpansion capacities, making it well-suited for the treatment of anatomically complex coronary lesions. The NAGOMI COMPLEX study applies a comprehensive definition for complex PCI, considering lesion as well as procedural aspects of the intervention. This will allow for a greater understanding of outcomes in this population of patients in daily clinical practice. To our knowledge, this is the largest prospective study on complex PCI, applying this broad definition, to date. In doing so, this study aims to generate valuable insights on the safety and effectiveness of the new bioresorbable polymer sirolimus-eluting stent, highlighting its potential as a viable treatment for challenging patient populations. It also seeks to generate data on the characteristics and clinical outcomes of these complex cases, offering valuable insights to better understand and address their unique needs.

Methods and analysis

Study design

The NAGOMI COMPLEX study is a prospective, multicentre, single-arm, cohort study designed to evaluate the clinical outcomes, safety and performance of the Ultimaster Nagomi bioresorbable polymer sirolimus-eluting coronary stent system in subjects undergoing complex PCI in daily clinical practice. The study deliberately uses a non-comparative, non-interventional design to ensure the results obtained reflect routine clinical practice as much as possible.

The study is conducted in 52 clinical investigation sites across Europe (online supplemental appendix 1). These sites are experienced in performing complex interventional procedures and will adhere to current European Society of Cardiology and the European Association for Cardiothoracic Surgery (ESC/EACTS) or national guidelines for intervention and subsequent patient management. The selection of sites aims to ensure a representative sample of European practices for complex lesions. Overall study conduct is guided by a Steering Committee (online supplemental appendix 2). Study enrolment started in April 2023. The study is expected to be completed in May 2027.

Subjects will be followed up longitudinally for 2 years post-index procedure. Data collection will occur peri-procedurally, at discharge and at scheduled follow-up intervals (30 days, 6 months, 1 year and 2 years). For subjects in whom no Ultimaster Nagomi stent is implanted, or who do not meet the complex intervention inclusion criteria as ascertained after the index procedure, follow-up will conclude at hospital discharge. These subjects will be considered screening failures for the primary cohort, but their procedural data will be collected.

All eligible subjects will receive the Ultimaster Nagomi stent as per the intended use and physician’s discretion for all lesions requiring intervention during the index procedure and any staged procedures.

Study device

The investigational device for this post-market clinical follow-up study is the Ultimaster Nagomi sirolimus-eluting coronary stent system (Terumo Europe NV, Leuven, Belgium). This device is CE-Marked (2022) under EU Medical Device Regulation 2017/745 and will be used according to its Instructions for Use.

The Ultimaster Nagomi stent system is a L605 cobalt-chromium (Co-Cr) alloy stent with an abluminal biodegradable polymer coating of Poly(D,L-lactide-co-caprolactone). This coating elutes sirolimus, a cytostatic drug intended to prevent neointimal thickening and restenosis by inhibiting smooth muscle cell proliferation. The stent is pre-mounted on a rapid exchange delivery catheter. This catheter is designed for enhanced safety and accurate placement, facilitated by two radiopaque markers. It has a working length of 144 cm, a dual-lumen design for a 0.014” guidewire, and a hydrophilic coating for ease of use. The system is low profile, compatible with a minimum 5 Fr guiding catheter (figure 1). It is suitable for both femoral and radial access routes.

Schematic representation of Ultimaster Nagomi sirolimus eluting coronary stent system. PT, platinum.

The semi-compliant delivery balloon provides a high nominal pressure (11 atm) and rated burst pressure (16 atm), ensuring reliable stent expansion. The stent platform features 80 µm struts, designed to maximise flexibility, optimise conformability and minimise vessel wall stress. Its in-phase, open-cell, 2-link architecture facilitates side-branch access while ensuring uniform scaffolding for optimal support in bifurcation anatomy.

Ultimaster Nagomi is available in diameters from 2.0 mm to 4.5 mm and lengths from 9 mm to 50 mm. The different sizes allow the treatment of long lesions without having stent overlap, thereby reducing the risk of thrombosis.24,26 The wide stent length range enables precise treatment of both focal and diffuse disease with fewer overlaps and device exchanges, reducing procedure time, radiation and contrast use while minimising restenosis risk.

The stent has optimised overexpansion capacities. The large nominal stent diameters (Ø3.50 mm to Ø4.50 mm) can be overexpanded up to 6.25 mm. This allows a single stent to ensure great apposition even in large vessels—reducing the risk associated with overexpansion and optimise complex bifurcations or left main interventions. The small stent diameters (Ø2.00 mm to Ø2.50 mm) and medium stent diameters (Ø2.75 mm to Ø3.00 mm) can be overexpanded up to 3.50 mm and 4.50 mm respectively, to accommodate proximal–distal tapering.

The Ultimaster Nagomi stent is indicated for treating stenotic or occlusive lesions in coronary arteries across a broad range of clinical scenarios, in men and women, including (non-) ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction ((N)STEMI), acute coronary syndrome (ACS), elderly and diabetes patients, and various complex anatomical and procedural subsets such as multivessel disease, ostial lesions, long lesions, small vessels, left main stenting, bifurcation lesions, CTO and ISR. In addition, it allows the possibility of a 1 month post-procedural dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) regimen in HBR patients.23 27 Investigators will use devices from their commercial stock.

Study population

Subjects are prospectively recruited from participating sites. Subjects will be considered for screening if they have an indication for PCI according to current ESC/EACTS or national guidelines, and the treating physician determination that the Ultimaster Nagomi stent is suitable to treat the subject’s vessel and lesion anatomy.

After providing written informed consent, subjects will undergo screening to confirm general eligibility. Final enrolment requires the successful implantation of at least one Ultimaster Nagomi stent during the index procedure and post-procedural confirmation of at least one of the predefined complex PCI criteria.

Subjects must meet all general inclusion criteria, at least one complex PCI inclusion criterion, and none of the exclusion criteria to be enrolled and followed up in the study (box 1). The key exclusion criteria were carefully selected to ensure study integrity. Likely need to discontinue DAPT early (before recommended duration as per ESC/EACTS or national guidelines) is excluded as it represents a major risk factor for stent thrombosis and adverse cardiac events, particularly crucial when evaluating long-term stent safety in complex interventions. Known hypersensitivity to stent components or essential concomitant medications is excluded to ensure subject safety. Life expectancy less than 1 year is excluded to allow adequate follow-up duration for assessing primary and secondary endpoints at the 1-year and 2-year marks. Concurrent participation in other studies is prohibited to prevent confounding factors, ensuring that observed outcomes are attributable solely to this study’s intervention and procedures.

All subjects meeting the eligibility criteria will be planned to undergo intervention with at least one Ultimaster Nagomi stent.

The procedure itself, including vascular access route (femoral or radial), use of ancillary devices (eg, intravascular imaging, lesion preparation devices like scoring balloons, rotational atherectomy, intravascular lithotripsy or rotablation) and techniques for stent implantation (eg, use of one or two stents for bifurcation lesions, proximal optimisation technique), will be performed according to current local hospital routine practice and at the treating physician’s discretion. If implantation of the Ultimaster Nagomi stent fails, any other commercially available stent can be used. Peri-procedural and post-procedural management, including anti-platelet and anti-thrombotic medication, will follow ESC/EACTS or national guidelines and standard hospital practice.

Follow-up procedures will be conducted at 30 days, 6 months, 1 year and 2 years post-procedure, taking place either through in-person outpatient clinic visits or telephone consultations. During these follow-ups, clinicians will collect comprehensive information regarding the subject’s clinical status, any adverse events, anti-platelet/anti-thrombotic medication usage. Additionally, subjects will complete both the EQ-5D-5L and SAQ-7 questionnaires to assess quality of life and angina-related outcomes (table 1).

Safety outcomes

Safety will be assessed through the systematic collection and reporting of all study-critical adverse events (death, MI, revascularisations, stent thrombosis, bleeding (BARC 1–5) and stroke),28 device and procedure-related adverse events as well as device deficiencies throughout the study period, from informed consent until 2 years+30 days post index procedure.

Statistical methods

Sample size

The study aims to enrol 3000 subjects undergoing complex PCI across European sites. This sample size is based on achieving high statistical precision for the primary endpoint—TLF at 1 year. An observed TLF incidence of 5.6% at 1 year is assumed, based on a POCE of 10.6%,33 and a POCE to TLR ratio of 0.53.6 With 3000 subjects enrolled, the SE of the TLF estimate will be 0.43%. This will result in a two-sided 95% CI width of less than 2%. This sample size also allows for meaningful subgroup analyses across key patient and lesion subsets.

Statistical analysis

A detailed statistical analysis plan will be finalised prior to database lock. Descriptive and inferential statistics will summarise all study outcomes: continuous variables by number of observations, mean, SD, median, minimum, maximum and IQR; categorical variables as frequency tables with counts and percentages. Time-to-event variables will be analysed using Kaplan-Meier methods with two-sided 95% CIs and analysed as incidence rates with 95% CIs, calculated using the Wilson score method (for rates<5% or >95%) or exact Clopper-Pearson method otherwise. Missing data will generally not be imputed; a complete-case approach will be used for the primary analysis. All analyses will be conducted using SAS software V.9.434 or later. A significance level of p<0.05 (two-sided) will be used where applicable for pre-specified subgroup analyses, though the primary focus is on descriptive statistics and estimation.

Three analysis sets will be defined: (1) Screened Set: All potentially eligible subjects who signed an Informed Consent Form, meet general inclusion/exclusion criteria and underwent the Index Procedure. This set will be used for disposition summaries, deliverability endpoints and safety analyses. (2) Full Analysis Set: All subjects who signed consent, meet all inclusion/exclusion criteria and achieved delivery success of at least one Ultimaster Nagomi stent. This is the primary population for the primary endpoint and most secondary effectiveness endpoints. (3) Per Protocol Set: Full Analysis Set subjects without major protocol deviations. This set will be used for sensitivity analyses of the primary endpoint.

The primary endpoint (TLF at 1 year) will be estimated from the Full Analysis Set as incidence rates with corresponding two-sided 95% CIs (Clopper-Pearson if % are≥5% and ≤95%, Wilson score method otherwise), with sensitivity analysis on the Per Protocol Set. Secondary endpoints (eg, TLF at other timepoints, death, MI, etc) are also analysed as incidence rates with 95% CIs. Kaplan-Meier methods will be used for time-to-event endpoints to estimate rates at 30 days, 6 months, 1 year and 2 years. Procedural outcomes (device, lesion success, procedure and delivery success) will be expressed as number and percentage with 95% CIs. The balance between bleeding and thrombotic events will be assessed using the methodology by Urban et al.35 Patient-reported outcomes (EQ-5D-5L, Seattle Angina Questionnaire-7) will be summarised at each assessment and analysed as changes from baseline with 95% CIs. Resource utilisation will be reported as summary statistics of numbers of devices and products used during the index procedure.

Incidence rates between subgroups will be compared with 95% CIs with p values (χ2 and Fisher Exact tests).

Data management and safety monitoring

Data will be stored in an electronic data management system. Data quality and consistency will be monitored throughout the system by periodic monitoring visits and a final close-out visit. Clinical events related to primary and secondary endpoints will be adjudicated by an independent Clinical Events Committee (online supplemental appendix 2), based on pseudonymised source documents.

Subject safety is paramount throughout the study. All adverse events and device deficiencies will be monitored from informed consent until completion of the 2-year follow-up period (+30 days) and reviewed periodically by an independent Data Monitoring Committee (online supplemental appendix 2), who may provide recommendations regarding study conduct or modification.

Further details regarding data management and safety monitoring are described in online supplemental appendix 4.

Ethics and dissemination

Ethical framework

This clinical investigation will be conducted in compliance with the Declaration of Helsinki, the EU Medical Device Regulation 2017/745, the EU Regulation 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation), Good Clinical Practice as defined by ISO 14155:2020, along with all applicable regional and national regulations. Written informed consent will be obtained from each subject before any study-specific procedures or data collection. Prior to study commencement at each participating site, the Clinical Investigation Plan (V.1.0, dated 22DEC22, and any subsequent amendments), informed consent form, subject recruitment procedures and other subject-facing materials underwent review and were approved by the relevant independent Ethics Committee or Institutional Review Board (eg, the Wales Research Ethics Committee 3 Cardiff—REC reference 23/WA/0179—IRAS project ID 325214). The Sponsor will maintain a list of all approving committees (online supplemental appendix 5). Any protocol or consent form amendments will require Ethics Committee approval before implementation, unless necessary to eliminate an immediate hazard to study subjects.

Subject confidentiality

Each subject will be assigned a unique identification code for use on all electronic Case Report Forms and study-related documents. Personal information will be treated as confidential in accordance with data protection laws. Access to identifiable information will be restricted to authorised study personnel, monitors, auditors, committee members (in pseudonymised form) and regulatory authorities as required. All electronic data will be stored in secure, password-protected systems.

Publication and dissemination strategy

Dissemination will include (1) presentation at an appropriate international scientific forum, (2) publication in a peer-reviewed scientific journal, (3) preparation of a comprehensive clinical investigation report. Should the study terminate early, results will be made available for public review.

The study is registered in ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05705973) with results to be updated as they become available. De-identified individual participant data underlying the primary publication may be made available to qualified researchers on reasonable request after an appropriate period, subject to compliance with data privacy regulations.

Discussion

The NAGOMI COMPLEX prospective observational study represents a significant step forward in understanding the performance of the Ultimaster Nagomi sirolimus-eluting coronary stent in patients with complex PCI. By enrolling 3000 patients across 52 European centres, this study will generate the largest registry of comprehensively defined, complex PCI to date, applying a comprehensive definition of complex PCI, encompassing anatomical and procedural-related factors. While randomised controlled trials remain the gold standard for inferring causal inference, the insights from this study may be complementary, due to the inclusion of a larger and more diverse patient population.36

This broad and inclusive approach reflects the evolving nature of complexity in PCI, which is no longer limited to lesion morphology but increasingly influenced by patient comorbidities, procedural strategies and clinical context. The study’s design allows for granular subgroup analyses that will help disentangle how individual components of complexity, such as CTO, long stent lengths, bifurcation techniques or HBR status, impact clinical outcomes. These insights are crucial for refining risk stratification and tailoring interventional strategies in daily practice.

Furthermore, the study will not only evaluate clinical outcomes, but will also consider patients’ perspectives, by evaluating general and disease-specific health-related quality of life, using the EQ-5D and SAQ-7, respectively. These outcomes are increasingly gaining importance in cardiac research, as with better treatments improving life expectancy, maintaining quality of life is equally important.37 Inclusion of these outcomes will allow assessment of the true impact of the procedure on patients’ daily lives.

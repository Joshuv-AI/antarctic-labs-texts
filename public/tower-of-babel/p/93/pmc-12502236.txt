Integrated bioinformatics analysis identifies glutathione metabolism-related genes as diagnostic biomarkers for periodontitis
Authors: Leilei Meng, Haoshu Fang, Wenjie Wen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPeriodontitis affects over 1 billion people globally, with oxidative stress and glutathione depletion playing crucial roles in disease progression. This study aimed to identify glutathione metabolism-related genes as diagnostic biomarkers for periodontitis using integrated bioinformatics approaches.MethodsWeighted Gene Co-expression Network Analysis (WGCNA) was performed on independent datasets. Key genes were identified in combination with GSE16134 dataset differential expression analysis, which were then validated in the GSE10334 dataset. Immune infiltration analysis using CIBERSORT algorithm, consensus clustering for molecular subtyping, and competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) network construction were conducted to explore regulatory mechanisms. Drug-gene interaction analysis and molecular docking studies identified potential therapeutic compounds. Experimental validation was performed using ligature-induced periodontitis in rats.ResultsThree key glutathione metabolism-related genes were identified: GSTA4 and GGT6 (downregulated) and SLC7A11 (upregulated) in periodontitis patients. The diagnostic model achieved superior performance with AUC values exceeding 0.8. Two distinct molecular subtypes were identified based on immune infiltration patterns. ceRNA regulatory network analysis revealed complex interactions involving all three genes. Drug-gene interaction analysis revealed erastin as a potential therapeutic compound targeting SLC7A11. Experimental validation in rat periodontitis confirmed the upregulation of SLC7A11 in periodontal tissues.ConclusionsOur study identifies SLC7A11, GSTA4, and GGT6 as robust periodontitis biomarkers with excellent diagnostic accuracy. The continuous up-regulation and experimental verification of SLC7A11 genes have established them as promising therapeutic targets, laying the foundation for precision medicine in the field of periodontal therapy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12903-025-06858-7.Keywords: Periodontitis, Glutathione metabolism, Biomarkers, SLC7A11, Precision medicine

Introduction

Periodontitis is a chronic inflammatory disease affecting the supporting structures of teeth, characterized by progressive destruction of periodontal ligament, alveolar bone, and gingival tissues [1]. In 2021, over 1 billion people were affected by severe periodontitis [2], representing one of the most common oral diseases worldwide with significant impact on quality of life and systemic health [3, 4]. The disease progression involves complex interactions between pathogenic biofilms, host immune responses, and oxidative stress mechanisms, ultimately leading to alveolar bone loss and tooth loss [5]. Despite advances in periodontal therapy, the heterogeneous nature of periodontitis and individual variations in treatment response highlight the urgent need for precision medicine approaches based on molecular biomarkers and personalized therapeutic strategies.

Oxidative stress has emerged as a central mechanism in periodontal pathogenesis, with reactive oxygen species (ROS) production exceeding antioxidant defense capacity, resulting in tissue damage and perpetuation of inflammatory processes [6]. The glutathione system, comprising reduced glutathione (GSH), oxidized glutathione (GSSG), and associated enzymes, represents the primary cellular defense mechanism against oxidative damage [7]. Glutathione is one of the most important redox regulators. Periodontitis patients have reduced total antioxidant capacity in whole saliva, and lower concentrations of reduced GSH in serum and gingival crevicular fluid, and periodontal therapy restores the redox balance [8]. The antioxidant master glutathione and periodontal health. This depletion of glutathione has been consistently demonstrated across multiple biological fluids, establishing it as a hallmark of periodontal disease progression. Furthermore, peripheral blood neutrophils from periodontitis patients are compromised in their ability to cope with oxidative stress and move, with a decrease in glutathione and chemotactic ability being confirmed, establishing a direct link between glutathione depletion and impaired immune cell function [9]. Despite the recognized importance of glutathione metabolism in periodontal health, systematic identification of key glutathione metabolism-related genes as biomarkers for periodontitis diagnosis and prognosis remains limited. Previous studies have focused primarily on individual antioxidant markers or enzymatic activities, lacking comprehensive analysis of the glutathione metabolic network and its clinical applications.

In recent years, the integration of multi-omics data analysis has opened up new avenues for discovering disease biomarkers and understanding disease pathogenesis [10, 11]. With the rapid development of microarray screening and high-throughput sequencing, bioinformatics analysis carries great significance in exploring the mechanisms, diagnosis, and prediction of prognosis of periodontitis. The combined use of multiple biomarkers can significantly improve the accuracy of classification models compared to using individual markers [12]. These computational approaches, combined with immune infiltration analysis and molecular subtyping, provide comprehensive insights into the molecular landscape of periodontitis and facilitate the identification of therapeutic targets and potential drug interventions.

This study integrates multiple analytical methods to identify and validate glutathione metabolism-related genes as potential biomarkers for periodontitis. Key glutathione metabolism-related genes in periodontitis were analyzed by WGCNA analysis and differential expression analysis. Next, we developed a robust diagnostic model and also explored the association between these biomarkers and immune cell infiltration patterns. In addition, we screened potential therapeutic compounds for key gene SLC7A11. Finally, it was validated by animal models. Our findings may provide new insights into the pathogenesis of periodontitis and promote the development of precision medicine approaches for periodontal therapy.

Materials and methods

Data acquisition and preprocessing

We got gene expression datasets related to periodontitis from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database. We selected two independent datasets: GSE10334 and GSE16134. GSE10334 contained 247 samples (183 periodontitis patients and 64 healthy controls). GSE16134 included 310 samples (241 periodontitis patients and 69 healthy controls). We identified and corrected batch effects between datasets using the ComBat function from the sva package. We removed probes without corresponding gene symbols. When multiple probes mapped to the same gene, we kept the probe with the highest average expression. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were defined based on a |log2 fold change| >1 and a false discovery rate (FDR)-adjusted p-value < 0.05. We got glutathione metabolism-related genes from the MSigDB database. We initially identified a total of 57 glutathione metabolism-related genes (Supplementary Table 1).

WGCNA analysis

Gene co-expression modules associated with periodontitis were identified using the WGCNA package in R [13]. To build the co-expression network, we determined an appropriate soft-thresholding power (β). We selected the soft-thresholding power that achieved a scale-free topology fit index (R²) > 0.9. Network construction was based on the topological overlap matrix (TOM), and hierarchical clustering was performed using 1–TOM as the distance metric. Modules were defined through dynamic tree cutting with parameters: minimum module size = 50, deepSplit = 2, and merge cut height = 0.25. Finally, we calculated the correlation between these modules and diseases.

Immune infiltration analysis

We performed immune infiltration analysis using the CIBERSORT algorithm [14]. This algorithm uses linear support vector regression to estimate the relative proportions of 22 immune cell types from bulk tissue gene expression profiles [15]. The LM22 signature matrix was used as a reference. Only samples with a CIBERSORT output p-value < 0.05 were included in downstream analysis. Differences in immune cell proportions between patient and control groups were assessed using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Associations between glutathione metabolism gene expression and immune infiltration were evaluated using Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient.

Molecular subtyping and consensus clustering

We performed consensus clustering analysis on periodontitis samples to identify molecular subtypes based on the expression patterns of glutathione metabolism-related genes. We used the ConsensusCluster package in R. We determined the optimal number of clusters by evaluating the consensus cumulative distribution function and delta area plots.

ceRNA network construction

We built the ceRNA network to explore the regulatory relationships between long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs), microRNAs (miRNAs), and the identified glutathione metabolism-related genes. We predicted lncRNA-miRNA interactions using the spongeScan database. We got miRNA-mRNA interactions from the miRanda, miRDB, miRWalk and TargetScan databases. We visualized the ceRNA network using Cytoscape software [16].

Drug-Gene interaction analysis

We identified potential therapeutic compounds targeting the glutathione metabolism-related genes using the Drug Signatures Database (DSigDB) [17]. We performed molecular docking studies using AutoDock Vina to evaluate the binding strength between selected compounds and protein targets. We got protein structures for SLC7A11 (PDB ID:7epz) from the Protein Data Bank (PDB). We retrieved ligand structures from the PubChem database. Finally, the docking results were displayed with Pymol software.

Rat model of periodontitis

We got male Sprague-Dawley rats (250 g) from Hefei Qingyuan Biotechnology Co., Ltd (China). We kept them under standard conditions on a light/dark cycle for 12 h. After 7-day adjustment, we randomly assigned rats to control (n = 5) and periodontitis groups (n = 5). We placed silk ligatures (4 − 0) around maxillary second molars in the periodontitis group. After 4 weeks, rats were anesthetized with sodium pentobarbital (50 mg/kg, i.p.) until loss of pedal withdrawal reflex, followed by cervical dislocation. All procedures were approved by the Ethics Committee of Wannan Medical College (No: WNMC-AWE-2024013) and conducted according to ARRIVE guidelines.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

Gingival tissue specimens were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde and decalcified in 10% EDTA for four weeks. After embedding in paraffin, 5 μm sections were cut, deparaffinized, and subjected to antigen retrieval in citrate buffer (pH 6.0). Endogenous peroxidase activity was blocked with 5% normal goat serum. Sections were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibody against SLC7A11 (1:100, BOSTER). The following day, HRP-conjugated secondary antibody was applied, and signal detection was performed using DAB substrate [18].

Micro-CT analysis

We scanned fixed maxillae using Bruker SkyScan 1276 micro-CT system. We reconstructed images using NRecon software and analyzed with CTAn software. We measured jaw bone loss as the distance from cementoenamel junction (CEJ) to alveolar bone crest (ABC) at standardized sites around maxillary second molars [19].

Statistical analysis

All statistical evaluations were performed in R software (version 4.4.2) and visualized using GraphPad Prism (version 10.4). Correlation analyses were conducted using either Pearson’s or Spearman’s method. Statistical significance was defined as p < 0.05.

Results

WGCNA identifies periodontitis-associated modules

To identify gene co-expression modules associated with periodontitis and screen for potential biomarkers, we performed WGCNA analysis. First, we determined the optimal soft threshold for network construction (β = 6) through scale-free topology analysis (Fig. 1A-B). Next, based on the selected soft thresholds, we constructed a gene co-expression network and preliminarily identified the gene modules by a dynamic tree cutting algorithm. By setting the threshold for merging similar modules to 0.25, we ended up with 7 co-expression modules, labeled with different colors (Fig. 1C). To determine the functional modules most associated with periodontitis, we calculated the correlation between each module and the disease phenotype (Fig. 1D). The turquoise module exhibited high positive correlation with gene expression patterns (Fig. 1E and F).

WGCNA Analysis. (A) Scale-free topology analysis. (B) Network connectivity analysis. (C) Comparison before and after module merging. (D) Module-trait correlation heatmap. (E) Scatter plot of gene significance versus module membership in the turquoise module. (F) Three-dimensional scatter plot of genes in the turquoise module

Based on the above analysis, we selected candidate hub genes from the turquoise module for subsequent analysis. To further investigate the role of glutathione metabolism-related genes in periodontitis, we integrated multiple datasets for cross-analysis. As shown in Fig. 2A, we performed Venn diagram intersection analysis of differentially expressed genes from independent periodontitis transcriptome dataset (GSE16134), glutathione metabolism-related genes, and pivot genes from the turquoise module identified by WGCNA. The results showed that the intersection of the three datasets contained four glutathione metabolism-related genes: GSTA4, GGT6, SLC7A11, and CHAC1. To further confirm the genomic distribution of these four genes in the human genome, we constructed a chromosomal circular diagram (Fig. 2B) showing the localization of GSTA4, GGT6, SLC7A11, and CHAC1 on different chromosomes.

Diagnostic performance evaluation of glutathione metabolism-related genes

We evaluated the expression patterns and diagnostic value of these genes (GSTA4, GGT6, SLC7A11, and CHAC1) in the GSE16134 and GSE10334 datasets. In the GSE16134 dataset (Fig. 3A), GSTA4 and GGT6 were significantly downregulated in the periodontitis group (p < 0.05), while SLC7A11 and CHAC1 were significantly upregulated in the periodontitis group (p < 0.05). To assess the potential of these genes as diagnostic markers for periodontitis, we performed ROC curve analysis. The AUC value for GSTA4, GGT6, SLC7A11, and CHAC1 were 0.855, 0.837, 0.812, and 0.750, respectively. To validate the reliability of these findings, we performed the same analysis in the GSE10334 dataset (Fig. 3B). The results showed GSTA4 and GGT6 were significantly downregulated in periodontitis patients (p < 0.05), while SLC7A11 and CHAC1 were significantly upregulated (p < 0.05). In addition, these genes also demonstrated good diagnostic performance in GSE10334, with AUC values for GSTA4, GGT6, SLC7A11, and CHAC1 were of 0.850, 0.824, 0.807, and 0.745, respectively. Referring to established literature [20], an AUC value exceeding 0.8 is regarded as excellent. Given that GSTA4, GGT6, and SLC7A11 all achieved AUC values above 0.8 across both datasets, they were identified as key genes worthy of further investigation.

Construction of periodontitis diagnostic model

In order to further improve the diagnostic efficacy, we integrated these three genes to construct a risk prediction model for periodontitis. First, we constructed a visual nomogram for individualized risk assessment (Fig. 4A). In this nomograph, decreased expression levels of GSTA4 and GGT6 both increased disease risk, while elevated SLC7A11 expression levels also increased disease risk, consistent with previous differential expression analyses. To evaluate the diagnostic performance of this ensemble model, we performed an ROC curve analysis (Fig. 4B). The results showed that the model integrating three glutathione metabolism-related genes showed excellent diagnostic performance with an AUC value of 0.887. To further verify the reliability of the model, a calibration curve analysis was performed (Fig. 4C). The calibration curve results show that the predicted probability of the model is very close to the actual probability. Decision curve analysis (DCA) showed that patients with periodontitis had a significant net benefit, suggesting a clinical benefit (Fig. 4D).

Immune microenvironment characteristics analysis

To enhance the statistical power and improve the reliability of immune cell infiltration estimates, our immune infiltration analysis was performed on the combined datasets (GSE10334 and GSE16134). Given that CIBERSORT algorithm performance benefits from larger sample sizes for more accurate deconvolution of immune cell proportions, we merged the two independent datasets after initial data preprocessing and batch effect correction using the ComBat function from the sva package. This approach produced a more robust cohort for immune infiltration analysis using the CIBERSORT algorithm. The PCA plots before and after batch effect correction are shown in Supplementary Fig. 1A-B.

To investigate the immune microenvironment features of periodontitis and their potential associations with glutathione metabolism gene expression, we analyzed the infiltration patterns of 22 immune cell subsets using the CIBERSORT algorithm. As shown in Fig. 5A-B, periodontitis and control groups exhibited distinct immune cell compositions. Based on the comparative analysis, several immune cell subsets showed significant differences between groups, with most cells activated, neutrophils, dendritic cells activated, and plasma cells demonstrating notable alterations in periodontitis samples compared to controls. The correlation analysis between immune cell subsets (Fig. 5C) revealed complex interaction patterns among different immune cell populations, with various correlation coefficients ranging from strong negative to strong positive relationships. SLC7A11, GSTA4, and GGT6 are associated with different immune cell populations, respectively (Fig. 5D-F).

Molecular subtype identification

Next, we performed consensus clustering analysis on periodontitis samples. The consensus clustering matrix (Fig. 6A) demonstrated stable clustering relationships between samples. Analysis of consensus cumulative distribution function (CDF) curves for different k values (Fig. 6B) and the corresponding delta area changes (Fig. 6C) indicated that k = 2 was the optimal number of clusters, as evidenced by the plateau in relative improvement when k increased from 2 to higher values. Principal component analysis (Fig. 6D) confirmed the production of two isotypes, with 189 samples in cluster A and 235 samples in cluster B. Comparison of immune characteristics between the two molecular subtypes revealed significant differences in both immune cell infiltration patterns and immune function pathway activation. The immune cell infiltration analysis (Fig. 6E) showed that multiple immune cell types exhibited statistically significant differences between cluster A and cluster B, with most comparisons reaching high levels of significance (p < 0.001). Similarly, immune function pathway analysis (Fig. 6F) demonstrated distinct activation patterns between the two subtypes across various immune-related pathways, including APC co-stimulation, CCR, cytolytic activity, HLA expression, inflammation-promoting, MHC class I, parainflammation, T cell co-stimulation, and Type I IFN response.

To investigate the regulatory mechanisms underlying the expression of glutathione metabolism-related genes in periodontitis and explore potential therapeutic interventions, we constructed a ceRNA network for SLC7A11, GSTA4, and GGT6. Our analysis revealed that these genes are embedded in a complex regulatory network that involves multiple miRNAs and lncRNAs (Fig. 7A). Given that SLC7A11 was upregulated in periodontitis, we conducted a drug-gene interaction analysis using the DSigDB database. The analysis identified five candidate compounds associated with glutathione metabolism-related genes: ursolic Acid, sertaconazole, erastin, clofibrium tosylate, and ethyl docosahexaenoate (Fig. 7B-C). Molecular docking studies showed that erastin has a strong binding affinity for SLC7A11 (binding energy=−9.1 kcal/mol). The results indicated that erastin successfully binds to SLC7A11, forming stable hydrogen bond interactions (Fig. 7D-E).

Experimental validation of periodontitis model

Finally, we established a ligation-induced periodontitis model in Sprague-Dawley rats. HE staining showed (Fig. 8A) that the periodontitis group showed structural disruption of periodontal tissue compared to intact periodontal tissue from control animals. Three-dimensional micro-CT reconstruction confirmed significant structural differences in alveolar bone morphology (Fig. 8B), and quantitative analysis showed a significant increase in CEJ-ABC distance in the periodontitis group (Fig. 8C). Immunohistochemical analysis of SLC7A11 expression showed light staining in the control periodontal tissues, while periodontitis samples showed strong positive immunoreactivity (Fig. 8D). Quantitative assessment of immunostaining intensity confirmed a significant increase in SLC7A11 expression in periodontitis tissues (Fig. 8E).

Discussion

In this study, a comprehensive survey method combining WGCNA and multi-omics analysis was used to identify potential biomarkers for the diagnosis and prognosis of periodontitis. Through WGCNA and diagnostic model construction, we successfully identified three key glutathione metabolism-related genes (GSTA4, GGT6, and SLC7A11), that demonstrated strong diagnostic efficacy (AUC>0.8). Notably, only SLC7A11 of the three key genes were up-regulated in periodontitis. Therefore, we believe that SLC7A11 gene can be used as both an early diagnostic marker and a more achievable therapeutic target.

The identification of GSTA4, GGT6, and SLC7A11 as hub genes in periodontitis aligns with the established role of oxidative stress in periodontal tissue destruction. GSTA4, a member of the glutathione S-transferase family, plays a crucial role in detoxifying lipid peroxidation products and protecting cells from oxidative damage. The significant downregulation of GSTA4 in periodontitis patients observed in our study suggests compromised antioxidant defense mechanisms, which may contribute to increased susceptibility to oxidative tissue damage [21]. Similarly, GGT6 (gamma-glutamyltransferase 6), which catalyzes the first step in glutathione degradation and facilitates the transport of amino acids across cell membranes [22], showed consistent downregulation in periodontitis samples.

We found that SLC7A11 expression was upregulated in periodontitis samples. SLC7A11 functions as a critical gateway for redox homeostasis by maintaining cellular levels of glutathione that counter cellular oxidative stress and suppress ferroptosis [23]. In the context of periodontitis, the increased expression of SLC7A11 likely represents a compensatory response to elevated oxidative stress, as ferroptosis characterized by iron-dependent lipid peroxidation has been shown to be involved in inflammatory diseases including periodontitis [24]. This upregulation may reflect the cellular attempt to maintain cystine import for glutathione biosynthesis under conditions of heightened oxidative challenge. However, high SLC7A11 expression can render cells vulnerable to oxidative stress under certain conditions, particularly when combined with elevated reactive oxygen species [25], suggesting a complex regulatory mechanism in periodontal inflammation. The functional implications of SLC7A11 upregulation in periodontitis may be more complex than initially anticipated. Recent mechanistic studies have revealed that glucose starvation can trigger disulfidptosis—a novel form of cell death characterized by disulfide stress—specifically in cells with high SLC7A11 expression [26]. This finding has particular relevance to periodontal pathophysiology, as post-surgical hypoglycemic conditions commonly occur in periodontal tissues following therapeutic interventions [27, 28]. Further research is warranted to explore how these metabolic interactions influence periodontal health and disease progression, and to determine the potential of SLC7A11 as a therapeutic target in periodontitis.

The relationship between glutathione metabolism and immune cell infiltration patterns revealed in our study provides new insights into the periodontal immune microenvironment. Recent evidence suggests that dysregulated immune responses in periodontitis can induce systemic inflammatory changes through neuroimmune interactions [29]. Our correlation analysis between glutathione metabolism genes and immune cell populations suggests that oxidative stress and immune dysfunction are intimately connected in periodontal pathogenesis. The identification of two distinct molecular subtypes based on glutathione metabolism profiles indicates that periodontitis may represent a heterogeneous group of conditions requiring personalized therapeutic approaches.

This molecular heterogeneity has important implications for precision medicine in periodontology. The distinct immune infiltration patterns observed between our identified subtypes suggest that biomarker-guided treatment stratification could improve therapeutic outcomes.

The analysis of the ceRNA regulatory network focused specifically on SLC7A11 genes, which were significantly upregulated by SLC7A11 in periodontitis. The upregulated expression pattern of SLC7A11 suggests an active compensatory mechanism in response to oxidative stress, representing a potential intervention point where modulation could either enhance the protective response or redirect it toward more effective therapeutic outcomes. Furthermore, the upregulation of SLC7A11 indicates ongoing cellular stress responses that could serve as early diagnostic markers before irreversible tissue damage occurs. The ceRNA network analysis and drug-gene interaction studies reveal potential therapeutic targets for modulating glutathione metabolism in periodontitis. Our identification of erastin as a compound with strong binding affinity to SLC7A11 is particularly relevant, as recent studies have established erastin as a ferroptosis inducer that inhibits SLC7A11-mediated cystine uptake [30]. Our drug-gene interaction analysis identified several promising therapeutic compounds, with erastin showing particularly strong binding affinity to SLC7A11. While erastin is primarily known as a ferroptosis inducer through its inhibition of SLC7A11-mediated cystine uptake [31], its potential therapeutic application in periodontitis requires careful consideration. Recent studies have shown that ferroptosis plays complex roles in periodontal disease pathogenesis, with protective mechanisms such as the LINC00616/miR-370/TFRC axis working to prevent ferroptosis in periodontal ligament stem cells [32]. Therefore, the therapeutic potential of modulating SLC7A11 activity in periodontitis remains to be determined and would require extensive preclinical validation to establish safety and efficacy.

Several limitations should be acknowledged in our study. First, the analysis relies on publicly available datasets, and the functional role of identified biomarkers requires experimental validation in cellular and animal models to confirm their mechanistic contribution to periodontal pathogenesis. Second, the clinical applicability of our diagnostic model needs to be validated in patient populations with different disease severity. Third, our drug-gene interaction analysis represents preliminary computational predictions without comprehensive ADMET (Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, Excretion, and Toxicity) assessments or experimental validation in periodontal disease models. None of the identified therapeutic compounds have been specifically tested in periodontal models, and future studies should include systematic ADMET predictions, in vitro cytotoxicity assessments, and preclinical validation before clinical translation.

Conclusion

In conclusion, our comprehensive bioinformatics analysis successfully identified three key glutathione metabolism-related genes (GSTA4, GGT6, and SLC7A11) as robust biomarkers for periodontitis diagnosis. The downregulation of GSTA4 and GGT6, coupled with upregulation of SLC7A11, reflects the complex dysregulation of antioxidant defense mechanisms in periodontal pathogenesis. The identification of two distinct molecular subtypes of periodontitis based on immune infiltration patterns provides a framework for personalized treatment strategies. Importantly, the strong correlations between these genes and specific immune cell populations reveal novel mechanistic insights into how glutathione metabolism modulates periodontal immune microenvironment. The ceRNA regulatory network analysis of SLC7A11 and drug-gene interaction studies identified potential therapeutic targets. The experimental validation using ligature-induced periodontitis demonstrated the upregulation of SLC7A11 in periodontal tissues. This integrated computational-experimental approach provides a foundation for developing precision medicine approaches in periodontal therapy and advancing our understanding of mechanisms in periodontal disease.

Anemia in chronic obstructive pulmonary disease: a comprehensive bibliometric analysis of global research hotspots and emerging trends
Authors: Tingting Bi, Li Fan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundChronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is a major global health burden frequently complicated by systemic comorbidities. Anemia, a common yet underrecognized condition in COPD, is associated with adverse clinical outcomes including reduced exercise capacity and increased mortality. However, the overall research landscape and evolving trends of anemia-related studies in COPD have not been systematically characterized. Therefore, this study aimed to identify research hotspots and emerging trends in anemia-related research in COPD using a bibliometric approach, and to inform future mechanistic and clinical investigations.MethodsPublications from 1954 to 2025 were retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection (WOSCC) database. Bibliometric and visualization analyses were performed using R Bibliometrix to characterize publication trends, global contributions, citation patterns, and thematic evolution.ResultsA total of 864 English-language original research articles from 80 countries were included in the analysis. The United States and China were identified as the leading contributors, together accounting for over one-third of total publications. Early research (1954–2019) focused predominantly on epidemiologic and outcome-based themes such as “anemia”, “outcomes”, and “risk factors”. Since 2020, the field has shifted toward integrative clinical-mechanistic directions, emphasizing “chronic kidney disease”, “iron metabolism”, “hypoxia”, “systemic inflammation”, and “comorbid complications”. Co-citation analysis identified Chambellan A et al. [2005] and Anker SD et al. [2009] as cornerstone works that bridged prognostic and metabolic dimensions. Emerging trends highlight an increasing focus on multi-organ interaction models, iron homeostasis biomarkers, and translational interventions targeting anemia and systemic inflammation in COPD.ConclusionsThis bibliometric analysis provides a comprehensive overview of global research on anemia in COPD, elucidating its thematic evolution from descriptive epidemiology to systemic, pathophysiology-informed investigation. The findings underscore a growing recognition of anemia as a dynamic mediator of disease progression, and point toward future research directions involving integrated comorbidity management, standardized biomarker evaluation, and mechanism-driven therapeutic strategies.

Introduction

Anemia, a common systemic comorbidity in chronic diseases, represents a critical but often underrecognized burden in patients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) (1,2). Beyond its hematologic implications, anemia in COPD reflects a complex interplay of chronic inflammation, impaired erythropoiesis, nutritional deficiency, and hypoxic stress, collectively contributing to exercise intolerance, frequent exacerbations, and increased mortality risk (3,4). The prevalence of anemia among COPD populations has been reported to range from 10% to 30%, with higher rates observed in advanced disease or during acute exacerbations (5). Despite its clinical relevance, anemia frequently remains underdiagnosed and undertreated in routine COPD management (6).

Over the past two decades, research on anemia in COPD has expanded from descriptive epidemiology and prognostic studies to mechanistic explorations involving iron metabolism, systemic inflammation, and hepcidin regulation (3,6). Clinical investigations have examined the association between low hemoglobin levels and impaired exercise capacity, quality of life, and hospitalization burden, while interventional trials have tested the efficacy of erythropoiesis-stimulating agents and intravenous iron supplementation in improving functional outcomes (1,7). Parallel basic studies have revealed that COPD-related inflammation and hypoxia may synergistically suppress erythropoietin signaling and disrupt iron homeostasis, leading to a phenotype of “anemia of chronic disease” with functional iron deficiency (8,9). Yet, heterogeneity in diagnostic definitions, patient cohorts, and outcome measures has limited the comparability of results, fragmenting the current knowledge landscape.

Although the volume of research in this field has increased substantially, evidence remains dispersed across disciplines and study designs, and a clear global overview of how research themes, intellectual foundations, and collaborative patterns have evolved over time is still lacking. In particular, the recent shift toward system-level mechanisms—such as iron dysregulation, hypoxia-driven pathways, and multi-organ comorbid interactions—has not been comprehensively synthesized from a longitudinal and structural perspective.

Bibliometric analysis offers a systematic and quantitative approach to map this evolving research field, assess its intellectual structure, and identify emerging hotspots and collaboration networks (10,11). Unlike traditional narrative or systematic reviews, bibliometric mapping integrates the full body of literature without temporal or regional constraints, capturing both foundational studies and emerging trends in a data-driven manner (11). At this stage of rapid knowledge growth and conceptual transition, a bibliometric approach is particularly suitable to objectively characterize the developmental trajectory of anemia research in COPD, identify influential contributors and landmark studies, and detect emerging research directions. Here, we apply bibliometric and knowledge-mapping methods to comprehensively visualize the global research landscape of anemia in COPD. By delineating publication trends, prolific contributors, co-authorship and co-citation networks, and thematic evolution, this study aims to clarify the developmental trajectory of the field, highlight underexplored frontiers—particularly those linking iron metabolism, systemic inflammation, and patient-centered outcomes—and provide guidance for future research priorities and clinical translation in the management of anemia among COPD patients. We present this article in accordance with the BIBLIO reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2025-1-2498/rc).

Methods

Data collection

A systematic literature search was performed in the Web of Science Core Collection (WOSCC) on 1st November 2025. The topic query was: TS = ((anemia) OR (hypohemia)) AND TS= ((chronic obstructive pulmonary diseases) OR (COPD) OR (chronic obstructive lung disease) OR (chronic obstructive pulmonary disease) OR (chronic obstructive airway disease) OR (COAD) OR (chronic airflow obstructions) OR (chronic airflow obstruction)). Records were limited to original research articles (document type: “Article”) published in English, with no temporal restriction (database inception to the search date). All retrieved records were screened based on titles, abstracts, and keywords to exclude non-relevant publications. Duplicate records and articles not primarily focused on anemia in COPD were removed. After removing duplicates and excluding non-relevant entries, a total of 864 articles were retained. Data were exported in both “Plain Text” and “BibTeX” formats for bibliometric analysis using R (bibliometrix) and related visualization tools (e.g., VOSviewer) (12). Analytical method

The R package Bibliometrix (version 4.1) was used to analyze the evolution through time of the literature volume in the field, the involvement of countries, institutions and authors, and the journals publishing the relevant articles (12,13). Furthermore, Bibliometrix facilitated a thematic analysis to explore the evolution of research themes and hotspots over time. Bibliometric indicators were calculated using a full counting method. Country and institutional contributions were determined based on authors’ affiliation information, allowing a single publication to be attributed to multiple countries or institutions when applicable. VOSViewer (version 1.6.17) was used for visualizing and analyzing citations and co-citations of authors, journals and articles to detect intense collaborations between different authors and journals, and correlations and similarities between articles (12). Microsoft Excel [2016] was used to arrange and summarize the analytical information related to countries, institutions, authors, articles, and keywords. This bibliometric study was conducted and reported in accordance with the BIBLIO checklist to ensure transparency, methodological rigor, and reproducibility.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using the R package Bibliometrix (version 4.1) and VOSviewer (version 1.6.17). Descriptive bibliometric indicators, including annual publication output, total citations, local citations (LCs), H-index, and collaboration metrics, were calculated to characterize the research productivity and academic influence in this field. Network-based analyses, such as co-authorship, co-occurrence, and co-citation analyses, were performed to explore collaboration patterns, intellectual structure, and thematic evolution of anemia-related research in COPD. All bibliometric indicators were calculated using a full counting method. As this study was based on secondary bibliographic data rather than individual-level clinical or experimental data, no inferential statistical tests were applicable or performed.

Results

Our initial search of the WOSCC identified 1,287 publications related to anemia within COPD. After excluding non-article records and non-English studies, 864 original research articles were retained for bibliometric visualization and analysis. This refined dataset provides a robust basis for mapping the evolution, collaboration networks, and thematic foci of anemia-related research within the COPD field. An overview of the dataset characteristics is provided in Figure 1B.

Evolution of academic interest in anemia and COPD

The annual scientific production curve (Figure 2A) reveals a long period of minimal activity from the 1950s through the late 1980s, followed by a gradual increase beginning in the early 2000s and an exponential surge after 2015. Before 1990, publications were sporadic (<5 articles per year on average), reflecting isolated clinical observations rather than a consolidated research domain. From 2000 to 2010, annual output rose modestly, averaging ≈10 articles per year, signaling a growing recognition of anemia as a systemic comorbidity in COPD. A pronounced acceleration occurred after 2015, with annual publications climbing steeply and surpassing 60 articles per year by 2024, representing the most prolific decade in the field’s history. The overall compound annual growth rate (CAGR) from 2000 to 2025 was estimated at ≈8.5%, underscoring the field’s sustained expansion and increasing academic engagement with anemia’s impact on COPD outcomes.

Citation analysis (Figure 2B) displays a distinct temporal pattern of academic influence. The earliest literature (1950s–1980s) accumulated negligible citation activity, while papers published between 2000 and 2012 exhibited intermittent citation peaks corresponding to seminal works that established the prognostic significance of anemia in COPD. The most influential publication cohorts emerged between 2008 and 2014, achieving average annual citation counts exceeding 10–15 citations per year. In contrast, recent articles (post-2020) have yet to reach equivalent citation maturity but show steady upward momentum, indicating continued attention and rapid integration into the contemporary research dialogue.

Country & affiliation distribution

In our dataset, research on anemia in COPD spans 80 countries, reflecting broad global engagement across both developed and emerging economies. The 15 most productive countries are distributed across Europe (n=7), Asia (n=5), North America (n=2), and Oceania (n=1) (Table S1). The United States ranks first with 1,210 publications (25.3%), followed by China with 548 publications (11.4%), together accounting for over 35% of total global output (Figure 3A). The United States pioneered early research into anemia as a systemic comorbidity in COPD and has maintained a leading position throughout the decades, while China entered the field in 2001 and subsequently experienced rapid expansion, surpassing Italy in 2024 to become the world’s second-largest contributor (Figure 3B).

The international co-authorship network demonstrates extensive cross-border collaboration (Figure 3C), where thicker brown edges indicate a higher number of jointly authored publications. Using the multiple-country publication (MCP) rate as a proxy for international collaboration intensity, the top three contributing countries show MCPs of 15.6% (United States), 9.6% (China), and 23.4% (Spain). Citation analysis underscores the United States’ dominant academic influence, with a cumulative total of 13,778 citations—nearly four times that of France (n=3,604)—highlighting its early-mover advantage and consistently high impact-per-paper profile, even as China continues to scale rapidly in overall publication volume (Figure 3D).

Overall, universities and medical schools dominate the institutional landscape, occupying the majority of the top 10 positions (Figure 3E). Within the international network, the United States remains at the collaborative core, represented by leading institutions such as Harvard Medical School (n=16), Baylor College of Medicine (n=17), Mayo Clinic (n=20), University of Pittsburgh (n=21), Duke University (n=23), and Johns Hopkins University (27 publications)—collectively reflecting sustained scientific leadership in this field. In Asia, research strength is concentrated primarily in China and South Korea. Among Chinese institutions, Henan University of Chinese Medicine (n=16), Capital Medical University (n=17), Central South University (n=19), Hebei Medical University (n=20), and Nanjing Medical University (21 publications) all rank within the top 15 globally. South Korean institutions, including Seoul National University (n=15) and Sungkyunkwan University (n=15), also demonstrate strong research productivity. Notably, the University of Belgrade (25 publications) emerges as a leading institution from Eastern Europe, underscoring the field’s increasingly multiregional and multicentric collaborative landscape. Institutional network mapping (Figure 3F) reveals several dense cooperative clusters, where node colors represent institutions and thicker inter-node links denote stronger research partnerships.

Reference citations & co-citation

Global citations (GCs) quantify how often documents are cited across the entire database. Up to the search date, research on anemia in COPD has accumulated 33,878 GCs. By GC ranking, seven articles have been cited more than 1,000 times (Table S3). Notably, Vos T et al. [2012] in The Lancet, “Years lived with disability (YLDs) for 1160 sequelae of 289 diseases and injuries 1990-2010: a systematic analysis for the Global Burden of Disease Study 2010”, tops the list with 4,526 GCs (14). This landmark global epidemiological work established that both COPD and iron-deficiency anemia rank among the leading contributors to worldwide disability-adjusted life years, underscoring their major roles in the global non-fatal disease burden. In addition, Simonneau G et al. [2013] in Journal of the American College of Cardiology provided a comprehensive revision of the clinical classification of pulmonary hypertension (PH), reassigning chronic hemolytic anemia-associated PH from Group 1 (pulmonary arterial hypertension) to Group 5 (unclear or multifactorial mechanisms) (15). This consensus reclassification reflected an evolving understanding of the complex pathophysiological links between anemia and pulmonary vascular dysfunction, offering conceptual support for mechanistic studies of systemic inflammation, oxidative stress, and hypoxia-driven remodeling in COPD with comorbid anemia (Figure 5A).

LCs, computed within our analyzed corpus, capture how often each document is cited by other papers in this dataset. Within our collection, 137 documents received at least one LC, yielding a total of 851 LCs, with seven articles exceeding 30 LCs (Table S4). The most influential within-set publication is Chambellan A et al. [2005] in Chest, which achieved the highest LC count (70 LCs) (16). Based on a cohort of 2,524 patients with severe COPD receiving long-term oxygen therapy (LTOT) in France, this seminal study systematically characterized the distribution and prognostic implications of hematocrit levels in COPD. It demonstrated that low hematocrit (<35%) was not uncommon among advanced COPD patients and was associated with a markedly reduced 3-year survival rate (24% vs. 70%) compared with those having normal or elevated hematocrit values. Mechanistically, the authors proposed that chronic systemic inflammation, nutritional depletion, and tissue hypoxia collectively suppress erythropoiesis, providing an early paradigm shift away from the historical view of polycythemia dominance in COPD. The study thus firmly established anemia as a clinically significant determinant of adverse outcomes in COPD (Figure 5B).

To delineate the intellectual structure and its evolution, we conducted a reference co-citation analysis using the Walktrap clustering algorithm, mapping linkages among 50 references (minimum co-citation edges =2) (Figure 5C). The classical review by Anker SD et al. [2009] in European Heart Journal, “ESPEN Guidelines on Parenteral Nutrition: on cardiology and pneumology”, exhibited the highest betweenness centrality [97, 118], signifying its pivotal bridging role in connecting nutritional, metabolic, and hematologic perspectives within COPD research (17). This work provided an essential conceptual framework linking malnutrition, cachexia, and anemia, emphasizing the shared mechanisms of reduced energy intake, increased energy expenditure, and impaired anabolic metabolism in chronic cardiorespiratory diseases. From a mechanistic standpoint, the review elucidated how nutritional deficits may disrupt iron metabolism, inhibit erythropoiesis, and alter bone-marrow microenvironments—ultimately aggravating anemia and perpetuating a vicious cycle of tissue hypoxia and cardiopulmonary strain. The authors advocated for early oral or enteral nutritional intervention to prevent progressive weight loss and mitigate systemic consequences. Collectively, this reference network highlights how the interplay among inflammation, metabolism, and erythropoietic regulation has shaped the conceptual foundations of anemia-related COPD research and continues to guide contemporary investigation in this domain.

Research focus & frontier analysis

Guided by the sustained growth of publications after 2020, we divided the timeline of anemia-COPD research into two periods (1954–2019 and 2020–2025) to delineate thematic evolution (Figure 6A). During 1954–2019, scholarship was centered on epidemiologic and outcome-oriented themes, such as “anemia”, “children”, “outcomes”, and “risk factors”, reflecting the early stage of descriptive epidemiology and clinical observation. Research during this era primarily focused on the identification of anemia as a comorbidity, its demographic distribution, and its impact on general health outcomes. After 2020, the field underwent a distinct clinical-mechanistic transition, characterized by the emergence of integrative themes including “chronic kidney disease”, “hypoxia”, “mortality”, “system”, and “complications”. This shift marks the evolution from population-based observation toward a systemic, comorbidity-centered paradigm, emphasizing the interrelations among anemia, oxygen transport dysfunction, systemic inflammation, and organ-specific outcomes in COPD.

Historiographic mapping (Figure 6B) revealed several interconnected author clusters dominated by Boutou AK, John M, Nickol AH, and Schönhofer B, whose studies collectively bridged the mechanistic, physiological, and clinical dimensions of anemia in COPD. These highly co-cited works highlight the transition from early physiological investigations on hypoxia and erythropoiesis to contemporary multidimensional research integrating biomarkers, inflammation, and prognosis.

The strategic thematic map (Figure 6C) delineates four quadrants of research maturity. The motor themes are represented by “anemia”, “COPD”, and “obstructive pulmonary disease”, denoting the core axis of the field with strong centrality and high developmental density. In contrast, basic themes such as “mortality”, “prevalence”, and “disease” form the methodological foundation for outcome-based analyses, whereas emerging/declining themes like “children”, “acute chest syndrome”, and “sleep apnea” represent narrower or population-specific extensions of the main topic.

Temporal trend analysis (Figure 6D) further corroborates this evolution. Early research signals were dominated by terms related to “blood transfusion”, “erythropoietin”, and “hemoglobin concentration”. From approximately 2010 onward, the focus gradually expanded toward “mortality”, “comorbidity”, “systemic inflammation”, and “iron metabolism”, paralleling the rise of multi-organ interaction models and chronic disease management frameworks. Since 2020, newly appearing keywords—such as “chronic kidney disease”, “complications”, and “hypoxia”—indicate an emerging integrative view connecting anemia-COPD comorbidity with cardiovascular and renal outcomes. Collectively, these findings depict a paradigm shift from epidemiologic observation to systemic, pathophysiology-informed clinical research, in which anemia is no longer viewed as a passive bystander but as a dynamic mediator influencing oxygen delivery, inflammation, and survival in COPD.

Overall, the bibliometric results reveal a sustained and accelerating growth of global research on anemia in COPD, particularly after 2015, with contributions spanning a wide range of countries, institutions, journals, and authors. Citation and co-citation analyses identified several landmark studies that established the prognostic and metabolic significance of anemia in COPD and formed the intellectual foundation of the field. Notably, thematic evolution and trend analyses demonstrate a clear transition from early epidemiologic and outcome-oriented research toward more integrative clinical-mechanistic themes in recent years, including iron metabolism, hypoxia, systemic inflammation, and multi-organ comorbidities.

Discussion

This bibliometric analysis provides a comprehensive overview of global research activity on anemia in COPD, highlighting its rapid expansion, evolving themes, and emerging interdisciplinary nature. The steady increase in publication volume after 2015 and the accelerated growth observed post-2020 indicate a transition from descriptive epidemiology to mechanistic and translational investigation. This trend reflects an expanding recognition of anemia as a clinically meaningful comorbidity in COPD rather than an incidental finding.

It should be noted that the findings presented in this study are derived from bibliometric analyses, which characterize publication patterns, thematic evolution, and intellectual structures of the literature, rather than directly assessing clinical efficacy or patient-level outcomes. Accordingly, the trends identified herein should be interpreted as reflections of research focus and knowledge development, serving primarily a hypothesis-generating role.

Beyond describing publication growth, these bibliometric trends indicate an important conceptual shift in research design, moving from isolated observational analyses toward integrative and system-oriented investigations. Future studies should increasingly adopt standardized definitions of anemia, incorporate longitudinal follow-up, and integrate hematologic, pulmonary, and systemic biomarkers to improve comparability and translational relevance.

The temporal and thematic analyses demonstrate that early research was primarily descriptive—focused on prevalence, demographics, and broad outcome associations. Over time, scholarship has progressively incorporated systemic mechanisms, including chronic inflammation, oxidative stress, nutritional imbalance, and disordered iron metabolism. These mechanisms collectively define anemia as part of the multisystem pathophysiology of COPD. The emergence of keywords such as “hypoxia”, “iron metabolism”, and “chronic kidney disease” after 2020 reflects a paradigm shift toward understanding the inter-organ axis linking the lungs, kidneys, bone marrow, and cardiovascular system. This systems-level perspective underscores the bidirectional relationship between impaired oxygen transport and systemic inflammation, positioning anemia as both a marker and a mediator of disease progression. This evolving systems-based perspective highlights the necessity of future research designs that explicitly account for multi-organ interactions and comorbidity burden, rather than treating anemia as an isolated laboratory abnormality.

Geographical and institutional analyses reveal strong leadership from the United States and China, complemented by increasing contributions from European and East Asian research networks. However, the collaboration intensity (MCP ratio) remains relatively modest among high-volume countries, suggesting opportunities for greater international cooperation and harmonization of study protocols. Similarly, the dominance of academic medical centers indicates that most data originate from tertiary hospitals, potentially limiting generalizability to community-based or low-resource settings where anemia and COPD co-occur most frequently. These findings underscore the importance of future multicenter and multinational collaborations, particularly involving underrepresented regions, to generate more generalizable evidence and to inform real-world clinical practice.

Citation and co-citation analyses highlight several seminal contributions that have shaped the intellectual framework of the field—from Chambellan A et al.’s identification of low hematocrit as a mortality predictor to Anker SD et al.’s conceptualization of nutritional and metabolic interactions in chronic disease (16,17). Together, these foundational studies bridge clinical epidemiology with mechanistic inquiry, establishing a conceptual continuum between systemic inflammation, disordered erythropoiesis, and impaired prognosis (3). Despite these advances, large-scale prospective studies integrating hematologic biomarkers, iron homeostasis indices [ferritin, transferrin saturation (TSAT), hepcidin], and longitudinal outcomes remain limited. Standardization of anemia definitions, adjustment for confounding comorbidities, and validation across diverse populations are essential next steps to improve evidence comparability. Addressing these gaps will be critical for designing future trials capable of translating mechanistic insights into clinically actionable interventions.

From a clinical perspective, the evolving evidence suggests that anemia is not merely an epiphenomenon of chronic illness but a modifiable determinant of exercise intolerance, hospitalization risk, and survival in COPD (18-20). Interventional trials of intravenous iron supplementation and erythropoiesis-stimulating agents have shown preliminary benefits in selected patients, yet optimal treatment thresholds, safety profiles, and long-term impacts remain uncertain. Integration of anemia assessment into COPD management algorithms—particularly in patients with overlapping cardiovascular or renal dysfunction—represents a promising but underdeveloped frontier. Clinically, these findings support a more proactive approach to anemia screening and management in COPD, especially among patients with significant comorbidities, where targeted correction of anemia or iron deficiency may improve functional status and overall outcomes.

Anemia in COPD should therefore be interpreted within a broader systemic disease framework. Major comorbidities such as chronic kidney disease, cardiovascular disease, malnutrition/cachexia, and persistent systemic inflammation frequently coexist with COPD and may exacerbate anemia through shared mechanisms, including impaired erythropoietin production, iron sequestration, and chronic inflammatory activation. Understanding these interactions is essential for developing comprehensive, multidisciplinary management strategies tailored to patients with advanced COPD.

This study has several strengths. It provides a comprehensive, longitudinal bibliometric overview of global research on anemia in COPD spanning more than seven decades, enabling systematic identification of publication trends, research hotspots, and intellectual foundations. The WOSCC was selected as the primary data source because it offers standardized bibliographic records and high-quality citation data, which are particularly suitable for bibliometric and co-citation analyses. In addition, the use of standardized bibliometric tools and visualization techniques allows objective mapping of collaborations, citations, and thematic evolution. Several limitations should also be acknowledged. As this analysis was based solely on the WOSCC and restricted to English-language publications, relevant studies indexed exclusively in other databases (such as Scopus or PubMed) or published in other languages may not have been captured, potentially leading to selection bias. Furthermore, bibliometric indicators reflect research activity and influence rather than clinical quality or causality. These inherent methodological constraints should be considered when interpreting the findings.

In summary, this study delineates the intellectual structure, research dynamics, and thematic evolution of the anemia-COPD field. The trajectory from epidemiologic observation to mechanistic exploration reflects a broader transformation toward precision medicine and systemic disease frameworks in respiratory research. Future investigations should prioritize multicenter collaboration, biomarker standardization, and mechanism-driven interventional studies, with the ultimate goal of integrating anemia management into routine COPD care to improve patient-centered outcomes.

Conclusions

This bibliometric analysis reveals a shift in anemia research in COPD from epidemiological studies to mechanism-driven investigations, emphasizing the roles of systemic inflammation, iron metabolism, and multi-organ dysfunction. These findings represent evolving research priorities and knowledge structures rather than direct clinical evidence. Anemia is increasingly seen as a key mediator of disease progression and patient outcomes. Emerging research focuses on iron homeostasis, hypoxia, and comorbidities like chronic kidney disease. By clearly distinguishing bibliometric insights from clinical interpretation, this study highlights future research priorities including standardized diagnostic criteria, integrated comorbidity models, and mechanism-driven interventional studies—that may ultimately inform patient-centered clinical strategies in COPD.

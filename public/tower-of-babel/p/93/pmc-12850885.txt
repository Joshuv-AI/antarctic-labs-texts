Unsedated transnasal endoscopy experience at a tertiary referral center in India
Authors: Ravi Teja Pasam, Srilekha Chava, Nagabhushanam Gollapalli, Bhaskar Rao Uppala, Jagan Mohan Rao Kodali
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Background and AimsUnsedated transnasal endoscopy (uTNE) was developed in the 1990s but has not gained widespread use. We present our experience with uTNE at a tertiary referral center in India.MethodsA retrospective analysis was conducted with prospectively collected data of patients who underwent uTNE for predominantly diagnostic EGD at our center between January 2014 and September 2017. Patients received 2% lidocaine spray over the back of the throat, xylometazoline, and 10% lidocaine sprays in the nostril 10 minutes before uTNE. Successful completion of TNE without the need for sedation, conversion to peroral EGD (PO-EGD), epistaxis, and tolerance of the procedure compared with prior PO-EGD were the outcomes of interest. Unsedated PO-EGD (uPO-EGD) is the standard of care at our center. Logistic regression was used for analysis.ResultsSuccessful completion of uTNE (414/440 patients) was significantly associated with male sex (odds ratio [OR], 2.67; 95% confidence interval [CI], 1.16-6.14). Conversion to PO-EGD (4.32%) was significantly associated with age (OR, .96; 95% CI, .94-.99), year of endoscopy (OR, .44; 95% CI, .24-.82), and male sex (OR, .33; 95% CI, .12-.89) in multivariable analysis. Rate of epistaxis was 6.76%, and 93.09% of patients (229/246) tolerated uTNE better than prior PO-EGD. There were no life-threatening or major adverse events requiring prolonged hospitalization.ConclusionsuTNE should be offered as an alternative to uPO-EGD for diagnostic EGD. Studies comparing tolerance and adverse events of uTNE with those of sedated PO-EGD are required given the costs and potential adverse events associated with sedation.

Unsedated transnasal endoscopy (uTNE) for diagnostic and therapeutic EGD was first described 3 decades ago.1 Although transnasal endoscopy (TNE) has been used for various therapeutic procedures such as biliary drainage, gastrostomy tube placement, and endoscopic submucosal dissection, uTNE has been primarily used for diagnostic EGD.2, 3, 4, 5 TNE is performed by inserting an ultrathin endoscope (≤6 mm diameter) through the nares.5 The nares are topically anesthetized with sprays or medication-soaked pledgets to avoid pain and discomfort with uTNE.5,6 A nasal decongestant is also administered to shrink the turbinates to ease the endoscope passage.7

TNE has advantages and disadvantages compared with peroral EGD (PO-EGD). Although TNE is usually performed with the patient unsedated, PO-EGD is performed with or without sedation based on regional or center preferences. The costs and adverse events of sedation can be avoided with uTNE. However, studies comparing tolerance and adverse events of uTNE with those of sedated PO-EGD are limited and have shown conflicting results.8, 9, 10, 11 On the other hand, multiple studies have compared uTNE with unsedated PO-EGD (uPO-EG). Hemodynamic changes and postprocedural anxiety are significantly higher with uPO-EGD compared with uTNE.10,12 Studies have demonstrated that uTNE is better tolerated than uPO-EGD with an ultrathin endoscope.13, 14, 15 While uPO-EGD is associated with a significant gag reflex, uTNE is associated with minimal to no gagging.7,12,13,16,17 On the other hand, TNE is also associated with adverse events, with epistaxis being the most common.18

Although studies have reported the safety, feasibility, and tolerability of uTNE in comparison with uPO-EGD for diagnostic EGD, data from India, where uPO-EGD is the usual practice, are scarce.19 In this study, we present our single-center experience with uTNE.

Methods

Study design

A retrospective analysis of prospectively collected data was conducted with data of patients who underwent uTNE at our center from January 2014 to September 2017. Data were collected for the purpose of an internal audit to monitor the tolerance and adverse events of uTNE as it was newly introduced at our center. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.20 The Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology checklist for the study is available in Appendix 1 (available online at www.igiejournal.org).

Patients and data collection

The risks and benefits of uTNE were explained to all adult patients (aged ≥18 years) and guardians or parents of pediatric patients who were referred for routine diagnostic endoscopy and those referred interventions using TNE. Consecutive patients who signed a written informed consent and underwent uTNE were included in the study. Demographic characteristics collected were age and sex, and procedural characteristics were endoscope model, year in which the endoscopy was performed, and additional procedures performed such as biopsy sampling or feeding tube placement.

Procedure

Local anesthesia was administered with 2% lidocaine spray over the back of the patient’s throat, xylometazoline, and 10% lidocaine spray in the nostril 10 minutes before the procedure. A 5.5-mm endoscope (model GIF XP 150 N; Olympus, Tokyo, Japan) and 5.9-mm endoscope (model EG 530 N; Fujinon, Tokyo, Japan) were used. Endoscopy was performed by 2 endoscopists (N.G. and J.M.R.K.) who were well versed with uTNE. The decision to convert to PO-EGD was made before endoscope insertion if the nasal passage was deemed to be too narrow to avoid trauma.

Outcomes

The outcomes of interest were successful completion of TNE, conversion to PO-EGD, tolerance of the procedure compared with prior PO-EGD experience, and epistaxis. Successful completion of uTNE was defined as the completion of TNE without the need for sedation. Tolerance was categorized as better versus equal or worse compared with each patient’s prior PO-EGD experience. This outcome was assessed only in those patients with a prior history of PO-EGD. uPO-EGD is the standard of care at our center. Epistaxis was graded as spotting noted on withdrawal of the endoscope and frank epistaxis with blood visibly trickling down from the nares.

Statistics

Percentages and means ± standard deviation were used to describe categorical and continuous variables, respectively. Age and year of endoscopy were considered as continuous variables. Sex, endoscope model, and performance of biopsy sampling and/or additional procedures were considered as categorical variables. Logistic regression was used conduct univariable and multivariable analyses. Variables with a P-value < .20 in univariable analysis were included in the multivariable analysis. Only variables with P < .05 in multivariable analysis were included in the final model. Ninety-five percent confidence intervals were calculated for odds ratios. Statistical analysis was performed using SAS, version 9.4 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA).

Discussion

Our study has several major findings. First, the endoscopy completion rate with uTNE was 94.09% and was noted to be significantly higher in male patients compared with female patients. Second, younger age, female sex, and earlier year of endoscopy were associated with conversion to PO-EGD; furthermore, 93.09% of patients reported better tolerance with uTNE compared with their prior PO-EGD experience. Finally, our rate of adverse events (epistaxis and impacted endoscope) associated with uTNE was 7.00%.

Studies have reported a 2% to 18% rate of conversion to PO-EGD,12, 13, 14, 15, 16,21, 22, 23, 24 which is comparable with the findings of our study (4.32%). Successful completion of TNE was significantly associated with older age and male sex in our study, which was also shown by Dumortier et al.23 We also demonstrated that the odds of conversion to PO-EGD decreased with the year of endoscopy in the multivariable analysis. Perhaps this reflects increasing confidence of the endoscopists as experience builds over the years.

Better tolerance associated with uTNE compared with uPO-EGD is consistent with the results of other studies.12, 13, 14,16,22,24,25 In our study, the tolerance was in comparison with patients’ prior PO-EGD experience. Although it is the standard of care to perform uPO-EGD at our center, data regarding patients’ need for sedation with their prior PO-EGD were not available. The rate of epistaxis (6.36%) was comparable with the rates seen in other studies (.55%-21.6%).14, 15, 16,21,22,26 However, the definition of epistaxis in our study included spotting found on withdrawal of the endoscope and frank bleeding, and 21 of 28 patients who were noted have epistaxis had spotting. Although we did not find a significant difference in the rates of epistaxis between a 5.5-mm and a 5.9-mm endoscope, some studies reported that epistaxis was associated with larger endoscopes.15,21 However, it should be noted that 95% of the patients in our study underwent uTNE with a 5.5-mm diameter endoscope. Performing biopsy sampling or any additional procedures was not associated with poor tolerance or epistaxis in our study. Moreover, all cases of epistaxis were self-limited.

The main advantage of uTNE over uPO-EGD is the significantly better tolerance. uTNE bypasses the areas in the oral cavity and pharynx that cause gagging on contact, which is the likely explanation for better tolerance.5 However, the advantages of uTNE increase when compared with sedated PO-EGD in the form of avoidance of sedation and resultant decrease in direct and indirect costs of the procedure such as postsedation monitoring and time off from work. This has huge economic implications, especially in countries where PO-EGD with sedation is the standard of care. This is especially true for patients with conditions such as Barrett’s esophagus, cirrhosis, and eosinophilic esophagitis who need periodic diagnostic endoscopy. On the other hand, TNE is also useful in patients with trismus and oropharyngeal cancers where oral access is limited.3,27,28

The limitations of TNE are a smaller working channel, relatively decreased maneuverability, and purported inferior image quality of ultrathin endoscopes.29, 30, 31 However, studies have demonstrated high sensitivity and specificity of diagnosing lesions with TNE10,17,26,32 and comparable image quality of TNE and PO-EGD,12,14,15,22 especially with the newer generation ultrathin endoscopes.33,34 On the other hand, the therapeutic uses of TNE are inadequate given the limited accessories for the smaller working channel of the ultrathin endoscopes.31,35 However, development of accessories for the ultrathin endoscopes has enabled performance of complex procedures such as endoscopic submucosal dissection.36

The major strengths of our study are that this is the largest study on uTNE from India, and the data were collected prospectively. The major limitation of our study is that it is a single-center observational study without a control group. Our results are therefore less generalizable. In addition, selection bias and recall bias are to be expected with this study design. Tolerance of the procedure was not rated using standardized scales. Finally, there was no formal assessment of the image quality of TNE compared with PO-EGD.

In conclusion, uTNE has an excellent completion rate with minimal adverse events and is well tolerated compared with uPO-EGD. It should be offered to all patients for diagnostic EGDs and certain therapeutic EGDs in patients with unfavorable oropharyngeal anatomy as an alternative to uPO-EGD in centers where uPO-EGD is the standard of care. Developing accessories for the smaller working channel of the ultrathin endoscopes will allow for wider applications of uTNE. Studies comparing uTNE with sedated PO-EGD for diagnostic EGD regarding tolerance, adverse events, diagnostic capability, image quality, and cost-effectiveness are required given the huge clinical and economic impact uTNE can have if shown to have comparable outcomes.

A pyrene-based fluorescent probe for highly sensitive and selective detection of hydroxylamine in environmental water samples
Authors: Wen-Jie Zhang, Yi Hu, Yan-Chan Huang, Si-Yu Tang, Su-Xiang Feng, Wen-Zhu Bi, Xiao-Lan Chen, Ling-Bo Qu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In this study, a pyrene-based fluorescent probe (PCPA) was designed, synthesized and developed for the detection of HA. In the presence of HA, PCPA was converted to the oxime derivative (PCO) through a nucleophilic addition–elimination reaction, accompanied by significant enhancement in fluorescence at 408/425 nm. Further investigations showed that PCPA displayed high sensitivity (LOD = 0.017 µM, λex = 365 nm) within 20 min and high selectivity over other analytes. Moreover, PCPA was capable of recognizing HA in environmental water samples and showed a recovery rate of 95–104% and relative standard deviations (RSD) of 0.56–2.59%. Portable PCPA-loaded paper-based test strips were facilely prepared, which enabled the on-site detection of HA with the aid of a smartphone. These results are expected to make PCPA a potential alternative for HA detection in the field of environmental monitoring.

Introduction

As an oxidized form of ammonia, hydroxylamine (HA), NH2OH, is a key intermediate in the global nitrogen cycle1 and involved in various physiological actions.2,3 Besides, HA is a vital industrial raw material, demonstrating numerous applications in the fields of industry,4–7 agriculture,8 drugs9 and chemical synthesis.10 However, HA is genotoxic and mutagenic11 and the extensive use of HA has made it an important water pollutant, posing a threat to both plants and animals. The maximum exposure limit for HA is 2 µg day−1 according to the International Consortium of Harmonisation (ICH).12 Therefore, the detection and quantification of HA is essential for minimizing harm and of great significance in environmental protection.

For the detection and quantification of HA, conventional methods, such as UV-Vis spectrophotometry,13,14 high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC),15,16 gas chromatography (GC)17,18 and electroanalytical methods,19 are usually cumbersome and suffer from complicated derivatization procedures. To overcome the disadvantages of those above-mentioned techniques, fluorescent methods have become a popular tool for monitoring environmental analytes due to their simple sample preparation and capability of on-site detection.20 However, few fluorescent probes for HA have been reported.21 As shown in Scheme 1 and Table S1, in 2017 and 2019, Yoon's group22 and Lin's group23 developed the first two probes, probe 1 and RhChr, realizing the detection of HA in vitro and in vivo. Nevertheless, the two probes suffer from low selectivity and complicated chemical structures. Following that, based on the common condensation–dehydration reaction of aldehyde group and HA, three probes (DIB,24PCHO25 and AnCHO26) with aldehyde group as recognition site were consecutively reported in 2020–2025. However, a relatively long response time was needed due to the low reactivity of aldehyde group.24 In order to enhance the reactivity and selectivity, another three probes (SWJT-22,27NS-CY28 and CSO-CY29) were designed and developed by introducing a highly hydroxylamine-reactive group through Knoevenagel condensation of aldehyde group with alkyl 2-cyanoacetate. Despite of the great success of these probes, the detection limits are still not low enough and their response to HA might be affected by the other nucleophilic compounds.22,24,27 Therefore, the development probes with ‘6S’ property (simpleness, speedy, selectivity, sensitivity, stability and smart) is still of great significance for the detection of hazardous HA in environmental water.

Fluorescent probes for the detection of HA.

Recently, pyrene-based fluorescent probes have been widely reported due to its high photoluminescence, excellent stability and relatively cheap starting materials.30–33 Based on this, a pyrene-based fluorescent probe (PCPA) for HA was designed using 2-(cyanomethyl)-1-methylpyridinium iodide as electron withdrawing group to enhance molecular solubility and sensing properties. PCPA showed weak fluorescence emission in aqueous solution. Upon addition of HA, PCPA was converted to the oxime derivative (PCO) through a nucleophilic addition–elimination reaction, accompanied by significant enhancement in fluorescence in aqueous solution. Furthermore, PCPA was successfully applied to the on-site detection of HA in portable PCPA-loaded paper-based test strips.

Experimental section

Materials and methods

The related materials, instruments and general analytical experiment procedure were all included in the supplementary information.

Synthesis of PCPA

2-(Cyanomethyl)-1-methylpyridinium iodide (NP-1) was prepared from 2-pyridylacetonitrile and iodomethane and directly used without further purification. 1-Pyrenecarboxalde-hyde (0.230 g, 1.0 mmol) and NP-1 (0.260 g, 1.0 mmol) were mixed and stirred in anhydrous ethanol (20 mL) in a 100 mL round-bottom flask equipped with a magnetic stirrer. The mixture was heated at 50 °C for 12 hours and monitored by thin-layer chromatography (TLC) (petroleum ether : ethyl acetate = 1 : 5). Then, the mixture was cooled to room temperature. The obtained precipitation was washed by cold anhydrous ethanol (10 mL × 3) and dried in vacuum to give PCPA as yellow solid with 95% yield. 1H NMR (400 MHz, DMSO-d6): δ: 9.33 (d, J = 6.0 Hz, 1H), 9.20 (s, 1H), 8.88–8.84 (m, 2H), 8.63–8.58 (m, 2H), 8.52–8.29 (m, 7H), 8.19 (t, 1H), 4.60 (s, 3H). 13C NMR (100 MHz, DMSO-d6): δ: 154.7, 149.0, 148.2, 146.9, 134.1, 131.1, 131.0, 130.7, 130.5, 130.3, 129.8, 128.6, 127.6, 127.4, 127.3, 127.2, 126.5, 126.2, 125.3, 124.1, 123.7, 123.6, 116.2, 103.8, 47.6. HRMS: [M − I]+: calcd for C25H17N2: 345.1386, found: 345.1403.

Synthesis of PCO

PCPA (0.0472 g, 0.1 mmol) was dissolved in DMSO (5 mL) and then hydroxylamine hydrochloride (0.0083 g, 0.12 mmol) was added to the solution. The mixture was stirred at room temperature and the reaction was monitored by TLC (petroleum ether: ethyl acetate = 1 : 3). After the reaction was completed, the mixture was quenched by H2O (5 mL) and extracted with dichloromethane (10 mL × 3). The organic phase was collected, washed with saturated saline solution and dried over Na2SO4. The crude product was purified by silica gel column chromatography (petroleum ether: ethyl acetate = 1 : 3) to give PCO as yellow powder with 73% yield. 1H NMR (400 MHz, DMSO-d6): δ: 11.62 (s, 1H), 9.16 (s, 1H), 8.81 (d, J = 7.4 Hz, 1H), 8.42 (d, J = 6.4 Hz, 1H), 8.35–8.27 (m, 4H), 8.25–8.20 (m, 2H), 8.11 (m, 1H). 13C NMR (100 MHz, DMSO-d6): δ: 147.9, 134.8, 131.3, 130.7, 128.8, 128.5, 128.4, 127.8, 127.0, 126.7, 126.3, 126.0, 125.6, 125.4, 124.6, 124.3, 123.8. Results and discussion

Synthesis and characterization

As shown in Scheme 2, the pyrene-based fluorescent probe PCPA was easily obtained and purified through the Knoevenagel condensation reaction of 1-pyreneformaldehyde and 2-(cyanomethyl)-1-methylpyridinium iodide (NP-1) in ethanol. The sensing product PCO was prepared by reacting PCPA with excessive hydroxylamine hydrochloride in DMSO with high isolated yield. The structures of these compounds were fully characterized by 1H-NMR, 13C-NMR and HRMS (Fig. S1–6).

Synthetic route of PCPA and the sensing product PCO.

Photophysical properties of PCPA toward HA

The photophysical properties of PCPA toward HA were investigated by fluorescence spectroscopy. Initially, the solvent and pH effect were investigated. As shown in Fig. 1a and S7, PCPA showed relatively weak fluorescence at 454–484 nm (λex = 365 nm) in the mixed solutions of DMSO/PBS buffer (pH 7.40) with volume ratio from 10/0 to 0/10 (Table S2). The weak fluorescence should be attributed to the formation of excimer of PCPA induced by the solvent through π–π stacking. The dynamic light scattering (DLS) analysis of PCPA in the 6/4 solvent system showed the formation of nanoparticles with average size of 4.0 nm (Fig. S8). While PCPA + HA gave strong fluorescence at 404–430/424–476 nm, corresponding to the pyrene monomer of the sensing product PCO.30 Considering the fluorescence intensity and HA's solubility in water, DMSO/PBS buffer with volume ratio 6/4 was chosen as the analytical solvent. The investigation of pH showed that PCPA had almost no change within pH 4.00–10.00. With the addition of HA, the higher fluorescence intensities were obtained at pH 5.00–7.40 (Fig. 1b and Table S3). Therefore, physiological pH value 7.40 was chosen in the following analytical experiments. Moreover, the fluorescence of PCPA + HA kept almost unchanged under continuous irradiation at 365 nm for 90 min, indicating excellent photobleaching resistance (Fig. 1c). Finally, the response time of PCPA toward HA was studied by fluorescence and UV-vis spectra. Upon addition of HA, the fluorescence emission peak at 408/425 nm significantly increased within 20 min (Fig. 1d and S9). Additionally, the UV-vis spectra of PCPA with HA also showed a gradually decreased absorption peak at 398 nm and an increased peak at 362 nm, and kept unchanged after 20 min (Fig. S10). These results indicated that PCPA had a relatively faster response to HA.

Sensitivity and selectivity of PCPA toward HA

The sensitivity of PCPA for HA was investigated by traditional titration experiments. As shown in Fig. 2a, the fluorescence peak at 408/425 nm gradually increased with the increase amount of HA. And the fluorescence color of the solution changed from colorless to blue under 365 nm UV light (Fig. S11).

(a) Fluorescence spectra of PCPA (10 µM) with different amounts of HA (0–50 µM); (b) The relationship between F and the concentration of HA at 408 nm; (c) fluorescence spectra of PCPA (10 µM) with other analytes (100 µM); (d) Fluorescence intensities of PCPA (10 µM) with different analytes (100 µM) in the presence of HA (100 µM), 1–20: Cys, GSH, Hcy, His, Arg, Lys, GABA, Put, Spd, Spm, Cad, HH, MgSO4, FeSO4, FeCl3, CuSO4, Zn(NO3)2, NaClO, NaHSO3 and HA. λex = 365 nm, slit width = 5/10 nm.

The linear equation of the fluorescence intensities (F) at 408 nm versus HA (5–50 µM) was defined as F = 109.76 C(HA) + 322.87 with correlation coefficient of R2 = 0.9985 (Fig. 2b). The limit of detection (LOD) was calculated to be 0.017 µM through the (3σ/k) method, where σ is the standard deviation of the blank sample (n = 10) (Table S4) and k is the slope of the linear calibration curve. The low detection limit indicated that PCPA may serve as a more secure fluorescence sensor than reported probes for sensitive and quantitative analysis of HA (Table S1). The sensing selectivity of PCPA was investigated by adding common compounds and metal salts to the PCPA aqueous solutions, including Cys, GSH, Hcy, His, Arg, Lys, GABA, Put, Spd, Spm, Cad, HH (Hydrazine), MgSO4, FeSO4, FeCl3, CuSO4, Zn(NO3)2, NaClO, NaHSO3 and HA. As shown in Fig. 2c, the strong fluorescence emission at 408/425 nm was observed only after the addition of HA under the excitation of 365 nm UV light. The anti-interference ability of PCPA was also studied and the results indicated that the selectivity of PCPA for HA was almost not affected by the presence of other analytes (Fig. 2d). The relatively lower fluorescence intensities of PCPA + HA in the presence of HH and NaHSO3 might attribute to formation of non-fluorescence compounds PCN and PCPA-SO3H, which were detected by HRMS (Fig. S12 and 13).

Sensing mechanism

According to the reported works,30PCPA showed weak excimer fluorescence emission through π–π stacking and no monomer emission due to its twisted conformation induced by the cyano group through a twisted intramolecular charge transfer (TICT) process.34 Upon addition of HA, PCPA was converted to the oxime derivative (PCO) through nucleophilic addition–elimination of HA to the β-site of double bond, accompanied by significant enhancement in fluorescence at 408/425 nm. To verify this mechanism, we carried out a round-bottom flask experiment and the proposed sensing product PCO was synthesized with high yield. The fluorescence spectrum of PCO was measured and showed the same maximum emission peaks with that of PCPA + HA (100 µM) (Fig. S14). 1H NMR spectra of PCPA and PCO showed that the peak at 9.20 ppm belonged to the hydrogen at β-site of double bond in PCPA shifted to the high field of 9.15 ppm and a new peak appeared at 11.62 ppm belonged to hydroxyl group of PCO (Fig. 3).

Environmental water sample testing

To validate the practical application of PCPA for testing HA in actual environmental water samples, tap water and lake water were employed. The results were calculated by the work curves (Fig. S15-16) and listed in Table 1. The recoveries of HA in tap water and lake water were 95–104% with low relative standard deviations (RSD < 3%). This result makes PCPA a promising fluorescence probe for HA detection in environmental monitoring with high accuracy.

Paper-based test strips

Furthermore, portable paper-based test strips with PCPA were facially prepared and studied for the on-site detection of HA with the aid of a smartphone (Color Recognition) (Fig. S17). As shown in Fig. 4a, PCPA-loaded test strip displayed yellow color under sunlight and non-fluorescence emission under 365 nm UV light. Upon addition of HA, the color of the test strips gradually faded and the fluorescence changed to blue-purple with the increase concentration of HA. The RGB values were determined and showed increased B values and decreased R values (Fig. 4b). Therefore, the B/R values were employed and calculated to be linearly dependent on the HA concentration with R2 = 0.99 (Fig. 4c). The distinct emission enhancement indicated the portable paper-based test strip with PCPA was capable of fluorescent detection of HA.

Conclusions

In summary, a pyrene-based fluorescent probe (PCPA) was designed, facially synthesized and developed for the detection of HA in aqueous solution. PCPA showed remarkable sensitivity and selectivity to HA over other analytes and demonstrated acceptable recoveries and RSD in environmental water samples. Moreover, portable PCPA-loaded paper-based test strips were facially prepared, which enabled the on-site detection of HA with the aid of a smartphone. The easy availability and high sensitivity as well as selectivity of PCPA are expected to make it an alternative for HA detection in environmental monitoring.

Clinical outcomes and plasmid-associated features in KPC-producing Klebsiella pneumoniae bacteraemia
Authors: Yu-Chia Hsieh, Jia-Wen Wu, Chong-Wei Huang, Shian-Sen Shie, Ching-Tai Huang, Yung-Ting Yang, Tim Yu-Ting Lee, Wei-Chao Liao, Ruei-Lin Chiang, I-Hsin Sung, Shiao-Wen Li, Yi-Jiun Pan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe genetic epidemiology of carbapenem-resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae (CRKP) is geographically distinct. This study aimed to investigate the association of capsular types and plasmid-mediated factors with mortality in patients with CRKP bacteraemia.MethodsPatients with CRKP bacteraemia were enrolled at two medical centres between 2017 and 2021. Capsular typing, antimicrobial susceptibility testing, polymerase chain reaction (PCR) for carbapenemase-encoding genes in all strains, and whole-genome sequencing (WGS) of selected strains were performed.ResultsAmong 402 patients with monomicrobial CRKP bacteraemia, 238 (59.2%) strains were carbapenemase-producing. Of the K47 strains (n = 188), 89.9% carried blaKPC; of the K64 (n = 44) strains, 43.2% carried blaKPC or blaOXA-48-like. In a multivariate Cox analysis, blaKPC was a significant predictor of mortality specifically in K64 (aHR = 3.64; p = 0.004) but not in K47. Other carbapenemase genes did not show significant association with their capsular types. WGS identified the 85 selected isolates as ST11 or ST11-1LV; among them, K64-blaKPC(+) strains (n = 11) clustered separately from K64-blaKPC(-) (n = 33) and K47-blaKPC(+) strains (n = 41). Plasmid structure analysis revealed the distinctness of K64-associated blaKPC plasmids which were generally larger than those in K47 and characterized by the presence of a 57-kb region encoding a T4SS module along with Escherichia coli virulence genes traT and traJ. Among 188 KPC-producing K. pneumoniae, independent mortality predictors included Pitt score (aHR = 1.27; p < 0.001), pneumonia (aHR = 1.84; p = 0.01), and ceftazidime-avibactam treatment (aHR = 0.38, p = 0.002). Capsular type was significantly prognostic (p = 0.02), with K47 showing a reduced risk of death (aHR = 0.33; p = 0.009) compared to other types. Additionally, traT was identified as a potential marker for fatal outcomes (aHR = 3.12; p = 0.05).ConclusionsCapsular types and the structural characteristics of blaKPC plasmids are associated with clinical outcomes in CRKP bacteraemia. Although traT is a putative marker for high-risk lineages, clinical severity remains the primary predictor of mortality. The capsular type–specific distribution of conjugative plasmids and the co-carriage of traT with blaKPC suggest a potential route for the concurrent dissemination of resistance and lineage-associated genetic features.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s41182-026-00987-7.

Introduction

Klebsiella pneumoniae is a major cause of healthcare-associated infections worldwide, including pneumonia, bloodstream, intra-abdominal, urinary tract, wound or surgical site infections, and meningitis. Under constant antibiotic selective pressure in many Asian and other tropical/subtropical settings, carbapenem-resistant K. pneumoniae (CRKP) has become endemic [1, 2], and is now ranked by the World Health Organization as a critical priority pathogen because of its high mortality and limited therapeutic options [3–5].

In Taiwan, the proportion of CRKP infections has been increasing in the intensive care units of medical centres, with an infection rate rising from 7.1% in 2009 to 41.5% in 2018 among all K. pneumoniae infections observed in the Taiwan Nosocomial Infection Surveillance system [6]; in addition, CRKP has a higher mortality rate than non-CRKP infections [7], highlighting a major nosocomial threat. Carbapenem resistance in K. pneumoniae is mainly mediated by plasmid‑encoded carbapenemases, particularly K. pneumoniae carbapenemase (blaKPC), followed by oxacillinase-48-like carbapenemase (blaOXA-48-like), and New Delhi metallo-ꞵ-lactamase (blaNDM) [8], as well as combinations of extended-spectrum β-lactamases (ESBLs) and ampicillinase class C (AmpC) enzymes with porin loss and efflux pump overexpression [8]. Patients with carbapenemase-producing K. pneumoniae infection generally have poorer outcomes than those with non-carbapenemase-producing K. pneumoniae infection, suggesting that carbapenemase-producing K. pneumoniae might be more virulent [9]. With the continuing evolution of more virulent CRKP strains through horizontal transfer of plasmids and recombination, a step toward developing new therapeutics is urgently warranted.

Capsular polysaccharides, resistant to complement-mediated killing and phagocytosis, contribute to K. pneumoniae virulence. Targeting the capsule of K. pneumoniae to develop novel preventative and therapeutic control strategies against CRKP infections is therefore biologically plausible. Recently, Kobayashi et al. (2018) showed that antibodies specific for capsule significantly enhanced human serum bactericidal activity and promoted phagocytosis and killing of this pathogen by human neutrophils [10]. However, studies on capsular types related to the clinical characteristics, disease severity, response to antibiotic treatment, and outcomes of CRKP strains are limited in the literature. This study aimed to investigate the clinical correlation and presence of carbapenemase genes with different capsular types in patients with CRKP bacteraemia. Whole-genome sequencing (WGS) was performed on representative isolates of predominant capsular types to characterize their specific features and possible carbapenemase-associated virulence genes.

Methods

Study design and population

This study was conducted at the Chang Gung Memorial Hospital (CGMH)-Lin Kou branch, a 3,700-bed medical centre in northern Taiwan, from January 2017 to December 2021, and CGMH-Kaohsiung branch, a 2,700-bed medical centre in southern Taiwan, from January 2020 to July 2021. K. pneumoniae bacteraemia was defined by the presence of ≥ 1 positive blood culture results for patients with signs and symptoms of infection after hospital admission. For patients with multiple episodes of bacteraemia, only the first episode was included. Patients aged < 18 years and those with incomplete medical records were excluded. Medical records were reviewed to collect clinical information which were shown in Additional file 1. The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the CGMH (approval number: 201801433B0, 202301379B0).

Microbiological studies, carbapenemase and virulence gene detection, and capsular typing

K. pneumoniae was identified using matrix-assisted laser desorption time-of-flight mass spectrometry. The minimum inhibitory concentrations (MICs) of the tested antibiotics were determined using a BD Phoenix™ M50 automatic detection machine. CRKP was defined as MICs of ≥ 4 μg/mL for either imipenem or meropenem or MICs of ≥ 2 μg/mL for ertapenem [11]. The carbapenemase genes (blaKPC, blaOXA-48-like, blaNDM, blaGES, blaVIM, blaIMP, blaSPM, blaGIM, and blaSIM), virulence genes (rmpA, rmpA2, iroN, iutA, and ybtA), and capsular synthesis gene (wzy) were screened using polymerase chain reaction (PCR) following methods from a previous study [12]. Additionally, traT, traJ, and traC were detected based on whole genome sequence data generated in this study. Primers designed in this study were shown in Table S1.

WGS and analyses

WGS was performed on a total of 85 isolates using a dual-platform strategy: 49 isolates were sequenced via long-read Nanopore technology, while 36 were processed using short-read Illumina sequencing. To ensure the accurate resolution of complex plasmid architectures and achieve complete assembly, all 11 KPC-producing K64 isolates and five representative KPC-producing K47 isolates were specifically prioritized for Nanopore sequencing. Detailed sequencing parameters and individual isolate metadata are available in Additional file 1 and Table S2, respectively.

Statistical analysis

Analysis of variance (ANOVA) test or Kruskal–Wallis test was performed for continuous variables, and Chi-Square or Fisher’s exact tests were performed for categorical variables. We used Cox regression models for 30-day all-cause mortality; covariates with a p < 0.1 in the univariate analysis were selected for the multivariate model. Stratified analysis based on different capsular types and the presence of blaKPC was performed to determine the impact of covariates on 30-day all-cause mortality. All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS 15.0. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

Results

Carbapenemase genes, capsular types, and antimicrobial susceptibility

The enrolment of study participants was shown in Fig. 1, in which 402 strains were included. Most strains collected in the study were from the CGMH-Lin Kou branch, likely due to a few carbapenemase-producing K. pneumoniae outbreaks during the study period. Among the 402 strains, carbapenemase genes were detected in 238. blaKPC, present in 188 (~ 79.0%) strains, was predominant. Other carbapenemase genes, blaOXA-48-like, blaNDM, blaVIM, and blaIMP, were found in 38 (~ 16.0%), 22 (~ 9.2%), 10 (~ 4.2%), and one strain, respectively. Three common CRKP capsular types, K47, K64, and K51 were screened by wzy PCR. The results showed that among the 238 carbapenemase-producing strains, the main type was K47 (170, ~ 71.4%), followed by K51 (23, ~ 9.7%) and K64 (19, ~ 8.0%); K47 was strongly associated with blaKPC, K51 was strongly associated with the co-carriage of blaOXA-48-like and blaNDM, and K64 was associated with blaKPC and blaOXA-48-like (Table S3).

Differences in antimicrobial susceptibility have been observed among various capsular types. The ertapenem, imipenem, meropenem, cefepime, and ceftriaxone resistance rates of other capsular-type strains were lower than those of K47, K51, and K64 strains. In general, K47 strains show high antimicrobial resistance to different antibiotics. Gentamicin resistance rates were lower in K51 strains (15%) compared to K47 (96%), K64 (84%), and other capsular-type (72%) strains (Table S4). Another observation across various capsular types was that, compared with K47 and K51, which both had high carbapenemase carriage rates (~ 90%), K64 had a carriage rate of only 43% (Table S3). Nevertheless, K64 still exhibited high carbapenem resistance rates: 50–84% resistance to meropenem, imipenem, and ertapenem (Table S4). This finding suggests that the remaining 57% of K64 strains without carbapenemases may exhibit carbapenem resistance through other mechanisms, such as porin loss combined with AmpC or ESBL β-lactamases [13].

The distribution of demographic and clinical factors by K. pneumoniae capsular-types

No significant differences among patients with different capsular types regarding sex and Charlson scores were observed (Table 1). Patients with K64 bacteraemia were oldest (p = 0.02), whereas patients with K51 bacteraemia had highest rates of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (p = 0.03), leukaemia (p = 0.03) and nosocomial infection (p < 0.001). Patients with K47 isolates had a highest rate of pneumonia (p < 0.001) and a lowest rate of receiving in vitro active antibiotic treatment within 24 h (p = 0.01). Ceftazidime-avibactam was introduced at CGMH in June 2020, and MIC data for this agent has been provided since that time. Other capsular types show higher resistance to ceftazidime-avibactam (p = 0.04). During the study period, the clinical use of ceftazidime-avibactam was primarily guided by phenotypic susceptibility results or clinical judgment. The use of ceftazidime-avibactam in each group of K types was not different. Pitt bacteraemia scores were significantly higher (p < 0.001) for K47, K51, and K64 strains. The K51 strains had the highest rate of 30-day mortality, followed by K47 and K64. Association between carbapenemase and outcomes by K. pneumoniae capsular-types

In the stratified analysis by capsular type (Table 2), after adjustment, in vitro active antibiotic treatment within 24 h (aHR = 0.61; p = 0.05), ceftazidime-avibactam use during the treatment course (aHR = 0.28; p < 0.001), pneumonia (aHR = 2.1; p = 0.003) and Pitt score (aHR = 1.29; p < 0.001) were associated with 30-day all-cause mortality in K47 strains. Gene blaKPC did not influence mortality outcomes in K47 and other capsular types but independently increased mortality in K64 (aHR = 3.64; p = 0.004). Among the 44 K64 isolates, 19 (43.2%) carried blaKPC or blaOXA-48-like carbapenemase genes. Supplemental testing of ceftazidime-avibactam MICs across all 44 K64 isolates revealed no evidence of resistance (Table 1). The lack of a significant mortality benefit for ceftazidime-avibactam in the K64 group likely reflects limited sample size rather than a lack of therapeutic efficacy. Gene blaOXA-48-like and blaNDM did not show significant association with their respective capsular types. The Pitt bacteraemia score was a predictive risk factor for mortality in all capsular types. Phylogenetic analysis reveals clade differentiation between ST11-K47 and K64 lineages

Considering that blaKPC was associated higher mortality in K64, but not in K47, we performed WGS on all 44 K64 strains (by Nanopore) and 41 randomly selected K47 strains (5 strains by Nanopore; 36 strains by Illumina) for genomic comparison. According to WGS, the 85 strains were recognised as either ST11 or ST11-1LV (Fig. 1B and Table S2). The total dataset, which consisted of 87 K. pneumoniae genome sequences, including two sequences from China (KP16932, a K47 strain, and KP47434, a K64 strain) were subjected to core genome single-nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) analysis, and these K47 and K64 strains clustered into one lineage (Fig. 2). Capsular types were strongly associated with lipopolysaccharide types, with K64 isolates predominantly associated with O2a. Strains of KPC-producing and non-KPC-producing K64 clustered into different clades. The former clustered together and diverged from KPC-producing K47 strains. The virulent K64 strain KP47434 from China, which was suggested to have evolved from K47 strain through capsule switching, was related to our K64-blaKPC(+) strains. Distinct blaKPC plasmid architecture and virulence gene enrichment in K64 compared with K47

We speculated that the K64 blaKPC plasmids were distinct from the K47 blaKPC plasmids, and thus virulence factors were evaluated by VirulenceFinder on the WGS of 41 KPC-producing K47 strains and 11 KPC-producing K64 strains. The results revealed the presence of two Escherichia coli virulence genes, traT in 81.8% (9/11) and traJ in 90.9% (10/11) of KPC-producing K64 strains. In contrast, these genes were detected at much lower frequencies in KPC-producing K47 strains, with traT present in only 2% (1/41) and traJ entirely absent (0/41). To ensure accurate characterization of plasmid structures and gene organization, comparative plasmid analysis was restricted to isolates with long-read nanopore sequencing data, including all 11 KPC-producing K64 isolates and 5 randomly selected KPC-producing K47 isolates. We found that plasmids of KPC-producing K64 strains varied in size, and most belonged to the IncFII group (Table 3). The two virulence genes, traT and traJ, were located in a ~ 57-kb region encoding an F-type type IV secretion system (T4SSF) (Fig. 3). Gene traT, associated with large, conjugative F-type plasmids (encoding T4SSF) was responsible for surface exclusion by F-type plasmids [14] and also known to be involved in pathogenesis by enhancing resistance of E. coli to serum [15] and phagocytosis [16]. TraJ, a regulator encoded by the tra operon of F-type plasmids, enhances the ability of E. coli to invade the central nervous system and cause meningitis in neonatal rats [17, 18]. While traT and traJ have been reported to contribute to virulence in E. coli, their functional roles in our K. pneumoniae isolates remain to be determined. The role of two E. coli virulence genes traT and traJ (cohort-wide PCR validation) in 30-day all-cause mortality

PCR detection was performed on the full cohort 402 strains to identify two documented virulence genes (traT and traJ) and traC, an ATPase gene involved in the type IV secretion system (T4SS). Both traJ and traC are part of the tra operon of F-type plasmids, which contains over 20 genes responsible for T4SS machinery enabling conjugation. Their detection was used as an indicator of T4SS presence.

K64 strains carried the highest rates of traT, traJ, and traJ/C (T4SS) (20–23%) compared to other capsular types (2–7%) (p < 0.001) (Table 4). The genes traT and traJ and traC were seen in 80–90% of K64-blaKPC(+) strains which validated their preferential association with K64-blaKPC plasmids (Table 3). Among 188 KPC-producing CRKP strains, multivariate analysis identified that traT (aHR = 3.12; p = 0.05, borderline significant) and capsular types (p = 0.02) were independently associated with 30-day all-cause mortality, irrespective of ceftazidime-avibactam usage (aHR = 0.38; p = 0.002), pneumonia (aHR = 1.84; p = 0.01), Pitt score (aHR = 1.27; p < 0.001) and traJ presence (aHR = 1.96; p = 0.48) (Table 5). Specifically, K47 demonstrated the lowest adjusted mortality (aHR = 0.33; p = 0.009) compared to other capsular types. Discussion

More than 80 capsular types of K. pneumoniae exist. However, only certain types have high virulence, fitness, or antibiotic resistance related to invasive disease [11, 19]. In the present study, outcomes of patients with CRKP bacteraemia varied depending on capsular types and the diversity of KPC-producing plasmid-harbouring potential virulence factors.

The mortality related to CRKP bacteraemia varies according to bacterial virulence, quality of the health care system, and advances in effective treatment. South America reported a 50% improvement in survival rates with effective treatment, while the USA had a 30% improvement [20]. Generally, the 30-day mortality in patients with carbapenemase-producing CRKP bacteraemia is about 40–45% [21], which is similar to our findings. Sequence type (ST) 258 was the most prevalent KPC-producing CRKP clone in North America, Latin America, and Europe [22]. ST258 persists because of mutations in genes encoding effectors of biofilm production and iron acquisition, bearing integrative conjugative element 10 (ICEKp10), and harbouring yersiniabactin and colibactin virulence factors for rapid dissemination in the hospital environment [20, 23]. ST258 mostly belongs to capsular types K106 and K107 in the USA [20]. ST11, a single-locus variant of ST258, circulates with K39 or K105 capsules in South America, and the K47 capsules in Asia, especially China [22]. Carbapenem-resistant ST11 strains acquired a virulence plasmid carrying rmpA2 and aerobactin biosynthesis genes to form a hypermucoid and hypervirulent clone, causing a deadly outbreak in China [24]. Since 2016, this particular clone has shifted its capsular type from K47 to K64 and carries a rmpA2/rmpA + peg-344 positive plasmid, which is associated with increased mortality [25]. K64/ST11 is more virulent, resistant to human neutrophils, and has enhanced transmission compared with K47/ST11 [25]. In Taiwan, K. pneumoniae ST11 with K47 capsules expressing blaOXA-48 and blaKPC-2 is the predominant circulating clone [26, 27]. Huang et al. reported that only one K47/ST11 carried rmpA/rmpA2 positive plasmid among 63 strains collected between 2012 and 2014 [28]. Consistent with these observations, only a few CRKP strains in our study carried rmpA or other classical virulence factors, and 89.9% (169/188) of K47 strains and 25% (11/44) of K64 strains carried blaKPC. Among KPC-producing CRKP, the mortality risk for K47 infection showed a 0.7-fold decrease compared to other capsular types after adjustment. Early detection of the K47 capsular type and prompt ceftazidime-avibactam treatment may potentially enhance the prognosis for K47 patients.

Studies have shown that KPC-producing Enterobacterales, predominantly K. pneumoniae, are significantly associated with poorer outcomes compared to non-carbapenemase- or NDM-producing Enterobacterales, indicating that KPC-producing plasmids might harbour other virulence factors contributing to higher mortality [9, 29]. However, types of KPC-producing plasmids relevant to clinical outcomes have rarely been characterized. In our study, blaKPC was an independent predictor of mortality, mainly in K64 strains but not in K47 or other strains.

Most interestingly, the structural distinctness of K64-associated blaKPC plasmids suggests a specialized evolutionary path for these high-risk lineages. These plasmids were found to be generally larger than those in K47 and are characterized by the presence of a 57-kb region encoding a T4SSF module, which implies their self-transmissible ability. This prediction is further supported by oriTfinder analysis, which indicates that 9 out of 11 K64 KPC-plasmids possess a complete conjugation module—including oriT, a relaxase, a type IV coupling protein (T4CP), and a T4SS gene cluster—whereas only 1 out of 5 K47 KPC-plasmids possesses a complete conjugation module. Notably, the K64 blaKPC plasmids were distinct from the previously reported highly transmissible and remarkably stable blaKPC plasmid pKPC2 [30] which has a different incompatibility group and lacks the T4SS found in the K64 blaKPC plasmids. Dissemination of carbapenemase genes is complex and diverse and may be facilitated by miscellaneous or single plasmids [31], or may undergo recombination and rearrangement to form hybrids [31].

The co-presence of the tra operon (essential for T4SS machinery) and tra-associated genes (e.g., traT) in these K64 plasmids is a notable genetic feature. Among the 10 T4SS encoding blaKPC plasmids of K64, 9 harbour a traT gene, which is known to be commonly associated with T4SS encoding regions [32]. The outer membrane lipoprotein TraT exposed at the cell surface plays a role in surface exclusion which blocks the conjugative transfer of plasmids to cells harbouring identical/closely related plasmids. Besides, traT was considered a virulence factor that confers serum resistance and phagocytosis resistance in E. coli and its presence has been implicated in the virulence of other bacteria as well [33]. While its functional role in K. pneumoniae pathogenesis requires further experimental validation, the co-distribution of the T4SS module and traT in these high-risk lineages suggests they serve as potential markers for their dissemination. Furthermore, they are co-localized on a carbapenemase-producing plasmid. To our knowledge, the co-carriage of blaKPC and traT on a single conjugative plasmid has not been previously highlighted in CRKP. In conclusion, while the T4SS machinery functions primarily as a conjugation engine for horizontal gene transfer rather than a direct virulence effector, its high-frequency transfer capability facilitates the concurrent dissemination of resistance and putative virulence markers, which may be associated with the persistence and rapid spread of these high-risk CRKP lineages in healthcare settings.

Our clinical findings point toward traT as a potential marker for virulent KPC-producing clones. However, caution is warranted because its independent association with 30-day all-cause mortality (aHR = 3.12; p = 0.05) is borderline significant and characterized by a wide confidence interval (1.0–9.7). Therefore, large-scale WGS studies are necessary to definitively determine whether traT is a direct virulence effector or a surrogate marker for high-risk CRKP lineages.

Our study had some limitations. First, these strains may not be sufficiently representative because they were collected from only two medical centres. Second, not all strains underwent WGS, which precluded a comprehensive high-resolution analysis of all bacterial genetic features across the entire cohort. Third, although traT was independently associated with 30-day all-cause mortality among KPC-producing CRKP (aHR = 3.12; p = 0.05) strains, we cannot exclude the possibility that other unknown factors associated with traT may be responsible for the observed outcomes rather than traT per se, meaning that the potential role of traT requires further investigation. Unfortunately, the multidrug-resistant nature of these strains makes it difficult to use a selective marker for the genetic manipulation required to construct deletion mutants; nevertheless, our findings suggest a possible association and point to the possibility that the presence of traT may be a potential marker for highly virulent KPC-producing clones, although its direct role in pathogenesis and its validity as a clinical indicator warrant further validation in larger cohorts.

Conclusions

In this study, we identified that the clinical outcome of KPC-producing K. pneumoniae bacteraemia is associated with capsular types and their characteristic carbapenemase-producing plasmids. Furthermore, we resolved the blaKPC plasmids structures from K47 and K64 strains; this comparative analysis revealed an enrichment of conjugation-associated and potential virulence-associated genes (e.g., traT and traJ) in K64 strains, providing genomic evidence of association for the hypothesis of conjugative resistance plasmids carrying potential virulence markers. Although an association between traT and mortality was observed, its borderline significance (aHR = 3.12; p = 0.05) and wide confidence interval (1.0–9.7) indicate limited statistical robustness. Thus, clinical severity remains the primary determinant of patient outcomes, as Pitt bacteraemia score and pneumonia were the most consistent and robust predictors of mortality across analyses. Consequently, while our findings point to a potential link between plasmid-associated factors and mortality, these results should be considered exploratory and hypothesis-generating, requiring further large-scale validation. Nevertheless, the capsular type–specific distribution of conjugative plasmids and the co-carriage of tra-associated genes (e.g., traT) with blaKPC suggest a potential route for the concurrent dissemination of resistance and lineage-associated genetic features. These findings provide valuable insights into the unique characteristics of emerging K. pneumoniae clones and have significant implications for global disease management and infection control.

Acing the Base in Adult Spinal Deformity
Authors: Reuben C.C. Soh
Published: 2026

The rigorous multicenter randomized trial by Elder et al. provides an important contribution to our evolving understanding of pelvic fixation strategies in adult spinal deformity (ASD) surgery. While pelvic fixation failure remains one of the most challenging mechanical complications in long-segment fusion, the authors’ demonstration of a significant reduction in radiographically evident pelvic fixation failures with the addition of triangular titanium implants (TTI) to standard S2-alar-iliac (S2AI) screws is encouraging. Their finding—an 11.0% failure rate in the S2AI+TTI group versus 20.4% in the S2AI-alone group—suggests that achieving 2 points of sacroiliac fixation may improve construct stability by enhancing load-sharing and reducing sacroiliac micromotion1.

The study’s strength lies in its randomized design, performance across 29 international centers, and high-quality computed tomography (CT)-based follow-up at 2 years. Its independent radiographic adjudication also gives it strong methodological validity.

TTI performed well in the study, with no implant migration or breakage, and sacral alar lucency rates were lower in the augmented construct. The latter is an observation consistent with improved biomechanical performance. These results position S2AI+TTI fixation as a technically feasible and potentially impactful strategy in complex ASD cases.

That being said, the findings should be interpreted with measured optimism. Notably, the 24-month follow-up period, while standard for early radiographic evaluations, is insufficient to fully appreciate the long-term behavior of these constructs. Many clinically meaningful mechanical failures, such as rod fractures, delayed loosening, or progressive junctional issues, often emerge beyond the 2-year mark. While the trend toward increased proximal rod fractures in the S2AI+TTI group did not reach significance, it raises the possibility that altering distal stiffness may redistribute forces proximally. Only extended follow-up—ideally 5 years or longer—will determine whether the early advantages of the dual-point fixation construct will translate into durable long-term benefits with successful fusion across the entire implant length.

A second important limitation is the heterogeneity of the bone mineral density in the cohort. Although patients with severe osteoporosis were appropriately excluded, the enrolled cohort included individuals with a broad range of bone quality. Given the known importance of sacral and iliac bone density in the performance of both S2AI screws and TTI, this heterogeneity introduces uncertainty into the study. For example, patients with osteopenia may respond differently from those with normal bone density in terms of lucency development, osseointegration, and implant longevity. Future studies incorporating routine DXA (dual x-ray absorptiometry) or opportunistic CT Hounsfield unit assessments, and analyzing outcomes according to bone quality, will clarify whether certain biologic bone environments derive disproportionate benefit from TTI-augmented fixation2.

Finally, it may be prudent to consider the additional cost of routine TTI for long constructs. While reducing pelvic fixation failures may lower the overall economic burden of revisions, TTI placement adds implant expense and operative resource utilization for payers. It would be valuable to run a formal cost-effectiveness analysis to understand the economic implications of routine dual-point SI fixation3.

Overall, this study represents a meaningful step forward, and it offers a compelling early argument for reinforcing S2AI fixation with TTI in long-segment ASD constructs. Future investigations into long-term durability, bone quality interactions, and economic considerations would be the next step toward adopting this technique. As our field continues to refine alignment goals and enhance construct resilience, innovations such as dual-point SI fixation hold promise—provided we continue to evaluate them with the same scientific rigor exemplified by this study.

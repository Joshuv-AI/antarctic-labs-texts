Efficacy and Safety of T‐DXd in HER2+ and Low/Zero Metastatic BCBM: A Retrospective Multicenter Real‐World Study
Authors: Qingru Zhou, Yuanyuan Li, Ni He, Guorong Zou, Mengqian Ni, Lulu Zhang, Shu Dun, Zhanhong Chen, Kejun Liu, Yabing Cao, Jiajia Huang, Fei Xu, Zhongyu Yuan, Shusen Wang, Yanxia Shi, Yonggao Mou, Anli Yang, Lixia Li, Xin An
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAccumulating data demonstrate the robust overall and intracranial activity of trastuzumab deruxtecan (T‐DXd) in HER2‐positive breast cancer brain metastasis (BCBM). Limited data are available on HER2‐low/zero BCBM, as well as patients with symptomatic active BM, leptomeningeal disease (LMD), and poor performance status. This multicenter, retrospective, real‐world study enrolled patients diagnosed with HER2‐positive or low/zero BCBM receiving T‐DXd. Progression‐free survival (PFS), intracranial progression‐free survival (IC‐PFS), overall survival (OS), objective response rate (ORR), disease control rate (DCR), IC‐ORR, IC‐DCR, and adverse events (AEs) were evaluated. From January 2020 to July 2024, 58 HER2‐positive and 30 HER2‐low/zero patients were enrolled. In the HER2‐positive cohort, the median PFS, IC‐PFS, and OS were 11, 13, and 46 months, respectively. ORR and DCR were 65.2% and 91.3% systemically, with IC‐ORR and IC‐DCR of 61.0%/90.2% (RECIST 1.1) and 59.5%/88.1% (RANO‐BM). In the HER2‐low/zero cohort, median PFS, IC‐PFS, and OS were 4, 5, and 26 months. ORR and DCR were 33.3% and 66.7%. IC‐ORR and IC‐DCR were 44.4% and 88.9% by RECIST 1.1 and RANO‐BM. HR+/HER2‐low patients had significantly longer PFS than HR‐/HER2‐low (10 vs. 3 months, p < 0.001). In the LMD cohort, the 12‐month IC‐PFS rates were 67.7% and 60.0% in HER2‐positive and low/zero cohorts. The most common AEs included fatigue (45.9%) and appetite loss (25.2%). Seven (8.0%) patients suffered interstitial lung disease (ILD), including 2 (2.3%) grade 3. T‐DXd showed substantial and durable overall and intracranial efficacy in HER2‐positive and low/zero BCBM, including challenging subgroups with active BM or LMD.Keywords: breast cancer brain metastasis (BCBM), HER2‐low/zero, HER2‐positive, leptomeningeal disease (LMD), trastuzumab deruxtecan (T‐DXd)

Introduction

Breast cancer (BC) is the most common malignancy worldwide, and the second most frequent cause of brain metastases (BM) among solid tumors [1]. Moreover, the incidence of BM has steadily increased over time alongside the improvement of systemic treatment and prolonged survival. The incidence of BCBM differs by molecular subtypes. As many as 50% of human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) positive advanced BC will develop BM [2]. The incidence of BM in HER2‐low/zero BC is not well described, among which triple negative disease shows a high prevalence of 25%–56%, while hormone receptor‐positive subtype has a low incidence of 8%–15% [3, 4, 5]. Compared with patients without BM, BCBM generally has poor survival outcomes and limited treatment strategies. Current standard treatment is local therapy including neurosurgical resection and radiation therapy (stereotactic or whole‐brain). Traditional systemic therapy showed less intracranial efficacy compared with extracranial metastases, probably due to the blood–brain barrier (BBB) that limits the penetrability of drugs, and thus patients with BCBM were generally excluded from clinical trials [6, 7]. Novel anti‐HER2 tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) and antibody‐drug conjugates (ADC) demonstrate promising intracranial efficacy. Anti‐HER2 TKI tucatinib is the first drug approved to improve survival in HER2‐positive BCBM [8]. Other anti‐HER2 TKIs including lapatinib, neratinib, and pyrotinib also demonstrate promising antitumor activity against central nervous system (CNS) metastases [9, 10, 11, 12]. Recent evidence shows the encouraging intracranial efficacy of the anti‐HER2 ADC drug T‐DXd in HER2‐positive BCBM. A pooled analysis of DESTINY‐Breast (DB) 01, 02, and 03 trials showed T‐DXd monotherapy achieved an intracranial objective response rate (IC‐ORR) of 45.2% in stable BM and 45.5% in active BM [13, 14]. The large‐scale phase 3b/4 DB 12 study further established the robust efficacy of T‐DXd in HER2+ BCBM [15]. In addition, preliminary results of several small‐sized prospective studies including DB 07, TUXEDO‐1, and DEBBRAH trial also demonstrated encouraging intracranial responses of T‐DXd in HER2+ mBC with active BM [16, 17, 18]. Even though patients enrolled in these trials were selected, those who were pretreated with anti‐HER2 TKIs, had significant neurological symptoms, or had leptomeningeal disease (LMD) are common in real‐world clinical practice but were seldom enrolled in these studies. Thus, the efficacy of T‐DXd in these scenarios remains unclear. On the other hand, T‐DXd also demonstrated remarkable extracranial efficacy in HER2‐low/zero mBC as reported in DB 04, 06, and Daisy trials, but limited data are available on its efficacy on HER2‐low/zero BCBM [19, 20, 21]. Only 24 patients with asymptomatic BM were enrolled in the DB 04 trial, which reported an IC‐ORR of 25% [22]. There has been no report of T‐DXd in HER2 zero BCBM to date.

To help make treatment decisions in real‐world clinical practice, we conducted this multicenter, real‐world study (RWS) to evaluate the efficacy and safety of T‐DXd in a diverse population of BCBM, including HER2‐positive and low/zero, stable and active, or having neurological symptoms, and LMD.

Patients and Methods

This multicenter RWS included patients with BCBM who had HER2‐positive, low, and zero disease and were treated with T‐DXd in Sun Yat‐Sen University Cancer Center (SYSUCC), Affiliated Hospital of Guangdong Medical University, Nongken Central Hospital of Guangdong Province, the Central Hospital of Pan‐Yu, Kiang Wu Hospital of Macau, the Third Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat‐Sen University, and Dongguan People's Hospital between January 2020 and July 2024 (Figure 1). The inclusion criteria were: (1) histologically confirmed BC; (2) brain metastases proved by magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) scan; (3) HER2‐positive was defined as HER2 IHC3+ or 2+ and FISH+. HER2‐low/zero diseases included HER2 IHC 0, 1+, and 2 +, FISH‐; (4) received at least 1 cycle of T‐DXd either as monotherapy or in combination with other drugs; and (5) available clinical data. The study was approved by the ethical committee of SYSUCC (No. B2024‐385‐01). The requirement for individual informed consent was waived by the committee due to the retrospective nature of the study.

Data Collection and Efficacy Evaluation

Data were extracted from medical charts within the hospital information system (HIS), including the demographics, symptoms, tumor characteristics, prior treatments, laboratory test results, and imaging scans. Patients received T‐DXd until disease progression, intolerable toxicities including financial toxicity, or death. The definition of active BCBM was as follows: (1) newly diagnosed and untreated BM; (2) CNS disease progressed after local treatment or systemic treatment. Progression‐free survival (PFS) was defined as the time from the first commencement of T‐DXd to disease progression either intracranial or extracranial or death; Intracranial progression‐free survival (IC‐PFS) was determined as the time from treatment initiation to brain metastases progression or death; Overall survival (OS) was defined as the time of starting to receive T‐DXd treatment to the date of death for any cause or the last follow‐up. Objective response rate (ORR) was the proportion of complete response (CR) and partial response (PR) reassessed by an independent radiologist according to RECIST1.1 criteria. DCR was defined as the proportion of patients who achieved CR, PR, and stable disease (SD). IC‐ORR includes the proportion of patients who achieved CR or PR for intracranial disease. Response assessment in neuro‐oncology brain metastases (RANO‐BM) criteria was also applied to fully assess intracranial response. OS, PFS, and IC‐PFS were evaluated in the whole population. Only patients with measurable lesions and continuous radical examination are eligible to evaluate ORR and IC‐ORR. Treatment‐emergent adverse events (TEAEs) were measured by type, incidence, and severity according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) V5.0. The cut‐off day was Dec. 2024.

Statistical Analysis

Continuous variables were presented with their median and interquartile range (IQR). Categorical variables were described as frequency counts and proportion percentages. The PFS and OS with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were assessed using Kaplan–Meier estimates and the Log‐rank test. All statistical analyses were carried out with SPSS version 25.0 software and R version 4.4.1. A two‐sided p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline Characteristic

A total of 88 patients were enrolled, including 58 with HER2 positive disease, 27 had HER2‐low disease, and 3 had HER2 zero. Baseline clinical and pathological characteristics were detailed in Table 1. The median age was 51 (range 33–79) in the HER2‐positive group and 45 (range 30–76) in the HER2‐low/zero group. The majority of patients (89.8%, 79/88) had coexisted extracranial metastases, including liver, lung, bone, and lymph nodes. Sixteen had LMD, including 4 patients who presented with isolated LMD, and 7 of them were treated in combination with bevacizumab. In the HER2‐positive cohort, 74.1% (43/58) patients were diagnosed with active BM, and 72.4% (42/58) presented with neural symptoms. In the HER2‐low cohort, 70% (21/30) of patients were diagnosed with active symptomatic BM. The median lines of previous systemic treatment for metastatic disease were 3 (range 1–10) in HER2‐positive group, and 4 (range 0–11) in HER2‐low/zero group. All HER2‐positive patients were pretreated with trastuzumab, and 60.3%, 84.5%, and 25.9% were pretreated with pertuzumab, TKIs, and T‐DM1, respectively. More than half of patients in the HER2‐positive (60.3%, 33/58) and HER2‐low/zero (70%, 21/30) cohorts had previous local therapy for BM, respectively.

Efficacy Data

HER2‐Positive Cohort

With a median follow‐up time of 13 months (95% CI: 9.1–16.9), the median PFS and OS were 11 months (95% CI: 7.0–14.9) and 46 months (95% CI:21.8–70.2), and the 12‐month PFS and OS rates were 42.9% (95% CI:29.4–62.7), and 88.7% (95% CI: 79.7–98.8). Median IC‐PFS was 13 months (95% CI: 10.0–16.0), and 12‐month IC‐PFS rate was 52.6% (95% CI: 38.2–72.3) (Table 2 and Figure 2). There was no difference in PFS and IC‐PFS between HR‐positive/HER2‐positive (HR+/HER2+) and HR‐negative/HER2‐positive (HR‐/HER2+) subtypes (Figure 3A, 3B). There were 46 and 41 patients who could be assessed for overall and intracranial response analysis. Overall ORR was 65.2% (95% CI: 49.7–78.2) and DCR was 91.3% (95% CI: 78.3–97.2), with 3 (6.7%) patients achieving CR. The IC‐ORR and IC‐DCR were 61.0% (95% CI:44.5–75.4) and 90.2% (95% CI: 75.9–96.8) by RECIST 1.1 criteria, and 59.5% (95% CI: 43.4–74.0) and 88.1% (95% CI: 73.6–95.5) by RANO‐BM criteria (Table 2 and Figure 4A, 4B). Subgroup analysis revealed that T‐DXd maintained efficacy irrespective of BM status (symptomatic vs. asymptomatic), prior TKI exposure, or poor performance status (ECOG ≥ 2) (Figure 5A).

HER2‐Low/Zero Cohort

With median follow‐up time of 10 months (95% CI: 4.5–15.5), the median PFS and OS were 4 months (95% CI: 2.5–5.5) and 26 months (95% CI:13.7‐ ‐‐). The 12‐month PFS and OS rates were 26.0% (95% CI:12.9–52.3) and 55.6% (95% CI: 39.5–78.2). The median CNS PFS was 5 months (95% CI: 3.2–6.8) and IC‐PFS rate at 12 months was 44.6 (95% CI: 26.4–75.4) (Table 2 and Figure 2). Additionally, patients in HR+/HER2‐low/zero subgroup (n = 19) showed better PFS and IC‐PFS than those in HR‐/HER2‐low/zero subgroup (n = 11), 10 versus 3 months, p < 0.001, not reached versus 4 months, p = 0.06, respectively (Figure 3C, 3D). There were 21 and 18 patients eligible for overall and intracranial response analysis. The overall ORR and DCR were 33.3% (95% CI: 15.5–56.9) and 66.7% (95% CI: 43.1–84.5), with none achieving CR. The IC‐ORR and DCR were 44.4% (95% CI: 22.4–68.7) and 88.9% (95% CI: 64.0–98.1) based on RESIST 1.1 criteria, and 44.4% (95% CI: 22.4–68.7) and 88.9% (95% CI: 64.0–98.1) by RANO‐BM (Table 2 and Figure 4C, 4D). Notably, among three patients who had HER2 0, one patient achieved PR, one maintained SD, and one patient experienced PD in intracranial disease (Figure 4D).

LMD Subgroup

Ten patients had HER2‐positive disease, including 2 with isolated LMD and 8 with concurrent BM. The median IC‐PFS was 16 months (95% CI: 8.4–23.7), with a 12‐month IC‐PFS rate of 72.0% (95% CI: 44.4–100). Six patients had HER2‐low/zero disease, with 2 patients with LMD only and 4 with BM. The median IC‐PFS was 5 months (95% CI: 3.4–6.6) and the 12‐month CNS PFS rate was 27.8% (95% CI: 5.4–100). One patient with HR‐/HER2‐low showed a PFS of 4 months. The median OS has not been reached in both HER2‐positive and low cohorts (Figure 6A,B). Additionally, among these 16 patients, 7 received combination therapy with bevacizumab; however, no statistically significant difference in IC‐PFS was observed with or without T‐DXd combination therapy (p = 0.58) (Figure 6C).

Toxicities

Treatment discontinuation due to adverse events occurred in 7 (8.0%) patients, with financial toxicity representing the leading cause (4, 4.6%). Most common TEAEs included fatigue (45.9%), loss of appetite (25.2%), and nausea (11.4%). Most grade 3 or higher AEs were hematologic toxicities, including six cases (6.9%) of neutropenia and four cases (4.6%) of thrombocytopenia. Interstitial lung disease (ILD) occurred in 7 (8.0%) patients, with 2 (2.3%) diagnosed with grade 3 ILD and discontinued treatment. No new safety signals were identified in this real‐world cohort (Table 3).

Discussion

The current study provides real‐world evidence of the efficacy and safety of T‐DXd in a diverse population of BCBM in China. Our cohort represents a clinically challenging but understudied subgroup: 70% with active symptomatic brain metastases, 18.2% with ECOG PS ≥ 2, 13.6% with GPA > 3.5, 18.2% had LMD, 30.1% were HER2‐low/zero, and 84.5% in the HER2‐positive cohort had prior TKI exposure (vs 2.3%–35.6% in pivotal trials). These patients were generally excluded from the prospective clinical trials we mentioned above. Even though we still observed promising efficacy of T‐DXd. IC‐ORR and PFS were similar to the overall ORR and PFS both in HER2‐positive and low cohorts, confirming the strong intracranial efficacy of T‐DXd.

In the HER2‐positive cohort, the median lines of previous systemic treatment for metastatic disease were 3. Both RECIST 1.1 and RANO‐BM criteria showed consistently high IC‐ORR and DCR. Specifically, IC‐ORR for active BM was 51.9% by RECIST 1.1 criteria, which was slightly higher than 45.5% in DB 01–03 trials pooled analysis (prior treatment lines: 3) [14], but lower than 71.7% of second‐line use of T‐DXd in DB 12 trial [15], and was closely consistent with the result in the recent retrospective ROSET‐BM study (54.1%) [23]. These results suggest the therapeutic advantage of earlier treatment with T‐DXd, as well as the possibility of delaying, even avoiding major surgery or radiation for intracranial disease for HER2‐positive BCBM. Notably, we observed patients with ECOG ≥ 2 still achieving an intracranial ORR of 58.3% and DCR of 91.7%, demonstrating that even patients with poorer conditions might also benefit from T‐DXd. In addition, the efficacy of TKIs in HER2‐positive BCBM has been well documented. The landmark HER2CLIMB study demonstrated an ORR of 47.3% in HER2‐positive BCBM patients treated with tucatinib plus trastuzumab and capecitabine, a regimen now considered the standard of care for this population. Notably, our data revealed a significant variation in TKI utilization patterns in China, with 84.5% of patients having received prior TKI therapy, a rate substantially higher than those reported in the ROSET‐BM (35.6%), DB01‐03 (11.5%), and DB12 (2.3%) trials [14, 15, 23]. Importantly, this heavy pretreatment did not appear to compromise subsequent treatment efficacy. The IC‐ORR in this TKI‐pretreated subgroup remained robust at 60.0%, comparable to TKI‐naive patients and numerically higher than in the HER2CLIMB study. These findings suggest that previous TKI treatment does not diminish the therapeutic effectiveness of T‐DXd. Additionally, no significant impact of HR expression on intracranial response or survival outcomes was observed in the HER2‐positive subgroup.

Patients in the HER2‐low/zero cohort also showed encouraging intracranial activity. Despite receiving a median of 4 prior lines of therapy (versus 3 in the DB04 trial), this cohort still received an IC‐ORR of 44.4% and a median IC‐PFS of 5 months, compared with 25% and 9.7 months in the DB04 trial's brain metastasis subgroup, which enrolled patients with clinically stable, treated BM [19]. The observed intracranial efficacy is further corroborated by results from the DEBBRAH trial (Cohort 2), which reported an IC‐ORR of 50.0% in treatment‐naïve patients with asymptomatic HER2‐low active BM (n = 12) [24]. This consistency across clinical trials and real‐world settings strengthens the evidence for T‐DXd's CNS penetration and activity in HER2‐low disease. Interestingly, we observed activity even in the HER2‐zero subgroup, with one of three patients achieving PR in intracranial disease, suggesting potential utility beyond conventional HER2‐low classifications. However, these preliminary findings require validation in larger cohorts. A critical finding of our analysis was the significant heterogeneity in outcomes based on HR status. Notably, we observed that patients with HR+/HER2 low/zero disease showed superior PFS and IC‐PFS compared to HR‐/HER2 low/zero counterparts. Median IC‐PFS for HR‐/HER2‐low/zero was only 4 months This stark contrast in clinical outcomes suggests fundamental biological differences between HR+/HER2‐low and HR‐/HER2‐low subtypes. Current monotherapy approaches may be inadequate for HR‐negative disease. There is an urgent need for novel T‐DXd‐based combination strategies.

Leptomeningeal disease continues to pose significant therapeutic challenges in breast cancer management. Historical data demonstrate uniformly poor outcomes, with a median overall survival (OS) of less than 4.4 months across all molecular subtypes [25]. However, emerging evidence suggests that T‐DXd may represent a promising therapeutic option for this devastating complication. The DEBBRAH trial enrolled 7 patients with HER2‐positive and low BC with LMD, all treated with T‐DXd, reporting a median PFS of 8.9 months and a median OS of 13.3 months [26]. Another small‐sized retrospective study of 8 HER2+ patients with active LM, T‐DXd achieved a median OS of 10.4 months [27]. Our data showed median IC‐PFS was 16 and 5 months for patients with HER2‐positive and low/zero LMD. The consistent demonstration of efficacy across multiple studies, including our real‐world experience, underscores the potential of T‐DXd to alter the treatment paradigm for breast cancer patients with LMD.

The toxicity profile of T‐DXd was in consistent with prior reports. The rate of pneumonitis in the current study was 8.0% and led to 2 treatment discontinuances. No unexpected toxicities were observed in our patients. These findings validate the known safety characteristics of T‐DXd while providing additional real‐world evidence from a Chinese population with BCBM. The consistency across studies supports the predictable nature of T‐DXd's toxicity profile, even in this heavily pretreated patient cohort with significant CNS involvement.

This study was limited by its retrospective design, which might introduce potential variability. MRI follow‐up was not conducted at fixed intervals but rather after every two cycles of T‐DXd. Furthermore, corticosteroid dosing varied among patients, ranging from 2.25 mg once daily to 10 mg per day, at the discretion of the treating physician. This variation in steroid use may have contributed to interpatient heterogeneity. However, all the patients enrolled were selective with comprehensive and well‐documented clinical data. Moreover, all the images were reassessed by an independent radiologist to minimize bias. Additionally, as the study was conducted exclusively in Chinese patients, its generalizability to other populations may be limited. Despite these limitations, this multicenter retrospective study had a relatively large sample size and included subgroups of patients often excluded from DB trials, such as those with poor PS, LMD, and TKI‐pretreated patients. Moreover, it is the first real‐world study specifically evaluating T‐DXd in HER2‐low/zero BCBM. Thus, this RWS provides supplementing data to support T‐DXd use in HER2‐positive and low/zero BCBMs in routine practice.

In conclusion, the present study showed a substantial and durable intracranial efficacy of T‐DXd in BCBM with both HER2‐positive and low/zero expression, stable and active disease, and patients with significant symptoms and LMD, thus providing valuable insights for routine practice.

Effect of prophylactic HPV vaccination and timing of administration on recurrence of cervical HSIL after LEEP: A retrospective study
Authors: Lianhua Liu, Ningxin Chen, Qian Zhu, Qiaoling Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The objective of this study was to identify risk factors for recurrent cervical high-grade squamous intraepithelial lesions (HSIL/CIN2-3) following loop electrosurgical excision procedure (LEEP), with a particular focus on evaluating the protective effect of human papillomavirus (HPV) vaccination, including effective timing of administration, against disease recurrence. This retrospective study analyzed 343 patients with colposcopy-directed biopsy-confirmed cervical HSIL who underwent LEEP, including 127 vaccinated and 216 unvaccinated individuals. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was employed to identify risk factors associated with HSIL recurrence. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was used to determine the optimal cutoff timing of HPV vaccination for preventing post-LEEP HSIL recurrence. Among the 343 patients, the post-LEEP HSIL recurrence rate was 9.0%. Multivariate logistic analysis identified CIN3 (OR = 2.956, 95% CI 1.046–8.352, P = .041), glandular involvement (OR = 3.517, 95% CI 1.165–10.621, P = .026), positive margins (OR = 2.496, 95% CI 1.100–5.667, P = .029), and persistent HPV infection (OR = 6.253, 95% CI 2.043–19.135, P = .001) as independent risk factors for HSIL recurrence. HPV vaccination was associated with a lower risk of HSIL recurrence (OR = 0.249, 95% CI 0.082–0.760, P = .015). ROC analysis indicated that administration of the HPV vaccine within 6 months post-LEEP was the optimal cutoff time for preventing recurrence (AUC = 0.851, P = .017). Vaccination either before LEEP or within this 6-month postoperative period significantly reduced the risk of HSIL recurrence (P = .034). CIN3, gland involvement, positive margins, and persistent HPV infection increase the risk of cervical HSIL recurrence after LEEP. HPV vaccination, when administered either before or within 6 months after LEEP, is associated with a lower risk of cervical HSIL recurrence.

1. Background

Cervical cancer remains one of the most prevalent gynecologic malignancies, ranking fourth in both incidence and mortality among female-specific cancers worldwide, thereby posing a substantial threat to women’s health.[1] High-grade squamous intraepithelial lesions (HSIL), previously termed cervical intraepithelial neoplasia grade 2–3 (CIN2–3), are a precancerous condition of cervical cancer. Without timely diagnosis and treatment, cervical HSIL carries a high risk of progression to invasive cervical carcinoma. Cervical conization, including loop electrosurgical excision procedure (LEEP) and cold-knife conization (CKC), is the most widely used surgical approach for managing HSIL.[2,3] However, several studies have reported a considerable recurrence risk following cervical conization in HSIL patients, with recurrence rates ranging from 5% to 25%.[4–6] Furthermore, recurrent cervical dysplasia represents a well-established risk factor for cervical carcinoma.[7,8] Moreover, repeated conization procedures may adversely affect subsequent pregnancy outcomes in patients with fertility preservation requirements. Thus, identifying risk factors for recurrence and establishing effective preventive measures in HSIL patients after cervical conization are of significant clinical importance.

Human papillomavirus (HPV) vaccination represents the most effective and cost-efficient primary prevention strategy against HPV infection, cervical precancerous lesions, and invasive cervical carcinoma. Current evidence has firmly established the efficacy of prophylactic HPV vaccines in preventing HPV-related diseases among uninfected women.[9–11] Since therapeutic HPV vaccines are not yet clinically available, the administration of prophylactic HPV vaccines in patients with cervical intraepithelial lesions has garnered significant interest. Although some studies have demonstrated that HPV vaccination reduces recurrence rates of cervical lesions in HSIL patients, conflicting findings have been reported in other investigations.[12–14] Moreover, the optimal timing of HPV vaccination for preventing HSIL recurrence after cervical conization remains inconclusive. Therefore, further clinical studies are warranted to elucidate its protective effects and establish the most effective administration window.

This study retrospectively analyzed the clinical data and HPV vaccination status of 343 patients with HSIL who underwent LEEP. Our objectives were to identify risk factors associated with postoperative recurrence of cervical HSIL and to evaluate the impact of HPV vaccination—particularly its timing—on reducing the recurrence risk. The findings aim to provide a theoretical foundation for the application of HPV vaccination in preventing recurrence among HSIL patients following conization.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Study design and ethical considerations

A retrospective analysis was conducted involving 343 patients who underwent LEEP at the Affiliated Jiangning Hospital of Nanjing Medical University between 2018 and 2022. Informed consent was obtained from all study participants, and the study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Jiangning Hospital of Nanjing Medical University (Approval No. 2023-03-093-K01). This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Inclusion criteria: (1) unpregnant women undergoing LEEP with histologically confirmed HSIL/CIN2-3; and (2) patients with good compliance with scheduled follow-up visits and data collection procedures.

Exclusion criteria: (1) conized patients with histopathological evidence of invasive cervical carcinoma; (2) patients undergoing hysterectomy for HSIL/CIN2-3; (3) patients with a history of malignant neoplasms or severe organ dysfunction; (4) patients with immunodeficiency disorders; (5) patients with psychiatric conditions impairing study participation; (6) patients with incomplete clinical records or lost to follow-up; and (7) patients with persistent cervical lesions.

2.2. Data collection

Clinical data were retrospectively obtained from the hospital’s electronic medical record system and supplemented by telephone follow-up interviews. The comprehensive dataset encompassed: (1) baseline demographic characteristics (age, menopause, gravidity, parity); (2) HPV status before LEEP and postoperative outcomes, including HPV status and cervical cytology results; (3) histopathological findings from both colposcopy-guided biopsies and post-LEEP specimens; and (4) HPV vaccination parameters (vaccination status and timing relative to LEEP) .

HPV status was assessed by polymerase chain reaction-based DNA genotyping, while cervical cytological evaluation was conducted using Thinprep Cytologic Test (TCT). Persistent HPV infection was defined as the presence of the same type of HR-HPV before treatment and at the first post-LEEP control.[15]

2.3. Follow-up and relevant definitions

Follow-up evaluations were conducted at scheduled intervals (3, 6, 12, and 24 months) following the LEEP procedure. All follow-up visits included routine co-testing (combined HPV DNA test and TCT). Colposcopy-guided biopsies were performed in cases with abnormal screening results for histological confirmation when medically warranted. For patients demonstrating abnormal co-testing results at the postoperative visit, subsequent follow-up protocols were individualized based on histological findings. The follow-up endpoint was defined as either: (1) histological confirmation of CIN, or (2) the last recorded visit with normal findings.

The recurrence or persistence of cervical lesions was determined based on the results of cervical biopsy. Recurrence was defined as the detection of any grade of CIN at ≥ 6 months after surgery, following normal 3-month postoperative follow-up results. Normal 3-month follow-up was defined as normal TCT/HPV findings, or abnormal TCT/HPV results with no histopathologic abnormalities on further evaluation. Persistence of cervical lesions after surgery was defined as the presence of histologically confirmed CIN of any grade at < 6 months following LEEP. Definition of the vaccinated group: Accumulating evidence has indicated that both two-dose and 1-dose HPV vaccination regimens may be effective,[16–19] and patients who received at least 1 dose of HPV vaccine were included in the vaccinated group in this study.

2.4. Statistical Methods

Data were analyzed using SPSS software (version 22.0). Categorical variables were expressed as numbers and percentages and compared using the χ2 test. Multivariate logistic regression was performed to identify independent risk factors for cervical HSIL recurrence, including variables with P < .05 in univariate analysis. Results were presented as odds ratios (OR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI). Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was used to determine the optimal cutoff timing of HPV vaccination for preventing HSIL recurrence after LEEP. A value of P < .05 was considered statistically significant.

3. Results

3.1. Population characteristics

A total of 343 patients were ultimately included in this study. The median age of the participants was 40 years (range: 22–73). All patients were treated based on histological confirmation of cervical HSIL/CIN2-3. Final histological diagnoses included CIN2 in 159 patients (46.4%) and CIN3 in 184 patients (53.6%). Among the participants, 127 women (37.0%) received at least 1 dose of HPV vaccination and were classified as vaccinated, while 216 (63.0%) remained unvaccinated. During the follow-up period, 31 patients (9.0%) experienced disease recurrence, with 4 cases (3.1%) occurring in the vaccinated group and 27 cases (12.5%) in the nonvaccinated group (P = .004). 3.2. Independent risk factors for cervical HSIL recurrence after LEEP

Multivariate logistic regression analysis showed that CIN3 (OR = 2.956, 95% CI 1.046–8.352, P = .041), glandular involvement (OR = 3.517, 95% CI 1.165–10.621, P = .026), positive margin status (OR = 2.496, 95% CI 1.100–5.667, P = .029), and HPV persistence (OR = 6.253, 95% CI 2.043–19.135, P = .001) were independent risk factors of cervical HSIL recurrence after LEEP. HPV vaccination was a protective factor of recurrent HSIL, with a recurrence risk reduction of 75.1% (OR = 0.249, 95% CI 0.082–0.760, P = .015). The results are presented in Table 2.

3.3. The timing of HPV vaccination

Of the 127 vaccinated women, 52 (40.9%) received the bivalent vaccine, 51 (40.2%) received the quadrivalent vaccine, and 24 (18.9%) received the nonavalent vaccine. Among the vaccinated women, 16 (12.6%) received the first dose before LEEP, 64 (50.4%) within 6 months after LEEP, and 47 (37.0%) were received the first dose prior to LEEP more than 6 months after treatment. Among the patients who received the vaccine, 4 cases experienced recurrence, all of whom were vaccinated more than 6 months after treatment. Among the recurrence cases, 1 case received the bivalent vaccine, and 3 cases received the quadrivalent vaccine. Table 3 shows general characteristics of the vaccinated women included in this study. The ROC analysis indicated that the timing of HPV vaccination had predictive value for HSIL recurrence (AUC = 0.851, P = .017; Fig. 1). The optimal cutoff value was 6 months after the surgery. The results showed that in patients who received vaccinations before the surgery and within 6 months after the surgery, the recurrence rate of HSIL was significantly reduced (P = .034) (Table 3).

4. Discussion

HSIL patients remain at high risk of recurrence after cervical conization, with reported recurrence rates ranging from 5% to 25% in previous studies.[4] Our results demonstrated a postoperative recurrence rate of 9% in HSIL patients, consistent with existing literature.[20] Given this substantial recurrence risk, identifying risk factors for HSIL recurrence and exploring effective interventions holds significant clinical implications. This study revealed that CIN3, glandular involvement, positive margins, and persistent HPV infection were independent risk factors for HSIL recurrence after LEEP. Conversely, HPV vaccination was associated with a lower risk of HSIL recurrence. Notably, administration of the HPV vaccine either before LEEP or within 6 months postoperatively significantly reduced recurrence risk.

This study confirmed that CIN3, positive margins, persistent HPV infection, and glandular involvement were independent risk factors for recurrence in HSIL patients following LEEP procedures. Ming Chen et al.[21]reported a higher recurrence rate in patients with CIN3 compared to those with CIN2, a finding that is consistent with the results of the present study. Positive resection margins and persistent HPV infection have been consistently recognized as significant risk factors for recurrence following conization in patients with HSIL, as reported in previous literature.[22–25] Giorgio et al.[26] conducted a retrospective multi-institutional study with a 5-year follow-up period involving 2966 patients who underwent cervical conization procedures for HSIL/CIN2+, including both laser conization and LEEP, and used a propensity-score matching algorithm to make patients’ characteristics between the 2 groups comparable. Their findings demonstrated that positive margins and HPV persistence were associated with 5-year recurrence following LEEP procedures. A meta-analysis incorporating 11 studies (n = 3065 patients) revealed that positive endocervical margins, in contrast to external cervical margins, were significantly associated with an increased risk of residual/recurrent CIN following resection.[27] Huang et al.[28] conducted a study to evaluate prognostic factors for HSIL patients following conization. Their findings demonstrated that positive margin status was significantly associated with higher rates of recurrence/residual disease during the 5-year follow-up period. Additionally, through Kaplan–Meier survival analysis and multivariate Cox regression, they identified glandular invasion as a critical predictor of increased recurrence/residual disease risk within both 1-year and 3-year intervals. The authors postulated that positive margin status and gland reflected more extensive intraepithelial neoplasia and residual viral infection, ultimately contributing to poorer clinical outcomes. Glandular involvement in the conization specimen has also been demonstrated by other investigators to increase the risk of residual HSIL or postconization recurrence.[29] These findings highlight the critical need for more intensive treatment protocols and enhanced postoperative surveillance—including closer and more frequent follow-up—in HSIL patients with risk factors such as CIN3, persistent HPV infection, positive surgical margins, or glandular involvement.

Accumulating data suggested that HPV vaccination in patients undergoing conization for HSIL/CIN 2-3 can reduce the risk of HSIL recurrence.[30–32] In this study, the postoperative recurrence rate among HSIL patients who received HPV vaccination was 3.14%, significantly lower than that in the unvaccinated group. HPV vaccination was associated with an approximately 75.1% reduction in recurrence risk among HSIL patients. A 2023 meta-analysis comprising 20 studies revealed that cervical conization combined with a 3-dose HPV vaccination regimen significantly reduced the recurrence of CIN2 + by over 69% compared to excisional treatment alone.[6] However, a study by Allan Hildesheim et al.[12] found no evidence of a reduction in post-LEEP disease associated with vaccination. These discrepancies may be attributed to variations in inclusion/exclusion criteria, inconsistent definitions of residual or recurrent disease, differences in the timing of HPV vaccination, and heterogeneous follow-up protocols across studies.

The timing of HPV vaccination has aroused the interest of researchers. Accumulating evidence suggests that earlier HPV vaccination timing may enhance protective efficacy against HSIL recurrence following treatment.[33] Carla Henere et al.[34] demonstrated that pre-LEEP HPV vaccination showed superior efficacy in preventing persistent/recurrent HSIL compared to postoperative administration, albeit without statistical significance—a finding consistent with the results reported by Freja L. Sand et al.[35] Furthermore, their study revealed a nonsignificant trend toward reduced HSIL recurrence/persistence risk in postoperatively vaccinated patients vs unvaccinated controls. Tjalma et al.[36] recommended that women should be vaccinated with a 9vHPV vaccine when they were treated for a CIN2 + lesion, but they believed that the first dose should be given before the conization. Nevertheless, emerging evidence confirms that postconization HPV vaccination can effectively prevent CIN2 + recurrence, offering an adjuvant therapeutic option for cervical HSIL patients who were unvaccinated before surgery.[14,36] The 2023 meta-analysis revealed that the protective effect was more pronounced in women receiving vaccination after excision (78% reduction) than in those vaccinated before or/and after the surgical procedure (48% reduction).[6] A prospective cohort study demonstrated that quadrivalent HPV vaccination significantly reduced recurrence rates in CIN2 and CIN3 patients following LEEP. In this study, the first vaccine dose was administered within 3 months post-LEEP, with the remaining 2 doses completed within the subsequent 6 months.[21] Giorgio Bogani et al.[37] demonstrated that conization plus HPV vaccination significantly reduced the risk of recurrent disease compared to conization alone. Notably, their analysis revealed no significant association between the timing of vaccination initiation (first dose administered within 1 month [2/70 cases] vs 1–3 months [0/12] vs 3–6 months [0/4] postprocedure) and subsequent recurrence rates. This study suggested that HPV vaccination before or within 6 months after LEEP was associated with a lower risk of HSIL recurrence. Due to the small sample size (with only 4 recurrent cases identified in the vaccinated group), the statistical power was limited. Accordingly, this finding is of an exploratory nature and requires validation in a larger cohort. In summary, current evidence supports immediate HPV vaccination after HSIL diagnosis.

To our knowledge, this is the first study to demonstrate that HPV vaccination—whether administered before LEEP or within 6 months postoperatively—significantly reduces the risk of recurrence in HSIL patients after LEEP. However, several limitations of this study should be specifically addressed. First, this was a single-center retrospective study, which may be subject to selection bias and warrants cautious interpretation of the results. Second, the lack of a prospective cohort prevented the assessment of several confounding variables, including health-seeking behavior, socioeconomic status, smoking history, sexual partner information, and vaginal microenvironment data. These factors may be associated with HPV infection, cervical lesions, and vaccination behavior, thereby introducing confounding effects on the study results. Although we adjusted for known clinically relevant factors in the multivariate analysis, residual bias from unmeasured confounders could not be completely ruled out, which may affect the accuracy of the effect estimates. In addition, due to the limited sample size, subgroup analyses stratified by vaccine type and number of doses were not performed. These factors may affect the interpretation of our findings. Therefore, future multicenter, large-sample prospective studies with long-term follow-up are needed to further clarify the optimal timing and number of doses of HPV vaccination, as well as the protective efficacy of different vaccine types.

5. Conclusion

In summary, patients with HSIL remain at substantial risk of recurrence following surgical treatment. Independent risk factors for HSIL recurrence include the presence of CIN3, glandular involvement, positive surgical margins, and persistent HPV infection. Importantly, HPV vaccination when administered either before or within 6 months after LEEP is associated with a lower risk of HSIL recurrence.

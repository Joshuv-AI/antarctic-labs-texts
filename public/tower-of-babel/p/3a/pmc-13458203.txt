A multimodal complementary feature fusion network for myocardial pathological segmentation in multi-sequence cardiac magnetic resonance images
Authors: Chang-Jiang Zhang, Qi-Bin Hong, Min Zhou, Fu-Qin Tang, Hai-Peng Cai, Li-Li Feng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAutomated myocardial pathology segmentation in multi-sequence cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) imaging plays an indispensable role in assessing myocardial viability and managing patients with myocardial infarction (MI). However, precise delineation remains exceptionally challenging due to low soft tissue contrast, highly irregular pathological geometries, residual cross-modal spatial misalignments, and scarcity of labeled datasets. This study aimed to develop a highly accurate, stable, and unified single-stage deep learning framework to simultaneously segment the left ventricular (LV) blood pool, right ventricular (RV) blood pool, left ventricular normal myocardium (LVM), myocardial edema, and myocardial scars from multi-sequence CMR images.MethodsWe propose the Multimodal CMR Image Pathological Segmentation Network (MCIPS-Net), an end-to-end framework built upon a five-layer U-Net architecture. To address inter-modal intensity distribution heterogeneity among balanced steady-state free precession (BSSFP), late gadolinium enhancement (LGE), and T2-weighted (T2) sequences, a plug-and-play multimodal feature fusion (MFF) module was introduced in the encoding path to independently extract and cascade modality-specific semantic features. Furthermore, an efficient channel attention (ECA)-based fusion module was embedded before upsampling to strengthen region of interest (ROI) focus. Training stability and performance under extreme class imbalance were optimized using a multi-scale deep supervision (DS) strategy and an adaptive composite loss function merging Dice and Focal losses. Center-cropped images (256×256 pixels) were expanded 15-fold via geometric and elastic data augmentations, and post-processing was executed to eliminate minor isolated prediction artifacts.ResultsEvaluated on the MyoPS 2020 Challenge test set (20 cases, 72 slices), MCIPS-Net achieved an average Dice score of 0.655±0.238 for scar segmentation and 0.714±0.086 for edema and scar joint segmentation, yielding a comprehensive average Dice score of 0.685. Ablation studies revealed that the MFF module contributed the most significant gain, increasing the baseline average Dice score by 4.5% (from 0.574 to 0.619). On the official leaderboard, our single-stage method outperformed the leading advanced single-stage method by 0.7% while demonstrating performance equivalent to the official second-place multi-stage pipeline. Crucially, the model exhibited the lowest standard deviation (SD) in Dice scores among all participating top algorithms, highlighting its superior stability.ConclusionsThe proposed MCIPS-Net establishes a simple, efficient, and robust single-stage solution that bypasses complex localization networks or cumbersome model integration. By effectively capitalizing on cross-modal complementary features, this stable pipeline offers a high-performance clinical tool for precise infarct size quantification and myocardial viability assessment.

Introduction

Assessing myocardial viability is crucial for diagnosing and treating patients with myocardial disease (1). Cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) is a medical imaging technique used to evaluate the function and structure of the heart. Combining CMR imaging with automatic myocardial pathology segmentation can assist physicians in conducting a more accurate and rapid assessment of myocardial viability, which plays a crucial role in the diagnosis and treatment management of myocardial infarction (MI) patients. The assessment of cardiovascular pathologies typically relies on three primary CMR sequences: T2-weighted (T2), late gadolinium enhancement (LGE), and balanced steady-state free precession (BSSFP), as shown in Figure 1 (1,2). The clinical interpretations of different modes vary, but they all provide different characteristic information about potential diseases (3). For determining the spatial distribution and extent of localized myocardial scarring and fibrosis, LGE imaging serves as a prevalent tool. Conversely, T2 sequences are particularly adept at detecting edematous regions alongside recent or acute ischemic damage within the heart muscle. BSSFP CMR images provide clear boundary information on the heart structure. In the context of cardiac segmentation tasks, LGE sequences are frequently integrated with supplementary CMR modalities such as T2 and BSSFP. Fusing T2 and LGE data supplies neural networks with comprehensive anatomical and pathological cues necessary for accurately delineating myocardial edema and scar tissue (4). Furthermore, BSSFP CMR provides clear boundary information about the left ventricle to assist in segmentation (5). Due to the high heterogeneity exhibited by CMR imaging, the intensity distribution of pathological regions between and within modalities is uneven, making the segmentation of CMR images challenging (1,2,6,7). Therefore, integrating and utilizing complementary information between multimodal images in heart segmentation are key to obtaining accurate segmentation results (8).

Routine clinical assessments typically classify the myocardial tissue into three distinct categories: healthy regions, edematous zones, and scarred areas. The accurate segmentation of these three areas is crucial for diagnosing and treating patients. This is a challenging task, as manual segmentation is often time-consuming and tedious and can be influenced by differences in experience between observers (9). Therefore, it is necessary to develop a method for the automatic segmentation of myocardial pathology using multimodal CMR images in clinical practice. However, segmenting normal, scarred, and edematous areas is challenging because (I) scars and edemas occupy very small areas and are distributed in different locations on the myocardium, and (II) it is difficult to distinguish between scarred and edematous areas in all three sequence images (2). As shown in Figure 1, on BSSFP and T2 CMR images, the boundary between myocardial scars and normal areas is blurred, whereas on LGE CMR images, it can only barely be recognized. In addition, myocardial edemas are difficult to distinguish in T2 and LGE images, and they are almost invisible in BSSFP images (9). Multimodal medical images can comprehensively reflect tissues’ structural characteristics and reduce the impact of data ambiguity within single-modal medical images. Therefore, the use of multimodal medical images to assist doctors in diagnosing and treating lesions has become mainstream in modern clinical practice (10).

Historically, the delineation of myocardial lesions has heavily depended on intensity-based thresholding techniques. Prominent examples of these conventional approaches encompass the signal threshold versus reference mean (STRM) (11), region growth (RG) (12), and full width at half maximum (FWHM) (13). However, image noise easily affects threshold methods, and segmentation results and expert descriptions can differ significantly (14,15). Moreover, this semi-automatic segmentation method is not suitable for large-scale clinical diagnosis. Recently, deep learning-based methods have made great progress in image segmentation. For example, a fully convolutional network (FCN) (16) based on an encoder–decoder structure was the first end-to-end model applied to semantic segmentation, and it could take images of any size as input and output prediction masks of corresponding sizes. However, due to the frequent loss of considerable feature information in the pooling layer, a simple encoder–decoder structure cannot recover the details of an image sufficiently for accurate prediction. A well-known medical image segmentation network, U-Net (17), has been proposed, built, and improved based on the FCN architecture to address this issue. By introducing skip connections between the encoder and decoder layers, pixel-level information and advanced extraction of semantic information are better integrated to incorporate more spatially structured low-level features in restoring feature map size and more accurate segmentation results. Due to the success of U-Net, many medical image segmentation methods based on the U-Net architecture have been proposed (18-21).

Contemporary deep learning strategies for outlining myocardial pathologies predominantly target single-sequence CMR, frequently relying solely on LGE (22). Nevertheless, utilizing multi-sequence CMR yields diverse, complementary insights into the global cardiac structure. To investigate how these various modalities can mutually enhance pathological segmentation, the Medical Image Computing and Computer Assisted Intervention (MICCAI) society, in conjunction with the Statistical Atlas and Computational Modeling of the Heart (STACOM) workshop, hosted the MyoPS 2020 Challenge. The dataset of the MyoPS 2020 Challenge includes multimodal CMR images of 45 individuals, including BSSFP CMR, LGE CMR, and T2 CMR images. The goal of this challenge was to accurately and automatically segment scar and edema areas in the myocardium. Due to the time-consuming and subjective nature of manual segmentation, there is an urgent need for the automatic segmentation of myocardial pathology. However, given the significant differences in myocardial shape among different cases, blurred and difficult to distinguish myocardial boundaries, and potentially poor image quality, the automatic segmentation of myocardial pathology is challenging. Specifically, the automatic segmentation of myocardial pathologies based on multimodal images faces three core challenges:

❖ Intensity distribution heterogeneity: In LGE and T2 CMR images, pathological myocardium exhibits significant spatial heterogeneity in gray-scale intensity, complicating feature extraction.

Among the strategies proposed for the MyoPS 2020 dataset, Zhai et al. (9) implemented a coarse-to-fine, two-phase framework, subsequently boosting predictive accuracy via diverse ensemble weighting techniques. Similarly relying on model ensembling for optimization, Zhang et al. (23) deployed a weighted bidirectional feature pyramid network (BiFPN) decoding structure coupled with a pretrained EfficientNet encoder. Furthermore, Li et al. (24) utilized a unified encoder to harvest cross-modal representations, pairing it with multi-scale blocks to strengthen feature derivation and a channel-reconstruction upsampling mechanism for spatial recovery. In another study, Li et al. (25) introduced Multi-scale Contextual Information Enhancement Network (MCIE-Net) to investigate inter-modality dependencies, alongside LR-Net, which was specifically designed to magnify lesion-specific attributes. Alternatively, Cui et al. (4) adopted a six-tier deep U-Net architecture as their foundation, pioneering the application of curriculum learning within cardiac pathology segmentation to substantially elevate the network’s efficacy.

These methods mostly adopt cumbersome multi-stage methods, including image synthesis using generative adversarial networks (GANs) (26), localization networks, transfer learning, and model integration. However, in multi-stage methods, the segmentation performance and task of the previous stage network significantly affect the next stage, and the training time and number of hyperparameters increase exponentially, making training multi-stage methods very challenging. Although model integration can significantly improve segmentation accuracy by integrating the performance of multiple different models, it requires training multiple different models simultaneously, which greatly increases the difficulty of model training. In light of this, this article proposes a simple, efficient single-stage network called Multimodal CMR Image Pathological Segmentation Network (MCIPS-Net). In the encoding path, we proposed a multimodal feature fusion (MFF) module to explore the correlation between different modal images and fully utilize complementary information to enhance feature extraction. In the decoding path, we added an attention module (ATT) to enhance attention to the regions of interest (ROIs) in the image. In order to prevent overfitting and enable the model to fully learn different proportions and position information, we extended the dataset using several data augmentation methods, including angle rotation, random elastic deformation, random pixel loss, and horizontal flipping. In addition, we adopted a deep supervision (DS) strategy for different scales in the encoding path to address the issues of vanishing network training gradients and slow convergence. This enables the shallow layers of the network to be fully trained and results to be more accurate.

Compared to the MyoPS 2020 challenge baseline method, our method uses a simple and efficient single-stage network architecture to avoid complex and tedious multi-stage network learning. Without using complex methods such as localization networks, image synthesis, model integration, and transfer learning, our method surpassed advanced single-stage solutions and achieved an overall performance comparable to the second-place algorithm on the original MyoPS 2020 Challenge leaderboard (https://myops-2020.grand-challenge.org/). Methods

This section first focuses on introducing proposed MCIPS-Net fusion strategy​, elaborating on the design of the MFF encoding module, and presenting the overall network framework of Net​, which is proposed for myocardial pathological segmentation. Subsequently, it discusses methods that effectively improve segmentation performance in our experiments, covering ATTs, combined loss functions, DS, image preprocessing, and post-processing (PP).

MFF encoding module

In multi-modal fusion strategies, input-level fusion (as shown in Figure 2A) fuses multi-modal images channel-by-channel into multi-channel inputs, learns the features after fusion, and trains segmentation networks. This approach can fully utilize the rich features of each modality. However, multi-modal myocardial pathological segmentation faces three main challenges: (I) low contrast between myocardial pathological regions and surrounding tissues; (II) large variations in the location, shape, and scale of edema and scar within and between patients; (III) inconsistent brightness distributions across modalities, which may lead to incorrect cross-modal information fusion.

General framework of fusion strategies for different multimodal image segmentation. BSSFP, balanced steady-state free precession; CMR, cardiac magnetic resonance; LGE, late gadolinium enhancement; MCIPS, Multimodal CMR Image Pathological Segmentation Network.

Output-level fusion (as shown in Figure 2B) treats each modality image as a single input for a segmentation network, sums the outputs of all networks, and obtains the final segmentation result. Its performance is generally better than input-level fusion (as a single network can better exploit the unique information of each modality and learn complex complementary features). However, output-level fusion requires more memory, and the network training time is almost doubled, imposing higher requirements on equipment conditions.

The MCIPS-Net fusion strategy developed in this study (as shown in Figure 2C) differs from the previous fusion strategies: BSSFP CMR, LGE CMR, and T2 CMR images are input into different encoding blocks, and the same-sized feature maps are connected in the channel dimension through the cascading of encoding blocks. Unlike input-level fusion, which is highly sensitive to pixel-to-pixel spatial errors, this independent encoding strategy extracts modality-specific semantic features before fusion, making the network inherently more tolerant and robust to residual cross-modal spatial misalignments. Meanwhile, following the U-Net encoder–decoder architecture, all three encoding paths output skip connections. In the decoder part, the outputs of the three skip connections are cascaded, and in the bridge part, the encoding outputs of the last encoding module are cascaded.

To effectively integrate and fully utilize multi-modal features, this paper encapsulates MCIPS fusion strategy and Atrous Spatial Pyramid Pooling (ASPP) (27) into a plug-and-play MFF encoding module (as shown in Figure 3).

MCIPS-net architecture

To explore the impact of complementary information among multi-modal CMR images on medical image segmentation, this paper designs a simple and efficient framework for multi-modal CMR image pathological segmentation, namely MCIPS-Net, based on the aforementioned proposed method.

The proposed segmentation network follows an encoder-decoder architecture, specifically designed to address the heterogeneity of multi-modal CMR images (with significant inter-modal and intra-modal intensity variations). It employs a multi-level strategy to enhance segmentation performance and robustness, as illustrated in Figure 4.

MCIPS-Net architecture. ATT, attention module; CMR, cardiac magnetic resonance; MCIPS-Net, Multimodal CMR Image Pathological Segmentation Network; MFF, multimodal feature fusion.

During the encoding stage, to mitigate cross-modal interference and erroneous fusion caused by direct channel-wise connections, an MFF is introduced. This module integrates feature maps from different encoders, expands the receptive field of CMR images, and extracts multi-scale features. The learned feature representations are fused across network layers, reducing cross-modal interference at a global level and improving segmentation performance.

A attention fusion module is embedded before the upsampling stage to enhance attention allocation to ROIs in both skip connections and channel-wise upsampled feature maps, thereby improving decoding precision.

To optimize training, the framework incorporates two residual blocks in each downsampling and upsampling block to alleviate gradient vanishing/explosion issues. Additionally, a DS method is adopted to improve learning efficiency for the target task. This involves assigning different weights to the output layer and the supervision layer, with independent objective functions, enabling the network encoder to automatically explore inter-modal correlations and strengthen the extraction of multi-modal image features.

Through this hierarchical design, the framework systematically addresses challenges in multi-modal segmentation, including heterogeneity, feature scale, attention allocation, and training stability, providing robust technical support for accurate CMR image segmentation.

Attention fusion module

Efficient channel attention (ECA) (28) is an efficient channel ATT for deep convolutional neural networks that avoids dimensionality reduction and can effectively capture cross-channel interaction information, as shown in Figure 5A. This paper improves this module and proposes a simple and efficient attention fusion module. As shown in Figure 5B, we simultaneously input the skip-connected feature map and the upsampled feature map into the ECA (28) module and add residual connections to the ECA (28) module. Finally, we fuse the output in the channel dimension to reduce the loss of original features and strengthen the attention to interest.

Metrics and loss function

The Dice coefficient, Jaccard index, and Hausdorff distance (29-31) are the most commonly used indicators for evaluating medical image segmentation performance. The Dice coefficient calculates the similarity or overlap between two samples, with a range of (0–1); the Jaccard index works similarly. The Hausdorff distance measures the shape similarity between samples, and the smaller the value, the higher the shape similarity.

The ranking of the MyoPS 2020 Challenge is based on the Dice scores of myocardial pathological regions (scar and edema + scar). The official evaluation toolkit also calculates the Dice scores for these regions. Therefore, in subsequent experiments, the Dice scores of scar and edema + scar regions are used to evaluate model performance.

In segmentation task optimization, Dice loss (32) and cross-entropy loss (16) are common objective loss functions. Cross-entropy tends to favor the background when the number of current scene pixels is far less than that of background pixels. The Dice loss is suitable for imbalanced samples. Considering that the foreground and background pixel distributions in our dataset are extremely imbalanced, and the standard label distributions of various categories are also extremely imbalanced, this study selects the Dice loss as the loss function. Since the contrast between pathological regions and surrounding tissues is low, the boundary is difficult to distinguish, and the target category segmentation is imbalanced, the focal loss (33), a popular subset based on the cross-entropy loss function, can enhance the model’s attention to more difficult tasks and weight different categories. To address the issue of category imbalance, larger weights are typically assigned to target categories, and smaller weights are assigned to non-target categories in experiments. Therefore, a composite loss function is ultimately used to optimize the segmentation results, which consists of Dice loss and Focal loss (DFL) in both voxel and regional dimensions. DS

Increasing the depth of a neural network can enhance its feature extraction capability. However, as the network depth increases, training difficulties such as vanishing and exploding gradients tend to emerge. Lee et al. (34) proposed the DS strategy, which introduces auxiliary branch classifiers in some layers of the neural network to assess the quality of hidden layer feature maps. Research has demonstrated that the DS strategy is an effective means to improve network performance.

The CMR dataset used in this paper has limited samples and extreme class imbalance, necessitating the use of a relatively deep network for training. This is prone to overfitting, vanishing gradients, and exploding gradients. To address these challenges, this paper adopts the DS strategy, which further improves network performance by assigning weights to different supervision layers and designing different objective functions for the output layer and supervision layers.

Pre-processing

On account of the small portion of the image that the target segmentation region occupies, memory limitations, and network design requirements, we crop the CMR images of three sequences from 400×400 pixels to 256×256 at the image center to remove irrelevant information (interference) and alleviate sample imbalance. Due to the large pixel values of magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), this study normalized the pixel values of the input image to have zero mean and unit variance within the cardiac region. In addition, we apply contrast-limited adaptive histogram equalization (CLAHE) (35) and histogram matching to obtain relatively consistent intensity among the three modal images.

Post-processing

To reduce errors in segmentation, a PP strategy is implemented to remove small, isolated prediction regions, smooth image edges, and remove incorrectly predicted edges. The study calculated the connection regions for each category in each slice. Voxels smaller than 40 are removed for the predicted scar area and replaced with surrounding labels. For the predicted edema and scar areas, voxels of less than 10 are removed and replaced with surrounding labels. In addition, this study also uses morphological image processing (36) for the predicted area. First, the holes in the image are filled, and the edges of the image are smoothed through dilation operations. Then, erroneously predicted edges are eliminated through erosion operations.

Results

Experimental dataset

To validate the efficacy of our proposed framework for delineating cardiac lesions, comprehensive experiments were executed utilizing the MyoPS 2020 Challenge dataset (https://zmiclab.github.io/zxh/0/myops20/). Co-hosted by the MICCAI society and the STACOM workshop, this initiative provided multi-sequence CMR scans from 45 patients, aiming to foster automated, high-precision segmentation of myocardial edema and scarring. In adherence to the established challenge guidelines, the entire corpus was divided into a public training set and a blind testing cohort. Specifically, the official training set comprises 102 slices derived from 25 patients, complete with publicly accessible ground-truth annotations. As depicted in Figure 1, individual slices encompass T2, LGE, and BSSFP sequences that have undergone spatial alignment and preprocessing to ensure uniform resolution within a standardized coordinate space. Furthermore, across all three sequences per slice, unified expert annotations are provided, meticulously curated by senior clinicians possessing profound expertise in cardiac imaging diagnostics. The annotation scheme delineates six distinct anatomical and pathological structures using specific values: the LV blood pool (labeled as 500), RV blood pool (600), and normal LVM (200). Pathological targets are identified as left ventricular myocardial edema (1220) and LV myocardial scar (2221). The independent test set encompasses the residual 72 slices from 20 patients; corresponding ground truths were withheld by the organizers to facilitate impartial benchmarking. Due to the absence of these test labels, algorithmic proficiency was quantified utilizing the official MyoPS 2020 evaluation portal, relying primarily on the average Dice scores achieved for the edematous and scarred tissues.

Given the restricted volume of training data, coupled with disproportionate foreground–background ratios and severe class imbalances within the annotated labels, the implementation of robust data augmentation strategies proved critical for optimizing the network’s overall performance. Our data augmentation methods in the experiment include a random loss of 5–20% pixels, 50% probability of random horizontal flipping, random cropping of 0–20 pixels at edges, random rotation (−90° to +90°), and random elastic transformation. Particularly, the random elastic transformation and geometric rotations serve to simulate the potential minor spatial distortions and misalignments between different CMR sequences, further encouraging the network to learn spatially invariant representations and thereby implicitly addressing the cross-modal misalignment challenge. The data augmentation method is applied to the original image and the gold standard label, as shown in Figure 6. The training dataset is increased by 15 times using data augmentation methods. After augmenting the official training dataset, it was split into a training dataset and a validation dataset in a 3:1 ratio for the experiment.

Experimental step

Our method was implemented using the TensorFlow platform and trained on a server equipped with an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU (NVIDIA, Santa Clara, CA, USA). Due to memory limitations, the training batch size was set to 8. Network parameters were optimized using the Adam optimizer (β1=0.9, β2=0.999). The training process was set for a maximum of 550 epochs. We employed a dynamic learning rate strategy, which reduced the learning rate by a factor of 0.5 if the validation Dice loss showed no improvement over 10 consecutive epochs. Furthermore, an early stopping mechanism was utilized to monitor the training process, terminating the training if the loss decreased by less than 1e−3 over 15 epochs.

Validity analysis of multimodal images

In the segmentation scenario of multimodal images, an inconsistency of image intensity changes between modalities and within modalities leads to heterogeneity in multimodal images. One difficulty is fusing more useful complementary information to reduce heterogeneity. Thus, it is necessary to quantitatively analyze the heterogeneity between modalities. Accordingly, we evaluate the network of different modal image combinations on the MyoPS 2020 test set, including single-mode, dual-mode, and three-mode networks. Table 1 shows the results: in the single-modal network, LGE CMR has the highest Dice score in scar segmentation among the three modalities, reaching 55.3% and indicating the superiority of LGE CMR in scar segmentation. Although T2 CMR performs worse than LGE CMR in scar segmentation, it performs best among the three modalities in scar+edema segmentation, with a Dice score of up to 60.2%, reflecting its superiority in edema segmentation. Although the BSSFP CMR performance in unimodal networks is unsatisfactory, it can improve segmentation performance when paired with other modalities. Thus, the clear boundary information provided by BSSFP CMR about the left ventricle has a certain role in myocardial pathology segmentation.

Effectiveness analysis of different components

To evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed components, we conduct ablation experiments to analyze the impact of each submodule. First, U-Net with a five-layer architecture is used to construct a baseline model, and each subcomponent discussed in Section 2 is incorporated sequentially into the baseline model for further ablation comparison.

Table 2 presents the average Dice scores obtained in the ablation experiments, quantitatively showing the contribution of each submodule to segmentation improvement and verifying the proposed method’s superiority. Table 2 shows that using an MFF gives the most significant improvement effect, with a Dice score increase of about 4.5%. Due to the uneven distribution of the foreground and background in the image and the extremely uneven distribution of various categories in the gold standard label, this may lead to the network paying more attention to learning non-target areas and limited segmentation accuracy. The attention fusion module can enhance the attention to areas of interest and reduce the impact of target areas occupying smaller image areas. Using the attention fusion module increased the Dice score by about 1.1%. To address the issues of overfitting, gradient vanishing, gradient exploding, and slow convergence caused by the five-layer U-Net baseline model, DS provides sufficient training for shallow layers, thus alleviating overfitting and accelerating convergence. Additionally, assigning weights to different supervision layers enhances the network’s learning of key tasks.

CE loss is a distribution-based loss function that describes the difference in probability distribution between predicted output and true labels. When the number of current scene pixels is much smaller than the number of background pixels, the model heavily leans toward the background, resulting in inferior performance. However, in the MyoPS 2020 dataset, the target area only occupies a small part of the image, so it performed poorly in the experiment, with an average Dice score of only 63.3%. Focal loss targets the problem of imbalanced samples by increasing the weight of misclassified samples in the loss function. However, adjusting weights becomes difficult due to the uneven distribution of label categories. Therefore, it performs well in the MyoPS 2020 dataset, with an average Dice score of 64.2%. The Dice coefficient is a widely used metric in image segmentation for calculating the similarity between two images. A Dice loss function is used to deal with extremely imbalanced datasets. Due to the limited number of datasets and imbalanced categories, the evaluation index of this dataset is the Dice coefficient, which leads to extremely imbalanced datasets. Therefore, the Dice loss function has a certain advantage in the MyoPS 2020 dataset, with an average Dice score of 66.6%. To integrate the advantages of the above loss function, we use the combined Dice and Focal losses by retaining the better results of Dice loss and combining the advantages of Focal loss. In detail, the network’s optimization is predominantly guided by the Dice loss during the initial phases of training. As the learning iterations advance, the regulatory influence of the focal loss progressively magnifies, ultimately elevating the mean Dice coefficient to 67.6%. This progression substantiates the efficacy of our designed hybrid loss formulation. Discussion

Beyond technical segmentation performance, the multi-class outputs of MCIPS-Net offer significant clinical relevance for viability assessment and risk stratification. The automated segmentation of myocardial scars enables the direct quantification of infarct size, a critical predictor of infarction severity and prognosis. Research indicates that a myocardial scar burden exceeding 20% of the left ventricle significantly elevates the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events, whereas a burden over 40% suggests a massive infarction with poor prognosis (44). Our model facilitates this by allowing the direct calculation of the scar-to-total-myocardium volume ratio to obtain accurate scar burden. Additionally, the segmented edema region represents the “myocardium at risk” or salvageable myocardium, which correlates with the intensity of the acute inflammatory response (45). Comparing the extent of edema to the scar area assists in viability assessment; a significantly larger edema region suggests stunned myocardium that may benefit from reperfusion therapy. High Dice scores in our model ensure the reliability of these calculated area proportions, effectively translating technical segmentation accuracy into clinically actionable information.

Although our method has achieved good results, certain limitations remain. First, even if our image data is pre-processed to reduce the size of the image, the target area still only occupies a small part of the image. However, our method belongs to a single-stage framework, and directly training the image data can easily lead to the network learning too many features from non-target areas, making it difficult to improve segmentation accuracy. Second, our method’s loss function only includes a subset of the most popular variants of Dice- and cross-entropy-based loss functions, which are only based on distribution or region, making it difficult to manage blurred or unclear boundaries in certain areas of a medical image. However, a large class of loss functions not included are boundary-based loss functions (46), another type of loss function that uses distance-based metrics to optimize contours. In medical images, there may be blurry or unclear areas, such as the junctions between organs or the edges of lesions. The boundary-based loss function can help the network distinguish these fuzzy regions better, thereby reducing the fuzzy parts in the results. By introducing boundary information, boundary-based loss functions can help the network to adapt to structures of different shapes and sizes, improving the model’s robustness and generalization. Third, and perhaps most importantly, the current study was evaluated exclusively on the MyoPS 2020 dataset. This lack of external validation inherently limits the proven generalizability of the proposed MCIPS-Net. In real-world clinical environments, CMR images exhibit significant domain shifts due to variations in acquisition protocols, magnetic field strengths (e.g., 1.5T vs. 3.0T), scanner manufacturers, and diverse patient demographics. Models trained on a single-center or specific cohort dataset may experience performance degradation when deployed on unseen external data due to these distribution shifts.

Future research will focus on the following aspects: First, we will address the class imbalance issue by incorporating a greater variety and number of loss functions. Second, a multi-stage approach will be employed, where coarse segmentation initially locates approximate target regions to remove redundant background areas, followed by fine segmentation to precisely extract the target regions. This method is commonly used and effective, typically achieving higher segmentation accuracy than single-stage approaches. Third, given the limited availability of medical image segmentation datasets, we will further investigate data augmentation techniques and multi-modal image registration methods, incorporating more MRI sequences to enhance segmentation performance. Finally, to address the issue of insufficient model generalization, we plan to validate the model in independent, multicenter clinical cohorts should datasets with similar structures become available in the future. Additionally, we will explore unsupervised domain adaptation and federated learning techniques to enhance the model’s robustness across different hospital settings while avoiding the need for extensive re-labeling of new data.

Conclusions

This paper proposes a deep learning network model, MCIPS-Net, for myocardial pathology segmentation in CMR images. This model utilizes our proposed MFF to adaptively integrate and utilize the complementary information of different modal images. The model adds an embedded attention fusion module after upsampling to enhance feature extraction for the ROIs of the image with skip connections and decoding paths. Furthermore, the robustness of our segmentation architecture is fortified through the integration of data augmentation techniques, DS mechanisms, and a hybrid loss strategy. Empirical evaluations demonstrate that, when benchmarked against the leading algorithms on the MyoPS 2020 Challenge leaderboard, our framework eclipses the premier single-stage methodologies. Notably, this superior predictive performance is achieved entirely without reliance on complex two-stage processing pipelines, ensemble modeling tactics, or transfer learning paradigms. Moreover, compared to other multi-stage algorithms, our method provides superior performance and outperforms other methods on the MyoPS 2020 Challenge ranking in terms of standard deviation.

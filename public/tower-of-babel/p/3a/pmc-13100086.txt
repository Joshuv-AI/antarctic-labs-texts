Bibliometric analysis of research trends and collaboration patterns in cardio-oncology focusing on lung cancer and heart failure
Authors: Zongrong Lin, Aishi Chen, Yining Dai, Sicheng Chen, Haomin Xie, Zhenyang Fu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundLung cancer (LC) and heart failure (HF) are being increasingly seen as related conditions, with rising concerns about the cardiotoxic effects of treatments.MethodsWe conducted a bibliometric study on research related to LC and HF from 2014 to 2024 using the Web of Science Core Collection. To examine research trends and collaborations, data were analyzed and visualized using R, CiteSpace, and VOSviewer.ResultsThe research encompassed 531 articles from 194 different journals, with contributions from 3963 authors. The United States and China have been the leading contributors to LC and HF research, with 146 and 120 publications, and 5307 and 1403 citations, respectively. Carmen Bergom from the United States, along with Aidan J. Cole, Suneil Jain, and Gerard M. Walls from the United Kingdom, are recognized leaders in this field due to their prolific publications. Lancet Oncology has consistently and significantly impacted the field, and an article in the Journal of Clinical Oncology, which is the most cited, examines cardiac toxicity after radiotherapy for Stage III non-small-cell lung cancer through a pooled analysis of dose-escalation trials delivering 70 to 90 Gy. The focus of recent research is on immunotherapy (ICIs), cardiac toxicity associated with radiotherapy, and palliative care.ConclusionThis innovative mapping research highlights an increasing focus on the link between LC and HF, particularly concerning treatment-induced cardiotoxicity. Nonetheless, studies on their two-way interaction are still scarce, pointing to a promising yet under-researched area. Combining oncology and cardiology could reveal new insights to tailor personalized care and enhance results. Our research provides a foundation for future interdisciplinary investigations in the developing field of cardio-oncology.

Introduction

Lung cancer (LC) continues to be the top cause of cancer-related deaths globally, representing a major challenge to global public health [1]. While tobacco smoking accounts for approximately 75–80% of LC deaths, recent data from the World Health Organization indicate that the incidence of LC in never smokers (LCINS) has risen to nearly 25%, ranking as the seventh most common cause of cancer-related death [1]. The evolving epidemiology of LC is highlighted by this trend, showing that awareness of risk factors is important for both smokers and those who have never smoked [2]. In addition to its high death rate, LC leads to serious health issues such as reduced lung function, low blood oxygen levels, respiratory failure, a higher risk of infections, and a significantly diminished quality of life [1, 2].

Heart failure (HF) is a clinical condition marked by compromised cardiac structure or function, leading to inadequate blood flow to satisfy the body’s metabolic needs, and is becoming an increasing global health challenge [3, 4]. It is typically classified into heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF), marked by decreased contractility and an ejection fraction below 40%, and heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF), associated with diastolic dysfunction and a preserved ejection fraction of 50% or higher [3, 5]. The prevalence of HF is expected to increase by over 50% in low- and middle-income countries over the next ten years, driven by an aging population and ongoing unhealthy lifestyle choices [6].

Recent findings indicate a significant clinical and biological connection between LC and HF [7]. Due to shared risk factors such as smoking, age, and systemic inflammation, these two conditions frequently appear simultaneously [8, 9]. Furthermore, cancer treatments such as chemotherapy, targeted therapies, and chest radiotherapy directly increase the risk of heart damage, raising the chances of HF in LC patients [10, 11]. Pre-existing or treatment-induced HF can, in turn, hinder cancer treatment by worsening complications like pneumonia and sepsis, reducing treatment tolerance, and leading to a poorer prognosis [12]. Although awareness is rising, there is still a lack of complete understanding of the research landscape at the junction of LC and HF. Current research is frequently disjointed, concentrating on particular treatments or mechanisms, without providing a comprehensive overview of trends, key areas, or knowledge gaps in this developing field.

We performed a bibliometric analysis of publications from 2014 to 2024 related to LC and HF to meet this requirement. By employing tools such as Bibliometrix R, CiteSpace, and VOSviewer, we systematically screened, quantified, and visualized relevant publications to assess research output, collaborations, thematic evolution, and emerging trends. Using this approach, we were able to recognize significant contributors, impactful articles, and crucial subjects that outline the current knowledge in this interdisciplinary area.

Our initial study offers a detailed summary of the intellectual terrain connecting LC and HF, highlighting a significant change in research emphasis towards treatment-related cardiotoxicity, survivorship care, and systemic interactions. By documenting these advancements, the research fills an important void in the literature and lays the groundwork for future clinical and mechanistic studies. The knowledge acquired not only aids in integrated disease management strategies but also directs resource distribution and emphasizes urgent research areas in the fast-developing field of cardio-oncology.

Materials and methods

Data sources and search strategy

This study leverages the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC), a database for bibliometric analysis, attributed to its extensive disciplinary scope, longstanding data retrospective capabilities, robust search functions, rich array of bibliometric indicators, and in-depth citation analysis features. These characteristics enable researchers to identify key research areas and recognize trends in their field. Despite WoSCC being recognized for its rigorous peer-review processes and unified citation indexing, it possesses inherent coverage limitations that may lead to biases in our outcomes: First, journals published in languages other than English, like Spanish and Arabic, constitute less than 10%, possibly leading to an undervaluation of research from regions where English is not predominantly spoken—especially important due to the increasing rates of LC and HF occurring together in Latin America and the Middle East. Second, it offers limited access to regional journals from low- and middle-income countries (LMICs), such as certain African journals, where the incidence of both diseases is rising quickly. Region-specific findings, including locally adapted cardiotoxicity treatment strategies, might be omitted as a consequence. Third, compared to PubMed, WoSCC has a smaller number of studies centered on clinical practice, which are vital for translating bibliometric findings into real-world cardio-oncology care.

Data on LC and HF publications were obtained from the WoSCC database for bibliometric analysis. To avoid any discrepancies due to data updates, the processes of searching, extracting data, and downloading were completed on the same day. The search query utilized was:

TS= ((“lung cancer” OR “pulmonary cancer” OR “lung carcinoma” OR “pulmonary carcinoma” OR “lung tumor*” OR “pulmonary tumor*” OR “lung neoplas*” OR “pulmonary neoplas*” OR NSCLC OR “non-small cell lung cancer” OR “non-small cell lung cancer” OR SCLC OR “small cell lung cancer” OR “bronchogenic carcinoma” OR “lung adenocarcinoma” OR LUAD OR “squamous cell lung carcinoma” OR LUSC OR “large cell lung carcinoma”)).

AND

TS= ((“heart failure” OR “cardiac failure” OR “myocardial failure” OR “cardiac dysfunction” OR “ventricular dysfunction” OR “left ventricular dysfunction” OR “cardiotoxicity” OR “chemotherapy-induced cardiotoxicity” OR “cancer therapy-related cardiac dysfunction” OR HFrEF OR HFpEF OR HFmrEF OR “cardiac adverse event*” OR “cardiovascular complication*” OR “myocardial injury” OR “cardiac damage”)).

In total, 531 literature sources were subjected to analysis, and the raw data, encompassing full records and cited references, were downloaded in text format.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria

Studies published from 2014 to 2024 that were categorized in the Web of Science under “Oncology,” “Cardiac & Cardiovascular Systems,” or “Respiratory System.”

Exclusion criteria

Documents not in English, as well as conference proceedings, editorials, commentaries, or letters. Articles that do not adequately address both LC and HF.

Data extraction

Bibliographic information such as title, authors, source, publication year, abstract, keywords, and cited references was extracted from each eligible study for further analysis. After removing duplicates and irrelevant entries, a final dataset was created for bibliographic processing.

Bibliometric analyses

Bibliometric and network analyses were performed using 3 primary software tools:

VOSviewer [13]

The co-occurrence maps for authors and keywords (Figs. 5 and 6) and co-citation references for journals, authors, publication years, and institutions (Fig. 7) were presented to highlight collaboration patterns and research hubs. Density visualizations were created to illustrate the development and intensity of key topics.

CiteSpace [14]

We conducted analyses of institutions, and keyword collaboration (Figs. 3, 6 and 8) to highlight emerging trends and rapidly changing research interests. The analysis was based on keyword clustering and timelines to identify dominant themes over time and the key content of the research (Fig. 8).

R

Charts were plotted to illustrate the increasing publication trend, country collaboration networks (Figs. 1 and 2), and the top 10 countries and institutions associated with LC and HF, including their article counts and journal impact indices (Figs. 2, 3 and 4). Additionally, keyword cluster maps, thematic maps, conceptual structure maps, and trend topic timelines were generated to highlight research trends and evolving research interests (Fig. 8).

Data visualization

Figures 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8 were designed to systematically depict the main aspects of the research landscape for LC and HF from 2014 to 2024: Fig. 1 offers a worldwide summary of publication trends, covering both yearly and cumulative outputs; Figs. 2, 3, 4 and 5 illustrate collaboration patterns and rank the top 10 countries, institutions, authors, and journals; Figure 6 shows keyword co-occurrence networks to spotlight research hotspots; Fig. 7 depicts co-citation links among journals, authors, and references to determine influential academic connections; and Fig. 8 presents keyword maps, collaboration networks, and timelines to track the thematic development of the field. Every figure was carefully examined to confirm they accurately represent LC and HF in clinical medicine. Standard conventions were used to produce all bibliometric visualizations, which helps in making the results reproducible and easier to interpret.

Global overview

As depicted in Fig. 1, the specified search strategy led to the retrieval of 531 relevant articles from the WoSCC database, covering the years 2014 to 2024. The field experienced a consistent rise in research activity, with publications increasing at an average yearly rate of 7.92%. The total number of citations for these publications reached 19,064, averaging 29.71 citations per article. Significantly, four articles were cited more than 200 times, reflecting their substantial academic impact. In total, 3,828 authors contributed to the body of work published in 194 journals worldwide. Among them, a mere 8 papers had a single author, which suggests that research in this domain is largely collaborative. The articles had an average age of 5.1 years, suggesting that the dataset represents fairly recent and current research topics.

Annual publication trend of LC and HF

As presented in Fig. 1, beginning in 2014, the annual volume of publications related to LC and HF has shown overall stability, with substantial cumulative growth, reaching nearly 15 times the 2014 output by 2024 (Fig. 1B). We used SPSSAU to build a trend analysis model, which showed a significant correlation between the number of publications and the year (Fig. 1C). The equation for regression was: log(µ) = −181.388 + 0.092×Year, where µ signifies the forecasted number of publications each year. The model accounted for 35.5% of the variance in yearly publications (McFadden R²=0.355), with the Year variable having a significant positive impact on the number of publications (regression coefficient = 0.092, z = 6.518, p < 0.001; OR = 1.096, 95%CI:1.066 ~ 1.127). Every year added was connected to a 9.6% boost in the number of yearly publications. The pattern implies that the scholarly interest in the connection between LC and HF is anticipated to rise, potentially producing more influential research findings in the near future.

Segmented trends around major clinical inflection points

To explore the impact of clinical advancements on publication trends, we divided the yearly publication data from 2014 to 2024 into three phases, based on two major clinical milestones in the field of cardio-oncology, as shown by keyword burst analysis in Fig. 6B and clinical guidelines:

During this era, the use of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) in LC was not widespread, and guidelines for cardio-oncology had not yet concentrated on HF associated with LC. The number of publications each year displayed a steady increase: 35 in 2014, 26 in 2015, 33 in 2016, 46 in 2017, and 53 in 2018. The Poisson regression analysis for this phase (n = 5) showed an annual growth rate of 15.8% (OR = 1.158, 95% CI: 1.046–1.281, likelihood ratio test χ² = 8.162, df = 1, p < 0.001). The equation for the model is log(µ) = −291.553 + 0.146×Year, where µ stands for the anticipated yearly publication number, and the McFadden R² value of 0.208 suggests that the year accounts for 20.8% of the variance in annual publications during this period. This moderate growth trend might correspond with the relatively undeveloped clinical demand in this area at the time.

By 2019, ICIs were broadly used for advanced LC, and their cardiotoxicity became a prominent research area. The annual publication figures rebounded after a brief dip: 29 (2019) → 45 (2020) → 51 (2021). In this phase (n = 3), Poisson regression analysis indicated a rapid annual growth rate of 30.6% (OR = 1.306, 95% CI: 1.050–1.625, likelihood ratio test χ² = 5.842, df = 1, p < 0.001). The equation for the model is log(µ) = −535.894 + 0.267×Year, where µ stands for the anticipated yearly publication number, and the McFadden R² value is 0.252, indicating that the year accounts for 25.2% of the variance in annual publications over this time frame. The rapid growth could be associated with the increasing research emphasis on cardiotoxicity related to ICIs after their broad clinical adoption.

The 2022 release of the 2022 AHA/ACC/HFSA Guideline for the Management of Heart Failure included specific recommendations for cardio-oncology comorbidities, leading to more focused research. The annual publication counts surged and maintained high growth: 72 (2022) → 66 (2023) → 75 (2024). The Poisson regression analysis (n = 3) indicated that the model was not valid (likelihood ratio test p = 0.949 > 0.05), with a McFadden R² of 0.003 and a Year coefficient that was not significant (0.021, z = 0.252, p = 0.801 > 0.05), suggesting that Year did not affect publications. The absence of a trend might indicate a shift in the field from broad quantitative growth to concentrated refinement after the guideline, with variations reflecting specific subtopic research instead of overall expansion.

To summarize, the segmented analysis identified three separate growth phases, highlighting two major clinical turning points: the broad adoption of ICIs in 2019 and the release of HF guidelines focused on cardio-oncology in 2022, appearing to correspond with variations in research emphasis. There might be a relationship between these two milestones and the observed changes in publication trends.

Highly cited papers

Table 3 lists the 10 most highly cited papers in the field. The Journal of Clinical Oncology’s 2017 publication, ‘Cardiac Toxicity After Radiotherapy for Stage III Non–Small-Cell Lung Cancer: Pooled Analysis of Dose-Escalation Trials Delivering 70 to 90 Gy,’ achieved the top rank with 50 local and 333 global citations. This investigation revealed that for patients with stage III NSCLC, cardiac toxicity had an independent association with both the radiation dose and pre-existing cardiac risk, potentially occurring earlier than anticipated. Seven out of the top 10 papers focused on cardiotoxicity linked to treatments such as radiotherapy, chemotherapy, and immunotherapy. Nonetheless, just a single article focused on the mechanisms of cardiotoxicity associated with immunotherapy or targeted therapies, highlighting a lack of mechanistic studies.

Co-occurrence analysis

In Fig. 6, keyword co-occurrence analysis elucidates the relationships and evolving themes within the literature (Fig. 6A). The main subjects focused on ‘lung cancer’ and ‘cardiotoxicity’. Left-side clusters highlighted pathogenesis (e.g., “inflammation,” “arrhythmia,” “myocardial infarction”), clinical symptoms (e.g., “atrial fibrillation,” “pleural effusion,” “cachexia”), and disease phenotypes (e.g., “NSCLC,” “cardiovascular toxicity”). Right-side clusters centered on interventions (e.g., “radiotherapy,” “immunotherapy,” “tyrosine kinase inhibitor”). These findings suggest a convergence of pathophysiology, clinical presentation, and treatment strategies between LC and HF. Keyword burst analysis (Fig. 6B) revealed an evolution from early focus on clinical symptoms to later emphasis on adverse cardiovascular events and prognosis. Research hotspots included radiotherapy, cardiotoxicity, and dose-escalation trials, with survival and mortality emerging as critical themes (Fig. 6C, D).

Recent trends in research

In Fig. 8, thematic term and keyword clustering analysis (Fig. 8C, D) indicated that current mature research areas involve cardiovascular complications of cancer treatment, such as those in breast cancer, coronary artery disease, and dose-escalation trials. Themes that were less developed included obstructive pulmonary disease and the results of surgeries. Emerging themes, such as metastatic breast cancer, tyrosine kinase inhibitors, and angiogenesis, reflect increasing focus on molecular and therapeutic outcomes. However, the mechanistic basis of LC and HF comorbidity remains insufficiently explored. Cluster analysis (Fig. 8C, D) revealed three thematic groups: (1) clinical manifestations and treatment-related cardiotoxicity (red cluster); (2) immunotherapy-related complications (green cluster); and (3) chronic complications, disease management, and prognosis (blue cluster). CiteSpace-based keyword clustering from 2014 to 2024 (Fig. 8A) and time trend visualization (Fig. 8B, E) showed that recent focus has shifted to treatment-induced cardiac toxicity and its clinical implications, highlighting these as emerging research frontiers.

Discussion

In this study, we conducted a comprehensive bibliometric analysis of 531 articles related to LC and HF, aiming to elucidate the knowledge structure, research hotspots, and developmental trends in this emerging interdisciplinary field. We used tools like Bibliometrix R, CiteSpace, and VOSviewer to systematically analyze publication trends by country, institution, author, journal, and keyword. Our study highlights an increasing interest in the two-way connection between LC and HF, especially regarding cardiotoxicity caused by cancer treatments and care for survivors. These findings address a notable gap in the literature regarding the systemic interactions between oncological therapies and cardiovascular complications, highlighting the increasing recognition of HF as a critical comorbidity in lung cancer patients.

The complementary segmented trend analysis identified three separate phases of research growth, each corresponding to two key clinical milestones (the adoption of ICI in 2019 and the release of guidelines in 2022), offering detailed insights into how clinical advancements influenced the field’s evolution beyond the general timeline. In this research domain, the United States stood out as the primary contributor, leading in publication numbers and citation impact, with a strong presence among top institutions and authors. This suggests that the U.S. has a robust research infrastructure and funding in cardio-oncology, potentially serving as a model for other nations looking to build expertise in this field. Our co-occurrence analysis identified “immune checkpoint inhibitors” as a frequently cited keyword, underscoring the growing concern about the cardiovascular side effects of ICIs in LC therapy. This is consistent with earlier research indicating that ICIs, particularly when used alongside chemotherapy, significantly increase the risk of acute heart failure, atherosclerotic incidents, and arrhythmias [16, 17]. Importantly, although ICIs monotherapy doesn’t seem to significantly increase cardiotoxicity risk compared to just chemotherapy, combination therapy presents a much higher risk, indicating a possible additive or synergistic effect [18].

Further supporting these observations, recent evidence demonstrates accelerated coronary plaque progression and heightened risk of acute coronary syndrome among patients receiving ICIs, particularly in the context of pre-existing cardiovascular disease [19]. These results align with the growing body of cardio-oncology research and offer a mechanistic understanding of how immune-related treatments might enhance cardiovascular issues, probably via vascular inflammation and immune-induced damage [16].

Beyond treatment-induced cardiotoxicity, the direct relationship between LC biology and cardiac performance is gaining recognition. For instance, patients with advanced LC often show a high rate of cardiac dysfunction (60%), which is strongly linked to tumor-related issues like malignant pleural and pericardial effusions [20]; These conditions might encourage local inflammatory reactions that indirectly hinder heart function, which aligns with our keyword co-occurrence analysis where the ‘inflammation’ node appears.Further validation is necessary to clarify the specific role of pro-inflammatory factors like IL-6 or TNF-α that are secreted by tumors. Furthermore, echocardiographic findings suggest that the advancement of LC is linked to declines in left ventricular ejection fraction and strain metrics, with cancer therapeutics-related cardiac dysfunction more frequent in patients with radiologically confirmed disease advancement, implying a potential bidirectional crosstalk where tumor burden exacerbates cardiac impairment [21]. Importantly, these direct interactions work both ways: cardiac dysfunction can influence tumor biology as well. For instance, systemic hypoxia caused by HF increases the levels of hypoxia-inducible factor-1α (HIF-1α) in LC cells, which enhances angiogenesis and tumor growth [22]. However, while these findings hint at direct tumor-cardiac interactions, most available research focuses on treatment toxicity or correlative changes, molecular mechanisms (e.g., specific cytokines or exosomal miRNAs) underlying this direct crosstalk are not well-documented in the available literature, highlighting a critical knowledge gap. Future studies should prioritize elucidating these direct pathways to inform targeted cardioprotective strategies in LC patients.

Cluster and timeline analyses further clarified the thematic evolution of this field. The early focus on “chronic obstructive pulmonary disease” has gradually shifted toward more treatment-specific themes, such as “non-small cell lung cancer,” “myocarditis,” “palliative care,” and “radiotherapy.” The evolution highlights the field’s move from studying epidemiological connections to considering therapy and survivorship. Using CiteSpace, we conducted keyword clustering. Our findings revealed a modularity Q of 0.4358, which generally suggests a reasonable community structure in bibliometric networks when the value exceeds 0.3. And the weighted mean silhouette S value of 0.7555 suggests that the clusters are well-defined, given that values over 0.5 indicate good homogeneity. These metrics confirm the robustness of the thematic delineation, with clear cluster separation and internal consistency. In particular, the emergence of radiotherapy as a prominent research trend is notable. Research increasingly shows that radiation exposure to cardiac substructures may cause a range of arrhythmias and ischemic injuries, with a significant number of patients experiencing atrial fibrillation. Radiotherapy-induced heart disease, which includes damage to the coronary arteries, pericardial issues, and myocardial fibrosis, is considered a major factor connecting cancer therapy to HF [23]. These results support earlier research highlighting the need to reduce cardiac dose, with contemporary methods like intensity-modulated radiotherapy demonstrating potential in lowering heart-related toxicity while maintaining cancer treatment effectiveness [23].

In addition to treatment-related mechanisms, exercise has recently gained attention as a potential intervention for mitigating cardiovascular risk in LC survivors [24]. Bibliometric clustering reveals growing attention towards exercise-based rehabilitation, especially for improving cardiopulmonary fitness and quality of life in cancer patients experiencing HF due to treatment or concurrently. Previous studies have demonstrated the efficacy of both aerobic and combined training modalities in improving peak VO₂ and alleviating symptoms such as fatigue and dyspnea [25]. Moreover, integrating relaxation and aerobic exercise has shown beneficial effects on emotional well-being in elderly patients [26]. These observations are consistent with clinical recommendations for individualized rehabilitation programs tailored to cardiac functional class, further stressing the role of non-drug strategies in comprehensive survivorship care [27]. Nonetheless, there is a lack of clarity regarding the specific impacts of endurance and resistance training on cancer-related fatigue, which warrants additional investigation.

Even though this analysis is comprehensive and systematic, it is important to recognize some limitations. First, it uses only the WoSCC for data, which is a considerable limitation of the database. Although WoSCC is appreciated for its strict peer-review criteria and uniform citation indexing, which guarantee the dependability of our bibliometric metrics like citation impact and co-citation networks, it does not entirely reflect the extensive clinical and biomedical research field. Particularly, leaving out databases like PubMed and Scopus, which have broader coverage in clinical and biomedical areas, might lead to selection bias. PubMed serves as a key database for biomedicine and clinical research, focusing on clinical studies such as retrospective clinical trials and real-world cardio-oncology care research, whose material is underrepresented in WoSCC and essential for highlighting trends in research related to clinical practice. Meanwhile, Scopus delivers wider regional coverage, especially for publications originating from low- and middle-income countries (LMICs) where the incidence of LC and HF is increasing. Excluding Scopus might result in missing region-specific studies and undervaluing the contributions of researchers from low- and middle-income countries, which could distort our analysis of global collaboration networks and regional research hotspots. Second, the period from 2014 to 2024 omits earlier essential research on disease comorbidity and might miss late-2024 publications that are not yet listed in WoSCC, limiting the breadth of long-term trend analysis. This probably resulted in an underestimation of the latest research outputs, such as new trends in the cardiotoxicity profiles of novel immunotherapies or AI-based cardio-oncology risk prediction models—subjects that might enhance our comprehension of current developments. Third, citation timing bias during the 2014–2024 timeframe undermines the objective evaluation of influential studies and key contributors. Research from earlier years, particularly before 2020, benefits from a longer citation period. The 2017 Journal of Clinical Oncology study on cardiac toxicity due to radiotherapy (Table 3) has accumulated 333 citations worldwide over 7 years. Research conducted after 2022, even on important issues like the molecular links between LC and HF, has only been available for 1–2 years. This amplifies the impact of established research groups, while downplaying the contributions of new researchers who are addressing important knowledge gaps in the field, such as the underexplored bidirectional interaction between LC and HF.

Nevertheless, we refrained from performing additional searches in Scopus or PubMed, mainly because of three practical reasons. To begin with, the overlap of primary literature sources in various databases removes the requirement for extra resources. A comparative analysis of the journal lists of Web of Science (WoS) and Scopus demonstrates that key English-language journals with high impact, like the Journal of Clinical Oncology and Lancet Oncology, are listed on both platforms, showing a significant overlap in major literature sources. Likewise, systematic evaluations of PubMed, Scopus, and WoS for biomedical research indicate that most clinical study records found in PubMed are also available in WoS. These results indicate that WoSCC already includes the majority of essential clinical evidence related to our subject, suggesting that one search in WoSCC is enough to obtain a representative sample of the literature for the present bibliometric study, making additional searches in Scopus or PubMed unnecessary. Secondly, combining Scopus with WoSCC would present considerable challenges in data cleaning. The integration of these databases produces a substantial amount of duplicate records that must be meticulously deduplicated and standardized. Notably, tools like VOSviewer and CiteSpace rely heavily on complete, consistent bibliographic metadata: even minor inconsistencies in author names, journal titles, or publication years can introduce noise into co-word, co-authorship, and other network visualizations, ultimately compromising the reliability of results. As a result, careful removal of duplicates and data purification are necessary for these visualization tools to be used effectively. However, merging multiple databases considerably heightens the workload linked to these tasks, which in turn boosts the probability of human error in the data processing stage. Third, our research goal of emphasizing consistency in network structure further justified using a single database. To prevent any bias in network structure due to variations in bibliographic formatting among databases, we decided to use WoSCC as our only data source. This method maintains the sample’s representativeness while reducing the likelihood of mistakes during cross-database data cleaning and format conversion. Based on the evidence above, using WoSCC as the only search database guaranteed the inclusion of key English journals, prevented numerous duplicates and format issues from merging multiple databases, and thus preserved data consistency for network analysis. For future research, other investigators could adapt our search strategy to conduct separate supplementary searches: one using Scopus to enhance regional coverage by including LMICs-related literature, and another using PubMed to enrich research dimensions with more clinical practice data. By using this strategy, the current constraints can be addressed while maintaining both representativeness and analytical thoroughness.

Although this bibliometric analysis highlights increasing research activity related to LC and HF—particularly in areas such as immunotherapy-induced cardiotoxicity and radiotherapy-related complications—it is noteworthy that studies specifically addressing the bidirectional interaction between LC and HF remain scarce. This scarcity may be attributed to two key factors: first, the topic necessitates interdisciplinary cooperation between oncology and cardiology; however, traditional research usually remains confined to single-discipline frameworks, causing fragmented studies of each disease separately; second, the two-way interaction includes intricate mechanisms, such as shared inflammatory pathways and oxidative stress crosstalk, which require multi-omics data and long-term prospective studies. Funding and clinical priorities often focus on more urgent issues like treatment-induced cardiotoxicity, neglecting the wider interactions. The absence of high-frequency keywords or citation clusters directly linking the mechanistic interplay between these two conditions suggests that this topic remains an underexplored area with significant potential—characterized by insufficient interdisciplinary investigations and unaddressed knowledge gaps in bidirectional interaction mechanisms. The growing overlap between cancer survival and HF prevalence necessitates an urgent need to comprehend their reciprocal effects. Examining this intersection at the molecular, cellular, and clinical levels may uncover fresh insights into disease pathophysiology and support the formulation of more precise, cohesive healthcare strategies. Progressing research in this domain would enhance the field of cardio-oncology and greatly boost long-term outcomes for patients suffering from both cancer and cardiovascular disease.

Conclusion

In summary, our results highlight the increasing complexity and clinical importance of the link between LC and HF. This comprehensive bibliometric analysis not only maps the development of this interdisciplinary field in cardio-oncology but also presents key observations regarding high-risk treatments and innovative therapeutic approaches. Yet, the scarcity of studies examining the mechanistic reciprocal relationship between these conditions suggests a fruitful area for future research. To fill this gap, specific approaches could include: 1) Establishing interdisciplinary multicenter cohorts to observe LC patients with pre-existing HF (and vice versa), collecting longitudinal clinical and multi-omics data to trace bidirectional pathophysiological pathways; 2)Employing AI to scrutinize real-world datasets, including clinical records, imaging, and lab results from large groups, to quantify the relationships between the progression of LC and HF trajectories, and to detect hidden temporal correlations;3) Performing preclinical research on common molecular targets like NF-κB and Nrf2 using animal models that represent two diseases to confirm possible treatments. The advancement of cardio-oncology necessitates urgent high-quality mechanistic studies to uncover causality, refine risk categorization, and guide ongoing management. Combining molecular cardiology with oncology offers potential for creating personalized treatments that reduce heart-related side effects and enhance therapeutic effectiveness. The analysis of bibliometric data provides a basis for advancing research and fostering interdisciplinary collaboration in this clinically significant yet underexplored area.

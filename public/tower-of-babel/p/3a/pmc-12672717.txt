Monocyte/macrophages may exert prominent roles in macrophage activation syndrome
Authors: Dongfei Yin, Jingshi Wang, Zhao Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Secondary Hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (sHLH) is a life-threatening complication mostly occurs in adult patients with rheumatic disease, infection or lymphoma. To adequately steer treatment, clear discrimination of sHLH entities is essential. We aimed to discriminate serum biomarkers which can separate MAS from EBV associated HLH (EBV-HLH) and Lymphoma associated HLH (L-HLH) adequately. Samples from patients aged > 15 diagnosed with sHLH were analyzed using Luminex Multiple Assays (34-plex for pre-2022; 11-plex for post-2022). 209 patients were divided into three cohorts due to change of Luminex kits. Among 86 patients enrolled pre-2021, 45 patients enrolled in 2021–2022, and 78 patients enrolled post-2022. Clinical data and serum biomarkers were analyzed among MAS, EBV-HLH and L-HLH in three cohorts by Kruskal-Wallis Analysis (K-W). The Heatmap and Principal Component Analysis (PCA) were used to describe the distinctive expression of serum biomarkers in MAS, EBV-HLH and L-HLH. Other than the relationship between clinical data and biomarkers was exhibited in the Heatmap according to the Spearman’s Rank Co-efficient. Furthermore Network analysis recognized the prominent biomarker in MAS. The blooding cells disproportion declined in MAS patients relative to EBV-HLH and L-HLH. Other than the level of sCD25 increased higher in EBV-HLH and L-HLH patients than MAS patients. Progressive levels of MIP-1α and IL-10 were elevated in patients with EBV-HLH and L-HLH relative to MAS. PCA and the heatmap of serum biomarkers expressions did not discriminate MAS from EBV-HLH and L-HLH efficiently. Although there was no significant difference in the relationship between clinical data and serum biomarkers, we observed the further positive association between myeloid and lymphoid-derived serum biomarkers in MAS compared with EBV-HLH and L-HLH. Further analysis revealed that IL-17 was as a prominent biomarker in pathology in MAS by Network Analysis. (1) The relationship between myeloid and lymphoid-derived biomarkers was further relevant in MAS than EBV-HLH and L-HLH. (2) The IL-17 released mostly by Th-17 cells was grouped together closely with other inflammatory mediators and we infer that IL-17 may exert an important role in MAS. These findings could guidance the efficacy of drugs targeting key cell and biomarkers specifically associated with MAS.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00277-025-06640-3.

Introduction

Cytokine release syndrome (CRS) [1] is a life-threatening complication resulting from a systemic pro-inflammatory response. Although the CRS shows substantial clinical and immunophenotypic overlap with other diseases, including hypercytokinemia, persistent fever, organomegaly, cytopenia, hyperferritinaemia, and so on, they can originate from different background diseases, such as hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH), Chimeric Antigen Receptor T-Cell Immunotherapy (CART) treatment, and even COVID-19 infection [2–4]. Mounting researchers have characterized COVID-19 and CART-associated CRS in recent years [5]. There are limited studies about cytokine profiling in HLH, especially in adults.

HLH is classified into familial HLH (fHLH) and secondary HLH (sHLH) [6]. FHLH is caused by mutations of genes coding for proteins involved in granule exocytosis, leading to defective cytotoxic activity of CD8-positive T cells (CD8 + T cells) and Natural Killer (NK) cells. S-HLH appears as complications of infections, lymphomas, or rheumatic diseases, in the absence of an identical genetic cause, and is apparently more common than fHLH, especially in adults. Mounting levels of cytokines play a pivotal role in HLH, regardless of fHLH or sHLH. Remarkably high levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines are reported in HLH murine models. Mounting evidence provides support for the pivotal pathogenic role of IFN-γ and CD8 + T cells in fHLH [7–9]. However in the modules of sHLH [10–12]such as repeated CpG administration, lymphocytes (including T, B, and NK cells) are not the primary effector cells driving the disease. Impressively, the activation of myeloid cells exhibits a pronounced pathogenic advantage. These data demonstrate that different cytokine profiles originating from different heterogeneous cells can lead to the assimilation of clinical manifestations. Therefore, it prompts us to characterize the profile of biomarkers in HLH, steering cytokine-directed treatment.

In recent years, abundant evidence supports the pronounced over-expression of IL-18 as the hallmark in MAS [13–15]. As with IL-18, differential expression of S100A12, MCP-2, MCSF, cathepsin B, and soluble FASL between sHLH and systemic juvenile idiopathic arthritis (sJIA) associated MAS further underscores potential differences in the underlying disease pathophysiology, despite large or even complete clinical phenotypic overlap between these entities [15].IL-1 and IL-6 have also been implicated as important cytokines in MAS pathology [16]. However, there are limited researchers characterizing the levels of biomarkers among EBV-HLH, L-HLH, and MAS, especially in adults. The limited results are derived from children mostly [15, 17]. In this research, we reported on a broad approach to identify specific serum biomarkers profiles in EBV-HLH, L-HLH, and MAS. We concluded that although the profiles of inflammatory cytokines did not completely differentiate MAS from EBV-HLH and L-HLH, myeloid and lymphoid-derived cytokines have further positive relationship in MAS relative to EBV-HLH or L-HLH.

Patients and methods

Study design

In this retrospective cohort study, serum samples from patients (> 15 years) with a clinical diagnosis of sHLH, including MAS, EBV-HLH, and L-HLH based on established clinical criteria66, were collected from patients admitted to the Hematology of Beijing Friendship Hospital of the Capital University. All samples were collected during active phases, whether patients had received immuno-suppressive therapies or not. We compared clinical data and serum biomarkers among sHLH. All patients had signed informed consent. The ethics had approved this procedure in Beijing Friendship Hospital, clinical trial registration BJFH-EC/2014–094).

Quantification of serum biomarkers

For quantification of biomarkers in the serum of all subjects, we used Luminex Multiplex assays to measure (The details were described in the Supplementary Material). We divided all patients into three cohorts as a result of the change of Luminex kits. Data acquisition and analysis were performed on Multiplex Analyst. Standard curves were established following a five-parameter logistic regression model (5PL). Sample concentrations were immediately interpolated from the standard curves. Values presenting a coefficient of variation beyond 15% were discarded before the final data analysis.

Definition

The diagnosis of HLH based on HLH-04 criteria [6] include fever (> 38.5℃ more than one week, splenomegaly, cytopenia affecting at least 2 lineages (Hemoglobin < 10 g/dL, Platelets < 109/L, Neutrophils < 109/L), hyper-triglyceridemia (> 3mmol/L) and/or hypo-fibrinogenemia (< 150 mg/dL), hemophagocytosis, hyperferritinemia (> 500 mg/dL), impaired NK cell’s function and elevated soluble CD25 (sCD25). The diagnosis of HLH requires the presence at least 5 out of 8 criteria. The terms MAS, EBV-HLH, and L-HLH are frequently diagnosed HLH secondary to rheumatic diseases, EB virus infection, and lymphomas respectively. The enrollments included in our research are patients diagnosed with HLH and did not receive any therapies or patients who were diagnosed with refractory or relapse HLH. Refractory or relapse HLH were defined on clinical criteria according to our previous study, which was described in the Supplementary Material [18]. All in all, the patients enrolled in our study were in active disease phase.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using Python 3.9.6 with Pandas 2.2.3 and Matplotlib 3.9.4. The distribution of mediator data was tested by D’Agostino and Pearson normality tests, revealing most data to be non-parametrically distributed. The Chi-Squared test was used for the comparison of proportions. Quantitative data were analyzed by Kruskal-Wallis tests, followed by the Bonferroni (BF) test for multiple comparisons of groups. Principal component analyses(PCA) were done using Scikit-learn 1.6.1 and Python. Heatmaps of log10 transformed, scaled, and centered plasma mediators data were generated using Scipy 1.13.1 with rows and columns split by dendrograms based on Ward’s minimum variance method (ward.D2) and Pearson Cor-relation Co-efficient. Z-scores were calculated using the mean and standard deviation of all values from all patients. Correlation matrix analysis was performed using Scipy 1.13.1 and Spearman’s Rank correlation of non-parametric data. Network analysis of inflammatory mediator values using Python (version 3.9.6, Networkx 3.2.1) was used to visualize mediator-to-mediator correlation based on Spearman’s Rank coefficient in which the edges represent Spearman coefficient > 0.6.

Discussion

In this study, we reported on a broad approach to identify clinical data and biomarker profiles among MAS, EBV-HLH, and L-HLH. Although the underlying background diseases were different, large or even complete clinical phenotypic overlapped in sHLH patients. We did not discriminate MAS from other sHLH according to the levels of biomarkers, we found a pronounced positive relation between myeloid and lymphoid-derived biomarkers in MAS relative to EBV-HLH and L-HLH. Meanwhile, network analysis characterized IL-17 as at the center of inflammatory biomarkers in MAS and may exert an important role in MAS.

Our study validated previous findings that the blooding cells disproportionately declined in MAS compared with EBV-HLH and L-HLH. Interestingly, we found the level of monocyte increased in MAS than other sHLH, in accordance with Piero Ruscitti’s study in which MAS patients showed an increase in circulating ‘classical monocytes’ than HC and AOSD patients [23]. The phenomenon provided evidence that monocyte/macrophages may be the pivotal pathogenic factor in MAS. The result needed to be carefully concluded because the level of monocyte was high in rheumatic disease before disease progression to MAS. Although the background diseases were distinctive among MAS, EBV-HLH and L-HLH, large or even complete clinical features (such as hyper-transaminase and hyper-ferritinemia) overlapped among these entities. In our research, we observed an interesting phenomenon that the level of sCD25 which was considered as a reflection of T cells hyper-activation was significantly elevated in EBV-HLH and L-HLH than in MAS [21]. T cells exerted an indispensable role in HLH pathogen, other than T cells were closely related with the background diseases in EBV-HLH and L-HLH, including EBV infected in T cells and T cells lymphomas. Therefore the abnormal T cells in background diseases and the hyper-activated T cells after disease progression to HLH may give an explanation for this phenomenon. A subset of serum biomarkers was selectively elevated in patients with EBV-HLH and L-HLH compared with MAS. Among these biomarkers, we observed IL-10 and MIP-1α were significantly increased in three cohorts. IL-10 as an immunosuppressive cytokine [24]which was considered as a regulatory molecule limiting the inflammatory process, possibly reflecting a secondary, albeit insufficient, counter-regulatory response to the CRS [25]. Much more studies indicated that patients with distinct cancers have increased IL-10 levels, in the serum and within the local TME. In consistent with IL-10, MIP-1α plays a key role in mediating pro-survival effects in various lymphoid malignancies [26]. In contrast to previous studies [18, 27]the extreme IL-18 levels were not significantly increased in MAS. Importantly, IL-18 elevation is especially characteristic of MAS concurrent with Still’s disease, such as SJIA and AOSD [28]however, the patients enrolled in this study embodied SLE, AOSD, KS, and other rheumatic diseases. Further the cor-relation analysis between clinical data and serum biomarkers indicated that many biomarkers had significant association with clinical data. But the specific relationship connecting biomarkers and clinical data was not specific in MAS, which was in contrast with previous conclusions that IL-18 correlated with the severity of laboratory abnormalities of MAS. We considered that IL-18 concentrations measured in the present study do not differentiate between free (i.e., circulating bio-active IL-18) and total IL-18 (i.e., free IL-18 and IL-18 complexed with IL-18BP). γOther than, a proportion of patients had received immunosuppressive treatment at enrollment may induce bias in biomarker concentrations Anyway, the biomarkers analyzed in our study could not separate MAS from EBV-HLH and L-HLH, and the expression of cytokines clustered by sHLH did not exhibit distinctive profiles.

The cor-relation between each biomarker was depicted in the Heatmap in three cohorts. We found the myeloid and lymphoid-derived cytokines had positive relation in MAS in three cohorts. Therefore we inferred that monocyte/macrophages were not only part of the vicious cycle induced by exaggerated activation of CD8 + T cells and NK cells but also may exert a leading role in pathology in MAS, as discovered in murine modules. MAS can be induced by repeated administration of the Toll-like receptor ligand CpG [29, 30] concurrent with blockade of the IL-10 (an immunosuppressive protein), indicating that hyper-stimulation of innate immune cells can be sufficient to generate a CRS. In recent years, multiple researchers have put more and more attention on IL-18, with considerable sensitivity and specificity in diagnosis and evaluation in MAS. Given that macrophages seem to be the main source of IL-18, reflecting the extent of macrophage activation in MAS [31] from the other side. Last but not least, the promising effectiveness of neutralization of cytokines produced by macrophages such as IL-1 receptor antibody (IL-1RA, Anakinra) and IL-6 receptor antibody (IL-6RA, Tocilizumab) provides evidence for the involvement of monocyte activation in MAS [27]. The exact pathway linking excessive cytokines and the expansion of monocyte/macrophages is not clear. However, the hemophagocytic activity of macrophages, the pathognomonic feature of MAS, appears to be induced by chronic stimulation of macrophages with cytokines.

The identification of cytokines that play the pivotal role in HLH pathogen is an area of active research, since selectively targeting these cytokines may be very effective therapeutic strategies. To our expectation, IL-17 was associated with numerous plasma biomarkers which were considered as pro-inflammatory responses, consistence with Paola Cipriani’s report [23]. The possible implication of IL-17 may be suggested by the elevated activation of Th-17 cells [32]. Deregulated tissue-reactive T helper (Th) cells are thought to be the instigators in chronic inflammatory diseases. Of note, previous studies reported that Th17 cells were in a positive feedback loop in the pro-inflammatory axis in T and monocyte/macrophage [33]. Myeloid-derived IL-1β promoted the expansion of a GM-CSF + Th-17 cells cell subset, thereby enhancing its encephalitogenic potential. More importantly, the activation of Th17 cells was pointed out in the development of HLH in an experimental model, proposing their possible pathogenic role in this context [34]. In conclusion, IL-17 closely correlated with a wider group of inflammatory mediators in MAS, which could be applied as the cytokine-directed therapy and need to be validated in the future.

Nonetheless, the findings in our study need to be discussed in view of three limitations. First, although patients who enrolled in this study were all in the active phase, a high proportion had received immuno-suppressive treatment, which may confound the concentrations of some cytokines. Secondly, we divided all patients into three cohorts, we cannot completely eliminate time and sample bias. At the same time, the number of patients in every cohort was limited. We need to enroll more patients to validate conclusions in our study. Thirdly, the biomarker concentrations in healthy control pre-2022 were not recorded; they should include HC biomarkers as comparisons. All in all, our study emphasized the prominent relation between myeloid and lymphoid-originated biomarkers in MAS. We discovered that monocyte/macrophages not only as a part in positive feedback cycles in MAS, but also exert an inevitable function in pathology. Multiple experiments and clinical researchers should be implemented to validate to steer of cytokine or even cell-directed treatment.

Conclusions

(1) The relationship between myeloid and lymphoid-derived biomarkers was further positive in MAS than EBV-HLH and L-HLH. (2) The IL-17 released mostly by Th-17 cells was grouped together closely with other inflammatory mediators and may exert an important role in MAS.

High fiber diet versus polyethylene glycol in management of chronic constipation in children with cerebral palsy: A randomized controlled trial
Authors: Vandana Arya, Sailesh Khyalia, Kapil Bhalla
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Constipation is one of the most common gastrointestinal symptoms in patients with cerebral palsy. This study was conducted with the aim to compare the efficacy of a high fiber diet and polyethylene glycol in the management of chronic constipation in children with cerebral palsy.Methodology:An open-label randomized controlled trial was conducted in the Department of Pediatrics of a tertiary care hospital in Northern India among cerebral palsy children aged 1–6 years having chronic constipation. Seventy children were enrolled in the study (35 in each group). Group A children received high fiber diet as per the diet chart prepared by the dietician. Group B received polyethylene glycol (0.5–1 g/kg/day) for a period of three months. At the end of four weeks and three months, all children were assessed for clinical improvement and both groups were compared in terms of stool frequency, consistency of stool, fecal incontinence, palpation of hard stool per abdomen, blood in stool, pain on defecation and overall satisfactory outcome. Satisfactory outcome was defined as defecation > two times weekly, soft stool, no pain on defecation, no palpation of hard stool on abdominal examination, no fecal incontinence, and no blood in stools. Children were also assessed for any adverse effects.Results:Baseline parameters and GMFCS were comparable in the two groups. Both group patients showed significant improvement in symptoms of constipation at the end of four weeks and three months compared to baseline. However, there was no significant difference between the two groups in terms of satisfactory outcome and parameters like frequency of stool, consistency of stool, palpation of hard stool, fecal incontinence, and blood in stools.Conclusion:A high fiber diet can be considered a good alternative to polyethylene glycol for the management of chronic constipation in children with cerebral palsy.

Introduction

Constipation is one of the most common gastrointestinal symptoms in patients with cerebral palsy (CP) with a prevalence of 25–75% and severity varying according to the degree of neurological dysfunction and GMFCS (Gross Motor Function Classification System) score.[1234] Pathophysiology of constipation in this patient population is multifactorial, the factors implicated include inherent abnormalities in neuromodulation, decreased intestinal motility, delayed transit time, spasticity of the pelvic floor, poor diet, swallowing difficulties, poor physical activity and use of medications affecting gut motility.[23] If left untreated, chronic constipation in CP children can lead to significant complications such as intolerance of feeds, poor appetite, fecal impaction, megarectum or megacolon, bladder dysfunction, volvulus, and perforation.[5] Studies have shown that it has also been associated with increased frequency of seizure episodes and overall poor quality of life in these children.[6] So, appropriate measures for prevention and timely treatment of chronic constipation in children with cerebral palsy are of utmost importance.

The various treatment modalities for chronic constipation include parental education, high fiber diet, increasing intake of fluids, and the use of laxatives and stool softeners.[78] Increasing fiber intake has been shown to ease defecation and increase both wet and dry stool weights and modulate gut transit time. Previous data suggested that a severely constipated nonambulatory population required fiber levels higher than normal to achieve a significant reduction in transit time in the gut. Side effects of high fiber include flatulence, distension, and bloating. Most of these side effects decrease with time, probably owing to alterations in colonic microflora, and can be minimized by increasing fiber intake gradually.[910]

Polyethylene glycol is a high molecular weight, nonpoisonous polymer that does not get absorbed after ingestion. It acts as an osmotic laxative, increases stool water content, and hence relieves the symptoms of constipation. Clinical studies have demonstrated that polyethylene glycol is effective in the treatment of constipation in adults and children.[1112] Its efficacy and safety in the management of chronic constipation in adults with cerebral palsy have also been studied.[1314] However, there is a paucity of studies investigating constipation and continence in neurologically impaired children and no specific treatment strategy is currently available. Most of the previous studies have evaluated the role of one or more osmotic laxatives. Osmotic laxatives can cause dependence and are not devoid of side effects. Considering high fiber diet as a low-cost, a nonpharmacological measure which can be used for the long term in the prevention and treatment of chronic constipation in children with cerebral palsy, the present study was done to compare its efficacy using a structured diet chart with polyethylene glycol in this patient population.

Methods

This open-label, randomized control trial was conducted in the Department of Pediatrics of a tertiary care hospital in Northern India. Patient enrollment was commenced after taking approval from the institutional ethics committee (IEC No. BREC/Th/20/Paed/001). The trial was registered under CTRI (Clinical Trials Registry of India) No. CTRI/2021/12/038649. Study participants were recruited from pediatric indoor wards and OPD. Children with cerebral palsy aged 1–6 years, having chronic constipation, were enrolled. The criteria for diagnosis of chronic constipation were the presence of at least two of the following criteria for a minimum of two months duration: hard/painful defecation, defecation frequency ≤ two times per week, large stool diameter, severe retention of stool, fecal incontinence (≥ once per week), hard fecal mass on rectal examination. Children receiving nutrition via alternate route, children receiving any drugs affecting the gut motility, and those with identified congenital malformations of the gastrointestinal tract were excluded from the study.

Attendants of the children were explained about the study and written informed consent was taken from them. After taking a thorough history, each participant underwent a detailed clinical examination and an X-ray abdomen was done to rule out fecal impaction. In individuals with fecal impaction, an enema was given for disimpaction first followed by the enrolment. The children were randomized into two groups using computer-generated variable size, block randomization (2, 4, or 6) sequence, and allocation concealment was done using a sealed envelope technique. In group A, children received a high fiber diet as per the diet chart prepared by a clinical dietician taking into account the minimum daily fiber requirement as recommended by ICMR. The diet was flexible and parents were educated to follow the general instructions provided to them. The fiber content in the diet was calculated as approx. 26.4 gm in children aged 1–3 years and 38.4 gm in children aged 4–6 years. In group B, children received polyethylene glycol (0.5–1 g/kg/day) in two divided doses for a period of three months. At the end of four weeks and three months, both groups were compared in terms of improvement in constipation clinically and achievement of satisfactory outcome using a prestructured questionnaire. Satisfactory outcome was defined as defecation > two times weekly, passage of soft stool, no pain on defecation, no palpation of hard stool on abdominal examination, no fecal incontinence, and no blood in stools. A two weekly follow-up was done telephonically for feedback from attendants of the child and any adverse effects of the two treatment modalities.

All data collected were entered in Microsoft Excel (MS Excel). Data were analyzed using the SPSS 21.0 version. The normality of data was checked by the Shapiro–Wilk test. The presentation of categorical variables was done in the form of number and percentage, the quantitative data were presented as means ± SD or median with interquartile range (25th–75th percentiles). Quantitative variables were compared using an independent t test and qualitative variables using the Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. A P value of <0.05 was considered significant.

Results

A total of 85 children with cerebral palsy aged 1–6 years having chronic constipation were assessed for eligibility. Out of 85, 15 were excluded (two children were receiving nutrition via an alternate route, 11 were receiving drugs affecting gut motility, and consent was not given by the parents of two children). After exclusion, 70 children were randomized to receive either high fiber diet or polyethylene glycol for three months (35 in each group) [Figure 1]. The baseline demographic and clinical data including age, gender, socioeconomic status, birth history of perinatal insult, GMFCS score, and growth abnormality were comparable in both groups [Table 1]. The baseline frequency of passing stools/week was comparable in the two groups (P = 0.873). Both groups children showed a significant increase in the frequency of bowel movements at four-week and three-month follow-up, but there were no significant between-group differences at four weeks (P = 0.636) and at three months (P = 0.593) [Table 2 and Figure 2]. The other clinical parameters like hard consistency of stool, presence of pain on defecation, palpable hard stools per abdomen, the passage of blood in stool, and fecal incontinence, when assessed at four-week and three-month follow-up, showed comparable improvement in the two study groups [Table 2]. Three children in group A and four children in group B reported minor adverse effects like nausea, abdominal pain, and flatulence at four-week follow-up. The number of children with adverse effects at three months increased to five and eight in the two groups, respectively. None of the children reported any severe adverse effect of the two treatment modalities. The proportion of children reporting satisfactory outcome at four weeks and three months were comparable between groups A and B. [at four weeks: 28.57% and 45.71%, respectively (P value = 0.056), at three months: 65.71% and 71.43%, respectively (P value = 0.174)].

Discussion

The present study was a randomized control trial involving children with cerebral palsy aged 1–6 years suffering from chronic constipation where the efficacy and safety of a diet containing high fiber (group A) with an approx. fiber content of 26.5 grams/day in children aged 1–3 years and 38.4 grams/day in 3–6 years old and polyethylene glycol (group B) in a dose of 0.5–1 g/kg/day were compared. Clinical evaluation at four weeks and three months showed that there was no significant difference between the two groups in terms of the proportion of patients relieved of symptoms like infrequent passage of stools, hard consistency of stool, fecal incontinence, palpation of hard stool, pain on defecation, and blood in stools. At four-week follow-up, 28.57% of group A children receiving a high fiber diet, and 45.71% of group B children receiving PEG, had shown satisfactory outcomes. Although a higher proportion of children in group B achieved satisfactory outcomes, the difference was not statistically significant (P value = 0.056). Additionally, at three months, 65.71% of group A and 71.43% of group B had shown satisfactory outcomes (P value = 0.174). There was no significant difference noted between the two groups in terms of adverse effects on follow-up. Only minor adverse effects, such as nausea, abdominal pain, and flatulence, were reported in individuals of both groups.

The index study defined the criteria for diagnosis of chronic constipation in cerebral palsy children as the presence of at least two of the following criteria for at least two months: hard/painful defecation, defecation frequency ≤ two times per week, large stool diameter, severe retention of stool, fecal incontinence (≥ once per week), and hard fecal mass on rectal examination. Similar criteria were used in a previous study conducted in Iran.[13] The definition proposed by Veugelers et al.[3] was scybalous, pebble-like, hard stools in 25% of defecations and defecation frequency less than three times a week, or large stools palpable on abdominal examination, or laxative use or manual dis-impaction of feces and was based on three different consensus papers dealing with the problem.

Studies investigating constipation and continence in neurologically impaired children are scarce, making it difficult to choose the optimal treatment. As in other patients with constipation, laxatives are frequently used but since cerebral palsy children suffer more frequently from slow transit constipation, the classically used osmotic laxatives, such as lactulose and PEG, might be less efficient compared to the normal constipated children.[4] Therefore, it is worthwhile to compare laxatives with other nonpharmacological measures in the setting of constipation in children with cerebral palsy. Studies have been conducted to study the role of various nonpharmacological measures like abdominal massage, physical therapy, and reflexology in treating constipation in children with cerebral palsy and are found to be effective in the relief of symptoms.[151617]

Children with cerebral palsy require long-term measure for prevention and treatment of chronic constipation. Therefore, the treatment modality used should be safe, effective, and without significant side effects. Moreover, it should be cost-friendly and should not confer additional financial burden on the caregivers of these children, especially in resource-limited countries like India. High fiber diet is a low-cost, nonpharmacological measure and caregivers of the child can be educated to make the required dietary changes. The index study showed that a high fiber diet when advised using a structured diet chart is comparable to polyethylene glycol in terms of efficacy and adverse effects and is quite a safe option for the management of chronic constipation in patients with cerebral palsy. Similar findings were reported by Tse et al.,[18] where an increase in the intake of dietary fiber from baseline 2 g/day to 17 g/day (stage 1) and 21 g/day (stage 2) resulted in relief of constipation and a significant reduction in the usage of laxatives in children with severe developmental disabilities. Improvement in stool frequency and stool consistency has also been reported in response to dietary fiber glucomannan when compared to placebo in this patient population.[19]

The limitations of our study include a small sample size. Larger multicentric studies with larger sample sizes are needed to further strengthen the recommendation for a high fiber diet in the management of chronic constipation in patients with cerebral palsy. In the present study, some parameters like time to the relief of symptoms and readmission rates were not evaluated. These parameters are also important to compare both groups. The duration between the follow-ups was quite long; therefore, in the meanwhile regular compliance could not be ensured. As the majority of the patient subset belonged to lower socioeconomic strata, therefore, compliance with high fiber diet and care posed a challenge.

Although many studies have already been done to evaluate the management of functional constipation in children, very few of them have enrolled patients with cerebral palsy. Also, there is no previous study that has compared the effectiveness of polyethylene glycol and high fiber diet in the management of chronic constipation in this patient population and it is the first of its kind of study conducted in the Indian population. A fairly reasonable number of cases were studied. So, it gave a fair idea of the effectiveness of a high fiber diet and polyethylene glycol and helped compare between the two. With a drastic increase in the prevalence of cerebral palsy patients in the community and chronic constipation being one of the most common symptoms for seeking primary care, this study bears high relevance for primary care providers in the community also.

Conclusion

Our study concluded that a high fiber diet, when advised as per a structured diet chart, taking into account the daily fiber requirement, has comparable efficacy and safety to polyethylene glycol. And thus, it is of potential benefit in long-term management of chronic constipation in children with cerebral palsy. However, further randomized control trials are required involving a large number of clinical subjects to strengthen the evidence for the use of a high fiber diet as an alternative agent.

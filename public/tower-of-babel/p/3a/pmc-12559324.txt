Variable gradient-based phase locked loop for accurate frequency estimation of distorted grids
Authors: Xiaoben Lei, Xinhua Hu, Jianwei Yang, Guangqi Li, Zhiyong Dai, Guoqing Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
A variable gradient-based phase locked loop (VG-PLL) is developed for distorted grids. Firstly, the large-signal model of the synchronous reference frame phase locked loop (SRF-PLL) with harmonic disturbances is established, and the large-signal model shows that the SRF-PLL has poor filtering effects in estimating the grid frequency. To address this issue, a VG-PLL is then proposed. Based on the variable gradient method, a Lyapunov function is developed to demonstrate that the proposed VG-PLL is input-state stability, even when the grids are distorted. In addition, the performance comparisons between the VG-PLL and SRF-PLL are provided. Compared with the SRF-PLL, the proposed VG-PLL has better filtering capability. Experimental tests also verify the superior performance of the proposed VG-PLL for accurate frequency estimation under grid distortions.

Introduction

In power systems, the information of the frequency and the phase angle is important to achieve high-performance control of grid-connected power converters1,2. This is known as grid synchronization. In past decades, many methods have been proposed to obtain the frequency and the phase angle3–5. In these methods, the synchronous reference frame phase-locked loop (SRF-PLL)6,7 is the most famous one and which has been widely used in practice8–10.

The SRF-PLL structure is shown in Fig. 111. It has three parts, and includes a phase detector (PD), a loop filter (LF) and a voltage-controlled oscillator (VCO). The PD block is a unit combining the Clarke and Park transformations and its output is proportional to the error between the estimated and actual phase angle. The LF is typically a proportional-integral (PI) controller and it suppresses the high-order components of the phase estimation error. The VCO is an integrator and its output is the estimated phase angle12. Due to the good accuracy and simple structure, the SRF-PLL is widely applied in many applications as mentioned previously.Figure 1Structure of the SRF-PLL.

Structure of the SRF-PLL.

The increasing integration of renewable energy sources, such as wind and solar power, into the grid has introduced new challenges due to their inherent variability and intermittency13. These fluctuations in grid voltage, particularly when harmonics or noise are present, significantly reduce the steady-state accuracy of the SRF-PLL14. Due to the structure of the SRF-PLL, grid voltage disturbances lead to substantial frequency estimation errors, which negatively affect system stability and performance.

Building on the SRF-PLL, certain effective methods have been proposed in the literature to enhance the robustness of the SRF-PLL15-16-17. A well-known one is the second-order generalized integrator-based PLL (SOGI-PLL)18-19. By placing the SOGI block as a prefilter before the SRF-PLL, the SOGI-PLL maintains a good robustness against harmonics. Similarly,20 and21 add delayed signal cancelation blocks prior the SRF-PLL, forming the delayed signal cancellation PLL (DSC-PLL). These cancellation blocks significantly enhance the harmonic suppression capabilities of the DSC-PLL. In22, by adding complex-coefficient-filters before the SRF-PLL, a novel PLL called complex-coefficient-filter-based PLL (CCF-PLL) is achieved. Taking the advantage of the complex-coefficient-filter block, the CCF-PLL has a good harmonic suppression performance. Other similar PLLs include MAF-PLL23, DDSRF-PLL7, RR-based PLL24, ZCD-based PLL25 and SOLC-based PLL26. However, all these methods enhance filtering performance at the cost of increased computational complexity.

In light of the above, a variable gradient-based phase locked loop (VG-PLL) is proposed for distorted power grids in this paper. Firstly, the large-signal model of the SRF-PLL is established, and it is found that the SRF-PLL has poor filter effect in estimating the grid frequency for power grids with harmonic disturbances. Then, the VG-PLL is designed, and its large-signal model is established for power grids with harmonic disturbances. Based on the variable gradient method, a Lyapunov function is provided to show that the VG-PLL method has good steady-state accuracy and fast dynamic performance even under harmonic disturbances. Compared with the SRF-PLL, the proposed VG-PLL has better filtering effects in estimating the grid frequency. Furthermore, compared with the prefilter-based PLLs, the proposed VG-PLL method is simpler in structure and requires less computation.

The rest parts of the article are arranged as follows. The performance of traditional SRF-PLL is analyzed in Sect. 2 for power grids with harmonic distortions. Section 3 proposes the VG-PLL method with the design process and large signal model. Then the performance analysis of the proposed VG-PLL is also given. Section 4 provides the experimental results. At the end, conclusions are made in Sect. 5.

Proposed variable gradient-based phase-locked loop

The above analysis implies that for power grids with harmonic disturbances, the SRF-PLL can not achieve a satisfactory result. This is due to the LF and VCO parts of the SRF-PLL introduce the derivatives of harmonic disturbances. To address this disadvantage, a novel VG-PLL is proposed in this section.

Comparisons of the VG-PLL, the SRF-PLL and the ANS-SRF-PLL.

Conclusion

In this paper, the VG-PLL has been developed for distorted grids. First, the pros and cons of the SRF-PLL have been discussed based on its large-signal model. It can be found that the SRF-PLL has a poor filtering effect in estimating the grid frequency of power grids with harmonic disturbances. To address this disadvantage, a VG-PLL has been proposed, and its large-signal model has been established. Based on the variable gradient method, a Lyapunov function has been provided to show that the proposed VG-PLL is with a good performance in steady-state accuracy for power grids with harmonic disturbances. Compared with the SRF-PLL, the proposed VG-PLL has a better filtering effect in estimating the grid frequency. Furthermore, compared with the prefilter-PLLs, the proposed VG-PLL has a simpler structure and smaller computational burden. The effectiveness of the proposed VG-PLL has been verified by extensive experimental tests.

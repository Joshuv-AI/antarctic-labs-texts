REAL208, a Liposome-based Formulation of Phosphodiesterase Inhibitor, Protects Acute Kidney Deficiency in Mice
Authors: Jui-Chi Tsai, Yi-Wen Lin, Chun-Yao Huang, Chun-Ming Shih, Yu-Tse Wu, Toshio Kurosaki, Yuki Inoue, Ying-Chi Du, Feng-Yen Lin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveAcute kidney injury (AKI) is a life-threatening condition characterized by inflammation, oxidative stress, and tubular cell apoptosis for which no specific pharmacological therapy currently exists. Dipyridamole, a phosphodiesterase inhibitor with antiplatelet, anti-inflammatory, and cytoprotective properties, has shown renoprotective potential; however, its clinical use in AKI is limited by pharmacokinetic constraints.Methods and ResultsTo address this issue, we developed REAL208, a liposome-encapsulated dipyridamole formulation and evaluated its safety and therapeutic efficacy in experimental AKI models. Safety testing in C57BL/6 mice demonstrated that REAL208 was well tolerated at doses up to 50 mg/kg, without evidence of hepatic or renal toxicity. In lipopolysaccharide (LPS)- and ischemia/reperfusion (I/R)-induced AKI models, intravenous administration of REAL208 (2.5–10 mg/kg) significantly improved survival, reduced serum blood urea nitrogen and creatinine levels, and restored glomerular filtration rate compared to that observed in the untreated controls. Histological analyses revealed preserved tubular architecture, reduced neutrophil and macrophage infiltration, and modulation of the expression of injury markers, including KIM-1 and PPARγ. Immunohistochemistry further showed attenuation of Nrf2 and HO-1 upregulation, indicating suppression of oxidative stress. In vitro, REAL208 protected HK-2 proximal tubular cells against LPS- or hypoxia/reperfusion-induced injury, maintained mitochondrial membrane potential, enhanced oxygen consumption rates, and reduced inflammatory signaling (nuclear p65 and Bcl-2).ConclusionsThese findings demonstrated that liposomal dipyridamole exerts robust renoprotective effects by preserving mitochondrial function, suppressing oxidative stress, and attenuating inflammation. REAL208 represents a promising therapeutic strategy for AKI and warrants further translational and clinical investigations.

Introduction

Dipyridamole, a phosphodiesterase inhibitor, is an antiplatelet agent primarily used to prevent blood clots in patients undergoing heart valve replacement and reduce the risk of stroke when combined with aspirin. It inhibits phosphodiesterase, increases cAMP levels, and prevents platelet aggregation. Beyond its cardiovascular role, dipyridamole exhibits anti-inflammatory and anti-aging properties by enhancing adenosine signaling, promoting interleukin (IL)−10 production, and reducing pro-inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α and IL-6 [1]. Additionally, it activates the adenosine A2B receptor and the AMPK/cAMP pathway, which may contribute to muscle maintenance and modulation of aging [2]. Emerging research has highlighted their potential in oncology, where they inhibit cancer cell proliferation by targeting heat shock protein 90 [3]. Although dipyridamole has been used for treating schizophrenia, it has shown limited antipsychotic efficacy [4]. Moreover, it enhances statin-induced apoptosis in hematological malignancies by disrupting the mevalonate pathway [5]. These findings suggest that the pharmacological benefits of dipyridamole extend beyond anticoagulation therapy to inflammation, aging, cancer, and neurological disorders.

Acute kidney injury (AKI) is characterized by a sudden decline in renal function due to various etiologies including ischemia, nephrotoxicity, and sepsis. Pathologically, AKI involves complex mechanisms such as inflammation, oxidative stress, and tubular cell apoptosis. Inflammation plays a pivotal role in both the intrarenal and systemic inflammatory responses that contribute to renal damage. Understanding these molecular pathways is crucial for developing effective therapies [6]. Current treatment strategies focus on addressing underlying causes, providing supportive care, and preventing further injuries. These include optimizing hemodynamics, avoiding nephrotoxic agents, and initiating renal replacement therapy in severe cases. Despite these interventions, AKI can lead to significant complications such as fluid overload, electrolyte imbalance, and an increased risk of progression to chronic kidney disease (CKD) due to maladaptive repair processes and renal fibrosis [7]. Early recognition and management are essential for mitigating adverse outcomes and improving patient prognosis.

Recent studies have explored the potential benefits of dipyridamole in the treatment of renal impairment. A retrospective analysis of patients with CKD stages 3–5 showed that dipyridamole treatment was significantly associated with improved renal outcomes and patient survival. In particular, dipyridamole use was correlated with a decreased risk of rapid estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) decline and progression to end-stage renal disease [8]. Additionally, dipyridamole demonstrated renoprotective effects in a rat model of ischemia–reperfusion (I/R)-induced AKI. This protection is mediated by adenosinergic A1 and A2A receptor agonism, leading to reduced oxidative stress and improved renal function [9]. These findings suggest that dipyridamole may offer therapeutic benefits for the management of renal injury and dysfunction. Although dipyridamole has demonstrated promising renoprotective effects in preclinical models and retrospective clinical analyses, its translation into the treatment of acute kidney injury (AKI) remains limited by pharmacokinetic and safety considerations, rather than by insufficient intrinsic biological activity. Specifically, dipyridamole is characterized by poor aqueous solubility and rapid systemic distribution, which may reduce its effective exposure at sites of renal injury. In addition, its vasodilatory properties can result in systemic hypotension—an undesirable effect in patients with AKI, for whom the maintenance of adequate renal perfusion pressure is essential. Collectively, these pharmacological features complicate dose optimization, increase the risk of off-target effects, and narrow the therapeutic window when dipyridamole is administered in its conventional formulation.

Liposomal drug delivery systems represent a well-established approach to addressing these challenges by improving drug solubility, extending circulation time, modulating biodistribution, and reducing systemic adverse effects. Accordingly, encapsulation of dipyridamole within liposomes may enhance its pharmacological efficiency while attenuating hemodynamic side effects, thereby facilitating the therapeutic exploitation of its anti-inflammatory and cytoprotective properties in the context of AKI. To ensure that dipyridamole can be used to treat renal injury, we aimed to develop a new dosage form based on liposome encapsulation and analyze its therapeutic effects on AKI.

Materials and Methods

Preparation of REAL208

REAL208 used in this study was purchased from Nippon Fine Chemical Co., Ltd., Japan. Dipyridamole (Product code: D2274) was purchased from Tokyo Chemical Industry Co., Ltd. (Tokyo, Japan) and encapsulated using liposome encapsulation technology to form particles of approximately 100–200 nm via extrusion. The prepared drug was tested for the presence of endotoxin and bacteria to ensure quality compliance before being stored at 4 °C for future use.

Drug Loading and Physicochemical Characterization of REAL208

The drug loading and physicochemical properties of the liposomal dipyridamole formulation REAL208 were characterized in an independent stability and quality assessment study conducted at Kaohsiung Medical University. Particle size and size distribution were measured by dynamic light scattering using a laser particle size analyzer after appropriate dilution with ultrapure water. The pH value of the formulation was determined using a calibrated pH meter at each testing time point. The dipyridamole content in REAL208 was quantified by high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC). Briefly, liposomal samples were disrupted with acetonitrile, followed by centrifugation to remove lipid debris. The supernatant was analyzed using a C18 reversed-phase column with UV detection at 285 nm. Drug concentration was calculated based on a standard calibration curve of dipyridamole.

Encapsulation efficiency was determined using two complementary methods: ultrafiltration centrifugation and equilibrium dialysis. For ultrafiltration, diluted liposomal samples were centrifuged through a 10 kDa molecular weight cut-off filter to separate free drug from encapsulated drug. For equilibrium dialysis, liposomal samples were dialyzed against purified water at 25 °C, and the retained drug content was quantified by HPLC. Encapsulation efficiency was calculated as the percentage of encapsulated drug relative to the total drug content. At baseline (month 0), REAL208 exhibited a mean particle size of approximately 108 nm with a narrow size distribution (polydispersity index < 0.1), a near-neutral pH (~ 6.9), and a dipyridamole loading content of approximately 4.9 mg/mL. Encapsulation efficiency exceeded 99% as determined by ultrafiltration and remained above 80% by equilibrium dialysis. These parameters remained stable during storage at 2–8 °C for up to six months, indicating good physicochemical stability of the formulation.

The in vivo doses (mg/kg BW) and in vitro treatment concentrations (µg/mL) used in this study were calculated based on the quantified dipyridamole content of REAL208, thereby ensuring accurate and reproducible delivery of encapsulated dipyridamole across all experiments.

Animal Study

Ethics Approval

Animal care conformed to the “Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals” published by the U.S. A. National Institutes of Health (NIH Publication No. 85–23, revised in 1996). This animal study was approved by the Institutional Animal Care Committee of the National Defense Medical Center (Certificate No.: IACUC 24–196/JUL 23, 2024) and Taipei Medical University (Certificate No.: LAC-2017–0118/MAY 10, 2017; LAC-2018–021/AUG 24, 2018; LAC-2022–0151/JUN 22, 2022; LAC-2024–0106/MAR 28, 2024), Taiwan. The mice were housed under standard conditions with a 12-h light–dark cycle and free access to food and water for at least one week before the experiment. The mice were monitored for body weight (BW) loss, lethargy, and survival. All mice received euthanasia via CO₂ inhalation followed by cervical dislocation.

Safety Testing of REAL208

According to prescription information for dipyridamole, the recommended single-use dose for adults is 0.57–0.84 mg/kg BW. Based on the body surface area normalization method [10], the corresponding dose for C57BL/6 mouse was 5–10 mg/kg BW. To further evaluate the safety of REAL208, 35 C57BL/6 mice were randomly divided into seven groups. Mice in the control group received a single dose of REAL208 (2.5, 5, 10, or 50 mg/kg BW) via tail vein injection, and the survival rate of the mice was continuously monitored. However, the unencapsulated dipyridamole (DPD; 10 mg/kg BW) or empty lipid vesicle (EV; 10 mL/kg BW) was used as controls. EV were administered at injection volumes identical to those of REAL208 to ensure equivalent lipid exposure across groups. Blood samples were collected from the tail vein before administration and seven days after injection for biochemical analyses, including assessment of alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), blood urea nitrogen (BUN), and creatinine levels. The heart, liver, spleen, lungs, and kidneys were harvested and weighed after the animals were euthanized.

Lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-induced Mouse Model of AKI and Animal Grouping

LPS-induced AKI is a well-established experimental model for studying sepsis-associated kidney injuries. This model mimics the systemic inflammatory response triggered by bacterial endotoxins, which leads to renal dysfunction and damage. Male C57BL/6 mice (8 weeks old, weighing approximately 20 − 25 g) were assigned randomly to several groups. LPS from Escherichia coli (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO USA) was dissolved in sterile phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) and administered via intraperitoneal (IP) injection at a dose of 15 mg/kg in a total volume of 100 μL. Additionally, experimental mice received a single dose of REAL208 (2.5, 5, or 10 mg/kg BW) or EV (2 mL/kg BW) via tail vein injection. Additionally, the remaining 70 C57BL/6 mice were randomly divided into seven groups as follows: group 1 (naïve control): control groups received an equivalent volume of PBS; group 2 (15 mg/kg BW LPS): mice received 15 mg/kg BW LPS dissolved in PBS; group 3 (LPS plus 2.5 mg/kg BW REAL208): mice received LPS followed by 2.5 mg/kg BW REAL208 injection after 1 h; group 4 (LPS plus 5 mg/kg BW REAL208): mice received LPS followed by 5 mg/kg BW REAL208 injection after 1 h; group 5 (LPS plus 10 mg/kg BW REAL208): mice received LPS followed by 10 mg/kg BW REAL208 injection after 1 h; group 6 (LPS plus 10 mg/kg BW DPD): mice received LPS followed by 10 mg/kg BW DPD injection after 1 h; and group 7 (LPS plus 2 mL/kg BW EV): mice received LPS followed by 5 mL/kg BW EV injection after 1 h. The survival rate of mice was monitored continuously for seven days. Mice were euthanized 48 h post-injection of REAL208, and the kidneys were harvested for histopathological examination using hematoxylin/eosin (HE) staining and immunohistochemistry assays.

Ischemia/reperfusion (I/R)-induced Mouse Model of AKI and Animal Grouping

The I/R-induced AKI model has been widely used to study the mechanisms underlying renal injury and therapeutic interventions. This model mimics the clinical AKI caused by conditions such as renal artery occlusion, transplantation, and cardiac surgery. C57BL/6 mice were anesthetized with isoflurane (2–3% for induction, 1–2% for maintenance), and body temperature was maintained at 36–37 °C using a heating pad. A posterior left abdominal incision (approximately 1.5 cm) was made under aseptic conditions to expose the kidneys, a silk thread was used to tie the blood vessels and ureters close to the left renal pelvis, and the right renal pedicles were clamped using microvascular clamps for 30 min. Successful ischemia was confirmed by the dark red or purple discoloration of the kidneys. After the ischemic period, the clamps were removed to allow reperfusion, during which time the kidneys gradually regained their pink coloration. Finally, the incision was closed using absorbable sutures and the mice received subcutaneous sterile saline to prevent dehydration. Postoperative care included monitoring until full recovery and administration of pain control drugs if necessary. In the present study, 30 C57BL/6 mice were randomly divided into six groups as follows: group 1 (naïve control): control groups received an equivalent volume of PBS; group 2 (I/R): mice received I/R injury and PBS; group 3 (I/R plus 5 mg/kg BW REAL208): mice received I/R injury followed by 5 mg/kg BW REAL208 injection after 30 min; group 4 (I/R plus 10 mg/kg BW REAL208): mice received I/R injury followed by 10 mg/kg BW REAL208 injection after 30 min; group 5 (I/R plus 10 mg/kg BW DPD): mice received I/R injury followed by 10 mg/kg BW unencapsulated dipyridamole injection after 30 min; and group 6 (I/R plus 2 mL/kg BW EV): mice received I/R injury followed by 2 mL/kg BW empty lipid vesicle injection after 30 min; Mice were euthanized 24 h post-injection, and the kidneys were harvested for histopathological examination using hematoxylin/eosin (HE) staining and immunohistochemistry assays.

Transdermal Measurement of Estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate (eGFR)

eGFR was measured using a MediBeacon GmbH transdermal fluorescence detection system (Mannheim, Germany). Mice were anesthetized with 2% isoflurane in oxygen and placed on a pre-warmed heating pad at 37 °C. The fluorescent tracer, MB-102 (fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC)-labeled sinistrin), a nontoxic small molecule cleared exclusively via glomerular filtration, was administered intravenously at a dose of 7.5 mg/kg BW. A transdermal fluorescence sensor was positioned on the shaved skin of the lateral abdomen to continuously monitor fluorescence decay over time. The fluorescence intensity was recorded at regular intervals, and the elimination kinetics of MB-102 were analyzed using MediBeacon analysis software to determine the real-time GFR (mL/min/100 g BW). The in vivo metabolic half-life of FITC-sinistrin (t1/2) was calculated using a fluorescence decay curve. Background autofluorescence was minimized by conducting the measurements in a darkened environment. Mice were monitored for post-imaging recovery.

Immunohistochemistry

Mice were euthanized at the end of 24 h and their kidneys were harvested for histopathological analysis using HE staining to assess tubular necrosis, brush border loss, and inflammatory cell infiltration. Markers of infiltrated neutrophils (MPO), macrophages (F4/80), oxidative stress (Nrf2 and HO-1), and kidney injury (PPARγ and KIM-1) were examined using immunohistochemistry.

In Vitro Study

Culturing of HK-2 Cells

HK-2 cells, a proximal tubular cell line derived from normal human adult male kidneys, were purchased from American Type Culture Collection (ATCC CRL-2190). The cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM)/F12 medium, and the medium was replaced every 3 days. The cells were maintained at 37 ℃ in a 5% CO2 incubator.

Cultivation of HK-2 Cells Under in vitro Hypoxia-reperfusion (H/R) Conditions

For the in vitro H/R injury model, HK-2 cells were seeded onto culture plates and grown to approximately 80% confluence under standard normoxic conditions for 24 h. To induce hypoxia, the culture medium was replaced with low-serum (2% fetal bovine serum (FBS)) DMEM and the cells were transferred into a hypoxia chamber flushed with a gas mixture containing 1% O₂, 5% CO₂, and 94% N₂. The cells were maintained under hypoxic conditions for 24 h to simulate ischemia. Reperfusion was initiated by replacing the hypoxic medium with a normal culture medium containing 10% FBS, followed by incubation under normoxic conditions (21% O₂, 5% CO₂) for an additional 6 h. REAL208 was administered prior to reperfusion. Following reperfusion, western blotting was performed to assess cellular responses.

MTT Assay

The 3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)−2,5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide (MTT) assay was used to assess the cytotoxicity of REAL208. For the assay, cells were seeded in a 96-well plate at a density of 5 × 10 cells per well. Following cell adhesion, REAL208 was added to the wells and the plates were incubated for 24 h. After treatment, 10 µL of the MTT solution (5 mg/mL in medium) was added to each well. The plate was then incubated at 37 °C for 2 h, following which the supernatant was gently removed and 150 µL of dimethyl sulfoxide was added to dissolve the formazan product. Absorbance was measured at 570 nm using a microplate reader. The optical density was directly proportional to the number of viable cells and cell viability was calculated.

Western Blotting

Total cell lysates were electrophoresed after the protein concentrations were measured. Rabbit anti-MPO (22,225–1-AP; Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA), rabbit anti-induced nitric oxide synthase (iNOS)(#20,609; Cell Signaling, Danvers, MA, USA), rabbit anti-Nrf-2 (80,593–1-RR; 22,225–1-AP; Thermo Fisher Scientific), rabbit anti-BAX (#2772; Cell Signaling), rabbit anti-Bcl-2 (#2876; Cell Signaling), rabbit anti-F4/80 (#70,076; Cell Signaling), and mouse anti-HO-1 (66,743–1-RR; 22,225–1-AP; Thermo Fisher Scientific) antibodies were used, and immunodetection was performed using a chemiluminescence reagent and a ChemiDoc-ItTM imaging system (UVP, Upland, CA, USA).

Seahorse XFp for Analyzing Bioenergy

A Seahorse XFp platform (Agilent, San Diego, CA, USA) was used to evaluate cellular energy metabolism. The following steps were performed per the manufacturer’s instructions. HK-2 cells (104) were added to each well of a plate. The cells were then treated with LPS or REAL208, incubated at 37 °C for 24 h, or subjected to H/R. One hour before loading the cells onto the Seahorse XFp platform, the cell culture growth medium was replaced with 180 μL of serum-free medium containing 2 mM L-glutamine. Next, specific compounds (20 μL of 0.5 mM oligomycin (Oligo), 22 μL of 0.5 mM carbonyl cyanide-p-trifluoromethoxyphenylhydrazone (FCCP), and 25 μL of 0.25 mM rotenone/antimycin A (Rot + AA) were added to the grooves in the probe plate. The Seahorse XFp platform was used to measure mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation and oxygen consumption in the immediate cellular environment.

Assessment of Mitochondrial Potential

Mitochondrial transmembrane potential was determined using JC-1 and DAPI fluorescent dyes (ChemoMetec, Denmark) with NucleoCounter® NC-3000™, a fluorescence image cytometer. In healthy cells, the negative charge developed by the intact mitochondrial membrane layer promotes the accumulation of fluorescent red JC-1 in the mitochondrial matrix. Consequently, in dead cells, the mitochondrial potential collapses and JC-1 localizes to the cytosol in its monomeric eco-friendly fluorescent form. Cells with collapsed mitochondrial potential exhibited reduced red/green fluorescence intensity. HK-2 cells were seeded in 6-well plates. After 24 h of incubation with LPS and REAL208 or treatment with H/R, the cells were collected via centrifugation. The method is described in detail in [11]. The cell pellets obtained were assessed immediately using 8-chamber NC slides.

Statistical Analyses

Values are expressed as the means ± SEM. Statistical evaluations were performed using the Student’s t-test or one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), followed by Dunnett’s test. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05. significant.

Results

REAL208 Was Well Tolerated in Healthy C57BL/6 Mice

To assess the safety profile of REAL208, C57BL/6 mice were administered single intravenous doses of 2.5, 5, 10, or 50 mg REAL208/kg BW. Biochemical analyses (Table I) revealed that BW remained stable in all groups. Serum BUN levels showed a decreasing trend in all REAL208-treated groups, with the most notable reduction observed in the 50 mg/kg group (from 37.1 ± 1.7 to 26.9 ± 5.1 mg/dL), indicative of the absence of renal burden. Serum creatinine levels remained consistent or were slightly reduced across all groups, with no signs of nephrotoxicity. ALT and AST levels did not increase after treatment. Both enzyme levels remained within the physiological ranges or showed a slight downward trend, further supporting the hepatic safety of using REAL208. Notably, ALT levels in the 50 mg/kg group remained comparable to the baseline (52.2 ± 9.6 to 59.2 ± 19.7 IU/L), while AST levels declined (27.3 ± 1.8 to 22.3 ± 2.4 IU/L). However, the DPD and EV did not induce the biochemical parameters change (Table II). Collectively, these findings demonstrated that a single intravenous dose of REAL208, up to 10 mg/kg BW, does not induce apparent hepatic or renal toxicity, as supported by stable survival, body weights, and biochemical indices. REAL208 Improves Renal Function and Preserves Histological Integrity in LPS-induced AKI

To investigate the renoprotective effects of REAL208 in the sepsis-associated AKI model, C57BL/6 mice were administered LPS (15 mg/kg BW) to induce systemic inflammation and kidney injury. Blood biochemical parameters were assessed at baseline and 48 h post-treatment (Table III). In the LPS group, BUN and creatinine levels increased markedly to 116.4 ± 73.7 mg/dL and 4.99 ± 1.32 mg/dL, respectively, indicating severe renal impairment. The levels of the hepatic enzymes, ALT and AST, also increased significantly, indicating liver injury. Treatment with REAL208 exhibited dose-dependent protective effects. At a dose of 10 mg/kg, REAL208 significantly reduced BUN level to 37.8 ± 12.4 mg/dL and creatinine level to 0.28 ± 0.15 mg/dL, while restoring ALT and AST levels to near baseline values. This adaptive response is consistent with the well-established role of the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway as a major cellular defense mechanism against oxidative damage, in which Nrf2 activation enhances the transcription of antioxidant and cytoprotective genes. Therefore, the observed increases in Nrf2 and HO-1 expression possibly reflect a compensatory mechanism aimed at mitigating LPS-induced oxidative injury in renal tubular epithelial cells. Notably, treatment with REAL208 attenuated the excessive induction of Nrf2 and HO-1 (Fig. 3G), suggesting that the compound effectively reduced the oxidative burden and restored the redox balance. Rather than relying on an overactivated stress response pathway, REAL208 appeared to mitigate upstream injury, thereby diminishing the need for compensatory Nrf2/HO-1 activation. These findings highlight the capacity of REAL208 to protect against LPS-induced oxidative stress and modulate the cellular adaptive response, contributing to its overall renoprotective effect.

Discussion

Dipyridamole, a well-established phosphodiesterase inhibitor, possesses multiple pharmacological properties including antiplatelet, anti-inflammatory, and antioxidant properties. However, its clinical application in AKI and sepsis-associated multi-organ damage is limited by its unfavorable pharmacokinetics and potential side effects. The recent rise in drug repositioning strategies has provided an opportunity to transform well-established safe agents into novel therapeutics. This study proposes a liposome-encapsulated dipyridamole formulation (REAL208) that overcomes the limitations of conventional preparations and demonstrates significant therapeutic potential. First, this formulation provided a breakthrough in terms of dose efficiency. Conventionally, ≥ 25 μg/mL dipyridamole is required in vitro to exert cytoprotective effects, whereas REAL208 achieves comparable protection of renal tubular mitochondrial function and suppression of inflammatory responses at only 0.25–2 μg/mL, indicating markedly improved bioavailability after encapsulation. Animal studies based on clinically converted doses (5–10 mg/kg BW) have shown that REAL208 effectively improves renal function, increases survival, and reduces tissue injury in both LPS-induced and I/R AKI models. In the safety evaluation conducted in healthy C57BL/6 mice, a modest but statistically significant reduction in serum BUN was observed following REAL208 administration, particularly at the highest tested dose (50 mg/kg BW). Importantly, post-treatment BUN values remained within the established physiological range for this strain (approximately 20–30 mg/dL), and no accompanying changes in serum creatinine, body weight, or hepatic enzymes were detected. Therefore, the observed decrease is most appropriately interpreted as a return toward physiological homeostasis rather than supraphysiologic enhancement of renal nitrogen clearance. Nevertheless, we acknowledge that statistically significant quantitative changes, even within the normal range, warrant cautious interpretation. Although no progressive or dose-dependent decline beyond physiological limits was observed in the present study, further dose-escalation and long-term studies will be necessary to fully exclude the possibility that substantially higher exposures could lead to non-physiologic reductions in BUN. Based on the current data, however, REAL208 did not produce biochemical evidence of renal overcorrection or functional suppression. Notably, REAL208 reduced ALT/AST levels, highlighting its multi-organ protective capacity, including hepatoprotection. The core innovation of this invention lies in the application of a liposomal delivery system to extend the circulation time, enhance tissue targeting, and achieve therapeutic efficacy at significantly lower doses. This addresses the pharmacokinetic limitations of dipyridamole and provides a safer and more effective therapeutic option for clinical use. Considering the well-documented safety profile of dipyridamole, this novel formulation be used soon for clinical testing, which aligns completely with the “new tricks for old drugs” strategy of drug repositioning [12]. REAL208 offers a pioneering therapeutic avenue for AKI and holds promise for broader applications in sepsis-related multiorgan protection and treatment of other inflammation-driven diseases. After formulation innovation and indication expansion, this concept can have high patentability and translational potential. Given the well-defined drug content of REAL208 (~ 4.9 mg dipyridamole per mL) and its high encapsulation efficiency (> 99%), the in vivo doses used in this study represent accurately delivered amounts of encapsulated dipyridamole, which directly underlie the robust renoprotective efficacy observed in both LPS- and I/R-induced AKI models.

No pharmacological therapy has been proven to reverse AKI or reduce adverse outcomes such as death or dialysis; contemporary care is largely supportive and guideline driven. Per the 2021 Surviving Sepsis Campaign, care bundles for sepsis-associated AKI emphasize timely crystalloid resuscitation and the use of early vasopressors to maintain a mean arterial pressure ≥ 65 mm Hg [13]. Large pragmatic randomized trials have further suggested that balanced crystalloids, compared with 0.9% saline, reduce 30-day major adverse kidney events (MAKE30) in the intensive care unit and ED populations (SMART and SALT-ED)[14, 15]. The PRESERVE trial did not show any benefit of using intravenous sodium bicarbonate over saline or oral N-acetylcysteine over placebo in terms of mortality, dialysis, or sustained renal decline for contrast-associated AKI prevention or for contrast-AKI itself [16]. Despite promising phase-2 signals with recombinant alkaline phosphatase (ilofotase alfa), the phase-3 REVIVAL study in sepsis-AKI did not improve 28-day survival (with exploratory reductions in MAKE-type outcomes), underscoring the absence of an approved organ-protective drug [17]. Mechanistically, dipyridamole inhibits equilibrated nucleoside transporters and increases extracellular adenosine, augmenting anti-inflammatory responses in human experimental endotoxemia [1, 18]. In preclinical renal I/R models, dipyridamole has been shown to confer renoprotection via adenosine-receptor-dependent pathways [9]. Liposomal drug delivery platforms can improve pharmacokinetics and lower off-target toxicity, and when appropriately engineered, nanocarriers (including targeted liposomes) can enhance the accumulation of therapeutics in the kidney [19, 20]. On this mechanistic and translational backdrop, evaluating a liposomal dipyridamole formulation as an adjunct to standard care for patients with AKI is clinically plausible, with kidney-centric composite endpoints such as MAKE30/MAKE90 providing relevant signals for efficacy [14].

The Seahorse XFp Mito stress test deconvolutes mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation in live cells by quantifying basal respiration, ATP-linked respiration (oligomycin-sensitive OCR), proton leakage, maximal respiratory capacity (FCCP-stimulated OCR), spare respiratory capacity, and coupling efficiency, which are standard parameters for interpreting cellular bioenergetics [21–23]. The JC-1 mitochondrial membrane potential assay, here read on a fluorescence image cytometer (e.g., NucleoCounter® NC-3000™), reports mitochondrial polarization as a red/green ratiometric signal that decreases with depolarization, providing a complementary orthogonal readout to OCR [24–26]. As mitochondrial membrane potential (together with ΔpH) constitutes the proton-motive force that drives ATP synthase, JC-1 trends mechanistically underlie Seahorse-derived indices of ATP production and coupling, linking changes in membrane potential to shifts in ATP-linked respiration and proton leak [25]. In our LPS injury experiments, REAL208 improved basal respiration, maximal respiratory capacity, ATP-linked respiration, proton leak, and coupling efficiency without materially altering spare respiratory capacity (Fig. 3E–F), consistent with preserved oxidative phosphorylation under inflammatory stress [21]. Concordantly, JC-1 analysis showed that REAL208 maintained mitochondrial membrane potential compared with LPS alone (Fig. 3C), aligning polarization with the observed restoration of OCR parameters [24–26]. In H/R injury, REAL208 dose-dependently rescued basal and maximal respiration, ATP production, coupling efficiency, spare respiratory capacity, and normalized non-mitochondrial OCR (Fig. 4B–C), indicating a broad recovery of respiratory control [21–23]. The results of the JC-1 assay corroborated these findings by demonstrating preservation of mitochondrial membrane potential after REAL208 treatment during H/R (Fig. 4A), which is expected to support ATP synthesis in injured proximal tubular cells [24–26]. Integrating the results of these orthogonal assays, we concluded that increases in ATP-linked respiration and coupling efficiency, together with maintained mitochondrial membrane potential, indicated improved mitochondrial efficiency and containment of pathological proton leak—hallmarks of cytoprotection during inflammatory and ischemic stress [21, 27, 28]. Mechanistically, such protection is biologically plausible for a dipyridamole formulation, considering that dipyridamole elevates extracellular adenosine and augments anti-inflammatory responses in human endotoxemia, and renal adenosine signaling is recognized to be protective in I/R-related AKI [1, 29].

Conclusion

In summary, our study demonstrated that liposomal dipyridamole (REAL208) confers robust protection against both LPS-induced and I/R-induced AKI by preserving mitochondrial bioenergetics, maintaining membrane potential, and attenuating oxidative and inflammatory stress. Using Seahorse XFp analysis and complementary JC-1 assays, we confirmed that REAL208 improves basal and maximal respiration, ATP-linked respiration, and coupling efficiency, while stabilizing the mitochondrial membrane potential under inflammatory and hypoxic stress. These findings highlight the pivotal role of mitochondrial preservation in mediating the renoprotective effects of REAL208 and underscore the value of drug repositioning strategies in the development of novel therapeutics for AKI. Considering its favorable safety profile and enhanced pharmacological efficiency at lower concentrations, REAL208 holds promise as a clinically translatable therapy for AKI and other systemic inflammatory and ischemic disorders. Future studies should focus on dose optimization, long-term outcomes, and clinical validation to advance the therapeutic applications of this formulation.

Limitations and Future Perspectives

The present study has several limitations. First, our findings were derived from murine AKI models and in vitro HK-2 cell systems, which may not completely recapitulate the heterogeneity and complexity of human AKI. Therefore, translation to a clinical setting requires cautious interpretation. Second, although REAL208 demonstrated dose-dependent protective effects and favorable safety in preclinical testing, the inherent vasodilatory properties of dipyridamole raised the possibility of developing hypotension in septic or critically ill patients, underscoring the need for rigorous hemodynamic monitoring in future studies. Third, although liposomal encapsulation enhances pharmacological efficiency and bioavailability, challenges in large-scale manufacturing, ensuring long-term stability, and regulatory validation of liposomal formulations remain. From a clinical perspective, future investigations should focus on defining optimal dosing regimens, establishing long-term renoprotective outcomes, and exploring the potential of REAL208 in preventing the AKI-to-CKD transition. Moreover, considering the observed hepatoprotective effects and systemic benefits of adenosine signaling, REAL208 may offer broader multi-organ protection in sepsis and I/R syndrome. Carefully designed randomized controlled trials, with endpoints such as MAKE30/MAKE90, renal recovery, and ICU-free days, will be essential to validate its therapeutic potential and advance REAL208 toward clinical application.

Graded levels of wheat oligopeptides relieve feed change-induced intestinal damage via modulation of the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway in broilers
Authors: Yutong Fu, Zhengda Lu, Xin Fang, Cangjue Bianba, Xiaoyue Shi, Qinghe Cai, Yinuo Zhao, Shimeng Huang, Qiugang Ma, Cheng Ji, Lihong Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The abrupt transition in feed composition and form during the starter (d 0 to 10) to grower (d 11 to 21) phase can damage intestinal barriers and affect broiler performance, triggering the feed change stress. This study aimed to elucidate the impact of feed change stress and investigate the effect and mechanism of wheat oligopeptides (WOP) on the intestinal damage induced by feed change stress in broilers. A total of 120 one-d-old male Arbor Acre broilers (initial body weight [BW] 45.38 ± 0.64 g) were randomly allocated to two treatments (feed change or not) with 6 replicates of 10 birds, with monitoring for 60 h after changing feeds on d 11. A total of 240 one-d-old male Arbor Acre broilers (initial BW 45.33 ± 0.55 g) were randomly allocated to four treatments supplemented with graded levels of WOP (0.0, 0.1%, 0.2%, and 0.3%) with 6 replicates of 10 birds. The experimental period was 42 d. Compared with the no feed change (FC) group, broilers exhibited decreased feed intake after 4 h of changing feeds (P = 0.006), downregulated CLDN2 expression, upregulated IL-8 and TNF-α expression (P < 0.001), suppressed intestinal stem cell (ISC) differentiation evidenced by reduced BMI1, LGR5, CHGA, SI, and MUC2 expression (P < 0.05). The stress induced by changing feeds in broilers may gradually diminish over time. Dietary WOP supplementation significantly improved growth performance of broilers. Compared with the control group, broilers fed diets supplemented with WOP showed higher BW at d 42 (P = 0.010), greater average daily gain (ADG) during d 11 to 42 (P = 0.010), and lower feed/gain ratio (F/G) during d 11 to 42 (P = 0.002), with 0.2% WOP being the optimal inclusion level. Mechanistically, WOP promoted ISC differentiation, partly by modulating the Wnt/β-catenin pathway, evidenced by upregulated Wnt, β-catenin, and c-Myc mRNA and protein levels (P < 0.05). Notably, WOP counteracted feed change-induced suppression of nutrient transporters and inflammatory responses. These findings indicate that changing feeds impairs intestinal health via ISC dysregulation, while WOP alleviates these effects by modulating ISC differentiation and the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway.

Introduction

In the modern poultry industry, broilers are generally fed with mash or crumbled feed in the starter (d 0 to10), pellet feed in the grower (d 11 to 21) and finisher (d 22 to 42). There are several reasons for feeding pellet feed to broilers during the grower and finisher phases, including providing balanced nutrients, enhancing palatability, increasing feed intake, improving growth performance, and reducing feed waste (Brink et al., 2022). However, sudden changes in feed composition and physical form from d 10 to 11 in broilers may lead to a reduction of feed intake, damage to intestinal barriers and health, and thus triggering the feed change stress. Extensive research has shown that stress can cause various physiological disorders in broilers, such as immune depression, metabolism disorders, and performance reduction (Virden and Kidd, 2009). However, most research has focused on heat stress and other factors, with no studies addressing feed change stress in broilers during the transition from starter to grower. Feed change stress may primarily impact the intestinal development and health of broilers.

The differentiation of intestinal stem cells (ISC) could reflect the development status of the intestine. The process could be briefly summarized as follows: ISC first differentiates into progenitor cells, which then differentiate into secretory cells and absorptive lineages. The secretory lineage further develops into goblet cells, enteroendocrine cells, and Paneth cells, collectively contributing to the intestinal barrier and maintaining epithelial integrity (Gehart and Clevers, 2019). Absorptive cells further differentiate into intestinal cells, where the nutrient transport carriers determine the host's ability to absorb nutrients (Yeung et al., 2011). Therefore, the differentiation function of ISC determines the health of the intestinal barrier and the transport and absorption function of nutrients (glucose, amino acids, oligopeptides, etc.) in broilers, which in turn affects the intestinal health and growth performance of broilers. The core of this complex regulatory mechanism lies in the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway, which is recognized as the primary regulator in controlling ISC proliferation and differentiation into intestinal epithelial cells, as well as supporting the structural and homeostatic stability of the intestine (Scoville et al., 2008). Conversely, an impaired Wnt/β-catenin pathway leads to dysfunction of intestinal regeneration (Li et al., 2019), highlighting its essential role in maintaining intestinal health.

Recent studies on nutritional intervention strategies have shown that various nutrients can alleviate different types of stress or modulate ISC differentiation by regulating the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway. Supplementation with vitamin C and chromium (Perai et al., 2014), creatine monohydrate (Wang et al., 2015b), resveratrol (Zhang et al., 2017), have been shown to mitigate the adverse effects of transport stress on meat quality of broilers. Supplementation with probiotics and prebiotics has been reported to protect the intestinal barrier of broilers from heat stress-induced damage (Ringseis and Eder, 2022). Dietary yeast cell wall polysaccharides inclusion could accelerate intestinal development in broilers, potentially by activating the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway of ISC (Guo et al., 2025). Lactobacillus reuteri promoted the expansion of ISC and intestinal epithelial cell renewal by regulating the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway, maintaining the integrity of the intestinal mucosal barrier in broilers (Ding et al., 2024).

Wheat oligopeptides (WOP) are isolated from hydrogenated wheat gluten, a by-product of the wheat starch production process (Asrarkulova and Bulushova, 2018), and have shown great potential as nutritional feed additives. Wheat oligopeptides is a kind of high-digestible plant-based protein, with nearly 60% of the mixture existing in the form of small peptides and containing a significant amount (about 30%) of glutamine (Gln). Wheat oligopeptides has been reported to exhibit diverse biological activities, such as anti-inflammatory (Kan et al., 2017), antibacterial (Egorov et al., 2005), antioxidant (Hong et al., 2014), and stress-relieving effects (Yin et al., 2015). Gln constitutes the largest proportion of amino acids present in WOP, a conditionally essential amino acid that needs to be consumed externally, especially under stress (inflammation, infection, trauma, etc). Gln has been shown to alleviate various types of stress in animals (Thomas et al., 2022; Ncho et al., 2023). Moreover, Gln has been reported to improve intestinal function by regulating intestinal barrier integrity and promoting enterocyte proliferation and survival (Wang et al., 2015a).

To date, no studies have examined the impact of feed change stress in broilers, and it remains unclear whether such stress affects intestinal development. Moreover, it is still unknown whether WOP alleviates feed change stress in broilers or whether their potential effect is mediated through the regulation of ISC differentiation. The objective of the present study was to investigate the impact of feed change in broilers from starter to grower, to investigate whether WOP exerts beneficial effects, and to elucidate the underlying mechanism. This study aims to offer a reference strategy for alleviating feed change stress and improving broiler growth performance.

Materials and methods

Animal ethics statement

All experimental procedures were conducted in accordance with the approval of the Laboratory Animal Welfare and Ethical Review Committee of China Agricultural University (approval No. AW21605202-1-08).

Experimental design

The first experiment aimed to explore the impact of feed change on 10-d-old broilers. A total of 120 one-d-old male Arbor Acre broilers (initial body weight [BW] 45.38 ± 0.64 g) were randomly allocated to two treatment groups, each consisting of 6 replicate cages with 10 birds per cage. All broilers received a starter mash diet during d 1 to 10. At d 11, broilers in the no feed change (no FC) group were still fed with a starter mash diet, and broilers in the feed change (FC) group were fed with a grower pellet diet. Feed intake was recorded after 2, 4, 8, 12, 24, 36, 48, and 60 h of changing feeds. Jejunum samples were collected from each replicate of the no FC group, and FC group after 12, 36, and 60 h of changing feeds. In comparison between the no FC and FC groups, the 12 h FC samples were used to represent the FC group. Sampling time points (12, 36, and 60 h) were chosen to capture the sequential phases of the feed change response. The initial 12 h mark aligns with the average gastrointestinal retention time in broilers (5–6 h; Svihus and Itani, 2019) to capture acute stress, while subsequent intervals were used to assess the sustained impact on the intestine. The second experiment aimed to explore the effect of graded WOP levels on broilers under feed change stress. A total of 240 one-d-old male Arbor Acre broilers (initial BW 45.33 ± 0.55 g) were randomly allocated to four treatment groups, each consisting of 6 replicate cages with 10 birds per cage. The trial was divided into three phases: starter (d 1 to 10), grower (d 11 to 21), and finisher (d 22 to 42). During the starter period, all broilers were provided with a basal mash diet. In the grower and finisher periods, broilers in the control (CON) group were provided with the basal pellet diet, while those in the three experimental groups received the same basal pellet diet supplemented with 0.1%, 0.2%, and 0.3% WOP. Wheat oligopeptides was supplied by Honest Biology Technology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). According to the previous study (Xie et al., 2024), the composition of WOP was identified by liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS; Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Waltham, MA, USA), and its molecular weight distribution was determined by using high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC; Agilent Technologies Inc., Santa Clara, CA, USA). The top 25 peptide sequences and the molecular weight distribution of WOP are provided in Table S1 and Table S2, respectively. A total of 202 peptides were identified in WOP, of which 128 contained Gln. Based on the high Gln content of WOP, a 0.2% Gln group (6 replicates of 10 birds) was included as a positive control. This allowed for a direct evaluation of whether WOP offers comparable or superior benefits for broiler performance relative to a pure Gln source.

Growth performance of broilers was recorded, including the BW and feed intake for each replicate on d 11, 21, and 42. Based on these measurements, average daily feed intake (ADFI), average daily gain (ADG), and ADFI/ADG ratio (F/G) were calculated for d 11 to 21, d 22 to 42, and d 11 to 42, respectively.

Sample collection and analysis

Broilers with BW closest to the replicate mean were selected, and jejunum samples were collected at d 42 for further analysis. Broilers were euthanized via cervical dislocation followed by exsanguination via the carotid artery. Mid-jejunum segments, located midway between the end of the duodenal loop and Meckel's diverticulum, were collected, rinsed, snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and fixed in 10% neutral buffered formalin for morphological analysis. All samples were subsequently stored at −80 °C.

Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining

Fresh jejunal tissues were fixed immediately in 4% paraformaldehyde solution. Fixed jejunal tissues were embedded in paraffin, then sectioned into 5-μm-thick sections, deparaffinized in xylene, rehydrated, and mounted on glass slides. These sections of jejunal tissues were stained with H&E. The slides were photographed on a microscope slide scanner (Panoramic MIDI, 3D HISTECH Ltd., Budapest, Hungary). Stained tissue sections were examined under an Olympus BX61 light microscope (Olympus Corporation, Tokyo, Japan) with × 200 magnification. Images were captured using an Olympus DP70 digital camera (Olympus Corporation, Tokyo, Japan).

Alcian blue-periodic acid Schiff staining (AB-PAS)

The jejunal tissues were embedded, sliced, deparaffinized, hydrated, and then stained with AB-PAS. Neutral and acidic mucins in the jejunal goblet cells were distinguished by the AB-PAS, then numbers of goblet cells were counted. The images were visualized by Case Viewer 2.3 (Servicebio Technology Co., Ltd., Wuhan, Hubei, China).

Transcriptional analysis

Total RNA was isolated from the jejunum samples using FastPure Cell/Tissue Total RNA Isolation Kit V2 (RC112, Vazyme Biotech Co., Ltd., Nanjing, Jiangsu, China) according to the manufacturer's instructions. The purity and concentration of the total RNA were measured in a NanoDrop-2000 spectrophotometer (ThermoFisher Scientific Co., Waltham, MA, USA) using the 260/280 nm absorbance ratio. Reverse transcription was done using the HiScript II Q RT SuperMix for qPCR (+gDNA wiper) (R223–01, Vazyme Biotech Co., Ltd., Nanjing, Jiangsu, China) following the manufacturer's instructions, and the complementary DNA (cDNA) was stored at −20 °C. Two-step quantitative real-time PCR was performed with Taq Pro Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix (Q712–02, Vazyme Biotech Co., Ltd., Nanjing, Jiangsu, China) on a Real-Time PCR Detection Systems (CFX Connect, Bio-Rad Laboratories, Hercules, CA, USA) according to the manufacturer's instructions. Pairs of primers were designed using GenBank from the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI). Oligonucleotide primers of genes for broilers were designed and synthesized by Sango Biotech Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Each sample was analyzed in triplicate under the following PCR conditions: 95 °C for 5 min, followed by 40 cycles of 95 °C for 10 s, 58 °C for 30 s, with a final extension at 72 °C for 5 min. Specificity of the PCR products was evaluated by the analysis of the melting curve. The relative levels of mRNA expression were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method (Livak and Schmittgen, 2001), which normalized to the reference mRNA level of β-actin. The values of the control group were used as a calibrator. Table S3 lists the quantitative real-time PCR primers used in this study and the full names of all gene abbreviations.

Western blotting

Total protein of jejunum samples was extracted and determined using BCA protein detection kit (P0012, Beyotime Biotechnology Inc., Shanghai, China). Equal amounts of proteins were separated by sodium dodecyl sulfate polyacrylamide gel (gel concentration, 10%; intensity of electricity, 120 V; time, 60 min) electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) and transferred onto the polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membranes. The membranes were blocked with TBST buffer containing 5% skim milk powder for 1 h at room temperature, then the protein bands were incubated with primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C. The primary antibodies used were specific for β-actin (66009-1-Ig, 1:8000, Proteintech Group, Inc., Rosemont, IL, USA), and c-Myc (TA0358S, 1:1000), Wnt3a (TD13430S, 1:1000) and β-catenin (CK02183S, 1:1000) from Abmart Inc. (Shanghai, China). The protein bands were then further incubated with secondary antibodies (SA00001–2, 1:8000; Proteintech Group, Inc., Wuhan, Hubei, China) for 1 h at room temperature. The immunoreactive bands were visualized by enhanced chemiluminescence and quantitatively analyzed by ImageJ software (version 1.54, National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD, USA). The quantified data were normalized to the internal reference protein β-actin and further expressed as relative protein expression.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using the GLM procedure in JMP Pro 16 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA). Differences between the no FC group and the FC group, the CON group and the 0.2% Gln group were analyzed using an unpaired two-tailed t-test. For the remaining experimental data, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was applied. The statistical model used is described by the following equation:Yij = μ + ai + £ij,where Yij is the observed value of the dependent variable; μ is the overall mean; ai is the fixed treatment effect, and £ij is the random error. Tukey's multiple comparison test was used to compare differences among treatments. Polynomial contrasts were conducted to analyze the linear and quadratic effects of dietary WOP levels on the growth performance of broilers. Statistical significance is determined at P < 0.05; P-values between 0.05 and 0.10 are considered indicative of a trend. Figures were generated by GraphPad Prism (version 9.5.0, GraphPad Software, Inc., San Diego, CA, USA).

Discussion

In commercial broiler production, the transition from the starter to the grower phase typically involves simultaneous alterations in both the physical form of feed and its nutritional composition. In this study, the experimental design defined the feed change group as simultaneously receiving both a different feed form and an altered nutritional composition, which reflects the real broiler production practice. The impact of changing feeds in broilers was evident, mainly reflecting in reduced feed intake, increased jejunal permeability, increased inflammatory response, restrained ISC differentiation, and declined jejunal secretory and absorptive function of broilers. Hence, the observed physiological stress may result from the combined effects of changes in feed structure and nutrient profile.

Feed intake of broilers was recorded at eight time points between 2 and 60 h after changing feeds. Across all monitoring times, broilers in the FC group consistently exhibited lower feed intake than those in the no FC group, indicating a decreased desire for broilers to consume feed after changing feeds. The most pronounced decrease in feed intake was observed at 4 h after changing feeds, likely reflecting the relatively short gastrointestinal retention time in broilers, which averages 5 to 6 h (Svihus and Itani, 2019), making the effects of changing feeds most apparent during this period. A similar situation has been observed in weaned piglets, where weaning induced stress responses causing a decline in both feed intake and growth rate (Xiong et al., 2019). Moreover, the pattern of feed intake in broilers after changing feeds can be considered as a similar pattern of fasting or delayed access to feed. A decline in mRNA expression levels of tight junction proteins of broilers after changing feeds revealed that the barrier function of the jejunum may be harmed. A previous study revealed that fasting periods of 4.5 and 9.0 h could increase intestinal permeability of 37-d-old male Ross 308 broilers compared to non-fasted broilers (Gilani et al., 2018b). Fasting for 9.0 and 19.5 h significantly decreased claudin-3 mRNA expression in the ileum of broilers compared to non-fasted broilers (Gilani et al., 2018a). Pelleting improved feed efficiency of growing-finishing pigs, but had a negative impact on gastric mucosa integrity and was associated with an increased harmful bacteria colonisation (Millet et al., 2012). In this study, the intestinal health of broilers was impaired after changing feeds, as evidenced by downregulated mRNA expression of tight junction proteins, upregulation of inflammatory cytokines, impaired function of epithelial and stem cells, reduced number of goblet cells, and diminished nutrient transport capacity.

During the process of ISC differentiation, there are corresponding cell marker genes that can reflect the relative abundance of the functional cells. The mRNA expression levels of cell marker genes and stem cell proliferation genes in the jejunum represent the differentiation status of ISC. BMI1 and LGR5 are stem cell marker genes (Yan et al., 2012), and LGR5 is recognized as a specific marker for the ISC population positioned at the bottom of intestinal crypts (Barker et al., 2007). The adhesion molecule EPCAM, produced by stem cells and their differentiated progenitor cells, can be used to indicate their relative proportions (Biton et al., 2018). The marker gene for secretory cells differentiated from progenitor cells is CHGA, and the marker gene for absorptive cells differentiated from progenitor cells is SI. And the marker gene for goblet cells differentiated from secretory cells is MUC2 (Liu et al., 2023). The results showed that changing feeds exerted a significant inhibitory effect on the expression of genes governing proliferation and differentiation of jejunal stem cells (such as BMI1, LGR5, CHGA, SI, and MUC2), indicating that the ability of intestinal regeneration is inhibited after changing feeds. In addition, the proliferation of ISC is regulated by several factors in the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway, including Wnt, β-catenin, and TCF4 (Fevr et al., 2007). In this experiment, the jejunal mRNA expression levels of Wnt signaling pathways (Wnt3a, Wnt5a, Wnt5b, Wnt10a, and TCF4) were significantly decreased after changing feeds, whereas the jejunal mRNA expression levels of β-catenin were significantly increased, indicating that changing feeds disrupted the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway in the jejunum of broilers. A previous study revealed that delayed access to feed after hatch could restrict intestine development of broilers, which was revealed as downregulation of MUC2 at the molecular level (Liu et al., 2020). Besides, the apoptosis of ISC could trigger intestinal inflammatory response (Wang et al., 2020), which was supported by the increased mRNA levels of IL-8 and TNF-α after changing feeds.

The principal role of goblet cells is mucin secretion, contributing to the integrity of the epithelial barrier (Gustafsson and Johansson, 2022). The differentiated regulatory genes of goblet cells can participate in the differentiation of secretory cells into goblet cells (Gipson, 2016). Genes c-Myc (Konsavage et al., 2012), KLF4 (Katano et al., 2020), and SPDEF (Noah et al., 2010) play a crucial role in the development and maturation of goblet cells. The number of goblet cells and the relative expression levels of genes regulating goblet cell differentiation can reflect the secretion function of intestinal epithelial cells in the body. A previous study revealed that delayed access to feed after hatch could decrease the goblet cells production (Liu et al., 2020). In the present study, gene expressions related to goblet cell differentiation were significantly reduced, indicating that the intestinal secretion function of broilers was impaired after changing feeds, and the mucosal barrier was damaged, which may increase the susceptibility of the intestine to pathogens and toxins.

Nutrients (such as glucose, oligopeptides, amino acids, etc.) enter the bloodstream or lymphatic system through intestinal epithelial cells transport carriers, for absorption and utilization by the body, and participate in nutrient metabolism, consequently impacting both intestinal health and growth performance of broilers. The absorption capacity of intestinal epithelial cells for nutrients mainly depends on the expression levels of various nutrient transporter genes (Kiela and Ghishan, 2016). A previous study demonstrated that reducing feed particle size enhances nutrient digestibility; which may be related to the increased secretion ability in the digestive tract (Amaral et al., 2015). In the present study, gene expressions of nutrient transporters decreased in the jejunum of broilers after changing feeds, indicating that changing feeds may have harmful effects on the jejunal secretion and absorption ability in broilers.

The detrimental effects of changing feeds on broiler intestinal health have been demonstrated. To further determine whether this stress exhibited time-dependent dynamics, jejunal samples of broilers at 12, 36, and 60 h after changing feeds. The observed increase in jejunal integrity (CLDN2), reduction in inflammatory (IL-8 and TNF-α) and apoptosis (BAX) markers, enhancement of absorptive function (SI) and ISC differentiation (TCF4), upregulation of goblet cells’ function (SPDEF), and elevated expression of amino acid transporters (SLC1A1 and SLC7A9) over time may indicate a progressive recovery of intestinal function following feed change stress. However, only these limited parameters showed time-dependent changes; these findings may not fully reflect the overall temporal dynamics of the intestinal response to changing feeds.

Finding an effective nutritional intervention measure is of great significance. Hence, graded levels of WOP were added to the grower and finisher diet to investigate whether they could relieve feed change stress in broilers. The results of growth performance of broilers showed that all graded levels of WOP (0.1%, 0.2%, and 0.3%) could improve BW and ADG of broilers, decrease F/G in different degrees compared with the CON group (changing feeds with no WOP added). Broilers fed diets supplemented with 0.2% WOP exhibited the highest BW at d 42 and ADG during d 11 to 42, as well as the lowest F/G during d 11 to 42. However, the lower BW observed at d 42 with 0.3% WOP supplementation compared to 0.2% WOP may be attributed to nutrient imbalance and metabolic effects associated with higher inclusion levels. Because WOP is rich in peptides, excessive inclusion could disrupt amino acid balance and limit the availability, while the increased free amino acids and small peptides may reduce nutrient absorption efficiency. Studies revealed that the inclusion of some protein hydrolysates in diets could enhance growth performance and feed efficiency in animals (Hou et al., 2017). However, as the most abundant amino acid in WOP, dietary inclusion of 0.2% Gln did not significantly improve the growth performance of broilers in this study. This suggests that the beneficial effects of WOP are likely attributable to the combined action of its various peptides and the specific bioactive peptide sequences, rather than the presence of Gln alone. Hence, these results indicate that graded levels of WOP could improve the growth performance of broilers experiencing feed change stress.

The integrity and height of intestinal villi are important indicators of intestinal function. A longer VH represents an increase in nutrient absorption area, the enhanced secretory function of the intestinal barrier and immune function (Moor et al., 2018). Intestinal tight junction proteins are important barriers to prevent harmful substances from entering the body and maintain the structural integrity of the intestinal epithelium (Buckley and Turner, 2018). Supplementation with dietary hydrolyzed wheat gluten alleviated ileal barrier dysfunction induced by Escherichia coli infection in broilers (Wu et al., 2022). Dietary WOP effectively protected against intestinal mucosal deterioration in aged mice (Xian et al., 2022). Administration of wheat peptides contributed to the protection of gut barrier integrity, thereby alleviating dextran sulfate sodium (DSS)-induced colitis in mice (Xie et al., 2024). In the present study, increased VH and tight junction mRNA expression in the WOP group revealed that WOP could recover the increased jejunal permeability induced by changing feeds in broilers.

The ISC is primarily situated at the base of the crypts, and almost all the intestinal function depends on the differentiation of ISC to various functional intestinal cells (Haber et al., 2017). The results indicated that dietary WOP supplementation enhanced the expression of genes associated with jejunal stem cell proliferation and differentiation (BMIL1, LGR5, CHGA, SI, MUC2, and EPCAM) to varying degrees, indicating that WOP may alleviate the impaired intestinal regeneration ability in broilers caused by changing feeds. The Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway plays an indispensable role in maintaining ISC activities. The pathway is activated by the binding of Wnt ligands to their receptors. Then, as the core participant, β-catenin plays a critical role in controlling cell proliferation, survival, differentiation, and migration (Liu et al., 2022). As a key effector of Wnt/β-catenin signaling, c-Myc is essential for intestinal homeostasis and regeneration (Sansom et al., 2007). Knocking out c-Myc leads to cessation of crypt self-renewal (Muncan et al., 2006). Intestinal damage induced by deoxynivalenol was attenuated by hydrolyzed wheat gluten through activation of ISC proliferation and differentiation mediated by Wnt/β-catenin signaling (Zhou et al., 2019). The current study revealed that WOP increased the mRNA expression levels of Wnt, β-catenin, and c-Myc. At the protein level, WOP upregulated Wnt3a and β-catenin, but c-Myc protein levels remained unchanged despite increased mRNA. This discrepancy suggests that post-transcriptional regulation, protein turnover, or translational control may contribute to the disconnect between mRNA and protein expression. Overall, these findings indicate that WOP modulates the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway at both the transcriptional and protein levels, although not all downstream targets show a parallel response. In addition, WOP supplementation promoted ISC differentiation, enhanced the mucus barrier, and improved nutrient absorptive function in the jejunum of broilers, thereby mitigating the intestinal damage induced by changing feeds.

In summary, this research provides the initial systematic evidence that the stress induced by changing feeds could induce harmful effects on various aspects of intestinal health in broilers, utilizing histological analysis, gene expression analysis, and physiological indicators for comprehensive evaluation. A reliable model of feed change stress in broilers was established, providing a solid experimental foundation for future research. The stress induced by changing feeds in broilers may be gradually alleviated over time. Besides, a nutritional intervention strategy and the potential mechanism were proposed and preliminarily elucidated. Wheat oligopeptides may alleviate the intestinal injury induced by changing feeds via modulating the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway to facilitate ISC differentiation, enhancing the secretory and absorptive function of the jejunum in broilers.

However, this experimental design did not reveal independent evaluation of the respective contributions of feed form and nutrient composition to the observed intestinal stress. Future studies employing a factorial design are warranted to dissect the individual and interactive effects of feed form and nutrient composition in broilers. Moreover, to achieve a more comprehensive understanding, further research is needed to extend the observation period after changing feeds and include a broader range of indicators. It is also important to investigate whether a gradual changing feeds strategy can effectively minimize the negative impact of feed change stress in broilers. The molecular mechanisms, especially in relation to the Wnt/β-catenin pathway, require deeper investigation. Future research should analyse the nuclear translocation of β-catenin to validate pathway activation and employ inhibitor-based interventions to precisely identify the key regulatory nodes and upstream activators. Moving forward, the use of organoid culture systems is necessary to provide more definitive evidence of WOP's impact on ISC activity and stem cell-driven regeneration.

Conclusion

This study is the first to reveal the impact of feed change stress in broilers, mainly by reducing feed intake, increasing jejunal permeability, increasing inflammatory response, restraining ISC differentiation, and decreasing secretory and absorptive function of the jejunum. The harmful effect of changing feeds in broilers may gradually decrease over time. Dietary supplementation of 0.1%, 0.2%, and 0.3% WOP was found to effectively alleviate intestinal damage caused by feed change stress, as evidenced by enhanced intestinal barrier function, enhanced ISC differentiation, and increased jejunal secretory and absorptive capacity, potentially involving modulation of the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway in broilers. Among the tested doses, the optimal recommended dosage of WOP was 0.2% based on the growth performance and intestinal health-related indicators in broilers. This study contributes to enhancing the knowledge of feed change stress in broilers while highlighting the potential of WOP supplementation in broiler diets, serving as a reference for practical poultry production.

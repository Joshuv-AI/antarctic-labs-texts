Assessment of the Quality of Medical Record Documentation using the CRABEL Score Method in a Tertiary Care Hospital in Eastern India
Authors: Ramkrishna Mondal, Sujeet Kumar Sinha
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Background:The Medical Record Department plays a crucial role in hospitals, ensuring proper documentation and patient care. The Royal College of Surgeons of England has provided guidelines for auditing medical record documentation, including the CRAwford- BEresford – Lafferty (CRABEL) score. This quick, simple, and valuable method assesses the quality of medical note-keeping, providing a useful image of hospital patient care. The current study analyses the assessment of medical record documentation using the CRABEL score.Methodology:The study involved 43,384 patients admitted from 01.01.2022 to 31.12.2022, distributed across 26 specialties and super specialties. A sample size of 400 was assessed using Taro Yamini’s formula. A pretested checklist based on the CRABEL Score assessed four sections of medical records: initial clerking, subsequent visits, consent, and discharge/death. MS Excel was used for simple statistical analyses.Results:Out of 400 case sheets of 26 different departments, the average CRABEL score found was 85.23% ±5.00. The highest being the Dentistry (93.00%) and lowest in Orthopedics (72.83%). The four different area-wise deduction/deficiency scores were, Initial clerking (0.84 ± 0.62), Subsequent visit (13.23 ± 4.97), Consent (0.54 ± 0.58), and Discharge/Death summary (0.04 ± 0.11). The total average deduction/deficiency was 14.64 ± 4.93, highest in Orthopedics and lowest in Dentistry.Conclusion:Completeness of medical records is essential for proper patient care but is a challenge in most organizations. Based on the deficiencies found in this study further study to mitigate the deficiencies may be proposed e.g., training needs assessment, development of training material, and the impact of training.

INTRODUCTION

Every physician or hospital is responsible for protecting patient medical records, a legally binding document,[1] a core element in the continuity of patient care,[2] and vital for future planning and policy-making of medical care, public health promotion, and monitoring.[134] Professional documentation by clinical staff is an integral part of practice to ensure safe and effective care,[5] and good record keeping has been mentioned as one of the fundamental requirements for clinical practice in any healthcare field.[6] Even improving medical records quality is a priority for every health organization, but it might be not easy to realize.[7] The Royal College of Surgeons of England provided guidelines and published the importance of medical record audits in 1990.[8] The said guideline-based CRAwford-BEresford–Lafferty (CRABEL) score as a quick, simple, reliable, and reproducible method with a numerical percent score for assessing the medical records documentation was described by Crawford et al., 2001[8] in the Annals of Royal College of Surgeons of England. Researchers have developed instruments to standardize the process, which were identified and introduced through a literature review. The research team searched for more texts using the names of instruments and information databases. They selected items that introduced instruments for quality and quantity control of medical records or parts of them. Quantity control and quality control are crucial aspects of medical record management, but none of the identified tools are comprehensive enough for comprehensive qualification control of all medical record data. [Table 1] The literature review of examined instruments revealed inconsistencies in terminology for quality attributes of healthcare data, highlighting the need for a common language for developing instruments for national/international medical record qualification control processes.

The Medical Record Department is a critical unit in any hospital, playing a vital role in clinical, administrative, and legal functionalities. Proper medical records provide documentation and a hospital image. Medical audits are essential for analyzing and regulating patient services, using clinical, para-clinical, and financial data to collect and protect patient information. Medical record keeping is crucial for research audits and medicolegal aspects. Medical records are used as tools for different individuals for different purposes.[6] Different studies reported that improved quality of medical records typically increased over consecutive audits by using CRABEL Score as an audit tool.[819202122] It is very good and essential for audits, especially both retrospective as well as prospective audits.[23] The Royal College of Surgeons of England provided guidelines and published the importance of audit[24] and in 1990 it published guidelines on medical record audit.[25] The guideline-based CRABEL score is used widely for the assessment of the quality of medical note-keeping.[26] This serving system is a quick, simple, and valuable method for auditing the quality of medical records with a numerical percent score.[8] Internal audits are not just measurement activities but necessary activities to support the organization in achieving its objectives assessing the quality of clinical care and maintaining high-quality professional performance.[7] To achieve patient satisfaction, the correct measurement of the quality of the services being offered to the patients is the basic quality of the medical service provider.[27] Medical records are critical documents and are vital for legal purposes for future planning of the hospital’s medical care.[28]

The primary objective of the study is to explore the medical record-keeping quality in a tertiary care teaching hospital in Eastern India and to assess quantitatively the quality of medical record-keeping with the use of the CRABEL score audit tool.

METHODOLOGY

Study design and study site: This is a cross-sectional study using the CRABEL Score system following the STROBE format. The study Centre is an Institute of National Importance situated in eastern India. It has 960 beds and has served almost all Specialty and Super Specialty services since 2012.

Study Population: All the medical records of all discharged patients from 01.01.2022 to 31.12.2022 in the study setting except OPD, Dialysis, Daycare, and Emergency. Based on the record the total discharged patients were found 43384 distributed through 26 different departments/units.

Sample size: According to Taro Yamini’s formula with a 95% confidence interval (CI) and 5% margin of error, the sample size of 400, divided among 26 departments/units as per proportionate distribution was taken for assessment.

Inclusion criteria: All Admitted patients admitted from 01.01.2022 to 31.12.2022.

Exclusion criteria: Out patients, Dialysis, Emergency, and Daycare patients are not included in this study.

Sampling Technique: Each department file was chosen based on the randomization technique.

Methods and Data Collection: This is a cross-sectional audit using a medical audit tool. A checklist was devised based on the CRABEL Score[8] system considering its reliability, acceptability, and easy applicability using a pilot study. The sample size was determined using last year’s admission (01.01.2022 to 31.12.2022) in each specialty and super specialty using Taro Yamini’s formula. The checklist was scored accordingly to find out deficiencies quantitatively in four different sections of medical records in 26 different specialties. Three volunteers were recruited to do the audit using the checklist for the desired number of samples. Any bias among the three volunteers in scoring was validated by the two authors separately to conclude. In case of persistent bias, the sample was discarded and a new sample was considered randomly.

Outcome Measures: Quantitative numerical score as percentage.

Statistical analysis: Simple Statistical analyses like mean and standard deviation were used for analysis using Microsoft Excel.

Ethical approval and permission: Though the study did not include any human participants, the study was done following Universal Helsinki’s declaration. Necessary permission was taken from the competent authority for data collection. The study proposal was approved by the Institute’s Ethical Committee and Research Advisory Committee.

DISCUSSION

The medical department still relies heavily on paper for medical registrations, making it challenging to share information for referrals. However, technology exists to replace handwritten prescriptions with reliable electronic registration methods. Patient registration in medical settings is a complex process with numerous discrepancies, causing difficulties in medical claims, benefits, and insurance money. Poor standards in specific departments lead to patient complaints. Other issues include the absence of networked Hospital Information Systems, lack of guidelines for discharge authority, lack of physician visitation hours, and financial inability of patients to pay bills on time. These issues highlight the need for operational improvements. All these multiple issues lead to poor documentation of the medical records and patient dissatisfaction. The study examines the effectiveness of a medical college hospital’s CRABEL scoring tool in improving medical record documentation. A sample of 400 medical records from 26 departments was scored, and feedback was provided. The study found average high-score files across all departments, indicating the hospital’s approach is successful as compared to the different studies available over the world. Medical records, also known as patient charts, provide crucial information on a patient’s demographics, disease diagnosis, treatment plan, procedures, referrals, and other patient-related communications within a hospital or clinic. Clinical notes are crucial in patient care, serving as a link between the present and future, especially when a third party with no prior information is involved. A well-written note from the previous doctor can facilitate easier adaptation and smooth transition between care providers.[5810] The study primarily involved clinical notes written by trainees, including resident doctors and interns, who were navigating their clinical postings at the departments during the study period. The previous publication defined implied consent in clinical practice as an individual entering a surgery, sitting, and allowing only clinical examination.[34] Obtaining informed consent, whether verbal or written, is crucial after thorough explanations of procedures, potential complications, and outcomes.[3536] Verbal consent is not admissible in court and can be denied by parents, regardless of cultural or moral reasons. In a study by Dosumu et al.,[29] signatures were present in all case notes, possibly due to supervised students. In this study, omitted signatures may be by qualified doctors, as students’ entries had to be counter-signed. This highlights the importance of proper documentation and supervision in medical cases.

Studies conducted in the USA, Australia, and Scandinavia revealed that record-keeping frequently falls short of accepted standards.[8] The development of a standardized scoring system by Crawford, Beresford, and Lafferty (CRABEL) for assessing clinical records has been adopted by the Royal College of Surgeons, which was initially used for medical record auditing. The study indicates that clinical note-taking in our setting is highly satisfactory, with over 90% of notes scoring within the 90th percentile, suggesting minimal remediation is needed to enhance the note-taking capabilities of hospital personnel.

The observation is like a previous report from the University College Hospital Ibadan, but significantly different from a UK study that reported scores on students in a pediatric dental clinic.[2937] The study found similarities between the two Nigerian institutions due to uniform training curriculums and superior supervision. In our settings, department and identification numbers are statutorily imposed at registration points, resulting in 100% compliance in our study and among undergraduate students in Ibadan, Nigeria.[29] The study found that dental undergraduates often omitted department notation in their studies, with only one case note containing a valid written consent record. This contrasts with Pessian and Beckert’s findings, which found that the notation of departments was mostly omitted. The lack of documentation for non-invasive procedures in Nigeria suggests that obtaining written consent is not yet a routine practice.[2937] Most pediatric dental clinic procedures are non-invasive and do not require written consent unless conscious sedation or general anesthesia is involved.[31] In cases requiring surgical and invasive analgesia/anesthesia, it is essential to obtain written consent for good reasons.[38]

Limitations: The study suggests that subjective variations in scoring assessments could be more effective in assessing the quality of surgical documentation. However, it also suggests that further research is needed to eliminate confounding factors and to provide evidence for its wider use in other hospitals and on a larger scale.

Future work: The CRABEL score, created in 2001, was designed to evaluate medical note-keeping quality. However, with the rise of electronic health records (EHR), many components have become redundant. Use of an e-CRABEL scoring may be the more effective for assessing EHR documentation quality. Further studies are needed to address the deficiencies, recommendations, or suggestions to improve the quality, and implementation of the feasible suggestions, etc. The said future studies are not in the scope of this current study. The process involves focused group discussion about training needs, arranging training to improve documentation, and re-assessing the CRABEL score after training, a continuous system of training, regular audits, and quality documentation can improve the record-keeping quality.

CONCLUSION

The study highlights the importance of medical records in clinical settings for patient care and service quality measurement, praising the teamwork and involvement of masters in the medical record department who are knowledgeable about current activities. The Average CRABEL Score in the current study is 85.37 ± 4.95% with a range from 78.58 to 90.85%. The subsequent entry section is the major factor for the reduction in the quality of notes (90.34%). Based on the deficiencies found in this study further study to mitigate the deficiencies may be proposed as mentioned in future work. The medical record data qualification control instruments were insufficiently comprehensive and lacked quality features. The hospital’s high care quality is evident in CRABEL scores in clinical notes, but documentation is lacking because of a lack of mandated policy.

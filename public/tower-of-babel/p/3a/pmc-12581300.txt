Longitudinal characteristics of plasma biomarkers in Chinese older adults with Alzheimer’s disease
Authors: Ruixian Li, Lin Liu, Jie Yang, Wenhui Chai, Mingkai Zhang, Min Wei, Yongzhe Wei, Xuanqian Wang, Shuyu Zhang, Jinghua Wang, Tengfei Guo, Ying Han
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundWhile cross-sectional associations between plasma biomarkers and Alzheimer’s disease (AD) pathology and cognition are well established, their longitudinal relationships remain less elucidated. We aimed to explore the utility of plasma β-amyloid (Aβ) 42/Aβ40 and glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP) in detecting and predicting AD progression, particularly considering neuroinflammation as a key contributor.MethodsUtilizing data from the Sino Longitudinal Study on Cognitive Decline (SILCODE), 455 older Chinese participants with available plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 and GFAP data were enrolled. Based on cohort-derived thresholds of plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 (A) and GFAP (As), participants were divided into four groups: A + As+, A + As-, A-As+, and A-As-. We employed generalized linear models to examine the cross-sectional and longitudinal associations between plasma Aβ42/Aβ40, GFAP, and their combination with plasma phosphorylated tau (p-tau), residual hippocampal volume (rHCV), and cognitive function.ResultsAmong 455 participants (mean [SD] age at baseline, 66.11 [6.73] years; 282 [61.98%] female), the A + As + group consistently demonstrated the highest baseline levels and fastest longitudinal increases in plasma p-tau, alongside the lowest baseline levels and fastest longitudinal deterioration of rHCV and cognition. Lower baseline plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 was associated with worse memory and language cognitive function and higher baseline and accelerated increases in plasma p-tau181 and p-tau217. Elevated baseline plasma GFAP was significantly associated with higher baseline plasma p-tau181 and p-tau217, reduced rHCV and cognition, and predicted more rapid increases in plasma p-tau181 and p-tau217, and faster declines in rHCV and cognition. Longitudinally, plasma GFAP slope was positively related to the slopes of plasma p-tau181 and p-tau217 and rapid decreases in global, memory, and executive cognitive function. In contrast, the plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 slope showed a negative correlation only with the p-tau181 slope.ConclusionsThese findings demonstrate the superior predictive utility of plasma GFAP in identifying and predicting plasma p-tau increase, neurodegeneration, and cognitive decline than plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 alone. Critically, the combination of both biomarkers yielded the most optimal results, highlighting their synergistic value for early detection and tracking of AD progression.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13195-025-01882-9.

Introduction

Alzheimer’s disease (AD) is the most prevalent neurodegenerative disorder leading to dementia, whose pathogenesis remains to be fully elucidated. The extensively studied amyloid-β (Aβ) cascade hypothesis postulates that the accumulation of Aβ in the brain is the initial causative event in the disease process, triggering tau deposition and ultimately leading to neurodegeneration and cognitive impairment [1–3]. However, numerous clinical trials targeting Aβ have not yielded desired therapeutic effects over the past several decades [4], suggesting that disease progression cannot be solely attributed to Aβ toxicity [5]. With increasing knowledge of AD pathophysiology, it is anticipated that the previous ATN system [6] may progress towards an expanded version denoted as the ATN (I) system, with “I” symbolizing a neuroinflammatory/immune process [7]. Neuroinflammation is currently considered the third prominent pathological feature, actively contributing to disease progression beyond the canonical Aβ and tau pathologies [8]. While previous studies have predominantly focused on microglial activation [9], there is increasing evidence suggesting that astrocyte activation, robustly reflected by glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP), plays a critical role in the onset and development of AD, notably as an active participant and intermediary link between Aβ accumulation and downstream neurodegeneration [8, 10, 11].

Recent research on anti-Aβ therapies has shown promising disease-reducing effects in Phase II or III trials [12, 13], albeit primarily in patients with Aβ-positive MCI and mild dementia. Consequently, scalable and cost-effective methods are urgently needed for the early detection of AD pathology in routine clinical practice [9]. Fortunately, the aggregation of Aβ and astrocyte activation are early features in the pathological process [14], which can be assessed by biomarkers in plasma or cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) and positron emission tomography (PET) imaging [15]. While the prohibitive costs and limited availability of CSF biomarkers and PET imaging hinder their population-level applications [16], plasma biomarkers have gained increasing recognition [17]. The utility of plasma Aβ in the diagnosis and prediction of AD remains debated. Some studies have suggested that plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 could reflect amyloid PET status and showed potential for predicting future amyloid deposits and subsequent cognitive decline [18–21], though conflicting findings have been reported [22]. Plasma GFAP, a biomarker of astrocyte activation [14], has demonstrated accuracy in the identification and prediction of AD pathology and cognitive deterioration [11, 23, 24]. However, some studies suggest its utility may be primarily confined to Aβ-positive individuals [22, 25, 26]. This suggests that astrocyte activation may occur early in the AD continuum and serve as an intermediary link between Aβ and tau pathology. Most importantly, both plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 and GFAP are known to become abnormal years before the onset of clinical symptoms, making them ideal tools for early detection, even in the preclinical stage [27]. Moreover, existing studies on plasma Aβ and GFAP have largely been cross-sectional, lacking large-sample longitudinal investigations with long-term follow-up, especially in Chinese older adults. Consequently, further evidence is needed to establish causal associations between plasma Aβ and GFAP with other pathological changes and subsequent cognitive decline.

In the present study, utilizing data from the Sino Longitudinal Study on Cognitive Decline (SILCODE) cohort, we aim to investigate (1) the cross-sectional and longitudinal alterations of plasma phosphorylated tau (p-tau), neurodegeneration, and cognitive function within distinct groups categorized by plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 and GFAP, (2) the associations of plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 and GFAP with plasma p-tau, neurodegeneration and cognition at baseline, and (3) the prognostic value of plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 and GFAP on longitudinal changes of plasma p-tau, neurodegeneration and cognitive function.

Materials and methods

Data and study population

SILCODE study is an ongoing, registered multi-center AD research project conducted within the Han Chinese community population in mainland China. The SILCODE study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Xuanwu Hospital, Capital Medical University, according to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki [28]. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants, and authorization for the publication of clinical details was secured. The inclusion/exclusion criteria of the SILCODE study can be found at https://www.clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/study/NCT03370744. All participants completed clinical and neuropsychological assessments, blood sample tests, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), and PET scans at enrollment and follow-up. Participants with available plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 and GFAP data were selected in this study.

Plasma biomarkers and APOE genotyping

Participants fasted for at least 6 h overnight, and their blood was drawn the next morning. The plasma was collected after centrifugation and stored at −80 °C. The concentrations of plasma Aβ40, Aβ42, and GFAP were quantified by commercial Simoa® NEUROLOGY 4-PLEX E (N4PE, cat: 103,670), p-tau181 (cat: 104,111), and p-tau217 kit in Simoa HD-X Analyzer™ (Quanterix Corp). The Aβ42/Aβ40 ratio was then calculated. We used the same methodology as the one employed in a previous study [11], positivity thresholds for plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 and GFAP were derived from our cohort using ROC analysis with the Youden index, established at ≤ 0.0609 and ≥ 136.45 pg/ml, respectively. Based on the positive (+)/negative (-) status of plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 (A) and GFAP (As), participants were divided into four groups: A + As+, A + As-, A-As+, and A-As-. The APOE genotype was determined using TaqMan™ SNP genotyping to detect the ε2, ε3, and ε4 alleles for the two single-nucleotide polymorphisms (rs429358, rs7412) with the DNA Isolation Kit.

Clinical and cognitive assessments

Clinical demographic data, including age, sex, and years of education, were collected. The cognitive profiles were evaluated via a sequence of cognitive ability scales, including (1) memory domain: the Auditory Verbal Learning Test-HuaShan version (AVLT-H), including AVLT-long delayed recall (AVLT-N5) and AVLT-recognition (AVLT-N7); (2) executive domain: the Shape Trails Test Part A and B (STT-A&B); (3) language domain: the 30-item Boston Naming Test (BNT) and the Verbal Fluency Test (VFT); (4) global cognition: the Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE), the Montreal Cognitive Assessment-Basic Version (MoCA-B) and the Memory and Executive Screening (MES).

Structural MRI and amyloid PET image acquisition and processing

MRI and Aβ PET scans were acquired using a simultaneous 3.0T TOF PET/MR scanner (SIGNA PET/MR, GE Healthcare, Milwaukee, WI, USA) at Xuanwu Hospital of Capital Medical University. Structural MRI images were segmented into different regions of interest (ROI) using Freesurfer (V7.2.0). The residual hippocampal volume (rHCV) was calculated as a biomarker of neurodegeneration by taking the hippocampal volume of both hemispheres and adjusting using the estimated total intracranial volume (ICV), as previously described [2].

Participants underwent a 20-min [18F]-florbetapir (FBP) or [18F]-D3FSP (FSP) [29] PET scan after a 50-min post-intravenous injection of 7 to 10 millicuries (mCi) radiotracer. Using in-house Matlab algorithms, the MRI and PET data were further processed. PET images and corresponding MRI structural images were co-registered in Statistical Parameter Mapping 12 (SPM12). Regional PET measurements were extracted from the co-registered images, and sixty-eight Freesurfer-defined cortical ROI were obtained by MRI segmentation. The standardized uptake value ratio (SUVR) of AD summary cortical regions, including the posterior cingulate cortex, precuneus, frontal lobe, parietal lobe, and lateral temporal, was calculated by normalizing the radiotracer uptake values of these typical AD brain areas against those observed in the cerebellum [30]. Participants were classified as Aβ PET positive if their global average SUVR voxels were ≥ 1.1121 for FBP and ≥ 1.0429 for FSP.

Statistical analysis

All the statistical analyses were conducted using R (v4.3.0). All two-sided p < 0.05 were deemed to be statistically significant. To account for multiple comparisons, the Bonferroni correction was applied. The normal distribution of data was determined using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Variables exhibiting non-normal distribution were subjected to logarithmic transformation. Inter-group differences were examined using the Chi-square test for categorical variables and the Kruskal-Wallis one-way analysis of variance for continuous variables with non-normal distribution. Data were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or No. (%) unless otherwise noted. Extreme values of 3 SDs above or below the mean were excluded. Slopes of markers were calculated with longitudinal data using linear mixed-effects models, adjusting for age, sex, education, and APOE status.

First, we compared the baseline and slopes of plasma p-tau181, p-tau217, rHCV, and cognition among four different groups: A + As+, A + As-, A-As+, and A-As-, using the generalized linear models (GLM). Baseline analyses were controlled for age, sex, education, and APOE-ε4 status. Subsequently, to examine the associations of plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 and GFAP with plasma p-tau181, p-tau217, rHCV, and cognition at baseline, we employed the GLM model while controlling for the covariates above. Furthermore, to assess the predictive effect of plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 and GFAP on the disease progression, GLM models were utilized to investigate associations between the baseline and slopes of plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 and GFAP with the slopes of plasma p-tau181 and p-tau217, rHCV, and cognition.

Results

Demographic characteristics of included patients

This study enrolled 455 participants, including 173 A-As-, 29 A-As+, 175 A + As-, and 78 A + As+, respectively. The characteristics of the participants were succinctly outlined in Table 1. Among them, 282 (61.98%) were female, and 129 (28.67%) were carriers of APOE-ε4, with an average age of 66.11 (6.73) and an average educational duration of 12.55 (3.49) years. Within the entire cohort, older age, female, and APOE ε4 carrier status were associated with lower plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 and higher plasma GFAP levels. Longitudinal data for plasma biomarkers and related cognitive scales were available for 290 participants, comprising 107 A-As-, 12 A-As+, 124 A + As-, and 47 A + As + individuals, with a median follow-up time of 2.03 (0.22–6.70) years and a median number of follow-up visits of 2 (1–6) years.

Baseline and longitudinal alterations of markers among different A/As groups

As indicated in Fig. 1 and eTable 1, the A + As + group exhibited elevated plasma levels of p-tau181 and p-tau217, and reduced rHCV compared to the A + or As + alone groups and the A-As- group. Besides, the A + As + group exhibited poorer global cognition as assessed by MMSE, MOCA-B, and MES, severe memory and language cognitive impairment as assessed by AVLT-N5/N7 and BNT, compared to the other groups, while also demonstrating inferior executive cognitive function when compared to the A + As- and A-As- groups. In addition, the A-As + group exhibited elevated levels of plasma p-tau181 compared to the A-As- group. Conversely, the A + As- group demonstrated a decreased concentration of plasma p-tau181 and an increased rHCV than the A-As + group, while also showing worse language cognition when compared to the A-As + and A-As- groups.

Association of plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 and GFAP with plasma p-tau, neurodegeneration, and cognition

At baseline, lower plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 was significantly related to elevated levels of plasma p-tau181 and p-tau217, as well as worse memory cognitive function in terms of long-delayed recall and recognition and language cognitive function (Fig. 3A). However, no correlation was observed with rHCV, global cognition, or executive cognitive function. Furthermore, higher plasma GFAP was significantly linked to increased plasma p-tau181 and p-tau217, reduced rHCV, poorer cognition, including global cognition and multiple cognitive domains (including memory, language, and executive function) (Fig. 3A).

Prediction effect of plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 and GFAP on AD pathology and cognition

As shown in Fig. 3B, baseline plasma GFAP demonstrated superior performance in predicting future AD pathological changes and cognitive decline compared to baseline plasma Aβ42/Aβ40. Lower baseline plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 was negatively associated with accelerated increases in plasma p-tau181 and p-tau217 levels, while no significant associations were discerned with the trajectories of rHCV and cognitive function. In contrast, higher baseline plasma GFAP was associated with faster increases in plasma p-tau181 and p-tau217, as well as more rapid declines in rHCV, global cognition, memory, and executive function.

Longitudinally, similar findings were observed. The decreased rates of plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 were inversely correlated with the increased rates of plasma p-tau181 but not with other markers (Fig. 4). In comparison, the slopes of plasma GFAP showed a positive association with the slopes of plasma p-tau181 and p-tau217, and a negative association with the slopes of global and memory cognition function.

Discussion

In this study, we investigated the cross-sectional and longitudinal associations of plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 and plasma GFAP, as well as their relations with plasma p-tau181 and p-tau217, rHCV, and cognition in a large Chinese aging cohort. Individuals with abnormal plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 and plasma GFAP (A + As+) consistently showed the most pronounced plasma p-tau181 and p-tau217 concentrations, neurodegeneration, and cognitive impairment, both cross-sectionally and longitudinally. Continuously, lower plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 and higher GFAP levels were correlated with higher plasma p-tau181 and p-tau217 and worse memory and language function. Notably, higher plasma GFAP was also associated with greater decreases in rHCV, as well as global and executive cognition. Longitudinally, lower baseline plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 levels were associated with faster rates of p-tau181 and p-tau217 increases but not with the slopes of rHCV and cognition. In contrast, participants with higher baseline plasma GFAP showed more increases in plasma p-tau181 and p-tau217, as well as faster decreases of rHCV and cognition. Similar results were observed in the predictive effect of slopes of plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 and GFAP. These findings revealed the cross-sectional and longitudinal characteristics of plasma biomarkers and their relations with p-tau accumulation, hippocampal atrophy, and cognitive decline in the Chinese aging population with a large dataset, providing significant references for detecting early abnormal signs and tracking disease progression in AD.

The association of baseline plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 with plasma p-tau181 and p-tau217 and accelerated rises in plasma p-tau217 were consistent with prior research [31], suggesting the strong correlation between AD core pathologies in plasma. Furthermore, we found that lower plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 was associated with memory and language cognitive decline rather than global cognition. Unlike our findings, one previous study found no significant correlation between plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 and cognitive decline [32], possibly because they utilized global cognitive measures like MMSE or MoCA. This may be attributed to the fact that memory, language, and executive cognitive domain damage may occur in the early stages of AD, compared with the abnormal global cognition indicators. It also supports that plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 may be a biomarker for early identification and screening of AD. It implies that future studies could expand beyond global cognitive capacities to other cognitive domains. Besides, discrepancies in prior studies are possibly due to varying testing techniques or inadequate stability. According to a previous study, minor errors in the measurement or pre-analytical mistakes in plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 can induce misclassifications, thereby impacting the sensitivity and specificity in amyloid-positive prescreening [33]. Therefore, the standardization of plasma biomarker measurements is crucial for clinical practice.

Significant cross-sectional and longitudinal associations were found between plasma GFAP and plasma p-tau, rHCV, and cognitive function, consistent with previous studies [31, 34, 35]. Most importantly, we observed significant associations between higher baseline plasma GFAP and a more rapid increase in plasma p-tau, as well as declines in rHCV and cognition, indicating that plasma GFAP changes before these markers. This is consistent with previous studies, which found that plasma GFAP changed approximately ten years before the onset of clinical symptoms, earlier than plasma p-tau181 [29, 36]. While Aβ was widely recognized as the initial trigger in AD pathogenesis, our findings demonstrated that elevated plasma GFAP, a marker of astrocyte activation, exhibits a more robust predictive value for downstream AD pathology and cognitive decline compared to plasma Aβ42/Aβ40. This seemingly counterintuitive observation can be reconciled by considering GFAP’s role as an intermediary and active driver within the AD cascade. Aβ accumulation indeed initiates the pathological cascade, leading to a reactive astrocytosis reflected by increased GFAP. However, once activated, astrocytes transition from a homeostatic state to a reactive phenotype that actively contributes to neuroinflammation, modulates tau phosphorylation, and participates in synaptic dysfunction and neuronal loss. Therefore, while Aβ represents the initiating event, GFAP serves as a more proximal and dynamic indicator of the ongoing neurodegenerative engine. This distinction solidifies their complementary roles: plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 acts as an upstream state marker to identify individuals on the AD pathway, while GFAP functions as a downstream dynamic progression marker reflecting the intensity of active disease.

It is important to acknowledge that plasma GFAP, as a general marker of astrocyte activation, can be elevated in various neurological disorders beyond AD, such as stroke or traumatic brain injury. However, within the context of the AD continuum, particularly in Aβ-positive individuals, elevated GFAP serves as a powerful and robust indicator of reactive astrocytes driven by amyloid pathology. Earlier studies have postulated that plasma p-tau and GFAP increase over time solely in Aβ PET positive individuals, demonstrating that Aβ pathology may be a precursor to subsequent tau pathology and astrocyte activation [27, 37]. In addition, the increase of plasma p-tau181 was found only in As and Aβ-positive individuals, suggesting astrocyte activation may be an intermediary link between them [22, 25, 26]. Previous studies have not extensively explored the combined effect of plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 and GFAP on AD pathology and cognitive function. Building upon this, our findings show that the A + As + group was much more effective than either marker alone in predicting higher levels of plasma p-tau, rHCV, and cognitive decline. Moreover, encouragingly, this predictive efficacy remains robust in the CU population. These results demonstrate that the combination of plasma biomarkers of Aβ pathology and astrocyte activation significantly improves the evaluation and prediction of disease progression, even in the preclinical stage. Another point to note is the A-As + subgroup, which represents individuals with elevated GFAP in the absence of amyloid pathology. Although the small sample size limits the statistical power of our comparisons and necessitates caution in interpreting non-significant findings—which may represent Type II errors [38]—this group is of significant clinical interest. This Aβ-negative, GFAP-positive profile is analogous to the concept of Suspected Non-AD Pathophysiology (SNAP) [39]. Potential underlying conditions could include early-stage primary tauopathies, cerebrovascular disease, or other neurodegenerative disorders. Overall, stratifying individuals by their combined amyloid A and astrocyte activation status may offer a powerful strategy for improving diagnostic accuracy and for enriching clinical trial cohorts with subjects at the highest risk of progression.

Despite its strengths, including a large Chinese cohort and comprehensive longitudinal follow-up, our study has several limitations. Firstly, the use of Youden index-derived thresholds for plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 and GFAP positivity is cohort-specific and may not be directly generalizable to other populations or assays without external validation. Secondly, while the Simoa assay is highly sensitive, future research utilizing more advanced methodologies like immunoprecipitation-mass spectrometry (IP-MS) for plasma biomarker quantification is also anticipated for enhanced precision.

Conclusion

This cohort study provides compelling evidence that while plasma GFAP demonstrates robust performance in identifying and predicting higher levels and more rapid increases of plasma p-tau, as well as lower and more rapid decreases in rHCV and cognitive function, the combination of plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 and GFAP yields the most optimal results for assessing and predicting AD progression. These findings underscore the utility of combined plasma biomarkers for identifying high-risk individuals and facilitating more precise and earlier diagnoses, particularly by elucidating the active role of neuroinflammation within the AD continuum.

Importance of Vaccine Adjuvants: A Comparative Study of Antibody Acquisition Rates and Levels Before and After Adjuvant Change in the Hepatitis B Vaccine
Authors: Keiichiro Yoneyama, Tatsuya Kurihara, Ayako Tsuboya, Fuyuhiko Yamamura, Yuka Okajima, Daisuke Tanaka
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Objective Hepatitis B (HB) is a significant global public health concern, and the World Health Organization has emphasized the importance of HB vaccination programs. Currently, there are only two commercially available HB vaccines in Japan: HEPTAVAXⓇ-II and BimmgenⓇ. Antibody acquisition and maintenance are crucial for HB vaccinations.Methods In this study, we used a modified version of HEPTAVAXⓇ-II (mpHBV) with a doubled aluminum-to-phosphate ratio of the adjuvant aluminum hydroxyphosphate sulfate to assess the vaccine's antibody acquisition rate and antibody levels six months post-vaccination compared to conventional HEPTAVAXⓇ-II.Patients A total of 741 second-year students from Showa University School of Medicine, Dentistry, and Pharmacy were included in this study.Results The antibody acquisition rate (≥10 mIU/mL) for mpHBV was 84% (321/382) with a confidence interval (CI) of 0.803-0.877, while that for HEPTAVAXⓇ-II was 68.5% (246/359) with a CI of 0.637-0.734. The geometric mean titer (mIU/mL) for mpHBV was 55.0 mIU/mL with a CI of 46.9-64.5, while that for HEPTAVAXⓇ-II was 24.1 mIU/mL CI 20.7-28.2. Both the antibody acquisition rate and geometric mean titer were significantly higher for mpHBV than for HEPTAVAXⓇII.Conclusion The adjuvant-modified HB vaccine was effective in maintaining vaccine antibody acquisition rates and levels. Adjuvants are important for antibody acquisition and maintenance during vaccine production.

Introduction

Hepatitis B (HB) is a significant global public health concern, with an estimated 254 million people worldwide suffering from chronic HB virus (HBV) infection in 2022 and 1.2 million new infections each year.

The World Health Organization (WHO) has recognized HB as a major global public health issue and emphasized the importance of vaccine programs (1-3). According to the WHO Global Hepatitis Report 2024, HB is the leading cause of liver-related morbidity and mortality, resulting in approximately 1.1 million deaths annually (1-3).

Currently, there are only two commercially available HB vaccines in Japan, HEPTAVAXⓇ-II and BimmgenⓇ (4,5). However, new HB vaccines are still under development in the United States and Europe. The goals of these efforts are to enhance vaccine safety, improve antibody acquisition rates, reduce the number of required vaccinations, and prolong the duration of antibody maintenance post-acquisition. In 2018, Merck Sharp & Dohme (MSD; Merck, Rahway, USA) introduced a modified version of HEPTAVAXⓇ-II (mpHBV), a new HB vaccine with an aluminum-to-phosphate ratio of aluminum hydroxyphosphate sulfate twice as high as that of the previous vaccine. Studies have demonstrated that HEPTAVAXⓇ-II (mpHBV) leads to higher antibody acquisition rates and antibody levels than HEPTAVAXⓇ-II, with no reported safety concerns. Consequently, the vaccine was made commercially available in Japan in 2019 (5).

In 2019, we conducted a study that emphasized the significance of both T-cell- and B-cell immune responses in the development of antibodies against the HB vaccine using cytometry (6).

Engerix-BⓇ (HB vaccine) has transitioned into a cytosine phosphoguanine adjuvant (HepB-CpG vaccine; HeplisavⓇ; Dynavax Technologies, Emerybille, USA) and is currently undergoing clinical trials in the United States and Europe (7,8). However, HeplisavⓇ is associated with adverse effects (9). PreHevbrioⓇ, VBI Vaccines, Cambridge, USA) is another HB vaccine produced through a different manufacturing process, containing three antigens (pre-S1, pre-S2, and S) that are believed to stimulate antibody production against HBV. CpG (cytidine-phosphate-guanosine) oligonucleotide is a commercially available nucleic acid adjuvant used in Europe and the United States (10,11). This indicates a new era in HB vaccine development.

In the present study, we retrospectively compared the antibody uptake rate and levels six months after administration of the modified HEPTAVAXⓇ-II (mpHBV) with those of the previous HEPTAVAXⓇ-II, known for its long-lasting effects.

Materials and Methods

A total of 741 second-year students from the Showa University School of Medicine, Dentistry, and Pharmacy participated in this study. The distribution of the students is presented in Table 1. All students who tested negative for HBs antigen and anti-HBs during the annual physical examination in April received the HBV vaccine. According to the vaccination guidelines of the Infectious Diseases Society of Japan, the second dose was administered 4 weeks after the first dose, and the third dose was administered 24 weeks after the first dose.

For each vaccination, a single dose [0.5 mL (10 μg hepatitis B surface antigen protein)] was administered subcutaneously in the right upper arm. In 2017, the HEPTAVAXⓇ-II (MSD, Tokyo) vial (0.5 mL) contained 0.25 mg aluminum hydroxyphosphate sulfate, 4.5 mg sodium chloride, and 35 μg borax as adjuvants. In 2018, a modified version of HEPTAVAXⓇ-II (mpHBV), which is a hydroxyphosphate sulfate vaccine with an aluminum-to-phosphate ratio double that of the previous version, was developed. Anti-HB titers were measured 52 weeks after the completion of the first vaccination (6 months after the third vaccination; Figure). Antibody titers were determined using chemiluminescent enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays, and according to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention guidelines, antibody negativity was defined as an anti-HBs titer ＜10 mIU/mL (12).

Data analyses

The data analyzed in this study included the results of anti-HBs antibody levels, sex, and age of antibody-negative students six months after their third vaccination. Differences between the mean values of the 2017 and 2018 student groups were assessed using Student's t-test, while significant differences were determined using the chi-squared (χ2) test. The significance level was set at a p value of ＜0.05 for both tests.

This study was approved by the ethics committee of Showa University (approval no. 2023-187-B).

Results

The antibody acquisition rate (AAR; ≥10 mIU/mL) of mpHBV was 84% (321/382) with a confidence interval (CI) of 0.803-0.877, while that of HEPTAVAXⓇ-II was 68.5% (246/359) with a CI of 0.637-0.734. The AAR of mpHBV was significantly higher than that of HEPTAVAXⓇII (p＜0.001).

The geometric mean titer (GMT; mIU/mL) of mpHBV was 55.0 mIU/mL with a CI of 46.9-64.5, while that of HEPTAVAXⓇ-II was 24.1 mIU/mL CI 20.7-28.2. The GMT of mpHBV was significantly higher than that of HEPTAVAXⓇII (p＜0.001; Table 2).

Discussion

In 1999, we investigated the AARs of three HB vaccines commercially available in Japan. One month after vaccination, the AARs were as follows: Engerix-BⓇ (Smith Kline Biologicals, Rixensart, Belgium), 96.6%; BimmgenⓇ (Kaketuken-Fujisawa, Kumamoto, Japan), 91%; and recombinant hepatitis B vaccine MITSUBISHⓇ (Mitsubishikasei-Daiichi, Tokyo, Japan), 88% (13).

In 2019, studies demonstrated that both T and B cell-based immune system activities are required for HB vaccine antibody acquisition. Significant increases in follicular helper T cells, activated plasmablasts, and plasma cells were observed in individuals who acquired HB antibodies and first-time vaccinees. However, memory B cells and antibody-secreting cells were more abundant in individuals who maintained the HB antibodies than in those who did not. Vaccination records indicated that a higher HB antibody titer acquisition was associated with long-term HB antibody retention, with levels positively correlated with the levels of serum interferon gamma and related chemokines. A second booster vaccination resulted in significantly higher HBs antibody titers than the first series, with coordinated activation of follicular helper T and B cell lineages involved in the acquisition and maintenance of HB antibodies. These findings underscore the importance of the adjuvant effects of vaccines (6).

The AARs of the HB vaccines used in this study one month after vaccination completion were 93.1% for MeinyuⓇ (Meiji Dairies, Tokyo, Japan), 91.3% for BimmgenⓇ (Kaketuken-Fujisawa), and 85.3% for HEPTAVAXⓇ-II (MSD, Tokyo, Japan) (6).

The present study compared the AAR and antibody levels of a modified HB vaccine with a doubled aluminum-to-phosphate ratio of aluminum hydroxyphosphate sulfate and HEPTAVAXⓇ-II, in which a conventional adjuvant was used. The results revealed that both AAR and GMT of the modified HB vaccine were significantly higher than those of HEPTAVAXⓇ-II. In addition, previous studies on antibody acquisition after HB vaccination have typically focused on the AAR one month after the third vaccination.

However, in the present study, AARs were evaluated six months after the third vaccination and one year after the first vaccination, which are relatively long periods after vaccination. The rates were higher for the HB vaccine with adjuvant change than for the conventional vaccine. These findings suggest the effectiveness of adjuvant changes in maintaining antibodies for a relatively long period. This indicates that adjuvants play an important role in antibody acquisition, not only for the HB vaccine but also for other vaccines (14). The side effects of mpHBV were similar to those reported by Kishino, with no observed problems (5).

The newly developed HB vaccine HepB-CpG (HeplisavⓇ) uses cytidine-phosphate-guanosine oligodeoxynucleotide as an adjuvant to activate the immune system (7). HepB-CpG is recommended for adults ≥18 years old as a 2-dose series with a 1-month interval. It is important to note that HepB-CpG is a newer formulation, and the long-term safety and durability of immunologic memory have not yet been established (7,8,9).

Taurine, parotin, and lithium have been studied as orally available adjuvants (15). The National Institute of Infectious Diseases in Japan reported that intranasal vaccines containing aluminum salt adjuvants stimulated interleukin-33 secretion from alveolar epithelial cells and IgA antibody production through a type 2 immune response (16). These findings suggest that the new HB vaccine adjuvants may be effective in treating HB and that intranasal vaccines with aluminum salt adjuvants may be effective in treating HB. Thus, the combination of new HB vaccine adjuvants with various immune system components may create a more effective and safer vaccine. Another novel HB vaccine called PreHevbrioⓇ contains three antigens (pre-S1, pre-S2, and S) that target all known subtypes of HBV and are thought to stimulate antibody production against HBV, and the adjuvant used in this vaccine is a conventional aluminum salt (10,11).

Conclusion

The HB vaccine is still undergoing development, with a focus on exploring new adjuvants to enhance its efficacy. These adjuvant modifications are not only expected to significantly improve and simplify the manufacturing process of the HB vaccine but also enhance antibody acquisition rates and ensure long-term antibody maintenance. However, it is crucial to consider safety issues, such as allergies and potential side effects caused by the adjuvant itself. Therefore, modifications to the adjuvants should be performed with caution. Continued research and development in the field of vaccine adjuvants are essential for the future advancement of vaccines.

A Rapid Systematic Review of U.S. Food and Drug Administration–authorized COVID-19 Treatments
Authors: David J Sullivan, Massimo Franchini, Michael J Joyner, Arturo Casadevall, Daniele Focosi
Published: 2025

TO THE EDITOR—Maglione et al [1] recently published a rapid systematic review of U.S. Food and Drug Administration–authorized COVID-19 treatments. Supplementary Table 2 notes the thrombotic events among trial arm totals from the 7 studies, where individual risk ratios and 95% confidence intervals were not significant. However, the abstract erroneously noted limited certainty for thrombotic risk with COVID-19 Convalescent Plasma (CCP) instead of no evidence of risk as noted in text. There are methodologic errors in the CCP data abstraction and analysis for thrombosis, bleeding, and infection categories, also noted in the abstract.

Bar et al 2021 did not report thrombotic events in publication [2]. Bar, 2021 data from NCT04397757 lists thrombosis severe adverse event (SAE) with 1 in each group. A separate SAE list embolism with 1 in CCP and 0 in control (Table 1). Maglione et al summed the numerators for the published Supplementary Table 2. However, it is not correct to add the 2 lines as they may occur in the same individual as a biologic process where deep venous thrombosis occurs with pulmonary embolism. The authors should have listed each SAE separately rather than the simple numerator sum. Addition of separate line-entry SAE numerators is a frequent error in published Supplementary Table 2 [1], that applies also to Ortigoza et al, 2022 [5] for thrombosis and Begin et al, 2021 [3] for both bleeding and infection(Table 1).

Another type of error in Supplementary Table 2 is entering an incorrect number different from results posted on clinicaltrials.gov website. This occurred in the thrombosis section for Begin et al, 2021 and Self et al, 2022 [6], the bleeding section for Bar et al, 2021 and Begin et al, 2021 and the infection section for Ortigoza et al, 2022 and Self et al, 2022. In the bleeding SAE for Ortigoza et al, 2022, the group numerators were transposed (Table 1). The infection section of Supplementary Table 2 also listed very high numerators for Korley et al, 2021 [4] with 33 CCP events and 40 control events, whereas the NCT04355767 results note only 0 events in CCP and 1 in control. In the infection section, it is not clear why Sullivan et al, 2022 [7] with 0 events in each group was included in Maglione et al published as Supplementary Table 2.

Most clinical studies in the tables were randomized control trials (RCT). However, there was a particular emphasis in the abstract on the Thompson et al, 2021 [8] single propensity matched non-RCT to implicate infection and bleeding to CCP transfusion with moderate certainty. The text of the Thompson et al manuscript in addition to the CCP group’s higher bleeding and infection also noted more lung complications and congestive heart failure as well as more aggressive care requiring intensive care unit admission and mechanical ventilation. The percentage in the CCP group receiving corticosteroids remdesivir and tocilizumab was double the unmatched control group. In other words, in this non-RCT of patients with hematologic cancers, the CCP group was possibly more ill at baseline than unmatched controls. The authors should consider footnoting that they chose the unmatched controls rather than matched in this propensity analysis, which should be removed from abstract, discussed in the text, but not compared to RCT data.

The sum of outpatient monoclonals thrombotic events noted 3 in intervention and 4 in controls totaling all the studies with denominators near 4400 in monoclonal intervention and 2600 in controls. The sum of inpatient remdesivir in the 2 outpatient antiviral drug trials, were similar, with 6 thrombotic events in the intervention group and 7 in the control group with denominators near 2300. Neither of the 2 comparisons was significant for either the monoclonal antibodies or remdesivir regarding thrombotic events (Supplementary Table 1). The remdesivir study mixed outpatients and inpatients. Grouping all the CCP studies using the data from Supplementary Table 2 in Maglione et al, despite the 5 hospital studies and 2 outpatient studies, results in 49 thrombotic events in the CCP group of the 2609 total and 83 in the control group of 2964, which translates into a significantly lower risk for CCP relative to controls (relative risk of 0.67 with a P = .025 and 95% confidence interval of .473-.9510). Hence, our analysis of the same published data indicates fewer thrombotic events, contradicting the findings of the Maglione et al.

To summarize, the authors need to consider correcting the CCP data and other data, separating out different SAEs rather than adding numerators and revising the abstract to fit their data. Confusingly, after noting SAE in methods and in results, the authors in numerous instances revert to adverse events. Here, we performed a partial review of SAE noted in the abstract. Certainly, the statement for increased risk of thrombotic events in the CCP group is not based on data tabulated or even the more accurate data in Table 1.

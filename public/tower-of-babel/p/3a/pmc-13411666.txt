Associations of lifestyle factors and stress levels on abnormal body weight among Chinese adults: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Jiahao Jiang, Sha Ma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHealth issues arising from abnormal body weight represent a significant threat to the public health system in China. Investigating modifiable factors is of considerable importance. This study aimed to explore the associations between lifestyle factors, stress levels, and the prevalence of abnormal body weight (including malnutrition, overweight, and obesity) among Chinese adults.MethodsA corss-sectional anaysis was conducted using data from 9,966 participants. The statistical analyses included percentage calculations for prevalence estimation, Chi-square tests for group comparisions, and multiple logistic regression analysis to identifiy factors associated with abnormal body weight (focusing on malnutrition and overweight / obesity) after adjusting for confounding variables.ResultsThe overall prevalence of overweight and obesity was found to be 25.25%, while the prevalence of malnutrition was recorded at 12.20%. After adjusting for confounding variables, the odds of being overweight or obese were significantly higher in several demographic groups compared to their respective counterparts: individuals aged 41 to 65 years, those with elementary or junior high school education, married participants, individuals engaging in inadequate physical activity, those exhibiting mild-to-moderate or severe nicotine dependence, recent alcohol consumers, and participants experiencing no stress.ConclusionsLifestyle factors significantly connected with the prevalence of overweight or obesity among Chinese adults. Consequently, public health initiatives aimed at managing overweight and obesity should prioritize the development of comprehensive interventions that address the identified modifiable risk factors to improve population health outcomes.

Background

Abnormal body weight serves as one of the most accessible and convenient indicator for assessing health status. Conditions such as marasmus, overweight, and obesity are well-established modifiable risk factors in the pathogenesis of cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases, including hypertension and coronary heart disease [1], as well as endocrine disorders like diabetes mellitus and hyperlipidemia [2]. In China, the prevalence of abnormal body weight has significantly increased over recent decades. According to nationally representative data, 34.3% of Chinese adults are classified as overweight while 16.4% are considered obese [3], this indicates that more than half of the adult population now exceeds the normal weight range. Concurrently, marasmus remains a concern for a subset of individuals, affecting approximately 5.9% of adult women and 2.9% of adult men as reported in 2022 [4]. Abnormal weight conditions significantly elevate healthcare expenditures and disminish quality of life. The rising prevalence of obesity has been documented across various socioeconomic groups, impacting not only high-income nations [5, 6], but also middle-income countries such as Malaysia [7] and China [8], with emerging trends noted in low-income countries [9]. As a result, the increasing incidence of obesity-related chronic diseases has led to higher per capita medical expenditures, exacerbated the disease burden, compromised the population’s healthy life expectancy, and imposed substantial societal pressure. Consequently, this public health challenge has become a priority concern within China’s healthcare system.

Exploring the modifiable factors that contribute to marasmus, overweight, and obesity is of paramount importance, as such research can facilitate the formulation of targeted interventions and establish an empirical foundation for future weight management strategies. Previous research has demonstrated that weight management is influenced by a complex interplay of individual, community, and structural determinants [10]. Interventions aimed at enhancing parenting skills and fostering individual motivation have shown efficacy in promoting healthy lifestyle behaviors among adolescents [11]. Despite the extensive literature in this field, there remains a notable scarcity of comprehensive studies examining the combined effects of sociodemographic characteristics, behavioral and psychological factors, and family health dynamics on marasmus, overweight, and obesity. Therefore, this study aims to elucidate the interrelationships among these multifaceted factors and their associations with nutritional status, thereby providing robust empirical evidence to inform the design and implementation of culturally tailored weight management interventions for Chinese populations.

Methods

Participants

This study employed publicly available data from the 2021 Psychology and Behavior Investigation of Chinese Residents (PBICR), a population-based cross-sectional study implementing a multi-stage stratified sampling methodology. This research initiative was collaboratively conceived and conducted by Peking University in partnership with various academic institutions and research organizations across China. Detailed descriptions of the sampling procedures and survey methodologies have been documented previously [12]. For this analysis, a sample consisting of 9,966 participants aged 18 years or older was extracted from the 2021 PBICR dataset.

Measures

The primary outcome variable was Body Mass Index (BMI), calculated by using height and weight data obtained from the PBICR database [BMI = weight (kg) / height (m)²]. The independent variables extracted from the PBICR database encompassed several domains, including dietary patterns, physical activity levels, alcohol consumption habits, nicotine dependence, stress levels, family health status, social support systems, and health literacy. Covariates derived from the PBICR database included demographic characteristics and social determinants, such as gender, age group, blood type, educational attainment, household composition, income level, marital status, and cohabitation arrangements.

BMI

The Body Mass Index (BMI) was calculated as the ratio of an individual’s weight in kilograms to the square of their height in meters (kg/m²). Participants were classified based on BMI values into distinct categories: individuals with a BMI less than 18.5 kg/m² were identified as suffering from marasmus; those with BMIs ranging from 18.5 to 23.9 kg/m² were categorized as having normal weight; and participants with a BMI equal to or greater than 24.0 kg/m² were classified as overweight or obese. These classification thresholds adhere to the Chinese national standard WS/T 428–2013 [13].

Health literacy

Health literacy (HL) was evaluated using the 12-item Health Literacy Scale [14]. This instrument comprises 12 items, each rated on a four-point Likert scale: very easy (4 points), easy (3 points), difficult (2 points), and very difficult (1 point). The total HL index was calculated using a standardized formula ranging from 0 to 50, with higher scores indicating greater proficiency in health literacy. The calculation formula is as follows: HL index = (mean − 1) × (50 / 3), where the mean represents the average score across all items for each participant. In this equation, the value of 1 corresponds to the minimum possible mean score, which results in an index value of 0. Conversely, the value of 3 reflects the difference between the maximum possible mean score of 4 and its minimum counterpart of 1. Additionally, a maximum achievable index value is set at 50. For this study, a cut-off score of 32.84 was established: individuals scoring above this threshold were classified as possessing high health literacy, while those scoring below were categorized as having low health literacy. The Health Literacy Scale demonstrated excellent internal consistency reliability (Cronbach’s α = 0.94) and construct validity as indicated by a Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin measure of 0.96.

Dietary behaviors

Dietary behaviors were assessed using the Chinese version of the Sakata Eating Behavior Scale short form (EBS-SF), which has been validated within the Chinese population [15]. This standardized 7-item scale encompasses several dimensions, including cognitive concerns regarding constitution, motivation for eating, feelings of satiety, abnormalities in eating rhythm, meal composition, substitute food and beverage consumption, and eating style. Each item was evaluated using a four-point Likert scale, with responses ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 4 (strongly agree). The composite score was calculated by summing the responses across all seven items, where higher scores indicate more maladaptive dietary behaviors. The dietary habits scale demonstrated excellent internal consistency reliability (Cronbach’s α = 0.88) and construct validity, as evidenced by a Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin measure of 0.91.

Stress

The present study utilized the stress scale developed by the PBICR research group [16], which consists of three items: (1) an assessment of stress management capability; (2) an evaluation of perceived stress levels over the preceding two-week period, encompassing both family and work domains; and (3) a rating of overall stress experience during the past year, including contexts related to family and work. Each component was rated on a 6-point Likert-type scale ranging from 1 (no stress) to 6 (extreme stress). Total stress scores were computed by summing individual item responses, with higher scores indicating greater levels of perceived stress. The stress scale demonstrated excellent psychometric properties, exhibiting high internal consistency reliability (Cronbach’s α = 0.96) and adequate construct validity as indicated by a Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin measure of 0.72.

Family health

The Family Health Scale comprises 10 items, with items 6, 9, and 10 necessitating reverse scoring [17]. For the remaining seven items, responses were evaluated using a five-point Likert scale: strongly disagree (1 point), somewhat disagree (2 points), neither agree nor disagree (3 points), somewhat agree (4 points), and strongly agree (5 points). The total family health score was calculated by summing all 10 items, with higher scores indicating superior family health functioning. The Family Health Scale exhibited adequate internal consistency reliability (Cronbach’s α = 0.77) and validity as indicated by the Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin measure (KMO = 0.90).

Perceived social support

The Chinese version of the Perceived Social Support Scale (PSSS) was employed to evaleate social support [18], encompassing three dimensions: family support, friend support, and other forms of support across 12 items. Each item was assessed using a 7-point Likert scale, resulting in a total score that ranges from 0 to 72; higher scores indicate greater perceived social support. The mean social support score within this sample was found to be 49.86. Participants were categorized into two groups based on their scores: those scoring below the mean were classified as having low social support, while those scoring above the mean were categorized as having high social support. The Perceived Social Support Scale demonstrated excellent psychometric properties, demonstrating high internal consistency reliability (Cronbach’s α = 0.96) and robust construct validity (Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin measure = 0.96).

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics utilizing percentages were employed to analyze the distribution of sociodemographic characteristics, behavioral patterns, and psychological factors. Subsequently, a Chi-square test was conducted to examine the associations between demographic variables, lifestyle factors, stress levels, and the prevalence of overweight or obesity. Furthermore, a multinomial logistic regression analysis was performed to investigate the independent effects of dietary habits, physical activity, and stress on outcomes related to marasmus, overweight, and obesity outcomes. Model fit was evaluated using the negative two log-likelihood criterion. Additionally, the internal consistency reliability and construct validity of the health literacy, scale as well as those for dietary behavior, stress management, family health dynamics, and perceived social support were assessed using Cronbach’s alpha coefficient and Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin measure, respectively.

Results

Socio-demographic characteristics of participants

The present study involved a total of 9966 participants aged 18 years and older, consisting of 5375 females (53.9%) and 4591 males (46.1%). Regarding age distribution, 53.50% of respondents were within the age brackets of 18 to 40 years, while 37.72% fell into the range of 41 to 65 years, the remaining participants (8.78%) were aged 66 years or older. Among those surveyed, blood type B was reported by 22.09%, and a significant majority (77.53%) had attained at least a technical secondary education level. Additionally, non-agricultural residents comprised 58.18% of the sample, with an approximately 38.98% reporing a monthly per capita income between RMB 3,001 to RMB 6,000. Furthermore, married individuals represented a substantial portion at 62.47%, while cohabitation with family members or freinds was reported by an impressive rate of 87.89%. A notable majority (66.48%) demonstrated low health literacy levels, conversely, positive dietary practices were noted in about 65.88% of participants surveyed. It is important to highlight that over half (54.0%) did not engage in regular physical activity and that nicotine dependence was absent in an overwhelming majority (86.14%). Moreover, alcohol abstinence was observed in approximately 57.89% of participants; around half (53.44%) experienced mild stress alongside low social support levelsas indicated by rates of 53.55%. Statistical analysis demonstrated a significantly higher prevalence of overweight or obesity across various demographics groups: males (32.65%), individuals aged 41 to 65 years (35.49%), those with blood type B (26.99%), high school graduates (35.7%), participants with a per capita family income ranging from 3001 to 6000 RMB (26.49%), cohabiting partners (25.82%), individuals reporting poor dietary habits (28.07%), recent alcohol consumers within the past 30 days (33.57%), those with elevated family health levels (27.05%), and participants receiving increased social support (25.88%).

Conversely, the findings indicated an increased susceptibility to marasmus, among specific demographic subgroups: females (16.07%), individuals aged 18 to 40 years (16.65%), those without formal education (18.9%), agricultural hukou holders (13.08%), unmarried individuals (19.14%), non-nicotine dependent participants (12.85%), those experiencing high stress levels (12.86%), and individuals lacking adequate family health support systems (12.98%) (Table 2).

Multivariate logistic analysis of marasmus, overweight, and obesity

Table 3 presents the odds ratios (ORs) that illustrate the associations between sociodemographic characteristics, behavioral and psychological traits, and the outcomes of marasmus, overweight, and obesity. Compared to participants with normal body mass index (BMI raging from 18.5 to 24), females demonstrated a significantly higher risk of marasmus compared to males (OR = 2.02, 95%CI: 1.74–2.36, p < 0.001). Furthermore, individuals who are illiteracy exhibited a 1.65-fold increased likelihood of experiencing marasmus compared to those with a college education or higher (OR = 1.65, 95%CI: 1.18–2.31, p < 0.001). Participants exhibiting moderate to severe nicotine dependence showed an increased likelihood of experiencing marasmus by a factor of 1.82 times when compared to non-nicotine dependent participants (OR = 1.82, 95%CI: 1.23–2.69, p < 0.001).

Furthermore, married participants exhibited a significantly lower prevalence of overweight or obesity than their unmarried counterparts (OR = 0.59, 95%CI: 0.51–0.69, p < 0.001).

In comparison to individuals with a normal BMI, participants aged 41 to 65 years showed a prevalence of overweight or obesity that was 1.28 times higher than those aged 66 years and older (OR = 1.28, 95%CI: 1.06–1.55, p = 0.01). The prevalence of overweight or obesity among participants with elementary education was found to be 1.36 times greater than that among those with technical secondary school education or higher (OR = 1.36, 95%CI: 1.11–1.68, p < 0.001). Similarly, the prevalence of overweight or obesity in participants who had completed junior high school education was observed to be 1.37 times higher compared to their counterparts with technical secondary education or higher (OR = 1.37, 95%CI: 1.17–1.59, p < 0.001). Furthermore, the likelihood of being overweight or obese among participants from non-agriculture households was found to be elevated by a factor of approximately 1.25 times higher when compared to those from agricultural households (OR = 1.25, 95%CI: 1.12–1.39, p < 0.001). Individuals in marital relationships exhibited a likelihood of overweight or obesity that was 40% greater than that observed in unmarried individuals (OR = 1.40, 95%CI: 1.23–1.60, p < 0.001). Participants lacking regular physical activity had a 1.19 times higher likelihood of overweight or obesity compared to their counterparts who engage in regular physical activity (OR = 1.19, 95%CI: 1.08–1.32, p < 0.001).

Respondents exhibiting mild nicotine dependence showed a 1.25-fold increased likelihood of being overweight or obese compared to those without nicotine dependence (OR = 1.25, 95%CI: 1.06–1.47, p = 0.01). Conversely, participants with moderate-to-severe nicotine dependence demonstrated a 1.56-fold higher likelihood of overweight or obesity relative to their non-nicotine-dependent counterparts (OR = 1.56, 95%CI: 1.23–1.97, p < 0.001).

Compared with participants experiencing high levels of stress, those with no stress and those with low stress exhibited 1.22-fold and 1.17-fold higher likelihood of overweight or obesity, respectively (no stress: OR = 1.22, 95%CI: 1.05–1.41, p = 0.01; low stress: OR = 1.17, 95%CI: 1.03–1.32, p = 0.02). Furthermore, females exhibited a significantly lower likelihood of developing overweight or obesity compared to males (OR = 0.61, 95%CI: 0.55 to 0.68, p < 0.001).

Participants aged 18 to 40 years were found to be 0.73 times less likely to develop overweight or obesity compared to those aged 66 years and older (OR = 0.73, 95%CI: 0.60–0.90, p < 0.001). Additionally, participants with blood type A demonstrated a lower likelihood of developing overweight or obesity than individuals in the bunknown lood type group (OR = 0.85, 95%CI: 0.73–0.98, p = 0.03).

Participants exhibiting a low risk of unhealthy dietary habits demonstrated a significantly lower prevalence of overweight or obesity compared to their counterparts with high-risk unhealthy dietary habits (OR = 0.51, 95%CI: 0.45 to 0.57, p < 0.001). Disscussion

Both marasmus and overweight or obesity represent significant risk factors for a wide range of chronic diseases. Therefore, the early identification of these risk factors provides substantial scientific evidence to support the development of evidence-based public health interventions aimed at weight management. The findings of this study indicate that nearly one quarter of Chinese adults are categorized as overweight or obese, while 12.20% of the participants experienced marasmus. This observation underscores the necessity of prioritizing weight management as a fundamental element in the prevention of chronic diseases, which consequently warrants increased attention within public health practices.

In accordance with the hypothesis that the adoption of a healthy lifestyle is correlated with a decreased incidence of marasmus, overweight, and obesity, while an unhealthy lifestyle increases the risk of these conditions, it was found that participants exhibiting moderate or severe nicotine dependence had a significantly higher prevalence of marasmus compared to their counterparties without such dependence. Furthermore, prolonged nicotine dependence presents a substantial barrier for individuals attempting to quit tobacco through self-directed method alone. Therefore, it is imperative to prioritize the acquisition of external support to systematically address an individual’s nicotine addiction. It is essential to recognize that nicotine dependence not only heightens the risk of marasmus but also exacerbates the likelihood of overweight or obesity. Additionally, high-risk dietary patterns, alcohol consumption, and a sedentary lifestyle can significantly increase the risk of overweight or obesity. This evidence indicates that weight management interventions should not depend solely on a singular behavioral approach; rather, they should focus on fostering a comprehensive array of healthy habits, which include cessation of tobacco and alcohol use as well as maintaining regular physical activity and adhering to a balanced diet.

Consistent with previous research [19], this study indicates that females exhibit a lower prevalence of overweight or obesity compared to males, while also demonstrating a higher prevalence of marasmus among females than their male counterparts. This phenomenon can be attributed to societal ideal that associate thinness with beauty, motivating females to strive for lower body weight. Furthermore, this study revealed that weight management strategies varied by genders: for males, the emphasis was on reducing caloric intake and increasing physical activity, while for females, the focus was on achieving nutritional balance and engaging in exercise to maintain a healthy weight [20]. Our analysis indicated that, a lack of formal education was associated with a higher prevalence of marasmus. However, this cross-sectional association should be interpreted with considerable caution. Low educational attainment is closely linked with numerous unmeasured confounders, including but not limited to life-course socioeconomic disadvantage, historical nutritional deprivation, and limited health literacy. The present data do not allow us to disentangle the independent effect of education from these deeply embedded factors. Therefore, this observation warrants further investigation using longitudinal designs that can more effectively elucidate the association.”

In the present study, the association between marital status and abnormal body weight was found to vary by weight category. Compared to individuals who are not in a marital relationship, those who are married exhibited a significantly lower risk of marasmus but a notably higher risk of overweight or obesity.The protective effect of marriage against marasmus may be attributed to more regular meal patterns and improved food security [21].Conversely, the increased likelihood of overweight or obesity among married adults may reflect reduced efforts and reduced exercise [22]. However, this study did not identify a significant association between income levels and overweight or obesity, which contrasts with findings from previous research [23] conducted among women of reproductive age that indicated an elevated risk of being overweight or obese among females from middle- or upper-income households.

In contrast to previous findings suggesting that high stress constitutes a risk factor for obesity [24], this study did not find a significant association between stress and overweight or obesity. Notably, participants who reported low levels of stress or no stress exhibited a higher prevalence of overweight or obesity. This phenomenon may be explained by the observation that individuals experiencing minimal or absent stress were more inclined to maintain a positive mental state, which in turn enableed them to allocate their time and energy effectively towards nutritional intake and overall health. Nevertheless, this positive mental state and effective resource allocation could also result in excessive nutrient intake and subsequent weight gain.

Recent research has revealed that moderate obesity among the oldest-old adults, specifically those aged over 80 years, is associated with the lowest morality rates [25]. In contrast, there remains insufficient evidence to support the idea that moderate overweight or obesity provides benefits for younger individuals. Consequently, the authors of this study argue that even young individuals experiencing minimal or no stress should prioritize weight management to alleviate potential health risks.

Conclusion

This study examines the impact of behavioral lifestyle factors on marasmus, overweight, and obesity. The findings indicate that significant social determinants, such as economic status and marital status, have a considerable effect on weight status. Furthermore, unhealthy behaviors including high-risk dietary patterns, insufficient physical activity, moderate-to-severe nicotine dependence, and alcohol consumption, are significantly correlated with overweight and obesity.

Future public health intervention programs aimed at reducing the prevalence of marasmus, overweight, and obesity should prioritize the development and implementation of comprehensive strategies that simultaneously address multiple lifestyle-related behaviors.

Limitations

Despite the emergence of new findings, this study is not without inherent limitations. Owing to its cross-sectional design, it provides only limited insights into the relationship between lifestyle factors and weight-related conditions such as overweight or marasmus. Furthermore, the cross-sectional nature of the study limits its capacity to establish causal relationships among the variables under investigation. Lastly, data were collected through self-reported measures, which may introduce a degree of response bias.

Directions for further research

The potential for conducting cohort studies to further investigate the causal relationships between dietary patterns, physical activity, nicotine dependence, and alcohol consumption in relation to marasmus, overweight, and obesity is significant. Furthermore, more precise and comprehensive assessments of lifestyle factors can provide robust evidence to support the development of effective evidence-based interventions.

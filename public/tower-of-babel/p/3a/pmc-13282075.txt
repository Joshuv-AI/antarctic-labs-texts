Dual-targeted therapy with risankizumab and upadacitinib in medically complex Crohn’s disease
Authors: Evan N Fear, Benjamin D McDonald, Megan S Kennedy, Alex J Mathew, Ross H McMillan, Zachary D Fine, Alexandra R McDermott, David Choi, Amelia Kellar, David T Rubin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThere are significant unmet needs for the treatment of Crohn’s disease (CD). We have previously described a novel dual-targeted therapy (DTT) of the interleukin-12/23 (IL-12/23) inhibitor ustekinumab and selective Janus kinase 1 inhibitor upadacitinib (UPA). Here, we evaluate the safety and effectiveness of the IL-23 inhibitor risankizumab (RISA) combined with UPA in patients with CD.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed data from our prospectively collected real-world database of patients with CD at the [Institution Name] treated with the combination of RISA and UPA from November 05, 2021 to September 18, 2024. Clinical response and remission, extraintestinal manifestation outcomes, and adverse events were evaluated in all patients, including ileostomy patients.ResultsNineteen patients with CD were treated with RISA and UPA (median age 38 years [interquartile range, IQR 33.5-43.5], 13/19 [68%] female, median disease duration at DTT initiation 14.1 years [IQR 10.9-22.8], 14/19 [73.7%] had prior inflammatory bowel disease [IBD] surgeries, 5/19 [26.3%] had ileostomies). At DTT initiation, most patients had been treated previously with more than 4 prior advanced therapies (median 5, IQR 4-6). Among patients with clinically active disease at baseline, 5/7 (71.4%) achieved clinical remission and 2/7 (28.6%) achieved clinical response without remission. All 4 ileostomy patients with active disease responded, and 2 achieved clinical remission. Eight patients had active EIMs at baseline; 7/8 (87.5%) improved, with 4 achieving resolution. Three patients discontinued DTT due to adverse events (acne [1], fatigue [1], and upper respiratory infection [1]).ConclusionDTT with RISA and UPA is effective and safe in medically resistant CD.Keywords: Crohn’s disease, risankizumab, upadacitinib, dual-targeted therapy

What is already known?

Risankizumab (RISA) and upadacitinib (UPA) are approved as monotherapies for the treatment of moderately to severely active Crohn’s disease (CD). Combination therapy approaches have been proposed as 1 way to break through the well-described therapeutic ceiling in CD.

What is new here?

This is the first case series describing the effectiveness and safety of dual target therapy with RISA and UPA in medically refractory CD.

How can this study help patient care?

Our study provides evidence that this novel dual target therapy may be an effective tool for managing specific cases of medically complex CD and provides the basis for larger prospective evaluations of this treatment strategy.

Introduction

Crohn’s disease (CD) is a chronic inflammatory disease affecting the gastrointestinal tract with frequent occurrences of extraintestinal manifestations (EIMs).1 There is an acknowledged treatment gap in CD resulting from a lack of predictive biomarkers combined with a large degree of primary non-response, non-remission, or loss of response to advanced therapies. A large proportion (30%-40%)2 of patients with CD may be refractory to multiple advanced therapies, leading to poor disease outcomes including repeated surgery, diminished quality of life, and disability. It is certainly an ongoing priority to develop novel treatments for medically resistant patients,3 but novel combinations and phased approaches with existing therapies offer additional possibilities. To this end, dual-targeted therapy (DTT) in CD is a fairly recently described treatment strategy that combines different mechanisms of action to target multiple inflammatory pathways.4–7

Risankizumab (RISA) is a high-affinity humanized immunoglobulin G1 monoclonal antibody that selectively binds to the p19 subunit of the interleukin-23 (IL-23) cytokine and inhibits its interaction with the IL-23 receptor, affecting the Janus kinase 2 (JAK2) and tyrosine kinase 2 pathways, indicated for CD, ulcerative colitis (UC), psoriatic arthritis (PsA), and plaque psoriasis.8–10 Upadacitinib (UPA) is a selective JAK1 inhibitor with downstream inhibition of IL-6 and IL-11 and is indicated for CD, UC, PsA, rheumatoid arthritis, axial spondyloarthritis, and atopic dermatitis.11–13 There have not been reports of DTT with this combination in CD. Here, we report our experience with DTT of RISA and UPA in patients with CD.

Methods

Patient selection

The University of Chicago maintains an IRB-approved real-world prospective database of patients with inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) who are being treated with advanced therapies. We used this database to perform a retrospective review of patients with CD at the (Institution Name) treated with combination RISA and UPA between November 05, 2021 and September 18, 2024.

Data collection and outcome measures

The Harvey–Bradshaw index (HBI) was used to assess clinical activity for patients without an ileostomy, with clinical remission defined as HBI ≤4 and clinical response defined as a decrease of 3 or more from baseline. Fecal calprotectin (FCP) and C-reactive protein (CRP) were used to assess biochemical disease activity, with remission defined as <150 μg/g (FCP) or <5 mg/L (CRP). Endoscopic remission was defined as Rutgeerts’ score ≤i2a or endoscopists’ assessment of remission, including no findings of ulceration, erosions, strictures, erythema, abscess, or fistula. Intestinal ultrasound (IUS) remission was defined as bowel wall thickness (BWT) <3.0 mm for the ileum and colon, BWT <4.0 mm for the rectum, and no presence of hyperemia, mesenteric fat wrapping, lymphadenopathy, or strictures. Endoscopic response, IUS response, EIM outcomes, and adverse events for all patients were evaluated by physician assessment. For ileostomy patients, clinical response (defined as symptom and/or biomarker improvement) and remission (return to baseline bowel function combined with normalization of biomarkers) were used.14 Baseline data were collected within 1 month of DTT initiation for clinical and biochemical outcomes, 3 months for IUS, and 6 months for endoscopic outcomes. The “time to response” metric was determined by the first event of clinical response or biochemical remission.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were expressed as median and interquartile range (IQR). Categorical variables were presented as frequency and percentage.

Ethical considerations

This case series was approved by the (Institution Name) Institutional Review Board. All patients signed an informed consent form prior to inclusion. Data were stored and analyzed in IRB-approved platforms.

Discussion

Our study provides evidence that this novel DTT may be an effective tool for managing specific cases of refractory CD and provides the basis for larger prospective evaluations of this treatment strategy.

In our cohort, approximately 80% of patients with active CD were in clinical remission after DTT, with a median follow-up of approximately 1 year despite multiple prior exposures to biologics and surgical histories. A systematic review of phase 3 randomized clinical trials for CD biologics (infliximab, adalimumab, vedolizumab, and ustekinumab) showed net clinical remission rates at 1 year ranging from 6.7%-34.8% based on a CD activity index <150.2 While comparisons are limited due to differing definitions of clinical remission and numbers of included patients, DTT of different inflammatory cascades may demonstrate a synergistic effect for the treatment of CD in select cases.

For example, 1 recent study used a transcriptomics dataset from patients with IBD to comprehensively identify immune pathways that fail to respond to anti-tumor necrosis factor (TNF) therapy in primary anti-TNF non-responders compared to those who did respond to anti-TNF therapy.15 The investigators then demonstrated that both RISA and UPA monotherapies act to beneficially modulate many of these same pathways, effectively circumventing the primary non-response to anti-TNF therapy. Notably, RISA and UPA each modulate semi-overlapping but distinct subsets of these pathways. Thus, in primary anti-TNF non-responders who have also had inadequate response with RISA or UPA alone, we hypothesize that combining these therapies may activate complementary sets of immune pathways, improving overall response.

A recent systematic review and meta-analysis of 279 patients with IBD treated with DTT demonstrated that pooled rates of clinical remission were 59%, with 6.5% experiencing serious adverse events. Although approximately 50% of patients were treated with anti-TNF and anti-integrin therapy in that study, there has been no consensus on which combination is more beneficial in DTT.5 The long-term safety profile of DTT is limited; a multi-center observation study of DTT (anti-TNF + anti-integrin, anti-TNF + anti-IL, anti-IL + anti-integrin, Jaki + anti-TNF, Jaki + anti-integrin) showed that 10% of patients with IBD had serious and/or opportunistic infections, with half leading to hospitalization that self-resolved.16 Both RISA and UPA have acceptable safety profiles as monotherapies.9,12 In our study, there were 3 cases of upper respiratory infections without the need for hospitalization and 4 cases of acne previously associated with UPA use.17,18 Our safety results presented here for UPA + RISA DTT are similar and consistent with our results from our prior UPA + UST report.19 Larger follow-up studies with direct comparisons to monotherapy will be required to determine whether this constitutes risk in excess of that conferred by each therapy alone.

Limitations of this work include our retrospective single-center study design based on a small number of patients with heterogeneous indications and outcomes. Additionally, the follow-up period was not sufficient to determine the long-term safety profile, especially related to malignancy. These shortcomings mean that we were unable to directly compare our results to monotherapies or other therapeutic options, and that our results may not be as generalizable to other populations or as precise as a larger study. However, we argue that these data nevertheless provide real-world proof-of-principle for the creative administration of DTTs in general, as well as the safety and efficacy of UPA + RISA in particular.

In the future, large prospective trials can compare the effectiveness of various DTT combinations to aid in forming a consensus on their use. At our single center, the combinations of ustekinumab + UPA and RISA + UPA both had clinical remission rates of approximately 80% in patients who had been largely refractory to diverse monotherapies.19 Further studies of RISA and UPA are required to assess which therapy should be initiated first, the optimal dosage and interval, and the consideration and timing of de-escalation of one or both treatments after remission is achieved.

Conclusion

This is the first case series demonstrating that the combination of RISA and UPA was overall effective and well tolerated in patients with medically complex CD, including those with EIMs. Although there is much more to learn regarding the effectiveness, long-term safety, and optimal strategy for DTT use in CD, these results hint at the expansive possibilities that could be achieved by combination or phased therapies. By continuing to explore these options, both in larger clinical studies and in molecular work that characterizes potential synergistic mechanisms, we may expand the arsenal of therapeutic options for refractory CD and narrow the CD treatment gap.

Konno procedure and Bentall for complicated aortic root replacement with calcified ventricular septal defect patch

Central Message

We believe this case illustrates the complementary nature of adult congenital heart disease and aortic programs and how patients often benefit from strong programmatic collaboration.

The Konno operation has been described as surgical treatment for patients with complex multilevel left ventricular outflow tract obstruction. We report a case of a Konno procedure performed in combination with a mechanical Bentall. The patient is a 45-year-old man with a history of double outlet right ventricle, bicuspid aortic valve (BAV), and coarctation of the aorta with surgical history significant for repair of coarctation of the aorta with subclavian flap and pulmonary artery band via left thoracotomy, followed by complete repair of double outlet right ventricle with autologous pericardium, pulmonary artery band takedown, and resection of subpulmonic stenosis. He now presents with severe symptomatic aortic regurgitation, an extremely calcified ventricular septal defect (VSD) patch, and a 4.9-cm aortic root aneurysm. Ross-Konno procedure was not performed largely because the patient desired a mechanical valve and the calcified patch was extremely close to the pulmonary valve annulus, which would have made autograft harvest potentially hazardous. Biologic aortic valve replacement was not considered because the patient is so young and the entire care team (including the patient) is motivated to limit future reintervention.

Case

This case had several notable findings. The aortic root was significantly aneurysmal. There was an anomalous aortic origin of the right coronary artery (RCA) that arose from the left coronary sinus near the left-right fused raphe of the BAV. There was a Sievers 1 BAV that was heavily calcified. The VSD patch was heavily calcified and the calcification extended onto the aortic annulus, which precluded placement of sutures for valve replacement. The operative plan was for mechanical Bentall, takedown of VSD patch with calcium debridement, and Konno-type interventricular patch reconstruction.

In the operating room, it was clear that the RCA needed to be extensively mobilized away from the infundibulum of the right ventricular outflow tract (RVOT) to safely perform a Konno procedure. The VSD patch was excised and the edges were extensively debrided. Once the patch had been completely excised, a large polyethylene terephthalate patch was fashioned to reconstruct the ventricular septum and enlarge the aortic annulus. The newly constructed aortic annulus was sized for a 33-mm mechanical valved conduit. This was implanted in the typical fashion for a Konno procedure. The remainder of the VSD patch was then folded over to close the RVOT incision and sealed with Preveleak (Baxter Advanced Surgery). The left coronary button and distal aortic anastomoses to the midascending aorta were performed in standard fashion. The heart and Bentall graft were then allowed to fill and the position of the right coronary button was very carefully selected to avoid kinking. The patient was weaned off cardiopulmonary bypass.

Postoperatively, the mean gradient through the mechanical valve was 4 mm Hg. No residual VSD was observed. The left ventricular outflow tract was widely patent. The patient awoke neurologically intact and had a routine, uneventful postoperative course. He was discharged home on postoperative day 8 and has done well in the outpatient setting. The patient is 18 months out from surgery and has been doing remarkably well. The most recent transthoracic echocardiogram showed ejection fraction of 49%, compared with 6 months prior when it was at 39%. Overall, the patient reports feeling quite well and has been very diligent with all follow-up and appointments.

Conclusions

This case highlights several important features of a complex patient with adult congenital heart disease (ACHD) presenting with aortic valve disease and a profoundly calcified VSD patch (Video 1). This illustrates the clinical overlap between congenital and complex aortic surgery for complex adult patients. This case in particular required multidisciplinary surgical expertise to manage an overriding anomalous aortic origin of the RCA along with a Konno procedure to reconstruct the heavily calcified VSD patch. We believe this illustrates the complementary nature of ACHD and aortic programs and how patients often benefit from strong programmatic collaboration. These patients often require slightly creative solutions for complex presentations that utilize the complementary skillsets of congenital ACHD and aortic surgeons.

Design and modeling of eye-shaped double-slot GaAs microring resonator for CO2 sensing
Authors: Faezeh Pakfetrat, Mahdi Bahadoran, Shadab Dabagh, Francesco Chiavaioli
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractMonitoring of CO2 is crucial because of its profound impact on both environmental and human health. A novel highly sensitive refractive index (RI) sensor, utilizing a double-slot microring resonating structure, has been designed and numerically assessed for the sensitive detection of gas media. The structure consisted of a circular microring resonator nested in a racetrack resonating configuration mimicking the structure of an eye-shaped microring resonator (ESMRR). This system was simulated and designed for a GaAs double-slot core waveguide deposited on a suitable Al0.5Ga0.5As substrate. Optical transfer function of ESMRR and related equations were derived using Mason rule, while the numerical analysis was performed using the variational finite difference time domain (var- FDTD) method. The free spectral range (FSR) was extended to 137.68 nm that led to a remarkable bulk sensitivity of 1217.39 nm/RIU and a resolution of 4.93 × 10−6 RIU. The proposed sensing structure was envisioned for CO2 sensing and demonstrated an impressive sensitivity of 24.4 pm/ppm for CO2 detection with an estimated detection limit of 0.82 ppm.Keywords: Microring resonator, Gas sensor, Double-slot waveguide, VarFDTD method, Monitoring CO2 levels, CO2 detectionSubject terms: Micro-optics, Photonic devices

Introduction

Integrated photonic Sensors. are critically important across several domains owing to their capacity to precisely and effectively detect and quantify physical, chemical, and biological data1–3. Microring resonators (MRRs) possess numerous advantages over traditional optical devices, such as compatibility with photonic integrated circuits, economical fabrication methods, potential for micro-scale production, and capability to generate narrow resonant peaks across a large free spectral range (FSR). Owing to these characteristics, MRRs exhibit significant applicability across various domains, including life sciences for real-time blood pressure4 and glucose level monitoring5optical communication systems for optical filters6 and switches7as well as optical Sensors. utilized in environmental monitoring8industrial applications9and biomedical and chemical sensing8,10. In sensing technology, micro-ring resonators (MRRs) are esteemed for their tiny dimensions, minimal power consumption, elevated Q-factor, and exceptional sensitivity. They provide label-free detection of gas and have distinct benefits, including rapid reaction times and accurate control over the wavelength and bandwidth of output signals, making them suitable for sensing applications11. When light is coupled into each ring resonator and fulfills the resonance condition, it can propagate through each ring and produce constructive interference. The resonance condition is highly tunable by the ring length, light wavelength, geometric parameters, and the medium’s refractive index12,13. By tuning the resonant mode numbers between the rings and regulating the light coupling through the couplers, the ring-based system can achieve subwavelength light confinement and dynamically shift resonances in response to environmental variations. Such functionality is ideal for integrated photonic circuits and photothermal therapy applications14.

Carbon dioxide (CO2), a trace element of the atmosphere, exacerbates persistent global warming via the greenhouse effect1. Precise measurement of atmospheric CO2 concentrations is crucial for investigating global warming and other environmental challenges resulting from anthropogenic greenhouse gas emissions15. Monitoring CO2 levels is crucial because of its substantial impact on the environment and human health. CO2 gas concentrations above 5000 ppm may be detrimental to human health, resulting in symptoms such as drowsiness, nausea, headaches, exhaustion, elevated heart rate, and respiratory issues15. Monitoring CO2 levels is essential for sustaining optimum indoor air quality, improving productivity, and safeguarding human health in many settings, including homes, schools, businesses, and healthcare institutions11. CO2, being a major greenhouse gas, has a crucial role in controlling Earth’s climate, weather events, temperature trends, and the general equilibrium of ecosystems11. There is an increasing need for miniaturized, cost-effective, highly sensitive, and dependable CO2 gas Sensors. capable of successfully monitoring green- house gas concentration trends over broad sensor networks15.

Nowadays, various types of optical-based gas Sensors., including sub-wavelength grating (SWG) slot waveguide16volatile organic compound (VOC) gas Sensors17,18. polymer-coated meta-surface waveguide19silicon ring resonator20semiconductors21laser diodes22solid electrolytes23Mach-Zehnder Sensors11. optical fiber Sensors24. photonics crystal-based sensor25–30 and non-dispersive infrared (NDIR) detectors1have been developed for monitoring and detecting CO2 concentration. Among these Sensors., the refractive index (RI)-based Sensors. leverage high accuracy and sensitivity to detect any tiny variation in gas concentration through changes in the optical features of resonances. The numerical study focused on designing a CO2 gas sensor utilizing a SWG slot-waveguide, resulting in a sensitivity of 12.9 pm/ppm16. A polymer-coated meta-surface waveguide made up of poly-hexamethylene biguanide (PHMB) was used as a perfect absorber for CO2 detection over the concentration range of 0-524 ppm and the configuration achieved a sensitivity of 17.3 pm/ppm19. The sensor’s sensitivity, utilizing the same PHMP polymer within a Mach-Zehnder sensing setup, was measured to be 74.78 pm/ppm, 34.46 pm/ppm, and 7.63 pm/ppm for sensing arm lengths of 15 μm, 10 μm, and 5 μm, respectively11. An alternative design for a fiber-optic CO2 sensor utilized a multimode silica glass fiber probe, coated with a hybrid xerogel layer, and paired with a bifurcated optical fiber for enhanced functionality. This setup exhibited a sensitivity of around 18 pm/ppm and had a linear response to CO2 concentrations between 0 and 30%24. At ambient temperature and atmospheric pressure, a photonic crystal sensor demonstrated a linear response to CO2 concentration, with a sensitivity of 4.3 pm/% CO225,31. The gadget exhibited a wavelength stability of 1 pm and a resolution of 0.2% CO2. NDIR Sensors. are often used to measure ambient CO2 concentrations because of their stability and robust resistance to influence from other air constituents, including contaminants. Moreover, NDIR Sensors. exhibit exceptional durability, making them the most dependable choice for ambient CO2 measurement32.

A silicon ring resonator including a PHMB functional layer applied to the waveguide structure was created for the detection of gas concentrations reaching 500 ppm, with a detection threshold of 20 ppm33. A single-slot hybrid micro-ring resonator was presented as a hydrogen gas sensor, with a sensitivity of 11.038 nm/%34. Gallium arsenide (GaAs) was often used in the fabrication of waveguides and resonators in optoelectronic devices owing to its compatibility with other materials. The GaAs-AlGaAs waveguides shown superior performance in the advancement of chemical and biological Sensors. within the near-infrared wavelength range of 0.9–25 μm. In comparison to silicon and silicon nitride, GaAs has several benefits, including a superior refractive index, resulting in enhanced light confinement and increased optical efficiency35. Additionally, GaAs encompasses a wider spectrum, rendering it appropriate for telecommunications and optoelectronic applications36. Furthermore, electrons in GaAs exhibit greater mobility than those in silicon and silicon nitride, leading to enhanced response times of the device36. The GaAs/AlGaAs layers are preferably grown using Molecular Beam Epitaxy (MBE) due to its ability to produce atomically smooth interfaces with precise control over thickness and composition. This technique ensures high optical quality by minimizing impurities and lattice imperfections. Nonetheless, challenges such as interface roughness, strain accumulation in multilayers, and oxidation of high-Al-content layers require careful optimization of growth parameters. Maintaining ultra-high vacuum conditions and accurate flux control is essential to preserve interface integrity and achieve high-Q photonic structures37. There are many experimental studies demonstrating the realization of GaAs/AlGaAs waveguides38. Using an Al₀.₅Ga₀.₅As substrate with a GaAs waveguide creates a refractive index contrast. This contrast supports mode confinement, improving sensitivity and reducing bending losses in microring resonators. Al₀.₅Ga₀.₅As is chemically stable and compatible with standard fabrication processes like selective etching. Together, they offer a balanced platform for high-performance, compact, and reliable photonic Sensors39.

The double-slot eye-shaped microring resonator offers several advantages over conventional optical sensor structures. Its compact footprint enables high integration density, while the double-slot design significantly enhances light–matter interaction, leading to improved sensitivity. The racetrack geometry allows for easier tuning of the coupling region without compromising the resonance condition. Moreover, the device exhibits a highly linear spectral response, which are desirable for precise sensing applications. From a fabrication perspective, the structure is CMOS-compatible and cost-effective, making it suitable for scalable production and lab-on-chip integration.

In this study, we present a complete theoretical study on a highly sensitive eye-shaped microring resonator (ESMRR) constructed using a double-slot GaAs core waveguide on an Al0.5Ga0.5As substrate. The proposed double-slot ESMRR gas sensor demonstrates a significant advancement over previously reported designs, achieving a high sensitivity of 1217.39 nm/RIU. The eye-shaped profile not only enhances field–gas interaction but also facilitates improved light confinement and mode overlap with the analyte. Furthermore, this design provides a favorable trade-off between sensitivity and figure of merit (FOM), enabling robust gas detection performance in integrated photonic platforms. This sensor is envisaged for real-time monitoring and rapid detection of CO2 concentration. We derived the optical transfer function (OTF) of the ESMRR structure and optimized the structural parameters of the sensor to achieve high sensitivity. The proposed sensor leverages high sensitivity, thus making it suitable for environmental monitoring and detection of hazardous gases. It might also be used to determine CO2 levels, so useful for environmental monitoring and industrial applications, where productivity and comfort for occupants could be enhanced, thus ensuring health and safety.

Theoretical background to derive the optical transfer function

As mentioned before, the schematic layout of the sensor illustrated in Fig. 1(a) features a circular MRR nested within a racetrack MRR, which mimics the ESMRR. The envisaged sensing system consists of a double-slot GaAs core waveguide placed on an Al0.5Ga0.5As substrate. The inclusion of a slot in the bus waveguide enhances the light–matter interaction by strongly localizing the optical field within the low-index gap region due to the discontinuity in refractive index. This field confinement is especially effective for transverse electric (TE) modes, where the electric field is maximized across the slot interface. In our design, the slot gap was chosen to balance strong field enhancement and fabrication feasibility. We optimized the slot width to achieve efficient sensing performance without compromising structural integrity. In a double-slot ESMRR sensor, the incident angle of incoming light affects how efficiently light couples into the ring and how strongly the evanescent field interacts with the surrounding medium. As the angle shifts away from optimal alignment, coupling efficiency may drop, leading to weaker resonance and reduced sensitivity. This angle variation can also change the polarization and excited modes, potentially altering the resonance condition and causing inaccurate sensing performance. Maintaining a near-normal, well-aligned input is key to preserving high sensitivity and stable operation. In microring resonator Sensors., variations in slot width enhance sensitivity by increasing the interaction between the evanescent field and the surrounding medium, though this may come at the cost of a reduced Q-factor due to higher scattering losses. Increasing the waveguide height improves optical confinement and raises the Q-factor by minimizing substrate leakage; however, it can diminish sensitivity as the evanescent field weakens. Similarly, narrowing the coupling gap strengthens the interaction between the resonator and the bus-waveguide, but may lead to over-coupling62reducing the Q-factor and detection precision. Therefore, an optimal balance between these geometric parameters is crucial to achieve high sensitivity, a sharp resonance peak, and overall sensor performance.

Tuning and optimization of structural geometrical parameters

To address tuning geometrical tolerances challenges in double-slot microring waveguides, researchers rely on advanced solutions like “electron-beam or deep-UV lithography” for precise slot definition and smoother sidewalls. Optimized “reactive ion etching (RIE)” ensures accurate depth control and reduces loss from roughness. Additionally, tuning geometrical tolerances (e.g., slightly widening the slots) can improve fabrication yield without significantly compromising performance63,64. Research indicates that the electric field strength within the slot region is markedly amplified in comparison to rib-high-index waveguides, primarily due to the discontinuity of the electric field at the rib interfaces. This discontinuity acts as a focal point for field enhancement and can be strategically exploited to optimize electric field localization, thereby significantly influencing the waveguide’s overall performance65. The width of the rib itself emerges as a critical parameter, directly affecting the waveguide’s modal propagation constants and guiding efficiency. Increasing the rib width tends to improve light confinement, which in turn intensifies the interaction between the confined optical mode and the electric field within the slots. However, achieving an optimal balance between slot width and waveguide confinement is essential; too narrow slots may limit electric field enhancement, while excessively wide ribs could compromise mode confinement and field distribution, ultimately diminishing device performance. The designed sensor comprises a 0.2 μm thick Al0.5Ga0.5As substrate, featuring a GaAs single-slot bus-waveguide coupled with a 2 × 2 GaAs double-slot microring resonator. The height of the core waveguide is 0.25 μm, and the average radius of the inner ring (R) is 5.02 μm. The results were obtained through simulations utilizing the variational finite-difference time domain (varFDTD) method in Lumerical Mode Solutions. The simulation employed a mesh size of 1 × 10−6 μm, perfectly matched layer (PML) boundary conditions, a total simulation duration of 5000 fs, and material models based on the Sellmeier equation. The var-FDTD (variational finite-difference time-domain) method is superior for photonic systems due to its exceptional computational efficiency. By reducing the 3D problem to a 2D simulation through effective index approximation, it drastically lowers memory and time requirements, making it ideal for rapid design and optimization. Unlike FEM, which solves for individual frequencies and modes, var-FDTD captures broadband responses in a single time-domain simulation, further enhancing its speed. Its use of a regular grid simplifies meshing, giving it a clear advantage in speed and ease of use. While FEM remains preferable for complex 3D geometries, in the context of planar photonics, var-FDTD’s efficiency and broad-spectrum capability make it the superior choice. In this simulation, the input wavelength range is 1.5 to 1.7 μm, PML boundary conditions are used in the X and Y directions and metal in the Z direction. The mesh size and type were selected to be 1 × 10−6 μm and auto non-uninform, respectively. In a double-slot microring resonator sensor, waveguide width and coupling length (LC) critically influence light confinement, mode distribution, and resonance behavior. Narrower waveguides enhance the evanescent field overlap with the analyte in the slot region, boosting sensitivity, but may also increase propagation loss. Conversely, wider waveguides confine light more tightly within the core, reducing sensitivity but improving Q-factor. The coupling length (LC), the interaction region between the bus waveguide and the ring, controls how much light is transferred into the resonator. A longer LC increases coupling strength, which can lead to over-coupling and broader resonances, while a shorter LC may cause under-coupling and weak resonance dips. Optimizing both parameters balances sensitivity, Q-factor, and detection limit, tailoring the sensor’s performance for specific applications68. The quality factor of a ring resonator-based system is defined as the ratio of the resonance wavelength to the full width at half maximum (FWHM) of the resonance peaks60.

Additionally, the parameter variations should not shift the position of the dips; this means that the chosen resonances must remain aligned at the same wavelength positions as the dips in the spectrum. It should be underscored that the waveguide width of the nested microring resonator in the ESMRR system significantly impacts the device sensitivity. The Al₀.₅Ga₀.₅As substrate with a GaAs double-slot waveguide offers several key advantages over plasmonic structures, primarily due to its low optical loss, confinement of light within the slots-which enhances light-matter interaction and improves sensitivity-high quality resonances, and compatibility with standard fabrication processes69–71. Since SPPs typically require a metal–dielectric interface, they do not naturally occur in standard GaAs/Al₀.₅Ga₀.₅As double-slot waveguides. However, highly doped GaAs or AlGaAs can mimic metallic behavior, allowing SPP-like modes under certain doping levels. These modes arise under TM polarization. This approach captures both photonic and plasmonic-like behavior in semiconductor platforms72. The MRR Sensors. are valued for their compactness, high Q-factors, and compatibility with CMOS fabrication, making them ideal for integrated photonic circuits33. However, their sensitivity is generally lower than that of plasmonic or graphene-enhanced Sensors. Plasmonic Sensors., leveraging surface plasmon resonance, offer exceptional sensitivity and subwavelength confinement but suffer from high optical losses and fabrication challenges33. Graphene-based Sensors. provide broadband tunability and fast response due to high carrier mobility, yet they introduce absorption losses and require complex fabrication processes73. Each platform presents a trade-off between sensitivity, integration ease, and optical performance.

Assessment of the performance by detecting different concentrations of CO2

According to the optimized design, a free spectral range of 137.68 nm and a full width at half maximum (FWHM) equal to 4.97 nm have been obtained. The sensing gas flows around the surface of the ring waveguide. Thanks to the optimized geometry, the propagating light in the ring evenly passes through the waveguide ribs and is confined within the slot areas filled with the sensing gas. As a result, the light efficiently interacts with the gas in the slot, causing specific changes in the waveguide’s group index. These changes lead to unique spectral variations in the output signal. The eye-shaped double-slot microring resonator, with its ultra-high sensitivity and compact footprint, can be primary applied in biochemical sensing, environmental monitoring, and label-free medical diagnostics, particularly for detecting trace analytes in aqueous environments83. This optimized design enhances light–matter interaction also makes it suitable for biosensing application as well.

Standard microring resonators are not inherently polarization-maintaining, especially in platforms like GaAs or silicon photonics, where TE and TM modes experience different effective indices, confinement, and losses. Although polarization effects were not explicitly considered in our design, polarization rotation (PR) within the ring resonator, especially due to waveguide bending and high index contrast, can significantly affect sensing performance. PR may lead to frequency-dependent polarization conversion, resulting in resonance distortion, reduced sensitivity, and polarization-dependent spectral shifts. These effects can degrade the accuracy and repeatability of the sensor. To counteract these effects, methods such as using symmetric waveguide structures, polarization-maintaining input coupling, and birefringence engineering can be employed, thereby ensuring stable and polarization-insensitive performance. In this study, the polarization angle is assumed to be constant and equal to zero.

The proposed double-slot eye-shaped microring resonator offers several advantages over conventional optical sensor structures. Its compact footprint enables high integration density, while the double-slot design significantly enhances light–matter interaction, leading to improved sensitivity. The racetrack geometry allows for easier tuning of the coupling region without compromising the resonance condition. Moreover, the device exhibits a highly linear spectral response, which are desirable for precise sensing applications. From a fabrication perspective, the structure is CMOS-compatible and cost-effective, making it suitable for scalable production and lab-on-chip integration.

Conclusion

A gas sensor based on a racetrack and a nested double-slot ring resonator has been introduced for detecting CO₂ concentration. A complete numerical analysis based on the variational finite difference time domain (var-FDTD) method has been conducted to derive the optical transfer function of the sensor. Based on parametric tuning, the suitable geometrical dimensions of the structure have been determined, resulting in a sensor with a linear response, high sensitivity of 1217.39 nm/RIU, and high resolution of 4.93 × 10⁻⁶ RIU as well. Given the performance attained, the proposed sensor has also demonstrated an impressive sensitivity of 24.4 pm/ppm for CO₂ detection, thus paving the way for its development and experimental demonstration of the attained performance in the field.

Single-channel EOG artifact removal using fixed frequency EWT and GMETV filter
Authors: Jammisetty Yedukondalu, Kalyani Sunkara, Venkata Kishore Kumar Rejeti, Y Murali Krishna, M Krishna Chaitanya
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPortable electroencephalogram (EEG) systems are increasingly used in healthcare due to their user-friendly and wearable design. However, accurate diagnosis can be hindered by electrooculogram (EOG) artifacts-low-frequency, high-amplitude signals caused by eye blinks. These artifacts are especially problematic in single-channel (SCL) EEG systems, necessitating robust artifact removal techniques. This study proposes an automated method for eliminating EOG artifacts from EEG signals using a Fixed Frequency Empirical Wavelet Transform (FF-EWT) integrated with a finely tuned Generalized Moreau Envelope Total Variation (GMETV) filter. The approach effectively separates artifact sources from the single-channel EEG by identifying contaminated components at the decomposition stage using kurtosis (KS), dispersion entropy (DisEn), and power spectral density (PSD) metrics. These components are then removed using the GMETV filter. The method was validated on both synthetic and real EEG datasets, demonstrating its capability to suppress EOG artifacts while preserving essential low-frequency EEG information. Performance evaluation revealed substantial improvements using the FF-EWT+GMETV technique, with lower Relative Root Mean Square Error (RRMSE) and higher Correlation Coefficient (CC) on synthetic data, and improved Signal-to-Artifact Ratio (SAR) and Mean Absolute Error (MAE) on real EEG recordings. This advancement offers strong potential for brain signal analysis, serving as an effective preprocessing tool in both clinical and research settings.Keywords: EEG, EOG, FF-EWT, Feature extraction, GMETV filterSubject terms: Neuroscience, Neurology, Engineering

Introduction

The advancement of handy single-channel (SCL) Electroencephalography (EEG) possesses significantly advanced brain-computer interfaces (BCI) alongside wellness tracking. EEG signals are typically employed to investigate functions of brain, notably cognitive states as well as brain conditions such as stroke alongside epilepsy. Nevertheless, these signals are often get contaminated by biological abnormalities such as electrocardiogram (ECG), electrooculogram (EOG), and electromyogram (EMG) signals1,2. In prolonged tracking, EOG artifacts present unique challenges as they are involuntary and difficult to circumvent, in contrast to other artifacts. Therefore, it is crucial to filter out eye-blink artifacts from EEG signals to prevent misinterpretation of cognitive activity3. Artifacts caused by eye blinks are most noticeable in the lower frequency range (0.5-12 Hz) of EEG signals.The rising incidence of chronic illnesses and an elderly demographic have propelled the need for home healthcare monitoring4. Portable EEG devices are utilized for multiple applications, including the examination of sleep disorders, evaluation of cognitive function in stroke patients, and surveillance of driver fatigue5,6. Recently, SCL portable EEG devices have been developed to reduce the burden on patients7,8.

In order to eliminate artifacts from multichannel (MCL) EEG data, Blind Source Separation (BSS) techniques like Canonical Correlation Analysis (CCA) and Independent Component Analysis (ICA). These hybrid methods are effective for MCL EEG but not commonly employed for SCL EEG recordings to eliminate EOG artifacts. Hence, it is essential to develop innovative methods specifically designed for the analysis of SCL EEG signals. An effective method for analyzing SCL EEG signals involves application of adaptive filters. The objective is to isolate EOG artifacts in EEG data, a technique was presented in9. To process the tainted EEG signal, adaptive filters need an exemplary signal. To obtain this reference, the tainted signal is fragmented utilizing discrete wavelet transform (DWT), and resulting low-frequency component is used as a reference for adaptive filtering, facilitating artifact elimination10. Additionally, data-driven decomposition algorithms such as Detrended Fluctuation Analysis (DFA), Empirical Mode Decomposition (EMD), and Ensemble EMD combined with ICA have been applied to denoise SCL EEG11,12. However, these approaches sometimes fail to completely remove artifact regions and can affect other regions of EEG signal.

Lately, singular spectrum analysis (SSA) has proven effective method for decomposing EEG signals. SSA is a subspace-based technique that efficiently separates oscillating noise elements from monovariate signals. Initial study on using SSA to remove EOG artifacts was conducted in13. The performance of SSA has been further improved by combining it with adaptive filters and new grouping techniques14,15, and it has also been employed as a smoothing filter. In this technique, selecting an appropriate criteria is crucial for accurate estrangement of EOG artifacts. To address issues related to threshold setting, a new automated process based on Diffusion Entropy was introduced, which eliminates the need for manual threshold adjustment16. However, some EOG artifact content may be lost due to mode mixing during the decomposition stage, potentially impacting data in the non-artifact regions.

In this manuscript, we introduce innovative structure which improves the proficiency of automated processes by integrating FF-EWT analysis and GMETV filter. This framework successfully identifies EOG artifacts in maintaining quality of non-artifact areas in SCL EEG. When tested employing synthetic alongside real EEG signals, the suggested method performs noticeably better than the current approaches. The manuscript is organized as: Section 2 summarizes existing techniques, including VMD, EMD, ICA, DWT, and SSA-based procedures, and outlines existing methodology of suggested approach. Section 3 presents the outcomes obtained from both real and synthetic datasets. Finally, the conclusion is presented in Section 4.

Existing techniques

Existing techniques for removing EOG artifacts from EEG signals include:

EMD: A data-driven method that decomposes signals into intrinsic mode functions (IMFs) based on their inherent oscillatory modes. It can separate EOG artifacts from EEG signals but may suffer from mode mixing, which can lead to the loss of important signal components17.

The Fixed Frequency Empirical Wavelet Transform (FF-EWT) is a more recent approach that improves upon these techniques. FF-EWT specifically targets fixed frequency ranges associated with EOG artifacts, providing a more focused and accurate removal while preserving the non-artifact content in the EEG signals. By combining FF-EWT with other filtering techniques, such as the GMETV filter, the framework enhances the effectiveness of artifact removal, leading to better preservation of signal quality compared to traditional methods.

EOG artifacts were effectively eliminated from both synthetic and real EEG data using the FF-EWT in combination with the GMETV filter. The FF-EWT efficiently decomposes the EEG signal into discrete sub-bands, isolating noise and artifacts based on their frequency properties. In synthetic data scenarios, the true EEG signal is available, allowing a direct comparison between the contaminated and clean signals. Therefore, the recovery performance can be quantitatively evaluated using metrics such as RRMSE and CC. However, in real-world applications, the true reference EEG signal is typically not accessible. As a result, performance evaluation in such cases relies on alternative metrics like MAE, SAR, and qualitative time-domain analysis, as demonstrated using two databases and illustrated in Fig. 6 and Fig. 7. Additionally, the PSD analysis shown in Fig. 8 and Fig. 9 provides further insight into the frequency-domain behavior of the denoising methods. The PSD plots reveal how well the proposed method preserves the power of low-frequency EEG components in non-artifact regions while effectively suppressing EOG artifacts. In contrast, some of the existing filtering techniques either excessively attenuate the power in the delta band-indicating over-smoothing-or fail to adequately suppress the artifact components, leading to insufficient recovery. These observations highlight the importance of maintaining a balance between artifact suppression and preservation of essential EEG signal features. Applications requiring accurate and clean EEG data can benefit from this dual-stage method, as it ensures excellent artifact removal effectiveness while preserving important EEG information.

Parameter tuning of proposed technique

The parameters A and play a crucial role in determining the output performance of the proposed GMETV-based cost function. The matrix A defines the nature of regularization applied to the filtered signal: when A=0, the regularization simplifies to the conventional Total Variation (TV), promoting piecewise smoothness but potentially introducing staircase artifacts; when , it leads to a Minimax-Concave (MC) penalty, enhancing sparsity and better preserving abrupt transitions such as EOG spikes; and when , it yields the Moreau envelope formulation, which provides controlled smoothing. The regularization weight adjusts the balance between data fidelity and regularization strength-higher values enforce stronger smoothing, while lower values retain more of the signal’s original structure. Conclusion

A novel FF-EWT method was employed to reliably eliminate EOG artifacts from single-chanrnel EEG signals. The estimation of EOG artifacts was based on two straightforward aspects of the EEG signal, with the EWT technique applied to remove residual EEG components from the calculated EOG artifact. Results indicate that the proposed approach effectively eliminates EOG artifacts while preserving the integrity of the original EEG components. The method leverages three key features-KS, DisEn, and PSD-which effectively differentiate between EEG and EOG components. By fine-tuning its parameters and incorporating the GMETV filter, the technique achieves precise removal of EOG artifacts. A comparative analysis with existing methods demonstrates that the proposed FF-EWT+GMETV approach outperforms alternatives in accurately filtering EOG artifacts. This method is particularly suited for preprocessing EEG signals in applications utilizing a limited number of frontal EEG channels or SCL configurations.

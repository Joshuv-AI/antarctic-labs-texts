An efficient and lightweight algorithm for detecting surface defects of steel based on SCCI-YOLO
Authors: Huixiang Zhou, Hong Zou, Gaojun Hu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractSteel is a crucial raw material in the industrial sector, and its surface defects significantly impact product quality. These defects are diverse in type, complex in shape, uneven in distribution, and varied in size, posing substantial challenges for performance and detection. Addressing the limitations of current deep learning-based steel surface defect detection algorithms in feature extraction, feature fusion, and multi-scale defect recognition—leading to high false detection rates, frequent missed detections, and low detection accuracy—this paper proposes a steel surface defect detection algorithm based on an improved YOLOv8n, named SCCI-YOLO. Firstly, the SPD-Conv module is introduced into the backbone network, utilizing adaptive weight allocation to focus convolutional kernels more on critical regions of the image, thereby improving detection accuracy for low-resolution and small objects. Secondly, we designed the C2f_EMA module, aimed at extracting more useful feature information and enhancing feature fusion effects. To further enhance the detection capability for small defects, a lightweight cross-scale feature fusion module (CCFM) is incorporated into the Neck network. This module integrates features from different scales, enhancing the model’s adaptability to scale variations and improving detection accuracy for small-scale objects. Finally, to address the weak generalization and slow convergence issues of existing IoU loss functions across different detection tasks, we employed the Inner-IoU loss function, which improves the model’s convergence speed and regression accuracy.The experimental results show that SCCI-YOLO achieves a mAP of 78.6% on the NEU-DET dataset, which improves the detection accuracy by 2.2% and 5.9% compared to YOLOv8n and YOLOv7, respectively, and reduces the number of model parameters by 43.9% compared to the original model, and the study demonstrates that the algorithm exhibits an excellent overall performance in the detection of defects on steel surfaces.Keywords: Defect detection, CCFM, Feature fusion, YOLOv8, Loss functionSubject terms: Engineering, Materials science

Introduction

Steel plates are essential raw materials in industries such as construction, automotive, shipbuilding, and home appliances, and are prone to defects during production, processing, and transportation. These defects can lead to a decline in product quality and safety incidents, making precise detection of steel plate surface defects crucial.

In recent years, the development of artificial intelligence technology has led to the increasingly widespread application of defect detection systems in industrial production 1–4 . Deep learning has rapidly evolved over the past decade, significantly enhancing image classification and feature extraction capabilities from network structures like AlexNet to ResNet and EfficientNet. Object detection, a core task in computer vision, has also made remarkable progress, benefiting from advancements in deep learning. From traditional HOG and SIFT features to real-time detection models like R-CNN5, YOLO6, and SSD7, object detection technology has continually improved in accuracy and efficiency. The breakthrough of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) significantly enhanced object detection performance, while Transformer models further advanced the field through self-attention mechanisms. Research in self-supervised learning, transfer learning, and lightweight models has enabled object detection to play a critical role in scenarios like autonomous driving, intelligent security, and industrial automation.Before the advent of deep learning, object detection primarily relied on traditional image processing and machine learning methods, such as the sliding window method8, cascade classifiers like the Viola-Jones algorithm9, HOG features combined with SVM10, and selective search methods11. These methods were computationally inefficient and limited in detection accuracy, yet they played a crucial role in the early stages of computer vision, laying the foundation for the development of modern deep-learning methods.

Deep learning-based object detection algorithms have made significant progress and can be divided into single-stage and two-stage methods. Single-stage methods like the YOLO series and SSD directly predict bounding boxes and categories on feature maps, enabling fast detection suitable for real-time applications12. Two-stage methods like R-CNN, Fast R-CNN, and Faster R-CNN achieve higher precision by generating candidate regions first and then classifying them, making them suitable for complex scenes and small object detection13. The YOLO series simplifies object detection by treating it as a regression problem, using a single neural network to simultaneously predict bounding boxes and categories for real-time detection. YOLOv2 introduced batch normalization and anchor mechanisms to improve detection accuracy and speed14. YOLOv3 enhanced the ability to detect objects of different sizes through multi-scale feature prediction and residual networks15.YOLOv4 combined CSPDarknet53 and Mosaic data augmentation to improve performance and stability.YOLOv5 further optimized code implementation and performance16. YOLOv7 maintained a leading edge in speed and efficiency. These improvements have achieved a good balance between speed and accuracy for the YOLO series, making them widely used in real-time detection fields like autonomous driving and security monitoring.For example, Li et al.17 based on YOLOv7 proposed EFC-YOLO, which adopts BiFPN structure to realize high-speed and high-precision steel defect detection.Zhang et al.18 proposed ESI-YOLOv8, which enhances the detection accuracy and speed through EP module and SPPF-LSKA module. Yuan et al.19 proposed GDCP-YOLO, which enhances the detection accuracy and speed through the DCNV2 module and the channel attention in the C2f mechanism, enhancing the YOLOv8n architecture, optimizing feature extraction and processing, and improving detection performance.

In the steel defect detection field, YOLO models are preferred for their fast, accurate, and automated characteristics, precisely detecting and locating various defects, and widely used to improve product quality and safety. To address the sample imbalance problem in the detection of surface defects in industrial products, several methods can be used to improve the recognition of defective samples. Data enhancement techniques increase the number of defective samples by rotating, flipping, cropping, etc., and cost-sensitive learning makes the model more focused on sparse categories by giving higher weights to defective samples. In addition, Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) can be used to synthesise realistic defective samples, thus alleviating the problem of uneven data distribution. Unsupervised methods use reconstruction error or embedding similarity for detection, while weakly supervised methods combine a small amount of labelled data with unlabelled data to improve the generalisation ability of the model. In practice, multiple strategies, such as data augmentation combined with cost-sensitive learning or augmenting data with GAN, are usually combined to improve the performance of the detection system in unbalanced sample scenarios20.However, existing deep learning-based industrial defect detection methods are not fully optimized for feature fusion and small defect detection. Traditional methods perform poorly for small defects due to their small size and indistinct features. Additionally, model complexity and computational resource requirements are challenges in practical applications. Therefore, to improve detection accuracy, it is necessary to optimize feature fusion, enhance the model’s detection ability for small defects, and achieve model lightweight to reduce computational complexity and resource consumption21.

In this paper, we propose an efficient and lightweight defect detection model for the key problems in steel defect detection. We first systematically investigate the performance of existing target detection algorithms on steel defect detection and analyze their limitations. On this basis, we optimize based on YOLOv8n and design a series of improvement strategies. Specifically, we thoroughly investigate the difficulties of defect feature extraction, propose SPD-Conv (Symmetric Positive Definite Convolution) to enhance the ability of local feature extraction, and construct C2f_EMA module by combining C2f and EMA attention mechanism to improve the feature expression ability. In addition, based on a large number of experiments and comparative analyses, we optimize the multi-scale information interaction mechanism and propose a lightweight cross-scale feature fusion module, CCFM, to enhance the model’s ability to perceive steel defects. Meanwhile, we revisit the limitations of the existing IoU computation methods and construct the Inner-IoU loss function to more reasonably measure the degree of target overlap and improve the bounding box regression accuracy.In the experimental part, we performed extensive data preprocessing and model training tuning to ensure the rigour and reproducibility of the experiments. We extensively tested the improved model on multiple public datasets, and manually annotated and organised some of the experimental data to enhance the generalisation ability of the model. Meanwhile, we conducted in-depth comparative experiments with several mainstream target detection algorithms under the same hardware environment to evaluate the detection accuracy, inference speed and computational cost of the model. In addition, we designed a series of ablation experiments to remove or replace each improvement module respectively, to analyse the specific contribution of SPD-Conv, C2f_EMA, CCFM and Inner-IoU loss functions to the detection performance, and to continuously optimise the model structure to ensure the effectiveness and stability of the final scheme.Overall, this study not only introduces a number of improvement strategies in the methodology, but also invests a lot of work in experimental validation and optimisation, and finally constructs a more efficient, accurate and lightweight steel defect detection model. The model can significantly improve the performance of steel defect detection and reduce the computational cost and resource consumption, as well as have good adaptability and generalisation, providing new methods and ideas for the target detection task. Related work

YOLOv8 model structure

YOLOv8 is an advanced SOTA (State Of The Art) model built on the successful YOLO version, introducing several new features and improvements designed to further enhance performance and flexibility.

It includes a new backbone network, Ancher-Free detection header, and loss function, and it provides five pre-trained model sizes: n, s, m, l, and x, which are categorized into different sizes based on the number of parameters and computational size to support fast, accurate, and easy-to-use target detection, image segmentation, and image categorization tasks. The YOLOv8 network architecture is shown in Fig. 1.

YOLOv8 model structure diagram.

The workflow of YOLOv8 is as follows: first, the input image is preprocessed and then multi-scale features are extracted using a new backbone network. Next, these features are fused by a feature pyramid network or a path aggregation network, and target center prediction and bounding box regression are performed using an unanchored box detection head. In the training phase, a new loss function is used to optimize the model parameters. In the inference stage, non-maximal suppression techniques are applied to eliminate duplicate detected frames and finally output the detected bounding boxes and the corresponding category labels.

The network structure of YOLOv8 consists of three main parts. The Backbone backbone network consists of a series of convolutional and anti-convolutional layers for feature extraction. From Fig. 2, we can see that the backbone network adopts a structure similar to CSPDarknet, which consists of five convolutional modules (Conv) and four C2f modules, and one SPPF module, and the overall residual connection and bottleneck structure are used to reduce the network size and improve the performance. The convolution module (Conv), also known as the CBS module, has the main structure of a 2D convolution (Conv2d), a BatchNorm2d, and a SiLU activation function. Methods

YOLOv8 is a state-of-the-art real-time target detection model that combines advanced backbone networks such as EfficientNet and CSPDarknet, and optimizes neck networks such as PANet and FPN to dramatically improve feature extraction and fusion capabilities. The improved detection head refinement design enhances bounding box localization and classification accuracy. The model uses data enhancement techniques such as Mosaic Enhancement, Hybrid Enhancement, CutMix, and optimized Balance Loss and IOU Loss to improve generalization capability and accuracy.

Despite its outstanding performance, YOLOv8 is deficient in handling small targets and targets with insignificant features. Its large sensory field makes it difficult to accurately locate and recognize small targets,the model may not be able to accurately classify non-significant target features or when they are similar to the environment,and the feature pyramid structure is simpler, leading to under-representation of features at different scales. To solve these problems, we propose a new defect detection model, SCCI-YOLO.

SCCI-YOLO introduces the SPD-Conv module, which replaces the traditional convolutional operation to retain more details and feature information, and is especially suitable for low-resolution images and small objects. In addition, the EMA attention mechanism is added to the C2f module of the backbone network to form the new C2f_EMA module.EMA extracts more useful feature information by weighted averaging of the feature maps, reduces the overfitting of the model to the training data, and improves the performance on unseen data. To enhance the feature representation capability, we introduce a lightweight cross-scale feature fusion module, CCFM, which provides powerful cross-scale feature fusion capability and reduces the need for C2f_EMA enhancement features, thus reducing the computation and memory overheads while maintaining a high feature representation capability.SCCI-YOLO also employs the Inner-IoU loss function, which introduces a scale factor ratio to control the scale of the auxiliary bounding box. SCCI-YOLO also employs the Inner-IoU loss function to introduce a scale factor ratio to control the scale size of the auxiliary bounding box. By using different scales of auxiliary bounding boxes for different datasets and detectors, the limitations of the existing methods in terms of generalization ability can be overcome. SPD-Conv

Small target detection is challenging, mainly due to the low resolution of small targets and the limited contextual information they provide. In addition, small targets usually coexist with large objects that dominate the feature learning process, making it difficult for small targets to be effectively detected. In complex tasks where images are blurred or objects are very small, traditional cross-row convolution and pooling are ineffective. To address these problems, we introduce SPD-Conv as an alternative architecture, which consists of a space-to-depth (SPD) layer and a non-spanning convolutional layer. the SPD layer downsamples the feature map but retains all the information in the channel dimensions. Although the spatial resolution of the feature map is reduced, no information is lost. The SPD layer achieves efficient transfer and compression of information by rearranging the information in the spatial dimension into the channel dimension. Specifically, the SPD layer generates a new feature map by rearranging each block of the input feature map into the channel dimension. This operation not only reduces the spatial dimension of the feature map, but also increases the number of channels, thus ensuring complete retention of information. In general, given any (original) feature graph X, subgraph is formed by all entries X(i, j) that satisfy the conditions i+x and j+y that are divisible by the scale factor. Thus, each subgraph downsamples X according to the scale factor. Figure 5a,b,c shows an example when the scale factor is 2. Four sub-maps , , and are generated, each of which has the shape and a downsampling factor of 2. These sub-feature mappings are then concatenated along the channel dimensions, yielding a feature mapping that decreases in spatial dimension by a scaling factor and the channel dimension is increased by the square of a scaling factor. In other words, SPD transforms the feature mapping into the intermediate feature mapping . An illustration is given in Fig. 5d for a scale of 2.

C2f_EMA module

To address the problem of different sizes and fuzzy features in steel defects, we introduced the EMA attention mechanism into the C2f module to form a new C2f_EMA module to enhance feature extraction and detection.The C2f (CSP2f) module is based on the CSPNet (Cross Stage Partial Network) design, which aims to reduce computation and memory usage while maintaining the accuracy of the model. The C2f module transforms the features, increases the nonlinear capability, and performs feature fusion through two convolutional layers (cv1 and cv2) and multiple Bottleneck modules. The forward propagation process of C2f starts with the input data first passing through the convolutional layer cv1, and then the output is divided into two parts, one part is passed directly to the output and the other part is processed by multiple Bottleneck modules. Finally, the two parts are spliced in the channel dimension and the final output is obtained by the convolutional layer cv2.

To enhance the capability of the C2f module, we add the EMA attention mechanism before Bottleneck, and the EMA structure is shown in Fig. 6. The EMA attention mechanism automatically selects and suppresses unimportant features before entering Bottleneck, which makes the features more effective and improves the model efficiency. By calculating the attention weights through exponential moving averages, the EMA mechanism smooths the noise in the feature map and enhances the robustness of the feature map. In combination with the C2f module, the EMA attention mechanism is even more effective. The C2f module enhances feature representation through feature grouping and multi-scale structure. The input feature map is divided into multiple sub-feature groups, and each group learns different semantics, enhancing feature learning in semantic regions and compressing noise. The EMA attention mechanism adopts a parallel sub-structure that combines 1x1 and 3x3 convolutional branching to aggregate information about the multi-scale spatial structure32. The C2f module first compresses the input feature maps into channels by 1x1 convolution and then processes them before Bottleneck by the EMA attention mechanism. 1x1 branch in the EMA attention mechanism encodes the channel information by a global average pooling operation, while the 3x3 branch captures the multiscale features. The output feature maps are aggregated by matrix dot product operations and Sigmoid functions to generate spatial attention maps that capture the global context. Finally, the output features of the C2f module are further processed by the feature fusion method to combine features of different scales and depths. This design not only improves the feature expression capability and model robustness, but also enhances the overall performance, especially in small target detection and low-resolution image processing.

CCFM module

Although YOLOv8 performs well in terms of accuracy and speed, its larger number of model parameters requires more computational resources and high-performance hardware. This not only increases the training time and cost, but also makes the model tuning complex, requiring more detailed hyperparameter optimization and longer experiment time. To improve detection performance and speed up training and inference, we introduce a multi-scale feature fusion module, CCFM, at the Neck layer.

CCFM is a technique used to improve the performance of CNNs when dealing with multi-scale features. It enhances the model’s adaptability to scale changes and the detection of small-scale objects by fusing features from different scales.CCFM can effectively integrate detailed features and contextual information, which improves the feature extraction capability while realizing a balance between model complexity and computational efficiency, thus enhancing the overall performance. The structure of the CCFM model is shown in Fig. 7.

CCFM structure diagram and its sub-structures.

CCFM enhances feature representation by fusing features at different scales, and its structure includes multi-scale feature extraction, feature fusion, and feature compression and adjustment. First, multiscale feature extraction utilizes multilayer convolution or other transform operations to extract features at different scales from the input feature map, ensuring that the feature map captures rich information at different scales. CCFM optimizes feature fusion by inserting multiple fusion blocks consisting of convolutional layers in the fusion path. Each fusion block contains multiple convolutional layers that interact between feature maps at different scales, integrating features from neighboring scales to generate a new feature map. The fusion block progressively aggregates multi-scale information to improve feature representation33. Finally, feature compression and tuning use operations such as 1 1 convolution to compress the number of channels in the fused feature map, control the amount of computation, and adjust the dimensionality of the feature map for subsequent processing. Through these steps, CCFM improves the feature representation capability of the model while maintaining computational efficiency for resource-constrained environments and real-time applications.

Inner-IoU loss function

Target detection is a fundamental task in computer vision, including target classification and localization, in which the bounding box regression (BBR) loss function is crucial in the localization branch. Existing IoU loss functions accurately characterize how well the predicted bounding box matches the GT box, but in steel defect detection, the traditional CIOU loss function often leads to slow convergence and inaccurate regression due to the small size of the target. We adopt the Inner-IoU loss, which calculates the loss by introducing scale factor ratios and generating auxiliary bounding boxes with different scales. Without adding new loss terms, Inner-IoU accelerates the regression process and improves the detection performance, which is especially suitable for small-target detection and significantly improves the effectiveness of steel defect detection18. Compared with IoU loss, when the scale factor is less than 1 and the auxiliary bounding box size is smaller than the actual bounding box, the effective range of the regression shrinks, but the absolute value of the gradient is larger than that obtained by IoU loss, which accelerates the convergence of the high IoU samples. On the contrary, when the scaling factor is greater than 1, the larger size of the auxiliary bounding box expands the effective range of the regression, which enhances the regression of the low IoU samples and thus improves the detection results34.

After introducing the Inner-IoU loss function, and after repeated experimental validation, we found that our SCCI-YOLO steel defect detection model can achieve the best detection results when the scale is 1.3.

Analysis of ablation experiment results

To better analyze and validate the improvements and effectiveness made by our proposed new model, we designed and implemented a series of ablation experiments and analyzed them for module comparison and result rating.

In Table 5, through the analysis of experimental data and the characteristics of each module, it is evident that each module plays a significant role in the original model, improving detection accuracy, lightweighting the model, and optimizing real-time processing performance. In Experiment Group 1, the introduction of the SPD-Conv module slightly reduced the parameter count compared to the original model, but both mAP@0.5 and FPS decreased. This indicates that while SPD-Conv reduces model complexity, it does not significantly enhance performance when used alone.In Experiment Group 2, the standalone introduction of the C2f_EMA module significantly improved detection accuracy, though FPS decreased. Experiment Group 3, which only introduced the CCFM module, improved detection accuracy but increased computational load, resulting in a decreased frame rate. Experiment Group 4, combining the SPD-Conv and C2f_EMA modules, slightly reduced the parameter count while significantly boosting both detection accuracy and FPS, with accuracy 2.1% higher than the original model. This indicates a synergistic effect in performance enhancement when these two modules are combined. In Experiment Group 5, combining the SPD-Conv and CCFM modules resulted in a reduction in parameter count but only limited improvement in accuracy, suggesting that this combination contributes to parameter optimization but has a limited effect on accuracy enhancement. Experiment Group 6, which combined the C2f_EMA and CCFM modules, saw improvements in both detection accuracy and FPS, though not as effective as the optimal combination. Finally, Experiment Group 7, which combined the SPD-Conv, C2f_EMA, and CCFM modules, exhibited the best performance across all metrics, showing the highest mAP@0.5 (78.6%), the lowest parameter count (1.68M), and a relatively high FPS (270.2). Although the FPS was lower than the original model, the SPD-Conv module improved feature extraction efficiency, the C2f_EMA module smoothed prediction results, and the CCFM module enhanced multi-scale object detection capability. The combination of these three modules ensures high detection accuracy while maintaining a low parameter count and high frame rate through their synergistic effects. The proposed new model demonstrates significant effectiveness in enhancing detection accuracy, optimizing model complexity, and maintaining a high frame rate.

Analysis of generalization experiment results

In order to fully evaluate the generalisation capability of our proposed model, we tested it on several datasets with different features and data distributions, including the Aluminum, PASCAL VOC2012, GC10-DET, COCO and PASCAL VOC2007 datasets. The Aluminum dataset contains samples of aluminium surface defects and can be used to validate the model’s suitability for cross-material defect detection tasks, the PASCAL VOC2012 and VOC2007 are classical target detection datasets covering 20 classes of objects, which can evaluate the model’s ability to migrate to generic target detection tasks, and the GC10-DET dataset contains 10 classes of steel surface defects, which is similar to NEU-DET but with different acquisition conditions and data distribution, and can be used to validate the model’s ability to generalise across datasets in the steel defect detection task, and the COCO dataset, which covers 80 classes of objects and contains complex backgrounds and multi-scale targets, helps to analyse the model’s detection performance in complex scenes. By experimenting on these different datasets, we are able to evaluate the model’s performance on unseen data more systematically, thus validating its effectiveness, stability and adaptability.

Conclusion

In this paper, we analyze and study the limitations of steel surface defect detection, including poor detection of small defects, insufficient feature extraction capability, large number of model parameters, and low average detection accuracy. To this end, we propose a new algorithm, SCCI-YOLO, based on the YOLOv8n model, which introduces SPD-Conv, which contains an improved convolution operation that not only reduces the computational complexity, but also enhances the feature extraction capability, and improves the resolution and expressive ability of the feature map. To enhance the feature fusion capability, we combine the C2f module with the EMA attention mechanism. The C2f module, through efficient feature fusion, is combined with the multi-scale feature perception capability of the EMA, which enables the model to understand and express the image information more comprehensively, significantly improving the feature expression capability and detection accuracy while maintaining efficient computational performance. In addition, we introduce a lightweight cross-scale feature fusion module, CCFM, which effectively integrates detailed features and contextual information, while the module uses lightweight convolutional operations and feature selection techniques to avoid the problem of large parameter redundancy in traditional feature fusion methods. Aiming at the problem that the existing IoU loss function has poor generalization ability and slow convergence speed in different detection tasks, we introduce a new loss function Inner-IoU. Experimental results show that compared with the original model, the average detection accuracy of our model improves by 2.2%, and successfully reduces the number of model parameters by 43.9%. Moreover,a large number of experiments further validate the generalisation and effectiveness of the model in detection, especially on the GC10, Aluminum and VOC2007 datasets, which show significant improvement in effectiveness.Despite the overall improvement of the SCCI-YOLO model compared with the original model, its performance and generalization ability still highly depend on high-quality training data and accurate annotation. When facing complex scenes or new datasets, especially when dealing with small or dense targets, its generalization ability needs to be further improved. In the future, we will work on solving the problem of small defects in steel defect detection, further improving the detection accuracy and real-time processing speed of the model, and exploring its applicability in other application scenarios.

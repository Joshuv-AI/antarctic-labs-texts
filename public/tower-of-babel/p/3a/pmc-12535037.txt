Construction and validation of a multi-dimensional health indicator-driven osteoporosis risk prediction model: a large-sample cross-sectional study based on two centers
Authors: Zhifeng Cai, Xun Lu, Hua Ni, Zhi Xu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundRising osteoporosis prevalence among elderly populations and limitations of current single-factor screening methods necessitate development of comprehensive multi-dimensional risk prediction models.MethodsA two-center cross-sectional study was conducted, enrolling 15,307 elderly participants in 2023. Among them, 11,957 participants from Zhangjiagang Center for Disease Control and Prevention were randomly divided into training set (8369 cases) and internal validation set (3588 cases) at a 7:3 ratio, while 3,350 participants from Zhangjiagang fifth People’s Hospital served as the external validation set. Multi-dimensional health data including demographic information, physiological indicators, lifestyle factors, nutritional supplementation, and laboratory examinations were collected. Osteoporosis diagnosis was performed using ultrasound bone density testing (UBD T-score ≤-2.5). Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were used to analyze risk factors, construct prediction models, and create nomograms. The predictive performance of five algorithms including logistic regression, random forest, extreme gradient boosting, support vector machine, and naive Bayes was compared. Model efficacy was evaluated using ROC curves, calibration curves, and decision curve analysis.ResultsThe prevalence of osteoporosis was 25.3% (3040/11957). Multivariate logistic regression analysis identified age and heart rate as independent risk factors, while BMI, education level, occupation type, exercise habits, daily milk consumption, hemoglobin, and triglycerides were protective factors. The logistic regression model demonstrated optimal and stable performance, with AUCs of 0.687 (95% CI: 0.674-0.700), 0.675 (95% CI: 0.655–0.696), and 0.679 (95% CI: 0.657–0.701) for the training set, internal validation set, and external validation set, respectively. Variable importance analysis showed that occupation type, daily exercise time, and hemoglobin demonstrated high and stable importance across all datasets.ConclusionThe osteoporosis risk prediction model based on multi-dimensional health indicators demonstrates good discriminative performance, calibration, and clinical utility, providing an effective tool for early screening and precision prevention of osteoporosis in elderly populations.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12891-025-09229-z.Keywords: Osteoporosis, Risk prediction model, Machine learning, Ultrasound bone density, Elderly population, Nomogram

Background

Osteoporosis is a systemic bone disease characterized by reduced bone mass and deterioration of bone tissue microarchitecture, seriously threatening the health and quality of life of elderly populations [1, 2]. With the acceleration of population aging, the incidence of osteoporosis and related fractures shows an increasing trend year by year, imposing heavy burdens on individuals, families, and society [3]. However, osteoporosis has insidious early symptoms, and most patients are only discovered after fractures occur, missing the optimal intervention timing [4]. Therefore, how to accurately identify high-risk populations in the early stages of disease and implement targeted preventive measures has become an important issue in current orthopedic and public health fields [5, 6].

Previous studies have identified multiple risk factors for osteoporosis including age, gender, and lifestyle factors, providing important references for disease prevention and control [7]. However, with economic and social development and lifestyle changes, the spectrum of disease risk factors may change. In recent years, China has witnessed significant improvements in residents’ nutritional status, enhanced exercise and fitness awareness, and substantially elevated healthcare levels, which may impact the patterns of osteoporosis occurrence [8]. Therefore, it is necessary to re-examine and update osteoporosis risk factors based on the latest epidemiological data to ensure the timeliness and specificity of prevention strategies. The 2023 health examination data of the elderly population in Zhangjiagang City collected by this study can truly reflect the current health status and lifestyle characteristics of the elderly in Zhangjiagang City, which has important practical significance and reference value.

Traditional osteoporosis screening mainly relies on bone density testing, but dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) equipment is expensive and complex to operate, making it difficult to widely apply in primary healthcare institutions. In recent years, ultrasound bone density testing has gradually gained attention due to its advantages of simple operation and low cost [9]. Meanwhile, the occurrence of osteoporosis is influenced by multiple factors, and relying solely on bone density values for risk assessment has certain limitations [10]. Traditional logistic regression models, although widely used in medical research, have deficiencies in handling high-dimensional data, nonlinear relationships, and complex interactions [11, 12]. Machine learning technology, with its powerful data mining and pattern recognition capabilities, can automatically identify complex relationships between variables and discover predictive patterns that traditional statistical methods struggle to capture, demonstrating enormous potential in disease risk prediction [13, 14]. Currently, research on intelligent prediction models for osteoporosis in Chinese elderly populations is relatively insufficient, and most studies have small sample sizes and single validation methods, requiring further verification of model clinical applicability and promotion value [15].

Based on the above background, this study adopts a two-center large-sample cross-sectional design, collecting multi-dimensional health indicator data from 15,307 elderly individuals and utilizing multiple machine learning algorithms to construct osteoporosis risk prediction models. The main objectives of the study include: (1) Based on the latest epidemiological data, comprehensively analyze risk factors affecting osteoporosis occurrence in elderly populations and identify independent predictive factors; (2) Compare the predictive performance of logistic regression with multiple machine learning algorithms to select the optimal prediction model; (3) Evaluate model stability and generalizability through internal validation and cross-center external validation. This study expects to provide scientific evidence for early identification and precision prevention of osteoporosis in elderly populations, promoting the optimization of osteoporosis prevention and control strategies and the enhancement of primary screening capabilities.

Materials and methods

Study design and participant selection

This study adopted a two-center design, and the patient screening process is shown in Fig. 1. At Center A (Zhangjiagang Center for Disease Control and Prevention), data were consecutively collected from elderly individuals participating in a health examination program in 2023. A total of 41,578 elderly participants who underwent bone density testing were initially identified. After data completeness checks and quality control, participants who did not meet the inclusion criteria were excluded, and a final cohort of 15,307 participants was enrolled for analysis. The study population at Center A was randomly divided into two subsets at a 7:3 ratio: 8369 participants (70%) were assigned to the training set, used for model development, and 3588 participants (30%) were assigned to the internal validation set, used for internal model validation and performance evaluation. Center B (Zhangjiagang Fifth People’s Hospital) served as an external validation cohort, independently collecting data from 3350 elderly participants to assess the external validity and generalizability of the developed model. The inclusion criteria for the external cohort were consistent with those of Center A to ensure comparability of the study populations. This study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Zhangjiagang Center for Disease Control and Prevention (approval number: ZJGCDCLL-2025001) and the Ethics Committee of Zhangjiagang Fifth People’s Hospital (approval number: yxll202507002). Since this project did not involve clinical intervention and all patient information was de-identified, the requirement for informed consent was waived.

Study flow chart and patient selection process

Data collection

A standardized electronic case report form (eCRF) was used to collect clinical data from study subjects, mainly including the following aspects: Basic demographic information: age, gender, body mass index (BMI), education level (illiterate, primary school, middle school, high school, university), occupation classification (primary industry, secondary industry, service industry, public institution, other, unemployed). Physiological indicators: basic vital signs including temperature, heart rate, systolic blood pressure (SBP), diastolic blood pressure (DBP), with all measurements performed under standardized conditions by trained medical personnel. Lifestyle factors: exercise frequency (no exercise, occasionally, twice a week or less, three times a week or more, daily), daily exercise time (≤ 1 h, 1–3 h, ≥ 3 h), dietary habits (balanced diet, unbalanced diet), smoking status (never smoke, quit smoking, current smoking), drinking habits (don’t drink alcohol, occasionally, frequently). Nutritional supplementation: daily milk consumption (DMC) and daily calcium supplement (DCS) usage, both categorized as “yes” or “no”. Laboratory examination indicators: hemoglobin (Hb), white blood cell count (WBC), platelet count (PLT), serum alanine aminotransferase (ALT), serum aspartate aminotransferase (AST), serum creatinine (SCr), total cholesterol (TC), triglycerides (TG), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C). All blood samples were collected after 12 h of fasting and analyzed using standardized testing methods by each center’s laboratory. Bone density testing: ultrasound bone density (UBD) T-scores were measured using ultrasound bone densitometry.

Osteoporosis diagnosis

While DXA is the gold standard for osteoporosis diagnosis, considering the large-scale population screening characteristics of this study, the selection of UBD has the following rational basis: Applicability: UBD equipment is simple to operate and cost-effective, making it suitable for application in primary healthcare institutions and large-scale epidemiological surveys [16]; Accuracy: Multiple studies have demonstrated that UBD T-score has good correlation with DXA, showing acceptable diagnostic accuracy in osteoporosis screening [17, 18]; Practicality: In resource-limited environments, UBD provides a feasible screening tool for early identification of high-risk populations, and its predictive value has been validated in multiple large-sample studies [19].

This study employed ultrasound bone density testing technology for osteoporosis diagnosis. Bone density testing was performed using a quantitative ultrasound bone densitometer (Model: OSTEOKJ3000+, Manufacturer: Nanjing Kejin Industrial Co., Ltd.), with the detection site selected at the distal 1/3 of the non-dominant hand radius. This site demonstrates good reproducibility and representativeness, making it suitable for large-scale population screening. Detection operation procedures: Subjects were seated with their non-dominant forearm placed flat on the testing platform in a naturally relaxed position. Prior to testing, the detection site skin was cleaned and an appropriate amount of coupling gel was applied to ensure good sound wave transmission. The probe was placed vertically against the skin surface, avoiding bubble formation. Each subject underwent three consecutive measurements, with the average value used as the final result. Quality control measures: All tests were performed by professionally trained and certified technicians. Equipment calibration and quality control testing were conducted daily before operation. Standard phantoms were regularly used for precision and accuracy verification to ensure stable equipment performance. The operation process strictly followed the equipment operation manual and standardized testing procedures. Diagnostic criteria: Based on the World Health Organization (WHO) diagnostic criteria for osteoporosis and combined with Chinese population characteristics, the following diagnostic classification was established: normal bone mass (UBD T-score >−1.0), low bone mass (−2.5 < UBD T-score≤−1.0), osteoporosis (UBD T-score≤−2.5). This study used UBD T-score≤−2.5 as the diagnostic criterion for osteoporosis, which demonstrates good correlation with the internationally used DXA bone density testing standard [20, 21].

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was conducted using R version 4.2.2. We checked continuous variables for normality with the Shapiro-Wilk test. Normally distributed data were reported as mean ± standard deviation (x̄ ± s), and comparisons between groups were made using independent t-tests. For non-normally distributed data, we used median and interquartile range [M (Q1, Q3)] and applied the Mann-Whitney U test for group comparisons. Categorical data were expressed as counts and percentages [n (%)], with group differences assessed using χ² tests or Fisher’s exact test when appropriate. For variable selection and model building, we first identified significant variables through univariate logistic regression. These were then included in a multivariate logistic regression model, refined using the backward elimination approach. We calculated odds ratios (OR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI). Using the multivariate regression results, we developed a risk prediction model for osteoporosis in older adults, visualized with a nomogram. To evaluate model performance, we assessed discrimination by computing the area under the curve (AUC) and its 95% CI using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves. Calibration was examined with calibration curves to check how well predicted probabilities matched actual outcomes. We also used decision curve analysis (DCA) to evaluate clinical usefulness, measuring net clinical benefit across various threshold probabilities to gauge the model’s practical value in decision-making. We compared the predictive performance of five machine learning algorithms: Logistic Regression (LR), Random Forest (RF), Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGB), Support Vector Machine (SVM), and Naive Bayes (NBM). Each was run with default settings, and we evaluated them using metrics like AUC, sensitivity, specificity, F1 score, positive predictive value, and negative predictive value. Radar charts were used to visualize each model’s performance across these metrics. For variable importance, we used the Random Forest model to assess each predictor’s contribution, measured by Mean Decrease Accuracy, and created plots to rank variable importance. For validation, we split Center A’s data into training and internal validation sets (7:3 ratio), while Center B’s data served as an external validation set to test the model’s generalizability across centers. Model stability and external validity were confirmed by comparing performance across datasets. Additionally, we conducted subgroup analyses to assess model robustness across centers, performing subgroup analyses for key baseline characteristics (age, education level, occupation, blood pressure, exercise, milk consumption, hemoglobin). Interaction testing evaluated significant differences in associations between centers, with all analyses adjusted for main model covariates. All statistical tests were two-tailed, with a significance threshold of P < 0.05.

Machine learning algorithm implementation and optimization

To ensure reproducibility and address potential overfitting, we implemented a systematic approach for machine learning model development. All analyses were conducted using R version 4.2.2 with the following packages: randomForest (version 4.7–1.1), xgboost (version 1.6.0.1), e1071 (version 1.7–12) for SVM, and naivebayes (version 0.9.7). Hyperparameter configuration: Initial model training was performed using default parameters to establish baseline performance, followed by systematic hyperparameter optimization using 5-fold cross-validation on the training set. Supplementary Table S1 Summarizes the final optimized parameters for each algorithm.

Model Optimization Process: 5-fold cross-validation was implemented on the training set to assess model stability and select optimal hyperparameters. Grid search was employed to identify optimal parameter combinations, with AUC as the primary optimization metric. Models were evaluated on both training and validation sets simultaneously to identify and mitigate overfitting. Early stopping criteria were applied where applicable. The model demonstrating the best balance between training performance and validation stability was selected for each algorithm.

Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analysis

Table 2 presents the results of univariate and multivariate logistic regression analysis of factors associated with osteoporosis. Univariate analysis showed that female gender (OR: 1.223) and increasing age (OR: 1.065) significantly increased disease risk, while higher BMI (OR: 0.951) had protective effects. Primary school education and above were all protective factors compared to illiteracy (OR: 0.628–0.473). Compared to primary industry, other occupation types (secondary industry, service industry, public institutions, unemployed, etc.) had lower risks (OR: 0.367–0.599). Increased heart rate was associated with increased risk (OR: 1.016), while elevated DBP was a protective factor (OR: 0.979). Daily exercise (OR: 0.636), 1–3 h of exercise (OR: 0.621), frequent drinking (OR: 0.726), and milk consumption (OR: 0.689) all showed protective effects. Elevated Hb, WBC, ALT, TC, and TG were associated with decreased risk, while elevated SCr was related to increased risk. Multivariate regression showed that independent risk factors were age (OR: 1.048) and heart rate (OR: 1.015). Protective factors included BMI (OR: 0.968), primary and middle school education (OR: 0.786, 0.745), occupation types (secondary industry, other occupations, unemployed OR: 0.778–0.385), DBP (OR: 0.986), daily exercise and exercise duration (OR: 0.721, 0.641), milk consumption (OR: 0.774), Hb (OR: 0.990), and TG (OR: 0.923).

Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analysis of risk factors associated with osteoporosis diagnosis

Construction of elderly osteoporosis risk prediction model and nomogram

Based on multivariate logistic regression results, an elderly osteoporosis risk prediction nomogram was constructed (see Fig. 2). Each predictive factor corresponds to a scoring axis in the figure: take the value along the variable axis and connect it upward to the score axis to obtain the factor score; sum all scores and map downward to the risk probability axis to obtain the individual osteoporosis occurrence probability. The nomogram shows that age has the most significant impact on the total score, with the widest scoring range; BMI and Hb levels rank high as protective factors; education level, occupation type, exercise habits, and daily milk consumption reflect the stable contribution of socioeconomic and health behaviors to risk prediction.

Model performance evaluation and clinical utility assessment

Figure 3 shows the model’s performance in the training set, internal validation set, and external validation set. The ROC curves and AUC values were 0.687 (95% CI 0.674–0.700), 0.675 (0.655–0.696), and 0.679 (0.657–0.701), respectively, indicating stable discriminative ability and cross-center generalizability. The optimal cutoff points determined in the training set, internal validation set, and external validation set were approximately 0.230, 0.297, and 0.196, respectively. At these thresholds, the model achieved sensitivities of 57.5%, 57.7%, and 47.0%, and specificities of 69.9%, 69.0%, and 75.4% on the three datasets, respectively. The calibration curves in all three datasets were close to the ideal diagonal line, indicating high consistency between predicted probabilities and actual incidence rates. Decision curve analysis validated the clinical utility of these thresholds, showing net clinical benefit across all datasets when risk probabilities ranged from 0.1 to 0.6. For practical clinical application, we recommend using 0.25–0.30 as the threshold range for high-risk classification. Considering the observed osteoporosis prevalence of 25.3% and the model’s performance characteristics, this threshold can identify approximately 30–35% of the elderly population as high-risk while covering 50–60% of actual osteoporosis cases, demonstrating good clinical practicality.

In the external validation set, occupation type maintained the top position (approximately 18), closely followed by daily exercise time and exercise frequency, while others such as diastolic blood pressure scored lower (≤ 4). Overall, occupation type, daily exercise time, and hemoglobin demonstrated high and stable importance across all datasets, while the importance ranking of other variables showed certain variations.

Discussion

This study employed a two-center cross-sectional design to construct and validate an osteoporosis prediction model for elderly populations based on multi-dimensional indicators. Through multivariate logistic regression analysis, age and heart rate were identified as independent risk factors for osteoporosis, while BMI, education level, occupation type, exercise habits, daily milk consumption, hemoglobin, and triglycerides demonstrated protective effects. The model exhibited stable performance across multiple validation sets and outperformed other algorithms, with occupation type, exercise time, and hemoglobin showing high importance across all datasets, reflecting the comprehensive influence of socioeconomic, lifestyle, and physiological indicators. Compared to traditional single-factor assessment approaches, this model integrates multi-dimensional data including demographic characteristics, physiological indicators, lifestyle factors, and laboratory examinations, and achieves individualized risk assessment through nomogram visualization tools, providing an effective instrument for early screening and precision prevention of osteoporosis in elderly populations.

The value of multi-dimensional indicator integration in osteoporosis assessment is supported by research evidence. Wang et al. [22] demonstrated that traditional Chinese medicine employs multi-factor formulations for osteoporosis treatment, showing great potential with the application of modern scientific tools. Porcelli et al.‘s [23] proteomic research revealed that osteoporosis involves abnormal expression of multiple protein markers, with single markers having insufficient specificity, while combined multi-marker detection can provide more comprehensive assessment. This study integrates demographic, physiological, lifestyle, and laboratory multi-dimensional information, demonstrating the practical application value of this concept in osteoporosis risk prediction. The key predictive factors identified through multivariate logistic regression analysis possess significant clinical implications. Age, as the most important risk factor, increases osteoporosis risk by 4.8% per year, reflecting the profound impact of age-related physiological changes on bone metabolism. With advancing age, osteoclast activity increases while osteoblast function declines, disrupting the balance between bone formation and resorption. Simultaneously, declining sex hormone levels, weakened vitamin D synthesis capacity, and reduced calcium absorption efficiency collectively contribute to accelerated bone loss [24]. Heart rate, as an independent risk factor, demonstrates remarkable predictive value with an OR of 1.015, fully illustrating the central role of cardiovascular health status in bone metabolism. Stein et al. [25] study similarly confirmed this viewpoint, showing significant associations between increased heart rate and decreased bone density, possibly related to sympathetic nervous system activation, inflammatory responses, and vascular dysfunction, suggesting that cardiovascular disease and osteoporosis share common pathophysiological foundations. The attenuation of gender effects from univariate (OR: 1.223, P < 0.001) to multivariate analysis (OR: 0.940, P = 0.383) indicates significant confounding by BMI, lifestyle behaviors, and occupational factors that mediate gender-related osteoporosis risk. This suggests that sex-specific prevention strategies should prioritize modifiable risk factors rather than biological sex alone.

Each unit increase in BMI reduces osteoporosis risk by 3.2%, reflecting the positive impact of appropriate weight loading on bone remodeling. This finding is consistent with Zhao et al.‘s [26] research conclusions regarding the positive correlation between BMI and bone density. Appropriate weight loading can stimulate osteoblast activity, promote bone matrix synthesis, while estrogen-like substances produced by adipose tissue regulate bone metabolism. Although the protective effect of education level has relatively small predictive strength, middle school education compared to illiteracy still reduces osteoporosis risk by 25.5%, indicating the important value of socioeconomic status in bone health maintenance. Occupation type shows significant protective effects, with unemployed populations having 54% lower osteoporosis risk compared to agricultural workers, reflecting the differential impact of various occupational exposures and lifestyles on bone health [27, 28].

Exercise habits demonstrate important protective effects, with daily exercise reducing osteoporosis risk by 27.9% compared to no exercise, and 1–3 h of exercise time reducing risk by 35.9% compared to ≤ 1 h. Cheng et al.‘s [29] meta-analysis confirmed the beneficial effects of regular exercise on bone density, where exercise activates osteocyte signaling pathways through mechanical stimulation, promoting osteoblast differentiation and bone matrix deposition. Daily milk consumption reduces osteoporosis risk by 22.6%, primarily due to adequate intake of calcium and vitamin D, providing essential materials for bone mineral deposition [30]. Each 1 g/L increase in hemoglobin level reduces osteoporosis risk by 1%, with its protective effect possibly related to overall nutritional status, hematopoietic function, and oxygen transport capacity it reflects. Good hemoglobin levels indicate active metabolism, facilitating normal bone tissue renewal [31]. Each 1mmol/L increase in triglycerides reduces osteoporosis risk by 7.7%, and although the predictive strength is relatively small, it indicates that lipid metabolism status has certain regulatory effects on bone metabolism.

Compared to traditional statistical methods, the machine learning algorithms introduced in this study exhibited characteristics different from traditional logistic regression in handling complex clinical data. Among the five prediction models, the logistic regression model demonstrated the best comprehensive performance and stability. Although the random forest model achieved a perfect AUC of 1.000 in the training set, its performance declined significantly in the internal validation set and external validation set, dropping to 0.651 and 0.626 respectively, indicating severe overfitting. The logistic regression model achieved AUCs of 0.687, 0.675, and 0.679 in the training set, internal validation set, and external validation set respectively, demonstrating excellent generalization ability and cross-center applicability. The study by Riley et al. [32] similarly confirmed that simple models often have better generalization ability in clinical prediction modeling, and complex algorithms do not always bring performance improvements.

Variable importance analysis based on the random forest model revealed the key factor composition of osteoporosis risk prediction. Results showed that occupation type exhibited the highest importance scores across all datasets, reaching approximately 30, 18, and 18 points in the training set, internal validation set, and external validation set respectively, fully demonstrating the central role of socioeconomic status in bone health. Daily exercise time ranked second, with importance scores maintained in the range of 12–25 points across datasets, reflecting the stable contribution of lifestyle factors to osteoporosis prediction. Hemoglobin level also showed high and stable importance, with scores in all datasets ranging from 10 to 15 points, reflecting the value of nutritional status and hematological indicators in bone metabolism assessment [33]. Notably, although age appeared as the most important risk factor in logistic regression analysis, it ranked relatively lower in variable importance ranking, which may be related to differences in variable importance assessment mechanisms between different algorithms. Random forest focuses more on the contribution of variables to model prediction accuracy, while logistic regression is more concerned with statistical significance and effect size of variables.

Cross-center external validation is the gold standard for evaluating model generalization ability, and this study’s use of Center B’s independent dataset for external validation holds significant importance [34, 35]. Despite significant demographic differences, lifestyle characteristic differences, and osteoporosis prevalence differences between the two centers (25.3% vs. 20.69%), the logistic regression model still achieved an AUC of 0.679 (95% CI: 0.657–0.701) in the external validation set, showing only a 0.4% improvement compared to internal validation, fully demonstrating the model’s cross-center applicability and good generalization ability. We also examined the prevalence difference between centers (25.3% vs. 20.69%) through subgroup analyses, which demonstrated stable model performance across most variables (6 out of 7 showed no significant interactions, P >0.05). Baseline differences in exercise patterns and nutritional supplementation between centers may contribute to this prevalence variation, while confirming the model’s applicability across different centers. This result indicates that the model can effectively address heterogeneity between different medical institutions and has the potential for widespread application in different clinical environments. Notably, the model maintained stable performance when facing inter-center differences, which may benefit from the comprehensive modeling strategy of multi-dimensional health indicators. By integrating multi-level information including demographic, physiological, lifestyle, and laboratory indicators, the stability of the model was effectively enhanced. The rigorous internal and external validation results provide a solid evidence-based foundation for the clinical translation and application of the model.

Compared with recent international studies, this research demonstrates unique clinical value and methodological advantages. Although the nomogram model constructed by Tong et al. [6] based on UK Biobank achieved a high C-index (0.823), it primarily targeted European Caucasian populations, whereas our study specifically focuses on Chinese elderly populations, filling the gap in osteoporosis prediction for East Asian populations. While the machine learning model developed by Si et al. [36] using NHANES data achieved an AUC of 0.972, it relied on self-reported diagnoses and lacked external validation. In contrast, our study employed a two-center external validation design, and although the AUC was 0.679, it provides a more practical screening solution for resource-limited settings through UBD diagnostic methods and integration of multi-dimensional health indicators, demonstrating stronger clinical generalizability.

The multi-dimensional health indicator-driven osteoporosis risk prediction model constructed in this study has important guiding significance for current prevention policies. The study found that socioeconomic factors such as occupation type and education level have prominent importance in osteoporosis prediction, suggesting that osteoporosis prevention policy formulation should develop differentiated prevention strategies for different occupational groups. Agricultural workers and low-education populations showed higher osteoporosis risk and should become key intervention targets, with comprehensive measures including health education promotion, nutritional improvement programs, and exercise promotion projects to reduce their disease risk [37, 38]. The protective effects of exercise habits and nutritional supplementation provide scientific basis for policy direction. Governments should vigorously promote national fitness programs, build community sports facilities, particularly exercise programs suitable for elderly populations, while strengthening nutritional health education and promoting dairy consumption and calcium supplementation.

For developing countries with similar development levels to China, this study has important reference value. These countries commonly face challenges such as accelerated population aging, increased chronic disease burden, and unbalanced medical resources. The prediction model established in the study integrates easily obtainable clinical indicators without requiring expensive testing equipment, making it suitable for promotion and application in resource-limited environments. The risk factors and protective factors identified by the model have cross-cultural universality, providing a reference framework for developing countries to formulate osteoporosis prevention strategies that conform to their national conditions [39, 40]. The model stability and generalization ability confirmed through multi-center validation provide reliable guarantee for its application in different regions and medical systems, helping to improve the early identification and prevention level of osteoporosis in developing countries.

This study has certain limitations. First, although we employed a comprehensive variable selection approach including both statistically significant and clinically relevant factors, some important variables such as family history, vitamin D levels, and medication use were not available in our dataset, which may limit the model’s completeness. Second, UBD has certain differences in diagnostic accuracy compared to DXA, which represents one of the limitations of this study. However, considering that the purpose of this research is to construct a risk prediction model suitable for primary care screening, the application of UBD has practical significance and promotional value. Third, the cross-sectional design limits our ability to assess the model’s predictive capacity for future fractures, which represents the most clinically relevant outcome in osteoporosis. To address this critical limitation, we plan to conduct a prospective cohort study with 3–5 years of follow-up to evaluate fracture prediction performance and validate the model’s clinical utility in preventing osteoporotic fractures among elderly populations, promoting the transformation from traditional passive treatment to active prevention medical models.

Conclusion

This study successfully constructed and validated a multi-dimensional health indicator-driven osteoporosis risk prediction model for elderly populations, which demonstrated excellent stability and generalization ability across multiple validation sets. The model provides an effective tool for early screening and precision prevention of osteoporosis, supporting evidence-based clinical decision-making and individualized risk management in elderly populations.

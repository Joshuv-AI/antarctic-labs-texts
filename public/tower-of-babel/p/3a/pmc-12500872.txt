Towards modular intelligent design method of subway station spatial with PointNet++
Authors: Peng Liu, Longlong Zhang, Youman Wei
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
PointNet++ has the functions of object recognition and semantic segmentation, and performs well in the recognition and classification of similar objects. It has been widely used in outdoor 3D scenes. The functional space layout of subway station buildings has the characteristics of similarity and replicability, so it is of great significance to adopt intelligent algorithm to modular design of functional space. In this study, plane data of subway stations in several cities were collected. Build 3D model and export cloud point data (X, Y,Z, rgbC), then enhance the data. The data set is divided into training set, verification set and test set according to the ratio of 8:1:1. The PointNet++ is used to train the 3D data set. Results show that the 3D data set derived from the building model runs well in the PointNet++ network, and can realize the effective transmission of information. Firstly, Pointnet++ model’s recognition and classification results of training set meet expectations, and the stable values of Training mean loss and Training accuracy are 0.42 and 0.76, respectively. Secondly, the training model is used to predict the test set. The Eval avg class Accuracy and Eval Accuracy are 0.80 + and 0.75 + respectively, and the mean intersection over union is about 60%. Thirdly, the prediction results of the test set in the training model show that the Ground Truth and Predict are highly matched, and the model has a strong self-learning ability and can actively optimize the scheme space. This paper proposes a modular design method based on deep learning of 3D spatial data, which can identify and classify architectural space efficiently and accurately.

Introduction

At present, computer intelligent assistance technology has been widely used in various fields such as artificial intelligence, unmanned driving, and robotics1.With the increase of functional requirements and the improvement of intelligence degree, the traditional design methods have obvious limitations in intelligent facility integration, energy-saving design embedding, parametric facade design and other aspects. In recent years, research on interdisciplinary application has become the main direction in the field of architectural design, such as: parametric facade design2, parametric form modeling3, etc., using “deep learning”, “neural network” and other data processing technologies to effectively solve the above problems, which promoting the rapid development of the construction industry. In particular, 3D data point cloud segmentation, as a basic technology for point cloud data processing and analysis, has been widely used in many fields, and PointNet model is an effective tool for processing 3D data.

Subway stations are widely distributed and abundant in urban areas. This type of building space has similar functional layout, with a high degree of “modularity” and repeatable design methods, which is suitable for searching for common features through deep learning methods. At present, subway station design is mostly carried out through the coordination of various majors, which has the following disadvantages: (1) Due to differences in majors, it is impossible to form a unified design logic and share and store information among participating majors4; (2) The “design experience” between similar projects cannot be effectively transferred or replicated5, resulting in duplication and inefficiency of many efforts. Therefore, the subway station architectural space design needs to establish a unified design logic with the help of other disciplines such as computer technology, and then identify, classify, learn and store the design language or information. Deep learning technology is very suitable for processing data information, producing and fitting change rules through multi-variable input6.

At present, there are many researches on point cloud data processing, and good results have been achieved, but most of them focus on 2D image recognition and point cloud segmentation. For example, reference7 proposes an end-to-end semantic segmentation framework for large-scale 3D scenes based on weak supervision to improve the classification accuracy of point clouds. Reference8 proposes a point cloud segmentation neural network model based on normal vector optimization, which is suitable for a dam environment. With the continuous development of deep learning theory, the technology methods of automatic object recognition and segmentation through 3D point cloud data are gradually increasing. In order to solve the problem of low segmentation accuracy of indoor 3D point cloud data, reference9 proposed a “3D-2D-3D” conversion mode to improve the recognition accuracy of spatial information. However, due to the disordered and unstructured characteristics of point cloud10, it is difficult for point cloud data to be directly used in deep learning, and the model can only be trained by converting 3D point cloud coordinates into 2D planes. However, in the process of data projection conversion, there will be a lot of information loss, which leads to inefficient accumulation of data and poor application effect. The PointNet network model proposed by Qi et al.11 at Stanford University, this method starts directly from the point cloud data, without any intermediate conversion operation, and solves the above problems from the source, thus retaining the inherent information of the point cloud. However, PointNet’s local feature extraction ability is poor, which makes it difficult to analyze complex scenes. Then, PointNet++ 12 improved on this basis and proposed a multi-level feature extraction structure, which can effectively extract local and global features. In addition, from the perspective of PointNet++ network framework, object classification and component semantic segmentation are operated in a unified framework with high integrity13. Therefore, this kind of neural network is very efficient and can be widely used in many fields.

The above content gives a detailed description of the modular characteristics of subway building space and the application of PointNet++ . With the iteration of intelligent algorithms and the development of large databases, deep learning has gradually shifted from early 2D plane recognition to 3D shape recognition10. In particular, it is more prominent in the intelligent design of auxiliary buildings14. According to literature statistics, 3D shape classification methods of intelligent algorithms have experienced diversified development in recent years, and can be roughly summarized into five types of network models (Fig. 1). Compared with random shapes, intelligent algorithms have significant advantages in the recognition and classification of the same type of shapes. For example, SEmi-Supervised Convolutional Autoencoder (SECA) Network can automatically extract relevant features from GPS segments and automatically supplement missing information15. PointNet++ and PointSIFT can efficiently use multi-layer perceptrons to learn similar shape features16. In terms of technical application, CNN is used to extract multi-modal features and expand scene classification17. The above literature shows that deep learning shows high accuracy and efficiency for the recognition and classification of 3D shapes of the same type. Subway station buildings have similar features of functional space layout. Therefore, the intelligent algorithm has a good theoretical basis in the modular aided design of buildings.Fig. 1A chronological overview of 3D shape classification methods based on deep learning.

A chronological overview of 3D shape classification methods based on deep learning.

To sum up, PointNet++ model has advantages in the identification and extraction of 3D point cloud data features. References research show that this technology is mostly applied to outdoor complex scenes, and there are few studies on indoor application in buildings. Therefore, semantic segmentation of 3D point cloud data in indoor scenes is still a challenging task. In order to verify the applicability of PointNet++ for cloud point segmentation of architectural space, this study takes subway station design as the research object to segment the point cloud data information of internal functional space. Then, through data enhancement, model training was conducted to further verify the feasibility and effectiveness of PointNet++ model combined with deep learning to automatically generate architectural space, so as to achieve the goal of computer-aided subway station architectural design.

Point Cloud is a collection of points on the 3D object surface. Each point contains 3D coordinates (X,Y,Z), as well as information such as RGB, Intensity, or normal vectors, which can represent the characteristics accurately24. The 3D information carried by it can be transmitted “end to end” without the need for secondary format conversion25. Building 3D model can directly export point cloud data. Therefore, this study chose the point cloud data format.

Data collection and collation

Deep learning requires large amounts of data for model training. In order to obtain sufficient point cloud data samples, this study collected 600 subway building planes as the original data. Then the 3D building model is established, and the information of each functional space is calibrated during the modeling process. Finally, the point cloud file is exported. The point cloud file contains XYZ-rgbC (coordinate information, color information and label C). In order to obtain more data, data enhancement code is written by provider.py tool in the process of model training. In addition, in order to ensure the independence of data samples, 600 point cloud data files were divided into training set, verification set and test set according to 8 (480) :1 (60) :1 (60). Training of point cloud data set

The PointNet++ network training strategy is briefly described as: (1) Load the dataset into a 20 * 20 * 20 area and empirically set the block_size to 10. A sample_rate of 1 means that all point clouds in the 20 × 20 × 20 region are randomly sampled at a 10 × 10 size. (2) A total of num_points = 4096 are obtained by sampling, and then sent to the PointNet++ network for training with batch_size of 8. In the above strategy, some parameters can be adjusted to obtain better training results. For example, if we make block_size smaller, we can sample more finely. If we make num_points larger, we will have more points in the cloud. These two modifications can express the spatial characteristics more comprehensively and improve the accuracy of the model. The exact value needs to be determined by testing.

System hyperparameter setting: (batch_size = 8, decay_rate = 0.0001, epoch = 200, learning rate = 0.001, lr_decay = 0.7, model = 'PointNet++ 2_sem_seg’, npoint = 4096, optimizer = ‘Adam’, step_size = 15). Detailed operation steps are as follows: (1) Set the basic parameters; (2) Training mean loss and Training accuracy monitoring; (3) Control of the number of round trip calculations in data set, suppose the number of epoch is 200; (4) Start the operation. In a complete calculation cycle, the variation trend of Training mean loss and Training accuracy data is shown in Fig. 6:Fig. 6Variation curves of training mean loss and training accuracy.

Result analysis

Comparison and analysis of prediction results

PointNet++ model prediction is different from model training in that model prediction requires loading all data sets rather than random sampling. The test set contains a total of 60 point cloud samples. The steps are as follows: Firstly, the data set is loaded into a 20 * 20 * 20 space area, and the block_size is set to 10.0, stride = block_size/2 = 5, stride is usually set to half of the block_size. Secondly, all the data in the 20 * 20 * 20 region is evenly and finely sampled. Finally, the generated point cloud is sent to the network for voting decision (Pointnet++ has built-in voting decision code), vote_num = 5 is set, and the prediction results were voted for 5 rounds, and the maximum value of the average voting score is taken as the optimal solution.

In the above way, the final prediction result output is two obj model files (Ground truth and Predicted value) and a txt predicted point cloud segmentation file. The visualization of the prediction results can be done using MeshLab software. An independent test set of 60 point cloud data was used for model prediction and validation, with a random selection of data for visualization. Accuracy evaluation of prediction results

The evaluation of prediction results is an important part to verify the feasibility of the experimental method. In this paper, the real data and predicted data in the eval.txt file are extracted and analyzed to evaluate the model training effect. Evaluation indicators include: Fit (R-squared), Eval avg class acc, Eval accuracy, Best MIoU, Eval avg class IoU, and each_class_IoU. These indicators are used to analyze and evaluate the effect of model prediction.

Test of goodness for fit

Fit test is to evaluate the degree of agreement between the predicted results of the existing model and the actual situation. The Training Loss and Eval Loss are usually selected as the basic parameters to diagnose the quality of the model, where the Loss function represents the difference between the predicted value and the true value. The changes of the two basic parameters can measure the degree of fitting of the model27. Figure 7 shows the fit analysis of the spatial prediction model for subway stations.Fig. 7Training Mean Loss and Eval Mean Loss change curves.

Training Mean Loss and Eval Mean Loss change curves.

During model training, the Training Mean Loss curve and the Eval Mean Loss curve should be used to assess the fit of the training results through comparative analysis. The results in Fig. 7 show that: (1) The Training Mean Loss curve and Eval Mean Loss curve have the same trend, with a rapid convergence stage 30 rounds ago and slowly approaching a stable state, it indicates that the model converges well. (2) During the whole training process, the Training Mean Loss is relatively lower than the Eval Mean Loss, and the two parameters eventually stabilized at around 0.4, which indicates that the robustness of model training is good (The abnormal fluctuation of the Eval mean loss around 150 rounds is caused by data anomaly factors, such as complex spatial shapes leading to data anomalies). Overall, this model has a good fit, which means that the predicted values are highly reliable.

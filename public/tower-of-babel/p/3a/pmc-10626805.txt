Utility of peripheral blood macrophage factor Apo10 and TKTL1 as markers in distinguishing malignant from benign lung nodules: a protocol for a prospective cohort study in Southern China
Authors: Chuanbo Xie, Qiyu Huang, Yuying Liu
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionLung nodules are one of the most prevalent diseases. Medical imaging methods have a high false positive rate for distinguishing malignant nodules from benign nodules. Therefore, developing new technologies with high accuracy for screening malignant nodules is of great importance for lung nodule surveillance. Use of flow cytometry to detect biomarkers in blood macrophages (epitop detect in macrophages/macrophages) has opened a new era for early and noninvasive diagnosis of cancer. This planned study aims to examine whether the peripheral blood macrophage factors Apo10 and TKTL1 accurately distinguish malignant nodules from benign nodules.Methods and analysesWe plan to enrol in this study 3825 participants with lung nodules who will attend their annual physical examination at Sun Yat-sen University Cancer Center. Apo10 and TKTL1 levels in all patients will be tested at 60 min after their last meal every 6 months during their 3-year follow-up. Biopsy or surgical pathology results will be collected as the gold standard to assess the accuracy of Apo10 and TKTL1 in distinguishing malignant nodules from benign nodules. The sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value, negative predictive value and area under the receiving operating characteristic curve will also be evaluated.Ethics and disseminationThe study is approved by the medical ethics committee of Sun Yat-sen University (SL-G2022-005-02). Introduction

Lung cancer is one of the leading causes of cancer-related death worldwide (18.4% of cancer deaths).1 Only a small proportion of lung cancer patients will survive over 5 years after diagnosis because a large proportion of patients have advanced disease when they are diagnosed.2 The National Lung Cancer Screening Trial suggested that a strategy of three consecutive annual Computed Tomography (CT) screenings may result in 20% lower mortality from lung cancer and 2% overall mortality than screening with chest radiography with a median follow-up time up to 6.5 years.3 Based on this research finding and evidence from other studies, the US Preventive Services Task Force recommends annual CT screening for persons aged 55–80 years with a smoking history of 30 or more pack-years who currently smoke or quit smoking no more than 15 years prior.4

One of the major clinical concerns of low-dose CT (LDCT)-based screening is appropriate management of lung nodules detected on a scan. Previous studies have shown that over 20% of participants who have one or more pulmonary nodules were referred for further investigation, and the proportion of invasive diagnostic procedures ranged from 1% to 4%.5 The risk of major complications was 4.5 complications per 10 000 persons screened, and 25% of the surgical procedures in the National Lung Screening Trial were performed on nodules that were determined to be benign. Accurate biomarkers that can triage malignant lung nodules from benign lung nodules are important for guiding clinical decision-making and have the potential to reduce costs and risk of morbidity and mortality in screening programmes.

Use of flow cytometry to detect biomarkers in blood macrophages (epitop detect in macrophages/macrophages, EDIM) has opened a new era for early and non-invasive diagnosis of cancer.6 7 It can detect biomarkers in macrophages carrying tumour substances in the blood. After macrophages engulf and digest tumour cells or their fragments, they leave the tumour and move into the blood through the vascular system. The tumour substances contained in these macrophages are not diluted by blood but rather become highly concentrated in the cells, which is an important reason for the high sensitivity of EDIM technology and the highly specific detection and elimination of tumour cells by the innate immune system.8 Accordingly, the sensitivity and specificity of EDIM detection were shown to be significantly better than those of other tumour markers. Our previous research9 showed that detection of the peripheral blood macrophage factors Apo10 and TKTL1 had high accuracy in differentiating early-stage lung cancer patients from healthy controls. In this protocol, we aim to further assess the reliability and accuracy of Apo10 and TKTL1 in differentiating malignant lung nodules from benign lung nodules using a prospective cohort study design.

Methods and analysis

Objectives

The primary objective of the study is to assess the efficacy of Apo10 and TKTL1 in differentiating malignant lung nodules from benign lung nodules using a prospective cohort study design. The secondary objective of the study is to develop a risk predictive model to screen high lung risk populations from patients with lung nodules after incorporating Apo10 and TKTL1 levels, sociodemographic characteristics, smoking history, family history of cancer, nodal size, nodal components and nodal size changes.

Settings

This study will be carried out in the Cancer Prevention Center at Sun Yat-sen University Cancer Center (SYSUCC) in Guangzhou, Guangdong, People’s Republic of China. The Cancer Prevention Center of SYSUCC is one of the largest physical examination centres in South China, and over 50 000 persons attend their annual physical examinations at this centre.

Design

This is a prospective cohort study aimed at determining the efficacy of Apo10 and TKTL1 in screening high-risk patients with lung cancer with lung nodules by following them up to 3 years.

Participants and recruitment

The investigated cohort will involve participants who attend their annual physical examination at the Cancer Prevention Center of SYSUCC. Healthcare professionals at SYSUCC will be informed about the protocol and their potential role, and an experienced researcher will be on-site to provide coordination.

All participants will meet the following criteria: (1) lung nodule size between 5 and 8 mm on the previous CT scan; (2) plan to have annual CT examination in the following 3 years; (3) no history of immunodeficiency diseases; (4) not taking antirheumatic drugs such as methotrexate, sulfasalazine, hydroxychloroquine or celecoxib; (5) no prior history of cancer and (6) absence of infectious diseases.

Sample size

We will stratify the participants into three risk groups (high-risk group, intermediate-risk group and low-risk group) and compare the rate of lung cancer incidence across these three groups. According to our experience and previous literature,9 10 the lung cancer incidence of participants with lung nodules is approximately 4%; the lung cancer rates in the high-risk group, intermediate-risk group and low-risk group are 20 per 1000 person-years, 10 per 1000 person-years and <5 per 1000 person-years, respectively. Our pilot study indicated proportions of the high-risk group, intermediate-risk group and low-risk group among patients with lung nodules of 10%, 23% and 67%, respectively. Therefore, to guarantee at least one patient in each risk group and that the difference across these three groups is significant (ɑ=0.05 and 1−β=0.8), we will need to include 3825 participants with lung nodules.

CT examinations

All participants will undergo annual chest CT examinations for 3 years. The characteristics of the lung nodules, such as size, nodule components (solid, part solid or non-solid) and margins (smooth, somewhat smooth, slightly irregular and irregular), will be recorded for all participants.

Apo10 and TKTL1 testing

We will collect 2.7 mL EDTA-anticoagulation venous blood from all participants at 60 min after their last meal. The blood samples will be stored at room temperature (15°C–25°C) before testing. IntraPrep Permeabilisation Reagent (Beckman Coulter, Krefeld, Germany) will be used for intracellular staining performed with antibodies against CD14 (OFC-14D) and CD16 (Hi-16a). After permeabilisation, the two intracellular antibodies TKTL1 (phycoerythrin-conjugated, provided by Zyagnum AG, Pfungstadt, Germany) and Apo10 (fluorescein isothiocyanate, provided by Zyagnum AG, Pfungstadt, Germany) will be added. One thousand macrophages (CD14+/CD16+) will be selected, and analyses will be performed using BD FACSDiva software V.8.0 (BD Biosciences, Heidelberg, Germany). All incubations in this protocol will be conducted at room temperature and in the dark. The EDIM score of the participants will be obtained by multiplying the proportion of CD14+/CD16+ cells containing Apo10 and TKTL1 by 10. The EDIM-combined score is calculated by adding the two scores of Apo10 and TKTL1 (APT).

Measurement of other lung cancer-related biomarkers

Four serum tumour markers will be measured before the CT scan. Carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) will be detected by a chemiluminescence method (Cobas e602, Roche Diagnostics, Germany); neuron-specific enolase (NSE), cytokeratin 19 fragment antigen 21-1 (CyFra21-1) and squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) antigen will be detected by an electrochemical luminescence method (Cobas e602, Roche Diagnostics, Germany) using commercial assay kits according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The reference intervals of CEA, NSE, CyFra21-1 and SCC in the serum are 0–5.0 ng/mL, 0–16.3 ng/mL, 0–3.30 ng/mL and 0–2.7 ng/mL, respectively.

Follow-up

The follow-up period will be 3 years in total. We will test all participants’ Apo10, TKTL1 and other lung cancer biomarkers every 6 months. All the participants will undergo LDCT or contrast CT examinations every year. We will follow-up all patients regarding outcomes, for example, lung cancer diagnosis and size changes in the nodules during the follow-up period, by reviewing medical records, telephone calls and face-to-face interviews. The WHO classification for lung cancer will be used to classify lung masses, with staging based on the eighth edition of the Tumour Node Metastasis (TNM) classification for Lung Cancer of the International Cancer Control and the American Joint Committee on Cancer staging system. Statistical analysis

Mean and standard deviation (±SD) or median and IQRs will be used to describe the distributions of Apo10, TKTL1, APT and the other lung cancer-related biomarker levels across the study period. Percentages will be used to describe sex and other categorical variables. We will stratify the participants into high-risk (APT score ≥ 260 and Apo10 ≥ 140), intermediate-risk (APT ≥ 245 and 130 ≤ Apo10＜140; APT＜260 and Apo10 ≥ 140) and low-risk (APT＜245 and 130 ≤ Apo10＜140; Apo10 < 130) populations according to APT and Apo10 levels. We will compare the 3-year lung cancer incidence across these three risk groups using the χ2 test. A nomogram that incorporates the Apo10 and TKTL1 levels, characteristics of the lung nodules and sociodemographic characteristics of the participants will be established to help accurately distinguish malignant nodules from benign nodules. The receiver operating characteristic curves will be generated and the area under the curve will be calculated to compare the diagnostic accuracy of Apo10, TKTL1, APT and the traditional cancer biomarkers in screening malignant nodules. Differences will be considered statistically significant at p <0.05.

Patient and public involvement

Neither patients nor the public has been involved in the design and conduct of this study, the choice of outcome measures or recruitment. There are no plans to include patients/public in dissemination efforts.

Ethics and dissemination

This study is approved by the institutional review board of SYSUCC (SYSUCC, ID:SL-G2022-005-02), and all procedures will be conducted according to the principles expressed in the Declaration of Helsinki. The authors will enrol participants using a chart review blinded to their personal identifying information (name, address, etc). Therefore, this study will not violate the rights or welfare of the subjects. Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants once the study’s objectives and procedures are fully explained.

Discussion

Overdiagnosis of lung cancer by LDCT screening has raised concerns globally. A previous study suggested that 96.4% of LDCT-screened pulmonary nodules are benign. How to distinguish benign lung nodules from malignant lung nodules is important for lung nodule surveillance.

Artificial intelligence and liquid biopsy are two widely used approaches to reduce the false-positive rate of LDCT-screened lung nodules.11 12 Previous studies have demonstrated that deep learning may improve the performance of lung cancer diagnosis by capturing specific malignant features from LDCT images. A majority of research has assessed the performance of liquid biopsies for diagnosis of pulmonary nodules, including Circulating tumour DNA (ctDNA), microRNAs (miRNAs),13 circulating tumour cells (CTCs)14 and autoantibodies15 (7AAb: P53, NY-ESO-1, CAGE, GBU4-5, SOX2, HuD and MAGE A4).16

The principle of EDIM is based on cancer cells expressing unique epitopes, which the immune system recognises as foreign, followed by attack by immune cells such as macrophages. EDIM measures the amount of antibodies against these epitopes in the blood, which can provide an indication of the presence of cancer. Compared with imaging modalities such as CT, EDIM has several advantages as a diagnostic tool for early cancer detection. First, it is minimally invasive and does not expose patients to ionising radiation or require use of contrast agents. Conversely, CT and MRI both require imaging procedures that can be more invasive and may carry some risks. This may be particularly advantageous for patients who are sensitive to these agents or who need to undergo repeated testing. Second, EDIM has the potential to detect cancer at an earlier stage than imaging modalities, as it can detect trace amounts of cancer cells or precancerous lesions that are not yet visible on imaging. This is because the immune system may detect cancer cells before they become detectable by imaging due to the presence of small amounts of cancer cells that have not yet grown large enough, thereby potentially allowing for earlier intervention and improved outcomes. Third, EDIM may be less expensive than imaging modalities, which can be costly due to equipment and staffing requirements. This might make EDIM a more accessible option for patients, particularly those who may not have access to specialised imaging centres. Last, EDIM can be repeated more frequently than imaging modalities, which may be limited by the amount of radiation exposure a patient can safely receive. This would be particularly advantageous for monitoring patients who have a higher risk of developing cancer or for monitoring the effectiveness of cancer treatments over time.

There are some limitations of this study. First, the follow-up time is only 3 years, and some indolent malignant nodules might not have been diagnosed during the follow-up period, which might have reduced the positive predictive value. Second, similar to other prospective studies, follow-up loss is a concern for this study. Thus, we will restrict the participants to those who plan to attend their physical examinations at SYSUCC in the following 3 years.

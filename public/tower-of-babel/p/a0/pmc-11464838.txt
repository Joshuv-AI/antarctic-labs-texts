Influence of stem and leaf phenotypes, physiological responses and cellular ultrastructure on defoliated sugarcane cultivars
Authors: Qiang Liang, Xiaoyan Liu, Xiu-Peng Song, Yijie Li, Li Lin, Krishan K. Verma, Guang-Feng Liang, Dong-Mei Li, Yang-Rui Li, Shanhai Lin
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Defoliation is a primary agronomic traits, its variation depends on different plant species or cultivars. The present article assess the leaf morphological responses, oxidative metabolites and enzymatic activities at sheath base of sugarcane cultivars during defoliation stage of plant leaves. The mature leaf sheath of GT47 strongly wrapped to the stem, and no stem was exposed. The upper and lower edges of the immature fusing abscission zone were parallel, and slightly lower browning area (+ 3 to + 7 leaf position). The ROC22 cultivar was monitored highest leaf sheath-based cellulose and lignin content, followed by GT60 and GT47. Peroxidase activity was higher in leaf sheath base edge (ROC22) as compare to other cultivars. The malondialdehyde content was found highest in GT60, followed by ROC22, and GT47. The exo-β-1,4-glucanase/ cellobiohydrolase activity was found highest in the margin of GT47 than lateral and medial axis of ROC22 and GT60. The axis activity increased exponentially, and ROC22 gradually decreased from the periphery of the mid-axis and lower than GT47 and GT60 in the lateral and mid-axis of leaf. In conclusion, the mature leaves are easy to defoliate mainly loose leaf sheaths, large leaf sheath inclination angles, more deformation during the growth period of the abscission zone, early with large cracks, and slow browning process. Leaf sheaths with high fibre and lignin content showed significant hardness and thickness. The sugarcane cultivars showed positive correlation between peroxidase and malondialdehyde content with the browning process at the base of mature leaf sheaths.

Introduction

Sugarcane (Saccharum spp. Hybrid) is a gramineous crop that naturally undergoes defoliation. China, following Brazil, India, Thailand, is the largest sugarcane producing country. The sugar industry contributing significantly to China’s GDP through high socio-economic developmental focus which is aimed at creating jobs, foreign exchange earnings, organizing resources, providing a source of income, and developing transport and communication networks1,2. The large-scale migration of rural labor to developed cities, high labor costs, and poor mechanization of sugarcane production restrict the sustainable development of cane industries in China2,3. Sugarcane leaf stripping has always been major problem affecting sugarcane production around the globe. The requirement of labour for leaf stripping estimated to account for more than 60% of the total labour during manual harvesting stage4. The limited countries, such as the United States, use to burn sugarcane leaves before harvesting. Brazil, used to burn sugarcane leaves before harvesting that have legislated and mandated green harvesting and cannot burn sugarcane leaves before harvesting. The raw cane defoliated manually or mechanically in China, then supplied to the sugar industries for processing. This approach improved the quality of raw cane and minimize the loss of sugar during processing. However, it is manual harvesting or mechanized harvesting, improving and utilizing sugarcane varieties with better defoliation responses or developing simple defoliation technological strategies to minimize the cost of sugarcane harvesting and improve the double benefits of sugar quality.

The research on sugarcane defoliation is very limited. Initially, researchers aimed to determine whether defoliation of sugarcane would affect agronomic traits and the quality of sugar raw materials. Research has found that manually removing some leaves during the developmental period of sugarcane is more beneficial for improving cane and sucrose yield5. The defoliated varieties not only maintained soluble sugar content but also increased sucrose production6. The effects of defoliation on agronomic traits at different growth stages were studied, and it was found that the moderate defoliation at the grand growth and maturity stages had no adverse effects on cane yield and quality7.

A research group screened and categorized new sugarcane varieties in Guangxi province, China from last few years and collected a large number of sugarcane varieties and germplasm materials with different types of defoliation mechanisms8–10. New sugarcane variety GT60 (Guitang 10-2003) capable for natural defoliation was successfully hybridized and selected by significant defoliation approaches. At the same time, some scientific groups have carried out research on the inheritance of leaf defoliation in sugarcane and found that the selection of combinations has the most significant impact on the defoliation of offspring. It is recommended that breeders pay more attention to parental combinations during screening parents and combinations11. The natural defoliation rate, leaf inclination and defoliation force can be used to quantitatively assess the defoliation type of sugarcane12. In terms of cytoskeletal substances and lytic enzymes, previous studies have found that the cellulose content in the leaf sheaths of easy-to-leave varieties significantly higher than hard-to-leave cultivars. The cellulose content positively correlated with chlorophyll content with water content of sugarcane leaf sheaths. The POD activity in leaves, leaf sheaths, and leaf scars were significantly correlated with the natural defoliation rate. The leaf sheath cellulase activity and plant shedding rate reached the highest during maturity stage, and there were differences in enzyme activities at different leaf positions (from top to bottom). The cellulase activity (+ 10 leaf) was found maximum and pectinase activity similar to the cellulase activity. The easy-to-leaf cultivars significantly higher than hard-to-leaf cultivars, significantly correlated with the natural defoliation rate11,13. The number of living cells in the leaf sheath detachment layer of the easy-shedding varieties were significantly lower than hard-to-shed cultivars. The DNA integrity of the leaf sheath abscission zone of the easy-shedding varieties was considerably lower than resistant cultivars. The DNA damage of the leaf sheaths of easy-shedding cultivars enhanced the loss of leaf position significantly increased. The rate of spontaneous defoliation was negatively correlated with the number of living cells in the sheath detachment zone and significantly correlated with the DNA damage14.

The present research focuses on the differences in sugarcane leaf defoliation morphology and main senescence aspects among different cultivars through phenotypic, anatomical, physiological and biochemical responses. Sugarcane defoliation is extremely easy but more challenging to defoliate. The defoliated type of leaf genotype can be varies in the appearance, cell and physiologic metabolism level, which provides a theoretical basis for further in-depth research.

Materials and methods

Sugarcane cultivars, i.e., GT47, GT60 and ROC22, with significant differences in defoliation were used as research materials. GT47 cultivar is very hard to defoliate. Mature and senescence leaf tightly wrapped from the cane stem, making falling off under strong external force. ROC22 cultivar is easy to lose leaves. The mature leaves form an apparent angle with the cane stem, and only a strong external force can cause the entire leaf to fall off, so GT60 is very easy to defoliate. Mature green leaves form a larger angle with the cane stem, and the whole leaf can fall off with less external force.

Experimental design and data observation

The test materials were planted in February 2021 at the field experiment station of the Sugarcane Research Institute of Guangxi Academy of Agricultural Sciences, Nanning, Guangxi, China (22°50′47″ north and 108°14′51″ east longitude). The use of plants in the present study complies with international, national and/or institutional guidelines. The annual agroclimatic variables were monitored as average temperature (21.6 °C), average maximum temperature (26.2 °C), average minimum temperature (18.5 °C), rainfall (1321 mm), sunshine (1724.6 h), and rainfall days (104.7 d) during demonstration. The experiment was designed according to completely randomized blocks (CRBs), single factor treatment, three biological replicates (n = 3), plot area 42 m2, row length and width 7*1.2 M and five rows per plot. The planting material was 8000 buds /667 m2.

The assessment was made from the basel node to the first leaf, i.e., the first leaf from the top of the stalk with a clearly visible dewlap. In this experiment, the observation and sample collection of sugarcane leaves (+ 5 to + 10 leaf position, top to bottom) were carried out. To conduct phenotypic observations on the leaves and stems of sugarcane cultivars demonstrated in October 2021 and 2022. The indicators include in vitro phenotype observations at the base of the leaf sheaths. The observation areas are as length of leaf, leaf width, leaf sheath length, leaf sheath width, leaf sheath length, leaf sheath thickness, green leaf weight, leaf sheath weight, leaf sheath inclination, senescence leaf weight, total number of cane stem nodes, number of dead leaf nodes, etc. The ultrastructure of leaf sheath was observed with a 40X optical microscope, and the image-Pro Plus 6.0 analysis software used to measure the air cavity area, leaf sheath thickness, red-stained area and corresponding tissue area, respectively. Biochemical responses such as cellulose, hemicellulose, lignin, peroxidase (POD), malondialdehyde (MDA), and 4-glucanase activities were observed.

Statistical analysis

Excel 2010 and DPS statistical analysis software were applied to comprehensively analyze the biochemical and physiological activities at the base of the leaf sheath.

Discussion

Throughout the whole life cycle of sugarcane plants, leaf and stem elongation development occurs, with succeeding leaves and their subtending internodes extending successively. Mature stems consist of a number of leaves still enclosed in the leaf spindle, a dozen or so green leaves and a number of senescent leaves, increasing in number with increasing age of the plants5,15. Leaf senescence involves a complex interplay between internal and external factors, which determine the timing, progression, and completion of senescence. The degradation of macromolecules like proteins, lipids, pigments, and nucleic acids caused by hormonal levels, and reproductive growth16–19. The degradation followed by recycling and mobilization of nutrients in the plants18.

During senescence, autotrophic carbon metabolism of the leaf is replaced by catabolism of cellular organelles and macromolecules. Metabolic profiling studies have revealed that N-containing and branched-chain amino acids accumulate in senescing leaves20,21. Interestingly, plants undergoing carbohydrate limitation metabolize proteins as alternative respiratory substrates21–24. The mechanism for chlorophyll breakdown associated different genes25. The modification of the chlorophyll-apoprotein complex structure and subsequent enzymatic breakdown of the complex’s components by stay-green proteins are the initial phage in the degradation of chlorophyll19,26,27. Partial shading of sugarcane plants resulted in higher carboxylation efficiency and electron transfer rate in the unshaded leaf28. An alternative explanation to the increased photosynthesis relates to altered water status in partially defoliated plants29,30. Water potential of the remaining leaf is less negative during the day, resulting in lower stomatal resistance, higher transpiration rate, and, therefore, higher photosynthetic rate30,31.

During leaf senescence, other pigments, including carotenoids are also reduced. It suggests that leaf senescence initiate at the leaf tip and the degradation of chlorophyll and carotenoids are interconnected19,32. Considering the investment of plants in nutrient acquisition, the remobilization of macronutrients and micronutrients during senescence is critical for efficient nutrient usage and for plant survival. The significant challenge for China sugarcane production required more laborers. In the southwest sugarcane production zone, laborers required for planting and harvesting because more than 50% of the sugarcane land situated in steep locations where mechanized equipments ineffective33. Government statistics indicate that cane sugar labor expenses have soared by 140% over the last five years and will continue to rise annually. Due to China’s growing impurity rate, declining efficiency, and rising costs, sugarcane defoliation is a severe issue for sugarcane harvesting34.

The leaf is the primary photosynthetic organ for energy production and nutrient assimilation at the growth and development phases35,36. During senescence and defoliation, mesophyll cells are dismantled in a programmed manner, undergoing changes in cell structure, metabolism, and gene expression21. Radiation interception and the rate of assimilation per unit radiation flux density have increased due to partial defoliation. Partial defoliation has been shown to accelerate photosynthesis in sugarcane plants5,6,15. The surviving leaves rejuvenated by partial defoliation, which restores the photosynthetic capability to comparable to the newly developed leaves. The improved leaf assimilatory capacity and effective N utilization following defoliation offset increased assimilates requirements for regrowth in the sugarcane plants37.

However, Gutierrez-Miceli et al.38 showed that the partial defoliation of sugarcane plants has less impact on sucrose concentration in the culm when compared to control plants. This finding suggests that the remaining intact leaves could maintain a minimal supply of carbon based on the demand from sink tissues. In addition, residues left in the field after cane harvesting make it more challenging to grow the ratoon crop33. Due to reduced green and dry leafy material during harvest, the easy-defoliating features have the potential to make cane harvesting easier. Breeders have discovered differences in traits that make plants easy to defoliate39,40.

The east-to-complex defoliation group included three cultivars, such as GT47, GT60, and ROC22. Over 60% of China sugarcane land cultivated ROC22, which originated from Taiwan in 199841 with easy defoliation. However, the defoliation abilities of the ROC22 offspring differed. It was difficult for breeders to improve defoliation by parent selection, but indirect selection for defoliation would not have any unfavorable impacts, according to the assessment of combining abilities42.

In conclusion, it appears that breeders can be able to develop sugarcane cultivars with the ability to self-defoliate, producing a minimum of four to six leaves per stem during the second half of the growth cycle. These cultivars can also be extremely productive in terms of total sugar production while maintaining proper recoverable sugar. To investigate the genes and their molecular networks connected to sugarcane defoliation, the transcripto-proteomics of simple and complex defoliation at various growth phages have been elucidated and will be identified in the near future for sustainable sugarcane.

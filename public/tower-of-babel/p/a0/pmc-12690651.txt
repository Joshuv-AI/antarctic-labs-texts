MENG-09 LOCATION, SIMPSON GRADE OF MENINGIOMAS : PREDICTIVE FACTORS OF TUMOR RECORDIANCE
Authors: Inès-Stéphanie Assouan, Landry Drogba, Mohamed Koné, Latif Ajagbe, Sié Karidioula, Wilfried Yao, Oka N’dri
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionWith an incidence of 9.12/100,000 per year increasing with age, intracranial meningiomas are the most common intracranial neoplasms. They represent 1/3 of CNS tumors with a female predominance. These are extra-axial tumors developed at the expense of arachnoid cells. In 35% of cases they are located at the convexity. Around 80% are considered benign and classified as WHO grade 1 ; slow progression with a good prognosis. The standard treatment is surgical excision, the extent of which is assessed by the Simpson grade. However, this excision can be difficult due to the location and thus influence the Simpson grade, thus favoring recurrences. The objective of this work is to study the correlation between location ; Simpson grade and occurrence of recurrence after surgical resection.MethodThis is a multicenter, retrospective, descriptive and analytical study carried out in three hospitals in Abidjan covering neuroimaging data, anatomo-pathological results and operative reports of patients operated on for meningiomas from 2014 to 2024. The data were analyzed with Excel software.ResultsWe collected 16 files of meningiomas. The average age was 45.81 years with extremes of 26 and 64 years. Located in 68.75% at the base of the skull and 43.75% of excisions were Simpson grade 2.ConclusionThe location of meningiomas influences the quality of excision and the risk of recurrence. The more complex shapes of the skull base require rigorous preoperative evaluation to optimize surgical and prognostic results.

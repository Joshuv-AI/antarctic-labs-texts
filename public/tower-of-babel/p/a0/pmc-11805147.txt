Audio–Visual Modeling and its Effect on Behavior of Pediatric Patient: An Observational Study
Authors: Sujata Kumari, Amreen Aijazuddin, Anand Nanasaheb Patil, Amish Diwanji, Ranadheer Eeraveni, Disha Makwani, Akshaya Ojha
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:The prognosis and patients’ cooperation are interdependent on each other. In case of children, this aspect becomes all the more highlighted because of heightened response to pain and anxiety.Material and Methodology:Hundred (4-to-10 years old) children who were rated as negative on Frankl Behavior Rating Scale were taken for the study. The children were shown a video of a positive child model, and also various procedures of the dental treatment were shown.Results:Sixty-one children out of 100 demonstrated positive behavior and 3 showed definitely positive behavior during preventive treatment procedures. On the other hand, 73 children out of 100 demonstrated positive behavior and 3 showed definitely positive behavior during main treatment procedures. Significant change was observed in behavior of children in before and after the video was shown to them (<0.001).Conclusion:Audio–visual modeling has been proved to be a very efficient method in developing positive behavior of children toward dental treatment.

INTRODUCTION

Children are usually very apprehensive regarding dental treatment and pain associated with it. This anxiety is either directly or indirectly passed on from friends and family members.[1] Those children who have no past dental experience and fear may be due to the uncertainty that they feel regarding about what awaits them after the initial appointment check-up.[2] This acts as barrier between dentist and a successful treatment. Hence, it is of high importance that pediatric patient cooperates during the preventive and treatment procedures for maximum outcome. There are various behavior management techniques that have proposed. Dental practitioners have a wide range of techniques available to them to treat children such as tell-show-do (TSD), modeling, relaxation, audio analgesia, distraction, systematic desensitization, hypnosis, and behavior rehearsal.[3]

Modeling is observing another child of same age or elder siblings having dental treatment fruitfully. This may have an encouraging influence on an anxious child.[2] Modeling can be of two types: (i) live and (ii) filmed.[1] One of the form of filmed modeling is audio–visual method (AV) of modeling.[3] In the present study, the effectiveness of AV methods in behavior management will be observed.

Method of collection of sample

OPD check-up was done for the patients. At this stage, Frankl Behavior Rating was checked. Children were directed to the audio–visual room. There the children were shown a video of a positive child model that went to her dentist for the first time, and also, the various procedures of the dental treatment were shown in the video.

The patients were then directed to the clinical area where preventive procedures like restoration or oral prophylaxis or fluoride application was performed on the patients. At this stage, Frankl Behavior Rating was checked by the same panel of doctors. After the procedure was performed for each patient, they were given another appointment and were sent off.

The patients were subjected to pulp therapy or extraction or root canal treatment, basically which requires administration of a local anesthetic agent. Again at this stage, Frankl Behavior Rating was observed. The data were sent for statistical analysis.

Statistical analysis

The data so obtained were compiled systematically. A master table was prepared, and the total data were subdivided and distributed meaningfully and presented as individual table and graph. Data compilation and presentation were done followed by statistical analysis.

Statistical analysis was done using statistical package of social sciences (SPSS VERSION 16.0; CHICAGO INC., USA). The statistical test employed for the obtained data in our study was the chi square test as it was a qualitative analysis.

DISCUSSION

The biggest concern of dentists worldwide is the fear or anxiety toward dental treatment. This remains to be the reason of delay in dental treatment in most of the cases. About 6 to 15% of the world population suffers from dental fear and anxiety due to bright light, strange sound etc., in dental operatory.[4] Hence, it becomes quite important to modify the children’s behavior pattern to attain confidence of the potentially cooperative children during dental treatment.

Behavior management includes nonpharmacological methods along with the pharmacological means. The present trend is toward using nonaversive techniques among which most popular is modeling. It may be live or filmed.[25]

In present study, the children showed significantly positive behavior pattern following the AV modeling. Similar results were observed by Agarwal et al. in his study where it was seen that cooperation level in potentially cooperative children increased after seeing educational video.[5] Similar techniques were applied by Prabhakar et al.[6] and Al-Khotani et al.[7] in their studies to analyze the behavior change by audio–visual methods. Both of them found the results to be highly appreciable.

CONCLUSION

Efficiency of treatment and anxiety of children for pain are two main concerns for dental practitioners. The audio–visual modeling has been proved to be an effective tool in reducing dental fear and anxiety.

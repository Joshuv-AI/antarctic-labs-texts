Investigation of FGM patch repairs for cracked aluminum structures: J-integral analysis
Authors: Moulgada Abdelmadjid, Sahli Abderahmane, Zagane Mohammed El Sallah, Mordjani Ahmed, Adjoudj Tahar, Zafer Kurt, Ecren Uzun Yaylacı, Murat Yaylacı
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFunctionally Graded Materials (FGMs) represent a highly advanced solution for the repair of cracked aircraft structures, offering superior performance compared to conventional homogeneous materials. FGMs contribute to more durable, lightweight, and structurally effective repairs, extending service life while maintaining safety and performance standards. This study investigates the effectiveness of functionally graded material (FGM) patches for reinforcing cracked aluminum alloy plates. Using three-dimensional finite element analysis implemented in ABAQUS with a USDFLD subroutine, spatial variations of the Young’s modulus were applied according to linear, logarithmic, and exponential gradation laws. The fracture behavior was quantified through J-integral calculations under plane strain conditions. Results show that FGM patches significantly reduce the J-integral at the crack tip compared to unrepaired plates: up to 56% reduction for linear gradation, 52% for logarithmic, and 44% for exponential gradation. Linear gradation is most effective in mitigating longitudinal crack propagation, while logarithmic gradation efficiently reduces local principal stresses. Contour convergence and comparison with homogeneous patch and unrepaired plate benchmarks confirm the reliability of the numerical results. These findings provide mechanistic insight into stiffness-gradient-driven stress redistribution, guiding the optimal design of FGM repairs for enhanced durability.Keywords: FGM, Patch, Integral J, Stresses, Functions, USDFLD subroutine, NotchSubject terms: Engineering, Materials science

Introduction

Functionally graded materials (FGM’s) have attracted attention in recent years due to their superior performance in areas such as improving stress distribution, enhancing crack performance, high fracture toughness and a high degree of design flexibility compared to traditional homogeneous materials1–3. Unlike traditional homogeneous materials4,5, FGMs offer unlimited design architecture with their limitless variation options. Along with the attractive solutions they provide, they are the subject of researchers’ work in all disciplines involving mechanics6. In addition, numerical methods such as semi-analytical methods7, boundary element method8 and finite element method9–15 are available in the literature to obtain the changes created by the non-homogeneous structure of FGMs and the non-isotropic nature of the materials used. Khatir et al.16 proposed a novel approach to optimize materials for middle ear prostheses in order to enhance auditory performance. A finite element (FE) model of the human ear incorporating a pure titanium total ossicular replacement prosthesis (TORP) was developed and validated against experimental data. Chama et al.17 introduce a novel approach for reinforcing notched aluminum plates using functionally graded composites (FGCs). By incorporating the volume fraction principles of Functionally Graded Materials (FGMs) into fiber–matrix mixture laws, the study presents an advanced patch reinforcement strategy aimed at improving structural performance. Chama et al.18 used a methodology leverages the volumetric fraction concept of Functionally Graded Materials (FGMs) to develop a Graded Cohesive Zone Model (GCZM). A spatially varying field, implemented via a Fortran-based USDFLD subroutine, is introduced to dynamically adapt the cohesive behavior of the interface within the GCZM framework. Izadi et al.19 demonstrated that the comparison between experimental data and the derived fracture limit curve confirms the effectiveness of the newly developed criterion for analyzing mixed-mode fracture in functionally graded materials. Abdelmadjid et al.20 concluded that stress distribution under varying loads shows a marked increase in intensity, particularly near the crack tip. This region, especially the upper part subjected to tensile forces, becomes highly stressed, potentially leading to crack initiation or opening and the development of significant localized stresses. Naderi et al.21 reported that these variables significantly affect the fracture formation rate and overall fatigue life of the FGM. In particular, square-shaped Si₃N₄ particles demonstrated superior efficiency in arresting crack propagation compared to other geometries, owing to their ability to promote a more favorable stress distribution. Slamene et al.22 employed a novel meshing technique in combination with the Gurson–Tvergaard–Needleman (GTN) damage model within ABAQUS/Explicit. The simulation, based on von Mises stress theory and the Voce hardening law, effectively captures plastic flow and progressive damage driven by void nucleation, growth, and coalescence. Kirk et al.23 proposed a design methodology for planning FGM gradient paths across any number of elements, aiming to avoid undesirable phases over a range of temperatures. The methodology also enables optimization based on specific cost functions. A case study within the Fe–Ni–Cr system demonstrated its effectiveness, successfully generating gradient paths from 316 L stainless steel to pure chromium that either minimize path length or maximize the separation from unstable phases. Mirzaali et al.24 investigated the fracture behavior of functionally graded soft hard composites containing a pre-existing crack aligned with the gradient direction. They designed, additively manufactured, and mechanically tested two primary types of FG composites those with step wise and continuous gradients. The results indicated that, with the exception of fracture strain, the fracture properties of the graded specimens generally decreased as the length of the transition zone increased. Kumar Singh et al.25 presented a distinctive study in which a fully novel, in house developed manufacturing process was used to fabricate thermoplastic gears based on functionally graded materials (FGMs) a concept not previously explored. The specific wear rate of the fabricated gears was subsequently evaluated using the proposed methodology. Yaylaci et al.26 examined a problem in which a functionally graded (FG) layer rests on a rigid foundation and is subjected to loading by a rigid punch. From the analysis, key parameters such as contact length, contact stress, initial separation distance, and the initiation and termination points of separation were determined and compared. The study concluded that the finite element method (FEM) results showed satisfactory agreement with the analytical solutions. Yaylaci et al.27 assumed that the material properties of the functionally graded beam vary exponentially along its thickness. Using both the Finite Element Method (FEM) and the Modified Layerwise Plate (MLP) approach, they computed the natural frequencies and critical buckling loads of cracked FGM beams under various boundary conditions, including clamped–free (C–F), hinged–hinged (H–H), and clamped–clamped (C–C).This work aims to evaluate the effectiveness of functionally graded materials (FGM) in repairing cracked metallic components through numerical analysis using the ABAQUS software. Safaei28 investigated the dynamic behavior of lightweight sandwich panels consisting of a polymeric foam core sandwiched between two composite surface layers, using a finite element method based on first-order shear deformation theory. In the study, different porosity distributions in the core (including uniform and functionally graded patterns) were considered, while the surface layers were modeled as fiber-reinforced laminates with various fiber orientations. Both free and forced vibration responses were analyzed to evaluate the effect of material and geometric parameters on overall structural performance. The results revealed that the vibration characteristics of the sandwich structure are significantly influenced by the porosity distribution in the core and the fiber orientation in the composite layers. Yang et al.29 investigated the durability and structural stability of functionally graded graphene nanoparticle-reinforced composite beams at high temperatures using mathematical methods. Yang et al.29 comprehensively analyzed the decisive effects of the distribution and geometry of graphene particles within the material on the structure’s static and dynamic buckling resistance. Using the analytical model they developed, the deformation limits of the arch under sudden or constant external loads and its responses to temperature changes can be determined with high accuracy. Their results reveal how changes in the material’s composition and thermal conditions transform the arch’s load-bearing capacity, providing critical data for engineering designs.

The J integral generally refers to the Jacobi integral used in dynamical systems and clasic classical mechanics. It represents a conserved quantity in a rotating reference frame, combining the effects of energy and angular momentum. It is commonly used in analyses of the three-body problem. The J-integral has been widely employed in fracture mechanics, as well as in rigid line inclusion problems and frictionless contact analyses30,31.

The study is based on analyzing stress distribution32 and calculating key fracture resistance indicators such as the Von Mises equivalent stress, principal stress, J-integral, and displacement at the patch level, in order to assess the risk of crack propagation. The impact of three different property variation laws within the patch (linear, exponential, and logarithmic) is also examined, with the objective of determining which provides the most favorable stress distribution and the highest repair efficiency.

The novelty of the present work lies in three key aspects. First, a directional comparison of FGM gradation functions is performed by analyzing their effects on fracture parameters along the xxx, yyy, and zzz directions, providing deeper insight into how stiffness variation influences crack driving forces. Second, the study specifically focuses on unilateral FGM patch repair, which is less explored in the literature despite its practical relevance for real structural repairs. Third, the FGM patch is implemented through an anisotropic composite-based formulation using a USDFLD subroutine, allowing precise control of spatial stiffness variation and realistic representation of material gradation. These aspects have now been explicitly emphasized in both the Introduction and the Conclusion to clearly distinguish the present contribution from existing studies.

Material and method

Presentation of model

The geometric model studied consists of a thin rectangular plate made of 2024-T3 aluminum alloy, with a height H = 250 mm, a width L = 125 mm, and a thickness epl = 2 mm. This plate has a lateral crack located at one of its vertical edges, oriented horizontally, and positioned perpendicular to the direction of the applied load, along the y-axis. The crack, of length a = 8 mm. To remedy this damage, a single reinforcement plate (patch) made of functionally graded material (FGM) is applied to a single face of the plate, precisely on the side where the crack is located. This patch has a height of h = 50 mm, a width of w = 25 mm and a thickness of ep = 1 mm. The adhesion between the plate and the patch is ensured by a layer of glue with a thickness of ec = 0.2 mm, allowing good transmission of mechanical forces and effective anchoring of the reinforcement. The assembly is subjected to a uni-axial tensile loading along the vertical axis (y), with a stress of 30 MPa. This configuration aims to analyze the influence of unilateral repair using an FGM patch on the reduction of stress concentration near the crack, and to evaluate the overall mechanical performance of the repaired plate as a function of the gradient of properties of the patch material. The functionally graded material (FGM) patch is modeled by spatially varying the Young’s modulus only, while Poisson’s ratio and density are assumed constant. This assumption is commonly adopted in FGM repair studies and allows isolation of the effect of stiffness gradation on crack driving forces. The elastic modulus is graded according to a prescribed analytical law (linear, logarithmic, or exponential) as a function of spatial coordinates, implemented via the USDFLD subroutie in ABAQUS38. The material behavior remains isotropic at each material point, and therefore all stiffness components are derived consistently from the local Young’s modulus and constant Poisson’s ratio.

Since isotropy is assumed, no preferential anisotropic axes are defined, and the material orientation is independent of the crack direction. The gradation direction is aligned with the patch geometry and crack plane to ensure effective stress redistribution around the crack tip. Boundary conditions and loading

Regarding the boundary conditions, the embedding of the lower face of the plate is applied by fixing all the components of the displacements: U1 = U2 = U3 = 0 (displacements in X, Y and Z) and UR1 = UR2 = UR3 = 0 (rotations around the X, Y and Z axes). This represents a rigid support condition at this face, preventing any movement (see Fig. 3).

Side of the plate, a uni-axial tensile stress of 30 MPa is applied, generating a mechanical stress on the cross-section of the plate. This configuration allows the analysis of the behavior of the plate in response to these loading conditions, taking into account the FGM patch repair.

The fracture analysis was performed within the framework of linear elastic fracture mechanics under plane strain conditions, which is appropriate for the considered aluminum plate thickness and crack dimensions. The J-integral was evaluated in ABAQUS using the domain-based integral formulation, with (05) five successive contours defined around each crack tip. For all analyzed cases and material gradation functions, the J-integral values showed excellent contour convergence, confirming the path independence of the integral and the numerical stability of the results (see Fig. 4). In particular, the convergence behavior was verified for the different FGM gradation laws (linear, logarithmic, and exponential), demonstrating that the observed variations in J-integral are attributable to material gradation effects rather than numerical artifacts. This ensures the reliability of the conclusions regarding the influence of Young’s modulus distribution on crack driving forces and repair efficiency.

Results and discussions

For the variation of the patch in FGM, we stress the plate of a patch with a maximum Young’s modulus at the interface (plate/patch) and a minimum Young’s modulus on the free side of the patch, and see the different results emerging from this simulation. This choice aims to optimize the redistribution of stresses and to minimize mechanical incompatibilities at the interfaces. The simulation allows observing the effects of this graduated variation on several aspects, such as, the reduction of the stress peak near the crack, as well as the evolution of the J integral, an energy indicator of the potential propagation of the crack.

The variation of FV1 in the FGM patch

Each Young’s modulus variation function applied in the FGM patch models a distinct scenario of stiffness distribution across the material. In this context, the field variable denoted FV (Field Variable), used to control the field-dependent material behavior, takes normalized values ​​between zero (0) and one (1). When FV = 0, this corresponds to the area closest to the crack, where the stiffness (Young’s modulus) is maximal, ensuring targeted mechanical reinforcement. Conversely, when FV = 1, the periphery of the patch is reached, where stiffness is minimal, promoting better compatibility with the surrounding structure and limiting stress concentrations at the interfaces. This approach thus makes it possible to grade the mechanical properties of the patch to optimize force distribution and reduce mechanical discontinuities. The variation of Von Mises stresses in the FGM patch directly reflects the influence of the gradation profile of mechanical properties imposed by each Young’s modulus variation function. Each adopted function (linear or exponential) defines a distinct stress distribution pattern along a given axis, and for different values ​​of the variation field. This distribution strongly depends on how the stiffness evolves inside the patch, influencing the latter’s ability to redistribute forces around the crack. Among the functions studied, it is particularly notable that the exponential function along the z-axis allows achieving the lowest Von Mises stress, with a minimum value of about 16.1 MPa, indicating an excellent capacity for mitigating localized stresses thanks to a smooth transition of mechanical properties in the thickness of the patch.

On observe that the exponential and logarithmic functions applied along the z-axis induce relatively low Von Mises stresses compared to the other variation laws, which underlines their effectiveness in grading stiffness across the patch thickness in a progressive and smooth manner. This vertical gradation allows for better stress dissipation and limits stress peaks around the crack. Furthermore, the linear function along the (x) axis also exhibits lower Von Mises stresses, but to a lesser extent compared to the exponential and logarithmic functions along z. This suggests that the distribution of mechanical properties in the longitudinal (x) direction also contributes to stress mitigation, although less effectively than a gradation across the thickness. Overall, these results highlight the importance of the choice of the variation law and its orientation in the optimal design of FGM patches.

Analysis of maximum principal stresses

The different stress distributions, it is necessary to rely on the main maximum stress, which clearly shows the stress distribution at the patch level for the different FGM functions at the plate/patch interface. The analysis of the principal stresses in the three directions (x, y, z) highlights the significant influence of the type of variation function used in the FGM patch. In the (x) axis, the linear function generates the lowest stresses, reaching 20.35 MPa, with relaxation in the vicinity of the crack, which reflects a good redistribution of forces. It is followed by the logarithmic function (approximately 26.5 MPa) and then by the exponential function (41.6 MPa), which presents higher stresses in this direction. On the other hand, in the y axis (direction of the stress), it is the exponential function that is distinguished by the lowest stresses, of the order of 14 MPa, with a localized concentration at the crack tip followed by an extended relaxation in the rest of the patch. Finally, in the z-axis (thickness), the exponential function continues to show excellent performance with stresses reduced to 10.4 MPa, and a very limited concentration at the crack tip. Beyond this zone, the effect of stresses becomes almost weak, reflecting an excellent capacity for absorption and diffusion of forces through the thickness. These results confirm that the judicious choice of the FGM function according to the direction can significantly improve the mechanical performance of the patch, by reducing stress concentrations and extending the lifetime of the repaired structure.

The variation of displacement in the FGM patch

Concerning the displacements induced in the FGM patch, the analysis focused on the U2 displacement, which corresponds to the displacement along the (y) axis, i.e. in the direction of the applied stress (traction). The results show that, whatever the variation function used (linear, exponential, and logarithmic) and whatever the axis considered (x, y or z), the displacements remain globally similar, with values ​​between 0.061 mm and 0.065 mm. This low variation indicates that, despite the differences in Young’s modulus gradation in the patch, the overall effect on the longitudinal deformation of the plate remains moderate. This suggests that the main role of the different FGM functions is not to significantly modify the overall flexibility of the system, but rather to reduce local stress concentrations without altering the overall displacement response under loading. Conclusion

This study emphasizes the significance of functionally graded materials (FGMs) as advanced and effective solutions for crack repair in structural components, particularly those made of aluminum alloys. The numerical analysis carried out using ABAQUS demonstrated the effectiveness of FGM patches in enhancing the mechanical performance of cracked structures by reducing the J-integral and distributing stresses more uniformly around crack tips The effectiveness of the patch depends on several factors, including its thickness, the quality of its adhesion to the surface, and its geometric distribution around the crack. All of these criteria play a significant role in enhancing structural durability and reducing the risk of crack propagation. The comparison of different gradation functions (exponential, logarithmic, and linear) also revealed a clear influence of each on the structural response, highlighting the importance of selecting the most appropriate function based on the specific application requirements.

The study of variations in the J-integral and maximum principal stresses for different FGM patches reveals that the gradation function of the Young’s modulus plays a key role in the mechanical effectiveness of the reinforcement. Overall, the linear function stands out for its ability to reduce the J-integral in both the x and y directions, while the logarithmic function proves particularly effective in limiting principal stresses in specific regions and facilitating stress relaxation near the crack.

However, the exponential function, while effective at absorbing stresses through the patch thickness in the z-direction, shows higher J-integral values in the x-direction, indicating a more limited capacity to dissipate crack propagation energy in that direction. These results confirm the importance of carefully selecting the gradation function to optimize stress redistribution, reduce stress concentrations, and ultimately improve the overall performance of FGM patch repairs.

These results highlight that the choice of the Young’s modulus gradation function significantly influences the distribution of the J-integral in different directions. In particular, while the exponential function is effective in the z-direction for reducing through-thickness stresses, it exhibits higher values in the x-direction, which may be less favorable for controlling longitudinal crack propagation. These observations emphasize the importance of tailoring the Young’s modulus gradation to the dominant loading conditions in order to optimize the performance of FGM patches and thereby enhance the durability and safety of cracked plate repairs.

Coronary artery bypass grafting with bilateral internal thoracic arteries and without cardiopulmonary bypass: very long-term survival
Authors: Daniel Navia, Mariano Vrancic, Fernando Piccinini, Iván Huespe, Leonardo Seoane, Juan Furmento, Juan Costabel, Marcelo Trivi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionCoronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) using bilateral internal thoracic arteries (BITA) without cardiopulmonary bypass (off-pump) has demonstrated improved mid-term survival with reduced perioperative risk. However, evidence regarding its long-term effectiveness and safety is lacking, particularly in Latin American populations.ObjectivesTo describe long-term survival (20 years) in a consecutive cohort of patients undergoing exclusively off-pump CABG with BITA and to assess the impact of age, ventricular function, angina type, diabetic status, and body mass index (BMI) on survival.Materials and methodsWe conducted a retrospective, open-cohort study of patients who underwent exclusively off-pump CABG with BITA between January 2003 and December 2023. Emergency surgeries, combined procedures, and patients with prior cardiac surgery were excluded. Follow-up ranged from at least 1 year to 20 years after surgery. Demographic data were obtained from medical records, and survival status was assessed through telephone contact. For the primary endpoint, survival tables and Kaplan-Meier curves were generated. Cox proportional hazards regression models were applied to compare time to death according to age, ventricular function, angina type, diabetic status, and BMI. Crude and adjusted hazard ratios (HR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI) were reported.ResultsA total of 4,495 patients were included during the study period, with a median follow-up of 6.3 years (interquartile range [IQR] 3–10). Of these, 95 patients reached 20 years of follow-up and 39 were followed for more than 20 years. Mean age was 64.07 years (SD 9.26), mean BMI 28.70 (SD 6.47); 91.6% (n = 4,120) were male, 28,2% (n = 1,269) were diabetic, and 48.7% (n = 2,182) presented with unstable angina. Overall survival was 70% (95% CI: 68–72%) at 10 years and 31% (95% CI: 28–34%) at 20 years. Among patients younger than 65 years, survival was 88% (95% CI: 85–89%) at 10 years and 60% (95% CI: 55–65%) at 20 years. Age ≥ 65 years and severe ventricular dysfunction were independently associated with higher postoperative mortality (adjusted HR 4.99; 95% CI: 4.33–5.75; p < 0.01 and adjusted HR 2.20; § 95% CI: 1.73–2.80; p < 0.01, respectively). Angina type was not an independent predictor of survival (adjusted HR 0.93; 95% CI: 0.81–1.06). Diabetes, both without obesity (adjusted HR 1.28; 95% CI: 1.10–1.48; p = 0.001) and with obesity (adjusted HR 1.35; 95% CI: 1.11–1.65; p < 0.01), was independently associated with increased mortality. Obesity without diabetes, however, was not (adjusted HR 1.05; 95% CI: 0.88–1.24; p = 0.603).ConclusionIn this 20-year retrospective cohort, off-pump CABG with BITA demonstrated high long-term survival in younger patients. Severe ventricular dysfunction and diabetes—particularly when combined with obesity—were independently associated with increased mortality. Neither obesity alone nor angina type were independent predictors of mortality.

Introduction

The use of the left internal thoracic artery (LITA) to graft the left anterior descending artery (LAD) has become the surgical gold standard for coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG), supported by robust evidence showing excellent early mortality and morbidity outcomes as well as high long-term patency rates [1]. These benefits have spurred growing interest in employing the right internal thoracic artery (RITA) [2]. Multiple studies have demonstrated that using one or both internal thoracic arteries (BITA) does not markedly increase peri-operative morbidity [1–3]. In addition, several retrospective series and meta-analyses suggest that BITA may confer superior long-term survival [2, 4–7].

Despite these potential survival gains, BITA adoption remains limited, likely because of its greater technical complexity and perceived peri-operative risks. Moreover, the Arterial Revascularization Trial (ART)—the only randomized trial comparing single ITA with BITA—did not show a survival advantage at extended follow-up [8].

Off-pump CABG (OPCAB) has emerged as an alternative to on-pump CABG (ONCAB), aiming to lessen complications linked to cardiopulmonary bypass and to reduce aortic manipulation [9, 10]. Debate over BITA use in OPCAB has persisted for more than three decades [11, 12]. Although combining BITA with OPCAB is theoretically attractive, evidence of a synergistic benefit is scarce, limited to case reports to date [13, 14].

Accordingly, our primary objective was to assess long-term survival in patients undergoing BITA OPCAB. Secondarily, we examined the association of ventricular function, diabetes mellitus (DM), and body-mass index (BMI) with time to death. Finally, we described the incidence of coronary angiography (CAG)–guided re-revascularization during follow-up and the proportion of occluded grafts.

Methods

Study design and setting

We performed a single-center, retrospective cohort study at the Instituto Cardiovascular de Buenos Aires. All adults (≥ 18 years) who underwent BITA OPCAB between January 2003 and December 2023 were eligible. The manuscript adheres to STROBE guidelines and was approved by the institutional ethics committee; informed consent was waived owing to the study’s retrospective nature.

Participants

We excluded emergency CABG, concomitant procedures (e.g., valve or aortic surgery), cases using saphenous-vein or radial-artery grafts, prior CABG, and any operation requiring cardiopulmonary bypass. The final cohort therefore comprised patients who received exclusive off-pump revascularization with two ITA grafts in a T configuration. To ensure complete arterial revascularization, only patients with two- or three-vessel coronary disease—defined as ≥ 70% stenosis in each affected territory—and implanted with at least one ITA graft per diseased territory were included.

Surgical technique

After commercial stabilizers became available in 2002, OPCAB was adopted. From 2003 onward, complete arterial revascularization with T-configured BITA OPCAB became standard. Both ITAs were skeletonized; the LITA was anastomosed to the LAD, and the RITA, used as a free sequential T-graft, was connected to the circumflex (Cx) and distal right coronary (RCA) arteries. The RITA-to-LITA anastomosis was placed on the anterior surface of the LITA at the left atrial appendage level. All procedures were off-pump. Anastomotic quality was assessed with transit-time flowmetry and Doppler probes (VeriQ, Medistim, Oslo, Norway).

Data collection and variable definitions

Baseline demographics (age, sex, BMI, diabetic status, left ventricular ejection fraction [LVEF], etc.) and intra-operative details were abstracted from the electronic medical record.

Long-term follow-up assessed survival and major adverse cardiovascular events, including the following postoperative complications: (1) acute kidney injury, (2) stroke, (3) peri-operative myocardial infarction, (4) mediastinitis, and (5) re-intervention procedures. Follow-up data came from electronic records, direct contact with patients, relatives and treating physicians, and telephone calls to confirm vital status at last contact.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are reported as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median (interquartile range [IQR]) according to Shapiro–Wilk normality testing. Categorical variables are expressed as counts and percentages. Group comparisons employed Student’s t-test or Mann–Whitney U test for continuous variables, and χ² or Fisher’s exact test for proportions. Early postoperative complications—including in-hospital mortality, myocardial infarction, respiratory failure, dialysis-requiring renal failure, stroke, and mediastinitis—are presented with absolute numbers and 95% exact confidence intervals (CI).

Thirty-day, 5-, 10-, 15-, and 20-year survival were estimated with life tables and displayed using Kaplan–Meier curves, stratified by age (< 65 vs. ≥ 65 years). Associations of age, BMI, LVEF, and DM with time to death were examined with Cox proportional hazards models: first unadjusted, then adjusted for potential confounders (age, sex, chronic respiratory disease, cerebrovascular disease, dyslipidemia, smoking, baseline aspirin, calcium-channel blockers, statins).

The proportional hazards assumption was assessed graphically using log-minus-log survival plots (Supplementary Figure S1). Given the large sample size of the cohort, formal tests based on Schoenfeld residuals were considered potentially overly sensitive to minor deviations lacking meaningful clinical relevance; therefore, graphical evaluation was prioritized as a complementary diagnostic approach. As a sensitivity analysis, all primary Cox regression models were repeated using the complete-case (non-imputed) dataset to assess the robustness of the findings obtained after multiple imputation.

Additionally, restricted cubic spline analyses were performed to investigate the continuous association between age and long-term mortality risk (Supplementary Figure S2). These analyses demonstrated a marked increase in hazard beginning at approximately 65 years of age, supporting the clinically selected cutoff used in the primary analyses.

Time to repeat coronary revascularization was assessed with cumulative incidence curves. Missing data (mainly comorbidities, ~ 10%) were assumed completely at random; multiple imputation by chained equations generated 20 imputed datasets as a sensitivity analysis (White et al., 2011). The proportion of missingness is shown in Table S1.

Results

Among 4 483 patients who underwent BITA OPCAB, the mean age was 64.1 ± 9.27 years, and 8.4% (n = 373) were women. Patients were followed for a median of 6.13 (IQR 2.6–10.36) years, of which 56% (n = 2,507) were censored (loss to follow-up) at 10 years and 72% (n = 3,255) at 20 years. Median pre-operative LVEF was 58% (IQR 50–64); 84.2% (n = 3 345) had normal or mildly reduced LVEF, while 15.8% (n = 630) had moderate or severe dysfunction. Urgent surgery accounted for 42.2% (n = 1 884). Median total arterial grafts were 3 (IQR 3–4), comprising one LITA and two RITA grafts on average. Baseline characteristics are in Table 1. Survival

Patients < 65 years showed 5-, 10-, and 20-year survival of 95.8% (95% CI 94.7–96.6), 87.8% (95% CI 85.8–89.6), and 60.8% (95% CI 55.6–65.7), respectively. Those ≥ 65 years had 5-, 10-, and 20-year survival of 86.2% (95% CI 84.5–87.7), 54.1% (95% CI 51.2–56.9), and 5.7% (95% CI 3.4–8.9) (Fig. 1).

Predictors of mortality

In unadjusted Cox models, moderate ventricular dysfunction carried a hazard ratio (HR) of 1.43 (95% CI 1.18–1.72; p < 0.001) and severe dysfunction an HR of 2.38 (95% CI 1.91–2.96; p < 0.001). After adjustment, HRs were 1.22 (95% CI 1.00–1.49; p = 0.049) and 2.21 (95% CI 1.74–2.80; p < 0.001), respectively. For diabetes, the crude HR was 1.47 (95% CI 1.30–1.66; p < 0.001) and the adjusted HR 1.30 (95% CI 1.15–1.47; p < 0.001). Subgroup analyses (reference = non-diabetic, non-obese) yielded: non-diabetic obese HR 0.84 crude (95% CI 0.71–0.99; p = 0.039) and 1.05 adjusted (95% CI 0.88–1.24; p = 0.605); diabetic non-obese HR 1.58 crude (95% CI 1.37–1.82; p < 0.001) and 1.30 adjusted (95% CI 1.13–1.50; p < 0.001); diabetic obese HR 1.13 crude (95% CI 0.93–1.38; p = 0.214) and 1.28 adjusted (95% CI 1.05–1.56; p = 0.015) (Figs. 2 and 3). A complete-case sensitivity analysis using the non-imputed dataset yielded results consistent with the primary analyses performed after multiple imputation. In unadjusted analyses, moderate ventricular dysfunction was associated with an increased risk of mortality (HR 1.38, 95% CI 1.14–1.67; p = 0.001), whereas severe dysfunction demonstrated a stronger association (HR 2.29, 95% CI 1.84–2.87; p < 0.001). After multivariable adjustment, severe ventricular dysfunction remained independently associated with mortality (HR 2.02, 95% CI 1.57–2.61; p < 0.001), while the association for moderate dysfunction was attenuated and no longer statistically significant (HR 1.20, 95% CI 0.97–1.49; p = 0.095).

Similarly, diabetes remained independently associated with mortality in both unadjusted (HR 1.47, 95% CI 1.30–1.67; p < 0.001) and adjusted analyses (HR 1.28, 95% CI 1.11–1.47; p = 0.001).

Re-intervention

Cumulative incidence of CAG-guided revascularization was 4.90% (95% CI 4.23–5.65) at 5 years, 8.86% (95% CI 7.84–9.97) at 10 years, and 15.63% (95% CI 13.25–18.19) at 20 years. Among 340 angiograms, 16 LITA-to-LAD grafts were occluded (4.71%, 95% CI 2.71–7.53); 33 RITA-1-to-Cx grafts were occluded (9.71%, 95% CI 6.78–13.36); and 56 RITA-2-to-distal RCA grafts were occluded (16.47%, 95% CI 12.69–20.85).

Discussion

This study represents a cohort of patients who underwent exclusive BITA OPCAB, with long-term (20-year) follow-up. Patients treated with this surgical strategy achieved excellent late survival, with 60% of those younger than 65 years alive at 20 years. Severe ventricular dysfunction and diabetes mellitus (DM) emerged as independent predictors of higher long-term mortality, whereas obesity alone did not. The overall cumulative incidence of CAG-guided revascularization was 15.6%.

Several observational studies and meta-analyses have suggested that BITA grafting confers superior survival compared with single ITA use [5, 15]. The ART trial reported no survival advantage at 10 years, but its findings have been criticized for high crossover, inter-centre technical heterogeneity, and extensive arterial-graft use in both groups, factors that likely diluted real differences [16, 17]. Low adoption of BITA despite its benefits may reflect the greater technical complexity, longer operative times, and higher risk of complications such as deep sternal wound infection. Our series reflects more than 20 years of experience with exclusive BITA OPCAB in multivessel disease [18]. Among the various BITA configurations, we used only the T-graft combination originally described by Tector et al. [19].

The off-pump, aortic no-touch strategy avoids clamping and manipulation of the ascending aorta, thereby reducing postoperative morbidities such as stroke. A recent meta-analysis showed that BITA OPCAB without aortic manipulation is associated with lower risks of postoperative stroke, mortality, renal failure, atrial fibrillation, bleeding, and intensive-care stay [20]. The low operative mortality and morbidity in our series likely reflect this technique.

The age limit for BITA benefit remains debated. Mohammadi et al. recently showed that the survival advantage of BITA or other arterial conduits disappears in patients older than 60 years [21]. In our study, patients ≥ 65 years had worse long-term survival; age was an independent predictor of late mortality.

Severe left-ventricular systolic dysfunction in the setting of significant coronary disease remains a potent adverse prognostic factor. The STICH trial consistently demonstrated that CABG improves survival and reduces cardiovascular mortality in patients with severe dysfunction [22]. Ikeda et al. reported, in a series of patients with BITA OPCAB and severe dysfunction (mean LVEF 24.8%) followed for 10 years, a hospital mortality of 1.65% and improved ventricular function in 75.2%. They identified LVEF < 30% and absence of BITA as independent predictors of death and cardiac events [23]. In our cohort, severe—but not moderate—dysfunction was an independent predictor of mortality.

DM doubles cardiovascular risk, and roughly 75% of deaths in diabetic patients are due to coronary disease [24]. DM is an independent predictor of long-term mortality after CABG [25]. Because type 2 DM produces diffuse, rapidly progressive disease in smaller vessels and is often accompanied by extracardiac comorbidities, OPCAB is an attractive low-morbidity option [26]. Renner et al. compared 355 diabetic OPCAB patients with 502 ONCAB patients and found lower 30-day mortality, fewer complications, and lower mortality at 6 and 12 months with OPCAB [27]. Srinivasan et al., in a propensity-matched analysis, reported a six-fold higher stroke rate and 2.3-fold higher renal-failure risk in on-pump versus off-pump surgery [28]. Using skeletonized BITA reduces deep sternal wound infection risk, an important concern in diabetics [29]. BITA OPCAB is therefore an ideal strategy: Park et al., in an exclusive BITA OPCAB series, found DM was not a risk factor for early or late outcomes [30]. In contrast, in our consecutive 20-year BITA OPCAB cohort, DM remained an independent predictor of worse survival and was associated with higher mediastinitis rates.

Evidence on obesity and post-CABG outcomes is conflicting, partly owing to heterogeneous definitions of obesity and varied endpoints. Terada et al. examined BMI and short-, mid-, and long-term mortality after CABG across different coronary-risk and diabetes strata, finding lower mid- and long-term mortality in overweight patients but no difference in obese patients [31]. Although these findings may be interpreted in the context of the so-called “obesity paradox,” they should be interpreted with caution. Body mass index (BMI) is an imperfect surrogate marker of adiposity and metabolic risk, and residual confounding related to nutritional status, frailty, body composition, or other unmeasured metabolic factors cannot be excluded [32].

Strengths and limitations

This study has several strengths. First, it constitutes one of the largest published cohorts of patients treated with the BITA OPCAB technique, providing some evidence of the therapeutic impact of this surgical approach. Second, the 20-year follow-up allowed us to assess overall long-term survival and to explore outcomes within subgroups defined by differing baseline characteristics. Finally, every surgeon in our team performs the same standardized operation, enabling us to include all eligible multivessel patients and thereby minimizing potential selection bias. The findings must, however, be interpreted in light of key limitations. Foremost, the retrospective design means that, although real-world observational data are provided, the study can demonstrate associations but not causality. Second, being single-centre limits the generalizability of the results. Third, despite adjustment for all available confounders, residual confounding due to unmeasured variables cannot be excluded. In particular, residual confounding may have influenced the observed associations between BMI, diabetes, and long-term outcomes. Fourth, this study did not include a comparison between BITA OPCAB and other surgical techniques, as its primary objective was to describe very long-term survival rather than to perform a comparative analytical evaluation. In this context, our group has previously published comparative analyses evaluating BITA versus LITA combined with saphenous vein graft or radial artery graft strategies [33–34]. Finally, emergency surgeries were excluded in order to maintain homogeneity within the study population; moreover, such cases represent less than 5% of the BITA OPCAB procedures performed at our institution.

Conclusion

This investigation represents one of the largest BITA OPCAB cohorts with very long follow-up (20 years). Among patients younger than 65 years, overall survival at 20 years was 60%. Severe ventricular dysfunction and diabetes mellitus—though not moderate dysfunction or obesity—were independent predictors of mortality. The cumulative 20-year incidence of catheter-based coronary re-revascularization was 15.6%.

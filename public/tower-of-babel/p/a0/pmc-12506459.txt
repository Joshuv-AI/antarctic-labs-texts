Pharmacokinetics and safety of subcutaneous nivolumab: results from the phase I/II CheckMate 8KX study
Authors: Sara Lonardi, Iwona Ługowska, Anne O’Donnell, Christopher Jackson, Loes Maria Latten-Jansen, Richard North, Rastislav Bahleda, Marcelo Garrido, Armando Santoro, Matías Rodrigo Chacon, Linghui Li, Devanand Joseph, Heather E Vezina, Urvi Aras, Bryan Bennett, Deepak Perumal, Balmeet Gurm, Wee-Teck Ng, R Donald Harvey, José Trigo, Aitana Calvo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCheckMate 8KX was a phase I/II study investigating the pharmacokinetics and safety of subcutaneous (SC) nivolumab±recombinant human hyaluronidase PH20 (rHuPH20).MethodsPatients with advanced solid tumors who were immune-checkpoint inhibitor-naïve were included. In parts A and B, patients received one single dose of nivolumab SC 720 mg or 960 mg±rHuPH20 2000 U/mL followed by nivolumab intravenous (IV) 480 mg every 4 weeks (Q4W). Patients receiving nivolumab IV could switch to nivolumab SC 1200 mg+rHuPH20 2000 U/mL Q4W in part C. Patients in part D received nivolumab SC 1200 mg+rHuPH20 Q4W. The primary objective was to describe the pharmacokinetics of nivolumab SC±rHuPH20. Secondary objectives included assessing safety and immunogenicity of nivolumab SC. Exploratory objectives included evaluating patient experience and preference and characterizing biomarker measures of immune function (pretreatment and on-treatment peripheral blood and tumor biopsies).ResultsA total of 103 patients with various solid tumor types were treated between December 4, 2018 and September 7, 2022. Mean duration of injection was <5 min for nivolumab SC±rHuPH20. Nivolumab exposures over the first dosing interval increased with increasing dose; geometric-mean time to maximum concentration across doses was 117–167 hours (5–7 days). Nivolumab SC+rHuPH20 was well tolerated; grade 3/4 treatment-related adverse events occurred in 0%–11.1% of patients; antidrug antibodies were reported in some patients but were not associated with altered safety; no neutralizing antibodies were detected. Overall, patients preferred SC delivery over IV or had no preference. Increased tumor and peripheral pharmacodynamic biomarkers were observed postnivolumab SC, consistent with historical data for nivolumab IV.ConclusionThe pharmacokinetics of nivolumab SC have been well characterized and enabled dose selection for further study. Nivolumab SC has an acceptable safety profile, allows for rapid administration, and is preferred by more patients than nivolumab IV. These results encourage large-scale investigation of nivolumab SC.Trial registration numberNCT03656718.

Introduction

Immune checkpoint inhibitors represent a major therapeutic advancement in the management of solid tumors.1 Nivolumab, an antiprogrammed death-1 (PD-1) immune checkpoint inhibitor, has demonstrated improved survival and durable responses in several tumor types, including non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), melanoma, renal cell carcinoma (RCC), malignant pleural mesothelioma, esophageal adenocarcinoma, gastroesophageal junction cancer, classical Hodgkin’s lymphoma, gastric adenocarcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma of head and neck, urothelial cancer, hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), and microsatellite instability high/deficient mismatch repair colorectal cancer (MSI-H/dMMR CRC).2 Nivolumab, as monotherapy or in combination with other agents, is widely approved for the treatment of several solid tumors as an intravenous (IV) infusion administered over 30 or 60 min.2 3

Monoclonal antibodies in cancer therapy are typically administered by IV infusions, and although nivolumab IV has dramatically improved clinical outcomes for patients across various tumor types, unmet needs associated with IV administration still exist for healthcare professionals (HCPs) and patients (eg, chair time, complex scheduling demands).4 5 Subcutaneous (SC) administration has been shown to reduce healthcare burden and is typically preferred over IV infusions by both patients and HCPs.6 Nivolumab SC may improve patient satisfaction and the administration experience by shortening the duration of treatment and time spent in clinics, improving scheduling efficiency, and alleviating anxiety.79 For HCPs, SC delivery of nivolumab may allow for rapid drug delivery, reduce the need for staff trained for IV injection, and optimize resource utilization and chair occupancy times at infusion clinics.9

Delivering doses requiring a large volume remains a challenge with SC injections. However, administration of recombinant human hyaluronidase PH20 (rHuPH20) enzyme, a US Food and Drug Administration-approved permeation-enhancing enzyme, co-formulated with anticancer therapies has enabled the safe and effective SC administration of large volumes of therapeutic agents for the treatment of breast cancer, certain lymphomas, and multiple myeloma.1012 rHuPH20 shortens SC administration time by reducing resistance to bulk fluid flow in the SC space through reversible degradation of the glycosaminoglycan hyaluronan.13 An SC formulation of nivolumab+rHuPH20 is currently under development to deliver the clinical benefits of nivolumab, while reducing the burden associated with its administration via the IV route. Here, we describe results for the pharmacokinetics, safety, and immunogenicity of nivolumab SC+rHuPH20 in CheckMate 8KX (NCT03656718), a phase I/II study conducted in patients with advanced or metastatic solid tumors. Additionally, we present results for exploratory study end points, including pharmacodynamics, and patient experience and preference.

Methods

Study design and participants

This trial was a multicenter, randomized, open-label, phase I/II study to investigate the pharmacokinetics, safety, and tolerability of nivolumab SC administered with or without rHuPH20 in patients across multiple solid tumor types. The study consisted of a 28-day screening period followed by a four-part treatment period (parts A–D) and a safety follow-up period. Eligible patients had histological or cytological confirmation of advanced (metastatic and/or unresectable) melanoma, squamous or non-squamous NSCLC, RCC, HCC, or MSI-H/dMMR metastatic CRC, and were immune checkpoint inhibitor-naïve. In addition, they were aged at least 18 years, had an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status of 0–1, and measurable disease per Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST V.1.1).

Patients were excluded, if they had active brain metastases or leptomeningeal metastases, ocular melanoma, active, known, or suspected autoimmune disease, or previous active malignancy within 2 years of first study treatment, except for locally curable cancers that had been apparently cured. Patients must not have had a condition requiring systemic treatment with either corticosteroids (>10 mg/day prednisone equivalent) or other immunosuppressive medications within 14 days of start of treatment assignment/randomization. Prior treatment with agents targeting T-cell co-stimulation or checkpoint pathways was not permitted. Additional details on eligibility criteria are provided in online supplemental file 1.

Randomization and masking

Enrolled patients were either allocated to single-dose treatment groups (part A group 1, part B group 3, and part D group 5) or randomized to single-dose treatment groups (part B group 2 and part B group 4), where a computer-generated randomization scheme was applied to allocate patients in a 1:1 ratio (figure 1).

Procedures

The study was conducted in four parts (part A, part B, part C, and part D). In part A, patients were allocated to receive a single dose of nivolumab SC 720 mg with rHuPH20 2000 units/mL (group 1). A population mean bioavailability of 75% was assumed and used to predict exposures and select the initial nivolumab SC dose of 720 mg (part A) in order to provide pharmacokinetics similar to that of a 480 mg IV dose.14 Based on data from part A, a single SC dose of 960 mg was tested in part B where, in order to acquire additional pharmacokinetic data, patients received a single dose of nivolumab SC 960 mg with rHuPH20 2000 units/mL (group 3) or were randomized to be treated with a single dose of either nivolumab SC 720 mg (group 2) or 960 mg (group 4), both without rHuPH20 (figure 1). Four weeks after the single dose of nivolumab SC, all patients in parts A and B received nivolumab IV 480 mg every 4 weeks (Q4W) until progression (per RECIST V.1.1), unacceptable toxicity, withdrawal of consent, completion of 104 weeks of treatment, or crossover to nivolumab SC in part C, whichever occurred first. Patients in parts A and B who were still on the study at the time of the protocol amendment were switched in part C to receive nivolumab SC 1200 mg with rHuPH20 2000 units/mL Q4W. In part D, patients were treated de novo with nivolumab SC 1200 mg with rHuPH20 2000 units/mL Q4W (group 5). In parts A and B, only the abdomen was used for the SC manual injection, while in parts C and D, the thigh was also considered as an administration site. All study treatments were administered by a physician in a hospital setting. Treatment in parts C and D continued until progression (per RECIST V.1.1), unacceptable toxicity, withdrawal of consent, completion of 104 weeks of nivolumab treatment (IV and SC), or death, whichever occurred first. Dose modifications were not permitted during the treatment period. Additional details about dose delays and criteria for discontinuing treatment can be found in online supplemental file 2.

Blood samples were collected throughout the study for nivolumab pharmacokinetic and antidrug antibody (ADA) assessments by validated assays. Intensive pharmacokinetic sampling was implemented over 28 days after the first SC dose in parts A, B, and D, followed by predose pharmacokinetic sampling throughout the remainder of the study. In part C, only predose samples were collected for pharmacokinetic and ADA assessments. The primary and secondary nivolumab pharmacokinetics end points were determined by non-compartmental analysis (NCA). After determining the area under the serum concentration-time curve at the end of the first dosing interval (AUCtau) by NCA, average serum concentration over the first dosing interval (Cavg) was determined by dividing AUCtau by the length of the dosing interval (28 days). Adverse events (AEs) were reported throughout the study period and graded per the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (V.5). For local injection-site reactions, the proposed injection site was evaluated prior to all nivolumab SC doses and 60 min after the cycle 1 day 1 injection. In parts C and D, patients receiving subsequent SC injections were monitored for 15–30 min postinjection at the investigator’s discretion. In addition, all participants were contacted approximately 24 hours after each nivolumab SC injection and evaluated at the next study visit.

Patients’ experience and preference for the mode of administration were measured using the Patient Experience and Preference Questionnaire (PEPQ), an internally developed, site-administered questionnaire. The questionnaire comprises eight items that assessed patient perceptions regarding the convenience, acceptability, and preference of mode of administration, any treatment-related symptoms, and satisfaction with treatment (online supplemental table 1). The PEPQ was administered immediately after completion of injection or infusion on day 1 of treatment cycles 1 and 2 in parts A and B, as well as on day 1 of treatment cycle 1 in parts C and D.

Pretreatment and on-treatment peripheral blood and tumor biopsies were collected and used to characterize the tumorous and peripheral biomarkers. Paired baseline and on-treatment cycle 1 day 15 tumor biopsies were assessed for CD8+ tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) and programmed death ligand 1 (PD-L1) expression on tumor cells by immunohistochemistry. Soluble PD-1 (sPD-1), interferon gamma (IFNγ), and chemokine ligand (CXCL) 9 and CXCL10 were assessed from cycle 1 day 1 to cycle 2 day 1 using ELISA, SIMOA immunoassay, and Luminex multiplex assay, respectively. Changes in sPD-1 were compared with unpublished data from nivolumab IV 3 mg/kg once every 2 weeks in study CA209-038 (NCT01621490); other biomarkers (IFNγ, CXCL9, and CXCL10) were compared with published data.15 Tumor assessments, using CT or MRI scans, were performed at week 12 after the first study dose, and every 8 weeks before the subsequent dose up to 104 weeks, until disease progression or treatment discontinuation. Changes in tumor measurements and responses were investigator-assessed based on RECIST V.1.1.

Objectives

The primary objective of this study was to describe the pharmacokinetics of nivolumab SC with or without rHuPH20. Blood samples were assessed for maximum serum concentration (Cmax), time to Cmax (Tmax), AUCtau, and serum concentration at the end of the first dosing interval (Ctau). Secondary objectives included assessments of safety and immunogenicity. The incidence of AEs, serious AEs, AEs leading to study drug discontinuation, treatment-related AEs (TRAEs), and treatment-related serious AEs (TRSAEs) was reported throughout the study and for a minimum of 100 days following discontinuation. Additionally, the incidence of AEs in the broad standardized Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities query for anaphylactic and hypersensitivity/infusion reactions, occurring within 2 days after study drug administration, was reported. Patients were monitored for local injection site reactions, such as signs of local irritation and inflammation (injection site erythema, pain, pruritus, hematoma, or swelling). Exploratory study end points included PEPQ assessment and change from baseline in the expression of tumorous and peripheral biomarkers of immune function.

Statistical analyses

Sample size calculations were based on the number of patients sufficient to obtain prespecified point estimates for pharmacokinetic parameters of nivolumab SC and were not based on clinical hypotheses. In parts A and B, administration of nivolumab SC to 20 patients would provide 90% confidence that the range between 90% and 111% of the point estimate of geometric mean AUCtau would encompass the true value. Administration of nivolumab SC to 10 patients would provide 90% confidence that the range between 86% and 116% of the point estimate of geometric mean AUCtau would encompass the true value. Pharmacokinetic analyses were performed for all treated patients with available pharmacokinetic data. Summary statistics were used to describe the pharmacokinetic parameters. Geometric means and coefficients of variation were presented for Cmax, Ctau, and Cavg, and the median (range) was presented for Tmax. Safety was summarized descriptively for all patients who received at least one nivolumab dose. Immunogenicity was reported for patients with baseline and at least one postbaseline ADA assessment. Similarly, descriptive statistics were reported for exploratory end points, including biomarker and patient preference assessments.

Results

Between December 4, 2018 and September 7, 2022, 103 patients received study treatment across parts A, B, and D of the study (part A, n=22; part B, n=45; part D, n=36), and 28 patients previously enrolled in part A and B proceeded to part C (figure 1). Baseline characteristics are described in table 1. The enrolled patient population was diverse, representing multiple solid tumors typically considered nivolumab-sensitive2 in the advanced or metastatic setting (table 1). The baseline characteristics varied by age, weight, and prior lines of therapy, allowing investigation of pharmacokinetics, safety, and immunogenicity of nivolumab SC with rHuPH20 across a broad range of patients. The median (range) duration of therapy in part C was 8.34 (0.0–10.2), 6.47 (1.2–9.5), 3.25 (0.0–7.4), and 1.43 (1.0–9.2) months in groups 1, 2, 3, and 4, respectively. The median (range) duration of therapy in part D was 3.81 (0.0–9.2) months.

Safety

A summary of AEs in patients who transitioned to part C and AEs reported in part D are provided in table 3. In part C, there were four (44.4%), four (66.7%), two (40.0%), and three (37.5%) patients with low-grade (grade 1/2) TRAEs in groups 1, 2, 3, and 4, respectively. No patients developed new or worsening grade 3 or higher TRAEs or TRSAEs, and no TRAEs led to discontinuation or death. In groups 1, 2, 3, and 4, there were three (33.3), two (33.3%), two (40.0%), and one (12.5%) patient(s), respectively, with low-grade TRAEs under the system organ class (SOC) of general disorders or administration site conditions (online supplemental table 2). In part D, any-grade TRAEs occurred in 27 (75.0%) patients, and grade 3/4 TRAEs occurred in four (11.1%) patients (online supplemental table 2). TRSAEs were reported in three (8.3%) patients: two (5.6%) were grade 3/4. Low-grade TRAEs for SOC/preferred terms of general disorders/administration site conditions were reported in 13 (36.1%) patients. Injection site-related AEs occurred in eight (22.2%) patients and were low-grade (online supplemental table 3).

Immunogenicity

The incidence of ADA development to nivolumab SC for parts A, B, and D can be found in online supplemental table 4. The number of patients who developed ADAs to nivolumab SC across parts A–D was small. One patient in part A was considered persistently ADA positive. No neutralizing ADAs were detected in the study. There was no association of ADA development with select AEs (ie, bronchospasm, hypersensitivity, or infusion-related reactions).

Patient preference

In total, 190 responses for the PEPQ were received from 103 patients. Across parts A–C, most patients preferred SC administration over IV infusion or had no preference, and patients on all regimens of nivolumab SC, including those enrolled in part D, were satisfied with the SC injection (online supplemental figure 1). On a scale of 0–10, patients reported minimal pain/discomfort (score <2) associated with SC injection (mean (SD) score: 1 (0.8) for part C; 1.5 (1.7) for part D) (online supplemental figure 2). Overall, most patients were not bothered by the amount of time it took for SC administration (online supplemental figure 3); over 90% of responses were recorded as ‘not at all’ for the item measuring how bothered the patients were with the amount of time it took for the SC injection. Furthermore, most patients stated that nivolumab SC administration took less time than expected or an acceptable amount of time. Most patients who participated in groups A–D reported no negative impact on the time available to talk to an HCP or to interact/socialize with other individuals (non-HCPs) (online supplemental figure 4).

Biomarkers

Across multiple tumor types, nivolumab SC with or without rHuPH20 showed a numerical mean increase from a baseline of 5.0% in CD8+ TILs at cycle 1 day 15, with the greatest increases observed in CRC, HCC, and melanoma (figure 3A). A mean increase from a baseline of 2.8% in PD-L1 tumor cell expression was observed across all tumor types at cycle 1 day 15, with the greatest increases in CRC, NSCLC, and melanoma (figure 3B). Among the peripheral biomarkers, sPD-1 increased progressively after the first nivolumab SC administration across dose cohorts (figure 4A). Increases in IFNγ and CXCL9 were observed after the first administration of nivolumab SC with or without HuPH20, with a strong trend of cycle-day effects but no apparent dose-dependence (figure 4B,C) in the serum across all tumor types. Discussion

This is the first comprehensive phase I/II study investigating nivolumab SC in a population of diverse solid tumor types and heterogeneous treatment histories. This study demonstrated that combining nivolumab with rHuPH20 allows SC delivery of nivolumab, with acceptable safety. The pharmacokinetic profile of nivolumab SC was well characterized.

Findings from the non-compartmental pharmacokinetic analysis suggested that nivolumab SC exposures after the first dose increased approximately proportional to dose. Maximum serum concentrations were observed 5–7 days after dosing, which is reflective of absorption processes following SC administration. Across the doses tested, the SC formulation of nivolumab with rHuPH20 greatly reduced the mean administration time to <5 min. Studies of SC rituximab, trastuzumab, and daratumumab have also reported reduced administration and chair time with SC administration in combination with rHuPH20.4 11 19

Nivolumab SC co-administered with rHuPH20 was well tolerated across doses, with a safety profile consistent with historical nivolumab IV.2 Local injection site reactions were observed with nivolumab SC+rHuPH20 but were generally low-grade and transient. ADAs to nivolumab SC were reported and were not associated with select AEs. The incidence of ADAs to nivolumab SC was generally higher than that seen with historical nivolumab IV,20 although patient sample sizes were small. Between 0% and 4.5% of patients across doses were persistently ADA positive and no patients were positive for neutralizing antibodies, indicating a low risk for nivolumab immunogenicity with SC administration in this study.

Although the number of respondents to the PEPQ was small, patients either preferred SC dosing over IV or had no preference. Most patients were very satisfied with how nivolumab was administered and reported minimal pain/discomfort associated with SC injection. Most patients were not bothered by the duration of SC administration and indicated that it did not negatively impact the time available to talk with their HCP or to interact/socialize with non-HCPs. These findings reflect patient acceptance and could potentially influence improved treatment adherence, as has been reported with oral chemotherapy.21

Across multiple solid tumor types, increases in tumor biomarkers and changes in key peripheral pharmacodynamic markers demonstrate immune activation postnivolumab SC consistent with the expected immunomodulatory effect of nivolumab IV as observed from historical studies.15 18 Overall, these data indicate downstream pharmacodynamic effects and immune activation following target engagement with nivolumab SC. Despite the limitations of cross-trial comparisons, these pharmacodynamic effects are consistent with those seen with nivolumab IV in patients with advanced melanoma.15

This study was designed to characterize the pharmacokinetics and initial safety of nivolumab SC and, therefore, has some limitations. First, as this is an early phase study, direct, quantitative comparisons of nivolumab SC and IV were not made. Second, in parts A–C, clear attribution of systemic TRAEs to nivolumab SC or IV delivery is not possible. This is because patients in part C were initially treated with nivolumab SC in parts A and B and then switched to nivolumab IV in part C to avoid potentially lower exposure to nivolumab in the event of low SC bioavailability and then switched back to nivolumab SC after the part C protocol amendment. Third, the design of the study prevented the assessment of tumor indication-specific pharmacodynamic effects and restricted the opportunity to collect on-treatment biopsies during the study.

The pharmacokinetics of nivolumab SC with rHuPH20 across multiple doses and tumor types were well characterized by non-compartmental analysis and the study enabled further model-based analyses to support dose selection.22 The nivolumab SC formulation with rHuPH20 enabled quicker administration while maintaining the tolerability of nivolumab. These results supported the evaluation of nivolumab SC+rHuPH20 in the phase III study CheckMate 67T (NCT04810078), which is investigating nivolumab SC in advanced RCC.23

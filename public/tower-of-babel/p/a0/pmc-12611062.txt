A Viewpoint on Reframing Genetic Myopathy Classification: A Unified, Semiotic, Tri-Axis Approach
Authors: Satish V Khadilkar, Hiral A Halani
Published: 2025

A Field at Crossroads

The field of genetic myopathies has become complicated with the exponential growth of information in genetics and pathophysiology. Classification systems have not been able to keep pace with these developments and have taken refuge in dealing with small segments of myopathies and analyzing them in a nonuniform, limited manner. As a result, confusion and lack of clarity have become unavoidable. There is a need for rethinking the genetic myopathy classification to mitigate the existing difficulties.

The Limits and Nonuniformities of Current Classification Systems

Current classification schemes are based on phenotypic markers such as onset age (congenital myopathies), affected muscle groups (limb-girdle muscular dystrophies (LGMD), distal myopathies), genetic variants (titinopathies), histology (myofibrillary myopathies), or pathophysiology (glycosylation defects), creating a fragmented map of muscle disorders with overlaps at multiple points. The field’s current frameworks are characterized by rigidity and redundancy and face challenges such as the lack of uniformity in disease designation, limitations of using a single anchor, and the genetic-phenotypic and pathomechanistic continuum across individual categories [Table 1].

The Need for a Paradigm Shift: A Unified Framework

The rigid boundaries and fragmentation of genetic myopathies into smaller categories, combined with varying anchors as the basis of classifications, have led to difficulties and overlaps. Continued use of the current frameworks is expected to increase complexities as more genes get identified in the future and newer pathomechanisms are recognized. We propose bringing various categories such as LGMDs, congenital myopathies, congenital muscular dystrophies, and distal myopathies under a unified framework using a single adaptable design. This will create a broader denominator for interpreting the existing knowledge and finding new associations.

Pragmatic Semiotics and Modal Logic as the Skeleton of the Unified Framework

A. Proposed tri-axis framework using pragmatic semiotics

We chose Pierce’s pragmatic semiotics,[9] the study of signs and symbols (semiotics), emphasizing their practical use and effects in real-world contexts (pragmatics), as a conceptual framework. This triadic model involves three interdependent components: a sign, an object, and an interpretant. We chose this model over other semiotic models for its similarities to genetic myopathy diagnostics. Using Peirce’s triad as a lens, the reclassification of genetic myopathies can be framed along three axes – Sign (genotype), Object (phenotype), and Interpretant (mechanism), where the sign represents the object to the interpretant [Figure 1].

The Tri-axis framework. AD: autosomal dominant, AR: autosomal recessive, VOUS: variant of uncertain significance

We now discuss the application of the three axes in this proposed framework [Figure 2].

Pierce’s semiotics for genetic myopathies

Genotype: The obligatory inclusion of a genetic signature in this framework incorporates the much-needed information for the accuracy of diagnosis, genetic counselling, and participation in clinical trials. The mode of inheritance will be included to account for the varying behavior of the causative gene variants. The genetic variant and pathogenicity can also be incorporated wherever feasible to enhance the mechanistic correlations. Such information is more applicable once an Artificial Intelligence (AI) platform is available to clinicians to decipher the clinic-genetic complexity. Phenotype: Clinical evaluation will continue to be the first step in analyzing a myopathy. However, the ability to reach a comprehensive diagnosis based on the phenotype is limited to a handful of myopathies, such as myotonic dystrophy and facioscapulohumeral muscular dystrophy. In most myopathies, however, only a broad clinical grouping is possible. The age at presentation and the patterns of affected muscle groups are two important considerations in this analysis. Four groups, as per age at onset, and seven broad groups based on the pattern of weakness have been created [Figure 2]. Apart from the age and the pattern of weakness, the associated features, such as prominent cardiac, respiratory, or Central nervous system (CNS) involvement, will be mentioned as additional phenotypic markers wherever relevant. This simplified approach reduces overlaps but does not lose its utility in the initial assessment. As we move into the genomic era, we have reluctantly refrained from using scientists’ names for diseases, as they do not provide comprehensive correlates; nonetheless, these important historical landmarks (e.g. Duchenne and Becker muscular dystrophy, Bethlem and Ulrich’s diseases) will retain their identities in ward rounds and discussions. Pathophysiology: The novel axis we propose is disease pathophysiology, encompassing both the pathomechanism and the pathology. It is important to document pathological hallmarks such as myofibrillar abnormalities, inclusion bodies, and nemaline rods whenever identified. This axis is crucial for progress in understanding disease processes and furthering therapeutic strategies.[1011] While information about this axis is largely unavailable to clinicians, it is vital for the advancement of the field. As explained further in the section on modal logic, a patient’s data can be entered into the framework even when some data points are unavailable by using appropriate symbols.

B. Applying modal logic to the semiotic tri-axis framework

As we know, the relationships of variables in the triadic framework are dynamic and context-dependent, as exemplified by one genotype, many phenotypes, and vice versa. To strengthen the dynamic and contextual interpretability of this framework, we propose the integration of modal logic through its core operators of necessity (□), possibility (◊), knowledge (K), obligation (O), and uncertainty (U). Modal logic offers a structured way to reason about possibilities, necessity, knowledge, and obligations. Modal logic supports the dynamic interpretant function in Peirce’s triad: the clinician can now reason not only about the current state of signs and objects but also about their necessity, plausibility, or clinical imperative. This is exemplified in Box 2.

Advantages of the modal semiotic framework: The modal semiotic framework accommodates nonlinear and many-to-many mappings. It acknowledges that a genotype sign does not deterministically map to one phenotype, but acquires its significance through biological interpretation, and that phenotypic meaning emerges from an interplay of genotypes and mechanisms. In this respect, the framework becomes dynamic and will continue to evolve without a change in the basic framework. As more data become available, the interpretation evolves, potentially leading to a reclassification or renaming of genetic disorders (knowledge). Based on the available knowledge, the required clinical actions (e.g., cardiac or pulmonary screening in a particular myopathy patient) can be notified, and uncertainties (of a genetic variant or its mechanism) will be acknowledged by “U” in this framework (obligation). Tri-axis Framework and AI: Insights from a Dataset of 84 GNE Myopathies

The power of AI can be utilized to operationalize the proposed tri-axis genetic myopathy framework by translating complex clinical and genetic data into structured, actionable insights aligned with genotype, phenotype, and pathophysiological axes.

We analyzed the clinical and genetic data of 84 patients with UDP-N-acetylglucosamine-2 epimerase/N-acetylmannosamine kinase (GNE) myopathies using the tri-axis framework and an AI program (ChatGPT). Basic clinical and genetic data of the 84 patients were entered. The program learned and could apply the modal logic format of the tri-axis framework to the patient data. It further divided the data according to genotype and arranged them in the tri-axis scheme. This led to prompting the most appropriate clinical actions in the given categories to help the clinical neurologist [Table 3].

Call to Action

We propose this framework not as a finished product but as a scheme in evolution. It invites collaboration, validation, and refinement by the neuromuscular community. Its strength lies in its adaptability: as discoveries emerge, the system evolves without requiring wholesale restructuring. This semiotic-modal tri-axis system offers a blueprint for precision neuromuscular medicine, integrating diverse data streams into a coherent, clinician-friendly model.

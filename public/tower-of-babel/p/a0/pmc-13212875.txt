The miR396f-GRF7 Module Confers Thermotolerance in Rice
Authors: Jinsheng Yu, Xinyu Jiang, Xinxin Huang, Yujun Xie, Qiuping Shen, Shen Ni, Nai wu, Bai Li, Qiuhong Luo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Heat stress is one of the major threats to global rice production globally. Uncovering key genetic regulators of thermotolerance is crucial for breeding climate-resilient crops. While microRNA396 (miR396) is known to be heat-responsive, the function and mechanism of its specific member, miR396f, remain elusive. Here, we report that miR396f is a heat-induced miRNA exhibiting predominantly in vascular bundles. Its transcriptional induction was more pronounced in a thermotolerant variety (HT54) than in a heat-sensitive one (HT14), correlating with natural variation in its promoter sequence. Through a multi-step screening, we identified growth-regulation factor 7 (GRF7) as its direct target under heat stress. Genetic evidence demonstrated that the miR396f-GRF7 module positively regulates thermotolerance at both the seedling and flowering stages: miR396f knockout lines (396fKO) were heat-sensitive, whereas GRF7 knockout lines (grf7) exhibited enhanced thermotolerance. Transcriptome analysis of grf7 mutant under heat stress suggested its potential involvement in sugar metabolism pathways. Our study unveils the miR396f-GRF7 module as a novel positive regulator of rice thermotolerance, providing valuable genetic targets for future breeding programs.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12284-026-00909-5.

Introduction

Rice (Oryza sativa L.) serves as the staple food for over half of the world's population, constituting the primary caloric source for more than 3.5 billion people (Wing et al. 2018). As a leading producer and consumer, sustainable rice production in China is of strategic importance for national food security. However, rice cultivation faces severe challenges from increasing heatwaves associated with global warming. High temperatures, particularly during sensitive developmental windows such as anthesis and fertilization, can drastically impair grain filling and lead to substantial yield losses (Satake and Yoshida 1978; Jagadish et al. 2007; Easterling et al. 2000). Developing heat-tolerant rice varieties through molecular breeding is therefore crucial for ensuring food security, which requires an in-depth understanding of the genetic modules and molecular mechanisms underlying thermotolerance.

Crop thermotolerance is a complex biological process influenced by genetic, environmental, and management factors. In recent years, conventional management strategies have proven insufficient against increasingly frequent heat stress, making genetic enhancement for thermotolerance an urgent priority for stable yield and quality (Xie et al. 2023; Xing et al. 2024). Map-based cloning has led to the identification of several quantitative trait loci (QTLs) and key genes conferring thermotolerance at seedling or reproductive stages, including OgTT1 (Li et al. 2015), OsHTAS (Liu et al. 2016), OsSLG1 (Xu et al. 2020), OgTT2 (Kan et al. 2022), OrHTH5 (Cao et al. 2022), OsEMF1 (Xu et al. 2022), OgTT3 (Zhang et al. 2022a), and QT12 (Li et al. 2025). Despite these advances, the breeding application of these genes is still underway, and some alleles or haplotypes are effective only in specific genetic backgrounds. Moreover, forward genetics approaches are often laborious and time-consuming, leaving our understanding of the genetic basis of rice thermotolerance fragmented and necessitating efficient reverse genetics strategies to uncover novel regulatory modules (Lu et al. 2025).

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) have emerged as key post-transcriptional regulators in plant responses to various abiotic stresses, including heat (Sunkar et al. 2012). They fine-tune gene expression by guiding the cleavage or translational repression of target messenger RNAs (mRNAs). Among them, the miR396 family is highly conserved in plants and primarily targets a class of Growth-Regulating Factors (GRFs), which are transcription factors known to play pivotal roles in leaf development, flower formation, and grain size (Omidbakhshfard et al. 2015). OsGRF1 was first discovered as a gibberellin-induced transcription factor (Van der Knaap et al. 2000). Subsequently, with the development of whole-genome sequencing and expressed sequence tags (ESTs), 12 and 9 GRF genes were identified in rice and Arabidopsis, respectively. The miR396/GRF regulatory module is highly conserved in land plants. Recent studies have shown that the miR396/GRF module plays important roles in plant growth, defense signaling, and stress responses (e.g., drought, high salt, low temperature, and UV). In rice, most identified GRFs are associated with grain size or yield traits (Gao et al. 2015; Che et al. 2015; Duan et al. 2015; Li et al. 2016; Miao et al. 2020; Zhang et al. 2020). Using CRISPR/Cas9 technology, Miao et al. (2020) found that miR396ef double knockout mutants showed yield increase in Hainan but not in Hangzhou, indicating their environment-dependent effects. Zhang et al. (2020) reported that besides improving yield under low nitrogen, disruption of miR396e and miR396f also affected gelatinization temperature and chalkiness rate. Liebsch and Palatnik (2020) comprehensively reviewed that miR396-GRF/GIF influences important agronomic traits and integrates endogenous and environmental signals during plant growth. However, to date, only OsGRF4 has been reported to be involved in thermotolerance (Mo et al. 2023). In Arabidopsis, miR396 contributes to leaf senescence and salt stress response (Debernardi et al. 2012; Liu et al. 2022). In rice, our previous work has shown that several miR396 members are responsive to heat stress (Miao et al. 2018; Ye et al. 2018a, b). However, the precise biological function, the in vivo targets, and the regulatory mechanism of specific miR396 members, particularly miR396f, in rice thermotolerance throughout the entire growth cycle remain largely unexplored.

Preliminary evidence suggests that miR396f is the most heat-responsive member of the miR396 family in an indica thermotolerant rice line HT54 (Ye et al. 2018a, b). This strong induction hints at its potential central role in the heat stress response network. Nevertheless, critical questions remain unanswered: What is the spatiotemporal expression pattern of miR396f under heat stress? Does its regulation contribute to the natural variation in thermotolerance among different rice varieties? If yes, what are its direct downstream targets mediating the heat response? Most importantly, what is the functional relevance of the miR396f-mediated pathway at both vegetative and reproductive stages?

To address these questions, we systematically investigated the expression regulation and biological function of miR396f. We confirmed that miR396f is a heat-inducible miRNA with distinct tissue specificity, predominantly in vascular bundles. We found that its transcriptional responsiveness to heat varies between thermo-tolerant and heat-sensitive varieties, correlating with polymorphisms in its promoter region. Through a multi-step screening strategy, we identified GRF7 as one of the direct targets of miR396f. Genetic evidence demonstrated that the miR396f-GRF7 module positively regulates thermotolerance not only at the seedling stage but also, crucially, at the flowering stage. Our findings unveil a novel genetic module that coordinates heat adaptation across different developmental phases in rice, providing valuable insights and genetic resources for breeding climate-resilient crops.

Material and Methods

Plant Materials and Growth Conditions

The rice materials included wild-type (WT) cultivars ‘Nipponbare’ and ‘Zhonghua 11’ (ZH11), indica thermotolerant line HT54 (Wei et al. 2013), indica heat-sensitive line HT14, and various transgenic lines in ‘Nipponbare’ or ‘ZH11’ background: miR396f-overexpressing (396fOE), miR396f knockout (396fKO), miR396 family target mimic (MIM396, Gao et al. 2015), GRF7 knockout (grf7), and proMiR396f:GUS lines. Plants were grown in growth chambers/greenhouses under standard conditions (12 h light/12 h dark, 28 °C, 70% relative humidity). For seedling-stage heat stress, 14-day-old seedlings were treated at 45 °C. For reproductive-stage stress, plants at flowering were exposed to 39 °C for 48 h.

Plasmid Construction and Plant Transformation

For 396fOE, the genomic fragment of pri-miR396f from HT54 was cloned into the pCAMBIA1300NU vector under the Ubiquitin promoter. For proMiR396f:GUS, a ~ 2.0 kb promoter fragment from HT54 was cloned into the DX2181b vector. CRISPR-Cas9 was used to generate miR396f knockout mutants. The grf7 line was obtained from Biogle (Lu et al. 2017; https://www.seedseek.cn/) and self-fertilized to homozygosity. All constructs were verified by sequencing and introduced into Agrobacterium tumefaciens strain EHA105 for rice transformation.

Promoter Sequence Analysis and Cloning

The 2100 bp sequence upstream of the OsmiR396f gene was retrieved from Ensembl (https://plants.ensembl.org/index.html). The CTAB method was used to extract HT54 genomic DNA from rice seedling leaves, and NanoDrop ONE (Thermo) was used to determine the DNA concentration. KOD FX high-fidelity polymerase (TOYOBO) was used, and the OsmiR396f promoter was amplified via nested PCR via the primers P OsmiR396f-2033 -F/P OsmiR396f-2033 -R in the first round and P OsmiR396f-2033 -FN/P OsmiR396f-2033 -RN in the second round (Table S1) due to sequence complexity.

RNA Extraction and qRT-PCR

Total RNA was extracted using TIANGEN RNAprep Pure Plant Kit. For miRNA, reverse transcription used stem-loop primers and Hifair® V First-Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit. For mRNA, Hifair® V one-step RT-gDNA digestion SuperMix was used. qRT-PCR was performed on a QuantStudio 6 Flex system using Hieff® qPCR SYBR Green Master Mix. U6 snRNA and UBIQUITIN11 (UBQ11) were used as internal controls for miRNA and mRNA, respectively. The 2^(−ΔΔCt) method was used for relative quantification.

GUS Staining and Activity Assay

Tissues from proMiR396f:GUS plants were incubated in GUS staining solution at 37 °C overnight, followed by chlorophyll clearance in 70% ethanol. Stained tissues were observed under a stereomicroscope. For paraffin sections, tissues were dehydrated, embedded, sectioned, and observed. Quantitative GUS activity was measured fluorometrically using MUG as a substrate.

5' RLM-RACE

Total RNA from Nipponbare seedlings was used for 5' RLM-RACE with the FirstChoice™ RLM-RACE Kit (Invitrogen™). Gene-specific primers were designed for candidate targets (Table S1). RACE products were cloned into pMD19-T vector and sequenced to identify cleavage sites.

Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay

Wild-type or mutated target sites of GRF7 and PIN3t were inserted into the 3’ UTR sensor vector (Addgene). The effector plasmid contained the miR396f precursor under the 35S promoter. Reporter and effector plasmids were co-infiltrated into Nicotiana benthamiana leaves via Agrobacterium (strain GV3101). Luciferase activities were measured 48–72 h post-infiltration using the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega, USA).

Thermotolerance Assays

For seedling stage, survival rates were calculated after 45 °C stress and a 7-day recovery. For mature stage, seed-setting rates of main panicles were determined after 39 °C stress during flowering and subsequent maturation.

Transcriptome Sequencing and Analysis

Total RNA was extracted from 14-day-old seedlings of grf7 and WT (ZH11) under control and heat stress (45 °C for 12 h) conditions. Three biological replicates were used per sample. Libraries were sequenced on Illumina NovaSeq 6000. Clean reads were aligned to the MSU v7.0 genome using HISAT2. DEGs were identified using DESeq2. GO enrichment was performed using AgriGO (https://systemsbiology.cau.edu.cn/agriGOv2/, Tian et al., 2017).

Bioinformatic Analysis

The miR396f promoter sequences from HT54 and HT14 were isolated and analyzed for cis-regulatory elements using NewPLACE (https://www.dna.affrc.go.jp/PLACE/) and PlantCARE (https://bioinformatics.psb.ugent.be/webtools/plantcare/html/). Putative target genes of miR396f were predicted using the psRNATarget web server with default parameters (https://www.zhaolab.org/psRNATarget).

Statistical Analysis

Data are presented as mean ± SD from at least three biological replicates. Statistical significance was determined by Student's t-test or one-way ANOVA with Duncan's test (p < 0.05).

Results

miR396f is a Heat-Inducible miRNA with Distinct Tissue Specificity

Previous reports (Miao et al. 2018; Ye et al. 2018a, b ) from our group have revealed that miR396f was strongly induced by heat. To dissect its expression pattern, we analyzed its spatial and temporal expression in 'Nipponbare'. The primary transcript (pre-miR396f) was predominantly expressed in stems at the seedling stage, and in stems, leaves, and panicles at the mature stage, with the highest levels in stems and young panicles (Fig. 1A). We then tested the heat responsiveness of the promoter. Compared with the control, GUS staining intensified after heat treatment (Fig. 2D), and quantitative assays showed that GUS activity increased approximately sixfold after 2 h of heat stress (Fig. 2E). These results collectively demonstrate that miR396f is a heat-inducible miRNA with significant tissue specificity.

The Transcriptional Response of miR396f to Heat Differs Between Varieties

We compared the dynamic response of miR396f to heat in the thermotolerant HT54 (Wei et al. 2013; Liu et al. 2016) and the heat-sensitive HT14. qRT-PCR analysis revealed that in HT54, miR396f expression was significantly upregulated by heat, peaking at 24 h. In contrast, its expression in HT14, while slightly higher than the control, changed gently without a significant induction peak (Fig. 3). We cloned and compared the miR396f promoter sequences from HT54 and HT14. Both contained core promoter elements and various cis-acting elements related to stress responses (Table S2). GRF7 is One of the Direct Targets of miR396f in Rice Under Heat Stress

To systematically identify the direct target of miR396f under heat stress, we employed a multi-step screening strategy (Fig. 4A). Bioinformatics prediction yielded 78 potential targets. Concurrently, transcriptome data from miR396f-overexpressing lines showed 690 and 8,416 significantly upregulated genes at seedling and panicle stages, respectively. The intersection of these datasets yielded 23 high-confidence candidate target genes (Fig. 4A, Figure S3A). Initial qRT-PCR screening using a miR396 family target mimic line (MIM396) showed that 10 of these genes were significantly upregulated (Figure S3A). Subsequently, these 10 genes were consistently downregulated in miR396f-overexpressing lines (Figure S3B) and, in two independent knockout lines, 7 showed a stable upregulation trend (Figure S3C). A 5' RLM-RACE assay confirmed that GRF7 was cleaved at the 11th-12th nucleotide of the complementary site with miR396f, consistent with the prediction (Fig. 4B). For PIN3t, the cleavage site was not at the predicted position, and no effective cleavage was detected for bZIP34 (Figure S4).

To further confirm the targeting relationship, we performed a dual-luciferase reporter assay. The co-expression of miR396f with a reporter containing the wild-type GRF7 3' UTR significantly reduced luciferase activity, whereas this suppression was abolished when the target site was mutated (Fig. 4C). No significant effect of miR396f was observed on the PIN3t reporter activity. Taken together, we conclude that GRF7 is a direct cleavage target of miR396f in vivo.

The miR396f-GRF7 Module Regulates Thermotolerance at the Seedling Stage

We validated the function of this module in seedling thermotolerance using genetic materials. After 45 °C heat stress, the survival rate of two independent miR396f knockout lines (KO3-4 and KO3-6, ~ 13–17%) was significantly lower than that of the wild-type (WT, ~ 37%), indicating that miR396f positively regulates seedling thermotolerance (Fig. 5A and 5C). Correspondingly, loss-of-function of GRF7 significantly enhanced plant thermotolerance, with the survival rate of grf7 knockout lines (~ 66%) being significantly higher than that of the WT (~ 9%) (Fig. 5B and 5D). The miR396f-GRF7 Module Regulates Thermotolerance at the Reproductive Stage

The thermoregulatory function of this module also held true at the mature stage. Under normal temperatures, the seed-setting rates of three independent miR396f-overexpressing lines (396fOE) did not differ significantly from the WT (Fig. 6A). However, after a 48-h 39 °C heat stress during flowering, the seed-setting rates of all three 396fOE lines (~ 15–16%) were significantly higher than that of the WT (~ 8.6%) (Fig. 6B). This demonstrates that enhancing miR396f expression also improves thermotolerance at the reproductive stage. To explore the downstream pathways, we performed transcriptome analysis on 14-day-old seedlings of grf7 mutants and the WT (ZH11) under control and heat stress conditions. Under heat stress, 9,425 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified in grf7 plants, with 4,736 upregulated (Fig. 7A) and 4,626 downregulated. Further analysis revealed that 62 of these 4,736 upregulated genes overlapped with direct targets of GRF7 from a published ChIP-seq dataset (Chen et al. 2024). Gene Ontology (GO) analysis of these 62 genes indicated that GRF7 is likely involved in biological processes such as oligosaccharide and disaccharide metabolism (Fig. 7B). Discussion

Heat stress poses a formidable threat to rice productivity, and unlocking the genetic codes of thermotolerance is imperative for future-proofing our staple crops. While miRNAs are established as key players in stress responses (Yu et al. 2025), the functional delineation of specific members often remains obscured. This study systematically deciphers a novel miR396f-GRF7 regulatory module that acts as positive regulator of thermotolerance in rice. Our work charts a pathway from natural variation in the promoter, through predominance in vascular bundles, to the post-transcriptional control of a key transcription factor, ultimately influencing physiological outcomes at both vegetative and reproductive stages.

We confirmed that miR396f is not merely heat-inducible but exhibits pronounced spatiotemporal specificity. Its predominant expression in vascular bundles, as revealed by promoter-GUS assays, offers a crucial topological clue. Vascular tissues are central to hydraulic conductance, nutrient redistribution, and systemic signaling under stress (Liu et al. 2019). The enrichment of miR396f in these tissues suggests its potential role in safeguarding or reprogramming the vascular system's function to maintain homeostasis under heat stress, possibly by modulating the flow of photosynthetic assimilates or stress signals.

Furthermore, the stark contrast in the transcriptional amplitude of miR396f between the thermotolerant HT54 and the heat-sensitive HT14 unveils a layer of natural variation critical for adaptation. The differential configuration of cis-acting elements in their promoters provides a plausible molecular basis for this divergence. We hypothesize that polymorphisms in key elements (Table S2, e.g., MYBCORE and ABRELARERD1 binding sites) may alter the binding affinity of heat shock factors or other stress-responsive transcription factors, thereby tuning the induction sensitivity of miR396f. To gain a deeper understanding of the natural variation of miR396f in natural populations, we conducted haplotype analysis on the mature form of miR396f and its upstream 2 kb sequence in 533 rice germplasm samples (Wu et al. 2022). There are mainly five haplotypes (P_HapI to P_HapV) at the miR396 locus, with almost all japonica rice samples clustered in haplotype P_HapI (Figure S5). HT54 and HT14 belongs to P_HapⅣ and P_HapШ, respectively. Further phenotypic analysis under high temperature conditions revealed significant differences on days 6, 14, 20, and 24 after high temperature treatment, except for day 10 ((Figure S6). Obviously, the natural variations within promoter of miR396f contribute, partly but substantially, to the different heat tolerance between HT54 and HT14. This positions the miR396f promoter as a valuable haplotype resource for molecular marker development, aiding in the selection of varieties with superior innate heat responsiveness.

Through rigorous multi-step screening and validation, we identified GRF7 as one of the direct targets of miR396f in vivo. The GRF family has been extensively studied in the context of growth and development (Omidbakhshfard et al. 2015), and some members have been implicated in stress responses (Vercruyssen et al. 2015; Chen et al. 2024), while only OsGRF4 recently implicated in thermotolerance (Mo et al. 2023). Our genetic evidence places GRF7 squarely within the heat stress signaling network as a negative regulator. The dramatically enhanced thermotolerance in grf7 knockout mutants underscores that miR396f, by cleaving GRF7 mRNA, effectively releases a "brake" on the plant's heat defense mechanisms. This defines a clear post-transcriptional regulatory layer in the heat stress response, where the induction of miR396f leads to the rapid downregulation of a transcriptional repressor of thermotolerance. One of remaining flaws in this study is that there was only one knockout line of GRF7 with only heat tolerance at the seedling stage presented, but considering its phenotype was opposite to that of the 396f knockout lines, and they are negatively regulated, it can be said that the above presented data is solid and reliable.

An intriguing aspect of our findings is the apparent stage-specific effect of miR396f manipulation. While its knockout rendered plants heat-sensitive at the seedling stage, its overexpression did not confer a significant advantage at this stage (data not shown). This can be plausibly explained by a combination of low basal expression and functional redundancy. In seedlings, miR396e is the dominantly expressed member, with the normalized reads for miR396f at the seedling stage only about 2.5 percent of those for miR396e (Table 2, Miao et al. 2018). At the reproductive stage, however, where miR396e and miR396f are more equally expressed, elevating miR396f levels tips the balance, leading to more effective suppression of GRF7 and a clear phenotypical advantage. This suggests that the regulatory influence of the miR396-GRF network is not static but is developmentally rewired, a concept critical for designing stage-targeted genetic interventions.

Our transcriptomic analysis of the grf7 mutant under heat stress offers a compelling, though preliminary, glimpse into the downstream processes. The significant enrichment of GRF7 ChIP-seq targets (Chen et al. 2024) among upregulated genes in grf7 involved in oligo- and disaccharide metabolism is highly suggestive. Sugars function beyond mere metabolites; they act as osmoprotectants, reactive oxygen species scavengers, and signaling molecules under abiotic stress. The downregulation of GRF7 may therefore unleash a metabolic reprogramming that boosts the accumulation of protective sugars, such as raffinose family oligosaccharides, which are known to stabilize membrane and protein structures under heat. This positions the miR396f-GRF7 module as a potential integrator of transcriptional and metabolic responses to heat stress, a hypothesis that warrants further targeted metabolomic and biochemical validation.

From a breeding perspective, our findings offer two tangible strategies. First, the natural variation in the miR396f promoter can be harnessed through marker-assisted selection to pyramid favorable haplotypes. Second, and more directly, CRISPR-Cas9-mediated knockout of GRF7 presents a powerful approach to constitutively release thermotolerance, as demonstrated by our mutant studies and aligned with the successful application of gene editing in crop improvement. The vascular-predominant activity of the native miR396f promoter also suggests that driving miR396f overexpression with its own promoter could be a precise strategy to enhance thermotolerance with minimal pleiotropic effects on overall growth.

In conclusion, we have delineated a coherent regulatory axis from promoter variation to a miRNA-transcription factor duo that positively regulates thermotolerance across developmental stages. This study not only identifies a new genetic module with significant application potential but also opens new avenues for exploring the intricate interplay between miRNA-mediated regulation, metabolic reprogramming, and stress adaptation in plants. Future work focusing on the direct downstream targets of GRF7 through integrated ChIP-seq and multi-omics approaches will be key to completing this signaling circuit and fully harnessing its power for climate-resilient crop design.

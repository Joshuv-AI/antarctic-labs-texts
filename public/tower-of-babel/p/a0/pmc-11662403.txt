Feasibility of a using chest strap and dry electrode system for longer term cardiac arrhythmia monitoring: Correspondence
Authors: Hinpetch Daungsupawong, Viroj Wiwanitkit
Published: 2024

Dear Editor,

We would like to comment on “Feasibility of a using chest strap and dry electrode system for longer term cardiac arrhythmia monitoring: Results from a pilot observational study [1].” Studies on the diagnostic performance of long-term external cardiac monitors (ECMs) provide basic insights, but the technique and data interpretation raise serious problems. The study's observational approach restricts the capacity to demonstrate a causative association or overall effectiveness of ECMs vs regular Holter monitoring, especially when both modalities were employed concurrently in Group A. Comparing the performance of ECMs as separate instruments may provide more detailed insights into their diagnostic capacity and patient acceptance.

Furthermore, the study did not clearly define criteria for severe arrhythmias, which could lead to diagnostic evaluations being overestimated due to incorrect detection events induced by motion artifacts. The selection criteria focused on individuals at high risk for arrhythmias, but it is necessary to consider whether these patients reflect a diverse group in terms of arrhythmia types, symptom duration, and past therapies. Such factors may have an impact on patient reported comfort levels and the monitoring device's overall performance. Furthermore, the comfort study was qualitative, with no clear quantitative metrics that may provide a more complete picture of the patient experience. Future research should involve employing validated questionnaires to completely assess wearability. Indicators may include cutaneous irritation or mental damage.

Although the majority of Group B participants reported a high level of comfort (94.9 %), it is vital to ask how this influenced their daily activities and general quality of life during the follow-up period. Furthermore, only a small proportion of patients (14 out of 34) were available to complete the survey and assess arrhythmia, which may have resulted in selection bias, a skewed perception of comfort, and inaccurate diagnostic outcomes for the larger population. A bigger and more diversified sample size, including randomization and control groups, would yield more generalizable conclusions and improve the trustworthiness of the findings.

To further study this, future research should concentrate on refining the ECM's design to eliminate motion artifacts, which drastically impacted the quality of ECGs in Group A. Examining the effect of different monitoring periods on different types of arrhythmias could assist determine the best monitoring regimen. Furthermore, putting in place a monitoring strategy that looks at how ECM results relate to long-term patient outcomes would provide useful information on the technology's clinical value. New approaches could include incorporating machine learning algorithms for real-time ECG signal analysis to reduce false positives caused by motion aberrations, boosting the diagnostic yield of ECMs.

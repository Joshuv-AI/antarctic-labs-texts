Clinical characteristics and outcomes of anti-MDA5 dermatomyositis: a retrospective study
Authors: Tulaton Sodsri, Tananchai Petnak, Thitiporn Suwatanapongched, Chayanin Nitiwarangkul, Nantakarn Pongtarakulpanit, Pintip Ngamjanyaporn
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAnti-MDA5 dermatomyositis (anti-MDA5 DM) is a rare but severe subtype of dermatomyositis associated with high mortality. This study aimed to describe clinical characteristics and identify variables associated with rapidly progressive interstitial lung disease (RP-ILD) and mortality among affected patients.MethodsPatients diagnosed with anti-MDA5 DM between January 2013 and October 2022 were included. Demographics, clinical manifestations, and laboratory data were collected. Univariable Cox proportional hazard regression was used to assess mortality predictors, while logistic regression models identified factors associated with RP-ILD.ResultsAmong 26 patients (mean age, 56.6 ± 15.1 years, 7 men), the most common clinical manifestation was Gottrón’s papules (80.8%), followed by ILD (73.1%), periungual erythema (69.2%), heliotrope rash (61.5%), V-sign (42.3%), arthritis (42.3%), weakness (38.5%), shawl sign (34.6%), mechanic’s hand (34.6%), and Raynaud’s phenomenon (19.2%). Among 19 ILD patients, 9 (47.4%) developed RP-ILD, and seven patients died over a median follow-up of 1.5 years. The overall mortality rate in this cohort was 26.9%, with 77.8% having RP-ILD. Mortality was associated with male sex (HR, 7.7; 95% CI, 1.49–39.9; P = 0.02) and RP-ILD, which had a risk difference of 77.8 (95% CI 50.6-104.9%; P = 0.001). Arthritis (P = 0.01) and ANA positivity (P = 0.03) were associated with reduced risk of RP-ILD. Patients with RP-ILD tended to receive aggressive treatments, including triple therapy regimens (55.6%), which consisted of a combination of intravenous methylprednisolone and intravenous cyclophosphamide with either rituximab or a calcineurin inhibitor. Additional treatments included intravenous immunoglobulin (22.2%) and plasma exchange (33.3%).ConclusionAnti-MDA5 DM was associated with a high prevalence of ILD, particularly RP-ILD, contributing to increased mortality, underscoring the need for early identification of high-risk patients and personalized management.Take-home messageMale sex was a key predictor of RP-ILD and poor prognosis in anti-MDA5 dermatomyositis patients. Background

Idiopathic inflammatory myopathies (IIM) are a group of systemic autoimmune disorders that primarily affect the skeletal muscles but may also involve the skin, lungs, and other organ systems. Dermatomyositis (DM), a subtype of IIM, is characterized by progressive muscle weakness and distinctive skin rashes, as defined by the Bohan and Peter diagnostic criteria [1]. A distinct subset of DM is associated with anti-melanoma differentiation-associated protein 5 (MDA5) autoantibodies and typically presents with prominent cutaneous manifestations and minimal to absent muscle weakness, a phenotype referred to as clinically amyopathic dermatomyositis (CADM).

The prevalence of anti-MDA5 DM varies by ethnicity, with higher rates reported in Asian populations (11–60%) compared to other ethnic groups (7–16%) [2]. This subtype is strongly associated with interstitial lung disease (ILD), which affects 50% to nearly 100% of patients with anti-MDA5 DM [3–5]. Among these, rapidly progressive ILD (RP-ILD), defined by the rapid deterioration of respiratory symptoms and ILD progression within three months of onset, is a major contributor to mortality, occurring in 30–100% of cases [2, 6, 7]. These findings underscore the critical need for early identification of high-risk patients and timely, prompt intervention to improve outcomes [7, 8].

Despite its significant clinical impact, particularly in Asian populations, data on factors influencing RP-ILD and mortality in anti-MDA5 DM remain limited. This study aimed to address this gap by retrospectively evaluating the clinical characteristics, treatment strategies, and variables associated with RP-ILD and mortality among patients with anti-MDA5 DM at a tertiary care hospital in Thailand.

Methods

Study design and subject selection

This single-center retrospective study was conducted at Ramathibodi Hospital, Bangkok, Thailand. Patients who underwent testing for myositis-specific antibody (MSA) panels using the EUROIMMUN immunoblots® assay between January 2013 and October 2022 were screened for eligibility. The study protocol was reviewed and approved by our institution’s Human Research Ethics Committee (COA. MURA2023/109). Patients were eligible for inclusion if they met al.l of the following criteria as follows: (1) age ≥ 18 years, (2) a diagnosis of DM was based on the Bohan and Peter criteria, with all patients classified as having probable or definite DM [1], and (3) a positive anti-MDA5 autoantibody results (defined as ≥ 1 + on the immunoblots® assay).

Baseline characteristics, follow-up, and outcome identification

Electronic medical records of eligible patients were thoroughly reviewed to collect on demographic data, clinical manifestations, results of laboratory tests, and imaging findings. Laboratory tests included complete blood count (CBC), erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), positivity of antinuclear antibodies (ANA) by using indirect immunofluorescence assay, creatine phosphokinase (CPK), and C-reactive protein (CRP).

The clinical features assessed included heliotrope rash, Gottrón’s papule, mechanic’s hands, periungual erythema, the V-sign, the shawl sign, palmar vasculitis, Raynaud’s phenomenon, cutaneous ulceration, fever, ILD, RP-ILD, muscle weakness, and arthritis. All clinical manifestations were documented by rheumatologists and/or a pulmonologist.

Of the 26 patients, 22 underwent volumetric chest high-resolution computed tomography (HRCT) or CT scans, with a median interval of 24 days (range: 0–251 days) before or after the diagnosis of DM. All HRCT/CT scans were performed using multidetector CT scanners at our institution, with a reconstructed slice thickness ranging from 1.0 to 3.0 mm. Two experienced thoracic radiologists jointly reviewed all HRCT and CT images to assess the presence and patterns of ILD, including organizing pneumonia (OP), nonspecific interstitial pneumonia (NSIP), or usual interstitial pneumonia (UIP) [9]. RP-ILD was defined as a rapid deterioration of ILD on chest imaging within three months of respiratory symptom onset.

The diagnosis of myositis was based on elevated serum creatine kinase or aldolase levels, with or without histopathological confirmation. Patients were followed from the time of diagnosis until death or the end of the study period (October 2023) to evaluate the clinical outcomes and disease progression. All medications administered to treat anti-MDA5 DM from initial diagnosis through the end of the study period were reviewed and categorized. These included intravenous methylprednisolone (IVMP), intravenous cyclophosphamide (IVCY), oral cyclophosphamide, rituximab (RTX), calcineurin inhibitors (CNIs), methotrexate, azathioprine, mycophenolate mofetil, intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG), plasma exchange, and combination (triple) therapy regimens. Triple therapy was defined as either IVMP + IVCY + RTX or IVMP + IVCY + CNIs.

Statistical analysis

Continuous data were summarized as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median with interquartile range (IQR), depending on the data distribution. Categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages. Comparisons between categorical variables were performed using the Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. For continuous variables, comparisons between two independent groups were performed using the independent samples t-test or the Mann–Whitney U test, depending on normality.

Univariate regression analysis was used to identify variables associated with the occurrence of RP-ILD, with reported as odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Univariate Cox proportional hazards regression analysis was performed to evaluate factors associated with mortality, and results were reported as hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% CIs. Survival analysis was performed using the Kaplan–Meier method to estimate cumulative survival rates. A P-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed using STATA version 10.0 (StataCorp LP, College Station, TX, USA).

Results

Between January 2013 and October 2022, a total of 1,666 MSA panels were performed, and 52 patients tested positive for anti-MDA5 antibodies. Among these, 26 patients were excluded because they did not meet the inclusion criteria: 5 were under the age of 18, and 21 did not have confirmed probable or definite DM based on the Bohan and Peter criteria, although some of these patients exhibited features such as RP-ILD (n = 3), non-RP-ILD and autoimmune features (n = 1), or cutaneous findings suggestive of DM (n = 2).

The final cohort included 26 patients who met the inclusion criteria for this study, all of whom had confirmed probable or definite DM. The mean age at diagnosis was 56.6 ± 15.1 years, and the majority were female (73%). Among these, 6 (23.1%) were diagnosed with classical DM and 20 (76.9%) with CADM. Five patients (19.2%) had overlapping connective tissue diseases, and malignancy was identified in 3 patients (11.5%), with one case each of breast, cervical, and skin cancer.

Cutaneous manifestations were common, with Gottrón’s papules observed in 21 patients (80.8%) and heliotrope rashes in 16 patients (61.5%). Less frequent findings included the V-sign (42.3%) and the shawl sign (34.6%). Among the 23 patients tested for ANA, 12 (52.2%) were ANA-positive. Discussion

This study provides valuable clinical insights into anti-MDA5 DM, highlighting its clinical manifestations, treatment strategies, and outcomes. The majority of patients were female, with CADM being the predominant phenotype. ILD was highly prevalent, affecting 73.1% of the cohort, with 47.4% of these cases progressing to RP-ILD. The overall mortality rate was 26.9%, and both male sex and RP-ILD were identified as significant predictors.

Only one prior report from the region, an abstract from a Singaporean cohort of 18 patients, describing baseline characteristics among anti-MDA5 DM patients compared to those who were anti-MDA5-negative [10]. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first cohort study from Thailand, and more broadly, from Southeast Asian, to report on clinical predictors of RP-ILD and mortality in patients with anti-MDA5 DM.

The predominance of female patients in our cohort is consistent with previous studies, which have reported a higher prevalence of anti-MDA5 DM among females [11–13]. Despite this, our findings demonstrated a strong association between male sex and adverse outcomes, including an increased risk of both RP-ILD and mortality. Prognostic tools, such as the CROSS score, have similarly identified male sex as a significant risk factor [14]. This disparity may be explained by underlying biological factors, including hormonal influences and genetic factors, which may contribute to sex-based differences in autoimmune disease susceptibility and progression [15].

The high prevalence of CADM in this study is consistent with findings from Asian cohorts [4, 16], where CADM is observed more frequently than in other ethnic populations [2]. Cutaneous manifestations, including Gottrón’s papules and heliotrope rash, were commonly noted in our cohort, while the V-sign and shawl signs were relatively less frequent, similar to previous studies [17].

The high prevalence of ILD in our study reinforces the strong association between anti-MDA5 DM and pulmonary involvement, particularly among Asian populations [4, 12, 16, 18–20]. This finding supports the hypothesis that both genetic and environmental factors contribute to the pathogenesis of ILD in these patients [21]. Male sex remained a significant risk factor for RP-ILD, consistent with previous studies [14, 22]. Conversely, arthritis and ANA positivity were associated with a lower risk of RP-ILD in our cohort. The observed association between arthritis and a lower risk of RP-ILD aligns with prior studies that categorized anti-MDA5 DM patients into three phenotypic clusters; in particular, the dermato-rheumatologic cluster, characterized by prominent arthritis, exhibited a lower incidence of RP-ILD [6].

Although anti-Ro52-positivity, particularly in conjunction with ANA-positivity, has been reported to be associated with an increased risk of RP-ILD, in previous studies [15, 24, 25], our study did not find a significant association between anti-Ro52-positivity and either RP-ILD or mortality. One potential explanation for this discrepancy is the small sample size and the relatively lower proportion of anti-MDA5 DM patients with anti-Ro52-positivity in our cohort (50%), compared to prior studies (65–75%) [14, 23, 24].

Similarly, although factors, such as older age, elevated CRP, fever, leukocytosis, increased neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio, cutaneous ulcers, and inverse Gottrón’s sign have been incorporated into prognostic scoring systems, such as the CROSS and FLAW models, and shown predictive value in earlier reports [7, 14, 25], we did not find significant associations with RP-ILD These discrepancies may be due to variations in sample sizes, population characteristics, or study methodology. Taken together, our findings underscore the need for future multicenter studies to validate the role of anti-Ro52 and other risk factors in disease progression among patients with anti-MDA5 DM.

The overall mortality rate in our study was comparable to previously reported rates in both Asian and European cohorts (range: 23.4–37.9%) [6, 7, 26, 27]. Consistent with earlier findings [22, 28], male sex was also associated with increased mortality in our cohort. One proposed explanation involves telomere shortening, which has been linked to male sex and may contribute to reduced survival [15]. This association between male sex, shortened telomeres, and increased mortality has also been observed in other connective tissue diseases [28]. Notably, in familial forms of ILD, men tend to have significantly shorter telomeres, which may underlie their adverse prognosis. Moreover, telomere-related gene (TRG) mutations associated with telomere shortening have been identified in genetic studies, suggesting a role in disease progression and potentially serving as a biomarker for disease severity [29].

The high mortality observed in patients with RP-ILD (78%) underscores the aggressive nature of this condition and its limited response to standard immunosuppressive therapy [30]. Emerging evidence now supports the use of upfront combined immunosuppressive agents early in treatment regimens to improve outcomes in high-risk patients [31]. Additionally, adjunctive therapies such as IVIG and plasma exchange have shown additional benefits [32, 33].

However, in our study, patients were enrolled beginning in January 2013, during a period when awareness and optimal treatment strategies for anti-MDA5 DM were still evolving. The standard treatment of anti-MDA5 DM typically includes corticosteroids in combination with other immunosuppressive agents. In our study, CNIs and RTX were more frequently prescribed for patients with RP-ILD than those without, reflecting current medication recommendations for aggressive immunosuppression in severe cases, in addition to the first-line IVMP therapy. This prescribing pattern aligns with currently established guidelines [34], which advocate for early, intensive treatment in high-risk patients.

Although plasma exchange and IVIG were administered to some patients with RP-ILD in our study, they were often used as salvage treatments rather than components of an early combination regimen, which may have contributed to the high mortality observed in severe cases. While triple therapy was more frequently prescribed for RP-ILD patients in our cohort, in line with current recommendations [34], only half of these patients received this combination therapy. This underscores the need for broader adoption of upfront combination therapy over monotherapy in anti-MDA5 DM patients with RP-ILD to improve outcomes.

This study has several limitations. First, it was a retrospective study conducted at a single tertiary care institution, which inherently limits the generalizability of the findings. Second, the small sample size, reflecting the rarity of anti-MDA5 DM, reduces the statistical power to detect significant associations. The retrospective design also led to missing data for certain prognostic factors, such as ferritin and lactate dehydrogenase levels. The limited number of male patients and RP-ILD cases is another constraint, making it difficult to determine whether the observed higher odds ratio of RP-ILD in male patients is consistent across broader populations. ILD data were unavailable in four patients who nonetheless fulfilled the inclusion criteria for anti-MDA5 DM. Additionally, the study relied solely on the Bohan and Peter criteria, which require evidence of muscle involvement [35], and may have excluded a significant portion of anti-MDA5 DM patients who have amyopathic DM. To address these limitations, future research studies should involve larger cohorts or prospective registries to validate our findings and improve predictive models for RP-ILD and mortality.

Conclusion

Anti-MDA5 DM patients demonstrated a high overall mortality rate, with male sex and the presence of RP-ILD as significant mortality predictors. Conversely, the presence of arthritis and ANA positivity might be associated with a lower risk of RP-ILD.

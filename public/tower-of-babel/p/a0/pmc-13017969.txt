Revisiting T cells: Innate actions and emerging links to innate memory response
Authors: Kim A Tran, Maziar Divangahi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe traditional view of the immune system distinguishes between the innate immune system, which serves as the host’s first line of defense against pathogens, and the adaptive immune system, which evolved to manage more complex or recurrent infections. However, the discovery of evolutionarily conserved mechanisms in innate memory cells (trained immunity) has revealed adaptive-like characteristics in innate immune cells, challenging the conventional dogma between innate and adaptive immunity. Simultaneously, growing evidence shows that T cells exhibit innate-like features. In this review, we explore unconventional T cells (γδ cells, natural killer T cells, mucosal-associated invariant T cells) and conventional αβ T cells (virtual memory, memory T cells) to emphasize the broad spectrum of beneficial effects that the adaptive immune system can particularly exert in early host defense strategies, including innate immune memory. These mechanisms confer clear advantages for immune protection and homeostasis, especially during early childhood, and provide a new perspective on canonical immune functions.Keywords: trained immunity, memory, T cells, innate-like T cells

Introduction

Selective pressures arising from the coevolution of microorganisms and complex species have shaped the development of immune systems unique to each organism. While the innate immune system is fundamental to all animal species, vertebrates also benefit from the additional advantages provided by an adaptive immune system. Lymphoid cells constitute the core of adaptive immunity, and their emergence can be traced back to prevertebrate deuterostomes, the most ancient living phylogenetic group with detectable lymphocytes.1 Adaptive immunity, as is defined in humans, is a key feature of jawed vertebrates and first arose in cartilaginous fish.2 B cell receptors (BCRs) and T cell receptors (TCRs) represent the hallmark of gnathostome acquired immunity and have been evolutionarily conserved over 500 million years. Genetic diversity of lymphocyte receptors is mediated by RAG enzymes which participate in somatic site-specific DNA rearrangements. Recombination enzymes are directed to recombination signal sequences (RSSs) for splicing of the variable (V), diversity (D), and joining (J) segments. The result is a massive immunologic repertoire that averages about 1014 Ig molecules and 1018 TCR molecules in humans.3,4 When the host encounters a pathogen or non–self-antigen, innate immune responses, particularly antigen-presenting cells (APCs), bridge the innate and adaptive immune system by presenting peptide antigens to T and B lymphocytes. Amid the vast repertoire of TCRs and BCRs generated by somatic rearrangements, APCs will prime naïve lymphocytes with corresponding specificity triggering clonal expansion of antigen-specific cells. These activated adaptive immune cells are fine-tuned to directly target and eliminate invading pathogens. Upon a secondary challenge, circulating memory cells can mount a more rapid and robust response which imparts the host with memory and enhanced protection to previously encountered microbes. Immunological memory is a hallmark of how vertebrates can fight off infections and provided the basis for vaccination which has revolutionized modern medicine.

This resulted in a clear distinction between the innate and adaptive immune systems. Innate cells act as a first line of defense acting antigen-independently while the adaptive immune cells appear later to generate an antigen-specific response that will arm the host against subsequent insults. However, 2 landmark discoveries in innate immunity have profoundly altered our traditional view of the innate immune system as a simple, nonspecific defense mechanism. First, the discovery of pattern recognition receptors in simple organisms (e.g. plants) and complex organisms (e.g. mammals) demonstrated that innate immune cells possess specificity in recognizing pathogens.5 Second, the discovery of trained immunity—describing the ability of innate immune cells to retain memory-like features through epigenetic, transcriptional, and metabolic reprogramming—has significantly shifted our perspective on innate and adaptive immunity.6 In contrast, lymphocytes recognize highly specific antigen configurations of pathogens and subsequently trigger clonal expansion, cellular differentiation and the production of antigen-specific T/B cells as well as antibodies.7 However, the distinction between innate and adaptive immunity cannot simply be drawn by “nonspecific” versus “specific,” by the diversity of somatically generated antigen receptors, or even by the capacity to generate memory responses.

During trained immunity, a training agent provides an initial stimulus that epigenetically reprograms innate immune cells, enabling them to mount a stronger response to subsequent challenges, even after returning to a nonactivated basal state for prolonged periods of time.8 Although this differs from memory responses in adaptive immunity, it indicates that the innate system can adapt a memory response based on past experiences. Indeed, in simple organisms lacking adaptive immunity, plants and invertebrates have evolved mechanisms to generate innate memory responses. For instance, plants can develop systemic acquired resistance (SAR), a form of immune memory, in which signals are sent to uninfected areas to protect against future infections.9 Importantly, the vast majority of the animal kingdom (∼>95%) is devoid of an adaptive immune system,10 thus it is imperative that the innate branch of immunity evolved memory-like traits in host defense against infectious diseases.

The observations of trained immunity in humans have had tremendous implications to improve strategies for host immunity whereby targeting the innate immune system can generate memory responses as well and contribute to protection against future challenges. The action of trained immunity has been primarily associated with the enhanced activity of myeloid cells. Beneficial reprogramming of monocytes and macrophages was exemplified with β-glucan or Candida albicans treatment which heightened the subsequent response to unrelated pathogen-associated molecular patterns and pathogens.11,12 This was associated with epigenetic marks located on the promoter sites of genes encoding for IL-6, TNF-α, and IL-1β. Enhanced production of IL-6 and TNFα cytokines is also shown following stimulation of monocytes isolated from Bacillus Calmette-Guérin (BCG)–vaccinated individuals, underpinning the role of these proinflammatory molecules in trained immunity.13 Mature innate immune cells are known to be short lived, with myeloid cells averaging a lifespan of 5 to 7 d. However, the benefits of trained immunity appear to last much longer than the duration of innate immune cell activity. Studies elucidated that the longevity of trained immunity could be perpetrated by reprogramming of hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells upstream of mature immune cells located in the bone marrow, which has since been termed “central trained immunity.” While central reprogramming of progenitor cells represents a pivotal feature of lasting effects from trained immunity and has been extensively reviewed,14,15 we postulate that the contribution of lymphocytes, particularly T cells, can augment innate immune memory in tissues, termed “organ trained immunity.”

During early life, neonates display a developing immune system and lack prior exposure to microbes that is critical in shaping a working adaptive immune system and specific memory. The transition from a semi-sterile fetal environment to a microbe-rich setting furthermore puts neonates at a high risk of infection, which account for nearly half of all neonatal deaths.16 Early life immunity primarily depends on transplacental transfer of maternal antibodies and breastfeeding. However, the protection conferred by these is estimated to only last a few months.17 Furthermore, T cells are not fully matured due to developing thymic output, low antigen exposure, and high levels of regulatory cytokines (IL-10, TGF-β).18,19 As such, neonates strongly rely on innate defenses to fight off infections. During development, the immune system is in a highly plastic state with immune cells undergoing constant adaptations to a changing environment. Early life innate immune cells have been shown to be sensitive to stimulation and exhibit trained immunity traits,20 even from in utero infections.21 Additionally, the neonatal system has evolved to produce innate-like lymphocytes to further contribute to early host immunity and compensate for the underdeveloped adaptive niche (Fig. 1). Much like what is observed in trained immunity, innate-like lymphocytes can acquire the ability to adapt and form memory. This supports the premise of how trained immunity evolved as a primitive form of adaptation in vulnerable hosts that depend mainly on innate defenses, to protect against infections. Over time, exposure to microorganisms shape the immune repertoire of antigen-specific lymphocytes enabling complete functionality of the immune processes. However, innate immune memory and innate-like lymphocytes remain significant contributors to defense strategies due to their ability to confer broad protection. Moreover, conventional lymphocytes that possess antigen-specific memory exhibit features of cross-protection, likely as an evolutionary mechanism to broaden immune coverage and build up innate memory functions already present. Increasingly, studies are highlighting immune functions for both innate and adaptive immune cells that fall outside the scope of their canonical role and converge toward innate immune memory. Although adaptive immunity is characterized by highly antigen-specific responses, T cells also exhibit numerous innate-like features.

In this Brief Review, we explore defined conventional and unconventional T cell subsets that have been reported to exhibit innate-like functional properties such as rapid cytokine production and reduced dependence on classical antigen priming. Unconventional T cell populations (γδ T cells, natural killer T [NKT] cells, mucosal-associated invariant T [MAIT] cells) are enriched early in life and display semi-invariant receptors and cytokine-responsive activation profiles which support an antigen-inexperienced neonatal immune system. Moreover, as the immune system matures and encounters microbes, the generation of memory T cells can impart broad immune coverage despite being antigen specific. We examine the cellular contexts and stimuli under which conventional memory T cells adopt innate-like behaviors, such as cross-reactivity and TCR-independent activation. Thus distinct T cell subsets can occupy functional states that challenge traditional innate-adaptive boundaries and how these properties can contribute to innate immune memory.

Unconventional T cells and their role in early life

T lymphocytes undergo cell maturation in the thymus, a specialized organ equipped with distinct architecture for optimized processes of T cell selection. Thymic activity reaches its peak during fetal development and first few years of life, then progressively reduces its output throughout adult years resulting in thymic involution.22 Consequently, several classes of unconventional T cells are generated with a key function of providing early protection during postnatal development. These subsets of unconventional subtypes include γδ T cells, NKT cells, and MAIT cells, all of which primarily home to barrier sites. While canonically T cell activation was largely described upon cognate antigen stimulation, studies in germ-free mice reported the presence of T cell with effector functions without exposure to any antigens which imparts the T cell compartment with innate properties before memory formation.23,24

γδ cells: The earliest contributors

Conventional T cells are marked with an αβ TCR and undergo stepwise thymic development through the double negative, double positive (DP), and finally single positive (SP) stages. In contrast, unconventional T cell maturation branch off at earlier stages which affords them with a broader spectrum of immune functions. The earliest T cell subset to develop in vertebrates are considered to be γδ T cells that possess a γδ TCR that spans to recognition of exogenous and endogenous ligands. γδ T cells can act independently of major histocompatibility complex (MHC)–presenting molecules and recognize conserved nonpeptide compounds including stress-induced antigens, bacterial metabolites, phosphoantigens, and lipoproteins.25,26 While conventional T cells require several signals for activation, γδ T cells show increased expression of activated markers early in their development which affords rapid initiation of effector functions upon recognition of ligands.27 Although γδ T cells constitute a fraction of circulating and peripheral lymphocytes, they preferably populate epithelial-rich tissue such as the skin and mucosal sites, like the lungs where they can compose up to 50% of T cells. γδ T cells follow a distinct developmental course with lineage commitment occurring at the double negative, which precedes αβ precursors. In mice, γδ T cells exit the thymus in successive waves, with each egress corresponding to a distinct subset of γδ T cell that possess unique functions and tissue tropism.28 These include dendritic epidermal T cells, which promote skin homeostasis and repair,29 and IL-17–producing γδ T cells that can rapidly recruit neutrophils to sites such as the gut and lungs, serving key roles in tissue maintenance and early immune defense.30 The homing of IL-17–producing γδ T cells to the lungs represents a crucial aspect of host defense against pulmonary infections. Given the constant exposure to environmental challenges, the lungs must serve as a robust immune barrier against invading pathogens. Up to 20% of resident pulmonary lymphocytes are γδ T cells suggesting a potent role in immune defense.31 Lung-resident γδ T cells are ideally located for rapid production of IL-17 during infection, which can initiate early inflammatory responses and limit microbial burden.32,33,34 Furthermore, γδ T cells have been shown to contribute to the resolution of inflammation following influenza infection with the production of IL-22.35 While IL-22 does not control viral replication, its impact reduces immune-mediated lung injury caused by inflammation and thus reduces the risk of secondary bacterial infection.35 In contrast to viral infections, IL-22 is required for protection against Mycobacterium tuberculosis (Mtb).36 Thus, the function of γδ T cells in host defense against infections is context dependent.

Studies into the murine repertoire of γδ T cells have elucidated various distinct γδ subsets for several barrier defenses. In mice, γδ T cells first appear in the fetal liver at 5 to 7 wk of gestation before transiting to the fetal thymus by 8 wk.37 However, the segregation within human γδ T cell subsets is not as evident as in mice. The Vγ9+Vδ2+ T cell subset initially predominates during fetal development. Even at this stage, studies have shown their ability to produce IFNγ when stimulated with bacterial metabolites, indicating evidence of early thymic programming.38 As gestation progresses, Vγ9-Vδ1+ cells become the dominant γδ T cell group seeding the postnatal skin, lungs, intestines, and colon epithelium. However, soon after birth a rapid expansion of Vγ9+Vδ2+ T cells overtakes the circulation accounting for 90% of γδ populations.39 Vγ9+Vδ2+ T cells recognize phosphoantigens and present clear cytotoxicity and IFNγ potential upon stimulation. Recently, the role of γδ T cells has been further explored against the pathogenesis of Mtb. Studies have reported the benefit of Vδ2+ T cell therapy and phosphoantigen administration in the protection against Mtb, with one clinical trial demonstrating enhanced survival of tuberculosis-infected nonhuman primates with Vδ2+ T cells immunization.40 While a growing effort has begun on utilizing γδ T cells for therapeutic purposes (e.g. chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) γδ T cells),41 additional work aimed toward stimulating these innate T cells would be widely beneficial particularly in the context of childhood infections.

Immunoregulatory role of NKT cells

The primary role of the immune system is to distinguish between “self” and “non-self.” NKT cells, which express surface markers indicative of NK cell and activated/memory T cell phenotype (NK1.1, CD25, CD44, CD69, CD122) develop in the thymus to undergo positive selection by self-ligands presented on the CD1d molecules of cortical thymocytes. This process gives rise to the expression of a CD1d-restricted semi-invariant TCR, which is in theory self-reactive.42 However, NKT utilize the recognition of endogenous lipids to detect changes in the microenvironment representing a distinct homeostatic role. NKT cells are generated between 12 and 20 wk of gestation and comprise a high frequency in the fetal thymus. NKT cell levels see a gradual egress during fetal development with low numbers found in the postnatal thymus indicating seeding of tissues during gestation. As opposed to γδ T cells, NKT cells branch off from the conventional thymic development at the DP stage.43 Following thymic exit, NKT cells home to the liver, spleen, and bone marrow. The CD1d molecule is expressed on most professional APCs and contains a hydrophobic antigen-binding domain ideal for lipid tails, making glycolipid ligands the prototypical antigens for NKT cells. Activation can occur when exogenous glycolipids are processed by APCs then delivered to NKT cells via CD1d molecules.44 Importantly, endogenous (self-)glycolipids can trigger activation with additional inflammatory signals. TLR signaling, cytokine production, and engagement of costimulatory molecules (CD28, CD40L, OX40) by activated APCs will trigger NKT activation even with a low TCR signal from a self-ligand.45,46

NKT cells are subdivided into 2 classes: type I and type II. Type I NKT cells possess an invariant αβ TCR and are thus commonly termed invariant NKT (iNKT) cells. The identification of the iNKT cell ligand α-galactosylceramide (αGalCer) has significantly facilitated iNKT cell studies with the use of αGalCer-loaded tetramers. The discovery that αGalCer is expressed in mammalian cells revealed that the level of this endogenous ligand is tightly regulated by a series of catabolic enzymes degrading α-ceramides into α-linked glycolipids, which in turn mediates NKT cell activation.47 These enzymatic pathways were further identified in monocytes and shown to be rapidly induced during inflammatory conditions.48 Thus, indirect activation with endogenous αGalCer appears to be a primary mechanism for iNKT cells. This process is moreover tightly controlled by the immune system via degradation pathways, likely due to the ability of NKT cells to generate immediate responses. In particular, the presence of iNKT cells within the lung vasculature and interstitial spaces has revealed their significant roles in host defense against pulmonary infections.49,50 Although present at low numbers under steady-state conditions, patrolling iNKT cells can rapidly migrate into lung tissue in response to infection or injury. In Mtb-infected mice, iNKT cells have been shown to rapidly produce GM-CSF which activates cDC1 to potentiate CD8+ T cell responses.51 In fact, treatment with αGalCer increases the number of Mtb-specific IFNγ-producing T cells in the lungs.49 In the context of viral infection, production of IFNγ by NKT cells following αGalCer treatment has shown to heighten CD8+ T cell and NK cell responses providing enhanced protection against various strains of influenza A virus (IAV).50,52 While still defined by a CD1d-restricted TCR that recognizes lipid antigens, type II NKT cells possess a diverse αβ TCR that primarily interacts with β-linked glycolipids and are thus referred to as diverse NKT (dNKT) cells. Fewer studies have investigated dNKT cells due to their scarcity and lack of unique surface markers. Sulfatide, an endogenous sulfated glycolipid, was the first identified antigen for dNKT cells and can be found in various locations including the liver, pancreas, kidney, and myelin of the CNS.53 NKT cells are positioned at key points of microbial entry near cells that express the lipid-presenting molecule CD1d.54,55 Cellular lipid gradients are tightly regulated and can be disrupted by number of stressors. NKT cells can quickly detect changes in the concentration or structure of these ligands enabling them to manage inflammation caused by internal stress, such as in autoimmunity, or external stress, such as during infection.56 While NKT cells can strengthen immune responses, they can also dampen overt inflammation by producing IL-10, promoting regulatory T cells and inhibiting autoreactive B cells.57,58 The recognition of self-lipid antigens makes NKT cells inherently autoreactive, which enables rapid immune modulation. However, this function can also potentiate chronic inflammation in specific disease contexts such as systemic lupus erythematosus, rheumatoid arthritis, and multiple sclerosis.59 The delicate balance between these opposing roles is crucial, as further work elucidates how we can better utilize NKT cells in host innate defense mechanisms.

MAIT cells mediate mucosal barrier integrity

The colonization of the skin and gut microbiome is instrumental in educating the immune system and maintaining proper homeostasis. As such, the immune system must develop alongside the populating commensal microbes without triggering an inflammatory response. MAIT cells, as their name implies, are largely enriches in mucosal tissues such as the intestinal tract and lungs and have been associated with homeostatic roles of preserving barrier integrity. Unlike the other classes of unconventional T cells, MAIT cells are not detected in germ-free mice which underscores the vital involvement of the microbiome-derived metabolites to MAIT cell development.60 This is likely why MAIT cells are more abundant in humans compared with mice due to our constant exposure to microbes, accounting for <1% of T cell in mouse but up to 5% of circulating T cells in humans reaching over 45% of lymphocytes in the liver with a gradual increase throughout adult life.61 MAIT cells exit the thymus at the DP stage during the second trimester of gestation. Although poised to home to mucosal sites early on, MAIT cells are considered naive during egress, with their expansion dependent on microbial colonization.62,63 The gradual accumulation of MAIT cells at barrier sites provides an adaptable immune population to discern commensal versus pathogenic invaders.

MAIT cells express an invariant TCR restricted to the MHC class I–related molecule MR1, which is a highly conserved molecule across mammals expressed on most cell types.60 MAIT cells recognize metabolites derived from the vitamin B2 (riboflavin) biosynthesis pathway presented on MR1 molecules.64 Vitamin B2 biosynthesis is distinct to yeast and bacteria, which grants a unique sensing mechanism for MAIT cells to recognize microbial agents. Specific ligands of the riboflavin pathway have recently been found which has aided in the identification of MAIT cells with tetramer staining: 5-OE-RU and 5-OP-RU.64 The presence of MAIT cells in the intestinal mucosa has likely evolved into a mutual relationship whereby commensal microbes induce the development of MAIT cells, and in return, MAIT cells function in immunosurveillance to sense any changes in the microbiota and maintain gut barrier integrity.65 The high evolutionary conservation of the MR1 molecule across mammalian species substantiates the crucial role MAIT cells play in our system. The recognition of foreign microbes has thus likely evolved into a fine-tuned balance between threshold levels of riboflavin metabolites and the requirement for additional inflammatory signals for optimal activation of MAIT cells. In fact, the Toll-like receptor (TLR) is vital in the activation of MAIT cells as treatment with ligand 5-OP-RU alone was insufficient to recapitulate the pulmonary MAIT cell expansion following Salmonella typhimurium infection due to a lack of TLR signaling.66 Moreover, MAIT cells can be indirectly activated by cytokines due to their enriched expression of IL-18 and IL-12 receptors, as well as type I IFN, rendering them sensitive to viral infections as well.67,68 Upon activation, MAIT cells can rapidly mobilize to the site of infection and induce proliferative expansion. Effector functions of MAIT cells involve prompt production of cytokines such as IFNγ, TNFα, IL-17, and GM-CSF, which stimulates myeloid cells for antimicrobial activity as well as promotes CD4+ T cell responses.69 Beyond immune sensing and antimicrobial roles, MAIT cells maintain homeostasis of their respective mucosal niches by contributing to tissue repair.70 Several studies have shown evidence of MAIT cell crosstalk with the epithelium, resulting in epithelial cell and keratinocyte regeneration.70 While rapid production of proinflammatory cytokines has been described, MAIT cells can also secrete IL-10 modulate inflammatory perturbations.71 Overall, MAIT cell presence is crucial during early microbial colonization of mucosal sites and maintenance of balanced responses to commensal and pathogenic microbes. Particularly in the gut, innate sensing of microbial-derived antigens has been linked with systemic effects seen distally such as the bone marrow and the brain.72,73 The regulation of mucosal immunity may thus have larger implications in which MAIT cells represent a uniquely positioned population to integrate inflammatory signals.

Early imprinting of effector functions

A key factor that distinguishes the majority of unconventional T cells from their conventional counterparts lies in the ‘preset’ effector programs early in development. Thymic differentiation programs equip unconventional cell subsets with cytotoxic properties or cytokine production prior to their egress. Several factors are important in the early imprinting of effector properties within the thymus. Notably, TCR signal strength is a critical determinant for γδ T cell maturation. While moderate affinity interaction between αβTCR-MHC induces positive selection of conventional T cells, strong γδTCR signaling provokes agonist selection of γδ T cells.74 Potent interactions with CD1d molecules similarly select for NKT cells with added CD28 costimulatory signals.75 Akin to αβ T cells, IL-7 signaling is indispensable for unconventional T cell development,76 particularly for γδ lineage commitment.77 The level of IL-7 signaling and availability profoundly impacts the maturation of thymic lymphocytes where low to moderate IL-7 enhances αβ T cell generation, and excessive amounts of IL-7 can block T cell development and subvert to alternative lineages such as B cells and innate-like lymphocytes (ILCs).78 Coordinated expression of IL-7 receptor (IL-7Rα) on the surface of unconventional T cell precursors in concert with other costimulatory molecules are thus imperative for proper development of innate-like thymocytes. In fact, costimulation with CD27 has further been linked with γδ T cells and IL-23 impacting MAIT cell thymic development.79 Extensive analyses have unveiled the transcriptional and epigenetic networks that govern the acquisition of differential effector programs in unconventional T cells. Discreet signaling and gene expression modalities associate with separate effector functions.80 Interestingly, NKT cells and MAIT cells follow a nearly identical transcriptional program under the promyelocytic leukemia zinc finger protein (PLZF) transcriptional factor.81 This master regulator acts to suppress naïve T cell program and modulates the gene expression of T-bet, GATA-3, and RORγt.82 Genetic ablation of PLZF abrogates tissue tropism, while overexpression of PLZF enhances tissue residency of conventional T cells.83 PLZF-deficient mice exhibit a noted decrease of innate-like T cell functions reflected by reduced IL-4+ NKT cells, IFNγ+ NKT cells, IL-17+ MAIT cells, and IFNγ+ MAIT cells.84 PLZF hence induces tissue-homing properties as well as immediate effector functions associated with the corresponding phenotype. Expression of PLZF in γδ T cells further elicits the generation of NKTγδ T cells, which bear several of the same markers and function as NKT cells such as IL-4 and IFNγ production.85 Despite shared differentiation thymic programs, NKT and MAIT cells home to different niches to fill alternate purposes. Predominantly T-bet+ NKT cells take up residency in the liver and spleen while RORγt+ MAIT cells are particularly enriched in the lungs and intestine, suggesting that tissue-homing properties occur within thymic imprinting. The thymus thus represents a distinct site for initial imprinting of effector functions in unconventional T cells. While typically thought to be an immune-privileged organ, the thymus, however, can also be directly infected, particularly by mycobacteria.86 Investigation into thymic output of conventional T cells indicates the generation of tolerant T cells following local thymic infection which significantly impairs subsequent T cell differentiation.87 Little is known about the consequences of thymic infection on other T cell subtypes. However, considering the pivotal role of intrathymic programming in unconventional T cells, early-life infection or vaccination that reaches the thymus could impact innate-like subsets and modulates neonatal immune defense systems.

ILCs can furthermore be generated outside the thymus. PLZF expression is implicated in the differentiation of ILCs with studies indicating PLZFhi precursors in the murine fetal liver and adult bone marrow show commitment to ILC lineage.88 ILC subsets (type 1 ILC [ILC1], ILC2, ILC3) have analogous effector functions to T helper effector subtypes, acting in a nonspecific innate manner and preferentially homing to barrier sties. An emerging field over the past two decades, ILCs significantly contribute to homeostatic tissue functions and host immunity at mucosal sites locations.89 Such properties are largely reminiscent of unconventional T cells, possibly owing to a similar transcription program directed by PLZF. Due to their development outside the thymus, however, ILCs are not restricted by processes of positive selection. Their lack of an antigen receptor provides their ability to respond to a broader range of stimuli without the restriction of antigen presentation, placing them at the forefront of innate defenses. Overall, coordinated PLZF expression appears to govern innate-like properties in innate-like lymphocytes while modulating the nonredundancy of prototypical T cell effector transcription programs. The variety of innate-like subtypes that reflect T cell effector properties reinforces the evolutionary benefit of favoring innate functions particularly at vulnerable sites.

Innate immune memory in innate lymphocyte populations

The imprinting of tissue residency and effector functions of unconventional T cells during early ontogeny results in the establishment of long-lived self-renewing populations at their respective barrier locations.90 As they persist, tissue-resident subsets are further shaped by their microenvironment. This is evidenced by the differential transcriptional signatures found in thymus isolated γδ cells compared with skin-isolated γδ T cells.91 Site-specific clustering is also observed across γδ-T cell–harboring organs underlining the heterogeneity that arises from tissue adaptations. Such a phenomenon has been described for MAIT cells, whereby MAIT skin cells presented a separate transcriptional pattern than MAIT cells from the spleen, lung, and liver.92 Additionally, unconventional T cells rely on distinct metabolic processes depending on their function. For instance, metabolic IL-17 γδ T cells show enhanced use of oxidative phosphorylation and fatty acid oxidation processes, as opposed to IFNγ-producing γδ T cells, which depend on aerobic glycolysis.93 MAIT cells similarly show upregulation of alternate pathways depending on effector potential with enhanced lipid uptake observed in IFNγ-producing MAIT cells compared with IL-17–producing MAIT cells in both mice and humans.94 These metabolic distinctions remain within the intrinsic programs acquired in the thymus. However, the plasticity of these cells provides some level of adaptability in peripheral tissues. NKT cell metabolic activity shifts following thymic development with an upregulation of glucose uptake to fulfill effector activity.95 Additionally, NKT cells isolated from murine spleens and liver showed enhanced reactive oxygen species compared with NKT cells from the thymus and adipose tissues.96 As such, external signals can further impact metabolic activity. For instance, in a study of an obese cohort, obesity-related low-grade inflammation disrupted glycolytic pathways of MAIT cells due to reduced mTORC1 signaling.97 Epigenetic remodeling can also occur as a result of external signals in unconventional T cells. Notably, ex vivo exposure of human PBMCs to S. typhi resulted in chromatin modifications in NKT cells, MAIT cells, and γδ T cells associated with upregulated H3K4me3, H4K20me3, and H4K20me1.98 Furthermore, antigen priming of NKT cells with α-GalCer induces epigenetic reprogramming resulting in enhanced cytotoxicity due to increased chromatin accessibility at several associated loci of granzyme molecules (Gzma, Gzmb, and Gzmk).99 Epigenetic states can likewise impact functional heterogeneity in γδ17 T cells whereby permissive chromatin configuration at the Il17a and Ifng loci in CD27− γδ precursors can generate coproduction of IL-17 and IFNγ in specific contexts.100 Considering the plasticity and adaptable nature of unconventional T cells, we can speculate that peripheral stimulations alter their responsiveness and activity in a context-dependent manner. Although innate immune memory has not been extensively investigated in unconventional T cells, transcriptional, metabolic, and epigenetic reprogramming underpins the mechanisms of trained immunity.

Classic memory responses can develop in unconventional T cells, whereby antigen-specific reaction triggers clonal expansion leading to enhanced protection during recall responses. For instance, memory γδ T cells can markedly mature to become more “adaptive” and recognize targeted antigens. This has been well documented in cytomegalovirus (CMV) infection in which CMV-specific γδ T cells acquire an effector memory T cell phenotype (CD45RA+CD27−) and respond specifically to CMV antigens.101 Characterization of peripheral blood of patients with persistent Mtb infection revealed the expansion of CD8+ γδ T cells with cytotoxic and NK-like properties, but antigen-specific for Mtb antigens.102 Expansion of long-lived antigen-specific γδ T cells has also been reported during S. aureus and Listeria monocytogenes infection in murine and nonhuman primate models.103,104 The development of antigen specificity and cytotoxicity suggests a level of adaptability in γδ T cells following infection and also involves them in the acute phase of both primary and secondary infection, uniquely bridging innate and conventional adaptive immunity. However, γδ-mediated protection has also been linked between heterologous infections. Immunization of rhesus macaques with vaccinia virus induced the enrichment of pulmonary and circulating Vγ9Vδ2+ T cells leading to an enhanced protection against monkeypox virus infection.105 Ex vivo stimulation of human Vγ2Vδ2+ T cells with mycobacteria also exhibited antiviral immunity against vaccina virus.106 Moreover, γδ T cells have been linked with the BCG-induced trained immunity.107,108 Several groups have noted the expansion of IFNγ-producing γδ T cells in BCG-immunized children. γδ T cells from BCG-vaccinated adults displayed altered transcriptional programs associated with potentiated responsiveness to lipopolysaccharide and Candida albicans stimulation.109 Regression of cutaneous melanoma lesions following intralesional BCG treatment was conferred by γδ T cell infiltration, which also trafficked to nontreated lesions, indicating intrinsic cell reprogramming by BCG.110 Overall, γδ T cells can contribute to classical adaptive memory as well as to innate memory responses, likely due to their unique plasticity at the interface of innate and adaptive functions.

Innate immune memory has also been described in NKT cells. Ex vivo stimulation of human NKT cells with IL-12, IL-15, and IL-18 7 d prior to stimulation with tumor cells induced enhanced IFNγ production, cytotoxicity, and proliferation.111 While studies on innate immune memory in NKT and MAIT cells are still emerging, there is strong evidence supporting well-established memory formation in ILCs. Hapten-sensitized ILC1s respond more robustly in a murine model of homologous skin contact hypersensitivity.112 Additionally, stimulation with an allergen or IL-33 can induce memory ILC2s, which respond and expand more strongly after a secondary stimulation.113 Isolation of human ILC2s from the dermis presented an enhanced activation transcriptional signature due to microbial exposure. This was associated with increased cytokine production following IL-33 challenge, suggesting externals stimuli can reprogram ILC2s to better respond against a heterologous challenge.114 ILCs are not restricted by an antigen receptor like T cells, which could explain their enhanced capacity to display innate immune memory. Given that ILCs, NKT, and MAIT cells share a common transcriptional program regulated by PLZF, it is plausible that they may also share adaptable properties, like memory capacity. The location of innate-like T cells at mucosal sites furthermore allows the encounter of an increased number of antigens, in contrast to conventional T cells which need to undergo antigen presentation in lymphoid organs. The antigen-rich environment of a nonsterile organ presents an ideal site for antigenic stimulation that could trigger downstream innate immune memory reprogramming and may not be limited to classical innate immune cell types.115,116 Further investigation is needed to elucidate innate memory programs in unconventional T cells.

Nonspecific memory in conventional lymphocytes

While these innate-like subsets of unconventional T cells offer early protection, conventional lymphocytes have also developed innate-like features that enable a faster initiation of immune responses in an antigen-agnostic manner. Despite minimal antigen-specific memory in early life, the developing immune system tends to generate memory-like T cells that are found to not have encountered foreign antigens. “Virtual memory” T cells can confer an additional layer of protection to the naïve host (see the following). Over time, microbial exposure shapes adaptive immunity and populates the system with conventional antigen-specific memory T cells. Although memory T cells strongly recognize a specific antigen, intrinsic properties imbue them with the capacity to cross-react with other antigens and undergo activation solely with cytokine stimuli. Moreover, recent research on trained immunity has revealed how innate immune cell functions can be enhanced by memory T cells, leading to enhance protection against infections.

Virtual memory T cells circumvent naïve T cell hurdles

Despite the presence of unconventional T cells prior to antigen exposure, αβ T cells have evolved additional mechanisms to ensure protection prior to the generation of antigen-specificity. An additional subset that has been characterized in germ-free animal models are CD8+ virtual memory (Tvm) and CD8+ innate memory T (Tim) cells, often grouped together as Tvm cells. Both subtypes describe T cells that possess phenotypical markers of memory T cells but are antigen inexperienced. This is largely identified by constitutively low expression of CD49d integrin, which reflects antigen exposure. Although still antigen specific, Tvm cells are specific for antigens that have never been previously encountered and are also predominantly dependent on cytokine stimuli for activation.117 A study has shown that naïve T cells with high CD5 expression are more predisposed to convert into Tvm cells.118 Given CD5 expression typically identify naïve T cells with a greater affinity for self-ligands, endogenous antigens may thus play a role in the development of Tvm cells. The difference between Tvm and Tim cells mostly lies in the distinction that Tim cells are IL-4 dependent and develop in the thymus, while Tvm cells are IL-15 dependent and develop in the periphery.117 It has been hypothesized that CD5hi naïve T cells that remain in the thymus will convert into Tim cells upon IL-4 contact, and CD5hi naïve T cells that egress the thymus mature into Tvm cells following interaction with peripheral IL-15.119

Lymphodepletion studies have especially aided in the characterization of Tvm cells. Adoptive transfer of naïve T cells into a lymphodepleted host will generate the proliferative expansion of Tvm cells in the absence of cognate antigen.120 This process may reflect a host strategy to develop a “preimmune” repertoire before natural antigen exposure, offering protection against infections, particularly during immunocompromised states such as lymphodepletion or fetal development. In fact, neonatal mice contain a large fraction of Tvm cells compared with adult mice, and Tvm human counterparts have been detected in cord blood and in the fetal thymus.121,122 A study by Smith et al.123 demonstrates that neonates possess T cell precursors with a higher propensity of becoming Tvm cells due to a marked increase in chromatin accessibility. Epigenetic changes at the loci of effector genes such as Eomes, Runx1, and Tbx21 resulted in enhanced expression and consequently augmented conversion of naïve cells to Tvm cells.123 The chromatin landscape of T cell precursors thus underlies the developmental timing of T cells. Open chromatin accessibility during early life favors the development of Tvm cells, which improves early host defenses against infection.123 The primary effector function of Tvm cells is rapid production of IFNγ during early stages of infection. Tvm cells respond more efficiently to an inflammatory insult compared with naïve T cells, underlining the benefit of naïve T cell conversion into Tvm cells in vulnerable hosts.118 Tvm cells can rapidly migrate to the site of infection, while naïve T cells must navigate to secondary lymphoid organs for priming and expansion before subsequently being recruited to peripheral tissues. High self-peptide affinity during Tvm cell development results in the induction of Eomes transcription factor, which enhances the expression of the IL-15 receptor, also known as CD122.124 Tvm cells possess larger clusters of CD122, providing them increased sensitivity to IL-15, and a lower threshold of cytokine-dependent activation during inflammation in concert with IL-12 and IL-18.125 During IAV infection, Tvm cell infiltration is detected in the lungs on the first day of infection and are hypothesized to contribute to early viral control. Upon activation, Tvm cells differentiate into IFNγ-producing short-lived effector cells or resident-memory T cells without having to home to a secondary lymph node for antigen presentation.126 In the absence of growth factors promoting tissue residency, Tvm cells preferentially differentiate into central memory T cells following primary immune challenge. Tvm cells have exhibited protective effects against several pathogens including Trypanosoma cruzi, L. monocytogenes, and Yersinia pseudotuberculosis.125,127,128 Thus, T cell precursors during early life are preprogrammed for conversion into Tvm cells to benefit host immunity by acting in an extralymphoid and antigen-independent manner.

T cells are inherently cross-reactive

Heterologous immunity affords a broader range of protection against pathogens and can be TCR dependent or independent. The former relies on the intrinsic capacity of T cells to be cross-reactive. The discovery of TCR gene rearrangements led to clonal selection theory, whereby a TCR is generated specifically to a single peptide in a perfect peptide-MHC complex interaction. However, unlike BCRs, once generated, the sequence of the TCR is locked and does not undergo further rearrangement after thymic exit. This means that naïve TCRs must somehow span the recognition of all potential foreign antigens despite having no previous exposure and solely relying on the genetic recombination process. Seminal papers in 1998 shifted this paradigm as scientists grasped the inefficiency of an immune system that would abide by the clonal selection theory.129 Given that a mouse possesses a theoretical repertoire of 1015 TCRs for proper immune coverage, the sheer weight of such a number of monospecific T cells would require a spleen that is 70 times larger.4 Additionally, the time that it would take for the initiation of a T cell response involving foreign peptides finding their match across such a large pool of naïve T cells would be exceptionally slow. Therefore, to ensure optimal responsiveness, T cells have evolved a balance to be intrinsically cross-reactive to foreign antigens without being autoreactive to self-antigens, in order to expand the breadth of antigens that can be recognized. TCRs recognize processed peptides 9 to 11 amino acids in length. However, crystallography imaging has revealed the degeneracy of T cell binding in the flexible conformation of TCR-peptide-MHC interactions.130 In fact, the TCR predominantly engages with the MHC molecule with only about 3 peptide-derived amino acid residues coming into contact with the peptide-binding groove. Numerous studies have gone on to expand on this work and demonstrate that a single TCR could potentially recognize up to a million different peptides.131 Such a property benefits the system not only by increasing the immune coverage of the host, but also by increasing the likelihood of polyclonal T cell responses given a single peptide may be recognized by several different TCRs. Evidence of cross-reactive T cells that lessen pathology to subsequent infection has been described in humans particularly between strains of IAV and between IAV and hepatitis C virus.132,133 Additionally, recent work has noted the incidence of T cells to SARS-CoV-2 antigens in unexposed individuals highlighting the cross-reactivity between common cold coronaviruses.134 Cross-reactive T cells have been well established in murine models between lymphocytic choriomeningitis virus (LCMV), Pichindé virus (PV), and vaccinia virus (VV).135 These studies provided an in-depth understanding of the frequency of cross-reactive T cells and how they can provide enhanced protection against a heterologous infection. Interestingly, cross-reactivity was not reciprocal between all infections as primary LCMV and PV infection conferred protection against VV, but VV did not enhance host defense against LCMV and PV.136 In fact, cross-reactivity seems to depend on more factors than just high homology between two viruses. For instance, strains of influenza B did not generate cross-reactive T cells to subsequent infection with influenza A despite similar genomes.137 Rather, it is hypothesized that larger viruses such as VV generate a larger number of peptides that boost the probability of encountering a cross-reactive T cell, while viruses with smaller genomes only encode few immunodominant epitopes. This may provide an explanation as to why some small RNA viruses such as Ebola and yellow fever virus are highly pathogenic. Memory T cells are particularly poised for cross-reactivity as they have a lower threshold for activation and can recognize peptides at a concentration 50-fold lower than peptide concentrations required to activate naïve T cells.138 Typically, a turnover of memory T cells occurs during acute infections whereby memory T cells that do not recognize epitopes from the ongoing infection undergo apoptosis.139 Therefore, it is hypothesized that cross-reactivity significantly shapes one’s memory T cell repertoire, as cross-reactive memory T cells are activated during heterologous infection and subsequently preserved.140 Davis et al.141 validated these ideas in which assessment of virus-specific CD4+ T cells in unexposed humans demonstrated cross-reactive T cells with up to 93% displaying a memory phenotype. This points toward the beneficial enrichment of cross-reactive T cells as an evolutionary mechanism of positive selection to provide broadened host defense against infectious diseases.

Memory T cells can be bystander activated

Memory T cells are also equipped with a heightened ability to underdo TCR-independent activation to further contribute to heterologous immunity by a process termed “bystander activation.” This has been evidenced by consistent activation of memory T cells even in the absence of a TCR.142 Upon the release of pathogen-induced cytokines IL-12 and IL-18, memory T cells can be activated to induce IFNγ to enhance early responses against acute infections without the need for cognate antigen recognition.143,144 In contrast to cross-reactivity, which triggers expansive cell division of activated memory T cells, bystander activation does not induce an increase of T cell numbers.145 Studies using poly(I:C) as a primarily stimulus indicate that the induction of type I IFN results in CD8+ memory T cells apoptosis, which offsets the cell division occurring from bystander activation. To counteract this loss, memory T cells seem to be highly sensitive to the secretion of IL-15 as a homeostatic proliferative signal to restore the CD8+ memory T cell pool.139 The synergistic impact of type I IFN, IL-18, IL-12, and IL-15 by bystander innate cells such as monocytes and dendritic cells can coordinate and optimize bystander activation of memory T cells. Beneficial bystander activation responses have particularly been noted in mice with CD8+ memory T cells in which early release of IFNγ confers protective effects against L. monocytogenes infection.143 In humans, primary HIV infection triggered the bystander activation of CD8+ memory T cells specific for CMV, Epstein-Barr virus, and IAV.146 Memory CD4+ T cells can also reportedly be bystander activated in LCMV infection and stimulation with lipopolysaccharide.147,148 Overall, cytokine-induced activation of memory T cells can contribute to an early response against infection, which adds upon the protective nature of cross-reactive T cells.

Memory T cells promote IFNγ-mediated trained immunity

The longevity of trained immunity is primarily mediated by epigenetic modifications and metabolic changes that can be detected in progenitors of mature immune cells in the hematopoietic compartment of the bone marrow.149,150 Potent inducers of trained immunity include the BCG vaccine and the fungal cell wall component β-glucan. When administered systemically in mice, both training agents can reprogram hematopoietic stem cells generating trained downstream myeloid cells that provide enhanced protection against several pathogens such as Mtb and C. albicans, and even some forms of cancer.150,151,152,153

Recently, we and others have described the ability of BCG to confer cross-protection against respiratory viruses IAV and SARS-CoV-2.154–156 As opposed to the mechanism of protection we found against Mtb, the protection against IAV and SARS-CoV-2 was reliant on the generation of CD4+ memory T cells that release IFNγ and train proximal pulmonary cells. During a vaccination period of 3 to 4 wk, sustained IFNγ production by CD4+ memory T cells trained resident alveolar macrophages (AMs), which strengthened antiviral immunity, given that AMs are the first cell to be encountered upon infection.154 In fact, trained AMs from BCG-vaccinated mice were capable of reducing viral replication in an ex vivo IAV infection, pointing toward IFNγ-induced AM reprogramming. This phenomenon was also described with the induction of IFNγ-producing CD8+ T cells following the administration adenoviral vaccine vector. IFNγ training was reported to induce “memory AMs” marked with enhanced expression of MHC complex II. These memory AMs showed better protection against Streptococcus pneumoniae infection and were able to self-sustain without the contribution of bone marrow-derived monocytes.157 Moreover, IFNγ-trained AMs also display antitumor properties in a murine model of melanoma, underscoring their functional plasticity.158 Prolonged IFNγ secretion by BCG-induced T cells also appeared to activate other myeloid cells such as monocytes and DCs.159 Interestingly, IFNγ exposure trained pulmonary epithelial cells as evidenced by the upregulation of interferon-stimulated genes, which also contribute to viral control. Both alveolar type I and type II pneumocytes exhibit altered transcriptomic signatures, and there is evidence that pulmonary mesenchymal stem cells, stromal cells, and endothelial cells can also engage in IFNγ-mediated crosstalk.155,156 This observed impact by IFNγ prior to infection may enlarge the scope of peripheral trained immunity to an organ.159,160 Differential imprinting on various organ and immune compartments could represent a coordinated effort to boost overall host strategies against infection in the event that one area becomes more vulnerable. In these studies, systemic BCG vaccination acts as a potent inducer of CD4+ memory T cells, which can be bystander activated for rapid IFNγ production. However, the peripheral innate immune training that occurs can largely be attributed to IFNγ alone because the protection against IAV and SARS-CoV-2 was recapitulated with administration of recombinant IFNγ.154,156 The protective effects of IFNγ production by BCG vaccine is not limited to respiratory viruses, as an earlier report demonstrated protection against VV dependent on CD4 T cell–mediated heterologous immunity.161 Notably, recent studies in nonhuman primates immunized with BCG intravenously showed sterilizing immunity against Mtb, which was primarily associated to IFNγ-producing airway CD4+ T cells.162 These findings reveal an unprecedented role for T cells in peripheral trained immunity, such that harnessing their capacity of IFNγ production can significantly boost the function of innate immune cells to infections.

Conclusions

Groundbreaking work has revealed the power of trained immunity whereby innate immune cells can adopt adaptive-like features and retain a form of immunological memory. Here, we present how the T cell compartment contribute to innate memory responses. Thymic output of innate-like T cells that are preprogrammed with effector functions provides rapid immune defenses at barrier sites. While conventional T cells require T cell priming events to mature, the generation of memory T cells over time can contribute to innate features via cross-reactivity and bystander activation (Fig. 2). These host strategies underline additional mechanisms at the intersection of innate and adaptive immunity that are incompletely understood. The development of unconventional T cells is critical for early life protection before the onset of adaptive memory responses modulated by progressive microbial exposure. The functional plasticity of unconventional T cells may promote the development of peripheral trained immunity, given their inherent ability to adapt within tissues. Even after the formation of antigen-specific responses, conventional memory T cells exhibit properties that support the development of trained immunity. From an evolutionary perspective, it benefits the host for various immune compartments to converge on innate memory functions. This convergence offers rapid protection against invading pathogens while preserving acquired immunity promoting pathogen elimination. Although in this review, we focus on the innate-like responses of T lymphocytes, B cells can also exhibit innate features. Innate B cells (IBCs) produce natural polyreactive IgMs independently of APCs or T cell help. Circulation of these antibodies can arm the host with humoral protection upon challenge without the initiation of B cell maturation in lymphoid organs. IBCs include B1 cells and marginal zone B cells. The selection process of IBC BCRs occurs against blood group–derived antigens and commensal bacteria and has hence been shown to be an evolutionary conserved mechanism due to IBCs’ ability to remove autoantigens from the circulation.163,164 IBC receptors recognize nonprotein antigens such as phospholipids and carbohydrates, which are present on many microorganisms.165 By these broad antigen processes, IBCs have been shown to respond rapidly to several pathogens and also through non–antigen-specific processes such as engagement of TLRs and cytokine receptors.166,167

Thymic generation of T cell subsets and their role in innate functions. Conventional T cells undergo distinct developmental stages in the thymus (double negative [DN], DP, SP) before exiting into the circulation as naïve T cells. Upon encounter with APCs, T cell priming will trigger clonal expansion of antigen-specific effector T cells before contracting into a long-lived population of memory T cells (effector memory T [TEM] cells, central memory T [TCM] cells). Naïve T cells that have high expression of CD5 can become Tim cells under the stimulation of IL-4 in the thymus, or Tvm cells under the stimulation of IL-15 in the circulation. Tim and Tvm cells bypass the need for maturation within lymphoid organs and can immediately act as memory T cells in the periphery. T cells can branch off at the DN stage and commit to γδ lineage within the thymus. Transcriptional programs (SKINT1, T-bet, RORγt) will promote differentiation into distinct γδ subsets (dendritic epidermal T cell, γδ1, γδ17). At the DP stage, T cells can differentiate into NKT or MAIT cells under the transcriptional regulation of PLZF. NKT cells will undergo positive selection with lipid antigens presented by CD1d molecules, while MAIT cells will be positively selected with vitamin B–derived antigens presented by MR1 molecules. Further transcriptional programming within the thymus (T-bet, RORγt, GATA-3) will generate defined NKT and MAIT subtypes (NKT1, NKT2, NKT17, MAIT1, MAIT17). Unconventional T cells (γδ T cells, NKT cells, MAIT cells) will egress the thymus as effector cells and preferentially home to mucosal sites in the periphery. In their respective niches, both conventional and unconventional can fulfill immune functions to contribute to innate memory (training of innate immune cells via reprogramming mechanisms), and adaptive memory (cellular and humoral antigen-specific memory). Many questions remain about how the immune system orchestrates this complex network of cells, particularly in terms of how we can leverage these intricacies for vaccine design and immunotherapy. Most recently, MAIT cells and Vδ2+ T cells were found to be key effectors in the early response to both messenger RNA and adenoviral SARS-CoV-2 vaccines by rapid cytokine responsiveness which orchestrated enhanced CD8+ T cells responses via IFNγ production.168 Research into stimulating these lesser known branches of immunity could be widely beneficial as other studies have yielded promising results. For example, the use of αGalCer as an immune adjuvant can activate NKT cells, enhancing vaccine responses and antitumor immunity.169,170 Chimeric antigen receptor γδ therapy shows potential in cancer immunotherapy by enabling Vδ2+ T cells to recognize tumor cells with dysregulated mevalonate metabolism.171 The administration of recombinant IL-7, which supports expansion and maintenance of unconventional T cells, has shown broad-spectrum antiviral effects against respiratory infections in mice.172 Future vaccines could also explore the stimulation of existing T cells such as bystander memory T cells or cross-reactive T cells, by integrating antigens that could stimulate preexisting immunity.173 Additionally, we postulate that the composition of the neonatal lymphocytic system can provide unique insight in the various innate-like strategies that arise prior to microbial exposure. In the quest for more broadly protective vaccines, often referred to as a universal vaccine, the focus should consider both classical antigen-specific memory and innate memory responses. As such, innate-like lymphocytes should be further explored particularly in the development of neonatal and mucosal vaccines. Neonatal vaccine design faces challenges due to Th2-biased immunity and weak response of conventional B cells. Targeting innate-like T cells that are specifically enriched at mucosal sites in early life could circumvent low immunogenicity challenges. While this review discusses the benefits of innate-like protection afforded by the T cell compartment and trained immunity, we did not cover the potential detrimental effects. Although nonspecific activation and endogenous stimuli can provide broad immune coverage, such triggers can also contribute to unwanted inflammation.174 Enhanced innate immune response afforded by trained immunity or unconventional T cells can worsen a number of diseases through elevated inflammatory activity and should be taken in consideration in the search of therapeutic strategies. Additionally, while we primarily focused on thymically derived innate-like T cells, we did not discuss in depth the features of ILCs. Given their well-defined description as innate lymphocytes, ILCs play key roles in innate immunity with many redundant functions seen with unconventional T cells and cytokine-driven T cell responses. Further work investigating ILC memory responses as well as the interplay between ILCs and the entirety of the T cell compartment would be beneficial in mapping out the strength of innate-like lymphocytes.

In conclusion, leveraging the dialogue between innate and adaptive memory responses can provide significant preventive strategies against emerging pathogens. Ongoing efforts to decipher the mechanisms of the immune system are unveiling innovative strategies to fight human disease and to leverage the full power of immune responses.

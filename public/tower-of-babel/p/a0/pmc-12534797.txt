Subxiphoid epicardial approach for catheter ablation of refractory perimitral atrial flutter: a case series
Authors: Pierre Ollitrault, Laure Champ-Rigot, Richard Descamps, Véronique Pottier, Virginie Ferchaud, Jonaz Font, Arnaud Pellissier, Paul-Ursmar Milliez
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPerimitral atrial flutter is a complex atrial tachycardia involving endocardial and epicardial structures. The standard ablation strategy includes endocardial radiofrequency and vein of Marshall ethanol infusion, but restoring sinus rhythm can be challenging even in experienced centres.Case summaryWe report the cases of three male patients (60-, 75-, and 58-year-old) with persistent and symptomatic atypical atrial flutter. They shared a previous history of numerous atrial fibrillation catheter ablations. Endocardial left atrial activation mapping found a perimitral macro-reentry, refractory to radiofrequency on the mitral isthmus line and in the coronary sinus. Vein of Marshall ethanol infusion was feasible in one patient, but failed to achieve complete mitral isthmus line block. The vein of Marshall was absent in the other two patients. A subxiphoid approach was performed under general anaesthesia, and mapping confirmed an epicardial critical isthmus located between the musculature of the coronary sinus (anteriorly) and the left inferior pulmonary vein (posteriorly). Epicardial radiofrequency applications resulted in conversion to sinus rhythm and complete conduction block along the mitral isthmus line. No complication was observed, and patients remained in sinus rhythm after at least 2 years of guideline-directed follow-up and without the need for antiarrhythmic drugs.DiscussionEpicardial bridging may account for a significant proportion of catheter ablation failure in perimitral atrial flutters. In such cases, a subxiphoid percutaneous epicardial approach appears to be a safe and effective strategy to target the epicardial gap, restore sinus rhythm, and obtain complete mitral isthmus line block.

Introduction

Perimitral atrial flutter (AFl) is a complex atrial tachycardia involving both endocardial and epicardial structures.1 The standard ablation strategy includes radiofrequency (RF) catheter ablation targeting the endocardial mitral isthmus (MI) line and the coronary sinus (CS), in addition to vein of Marshall ethanol infusion (VOM-EtOH), to restore sinus rhythm and achieve complete MI line block.

We report three cases of perimitral AFl refractory to RF catheter ablation and VOM-EtOH, in which subxiphoid epicardial approach led to successful and safe catheter ablation.

Summary figure

Patient 1

A 50-year-old patient was admitted for a persistent and symptomatic atypical AFl (Figure 1A). He previously underwent RF cavotricuspid isthmus ablation for common AFl at age 40, a first RF pulmonary vein isolation (PVI) for paroxysmal atrial fibrillation (AF) at age 43, and a redo RF PVI for recurrent paroxysmal AF at age 44. A new catheter ablation procedure was performed during general anaesthesia. Left atrial (LA) activation mapping found a 210 ms cycle-length dual-loop macro-reentry, with pulmonary veins (PVs) isolated. Entrainment confirmed a perimitral macro-reentry with an additional loop around the left PVs. A posterior wall (PW) RF line was performed, which lengthened the cycle to 230 ms. An endocardial MI line combined with RF applications in the CS and VOM-EtOH restored sinus rhythm (Figure 1B). Pulmonary vein isolation and complete block on the PW line were confirmed, but MI line block was incomplete because of an epicardial gap. An epicardial gap was defined by a discontinuous wave front of endocardial activation with a gap in activation resulting in focal endocardial breakout distally and activation back towards the gap. The activation gap was defined as a missing amount of cycle length (in milliseconds) not recorded with detailed endocardial mapping, with a requirement of >5% of total cycle length, as commonly defined.1 Subsequent RF applications failed to achieve complete block on the MI line. The procedure was interrupted; the patient was not inducible at the end of the procedure.

Two months after the procedure, the patient was admitted for recurrent palpitations and atypical AFl (Figure 1C). Due to the probability of a perimitral AFl resulting from the epicardial gap on the MI line, a subxiphoid epicardial approach was performed under general anaesthesia (institutional standardized workflow is detailed in Table 1). Endocardial LA activation mapping confirmed a perimitral macro-reentry with a 254 ms cycle length. Fifty milliseconds (19.7%) of the cycle length was unaccounted for across the MI (Figure 1D). Epicardial LA activation mapping confirmed the epicardial gap on the MI line, located between the CS musculature and the left inferior PV. Epicardial RF applications restored sinus rhythm, with subsequent complete MI block (both epicardial and endocardial; Figure 1E; Supplementary material online, Video S1). The patient was non-inducible at the end of the procedure (procedure characteristics are detailed in Table 2). No post-operative complication occurred.

Patient 2

A 75-year-old patient was admitted for a persistent, symptomatic atypical AFl (Figure 2A). He previously underwent a first RF PVI for paroxysmal AF at age 66 and a redo RF PVI for recurrent paroxysmal AF at age 67. A new catheter ablation procedure was performed during general anaesthesia. Left atrial activation mapping found a 230 ms cycle-length perimitral macro-reentry, with PVs isolated. Radiofrequency applications were delivered on the endocardial MI line and in the CS, with a subsequent prolongation of the cycle length up to 270 ms. Atrial flutter degenerated to AF, so a PW line and direct current cardioversion (DCC) were performed. Pulmonary vein isolation and complete block on the PW line were confirmed, but MI line block was incomplete because of an epicardial gap. Careful CS venography found no evidence of VOM (Figure 2B; Supplementary material online, Video S2). Subsequent RF applications failed to achieve complete block on the MI line. The procedure was interrupted; the patient was not inducible at the end of the procedure.

Two years after the procedure, the patient was readmitted for recurrent palpitations and atypical AFl. A subxiphoid epicardial approach was performed under general anaesthesia and epicardial LA activation mapping confirmed a perimitral macro-reentry with a 235 ms cycle length and an epicardial gap on the MI line (Figure 2C). During epicardial mapping, the critical isthmus was bumped and sinus rhythm was restored. Epicardial LA activation mapping was performed during CS pacing close to the MI line and confirmed the MI line gap (Figure 2D). Epicardial RF applications were delivered on the epicardial gap, between the left inferior PV and CS musculature, to achieve complete MI line block (Figure 2E). The patient was non-inducible at the end of the procedure (Table 2), and no post-operative complication occurred.

Over the 3-year follow-up period, there was no recurrence of AFl or AF without the use of antiarrhythmic drugs.

Patient 3

A 58-year-old patient was admitted for a persistent, symptomatic atypical AFl (Figure 3A). He previously underwent a first RF PVI for persistent AF-induced cardiomyopathy at age 54 and a redo RF PVI (minimal right superior PV reconnection) plus SVC isolation, PW isolation, and MI line ablation for recurrent persistent AF at age 57. A new catheter ablation procedure was performed during general anaesthesia. Left atrial activation mapping found a 230 ms cycle-length perimitral macro-reentry, with PVs and PW isolated. Radiofrequency applications on the endocardial MI line and in the distal CS led to a prolongation of the cycle length. CS venography found no evidence of VOM. Atrial flutter was converted into sinus rhythm by DCC. At the end of the procedure, the PV and PW were isolated, but the block on the MI line was incomplete (Figure 3B and C).

Three months after the procedure, the patient was readmitted for recurrent palpitations and atypical AFl. A subxiphoid epicardial approach was performed under general anaesthesia and LA activation mapping found a perimitral macro-reentry with a 240 ms cycle length. Epicardial LA voltage mapping confirmed the epicardial gap between the left inferior PV and the CS musculature (Figure 3D and E). Epicardial RF applications were delivered on the epicardial gap, with conversion to sinus rhythm and complete MI line block (Figure 3F). The patient was non-inducible at the end of the procedure (Table 2), and no post-operative complication occurred.

Over the 2-year follow-up period, there was no recurrence of AFl or AF without the use of antiarrhythmic drugs.

Discussion

Catheter ablation is the cornerstone of AF management but recurrence as a persistent LA tachycardia is not uncommon. Perimitral AFl is a common but complex macro-reentrant atrial tachycardia frequently involving both endocardial and epicardial atrial structures, in patients with previous LA catheter ablation or surgery.1 The standard ablation strategy includes a RF MI line extending from the posterolateral mitral annulus to the left inferior PV, possibly in conjunction with coronary sinus ablation and VOM-EtOH, to achieve complete conduction block on the MI.2 Despite this, the success rate of perimitral AFl ablation is limited to approximately 80% in expert centres.3 Increased MI thickness has been associated with lower procedural success,4,5 and an epicardial gap and/or absence of the VOM—as observed in Patients 2 and 3—may represent the most common cause of perimitral AFl ablation failure.6 Importantly, a repeat VOM-EtOH might not be feasible in most patients.7

We present a case series illustrating a strategy targeting the epicardial critical isthmus in patients with refractory perimitral AFl, as previously described.12 This innovative approach requires pre-procedural planning (Table 1) and operator/centre expertise in order to perform efficient and safe subxiphoid puncture. Alternative strategies may be considered in such patients. One option includes a modified anterior line connecting the anterior/anterolateral mitral annulus with the left superior PV.8 However, this approach is often limited by epicardial bridging through the fibres of Bachmann’s bundle, and it might expose to a risk of inadvertent LA appendage electrical isolation in patients with prior RF ablation along the ‘conventional’ MI line, such as in our patients.8,13 The use of RF ablation with half-normal saline irrigation has been shown to enhance lesion depth in ventricular tachycardia ablation.9 However, this approach has not been evaluated in the atria, and the risk of cardiac perforation in this context is unknown. Complete CS disconnection might also be considered to improve procedural success of perimitral AFl catheter ablation,6 but it may significantly alter LA mechanical activation.4 Finally, endocardial pulsed-field ablation (PFA) on the MI line might offer a high acute success rate, but at the price of a risk of acute coronary artery spasm and/or late recurrence.10,11 Transmurality and durability of PFA-induced atrial lesions outside of the PVs are currently unknown.

Patient’s perspective

Although perimitral AFl is rarely refractory to both RF catheter ablation and VOM-EtOH, it remains a highly symptomatic atrial tachycardia that can significantly impair quality of life and lead to heart failure. In experienced centres, a subxiphoid epicardial approach targeting epicardial critical isthmus might avoid a surgical thoracoscopic approach and/or a pace-and-ablate strategy, in otherwise healthy patients.

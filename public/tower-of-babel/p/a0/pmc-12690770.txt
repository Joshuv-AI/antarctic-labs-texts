SBPS-08 Immunohistochemical Profile of Pituitary Adenomas: An Eleven-Year Retrospective Study in Abidjan, Ivory Coast
Authors: Tcheutchoua Kathy F, Brahima Doukoure, Drogba Landry
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDiagnosis and management of brain tumours is solely based on their histologic profile, as highlighted by the latest WHO classification of brain tumours. The progression of molecular biology has contributed to a deeper knowledge of invasive pituitary adenomas, positively impacting clinical outcomes. In resource-scarce nations such as Ivory Coast, the classification of pituitary adenomas relies on tumour morphology and immunohistochemical hormonal profiling to ensure accurate diagnosis and optimal management. Nevertheless, a limited number of studies have been undertaken to examine this subject.Aimthe goal was to determine the immunohistochemical profile of pituitary adenomas diagnosed histologically at the Mami ADJOUA Medical Centre Laboratory in Abidjan.MethodsThis was an eleven-year retrospective study conducted using histopathology records from the Mami ADJOUA Medical Centre Laboratory. Demographic and histopathological data were collected, compiled using Google Forms and Sheets, and analysed with SPSS version 23.ResultsA total of 39 cases were identified, with one case excluded due to insufficient data. Among the remaining 38 cases, 60.5% (23) were males and 39.5% (15) were females. Functioning adenomas accounted for 94.7% (36) of cases, while non-functioning adenomas represented 5.3% (2). Among the functioning adenomas, the most common subtype was plurihormonal (52.6%), followed by gonadotroph (36.8%), corticotroph (2.6%), and somatotroph (2.6%) adenomas.ConclusionThis study highlights a higher prevalence of functioning pituitary adenomas in males, with plurihormonal adenomas being the most common subtype. These findings emphasize the crucial role of immunohistochemical profiling in the accurate classification and management of pituitary adenomas, particularly in resource-limited settings. To improve diagnostic precision and enhance patient care across the country, it is recommended to expand access to immunohistochemical studies and encourage their routine use in pathology laboratories nationwide. Further multicentric research is also needed to build a more comprehensive epidemiological profile of pituitary adenomas in Ivory Coast.

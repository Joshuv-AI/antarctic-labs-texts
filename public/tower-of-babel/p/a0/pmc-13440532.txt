Peripheral Measurable Residual Disease Activity Assessment by MALDI-TOF Mass Spectrometry in Patients With Newly Diagnosed Multiple Myeloma in the Phase III GMMG-HD7 Trial
Authors: Jia xiang Jin, Varun R Ginde, Stefanie Huhn, Philipp Reichert, Uta Bertsch, Roland Fenk, Britta Besemer, Amelie Boquoi, Roland Schroers, Ivana von Metzler, Mathias Hänel, Christoph Mann, Lisa B Leypoldt, Bernhard Heilmeier, Christof Scheid, Thorsten Peters-Regehr, Igor W Blau, Carsten Müller-Tidow, Steffen Luntz, Tobias AW Holderried, Karolin Trautmann-Grill, Deniz Gezer, Maika Klaiber-Hakimi, Martin Müller, Evgenii Shumilov, Wolfgang Knauf, Christian S Michel, Thomas Geer, Hendrik Riesenberg, Christoph Lutz, Marc S Raab, Niels Weinhold, Martin Hoffmann, Axel Benner, Peter Findeisen, Katja C Weisel, Hans J Salwender, Elias K Mai, Hartmut Goldschmidt, for the German-Speaking Myeloma Multicenter Group (GMMG) HD7 Investigators

ABSTRACT
AbstractMass spectrometry (MS) offers a minimally invasive approach for detecting monoclonal proteins in multiple myeloma (MM), but large prospective evaluations remain limited. We analyzed 3,301 serum samples from 617 patients enrolled in the phase III GMMG-HD7 trial to characterize the analytical performance and clinical relevance of MS-based measurable residual activity. MS reliably distinguished therapeutic antibodies, tracked diagnostic M proteins longitudinally, and demonstrated strong prognostic value across defined time points. MS negativity was associated with superior progression-free survival (PFS), with the greatest separation at later time points (12 months of maintenance: hazard ratio, 0.25 [95% CI, 0.15 to 0.43]; adjusted P < .001). Combined assessment with bone-marrow measurable residual disease (MRD) further refined risk stratification: MS/MRD double-positive patients exhibited the worst PFS, whereas all other groups showed comparably favorable outcomes. MS/MRD concordance increased over time and was influenced by immunoglobulin isotype, with expected immunoglobulin G-recycling–associated early discordance. MS provided accurate M-protein detection and outperformed serum protein electrophoresis at low concentrations, reliably quantifying low-level residual disease. These findings establish serum MS as a sensitive, reproducible, and practical biomarker that complements MRD and supports minimally invasive disease monitoring in MM, with potential integration into future response assessment and risk-adapted treatment strategies.

INTRODUCTION

Mass spectrometry (MS) offers many minimally invasive diagnostic tools for multiple myeloma (MM) to detect and track M protein in peripheral blood, categorized generally into top-down, light-chain–based, and bottom-up, clonotypic approaches.1 Prior reports suggest that top-down matrix-assisted laser-desorption/ionization time-of-flight (MALDI-TOF) MS show potential to sensitively identify residual M-protein persistence and recurrence, thus complementing conventional bone-marrow measurable residual disease (MRD), one of the strongest surrogate biomarkers of progression-free-survival (PFS) and overall survival in MM.2-7 Despite therapeutic advances, relapse remains common,8 with current MRD methods requiring invasive, resource-intensive bone-marrow sampling that may only incompletely reflect systemic disease.

Here, we present a large patient cohort derived from the GMMG-HD7 trial,9,10 evaluating MALDI-TOF MS for the assessment of measurable residual activity (MRA) in MM, leveraging longitudinal baseline and follow-up samples.

METHODS

Our aim was to correlate MS-based MRA (EXENT11,12) with clinical outcomes, and establish the utility of this emerging technology as a practical tool aiding disease monitoring in clinical practice. The analysis comprised 3,301 serum samples from 617 patients (Table 1) at defined time points: baseline, after induction (IND), after autologous stem-cell transplant (ASCT), 12 months from start of maintenance (12M MT), 24 months from start of maintenance (24M MT), end of study (EOS), and progressive disease (PD).9,10 Bone-marrow MRD was assessed using next-generation flow cytometry13 at a sensitivity cutoff of 10−5.

RESULTS

Prognostic Impact of MRA Testing by MS

In line with prior reports,2-5 the EXENT System distinguished accurately between M protein and therapeutic antibodies across longitudinal samples, reliably reidentifying baseline M proteins at relapse, including cases with mass-shifted light chains (Data Supplement, Fig S1). As shown in Figs 1 and 2A, MS status demonstrated a statistically significant prognostic value for PFS (eg, 12M MT: hazard ratio [HR], 0.25 [95% CI, 0.15 to 0.43]; adjusted P < .001). Prognostic Impact of Combined MRA and MRD Assessment

MS/MRD double-negative patients exhibited significantly superior PFS versus double-positive patients (eg, 12M MT: HR, 0.08 [95% CI, 0.04 to 0.17]; P < .001) when combined with paired bone-marrow MRD assessments. By contrast, MS–/MRD+ and MS+/MRD– patients showed PFS comparable with double-negative patients (eg, 12M MT: MS+/MRD–: HR, 0.11 [95% CI, 0.04 to 0.33]; adjusted P < .001; MS–/MRD+: HR, 0.11 [95% CI, 0.01 to 0.87]; adjusted P = .108). This trend was mirrored in exploratory subgroup analysis for free-light-chain MM (Data Supplement, Fig S2) and for continued (IND to ASCT) MS/MRD analysis (Data Supplement, Fig S3). Assessment of later time points will be prioritized as additional follow-up data accrue.

In all, we observed an MS/MRD concordance rate of 62.7% after IND, rising to around 85% after 12M MT with an overall concordance rate of 74.4%, comparable with previous studies3 (Fig 2B). This was in part driven by early immunoglobulin G (IgG)-recycling–associated14-17 elevated MS positivity despite deep therapeutic response (Data Supplement, Fig S4). Overall MS-/MRD+ rates were low (3.8%). For PD samples, MS/MRD concordance rate was at 92.9% with no MS–/MRD+ samples observed. The small group (n = 3) of concordant negative PD samples comprised patients who experienced hyposecretory/asecretory radiologic PD below the serum electrophoresis (SPEP) quantifiability threshold. After IND, ASCT, and 12M MT, 22.7%, 48.9%, and 78.1% of all patients, respectively, were negative by MS (Fig 2C). An MS negativity rate of around 78% was observed for time points after 12M MT.

MS Enables Enhanced Assessment of M Protein

MS closely matched SPEP regarding M-protein concentrations in samples positive by both while showing superior accuracy at low concentrations. MS yielded higher values for MRD+ versus MRD– samples and identified more samples within 3 months of PD when lower thresholds (from ≥5 to ≥3 g/L increase from nadir) were applied (Data Supplement, Fig S5). Across time points, MS showed the highest positivity rates and, together with MRD, SPEP, and immunofixation (IFE), stratified risk, with all-negative patients exhibiting superior PFS (Data Supplement, Fig S6). The largest groups comprised samples concordant across all tests.

Approximately 13% of patients had a mass-shifted light chain (MSL) as the baseline major clone. Multivariate Cox models did not detect a significant overall risk associated with MSL status; however, subgroup analyses suggest a potentially adverse effect for MS-positive IgG MM, most apparent after induction and during maintenance (Data Supplement, Fig S7). As demonstrated alongside Data Supplement (Fig S4), MS-based stratification yielded stronger curve separation and discriminative efficiency in IgA MM compared with IgG MM during maintenance.

DISCUSSION

In this study, we demonstrated that serum MALDI-TOF MS reliably detects and tracks M proteins with high analytical sensitivity and strong prognostic relevance. MRA by MS closely mirrors and complements bone-marrow MRD assessment, highlighting its role as a minimally invasive biomarker for disease monitoring in MM.

MRA negativity was consistently associated with superior PFS across time points, and MRD/MRA double-positive patients consistently exhibited inferior outcomes, confirming that residual disease activity remains a key driver of relapse even in the era of highly effective therapy regimens. The prognostic separation deepened over time, as shown in previous reports,2-4 underscoring the clinical significance of continued systemic clearance of monoclonal protein as a surrogate for deep therapeutic response. Notably, even qualitative-only exploratory assessment of free-light-chain-type MM based on total light chain (κ/λ) immunopurification was already predictive of PFS. Even at this early time point, continuous MS/MRD negativity between IND and last ASCT suggested superior PFS. Comprehensive interpretation of longitudinal MS trajectories is constrained by the currently immature survival data; however, forthcoming results from the second randomization of the GMMG-HD7 trial will be critical to allow a more robust analysis.

Although MRA and MRD became increasingly concordant at later time points, partial discordance was common early in treatment, especially among IgG-type MM. This likely reflects the biological persistence of IgG due to FcRn-mediated recycling and thus extended serum half-life14-17 rather than actual residual clonal activity in many cases, an important limitation previously discussed.2,3 By contrast, patients with IgA type or free-light-chain-type MM showed closer temporal MRA/MRD alignment, supporting the interpretation that shorter-lived immunoglobulins better reflect dynamic disease clearance. These immunobiologic differences will be important to consider when integrating MS into response evaluation frameworks.

MRA demonstrated superior sensitivity over IFE and strong quantitative correlation with SPEP with heightened accuracy and sensitivity particularly in the low-concentration range where SPEP loses adequate resolution.18 Importantly, MS identified low-level residual M protein in a subset of patients deemed negative by conventional assays. In parallel, MRD positivity correlated with higher M-protein concentrations by MS. Among most frequent MS/IFE/SPEP/MRD combinations, the all-negative group showed the best PFS versus the all-positive group. Next were patients with MS-only positivity, suggesting very low, otherwise undetectable MRA, then those with MRD-only negativity, possibly reflecting asymmetric systemic MRA inaccessible to local sampling.

This study thus provides comprehensive evidence that serum MS is a clinically meaningful tool for assessing residual disease in MM. Limitations include the absence of standardized quantitative cutoffs for defining biochemical relapse based on MS results and the need for extended follow-up to establish the long-term prognostic value of persistent low-level positivity. Among patients in complete response, MS status currently shows limited prognostic discrimination, pending longer follow-up. Nonetheless, our current data already demonstrate that MRA can potentially serve as a complement to bone-marrow MRD analysis, especially in secretory disease with asymmetric marrow involvement, offering a practical, noninvasive means of disease surveillance with potential to guide therapy intensification or de-escalation strategies. The progressive convergence of MS and MRD results suggests that, at later time points, serum MS may function as an initial screening tool before considering bone-marrow biopsy. Whether this approach is sufficient to safely omit invasive sampling in selected patients will require more mature survival data.

In summary, MRA assessment by MS provides a sensitive and reproducible biomarker, supplementing MRD assessments, especially when considering therapy de-escalation. As treatment strategies move toward sustained MRD-driven precision management, incorporation of serum MRA into future response criteria and clinical trial end points may help define deeper remission states and refine risk-adapted care in MM.

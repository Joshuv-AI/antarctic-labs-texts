Meconium-stained amniotic fluid and its associated factors among mothers who gave birth in Ethiopia: systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Bacha Merga Chuko, Girma Teferi Mengistu, Fikru Assefa Kibrat, Abera Cheru, Shambal Negese, Nebiyu Taye
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundMeconium-stained amniotic fluid (MSAF) happens during childbearing when the amniotic fluid carries traces of meconium, the initial stool passed by a newborn. Often signaling fetal distress, meconium-stained amniotic fluid is linked to heightened risks for both the mother and the newborn. In Ethiopia, there is inadequate attention given to this condition. Despite varied study findings showing a significant range in the occurrence of meconium-stained amniotic fluid, there is a lack of a comprehensive national overview. Therefore, this systematic review and meta-analysis aimed to assess the aggregated prevalence of meconium-stained amniotic fluid among mothers who gave birth and its associated factors in Ethiopia, providing an amalgamated understanding for healthcare strategies and policies.MethodsStudies were rigorously searched using worldwide databases from PubMed, Google Scholar, Cochrane Library, And Embase from June 30 to July 15, 2024. By using the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) quality Appraisal checklist for a cross-sectional study design, the quality of the articles retrieved was evaluated. The random effect model was used for the meta-analysis, and STATA version 14 software for Windows was used for the statistical analysis. The Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guideline was used for reporting results.ResultsA total of six observational cross-sectional studies were included in this systematic review. The pooled prevalence of meconium-stained Amniotic fluid among mothers who gave birth in Ethiopia was 19.58% (95% CI = 18.23–20.93). According to the pooled odds ratio, premature rupture of membranes (AOR = 2.15; 95%CI: 1.24.–3.07), obstructed labor (AOR = 3.36; 95%CI: 1.07.–5.65) and preeclampsia (AOR = 4.26; 95%CI: 1.51–7.01) had significant association with meconium-stained amniotic fluid.ConclusionEvidence suggested that premature rupture of the membrane, obstructed labor, and preeclampsia are positively related to meconium-stained amniotic fluid. This highlights the need for targeted interventions to reduce meconium-stained amniotic fluid incidence and lessen related adverse outcomes in Ethiopia.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12884-025-08207-6.

Introduction

Meconium is the green viscous fluid that consists of fetal gastrointestinal secretions, cellular debris, mucus, blood, lanugo, and vernix [1]. Meconium consists of undigested, swallowed debris and numerous products of secretion, excretion, and desquamation via the gastrointestinal tract. Its passageway into the amniotic fluid considerably raises the concentration of particulate debris inside the fluid and successfully raises its toxicity [2].

Meconium-stained amniotic fluid (MSAF) happens in 12 to 20% of laboring mothers and it is an unclear issue because it can be due to either a physiologic or a hypoxic insult to the fetus [3]. Birth depression occurs in infants born through MSAF and is likely caused by chronic asphyxia and infection that may lead to passage of meconium and fetal gasping [3–5]. This suggests that meconium-stained amniotic fluid would be observed as a symptom rather than a syndrome becoming more prevalent with increasing term and which might be linked with complex levels of infection or asphyxia [4, 6].

The findings of various investigations conducted in Ethiopia indicate that the percentage of meconium-stained Amniotic fluid varied between 12.1% − 30.3% [7–9]. Meconium-stained amniotic fluid (MSAF) is an alarming sign of fetal compromise and is associated with a poor perinatal outcome [6]. Perinatal mortality increased in mothers with MSAF, even in those with a low risk of obstetric complications [10, 11].

Premature rapture of membrane, preeclampsia, prolonged premature rapture of membrane duration of labor, prolonged labor, non-reassuring fetal heart rate patterns, post term pregnancy, obstructed labor, foetal tachycardia and Bradycardia were factors associated with meconium-stained amniotic fluid [7–9, 12].

As the results many study carried out in Ethiopia exposed that fetal and neonatal complications associated with MSAF were increased risks of birth asphyxia and fetal distress, low birth weight, low Apgar scores, increased need for neonatal resuscitation, increased incidence of neonatal intensive care unit (NICU) admission, meconium aspiration syndrome, and early neonatal death [6, 13]. In deliveries complicated with MSAF, increased risk of cesarean section, instrumental delivery, chorioamnionitis, and puerperal sepsis which results in higher morbidity and mortality in the mothers [14].

Despite conducting systematic reviews and meta-analyses in Ethiopia. This study was conducted by including more studies and variables which is not included in prior systematic reviews and meta-analyses of meconium-stained amniotic fluid among mothers who gave birth with several primary studies conducted. To sum up, knowing and reducing factors associated with MSAF will be priceless in saving neonatal life. Thus, it is of the utmost importance to estimate the national level of prevalence of MSAF and factors associated with MSAF to help make evidence-based solutions to reduce MSAF. This systematic review and meta-analysis will aim to determine the prevalence of MSAF and its factors associated with MSAF to take appropriate interventions in Ethiopia.

Methods

Study design and setting

A systematic review and meta-analysis were conducted to determine the pooled prevalence of MSAF and contributing factors among mothers who gave birth in Ethiopia.

Reporting

The Findings of this review were reported based on the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis statement (PRISMA) guideline [15]. Nevertheless, it was not listed on the prospective registration of systematic review and meta-analysis (PROSPERO) which is addressed in Limitations of the Study.

Search strategies

A comprehensive search of the study was done using distinct databases such as PubMed, Embase, Science Direct, Cochrane Library, Google Scholar, and Web of Science. At the start, studies were widely searched using the full title (“prevalence and factors associated with MSAF among mothers who gave birth in Ethiopia”) and keywords (“prevalence of meconium-stained amniotic fluid,” “mothers who gave birth,” “determinant factors,” “related factors,” and “in Ethiopia”). Boolean operators “OR” or “AND” were used in combination or individually to link these keywords And to set up the search terms in English publications. Besides, reference lists of wholes comprised studies were considered to find other lost studies. A literature search was conducted from June 30 to July 15, 2024(Fig. 1).

Population

The population, exposure, comparison, and result statement were similarly used in this review; population: (mothers who gave birth) in Ethiopia; exposure: determinants of meconium-stained amniotic fluid as obstetrics features; comparison: described reference group in each respective study; and outcome: meconium-stained amniotic fluid among mothers who gave birth.

Eligibility criteria

Inclusion criteria

To establish the inclusion and exclusion criteria for this systematic review and meta-analysis, the authors used the PICO technique, which primarily includes condition, context, and population (CoCoPop) questions for prevalence studies.

Study area

Studies done in Ethiopia.

Study design

Observational Cross–sectional and case control studies that report either the proportion or prevalence and associated determinants factors or predictors of meconium-stained amniotic fluid. Studies with the meconium-stained amniotic fluid published and unpublished in the English language for the sake of clarity and ease of interpretation were included. Because, there was no study done other than English-language in the country related to this topic.

Publication condition

published and unpublished studies.

Time

No limitation of time was made for the time of publication and all studies done in Ethiopia were included.

Exclusion criteria

Records with unrelated outcome measures; and articles with missing or insufficient outcomes; reviews, commentaries, editorial, case series/reports, qualitative study, articles without an abstract and/or full text, duplicate studies and patient stories were excluded from the systematic review.

Measurement of the outcome of interest

The major outcome of this systematic review and meta-analysis is the prevalence of meconium-stained amniotic fluid among mothers who gave birth in Ethiopia. The secondary outcome variable was determinant factors of meconium-stained amniotic fluid which were estimated using a pooled adjusted odds ratio (OR) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs).

Statistical methods and analysis

The extracted data from the Microsoft Excel spreadsheet were imported into STATA™ 14 statistical software for analysis. To calculate the pooled estimation of the overall prevalence of meconium-stained amniotic fluid meta-analysis software was used. The percentage of total variation among studies that is attributable to heterogeneity rather than chance is expressed by the I2 statistic, which we calculated to evaluate the degree of study heterogeneity. A score of 30% to 60% on the I2 statistic may show moderate heterogeneity, A value of 50% to 90% may indicate substantial heterogeneity, And a value of 75% to 100% may indicate significant heterogeneity [19]. Therefore, A random effects model was used to estimate the pooled meconium-stained amniotic fluid. The OR and 95% CIs were constructed from the included studies.

Subgroup analyses and heterogeneity

To identify possible causes of heterogeneity among involved studies, subgroup analyses based on geographic regions and study quality were done.

Results

A total of 1102 published And unpublished studies were included after a search of studies through various worldwide databases. The obtained studies were screened using the Mendeley reference manager, and duplicate studies were eliminated. Thus, 413 Articles were enrolled for the abstract and title screen, 689 Articles were removed from consideration due to duplications and irrelevancies, and 6 complete papers were retrieved. Finally, 6 studies that satisfied the inclusion criteria were included in this systematic review and meta-analysis.

Characteristics of selected studies

As described below (Table 1), All of these papers were published between 2018 And 2024. In the current systematic review And meta-analysis, 3247 mothers who gave birth participated in estimating the pooled prevalence of meconium-stained Amniotic fluid among mothers who gave birth in Ethiopia. All study designs included in this study were cross-sectional studies. The prevalence of meconium-stained amniotic fluid among mothers who gave birth ranged from 12.05% to 30.16%. Six geographical regions including the nation’s capital city of Addis Ababa were included in this meta-analysis; two studies included in this study were from the Amhara region [7, 8, 18], two studies were from Oromia [3, 17], and one study from Addis Ababa city [9].

Prevalence of meconium-stained amniotic fluid

The overall pooled prevalence meconium-stained amniotic fluid among mothers who gave birth in Ethiopia [3, 7–9, 17, 18] was found to be 19.58% (95% CI = 18.23, 20.93) (Fig. 2). The percentage and p value of I2 statistics noticed the presence of marked heterogeneity (Fig. 2).

Publication bias and heterogeneity

In order to check the heterogeneity of the studies, the analysis of the Cochrane Q test and I2 with its corresponding p-value were used. A value of 0, 25, 50, And 75% was used to declare the heterogeneity test as no, low, medium and high heterogeneity respectively. Analysis of a random effect model is computed when there is evidence of heterogeneity. The I2 (variation in ES attributable to heterogeneity) test of the result showed the presence of high heterogeneity with I2 = 93%, at p < 0.001. The studies included in the review were assessed for publication bias among the researchers. The graph output of the funnel plot indicated a symmetrical distribution of the studies through visual inspection. The symmetrical distribution indicates the absence of potential publication bias (Fig. 3).

Subgroup meta-analysis of the prevalence of meconium-stained Amniotic fluid by quality of included studies showed that the prevalence of meconium-stained amniotic fluid was 20.78% (95%CI: 19.1–22.46) And 17.06% (95%CI: 13.99–20.13) among moderate risk and low-quality studies respectively (Fig. 4).

Determinants of meconium-stained amniotic fluid

Because of inconsistent categorization (grouping) of the exposures about the outcome (meconium-stained amniotic fluid), some of the components Associated with meconium-stained amniotic fluid in this review were quantitatively pooled while others were not. From the included studies, 8 variables were taken to determine the major determinants of meconium-stained Amniotic fluid. Finally, 3 variables (premature rupture of membrane, preeclampsia, and obstructed labor) were identified as significant determinants of meconium-stained amniotic fluid.

According to the findings of three studies, mothers who had A premature rupture of the membrane were more likely to develop MSAF compared with their counterparts. Thus, the pooled data showed that mothers who had a premature rupture of the membrane were 2.15 times more at risk of developing MSAF than mothers who had no premature rupture of the membrane (AOR = 2.15; 95%CI: 1.24.–3.07) (Fig. 6).

Discussion

This systematic review and meta-analysis mapped out the assessment of factors related to meconium-stained amniotic fluid among mothers who gave birth in Ethiopia. The review emphasized the distribution, design, and quality characteristics of the studies. The review included six studies from different regions of Ethiopia all of which used primary data. All the included studies were facility-based studies. Two studies included in this review had a moderate risk, either lacked information on the selection of the sample population.

The pooled prevalence of meconium-stained Amniotic fluid in Ethiopia was 19.58% (95% CI = 18.23–20.93). This was in line with the systematic review done in Ethiopia (20%) [20]. However, the finding of this study finding was higher than the primary study of done at Felege Hiwot comprehensive specialized referral hospital, North West Ethiopia (17.8%) [18], at Jimma University Specialized Teaching Hospital, South West Ethiopia (15.4%) [3] and at St Paul’s Hospital Millennium Medical College, Addis Ababa, Ethiopia (12.1%) [9]. The results of the pooled prevalence of meconium-stained amniotic fluid were lower than primary study conducted at Adama Hospital Medical College, Ethiopia (23.9%) [21], and at North Shoa Zone hospitals, Amhara Region, Ethiopia (30.3%) [7]. Several factors may contribute to the observed difference. One notable feature is the reflection of post-term pregnancies in some studies, potentially prompting the reported prevalence rates. Furthermore, variations in sample characteristics and methodologies across studies may contribute to variation in the reported prevalence of meconium-stained amniotic fluid.

The findings of sub-group analysis by region revealed that the highest prevalence of meconium-stained amniotic fluid was reported in the Amhara regional state and the lowest was reported in the Oromia regional state of Ethiopia. This difference might be due to the difference between the accessibility and the quality of services in the study area.

According to the results of this systematic review and meta-analysis, several factors were associated with meconium-stained amniotic fluid among mothers who gave birth in Ethiopia. The meta-analysis showed that mothers who had a premature rupture of the membrane were positively associated with meconium-stained amniotic fluid. This finding was supported by a primary study conducted at Adama Hospital Medical College, Ethiopia [17], and North Shoa Zone hospitals, Amhara Region, Ethiopia [7]. The reason might be due to certain factors, like an increase in the risk of maternal infection in mothers with premature rupture of the membrane, such as an increasing ascending infection through the vaginal canal followed by foetal distress resulting in the loss of protection of the umbilical cord by rupture of the membrane and then the presence of meconium in the amniotic fluid.

This meta-analysis revealed that obstructed labor was significantly associated with meconium-stained amniotic fluid. This finding was similar to a primary study conducted at Felege Hiwot Comprehensive Specialized Referral Hospital, North West Ethiopia [18] and at North Shoa Zone hospitals, Amhara Region, Ethiopia [7]. The reason could be due to the possibility of maternal dehydration, maternal distress and shock, which may result in intrauterine fetal hypoxia secondary to deficient placental perfusion then the passage of meconium into the amniotic fluid.

Furthermore, this study found that preeclampsia had a positive association with meconium-stained amniotic fluid. This was congruent with a study conducted at St Paul’s Hospital Millennium Medical College, Addis Ababa, Ethiopia [9] and Felege Hiwot comprehensive specialized referral hospital, North West Ethiopia [18]. This might be due to the possibility of a placental factor in mothers with preeclampsia that results in intrauterine hypoxia. The reason might be described by the possibility of placental insufficiency in preeclampsia that leads to intrauterine fetal hypoxia or intestinal ischemia. This intrauterine hypoxia ultimately deteriorates the action of the rectal sphincters and leads to the passage of meconium.

Limitation

Meconium-stained amniotic fluid is not widely studied in all region of the country and this gave rise to the finding of low articles. This study was not registered in PROSPERO. On the other hand, more variables were not constantly studied across the studies, and it was hard to match the variables from different studies for this Meta-Analysis. Additionally, we were unable to include all unpublished studies from universities nationwide, which may have led to the omission of some relevant data and this may influence the results of these findings.

Conclusion

In summary, this review highlights that meconium-stained amniotic fluid was a prevalent issue that poses hostile conditions for both the fetus and the neonate. The findings highlight a significant association between premature rupture of the membrane, obstructed labor, and preeclampsia with meconium-stained amniotic fluid. Subsequently, these results underscore the necessity for targeted interventions aimed at reducing the incidence of meconium-stained amniotic fluid and its related factors in the Ethiopian to reduce adverse outcome on maternal and neonates. Government and nongovernmental organization (NGO) working on maternal health improvement in Ethiopia should focus on reducing meconium-stained amniotic fluid. This finding could help policymakers and medical professionals to take appropriate action. We advise other researchers who are interested in conducting similar trials to take other factors into account.

Implication of the study

The findings of this study imply that high pooled meconium-stained amniotic fluid and a premature rupture of the membrane, obstructed labor, and preeclampsia increases risk of developing meconium-stained amniotic fluid. This review study highlights the importance of risk identification and tailored interventions to improve maternal and neonatal outcome by providing evidence-based management for this complication.

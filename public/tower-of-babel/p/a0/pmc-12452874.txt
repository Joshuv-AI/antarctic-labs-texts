Embolization of a brain arteriovenous malformation using combined transarterial and transvenous dual-lumen mini-balloon catheters
Authors: Jinlu Yu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
For brain arteriovenous malformations (BAVMs), in which transarterial embolization (TAE) is not feasible, transvenous embolization (TVE) offers an alternative. In TVE, the reverse “pressure cooker” technique using coils or glue as a venous plug is necessary. A dual-lumen mini-balloon catheter can be used in place of the coils or glue as a venous plug, but this has rarely been reported. An 18-year-old male experienced rebleeding due to previous incomplete TAE for a ruptured BAVM. On admission, physical examination did not detect positive signs, but computed tomography revealed right frontal lobe rebleeding. Digital subtraction angiography (DSA) revealed a frontal lobe residual BAVM supplied by the distal anterior cerebral artery (ACA) and drained via the anterior cerebral vein (ACV), the basal vein of Rosenthal, and the vein of Galen into the straight sinus. After a transvenous TJWY dual-lumen mini-balloon catheter (TJWY Medical, Beijing, China) reached the origin of the ACV near the BAVM. A second transarterial TJWY dual-lumen mini-balloon catheter was placed across the origin of the feeding artery from the ACA. Once both balloons were inflated, TVE was performed by injecting EVAL-I (SUCCESS, Shandong, China) through the balloon in the ACV. The residual BAVM was obliterated. Postoperatively, the patient had no new neurologic deficits and resumed his studies. Three-month follow-up DSA confirmed BAVM cure. This report shows that combining transarterial and transvenous dual-lumen mini-balloon catheters may be feasible when performing TVE for a BAVM. EVAL-I is a suitable embolic agent for TVE.

Introduction

Endovascular treatment (EVT), comprising transarterial or transvenous embolization (TAE or TVE), constitutes a vital therapeutic approach for brain arteriovenous malformations (BAVMs) [1]. The TAE has been used for more than 60 years. TAE is challenging in cases involving supplication by very thin or perforating arterial branches or the absence of potential feeder arteries. If a BAVM has an easily catheterized thick draining vein, TVE can be used. TVE has been a popular technique since 2010 [2].

In TVE, the key is that a microcatheter is placed in the draining vein to retrogradely fill the nidus with a liquid embolic agent [2]. To prevent reflux of the embolic agent in the draining vein, the reverse “pressure-cooker” technique, which involves coiling the proximal draining vein or occluding the proximal draining vein with glue with another microcatheter, is often necessary. However, placing 2 microcraters in the draining vein can be technically complex. This challenge can be addressed by the use of a single dual-lumen mini-balloon catheter. Once the balloon is inflated, the liquid embolic agent can fill the nidus retrogradely without reflux when the feeding artery is occluded.

The Scepter dual-lumen balloon catheter (Microvention, Aliso Viejo, CA, USA) can be catheterized through the draining vein [3]. It has been successfully applied to the draining vein to embolize dural arteriovenous fistulas [3,4]. However, the application of this technique for embolizing BAVMs has rarely been reported. At our institute, an excellent Chinese-made dual-lumen mini-balloon catheter is available. It is the TJWY dual-lumen mini-balloon catheter (TJWY Medical, Beijing, China), which was approved by the China Food and Drug Administration in 2023. Additionally, EVAL-I (SUCCESS, Shandong, China), a Chinese product similar to Onyx (Medtronic, Irvine, CA, USA), was used to embolize residual frontal BAVMs using a combination of transarterial and transvenous TJWY dual-lumen mini-balloon catheters. The application of techniques and products for TVE has never been reported.

Case presentation

The patient was an 18-year-old male. Two years prior, he experienced sudden-onset headache accompanied by vomiting. Physical examination revealed an intact command-following ability. Muscle strength was grade 5/5 in all extremities, with no focal neurologic deficits observed. The laboratory findings revealed leukocytosis (WBC 14.02 × 10⁹/L, reference range 4.1–11.0) with otherwise normal parameters. Computed tomography (CT) revealed hemorrhage of the anterior horn of the lateral ventricle (Fig. 1A). Both CT angiography (CTA) and digital subtraction angiography (DSA) confirmed the presence of a ruptured BAVM in the right frontal lobe (Fig. 1B-C). He denied any history of stroke, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, infections, surgery, etc. There was no family history of similar conditions. The BAVM then underwent TAE via the anterior cerebral artery (ACA) (Fig. 1D). Postprocedural assessment confirmed that there were no new neurological deficits. Under general anesthesia, left femoral artery and right femoral vein access were established. A 6F guiding catheter was inserted through the left femoral artery to the petrous segment of the internal carotid artery (ICA). DSA confirmed the residual BAVM. The feeding artery was short; only the draining deep vein was straight (Fig. 2C). A second 6F guiding catheter was inserted into the right femoral vein and then into the origin of the internal jugular vein. A transarterial roadmap revealed venous drainage of the BAVM via the straight sinus into the right hyperplastic occipital sinus and internal jugular vein (Fig. 3A). A 4.6F distal access catheter (AccuMedical, Beijing, China) was navigated through the 6F guiding catheter via the occipital sinus into the straight sinus. A transvenous TJWY dual-lumen mini-balloon catheter (2.2 mm-9 mm) guided by an ASAHI CHIKAI 0.010′’ guidewire (ASAHI INTECC Co., Ltd., Japan) was positioned at the origin of the draining vein (ACV) (Fig. 3B). After 1 month of home recovery, the patient returned to school. Discussion

In 1999, Massoud et al. proposed the well-known principle of transvenous retrograde nidus sclerotherapy under controlled hypotensive anesthesia (TRENSH) for guiding TVE to treat BAVMs in a swine model [5]. TVE has since been utilized for BAVM treatment. Current evidence indicates its applicability is limited to specific BAVM cases, such as those in which the nidus is small (<3 cm), those involving deep-seated lesions, those requiring supplication by very thin or perforating arterial branches, those in which potential feeder arteries are absent, and those in which a single draining vein is present [6]. This case conforms to the criteria for TVE. The BAVM has a single deep vein drain that is larger in caliber than its counterpart arterial feeder, which enables the possibility of TVE [7]. In addition, previous incomplete TAE complicated a second TAE, supporting the choice of TVE.

When performing TVE, reducing or stopping arterial inflow into the nidus is essential to allow the liquid embolic agent to overcome feeding arterial pressure and enter the nidus retrogradely. This can be achieved by occluding the feeding artery with a mini-balloon and achieving temporary systemic hypotension to lower the pressure gradient and blood flow to the nidus. According to the TRENSH principle, the mean systemic pressure should be reduced to 40-50 mmHg or 20% below the baseline mean arterial pressure [5]. Intraoperative and postoperative hypotension remains a critical cornerstone for managing BAVMs to prevent hemorrhage and edema after TVE. In this patient, we did not lower the mean systemic pressure and occluded only the feeding artery with a dual-lumen mini-balloon catheter, as the feeding artery of the BAVM could be completely occluded with this mini-balloon.

Several balloon types can be used, including the Scepter or mini-Scepter balloon, Eclipse balloon (Balt Extrusion, Montmorency, France), Ascent balloon (DePuy Synthes, Raynham, Massachusetts, USA), and Hyperglide or Hyperform (Medtronic, Minneapolis, Minnesota, USA) [[8], [9], [10]]. In China, the TJWY dual-lumen balloon catheter is available in sizes of 4.0 mm-10 mm, 15 mm, and 20 mm, and a miniature version with a size of 2.2 mm-9 mm is available. This mini-balloon is suitable for occluding the ACA (Fig. 4C).

In TVE, excessive reflux of the liquid embolic agent cannot be tolerated, making it difficult or dangerous to remove the working microcatheter of the casting liquid embolic agent in the draining vein. The reverse “pressure cooker” technique can be employed with coils or N-butyl 2-cyanoacrylate (NBCA) as a venous plug [9]. The earliest description of the transvenous “pressure cooker” technique dates back to the 2003 ABC WIN seminar in Val d’Isère (France) by Houdak and was also described in 2005 by Houdart and Chapot at the same meeting [11]. In 2014, René Chapot et al. detailed the pressure cooker technique, which involves the navigation of 2 microcatheters (1 detachable tip and the other nondetachable) as close as possible to the nidus via an enlarged and arterialized primary vein [12].

We did not use 2 microcatheters to establish a “pressure cooker” effect. Instead, we replaced it with a dual-lumen mini-balloon. The mini-size TJWY dual-lumen balloon catheter was successfully navigated to the proximal draining vein (ACV), facilitating TVE. With respect to the use of liquid embolic agents in TVE, owing to the rapidity of polymerization, NBCA is not suitable for TVE, as immediate occlusion of the draining vein can be disastrous [6,13]. Since its introduction in 2005, Onyx has been preferred for TVE because of its more cohesive nature and milder adhesive properties. Onyx can be injected over several minutes to more than an hour, allowing for more controlled embolization [6,13]. During TVE for BAVM, the Onyx adheres to the venous wall before the lumen is occluded, permitting retrograde nidus filling once anterograde flow is restricted before total vein occlusion occurs [5]. We used a product similar to Onyx, called EVAL-I, which was approved by the China State Food and Drug Administration in 2012 [14]. EVAL-I is an EVAL/DMSO/ethanol mixture used as an embolic agent for treating BAVM and is comparable to Onyx-16. In this patient, EVAL-I was successfully used for TVE, increasing the choice of liquid embolic agents that can be used for TVE.

Conclusion

TVE using a combination of transarterial and transvenous dual-lumen mini-balloon catheters may be feasible when performing TVE for a BAVM. In addition, EVAL-I is a good embolic agent for TVE.

Development and validation of a model for predicting central lymph node metastasis in colorectal cancer based on CT textural features and clinical indicators
Authors: Long Zhang, Guoxin Liu, Jiahui Huo, Weihong Wu, Hao Xu, Shuyuan Guo, Yan Wu, Hao Jiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCentral lymph node metastasis (CLNM) in colorectal cancer (CRC) is an important factor affecting patient prognosis and the assessment of surgical extent. However, conventional imaging remains limited in the preoperative assessment of LNM. This study aimed to investigate the value of a combined model based on computed tomography (CT) texture features and clinical indicators for the preoperative identification of CLNM in patients with CRC.MethodsIn this retrospective study, 1,187 patients who underwent laparoscopic radical resection for CRC at two hospitals were screened, and 228 were ultimately included. Based on postoperative pathology, patients were divided into the CLNM-positive group (n=76) and the CLNM-negative group (n=152). Patients from one hospital were randomly assigned to a training set (n=126) and a test set (n=54) in a 7:3 ratio, while patients from the other hospital were used for the external validation set (n=48). Radiomic features extracted using MaZda software were used to construct the radiomics model, while preoperative clinical indicators were used to construct the clinical model. Subsequently, radiomic features and clinical indicators were incorporated to construct five combined predictive models, including logistic regression (LR), support vector machine (SVM), neural network (NN), random forest (RF), and gradient boosting machine (GBM). Their performance was compared using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, calibration curves, and decision curve analysis (DCA).ResultsSignificant differences in T stage, N stage, preoperative serum carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA), and neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) were observed between the CLNM-positive and CLNM-negative groups (all P<0.05). S(1,0)DifEntrp and S(0,1)SumEntrp were selected for the radiomics model, while CEA and NLR were selected for the clinical model. The combined model incorporated all four predictors and performed better than the clinical and radiomics models in both the test and external validation sets. It achieved areas under the curve (AUCs) of 0.816 and 0.775, respectively. Among the five models, GBM, RF, and NN showed larger AUC differences between the test set and the external validation set, with differences of 0.349, 0.208, and 0.096. In contrast, the differences were smaller for LR and SVM, at 0.041 and 0.003, respectively. Based on the ROC curves, calibration curves, and DCA, the LR model showed better overall predictive performance and stability in this study.ConclusionsThe combined predictive model based on CT texture features and clinical indicators showed potential value for the preoperative identification of CLNM in CRC. It may provide an additional reference for the preoperative assessment of CLNM in patients with CRC.

Key findings

• The combined model incorporating computed tomography (CT) texture features and clinical indicators showed better predictive performance than the clinical or radiomics model alone for preoperative identification of central lymph node metastasis (CLNM) in colorectal cancer (CRC). Among the five combined models, the logistic regression model showed relatively stable performance across the test and external validation sets.

What is known and what is new?

• Some clinical indicators, such as carcinoembryonic antigen, carbohydrate antigen 19-9, and neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio, are closely associated with CLNM in CRC.

• Combining clinical indicators with CT texture features may be helpful for predicting CLNM in CRC.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• The combined model may serve as an auxiliary tool for preoperative CLNM risk assessment in CRC, but further prospective multicenter validation is needed before clinical application.

Introduction

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is one of the most common gastrointestinal malignancies worldwide. It ranks third in incidence and second in mortality among all malignant tumors (1). Its global annual incidence continues to increase by approximately 2–4%, making it an important clinical problem that needs to be addressed (1). Lymph node metastasis (LNM) status is not only an important component of tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) staging but also a key factor affecting patient prognosis and treatment decisions (2). For patients with resectable CRC, radical surgery combined with central lymph node dissection remains the main treatment strategy (3). Central lymph nodes are located at the roots of the superior mesenteric artery (SMA) or inferior mesenteric artery (IMA). They represent an important site of lymphatic spread in CRC, and their involvement is often associated with poor prognosis (4).

However, different studies and guidelines have not reached a consensus on the anatomical boundaries of central lymph nodes or the optimal dissection strategy (5,6). Although extended dissection may increase the number of lymph nodes retrieved, it may also increase the risk of surgical injury and postoperative complications. Therefore, accurate preoperative assessment of LNM is important to help surgeons make clinical decisions (7).

Currently, various imaging methods and predictive models have been used to assess LNM in CRC (8,9). However, most previous studies have focused on the overall risk of regional LNM (10,11). Studies on central lymph nodes as a specific region remain limited. Chen et al. (12) used multivariate logistic regression (LR) analysis to identify risk factors for No. 253 LNM and developed a nomogram for preoperative assessment, with an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.865. Zhang et al. (13) developed an LR model using clinical features, computed tomography (CT) lymph node features, and radiomics features to predict No. 253 LNM, and the combined model achieved an AUC of 0.800 in the external validation set. Wang et al. (14) used several machine learning algorithms to predict LNM at the root of the IMA, which also showed potential clinical value. These studies suggest that models based on preoperative clinical and imaging information may be feasible for predicting central lymph node metastasis (CLNM).

However, most previous studies on central lymph nodes have focused primarily on the No. 253 lymph node group alone, whereas predictive studies evaluating the overall metastatic status of central lymph nodes remain limited. In addition, differences in predictor selection and model development methods across studies may limit the comparability of existing findings and their applicability to the overall assessment of CLNM. In addition, although complex machine learning models may offer predictive potential, their performance, stability, and applicability require further investigation and validation. Previous studies have shown that conventional imaging modalities, such as CT and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), have limitations in assessing LNM. It has been reported that up to 70% of metastatic lymph nodes are smaller than 5 mm in diameter (15). Because these lymph nodes are small and often lack specific morphological features, they may be missed or overlooked during preoperative imaging assessment. These limitations may affect the accuracy of preoperative staging and the planning of lymph node dissection (16,17).

To improve diagnostic performance, radiomics has emerged as a promising technique that enables high-throughput extraction of quantitative texture features from tumors and lymph nodes on medical images (18). This approach may help compensate for some limitations of conventional imaging (19,20). In particular, evidence remains limited on the systematic integration of radiomic texture features with clinical indicators. Therefore, this study aimed to develop and validate predictive models based on CT-derived texture features and clinical indicators for predicting CLNM in CRC. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2026-0682/rc).

Methods

Patient selection and clinical information

A total of 1,187 patients who underwent laparoscopic radical resection for CRC were retrospectively screened from two centers: Hongqi Hospital Affiliated to Mudanjiang Medical University between May 2022 and May 2026, and Changxing County People’s Hospital between January 2022 and May 2026. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (I) pathologically confirmed CRC; (II) contrast-enhanced CT performed within 2 weeks before surgery; and (III) no history of other malignancies. Exclusion criteria included: (I) poor CT image quality that affected lymph node assessment; (II) receipt of radiotherapy or chemotherapy before surgery; and (III) incomplete clinical or pathological data. Patients were classified as CLNM-positive if postoperative pathology confirmed metastatic central lymph nodes; otherwise, they were classified as CLNM-negative. Given the low incidence of CLNM and the marked imbalance between CLNM-positive and CLNM-negative cases, stratified random sampling was performed at both centers. CLNM-positive and CLNM-negative patients were included at a ratio of 1:2. Patients from Hongqi Hospital Affiliated to Mudanjiang Medical University were used as the internal cohort and were randomly divided into the training and test sets at a ratio of 7:3. Patients from Changxing County People’s Hospital were used as an independent external validation cohort. All feature selection and model development procedures were performed only in the training set. The test set and external validation cohort were used only to evaluate model performance. Finally, 228 patients were included in this study (Figure 1). The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Hongqi Hospital Affiliated to Mudanjiang Medical University (No. 2026020) and the Clinical Research Ethics Committee of Changxing County People’s Hospital (No. 2026-EC-57). Informed consent was waived due to the retrospective nature of this study.

Clinical data, including age, sex, tumor TNM stage, tumor location, lymph node location, serum carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA), carbohydrate antigen 19-9 (CA19-9), and neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), were collected from both centers. TNM stage was used only for baseline description, whereas the remaining variables were used as clinical features for feature selection and model development.

Surgery and pathological

All patients underwent laparoscopic radical resection for CRC combined with D3 lymph node dissection. All procedures were performed by general surgeons with more than 10 years of experience in abdominal surgery. Intraoperative lymph node dissection and pathological assessment were conducted according to the recommendations of the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) and Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) guidelines, as well as the Chinese Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Treatment of CRC. At least 12 lymph nodes were examined in each patient (21). All tumor and lymph node specimens were evaluated by dedicated pathologists (22). Patients were classified as CLNM-positive if postoperative pathology confirmed metastatic central lymph nodes. Otherwise, they were classified as CLNM-negative.

CT imaging examination

CT examinations were performed using different CT scanners at the two hospitals. At Hongqi Hospital Affiliated to Mudanjiang Medical University, the scanning parameters were as follows: tube voltage, 100–140 kV; tube current, 80–350 mA; gantry rotation time, 0.30–0.80 s/rotation; and slice thickness, 1.25 mm. At Changxing County People’s Hospital, the scanning parameters were as follows: tube voltage, 100–140 kV; tube current, 60–300 mA; gantry rotation time, 0.35–0.75 s/rotation; and slice thickness, 1.0 mm. All patients fasted from food and water for at least 6 hours before the examination and underwent whole-abdominal contrast-enhanced CT within 2 weeks before surgery. Patients were placed in the supine position, and contrast agent was administered through a peripheral vein. All patients cooperated with the radiologists throughout the examination.

Segmentation and feature extraction of lymph nodes

Because accurate matching between CT images and pathological specimens is difficult, the largest suspected lymph node was selected for radiomic feature extraction. Texture analysis was performed using MaZda software (version 4.6; https://www.mazda.imt.p.lodz.pl/) (23,24). To reduce the influence of differences in CT scanning parameters and scanner types on the stability of texture features, gray-level normalization was performed for each region of interest (ROI) using the µ ± 3σ method (25). Preoperative contrast-enhanced CT images in Digital Imaging and Communications in Medicine (DICOM) format were exported from the picture archiving and communication systems (PACS) of the two hospitals. ROIs were manually delineated by two radiologists with 10 years of experience in abdominal imaging, who were blinded to the pathological results. ROI delineation was performed on the slice where the target lymph node was most clearly displayed and showed the most typical morphology. Areas of necrosis, calcification, artifacts, and adjacent bowel that could affect texture analysis were carefully avoided. When multiple suspicious lymph nodes were present, the most representative lymph node was selected for ROI delineation. Two radiologists with 10 years of experience in abdominal imaging independently performed ROI segmentation while blinded to the pathological results to assess interobserver agreement. Any disagreement during ROI delineation was resolved through discussion and consensus.

Selection of clinical and texture features

For clinical features, age, sex, tumor location, lymph node location, serum CEA, CA19-9, and NLR were collected. Univariable LR analysis was used to evaluate the association between each clinical indicator and CLNM. Variables with statistical significance (P<0.05) were then included in multivariable LR analysis to identify independent risk factors for CLNM. Radiomic features were extracted using MaZda software. A total of 277 texture features were extracted, including: (I) first-order features, such as gray-level maximum, gray-level minimum, median, variance, skewness, kurtosis, and entropy; (II) second-order features, including features derived from the gray-level co-occurrence matrix and gray-level run-length matrix; and (III) higher-order features, such as wavelet- and spectral-based features. Each feature was first standardized. The Mann-Whitney U test was then used to identify features that differed significantly between the two groups (P<0.05). For the selected texture features, interobserver agreement between the two readers was assessed using the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC). Features with an ICC ≥0.90 were considered to have good agreement and were included in subsequent analyses. Random forest (RF) analysis was then used to rank the importance of the radiomic features, and correlation analysis was performed to remove redundant features. Finally, the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression, combined with 10-fold cross-validation, was used to select the optimal radiomic features for model development. Model development

This study was designed for predictive model development and validation. Patients from Hongqi Hospital Affiliated to Mudanjiang Medical University (n=180) were randomly divided into a training set (n=126) and a test set (n=54) at a ratio of 7:3 using stratified random sampling.

Five-fold stratified cross-validation was performed in the training set. In each iteration, four folds were used for model training, and one-fold was used for internal validation. All models were trained using only the training set. The test set and external validation cohort were used solely for final performance evaluation and were not involved in model training. Clinical indicators identified by multivariable LR analysis (P<0.05) were used to construct the clinical model, whereas radiomic features selected by LASSO regression were used to construct the radiomics model. Considering that the training set included 42 CLNM-positive cases, four variables were finally included in the model according to the events per variable (EPV) principle, with the aim of reducing overfitting and improving model stability. Five predictive models were then developed, including LR, support vector machine (SVM), RF, gradient boosting machine (GBM), and neural network (NN).

Model validation

Patients from Changxing County People’s Hospital (n=48) were used as the external validation cohort to evaluate model performance. Model performance was further assessed in both the test set and the external validation cohort. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and the AUC were used to evaluate the ability of each model to distinguish CLNM-positive from CLNM-negative patients. According to previous studies, an AUC ≥0.90 was considered excellent, an AUC ≥0.80 was considered considerable, and an AUC ≥0.70 was considered fair (26). Calibration curves were used to assess the agreement between predicted probabilities and observed outcomes. Decision curve analysis (DCA) was performed to evaluate the clinical net benefit and potential clinical utility of the models across different threshold probabilities. Finally, the clinical value of the predictive models was comprehensively evaluated based on AUC, sensitivity, specificity, Brier score, and DCA (27,28).

Statistical analysis

Data analysis and statistical graphing were performed using SPSS 27.0, GraphPad Prism 10.0, and R 4.4.0. Normally distributed continuous variables were expressed as the mean ± standard deviation, whereas non-normally distributed variables were expressed as the median. The Mann-Whitney U test was used for between-group comparisons. Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages, and between-group comparisons were performed using the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test. A two-sided P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. LASSO regression analysis was performed, and the optimal penalty parameter λ was determined using 10-fold cross-validation to select key texture features associated with LNM. The ICC was used to assess interobserver and intraobserver agreement. ICC values ≥0.90 were considered to indicate excellent reliability, values of 0.75–0.89 good reliability, and values of 0.50–0.74 moderate reliability (29). ROC curves were used to evaluate the diagnostic performance of each model, and the AUC with 95% confidence intervals (95% CIs) was calculated. Calibration curves and DCA were plotted to assess model calibration and quantify the clinical net benefit across different decision thresholds, respectively. Finally, the generalizability of the models was evaluated in the test set and external validation cohort.

A total of 1,187 patients were initially screened in this study. After applying the inclusion and exclusion criteria, 228 patients were finally included, comprising 76 CLNM-positive patients and 152 CLNM-negative patients. Significant differences were found between the two groups in T stage, N stage, preoperative CEA level, and NLR (all P<0.05). No significant differences were observed in age, sex, tumor location, distribution of central lymph nodes, or preoperative serum CA19-9 levels (all P>0.05). Texture feature selection

A total of 277 texture features were extracted from the central lymph node regions using MaZda software. To evaluate the repeatability and stability of feature measurement, ICC analysis was performed. A total of 189 texture features showed ICC values greater than 0.90, indicating excellent consistency and stability. These features were retained for subsequent feature selection. First, the Mann-Whitney U test identified 91 features that differed significantly between the D3LNM-positive and D3LNM-negative groups (P<0.05). Then, an RF model was used to assess feature importance, and the top 20 features were selected for correlation analysis. After removing highly collinear features based on the correlation heatmap, 14 features with low intercorrelations were retained (Figure 2). LASSO regression analysis combined with 10-fold cross-validation was further performed to determine the optimal penalty parameter (λ=0.015). This process identified 11 texture features with relatively high discriminative value. Considering the EPV principle and to reduce the risk of model overfitting, only the two texture features with the highest regression coefficients were retained for subsequent prediction model development (Figure 4). Based on these two optimal texture features, a radiomic LR model was constructed.

Development and validation of prediction models

A clinical LR model and a radiomic LR model were constructed using the two selected clinical features and two selected radiomic features, respectively. A combined model was then developed by integrating all four features (Figure 5). To evaluate model performance, the AUC, sensitivity, and specificity were calculated. Model performance was further assessed in the test set and the external validation set (Table 3). The combined model showed better discriminative performance than the single clinical and radiomic models in both the test set and the external validation set, with AUC values of 0.816 (95% CI: 0.691–0.941) and 0.775 (95% CI: 0.641–0.910), respectively.

To further evaluate the accuracy of probability prediction and the potential clinical benefit of the five combined models, calibration curves and DCA were plotted for both the test set and the external validation set. In the external validation set, the LR model had the lowest Brier score of 0.184, suggesting relatively good agreement between the predicted probabilities and the observed outcomes. DCA showed that the LR model achieved a higher net benefit across the main range of threshold probabilities, indicating its potential value in supporting clinical decision-making (Figure 6).

Discussion

In this retrospective study, we developed and validated a combined model based on clinical indicators and radiomic features to predict CLNM in patients with CRC. Compared with the clinical model and the radiomic model alone, the combined model showed relatively better discriminative performance in both the test set and the external validation set. Among the five diagnostic models, the LR model showed relatively good stability and may, to some extent, provide an auxiliary reference for the preoperative assessment of CLNM.

In previous clinical practice, the preoperative assessment of LNM in CRC mainly relied on conventional imaging findings, such as CT and MRI, as well as physicians’ clinical experience (16). Although the short-axis diameter of lymph nodes is commonly used as an important imaging reference for evaluating the risk of LNM, conventional imaging often has limited ability to identify lymph nodes that are small in size or atypical in morphology (30). Langman et al. (17) found in a study of 334 patients with rectal cancer that some malignant lymph nodes had a diameter of less than 3 mm. Shkurti et al. (31) retrospectively analyzed 44,471 patients and found that conventional CT had limited diagnostic performance for assessing LNM in CRC, with a specificity of 70% and a sensitivity of 62% for N1–N2 tumors. These findings suggest that relying solely on conventional imaging techniques to distinguish benign from malignant lymph nodes still has certain limitations.

This study aimed to explore a preoperative prediction method that complements conventional CT-based morphological assessment and improves the discrimination of CLNM. CT texture analysis can quantify intralesional texture heterogeneity within the ROI based on entropy, gray-level distribution, and contrast, thereby providing additional information for assessing lymph node metastatic status (32). The selected features, S(1,0)DifEntrp and S(0,1)SumEntrp, are entropy-based texture features derived from the gray-level co-occurrence matrix. They reflect intralesional texture complexity and heterogeneity within the ROI from the perspectives of gray-level difference distribution and gray-level combination distribution, respectively (33). Both features showed relatively high regression coefficients in the LASSO model. Group comparisons further showed that their levels were significantly higher in the CLNM-positive group than in the CLNM-negative group (Figure 4). This finding suggests that metastatic central lymph nodes may have more complex and irregular gray-level distribution patterns. Previous studies have shown that entropy is associated with intratumoral heterogeneity and patient prognosis, suggesting its potential value as an imaging biomarker (32,34-36). However, texture features cannot replace the pathological gold standard. In addition, this study did not further integrate histopathological features or survival outcomes. Therefore, the biological significance of these features should be further validated in large-scale, multicenter studies that combine pathological and imaging data.

This study also found that serum CA19-9 and NLR levels were significantly associated with CLNM, whereas CEA showed no significant association. This finding differs from some previous studies (37,38). The discrepancy may be related to differences in sample size, patient composition, and the anatomical definition of lymph nodes. Previous studies mainly focused on lymph nodes at the root of the IMA in CRC. In contrast, the present study evaluated metastatic involvement of lymph nodes at both the roots of the IMA and SMA. Therefore, even for the same clinical indicators, their statistical performance in this study may not be fully consistent with that reported in previous studies.

The two selected texture features and two clinical indicators were used to develop the radiomics model and clinical model, respectively. Both models showed moderate predictive performance in the test set, with AUC values of 0.767 (95% CI: 0.640–0.895) and 0.806 (95% CI: 0.679–0.933), respectively. However, with the development of precision medicine, relying solely on individual clinical indicators or radiomic features may not fully capture the risk of LNM in patients with CRC (39,40). For example, He et al. (38) developed a combined model based on quantitative dual-energy CT parameters and clinical features to assess LNM in CRC. Based on this rationale, we integrated the four selected features and further constructed a combined model. The combined model achieved an AUC of 0.816 (95% CI: 0.691–0.941) in the test set, with performance comparable to or slightly better than that of the single-modality models. These results suggest that integrating radiomic features with clinical indicators may provide additional information for assessing the risk of CLNM.

Zhang et al. (13) developed an LR model to predict metastatic involvement of No. 253 lymph nodes, and their findings suggested that this model had potential value for LNM risk assessment. Building on this evidence, we constructed and compared five prediction models, including LR, SVM, GBM, RF, and NN, to evaluate the predictive value of different algorithms for CLNM. By comparing the AUC, Brier score, and DCA results across different models, we found that the LR model showed relatively favorable discrimination, predictive performance, and clinical utility. In contrast, more complex models, such as GBM, RF, and NN, showed weaker performance. Therefore, under the conditions of the present study, the LR model may be more suitable for assessing the risk of CLNM.

The prediction model integrating radiomic features and clinical indicators showed better discriminatory performance than the single-modality models. Given the current sample size, the LR model demonstrated more stable predictive performance than the more complex models. However, this study still has several limitations. First, the overall sample size was relatively limited. A total of 228 patients were included, and the external validation set included only 48 patients, which may have affected the predictive performance of the models. Second, this was a retrospective study. Prognostic outcomes were not evaluated, and a multicenter prospective study was not conducted. Therefore, the clinical applicability and generalizability of our findings require further assessment. Finally, the CT acquisition parameters differed between the internal and external validation cohorts. Although the models still showed a certain level of predictive performance and potential clinical value under these conditions, differences in equipment and scanning parameters may affect the extraction and analysis of texture features. Future studies with larger, multicenter, and standardized cohorts are needed to further validate the robustness of the models.

Conclusions

Overall, this study developed and validated a prediction model integrating CT texture features and clinical indicators. Compared with the single-modality models, the combined model showed potential for identifying central lymph node metastatic status in patients with CRC before surgery. Among the five models developed in this study, the LR model demonstrated relatively stable predictive performance. This model may provide an additional reference for the preoperative assessment of CLNM in patients with CRC.

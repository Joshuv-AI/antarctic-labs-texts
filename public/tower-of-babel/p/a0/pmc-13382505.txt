Quality of life and psychological outcomes with albumin-bound paclitaxel-carboplatin versus solvent-based paclitaxel-carboplatin as first-line chemotherapy in stage IC–IV ovarian carcinoma
Authors: Nidhi Mahlawat, Atal Sood, Poorva Vias, Sushma Sawaraj
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDeclining psychological well-being and health-related quality of life represent an understudied burden in the treatment of ovarian cancer, caused by the disease and its chemotherapy.ObjectiveTo compare quality of life using the ovarian cancer-specific module (EORTC QLQ-OV28) and depression severity using the Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HDRS) in a pre-specified secondary analysis of a randomized controlled trial comparing nab-paclitaxel-carboplatin and solvent-based paclitaxel-carboplatin as first-line chemotherapy for Stage IC–IV ovarian carcinoma over 12 months.MethodsPre-specified secondary analysis of a prospective, open-label, parallel-arm, exploratory randomized controlled trial registered (CTRI/2024/05/067283). Seventy-four patients were randomized 1:1 to Group A(nab-paclitaxel) (n = 38) or Group B(sb-paclitaxel) (n = 36). EORTC QLQ-OV28 and HDRS were administered at baseline (prior to administration of the first chemotherapy cycle), 6 months, and 12 months.ResultsGastrointestinal symptom burden declined significantly within both groups over 12 months (within-group p = 0.001), with no significant between-group difference. Peripheral neuropathy showed no significant between-group differences at any timepoint (baseline p = 0.054; 6 m p = 0.36; 12 m p = 0.056), with both groups reaching near-zero scores by 12 months. Sexual functioning scores were comparable at baseline p = 0.86, 6 m p = 0.63; 12 m p = 0.67. HDRS scores showed no significant between-group differences at any timepoints, with significant within-group improvement by 12 months in both groups.ConclusionBoth regimens were associated with improvements in quality of life over 12 months. Nab-paclitaxel-carboplatin demonstrated a favorable quality-of-life profile, particularly with respect to chemotherapy-related side effects, while other quality-of-life domains were comparable between the treatment groups.Keywords: Ovarian carcinoma, Quality of life, EORTC QLQ-OV28, Nab-paclitaxel, HDRS, Depression

Introduction

Ovarian cancer (OC) is the leading cause of death from gynecological malignancies globally. (Sung et al., 2021) The majority of patients present with advanced-stage disease, and patients receive multiple lines of chemotherapy depending on the response. The physical, psychological, and social consequences of both the disease and its treatment cause a burden on health-related quality of life (HRQoL) that is increasingly recognized as a primary endpoint alongside survival in oncology trials. (Wilson et al., 2018)

Depression and anxiety are approximately two to three times higher in OC patients than in healthy female populations, with clinically significant depressive symptoms reported in 20–40% of patients during the first year of treatment. (Liu and Yang, 2019) Peripheral neuropathy, myelosuppression, arthralgias, myalgias, and skin reactions are among the side effects of first-line solvent bound(sb)-paclitaxel chemotherapy that may negatively impact the quality of life (QoL). Peripheral neuropathy, in particular, represents a dose-limiting toxicity with significant implications for functional status, a negative predictor of HRQoL and treatment adherence. (Cella et al., 2003) HRQoL first deteriorates during initial chemotherapy and, by directly connecting patient-reported outcomes to survival-relevant outcomes, can predict treatment withdrawal, dose change, and decreased adherence to follow-up. (Wenzel et al., 2005)

Beyond the fundamental toxicities of the therapy, sb-paclitaxel, which is produced in Cremophor EL and ethanol, requires corticosteroid premedication and a lengthy 3-h infusion causing treatment-related weakness, steroid-induced mood alterations, and sleeplessness. (Gelderblom et al., 2001; Zietse et al., 2025)

Nab-paclitaxel is a Cremophor EL-free, albumin-bound nanoparticle formulation administered over 30 min without corticosteroid premedication. The elimination of Cremophor-related toxicities, steroid premedication burden, and reduced infusion time collectively suggest potential HRQoL advantages over sb-paclitaxel, but no prospective comparative data exist for the first-line OC setting, particularly from the Indian subcontinent. (Wang et al., 2023)

The parent randomized controlled trial was designed to compare hypersensitivity reactions, CA-125 response, and the hematological and biochemical safety profile of nab-paclitaxel-carboplatin versus solvent-based paclitaxel-carboplatin as first-line chemotherapy in ovarian carcinoma. The present manuscript reports a pre-specified secondary analysis of that trial evaluating longitudinal patient-reported health-related quality of life using the EORTC QLQ-OV28 and psychological outcomes using the Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HDRS).

Materials and methods

This was a randomized controlled trial conducted at the Department of Radiotherapy and Oncology and the Department of Pharmacology. The trial received approval from the Protocol Review Committee and from the Institutional Ethics Committee. The trial was registered with Clinical Trials Registry - India (CTRI/2024/05/067283).

Participants were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to Group A (nab-paclitaxel) or Group B (sb-paclitaxel) using computer-generated random numbers. Seventy-four women aged 18–80 years with newly diagnosed, histologically confirmed Stage IC–IV ovarian carcinoma were enrolled. Eligible patients had a Karnofsky Performance Scale score ≥ 70, no significant medical co-morbidities, and normal hematological, hepatic, and renal function. Patients with a history of prior chemotherapy or radiotherapy, pre-existing neuropathic disorders, pregnancy, or lactation were excluded.

Written informed consent was obtained from all participants in their native language prior to enrolment.

Group A received nab-paclitaxel, 175 mg/m2 administered over 30 min without premedication plus carboplatin AUC 5–6, n = 38; Group B received sb-paclitaxel 175 mg/m2 administered over 3 h with dexamethasone/diphenhydramine/ranitidine premedication plus carboplatin AUC 5–6, n = 36, every 21 days. All patients completed the planned six cycles of chemotherapy, and additional cycles were administered in selected patients based on clinical indication.

Patient-reported outcomes were assessed using the EORTC QLQ-OV28 ovarian cancer-specific module and the 17-item Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HDRS).

The European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer Quality of Life Questionnaire Ovarian Cancer Module 28 (EORTC QLQ-OV28) is a validated, ovarian cancer- specific patient-reported outcome instrument. (Kristensen et al., 2003) It contains 28 items grouped into seven multi-item subscales: Gastrointestinal (GI) symptoms, Peripheral neuropathy, Other chemotherapy side effects (like hair loss, upset by the loss of hair, food and drink taste different from usual? Have any aches or pains in your muscles or joints? any problems with hearing? urinate frequently? any skin problems eg. itchy, dry?), Hormonal/menopausal symptoms, Body image, Attitude to disease and treatment and Sexual functioning.

All items are rated on a 4-point Likert scale (1 = Not at all to 4 = Very much). Subscale scores are linearly transformed to a 0–100 scale using the standard EORTC formula: Score = [(raw mean score − 1) / 3] × 100. For symptom, attitude, and body image domains, higher scores indicate greater symptom burden (worse QoL). For sexual functioning higher scores reflect greater function (better QoL). A between-group or within-group difference of ≥10 points is considered the threshold for clinical meaningfulness. (Kristensen et al., 2003)

The 17-item clinician-administered Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HDRS) is the reference standard for quantifying depression severity in clinical research. (Hamilton, 1960) Each item is rated 0–2 or 0–4. Total score interpretation: 0–7 = no depression; 8–13 = mild; 14–18 = moderate; 19–22 = severe; ≥23 = very severe.

Both EORTC QLQ-OV28 and HDRS were administered by a trained clinician. Both instruments were administered at three time points: baseline (before chemotherapy cycle 1), 6 months, and 12 months from treatment initiation. All questionnaires were administered in native language by trained personnel.

This study represents a pre-specified secondary analysis of a randomized controlled trial. The parent trial was designed to evaluate hypersensitivity reactions, CA-125 response, and safety outcomes. No separate sample size calculation was performed for this secondary quality-of-life analysis because the study population was derived from the parent randomized trial. Accordingly, all quality-of-life analyses should be interpreted as exploratory. The sample represents consecutive eligible patients enrolled over the first six months of the study period. Data are expressed as mean ± SD. The Wilcoxon signed-rank test was used for within-group comparisons across time points (paired, non-parametric). The Mann-Whitney U test was used for between-group comparisons at each time point (unpaired, non-parametric). Sexual functioning scores, which were normally distributed, were compared between groups using Student's independent t-test. No correction for multiple comparisons was applied given the exploratory nature of this secondary analysis; all domain findings should be interpreted accordingly. A p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. No imputation was performed for missing data.

Both groups demonstrated substantial within-group reduction in GI symptom (Fig. 1) scores across 12 months. Within-group improvements were highly significant in both groups (Group A, nab-paclitaxel: baseline vs 6 m p = 0.001, baseline vs 12 m p = 0.001, 6 m vs 12 m p = 0.001; Group B, sb-paclitaxel: baseline vs 6 m p = 0.001, baseline vs 12 m p = 0.001, 6 m vs 12 m p = 0.01). No between-group difference was present at baseline (p = 0.26), 6 months (p = 0.43), or 12 months (p = 0.86). Both groups converged to very low GI symptom scores by 12 months, representing a clinically meaningful reduction of approximately 75–80% from baseline in both arms.

Comparison of Gastrointestinal Symptoms between two groups.

Both groups showed substantial within-group reduction in peripheral neuropathy (Fig. 2) scores by 12 months. No significant between-group difference was present at baseline (p = 0.054), 6 months (p = 0.36), or 12 months (p = 0.056). The p-values at baseline and 12 months are borderline, numerically favoring Group A(nab-paclitaxel) at both timepoints, but neither reaches statistical significance. Both groups reached near-complete neuropathy resolution by 12 months (Group A, nab-paclitaxel: p = 0.001 from 6 m to 12 m; Group B, sb-paclitaxel: p = 0.001 from 6 m to 12 m).

Comparison of Peripheral Neuropathy between two groups.

Baseline scores were comparable between groups in terms of other chemotherapy side effects (Fig. 3) (p = 0.40). At 6 months, scores remained equivalent between groups (p = 0.47). At 12 months, Group A(nab-paclitaxel) showed significantly lower other chemotherapy side-effect scores than Group B, sb-paclitaxel (p = 0.015), representing the only statistically significant between-group difference in the entire QoL analysis. Within-group improvements from baseline to 12 months were significant in both arms (Group A, nab-paclitaxel: p = 0.001; Group B, sb-paclitaxel: p = 0.001).

Hormonal/menopausal symptom (Fig. 4) scores showed no significant between-group difference at baseline (p = 0.13), 6 months (p = 0.50), or 12 months (p = 0.33). Both groups showed modest within-group improvement, reaching significance only in the later follow-up period (Group A, nab-paclitaxel: 6 m vs 12 m p < 0.05; Group B, sb-paclitaxel: baseline vs 12 m p = 0.02, 6 m vs 12 m p < 0.05).

Body image (Fig. 5) scores were low and comparable between groups at all three timepoints: baseline (p = 0.27), 6 months (p = 0.70), and 12 months (p = 0.83). No significant within-group changes were observed in either group at any timepoint.

Both groups showed comparable and progressively declining scores, reflecting reduced perceived burden of disease and treatment over time (Fig. 6). No between-group differences were present at any time point (baseline p = 0.82; 6 m p = 0.77; 12 m p = 0.46). Group A(nab-paclitaxel) showed significant within-group improvement at all three comparisons (BL vs 6 m p = 0.01, BL vs 12 m p = 0.001, 6 m vs 12 m p = 0.001). Group B(sb-paclitaxel) showed significant improvement only from 6 months to 12 months (p = 0.001); improvements from baseline to 6 months (p = 0.07) and baseline to 12 months (p = 0.067) did not reach statistical significance.

The proportion of patients reporting any sexual activity (Fig. 7) (Q56) increased numerically in both groups over time, from 11/38 (28.9%) to 20/38 (52.6%) in Group A and from 10/36 (27.8%) to 20/36 (55.6%) in Group B; however, no formal statistical testing was performed for this binary outcome. Sexual functioning scores were comparable between groups at baseline (28.0 ± 8.6 vs. 27.5 ± 4.0; p = 0.86), 6 months (30.4 ± 6.8 vs. 31.2 ± 3.7; p = 0.63), and 12 months (29.6 ± 7.4 vs. 31.2 ± 3.7, p = 0.67), with no significant intergroup differences at any time point. Within Group B, sexual functioning improved significantly from baseline to 6 and 12 months (p = 0.02), while no significant within-group change was observed in Group A.

Discussion

To our knowledge, this represents the first prospective, head-to-head, longitudinal comparison of patient-reported HRQoL between nab-paclitaxel-carboplatin and sb-paclitaxel-carboplatin as first-line chemotherapy in ovarian carcinoma. In the parent efficacy analysis of the same cohort, no statistically significant differences in progression-free or overall survival were observed between the two treatment arms. Across seven EORTC QLQ-OV28 domains and HDRS assessed at three timepoints over 12 months, the two regimens produced broadly comparable QoL trajectories.

The similar gastrointestinal symptom trajectories observed with nab-paclitaxel-carboplatin and solvent-based paclitaxel-carboplatin indicate that the paclitaxel formulation does not appear to differentially influence gastrointestinal recovery during first-line treatment. This pattern is consistent with evidence from Lee et al., who demonstrated that effective platinum-taxane chemotherapy progressively reduces GI symptom burden through tumour control and ascites resolution. (Lee et al., 2022) The equivalent trajectories in both arms suggest that the formulation vehicle Cremophor EL versus albumin-bound does not meaningfully differentiate GI outcomes at the 175 mg/m2 dose schedule used here (Scripture et al., 2005).

Peripheral neuropathy scores showed no statistically significant between-group difference at any timepoint, though the borderline p-values at baseline and 12 months numerically favoured Group A(nab-paclitaxel). Both arms reached near-complete neuropathy resolution by 12 months, with highly significant within-group improvement from 6 to 12 months in both groups. This pattern aligns with the dose-dependency reported by Gradishar et al., who found that while nab-paclitaxel at 260 mg/m2 increases neuropathy risk, its neurotoxic profile is equivalent to solvent-based paclitaxel at the 175 mg/m2 dose used in this study (Gradishar et al., 2005).

Supporting data from the CA002-0LD trial further confirms that this 175 mg/m2 schedule mitigates severe (Grade 3/4) neuropathy. Therefore, our results do not support a claim of differential neurotoxicity between formulations at this dose; rather, they suggest that long-term recovery is driven by standardized dose intensity rather than the specific drug delivery system (Tsurutani et al., 2021).

The composite “Other chemotherapy side effects” domain was the only QoL domain demonstrating a statistically significant difference between treatment groups. This finding is biologically plausible given the established pharmacological differences between nab-paclitaxel and solvent-based paclitaxel, including the absence of Cremophor EL and the lack of routine corticosteroid premedication with nab-paclitaxel. (Tsurutani et al., 2021; Sparreboom et al., 2005) However, the magnitude of the observed difference was small and should be interpreted cautiously in view of the established thresholds for clinical relevance.

The absence of a difference in hormonal and menopausal symptoms between treatment groups is biologically expected, as neither formulation has a direct effect on the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis (Wu et al., 2022).

Body image scores were low at baseline in both groups and remained so throughout follow-up, with no significant within-group or between-group changes at any timepoint. This is a notable finding: despite the well-documented impact of alopecia and weight change on body image in cancer patients receiving taxane-based chemotherapy (Cella et al., 2003) both groups in this cohort reported minimal body image disturbance throughout. The reasons for the uniformly low body image scores in this cohort are unclear; floor effects at baseline leaving limited room for further deterioration may partly explain the absence of within-group change (Brundage et al., 2012; Zhao et al., 2022).

Neither taxane formulation appeared to confer a measurable advantage in recovery of sexual functioning over 12 months. This finding is clinically reassuring and is consistent with previous reports demonstrating that recovery in sexual health following ovarian cancer treatment is influenced by multiple physical and psychosocial factors rather than chemotherapy formulation alone (Domenici et al., 2016; Kim et al., 2015). The clinically meaningful increase in sexual activity in both groups likely reflects recovery in physical comfort, reduction in treatment-related fatigue, and psychosocial adaptation over time. The modest mean score change despite improved activity rates is attributable to the conditional scoring structure of EORTC QLQ-OV28 Q57–Q58, which captures responses only from sexually active patients, thereby limiting the sensitivity of the scale mean to participation-level changes.

The comparable improvement in depressive symptoms observed with both treatment regimens suggests that psychological recovery is unlikely to differ according to paclitaxel formulation, consistent with reported rates of clinically significant depressive symptoms in 20–40% of ovarian cancer patients during the first year of treatment (Santi et al., 2023). Group A(nab-paclitaxel) showed a transient non-significant rise at 6 months before significant improvement by 12 months, while Group B(sb-paclitaxel) demonstrated a steady decline reaching significance by 12 months. This trajectory in Group A(nab-paclitaxel) may reflect the acute psychological adjustment burden during active chemotherapy fatigue, uncertainty, and side-effect distress which resolves as treatment concludes and disease response becomes apparent. The pattern is consistent with the previous observation of dynamic change in depression scores across the chemotherapy trajectory in ovarian cancer. Both groups ultimately achieved equivalent and meaningful psychological improvement by 12 months, underscoring the value of serial psychological assessment and timely psychosocial support during treatment (Yeoh et al., 2024; Meraner et al., 2012).

Strengths of the study include its prospective randomized design, uniform chemotherapy protocols in both groups, and longitudinal assessment of quality of life and psychological outcomes at multiple predefined timepoints over 12 months. Additionally, the study reflects real-world clinical practice in a tertiary care setting with a well-defined and relatively homogeneous patient population, enhancing the internal validity of the findings.

The primary limitations of this study are its open-label design; blinded administration is not feasible for this formulation comparison but represents a methodological limitation that cannot be fully eliminated. No formal sample size calculation was performed for this exploratory secondary analysis. Future trials should stratify randomisation by FIGO stage, menopausal status, and surgical debulking status to better isolate formulation effects from disease-load effects. Additionally, the absence of a structured psychosocial support system in our institution during the study period may limit the generalizability of the observed HDRS outcomes to settings where formal psycho-oncology services are available.

Conclusion

This prospective comparative analysis of patient-reported outcomes demonstrates broadly equivalent QoL trajectories between nab-paclitaxel-carboplatin and sb-paclitaxel-carboplatin as first-line chemotherapy in ovarian carcinoma over 12 months. Both regimens showed significant within-group improvements across most domains, reflecting disease response and recovery from active treatment. No significant between-group differences were found in GI symptoms, peripheral neuropathy, hormonal symptoms, body image, sexual functioning, or depression scores at any timepoint. These findings support the comparable QoL profile of nab-paclitaxel-carboplatin as a first-line alternative in ovarian carcinoma, suggesting a possible difference in the composite chemotherapy side-effect domain. Adequately powered, multicentre trials with denser QoL assessment timepoints are needed to confirm these findings.

Ayurvedic management of hemangiomatous ulcer: A case report
Authors: Praveen Kumar Yadav, SD Laxmikant
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe most frequent infantile tumor is a hemangioma, and its most common complication is ulceration. A quickly progressing ulcer called a hemangioma can also result in secondary infection. The mechanism of ulcer formation is unknown but is thought to develop during the rapid growth phase. Infantile hemangiomas are diagnosed clinically, based on the progression of the lesion's history and a clinical examination. Physical interventions (laser surgery, cryosurgery, excision) and systemic corticosteroids or beta-blockers are the usual course of treatment; all carry the potential for substantial side effects. A two-month young female baby approached us, with a diagnosis of hemangiomatous ulcer. Skin grafting was advised by a plastic surgeon, but the baby's parents approached for Ayurveda treatment. The case was treated with oral medicine bilwadi agada and external treatments vranaprakshalana with triphala kwatha and jatyadi taila local application for 2 months. There was complete healing of the ulcer.

Introduction

Based on endothelial cell kinetics, hemangiomas are congenital vascular malformations that go through a phase of fast growth followed by a sluggish progression [1]. About 4–5% of Caucasian children develop infantile hemangioma (IH), a benign endothelium tumor [2]. Up to 12% of newborns can develop IH within their first year of life, making it the most prevalent vascular tumor of infancy [3]. Preterm newborns had a higher incidence (23%) [1], and females are afflicted more frequently than males in a ratio of 3:1 [4]. Although they can sometimes be seen before birth, they are more frequently noticed in the first two months of life. They are identified by an area of pallor or an erythematous or blue mark, which later evolves into a fine telangiectatic pattern before the expansion phase. An ulceration of the perineum or lip may occasionally be the presenting symptom [5]. IH usually occurs in a single person (80%), and it affects the head and neck (60%), trunk (25%), or extremities (15%) [2]. They often appear soon after birth, go through a quick phase of proliferation (growth phase), which can last up to 12 months, and then slowly involute (regression phase) over a period of years, which can last up to 10 years [[6], [7], [8]]. They are frequently referred to as "strawberry marks" because of their clinical look. IHs frequently affect the head and neck and may cause local tissue damage, ulcers, infections, bleeding, functional effects, and deformity [9]. The most frequent tumor in infancy is a hemangioma, and its most frequent consequence is ulceration [10]. The majority are minor facial cutaneous vascular abnormalities, but they can potentially be big, debilitating lesions with grave implications [11]. Extracutaneous problems are a significant risk for infants with large hemangiomas, especially those with a segmental distribution or hemangiomatosis. They may also be linked to other congenital anomalies such as PHACE [12], PELVIS, or SACRAL syndrome [13,14], as well as other conditions. The majority of hemangiomas do not require treatment, but about 10% of them result in considerable disability and 1% are life-threatening due to their location. Hemangiomas near the airway can restrict the airway, while those near the eyes can impair eyesight. A frequent consequence that might result in subsequent infection is ulceration [15]. Despite proper wound care, ulcerated IHs frequently heal slowly, resulting in pain, bleeding, infection, disruption of sleep and feeding, and disfiguring scarring [16]. Conservative approaches are advised because IH can be unharmful, but in a small percentage of patients, therapeutic intervention is needed when problems such as blockage, bleeding, ulceration, or superinfection happen [17]. Approximately 4% more treatment is required and 5% more complications are likely for every 10 cm2 increase in lesion size. Pharmacologic (topical or systemic), surgical, or laser procedures are among the therapeutic options for complicated IHs [18] (see Fig. 1, Fig. 2, Fig. 3, Fig. 4).

In Ayurveda, a hemangiomatous ulcer can be taken under pitta-kaphaja dushtavrana as the ulcer was rapidly progressive, with reddish surrounding tissue, and thick jelly-like discharge. We present a case of the hemangiomatous ulcer which was initially tried to treat with oral ofloxacin and metronidazole syrup but the ulcer did not heal and hence was suggested for skin grafting by an allopathic surgeon. However, the parents denied grafting and approached us for conservative Ayurveda treatment. The patient had external treatment for two months, using triphala kwatha to clean the wounds, and local treatment for two months, using jatyadi taila to clean and heal the wound. The patient was also given oral medication, bilwadi agada. There was complete healing of the ulcer after two months.

Patient information

We present a case of a 2-month-old, 4-kg, full-term female infant, the daughter of middle-class income parents (businessman father and nursing staff mother), with a non-contributory prenatal and family history who was delivered in a district hospital in Nepal and who had a typical adjustment to an extra-uterine living environment but experienced ten to fifteen episodes of loose stools per day since birth. She was discharged after the third postpartum day without experiencing any clinical recurrences after her in-hospital physical examinations and stool tests were unremarkable. The newborn's mother noticed a red lesion in the left gluteal area at around day fifteen of life that wasn't there at birth. She put protective barrier cream on the lesion twice a day for the next few days, but the extent of the lesion and the erythema around it grew bigger. For this reason, the parents took their newborn to a pediatric surgeon in a large hospital when she was 34 days old when the lesion was initially noticed and the child was admitted. Based on the results of the wound swab culture and sensitivity tests, the surgeon recommended a ten-day treatment of oral metronidazole and ofloxacin, and daily povidone-iodine dressings before applying mupirocin ointment. However, the wound didn't heal; instead, it got bigger and developed erythema. She was eventually discharged from the hospital on 41 days of her life and advised to contact a plastic surgeon. The plastic surgeon advised her for skin grafting. Then her parents approached us for conservative Ayurveda treatment.

Clinical findings

The neonate was moderately built with good nutrition. A general examination revealed a pulse rate of 110 bpm, and a temperature of 98.40 F. Systemic examination, i.e., the central nervous system, cardiovascular system, and respiratory system were found normal. However, when her diaper and left gluteal region were moved, she became agitated and uncomfortable, and her parents also reported that she had been having 10 to 15 bouts of loose stools each day since birth.

Diagnostic assessments

The diagnosis was established clinically as hemangiomatous ulcer as per contemporary science and pittakapha pradhana dushtavrana as per Ayurveda.

Therapeutic intervention

The treatment was planned based on the line of pittakapha dushtavrana principles. Vranaprakshalana with Triphala kwatha and Syrup Bonnisan as oral medication was prescribed. Follow-up and outcomes

After 1 month of treatment, the wound was healing but the diarrhea did not get controlled. Then the baby was advised to take Bilwadi agada 125 mg per day in three divided doses with mother's milk for 15 days in place of syrup bonnisan and diarrhea got controlled. The vranaprakshalana with triphala kwatha and jatyadi taila pichu was continued for 2 months. After 2 months of treatment, the ulcer got healed completely and the jatyadi taila local application was done for 15 days more. There were no adverse effects of the medicines observed during the intervention period. The patient is leading a good quality of life with no ulcer or bowel issues.

Discussion

The most frequent benign soft-tissue tumor in children is IH, which often multiplies in the first year of life and then spontaneously involutes over months or years [19]. Intervention is typically not required. Therapy is recommended for life-threatening or functional consequences, such as ulceration, permanent deformity, obstruction of the oral, nasal, or auditory orifices, or bleeding, as well as for reducing psychosocial stress and avoiding surgery that might leave scars [20]. The ulcer was diagnosed as Pitta-kaphaja dushta vrana as per Ayurveda.

Because it is kaphapittaghna, kushthahara, mehahar, vranaropaka, kledanashaka, etc., Triphala Kashaya was employed for Vranaprakshalana [21]. Triphala's alcohol extract has demonstrated in vitro antibacterial efficacy against microorganisms that cause wounds, including Staphylococcus aureus, Pseudomonas aeruginosa, and Streptococcus pyogenes [22]. Studies have verified a number possible uses of Triphala, which include free radical scavenging, anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, immunomodulating, appetite stimulation, gastric hyperacidity reduction, dental caries prevention, antipyretic, analgesic, antibacterial, antimutagenic, wound healing, anti-cariogenic, antistress, adaptogenic, hypoglycemic, anticancer, hepatoprotective, chemoprotective, radioprotective, and chemopreventive effects [23]. Triphala administration reduced the production of inflammatory mediators (including TNF, IL-1, IL-6, MCP-1, VEGF, NO, and PGE2), intracellular free radicals, inflammatory enzymes (including iNOS and COX-2), and lysosomal enzyme release in lipopolysaccharide-stimulated macrophages [24]. Triphala contains chebulagic acid, which has been shown to inhibit COX and 5-LOX, two key enzymes implicated in inflammation and carcinogenesis [25]. The formula's main ingredients include tannins, gallic acid, ellagic acid, and chebulinic acid, which are strong antioxidants and may be responsible for some of the formula's immunomodulatory effects [26,27].

Jatyadi taila (JT) is made up of the herbs Jati (Myristica fragrans Houtt), Kushtha (Saussurea lappa CB Clarke), Padmaka (Prunus puddum Roxb Ex. Wall), and Daruhardira (Berberis aristate DC). It has qualities that are kleda shoshana (drying of exudates), tikta (bitter) rasa (taste), kashaya (astringent), lekhana (scraping), etc. [28]. The presence of tannins and phytosterols in JT material speeds up the pace of epithelization, which in turn increases the capillary formation and fibroblast proliferation [29]. When employing JT, significant outcomes for granulation tissue were seen. The raktadhatu is strengthened by Manjishta (Rubia cordifolia Linn) and Sariva (Hemidesmus indicus R. Br), which also increase circulation [30]. The katuka (Picrorhiza kurroa) found in the JT promotes neovascularization, re-epithelialization, fibroblast, dermal myofibroblast, and endothelial cell migration into the wound bed [31]. In vitro and in vivo wound studies, tuttha (CuSO4) stimulates angiogenic responses, aiding in the quick granulation tissue filling of wound regions [32]. Initially, Bonnisan syrup was utilized because it has digestive properties that aid in improved food absorption and utilization. Bilwadi agada was used because it is indicated in visuchika, ajeerna, garavisha, etc [33]. A powerful anti-fungal and anti-microbial agent is bilwadi agada [34].

Conclusion

A hemangiomatous ulcer is the most common complication of infantile hemangioma that needs proper evaluation of the symptoms and management. The ulcer can be treated with a conservative Ayurvedic approach under the treatment principles of dushtavrana. The treatment chosen has produced positive effects and enhanced the patient's quality of life. On the other hand, to provide safety and results that are supported by the available evidence, case series, and randomized controlled trials are needed.

Patient's perspective

The patient's parents were thrilled with the outcome of the treatment, and they now trust Ayurveda. The therapy has raised the patient's quality of life. Overall, the child's parents expressed satisfaction with the nonsurgical approach and expressed appreciation for it.

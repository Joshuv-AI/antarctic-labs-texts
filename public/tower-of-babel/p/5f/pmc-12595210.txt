Microcargos with Megaeffects: A Literature Review of Immunomodulatory, Diagnostic, Prognostic, and Therapeutic Dimensions of Exosomal MicroRNAs in Glioblastoma Multiforme
Authors: Amirhossein Kamroo, Arash Esmaeili, Kiana Tahan, Milad Shafizadeh, Nima Rezaei
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractGlioblastoma multiforme (GBM) is a complex and aggressive central nervous system (CNS) tumor that has a poor prognosis, and restricted therapeutic options are available despite the increasing research conducted. Moreover, the cells in our body package microRNAs, ubiquitous modulators of numerous biological processes, into exosomes for cell-to-cell signaling. Indeed, exosomal miRNAs contribute to several aspects of glioma, such as development, occurrence, metastasis, and immune evasion. Additionally, exosomal miRNAs play a key role in cellular functions and glioma pathogenesis by regulating numerous pathways, including the Wnt/β-catenin, PTEN/PI3K/Akt, EGFR/MAPK, notch signaling, and NF-κB. Notably, exosomal miRNAs are recognized to have promising potential in clinical applications; in fact, exosomal miRNAs are emerging biomarkers for glioma diagnosis and prognosis and are additionally considered as putative therapeutic candidates by inhibiting tumor progression, occurrence, and metastasis. This review presents the current knowledge regarding clinical potential and application of exosomal miRNAs in glioma, as well as the miRNA-mediated regulatory network underlying glioma immunopathogenesis.Graphical AbstractDifferent aspects of exosomal microRNA (miRNA) in the diagnosis, prognosis, and therapy of glioblastoma multiforme (GBM). Exosomal miRNAs play a multifaceted role in the diagnosis of GBM, predicting the survival outcome of GBM, and as innovative therapeutic measures for GBM. Additionally, exosomal miRNAs are involved in the immunomodulation of GBM, inducing an immunosuppressive status in microglia, macrophages, and myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs). Background

Glioma is a primary brain neoplasm that develops from glial cells. In fact, there are different types of gliomas dependent on the affected cell, like oligodendroglioma, oligoastrocytoma, and astrocytoma. As reported by the World Health Organization (WHO), astrocytoma is categorized as grade I to grade IV, ranging from benign to malignant subtypes (Pierpaolo Peruzzi 2024). One class of grade IV diffuse astrocytoma is glioblastoma multiforme (GBM) (Aldape et al. 2015). GBM is the most widespread primary brain neoplasm in adults (Tan et al. 2020), responsible for about 14 percent of brain tumors and about 51 percent of malignant tumors (Ostrom et al. 2023). GBM cells spread rapidly and uncontrollably, resulting in diffuse infiltration across the healthy adjacent brain tissue, which makes a complete resection impossible. Moreover, the innate heterogeneous GBM tumor tissues promote tumor proliferation and resistance to common therapeutic options (Vieira de Castro et al. 2020; Zhang and Liu 2020). Despite different interventions such as resection, radiotherapy, or chemotherapy, the prognosis of the disease remains poor, with less than 2 years of survival (Brodbelt et al. 2015).

The tumor microenvironment (TME) is a pivotal element in tumorigenesis and treatment failure. TME accelerates the diffusion of GBM tumor cells to the adjacent tissues through different mechanisms, such as angiogenesis and cytokine discharge, which exacerbates the GBM’s invasive nature (Polyak et al. 2009). The micro-atmosphere of TME includes an array of cellular and molecular items other than neoplastic elements; the infiltrated immune cells of glioma-associated macrophages/microglia (GAMs) are considered as cellular contents, while extra-matrix-associated components and water-soluble particles represent molecular contents (Chen and Hambardzumyan 2018). Added to GAMs and extra-matric components, extracellular vesicles (EVs), the nanocarriers for cargo delivery, have gained attention in the context of TME (Yekula et al. 2020). Cellular–exosomal–molecular arrangement of the TME contributes to the highly immunosuppressive traits of GBM and is the reason behind the numerous failed immunotherapeutic approaches to this malignancy (Huang et al. 2020).

Extracellular vesicles (EVs) have garnered attention as small secreted organelles that play a key role in normal physiological functions. Discovery of EVs took place in the late 1960s; they were initially identified as “cell dusts” budding from chondrocytes and platelets that, respectively, contributed to the formation of hydroxyapatite crystals and blood clots (Pegtel and Gould 2019). EVs are single-membrane vesicles delivered by almost all cells and are classified into different categories, but the majority are categorized as ectosomes and exosomes. Ectosomes consist of vesicles 50 nm to 1 μm in diameter that are invaginated from the membrane (Kalluri and LeBleu 2020), whereas exosomes originate from the endosome and multivesicular bodies (Zhang and Yu 2019) and mostly display spherical morphology under cryo-electron microscopy with a size range of 30 to 200 nm (Pegtel and Gould 2019). The exosomal cargo contains significant biological substances, namely proteins, lipids, glycoconjugates, and nucleic acids, e.g., microRNAs (miRNA) that are pivotal in autocrine and paracrine signaling, and can vastly affect the extracellular matrix and the adjacent cells (Amiral 2021; Pegtel and Gould 2019; Sharma et al. 2020; Zhang and Yu 2019); concerning this knowledge, there is a mounting evidence suggesting the regulatory mechanisms of exosomes in pathological conditions, especially in neoplasia (Amiral 2021). Neoplastic cells discharge a greater amount of exosomes compared to healthy cells (Zhang and Yu 2019); the discharged exosomes, in turn, induce the formation, growth, and metastasis of cancer (Sharma et al. 2020). For instance, the invasiveness of tumors is considered to be linked to the acidic or hypoxic tumor microenvironment created by tumor-derived exosomes containing cytokines like IL-1β (Sharma et al. 2020). Exosomes can also result in drug resistance by manipulating the cellular function of neighboring cells (Kalluri and LeBleu 2020).

Although being involved in the deterioration of malignancy, exosomes have beneficial roles that promise an evolution in theranostic advances. They contribute to angiogenesis, tissue repair, and tissue remodeling (Amiral 2021), neural communication, antigen presentation, development of organs, and reproductive efficacy (He et al. 2018). Exosomes can prolong the half-life of their cargoes by decreasing the chance of their clearance in biological fluids. Thus, these nanoparticles can be applied as promising tools to administer drugs and circumvent the blood–brain barrier (BBB) (He et al. 2018; Zhang and Yu 2019; Karami Fath et al. 2022). Moreover, clinical trials have tested the potential utilization of exosomes as prognostic biomarkers and suggested the promising therapeutic potential (Zhang and Yu 2019).

Having said the diverse biological processes mediated via exosomal cargos, noncoding RNAs (ncRNAs), especially miRNAs, are among the abundant contents of Exosomal cargos (Tang et al. 2021). About 75% of the human genome is transcribed into RNA, yet only 3% is transcribed into protein-coding mRNAs (Yan and Bu 2021). MiRNAs are short 22-nucleotide ncRNAs that have a robust role in gene expression at the mRNA level. Indeed, miRNAs target specific regions of mRNA called untranslated regions (UTR), leading to mRNA degradation and post-transcriptional gene silencing (Pietrzykowski 2010; Saliminejad et al. 2019). Moreover, studies have suggested that exosomes orchestrate tumor proliferation and pathogen evasion by regulating gene expression mostly via miRNA cargos and thereby inducing cancer progression (Li et al. 2021a). Two main mechanisms have been recognized in miRNAs' action: 1. regulating gene expression in targeted genes and 2. activating immune response by binding as ligands to toll-like receptors (TLRs) (Elewaily and Elsergany 2021). Furthermore, exosomal miRNAs modulate immune response by regulating macrophage polarization, their phenotypic shift, and acting through pro-inflammatory mediators such as Interleukin (IL)-8 (Li et al. 2021a).

This review article will explain the role of glioma-derived exosomal miRNAs in immunoregulation and open new avenues to the diagnostic and therapeutic approaches of glioma.

Exosome Biogenesis

Exosome biogenesis has been widely investigated to enlighten its intricate molecular dynamics. The generation of exosomes embarks primarily with the formation of an early-sorting endosome (ESE) (Fig. 1), a concave vesicle with tubular and vacuolar domains (Essandoh and Fan 2015). The formation of ESEs and cargo loading is facilitated by the trans golgi network, the endoplasmic reticulum, and the mitochondria (Kalluri and LeBleu 2020). ESE invaginates from the plasma membrane and contains proteins from both the membrane and the extracellular fluid. Initial invagination of endosomes is either dependent on clathrin and its interaction with a GTPase protein, dynamin2, or clathrin-independent endocytosis relying on mechanisms like caveolae, “ADP-ribosylation factor 1” (Arf1), and flotillin-1 (Essandoh and Fan 2015). ESEs then turn into late-sorting endosomes (LSEs) and subsequently form multivesicular bodies (MVBs) (Fig. 1). The endosomal membrane of MVB is invaginated to develop into intraluminal vesicles (ILVs), also known as exosomes (Kalluri and LeBleu 2020; Essandoh and Fan 2015). From this point onward, MVBs could encounter different pathways: They either disintegrate and get recycled by fusing with lysosomes or autophagosomes, or merge with the plasma membrane to secrete ILVs as exosomes (Kalluri and LeBleu 2020; Wei et al. 2021b). The cholesterol level within MVBs has a pivotal role in the fate of endosomal bodies. High levels of cholesterol are sensed by an “oxysterol binding protein” (OSBP) like ORP1L, which is vital for exosome movement along microtubules and thus secretion (Pfrieger and Vitale 2018), whereas below threshold levels of cholesterol result in lysosomal degradation (Doyle and Wang 2019; Hessvik and Llorente 2018). “Apoptosis-linked gene 2-interacting protein X” (ALIX), an “endosomal sorting complexes required for transport” (ESCRT)-associated protein rich in exosomes, appears essential for balancing cholesterol content within MVBs (Pfrieger and Vitale 2018). Marked MVBs, determined to be released as exosomes, can be secreted intrinsically by the trans Golgi network (Fig. 1), or they can be influenced by regulatory mechanisms to transport and release them as ILVs (Lai et al. 2022). ILV trafficking to the plasma membrane can be modulated by various factors, including cytoskeletal molecules of actin, myosin, and microtubules and Rab GTPase (Rab27 and Rab35) (Yue et al. 2020; Xu et al. 2023; Lv et al. 2020; Pfeffer 2010). Further fusion and discharge of exosomes are mediated via SNARE, syntaxin-4, SNAP-23, and VAMP-7 proteins to form a complex that guarantees the accuracy of the process (Fig. 1) (Liu et al. 2023a; Krylova and Feng 2023). In contrast, the activation of mTORC1 may inhibit exosome release by negatively regulating docking and fusion processes (Zou et al. 2019). Also, Ca2+ concentration is closely associated with exosome trafficking and release (Xu et al. 2023; Messenger et al. 2018). Exosomes are generated by almost all cell types and have been isolated from several body fluids, including plasma, serum, malignant ascites, lymph, bile, and gastric acid (Doyle and Wang 2019; Keller et al. 2011, 2007; Zanganeh et al. 2023). Ultimately, exosome uptake happens through direct membrane fusion and juxtacrine signaling, phagocytosis, macropinocytosis, or receptor-mediated endocytosis (McKelvey et al. 2015) (Fig. 2).

ESCRT-dependent/-independent overview. In the ESCRT-dependent pathway, the ESCRT machinery cooperates to sort monoubiquitinated cargo into endosomal regions abundant in PI3P in a stepwise manner. ESCRT-0 and its helper proteins STAM and HRS initiate recognition, after which ESCRT-I and -II are sequentially recruited. The assembly of these components, facilitated by TSG101 subunit, shapes and cleaves the budding vesicles inside endosomes. ESCRT-III is later recruited by CHMP4 and, with the assistance of the Vps4 ATPase, drives membrane scission. Ultimately, the machinery is then disassembled, and deubiquitinating enzymes are employed to remove ubiquitin tags from the sorted cargo. ESCRT-independent pathway refers to mechanisms regulated by lipid-raft microdomains containing ceramides, tetraspanin family proteins, ALIX–syndecan–syntenin complex, and GTPases such as Rab proteins. These pathways operate as alternatives to ESCRT-dependent pathways and may be context dependent. For instance, under hypoxic conditions in the tumor microenvironment (TME), ESCRT-independent pathways are often amplified

Formation of early endosome (ESE), 3. Intraluminal Vesicles Formation, 4. Cargo in and out, 5. Plasma membrane transportation, 6. Exocytosis and fuse with the membrane. ESCRT Complex, the Regulatory Machinery Behind Exosome Formation

The pathways regarding exosome generation and regulation of cargo sorting are categorized into classic and direct pathways (Wei et al. 2021b). The classic pathway is largely dependent on ESCRTs, composed of four compact subunits, ESCRT-0, ESCRT-I, ESCRT-II, and ESCRT-III, along with associated proteins like ALIX, Tsg101, STAM1, CHMP4, VTA1, and the AAA ATPase Vps4 complex (Yue et al. 2020; Hessvik and Llorente 2018; Li et al. 2018). ALIX protein has diverse applications in exosome biogenesis. Indeed, it interacts with Tsg101 and CHMP4 and acts as a bridge between ESCRT-I and ESCRT-III, thereby assisting in the formation and scission of endosomal membrane budding (Zhang et al. 2019; Odorizzi 2006). Modules of ESCRT-0, STAM, and HRS complex are in charge of recognizing and sorting the monoubiquitinated proteomic cargo into particular regions abundant in phosphatidylinositol-3-phosphate (PIP3) (Schmidt and Teis 2012; Gurung et al. 2021). This process leads to a cascade of reactions that assemble other parts of the ESCRT machinery. HRS recruits the TSG101 component of ESCRT-I, which then recruits ESCRT-II and ultimately ends in recruitment and oligomerization of the CHMP4 subunit of ESCRT-III (Schmidt and Teis 2012; Colombo et al. 2013). Tightly associated complexes of ESCRT-I and ESCRT-II create stable neck buds to initiate the deformation of the endosomal membrane (Yue et al. 2020). Further cleavage of the membranous neck and the scission of ILVs (Zhang et al. 2019) are mediated by the dome-shaped filaments formed by ESCRT-III (Boura et al. 2012). ESCRT-III is reorganized and powered by the Vps4 protein through ATP hydrolysis (Alonso Y Adell et al. 2016). Finally, the ESCRT machinery recruits deubiquitinating enzymes to unload the ubiquitin from cargo proteins (Urbanelli et al. 2013). ALIX protein has diverse applications in exosome biogenesis. Indeed, it interacts with Tsg101 and CHMP4 and acts as a bridge between ESCRT-I and ESCRT-III, thereby assisting in the formation and scission of endosomal membrane budding (Zhang et al. 2019; Odorizzi 2006).

ESCRT-Independent Pathways Involved in Exosome Formation

The multifaceted role of ESCRT complex in exosome biogenesis has been well established, as an increasing number of studies specifically explore its involvement in glioblastoma-derived exosomes. However, studies have found that the complete absence of ESCRT components does not entirely block the process but instead reduces and alters the molecular dynamics of ILV production..Consequently, this observation has led to the proposal of an alternative regulatory pathway for exosome biogenesis, namely an ESCRT-independent mechanism. The ESCRT-independent mechanism is regulated by lipid-raft microdomains containing ceramides, tetraspanin family proteins, ALIX–syndecan–syntenin complex, and GTPases such as Rab proteins (Babst 2011; Wei et al. 2021b; Zhang et al. 2019). The current evidence has suggested that the ceramides generated predominantly through nSMase2 activity play an essential role in exosome formation by inducing its membrane curvature and sorting of proteolipids (PLPs) into ILVs (Elsherbini and Bieberich 2018; Trajkovic et al. 2008). Tetraspanins, a small integral supergroup of proteins enriched in MVBs, form specific membrane domains known as tetraspanin-enriched microdomains (TEMs) (Andreu and Yáñez-Mó 2014). In addition, the tetraspanin module, comprising CD9, CD63, CD81, and CD82, participates in the induction of membrane curvature, stabilization of exosome structure, regulation of cargo sequestration, release of ILVs, and their adhesion to targeted cells (Andreu and Yáñez-Mó 2014; Dey et al. 2023; Pols and Klumperman 2009; Hessvik and Llorente 2018; Böker et al. 2018; Dogrammatzis et al. 2019; Bahadorani et al. 2024; Zhang et al. 2019; Perez-Hernandez et al. 2013). Conversely, “tetraspanin-6” (TSPN6) directs the fate of late endosomes toward lysosomal degradation by engaging with the ALIX–syndecan–syntenin complex (Ghossoub et al. 2020). The ALIX–syndecan–syntenin complex is recognized to enhance exosome formation. Syntenin, the cytosolic domain of syndecan proteoglycans, binds directly with ALIX and links ESCRT and syndecans. By this means, this complex aids in the regulation of exosomal cargo budding and selection (Baietti et al. 2012; Friand et al. 2015). The third group of biomolecules involved in the ESCRT-independent mechanism is Rab GTPases. Rab GTPases have been recognized as key factors in exosome biogenesis. Indeed, Rab5 facilitates clathrin-mediated early endosome formation and cargo sorting. Rab5 transition to Rab7 drives the formation of late endosomes. Rab7 orchestrates the lysosomal degradation of late endosomes, whereas Rab31 prevents it. Rab11, Rab27, and Rab35 are other Rab elements involved in ILVs’ docking and trafficking to the plasma membrane (Wei et al. 2021a; Blanc and Vidal 2018; Xu et al. 2024). The fourth group of biomolecules involved in the ESCRT-independent mechanism is Rab GTPases. Rab GTPases have been recognized as key factors in exosome biogenesis, orchestrating endosome formation and cargo sorting, determining degradation or preservation of late endosomes, and controlling the docking and trafficking of vesicles to the plasma membrane. Indeed, Rab5 facilitates clathrin-mediated early endosome formation and cargo sorting.

The importance of ESCRT-independent pathways is even more pronounced in tumor microenvironments, as their role is amplified under hypoxic conditions (Kumar and Deep 2020). Given the notable presence of ESCRT-independent components in exosomal cargo, a probable function of the ESCRT-independent mechanism in the biogenesis of glioblastoma-derived exosomes may be proposed. In glioblastoma, these routes have been implicated in promoting tumorigenesis, involving factors like syntenin-1 (Pintor-Romero et al. 2023), Rab32 (Chen et al. 2023), and tetraspanin-6 (Sa et al. 2024). Thus, both mechanisms likely cooperate complementarily and contextually, with hypoxia potentially biasing toward an ESCRT-independent biogenesis (Di Giulio et al. 2024). As previously mentioned, a pivotal factor in this pathway is ceramide, generated by neutral sphingomyelinase (nSMase) (Dai et al. 2024). Xu et al. investigated the pharmacological inhibition of nSMase2 using GW4869 in glioma models and demonstrated that GW4869 treatment was associated with decreased tumor size and improved survival (Xu et al. 2021). Evidence from oligodendroglial cells also indicates that exosome secretion can be independent to ESCRT proteins like TSG101 and ALIX, and relies on ceramide and its synthesizing enzymes (Whitehead et al. 2019). Taken together, these findings underscore that the ESCRT-independent pathway, particularly ceramide-mediated, could serve as a complementary route for exosome biogenesis in glioblastoma. Nonetheless, studies are far from conclusive, and further research is needed to establish the relative contribution of ESCRT-independent versus ESCRT-dependent pathways in glioma-derived exosomes and clarify their respective significance. Crafting Exosome Cargo and Mechanisms of Sequestration

The subject of exosome composition has been widely explored, as cargo analysis seems to indicate exosomes’ potential as promising biomarkers and therapeutic agents. Exosomes carry an broad variety of proteins, RNAs, and lipids that reflect their origin cells. An all-inclusive database of exosome cargo is compiled in accessible online databases like ExoCarta (Keerthikumar et al. 2016), exoRBase (Li et al. 2017), and ExMdb (Qi et al. 2024).

Proteomic Cargo

Packaging of proteins into MVBs occurs through post-translational modifications (PTMs). The attachment of small polypeptide chains to targeted proteins, such as ubiquitin (ubiquitination), SUMO (SUMOylation), NEDD8 (NEDDylation), and ISG15 (ISGylation), selectively adjusts them to their destination and function. Other mechanisms, including phosphorylation, N-linked protein glycosylation with mannose and polylactosamine, citrullination of positively charged arginine amino acids, and oxidation, contribute to the localization and sorting of specific proteins. Similar to the previously explained biogenesis mechanisms, the sequestration of ubiquitinated cargo is mediated via the ESCRT machinery or independent pathways such as tetraspanins (CD81, CD63) and lipid-raft milieu (Anand et al. 2019). Proteomics analysis has proven protein-rich exosomes to be associated with lipid rafts such as integrins or tetraspanins, adhesion molecules like CAMs, ESCRT, transmembrane glycoproteins associated with lysosome (LAMP-1 and 2B), the SNARE complex, molecules orchestrating membrane fusion (flotillins, annexins, and Rab GTPases), chaperones like heat shock proteins (hsp60, 70, and 90), the major histocompatibility complex (MHC), cytoskeletal molecules, and proteins mediating signaling pathways (β-catenin, Wnt5B, Delta-like 4, etc.) (Choi et al. 2015; Urbanelli et al. 2013; Gurung et al. 2021; Pegtel and Gould 2019; Wei et al. 2021b; Yue et al. 2020). Moreover, some cargo proteins are specifically secreted by origin cells to fulfill certain roles. Indeed, a proteomic study concerning glioblastoma exosomes has identified proteins such as vimentin, “programmed cell death 6-interacting protein” (PDCD6IP), annexin A1 and A2, CD44, and tenascin-c, which enhance tumor progression (Naryzhny et al. 2020).

Nucleomic Cargo

RNAs (mRNAs, ncRNAs like miRNAs, rRNAs, tRNAs, circRNAs, snRNAs, snoRNAs, and piwi-interacting RNAs) and DNA fragments are the prominent nucleic acids found in exosomes (Schageman et al. 2013; Sansone et al. 2017; Pegtel and Gould 2019; Janas et al. 2015). RNA-binding proteins like “heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoproteins” (hnRNPs), “Synaptotagmin-binding cytoplasmic RNA-interacting protein” (SYNCRIP), “Argonaute 2” (Ago2), “Major vault protein” (MVP), and “Y-box binding protein 1” (YBX-1) are among the proposed pathways that incorporate RNAs into exosomes (Chen et al. 2021c). The mechanisms regarding the sequestration of miRNAs involve an active mechanism for essential alterations (Villarroya-Beltri et al. 2014).

Lipidomic Cargo

ILVs' Lipid content depends on the cell of origin. Cholesterol, phosphoglycerides, ceramides, sphingomyelin, glycosphingolipids, and phosphatidylserine are found to be 2–3 times higher on exosome membranes compared with the origin cell (Skotland et al. 2019, 2017). There has been a restricted amount of research to delve deep into lipid loading; however, lipid packaging appears to be is completely different from protein sorting (Chen et al. 2021c; Anand et al. 2019) (Fig. 2).

Birth of MicroRNAs

MicroRNA biogenesis is an intricate process consisting of multiple steps from transcription to miRNA maturation in the cytoplasm; it is classified into canonical and non-canonical pathways (Ali Syeda et al. 2020; Bofill-De Ros and Vang Ørom 2024; O'Brien et al. 2018). MiRNA biogenesis is initiated via the post-transcriptional or co-transcriptional processing of RNA polymerase II/III products. Indeed, half of the miRNAs recognized so far are intragenic, whereas the remaining are intergenic and autonomously processed from exons (O'Brien et al. 2018).

Canonical Pathway

The first step in MiRNA biogenesis initiates with primary miRNA (pri-miRNA) transcription by RNA polymerase II (Ali Syeda et al. 2020). Pri-miRNA transforms into a hairpin-shaped structure, which is recognized by the microprocessor complex consisting of the RNase III enzyme, DROSHA, and its partner protein DGCR8. The microprocessor complex then cleaves the transcripts to produce the hairpin-shaped precursor miRNA (pre-miRNA). The developing pre-miRNA is afterward loaded into the Exportin 5 (XPO5) protein to be translocated from the nucleus to the cytoplasm. Upon entering the cytoplasm, the pre-miRNA is further processed by the DICER enzyme along with its TRBP cofactor; DICER/TRBP complex makes a double-strand cut in the pre-miRNA, where is adjacent to the loop to form the mature form of miRNA along with the complementary strand, often called the passenger strand or miRNA* (Bofill-De Ros and Vang Ørom 2024; Conrad et al. 2014; Medley et al. 2021; O'Brien et al. 2018). The miRNA duplex remains intact and is loaded into Argonaute (AGO) protein with the assistance of chaperone proteins (HSC70/HSP90) (Ali Syeda et al. 2020; Bartel 2018). AGO2 first deforms the miRNA duplex by nicking with the help of slicer activity. Next, RNA helicase, like RNA helicase H and P68, unwinds the RNA duplex (Stavast and Erkeland 2019). Once completed, the newly formed complex is called the pre-RNA-induced silencing (pre-RISC) complex. Pre-RISC complex preserves the mature miRNA strand and expels the passenger strand to ultimately assemble the effector complex named RISC (Iwakawa and Tomari 2022). Moreover, the process by which one strand is designated to be the mature miRNA strand/passenger strand in the formation of the RISC is called strand selection. Strand selection is an intricate process; thermodynamic features of the duplex are identified to have an important role in distinguishing the strands. The strand with the weaker thermodynamic binding at the 5’-end of the duplex is marked as the mature miRNA, while the strand with stronger thermodynamic stability at the 5’-end is ejected (Medley et al. 2021; Meijer et al. 2014). During the assembly of the RISC complex, the miRNA guide strand is segmented into specific regions, called the seed and tail regions; the seed region plays an important role in recognizing and binding to the targeted sequence (Nakanishi 2024). Indeed, the mature RISC binds tightly to the complementary mRNA and consequently interferes with mRNA expression via slicing or destabilizing (Iwakawa and Tomari 2022).

Non-Canonical Pathway

MicroRNAs are proved to be generated through other of the canonical pathways, named non-canonical pathways. Non-canonical microRNA synthesis has also been identified to have a pivotal role in different human diseases, such as cancer. Non-canonical pathways utilize a similar group of proteins applied in the canonical pathway, namely DROSHA, DICER, and AGO2 (Abdelfattah et al. 2014; O'Brien et al. 2018). Non-canonical miRNA biogenesis is classified into 4 groups: 1. miRNA-derived introns (miRtrons), 2. Dicer-Independent miRNAs, 3. small Nucleolar RNAs (snoRNA)-derived miRNAs, 4. tRNA-derived miRNAs (Abdelfattah et al. 2014). Mirtrons are the dominant form of non-canonical miRNAs. Indeed, mirtrons, as their name implies, are miRNAs derived from introns. Microprocessor complex, Drosha/DGCR8, is unable to recognize mirtrons; as a result, mirtrons are processed in a Drosha-independent manner (Salim et al. 2022). The spliced intron product has a lariat structure; indeed, the 3’ branchpoint binds to the 5’ end of the intron. Upon the formation of the lariat, the lariat debranching enzyme 1 (DRB1) modifies the lariat into a pre-miRNA structure, which ultimately enters the canonical pathway and is transported by XPO5 to the cytoplasm (Stavast and Erkeland 2019; Westholm and Lai 2011). Although the Dicer enzyme is considered a multifaceted vital element of miRNA production, miRNAs can be synthesized independently from Dicer: dicer-independent miRNAs. MiRNA-451 is synthesized in a Dicer-independent manner; its synthesis remains refractory to the loss of the Dicer enzyme mainly because of its Ago2-dependent production fashion (Jiang 2020). MiRNA-451 does not follow the typical rules of other miRNAs. For instance, the pre-miR-451 is only 17 base pairs (bp) in length and shorter than the ordinary Dicer enzyme substrates. Besides, mature MiRNA-451 is 23-nucleotide long, which extends from the terminal loop of its hairpin precursor (Yang and Lai 2010). SnoRNAs are another subset of ncRNAs that play an important role in tRNA, mRNA, and especially rRNA modification. SnoRNAs are amply found in the nucleus and are found in many organisms (Huang et al. 2022). DBR1 and other complexes are involved in the biogenesis of snoRNAs (Kim et al. 2000; Webster and Ghalei 2023). Following the initial debranching by DRB1, XRN1/XRN2 shape the mature snoRNA via imposing exonucleolytic activities (Miki and Großhans 2013; Tycowski et al. 1993; Kiss and Filipowicz 1993, 1995). As an example of snoRNAs, human snoRNA (ACA45), processed in a Drosha/Dgcr8 independent manner, targets a potential mRNA, suggesting its role in gene expression (Abdelfattah et al. 2014). The fourth group of non-canonical miRNAs is tRNA-derived small RNAs (tsRNAs), incorporating a subtype known as tRNA fragments (tRFs) (Keam and Hutvagner 2015). TRFs are created by endonucleolytic splitting of precursor/mature tRNA, commonly close by TψC/D-arms (Thompson and Parker 2009; Gebetsberger and Polacek 2013). There are four types of tRFs, namely tRF-5, tRF-3, i-tRF, and tRF-1 (Kuscu et al. 2018). TRF-3 s are identified to be the most abundantly expressed tRNA fragments, and numerous functions have been designated to them. For instance, CU1276 is a tRF-3 member; indeed, CU1276 is synthesized in a DICER-dependent manner as it has a perfect matching length to the DICER enzyme (Maute et al. 2013). Another example is 5'-tiRNAVal, which is a tRF-5 member to mediate breast cancer cell progression. 5'-tiRNAVal is downregulated in breast cancer tissues and exerts a therapeutic role via targeting FZD3 and consequently suppressing the Wnt/β-Catenin pathway (Mo et al. 2019). MiRNAs undergo post-transcriptional modifications before their final designation, leading to a change in their destination. For example, 7-methylguanosine (m7G) methylation in miRNAs' cap can initiate a completely different synthesis pathway (Pandolfini et al. 2019). In addition, m7G-capped pre-miRNAs circumvent the microprocessor complex and are exported to the cytoplasm by Exportin 1 (Exo1). In contrast with other miRNAs, the 3p strand of m7G-capped pre-miRNAs becomes the guide strand and is loaded to AGO 2 (de Mello et al. 2024) (Fig. 3).

Afterbirth

MicroRNAs are modulators of an array of biological processes; in fact, miRNAs exert their effects through two main mechanisms (Hill and Tran 2021): 1. The canonical pathway mediates the expression of roughly 30% of all proteins, regulating at the mRNA level by targeting the 3’ untranslated region (UTR) (Hill and Tran 2021; Quarton et al. 2018), 2. The alternative/non-canonical pathway involves miRNAs targeting ncRNAs.”(Hill and Tran 2021).

Exosomal miRNAs Mediating Glioma-Driving Cascades

Exosomal miRNAs are responsible for mediating numerous signaling pathways that contribute to tumor progression and invasion. MiRNAs exert their effect by regulating signaling pathways like the Wnt/ β-catenin, PI3K/AKT/mTOR, EGFR/MAPK, Notch Signaling, NF-κB, etc. Exosomal miRNAs induce glioma cell proliferation and invasion. For example, MiR-133b, an exosomal miRNA derived from MSC, disrupts the glioma development through suppressing the EZH2, and consequently downregulating the Wnt/β-catenin pathway (Xu et al. 2019b; Yang et al. 2024b). Moreover, exosomal miR-301a, which is prominently found in the serum of glioma patients, augments glioma cell proliferation and invasion by reducing the PTEN expression and consequently activating the Akt signaling pathway or PI3K/AKT/mTOR (Lan et al. 2018a). Additionally, exosomal miR-148a-3p promotes tumor cell angiogenesis and proliferation by activating the EGFR/ MAPK pathway or the signaling axis of miR-148a-3p/ERRFI1/EGFR/MAPK (Wang et al. 2020a). MiRNAs regulate another pathway named Notch signaling. Indeed, miRNAs mediating Notch signaling promote glioma progression and notably affect therapeutic responses (Javed et al. 2021). The NF-κB pathway is also mediated by exosomal miRNAs. For instance, miR-1246 in hypoxic glioma-derived exosomes blocks the NF-κB signaling, thereby causing the M2 macrophage polarization and enhancing an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment (Qian et al. 2020b). Other pathways mediated by exosomal miRNA also exist, like the signaling axis of m6A/lncRNACHASERR/miR-6893-3p/TRIM14, which plays a role in glioma progression (Wu et al. 2024). In addition, other methods and pathways need to be considered in understanding glioma pathogenesis and therapy. In fact, lipid metabolism and potential usage of the electrical field need to be considered in the pathogenesis and therapy of glioma, respectively (Jiang et al. 2025; Qian et al. 2025).

An Overview of Exosomal MiRNAs in the Immune System

Glioma-associated leukocytes are considered an important component in the TME, which accelerates tumor progression and enhances the immune evasion of glioma (Quail and Joyce 2017; Cheng et al. 2020). Myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) and glioma-associated macrophages/microglia (GAMs) are two of the most densely populated leukocytes in the glioma micro-atmosphere. Similar to the general role of glioma-associated leukocytes, both MDSCs and GAMs impose pro-tumorigenic measures and drive immunosuppression within the TME (Locarno et al. 2020; De Leo et al.) mainly due to their transformation toward the M2 status (Grabowski et al. 2021). Importantly, the immunomodulation of macrophages/microglia toward the M2 status (Qian et al. 2020a) and proliferation/aggregation of tumoral MDSCs (Guo et al. 2019a) are closely associated with the miRNA content of the glioma-derived exosomes. As a result, exosomal miRNAs are functional elements in tumor proliferation, immune evasion, immunosuppression, and immunomodulation of glioma TME.

Microglia and Macrophages

The most frequently found tumor-penetrating cells in the glioma TME are GAMs, constituting almost 30% of the whole tumoral mass (Lanza et al. 2021). GAMs are associated with glioma growth, immune suppression, and tumor invasiveness. (Carvalho da Fonseca and Badie 2013). Numerous signaling factors of glioma are responsible for supporting the GAMs, including the exosomal content derived from glioma (Afshar et al. 2025). Van der Vos et al. investigated the impacts of exosomal miR-21 and miR-451 on microglia/macrophages. MiR-21 and miR-451, known for their pro-tumorigenic measures, inhibited c-Myc mRNA in microglia/macrophages. Microglia/macrophages' absorption of exosomes containing these miRNAs resulted in alterations in cytokine discharge toward an immunosuppressive phenotype (Van Der Vos et al. 2015). Another miRNA to trigger immunomodulation is miR-214-5p. The upregulated miR-214-5p is possibly transshipped from tumoral cells to the microglia via exosomes, to inhibit the “C-X-C motif chemokine receptor 5” (CXCR5) expression and enhance the expression of IL-6/8, and tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) in the receiving microglial cells (Yang et al. 2019).

Tumor-associated macrophages (TAM) are another immunological element in the glioma micro-atmosphere. In general, macrophage functionality is divided into M1/M2 phenotypes. M1, denoting the classical activation, and M2, denoting the alternative activation, have respectively anti-tumorigenic and pro-tumorigenic traits (Pan et al. 2020). However, a recent study suggested the role of macrophage dynamics instead of the generally accepted M1 and M2 classification for macrophages (Afshar et al.).

Glioma-derived exosomes can manipulate M2 macrophages by their miRNA content. Qian et al. investigated the immunomodulatory roles of miR-1246 in M2 polarization, and according to their findings, the level of miR-1246 was elevated in “Hypoxia Glioma-Derived Exosomes” (H-GDE). The mechanism behind the M2 phenotypic shift involves miR-1246 targeting the “Telomeric Repeat Binding Factor 2 Interacting Protein” (TERF2IP), activation of STAT3, and inhibition of NF-κB (Qian et al. 2020a). Furthermore, miR-3591-3p cargo in the tumor-derived exosomes was able to shift the macrophages toward the M2 status. MiR-3591-3p has a straightforward target of CBLB in macrophages. M2 polarization by exosomal miR-3591-3p is dependent on the activation of JAK2/STAT3 and JAK2/PI3K/Akt/mTOR bio-signaling axes. Interestingly, miR-3591-3p is capable of exerting “cell cycle arrest” and triggering cellular death in tumoral tissues via MAPK1 inhibition (Li et al. 2022). Moreover, Exosomal miR-3184-3p is capable of inducing the M2 phenotypic shift. The “Radical SAM Domain‐containing protein 2D” (RSAD2) is targeted by miR-3184-3p to shift macrophages toward the M2 status (128). RSAD2 is a critical enzyme in the innate immune system, whose upregulation is tightly linked to inflammatory stimulants (Honarmand Ebrahimi et al. 2020), and is elevated in the M1 status (Li et al. 2021b). miR-3184-3p also acts as a pro-tumorigenic miRNA to promote the expansion, mobility, and invasiveness while suppressing apoptosis (Xu et al. 2022). Additionally, Huang et al. explored the immunomodulatory function of exosomal miR‐6733‐5p in M2 polarization. The mechanism behind the action of miR-6733-5p in M2 phenotypic shift was mediated through the activated expression of “Insulin‐like Growth Factor 2 Binding Protein 3” (IGF2BP3) mRNA in macrophages, which activates the activation of AKT (Huang et al. 2023). Lastly, miR-25-3p is also associated with the M2 transformation of macrophages. H-GDE containing miR-25-3p activated the PI3K/AKT/mTOR axis in macrophages/microglia to further shift them toward the M2 status, and noticeably, gene silencing of miR-25-3p meaningfully reduced the expansion of glioma (Xue et al. 2024).

MDSCs

Other immune components of the glioma micro-atmosphere are MDSCs. MDSCs proliferate during carcinogenesis to boost the tumoral expansion and invasion, impede the activation of NK cells and T lymphocytes, and protect the neoplastic tissue against immunotherapy (Law et al. 2020). Glioma-derived exosomes promote the proliferation of MDSCs. For instance, GBM cell-derived Exo-AGAP2-AS1 regulates TGF-β1 signaling, thereby facilitating tumor growth and metastasis through the MDSC pathway(Tian et al. 2024).

Guo et al. investigated the role of H-GDE and miR-21 and miR-10a as its cargos in supporting the MDSCs. They found the H-GDE imposed stronger immunomodulatory effects compared to “Normoxia Glioma-Derived Exosomes” (N-GDE). Noticeably, H-GDEs augment the development of MDSCs by stimulating PTEN/PI3K/AKT via miR-21 and RAR-related Orphan Receptor Alpha (RORA)/IκBα/NF-κB signaling axis via miR-10. The number of splenic MDSCs was also severely decreased after miR-21 and miR-10 transfection (Guo et al. 2018). Moreover, miR-29a and miR-92a are other immunomodulatory H-GDE miRNAs that drive the expansion of MDSCs by inhibiting the “high-mobility group box transcription factor 1” (Hbp1) via miR-29a and “cAMP-dependent type I regulatory subunit alpha” (Prkar1a) via miR-92a (Guo et al. 2019a). In addition, miR-1246 is involved in supporting the MDSCs to further modulate the immunological aspects of glioma. Glioma-derived exosomes carrying miR-1246 block the activation/expansion of CD8+ T lymphocytes and activate the “Dual Specificity Phosphatase 3” (DUSP3)/ERK signaling axis, which in turn upregulates IL-10 and “Transforming Growth Factor-beta” (TGF-β) (Qiu et al. 2021a). Glioma-derived miR-1298-5p boosts the immunosuppressive traits of MDSCs by upregulating the production of TGF-β and Nitric Oxide (NO). The main target of inhibition for miR-1298-5p in MDSCs was MSH2. The MSH2 negatively mediated the generation of TGF-β and NO as an obstacle to T-lymphocyte inhibitory measures of MDSCs (Qi et al. 2022b).

Aside from the tumoral cells, the role of MDSCs in the glioma micro-atmosphere can be accelerated by miRNA content from “Glioma-associated Mesenchymal Stem Cells” (GaMSCs). The pro-tumorigenic, immunomodulatory miR-21 is loaded into GaMSC-derived exosomes to enhance the immunoinhibitory traits of MDSCs via initiating the miR-21/SP1/DNMT1 loop. The miR-21/SP1/DNMT1 signaling loop has a self-promoting capability to upregulate the transcription of miR-21 in MDSCs. Moreover, miR-21 upregulates CD73 expression in MDSCs through the PTEN/PI3K/AKT/HIF-1α signaling axis to enhance tumoral expansion (Qiu et al. 2023).

NK cells

Contrary to the extensive amount of literature on the immunomodulatory roles of exosomal miRNAs on MDSCs and macrophages, their direct role on the NK cells (Guo et al. 2023). In a study on the exosomal miRNA-378a-3p from glioma and cervical cancer, it was revealed that the cytotoxic measures of NK cells were reduced due to the downregulation in the expression of granzyme-B. The trigger for the overexpression of exosomal miRNA-378a-3p was found to be irradiation-induced DNA hypomethylation of the miR-378a promoter in the glioma cells (Briand et al. 2020). In another study on the effect of glioma-derived miR-92a on the NK cells, it was revealed that glioma cells were able to induce an IL-6+ IL-10+ phenotype in the NK cells via miR-92a. The modulated NK cells revealed weakened anti-tumor measures and a CD8+-T-lymphocyte-suppressing trait. Paradoxically, glioma-discharged exosomal miRNA can have positive immunomodulatory effects. Exosomal miR-1983 activates TLR7 on NK cells, and further upregulation of IFN-β, ultimately accelerating the activation of NK cells and improving the anti-glioma measures of the NK cells (Shah et al. 2021).

T Lymphocytes

Dissimilar to the studies focused on the direct immunomodulatory effects of exosomal miRNA on macrophages, MDSCs, and NK cells, exosomal miRNA has not been studied for its direct effects on T lymphocytes. Exosomal miR-10a and miR-21 promoted the activation of MDSCs, and further MDSC-mediated suppression of anti-glioma CD8+ T lymphocytes. Genetic knockdown of miR-10a significantly impaired the suppression of CD8+ T lymphocytes, yet the knockdown of miR-21 did not alter the MDSC-mediated immunomodulation (Guo et al. 2018). Similarly, exosomal miR-1246 serves as an indirect suppressor of T lymphocytes via MDSCs. MDSC-mediated effects of exosomal miR-1246 are the weakened proliferation of CD8+ T lymphocytes, imposing T cell exhaustion via upregulating PD-L1 expression in MDSCs, and overall diminished anti-tumor immunity of CD8+ T lymphocytes (Qiu et al. 2021b). Exosomal miR-29a and miR-92a could also impose anti-proliferative effects on T lymphocytes via MDSCs. In vivo and in vitro knockdown of miR-29a and miR-92a resulted in reduced glioma suppression of T lymphocytes (Guo et al. 2019b). Exosomal miR-1298-5p indirectly suppresses the proliferation of T lymphocytes via MDSCs (Qi et al. 2022a). Lastly, Domenis et al. investigated the immunomodulatory effects of GSC-derived miRNA-containing exosomes, revealing that the proliferation of CD4+ and CD8+ T lymphocytes, T helper I and T helper II cytokines, and the viability of T lymphocytes were impaired due to the MDSC-mediated effects (Domenis et al. 2017).

Clinical Importance

Diagnosis

The absence of specified diagnostic methods for the early recognition of glioma is a major reason for the considerable morbidities. In the case of a specific, early detection procedure, glioma could be treated more effectively in its early stages. Despite the urgent need for accurate early detection, the current examination of glioma is strongly dependent on pathological samples and MRI. MRI lacks satisfactory specificity and is unable to detect the initial 2 mm to 3 mm warning signs (Shboul et al. 2019). Pathology-based examinations also face certain limitations. In fact, due to the invasive nature of direct sampling, the procedure might cause unwanted injury to the healthy brain matter, imposing numerous dysfunctionalities in brain physiology. Besides, pathology-based examinations are not able to fully portray the dynamic environment of the tumor (Aili et al. 2021). Altogether, the design and implementation of a specific, non-invasive, precise detection method, such as a liquid biopsy, is necessary. In contrast to the aforementioned detecting tools, liquid biopsy detection is capable of precisely detecting glioma changes. For example, the miRNA content of glioma-derived exosomes in bodily fluids can be employed for early detection (Chistiakov and Chekhonin 2014). Additionally, exosomes have also revealed their potent diagnostic efficacy with a pooled sensitivity of 0.82 and specificity of 0.91 in a recent meta-analysis by Zhang and colleagues (Zhang et al. 2025). Taking all aspects together, the application of exosomal miRNA in diagnosing glioma should be carefully taken into consideration (Wu et al. 2021).

The expression of miR-21 is linked to an array of cancers (Kumarswamy et al. 2011) and is widely considered as one of the warning biomarkers for glioma initiation (Aili et al. 2021). The Cerebrospinal Fluid (CSF) biopsy samples of glioma cases from a validation cohort represent an elevated expression of miR-21, with the “Area Under the Curve” (AUC) detection of 0.872 in comparison to the normal individuals (Table 1) (Shi et al. 2015). Moreover, findings from another validation cohort indicated that the miR-124-3p and miR-222 expression in high-grade glioma cases is considerably elevated compared to low-grade glioma cases and normal controls, with an AUC detection of 0.84 for miR-21, 0.78 for miR-124-3p, and 0.80 for miR-222 (Table 1) (Santangelo et al. 2018); besides, the expression levels of miR-21, miR-124-3p, and miR-222 are respectively downregulated and upregulated after surgical excision and tumor relapse, indicating their potential role as a clinical tool for detection, grading, and possibly treatment monitoring (Santangelo et al. 2018). Findings from a discovery cohort showed that the exosomal miR-221 is also closely linked to the early detection and grading of glioma as a differentially expressed RNA in high-grade glioma with an AUC detection of 0.78 (Yang et al. 2013). Serum levels of exosomal miR-210 from a validation cohort were also higher in glioma cases in comparison to normal controls, indicating the positive relation between tumor grade and the increased expression of miR-210 (Table 1) (Lan et al. 2020). MiR-9, a prominent cargo of Glioma Stem Cell (GSC)-derived exosomes, represents higher expression compared to normal neighboring tissues with an AUC detection of 0.80 in a validation cohort (Table 1) (Geng et al. 2022). A validation cohort portrayed that miR-301a, an important miRNA in investigating glioma-derived exosomes, revealed elevated expression in serum samples compared to normal controls, with an AUC detection of 0.83. Moreover, this validation cohort indicated that miR-301a expression was significantly lowered as a result of surgical excision, while significantly elevated as a result of tumoral relapse. Thus, miR-301a can depict the tumoral grade, progression, and response to treatment in glioma cases (Table 1)(Lan et al. 2018b). Also, the diagnosis of glioma can be associated with a reduced expression of specific miRNAs. In a validation cohort study, a lower miR-205 expression is witnessed in glioma cases compared to normal controls, and is even lower in high-grade glioma (Yue et al. 2016). A validation cohort signified that the exosomal miR-2276-5p, similar to miR-205, represents a decreased expression in glioma cases with an AUC detection of 0.8107 (Sun et al. 2021). The application of gene chips and functional genomics in a discovery cohort revealed the clinical advantages of differentially expressed miR-320 and miR-574-3p in serum biopsy for glioma diagnosis (Manterola et al. 2014). Besides, the Gene Expression Omnibus database utilized as a discovery cohort uncovered the high diagnostic value of miR-19b-3p, miR–98–5p, miR–183–5p, and miR–323–3p as biomarkers of GBM, with a pooled AUC detection of nearly 1 (Yang et al. 2022). Furthermore, machine learning approaches can even be integrated for enhanced miRNA-based diagnosis of glioma. Ebrahimkhani et al. investigated a total of 26 miRNAs in a discovery cohort and selected 7 miRNAs of miR-182-5p/328-3p/339-5p/340-5p/485-3p/486-5p/543 as the steadiest contents for the diagnosis of GBM, with a final AUC detection of ≥ 0.7 (Ebrahimkhani et al. 2018). Moreover, exosomal miRNAs can be utilized not only as a diagnostic tool, but also to distinguish glioma from intracranial lymphoma (IL). A validation cohort study of exosomal miR-376 b-5p and miR-766-5p marked a differential expression for tumor identification, with an AUC glioma detection of 0.7688 and 0.8883, yet an AUC IL detection of 0.8542 and 0.9271 (Wang et al. 2020b). Exosomal miRNAs are also involved in the distinction between brain metastasis and gliomas. For instance, a validation cohort study revealed that the serum exosomal content of miR-21 was raised both in high-grade glioma and brain metastasis, and miR-124-3p expression was considerably lowered in brain metastasis, and increased in high-grade glioma (Table 1)(Catelan et al. 2022). Numerous miRNAs might be employed in exosome-based liquid biopsy detection. For example, exosomal miR-181, studied in a validation cohort study, is capable of disclosing glioma in the early stages of pathogenesis, as its elevated expression is closely linked to tumor diagnosis and higher grades of glioma (Yang et al. 2018). MiR-124, engaged in glioma protective measures against glioma, such as anti-proliferative measures (Bian et al. 2021), can also be utilized as an early-detection biomarker, as indicated in a validation cohort (Shi et al. 2014). However, the findings regarding miR-124 are contradictory; a systematic review revealed its downregulation in glioma patients (Ghasemi et al. 2024). MiR-128 expression is considerably lowered in glioma patients with an 8.07 positive likelihood ratio and a 0.13 negative likelihood ratio, suggesting its plausible diagnostic value, as suggested in a systematic review (Liang et al. 2017). To summarize, there are plentiful studies suggesting the role of exosomal miRNA as a future diagnostic tool for the identification, grading, and distinguishing of glioma. Diagnosis: Molecular Insights

MiR-124 plays a crucial role in various cellular pathways and is recognized to be a valuable biomarker for both diagnosis and prognosis in glioma. MiR-124 exerts its role by negatively mediating the TP73/AS1 and iASPP. Indeed. MiR-124 is downregulated in glioma tissues and has an anti-proliferative effect on glioma cells (Ghasemi et al. 2024). Moreover, MiR-24, which is upregulated in Glioma, stimulates cell growth via the CDX1/PI3K/Akt signaling axis. In fact, MiR-24 is proposed to have diagnostic value in Glioma (Lei et al. 2021). Another microRNA recognized to have valuable diagnostic value is miR-21. In fact, miR-21 is upregulated in glioma and exerts an anti-apoptotic role through decreasing PTEN and increasing PI3K and Akt (Dabbagh Ohadi et al. 2023). In addition, MiR-205, a diagnostic biomarker of Glioma, with the mechanism of action of miR-205/ZEB1/Akt/mTOR, is downregulated and is negatively associated with ZEB1 (Chen et al. 2018). Similarly, miR-9 targets the signaling axis of CXCR4, which is responsible for oncogenesis in GBM, highlighting its role in the diagnosis of Glioma (Chien et al. 2020).

Prognosis

Various non-invasive markers have been identified as prognostic markers of glioma; a moderately modern approach that might change the current clinical anticipation of glioma patients (Elias et al. 2025). In this context, various state-of-the-art biomarkers have been identified. For example, transient receptor potential melastatin 2 (TRPM2) has been widely studied in the pathophysiology and prognosis of glioma, and is associated with an elevated expression, proliferation/invasion/migration characteristics of glioma, and a higher grade of the tumor (Ji et al. 2022). Similarly, inflammatory biomarkers such as IL-6 and p53, and signaling elements such as RTK, PI3K, and MAPK, have revealed a negative correlation with the prognosis of glioma (Bulut and Simsek 2023). Another marker associated with glioma prognosis is Mps1, whose capability in predicting glioma outcomes has been explored (Jemaà and Kifagi 2024). Importantly, non-coding RNAs have gained attention in this matter. lncRNA-mediated dysregulation in the TGF-β signaling pathway has been studied as a prognostic factor for glioma, revealing that 15-lncRNAs of AC010173.1, HOXA-AS2, AL606970.4, HOXC-AS1, AGAP2-AS1, AC013472.3, AC093895.1, AC007938.1, AC010729.1, AC131097.4, AC074286.1, AL592424.1, DRAIC, AC002456.1, and HOXC13-AS could serve as a credible prognostic biomarker (Duan et al. 2024). In line with the findings of signaling, inflammatory molecules, and non-coding RNAs, exosomes could serve as another source of information for predicting the clinical outcome of glioma patients (Yang et al. 2024c); miRNA contents of glioma-derived exosomes have the potential to prognose glioma.

Liquid biopsy from blood and CSF unveiled the negative correlation between higher exosomal expression of miR-21 to patients’ Overall Survival (OS) time, and to recurrence time in glioma cases (Table 2) (Shi et al.). miR-301a upregulation, a diagnostic/prognostic biomarker of glioma, is associated with a decreased “Karnofsky Performance Scale” (KPS), with a hazard ratio (HR) of 2.31, indicating a poor progression (Lan et al.). Reaffirming the prognostic role of miR-301a, a meta-analysis of exosomal miRNA prognostic values in solid tumors revealed the link between poor prognosis of glioma to the elevated expression of miR-21/4257/375/23b-3p/21–5p/19a-3p/194–5p/1290/10b-5p/7 g-5p/451a/665/301a/19a/4772–3p/6803–5p/203/373/200a/200b/200c/1246 and the lowered expression of miR-34/125b/638/6869–5p/190b/26a-1–3p/145–3p/200a-3p/7i-5p/9–5p/615–3p (Zhou et al. 2019). Moreover, glioma cases with elevated expression of exosomal miR-210 have a lower OS compared to cases with lower expression profiles (Table 2)(Lan et al. 2020). Increased expression of GSC-derived exosomal miR-26a is also closely linked to the decreased OS of glioma cases, with an HR of 1.42 (Wang et al. 2019). miR-10b, unlike the healthy surrounding tissues, is merely elevated in glioma tumoral cells. Indeed, elevated expression of miR-10b represents a positive correlation with higher grades of the tumor and lower OS (Gabriely et al. 2011). Conversely, a lowered expression of exosomal miR-2276-5p is positively associated with lower survivability of glioma cases (Table 2)(Sun et al. 2021). The expression level of other miRNAs can also be closely linked to the survival outcome of glioma. For example, increased plasma levels of miR-221/222 represent poor survivability (Zhang et al. 2016). miR-181b upregulation was negatively associated with functional outcome parameters of EORTC QLQ-30, QLQ-BN20, and KPS (Stakaitis et al. 2020). KPS and OS were both negatively associated with the expression of miR-205 and its serum levels (Table 2)(Yue et al. 2016). Besides, elevated expressions of miR-28-5p and miR-1224-5p were followed by a better projection of glioma (Wei et al. 2022). In contrast to miR-181b, the higher expression of miR-181d was positively correlated with functional outcome parameters of EORTC, QLQ-30, QLQ-BN20, and KPS (Stakaitis et al. 2020). Briefly, the application of glioma-derived exosomes combined with corresponding immune-informatics analysis can uncover the prognosis, survival outcomes, and response to treatment in glioma patients. Therapeutics

Rashed et al. 2017). Indeed, scientists are considering exosomal manipulation, exosomal cargo-loading blockage, and other alternatives for cancer therapy (Cheng et al. 2020) (Table 3).

Delivering Tumor Inhibitory miRNAs

Restoration of decreased tumor inhibitor miRNAs suppresses the expansion of glioma cells, suggesting the anti-glioma value of particular miRNAs for utilization in treatment (Peng et al. 2022). On one hand, the application of viral and liposomal delivery systems seems unfitting due to the safety concerns and questionable efficacy (Broderick and Zamore 2011). On the other hand, exosomes function as natural transporters of miRNA content and can be utilized to deliver tumor inhibitor miRNA content to targeted tumoral cells.

Fareh et al. designed a cellular model of GSCs expressing anti-glioma miRNAs. Engineered GSCs were manipulated to express the tumor inhibitor cluster of miR-302–367, and to package the miR-302–367 into exosomes to be delivered to the surrounding tumoral cells, which results in the decreased the tumorigenicity and cellular expansion of tumoral cells (Fareh et al. 2017) (Table 3). In addition, blocking miR-1 loading into exosomes resulted in a reduced glioma invasiveness and neovascularization(Bronisz et al. 2014).

Inhibition of Tumor Driver miRNA: Molecular Insights

Once the exosomal miR-302–367 are delivered, and the process of exosome internalization has occurred, internalized miR-302–367 imposed the inhibitory effects over the targeted molecules of “C-X-C motif chemokine receptor 4” (CXCR4), “E2F transcription factor 1” (E2F1), Cyclin A, and Cyclin D1, which resulted in promising miRNA-mediated inhibition (Fareh et al. 2017). Regarding the rationale for miR-1 blockade, it could be considered that its inhibition leads to inhibiting the “annexin A2” (ANXA2), a straightforward target of miR-1, and an important element in the glioma neovascularization (Bronisz et al. 2014).

Inhibition of Tumor Driver miRNA

Decoys or similar-to-sponge structures can be implemented for inhibiting certain miRNAs. The decoy’s usefulness in inhibiting their targeted miRNA is mediated by binding to the targeted miRNA (Bak et al. 2013). Monfared et al. utilized manipulated exosomes carrying a similar-to-sponge structure to repress the miR-21 in glioma cell lines of U87 and C6, which resulted in increased apoptosis and decreased tumoral expansion (Monfared et al. 2019).

Inhibition of Tumor Driver miRNA: Molecular Insights

MiR-21 is a known tumor driver miRNA in the pathophysiology of glioma. MiR-21 is highly expressed in tumoral cells, to play an important role in increasing tumor viability and weakening apoptosis (Shaikh et al. 2024) by downregulating tumor suppressors genes like Caspase-3/9, PDCD4 (Zhou et al. 2010; Wang et al. 2023; Chen et al. 2021b); likewise, miR-21 enhances the glioma invasion and migration by direct targeting of RECK and TIMP3 (Han et al. 2012; Beylerli et al. 2022). Besides, miR-21 accelerates glioma vascularization when co-expressed with HIF-1α and VEGF (Hermansen et al. 2016; Saadh et al. 2025). Given the pro-tumorigenic roles of miR-21, active targeting of this multimodal element can help in strengthening the anti-glioma treatment.

MSC-Derived miRNA-Carrying Exosomes

As an alternative method, mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) can induce anti-glioma therapeutic measures (Yang et al. 2014). MSCs possess considerable advantages: the ability to traverse the BBB, weak immunostimulatory traits, and affinity to tumor cells (Reagan and Kaplan 2011). In line with the direct delivery of tumor inhibitory miRNAs, many studies have addressed the MSC-derived exosomes containing the tumor inhibitory miRNAs.

MiR-124a, an efficient anti-glioma miRNA, was abundantly found within MSC-secreted exosomes. Indeed, the exosome-delivered miR-124a suppressed the sustainability and tumorigenicity of GSCs, and the procedure was successfully able to restrain the intracranial GSCs in a mouse model. (Lang et al. 2017). Yan et al. revealed the poor expression of miR-512-5p, in glioma tissues. Bone mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs) were engineered to produce miR-512-5p and package them into exosomes to be further internalized by glioma cells, which inhibited the tumoral proliferation (Yan et al. 2021) (Table 3)(Zhang et al. 2021). MSCs were engineered to produce miR-29a-3p and transport them to glioma cells via exosomes. Ultimately, the procedure of exosomal delivery of miR-29a-3p resulted in a successful in vivo and in vitro restraint of glioma proliferation (Zhang et al. 2021). Zhang et al. examined the role of exosomal miR-7 delivery, noticing its in apoptosis and restraining glioma proliferation (Zhang et al. 2017). In addition, other miRNAs can be applied to anti-glioma therapy through an exosome-based delivery. MSC-derived exosomes that directly deliver miR-133b effectively suppressed the cellular expansion, mobility, and invasiveness of glioma cells. (Xu et al. 2019a). Additionally, exosomal miR-584-5p originated from MSCs is associated with anti-proliferative and anti-metastatic traits to suppress glioma (Table 3)(Kim et al. 2018). Moreover, BMSC-derived exosomes with miR-506 suppressed the tumoral expansion of glioma cell lines and triggered tumoral death via apoptosis. The mobility of the glioma was severely impaired by the exosomal delivery of miR-506 (Zhang et al. 2023). Exosomal miR-199a and miR-375 also possess some anti-glioma measures, whose delivery resulted in an in vivo restraint of tumoral expansion, mobility, invasiveness, and increased susceptibility to TMZ (Yu et al. 2019). Likewise, Human Marrow Stromal Cells (hMSCs)-derived exosomes contain miR-375 restrains the tumoral expansion, mobility, and invasiveness (Deng et al. 2020). Moreover, intra-tumoral injection of MSCs that produces miRNA-containing exosomes depicted promising results. Katakowski et al. unraveled the therapeutic role of this translational approach with MSCs-based miR-146b that resulted in restraining the proliferation of glioma in a rat model of glioma (Table 3) (Katakowski et al. 2013).

MSC-Derived miRNA-Carrying Exosomes: Molecular Insights

MiR-124a has inhibitory effects on “Forkhead box” (FOX)A2. Inhibition of FOXA2 results in the dysfunction in the lipid metabolism, accumulation of lipid content, and ultimately poisoning the GSCs (Aili et al.). Besides, miR-124a inhibits the tumoral proliferation by suppressing β-catenin, cyclin D1, and “IQ motif containing GTPase activating protein 1” (IQGAP1). IQGAP1 is an important regulator of actin polymerization, cellular mobility, and tumoral expansion (Lu et al. 2014). MiR-512-5p is known to target “Jagged 1” (JAG1); hence, it interferes with cellular expansion and triggers cell cycle arrest. JAG1 forms an important transcription regulator with SMAD3 to enhance TWIST1 and enhance glioma endurance (Kim et al. 2023). MiR-29a-3p directly aims for “Roundabout Guidance Receptor 1” (ROBO1), which is involved in a tumor driver mechanism called vasculogenic mimicry (VM). VM denotes the microcirculation of tumor cells that lacks the proper endothelial lining, with structure of numerous loops in loops of pseudo-vasculature networks (Maddison et al. 2023). Exosomal miR-7 delivery results in triggering TRAIL-dependent apoptosis of glioma cells. “TNF-related apoptosis-inducing ligand” (TRAIL) is a member of TNF family that plays an important role of inducing apoptosis in cancer cells (Pimentel et al. 2023). MiR-133b disorganizes the Wnt/β-catenin axis, downregulating the expression of EZH2. The expression of EZH2 is noticeably involve in the development of resistance to treatment via catalyzing the trimethylation of histone H3 (Kaur et al. 2024). MiR-584-5p lowers “cytochrome P450 family 2 subfamily J member 2” (CYP2J2); triggers apoptosis via lowering the levels of the anti-apoptotic element of “B-cell lymphoma-2” (BCL-2), and enhancing the levels of the pro-apoptotic element of “BCL-2 associated X” (BAX). Exosomes carrying miR-199a block the activity of “ArfGAP with GTPase domain, ankyrin repeat and PH domain 2” (AGAP2). AGAP2 forms a RNA axis of AGAP2-AS1-miR-9-5p-MMP2/MMP9 that has implications in the development, proliferation, and migration of glioma (Luo et al. 2021). MiR-375 hinders solute carrier family 31 member 1 (SLC31A1). SLC31A is a cuproptosis-related protein that enhances the immune cell infiltration and progression of glioma (Liu et al. 2024). MiR-146b blocks pro-tumoral elements of NF-κB, EGFR, and SMAD4, all of which with proven role in proliferation, migration, invasion, immune evasion, and resistance to treatment of glioma (Shi et al. 2023; Rodriguez et al. 2023; Feng et al. 2025).

Synthetic Nanoparticles vs Natural Exosomes

Natural exosomes with a size of 30–100 nm have the size criterion of nanoparticles. This size matching empowers exosomes to be administered as drug carriers. In addition, exosomes are customized to their parent cells with specific effects and targets. The specificity of exosomes and their cargos helps to meet the need for personalized medicine and more accurate therapy (Sun et al. 2013). Natural exosomes are more favorable compared to the synthetic/ artificial nanoparticles, especially in terms of therapy. Indeed, despite the efforts of scientists and in vitro findings, patients administered with artificial exosomes experienced insufficient delivery of the drug, and consequently failed in treatment. Moreover, natural exosomes are considered to be more effective as they are target-specific, non-cytotoxic, and high payload(Askenase 2021; Sun et al. 2013). Additionally, Artificial/ synthetic exosomes, in contrast to natural exosomes, activate the immune system, which creates toxic negative outcomes(Askenase 2021). In addition, biological pathways have systemic advantages over synthetic nanoparticles. For example, METTL3-dependent m6A modification of SNAP29 causes autophagy-mitochondrial crisis (Yang et al. 2025). Also, in a natural biological system, despite the artificial systems, various biomarkers like LIP can be applied for a better experience of therapy and diagnosis (Liu et al. 2022). Natural exosomes can play roles in astrocytopathy (Lin et al. 2024), and are recognized to probably have roles in linking single-cell and GWAS traits of human (Yin et al. 2024).

Human Samples Analysis

Having discussed the therapeutic applications of exosomal miRNA in glioma, herein we test the possible association between the expression levels of the aforementioned miRNA in the survival analysis of patients with low-grade glioma (LGG). ENCORI/starBase (https://rnasysu.com/encori/index.php) systematically gathers functional RNA interactions and gene expression data across 32 cancers (Li et al. 2013). The survival analysis for miR-29a-3p/133b/148a-3p/375/512-5p/584-5p was performed in LGG patients (Fig. 3), revealing the HR of 1.13, 1.13, 0.9, 2.03, 1.4, 0.95, 1.31, respectively. Only miR-133b and miR-512-5p were associated with a protective HR, which emphasizes the need for further studies of miRNA analysis regarding survival outcomes and therapeutic effects. The intricate cooperation of non-coding RNAs with the signaling elements of the cell (Esmaeili et al. 2025) should be carefully addressed; the present survival analysis lacks an in-depth investigation of intracellular human signaling events. Besides, the innate complexity of exosomes and the interaction of miRNAs in the cellular micro-environment severely limit conclusions from these plots (Fig. 4).

Challenges and Future Perspectives

Between-Study Heterogeneity

As discussed in this review paper, exosomal miRNAs can be applied as a state-of-the-art biomarker for diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment of glioma. Despite the important advantages of non-invasive nature and high predictability values, the clinical application of miRNAs faces the important challenge of between-study heterogeneity and even inconsistent findings. As the most considerable challenge, the between-study heterogeneity in terms of source for liquid sampling (blood and CSF) (Shi et al. 2015; Lan et al. 2020), methods for miRNA selection (computer-based analysis or bulk human-mediated evaluation) (Yang et al. 2022; Ebrahimkhani et al. 2018), and the discriminative role (differentiating healthy cases from glioma patients or metastasis/other tumors from glioma patients) (Wang et al. 2020b). Additionally, findings about a number of exosomal miRNAs might not be completely consistent with each other. As an important examples, miR-124 is both marked as upregulated (Shi et al. 2014), and downregulated (Ghasemi et al. 2024). Overall, a more in-depth investigation of exosomal miRNAs in the human environment is recommended for a better evaluation.

Alternative Sources for Liquid Biopsy

The sources for liquid biopsy for further examination of exosomal miRNAs have been solely limited to blood and CSF. However, the current scope of liquid biopsy sampling could be extended to encompass saliva and urine. Examined by Safari et al., saliva could serve as a promising source for exosomes and exosomal miRNAs (Safari et al. 2023); authors claim that “Collectively, it seems that salivary exosomal microRNAs could be used as a diagnostic biomarker in oral diseases.” In parallel with their findings, and the recent proteomic study on the salivary content of exosomal contents (Muntiu et al. 2024), exosomal miRNA in the saliva could also be investigated as a possible diagnostic/prognostic marker in glioma. In parallel with saliva, urine and urinary exosomes could be explored. Urinary exosomal miRNA-21 have revealed promising outcomes for early diagnosis and predicting the prognosis of bladder cancer (Yang et al. 2024a). Given the current perspective of urine liquid biopsy in glioma patients (Kurimoto et al. 2023), urinary exosomal miRNA could also be surveyed to check its feasibility of being a diagnostic/prognostic biomarker.

Alternative Sources for MSCs

Other approaches for the production of exosome-producing MSCs could be suggested for future studies, including Wharton’s jelly (WJ), adipose-derived, and umbilical cord as alternative sources. WJ stem cells represent a higher proliferative capability and significantly lower immunostimulatory traits. Hence, their application for further replacement in future studies could be recommended (Wang et al. 2004). In parallel with WJ-based therapeutics, adipose-derived stem cells could be implemented in anti-glioma treatments. For example, exosomal delivery of miR-4731-5p from adipose-derived stem cells triggered apoptosis and inhibited the expansion and mobility of glioma cells (Babaei et al. 2024). The third innovative option for the source of MSCs can be the umbilical cord. In fact, a translational exosome-based delivery of miR-124 and “programmed cell death protein-1” (PD-1) highlighted the anti-tumoral measures of this translational method (Yueh et al. 2025).

Integration with Genome Engineering

Other genome-based technologies can merge into exosomes and miRNA-based treatments. Gene editing innovation of CRISPR/Cas9 can robustly perform whole-genome screening and disclose genetic susceptibilities of cancers. For instance, CRISPR/Cas9-based whole-genome screening can mark the genetic elements with the capability of influencing the anti-cancer medication (Wei et al. 2020). Hence, CRISPR/Cas9-based screening technologies can open the avenue for numerous anti-cancer measures (Xu et al. 2019c). Despite all the advancements, CRISPR/Cas9 faces an important obstacle of suitable delivery to the target of interest. To overcome the challenge of delivery, exosome-mediated transportation can be utilized (Liu et al. 2023b). The integration of exosome-mediated delivery of CRISPR with genome editing of the specific pro-tumoral miRNAs could also be recommended.

FUS-assisted Exosome Penetration

Exosomal delivery can even be an adjuvant to other medical procedures to restrain the tumoral capacity. The delivery of MSC-derived exosomes containing miR-1208 was followed by focused ultrasound. Indeed, the application of ultrasound increased the BBB permeability to increase the efficiency of exosomal miR-1208 and tumoricidal measures (Zhan et al. 2023). In another translational study, CRISPR-loaded exosomes were applied adjuvant to ultrasound alleviate the neuronal damage in a mouse model of Parkinson’s disease (Kong et al. 2024). This approach could also be explored in the context of glioma treatment, and ultrasound-assisted CRISPR-loaded exosomes have been currently underexplored in this context.

Conclusion

MicroRNAs are reported to have a key role in orchestrating numerous cellular pathways. Additionally, exosomes have been broadly recognized as the primary carriers of miRNAs in cells and body fluids. Altered expression of miRNAs can lead to cellular dysfunction and ultimately human diseases, such as glioma. A growing body of research has indicated the interlink between exosomal miRNA alteration and glioma pathogenesis. GAMs and MDSCs are the two main immune modulators in TME that exert a pivotal role in glioma progression and immune evasion. This review thoroughly discusses the regulatory roles of exosomal miRNAs in these immune cells. Despite advances in diagnostic and therapeutic approaches for GBM, the prognosis and diagnosis of patients remain poor. Notably, exosomal miRNAs promise new avenues in GBM prognosis and theranostics. In this study, different miRNA-based glioma therapy methods were explored, namely, MSC-derived miRNA-carrying exosomes. Future work may investigate other MSCs within physiological settings. Furthermore, miRNAs have untapped potential in the prognosis and diagnosis of glioma; hence, In-silico methods of miRNA analysis can hopefully meet the demand for more accurate and precise diagnostic strategies.

A hybrid compulsory-flexible course in postgraduate dental basic research education: improving student performance and satisfaction
Authors: Qianmin Ou, Deqian Tang, Manqing Liu, Xiwei Yuan, Botian Tan, Chenxi Sun, Lisa R Amir, Zetao Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study evaluates the effectiveness of a novel compulsory–flexible course in Postgraduate Dental Basic Research Education (PDBRE). The course was designed with a mandatory core component to ensure foundational research competencies for all students, complemented by flexible elective activities to support personalized learning. We assessed its effectiveness in improving research capabilities and student satisfaction, with particular attention to differences between clinical and research postgraduates.MethodsWe conducted a comprehensive evaluation using objective academic outputs and a cross-sectional survey of 111 postgraduate students. The survey assessed multiple dimensions of the learning experience, and independent-samples t-tests were employed to compare perceptions between clinical and research postgraduate students.ResultsThe implementation of the PDBRE course was associated with improved research output and positive student evaluations. Clinical postgraduate students reported higher overall satisfaction (3.54), compared with 3.37 among research postgraduate students. Among the evaluated dimensions, assistance with individual research received the highest rating (3.93), whereas communication channels received the lowest score (2.89). Although research postgraduates expressed relatively lower satisfaction in certain aspects, both groups recognized the course’s contribution to research skill development and long-term academic growth.ConclusionThe hybrid compulsory–flexible model is an effective approach for enhancing research competence in dental postgraduate education. Institutions may consider implementing a curriculum that combines a compulsory foundational module with flexible elective components to accommodate diverse learning needs. This approach appears particularly beneficial for clinical postgraduate students by promoting research engagement and strengthening foundational research knowledge.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12909-026-09071-4.Keywords: Dentistry, Basic Research Education, Educational Evaluation, Curriculum Reform

Introduction

With the growing societal demand for oral health, the cultivation of high-quality dental professionals with solid theoretical foundations and innovative abilities has become an urgent priority [1, 2]. The postgraduate phase is a key period when dental students begin formal research in oral health, making it vital for developing future dental researchers [3, 4]. Postgraduate Dental Basic Research Education (PDBRE) becomes especially important in this context, as it helps students develop critical thinking and research skills, all of which are valuable for their professional growth and career development [5, 6].

However, compared with medical education, dental research education faces several distinctive challenges. Dental curricula are frequently highly procedure-oriented and densely structured, leaving limited opportunities to integrate basic biomedical sciences with clinical training [7]. Furthermore, dental research frequently involves specialized domains such as oral basic sciences, dental biomaterials, and dental device technologies, requiring interdisciplinary knowledge spanning biology, materials science, and engineering [8, 9]. Compared with large medical faculties, dental schools often have smaller research infrastructures and fewer dedicated research training pathways. These structural and disciplinary factors contribute to persistent challenges in dental education, including limited integration of basic science, curricular congestion, faculty limitations, and insufficient research exposure during clinical training [10–12]. Additionally, there is a lack of dedicated research on the optimal extent, timing, and focus of these subjects within dental education [10]. Updating teaching approaches is crucial to enhance the quality of dental education and bridge the gap between theoretical knowledge and practical application in the field of oral healthcare [13].

Students with different educational backgrounds have distinct course requirements and expectations [14]. Research-oriented students often focus on the development of research methods and innovation capabilities [15, 16]. They tend to value courses that enhance their ability to conduct independent research, analyze data, and develop novel ideas [17]. On the other hand, professional-oriented students emphasize the practical and clinical applications of their knowledge [18]. They prefer courses that provide systematic theoretical foundations while also offering clear connections to real-world practices and clinical situations [19]. These differences in educational backgrounds and learning expectations highlight the importance of developing research education models that can accommodate diverse student needs while ensuring the acquisition of essential research competencies.

Several initiatives have attempted to strengthen research training in dental education, including elective research tracks, short-term research projects, and student research programs [20]. While these programs provide valuable opportunities for students interested in academic careers, their voluntary nature typically limits participation to a subset of highly motivated students [21]. Moreover, most programs function as isolated educational activities rather than being systematically integrated into the core curriculum, reducing their effectiveness in developing research competencies among all students [22].

To address this, we developed a hybrid compulsory–flexible course for PDBRE based on competency-based education (CBE) principles. CBE emphasizes clearly definedand measurable competencies that all learners must achieve, thereby ensuring that every postgraduate student acquires essential research skills within a structured and time-efficient framework [11, 23]. The course includes a compulsory core module that provides essential research skills for all students within a limited and structured timeframe, while flexible modules allow students to selectively engage with advanced topics based on their academic interests and career goals. This structure enables the integration of research training into the existing curriculum without substantially increasing curricular burden, while still accommodating diverse learning needs. This model’s effectiveness was confirmed in enhancing student engagement and outcomes in our undergraduate program [24].

In this study, we surveyed participants of the PDBRE course at Sun Yat-sen University using a well-designed questionnaire. Feedback was collected across several dimensions, including teaching effectiveness, teacher-student interaction, and suggestions for course improvement, to better understand the differences in course experiences and needs among students from diverse educational backgrounds. Through detailed data analysis, our research aims to offer evidence-based support for course reform and guide curriculum optimization.

Materials and methods

Course structure of PDBRE

The Postgraduate Dental Basic Research Education (PDBRE) at Sun Yat-sen University is a compulsory 12-week course that has trained over 300 postgraduate students in stomatology over three years. The course is structured around a balanced combination of compulsory and flexible elements, ensuring all students acquire essential research skills while allowing them to explore personal interests and career goals.

The compulsory component is organized into modular courses covering core theoretical knowledge, experimental techniques, frontier research seminars, and medical humanities (Table 1). The theoretical module (70% of content) remains stable year-to-year, focusing on foundational concepts, while experimental and frontier modules (30% of content) are updated annually to reflect advances in the field.

The flexible component includes two main activities designed to support students’ professional growth and scientific thinking. The Academic Career Exploration Series invites professionals from diverse fields including clinical practice, basic science, and science policy, to share their career paths and experiences. This series helps students better understand future career options and reduce uncertainties about their professional direction. Additionally, the Distinguished Scholar Lectures bring leading researchers to present cutting-edge work and discuss how they identify and solve scientific problems. These sessions broaden students’ perspectives and enhance their critical thinking and problem-solving abilities. Students were given the opportunity to select from these modules based on their interests and career goals.

Study population and cohort comparability

The PDBRE course was first implemented in 2022 as a structured research training initiative for postgraduate students. Prior to its introduction, research training was primarily conducted through individual mentorship rather than a formalized course. During the study period (2019–2024), no other major or systematic educational interventions related to research training were introduced. Throughout the study period (2019–2024), the students were admitted through the national postgraduate entrance examination. Regarding student demographics, the composition of cohorts showed no systematic differences.

Survey design

To enhance the teaching quality of the PDBRE course and ensure that the evaluation system meets medical education standards, we designed a survey to collect students’ feedback and suggestions for improvement, considering their diverse educational backgrounds (Supplementary file 1). Participants were informed that their participation was voluntary and anonymous, with informed consent obtained prior to completing the questionnaire. The questionnaire, specifically developed for this study, was designed to ensure that participants answered all questions and that the returned electronic survey forms were complete. Content validity was evaluated through expert review by five faculty members with expertise in dental education and research methodology. Experts assessed the questionnaire items for relevance, clarity, and representativeness using structured ratings and written feedback. Based on their recommendations, several items were refined to improve clarity and wording. The instrument was pilot-tested with 30 postgraduate students, and internal consistency was assessed, yielding a Cronbach’s α of 0.80, indicating good reliability.

Survey implementation

The survey was conducted in January 2025 using the Wenjuanxing platform (https://www.wjx.cn/). Electronic questionnaires were distributed via WeChat to master’s and doctoral students at the Sun Yat-sen University Guanghua School of Stomatology. A total of 111 valid responses were collected and analyzed, yielding a response rate of approximately 75%. The survey was administered after course completion to allow students to evaluate the full learning experience.

Participants

The survey was distributed to 148 postgraduate students who had completed the PDBRE course and were eligible for inclusion during the study period. A total of 111 students responded (response rate: 75%), including 61 clinical and 50 research postgraduate students. Because the survey was conducted anonymously, individual-level information from non-respondents was not available for direct comparison. However, the respondents included students from both clinical and research-oriented training pathways, suggesting that the sample captured perspectives from the major student groups participating in the course. The diversity of educational backgrounds allowed for meaningful comparisons in course evaluation.

Statistical analysis

For statistical analysis, the means and standard deviations of numerical variables were calculated, while frequencies and percentages were computed for categorical variables. Independent samples t-tests were used to analyze the differences in the evaluation of teaching methods between students with different educational backgrounds, with a significance level set at P < 0.05. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 25 software. Likert-scale responses were treated as continuous variables for group comparisons, an approach widely used in educational research when items have multiple response categories and distributions are approximately symmetric.

Results

Research output and academic performance

To evaluate the outcomes of the PDBRE course, we compared the research productivity of postgraduate cohorts who had sufficient time after completing the course to generate measurable academic outputs. Specifically, the classes of 2022 and 2023, who had completed the course and had at least one year of follow-up time, were compared with three preceding cohorts (the classes of 2019, 2020, and 2021) who had not received the course. The analysis focused on three key metrics: the number of research projects initiated, the number of scientific papers published, and the time to first publication. “Research projects initiated” were defined as research studies formally approved by the institution in which the student was a participant under faculty supervision. “Scientific papers published” referred to peer-reviewed articles in which the student was listed as first author or co-author, and that were published or accepted for publication in indexed journals. “Time to first publication” was calculated as the number of months from the student’s enrollment date to the acceptance date of their first paper, and only students who published at least one paper during their program were included in this analysis.

The implementation of the PDBRE course was associated with a significant increase in research output. As shown in Fig. 1A, the average number of research projects initiated per cohort rose from 3.3 in the pre-PDBRE cohorts to 8.0 in the post-PDBRE cohorts. Similarly, Fig. 1B illustrated that the average number of peer-reviewed publications per cohort increased markedly from 24.3 to 46.7. A notable finding was the acceleration of early research productivity. Among students who successfully published a paper as first author, the average time from the start of their postgraduate program to the acceptance of their paper was significantly shorter in the PDBRE groups (Fig. 1C).

These objective findings demonstrate that the introduction of the compulsory PDBRE course is strongly correlated with enhanced research engagement, greater scientific output, and a faster transition to active research participation among postgraduate students.

Survey participant characteristics and overview

Following the assessment of objective academic outcomes, a survey was administered to evaluate students’ subjective perceptions of the PDBRE course. A total of 111 postgraduate students participated, including 61 clinical postgraduates and 50 research postgraduates, with a balanced gender distribution (54 males, 57 females). Detailed item-level results for all survey dimensions are presented in Supplementary Table 1.

Overall satisfaction

As shown in Fig. 2, participants demonstrated a generally positive perception of the PDBRE course. The clinical postgraduate students showed a higher level of satisfaction, with the majority expressing either Very Satisfied (23%) or Satisfied (52%), and a smaller proportion remaining Neutral (25%). In contrast, research postgraduate students most frequently selected Neutral (44%), followed by Satisfied (38%) and Very Satisfied (12%), with 6% reporting Dissatisfaction. The notably higher proportion of neutral responses among research postgraduate students suggests a more reserved or critical evaluation of the course. This difference may reflect the distinct academic backgrounds of the two groups. Research postgraduate students typically have greater prior exposure to research training and may therefore perceive some course content as less novel or insufficiently advanced, leading to a more neutral or occasionally negative assessment.

Comprehensive evaluations of the PDBRE by students with different educational backgrounds

Overall, these findings indicate that while the course was generally well received, research postgraduate students may have higher expectations for depth or specialization in research-oriented training.

Evaluation of educational content

As shown in Fig. 3, satisfaction levels across different teaching dimensions varied between the two groups. Overall, satisfaction with the allocation of teaching time was relatively highest, with an average rating of 3.63, while satisfaction with course materials and teaching resources was the lowest, averaging 3.22. When analyzed by group, clinical postgraduate students generally reported higher satisfaction across all dimensions, with an average score of 3.54, compared to 3.37 among research postgraduate students. Clinical students tended to rate each indicator more positively, reflecting a more favorable perception of the course.

These findings suggest that students at different training stages perceived the same course differently. Clinical postgraduate students, who are primarily engaged in clinical practice and may have limited exposure to research-oriented concepts, likely found the course content more valuable for strengthening their foundational knowledge. In contrast, research postgraduate students, who may already be familiar with some of the covered material, perceived the course as relatively less novel or comprehensive.

Teaching effectiveness

The teaching effectiveness was evaluated across six dimensions: (1) Understanding the importance of basic oral research, (2) Understanding the learning methods for basic oral research, (3) Understanding the selection of topics in basic oral research, (4) Stimulation of learning interest, (5) Assistance with individual research, and (6) Contribution to future career development.

Figure 4 compares the perceptions of clinical and research postgraduate students across six aspects of teaching effectiveness. Overall, both groups showed relatively high satisfaction (scores above 3.5) regarding the course’s role in helping with individual research and understanding the importance of basic oral research.

Notably, research postgraduate students showed higher approval (score = 3.8) on how research contributes to their career development, highlighting the importance of research skills for their future. In contrast, clinical postgraduate students reported higher satisfaction overall, especially in terms of understanding learning methods and understanding research topics.This suggests the course effectively met their learning needs and increased their engagement with basic research concepts.

Communication and interaction

We assessed three dimensions of communication and interaction in postgraduate stomatology education: instructors’ openness to student assistance, availability of communication channels, and perceived impact on skill development (Fig. 5). Mean satisfaction scores for instructors’ openness to assistance averaged 3.79 across groups. In contrast, satisfaction with communication channels was substantially lower, with a mean of 2.89 overall and particularly low among research postgraduate students (2.8). For perceived impact on skill development, clinical postgraduate students reported higher satisfaction (3.93) compared to research postgraduate students (3.64), suggesting a potential expectation among research students for greater knowledge gain from these interactions. These results highlight teacher-student communication as a key area for improvement, particularly for research postgraduates.

Student interest in potential course content

Students were presented with seven potential course topics and allowed to select multiple options. As shown in Fig. 6, Artificial Intelligence was the most frequently selected topic, chosen by 48.6% of respondents, followed by Experimental Techniques (43.2%). Interest in Oral Clinical Medicine was also relatively high, particularly among clinical postgraduate students. In contrast, Basic Science of Stomatology attracted greater interest from research postgraduate students. In addition to these pre-defined options, 21 students (18.9%) selected “Others” and provided free-text suggestions. A preliminary content analysis of these responses revealed two emerging themes: (1) clinical research methodology (e.g., “clinical trial design,” “patient-centered research”), and (2) interdisciplinary topics (e.g., “biomaterials,” “digital dentistry”). While the number of responses was limited, these suggestions indicate student interest in content that bridges clinical practice and research, as well as emerging technologies. The high level of interest in these areas suggests that students are eager to engage with contemporary and practical content, potentially indicating areas for future curriculum development to better align with student interests.

Discussion

Basic science training in dental research is essential for developing key academic competencies, such as critical thinking, experimental design, and scholarly communication [25, 26]. These competencies are pivotal for improving research quality and fostering long-term career development in dentistry [27]. At the postgraduate level, structured research courses provide a crucial entry point, particularly considering that undergraduate programs in most countries offer limited exposure to basic dental research training [11, 28, 29]. This gap underscores the importance of postgraduate research programs as a vital bridge to scientific engagement for aspiring clinicians and researchers.

Despite being a relatively new course, PDBRE plays an essential role in assisting students with the development of their research skills [20]. The hybrid compulsory-flexible model implemented in our PDBRE course was designed to address this gap systematically. The compulsory core ensured that all students, regardless of their prior experience or career orientation, achieved a foundational level of research literacy. This pattern was accompanied by an increase in the number of research projects and publications across the student cohort. At the same time, the flexible components appeared to accommodate diverse learning needs, as reflected in the differential perceptions between clinical and research postgraduate students. These findings suggest that a differentiated emphasis within the hybrid model may be beneficial: clinical postgraduate students may benefit most from strengthened foundational and guided training, whereas research postgraduate students may require greater autonomy, advanced content, and opportunities for in-depth academic interaction.

Compared with previous interventions—such as purely elective research tracks or short-term student projects, which typically attract only highly motivated subgroups and show limited overall impact [21]—our hybrid compulsory–flexible approach ensured universal participation while still offering personalization. Our students reported mean satisfaction scores of 3.2–3.8 (on a 5-point Likert scale) across key domains. For context, similar hybrid teaching modules in dental training have reported mean satisfaction scores ranging from approximately 3.29 to 3.67 on five-point Likert scales [30], which is comparable to the range observed in our study. The novelty lies in achieving these scores alongside objective improvements in research output and without increasing curricular burden—an outcome rarely reported in prior elective or traditional programs. Thus, the hybrid compulsory–flexible model appears to offer a more balanced and scalable solution than existing interventions in dental and medical education.

However, the evaluation of student feedback reveals areas for improvement rooted in this hybrid structure. The limited communication channels, pointed out particularly by research students, highlight a potential weakness in the support system for the more self-directed learning that flexible pathways require. Furthermore, the strong student interest in topics like artificial intelligence and laboratory operations indicates a demand to expand the flexible electives to include more advanced and practical modules. The rapid integration of artificial intelligence (AI) in dental research, from automated image analysis to predicting treatment outcomes, offers significant opportunities to enhance both scientific exploration and clinical application [31–33]. Our data show strong student demand for AI-focused modules, linking new technological skills with research training needs. Future courses could include training on using AI to create personalized learning resources and explore its application in advanced dental technologies [34, 35]. These innovations will prepare students for future developments while establishing a feedback loop to ensure the curriculum adapts alongside technological advancements.

Implementation of the compulsory–flexible model requires coordinated faculty participation, basic research training materials, and opportunities for student–mentor interaction. During implementation, maintaining effective communication between instructors and students and organizing flexible elective activities were identified as practical challenges. Nevertheless, the model does not depend heavily on specialized infrastructure and may be adaptable for institutions with fewer resources. For example, core modules could be delivered through shared lectures or online platforms, while flexible components may be implemented through journal clubs, small-group discussions, or mentorship-based activities.

This study has several limitations that should be considered. First, this evaluation was conducted at a single institution, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to other educational contexts. Second, although multiple objective academic outcomes and student perceptions were assessed, long-term follow-up data on sustained research engagement and career outcomes were not available. Third, although the response rate was relatively high (75%), the possibility of non-response bias cannot be completely excluded, especially since the survey was anonymous and detailed information on non-respondents was unavailable. Students with stronger opinions—either positive or negative—may have been more likely to respond, which could influence the distribution of satisfaction ratings. Future multi-center studies with longitudinal designs and validated evaluation tools are needed to further assess the effectiveness and transferability of the compulsory–flexible teaching model.

Conclusion

The hybrid compulsory–flexible PDBRE course was associated with improved postgraduate research engagement and academic productivity. Differences in satisfaction between clinical and research-oriented students suggest that this model can accommodate diverse learning needs for clinically focused students while supporting advanced academic development for research-oriented students. From an educational perspective, the model may be particularly suitable for institutions seeking to strengthen research capacity while addressing curriculum congestion. By integrating a compulsory foundational module with flexible elective components, the curriculum can ensure core research competencies while allowing students to pursue individualized academic interests. Therefore, this framework may serve as a practical reference for dental schools with heterogeneous postgraduate training pathways.

Bridging the p-type gap in oxide electronics with 2D semiconductors
Authors: Taikyu Kim, Seokhyun Hwang, Jae Kyeong Jeong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Monolithic three-dimensional (3D) integration—collocating logic and memory in the back-end-of-line (BEOL)—offers higher bandwidth, lower latency, and improved energy efficiency for AI, cloud, and edge systems. Pairing two-dimensional p-type semiconductors with oxide n-channel transistors establishes a practical basis for complementary BEOL CMOS. This Review assesses manufacturing-aligned pathways to high-performance 2D p-channel transistors, covering transfer-free low-temperature growth, clean van der Waals contacts, gentle p-doping, and mitigation of crystallization, volatility, and interdiffusion. Recent gain-cell and vertical complementary field-effect transistor (CFET) demonstrations are examined, and a pragmatic outlook for BEOL p-type 2D semiconductors is outlined toward manufacturable, dense, low-power monolithic 3D chips.

Introduction

Rapid advances in AI, cloud computing, and edge systems are intensifying demands on semiconductor hardware, particularly in power delivery, thermal management, and interconnect bandwidth. As conventional scaling slows, minimizing data movement between memory and logic has become increasingly important for improving energy efficiency and system throughput1,2. Monolithic three-dimensional (3D) integration has therefore emerged as a promising architecture because vertically stacked device tiers can shorten interconnects, reduce parasitic delay and energy consumption, and enable dense logic–memory integration without through-silicon vias3–11. Realization of monolithic 3D strongly depends on back-end-of-line (BEOL)-compatible CMOS technologies that can be processed at ≤400 °C while maintaining high field-effect mobility (μFE), low subthreshold swing (SS), ultralow leakage, and scalable device dimensions12–14. These requirements largely preclude conventional high-temperature Si processing for upper tiers and motivate alternative low-temperature channel materials and integration strategies.

Among the candidate materials, two-dimensional (2D) semiconductors and amorphous oxide semiconductors have attracted particular interest. 2D semiconductors offer atomically thin channels, dangling-bond-free surfaces, and excellent electrostatic control suitable for aggressively scaled devices. However, transfer-free, wafer-scale growth of high-crystallinity 2D films on Si-compatible substrates at ≤400 °C remains challenging, and BEOL-compatible 2D platforms have not yet consistently outperformed amorphous oxide semiconductors under comparable thermal and leakage constraints. In contrast, amorphous oxide semiconductors such as InGaZnO (IGZO) and In2O3 can be deposited at low temperature by sputtering or atomic layer deposition (ALD), maintain excellent electron transport even in the amorphous phase, and exhibit ultralow off-state leakage owing to their wide bandgaps15–44. Nevertheless, realizing complementary oxide CMOS remains difficult because high-performance p-type oxides are fundamentally limited by localized O 2p-derived valence bands, large hole effective masses, and the instability or high-temperature processing requirements of candidate materials such as CuXO and SnO45–52.

In this context, p-type 2D semiconductors, particularly tungsten diselenide (WSe2)53–82 and tellurium (Te)53,83–95, have emerged as promising candidates to close the p-channel gap in oxide electronics while remaining compatible with BEOL thermal constraints. Compared with low-temperature p-channel oxide transistors, which typically exhibit μFE ≈ 1–5 cm2/Vs, SS ≈ 0.1–2 V/dec, and ION/OFF ≈ 103–10845–52, BEOL-compatible p-type 2D transistors have demonstrated substantially higher mobility and improved electrostatics. Although direct quantitative comparisons are challenging because the reported metrics were extracted using different device structures, bias conditions, and extraction methodologies, the overall performance trends support the potential of p-type 2D semiconductors as practical p-channel complements to mature n-type oxide platforms.

In this Review, we evaluate WSe2 and Te as BEOL-compatible p-channel counterparts to amorphous oxide n-channel transistors for monolithic 3D CMOS integration. We first discuss the electronic structure and low-temperature growth strategies of these materials, focusing on transfer-free wafer-scale WSe2 growth and crystallization-controlled Te deposition. We then examine critical integration bottlenecks, including contact resistance, p-type doping, interdiffusion, and channel volatility under BEOL thermal budgets. Finally, we review recent demonstrations of oxide/2D hybrid CMOS architectures including DRAM gain cells, vertical complementary field-effect transistors (CFETs), and SRAM and outline remaining challenges and opportunities for realizing practical low-temperature monolithic 3D CMOS systems. It is worth noting that the reported device metrics summarized in this review were extracted using different bias conditions, device architectures, and parameter extraction methodologies, making direct apple-to-apple comparisons inherently challenging. Nevertheless, the overall performance trends observed in p-type 2D semiconductors support their potential as practical p-channel complements to mature n-type oxide platforms under BEOL-compatible integration constraints.

BEOL-compatible growth strategies for WSe2 and Te

Transfer-free, BEOL-compatible growth of WSe2

WSe2 is a layered 2H transition-metal dichalcogenide composed of covalently bonded Se–W–Se layers stacked by van der Waals (vdW) interactions, resulting in atomically clean, dangling-bond-free surfaces and a relatively delocalized Se-p/W-d valence band53,54. Its moderate bandgap—indirect in bulk (≈1.2 eV) and direct in the monolayer (≈1.6–1.7 eV)—supports p-type transport while maintaining low off-state leakage under scaled electrostatics53. Because p-type behavior is mainly achieved through work-function alignment and charge-transfer modulation rather than high-temperature acceptor activation, WSe2 remains attractive for BEOL-compatible CMOS. Nevertheless, achieving transfer-free, wafer-scale growth with controlled stoichiometry, crystallinity, and low defect density remains a major challenge53–70.

Direct low-temperature CVD growth

Naylor et al. demonstrated 300 mm wafer-scale WSe2 growth at ≤450 °C using metal-organic CVD (MOCVD), followed by fabrication of few-layer transistors with Au/Ru contacts59. The resulting 30 nm-channel devices exhibited ION up to 15 μA/μm, demonstrating the feasibility of direct BEOL-compatible WSe2 growth. Wang et al. further developed a non-local LPCVD scheme that decouples precursor and substrate temperatures, enabling transfer-free monolayer WSe2 growth on SiO2 at 450 °C68. By maintaining the WO2 + KCl precursor zone near 890 °C while keeping the substrate at 450 °C, sufficient WO2Cl2 precursor flux was sustained to form triangular single-crystal monolayer domains (~20 µm) (Fig. 1a). Raman spectra showed only minor FWHM broadening at 450 °C compared with higher growth temperatures (Fig. 1b), while cross-sectional HRTEM confirmed clean, damage-free WSe2/SiO2 interfaces at both channel and contact regions (Fig. 1c). The fabricated monolayer devices achieved μFE of 45 cm2/Vs and ION of 64 μA/μm at L = 1 μm. More notably, short-channel devices with L = 100 nm exhibited ION/OFF of 3.6 × 107, gm of 190 μS/μm at VDS = − 1 V, and ION of 350 μA/μm (Fig. 1d–f). Growth-based vertical CMOS integration

Kim et al. introduced a confined-selective growth strategy that produces single-crystalline WSe2 directly on amorphous BEOL dielectrics (a-HfO2) at <400 °C, enabling growth-based monolithic 3D without transfers70. Patterned SiO2 pockets atop a-HfO2 acted as heterogeneous nucleation traps: as the temperature was lowered to 385 °C, nucleation became edge-dominated (→100% at trench edges), yielding one nucleus per trench and preventing secondary nucleation before lateral coalescence completed (Fig. 2a). Density functional theory (DFT) corroborated the method, showing weaker binding on a-HfO2 than on c-HfO2 and a binding-energy increase at SiO2 edges, which together rationalized why edge-guided nucleation succeeded at the low temperature. Film crystallinity was verified by photoluminescence (PL) with FWHM of ~50 meV and Raman that remained essentially unchanged down to 385 °C, and by high-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM) confirming single-domain WSe2 without grain-boundary signatures under optimized confinement (Fig. 2b–d). Building on this method, the authors fabricated single-crystal WSe2 transistors on a-HfO2 at 485 °C, encapsulated them, and then demonstrated single-crystal MoS2 transistors at 385 °C directly on top to realize a seamless vertical CMOS stack (Fig. 2e). The low-temperature top growth preserved the underlying WSe2, and marked degradation only appeared when the top growth was raised to 485–585 °C (Fig. 2f). The WSe2 transistors before the MoS2 growth exhibited an ION of 82.9 μA/μm at L = 400 nm and VDS = 0.5 V, while achieving a high ION/OFF of 6.59 × 106. The arrays exhibited small device-to-device spreads in ION (~16.95% for WSe2 and ~12.86% for MoS2) and a 93.8% yield across 32 CMOS inverters. Inverters formed from the vertical CMOS showed strong voltage gain and large noise margins over VDD = 0.5–1.5 V (Fig. 2g), and the platform scaled to NAND/NOR logic with stable timing (Fig. 2h). BEOL-compatible Te growth and crystallization control

Elemental Te crystallizes in a trigonal structure of covalently bonded helical chains weakly coupled by vdW interactions, producing highly anisotropic, 1D-like valence transport53,83. Its bandgap is thickness-dependent, increasing from ~0.33–0.35 eV in bulk to ~0.5–1 eV in few-layer tellurene due to quantum confinement53,83. Te has therefore emerged as a promising BEOL-compatible p-type semiconductor because it combines strong electrical performance with highly manufacturable low-temperature deposition methods such as PVD and ALD83–95. However, practical integration requires suppression of Te’s explosive crystallization, where rapid chain ordering forms ultrafine defective grains even near room temperature, increasing grain-boundary scattering, trap density, and device variability, particularly in scaled devices.

Wafer-scale evaporation at cryogenic temperature

Zhao et al. suppressed Te’s explosive crystallization by thermally evaporating Te onto cryogenic substrates (−80 °C), reducing adatom mobility and slowing nucleation/coalescence84. As the substrate temperature decreased from −10 to −60 °C, domain size increased monotonically, whereas room-temperature growth produced discontinuous nanoparticle-like films (Fig. 3a–c). Uniform structural characteristics were achieved across 4-inch wafers, confirming scalability. The resulting 8-nm-thick Te transistors exhibited μeff of ~35 cm2/Vs, ION/OFF of ~104, and SS of ~108 mV/dec with tight device-to-device variation (Fig. 3d–g). The improved mobility at −80 °C was attributed to reduced grain-boundary scattering from enlarged domains. Devices also maintained stable electrical characteristics for 30 days (Fig. 3h). Thickness scaling revealed the expected trade-off: thicker films improved mobility but reduced ION/OFF due to bandgap narrowing (Fig. 3i). In-situ oxygen-radical incorporation

Kim et al. demonstrated a fully CMOS-compatible route to p-type Te using room-temperature DC sputtering, low-temperature post-deposition annealing (PDA), and ALD Al2O3 encapsulation87. This process stabilized Te against sublimation and oxidation, reduced residual TeOX during ALD, and promoted grain coarsening through interfacial-energy-driven crystallization. Polarized light microscopy (PLM) and cross-sectional HRTEM revealed significant domain growth and improved crystallinity after 150 °C annealing and Al2O3 encapsulation, while amorphous TeO/TeO2 phases disappeared (Fig. 4a–c). X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) showed TeO2 formed during PDA was reduced to metallic Te after encapsulation due to trimethylaluminum-assisted reduction during Al2O3 growth (Fig. 4d). Building on this low-temperature route, oxygen radicals were later introduced during reactive sputtering to intentionally suppress Te’s explosive crystallization88. By tuning the oxygen-plasma fraction [PO = O2/(Ar+O2)], oxygen radicals stabilized amorphous as-deposited Te films by interrupting runaway chain ordering (Fig. 4g). Subsequent low-temperature Al2O3 encapsulation then induced controlled crystallization into Te. Grazing incidence X-ray diffraction (GIXRD) showed that an optimal PO of ~7% preserved dominant (100)/(101) orientations associated with layer-parallel stacking, while suppressing minor randomly oriented grains (Fig. 4h). Excess oxygen (>7%) degraded crystallization and altered the preferred orientation. After encapsulation, 4-nm Te transistors fabricated with PO ≈ 7% achieved the best performance, with μFE up to 40.8 cm2/Vs and ION/OFF ≈ 1.1 × 106, whereas films without oxygen suffered from random polycrystallinity and films with excessive oxygen showed transport degradation from residual amorphization (Fig. 4i). These studies established oxygen-assisted TeOX formation as an effective kinetic knob for controlling Te crystallization and microstructure at ≤150 °C, enabling uniform wafer-scale p-type Te transistors compatible with BEOL integration.

Critical challenges for WSe2and Te

Even with BEOL-compatible growth, circuit-level performance and manufacturability remain limited by integration-sensitive bottlenecks that differ between WSe2 and Te. For WSe2, metal-induced gap states (MIGS) and process-induced disorder often increase contact resistance (RC). For Te, metal interdiffusion and material volatility under BEOL thermal budgets can degrade junction integrity and device uniformity despite favorable channel mobility. In this section, we compare these key limitations and discuss integration-compatible mitigation strategies for each material system.

Critical role of clean contacts and p-type doping for WSe2

At scaled L, the WSe2/metal edge sets the performance floor. Evaporated metals tend to hybridize with the chalcogenide and create MIGS, which pin the Fermi level (EF) away from the valence band and impose a large, thick hole barrier that dominates total resistance even when the channel is ideal54–56. This problem is particularly acute for BEOL tiers, where high-temperature silicidation, aggressive implantation, or alloy-forming contact schemes are generally incompatible with thermal-budget and materials-integration constraints. Consequently, practical routes to low RC in p-type WSe2 have converged on two integration-aligned directions: (i) reducing interfacial disorder to approach a clean, weakly interacting (vdW-like) contact, and (ii) re-shaping the near-contact band alignment through low-temperature p-type doping, ideally confined to the access/contact vicinity to avoid degrading channel electrostatics.

Clean vdW contacts with unpinned interfaces

One major strategy for lowering RC in p-type WSe2 is contact engineering aimed at suppressing interfacial reactions, minimizing defects, and reducing MIGS-driven Fermi-level pinning. Besides conventional evaporated metals, transferred metals and semimetal contacts have been explored to form cleaner, less reactive interfaces with improved hole injection65,66. However, even high-work-function contacts can suffer from large effective hole barriers if interfacial disorder or contamination remains. These studies highlight that contact fabrication conditions—such as vacuum level, thermal load, and deposition sequence—can be as important as the contact material itself because interfacial electronic structure often dominates RC.

Patoary et al. showed that RC in p-channel WSe2 transistors is strongly influenced by process-induced disorder in the under-contact region rather than solely by the intrinsic Schottky barrier67. By moving from standard e-beam evaporation (~10−5 Torr) to higher-vacuum deposition (~10−6–10−7 Torr) and temperature-controlled stepped deposition, they minimized interface damage during metallization. Using Pt/Pd contacts, this low-damage process reduced RC to ~5.7 kΩ·µm and improved hole injection, demonstrating the importance of BEOL-compatible deposition control.

Wang et al. further realized clean, transfer-free vdW Pt contacts on WSe2 by maintaining near-room-temperature conditions during e-beam evaporation, thereby suppressing deposition-induced damage and Fermi-level pinning64. Annular dark field scanning TEM (ADF-STEM), XPS, and electron energy loss spectroscopy (EELS) confirmed chemically abrupt, unreacted Pt/WSe2 interfaces, with a Pt–Se separation of ~2.3 ± 0.1 Å and no detectable PtSe2 formation [Fig. 5a(i),(ii)]. The resulting few-layer WSe2 transistors exhibited pure p-type behavior with μFE of 190 ± 10 cm2/Vs, ION/OFF of ~107 (Fig. 5b,c), and RC of 3.3 kΩ·µm (Fig. 5d), attributed to a low hole Schottky barrier height of 200 ± 20 meV. Devices with damaged or intermixed interfaces showed markedly degraded performance. Contact-proximal p-type doping

A second, complementary strategy for reducing RC in p-type WSe2 is contact-proximal p-type doping, which lowers the effective hole barrier while suppressing electron injection. Two major approaches have been explored. Substitutional acceptor doping provides robust covalent incorporation72–74, but often requires complex processing and strict defect control that can complicate BEOL integration. In contrast, surface charge-transfer doping uses high-work-function caps or adsorbates to withdraw electrons from WSe2 and modulate carrier density55,56,75–81. Because it is typically low-temperature and minimally disruptive to the lattice, this approach is particularly attractive for BEOL-compatible processing82.

Yang et al. demonstrated a selective UV/ozone charge-transfer doping scheme that selectively dopes the contact regions of WSe281. UV/ozone treatment at 100 °C converted only the exposed WSe2 near the source/drain overlap into an amorphous WOX cap, while hBN encapsulation protected the channel from oxidation, forming a p–i–p structure with a sharp WOX/WSe2 interface. The high-work-function WOX induced upward band bending near the contacts, reducing the hole barrier and enabling Ohmic-like transport. As UV/ozone exposure time increased, RC decreased and eventually saturated while channel resistance remained nearly unchanged, confirming that the improvement originated from contact modulation. The treated devices showed substantially improved μFE and ION/OFF while maintaining normally-off p-type operation.

Lan et al. developed a CMOS/BEOL-compatible NO annealing process for WSe2 that improved p-type injection through low-temperature gas-phase doping without plasmas or high-temperature treatments56. DFT calculations showed that NO molecules occupying Se vacancies introduced acceptor-like states near the mid-gap region, shifting the Fermi level toward the valence band maximum (VBM) (Fig. 5e, f). XPS and Raman measurements confirmed that moderate NO annealing preserved crystallinity while enabling p-type charge transfer, whereas excessive treatment caused oxidation and degraded transport. Optimized NO annealing conditions (120–130 °C) reduced the hole Schottky barrier while increasing the electron barrier, producing unipolar p-type transport and suppressing the electron branch. Monolayer WSe2 devices showed ION/OFF > 109 with improved SS and positive threshold-voltage shifts (Fig. 5g, h). Short-channel devices with L = 55 nm achieved ION of ~300 μA/μm at VDS = –1 V, while bilayer devices reached ION of ~448 μA/μm and low RC of ~390 Ω µm (Fig. 5i, j), approaching leading n-type monolayer transistor performance. By locally engineering acceptor states near the contacts, this NO treatment simultaneously lowered RC and enforced p-unipolarity, making it one of the most BEOL-compatible contact-improvement strategies reported for WSe2.

Interdiffusion and volatility constraints under BEOL budgets for Te

Elemental Te consists of trigonal helical chains weakly coupled by van der Waals interactions, giving rise to low cohesive energy and high grain-boundary diffusivity even at relatively low temperatures. As a result, metal/Te interfaces are highly susceptible to interdiffusion and reactive telluride formation within typical BEOL thermal budgets90. In addition, Te exhibits relatively high vapor pressure, enabling sublimation even under mild heating96,97. Because the vapor phase above solid Te is dominated by Te298, small temperature increases can cause significant Te loss and thickness or stoichiometry variation in ultrathin films. These effects lead to surface roughening and contact-edge recession, particularly near metal junctions. Together, interdiffusion and volatility impose major constraints on BEOL integration, requiring careful control to maintain junction integrity and reliable circuit-level performance.

Thermally stable Ru contacts

Rahman et al. systematically investigated contact-metal stability for Te under BEOL thermal budgets90. Annealing studies showed that Ni and Pd rapidly intermixed with Te through diffusion-driven reactions, whereas Ru exhibited no measurable interdiffusion up to 250 °C for 2 h (Fig. 6a-c). Pt showed only limited diffusion. At higher temperatures (~300 °C), Te also diffused into reactive metals, causing Te depletion near contacts, while Ru still remained comparatively stable. These results established a clear hierarchy in contact stability: Ni/Pd formed broad mixed-phase reaction regions, whereas Ru maintained a relatively abrupt interface due to suppressed diffusivity. This interfacial stability directly translated into transistor performance. Ru formed thermally robust, near-Ohmic contacts to p-type Te with RC of ~1.25 kΩ μm. Temperature-dependent transfer characteristics further substantiated Schottky-barrier–free injection at the Ru contacts (Fig. 6d). As temperature decreased, the ID-VGS curves exhibited a clear mobility enhancement consistent with suppressed phonon scattering, accompanied by a negative shift in threshold voltage. The Arrhenius analysis of ln[(ID)/T3/2] versus 1000/T showed a positive slope that saturated at low temperature (Fig. 6e). Within the thermionic-emission framework for 2D Schottky transistors, the positive slope signals mobility improvement at reduced temperatures, while the low temperature saturation is indicative of a vanishing contact barrier, providing additional evidence for ohmic Ru–Te interfaces. In parallel, contact-metal robustness in Te transistors under BEOL budgets was investigated. Although Ni initially exhibited similar RC, degradation emerged above ~175 °C, where RC and ION/OFF deteriorated due to Ni–Te alloy formation (Fig. 6f–i). At temperatures above 200 °C, alloying propagated across the channel and electrically shorted the devices. BEOL CMOS integration with oxide semiconductor

Monolithic BEOL CMOS that integrates p-type 2D semiconductors with n-type amorphous oxides provides a pragmatic solution to the long-standing p-channel deficit in oxide electronics while respecting ≤400 °C thermal budgets. By combining 2D p-channels with the process uniformity and ultralow leakage of oxide n-transistors, this heterostack enables low-VDD complementary operation, and compatibility with established BEOL dielectrics and metals, thereby defining a manufacturable pathway to sequential 3D integration. In this section, we review recent vertical CFET demonstrations, emphasizing the architectural leverage of stacked p/n tiers.

Te-IGZO CFET

Niu et al. demonstrated the first wafer-scale, BEOL-compatible Te–IGZO CFETs, establishing Te as a viable p-type counterpart to oxide n-channels within a fully ALD-based CMOS-compatible process window91. ALD-grown TeOX/Te heterostructures showed excellent conformality even on fin-type topographies, highlighting the manufacturability of the process. The Te transistors exhibited ION/OFF of ~103 at room temperature, improving to 106–107 at 33 K, with Ni-contact RC of 25.6 Ω mm. Under a thermal budget of ≤225 °C, ALD Te transistors were vertically integrated above ALD IGZO transistors in a shared-gate CFET architecture (Fig. 7a, b). The resulting CFETs achieved rail-to-rail switching, a voltage gain of 116.5 V/V at VDD = 5 V, and a noise margin of 88%, validating oxide-based BEOL CMOS with a true p-type component. Sun et al. further reported enhanced gain in Te/ITO complementary inverters at cryogenic temperature, suggesting additional performance gains for Te/IGZO CFETs under low-temperature operation92. Te0.8Se0.2-IGZO DRAM, CFET and SRAM

While Te offers intrinsic p-type conductivity and low-temperature BEOL processability, its narrow bandgap leads to high IOFF, motivating bandgap engineering rather than replacement of the Te platform. Xu et al. addressed this by co-sputtering Te and Se to form Te1−XSeX alloys using BEOL-compatible processes, including room-temperature deposition of 7-nm channels, Al2O3/HfO2 encapsulation, and 150 °C PDA93,94. DFT calculations showed that Se alloying widened the bandgap through opposite shifts of the CBM and VBM (Fig. 7c), while XPS confirmed stable, largely non-oxidized Te and Se states after encapsulation. Increasing Se content suppressed IOFF and improved SS, with optimized Te0.8Se0.2 transistors exhibiting μeff of ~24 cm2/Vs, ION/OFF > 104, and SS of ~300 mV/dec. Importantly, short-channel devices with L = 50 nm maintained robust electrostatics and competitive drive current, demonstrating scalable p-channel operation with reduced leakage. Beyond device optimization, vertically integrating Te0.8Se0.2 with IGZO enabled BEOL-compatible 3D N–P DRAM with >1000 s retention (Fig. 7d, e), where Te0.8Se0.2 and IGZO served as read and write transistors, respectively. The authors also demonstrated a vertical CFET inverter with gain of ~38 at VDD = 2.5 V (Fig. 7f, g), followed by the first CFET-based 4 T oxide SRAM (Fig. 7h, i), validating dense, monolithically stackable logic–memory architectures under BEOL thermal budgets. Collectively, Se-alloyed Te provides a practical route to scalable, low-temperature p-type transistors that complement oxide n-channels for monolithic 3D integration.

Toward BEOL oxide/2D hybrid CMOS

Performance evaluation at the state of the art

To benchmark the current state of the art under BEOL-compatible constraints, we compare representative WSe2 and Te transistor demonstrations using the key electrical figures of merit and short-channel status achieved within BEOL thermal ceilings. Because these metrics are strongly influenced by contact formation, dielectric scaling, and process thermal budget, cross-study comparisons are interpreted within BEOL-aligned integration windows rather than as absolute best-case values.

Within this BEOL-aligned dataset, WSe2 and Te occupy distinct performance envelopes. WSe2 devices report μFE ≈ 30–60 cm2/Vs56,68,70. They also exhibit SS ≈ 80–200 mV/dec56,68 ION/OFF ≈ 106–10956,64,68,70, and RC ≈ 0.4–3 kΩ·µm56,64, with short-channel scaling down to 55 nm56. For Te, representative reports show μFE ≈ 20–40 cm2/Vs (typically for ~4 nm channels with thickness-dependent transport)84,87,88,93,94, SS ≈ 100–300 mV/dec84,93,94, ION/OFF ≈ 104–10684,87,88,90,93,94, and RC ≈ 1–2 kΩ µm90, with L scaled to 50 nm93,94. These metrics directly impact circuit-level operation in BEOL CFETs and logic–memory fabrics, where RC determines drive strength and switching speed, while SS and ION/OFF govern leakage and retention under stacked-tier operation. For BEOL-integrated p-type power switches in BSPDN-enabled 3D systems, commonly targeted metrics include RON⋅W ≈ 1 kΩ⋅μm and ION/OFF > 105. Under this perspective, WSe2 is naturally advantageous for leakage-sensitive operation due to its larger bandgap and stronger electrostatics, whereas Te requires simultaneous optimization of leakage and contact resistance within BEOL thermal budgets. From a manufacturing standpoint, Te currently provides the most practical route to BEOL-compatible p-channel transistors, whereas WSe2 remains the stronger long-term candidate for ultra-low-leakage applications. Remaining challenges for BEOL-compatible p-type 2D semiconductors. Achieving truly complementary low-temperature BEOL CMOS at VDD ≤ 1 V requires coordinated progress in materials, interfaces, and architecture. Although recent WSe2 and Te devices have individually approached targets for IOFF, RC, and ION, no single BEOL-compatible platform yet satisfies all metrics simultaneously within one integration flow. The remaining priorities are therefore defined by circuit-level requirements for oxide/2D CFETs and logic–memory systems, including low-VDD operation, leakage suppression, variability control, and stacked-tier reliability.

At the channel level, transfer-free growth must evolve from wafer-scale feasibility toward line-width-scale uniformity. Manufacturable monolithic 3D integration requires tight distributions in VTH, ION, RC, and leakage. These challenges are especially severe for Te because crystallization and volatility can worsen under stacked-tier self-heating. For Te-based devices, microstructural stabilization combined with Te–Se bandgap engineering will be essential to achieve IOFF ≈ 10−13 A/μm together with ION ≈ 1 mA/μm in short-channel devices. Additional approaches, such as ultrathin seed layers, may further improve wafer-scale crystal orientation95.

Contact engineering must also progress toward RC ≤ 500 Ω μm with minimal variability. Clean vdW contacts and mild charge-transfer doping are promising BEOL-compatible strategies for reducing RC and enforcing p-unipolarity in both WSe2 and Te. However, aggressive contact doping can introduce disorder, oxidation, VTH instability, and reliability degradation. In addition, the effectiveness of contact de-pinning can depend on channel thickness and electrostatic screening because MIGS penetration evolves with layer thickness. Ultrathin chalcogenide channels are also vulnerable to interface evolution during sequential BEOL integration, including oxidation, interdiffusion, and trap generation under thermal stress and self-heating conditions. Consequently, contact optimization must balance resistance reduction with long-term stability, variability control, and process compatibility.

Reliability is another critical requirement for stacked-tier monolithic 3D integration, where electrical operation is strongly coupled to self-heating, inter-tier thermal coupling, and process-induced mechanical stress. Future qualification protocols should systematically evaluate bias-temperature instability, hot-carrier degradation, and dielectric breakdown across both semiconductor/dielectric and metal/semiconductor interfaces. At present, reliability reporting for BEOL-compatible p-type 2D transistors remains limited and non-uniform, hindering meaningful benchmarking. Standardized evaluation of moisture permeation, oxidation, and stress-induced defect evolution under BEOL thermal excursions is therefore urgently needed.

Beyond individual devices, system-level parasitics can ultimately limit the advantages of monolithic 3D integration. For oxide/2D CFETs, implementing gate-all-around architectures remains challenging because wrap-around gating of ultrathin 2D channels requires precise control of edge damage, sidewall cleanliness, and contact–gate separation. In addition, vertically stacked tiers introduce parasitic capacitances that can degrade noise margins, increase dynamic power, and offset delay reductions. A recent benchmarking study showed that system throughput can become parasitic-capacitance-limited even when transistor-level metrics remain favorable99. Thermal coupling and mismatches in thermal expansion between oxide and 2D layers can further induce stress and warpage in hybrid stacks. As tier density increases, overlay accuracy, routing congestion, and interconnect RC delay must therefore be co-optimized together with device geometry and process conditions through integration-aware DTCO frameworks.

All-oxide CMOS remains constrained by the absence of high-performance BEOL-compatible p-type oxides, while all-2D CMOS still faces major manufacturability barriers associated with wafer-scale growth and contact control. In this context, oxide/2D hybrid CMOS provides a pragmatic pathway by combining mature, ultralow-leakage oxide n-channels with BEOL-compatible p-type 2D semiconductors. With continued advances in low-temperature growth, stable contacts, encapsulation, leakage-aware band engineering, and DTCO-guided integration, p-type 2D semiconductors integrated with oxide transistors could form a practical BEOL device platform for dense and energy-efficient monolithic 3D CMOS beyond conventional scaling.

Case Report: Short-term response to mesenchymal epithelial transition inhibitor/epidermal growth factor receptor tyrosine kinase inhibitor combination in EGFR-mutated non-small cell lung cancer with acquired MET overexpression after progression and discussion on comprehensive management
Authors: Xiaofeng Zhou, Niharika Prasad, Sanjay Jaiswal, Kui Wu, Wenzheng Sun, Li Shen, Lihua Wang, Xueping Xiang, Xiaojuan He, Lin Zheng, Chao-Chao Qin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Epidermal growth factor receptor tyrosine kinase inhibitor (EGFR-TKI) is a priority and important treatment for EGFR-mutated non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and significantly improves patient’s survival. However, these patients eventually develop disease progression due to acquired resistance to EGFR-TKI. Mesenchymal epithelial transition (MET) gene alteration is one of the common EGFR-independent mechanisms to resist EGFR-TKI. This study reports that a case with chief complaint of dry cough for six months was diagnosed with right lung adenocarcinoma (cT4N3M1c, stage IVB). After first-generation EGFR-TKI treatment, the patient underwent disease progression. After a multi-disciplinary team (MDT) discussion, a re-biopsy and next-generation sequencing (NGS) profiling were performed again, which indicated that the patient harbored the EGFR exon21 L858R mutation and the exon20 T790M mutation. According to the MDT discussion, the patient received the third-generation EGFR-TKI treatment and stereotactic body radiation therapy (SBRT), achieving long-term survival with a high quality of life. After three years of the third-generation EGFR-TKI treatment, the disease progressed again, and a biopsy indicated multiple mutations. After disease progression during the third-generation EGFR-TKI treatment and radiotherapy, the patient received combined treatment with first-generation and the third-generation EGFR-TKI, but could not tolerate the combined treatment. After three courses of chemotherapy plus bevacizumab targeted therapy, the disease continued to progress. Without other effective treatments, the patient received dual-targeted therapy with the third-generation EGFR-TKI and MET-TKI for one month, and the patient experienced a reduction of 51.4% in the size of the lesions in the chest. Unfortunately, the patient developed a lung infection and died of a lung infection. With the assistance of MDT recommendations, the patient received comprehensive treatment and remained asymptomatic, with persistent improvement in the quality of life for 4 years. The overall survival was approximately 56.5 months. Patients with metastatic EGFR-mutated NECLC require multidisciplinary discussions and comprehensive treatment. When the disease progresses, rebiopsy and NGS profiling are required to identify sensitive targeted drugs. Whether MET overexpression is a mechanism for the resistance to third-generation EGFR-TKI in NSCLC patients with EGFR mutations remains to be further investigated.

Introduction

Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) accounts for approximately 85% of lung cancer cases (1). The IRESSA Pan-Asia Study (IPASS) marked a milestone in the clinical management of NSCLC, pioneering the era of EGFR-targeted therapy (2). EGFR-TKIs significantly improve progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) in patients with EGFR-mutated NSCLC (3). However, patients eventually develop disease progression due to acquired resistance.

Mesenchymal epithelial transition (MET) gene is an oncogene located on chromosome 7 long arm (7q21-q31) and encodes a transmembrane receptor tyrosine kinase (4). Hepatocyte growth factor (HGF) binding activates the HGF/MET signaling axis, triggering intracellular cascades that regulate tumor proliferation and metastasis (4). Abnormal activation of the MET is a major bypass resistance mechanism driving EGFR-TKI resistance in NSCLC (5).

Here, we present a case with stage IV NSCLC, who received comprehensive treatment, and achieved a long and high-quality survival. This NSCLC patient with EGFR mutations and MET overexpression achieved a short-term response to a combined treatment of osimertinib and gumarontinib after progression on multiple lines of therapy.

Case presentation

Initial treatment

A 62-year-old non-smoking Chinese female peasant without family history of malignancy was admitted to our hospital on July 25, 2019, with a 6-month history of progressive dyspnea and dry cough. Her Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status was 0. This patient had one daughter and two sons. Her families were all in good health. The patient’s right lung had weakened breathing sounds and no rales. The respiratory sounds of the left lung were normal. Dual-target therapy of osimertinib and gumarontinib

This patient harbored EGFR L858R/T790M/C797S in trans/cis mutations, with MET overexpression (IHC 2+), without definite and effective treatment. A phase III study (9) revealed MET-TKI plus erlotinib improved both PFS and OS in 211 MET-overexpressed (IHC 2+) NSCLC patients. A pooled analysis suggested gumarontinib (a MET-TKI) showed promising efficacy in driver-gene negative locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC patients with MET overexpression (IHC 3+), with an ORR of 37.5% (10). This patient may benefit from the dual-target therapy of third-generation EGFR-TKI plus MET-TKI. With MDT approval and family consent, the patient initiated osimertinib 80 mg daily plus gumarontinib 150 mg daily on February 29, 2024. No adverse toxicities were observed within two weeks, and the gumarontinib dose was escalated to 200 mg daily until April 15, 2024. After one month of dual-targeted therapy, chest CT showed the sum of the maximum diameters of the three target lesions in the chest decreases by 51.4% (Figure 4). The reduction of these lesions could not be regarded as a partial response because at that time the patient’s ECOG score was relatively high and a comprehensive examination had not been conducted, which may not rule out the possibility of new lesions appearing. However, the patient still had severely limited mobility and was bedridden, with an ECOG score of 4. The lung infection also became more severe than before. A thorough auscultation of the patient’s lungs reveals diffuse coarse crackles. Imipenem was administered for anti-infection treatment. Ultimately, the patient’s lung infection gradually worsened. The patient’s family gave up the treatment, and the patient died on April 16, 2024. No toxicity related to osimertinib and gumarontinib was observed.

In summary, this patient was diagnosed with NSCLC harboring EGFR L858R mutation on July 30, 2019, and died on April 16, 2024. The patient remained asymptomatic and enjoyed a high-quality life (ECOG 0-1) for 4 years with the assistance of comprehensive treatment. The overall survival was approximately 56.5 months.

Discussion

We described an NSCLC patient harboring primary EGFR L858R mutation, and secondary EGFRT790M/C797S mutations plus MET overexpression, without MET amplification or exon 14-skipping. For EGFR-mutated NSCLC patients, when resistance to EGFR-TKI occurs, re-biopsy and NGS profiling are critical for identifying resistance mechanisms and guiding individualized treatment regimen for EGFR-TKI-resistant NSCLC. This patient achieved a long survival with high-quality life (ECOG 0-1), mainly due to the following reasons. Firstly, when the patient developed disease progresses, we conducted re-biopsies and NGS, which helped to identify effective treatment regimen. After the third disease progression and biopsy, there was no definite and effective treatment for this patient harboring EGFR L858R/T790M/C797S in trans/cis mutations, with MET overexpression. This patient and her families chose to attempt to receive erlotinib and osimertinib, chemotherapy, as well as osimertinib combined with gumarontinib. Secondly, we successfully implemented a comprehensive treatment approach. When local progression occurred, we recommended targeted therapy combined with SBRT to persistent lesions based on the guidelines, previous studies and MDT discussions. Consistent with previous studies (11, 12), the MDT treatment model significantly improved survival outcomes, with the 56.5-month OS of this patient far exceeding the real-world median OS of 40.60 months for EGFR-TKI-treated NSCLC patients (13).

After 34 months of taking osimertinib, this patient experienced disease progression again and developed EGFR C797S mutation and MET overexpression. The secondary resistance mechanisms to osimertinib can be classified into two categories: EGFR- dependent mechanisms with the C797S mutation being the most common and EGFR-independent mechanisms, such as MET alterations (14–17).

Due to osimertinib binding the C797 residue, the C797S mutation impairs its binding capacity, thus resulting in resistance (18). Recent study has found that 82% of patients develop C797S in cis with T790M, while 10% develop it in trans, 6% develop C797S alone without T790M, and 2% develop two co-existing C797S clones (one in cis with T790M and one in trans) (19). C797S/T790M in cis or trans conformation is important because this conformation can affect the sensitivity of subsequent treatment (20). It was demonstrated that if C797S and T790M occur in trans, cancer cells will become resistant to third-generation EGFR-TKIs, but sensitive to a combination of first- and third-generation TKIs. If they are in cis, cancer cells will resist any EGFR-TKIs alone or in combination (20). This case was tested in two different laboratories. One laboratory detected an in trans mutation, while the other detected an in cis mutation.

The possible reasons for these two different test results are as follows. Firstly, different subclonals and biopsy samples, regarded as tumor heterogeneity, may lead to different test results. Zhao et al. reported that resistance to osimertinib is highly heterogenous not only among different individuals but also within a single patient (21). This patient received erlotinib plus osimertinib after developing resistance to osimertinib. However, she developed disease progression two months after taking erlotinib plus osimertinib. This might suggest the proportion of the clones with C797S in trans mutation in our case was relatively low. Short-term administration of erlotinib plus osimertinib for two months may resulted in the therapeutic effect might not have yet manifested. It seems that the clones with C797S in trans and in cis mutation can coexist in a single patient. Secondly, different detection platform, technique and reagent in our report may lead to different test results from two laboratories. The detection platforms, techniques and reagents used in the two laboratories had been certified. It’s difficult to determine which laboratory’s test result was accurate in a single patient. Actually, we couldn’t re-test the C797S mutation in the third laboratory due to the lack of sufficient samples. Among several possible influencing factors, we are inclined to believe tumor heterogeneity may play an important role and resistant mechanisms of C797S in trans and cis against osimertinib coexist. However, it’s necessary to interpret the results of C797S mutation in this case cautiously.

MET overexpression can arise from MET amplification, mutation or transcriptional enhancement (22). MET fusion, point mutation, amplification and exon 14-skipping were not detected in both tests after the third biopsy. FISH, considered the gold standard for the detection of MET amplification (23), did not detect MET amplification (Figure 2E). Therefore, MET overexpression was independent of MET amplification or exon 14-skipping in the present case.

The MET overexpression have recently emerged as a potential target for targeted therapy. The combination of EGFR-TKI and MET inhibitor improved the OS and PFS of patients with MET overexpression (IHC ≥ 2+) (9, 24). Gumarontinib, a highly selective MET-TKI, demonstrated a favorable safety profile and promising efficacy (10, 25, 26). The present case chose to attempt the dual-targeted therapy of osimertinib and gumarontinib for one and half a month. Although chest CT showed a reduction of 51.4% in the size of the lesions in the chest after one month of dual-targeted therapy, it’s difficult to determine whether the dual-targeted therapy caused the lesions to shrink. For EGFR-mutant NSCLC patients with MET overexpression, dual-targeted therapy of EGFR-TKI with MET inhibitors may be worthy of further investigation and has potential feasibility. This case report may provide possible hints for the treatment of EGFR-mutated NSCLC after resistance to third-generation EGFR-TKI. It’s necessary to explore the resisting third-generation EGFR-TKI mechanism related to MET overexpression, as well as how the MET gene signaling pathway interacts with the EGFR signaling pathway to develop this resistance.

We recognize several limitations in this study. Firstly, it was a case report with potential for bias. This limitation reminds us that the results should be interpreted with caution. Secondly, since the dual-targeted therapy of osimertinib and gumarontinib was only administered for one month and a half, it was not sufficient to assess the efficacy and toxicity of it. The therapeutic effect and toxicity need to be evaluated over a longer period of time. Thirdly, the interval between the dual-targeted therapy of osimertinib plus gumarontinib and the previous treatment was too short to assess the efficacy of this dual-targeted therapy. Finally, as a case report, we didn’t validate the function of MET overexpression. To reduce these biases and limitations, it is necessary to conduct further investigation.

Conclusion

For patients with stage IV EGFR-mutated NSCLC, the first-line treatment is undoubtedly EGFR-TKI targeted therapy. When they developed disease progresses, we need to conduct re-biopsies and NGS, which helped to identify effective targeted therapy regimen. EGFR exon20 T790M/C797S mutations are the common secondary resistance mechanisms. Resisting third-generation EGFR-TKI mechanism related to MET overexpression remains to be further investigated. Due to the limitations, we should interpret these preliminary results cautiously. Additional research is warranted to evaluate the safety and efficacy of dual-targeted therapy with osimertinib and gumarontinib for EGFR-mutated NSCLC patients harboring secondary MET overexpression.

Hydrogen storage efficiency and optoelectronic properties of structutal, mechanical, and dynamical stable perovskite (Li/Na/K)BH3 hydrides for sustainable energy applications
Authors: Hudabia Murtaza, Mohamed Shalaby, Quratul Ain, Junaid Munir, Ahmed B M Ibrahim, S E Shcheklein, M A Habib, Mokhtar S S Al-Salimi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAlkali-based perovskite hydrides, with their exceptional stability and high hydrogen storage capacity, stand out as highly promising candidates for advanced hydrogen storage technologies. We have analyzed the physical traits of (Li/Na/K)BH3 by using first principles investigation under the density functional theory framework. The exchange correlation effects within the crystal structures are analyzed deeply. The tolerance factor, ground state energies for (Li/Na/K)BH3 confirms structural integrity. All structures are also dynamically stable. The optimized lattice parameters of the studied hydrides are obtained via Birch’s equation of state. Three independent elastic constants and mechanical features for (Li/Na/K)BH3 are explored. The computed moduli are in the order of KBH3 > NaBH3 > LiBH3, which implies that KBH3 is stiffer than the others. For each crystallographic plane, the directional sound velocities are also computed. The electronic band structures reveal the metallic nature of (Li/Na/K)BH3. An in-depth investigation of the optical properties of (Li/Na/K)BH3 has also been carried out. The hydrogen storage features for LiBH3 (12.61 wt%), NaBH3 (7.55 wt%), and KBH3 (5.36 wt%) suggest that the studied hydrides have achieved the energy storage target set by the United States Department of Energy (2025) for metal hydrides.Keywords: Hydrogen Storage, Volumetric Ratio, Gravimetric Ratio, Wien2K, Desorption TemperatureSubject terms: Chemistry, Materials science, Physics

Introduction

The widespread usage of fossil fuels has greatly expedited the advancement of technology and industry but their usage has severely damaged the environment1. Over the past few decades, the energy generated from fossil fuels has been considered the primary source of energy, which is predicted to rise by almost 56% by 20402. The ignition and widespread usage of fossil fuels release CO2, which has contributed to global warming so this issue can be resolved by converting to renewable energy sources3. A sustainable strategy is needed to balance the supply and demand for energy in order to maintain ongoing industrial and infrastructure growth4. Compared to traditional techniques of energy production, renewable energy sources are cleaner with less emission of carbon dioxide5. Hydrogen, which is the second most abundant element, has achieved universal recognition as a clean energy carrier6. Hydrogen energy has a variety of uses, which can be utilized to improve innovation and technological advancements that will spur economic growth7. Recently, solid-state hydrogen storage has drawn a lot of attention worldwide as a game-changing answer to the “last mile” problem a large scale, as it is a fairly new technique and is a safer option for hydrogen energy applications8,9. Chemical and physical procedures are the two common ways that a hydrogen atom can be linked to a material, but high temperatures are required for the release of chemically bound hydrogen, whereas low temperature is needed to produce considerable storage capacity with physically bound hydrogen10. Perovskite materials are gaining a lot of attention as these materials have shown higher hydrogen adsorption in comparison with standard metal hydrogen storage materials11,12. Moreover, metal perovskite hydrides have been reported to have significant hydrogen storage through reversible chemical processes, making them appropriate for hydrogen storage13. On the other hand, alkali-based metal hydride perovskites are thought to be the favorable materials for hydrogen, as these materials are light, which aids in releasing the hydrogen from the material14. Numerous alkali-based metals have revealed promising hydrogen storage capacity, like LiBHx (X = 1, 4) reports the gravimetric storage capacity for LiBH is computed as 5.05 wt%, which is drastically increased to 15.53 wt% for LiBH415. LiMgH3 shows immense hydrogen storage ability16. The hydrogen storage capacity of LiSnH3 and KSnH3 infers that these are appropriate for hydrogen storage17. RbSnH3, CsSnH3, and FrSnH3 report moderate gravimetric ratios, which implies that these materials demonstrate the potential for devices and applications involving hydrogen storage18. The mechanical traits of LiInH3 and KInH3 signify that both are ductile, whereas the hydrogen storage characteristics reveal that these hydrides are accomplished of storing hydrogen proficiently19. The hydrogen storage abilities for LiPtH3, NaPtH3, KPtH320, NaAlH3 and KAlH321, NaXH3 (X = Ti, Cu)22, AMgH3 (A = Be, Ca)23 and ZrXH3 (X = Zn, Cd)24 show that these can be used in future applications that involve the hydrogen storage. The scrutiny of BeXH3 reports the immense potential for optoelectronic and could be used to store hydrogen as these materials meet the US-DOE, 2025 ultimate gravimetric capacity requirements25. Magnesium-based hydrides MgAlH3, MgScH3, and MgZrH3 exhibit the most absorption and optical conductivity according to their optical traits and the hydrogen storage capacities reveal that these hydrides are favorable contenders for hydrogen storage26. One of the biggest challenges to hydrogen’s extensive application as a clean source of energy is still its cost-effectiveness, safety, and efficient storage ability. In the ongoing hunt for effective and sustainable hydrogen storage solutions, researchers are investigating novel materials that can provide higher energy density, minimal material degradation, and safety. This work presents a systematic first-principles study of Li/Na/KBH₃ perovskite hydrides within a unified computational framework to analyze structure–property relationships across the alkali-metal series. Unlike earlier studies focused on individual compounds or limited properties, it provides a comprehensive multi-property evaluation including structural, mechanical, dynamical, electronic, optical, and hydrogen storage characteristics. The key novelty lies in identifying clear compositional trends from Li to Na to K, showing how cation substitution governs lattice expansion, mechanical softening, band structure modulation, and hydrogen storage behavior. All results are benchmarked against available theoretical and experimental data for related borohydride systems. This enables a consistent comparative framework within the broader class of hydrogen storage materials. Overall, the study offers a correlated understanding of alkali-metal effects, supporting rational design of advanced solid-state hydrogen storage perovskites. For this reason alone, we have assessed the hydrogen storage aptitudes for (Li/Na/K)BH3 along with its physical characteristics using first principles analysis. These days, US-DOE is promoting those materials that have the ability to transcend the criterion of hydrogen storage in metal hydrides.

Computational analysis

Density functional theory operates on the fundamental idea that an interrelating system of fermions can be described by its electron density rather than by the many-body wave function27. DFT can handle both periodic and non-periodic systems with a large number of electrons, it is becoming a preferred method of calculation28. To compute the fundamental properties of (Li/Na/K)BH3, we have employed the FP-LAPW method that is integrated in the Wien2K. This method is a sophisticated approach to solve the Kohn-Shams equations with high computational accuracy29. It divides the unit cell into an interstitial zone and muffin-tin spheres. The interstitial region’s potential is fixed and a set of plane waves is needed to expand the wave function, the muffin-tin region’s potential is flexible and requires a set of spherical harmonics30. The exchange-correlation functional is handled with the GGA approximation. Due to the underestimation of anionic hydrogen binding and related electron localization effects, semi-local exchange-correlation functional like GGA is known to introduce systematic errors in hydride systems. These errors may affect absolute formation energies and derived thermodynamic quantities like desorption temperatures. However, since all of the chemicals (LiBH3, NaBH3, and KBH3) in this study are handled uniformly inside the same GGA framework, it is anticipated that these mistakes would mostly balance out when evaluating relative stabilities and trends. Furthermore, rather than tightly localized hydride states, where GGA restrictions are usually more severe, charge density and bonding investigations show mostly ionic–covalent mixed bonding. Therefore, it is anticipated that the general trends in hydrogen binding strength and stability will continue to be trustworthy even though there may be some variations in absolute values. Although more sophisticated functional might improve numeric accuracy, they are not anticipated to alter the work’s qualitative findings. The lattice constants for (Li/Na/K)BH3 are acquired from Birch’s equation. For self-consistency computations, the RMTKmax =3, lmax=11 and Gmax=20 (a.u)−1 are selected. The charge leakage is avoided by selecting cut off energy as −8 Ryd. All atoms in the unit cell are relaxed until the charge and energy convergence criteria is obtained as 0.01 eV/A and 10− 5 eV/f.u. The RMT radii of Li, Na, K, B and H are selected as 2.49, 2.43, 1.49, 1.33 and 0.89 (a.u)−1. A total of 1000-K points are selected for accuracy and precision in the calculations. The structural Wyckoff positions of (Li/Na/K)BH3 are drawn with software VESTA31. The elastic constants for (Li/Na/K)BH3 are calculated with Thomas Charpin’s method32. The optical properties are obtained through Kramer’s equations33. The abilities of the hydrides to store hydrogen are computed through the gravimetric and volumetric ratios. Furthermore, desorption temperatures are evaluated with Gibbs’ free energy relation.

Structural analysis

The structural behavior of the material is important to analyze, as it help in identifying the material’s structural and chemical behavior. A material’s optical properties, thermal stability, electrical conductivity, and mechanical strength are determined by the arrangement of its atoms, ions, or molecules. Perovskite materials have general formula of ABX3, where A-position and B-position are cations and X-position is an anion. For alkali perovskite hydrides, the general formulae change to ABH3 and the position A is engaged by the alkali earth elements, the position B is seized by transition elements and the X-position is occupied by the hydrogen atom. Crystallographic information regarding the crystal’s atomic arrangement, bond lengths and crystal structure formation is obtained from VESTA software. The studied compounds are relaxed, so the final ideal energy of the material is achieved. The total energy of the crystal systems is analyzed over a certain range of volume, which aids in detecting the material’s ground state energy. Mechanical properties

The materials’ restoring ability after the removal of external forces is assessed through mechanical properties39. Determining the material’s elastic characteristics is essential since these computed values are used to examine the material’s mechanical stability and technological application possibilities40. By using materials within their limitations and preventing structural failures, an understanding of the mechanical properties promotes the safety and dependability of engineering projects41. To analyze the mechanical properties of LiBH3, NaBH3 and KBH3 we have employed the technique introduced by Thomas Charpin42. The number of these elastic constants varies as the symmetry of the material is changed. For example, there are 21 elastic constants that correspond to stresses in different directions43 but for cubic symmetry, they reduce to C11, C12 and C44. The longitudinal and transverse stresses are accommodated by C11 and C12, while C44 accommodates any variation that occurs in the material’s shape when it is under stress44. All computed elastic constants are positive; moreover, the Born’s stability criteria for the cubic crystal given as C11-C12 > 0; C11+C12 > 0 and C44 > 0 are satisfied for LiBH3, NaBH3 and KBH3. Furthermore, all the computed constants for LiBH3, NaBH3 and KBH3 are greater than zero, which implies that these elastic constants obey Born stability standard for mechanical properties45. The computed elastic properties for LiBH3, NaBH3 and KBH3 are stated in Table 2. The material’s bulk modulus is an important mechanical property as it elaborates on the material’s resistive nature against the external agents46. The computed bulk modulus is in the order of KBH3 > NaBH3 > LiBH3, which implies that KBH3 is stiffer than the others. The Young modulus of the material is also significant to study as it reveals the material’s ability to withstand lengthwise changes when the tension and compression forces are applied47. Again, the evaluated value for the Young modulus is in order of KBH3 > NaBH3 > LiBH3, which signifies that KBH3 is stiffer than the other two studied halides. Any changes that occur in the material’s overall shape are observed through the shear modulus48. The harder it is to deform a material’s shape, the greater its shear modulus value; conversely, a lower shear modulus suggests that the material is more easily damaged49. The shear modulus for LiBH3, NaBH3 and KBH3 is computed using the VHR approximations50. According to the computed shear modulus, it is clearly noticed that KBH3 possesses a higher shear modulus value as compared to the other two studied halides, which infer that it is more difficult to distort the shape of KBH3 than LiBH3 and NaBH3. The degree to which a material’s mechanical qualities change with direction is known as the anisotropy factor in mechanical properties51. For anisotropic material, 0 < A < 1, which depicts that the properties of the material will change as the direction is changed, while for isotropic materials, A = 1 or A = 0, which concludes that the materials’ mechanical attributes are not directionally dependent. The anisotropy factor for all studied hydrides reveals that these are anisotropic in nature. The brittleness of the material is defined as the tendency of the material to break under stress. There are a few criteria according to which the ductile and brittle character is analyzed. Those materials that have a B/G value less than 1.75 are termed brittle, and if B/G greater than 1.75, the material is considered ductile52. The evaluated B/G values for LiBH3, NaBH3 and KBH3 indicate that all are brittle, as revealed in Fig. 2 (a). Poisson’s ratio also describes materials’ ductile and brittle criteria. Such as, v > 0.26 implies material being ductile, while v < 0.26 infers that the material is brittle53. The computed Poisson’s ratio for all studied halides is less than 0.26, which means these materials are of a brittle nature, as shown in Fig. 2 (b). The positive Cauchy’s pressure implies that materials are ductile, while the negative values report brittle characteristics54. All studied hydrides have negative Cauchy’s pressure, which implies these materials are brittle. The hardness factor is used to establish the materials’ functional capability in production procedures, and hardness testing is a rapid and affordable technique to assess the quality and consistency of materials used in manufacturing activities55. All studied perovskite reports higher values of the hardness factor, suggesting that these materials possess higher wear resistance and higher density. The machinability index provides details about how easily and successfully it can be cut, molded, and finished using different machining techniques56. All studied halides report a lower value of the machinability index, which implies that these materials are difficult to handle in machinery. Electronic properties

A broad knowledge of the material’s bandgap reveals fundamental knowledge about the materials’ nature. The material’s nature analysis paves the way for its application in various photovoltaic and optoelectronic applications. The electronic traits for (Li/Na/K)BH3 are obtained using the GGA potential, which is plotted in Figs. 4 and 5. The electronic energy (eV) is plotted on the y-axis, whereas the high symmetry points are drawn on the x-axis for all studied perovskite hydrides. The point where the electronic energy is zero is referred to as the Fermi energy, which is depicted by a blue dotted line. Determining the Fermi level’s location in relation to the bands is essential to understanding the material’s electrical activity. The electronic band structure for LiBH3, NaBH3 and KBH3 reveals an overlapping of dispersion curves in the valence and conduction bands leading to zero bandgap. This report states that the studied hydrides are metallic, as shown in Fig. 4. As the atomic mass is enhanced from “Li” to “K”, the dispersion curves in intermediate bands show a significant reduction. The total density of states is plotted in Fig. 4. The TDOS is important to analyze as it indicates how many possible electronic states are available per unit volume and per unit energy63. It is noticeable from the TDOS plots for the studied hydrides that the number of available energy states has enhanced as the atomic mass increases from “Li” to “K”. The changes in the band structures for all three hydrides perfectly align with the energy states available in TDOS plots, as shown in Fig. 4. Hydrogen storage properties

The two main key factors that aid in assessing the hydrogen storage properties are measuring the material’s gravimetric density or the desorption temperature. It is the temperature at which the material desorbs the absorbed hydrogen66. It is crucial to find those types of materials that have higher gravimetric density. The higher gravimetric density of the material implies that the material can store a large amount of hydrogen in a lower volume67. The U.S. Department of Energy (DOE) objectives, which specify a gravimetric capacity of 5.5 wt% and a volumetric capacity of 40 g H2/L for feasible onboard hydrogen storage systems, are frequently used to compare the hydrogen storage performance of materials. Because of their thorough assessment of energy density, weight, and system efficiency, these benchmarks are extensively used in the scientific community as a reference point for evaluating new materials. The DOE criteria continue to be the most widely used worldwide reference for assessing hydrogen storage potential, even though a number of other organizations, such as the International Energy Agency and the European Commission’s Hydrogen Europe initiative, have put forth similar goals. Conclusion

In recent years, materials that store hydrogen have drawn interest from all over the world because of their high energy density, which makes them perfect for large-scale energy storage. We have explored the physical features of (Li/Na/K)BH3 using the FP-LAPW technique. These alkali based perovskite hydrides have shown excellent structural and thermodynamic stability. The computed mechanical properties reveal that these materials have an anisotropic nature and they also display brittle characteristics. Additionally, sound velocities are calculated along [100], [110], and [111] planes. For (Li/Na/K)BH3, the electrical characteristics showed metallic behavior. The material’s optical characteristics reveal that it has significant absorption and optical conductivity in the UV region. In addition, the hydrogen storage capabilities for XBH3 (X = Li, Na, K) are calculated and the results show that our estimated LiBH3 and NaBH3 hydrogen storage capabilities meet the US DOE’s 2025 target, which signifies that these perovskite hydrides are a viable option for fuel cell technology, solid-state hydrogen storage, and hydrogen transportation.

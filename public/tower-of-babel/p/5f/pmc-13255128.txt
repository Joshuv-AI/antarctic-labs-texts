User Engagement and Demographics of a Medical Society-Led Website for Scoliosis: An Analysis using Google Analytics
Authors: Shuhei Iwata, Toshiaki Kotani, Toru Hirano, Kanichiro Wada, Katsumi Harimaya, Yuichiro Abe, Takahiro Iida, Yuki Taniguchi, Naoyuki Nakamura, Katsuhisa Yamada, Hideki Murakami, Shoji Seki, Taichi Tsuji, Hidetomi Terai, Haruo Misawa, Takuya Yamamoto, Seiji Ohtori
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroduction Patient-facing websites operated by medical societies are crucial for disseminating reliable health information; however, their real-world usage and effectiveness are often unquantified. This study investigated the user engagement and demographics of “Scoliosis Town,” a patient education website developed by the Japanese Scoliosis Society, to evaluate its performance. Methods We conducted a retrospective analysis of the website's traffic data from June 1, 2018, to March 31, 2023 (58 months), using Google Analytics. Key metrics included the number of users and sessions, user demographics (age and gender), access patterns (device type and acquisition channel), and popular content. Results The website attracted 2,333,184 users over 3,111,702 sessions. The primary users were female (68.1%), with the 35-44 age group being the largest demographic (29.4%), suggesting the site effectively reached its target audience of patients' mothers. The most frequently viewed pages were those detailing the “causes” and “treatment” of scoliosis. Most users accessed the site via mobile devices (83.3%), primarily through organic search (88.0%), with traffic peaking on weekday evenings. Conclusions The Japanese Scoliosis Society patient education website successfully reached its intended demographic of caregivers and was actively used to seek fundamental information about scoliosis. This study demonstrates the value of medical society-led digital platforms and validates web analytics as a powerful tool for assessing real-world patient information needs. Keywords: scoliosis, patient education, e-health, web analytics, Google Analytics, medical society

Introduction

The internet is a primary source of medical information for patients and their families; a trend particularly pronounced in the context of scoliosis. Reports indicate that individuals with scoliosis seek condition-specific information online more often than those with other orthopedic conditions1-3). This reliance on digital resources highlights the critical need for online information to be accurate, understandable, and reliable, especially for caregivers making healthcare decisions for pediatric and adolescent patients.

Despite this widespread use, the quality of online information about scoliosis is often poor. Research shows that only a low percentage―approximately 15%―of pediatric orthopedic content online is of high quality4). Moreover, the vocabulary is frequently too technical for most patients to understand5-7). Providing accessible information is critical because it has been shown to enhance patient satisfaction and quality of life8).

To address this gap, the Japanese Scoliosis Society (JSS) launched “Scoliosis Town,” a patient-focused website. The JSS designed this resource to provide accessible and clear information, thereby fulfilling the specific requirements of patients and their families. The usage of general medical websites has been previously examined9-12). However, there is a lack of research focusing on websites created by professional societies for scoliosis patients. Understanding the usage patterns and effectiveness of these specialized platforms is, therefore, an important area for investigation.

Therefore, this study aims to analyze the usage patterns of the “Scoliosis Town” website to evaluate its effectiveness in reaching and meeting the informational needs of its target audience.

Materials and Methods

Study design

This study was a retrospective, observational analysis of web traffic data from a specialized, patient-focused website. The objective was to describe and analyze the usage patterns of the site's visitors over a defined period.

The “Scoliosis Town” website

In 2017, the JSS initiated and funded the development of a patient-friendly website named “Scoliosis Town.” The project's goal was to create and disseminate accessible information for individuals with scoliosis and their caregivers (parents or guardians). A multidisciplinary committee, comprising orthopedic surgeons, patients, JSS administrative staff, and web designers, was formed to develop the site's design and content. Following a one-year development period, the website was launched in Japanese in June 2018. The JSS Public Relations Committee is responsible for its governance and conducts regular content reviews to ensure the information remains accurate and up to date.

“Scoliosis Town” provides comprehensive information on topics such as screening, diagnosis, and treatments, including bracing and surgery. To enhance user understanding, content is delivered not only as text but also through animations and videos. The JSS has created not only a website but also a YouTube channel accessible from it, where they publish easy-to-understand videos about scoliosis using animated characters (Figure 1). The development process included a rigorous review to ensure readability; drafts written by physicians were first reviewed by nurses and subsequently by patients and non-medical staff to simplify technical terms and improve clarity. The website also features patient interviews about their experiences with treatment and uses original animated characters to explain medical concepts in an approachable manner.

Data collection and analysis period

To understand the usage of “Scoliosis Town,” we used the web analytics tool Google Analytics (Google LLC, Mountain View, CA, USA). Web analytics is defined as “the measurement, collection, analysis and reporting of web data for purposes of understanding and optimizing web usage.” The analysis period for this study covered 58 months, from June 1, 2018, to March 31, 2023.

Key metrics

The following key metrics were collected and analyzed:

•User base and demographics: Total unique users, new vs. returning users, gender, and age group. Age data is reported in brackets provided by Google Analytics, which excludes users under the age of 18.

•Geographic distribution: User numbers were analyzed at the prefectural level, both as absolute counts and as rates per 10,000 population, to account for regional population differences. This normalization was crucial to assess the relative intensity of informational interest across different regions, independent of overall prefectural population size.

•User engagement: Pages per session, average session duration, and bounce rate.

•Content popularity: The most frequently viewed pages.

•Access trends: User activity patterns by day of the week and time of day.

•Acquisition and technology: Primary user acquisition channels and device types used for access.

Statistical analysis

The analysis in this study was descriptive. Data were aggregated, summarized, and visualized using the standard reporting functions within the Google Analytics platform. No inferential statistical tests were performed, as the primary goal was to report and describe the de-identified, aggregate usage patterns of the website.

Ethical considerations

The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of the medical society. This research utilized pre-existing, de-identified data, and therefore, the requirement for individual informed consent was waived. The “Scoliosis Town” website transparently discloses its use of Google Analytics and provides users with instructions on how to opt out of data tracking, ensuring user privacy and control.

Results

Overall website traffic

Over the 58-month analysis period, the “Scoliosis Town” website attracted 2,333,184 users in 3,111,702 sessions, accumulating 11,267,870 page views. New visitors accounted for the majority of traffic (85.8%). The average session duration was 2 minutes and 31 seconds, with an average of 3.62 pages viewed per session. User demographics and access methods

The primary users of the website were female (68.1%) and those in the 35-44 age group (29.4%). The youngest reported demographic, the 18-24 age group, comprised 13.1% of users. The main acquisition channel was organic search (88.0%), and the primary method of access was via mobile devices (83.3%). Detailed data on user demographics and access methods are shown in Table 2. It is important to note that Google Analytics does not report data for users under 18; therefore, the adolescent patient demographic is not represented in this analysis.

Access patterns

Analysis of access patterns revealed distinct temporal trends. Website traffic was consistently higher on weekdays compared to weekends (Figure 4). Furthermore, usage varied significantly by time of day, with traffic remaining low during morning and daytime hours before increasing in the evening, typically peaking around 8:00 PM (20:00) (Figure 5).

Discussion

This analysis of the “Scoliosis Town” website provides the first detailed overview of user engagement with a specialized, medical society-led resource for scoliosis in Japan. The study reveals that the platform successfully reached a large and relevant audience, which is strongly inferred to be the mothers of scoliosis patients, although the demographic may also include patients with adolescent-onset adult idiopathic scoliosis in the 35-44 age group. User behavior indicated a strong interest in fundamental topics such as the causes and treatments of scoliosis.

The success in reaching this specific demographic was a key finding, as previous research has highlighted that parents are the principal information seekers following a child's scoliosis diagnosis13). The access patterns observed (high traffic from mobile devices during weekday evenings) further support this interpretation, reflecting the behavior of caregivers seeking information outside of typical work hours. By successfully engaging this group, the website provides an essential tool for patient empowerment and education, a key function of digital health platforms in other chronic conditions14).

Although parents are a primary audience, this study also highlights engagement from young adults, with the 18-24 age group constituting a substantial 13.1% of users. This suggests that the website is serving not only caregivers but also patients who are transitioning into adulthood. The absence of data for users under 18, a limitation imposed by Google Analytics' privacy regulations, means that the engagement of the core adolescent patient population cannot be directly measured through this method. This underscores the need to create content specifically tailored to the unique informational needs of teenagers and young adults. We design the website to reassure patients under observation, motivate those using a brace with evidence-based guidance, and support surgical candidates with postoperative interviews, while also addressing psychosocial aspects and practical advice for daily life and social activities15,16).

The geographic analysis revealed that user engagement, even when normalized for population, was highest in major metropolitan areas. We consider the disparity in the amount of medical information obtained based on geographic location to be a significant issue17). We believe that access to reliable information sources for scoliosis should be equitable, reaching patients and caregivers regardless of their geographic location.

A significant challenge in online patient education is readability. Health authorities recommend that materials be written at a sixth-grade reading level; however, studies show most resources from orthopedic societies are too complex18-24). “Scoliosis Town” was designed to address this by using multimedia and a multi-stakeholder review process. A recent study, which analyzed 143 patient materials from seven orthopedic-related societies in Japan, highlighted a crucial nuance: while “Scoliosis Town” was rated as having sufficient information quality (the only resource to do so), the same study found that its readability remained too difficult25). This finding reveals a key challenge in digital health: translating high-quality, comprehensive information into a truly accessible format.

This study's methodology also demonstrates that website analytics can serve as a valuable data source for understanding the real-world information needs of patients. Our analysis provides direct, quantitative evidence of the topics of greatest concern to users. This data-driven strategy allows for the creation of a responsive educational resource that directly addresses patient needs, which in turn can lead to greater treatment satisfaction and help guide families toward appropriate care.

This study has several limitations. First, the use of anonymous Google Analytics data precludes any direct linkage between website usage and clinical outcomes. Second, the demographic analysis provides strong inferential evidence that the primary users are the mothers of patients; however, without direct surveys, this remains an inference. Furthermore, though web analytics quantifies what information users are seeking, it cannot assess user satisfaction, information comprehensiveness, or whether the provided information met the quality and depth of their specific needs. This remains a significant limitation of this purely observational, quantitative approach. Finally, the exclusion of users under 18 from the demographic data means that the core adolescent patient population could not be directly analyzed. Despite these constraints, this study represents the first detailed analysis of user engagement with a Japanese medical society-led website for scoliosis, providing valuable insights into its real-world application.

In conclusion, this study demonstrated that a medical society-led, patient-centered development process can produce an educational website with high information quality. Our analysis confirmed that “Scoliosis Town” reaches its target demographic―the parents of adolescent patients―and is used to seek fundamental information on the causes and treatments of the condition. This validates web analytics as a powerful tool for understanding real-time patient needs. Although the site's information quality was rated favorably, readability remains a challenge to be addressed. Future efforts should focus on improving the accessibility of the content and integrating qualitative user feedback. The “Scoliosis Town” project serves as a practical model for the data-driven development and evolution of digital patient education.

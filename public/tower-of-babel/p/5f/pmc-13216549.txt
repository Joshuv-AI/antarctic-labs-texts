Bioanalytical method development and validation of isosorbide mononitrate in human plasma by using LC–MS/MS
Authors: Ramanlal N Kachave, Pallavi S Thombare, Hemant U Chikhale, Prashant L Pingale, Sunil V Amrutkar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo analyze Isosorbide Mononitrate in human plasma, a highly sensitive and accurate LC–MS/MS bioanalytical method was developed and validated using Isosorbide mononitrate-¹³C₆ as the internal standard. Validate a suitable analytical method for the estimation of unknown drug concentrations in plasma. It can be used for the quantitation of isosorbide mononitrate in K₂EDTA human plasma for bioequivalence and bioavailability studies. The method was found to be specific, sensitive, linear, precise, accurate, and reproducible for the extraction and analysis of isosorbide mononitrate in K₂EDTA human plasma samples over the investigated concentration range of 5.000 ng/mL to 1800.000 ng/mL using a processing volume of 0.100 mL. Chromatographic separation was achieved using a 2 mM ammonium acetate (acidified) mobile phase with acetonitrile containing 0.1% acetic acid in a ratio of 55:45 (v/v) at a flow rate of 0.5 mL/min. Detection was carried out using multiple reaction monitoring with transitions (m/z) of 249.90 → 59.00 for isosorbide mononitrate and 255.90 → 58.90 for the internal standard. The analytes were detected using tandem mass spectrometry (LC–MS/MS). The proposed method was comprehensively validated in terms of linearity, accuracy, precision, specificity, sensitivity, recovery, and stability, with results meeting the acceptance criteria. The developed and validated method was found to be simpler, faster, more specific, precise, and cost-effective compared with previously reported methods.Keywords: Isosorbide mononitrate, Isosorbide 13C6 mononitrate, LC-MS/MS, Human plasma, Bioanalytical method development, Bioequivalence and bioavailabilitySubject terms: Biological techniques, Chemistry

Introduction

Angina, also known as Ischemic chest pain, is a form of chest discomfort produced by a decrease in blood flow to the heart. It is a pain syndrome caused by the induction of an abnormal oxygen supply or demand in a region of the myocardium. Angina is a sign that you have coronary artery disease. Isosorbide Mononitrate (ISMN) is chemically (3R, 3aS, 6S, 6aR)-6-hydroxy-hexahydrofuro [3, 2-b] furan-3-yl nitrate. The chemical structure is shown in Fig. 1. It is an organic nitrate, widely preferred for its vasodilating properties1. It is a drug used to treat heart failure, esophageal spasms, and chest pain (angina). Although it can be used to treat and prevent heart-related chest discomfort, beta blockers and calcium channel blockers are typically more recommended. It’s consumed orally. It was patented in 1971, given medical approval in 1981, and is now a generic drug. The long-acting metabolite of isosorbide dinitrate, isosorbide-5-mononitrate, is used to treat angina pectoris. By widening the blood arteries, it lowers blood pressure and lowers left ventricular preload and after load, which lowers the need for myocardial oxygen. It is used to treat acute and chronic angina pectoris, hypertension, and myocardial infarction as well as to prevent angina pectoris brought on by coronary artery disease. Isosorbide Mononitrate is transformed into nitric oxide (NO), an active intermediate substance that activates the enzyme guanylate cyclase, similarly to other nitrites and organic nitrates (Atrial natriuretic peptide receptor A). This increases the production of cyclic guanosine 3’, 5'-monophosphate (cGMP), which in turn triggers a cascade of protein kinase-dependent phosphorylations in the smooth muscle cells, ultimately leading to the dephosphorylation of the smooth muscle fiber’s myosin light chain. The smooth muscle cells relax and the blood vessels dilate as a result of the following release of calcium ions. Unlike oral isosorbide dinitrate, which is sensitive to first-pass metabolism, isosorbide mononitrate is largely metabolised by the liver. 96% of the supplied dose of isosorbide mononitrate is excreted in the urine within 5 days, and just about 1% is removed in the faeces. Isosorbide mononitrate is cleared via denitration to isosorbide and glucuronidation as the mononitrate. At least six distinct substances, including at least five metabolites, and around 2% of the dose were found in urine samples.

Chemical structure of isosorbide mononitrate (analyte).

The literature survey of ISMN revealed several methods for estimation of Isosorbide Mononitrate in biological fluids such as LC-TMS1, LC–ESI–MS/MS2,3, HPLC4, UPLC-TMS5, pharmacokinetics study6, LC–MS/MS7, orbasic LC technique8. With the advancement of electrospray ionization (ESI), a robust interface is now possible with the exciting but simple LC–ESI–MS technique9. Many published methods employ the solid phase extraction or liquid–liquid extraction approach, which makes the sample preparation labor-intensive, challenging, or both. The goal of the study was to create and validate a robust, high throughput, sensitive, affordable, and cost-effective bioanalytical method based on LC–MS/MS detection for routine sample analysis. In the current study, an effort was made to create an analytical method based on LC–MS-MS that is extremely sensitive, exact, and quick for estimating isosorbide mononitrate in human plasma while using isosorbide 13C6 mononitrate as an internal reference. Isosorbide 13C6 mononitrate is chemically (3S,3aR,6R,6aS)-3-hydroxy-2,3,3a,5,6,6a-hexahydro(2,3,4,5-13C4) furano[3,2-b] (2,3,4,5-13C4) furan-6-yl] nitrate. The chemical structure is shown in Fig. 2 Because it enables fast molecular weight confirmation and structural identification; LC–MS is often employed in drug development. The generation, testing, and validation of a discovery from a wide range of items with possible applications is accelerated by these features. For sample preparation protein –protein extraction method has been used which is a single step procedure and therefore is less time-consuming and less stringent. LC–MS/MS is the gold-standard technique in bioanalysis due to its exceptional sensitivity, selectivity, and robustness, allowing reliable quantification of analytes in complex biological matrices during drug development, clinical trials, and biomarker studies. As demonstrated by Dodda et al. LC–MS/MS enables accurate and precise quantification of analytes at very low concentrations while minimizing matrix interferences through efficient chromatographic separation and tandem mass detection. Its ability to detect analytes in the picogram to nanogram per millilitre range makes it indispensable for pharmacokinetic, toxicokinetic, and bioequivalence studies. Overall, LC–MS/MS serves as a foundational technology in modern bioanalysis, combining high sensitivity, specificity, and regulatory reliability10–14.

Chemical structure of isosorbide 13C6 mononitrate (internal standard).

With the advancement of electrospray ionization (ESI) technology, a more robust and efficient interface between liquid chromatography and mass spectrometry became possible. LC–ESI–MS allows rapid molecular weight confirmation and structural identification with high accuracy. However, many existing LC–ESI–MS methods for ISMN analysis still involve multi-step sample preparation and have limited throughput, which restricts their utility in high-sample-volume bioequivalence and bioavailability studies. The developed method achieved a validated concentration range of 5.0–1800.0 ng/mL with excellent linearity, precision, and recovery, demonstrating superior sensitivity compared to previously reported LC or HPLC methods. The total run time was minimized due to optimized chromatographic conditions, enabling rapid quantitation suitable for high-throughput studies. Additionally, the method was found to be simple, reproducible, and cost-effective, making it ideal for routine analysis in bioequivalence and bioavailability studies. Thus, when compared with earlier published approaches, the proposed LC–MS/MS method offers clear advantages in terms of simplicity, sensitivity, speed, reproducibility, and cost efficiency, providing a significant improvement over existing analytical methodologies for ISMN determination in human plasma.

Materials and methods

Chemicals

Isosorbide Mononitrate and Isosorbide -13C6-Mononitrate were provided by SimSon Pharma (Mumbai, India). The plasma was provided as a gift sample by Arpan Blood Bank, Sangamner. The purity of ISMN is 99.77% and that ofisosorbide 13C6 mononitrate is 100%. All additional chemicals, including acetonitrile of the HPLC grade, were purchased from Merck (Mumbai, India). A Milli-Q system was utilised to purify the water.

Instrumentation

The LC–MS/MS analysis was performed using a Triple Quad 4500 mass spectrometer (SCIEX) coupled with an Agilent 1290 Infinity II HPLC system15.

Chromatographic conditions

Chromatographic separation was achieved onAgilent ZORBAX Eclipse XDB C18 column, (4.6 mm × 150 mm),5 µm particle size. 2 mM Ammonium Acetate: Acetonitrile Adjusted pH 4.7 with 0.1% Acetic Acid in the Ratio of 55:45 v/v utilised throughout the analysis as the mobile phase for separation. The flow rate of the mobile phase was 0.5 ml/min, using injection volume 10 µL and mass spectrometer (ESI) as detector. The autosampler was held at a temperature of 5 °C, while the column oven was kept at 35 °C. Retention time for the analyte and internal standard was 3.30 min out of a total run time of 4.5 min.

Mass spectrometer conditions

Under electrospray ionisation (ESI) source conditions, an API 4500 mass spectrometer (SCIEX) carried out the mass spectrometric measurement. ISMN and 13C6 ISMN were both analysed using the detection in the multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) scan mode “m/z 250.0 → 59.1 for ISMN and m/z 256.1 → 59.1 respectively for the internal standard.” The optimal mass spectrometric parameters included an ion spray voltage of 4500 V, nebulizer gas (GS1) and heater gas (GS2) pressures of 40 psi and 55 psi, respectively; a declustering potential of –10 V; entrance potentials for ISMN and ISTD of –12 V and –14 V, respectively; a collision energy of –25 V; and collision cell exit potentials of –15 V and –11 V for ISMN and ISTD, respectively, which are presented in Table 116–18.

Isosorbide -13C6 5-Mononirate standard was carefully weighed at 1.0 mg, and the proper volume of methanol was then added to make the final concentration of Isorsorbide-13C6 5-Mononirate equal to 1 mg/mL.

Preparation of ISTD dilution, 2000 ng/mL

Measure and transfer 0.100 mL of ISTD stock solution, into a 50 mL volumetric flask, add 1 mg/mL. Using water and methanol as diluent, increase the volume to 50.0 mL. Store in refrigerator 5 ± 3 °C.

To make the final concentration of isosorbide mononitrate equal to 1 mg/mL, the correct volume of methanol and about 2.0 mg of isosorbide mononitrate standard were added. Store in the refrigerator at 5 ± 3 °C.

Preparation of plasma sample

Before sample preparation, plasma samples were thawed to room temperature. Isosorbide mononitrate was extracted from plasma using a protein precipitation method. Acetonitrile was used as the extraction solvent. A 100 µL plasma aliquot (10 ppm) was transferred into a polypropylene tube, and 0.5 mL of acetonitrile was added. The mixture was vortexed for 5 min and then centrifuged at 4000 rpm for 10 min. The resulting supernatant was injected into the LC–MS/MS system.

Biological matrix

During technique validation, human plasma that included ethylene diamine tetra acetic acid (K2EDTA) as an anticoagulant was used as the biological matrix.

Method validation19–30

Specificity and selectivity

The selectivity of the bioanalytical method refers to its ability to accurately quantify and differentiate the target analyte and internal standard in the presence of other substances that may be present in the sample. The specificity of the proposed method was established by screening standard blank plasma (without spiking the analyte or internal standard), along with calibration curve standards and two sets of batch-qualifying quality control (QC) samples at the HQC, MQC1, MQC2, and LQC levels. A total of 11 different plasma batches were evaluated, including seven normal, two lipemic, and two hemolyzed plasma samples containing the K₂EDTA anticoagulant. Specificity was assessed by comparing the responses of any interfering peaks at the retention times of the analyte and internal standard in blank plasma with the response of the corresponding extracted lower limit of quantification (LLOQ).

LLOQ and linearity

Linearity is the ability of the bioanalytical procedure to obtain test results that are directly proportional to the concentration of analyte in the sample. Lower limit of quantification (LLOQ) was described as the lowest concentration on the calibration curve and represented the sensitivity of the calibration curve. Using standard plots connected to the 9 point standard curve, including LLOQ and ULOQ, the method’s linearity was assessed.

Precision and accuracy

At the LLOQ QC, LQC, MQC-1, MQC-2, and HQC levels, precision and accuracy were retrieved. Additionally, inter- and intra-batch precision tests were conducted. a single set of calibration curves made up a single Precision and Accuracy Batch. For each concentration of LLOQ QC, LQC, MQC-1, MQC-2, and HQC, standards and six replicates of extracted samples were used. For samples other than those at the LLOQ QC level, the % RSD should not exceed 15% and should not exceed 20% for LLOQ QC level samples. For LLOQ QC samples, the accuracy should fall between 80 and 120 percent, and for non-LLOQ QC samples, 85 to 115%.

Recovery for analyte and internal standard

By comparing the mean peak areas of the six replicates of extracted plasma quality control samples at high, medium, and low concentrations against the corresponding mean peak areas of the six replicates of post-extracted quality control samples at high, medium, and low concentrations, the percentage mean recovery for the analyte was calculated. % Mean Recovery of Analyte = (Mean extracted peak area / Mean post-extracted peak area) × (Total Volume of sample /Volume of sample added) × 100. By comparing the mean peak area of internal standard in the extracted plasma quality control samples at middle concentration to the mean peak area of internal standard in the post-extracted quality control samples at middle (MQC1) concentration, the percentage mean recovery for internal standard was calculated. % Mean Recovery of ISTD = (Mean extracted ISTD peak area / Mean post-extracted ISTD peak area) x (Total Volume of sample /Volume of sample added) × 100The % CV of recovery at each QC level and % CV of Overall recovery should be ≤ 15% for both analyte and internal standard.

Matrix effect

Following extraction, the extracts were spiked with the analyte at concentrations equivalent to those of the low and high QC extracted samples (n = 1 at each level for each lot of biological matrix) and with the internal standard at its working concentration. Ten different batches of biological matrix containing the same anticoagulant were processed.

Hemolysis and lipemic effect

By processing six aliquots of high- and low-quality control samples from one lot of screened lipemic plasma and one lot of screened hemolyzed plasma, respectively, hemolysis and lipemic effect were performed. At least two sets of batches qualifying QCs and freshly prepared CC standards prepared in normally screened plasma were used to examine the samples (at higher, middle and lower level). For LQC and HQC samples, the percent CV should be under 15%, and the percent recovery should be between 85 and 115%.

Stability

Six duplicates of LQC and HQC samples were examined under four distinct exposure circumstances to determine the stability of ISMN in human plasma. (1) Benchtop stability at room temperature for 19 h; (2) Autosampler stability; (3) Short-term stability at room temperature for 6 h; and (4) five freeze–thaw cycles at -20 to 5 °C and below -65 °C. The% mean accuracy (% Mean RE or% mean Bias and% CV of LQC and HQC samples were used to evaluate the stability. If the assay values are between 90.00 and 110.00% of accuracy and the CV is less than 15.00%, the samples were deemed stable.

Result and discussion

Development of LC–MS method

The purpose of the current study was to design and validate a high throughput, sensitive, economical, and robust bioanalytical approach based on LC–MS/MS detection for routine sample analysis. Isosorbide 13C6 mononitrate is used as an internal standard; this is because of similar structure and physicochemical properties of both analyte and internal standard. This also helps in reducing the matrix effects the analyte being quantified more accurately. An API 4500 mass spectrometer (SCIEX) was utilised to detect the analyte under mass spectrometry settings. The internal standard (m/z 256.1 → 59.1) and analyte (m/z 250.0 → 59.1) were chosen by optimizing MS transitions. Chromatographic separation was achieved on TRIPLE QUAD 4500 LC/MS–MS. During method development Agilent ZORBAX Eclipse XDB C18 column was used for the wide range of pH (pH 4.7). Peak shape and resolution were found to be good with this column. Considering the usefulness of mobile phase at low-cost effect with acetonitrile as organic modifier was investigated. To achieve peak of good resolution there should be importance of presence of buffer solution in mobile phase along with good sensitivity in reverse phase chromatography. After trying out several trials 2 mM Ammonium Acetate and acidifying agents 0.1% Acetic Acid as buffer with Acetonitrile has been optimized to enhance ionization of ISMN.

Conclusion

Therefore, the goal of the research was to create and validate an appropriate method for estimating an unknown drug concentration in plasma. It can be used for the quantitation of Isosorbide Mononitrate from K2EDTA based Human Plasma in Bioequivalence and Bioavailability Study. The method is considered specific, sensitive, linear, precise, accurate and reproducible; Utilizing a processing volume of 0.100 mL, isosorbide mononitrate was extracted from and analysed in K2EDTA human plasma samples at the examined concentration range of 5.000 ng/mL to 1800.000 ng/mL using commercially available IS Form small volumes of human plasma and a straightforward protein precipitation technique, a highly accurate, sensitive, specific, and reproducible LC-MS/MS approach for the measurement of isosorbide mononitrate was designed and validated. Internal standards should, whenever possible, be identical to the analyte, according to FDA guidelines (US Food and Drug Administration, Center for Drug Evaluation and Research (CDER) 2001). The developed and validated analytical approach was discovered to be simpler, quicker, more precise, and more cost-effective than methods that had been reported.

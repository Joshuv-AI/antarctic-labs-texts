Frontiers in Solar Photovoltaic Materials
Authors: Justin M Hodgkiss
Published: 2024

Introduction

Solar photovoltaic (PV) cells are playing a critical role in the global transition to renewable energy technologies, and their rapid adoption is sure to continue. Alongside established silicon-based PV technologies, emerging classes of solar PVs promise to enable lower cost manufacturing routes, along with a host of other benefits that will bring about new applications for solar PVs. These other benefits include lightweight and flexibility (enabling easy transport and installation on a variety of structures), and tunable color (for windows and other building-integrated applications). Materials chemistry is the key to unlocking these benefits, and chemists have taken up the challenge of creating new classes of materials that effectively convert solar energy into electricity and uncovering their hidden design rules.

In this Collection, we present 16 recently published works in issues of JACS Au, including Articles, Letters, and Perspectives. These publications explore the frontiers of new classes of solar PV materials, including organic PVs and metal halide perovskites, and they also span different aspects from understanding photophysics, to improving device lifetimes, and exploiting robotics-based material screening for high-throughput PV material discovery.

Organic PVs and Dye Sensitized Solar Cells

Organic PVs comprise carbon-based π-conjugated materials as their semiconducting active layers, performing the functions of absorbing light and separating/transporting electron and hole charge carriers for collection at the respective electrodes, while retaining an appreciable photovoltage. On the one hand, π-conjugated materials are attractive on account of their strong and tunable optical absorption, and reasonably high charge mobilities. But on the other hand, their intrinsically low dielectric constants lead to strong Coulomb binding between charge pairs. This constraint defines the field of organic PV cells because it means that nanostructured blends of electron donor and acceptor materials are required to separate electrons and holes, and significant effort must be invested in not only discovering new materials, but also optimizing combinations with the right optical, electronic, and morphological properties.

Yang, Lin and co-workers report a novel derivative of the benchmark acceptor Y6, in which a glycolated side chain is asymmetrically incorporated to increase the dielectric constant of the acceptor material [DOI: 10.1021/jacsau.1c00306]. The authors show that this substitution increases the dielectric constant from εr = 3.36 to 5.13, and the corresponding solar PV cells exhibit substantially higher charge separation yield and ultimately power conversion efficiency. Moreover, this substitution strategy also makes the material processable in ecocompatible nonhalogenated solvents, and its 15.2% power conversion efficiency is the highest among as-cast nonhalogenated processed OPVs.

The long-term stability of OPV cells has improved markedly and remains a key material design criterion. Xu, Hou and co-workers address the tendency of OPV materials to absorb environmental moisture over time, degrading interlayer molecules and causing concommitent efficiency decay [DOI: 10.1021/jacsau.2c00307]. By introducing a napthlalene-diimide-based interlayer material with a covalently coupled zwitterion pair, the authors demonstrated that the material adopts a structure that is nonhygroscopic, and results in prolonged device lifetime under humid conditions. Kim, Kim, and co-workers addressed the mechanical stability of OPVs by blending a high molecular weight acceptor into blends containing small molecule acceptors [DOI: 10.1021/jacsau.1c00064]. They showed that even 10–20 wt % of the polymer is sufficient to increase the mechanical ductility by 4-fold, as well as enhancing the power conversion efficiency.

The vast parameter space of organic semiconducting materials has stimulated exploration of other mechanisms to facilitate conversion of light into electricity. Bossanyi, Clark, and co-workers examine the spin statistics of triplet–triplet annihilation upconversion–a process that can upconvert the energy of photons in order to meet the bandgap criterion of a PV cell [DOI: 10.1021/jacsau.1c00322]. Using a range of spectroscopic methods, the authors probe the process by which annihilating triplets form emissive singlet states and show how factors like molecular orientation profoundly influence the spin statistical factor. In their perspective, Hodgkiss and co-workers review the evidence for intrinsic charge generation in new classes of organic materials that can generate photocurrent without requiring donor/acceptor blends, and they explore how this photophysics may enable new OPV architectures [DOI: 10.1021/jacsau.4c00046]. While noncovalent interactions have long been used to tune the conformation of conjugated polymers, Pan and co-workers present a systematic study to show that noncovalent interactions between heteroatoms also have further effects on electronic structure, beyond what can be explained by conformational control alone [DOI: 10.1021/jacsau.1c00284].

Dye sensitized solar cells also feature π-conjugated organic or coordination compounds as sensitizers that strongly absorb light and undergo electron transfer into a high bandgap semiconducting material. These solar cells remain a powerful platform for understanding photoinduced electron transfer processes, as well as exploring different cell colors with fewer other constraints than in OPVs. Petrone, Rega and co-workers present electronic dynamics simulations on the early femtosecond time scale of Ru-based polypyridyl complexes [DOI: 10.1021/jacsau.2c00556]. In doing so, they directly track the evolution of electronic states and the interaction between critical metal-to-ligand and interligand electron transfer states. Sauvage and co-workers show how chemistry can enable entirely new solar cell properties for building applications and window coatings. They present a transparent dye sensitized solar cell that incorporates a sensitizer with sharp absorption confined to the near-infrared region where the human eye is insensitive, yet sufficient solar energy is present to enable over 3% power conversion efficiency [DOI: 10.1021/jacsau.1c00045].

Metal Halide Perovskite Solar Cells

Metal halide perovskite solar cells have made rapid progress over the past decade, in part because the existing field of researchers in dye-sensitized and organic solar cells were poised to apply their knowledge and skills to the discovery and optimization of new classes of PV materials. Yet, both the promise, and the remaining challenges associated with metal halide perovskite solar cells arise from their key differences with organic- and dye-sensitized solar cells. While metal halide perovskites have a semiconducting band structure that promotes facile photogeneration of long-lived charge carriers, new challenges arise from controlling and stabilizing crystalline phases and interfaces.

Ultrafast optical spectroscopy has been critical to establishing the nature and dynamics of photogenerated species in metal halide perovskite solar cells, but these experiments are usually conducted under conditions of much higher excitation density than normal operating conditions. Zhang, Ren and co-workers present a highly sensitive transient absorption spectrometer that enables them to probe dynamics under solar-relevant conditions, and identify various trapping and trap-assisted recombination processes spanning picosecond to microsecond time scales [DOI: 10.1021/jacsau.2c00581]. Cui and co-workers examined charge transfer processes in heterojunctions of 2D perovskite via DFT calculations [DOI: 10.1021/jacsau.1c00192]. They found that spin–orbit coupling is key to enabling asynchronous photoinduced electron transfer across these structures.

It is well established that the photovoltaic performance of 3D metal halide perovskites is degraded when the material undergoes a tetragonal-to-orthorhombic phase transition, but the phase transition kinetics were not understood. Wang and co-workers elucidated this phase transition pathway using a stepwise computational method, revealing that the process is driven by the tilting of PbI64– units [DOI: 10.1021/jacsau.3c00060].

While the crystalline properties of metal halide perovskites are important to their photophysics, they also result in grain boundaries and interfacial instabilities that negatively impact the performance and lifetime of PV cells. Zhao, Chen and co-workers present a 1-step method for creating 1D/3D heterojunctions without the need for passivation post-treatment [DOI: 10.1021/jacsau.3c00469]. In their letter, Oliviera, Nogueira and co-workers also report an organic salt that is effective for passivating defects owing to its bidentate anilinium cations, and they reveal a significant impact of the counteranion [DOI: 10.1021/jacsau.2c00151]. Chen, Chou and co-workers introduced a series of organic zwitterions as boundary modulators, identifying four functions that they play: balancing carrier transport, passivating Pb2+ defects, enhancing moisture resistance, and reducing ion migration [DOI: 10.1021/jacsau.2c00160].

Finally, recognizing the dominant role that Pb2+ ions play in both the favorable band structure, and also some of the remaining problems of metal halide perovsite materials, Zeng and co-workers take a computational approach to designing 3D perovskite semiconductors that are entirely metal-free [DOI: 10.1021/jacsau.1c00014]. The authors reveal a clear correlation between the bandgap of A(NH4)X3 perovskites and the LUMO energy of the A2+ cation, leading them to predict a metal-free perovskite structure with a desirable bandgap of 1.74 eV.

Outlook

One thing that stands out as both an opportunity and a challenge in each of the frontier classes of PV materials described in this virtual issue is that they span a vast material parameter space. The optoelectronic properties of these materials depend not only on molecular structures, but on noncovalent interactions, crystal structures, phase behavior, interfaces, and many more factors. While advanced experimental methodologies have been critical to establishing photophysical mechanisms, it is clear that computational approaches are beginning to fulfill their promise of delivering high-throughput virtual screening. This research field is also ripe for high-throughput robotics-based experimental screening of PV materials. Saeki and co-workers used a robotics-based measurement approach to screen over 500 films of quarternary blended wide bandgap Cs–Bi–Sb–I semiconductors with various additives and processing conditions [DOI: 10.1021/jacsau.3c00519]. Not only did the authors rapidly identify the optimal additive recipe, they also generated a sufficiently large data set for meaningful application of machine learning. This study is a glimpse of the possibilities that lie ahead when the large body of work on structure–function relationships in PV materials is augmented with emerging materials data-science approaches.

Preoperative echocardiographic predictors of atrial fibrillation after coronary artery bypass graft surgery (CABG): a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Zahra Teimouri-Jervekani, Naghmeh Samadi-Broujeni, Saeide Bahrani, Azam Soleimani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPostoperative atrial fibrillation (POAF) is a common complication after coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG), which is associated with increased morbidity, longer hospital stays, and higher healthcare costs. Preoperative echocardiographic parameters may play an important role in predicting the risk of POAF.ObjectiveTo identify and evaluate preoperative echocardiographic parameters that can predict the development of POAF in patients undergoing CABG.MethodsA systematic search was performed from January 2010 to October 2025 in PubMed, Scopus, Embase, and Web of Science. Risk of bias was assessed with ROBINS-I tool for observational studies.ResultsTwenty-three studies (n = 2,983 patients) were included. POAF incidence ranged from 22% to 40%, with a pooled rate of 28.8% (95% CI: 21%–34%). Meta-analyses showed no significant pooled associations for left atrial volume index (LAVI) (OR 1.31; 95% CI: 0.92–1.87; I² = 99.9%), peak atrial longitudinal strain (PALS) 0.65 (95% CI: 0.38–1.11); I² = 99.9%), or LA contraction strain (LASRa) (OR 1.91; 95% CI: 0.83–4.41; I² = 98.1%). Heterogeneity was substantial across all outcomes. Limited data suggested potential predictive value for right atrial indices and single-study parameters such as LA kinetic energy.ConclusionCurrent echocardiographic markers including LAVI, PALS, and LASRa, do not demonstrate consistent or robust predictive value for POAF when pooled across heterogeneous studies. Standardized measurement protocols and larger prospective cohorts are needed to clarify the role of atrial structural and functional indices in POAF risk stratification.Keywords: Echocardiography, Atrial fibrillation, Predictors, Coronary artery bypass grafting

Introduction

Postoperative atrial fibrillation (POAF) has been reported as the most common cardiac problem that may occur after coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) [1, 2]. The risk escalates with age, impacting nearly 50% of patients aged over 80 years [3, 4]. While POAF is commonly temporary and resolves on its own, it holds clinical importance due to its association with a higher risk of extended hospital admissions, cardiovascular-related mortality, and thromboembolic events. POAF can significantly affect the quality of life of patients and increase their healthcare costs [5, 6].

While many studies have established general clinical risk factors for POAF such as chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), smoking, decreased partial pressure of oxygen in the air, congestive heart failure, coronary artery disease (CAD), male gender, hypertension, prolonged cross-clamp time, and intraoperative inotropic exposure [1, 3, 5], all these factors represent population-level predictors. They may not fully capture individual patient risk.

Preoperative echocardiography has significant added value over other traditional and routine methods by providing patient-specific insights into structural and functional cardiac abnormalities that may underlie POAF. Parameters such as left atrial volume (LAV), left atrial diameter (LAD), left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), diastolic dysfunction, and valvular abnormalities can be analysed and monitored non-invasively and cost-effectively [4, 7–9].

Tissue Doppler Imaging (TDI) and Speckle Tracking Echocardiography (STE) including longitudinal, circumferential, radial strains, and strain rate imaging are specialized echocardiographic techniques that has been recently used to assess myocardial velocities and the formation indexes. These techniques are mainly useful in detecting subclinical atrial dysfunction that may not be apparent in a routine electrocardiogram (ECG) or clinical evaluation. For example, reduced left atrial strain detected via STE may indicate early atrial remodeling and increased susceptibility to POAF, even in patients without overt symptoms [10–12]. STE is angle non-dependent and has fewer dropouts and noise effects, and is commonly used for AF evaluation [9]. Therefore, obtaining echocardiographic findings can be useful in preoperative assessment, improve risk prediction, and guide surgical strategies to reduce the incidence of POAF. In this regard, the aim of the present systematic review is to critically evaluate and synthesize the available evidence on the ability of preoperative echocardiographic findings to predict early POAF in patients undergoing CABG surgery.

Methods

Although the protocol for this systematic review was not registered with any organization, all steps were performed accordance to PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-Analyses) 2021 statement. Each step was performed by two independent reviewers and any discrepancies between reviewers were resolved through discussion or adjudication by a third reviewer.

Search strategy

A comprehensive literature search was conducted across PubMed, Scopus and Web of Science for studies published between January 2010 and October 2025 using related keywords and Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) terms. Citation tracking was also conducted to identify additional studies. “OR” operation was used to combine synonyms/related terms within the same concept. “AND” operation was used to combine different concepts. Deduplication was performed using EndNote X9 automatic duplicate removal, followed by manual verification by two reviewers before screening. The search string and strategy in different databases is presented in Appendix 1.

Eligibility criteria

Eligible studies included observational studies that investigated the association between preoperative echocardiographic findings on early/immediate POAF following CABG. Immediate POAF defined as new onset of AF occurring in the immediate postoperative period (in-hospital/ up-to discharge/before 30 days) and not resolved after electrolyte correction. Regarding the surgical technique, both on-pump and off-pump CABG studies were eligible for inclusion. Exclusion criteria included reviews, studies lack sufficient outcomes, and studies with POAF occurring after 30 days.

Data extraction

By carefully reading the full text of the included studies data were extracted including first author’s name, year, study design, sample size, echocardiographic findings, incidence of POAF, and follow-up duration. Effect estimates were harmonized prior to pooling to ensure consistent directionality of association for left atrial strain parameters.

Risk of bias assessment

The quality of included studies was assessed using appropriate tools based on study design: Cochrane RoB 2.0 for randomized trials, ROBINS-I for non-randomized studies. GRADE (Certainty of Evidence) assessment was conducted per key predictor.

Echocardiographic definitions and measurement conventions

LA strain was classified into standard phasic components namely, reservoir (LASr), conduit (LAScd), and contraction (LASct/LASra), and PALS was defined as the maximum reservoir strain during ventricular systole. Most studies measured strain using point tracking from a 4-chamber apical view (sometimes with the addition of 2/3-chamber views), with vendor-specific software, frame rates of 50 to 90 frames per second, and primarily R-wave gating. For LA size, LAD was generally obtained from a 2D parasternal long-axis view, whereas LAVI was calculated using a two-plane disc method and indexed to BSA in most studies.

Meta-analysis

Data from the included studies were synthesized to evaluate the association between specific preoperative echocardiographic parameters and the risk of developing early POAF after CABG. Where at least 3 clinically and methodologically comparable studies report effect estimates for the same outcome, odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were extracted or calculated for that outcome. Random-effects model with REML estimation was applied due to anticipated clinical and methodological heterogeneity across studies. Reported odds ratios and hazard ratios were converted to log-ORs with corresponding standard errors for pooling, and units were harmonized across studies when required (e.g., per-unit increase for continuous predictors; low vs. high categories for PALS). The pooled incidence of POAF was estimated using a single-arm random-effects meta-analysis of study-specific proportions, conducted separately from the comparative analyses of echocardiographic predictors.

Statistical heterogeneity was assessed using the Cochran’s Q test and the I² statistic. When at least ten studies were available, sub-group analysis or/and meta-regression analyses were conducted. Publication bias and small-study effects were assessed using funnel plots and Egger’s or Begg’s tests, and, when bias was detected, the trim-and-fill method was applied for adjustment. All analyses were performed using Stata (version 17) software.

Results

A total of 23 studies including 2,983 CABG patients were included after the comprehensive search and screening process [13–34]. Most studies are prospective observational cohorts, with a smaller number of retrospective cohorts and one case–control study. All studies had short-term follow-up durations, typically within 7 days post-surgery or until hospital discharge. Of 23 included studies, 22 used on-pump CABG and only 1 study on- and off-pump compared [25], providing consistency in surgical exposure.

Independent predictors of POAF

The most frequently reported echocardiographic predictors of POAF were LAVI, LASRa, and PALS. Several parameters were reported as independent predictors in only a single study. Long-axis LA dimension was identified by Lacalzada et al. (2016) as an independent predictor (OR: 6.24, 95% CI: 1.97–8.23; P = 0.0017) [35]. Regarding the Right Atrial Volume Index (RAVI), only Aksu et al. (2019) reported it as an independent predictor (OR: 3.1, 95% CI: 2.2–6.3; P = 0.033)[16] identified RAVI as an independent predictor of POAF in regression analysis (OR: 3.1, 95% CI: 2.2–6.3, P = 0.033). Other single-report predictors included: LV peak untwisting velocity (UntwV) [18], LV global longitudinal strain (LVGLS) [27], left atrial kinetic energy (LAKE) [30], LA lateral atrial electromechanical delay [32], LASs (OR 1.63, 95% CI, 1.19–2.22) [33], and PA-TDI duration [20].

Meta-analysis

Meta-analyses were conducted for the overall incidence of POAF, and for the parameters that were reported in at least three independent studies that are LAVI, PALS, and left atrial strain during atrial contraction (LASRa).

LAVI

The primary meta-analytic synthesis of nine studies showed pooled OR (random-effects model):

1.31 (95% CI: 0.92–1.87) (Fig. 3). The pooled effect suggests 31% increased odds of POAF per unit increase in LAVi, but the 95% CI crosses 1.0, meaning the overall association is not statistically significant (p = 0.13). Thus, LAVi is not a consistent or strong predictor of POAF across studies when pooled. The heterogeneity (I² = 99.99%, τ² = 0.29, Q-test p = 0.00) is extremely high, suggests major inconsistencies between studies.

Forest plot of association between LAVi and prediction of POAF based on reported ORs

PALS

Among the included studies for PALS, Abdelrazek and Basaran reported ORs per unit increase in PALS, where OR < 1 indicates a protective association of higher PALS, whereas Lubis et al. reported a categorical comparison (PALS < 28% vs. ≥ 28%). We mathematically inverted the Lubis effect estimate to ensure that all pooled ORs reflected the same clinical contrast (higher PALS versus lower PALS). The meta-analysis of three studies evaluating PALS showed a pooled OR of 0.65 (95% CI: 0.38–1.11). (Fig. 4). Since the confidence interval crosses 1.0 therefore, the association is not statistically significant. Egger’s regression test indicated asymmetry in the PALS analysis (p = 0.014), although its reliability is limited by the small number of studies (k = 3).

The primary meta-analytic synthesis of 3 studies showed pooled OR: 1.91 (95% CI: 0.83–4.41) meaning the overall association is not statistically significant (p = 0.13) (Fig. 5).

Publication bias

Meta-analyses demonstrated extremely high heterogeneity (I² > 95% for all outcomes), inconsistent effect estimates, and wide CI indicating serious imprecision. Egger’s tests indicated significant publication bias for LAVI (p = 0.014), PALS (p < 0.001), and LASRa (p = 0.019).

Risk of bias assessment

Risk-of-bias (RoB) assessment for the 23 included studies was performed using the ROBINS-I framework (appropriate for non-randomized observational studies). Many studies are observational and although several reported multivariable models, the set of adjusted covariates varied and often did not include all important clinical confounders for POAF. Therefore, most prospective cohorts are “Moderate” for confounding; retrospective cohorts and small case–control studies are Serious because of greater likelihood of unmeasured confounding and limited covariate adjustment. Discussion

So far, many systematic reviews reported data on several independent medical predictive factors of POAF, including a higher CHA₂DS₂-VASc score, severe obesity, older age [37, 38], hypertension, preoperative β-blocker use, preoperative antiplatelet therapy, and renal failure [39, 40]. But in our study the focus was on finding echocardiography predictive factors of POAF. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first comprehensive systematic review that focuses on the association between echocardiographic parameters and the incidence of POAF after CABG.

POAF incidence across studies ranged from 22% to 40%, with most clustering between 25% and 33%. When data were pooled across all eligible groups, the overall incidence rate was 28%, which is consistent with previously published reports. Previous meta-analyses have similarly shown that POAF occurs in approximately one-quarter of patients undergoing isolated CABG surgery, with a pooled incidence rate of 26% [37, 41, 42].

Although several included studies suggested that LAVI may be associated with the risk of POAF, pooled meta-analytic evidence showed inconsistent findings across studies and did not demonstrate a statistically significant association with POAF (OR = 1.31; p = 0.13). Similarly, pooled analyses for PALS and LASra did not reach statistical significance, with pooled ORs of 0.65 (p = 0.13) and 1.91 (p = 0.13), respectively. Taken together, these findings indicate that current evidence is insufficient to confirm LAVI, PALS, or LASra as reliable standalone predictors of POAF across diverse patient populations.

PALS, which reflects early mechanical atrial dysfunction, appears to be a more sensitive marker of the arrhythmogenic atrial substrate and may provide a more clinically meaningful measure for predicting POAF than structural indices such as LAVI. In support of this, Lubis et al. showed that PALS value below 28% was associated with a hazard ratio of 4.8 (≈ OR 3.41; 95% CI 1.31–6.26)[25], highlighting its strong predictive potential. These findings suggest that future risk-stratified prevention strategies and prediction models should prioritize PALS, while LAVI could serve as a complementary parameter when assessed using standardized, harmonized methods. By focusing on atrial functional assessment, clinicians can better identify high-risk patients and tailor early interventions. LAVI alone, based on current pooled evidence, should not be relied upon as a stand-alone predictor for POAF.

Reduced LVEF and diastolic abnormalities point to a wider range of atrial and ventricular interaction that affects atrial electrophysiology and structural changes [20–23]. Only one study (Aksu et al., 2019) identified RAVI as an independent predictor of POAF [16]. Although right atrial indices were reported in only a few studies, their predictive value is biologically plausible. Enlargement of the right atrium (e.g., elevated RAVI) may reflect chronic pressure or volume overload, impaired atrial compliance, and heightened wall stress, all of which promote electrical remodeling and increase susceptibility to postoperative AF [43, 44]. Kosmala et al. found an association between LV untwisting rate and E/e′ (a marker of filling pressures), highlighting how untwisting may reflect elevated LV filling pressures [45]. Similarly, single-study predictors such as LV untwisting velocity, LVGLS, LAKE, and others likely capture different aspects of atrial–ventricular interaction, delayed conduction, or impaired atrial contractile function [46–49].

Future recommendations

We recommend that future research involves larger prospective multicentre studies that perform the following: [1] measurement of PALS and LAVi using harmonized protocols; [2] report both unadjusted and consistently adjusted effect estimates; [3] stratify by surgical technique; and [4] provide IPD or detailed subgroup data to permit meta-regression and sensitivity analyses. Analytically, future meta-analyses should perform influence diagnostics (leave-one-out), meta-regression for key modifiers (mean age, proportion on-pump, echo timing, POAF monitoring method), and sensitivity analyses excluding extreme outliers.

Limitations

Several limitations should be acknowledged. Substantial heterogeneity was observed, likely reflecting differences in echocardiographic measurement methods, predictor coding, analytic approaches, and timing of echocardiography relative to surgery. The small number of eligible studies for several parameters, including PALS, along with limited sample sizes and low event rates, reduced precision and restricted the feasibility of fully stratified analyses. In addition, variability in POAF surveillance strategies, despite a consistent 30-day outcome definition, may have influenced event detection and contributed to residual heterogeneity. Consequently, findings, particularly for PALS should be interpreted cautiously and require confirmation in larger, methodologically standardized studies.

Conclusion

Our findings show that although several echocardiographic indices of left atrial structure and function, particularly LAVI and PALS, have been proposed as predictors of POAF, the pooled evidence does not confirm consistent or strong associations. Overall, current echocardiographic markers alone are insufficient for reliable POAF prediction, highlighting the need for standardized, high-quality prospective research.

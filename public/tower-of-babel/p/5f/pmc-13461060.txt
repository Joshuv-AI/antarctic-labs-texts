Mini-incision oblique lumbar interbody fusion (OLIF) and anterolateral screw-rod fixation for surgical treatment of L5 spondylolisthesis
Authors: Kaihua Zhou, Jinghui Lei, Yuheng Chen, Libo Jiang, Yutong Gu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective:To explore the clinical efficacy of mini-incision oblique lumbar interbody fusion (OLIF) and anterolateral screw-rod fixation through the approach between the great vessels and the psoas muscle for the surgical treatment of L5 spondylolisthesis.Methods:From December 2019 to February 2021, patients with L5 spondylolisthesis undergoing OLIF51 (L5–S1 OLIF) using the OLIF25 (L2–5 OLIF) cage and anterolateral screw-rod fixation were enrolled in this retrospective study. The gender, age, BMI, BMD, operation time, intraoperative blood loss, intraoperative fluoroscopy, surgical segments, cage length and height, incision length, drainage removal time, hospitalization time, intraoperative and postoperative complications, and other relevant data of all patients were recorded. Visual analog scale (VAS) and Oswestry disability index (ODI) were used to evaluate the symptoms, signs, and neurological function of all patients before surgery and during postoperative follow-up.Results:There were 26 cases of L5 spondylolisthesis enrolled. The average operation time was 117.3 ± 13.0 minutes. The mean intraoperative fluoroscopy usage was 11 (8–19) times. The mean blood loss was 20 (10–55) mL, and the average incision length was 48.5 ± 3.7 mm. The drainage removal time was 2 (1–3) days. The mean hospital stay duration was 5 (3–6) days, and the follow-up duration was 29.8 ± 4.1 months. For the clinical evaluation, the VAS of back and leg pain significantly dropped after surgery (P < 0.01), and the ODI significantly decreased from 73.2 ± 12.5% to 13.8 ± 4.3% 2 years after surgery (P < 0.01). Anterior intervertebral space height, posterior intervertebral space height, lumbar lordosis, and segmental lordotic angle significantly improved after surgery (P < 0.05). Fusion grades based on the Bridwell grading system at the 2-year follow-up were grade I in 20 segments (76.9%) and grade II in six segments (23.1%). No patients experienced any form of permanent iatrogenic nerve damage or major complications.Conclusion:This study introduces a promising novel approach for treating L5 spondylolisthesis using a mini-incision OLIF51 with a larger OLIF25 cage and anterolateral screw-rod fixation. This method may offer advantages such as reduced blood loss, rapid postoperative recovery, and enhanced support and biomechanical stability.

Introduction

Lumbar interbody fusion is a common surgical treatment for lumbar spondylolisthesis. Initially, posterior lumbar interbody fusion (PLIF) and transforaminal lumbar interbody fusion (TLIF) were the mainstay techniques[1]. As open procedures, they are often accompanied by significant blood loss, substantial tissue trauma, a high risk of infection, and a prolonged recovery period. The advent of minimally invasive transforaminal lumbar interbody fusion (MIS-TLIF) brought some improvements[2]. This approach uses an intermuscular route through the paraspinal muscles, aiming to preserve muscular and ligamentous structures. As a result, it helps reduce blood loss and the incidence of complications[3]. In more recent years, endoscopic lumbar interbody fusion has emerged as a new option[4,5]. Employing a spinal endoscope for neural decompression and fusion minimizes blood loss. However, it has its drawbacks, such as increased fluoroscopic exposure, higher surgical costs, and the need for multiple incisions, which can extend the operative time.HIGHLIGHTSTraditional posterior surgery for L5 spondylolisthesis often causes significant trauma and complications. Anterior lumbar interbody fusion (ALIF) was once a recommended choice due to its clinical and biomechanical benefits. But its use has declined because it involves critical structures like the peritoneum, intestines, and major blood vessels, increasing the associated risks[6]. With the progress of minimally invasive techniques, lateral approach fusion procedures, including extreme lateral interbody fusion (XLIF), lateral lumbar interbody fusion (LLIF), and direct lateral interbody fusion (DLIF), have regained attention[7–10]. These methods follow a retroperitoneal path through the psoas muscle to implant large interbody cages. This approach preserves the posterior paraspinal muscles and osseous structures, leading to minimal trauma, reduced blood loss, and rapid recovery. Moreover, the use of larger cages makes it easier to reduce spondylolisthesis and achieve fusion compared to PLIF, TLIF, MIS-TLIF, and endoscopic fusion[11]. However, the transpsoas approach carries the risk of nerve injury.

Oblique lumbar interbody fusion (OLIF), introduced by Silvestre et al[12], is similar to XLIF, LLIF, and DLIF. It utilizes a natural retroperitoneal space, avoiding disruption of posterior spinal elements and nerve roots, and allows for the implantation of larger cages to achieve indirect neural decompression. The OLIF25 (L2–5 OLIF) procedure takes an oblique approach anterior to the psoas muscle to access the intervertebral space, bypassing the psoas muscle. This significantly reduces the risk of nerve injury compared to XLIF, LLIF, and DLIF[13,14]. Usually, OLIF25 requires supplemental pedicle screws and rod fixation to enhance biomechanical stability[15]. The stand-alone OLIF25 technique simplifies the procedure but lacks posterior instrumentation, resulting in insufficient stability, especially in osteoporotic patients[16]. This insufficiency raises the risk of cage subsidence and higher revision rates[17]. In our previous practice, we inserted pedicle screws into the vertebral bodies and fixed the rod over the screws from the anterolateral side after cage placement through the same mini-incision used in OLIF25 to treat lumbar intervertebral instability, including spondylolisthesis. The results showed that all patients achieved fusion at the 2-year follow-up, with no instrument failure observed[18,19].

Applying OLIF at the L5–S1 level (OLIF51) poses unique challenges. Vascular issues are more likely to occur, and the lower the iliac vein confluence, the more difficult it is to access the L5–S1 intervertebral disc space through the channel between the bilateral iliac vessels. Some surgeons have reported that the vascular injury rate in OLIF51 ranges from 4.3% to 15.0%[15,20,21]. Therefore, there is an urgent need for technical modifications and innovations to improve the safety and efficacy of the OLIF51 procedure. This study aims to retrospectively assess the feasibility, effectiveness, and safety of a modified OLIF51 technique. This technique involves implanting an OLIF25 cage via an approach between the vasculature and the psoas muscle, combined with anterolateral screw-rod fixation through the same mini-incision, for the treatment of L5 spondylolisthesis, based on long-term follow-up data. This retrospective case series study adheres to the STROCSS criteria[22].

Materials and methods

This retrospective case series study received approval from the Medical Ethics Committee of Zhongshan Hospital affiliated with Fudan University (No. B2022-464 R), and informed consent was obtained from all participants. The study focused on patients with L5 spondylolisthesis and neurological symptoms who underwent mini-incision OLIF51 combined with anterolateral screw-rod fixation via the corridor between the great vessels and psoas muscle from December 2019 to February 2021.

Inclusion criteria were: (1) symptoms of low back pain or lower limb pain/numbness due to L5 spondylolisthesis; (2) mild spondylolisthesis (Meyerding grade I–II)[23]; (3) failure of at least 6 months of conservative treatment; (4) a minimum follow-up of 2 years; (5) complete imaging data. Exclusion criteria were: severe spondylolisthesis (Meyerding III–IV); spinal tumor or infection; contraindications to surgery or significant organ dysfunction; and incomplete medical records.

All patients underwent comprehensive imaging, including lumbar anteroposterior/lateral, flexion-extension, and full-length standing radiographs, MRI, CT with 3D reconstruction, and bone density testing. L5 spondylolysis, iliac crest height, and the anatomical relationship between the abdominal aorta and left psoas major were evaluated. Electromyography or lower-limb arterial CTA was performed as needed. Surgical and anesthesia contraindications were excluded, with multidisciplinary consultation when required. Bowel preparation involved a liquid diet 1 day before surgery and oral He Shuang (65 g) 18 hours prior. Patients fasted for 10 hours and avoided fluids for 6 hours before surgery. Cefuroxime sodium (1.5 g) was administered 30 minutes before the incision for infection prophylaxis.

Under general anesthesia, patients were placed in the right lateral decubitus position. A 4–5 cm oblique incision was made anterior to L5–S1 and adjusted medially if it overlapped the iliac crest. The layers were dissected to access the retroperitoneal space, with careful retraction of the peritoneum and protection of the great vessels and ureter. Segmental vessels or iliopsoas veins were ligated if necessary. The L5–S1 disc was exposed, the annulus fibrosus was incised, disc material was removed, and endplate cartilage was excised. A trial implant confirmed appropriate size and positioning, followed by the insertion of an OLIF25 cage filled with allograft bone (Fig. 1). Fluoroscopy verified cage placement. Through the same incision, anterolateral screws were inserted near the endplates adjacent to the cage. Two 6.5-mm pedicle screws were placed in adjacent vertebrae, with the proximal screw directed upward and backward and the distal screw directed downward and backward. A rod was fixed to the screws, a drain was placed, and the wound was closed in layers. Figure 1.Schematic diagram of the OLIF51. Prophylactic antibiotics were administered for no more than 24 hours. Patients were encouraged to ambulate with a brace 2–3 days after drain removal (drainage < 20 mL/24 h). Bracing continued for 4–12 weeks, depending on bone quality and fixation. Follow-up assessments were conducted immediately postoperatively and at 1, 3, 6, and 12 months, as well as at 2 years.

The Oswestry disability index (ODI) was evaluated preoperatively and at 2 years. Visual analog scale (VAS) scores for back and leg pain were recorded preoperatively, immediately postoperatively, and at 3 months. Complications were documented throughout the perioperative and follow-up periods.

Lumbar 3D CT scans were used to measure Anterior intervertebral space height (AISH), posterior intervertebral space height (PISH), lumbar lordosis (LL), and segmental lordotic angle (SLA), and fusion status. Fusion was assessed using Bridwell criteria on sagittal and coronal CT reconstructions, with continuous trabecular bone bridging indicating fusion[24]. All imaging and clinical outcomes were evaluated by a single physician.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS 21.0. A P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. One-way analysis of variance for independent samples, followed by Tukey post hoc analysis, was used for VAS, intervertebral space height, lumbar lordotic angle, and operative SLA comparisons at different time points. The ODI score before and after treatment was compared using a t-test.

Results

A total of 26 patients with L5 spondylolisthesis, including 14 women and 12 men with a mean age of 55.8 ± 11.8 years, were included in this study (Fig. 2). The average operation time was 117.3 ± 13.0 minutes, with a mean intraoperative fluoroscopy usage of 11 (6–13) times. Intraoperative blood loss averaged 20 (15–26) mL, consistent with the reduced bleeding of minimally invasive techniques, and the average incision length was 48.5 ± 3.7 mm. Drainage was removed at a mean of 2 (1–3) days postoperatively, and the median hospital stay was 5 (3–6) days. Figure 2.STROBE flow chart. The mean follow-up duration was 29.8 ± 4.1 months. The VAS score for back pain decreased significantly from 7 (6–8) preoperatively to 1 (1–2) immediately after surgery (F = 323.1, P < 0.01) and further to 0 (0–1) at 3 months and 2 years postoperatively (F = 2654.5 and 2374.6, P < 0.01). Similarly, the VAS score for leg pain improved markedly from 8 (7–10) preoperatively to 1 (0–2) immediately after surgery and to 0 (0–2) at 3 months and 2 years postoperatively (F = 556.9 and 609.3, P < 0.01). The ODI was significantly reduced from 73.2 ± 12.5% preoperatively to 13.8 ± 4.3% at the 2-year follow-up (P < 0.01).

Postoperative radiographs and CT scans confirmed satisfactory implant positioning. Immediately after surgery, significant improvements were observed in AISH (13.2 ± 1.8 mm; P < 0.01), PISH (9.4 ± 1.0 mm; P < 0.01), LL (41.5 ± 7.3°; P < 0.01), and SLA (14.1 ± 2.9°; P < 0.01) compared to preoperative values. No significant changes in these radiographic parameters were observed at the 2-year follow-up compared to immediate postoperative measurements (P > 0.05), indicating maintained alignment without instability at the fused level (see Table 1; Figs 3 and 4). According to the Bridwell interbody fusion grading system, all cases achieved fusion by the 2-year follow-up, with 20 segments (76.9%) classified as grade I and six segments (23.1%) as grade II (Fig. 5). No other major complications were observed, supporting the safety of meticulous minimally invasive approaches. Discussion

Applying the standard OLIF technique, typically used for the L2–L5 segment (OLIF25), at the L5–S1 level (OLIF51) presents significant challenges due to the unique anatomy of this region[25,26]. These challenges primarily include:

1. Vascular factors: The L5–S1 disc space is anteriorly covered by the iliac vessels and their branches, which vary in location and are often adherent. The incidence of vascular injury during anterior approaches to this level ranges from 4.3% to 15.0%[27].

2. Bony obstruction: The prominent iliac crest often obstructs both the visual and instrumental access to the lateral aspect of the L5–S1 disc space.

3. Segmental characteristics: The L5–S1 disc space is generally narrower, has a greater degree of lordosis, and is reinforced by a thicker anterior longitudinal ligament, making disc space distraction and cage implantation more difficult[25]. If the restoration of PISH is suboptimal, the effectiveness of indirect decompression may be compromised, potentially necessitating direct posterior decompression[28].

This study innovates by systematically addressing these technical challenges in OLIF51. First, we used an approach between the great vessels and the psoas muscle, without focusing on the aortic bifurcation, inferior vena cava, or the distance between the bilateral iliac vessels. It’s important to note that segmental vessels or the iliopsoas vein may cover the lateral side of the L5–S1 intervertebral disc and should be ligated and cut off before cage implantation. Second, a left-sided approach with a modified oblique incision parallel to the pelvic rim is used, positioned at the front edge of the intervertebral space. When encountering the iliac crest, the incision is moved one finger width medial to it. Entering the L5–S1 interspace as anteriorly as possible, with the instrument handle vertical to the sagittal plane and anterior to the iliac crest, avoids obstruction without the need for specially angled tools. Third, a lateral approach with parallel distraction facilitates intervertebral space opening and cage placement.

A large-sized OLIF25 cage was employed in our novel OLIF51 technique, offering superior intervertebral space distraction. In our study, postoperative AISH, PISH, SLA, and LL were significantly higher than those measured preoperatively. Goh et al[29] compared the biomechanical properties of cages of different sizes using human lumbar spine functional units and found that only large fusion devices can improve stiffness in flexion, extension, lateral flexion, and axial rotation. The OLIF25 fusion device, which is larger than TLIF cages and other OLIF51 cages, provides stronger biomechanical stability. Lateral implantation of the OLIF25 large fusion cage, perpendicular to the sagittal shear force direction of L5–S1, along with the frictional force between the large fusion device and the entire transverse diameter of the vertebral endplate, enhances biomechanical stability. Currently, the insertion direction of the cage used in OLIF, ALIF, or TLIF at L5–S1 is almost parallel to the sagittal shear force, which may lead to a loss of intervertebral height and a reduction in spondylolisthesis correction. Additionally, the large OLIF25 cage allows for substantial bone grafting and a large contact area with the vertebral endplate, promoting good fusion. In this study, fusion was achieved in all cases at the 2-year follow-up.

A large-sized OLIF25 cage demonstrates a superior capacity for balanced bilateral expansion of the intervertebral space on the coronal plane. This characteristic is instrumental in reducing spondylolisthesis and increasing both the anterior and posterior disc height. Notably, the restoration of the PISH plays a pivotal role in tightening the posterior longitudinal ligament, thereby enlarging the cross-sectional area of the spinal canal and intervertebral foramen. This process effectively alleviates pressure on neurologic elements, achieving indirect decompression. Our study revealed that after OLIF51 with an OLIF25 cage, the PISH reached 9.4 ± 1.0 mm, significantly higher than the preoperative level of 2.9 ± 1.0 mm (P < 0.01). Concurrently, the VAS score for leg pain and the ODI exhibited a marked decline. Parallel distraction proves to be more effective in restoring intervertebral disc height, particularly PISH. Compared to our previously reported OLIF51 technique using a self-locking cage inserted between the bilateral iliac vessels for the surgical treatment of L5 spondylolisthesis, the postoperative PISH of L5–S1 in this study was significantly higher (9.4 ± 1.0 mm vs. 5.5 ± 2.1 mm)[28]. Insufficient posterior disc space distraction occurs when the cage is inserted from front to back, leading to unbalanced distraction of L5–S1. This imbalance compromises the efficacy of indirect decompression in OLIF51, necessitating direct decompression procedures such as percutaneous transforaminal endoscopic surgery.

Finite element analysis was conducted to investigate the biomechanics of OLIF alone under three different bone density conditions[30]. The findings indicated that as the degree of osteoporosis increases, the pressure on the fusion segment endplate rises significantly, along with an elevated risk of implant subsidence. OLIF-alone surgery fails to provide adequate stability for patients with osteoporosis, often requiring supplementary internal fixation. Studies have demonstrated that bilateral pedicle screw fixation offers the best biomechanical effect, providing superior stability for the spine and reducing the likelihood of second-stage revision fixation. However, this approach necessitates intraoperative position changes, double operations via the anterior and posterior approaches, prolonged operation time, and increased interference with the posterior paravertebral muscles. In our study, through the same small anterolateral incision, vertebral body screws (utilizing standard pedicle screws) were implanted. The entrance point was located at the anterolateral aspect of the vertebral body, immediately adjacent to the endplate adjacent to the OLIF cage, to avoid damaging the segmental vessels, the iliolumbar vein, and the psoas muscle. The screw trajectory was oriented nearly perpendicular to the sagittal shear force at the L5–S1 level, exhibiting superior pull-out strength compared to posterior pedicle screws. No instances of fixation failure were observed.

The modified single-incision MIS-TLIF technique has been proven to significantly reduce intraoperative blood loss (104.5 ± 24.53 vs. 214.9 ± 32.33 mL, P < 0.05) and postoperative drainage (141.4 ± 21.46 vs. 224.5 ± 25.53 mL, P < 0.05), leading to faster postoperative recovery and better functional outcomes compared to open surgery[31]. However, the novel OLIF51 technique in our study was associated with even less blood loss, shorter operative time, earlier drain removal, and fewer postoperative complications. Compared to posterior MIS-TLIF, this technique (OLIF51 combined with anterior anterolateral fixation) completely avoids intraspinal manipulation, theoretically reducing the risks of dural tear, direct nerve root injury, and postoperative intraspinal scar adhesion. Some scholars adopt a combined anterior–posterior approach (integrating OLIF51 with posterior pedicle screw-rod fixation) for the treatment of L5 spondylolisthesis. This involves initially placing the interbody cage via a trans-intervascular approach between the bilateral iliac vessels, followed by supplemental posterior fixation. This approach inevitably prolongs operative time, increases intraoperative blood loss, and raises the risk of vascular-related complications. Tong et al reported a mean operative time of 217.0 ± 91.9 minutes and a mean blood loss of 220.9 ± 166.1 mL, while data from Wu et al showed a mean blood loss of 335.4 ± 111.7 mL[32–34]. Our one-stage OLIF51 technique eliminates the need for posterior pedicle screw fixation, thereby reducing operative time, paraspinal muscle injury, and surgical bleeding.

As an exploratory case series analysis, this study has several limitations. First, the sample size is relatively small (26 cases) and originates from a single-center study. Larger sample sizes and multi-center studies would enhance the credibility of the results. Second, the average follow-up duration was 29.8 months. Although this exceeds 2 years, longer-term follow-up (e.g., 5 or 10 years) is necessary. Third, the study lacks a direct control group (e.g., comparing to traditional PLIF/TLIF, ALIF, or stand-alone OLIF51). The inclusion of a control group would strengthen the persuasiveness of the results. Fourth, this study only included cases of mild spondylolisthesis (Meyerding grades I-II). The applicability and reduction capability of this technique for more severe spondylolisthesis (grades III-IV) require further investigation.

In conclusion, this study presents a promising novel approach for mini-incision OLIF51 using an OLIF25 bigger cage combined with anterolateral screws and rod fixation to treat L5 spondylolisthesis. This technique may offer advantages such as less blood loss, rapid postoperative recovery, and superior support and biomechanical stability.

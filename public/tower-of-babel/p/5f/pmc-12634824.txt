Applying quality indicators and multidisciplinary team care to improve long-term survival in early-stage hepatocellular carcinoma
Authors: Hui-Ling Huang, Kuan-Chen Pan, Te-Sheng Chang, Shu-Chen Chen, Hsing-Ju Lu, Sheng-Nan Lu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveEarly-stage hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is potentially curable; however, long-term survival depends on timely treatment and effective surveillance. In Taiwan, national quality indicators and multidisciplinary team (MDT) care have been promoted to improve cancer outcomes. This study aimed to examine the association between two quality indicators—curative treatment and post-treatment imaging—and MDT care among long-term survivors of early-stage HCC.MethodsWe retrospectively reviewed 79 patients with early-stage HCC (Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer stage 0 or A) diagnosed between 2010 and 2020 who survived for more than 10 years. Data collected included treatment type, disease progression, MDT involvement, and surveillance frequency.ResultsTumor-free survival was achieved in 75.9% of patients after up to three treatment sessions. Patients who received curative treatment demonstrated better tumor control (49.0% vs. 26.2%). Among those with persistent disease, 89.5% received MDT care. Only 27.3% of patients with stage progression underwent the recommended imaging, compared with 87.1% of those without progression (P ​< ​0.001).ConclusionsAdherence to quality indicators and engagement in MDT care contribute to improved long-term survival among patients with early-stage HCC. Early detection of recurrence and timely initiation of treatment or retreatment are key to sustaining survival. Oncology nurse case managers play a vital role in coordinating care and ensuring adherence to surveillance protocols.

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) remains a significant global health burden, distinguished by a high mortality to incidence ratio.1 According to the 2022 Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer (BCLC) clinical practice guidelines, patients diagnosed at an early stage (stages 0 and A) are expected to achieve a median survival of more than five years, for whom curative treatment modalities are recommended.2

In Taiwan, the national median survival for patients diagnosed between 2011 and 2019 was 9.7 years for BCLC stage 0 and 6.3 years for BCLC stage A.3 Clinical management of HCC involves three levels of prevention. Primary prevention targets the elimination of risk factors, while secondary prevention emphasizes early disease detection. Tertiary prevention aims to reduce recurrence and improve outcomes through timely, appropriate treatment. Both secondary and tertiary prevention are critical to improving the prognosis of HCC.4 Nearly all practice guidelines outline stage-specific algorithms to guide the selection of treatment modalities. This guidance also applies to cases of recurrence. Therefore, we propose that the early detection of recurrence, followed by prompt treatment, represents a crucial strategy for improving outcomes, particularly for patients initially diagnosed in early stages with the potential for long-term survival.

To improve the quality of cancer care, Taiwan has implemented several national strategies, including the use of quality indicators and multidisciplinary team (MDT) care.5, 6, 7 Quality indicators, such as the proportion of patients in early stages receiving curative treatment and the frequency of post-treatment imaging, are used to assess and guide clinical performance, and their outcomes serve as benchmarks for cancer hospital certification. MDT involvement further enhances patient-centered decision-making and coordinated care. Within this framework, oncology nurse case manager play a pivotal role by coordinating weekly MDT meetings, supporting treatment decision-making, and overseeing the collection of annual performance indicators. Beyond administrative functions, oncology nurse case manager implement standardized follow-up protocols, deliver structured patient education, and provide reminder systems to ensure adherence to surveillance schedules and timely access to curative treatments.8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16 By facilitating communication across specialties and engaging patients in shared decision-making, oncology nurse case manager strengthen continuity of care and directly support the quality indicators assessed in this study.

Here, we investigated long-term survivors (≥ 10 years) of early-stage HCC at a regional hospital to evaluate the clinical impact of two key quality indicators on survival. Specifically, we sought to answer five clinical questions: (1) What proportion of early-stage patients achieved tumor-free survival (TFS) after up to three treatment sessions? (2) Is TFS associated with receiving curative treatment (Quality Indicator 1)? (3) What role did MDT care play in patients who did not achieve TFS? (4) Is stage progression (SP) beyond BCLC stage A associated with a poorer prognosis? (5) Is the frequency of surveillance within one year of locoregional treatment associated with earlier detection before SP (Quality Indicator 2)?

Methods

This retrospective observational study was conducted at a regional hospital in southern Taiwan. Between January 2010 and December 2020, a total of 3590 patients were newly diagnosed with HCC. Among them, 1491 were classified as having early-stage disease, defined by the BCLC staging system as stage 0 or A. Of these, 79 patients who survived more than 10 years were selected for further analysis (Fig. 1). Clinical data including treatment history, recurrence pattern, frequency of surveillance and survival outcomes were collected from the hospital cancer registry and electronic medical records.Fig. 1Flow diagram showing the disposition of participants in the study. To evaluate care quality, several quality indicators for HCC were adopted from the Cancer Care Quality Accreditation Program established by the National Health Research Institutes (NHRI) in Taiwan.16 Among these, two indicators specific to early-stage HCC were evaluated in this study.

Quality Indicator 1 assessed the proportion of early-stage HCC patients receiving curative-intent treatment. The denominator consisted of patients diagnosed with BCLC stage 0 or A, while the numerator included those who received curative therapies such as surgical resection, local ablation, or liver transplantation.

Quality Indicator 2 measured the proportion of patients who received at least three imaging studies within one year after undergoing curative treatment or transarterial chemoembolization (TACE). The denominator included patients who received curative-intent or TACE therapy, and the numerator was defined as those who completed three or more imaging studies—ultrasound, computed tomography (CT), or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)—within one year following treatment.

TFS was defined as the absence of recurrence after no more than three treatment sessions while consistently remaining within early-stage HCC (BCLC 0 or A).2 The cutoff of three sessions was adopted based on prior HCC cohort studies, which demonstrated that prognosis is closely associated with the number of treatment attempts required to achieve local tumor control, with most patients attaining control within three sessions.17 Tumor control was defined as the absence of viable tumor on follow-up imaging (CT, or MRI), in line with international guidelines. SP was defined as any advancement beyond the baseline BCLC stage, including increase in tumor size or number, vascular invasion, or extrahepatic spread. Curative treatments were defined as liver transplantation, surgical resection, and tumor ablation, whereas all other interventions were classified as non-curative.2,6,7

To analyze the association between treatment factors and long-term survival, univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression models were applied. These variables included age, sex, tumor number, viral hepatitis status, Child–Pugh grade, alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) level, diabetes mellitus, hypertension, alcoholism, whether curative treatment was administered at the initial session, the treatment modality used at the third treatment session (or at the last session if fewer than three were performed), and the occurrence of SP beyond BCLC stage A or beyond BCLC stage B. Stepwise regression was used to identify independent risk factors. Categorical variables were compared using chi-square tests or Fisher's exact tests, as appropriate. Kaplan–Meier analysis was used to estimate survival following progression beyond BCLC stage 0 or A. A two-sided p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Swimmer plots were constructed to visualize each patient's treatment course and disease progression. All statistical analyses were performed using R version 4.2.3 and IBM SPSS Statistics version 26.0.

Discussion

The present study highlights several important findings. First, baseline characteristics at the time of diagnosis were not strongly associated with long-term survival, with the exception of age in the TFS group. Notably, nearly one-third of the long-term survivors were over 70 years of age at diagnosis, suggesting that treatment selection and disease course after diagnosis played a more decisive role in long-term outcomes than baseline demographics. Second, TFS was identified as the only statistically significant independent prognostic factor in the overall multivariate analysis. This underscores the clinical importance of achieving TFS, as patients who remained recurrence-free within three treatment sessions demonstrated significantly improved survival. Third, among patients who achieved TFS, receipt of curative-intent therapy at either the initial or subsequent treatment sessions was significantly associated with favorable outcomes, reinforcing the relevance of the first quality indicator examined in this study—the proportion of early-stage HCC patients receiving curative treatment. Lastly, although the number of patients in the non-TFS group was small (n ​= ​19), limiting subgroup analyses, SP remained statistically significant in both univariate and Kaplan–Meier analyses. This finding highlights the importance of the second quality indicator—frequent post-treatment imaging—given that extended survival is particularly difficult to achieve in patients with recurrent disease.

Our findings suggest that adherence to HCC quality indicators is closely associated with long-term outcomes. In particular, receipt of curative treatment within the first three treatment sessions was strongly linked to achieving TFS and extended survival in patients with early-stage HCC. These results are consistent with both national and international guidelines, which emphasize timely curative treatment for BCLC stage 0 or A disease.2,6,7,18 This study further aimed to identify modifiable care factors among early-stage HCC patients who achieved exceptional survival. From the perspective of oncology nursing and case management, we emphasized compliance with quality indicators and the role of MDT care.9,14 We propose extending the common principle of “early diagnosis and prompt treatment” to encompass “early diagnosis of occurrence and recurrence, followed by prompt treatment and re-treatment.”

In addition, our data highlight the prognostic significance of surveillance and disease monitoring. Once disease progresses to an advanced stage, treatment options become limited and survival declines sharply. Therefore, post-treatment imaging is essential for timely detection of recurrence and re-intervention. Among early-stage patients who survived beyond 10 years, SP—particularly progression to advanced stage—was a key determinant of survival. Surveillance three times per year with ultrasonography and AFP measurement was significantly associated with early detection of SP.6,18,19 Current quality indicators recommend at least three imaging studies following locoregional treatment.16 Based on our findings, we suggest that all survivors, even those who are tumor-free, should remain under structured surveillance.

High quality ultrasonography performed by experienced physicians appeared sufficient for detecting intrahepatic progression; however, CT and MRI remain indispensable for identifying extrahepatic spread, lymph node involvement, and vascular invasion, which cannot be reliably detected by ultrasound alone. Notably, only five patients in our study progressed to BCLC stage C, with just two cases involving extrahepatic spread, limiting our ability to demonstrate the incremental benefit of CT/MRI over ultrasonography. This limitation underscores the need for larger multicenter studies to clarify the comparative effectiveness, cost-effectiveness, and optimal integration of ultrasound, CT, and MRI in long-term surveillance strategies for early-stage HCC.

Guidelines universally recommend that patients with early-stage HCC receive curative treatment, such as transplantation, resection, or tumor ablation. In this study, 98 of 140 (70.0%) treatments among cancer-free survivors were curative, and 48 of these (49.0%) resulted in complete tumor control. These figures indicate room for improvement in both the frequency of curative treatment selection and the effectiveness of curative treatments.

Importantly, our findings show that not all early-stage HCC patients are able to achieve a tumor-free state. In this cohort, 19 patients survived more than 10 years with persistent or recurrent disease. MDT care was highly prevalent in these cases and may have contributed to sustained survival. Current clinical guidelines recommend stage-specific management, typically requiring input from different specialties. MDT collaboration enables tailored treatment planning and comprehensive patient support, especially for those with complex clinical trajectories.9,10,20 Oncology nurse case managers serve as essential members of the multidisciplinary care team, acting as care coordinators to ensure continuity throughout complex cancer treatment trajectories. They play a key role in facilitating communication among health care professionals, promoting patient adherence to individualized care plans, and providing appropriate social resources and support to both patients and their families.8,9 Their advanced nursing expertise is particularly critical in the care of individuals with recurrent or persistent disease, contributing meaningfully to long-term survivorship and enhancing the overall quality of cancer care. In addition, senior oncology nurse case managers in leadership roles have a broader impact on the delivery of nursing services, helping to standardize care practices, mentor junior staff, and lead quality improvement initiatives across the cancer care continuum.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, the patient inclusion period spanned a decade, during which treatment strategies and standards of care may have evolved. Although this is an inherent challenge in long-term survival research, our focus on early-stage disease likely minimized the effect of temporal variability, as most major therapeutic advances during this interval primarily influenced advanced HCC. Second, the sample size was relatively small and derived from a single-center cohort, which may limit the generalizability of our findings. Nevertheless, the primary objective of this study was to evaluate quality-of-care indicators within a multidisciplinary and systems-based framework. Because all patients survived for at least 10 years, baseline heterogeneity is unlikely to have substantially affected the long-term outcomes. Importantly, our findings suggest that multidisciplinary management may contribute to consistent long-term outcomes in early-stage HCC, but these observations require validation in larger, multicenter cohorts before broader conclusions can be drawn. Finally, we acknowledge that patient-centered outcomes such as quality of life, mental health, and socioeconomic status were not assessed in this study. These dimensions are critical to understanding survivorship beyond clinical outcomes, and future research should integrate such measures to provide a more comprehensive evaluation of long-term survivors with early-stage HCC.

Conclusions

Achieving TFS, through curative treatment and maintaining consistent frequency of surveillance are critical to long-term survival in patients with early-stage HCC. Even in cases with recurrence, MDT care contributes to sustained survival by supporting individualized, guideline-based treatment planning. This study reinforces the value of two quality indicators—the rate of curative treatment and the frequency of post-treatment imaging—as practical tools for assessing and improving cancer care. Oncology nurse case manager play a pivotal role in promoting adherence to these indicators and coordinating effective MDT collaboration. These findings support the continued integration of quality-driven strategies to enhance outcomes for early-stage HCC patients.

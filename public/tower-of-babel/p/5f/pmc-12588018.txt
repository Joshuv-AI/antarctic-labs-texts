Family expectations of grief care from home health nurses: a scoping review protocol
Authors: Yuta Himeno, Honoka Wada
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionIn preparation for becoming a super-aged society with high mortality, Japan is strengthening its comprehensive community care systems and promoting end-of-life care at home. Many nurses realise that providing grief care to families experiencing loss and bereavement is important, but several studies have identified a lack of competence when specialised care is provided to families in need. The aim of this study is to systematically review research on grief care to target families utilising home nursing services in Japan and to identify methods of care that have been published and research that has been reported in the Japanese literature.Methods and analysisThis scoping review will follow the Arksey and O’Malley methodology framework. An exploratory review of the literature on grief care in home healthcare will help to clarify the research question (step 1). A broad range of electronic databases (ICHUSHI-Web, PubMed/MEDLINE, CINAHL/EBSCO and SCOPUS) and search terms will be used to retrieve relevant articles published between 1983 and 2024 (step 2). Studies are systematically selected by two independent reviewers based on eligibility criteria (step 3). The title and abstract are scrutinised to determine if the article meets the eligibility criteria. Full-text screening is then conducted to retrieve only relevant publications. Data are then extracted, collated and charted (step 4), and a summary of the aggregate results is presented (step 5).Ethics and disseminationThis scoping review will collect data from publicly available sources and will not require ethics review. Once data collection and summarisation are complete, the results will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications, and the key findings of the review will be presented at relevant conferences.

Introduction

In Japan, as the population rapidly ages, there is an increasing emphasis on facilitating end-of-life care in home settings. Families providing end-of-life care experience grief both before (anticipatory grief) and after death (bereavement). Therefore, home health nurses are expected not only to deliver medical and nursing care but also to offer emotional support to patients and their families throughout the dying process.1 2 Grief care—defined as care that supports individuals experiencing emotional distress, sadness or adjustment difficulties related to death or anticipated loss—has been recognised as an essential yet underdeveloped aspect of home health nursing practice.38

Since the inclusion of home health nursing within the national insurance system in 1983, the number of home deaths has steadily increased.9 However, research on grief care in Japan has primarily focused on hospital, hospice or institutional settings, often describing nurses’ emotional burdens or coping strategies in bereavement support.1013 Consequently, little is known about the grief care needs, expectations and experiences of families cared for in the home environment. Recent work has also indicated that many home-visit nurses in Japan feel inadequately prepared to provide psychological or bereavement support, and that grief care training is inconsistent across organisations.14

Internationally, grief care is increasingly regarded as an integral component of family-centred palliative care.1520 Studies in Western countries have described structured bereavement programmes, communication interventions and models, emphasising family adaptation and meaning-making after loss.21 Moreover, meta-analytic evidence has demonstrated that structured bereavement interventions can significantly reduce depression, anxiety and grief intensity among caregivers.22 However, the applicability of these approaches to Japan remains uncertain, given the unique sociocultural context in which family members often assume strong caregiving roles, and open discussions about death and grief are socially constrained. These cultural factors may influence both the delivery of and expectations for grief care in home health nursing.

Nonetheless, some existing Japanese studies provide insight into related areas that underscore the relevance of this review. For example, Ono23 examined relationships among actions, antecedents and outcomes of grief care provided by visiting nurses and found that both pre-death and post-death care behaviours were influenced by years of nursing experience and participation in grief care training. Asaumi et al24 reported that home-visit nurses identify caregiver distress and subtle changes in patient conditions as triggers for initiating end-of-life discussions, highlighting the importance of early recognition of family needs. Similarly, Ozawa et al25 showed that while most home-visit nurses provide some form of bereavement support, extended or long-term follow-up with bereaved families was rare, and provision varied by facility type and nurse experience. These findings emphasise the need for structured and culturally responsive grief care that aligns with family expectations in home settings.

To our knowledge, no scoping or systematic review has comprehensively synthesised evidence on family expectations of grief care provided by home health nurses in Japan. Therefore, this review aims to map existing research, clarify conceptual and practical gaps, and provide evidence to inform culturally appropriate, family-centred approaches to grief support in home health nursing.

In this review, ‘family’ refers to close relatives or primary caregivers who provide care and support to patients in the home setting. ‘Grief care’ is defined as professional or informal care that includes both anticipatory grief care (support before death) and bereavement care (support after death), addressing emotional, psychological and social needs of bereaved families.

Methods and Analysis

Protocol design

A scoping review is an appropriate methodology for reviewing research on the grief care expectations that families have of home care nurses, for providing an overview of the research conducted in this field, for determining the scope of available research, for understanding the key findings and for identifying gaps in the evidence.

The research team consists of two members with complementary expertise. YH holds a PhD in Nursing and has clinical experience in home health nursing, including direct practice in providing grief care. HW is a nurse with limited prior research experience, and through this project, aims to acquire scoping review skills under the supervision of YH. This combination ensures both methodological rigour and the training of early-career researchers in the field of nursing science.

This scoping review will be conducted using the framework recommended by Arksey and O’Malley: Step 1: identifying the research questions; Step 2: identifying relevant studies; Step 3: selecting studies; Step 4: charting data; Step 5: collating, summarising and reporting the results.26

Step 1: Identifying the research questions

Research questions will be formulated through discussions with the research team, experts and practitioners in home healthcare nursing. The main research objectives are to understand how grief care for families utilising home nursing services is defined and practised and to identify and characterise gaps between studies.

The following questions will guide the review:

Step 2: Identifying relevant studies

A comprehensive search strategy was developed in consultation with the research team to ensure the identification of relevant studies. The search will target studies published between 1983, when insurance coverage for home health nursing began in Japan, and 2024. Four electronic databases will be searched: ICHUSHI-Web, PubMed/MEDLINE, CINAHL/EBSCO and SCOPUS.

The PICO framework will be used to map the research questions and identify relevant search terms, thereby ensuring comprehensive coverage of the literature while avoiding unnecessary searches.

The search strategy will consist of three main components: family (target), grief care provided by visiting or home health nurses (intervention) and the expectations, acceptance or satisfaction of families (outcomes). To comprehensively capture relevant studies, the strategy will combine terms related to family, grief care and home health nursing. Both controlled vocabulary (e.g., MeSH terms) and free-text keywords will be applied.

In addition to database searches, we will also conduct manual reference checks (hand-searching) of included studies and key journals. Grey literature will not be included in this review. The complete search strategies for each database are provided in online supplemental appendix 1.

Inclusion criteria: Original primary studies were conducted in Japan and published in Japanese or English between 1983 and 2024. Eligible designs include randomised controlled trials (RCTs), self-controlled before-and-after studies, case-control studies, cross-sectional surveys, observational studies, qualitative interviews, focus groups and case studies. Both quantitative and qualitative research are included without restriction, provided that the study explores family expectations, experiences or perceptions of grief care provided by home health nurses.

Exclusion criteria: Reviews without primary data, conference abstracts without full text and studies limited to hospital or hospice settings will be excluded.

Patient and public involvement

None.

Step 3: Study selection

Search results will be exported to Endnote for data management (including duplicate removal and referencing). After removing duplicates, data will be organised in Excel for screening and extraction. Screening will be done in two stages, the first being screening of titles and abstracts. This will be conducted by two independent reviewers who will select studies related to the Population, Intervention, Comparator and Outcome (PICO) criteria, as described above.27 The PICO tool will be used to map research questions and identify relevant search terms. This tool guides comprehensive coverage of relevant evidence to focus on the scope of the study and avoid unnecessary searches.28 Disagreements between the reviewers will be resolved through discussion to determine whether to include or exclude studies. Studies that cannot be assessed for inclusion criteria based on a review of the title and abstract will proceed to full-text assessment, where eligibility will be determined.

The second stage is full-text screening. To determine which studies will be the subject of data extraction and synthesis, all studies not excluded in the first step will be read and reviewed in full text. The two independent reviewers will conduct the full-text review. Discrepancies in opinions will then be discussed, and a final decision will be made on whether to exclude them or not. Studies that do not meet the inclusion criteria will be excluded, and the reasons for exclusion will be documented in the final report. The search results will be reported in full in the final report and presented in the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) flow diagram. Understanding of the inclusion and exclusion criteria will be standardised by critically reviewing several studies that strictly adhere to the PICO criteria.

Study selection and data extraction will be conducted independently by YH and HW. YH holds a PhD in nursing and possesses clinical expertise in home-based medical care nursing, in addition to experience conducting narrative reviews. HW is a nurse with limited research experience and will conduct the review process under YH’s supervision as part of methodological training. Disagreements will be resolved through discussion, and external advisors will be consulted as needed to ensure rigour.

Step 4: Charting the data

Data will be organised and structured by creating a framework. This framework will evaluate full-text articles that meet the inclusion criteria. The following key information will be collected from the selected articles: year of publication, country and context, study design, conceptual framework used, objectives, methods and study results. This format will be used to collect data on the intervention strategy (content of care). To ensure the validity of this process, YH and HW will create experimental formats for several studies and discuss them. They will then extract data as independent reviewers, and any discrepancies in opinion will be resolved through consensus among the team members.

Step 5: Collating, summarising and reporting the results

Data will be synthesised using both qualitative and quantitative approaches. Qualitative data will be analysed thematically to identify recurring concepts and categories related to family expectations of grief care. Quantitative data will be summarised using descriptive statistics, such as frequencies and proportions, to provide an overview of study characteristics and reported outcomes. The data summary analysis and reporting process will follow the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-Analyses extension for Scoping Reviews guidelines (PRISMA-ScR) guidelines for systematic reviews and meta-analyses.29 The PRISMA-ScR checklist includes 20 required reporting items and two optional items.

The results of this scoping review aim to create meaningful tables that demonstrate effective grief care as perceived by families. These results will be made available to our project and to the community of researchers in home care nursing to collect feedback on the findings for further interpretation and analysis. This step is essential in knowledge sharing, which is the foundation of our work.

Implications

This review will provide insights for clinical practice (guidelines for grief care training in home health nursing), inform policy-making (evidence for home care service standards), and identify gaps for future research (areas requiring new primary studies).

Protocol registration

This scoping review protocol has been registered with the Open Science Framework (OSF) to ensure transparency and reproducibility of the review process. The registration record is available at: https://osf.io/4b7w8

Ethics and Dissemination

No ethical review is required in this study because this scoping review collects and integrates data from publicly available sources. The results of this scoping review will benefit various stakeholders, including researchers, home nursing practitioners, hospitals and patients. Once data collection and summarisation are complete, an article reporting the scoping review results will be prepared and published through open access in relevant home nursing journals. We also plan to present and disseminate the results at relevant conferences.

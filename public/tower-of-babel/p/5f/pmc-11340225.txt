Real-world status, efficacy and prognosis analysis of first-line treatment for unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma in patients treated at multiple centres
Authors: Jing Liu, Qianyu Du, Yu Shao, Han Xu, Xiuli Liu, Wenting Zhang, Mingxi Wang, Zhengguang Zhou, Qingsheng Kan, Yan Yang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo present the real-world status and explore the predictors of the efficacy and prognosis of first-line treatment for unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma (uHCC).MethodsReal-world data of uHCC patients who underwent first-line treatment at 4 hospitals in Northern Anhui, China, from July 2019 to December 2022 were retrospectively collected. The clinicopathological features, haematological indicators, including superoxide dismutase (SOD) and vascular endothelial growth factor-A (VEGF-A), efficacy and safety data were analysed.ResultsA total of 153 patients were enrolled and most of them treated with targeted therapy combined with immunotherapy (TI). Compared to patients treated with TI, patients who were administrated with TI plus locoregional therapy (TIL) showed longer median progression-free survival (mPFS) and median overall survival (mOS) times (both p < 0.05), with manageable safety profiles. Moreover, compared to patients with low baseline serum levels of SOD, patients with high baseline serum SOD levels had a better treatment efficacy and had longer mPFS and mOS times (all p < 0.05). Subgroup analyses indicated that patients with low SOD levels had longer mPFS times when receiving TIL than when receiving TI (p = 0.005), but, among patients with high SOD levels, their prognoses were not substantially different between TIL and TI (p > 0.05). Additionally, patients in the low-VEGF-A group had a longer mOS time than patients in the high-VEGF-A group (p = 0.004). In comparison with TI, TIL can improve the survival time among patients with high VEGF-A levels but not among patients with low VEGF-A levels.ConclusionsTI was the most commonly first-line systemic therapy for uHCC patients, with better efficacy and outcomes when combined with locoregional therapy in a certain population. Baseline serum SOD and VEGF-A were found to be potential predictive biomarkers for decision-making, treatment response, and outcome in patients with uHCC in the primary care setting.

Introduction

Primary liver cancer is the sixth most commonly diagnosed cancer and the third leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide [1]. Of these, hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is the most common pathological type and has a poor prognosis [2]. Available treatment for HCC has changed in recent years. In the IMbrave 150 study [3, 4], researchers first confirmed the effectiveness of targeted therapy combined with immunotherapy (TI) and showed that the immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) atezolizumab combined with bevacizumab could significantly improve the survival of patients compared with sorafenib in terms of median overall survival (mOS) (19.2 months vs. 13.4 months, p < 0.001) and median progression-free survival (mPFS) times (6.9 and 4.3 months, p < 0.001). The phase III HIMALAYA study [5] indicated that compared to sorafenib, the combination of tremelimumab and durvalumab significantly ameliorated OS (p = 0.0035) in unresectable HCC (uHCC) patients with no history of prior systemic treatment. In addition to the combination of atezolizumab and bevacizumab, as well as the combination of tremelimumab and durvalumab, there is a wider range of preferred combination regimens for first-line systemic therapy available to treat patients with uHCC in China. The phase III ORIENT-32 study [6] showed that sintilimab plus a bevacizumab biosimilar (IBI305) conferred a significantly longer mPFS time (4.6 months vs. 2.8 months, p < 0.0001) and mOS time (not reached vs. 10.4 months, p < 0.0001) than sorafenib in Chinese uHCC patients. The combination of apatinib and camrelizumab led to a mPFS time of 5.7 months and a mOS time of 20.1 months in Chinese patients with advanced HCC, as demonstrated in the phase II RESCUE study [7]. Based on the above studies, TI, a new therapeutic option that increases survival times, has now become the preferred first-line systemic therapy for uHCC.

Chinese patients often have a high tumour burden and are subjected to late tumour staging at initial diagnosis [8], and most patients are likely to lose the opportunity for second-line treatment after receiving first-line treatment. Therefore, improving the success rate of first-line treatment for Chinese uHCC patients is a pressing clinical challenge. Professional guidelines have explicitly recommended first-line treatment regimens for uHCC, based on currently available locoregional and systemic therapies in China; however, the actual strategies and types of combinations are diverse in the context of complex clinical practice. In the meantime, despite ongoing progress of these diverse strategies for the treatment of uHCC patients, there are still some patients who are unable to benefit from systemic therapy due to a lack of effective biomarkers. More and more scholars have emphasized the role of locoregional therapy in HCC and even in metastatic liver tumours [9], but whether the local treatment is a useful adjuvant for systemic therapy for uHCC as first-line treatment remains to be elucidated. Therefore, the aim of this study was to present real-world scenarios of uHCC patients undergoing first-line treatment in Northern Anhui, China, to explore potential predictive/prognostic biomarkers and establish clinically applicable indicators to guide treatment decisions for uHCC patients with a dismal prognosis after first-line treatment.

Materials and methods

Patient selection

The clinical data of HCC patients were retrospectively derived from four hospitals in northern Anhui Province, China, from July 2019 to December 2022. The inclusion criteria included the following: age ≥ 18 years; Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status score ≤2; histologically confirmed or clinically diagnosed HCC; CT/MRI confirmed uHCC; Child–Pugh classification score ≤7; Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer stage (BCLC) C or unsuitable for radical surgical resection BCLC B; at least one measurable target lesion evaluated by CT/MRI based on Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumours guidelines version 1.1 (RECIST 1.1); no prior systemic therapy for uHCC; all patients received at least two cycles of systemic therapy. The exclusion criteria were as follows: simultaneous presence of other active malignancies; liver transplant patients; and incomplete and untraceable clinical data. This retrospective study followed the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the First Affiliated Hospital of Bengbu Medical University (BYYFY-2018KY46). Given that this study was conducted by retrospectively collecting data from routine clinical practice, the ethical committee waived the consent, and individual informed consent was not obtained. In this retrospective study, no patient identifiers were used and data were anonymized.

Treatment administration

Patients who received either targeted therapy, immunotherapy, chemotherapy, or any combination of them as first-line systemic therapy regimens were eligible. Selection of treatment regimens for all enrolled patients was based on the international or domestic authoritative guidelines or standardization of diagnosis and treatment for HCC. The specific therapy regimens and dose modifications were decided by clinicians at each study centre based on the patient’s disease status, physical condition, organ function, economic condition, drug tolerance, etc. First-line treatment was discontinued in instances of disease progression or intolerable adverse events. In addition, patients with concomitant hepatitis B virus (HBV) or hepatitis C virus (HCV) infection simultaneously underwent antiviral therapy, and HBV-DNA or HCV-RNA levels were regularly monitored.

Collection of clinicopathological data and haematological indicators

Clinical data such as the patient’s age, sex, ECOG performance status score, aetiology, Child–Pugh classification, tumour size, macrovascular invasion, extrahepatic metastases, BCLC stage, prior locoregional therapy, therapeutic efficacy, and survival outcome were collected. Given that serum vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGF-A) has shown potential as a biomarker in predicting the patients’ survival in certain malignant tumours [10, 11], while serum superoxide dismutase (SOD) plays a controversial role in this process [12, 13], this study aimed to explore the values of these two indicators in uHCC patients. Therefore, haematological indicators including VEGF-A, SOD, and alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) within 1 week before first-line therapy were obtained. According to the manufacturer’s instructions, VEGF-A detection was performed using a Weigao JR-1 Chemiluminescent Immunoassay Analyser and Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor Assay Kit (chemiluminescence) (Shandong Weigao Group Medical Polymer Co., Ltd., Weihai, China), the SOD assay was performed using a Roche Cobas 8000 Biochemical Analyser and SOD Assay Kit (colorimetric method) (Anhui Iprocom Biotechnology Co., Ltd.), and AFP was detected using an Abbott Architect i2000 Chemiluminescent Immunoassay Analyser and AFP Assay Kit (chemiluminescence microparticle immunoassay) (Abbott Laboratories Trading (SHANGHAI) Co., Ltd.). The reference ranges of serum VEGF-A, SOD, and AFP were 0–160 pg/ml, 129–216 KU/L, and 0–20 ng/ml, respectively.

Efficacy and safety assessments

Tumour responses, including complete response (CR), partial response (PR), stable disease (SD), and progressive disease (PD), were evaluated based on RECIST 1.1 and hepatocellular carcinoma-specific modified RECIST. The objective response rate (ORR) was equal to the proportion of patients with CR and PR, and the disease control rate (DCR) was equal to the proportion of patients with CR, PR, and SD. PFS was defined as the time from the initiation of first-line treatment to the occurrence of PD or death from any cause, and OS was defined as the time from the initiation of first-line treatment to death from any cause. Treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) were graded according to Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events, version 5.0.

Follow-up

TRAEs and survival data were collected through inpatient, outpatient, and telephone follow-ups until the patients were lost to follow-up or died. The cut-off date for follow-up was April 30, 2023, with a median time of 10.1 months [interquartile range (IQR) 6.4–17.6 months].

Statistical methods

SPSS statistical software (version 25.0) was utilized to perform the statistical analyses. Categorical variables are presented as numeric values (percentages), while continuous variables are presented as medians (IQRs). The chi-square test was used for the analysis of differences in categorical variables in different groups. The optimal cut-off values of continuous variables related to short-term therapeutic response were determined by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, and outcome-based cut-off optimization was performed using X-tile software (version 3.6.1). PFS and OS were calculated by the Kaplan–Meier method, and log-rank tests were used for survival comparisons. Univariate Cox regression analysis was first conducted to screen for candidate variables related to PFS and OS, and variables with p < 0.05 or clinical significance were selected to perform the multivariate Cox regression analysis. Based on the optimal cut-off values of SOD and VEGF-A, patients were divided into high- and low-secreting groups. In all statistical tests, p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Current status of the first-line treatment for uHCC in Northern Anhui Province, China

A total of 153 patients were enrolled. Table 1 presents their baseline characteristics in detail. Regarding the first-line treatment regimens, patients were treated with diverse regimens, including TI, targeted therapy + immunotherapy + locoregional therapy (TIL), targeted therapy (± chemotherapy), targeted therapy (± chemotherapy) + locoregional therapy, immunotherapy (± chemotherapy), immunotherapy + locoregional therapy, and oxaliplatin-based systemic chemotherapy. The targeted therapy drugs involved in this study included apatinib (n = 49), lenvatinib (n = 41), bevacizumab (n = 26), sorafenib (n = 22), donafenib (n = 4), and anlotinib (n = 7). The ICIs used as immunotherapies were as follows: camrelizumab (n = 60), sintilimab (n = 35), tislelizumab (n = 16), toripalimab (n = 7), and penpulimab (n = 5). Locoregional therapy for HCC lesions as first-line treatment in this study included interventional therapy and ablation. TI was the most common regimen in 81 (52.9%) patients, and TIL was most common in 39 (25.5%) patients. Another 33 (21.6%) out of 153 patients underwent other regimens. By the end of the follow-up, 114 (74.5%) patients had PFS data, and 84 (54.9%) patients had OS data. In the overall cohort, the mPFS and mOS times were 7.0 months (Figure 1(B)) and 15.0 months (Figure 1(C)), respectively. In this study, there was no hyperprogression in the evaluation of efficacy among all of the patients. Among all enrolled patients, TI-containing regimens remained the most commonly used regimens (n = 120). Considering that TI or TI-containing regimens are the most preferred regimens based on both the current evidence-based medical proof, and our real-world data, we divided the total population into two categories: TI-containing regimens and other regimens. The DCR was higher in patients treated with TI-containing regimens than in patients treated with other regimens (75.0% vs. 57.6%, χ2 = 3.835, p = 0.050, Figure 1(D)). The specific therapeutic response information is provided in Table S1. The mPFS time was significantly longer in patients treated with TI-containing regimens than in patients treated with other regimens (7.2 months vs. 4.5 months, p = 0.003, Figure 1(E)). Patients treated with TI-containing regimens had longer mOS times than patients treated with other regimens (15.8 months vs. 12.1 months, p = 0.113, Figure 1(F)). A total of 120 patients treated with TI-containing regimens had complete PFS and OS data. The mPFS time was 5.6 months in patients treated with TI and 12.8 months in patients who accepted TIL (p = 0.036, Figure 1(G)), and TIL led to a longer mOS time than TI (25.8 months vs. 13.3 months, p = 0.033, Figure 1(H)).

As mentioned above, the vast majority of patients received TI-containing regimens, resulting in better therapeutic efficacy and outcomes than other regimens. Therefore, we have chosen 120 patients who accepted TI-containing regimens for the following efficacy and prognostic analysis. The baseline characteristics of these 120 patients stratified by therapy regimens are presented in Table S2, showing more patients in the group of TI exhibited extrahepatic metastasis and staged at BCLC C (both p < 0.05), which findings are in line with the recommendations from professional guidelines.

Analyses of prognostic factors affecting PFS and OS

The correlation between SOD and the therapeutic efficacy and prognosis of several malignant tumours has been demonstrated, but the findings in studies with different types of cancer or even in different study populations within the same type of cancer are still controversial [12–15]. Therefore, this study explored the relationship between serum SOD and the therapeutic efficacy and prognosis of first-line treatment for uHCC. Before conducting prognostic analyses, the optimal cut-off values of SOD affecting PFS and OS were determined to be 146 KU/L and 132 KU/L using X-tile software, respectively (Supplementary Figures 1 and 2).

Together with clinical features, haematological indicators, including SOD and AFP, were selected as candidate factors for the univariate Cox regression analyses. In the univariate Cox regression analyses, the ECOG PS (p = 0.005), combination with or without locoregional therapy (p = 0.038), and SOD level (p = 0.000), which correlated with PFS, were screened out (Figure 2(A)). With or without tumour size >10 cm or diffuse type, macrovascular invasion, and extrahepatic metastases, which were considered potential predictors because of clinical significance, were selected for the multivariate Cox regression analysis. After multivariate Cox regression analyses, ECOG PS (p = 0.048), combination with or without locoregional therapy (p = 0.023), and SOD level (p = 0.000) were found to be independent prognostic factors for PFS (Figure 2(B)).

Cox regression analyses of PFS in 120 uHCC patients who received TI-containing regimens. (A) Forest plot of univariate Cox regression analyses. (B) Forest plot of multivariate Cox regression analyses.

Similarly, the two haematological indicators (SOD and AFP) and clinical features were candidate factors affecting OS for the next regression analysis. Based on univariate analyses, ECOG PS (p = 0.022), Child–Pugh classification (p = 0.000), tumour size >10 cm or diffuse type (p = 0.006), combination with locoregional therapy (p = 0.036), and SOD level (p = 0.000) correlated with OS (Figure 3(A)). Multivariate Cox regression analyses revealed that tumour size >10 cm or diffuse type (p = 0.031), combination with or without locoregional therapy (p = 0.017), and SOD level (p = 0.003) could independently predict the OS of patients (Figure 3(B)).

Cox regression analyses of OS time in 120 uHCC patients who received TI-containing regimens. (A) Forest plot of univariate Cox regression analyses. (B) Forest plot of multivariate Cox regression analyses.

Baseline serum SOD as a potential factor influencing the efficacy and prognosis of TI-containing regimens

Before conducting therapeutic efficacy analyses, the ROC curve of baseline serum SOD was plotted in Figure 4(A). Then, the optimal cut-off value of SOD was determined to be 145.5 KU/L, dividing patients into low- and high-SOD groups. When receiving TI-containing regimens, patients in the low-SOD group exhibited a lower DCR than patients in the high-SOD group (62.2% vs. 82.7%, χ2= 6.270, p = 0.012, Figure 4(B) and Table S3). Based on the optimal cut-off values of SOD affecting PFS and OS, patients were divided into high- and low-SOD expression groups. Patients in the high-SOD group had a longer mPFS time than patients in the low-SOD group (12.6 months vs. 5.0 months, p = 0.000, Figure 4(C)). Similarly, the mOS value in high-SOD patients was also better than that in low-SOD patients (25.1 months vs. 8.3 months, p = 0.000, Figure 4(D)).

To elucidate whether the baseline serum SOD level has the potential to aid in the decision-making of first-line therapeutic strategies for uHCC patients, we performed subgroup analyses. Patients in the low- and high-SOD groups were classified into TI and TIL subgroups, respectively. In the low-SOD group, the mPFS time of TIL patients was significantly prolonged compared to the TI subgroup (12.8 months vs. 3.3 months, p = 0.005, Figure 5(A)). Meanwhile, patients in the TIL subgroup also showed a longer mOS time than those in the TI subgroup (13.9 months vs. 6.8 months, p = 0.053, Figure 5(B)). However, in the high-SOD group, there was no significant difference in patients’ treatment outcomes between the TI and TIL subgroups (both p > 0.05; Figure 5(C and D)).

Baseline serum VEGF-A as a potential factor influencing the efficacy and prognosis of TI-containing regimens

Due to the importance of antiangiogenic therapy for HCC [16, 17], we, therefore, firstly examined the role of VEGF-A in HCC from public databases. In the TCGA database, VEGF-A mRNA level was showed to be markedly over-expressed in HCC tissues and was associated with poor outcomes of HCC patients (p < 0.05, Supplementary Figure 3). The value of serum VEGF-A was then explored in HCC patients of our cohort. In this study, baseline serum VEGF-A was available for 54 of 153 patients, and the clinical value of VEGF-A as a predictive biomarker for first-line treatment efficacy and outcome of uHCC was explored separately. First, the ROC curve of VEGF-A for predicting poor prognosis is shown in Figure 6(A). The area under the curve of VEGF-A was 0.585, and the optimal cut-off value was 158.45 pg/ml. Therefore, the value was used to divide patients into low- and high-VEGF-A groups. When receiving TI-containing regimens, patients in the low-VEGF-A group exhibited a higher DCR than patients in the high-VEGF-A group (78.8% vs. 52.4%, χ2 = 4.149, p = 0.042, Figure 6(B) and Table S4). Subsequently, we used X-tile software to redefine the optimal cut-off values of VEGF-A affecting PFS and OS to be 113.4 and 185.8 pg/ml, respectively (Supplementary Figures 4 and 5). These two cut-off values were used to divide patients into two VEGF-A categories. As shown in Figure 6(C), no significant difference in mPFS between the low-VEGF-A and high-VEGF-A groups was observed; however, there was a prolongation in the mPFS time, up to 3.3 months in the low-VEGFA group (8.1 months vs. 4.8 months, p = 0.151). In terms of OS, patients in the low-VEGF-A group exhibited a longer mOS time than patients in the high-VEGF-A group (20.5 months vs. 6.1 months), which was a notable difference (p = 0.004, Figure 6(D)).

To explore the potential baseline VEGF-A value to guide the treatment decision for uHCC patients, patients in the low- and high-VEGF-A groups were further subclassified into TI and TIL subgroups, and a survival analysis was carried out between the TI and TIL subgroups. In the low-VEGF-A group, no significant difference was seen between the TI and TIL subgroups (all p > 0.05; Figure 7(A and B)). However, in the high-VEGF-A group, the mPFS time in the TI and TIL subgroups was 3.3 and 11.1 months, respectively, with a significant difference (p = 0.010, Figure 7(C)). Meanwhile, the mOS time in the TIL subgroup also conferred a survival advantage over that in the TI subgroup (15.8 months vs. 5.3 months, p = 0.036, Figure 7(D)).

Safety profiles

Table 2 presents TRAEs with an incidence ≥5% in this study. A total of 136 (88.9%) of the 153 enrolled patients experienced at least one TRAE. The most common TRAEs were hypertension (39.2%), thrombocytopenia (32.7%), decreased appetite (28.8%), increased aspartate aminotransferase (25.5%), increased alanine aminotransferase (23.5%), and hyperbilirubinemia (21.6%). The top five TRAEs with grades 3 ∼ 5 were hypertension (21.6%), thrombocytopenia (6.5%), increased aspartate aminotransferase (4.6%), hyperbilirubinemia (4.6%), and neutropenia (4.6%). In addition, one patient died from hepatic failure due to therapeutic drugs.

Discussion

The combination of TI has reshaped the treatment pattern of uHCC. Based on this real-world data derived from the northern area of Anhui Province, China, we observed that the mPFS and mOS times of TI as first-line therapy for uHCC were 5.6 and 13.3 months, respectively. This real-world study confirmed the effectiveness of this combination therapy in uHCC patients by its superior therapeutic efficacy and prognosis compared to other regimens, which was consistent with the results of numerous clinical trials [3, 4, 6, 7]. Nevertheless, there is a certain deviation in mPFS and mOS from a numerical perspective between our research results and the results of clinical trials. We speculated that one reason may be the different features of our enrolled patients, such as high tumour burden (Child–Pugh classification B accounting for up to 20% and extrahepatic metastasis accounting for 53.3%) and nonuniform treatment regimens. Another important reason is that China was experiencing the COVID-19 pandemic during the implementation of this research, which led to some impact on medication treatment, follow-up, and tumour evaluation. Collectively, our data could represent, to a certain extent, the real-world status of uHCC among the Chinese population.

The subgroup analysis in this study revealed that the efficacy of TIL is superior to that of TI. As patients in the TI group tended to have a heavier tumour burden than in the TIL group from disease characteristics analysis, this conclusion needs to be interpreted with caution and criticism. Considering our findings were consistent with the results of latest researches [18–20], it is believed that TIL has its superiority. We supposed that the higher ORR and better survival benefits achieved in our study may be due to the synergistic effect of targeted therapy, immunotherapy, and locoregional therapy. Studies have confirmed that interventional therapy could enhance tumour immunogenicity by the release of tumour antigens and increase the abundance of CD4+ and CD8+ T lymphocytes, which helps to potentiate the response to immunotherapy in HCC patients [21–23]. TACE might lead to an increase in vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) in HCC patients [24], while VEGF can drive abnormal tumour angiogenesis, leading to the formation of an immunosuppressive microenvironment [25]. Meanwhile, anti-VEGF agents can promote the nor­malization of tumour angiogenesis, increase the infiltration of T lymphocytes [26–28], reduce tumour neovascularization induced by locoregional therapy [29, 30], and thereby augment antitumour efficacy. Our results support that the triple combination therapy of TIL can be an optimal option for certain advantaged patients.

Anti-VEGF agents play an important role in the treatment of HCC [16, 17], while VEGF can increase after locoregional therapeutic intervention [24]. Therefore, we considered that serum VEGF-A may serve as a valuable indicator and analysed its role in uHCC treatment. We identified that high VEGF-A levels were negatively associated with efficacy and prognosis of systemic TI therapy for uHCC. Similarly, Miao et al. [31] reported that HCC patients with high VEGF-A expression had a shorter disease-free survival time than patients with low VEGF-A expression. Lacin et al. [32] also depicted the negative relationship between serum VEGF-A levels and clinical outcomes in Turkish patients with HCC, regardless of the stage and treatment protocol of the enrolled patients. In addition, our previous studies have confirmed the value of baseline serum VEGF-A in predicting the survival outcome in lung cancer [10] and breast cancer [11] patients. Thus, the role of VEGF-A needs to be explored in HCC. One of the most encouraging findings of this study was that baseline serum VEGF-A levels had the potential to identify uHCC patients who may benefit from triple combination therapy with TIL. Because patients with high serum VEGF-A levels generally have a heavier tumour burden [32, 33], we consider that for high-VEGF-A secreting patients with a dismal prognosis, TIL is recommended as first-line treatment according to our findings. Considering the noninvasiveness, convenience, and repeatability of serum VEGF-A detection, VEGF-A is a potentially promising and useful biomarker, and may be helpful in optimizing implementation in the primary care setting.

Importantly, this study also found the value of SOD as an indicator of therapeutic efficacy and prognosis in uHCC patients. Patients with high SOD levels were believed to have a better DCR and longer mPFS and mOS times than patients with low SOD levels. From the results of the subgroup analyses, it was observed that in comparison with TI, TIL could improve survival times among patients with low SOD levels but not among patients with high SOD levels. Therefore, we recommend prioritizing the TIL regimen as the first-line treatment for uHCC patients with low baseline serum SOD levels. SOD, an oxidoreductase, plays a vital role in catalysing and scavenging oxygen-free radicals [34]. The regulatory function of SOD in the occurrence and development of tumours is gradually being recognized. The expression levels of extracellular SOD are significantly downregulated across a vast majority of cancers, and overexpression of extracellular SOD inhibits cancer cell growth, invasion, and metastasis in numerous in vitro and in vivo studies, suggesting a tumour sup­pressive role [35]. Generally, only a few studies have investigated the role of SOD in serum and its probable relationship with therapeutic efficacy and prognosis of malignant tumours. High-serum SOD activity was reported to be correlated with inferior survival outcomes in patients with nasopharyngeal carcinoma [12]. However, serum SOD levels were positively associated with the prognosis of patients with oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma, as demonstrated by another study [13]. Thus, the role of serum SOD in predicting the therapeutic efficacy and prognosis of malignancy remains controversial. Based on our findings, we speculated that the possible reason for the better prognosis of uHCC patients with high SOD levels is that SOD can enhance the patient’s ability to clear oxygen-free radicals and protect against DNA and cell membrane damage, all of which can ultimately lead to better survival outcomes for such patients. The prognosis of early-stage liver cancer patients is significantly correlated with preoperative SOD levels, and patients with high preoperative serum SOD levels may have a better prognosis [14]. This result supported our findings. Based on the current research status and our findings, more studies are needed to explore the role of serum SOD in uHCC treatment, as SOD is a feasibly measured predictive/prognostic biomarker in clinical application that could enable rational treatment decisions and optimizes patient management.

There are some limitations in this real-world study. First, selection bias is inevitable in a retrospective design. Second, although this study was conducted at four institutions, the sample size was relatively small. Finally, the targeted agents and ICIs received by enrolled patients were heterogeneous. The diversity between different treatment regimens may have an impact on the results. Therefore, the results of this study warrant validation by expanding the sample size in future well-designed prospective multicentre randomized clinical trials.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this real-world study in northern Anhui Province, China, confirmed the superiority and effectiveness of TI in the real-world treatment of uHCC. In addition, TIL might conferred better efficacy and better outcomes than TI in certain uHCC populations. Serum SOD and VEGF-A were found to be potential predictors of first-line therapeutic efficacy and treatment outcome in uHCC patients and might help to identify patients who may benefit from TIL. These findings will be essential for deciding the optimal first-line treatment and management of uHCC.

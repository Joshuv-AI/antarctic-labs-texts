Efficacy and safety of loncastuximab tesirine in relapsed or refractory diffuse large B-cell lymphoma: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Abat Khan, Hira Habib, Ayesha Imran Butt, Iftikhar Khan, Khushbakhat Aziz, Ehsanullah Alokozay, Haneen Kamran, Soban Ali Qasim, Ammara Saleem, Shaiq Ijaz, Bilal Khan, Muhammad Siddique Karimi, Saad Javed Malik, Asim Elahi, Jose Sandoval-Sus
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundRelapsed/refractory diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (R/R DLBCL) remains a therapeutic challenge, particularly for patients ineligible for stem-cell transplantation or CAR T-cell therapy. Loncastuximab tesirine, a CD19-directed antibody-drug conjugate, has demonstrated promising single-agent activity in recent studies and is approved in this setting. In this meta-analysis, we aim to assess the efficacy and safety of loncastuximab tesirine monotherapy in patients with R/R DLBCL.MethodsA systematic review was conducted per PRISMA 2020 guidelines, searching PubMed, Embase, and Cochrane CENTRAL from inception until March 2025. Studies reporting clinical outcomes of loncastuximab tesirine monotherapy with defined efficacy and safety endpoints were included. Seven studies met the inclusion criteria, including three clinical trials and four observational cohort studies. Two reviewers independently extracted data. Pooled analyses were conducted in R (version 4.5.1) using absolute risk and random-effects modeling. Heterogeneity was assessed using the I2 statistic; publication bias was evaluated via Egger’s test and funnel plots.ResultsThe pooled overall response rate (ORR) was 47.3% (95% CI 33.5–61.3%), and the complete response (CR) rate was 22.0% (95% CI 16.7–27.7%). One-year overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) were 48.2% (95% CI 17.8–79.3%) and 31.2% (95% CI 9.2–58.9%), respectively, with substantial heterogeneity across studies. In the clinical trial subgroup, ORR and CR were 47.3% (95% CI 33.3–61.5%) and 23.9% (95% CI 21.9–26.1%), while 1-year OS and PFS were 43.8% (95% CI 25.2–63.3%) and 28.0% (95% CI 14.6–43.8%), respectively. No publication bias was detected. Treatment-emergent adverse events (AEs) occurred in 98.7% of patients, with grade ≥ 3 events in 82.6%. Common hematologic AEs included anemia (47.7%; grade ≥ 3: 13.6%), leukopenia (16%, grade ≥ 3: 9.7%), neutropenia (32.9%; grade ≥ 3: 22.4%), and thrombocytopenia (24.3%; grade ≥ 3: 16.1%). Frequent non-hematologic AEs included fatigue (20%), fever (15.6%), vomiting (12.4%), and abdominal pain (11.4%). Other reported events included hypokalemia (25.5%), rash (20%), and peripheral edema (23.7%).ConclusionThis meta-analysis supports the efficacy of loncastuximab tesirine monotherapy in heavily pretreated R/R DLBCL, with an ORR of ~ 47% and manageable toxicity. These findings reinforce its role as a valuable treatment option in patients with limited alternatives and highlight the need for randomized studies to optimize patient selection and sequencing strategies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13643-026-03216-8.

Introduction

Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL), the most common subtype of non-Hodgkin lymphoma, accounts for 30–40% of cases worldwide and typically presents as an aggressive disease with rapidly enlarging nodal or extranodal masses [1]. Classified by the World Health Organization as a malignant proliferation of large B lymphoid cells, DLBCL encompasses biologically diverse subtypes that differ in clinical behavior, prognosis, and treatment response [2].

Despite its aggressive nature, DLBCL is potentially curable. Patients with localized disease achieve 5-year progression-free survival (PFS) rates of 80–85%, whereas those with advanced-stage disease have lower survival rates of approximately 50% [3]. Diagnosis relies on excisional biopsy with histopathological and immunophenotypic confirmation, alongside molecular characterization such as cell-of-origin and high-grade variants identified through Fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) [4].

Rituximab with Cyclophosphamide, Doxorubicin, Vincristine and Prednisone (R-CHOP) regimen remains the backbone of first-line therapy, overall curing 60% of newly diagnosed patients [5]. However, 30–50% of patients relapse or are refractory (R/R) to initial treatment, with poor outcomes, especially in early relapses [6–8]. Second-line options such as high-dose chemotherapy followed by autologous hematopoietic stem-cell transplantation (HCT) and chimeric antigen receptor T-cell (CAR-T) therapy improve outcomes in selected patients, but many relapse after these interventions, leaving limited effective treatments [9]. This underscores the urgent need for novel agents with durable efficacy and manageable safety in relapsed or refractory diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (R/R DLBCL) [10].

Loncastuximab tesirine (LoncaT) is a CD19-directed antibody-drug conjugate that delivers a pyrrolobenzodiazepine (PBD) dimer toxin to B-cells, producing targeted cytotoxicity [11]. Clinical trials have shown encouraging activity in R/R DLBCL, even in heavily pretreated patients with high-risk features, with a manageable safety profile [12]. It is currently being investigated in earlier lines of therapy across different clinical trials.

This meta-analysis addresses a critical unmet need in R/R DLBCL by providing the first comprehensive synthesis of LoncaT efficacy and safety across multiple clinical studies. Previous reports have been limited to individual trials with small sample sizes and variable outcomes, leaving clinicians and policymakers without a consolidated evidence base. By pooling data systematically, our work provides stronger statistical power, clearer benchmarks for response and survival, and a rigorous assessment of safety profiles. These insights are urgently needed to guide therapeutic decisions in a population with limited options and poor prognosis, and they position loncastuximab tesirine within the broader treatment landscape more definitively than any prior study.

Methods

We conducted a systematic literature search across PubMed, Embase, and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL), spanning the inception of the databases to March 2025. The search strategy combined controlled vocabulary and free-text terms related to diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (“diffuse large B-cell lymphoma,” “DLBCL”), loncastuximab tesirine (“loncastuximab tesirine,” “loncastuximab,” “ADCT-402”), and disease status (“relapsed,” “refractory,” “relapsed/refractory”). No language restrictions were applied. The full reproducible database-specific search strategies, including exact syntax and search fields, are provided in Table S1. To ensure completeness, the reference lists of included articles and relevant reviews were manually screened. The review was conducted in accordance with the PRISMA guidelines [13].

Studies were eligible if they met the following criteria: (1) adult patients with relapsed or refractory DLBCL, (2) treatment with LoncaT as monotherapy, and (3) reporting of at least one efficacy outcome, overall response rate (ORR), complete response (CR), progression-free survival (PFS), or overall survival (OS), or at least one safety outcome such as treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs). We included phase I–III clinical trials, and prospective or retrospective cohort studies. Case reports, reviews, preclinical studies, and abstracts without sufficient data were excluded.

Two reviewers independently extracted data using a predesigned form. Study selection was performed in two stages. First, titles and abstracts were screened independently by two reviewers for potential eligibility. Second, full-text articles of selected records were assessed against predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria. Duplicate and overlapping records referring to the same study were identified and removed prior to screening. Study-level information included the first author, year of publication, study phase and design, sample size, median follow-up time, and patient demographics (median age, sex, prior therapies, HCT, and CAR-T history). Outcomes extracted included ORR, CR, OS, and PFS, as well as TEAEs and grade 3 or higher toxicities, including hematologic and non-hematologic adverse events. Disagreements were resolved through consensus and discussions, and when multiple reports were available for the same trial, the most complete and updated data were used.

Statistical analyses were performed using R version 4.5.1 (2025–06–13). Meta-analyses were conducted using the meta package (version 8.2.1), with additional analyses performed using the metafor package (version 4.8.0). Adverse events were synthesized as pooled absolute risks using a random-effects model with Freeman-Tukey double arcsine transformation and Hartung-Knapp adjustment, with prediction intervals calculated to account for between-study variability. All primary analyses were conducted using a random-effects model, fixed-effect (common-effect) estimates are additionally presented for comparative purposes and should be interpreted as sensitivity analyses rather than the primary inference. Between-study heterogeneity was quantified with the I2 statistic. Small-study effects such as publication bias were evaluated using Egger’s regression test and funnel plot visualization.

The prospective single-arm clinical studies were assessed using the Methodological Index for Non-Randomized Studies (MINORS). For non-comparative studies, MINORS includes eight domains: clearly stated aim, inclusion of consecutive patients, prospective data collection, appropriate endpoints, unbiased assessment of endpoints, adequate follow-up period, loss to follow-up < 5%, and prospective calculation of study size. Each domain was scored from 0 to 2, with a maximum score of 16 [14]. For non-randomized studies, the Newcastle–Ottawa Scale (NOS) was applied (https://ohri.ca/en/who-we-are/core-facilities-and-platforms/ottawa-methods-centre/newcastle-ottawa-scale). Assessments were performed independently by two reviewers, and disagreements were resolved through consensus.

Results

The study selection process is illustrated in Fig. 1 (PRISMA diagram). A total of 299 records were identified across PubMed, Embase, and Cochrane CENTRAL. After removal of 200 duplicate and overlapping records, 99 records underwent title and abstract screening, of which 91 were excluded. Eight reports were sought for full-text retrieval, all of which were successfully obtained and assessed for eligibility. Of these, 1 study was excluded due to not meeting inclusion criteria. Ultimately, 7 studies [10, 15–20], including 3 prospective single-arm clinical trials and 4 observational cohort studies, were included in the final meta-analysis.

Baseline characteristics

Across the included studies, 748 patients with R/R high-grade B-cell lymphomas, including DLBCL, HGBCL including double/triple hits were included. Across the included studies, the median age was 66 years, with reported ages ranging from 20 to 95 years, with male patients comprising 58% to 69% of the cohorts. Most patients had advanced disease, with stage III–IV reported in 77% and 93%, respectively. Most patients had received ≥ 2 prior lines of therapy. The ECOG performance status was 0–1 in the majority of patients. The baseline characteristics of the included studies are listed in Table 1.

Efficacy outcomes

In the overall pooled analysis, the overall response rate (ORR) was 47.3% (95% CI 33.5–61.3) and the complete response (CR) rate was 22.0% (95% CI 16.7–27.7%) (Fig. 2). These estimates were associated with moderate-to-high heterogeneity, particularly for ORR (I2 = 89.4%). Progression-free survival (PFS) within 1 year was 31.2% (95% CI 9.2–58.9%) and overall survival (OS) within 1 year was 48.2% (95% CI 17.8–79.3%), both demonstrating very high heterogeneity (I2 > 95%) with wide confidence and prediction intervals, reflecting substantial between-study.

Pooled incidences of efficacy outcomes with loncastuximab

To address this, a pre-specified sensitivity analysis restricted to prospective single-arm clinical trials (n = 3) was performed (Fig. 3). In the sensitivity analysis, efficacy estimates remained stable, with ORR of 47.3% (95% CI 33.3–61.5%) and CR of 23.9% (95% CI 21.9–26.1%), while heterogeneity was substantially reduced (I2 = 28.6% for ORR and 0% for CR). One-year PFS (28.0%, 95% CI 14.6–43.8%) and 1-year OS (43.8%, 95% CI 25.2–63.3%) showed similar trends with more moderate heterogeneity (I2 = 51% for PFS and 55.7% for OS), suggesting that variability in the overall analysis is likely driven by differences in study design and baseline risk rather than inconsistent treatment effects.

Pooled incidences of efficacy outcomes with loncastuximab in prospective single-arm clinical trials only

Assessment of small-study effects was performed using funnel plots (Supplementary Fig. S1) and Egger’s regression test for ORR and CR, as these outcomes included the highest number of studies (k = 7). For ORR, Egger’s test did not demonstrate evidence of funnel plot asymmetry (t = 0.12, p = 0.91). Similarly, for CR, no significant asymmetry was observed (t = 0.31, p = 0.77). Visual inspection of funnel plots did not suggest marked asymmetry although the funnel plot for ORR was broad (Supplementary Fig. S1). However, given the limited number of included studies being less than 10, these analyses should be interpreted cautiously.

Safety outcomes

Safety outcomes are summarized in Figs. 4, 5, 6 and 7. Treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) occurred in 98.7% of patients (95% CI 94.2–100%), with grade ≥ 3 events reported in 82.6% (95% CI 0–100%), reflecting imprecision due to limited study numbers (see Fig. 4). Selected systemic toxicities included fatigue (20.0%, 95% CI 0–100%), fever (15.6%, 95% CI 0–80.4%), and rash (20.0%, 95% CI 5.2–40.9%) (see Fig. 4).

Hematologic toxicities (Fig. 5) were frequent, including anemia (47.7%, 95% CI 0–100%), neutropenia (32.9%, 95% CI 0–100%), and thrombocytopenia (24.3%, 95% CI 0–100%), with grade ≥ 3 events observed in 13.6% (95% CI 0–83.8%), 22.4% (95% CI 0–83.9%), and 16.1% (95% CI 0–54.5%), respectively.

Fluid and electrolyte-related toxicities (Fig. 6) included hypokalemia (25.5%, 95% CI 0–100%), pleural effusion (14.8%, 95% CI 0–88.7%), and peripheral edema (23.7%, 95% CI 14.6–34.3%). Hepatic enzyme elevations were observed with AST (38.2%, 95% CI 0–100%) and ALT (31.6%, 95% CI 0–100%), both demonstrating substantial variability across studies (Fig. 6).

Gastrointestinal and abdominal toxicities (Fig. 7) included vomiting (12.4%, 95% CI 3.6–25.1%), abdominal pain (11.4%, 95% CI 8.8–14.3%), constipation (11.9%, 95% CI 6.3–18.9%), and decreased appetite (13.8%, 95% CI 0–43.4%), generally with low to moderate heterogeneity.

Quality assessment

Regarding observational studies, the methodological quality of the four included cohort studies was variable, as assessed by the Newcastle–Ottawa Scale. Overall, study scores ranged from 3 to 8, reflecting a range from high to low risk of bias; specifically, Nastoupil et al. [19] achieved the highest score of 8/9 by addressing selection (representativeness of the exposed cohort, selection of the non-exposed cohort, ascertainment of exposure, and absence of baseline outcome), comparability (awarded one point for controlling key confounders), and outcome domains (assessment of outcome, follow-up duration, and follow-up completeness). Iqbal et al. [20] scored 6/9; the study was strong in cohort representativeness, exposure ascertainment, absence of baseline outcome, outcome assessment, follow-up duration, and follow-up completeness, but lost points for selection of the non-exposed cohort and comparability (no adjustment for important confounders). Epperla et al. [17] scored 4/9, with strengths in cohort representativeness, exposure ascertainment, and absence of baseline outcome, but weaknesses in selection of the non-exposed cohort, comparability, follow-up duration, and follow-up completeness (retaining a point only for outcome assessment). Ayers et al. [18] scored 3/9, with strengths in cohort representativeness, exposure ascertainment, and absence of baseline outcome; however, they lost all points for the selection of the non-exposed cohort, comparability, and outcome domains (See Supplementary Table S2). The three prospective single-arm clinical studies were assessed using the Methodological Index for Non-Randomized Studies (MINORS). The overall MINORS scores ranged from 10 to 15 out of a maximum of 16, reflecting moderate-to-high methodological quality. The LOTIS-2 trial [10] demonstrated the highest quality (15/16), supported by robust study design, appropriate endpoints, and adequate follow-up. The phase I study [16] had a lower score (10/16), primarily due to absence of formal sample size calculation and limited follow-up reporting. The multicenter phase II study [15] demonstrated moderate-to-high quality (12/16). Detailed domain-level scores for MINORS assessment are provided in Supplementary Table S3.

Discussion

R/R DLBCL remains a significant therapeutic challenge, particularly for patients who have previously received HCT or CAR T-cell therapy. Loncastuximab tesirine, a CD19-directed antibody-drug conjugate, has emerged as a novel treatment option in this difficult-to-treat population [21]. This meta-analysis, synthesizing evidence from seven studies, including three prospective single-arm clinical trials and four observational cohort studies provides a comprehensive synthesis of the currently available evidence of Lonca monotherapy in R/R DLBCL.

The pooled overall response rate of 47.3% demonstrates meaningful single-agent activity, suggests clinically relevant single-agent activity with nearly half of patients achieving tumor reduction despite heavy pretreatment. The complete response rate of 22.0% confirms that durable remissions are achievable, indicating that durable remissions may be achievable in a subset of patients. Survival outcomes were variable with a pooled overall survival (OS) rate of 48.2% and a progression-free survival (PFS) rate of 31.2%. Notably, variability in efficacy outcomes may in part be influenced by individual studies such as Epperla et al. [17], which demonstrated comparatively lower response and survival estimates. This study represents a real-world cohort enriched with heavily pretreated patients, including those with prior CAR T-cell therapy, which may reflect more aggressive disease and treatment-resistant biology. Differences in baseline risk, prior therapies, and real-world treatment settings likely contribute to the observed heterogeneity in pooled outcomes. These findings underscore the importance of interpreting pooled estimates in the context of underlying study populations. Importantly, subgroup analysis restricted to the prospective single-arm clinical trials yielded consistent ORR and CR estimates with substantially reduced heterogeneity (I2 = 28.6% for ORR and 0% for CR), suggesting consistent response estimates in trial settings. In contrast, OS and PFS remained more heterogeneous, likely reflecting differences in follow-up duration, prior treatment exposure, and patient selection.

Compared with other FDA-approved therapies, our pooled results fall below the ORR and CR observed with polatuzumab vedotin plus bendamustine and rituximab (GO29365: ORR 62.5%, CR 40%) [22] and tafasitamab plus lenalidomide (L-MIND: ORR 57.5%, CR 40%) [23]. However, these regimens were tested in less heavily pretreated populations and in combination settings, whereas our synthesis isolates the single-agent effect of Lonca in a more refractory cohort. The recent indirect comparison by Wilson et al. (2025) further underscores these nuances, suggesting that while combination regimens may deliver higher absolute response rates, Lonca offers a practical “off-the-shelf” option with competitive outcomes in harder-to-treat populations [24].

Treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) were nearly universal (98.7%), with grade 3 or higher events in 82.6% of patients. Hematologic toxicities, including anemia, neutropenia, and thrombocytopenia, were the most frequent and clinically significant, while non-hematologic events such as fatigue, fever, vomiting, and edema were common but generally manageable. Elevated liver enzymes were inconsistently reported.

Our pooled safety profile reproduces patterns from the LOTIS program. It is consistent with the dedicated pooled safety analysis by Tesoro et al. [25], which confirmed that myelosuppression represents the principal and predictable toxicity of Lonca [12]. By combining efficacy with pooled grade 3 or higher event counts, our analysis provides a conservative, clinically useful benefit-risk estimate that supports proactive monitoring, prophylactic growth factor use, and careful infection surveillance.

Comparison across regimens emphasizes Lonca’s distinct toxicity profile. Polatuzumab-based combinations frequently lead to infections and peripheral neuropathy [22]. In contrast, tafasitamab–lenalidomide is associated with neutropenia but fewer non-hematologic toxicities [23], while cytopenias, rashes, peripheral edema and pleural effusions consistently characterize Lonca. This differentiation is clinically relevant, as treatment choice often depends as much on comorbidity and tolerance to toxicity as on efficacy.

Lonca’s therapeutic activity is rooted in its design: a CD19-directed antibody linked to a pyrrolobenzodiazepine (PBD) payload that induces DNA crosslinking and apoptosis [11]. This mechanism explains activity in patients resistant to chemotherapy and immunotherapy and highlights its value even in post-CAR-T relapse, where few alternatives exist [10]. Preclinical and translational work continues to expand understanding of ADCs in lymphoma, and reviews by Juárez-Salcedo et al. (2024) emphasize Lonca’s potential role in rational combinations to overcome resistance [26].

Where our pooled estimates align with LOTIS-2 (ORR ≈ 48%, CR ≈ 24%) [10], the meta-analytic approach consolidates evidence across heterogeneous settings, improving generalizability. Where they diverge from real-world reports showing lower ORR and shorter PFS in heavily pretreated and post-CAR-T patients, our heterogeneity analysis quantifies these discrepancies and attributes them to differences in baseline risk and prior therapy intensity. By integrating trial and observational evidence, this study moves beyond description to explain why outcomes vary, giving clinicians a clearer benchmark for interpreting Lonca’s real-world effectiveness.

Limitations, strengths, and future directions

Importantly, the findings of this meta-analysis should be interpreted with caution. All included studies were non-randomized, consisting of prospective single-arm trials and observational cohort studies, which are inherently subject to selection bias and confounding. In addition, substantial heterogeneity was observed across several outcomes, particularly survival endpoints and some safety measures, likely reflecting differences in patient populations, prior therapies, and follow-up duration. Accordingly, the subgroup analyses should be interpreted as design-based descriptive comparisons rather than comparative efficacy analyses. A key limitation of this study is the heterogeneity of the included populations, as data were derived from both clinical trials and real-world studies. This variability introduces potential confounding and selection biases that could not be adequately corrected for in the analysis. Furthermore, safety data were derived from a limited number of studies, which may affect the precision and generalizability of toxicity estimates. Data were available from only two studies, both of which were prospective clinical trials. Although adverse events were reported using standardized criteria, likely consistent with Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE), variability in patient populations, prior treatment exposure (including prior CAR T-cell therapy), treatment duration, and follow-up may influence the incidence and severity of reported toxicities. In addition, safety outcomes are inherently context-dependent, and pooled estimates derived from a small number of studies may not fully capture real-world variability. Therefore, the pooled results should be considered descriptive and hypothesis-generating rather than definitive evidence of comparative efficacy or survival benefit.

A key strength of this study is the use of contemporary meta-analytic methods, including random-effects modeling with Hartung–Knapp adjustment and prediction intervals, which provide a more conservative and clinically realistic estimate of treatment effects across heterogeneous study settings. The use of proportion meta-analysis with Freeman–Tukey transformation, while appropriate for stabilizing variance in proportion data, may limit direct clinical interpretability and contributed to wide confidence intervals for several outcomes, particularly where the number of studies was small. In addition, wide prediction intervals observed for several outcomes reflect substantial between-study variability and highlight the uncertainty in applying pooled estimates to individual clinical settings. Assessment of small-study effects was limited by the small number of included studies (< 10), and therefore publication bias cannot be definitively excluded.

Future research should focus on refining patient selection, exploring rational combination approaches with immune checkpoint inhibitors or other novel targeted agents, and evaluating loncastuximab tesirine in earlier lines of therapy. Dedicated prospective cohorts in the post-CAR-T setting are urgently needed, given both the activity and variability observed in our pooled analysis.

Conclusion

This meta-analysis suggests that loncastuximab tesirine demonstrates clinically meaningful single-agent activity in heavily pretreated R/R DLBCL, with relatively consistent response rates across studies and a manageable but notable toxicity profile. However, these findings should be interpreted with caution given the predominance of non-randomized and single-arm studies, the substantial heterogeneity observed in survival outcomes, and the limited safety data available. Rather than establishing definitive comparative efficacy or survival benefit, these results provide a consolidated summary of existing evidence and may serve as a reference point for clinical decision-making in selected patients. Further well-designed prospective and comparative studies are needed to better define the role of loncastuximab tesirine within evolving treatment algorithms.

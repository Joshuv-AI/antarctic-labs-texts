Exploring the predictive value of mammography combined with clinical factors in recurrent metastasis of breast cancer
Authors: Dan Wu, Wei Qin, Nazhan Shaya, Zeng Wu, Yiwei Chen, Yang Zhang, Yihai Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study was to construct a radiomics nomogram based on mammography (MG) combined with clinical factors for the prediction of recurrent and metastasis in breast cancer. A total of 762 patients, all diagnosed with pathologically confirmed and undergoing MG examination, was encompassed within this article. These patients were initially diagnosed at the Affiliated Cancer Hospital of Xinjiang Medical University between January 1, 2018, and December 31, 2022, the patients were Randomly divided in a 7:3 ratio into a training set of 534 cases and a validation set of 228 cases. Using the collected data, a combined predictive model was established based on statistical methods. In forecasting the recurrence of metastasis in breast cancer, the combined model (AUC of 0.851 and 0.809 for the training and validation sets, respectively) had a higher predictive performance compared to the clinical model (AUC of 0.757 and 0.753 for the training and validation sets, respectively) and the imaging histology model (AUC of 0.802 and 0.748 for the training and validation sets, respectively).The combined model outperforms the single-modal model in terms of efficacy, which can be used as an important reference for clinically determining the prognosis of breast cancer patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40001-025-03319-3.Keywords: Mammography, Radiomic features, Columnar maps, Breast cancer

Introduction

Breast cancer ranks among the most prevalent cancers globally [1]. By 2022, breast cancer had become the second most prevalent cancer among women in China [2]. To date, surgery, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy remain the cornerstones of cancer treatment. With advancements in medical treatment methods, targeted therapy and immunotherapy have emerged, and their targeted treatment approaches continue to evolve. The core of these approaches lies in “precision” and “personalization,” shifting from the traditional “nondiscriminatory” treatment model to a “targeted, sustained, and low-toxicity” treatment paradigm. The tumor microenvironment (TME) is the local niche surrounding tumor cells, which contains tumors, stroma, and immune cells that interact to promote cancer progression [3].Although treatment options for cancer continue to evolve and improve, there are still a large number of patients, especially those with distant metastases, who have exhausted all available treatment options and have no remaining viable clinical treatment choices. Recurrence or metastasis of breast cancer also significantly affects the prognosis of breast cancer patients and is one of the major causes of death among patients. Currently, recurrent metastasis in breast cancer patients relies mainly on imaging and pathologic examination of recurrent metastatic lesions, yet its diagnosis is often made after the onset of symptoms. Advances in early cancer detection may allow more patients to be treated before metastatic disease occurs and may reduce the number and heterogeneity of carcinogenic changes. Other cancer treatments currently under development and improvements to existing cancer treatments are critical to improving clinical care for cancer patients [4].

Radiomics has now been shown to be an accurate, quantitative and non-invasive method for improving cancer diagnosis, prognosis and prediction [5, 6]. MG can clearly visualize small calcified foci in the breast gland, and the type of calcification can predict the prognosis of breast cancer [7]. Using the Lasso regression method, the optimal λ can be selected through cross-validation (such as tenfold cross-validation), striking a balance between “retaining valid features” and “avoiding overfitting.” Numerous researchers have confirmed the tremendous value of this method in constructing prognostic models [8–10].Numerous studies have demonstrated a correlation between clinicopathologic features and prognosis of breast cancer patients, including patients' menstrual status, hormone receptor levels, molecular typing, lymph node metastasis, etc [11, 12]. However, there is a lack of reports related to the construction and validation of breast cancer recurrence metastasis prediction models based on MG combined clinical features. In view of this, this study will investigate the predictive value of MG in combination with clinical factors for the recurrence and metastasis of breast cancer, and develop a more precise treatment plan for patients at risk of recurrent metastasis so as to improve their long-term prognosis. The technical roadmap of this study is detailed in Supplementary material 1.

Information and research methodology

Clinical information

Sample size: The sample size for this study was calculated using the formula N = Z2α/2P(1-P)/d2, where Zα/2 is the critical value corresponding to an area of α/2 in the standard normal distribution, P is the positive rate at the outcome time, and d is the acceptable error. Based on extensive literature reviews and clinical data, the overall recurrence and metastasis rate for breast cancer is approximately 30%. In this study, P was set at 30%, α = 0.05, Zα/2 = 1.96, and d = 0.05. The calculated sample size was 323 cases, indicating that the minimum sample size for this study was 323 cases. A total of 762 subjects were included in this study, meeting the requirements.

A total of 534 cases were assigned to the training set, which included 153 cases in the recurrent metastasis group and 381 cases in the nonrecurrent metastasis group. Inclusion criteria: (1) patients were female, ≥ 18 years old (2) first diagnosed in our hospital from January 1, 2018 to December 31, 2022, underwent MG examination, diagnosed by pathology as breast cancer and no history of malignant tumors in other parts of the body (3) The patient underwent comprehensive antitumor treatment in line with the established guidelines and standards for diagnosing and managing breast cancer (4) clear pathologic diagnosis of breast cancer, with metastatic foci confirmed by pathology and imaging (5) complete clinical, MG imaging data.

Exclusion criteria: (1) male patients (2) previous history of other tumors (3) stage IV breast cancer and recurrent breast cancer at the first diagnosis (4) bilateral breast cancer; (5) incomplete information of follow-up data.

MG image data

All patients underwent CC and MLO view mammography at the time of the initial consultation; image segmentation was performed using ITK-SNAP software, two radiologists, one radiologist has two years of experience, while the other is a senior radiologist with more than ten years of expertise, delineated two-dimensional (2D) regions of interest (ROIs) to encompass the entire lesion on the MG view; using Matlab R2016b software, four sets of radiomic features were selected from the ROIs. These features were then standardized, and the LASSO logistic regression algorithm was used to select the most significant image features in the training set; using the selected features, an imaging histology model was developed, and the AUC for the radiological histology features was computed, lastly, validate in the validation queue (Flowchart: Fig. 1).

Flowchart of radiomics model construction

Construction of joint models

A multifactorial logistic regression analysis was performed by combining radiomics features with molecular staging, cN staging, tumor maximum diameter, and tumor signs, and further lines of ten-fold cross validation were performed to obtain relatively corrected performance. The above results were utilized to develop a combined predictive model, while nomogram were constructed. Ultimately, evaluating the predictive performance of models using calibration curves and decision curves.

Statistical methods

SPSS 27.0, R 4.4.3 software was used in analysis. Measurement data were expressed as X ± S or M (Q1, Q3) according to whether they conformed to normal distribution, an independent samples t test or a Mann–Whitney U test was applied for group comparisons; The number of cases or percentages represented count data, and group comparisons were conducted using the Chi-square test or Fisher's exact test, depending on the nature of the data; Logistic regression analysis was performed on the clinical and radiomic features of the patients to identify factors with statistically significant differences. p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

In general, there was a strong consistency between two cohorts, individual indicators such as: ER status p value < 0.05, this may be due to differences caused by random grouping. The outcomes of the univariate logistic regression analysis performed that ER status, PR status, molecular typing, cT, cN, clinical stage, maximum tumor diameter, tumor sign, and site of primary focus were associated with recurrent metastasis of breast cancer (p < 0.05) (see Table 1), and the results of incorporating the above variables into multifactorial logistic regression analysis showed that molecular typing, cN stage, maximum tumor diameter and tumor signs were independent influencing factors of ending indicators(p < 0.05) (Table 2). The clinical characteristics prediction model, constructed using molecular typing, cN staging, maximum tumor diameter, and tumor signs as independent factors, yielded an AUC of 0.757 (95% CI 0.712–0.802) for the training cohort. In contrast, the validation cohort showed an AUC of 0.753 (95% CI 0.678–0.829).

Imaging histology model construction

A total of 849 features were extracted from the MG image, including 18 first-order features, 12 shape features, and 75 texture features. Through regularization, seven key image features, including one texture feature (NGTDM feature) and six wavelet features, were screened from training set using LASSO regression, which were incorporated into the multifactor logistic regression analysis to construct the imaging histology feature model, the results indicated that the AUC for the training set was 0.802 (95% CI 0.758–0.846), while the validation set achieved an AUC of 0.748 (95% CI 0.675–0.822).Fig. 2A,B illustrates the results of feature selection based on the log(λ) parameter and the mean absolute error. Moreover, the LASSO analysis provides the coefficients for each feature. The primary purpose of the Radiomics Score (Rad-Score) is to convert intricate imaging data into a measurable decision-support tool, encompassing all relevant information derived from the selected features. Figure 2C,D presents the Rad-Scores for patients. The recurrent metastatic metastases of the patients are indicated by colored bars.

Joint modeling

A multifactorial logistic regression analysis was performed by combining radiomics features with molecular typing, cN staging, tumor maximum diameter, and tumor signs, and further rows of tenfold cross-validation were performed to obtain the performance of relative correction. Figure 3 displays the outcomes of developing the joint prediction model based on the multifactor logistic regression analysis. The model achieved an AUC of 0.851 (95% CI 0.793–0.914) for the training set and 0.809 (95% CI 0.743–0.876) for the validation set. A column-line graph was also constructed based on the clinical characteristics and radiomics scores (Fig. 4), showing that radiomics characteristics were dominant in evaluating the degree of risk of recurrent metastasis in breast cancer. The calibration curves indicate that the joint prediction model demonstrates superior calibration in both the training and validation sets (Fig. 5). In addition, the DCA revealed that the model offers higher net clinical benefit (Fig. 6).

Discussion

The prevalence of breast cancer in China has been rising steadily each year, there are over 300,000 new cases diagnosed annually. The risk of recurrence and metastasis is high within 1–7 years after surgery, and the survival period of patients with multiple metastases can be shortened to several months. Our research shows that, a joint prediction model was constructed by integrating clinicopathologic factors, traditional imaging indexes and radiomic features to provide a noninvasive and objective prediction tool for breast cancer recurrence and metastasis [13].

Previous studies have shown that breast cancer molecular typing and lymph node metastasis are prognostic factors for recurrent breast cancer metastasi [14, 15], consistent with the present outcome study. It is worth noting that the maximum diameter of the tumor, molybdenum target signs (dense shadow, structural distortion, asymmetric shadow, etc.) also have independent predictors. The conventional imaging analysis is mainly based on the physician's summary of the differences in the morphology, margins, and internal enhancement characteristics of the lesions to find meaningful imaging differentiation indexes, which is somewhat limited and subjective [16]. Radiomics can quantitatively characterize tumor heterogeneity and reduce human bias through high-throughput feature extraction, and is now widely used in the fields of efficacy assessment, grading, and prognosis prediction, with numerous achievements in accurately and non-invasively diagnosing and predicting cancers [17–20].

In a recent study, Yang Jingbo and colleagues employed MG imaging histology features to predict axillary lymph node metastasis in breast cancer patients before surgery. The radiological histological features effectively distinguished axillary lymph node metastases in breast cancer, demonstrating strong discriminatory capability [21]. Compared with MG, more researchers have utilized MRI and ultrasound imaging to predict recurrent metastasis in breast cancer.MA et al. predicted TNBC recurrence by extracting the imaging histology features of DCE-MRI images before and after neoadjuvant chemotherapy, and the results showed that the imaging histology model constructed based on the MRI features before and after neoadjuvant chemotherapy showed good performance in predicting whether systemic recurrence would occur within 3 years of neoadjuvant chemotherapy in patients with TNBC [22]. Building on the predictive significance of ultrasound quantitative parameters combined with the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) for forecasting recurrent metastasis in breast cancer patients, Li Qi demonstrated that the ultrasound quantitative parameters combined with the NLR have a high predictive risk of recurrent metastasis in breast cancer patients value [23]. The above researchers have confirmed the value of radiomics features in predicting breast cancer recurrence and metastasis. This study used MG in combination with clinical and pathological factors, integrated MG reports, analyzed prognostic factors for breast cancer recurrence and metastasis, and further constructed a line chart to visualize the influencing factors. This study can assess the risk of recurrence and metastasis in breast cancer patients at an early stage of diagnosis and treatment, and provide early intervention measures. The MG images used in this study are more widely used than MRI and ultrasound in the screening and initial diagnosis of breast cancer patients, providing a large amount of image data for research. Furthermore, MG is more economical and convenient than MRI and PET-CT, making it easier for patients to accept. Compared to other studies, this study included more clinical pathological factors and MG imaging reports, making it more likely to uncover factors related to imaging outcome indicators. However, this study is a retrospective, single-center study that uses radiological features extracted from 2G MG images, which is the most significant disadvantage of this study and will be discussed below.

In this study, after a series of processing of the MG images of the study subjects, seven key image features were finally screened from the training set by LASSO regression method, These factors were incorporated into the multivariable logistic regression analysis to build the imaging genomics model. The imaging genomics model was developed by incorporating these factors into a multivariable logistic regression analysis, and the texture features can comprehensively reflect tumor heterogeneity and biological behavior [24], Indirectly reflecting the tumor growth, malignancy, invasive ability and prognosis, recurrence metastasis deterioration. The joint model was then constructed with clinical factors (molecular typing, cN staging, tumor maximum diameter, and tumor signs).The calibration curves indicates the good stability of our model. However, the AUC of the combined model in this study was lower than that of previous studies using MRI [22, 25]and ultrasound [23, 26]imaging to predict breast cancer recurrence metastasis, considering that MRI and ultrasound were able to extract more comprehensive tumor heterogeneity.

Clinical multifactorial logistic regression analysis showed that tumor signs (dense shadow, structural distortion, asymmetric shadow, etc.) and the maximum diameter of the tumor have independent predictive value, and it can be concluded that although traditional imaging analysis is subjective and occupies a strong, it has irreplaceable advantages in the prediction of recurrence and metastasis of breast cancer. The results of the differential analysis in this study indicate that the training set and validation set are generally consistent, with individual indicators such as the ER status p value < 0.05. Considering that ER hormone receptors have a certain impact on breast cancer prognosis, to investigate whether ER receptors significantly influence the results of this study, the authors further validated the sample inclusion criteria and the correctness of random grouping. By removing sensitive factors and reconstructing the clinical model, the results showed (see Supplementary material 4) that the clinical multifactor logistic regression analysis results were consistent with the results of this study. By further establishing a clinical predictive model, the results showed (see Supplementary material 5) that the training set AUC was 0.774 and the validation set AUC was 0.773, with no significant difference from the results of this study. Considering the impact of ER status on breast cancer prognosis and to ensure the authenticity of the study, it is concluded that this result does not affect the conclusions of this article.

The following are the shortcomings of this study and measures for improvement. First, it is a retrospective analysis, and the level of evidence for the conclusions of the sample study is low; second, the study subjects in this study all originated from the same hospital in the same region, single-center studies are susceptible to institution-specific factors, leading to various biases. For example, selection bias may result in the sample failing to represent the target population, causing results to be skewed and preventing them from being generalized to other regions or institutions, thereby affecting the universality of the results. Therefore, it is necessary to conduct external validation of the model through multicenter studies. By increasing the number of study subjects and collaborating with multiple institutions to collect multicenter medical records, the universality of the model can be further enhanced; Third, currently, breakthroughs are being made in genomics and transcriptomics, which have achieved excellent results in various fields of oncology. Genomics is closely related to cancer subtypes. Some researchers have proven that cuproptosis may affect this subtype of renal clear cell carcinoma and has certain application value in its immunotherapy [27]. Researchers have identified a set of biomarker genes for CD8Tex in breast cancer. Based on the CD8Tex subtype system and prognostic model, it performs well in separating patients with different immune-related characteristics and survival rates [28]. The above research has laid a solid foundation for us to explore the predictive value of genomic factors (such as the expression of long non-coding RNAs) that jointly influence breast cancer prognosis in different subtypes of breast cancer, thereby further guiding the clinical treatment of breast cancer; Fourth, currently, the clinical tools used for breast cancer (BC) diagnosis are still insufficient. However, liquid biopsies of different body fluids have recently emerged as a minimally invasive strategy, providing real-time snapshots of tumor biomarkers for early diagnosis, active monitoring of progression, and detection of recurrence after treatment. Extracellular vesicles (EVs) are small vesicles secreted by most types of cells and play an important role in intercellular communication by transferring bioactive cargo (such as microRNA (miRNA)) to recipient cells. An increasing number of studies have demonstrated that variable RNAs in exosomes can be transported between cells and have oncogenic or anticancer functions in recipient cells [29]. miRNAs are a class of small non-coding regulatory RNAs that can mediate the communication between cancer and the host and participate in cancer metastasis by adapting to the cellular niche [30]. miR-9 is upregulated in various breast cancer cell lines and has been identified as a pro-metastatic miRNA. It affects the characteristics of human mammary fibroblasts and enhances the transformation of the cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) phenotype, thereby promoting tumor growth. It was also found that the expression level of miR-9 is higher in CAFs of triple-negative breast cancer, providing new insights into the role of miR-9 as an important player in the crosstalk between cancer cells and the stroma [31]. In the blood, encapsulating miRNAs in sEVs can provide a stable environment and facilitate long-distance intercellular communication. Tumor-derived sEVs carrying miRNAs released into the blood may serve as important markers for disease diagnosis and prognosis [32, 33]. BC, sEV, and miRNA have been identified as potential diagnostic, prognostic, and chemotherapy resistance biomarkers, as well as potential therapeutic targets [34]. In subsequent work, we will adopt minimally invasive strategies such as adding components of liquid biopsy of body fluids (e.g., EV) and combine MG and pathological factors to further improve the predictive performance of this model for breast cancer recurrence and metastasis. Fifth, studies have shown that genomic factors are well associated with breast cancer recurrence metastasis metastases [35], The BRCA gene is associated with breast and ovarian cancer. Family members with BRCA mutations have an increased risk of developing breast and ovarian cancer at any age [36], there have been reports that tumors in BRCA mutation carriers grow at twice the rate of those in non-carriers [37]. BRCA-positive breast cancer has been shown to have imaging characteristics commonly seen in benign lesions. The most commonly described mammographic features of BRCA-positive breast cancer are round masses with sharp, well-defined margins and no malignant calcifications [38, 39]. BRCA-positive breast cancer typically presents on ultrasound as a round, well-defined, hypoechoic, and homogeneous mass with enhanced sound transmission, similar to benign lesions, especially fibroadenomas [40, 41]. In a recent study, the most common MRI features in BRCA-positive breast cancer were described as linear or focal non-mass enhancement [42]. With the continuous innovation of omics technologies, researchers are able to obtain multilayered information from the genome, epigenome, transcriptome, proteome, metabolome, and other sources. Through the use of diverse omics technologies, they can provide a comprehensive view of tumor behavior at different molecular levels. Based on this, this study will incorporate genomic characteristics that influence breast cancer recurrence and metastasis, such as BRCA mutations and long noncoding RNA expression, into further research. Through multi-omics integration (such as deep learning and artificial intelligence fusion), the ability to predict breast cancer recurrence and metastasis will be improved, thereby achieving precision medicine. Sixth, since MG is primarily 2D imaging, the ROI delineation in this study's radiomics feature extraction process was based on 2D delineation of the largest MG lesion plane. Compared with 3D planes such as MRI, this may omit tumor characteristics and fail to fully reflect tumor heterogeneity. Currently, some researchers [43] have utilized multiple imaging images to construct a multimodal image prediction model, Subsequent research will further enhance the predictive performance of this predictive model by using MG's 3D layer images or combining MRI and ultrasound images to create 3D outlines.

The combination of MG radiomics and clinicopathological factors can achieve multi—dimensional associations of "imaging phenotype—molecular pathology—clinical outcome". It can capture the heterogeneity of tumors more comprehensively than a single model (using only imaging or clinical factors), and significantly improve the prediction accuracy. The core application potential lies in improving the prediction accuracy of recurrence and metastasis, reducing "missed diagnoses" and "misdiagnoses", and more accurately distinguishing high-risk and low-risk patients. Supporting personalized treatment decisions and avoiding overtreatment. For high—risk patients, more intensive follow-up (e.g., re—examination every 3 months) or enhanced adjuvant therapy (e.g., targeted therapy + chemotherapy) is recommended. For low—risk patients, unnecessary radiotherapy/chemotherapy can be reduced, side effects of treatment can be decreased, and the quality of life can be improved. This is in line with the core goal of precision medicine—providing the most appropriate treatment for the right patients. Assisting clinical decision-making and optimizing the allocation of medical resources. In terms of preoperative prediction, by combining MG radiomics features with the results of puncture pathology, the risk of postoperative recurrence and metastasis of patients can be predicted in advance, helping physicians formulate more comprehensive surgical plans (e.g., whether lymph node dissection is needed). In terms of postoperative management, high-risk patients are guided into a more rigorous follow-up process (e.g., PET/CT monitoring), while low-risk patients undergo routine follow-up, reducing the waste of medical resources. Promoting the design of clinical trials and accelerating the research and development of new drugs. The combined model can accurately screen research subjects. For example, in clinical trials of adjuvant therapy for breast cancer, only patients with "high recurrence risk" predicted by the model are included, which can not only improve the statistical power of the trial but also more quickly verify the efficacy of new drugs (e.g., the benefits of targeted drugs for high-risk patients).

Although the combined model has great potential, problems such as the generalization ability of the model (the consistency of MG images from different centers and different devices), the stability of features (radiomics features are easily affected by scanning parameters), and clinical acceptance (physicians' trust in the prediction model) still need to be solved. With the further development of deep learning (DL) technologies (such as CNN and U-Net), genomics, and the establishment of multicenter large-sample cohorts, the combined model is expected to be more widely applied in clinical practice and become an important tool for precise prognostic management of breast cancer.

In general, the combination of MG radiomics and clinicopathological factors can significantly improve the ability to predict breast cancer recurrence and metastasis through the complementarity of "quantitative imaging features + known prognostic factors", providing a more accurate basis for personalized treatment, clinical decision—making, and clinical trial design. It is one of the important directions for the development of precision medicine in breast cancer.

Conclusion

Despite these limitations, the combined model in this study demonstrated high performance in predicting recurrent metastasis of breast cancer, providing a noninvasive and actionable predictive tool for clinical use. Physicians can develop personalized treatment plans for patients based on the model results, this would, in turn, contribute to enhancing patient survival and overall quality of life. Future studies could further integrate genomic features as well as combine deep learning algorithms to further enhance the predictive ability.

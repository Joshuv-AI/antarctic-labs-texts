Quality and reliability of pediatric inguinal hernia treatment information on TikTok and Bilibili
Authors: Tengfei Chen, Bingdong Zhang, Sidan Li, Yongbing Chen
Published: 2026

Introduction

Pediatric inguinal hernia (PIH) is one of the most common surgical conditions in children, characterized by a high prevalence and imposing a significant burden on patients’ families and the healthcare system [1, 2]. Failure to diagnose and treat PIH in a timely manner can lead to severe complications such as intestinal incarceration, strangulation, and necrosis [3]. Therefore, effective parental education regarding the clinical manifestations of the disease and the necessity of prompt medical intervention is a critical component of public health management.

In the digital era, patients’ families are increasingly turning to short-video social media platforms (such as TikTok and Bilibili) to obtain health information [4, 5]. These platforms utilize powerful recommendation algorithms and highly interactive visual formats to disseminate medical knowledge rapidly and widely. However, this unregulated environment poses a significant public health challenge. The rapid dissemination of information has also raised concerns regarding content quality, reliability, and the propagation of misinformation. Previous studies on other health topics (such as knee osteoarthritis or cataracts) have found that video quality is often suboptimal and that user engagement (e.g., likes and shares) does not correlate with medical accuracy [6, 7]. For an acute surgical condition like PIH, misleading information could lead to delayed diagnosis and potentially harmful attempts at non-surgical management [8–10].

To our knowledge, no study has yet systematically evaluated the quality and reliability of information regarding pediatric inguinal hernia on TikTok and Bilibili. This study aims to fill that gap. We systematically evaluated the content characteristics, quality, and reliability of PIH-related videos on both platforms. Furthermore, we sought to determine the impact of uploader identity (e.g., specialists vs. non-specialists) on video quality and analyzed the correlation between video engagement metrics and objective content quality.

Materials and methods

Study design and ethical considerations

This study was conducted from November 1, 2025, to November 17, 2025, employing a cross-sectional design. The study did not involve any human or animal subjects. All data were sourced from publicly available videos on the TikTok and Bilibili platforms. As the data analyzed were publicly accessible and did not involve any personal privacy information, ethical approval was not required for this study.

Search strategy and video selection

We performed a systematic search on two mainstream Chinese video-sharing platforms, Bilibili and TikTok, using the keywords‘Infantile inguinal hernia and treatment‘(小儿腹股沟疝及治疗). To minimize personalization bias from recommendation algorithms, all searches were conducted in a logged-out (incognito) mode [11]. Based on the platforms’ default “comprehensive sort” algorithm, we retrieved the top 150 videos from each platform, forming an initial pool of 300 videos [12, 13].

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Two researchers independently screened the videos. Any disagreements were resolved through discussion or consultation with a third senior physician. Videos were excluded if they met any of the following criteria: (1) Advertisements; (2) Irrelevant to the subject; and (3) Release time less than one week. Data extraction

We extracted the following general information and engagement metrics for each included video: platform, video duration (seconds), number of likes, collections, comments, and shares. Video content was categorized based on the information provided, including “disease’s causes,” “control risk factors,” “surgical intervention,” “hernia belt,” “emotion management,” “acupuncture,” and “Chinese herbal medicine.”

Video quality and reliability assessment

To ensure assessment reliability, both reviewers participated in a formal training and calibration session before the study. Video quality was independently assessed by two trained medical researchers. They independently evaluated an initial set of 20 videos to standardize the interpretation of GQS, mDISCERN, JAMA, and PEMAT criteria. Any scoring discrepancies were resolved through discussion or consultation with a third senior physician [14]. We used four validated instruments to assess the videos. The Global Quality Score (GQS) (Table 1): a 5-point scale to evaluate the overall quality and educational value of the video. The modified DISCERN (mDISCERN) (Table 2): a 5-point scale to assess the reliability of the health information [13–16]. The Journal of the American Medical Association (JAMA) benchmark criteria (Table 3): a 4-point scale to assess transparency [17–19]. The Patient Education Materials Assessment Tool (PEMAT): used to evaluate content usability, divided into PEMAT-U (Understandability) and PEMAT-A (Actionability).

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS. As data were non-normally distributed, continuous variables were presented as median and interquartile range (IQR). Inter-rater reliability for video screening and quality scoring (GQS and mDISCERN) was assessed using Cohen’s kappa coefficient.The Mann-Whitney U test was used for comparisons between two independent groups. The Kruskal-Wallis H test was used for comparisons among multiple uploader categories, followed by Bonferroni-corrected post-hoc tests for pairwise comparisons.

Results

Video characteristics

Based on the established inclusion and exclusion criteria, we screened the top 150 videos from Bilibili and TikTok, resulting in a final sample of 198 videos. The inter-rater reliability for quality assessment was excellent, with Cohen’s kappa coefficients of 0.78 for GQS and 0.76 for mDISCERN.The detailed screening process is shown in Fig. 1. We analyzed the 198 videos from TikTok and Bilibili, comprising 80 from the Bilibili group and 118 from the TikTok group. The median video duration on Bilibili was significantly longer than on TikTok (155.50 vs. 86.00). Conversely, TikTok exhibited significantly higher medians for likes (338.50 vs. 22.00), comments (122.50 vs. 5.50), and shares (288.00 vs. 15.00) compared to Bilibili. All four metrics showed statistically significant differences (P < 0.001). The general characteristics of the videos are presented in Table 5.

Video content analysis

In the video content analysis, 122 of the 198 videos (62.0%) mentioned “disease’s causes,” and 108 (54.5%) mentioned “control risk factors”; there was overlap between these categories, and both were mentioned at higher rates on TikTok. Acupuncture was recommended in 16.2% of videos, with a significantly higher recommendation rate on TikTok than on Bilibili. Chinese herbal medicine was mentioned in 5.6% of videos. Non-pharmacological treatments included “Emotion management,” “Hernia belt,” and “Surgical intervention.” Specifically, 164 videos mentioned Surgical intervention, 38 mentioned Emotion management, and 33 mentioned Hernia belts, with TikTok showing higher proportions for all categories compared to Bilibili (Table 5).

Regarding disease-specific medical accuracy, further sub-analysis revealed that while the importance of standard care was widely recognized, the coverage of critical clinical nuances was suboptimal. Specifically, the risk of hernia incarceration and urgent emergency indicators (e.g., irreducible mass, persistent crying, vomiting, or abdominal distension) were explicitly discussed in only 34.3% (68/198) of the videos. The limitations of spontaneous resolution and the optimal timing of surgery (typically indicated upon diagnosis or beyond 6 months of age to prevent incarceration) were detailed in 42.9% (85/198) of the content. Crucially, regarding alternative interventions, 16.7% (33/198) of the videos mentioned non-standard approaches such as hernia belts, acupuncture, or herbal medicine; however, only 45.5% (15/33) of these videos accurately warned parents about their potential clinical risks, including severe local skin breakdown, fascial scarring, and dangerous delays in definitive surgical care.

Discussion

This study systematically evaluated the quality and reliability of information regarding “pediatric inguinal hernia” on dominant Chinese short-video platforms. Unlike previous studies on other diseases[6 7], a key finding of this study is that the overall quality of videos related to pediatric inguinal hernia was very high (median GQS = 4.00, median mDISCERN = 3.00). We believe the fundamental reason for this phenomenon lies in the professionalism of the content sources. Our data indicates that among the 198 videos, a substantial 74.2% (n = 147) were posted by professionals. More specifically, “General surgeons” were the absolute dominant force in content creation (accounting for 56.1% of the total), and their video quality remained at the highest level (median GQS = 4.00). In contrast, videos produced by non-specialists showed relatively lower quality scores (median GQS = 3.00), although the difference in some sub-groups was less pronounced.

Despite the overall high quality of video content, this study revealed a core issue: a severe disconnect between video popularity (engagement) and its medical quality. Our analysis (Table 6) confirmed that although videos from professionals were significantly superior to those from non-professionals in quality and actionability (PEMAT scores) ( P < 0.001), there were no statistically significant differences between the two groups in the number of likes ( P = 0.735), collections, or shares. This indicates that the audience failed to effectively distinguish between high-quality and low-quality content [20–22]. This poses a considerable challenge to public health safety [1, 23].

Furthermore, in terms of platform comparison, TikTok not only demonstrated higher engagement but also possessed significantly higher video quality than Bilibili (GQS = 0.001). Regarding content, videos were highly concentrated on “surgical intervention” (82.8%), whereas discussions on “risk factor control” were relatively insufficient (54.5%). Therefore, future interventions should educate the public to shift towards verifying creator credentials, while platforms should also prioritize the promotion of certified professional content [6, 24].

The impact of platforms and uploaders on video quality and reliability

This study systematically evaluated 198 videos on “pediatric inguinal hernia.” Unlike studies on many other disease topics that report suboptimal quality, the overall quality and reliability of videos in this study were found to be at a high level. Between the two platforms, TikTok significantly outperformed Bilibili in both quality and popularity. TikTok videos not only had significantly higher engagement, but their content quality and reliability were also significantly higher [25, 26].

This rare phenomenon of high quality can be attributed to the professional composition of uploaders. Content creation was absolutely dominated by professionals, with “General surgeons” being the core force [27, 28].

Uploader identity was the most critical factor determining content quality. Videos from professionals were significantly superior to those from non-professionals (n = 51) across all quality dimensions. The median PEMAT-U for professionals was 82.00 ; the median PEMAT-A was 75.00. Furthermore, among the professionals, general surgeons, pediatricians, and urologists exhibited significantly higher video quality scores compared to other specialists. This superiority is likely attributed to their profound familiarity with the specialized anatomical structures involved in pediatric inguinal hernia (Table 4). This implies that for a highly specialized surgical disease like pediatric inguinal hernia, the creator’s medical background is the core element ensuring information accuracy and actionability [24–29].

However, although the quality of professional videos was high, content completeness remains insufficient. As shown in Table 5, content was highly concentrated on “surgical intervention”, while mention rates for “disease’s causes” and “control risk factors” (54.5%) were notably lower. While reinforcing the importance of surgical intervention, it is equally essential for content creators to incorporate more educational information regarding the prevention and etiology of hernias to ensure a more balanced and comprehensive public health message. Regarding disease-specific medical accuracy, further sub-analysis revealed that while the importance of standard care was widely recognized, the coverage of critical clinical nuances was suboptimal. Specifically, the risk of hernia incarceration and urgent emergency indicators (e.g., irreducible mass, persistent crying, vomiting, or abdominal distension) were explicitly discussed in only 34.3% (68/198) of the videos. The limitations of spontaneous resolution and the optimal timing of surgery (typically indicated upon diagnosis or beyond 6 months of age to prevent incarceration) were detailed in 42.9% (85/198) of the content. Crucially, regarding alternative interventions, 16.7% (33/198) of the videos mentioned non-standard approaches such as hernia belts, acupuncture, or herbal medicine; however, only 45.5% (15/33) of these videos accurately warned parents about their potential clinical risks, including severe local skin breakdown, fascial scarring, and dangerous delays in definitive surgical care.

In summary, these findings provide clear guidance for platforms and patients’ families. For platforms, certification of professionals such as “General surgeons” should be strengthened, and high-quality, highly actionable content should be prioritized for promotion [20–26]. For Patients’ families, when obtaining information, they should not rely on likes and comments (as popularity is unrelated to quality) but should actively verify the uploader’s professional credentials.

Correlation analysis between video features and quality

To deeply explore the relationship between video features, popularity, and quality, we conducted Spearman correlation analysis (Fig. 5). The results strongly supported our findings in Table 6 regarding the comparison between professionals and non-professionals and revealed fundamental differences in content between the two platforms. On both platforms, engagement metrics such as likes, comments, collections, and shares were strongly positively inter-correlated [30]. This meets expectations, indicating that “popularity” is consistent; a video high in one metric is usually high in others. On TikTok, the correlation between all engagement metrics and GQS, mDISCERN, PEMAT-U, and PEMAT-A scores was nearly zero. This disconnect was also present on Bilibili. Notably, interaction metrics on Bilibili even showed a moderate negative correlation with PEMAT-U. This suggests that on Bilibili, the more popular a video is, the more difficult its content may be to understand [31]. In conclusion, neither video popularity nor video duration can serve as valid indicators for judging the quality and reliability of pediatric inguinal hernia video content. The only reliable indicator is the uploader’s professional identity.

Content analysis: high concentration on standard care

The predominant focus on “surgical intervention” observed in this study is a significant finding that aligns with the clinical gold standard, as surgery remains the only definitive cure for pediatric inguinal hernia [32, 33]. This strong commitment to evidence-based medicine is a fundamental driver of the high video quality scores observed here, setting this topic apart from other medical fields where misinformation is more prevalent.

Furthermore, this content concentration is intrinsically linked to uploader identity. As general surgeons represent the primary force in content creation, the mainstream information encountered by patients’ families naturally mirrors professional surgical practice. It is noteworthy that non-surgical or alternative interventions—such as conservative management, acupuncture, or herbal medicine remained marginal in our analysis. This is a highly positive phenomenon for pediatric inguinal hernia, as it suggests that these platforms are not saturated with ineffective “cures” that could lead to dangerous treatment delays. Consequently, the concentration on standard care further reinforces the overall reliability of the information available to the public.

Our study highlights a critical public health concern regarding the promotion of non-standard alternative interventions (accounting for 16.7% of the videos), such as hernia belts, acupuncture, and traditional herbal medicine. Clinically, these modalities pose significant, well-documented hazards for pediatric inguinal hernia (PIH) management. The prolonged use of commercial hernia belts often causes chronic compression, leading to localized skin breakdown, tissue ischemic changes, and dense fascial scarring. This sub-serosal inflammation significantly distorts the local anatomical planes, inadvertently increasing the technical difficulty and complication rates of subsequent laparoscopic or open herniorrhaphy. More critically, reliance on acupuncture or herbal medicine fosters a false sense of security among caregivers. This inevitably delays definitive surgical correction, exponentially elevating the cumulative risk of acute hernia incarceration, bowel necrosis, and gonadal ischemia in young infants. Short-video platforms must cross-reference content with current clinical guidelines to actively curb the dissemination of these high-risk alternative practices.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, as a cross-sectional study, this analysis provides only a static snapshot of a rapidly evolving information landscape. Second, a notable limitation of this study lies in our search strategy. We utilized a single, relatively formal medical search term, “pediatric inguinal hernia and treatment” (小儿腹股沟疝及治疗) in Chinese. While this term ensured the retrieval of videos strictly relevant to clinical interventions, it poses a potential selection bias. In real-world scenarios, parents or caregivers are more likely to use colloquial or symptom-based terms, such as “hernia” (疝气), “baby hernia” (宝宝疝气), or “intestinal hernia” (小肠气), when seeking health information online. Consequently, our search strategy may have preferentially retrieved content produced by medical professionals or healthcare institutions, which inherently presents higher quality and reliability scores. This selection bias could partly explain the relatively high overall GQS and mDISCERN scores observed in our sample compared to other disease topics, and the results should be interpreted with caution. Future research should incorporate a broader range of layman terms to conduct a more comprehensive sensitivity analysis of social media content.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this specific study demonstrated a relatively high overall quality and reliability of pediatric inguinal hernia (PIH) treatment information on TikTok and Bilibili, which is heavily driven by the high proportion of certified professionals (74.2%) retrieved through our professional search strategy. Our findings confirm that the uploader’s medical background is the single most critical determinant of information accuracy, understandability, and actionability, with specialist-generated content being statistically superior to that of non-specialists (P < 0.001).

However, a critical public health concern unveiled by this work is the profound disconnect between video popularity and objective medical quality. High user engagement metrics (likes, comments, and shares) do not correlate with clinical accuracy, posing a substantial risk that misinformation or non-standard alternative therapies could popularize and potentially delay timely surgical interventions. Therefore, short-video platforms must urgently optimize their recommendation algorithms to prioritize verified medical credentials. Concurrently, clinical practitioners must actively educate patients’ families to critically evaluate online sources based on professional authority rather than misleading popularity metrics. Future studies utilizing a wider spectrum of colloquial search terms are warranted to provide a more dynamic, comprehensive sensitivity analysis of the digital health education landscape.

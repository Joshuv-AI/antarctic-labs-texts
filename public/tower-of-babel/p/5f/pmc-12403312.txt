Efficacy and safety study of tranexamic acid combined with low-molecular-weight heparin and nadroparin calcium in postoperative application after joint replacement: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Abuduwupuer Haibier, Shalayiding Aierxiding, Alimujiang Yusufu, Hang Lin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundLow-molecular-weight heparin (LMWH) and nadroparin calcium are commonly used to prevent deep vein thrombosis (DVT) following joint replacement surgery. In this study, we compared the effects of tranexamic acid combined with either LMWH or nadroparin calcium in preventing DVT after joint replacement surgery.MethodsA retrospective analysis was conducted on patients who underwent unilateral THA/TKA at the Sixth Affiliated Hospital of Xinjiang Medical University from September 2021 to June 2023. Patients were divided into two groups based on the anticoagulant used: Tranexamic Acid + Low-Molecular-Weight Heparin group (n = 80) and Tranexamic Acid + Nadroparin Calcium group (n = 85), both treated for 2 weeks. The perioperative indicators [blood loss, hospital stay, operation time, transfusion rate, transfusion volume, and total hospitalization cost], complications DVT, muscle compartment vein thrombosis (MCVT), incision infection, pulmonary thromboembolism, and postoperative hematoma], coagulation indicators [D-dimer, prothrombin activity, international normalized ratio, and fibrinogen], hematological parameters [platelets, hemoglobin, hematocrit], and inflammatory factors [procalcitonin (PCT), interleukin-6 (IL-6), interleukin-8 (IL-8), and tumor necrosis factor (TNF-α)] were compared between the two groups.ResultThe preoperative indicators of both groups were statistically indistinguishable (P > 0.05). Comparisons of intraoperative blood loss, overt blood loss, covert blood loss, total blood loss, hospital stay duration, operation time, transfusion rate, and transfusion volume showed no significant differences between the groups (P > 0.05). The TXA + LMWH group exhibited significantly lower D-dimer, PT activity, international normalized ratio, and fibrinogen levels on postoperative days 1 and 5 compared to the TXA + Nadroparine group, with significant differences observed (P < 0.05). No significant differences between the two groups were found in platelet count, hemoglobin, hematocrit, PCT, IL-6, IL-8, and TNF-α levels on postoperative days 1 and 5 (P > 0.05). The total hospitalization costs in the TXA + LMWH group were significantly lower than those in the TXA + Nadroparine group, with a significant difference (P < 0.05).ConclusionThis study found that the clinical efficacy and safety of the TXA + Nadroparine and TXA + LMWH groups were basically the same after joint replacement surgery. However, the TXA + LMWH group demonstrated better prevention of thrombosis and significantly lower overall hospitalization costs compared to the TXA + Nadroparine group.Keywords: Osteoarthritis, Total hip arthroplasty, Total knee arthroplasty, Low-molecular-weight heparin, Nadroparin calcium, Coagulation indicators, Thrombosis

Introduction

Venous thromboembolism (VTE), encompassing DVT and pulmonary embolism (PE), is a major complication following joint replacement surgery. Previous studies indicate that the incidence of DVT in China following joint replacement surgery ranges from 11.8 to 47.1% [1–2]. With the improvement of perioperative treatment regimens and the implementation of enhanced recovery after surgery, the risk of VTE following joint replacement surgery has significantly decreased. Consequently, balancing the benefits of various VTE prophylactic drugs against the risks of adverse events related to anticoagulation has become a research focus [3–4].

Tranexamic acid, a synthetic amino acid derivative, primarily exerts its hemostatic effect by occupying functional target sites on plasmin and plasminogen, thereby halting the binding of plasmin to fibrin and reducing fibrinolysis, ultimately achieving hemostasis. It is currently the most widely used antifibrinolytic agent in joint replacement surgery [5]. Low-molecular-weight heparin and sodium pentosan polysulfate are common methods for preventing deep vein thrombosis following total hip arthroplasty [6]. Reports indicate that [7] LMWH exerts anti-thrombotic effects by inhibiting the activation of thrombin and coagulation activity factors. However, its adverse effects are pronounced, and the overall efficacy is unsatisfactory [8]. Nadroparin, as a Low-Molecular-Weight heparin, exhibits favorable anticoagulant properties, capable of inhibiting the activity of coagulation factors in the blood, such as thrombin and factor Xa, thereby preventing thrombus formation [9]. Nadroparin’s anticoagulant effect combined with its ability to promote blood flow effectively reduces the risk of thrombus formation [10]. The combination of tranexamic acid with LMWH or Nadroparin Calcium had similar effectiveness in reducing the incidence of thrombosis-related complications such as DVT and PE. In this study, we compared the preventive effects of tranexamic acid combined with Low-Molecular-Weight heparin and nadroparin calcium on deep vein thrombosis of the lower extremities following total hip arthroplasty. Our findings can provide a reference for selecting appropriate anticoagulant therapy after THA/TKA.

Subjects and methods

Design

Retrospective comparative trial.

Time and location

The trial was conducted in the Department of Orthopedics at the Sixth Affiliated Hospital of Xinjiang Medical University from September 2021 to June 2023.

Inclusion criteria

①Meet the diagnostic criteria for osteoarthritis [11] and aged 60–85 years; ②Scheduled for primary total hip arthroplasty due to end-stage osteoarthritis or femoral head necrosis; ③No previous history of THA/TKA surgery; ④Complete data and follow-up results;

Exclusion criteria

①Revision or bilateral surgery; ②ASA (American Society of Anesthesiologists) classification [12] > grade 3;③Contraindications for using Low-Molecular-Weight heparin and nadroparin calcium, including allergy or history of thrombosis within 6 months; ④Preoperative anemia with hemoglobin levels for females < 12 g/dl and males < 13 g/dl [13]; ⑤Body mass index > 35 kg/m2; ⑥Immunodeficiency or abnormal immune function; ⑦Pregnant or lactating women; ⑧Comorbid severe internal medical diseases.

Methods

Knee arthroplasty

Both groups underwent routine TKA procedures by the same team of lead surgeons and anesthesiologists. These surgeries were performed by skilled physicians at our hospital with associate senior level or above. The surgical approach was through the medial parapatellar incision of the knee. The procedures were conducted under general anesthesia or epidural anesthesia. A pneumatic tourniquet was used throughout the surgery (pressure set at 53.0 kPa), inflated before the first skin incision, and deflated before the completion of wound closure. All knee prostheses used intraoperatively were provided by Johnson & Johnson (Shanghai) Medical Devices Co., Ltd. A “cocktail compound solution” was injected evenly around the joint capsule, totaling 50 ml, before the joint capsule was sutured. The composition of the solution included: 1 mg of Betamethasone Acetate Injection (manufactured by Tianjin Jinyao Group Hubei Tianyao Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., National Drug Approval Number H42020019), 10mL of Ropivacaine (20 mg, manufactured by Yichang Humanwell Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., National Drug Approval Number H20103552), 0.3mL of Epinephrine Hydrochloride Injection (0.3 mg, manufactured by Sinopharm Holding (China) Co., Ltd., National Drug Approval Number H42021700), and 50mL of saline. Tranexamic Acid Protocol (5 ml: 0.5 g, Yangtze River Pharmaceutical Group Nanjing Hailin Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., National Drug Approval No. H20123005): Administer 1 g of tranexamic acid in 250mL of normal saline 30 min prior to tourniquet inflation. After successful implantation of the prosthesis, an additional 1 g in 250mL of normal saline is injected for local soak application. The dosage mentioned above is determined based on national expert consensus and recommendations from our hospital’s anesthesia team (see Fig. 1 [14] for details)

Expert Consensus on the Application Protocol of Antifibrinolytic Agents Sequential to Anticoagulants in the Perioperative Period of Hip and Knee Arthroplasty in China (2015) [14]

Hip replacement surgery

All THAs are performed using the posterior lateral approach, implemented by a surgical team consisting of two senior orthopedic surgeons. Intraoperatively, general anesthesia and controlled hypotensive anesthesia (BP < 100/60 mmHg) were employed. The prostheses used were a cementless acetabular component (Johnson & Johnson Pinnacle acetabular component) and a femoral stem (Johnson & Johnson Corail femoral stem). The aminocaproic acid group protocol (10 ml:2 g, Yangzhou Zhongbao Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., National Drug Approval No. H32025998) involved administering 2 g of aminocaproic acid in 250mL of normal saline 10 min before the tourniquet was inflated. The prosthesis will be recharged with 2 g in 250 ml of saline for local immersion application after successful implantation.

Preoperative Preparation

Before undergoing total hip and knee arthroplasty, patients undergo X-ray, CT, MRI, and routine blood tests, liver, and kidney function tests, as well as a comprehensive assessment of their condition.

Medication

Low-molecular-weight Heparin Group: Postoperative administration of half the normal dose of subcutaneous low-molecular-weight heparin calcium at 4–6 h. The full dose is administered the next day (once daily). (Refer to Fig. 2 for details)

Details of the three drugs

Nadroparin Calcium Group: Subcutaneous injection of 4100U starting 6 h postoperatively (once daily). The treatment duration is 2 weeks. The selection of anticoagulant medication is jointly determined by the doctor and the patient after consultation. (See Fig. 2 for details)

Postoperative management

①All patients were routinely administered a 5% glucose injection (500 ml: 25 mg, Sichuan Kelun Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd.) + Sodium Lactate Ringer’s Injection (500 ml, Xinjiang Huashidan Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd.) within 2 h postoperatively to replenish blood volume and maintain electrolyte balance. Patients were then transferred to the anesthesia recovery room and returned to the inpatient ward after 2 h. ②All patients received routine postoperative care and thrombosis prevention before weight-bearing walking, with walking and strength training beginning on the first postoperative day. Oral rivaroxaban (Xarelto, 10 mg, Bayer AG) was administered post-discharge for thromboprophylaxis for 15 days. Deep venous thrombosis was routinely investigated by Doppler ultrasound 3 days after surgery, 1 week after surgery, 2 weeks, at discharge, 1 month after surgery, or at suspected DVT. Chest CT-PA was used to detect pulmonary embolism if necessary. The transfusion protocol was: for patients with a hemoglobin level below 70 g/L, or those with a hemoglobin value between 70 and 100 g/L accompanied by symptoms related to anemia, such as dizziness, palpitations, shortness of breath, or reduced exercise tolerance [15].

Clinical data collection

Basic information

Age, sex composition, body mass index (BMI), surgical site, type of surgery, comorbidities (hypertension, diabetes, coronary heart disease, neurological diseases, respiratory diseases, metabolic diseases), and other preoperative indicators in both groups.

Evaluation indicators

(1) perioperative indicators

Blood loss, length of hospital stay, duration of surgery, transfusion rate, amount of transfusion, and total hospitalization costs.

Total blood loss is calculated based on the Gross Eq. [7] and the Nadler [8] equation method: Total blood volume = k1 × height (m)³ + k2 × weight (kg) + k3, where the coefficients for males are k1 = 0.3669, k2 = 0.03219, k3 = 0.6041, and for females are k1 = 0.3561, k2 = 0.03308, k3 = 0.1833. Total blood loss = blood volume × (preoperative hematocrit - postoperative minimum hematocrit) / average hematocrit. Evident blood loss is defined as intraoperative blood loss + drainage volume. Hidden blood loss = total blood loss - evident blood loss + transfused red blood cell volume.

(2) complications

DVT of the lower extremities, muscle compartment vein thrombosis (MCVT), incision infection, pulmonary thromboembolism, and postoperative hematoma.

(3) coagulation indicators

D-dimer, prothrombin activity, international normalized ratio, and fibrinogen.

(4) complete blood count

Platelets, Hemoglobin, Hematocrit.

(5) inflammatory markers

Procalcitonin (PCT), Interleukin-6 (IL-6), Interleukin-8 (IL-8), and Tumor Necrosis Factor-alpha (TNF-α).

Statistical analysis

In the data analysis part of this study, SPSS 26.0 software was used. Continuous data distribution was expressed as the mean ± standard deviation, and the difference analysis was one-way ANOVA or non-parametric rank sum test according to the chi-square test/fisher exact probability method. For the analysis of the time effect of the preoperative planned prosthesis position parameters and the postoperative measurements, repeated measures were used. Under the premise of the overall difference, the α, and the adjusted P < 0.05, α = 0.05.

Participant number analysis

A total of 165 patients undergoing THA/TKA were included and divided into two groups based on medication: 80 patients in the tranexamic acid + Low-Molecular-Weight heparin group and 85 patients in the tranexamic acid + nadroparin calcium group. All patients were included in the analysis of the results with no dropout data.

Discussion

Summary of evidence

After joint replacement surgery, the prevention of DVT is one of the priorities of clinical work, which is related to the postoperative rehabilitation and long-term prognosis of patients. Reasonable anticoagulation therapy can not only effectively reduce the risk of DVT, reduce the incidence of serious complications such as pulmonary embolism, but also guarantee the effect of surgical treatment to a certain extent [16–17].This study focused on exploring the effect of different anticoagulants combined with tranexamic acid after joint replacement, and the results showed significant differences in multiple hematology indexes. The TXA + LMWH group, when compared with the TXA + Nadroparine group, Statistically significant differences in D-dimer levels (4.02 ± 2.60 mg/L vs. 4.92 ± 2.51 mg/L), PT activity (98.02 ± 12.81% vs. 10.2.52 ± 15.34%), international normalized ratio (1.14 ± 0.10 ± 0.10 vs. 1.23.36), and fibrinogen (4.56 ± 1.05 vs. 5.00 ± 1.18), Showed that the TXA + Nadroparine group was slightly higher than the TXA + LMWH group in all hematological measures. This aligns well with the findings of several earlier studies, which have found some differences in similar hematology indicators when used in combination with tranexamic acid. This study further verified the existence of this difference, and provides a certain theoretical basis for the clinical selection of appropriate anticoagulation and tranexamic acid combination regimen after joint replacement. However, in view of the possible differences in sample size, study subject characteristics and experimental conditions in different studies, the specific mechanism behind this difference still needs to be further studied and discussed.

Tranexamic acid, a lysine analog, inhibits the binding of fibrin, plasminogen, and plasmin, preventing hyperfibrinolysis and achieving hemostatic effects [18]. Studies have shown that preoperative and postoperative intravenous use of tranexamic acid can reduce bleeding more effectively [19]. However, THA and TKA surgeries inevitably cause excessive stretching and damage to venous vessels, requiring patients to remain immobilized postoperatively, slowing blood flow, which can lead to blood stasis, exacerbating the hypercoagulable state of the blood, and increasing the risk of postoperative thrombosis [20].

Low-molecular-weight heparin (LMWH) is widely used in the prevention of perioperative deep vein thrombosis in orthopedic major surgeries, belonging to the first-line anticoagulant drugs. It effectively reduces the biological activity of activated coagulation factors, thereby inhibiting platelet aggregation, inducing fibrinolysis, and achieving the purpose of clearing venous thrombus emboli. LMWH calcium can bind with antithrombin III, inhibiting the activity of activated factor Xa and thrombin, possessing anticoagulant properties, and featuring a long half-life and high bioavailability [21–22]. Nadroparin calcium, with a molecular weight of 4500Da, selectively inhibits the activities of thrombin and factor Xa. After subcutaneous injection, its bioavailability reaches up to 90.00% [23]. In pharmacological analysis [24], nadroparin’s inhibition of thrombus is attributed to its relatively high anti-factor Xa activity and low anti-factor IIa activity, with the ratio of anti-factor Xa to anti-factor IIa activity being greater than 4. This inhibition of thrombin generation serves to prevent coagulation and hemolysis. Simultaneously, this medication inhibits platelet aggregation, further preventing thrombus formation, clinically used for the prevention of myocardial infarction, transient ischemic attacks (TIA), atrial fibrillation (AF), arteriovenous fistulas, thrombus formation after artificial heart valves, or other surgical procedures.

D-dimer originates from cross-linked fibrin clots lysed by plasmin and primarily reflects fibrinolytic function [25].PT activity, as an indicator reflecting the levels and activities of relevant coagulation factors in the intrinsic and extrinsic coagulation pathways of the human body, has been widely used in the determination of coagulation function [26]. INR is primarily used to evaluate the antithrombotic effect of anticoagulant drugs in the human body. Fibrinogen can promote platelet aggregation, stimulate the growth, proliferation, and contraction of smooth muscle and endothelial cells, increase blood viscosity and peripheral resistance, cause endothelial cell damage, and promote red blood cell adhesion and thrombus formation [27]. This study shows that on the 1st and 5th days post-treatment, there are differences in the comparison of D-dimer, prothrombin activity, international normalized ratio, and fibrinogen differences between the two groups, with the TXA + LMWH group significantly lower than the TXA + Nadroparine group. This suggests that the combination of tranexamic acid with low-molecular-weight heparin has better anticoagulant effects in joint replacement patients, improves coagulation function, and exhibits good anticoagulant efficacy. ZHANG et al. [28] found that low-molecular-weight heparin can reduce the activity of coagulation factors, decrease blood viscosity, and significantly alleviate hypercoagulable states. This is almost similar to the findings of the present study.

Monitoring platelet counts and function helps assess the risk of thrombosis and reduces it through measures like anticoagulation therapy [29]. Elevated hematocrit levels can lead to increased blood viscosity, thereby increasing the risk of thrombosis. Hemoglobin levels are not directly linked to thrombosis, but changes in hemoglobin due to conditions such as anemia or polycythemia may affect blood rheology. The direct role of calcitonin in thrombosis is unclear, but it may indirectly influence thrombosis by affecting blood rheology or vascular wall function [30–31]. IL-6, IL-8, and TNF-α inflammatory cytokines may play certain roles in thrombosis, such as promoting vascular endothelial cell damage and activating platelets, and the coagulation system [32]. This study shows that there were no differences in the differences of platelets, hemoglobin, hematocrit, PCT, IL-6, IL-8, and TNF-α before and after treatment between the two groups. The study indicates that the combination of Low-Molecular-Weight heparin and nadroparin calcium with tranexamic acid results in similar changes in hemorheology in patients undergoing total hip and knee replacement.

LMWH is favored by clinicians due to its effectiveness and safety, making it one of the preferred drugs for DVT prophylaxis [33]. The results of this study show that there were no differences in blood volume, total blood loss, hidden blood loss, overt blood loss, and intraoperative blood loss between the two groups of patients, further indicating that the hemostatic effects of Low-Molecular-Weight heparin combined with nadroparin calcium and tranexamic acid are similar in patients undergoing total hip and knee arthroplasty. The total complication rate in the TXA + LMWH group was lower than that in the TXA + Nadroparine group, but the difference was not statistically significant, suggesting that the combination of Low-Molecular-Weight heparin calcium and tranexamic acid offers higher safety in treating patients undergoing total hip and knee arthroplasty. The authors speculate that this may be related to the following reasons: Low-Molecular-Weight heparin calcium is not neutralized by platelet factor VI, has minimal impact on platelet function, does not affect platelet aggregation and the binding of fibrinogen to platelets, and poses a lower risk of inducing adverse reactions such as bleeding while exerting antithrombotic effects [34]. DENG et al. [35] found that the use of tranexamic acid combined with Low-Molecular-Weight heparin calcium in patients undergoing total hip and knee arthroplasty is safer than that of tranexamic acid combined with rivaroxaban, further confirming the safety of tranexamic acid combined with Low-Molecular-Weight heparin calcium.

The local use of TXA is mainly applied to surgical wounds, such as the joint cavity or bone surface. This local administration method has unique advantages, enabling the drug to reach a higher concentration at the bleeding site and more accurately exert its anti-fibrinolysis effect, thus effectively reducing local bleeding. The local use of TXA is also important for the application of cementless acetabular components and femoral components. During the implantation of cementless implants, the bleeding control of the surgical wound is particularly critical. Local application of TXA can directly act on the wound surface, inhibit the dissolution of fibrin, promote the coagulation process, help to reduce intraoperative bleeding, and create good conditions for the smooth implantation of the prosthesis. At the same time, in the postoperative recovery stage, the local high concentration of TXA can also continue to play an anti-fibrinolytic effect, reducing the risk of postoperative bleeding and hematoma formation, which is conducive to the recovery of patients. From the overall therapeutic effect, the local use of TXA combined with the application of cementless prosthesis can further optimize the surgical effect. While cementless prostheses provide stable support through biological association with the host bone, topical application of TXA helps to maintain hemodynamic stability in the surgical area and reduce the occurrence of bleeding-related complications. This comprehensive treatment strategy has significant advantages in improving surgical success rates and improving patient outcomes. In practical clinical operations, we strictly follow the relevant medication specifications and operating procedures to ensure the safety and effectiveness of the topical dose and method of TXA. By precisely controlling the dosage and administration location of TXA, to maximize its antifibrinolytic effect, while avoiding possible adverse effects [36–37].

Implications for future research

The article compares and summarizes the efficacy and safety of medications commonly used in clinical practice to prevent deep vein thrombosis following joint replacement surgery. This retrospective cohort study found that both medications are effective in terms of clinical effectiveness and safety. However, in terms of total hospitalization costs, the TXA + LMWH group had significantly lower costs than the TXA + Nadroparine group. These results provide some theoretical guidance for clinicians in selecting medications for preventing deep vein thrombosis after joint replacement surgery.

Limitations of the Article

①This study is retrospective research with a certain degree of selection bias; ②The article is a retrospective study, which may introduce outcome bias; however, the research team strictly recorded all preoperative clinical indicators and carefully followed up on all postoperative test indicators for both groups to ensure the completeness and reliability of clinical data; ③Due to the diversity of patient sources, varying individual constitutions, uneven educational levels, differences in medical conditions and resources, dissimilar research methods and follow-up times, and other variable factors, the reliability of the above conclusions still requires further prospective studies to be confirmed. The grouping method in this study is relatively simple, which may lead to subjective biases in the results. Therefore, to gain a deeper understanding of the association between the effects of low-molecular-weight heparin and nadroparin calcium in preventing DVT following joint replacement surgery, a large-scale, long-term, multi-center, and multidisciplinary collaborative study is needed.

Conclusion

In summary, the TXA + Nadroparine group and the TXA + LMWH group showed essentially consistent clinical efficacy and safety after joint replacement surgery. However, the TXA + LMWH group demonstrated superior thrombosis prevention compared to the TXA + Nadroparine group, and the total hospitalization costs in the TXA + LMWH group were significantly higher than in the TXA + Nadroparine group.

Spin-Promotion Effect to Oxygen Evolution Reaction
Authors: Anke Yu, Tianze Wu, Xiao Ren, Zhichuan J. Xu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ConspectusThe spin-promotion effect in the oxygen evolution reaction (OER) in ferromagnetic (FM) materials has recently received increasing interest. However, the mechanistic picture has not been clarified. Here, we present a comprehensive overview of the mechanisms behind the spin-promotion effect in the OER, bridging the atomic-scale spin interaction with the macroscopic role of the magnetic domains. As a conclusion, FM catalysts have a spin-promotion effect on the OER at a high pH electrolyte. The high pH enables the formation of M–O• oxyl radicals through surface deprotonation. The spins of the single electrons on two neighboring M–O• oxyl radicals are aligned on a magnetic domain but not aligned on a magnetic domain wall. Thus, the triplet O2 formation is faster on a magnetic domain than on a domain wall. Magnetization leads to converting domain-wall regions to domain ones, and thus the promotion effect can be observed. Such an increment is determined by the domain structure of the magnetic catalysts, i.e., nonsignificant domain wall occupation cannot lead to a remarkable increment by spin-promotion effect. However, one should be reminded that any catalysts can give remarkable increments at a high current density when applied with an external magnetic field, which is due to the promoted bubble removal.

Introduction

The oxygen evolution reaction (OER) is a key half-reaction in electrochemical water splitting and other energy conversion technologies. However, its intrinsically slow reaction kinetics pose a major bottleneck to overall efficiency. This challenge is mainly associated with the scaling relationship between oxygen intermediates, which constructs a thermodynamic limitation in OER.

In recent years, manipulating the spins in the OER has emerged as a promising approach to overcome this limitation, leading to enhanced the OER activity. The spin effect in OER originates from the different ground states between H2O/OH– (singlet state) and O2 (triplet state), in which a singlet-to-triplet state conversion is required to produce triplet oxygen. However, the precise role of the spin-promotion effect during the OER is complicated yet essential for understanding the spin-related reaction mechanisms and guiding the rational design of spin-enhanced electrocatalysts. Here, we present a comprehensive picture of the spin-promoted OER. We elucidate the atomic-level understanding of the spin-aligned M–O• oxyl radicals in facilitating triplet oxygen production. We then discuss the spins in the surface active sites influenced by the bulk ferromagnetic (FM) material via the spin pinning effect, highlighting the importance of long-range magnetic ordering in domains for the spin-promotion effect. Based on this understanding, we emphasize the origin of magnetization-induced OER enhancement as the disappearance of the domain walls, since spin-disordered domain walls lack the spin-promotion effect. In addition, we caution the readers that the OER activity improvements observed under magnetic fields are not always indicative of spin-promotion effects, due to the presence of non-spin-related effects in any catalysts.

Fundamentals of Spin-Promotion Effect in OER Pathways

The electrons conserve their spins during the electron transfer processes in the OER. To realize a singlet-to-triplet spin conversion with a lower enthalpy barrier, spin manipulation in the OER pathways is required to satisfy the spin configuration for triplet-state oxygen.

Recent studies using FM catalysts have provided valuable insights into this spin-promotion effect (Figure ). A key breakthrough for understanding the spin-promotion mechanism in the OER pathway is the discovery of pH dependency in magnetic-field-enhanced OER. ,, Notably, recent studies have shown that the enhancement under magnetic fields is notable only at high pH values (typically pH >12 ,, ), whereas it becomes negligible at lower pH values. This pH dependence suggests that the nature of some OER intermediates, specifically their spin properties, can determine the spin-promotion effect.

Timeline of key milestones toward a complete picture of spin-promotion effect in the OER.

Traditionally understood OER intermediates, including M (metal site)–OH, MO, and M-OOH species, are generally considered to lack well-defined unpaired electrons on oxygen. These closed-shell configurations indicate that probably no spin exchange interaction between oxygen intermediates and catalyst metal sites can occur, making them insensitive to spin interactions for enhancing the OER. A key advancement for understanding such pH dependency comes from the identification of the M–O• oxyl radicals as the critical spin-active intermediate during OER. Previous studies have reported that M–O• oxyl radicals are likely generated during the deprotonation of M–OH. At high pH, the M–O• oxyl radicals become more abundant due to the facilitated M–OH deprotonation (Figure a), whereas MO species dominate at low pH, accounting for the pH dependency of the spin-promotion effect.

The unpaired electron enables the M–O• species to have distinctive magnetic properties. Unlike MO species with paired electrons, the ligand oxygen in the oxyl radical has an unpaired electron in the pπ orbital, where its spin direction is aligned with the electron in the metal dπ orbital through FM exchange interaction (Figure a). As a result, the spin alignment in FM catalysts can directly influence the spins in M–O• radicals during the reaction.

Based on these understandings, the atomic-level picture for the spin-promoted OER pathway in FM catalysts is then proposed, emphasizing the role of M–O• species. The M–O• can be involved in the three well-known OER mechanisms: the adsorbate evolution mechanism (AEM), the lattice oxygen oxidation mechanism (LOM), and the interaction of two M–O entity mechanism (I2M). In general, the LOM and I2M pathways involve the O–O coupling between two oxyl radicals, whereas the AEM happens on the single M–O• site. Among these, recent findings demonstrated that the spin-aligned O–O coupling is responsible for the spin-promotion effect, whereas the effect of under AEM is negligible to the overall reaction rate (Figure b). To verify this, three configurations of Ni species on Fe3O4 catalysts were designed, including the Ni isolated atoms (IAs), clusters, and shell, which controls the dominant reaction pathways. In isolated Ni, the relatively large atomic distances between Ni only favor the AEM pathway based on the DFT calculation. In contrast, the dual-site I2M pathway has a lower energy barrier on the Ni cluster and shell catalysts due to their short Ni–Ni interatomic distances. Interestingly, Ni shell catalysts exhibited more notable OER enhancement under magnetic fields than the isolated Ni catalyst, indicating that the spin-promotion effect may facilitate the O–O radical coupling in I2M. Under I2M, the two M–O• radicals couple to form triplet O2 with a reduced energy barrier, whereas a spin-mismatched coupling is energetically less favorable. For catalysts under LOM, the M–O• radicals originate from lattice-oxygen oxidation, where electrons are removed from the O nonbonding state. Similar to the O–O coupling under I2M, when such lattice-derived O• species are generated and spatially proximate, their spin-aligned coupling can lower the barrier to triplet O2. Therefore, on the spin-ordered FM catalyst, the two adjacent M–O• radicals with the same spin direction are coupled with a lower energy barrier to produce triplet oxygen. On the other hand, in AEM, the O–O coupling is realized by the nucleophilic attack of H2O/OH– on a single M–O• site to form an M–OOH intermediate. The O–O coupling under AEM is not the potential-limiting step (PLS); instead, the following dissociation of *OOH toward O2 poses a larger energy barrier. As such, although the spin selectivity can happen in the AEM pathway, the overall influence of magnetic fields on this pathway remains limited. Overall, the spin-aligned O–O coupling effectively explains the enhanced OER, identifying the mechanistic role of spin in the OER.

The varied spin promotion process in different reaction pathways highlights the need to identify the dominant reaction pathway when investigating the spin effect in OER. First, the AEM can be identified by pH-dependent studies, as its proton-coupled electron transfer (PCET) steps make the reaction independent of the electrolyte pH. In contrast, I2M and LOM pathways can exhibit stronger pH dependencies due to the different surface abundance of *O• radicals under different pH values. In addition, the LOM pathway can be probed by isotope-labeling experiments (e.g., using 18O), which allow direct tracking of lattice oxygen participation. Therefore, these approaches provide practical tools to investigate the spin promotion effect in different reaction pathways.

Spin Pinning Effect

To further explore the spin-promotion effect on real FM electrocatalysts, it is essential to understand the spins on the catalyst surface, which determine the spin configurations of the M–O• species. Typically, the spin-promotion effect is realized in FM catalysts. However, most electrocatalysts will undergo surface reconstruction under harsh OER conditions, losing their intrinsic surface ferromagnetism. This raises an important question, i.e., how does the bulk magnetization of FM catalysts influence the spins on the nonferromagnetic surface?

To address this, we demonstrated the spin pinning effect in the surface reconstructed Co3–x Fe x O4/Co­(Fe)­O x H y catalyst, where FM precatalysts reconstructed with a paramagnetic (PM) surface, regulating the surface spins in catalysts. The spin pinning effect originates from the strong exchange coupling at the interface between the FM and antiferromagnetic (AFM) (or PM) spins, where the AFM (or PM) spins can align with the FM spins. ,, In other words, this inherent spin interaction can “pin” the AFM (or PM) spins adjacent to the interface at a certain direction, which cannot be easily changed by the external magnetic fields. As a result, the reconstructed Co­(Fe)­O x H y surface can exhibit an FM spin ordering, according to the underneath FM core, thus facilitating OER. Under a magnetic field, the OER is greatly enhanced for FM-core Co3–x Fe x O4/Co­(Fe)­O x H y catalyst through the spin pinning effect, but remains unchanged in the AFM-core Co3O4/CoO x H y .

The presence of the spin pinning effect in the reconstructed electrocatalysts can be experimentally verified by the magnetic hysteresis loop under field-cooled (FC) and zero-field-cooled (ZFC) modes (Figure a). By field cooling, AFM spins near the interfaces tend to align with the FM spins along the magnetization direction by FM exchange interaction. When the field is reversed, the frozen AFM spins will exert an additional torque to the FM spins, which increases the saturation field strength to reverse their magnetizations. Accordingly, the spin-pinned system manifests as a shift in the FC-mode magnetic hysteresis loop, known as an exchange bias. The exchange bias in CoFe2O4/Co­(Fe)­O x H y catalyst is ∼600 Oe, but it is negligible in pristine FM CoFe2O4 catalyst, confirming the spin pinning effect.

Importantly, the spin pining is limited to regions near the FM/AFM or FM/PM interfaces (Figure b), while the bulk AFM (or PM) materials have zero net magnetization. The effective distance for spin pinning of a typical reconstructed oxideFM/oxyhydroxide interface is usually within ∼5 nm2, indicating that a thicker surface layer may lead to a weaker spin-enhanced effect. For example, the shell thickness of Fe3O4@Ni­(OH)2 core–shell catalysts has been found to influence the OER enhancement by magnetization. A thin AFM Ni­(OH)2 shell of 7 nm (effective spin pinning) showed ∼45% enhancement in the OER under magnetic fields, while it decreased to ∼20% enhancement as the shell thickness increased to 43 nm.

Spin-Promotion Effect on Magnetic Domains

The spin-promotion effect in the OER pathways provides key insights for understanding the experimentally observed OER enhancement by magnetic fields. In the past few years, the extent of OER enhancement under magnetic fields has been closely related to the magnetic domain structure in FM catalysts. ,,, The magnetic domains are defined as the region within FM materials in which all atomic magnetic moments are uniformly aligned through the FM exchange interaction (Figure a). To minimize the magnetostatic energy, FM materials usually spontaneously divide into separate domains with different magnetization orientations. The transition regions between adjacent magnetic domains are known as the magnetic domain walls. Within the domain wall region, the magnetic moments gradually rotate from the orientation of one domain to the other, forming a region without long-range spin ordering.

In 2021, the influence of domain structure on magnetic-field-enhanced OER was first demonstrated using ferromagnetic–antiferromagnetic (FM-AFM) coupling core–shell nanoparticles. The domain structures of Fe3O4@Ni­(OH)2 core–shell catalysts are tuned by controlling the size of core Fe3O4 (Figure b). The critical size (R C) to achieve a single domain Fe3O4 particle is around 26–40 nm. , When the particle size exceeds R C, the multiple-domain structure could be formed to reduce the magnetostatic energy, while the particle remains in a single-domain state when its size is below R C. The transition from single-domain to multi-domain behavior by increasing the Fe3O4 core sizes was evidenced by the enhanced saturation field strength (HS), where the domains rotate progressively in multidomain Fe3O4. Under magnetic fields, the level of the enhanced OER varied across different core Fe3O4 sizes with the same shell thickness. Notably, pronounced OER enhancement occurs in bulk and 120-nm-core (multi-domain) samples, whereas the 22-nm-core (single-domain) one showed a negligible OER enhancement. Intrinsic OER activity in multi-domain catalysts also greatly improved upon magnetization, as reflected by an increase in turnover frequency (TOF) from 0.263 to 0.346 molecules per second, demonstrating the important role of magnetic domains for modulating the OER.

Subsequent research has further elucidated the comprehensive role of magnetic domains in governing the OER enhancement by the spin-promotion effect, attributing the origin of the enhancement to the disappearance of domain wall regions upon magnetization. In 2023, the relationship between domain wall disappearance and OER enhancement is demonstrated by the NiFe thin film catalysts. The magnetic domain structure of the NiFe films is tuned by varying the film thickness from 200 nm to 800 nm (Figure c), where increased thickness favors broader stripe domains with reduced domain wall areas. The corresponding OER enhancement (overpotential drops) under magnetic fields showed a strong correlation with the calculated domain wall disappearance ratios (Figure d).

These findings suggest that the magnetic field does not enhance the OER by regulating net spin angular momentum or flipping of unpaired electron spinswhich is impossible under such conditions. Instead, the magnetization process in the NiFe films can align multiple domains into a single-domain state, effectively eliminating spin-disorder domain wall regions. On the domain regions, the long-range spin ordering favors the spin-aligned M–O• radicals, which requires a lower energy barrier to produce triplet oxygen. However, the spin promotion effect cannot be achieved on the spin-disordered domain walls, which leads to a high energy barrier for the O–O coupling.

Furthermore, this spin-promotion effect in magnetic domains was also validated using single-domain CoFe2O4 nanoparticles (Figure b) without the external magnetic fields. It is found that, without magnetic fields, the multi-domain nanoparticle exhibits lower OER activity than the single-domain one. By applying magnetic fields, single-domain nanoparticles showed negligible change in the OER activity due to the absence of domain walls, whereas pronounced enhancement of the OER occurs in multi-domain nanoparticles (see Figures e and f). Notably, the TOF of multi-domain CoFe2O4 increases from 0.220 s–1 to 0.291 s–1 by magnetization, closely approaching the value of single-domain CoFe2O4 (0.309 s–1; see Figure g). Such a high level of the OER activity of single-domain CoFe2O4 suggests that the spin-promotion effect is inherently achieved on single-domain catalysts without external magnetic fields. Overall, these findings not only clarify the critical role of domain structure in regulating spin-promoted OER, but also open a new direction for designing magnetic electrocatalysts to realize spin-promoted OER without external magnetic fields (Figure ).

Overview of the spin-promoted OER mechanisms. Spin-promotion arises from the spin alignment of M–O• radicals within ferromagnetic domains, facilitating the formation of triplet oxygen with a lower energy barrier. A more pronounced spin promotion effect happens at high pH conditions where the oxyl radicals are more abundant. At low pH, the OER proceeds through oxo species without spin alignment, making the reaction largely insensitive to the catalysts’ spins. The long-range magnetic ordering on magnetic domains favors the spin alignment of surface active sites through the spin pinning effect, which facilitates low-barrier O–O coupling. Under magnetic fields, the OER enhances due to the disappearance of domain walls, while in single-domain catalysts, no further enhancement is observed because the spin configuration is already optimized. To accurately investigate the spin-promotion effect, catalysts with identical surface chemistry but different domain structures should be compared, avoiding interference from surface property variations in different materials. Besides, non-spin-related effects such as bubble removal contribute at high current densities in all catalysts. Key references are indicated.

Beyond magnetic domains, spin promotion can also be achieved on other catalyst systems. For example, the spin polarization can promote OER through chiral-induced spin selectivity (CISS). On chiral electrodes, the spin polarization is generated differently from ferromagnetic materials, as the intrinsic molecular chirality induces preferential transmission of one spin orientation over the other, thus affecting the OER. Another example is the spin promotion effect on the single-atom Ni1/MoS2 catalyst. In this catalyst system, the spin of oxygen intermediates (e.g., *OH and *O species) is affected by the spin interactions in active sites, where the O–O coupling between two ferromagnetic-coupled active sites is energetically more favorable than that between the antiferromagnetic-coupled ones. These examples highlight that local spin interactions in different catalyst systems can effectively control the spin configuration of oxygen intermediates and lower the energy barriers for the O–O coupling, tailoring spin effects in the OER catalysis.

Interpreting Activity Enhancement under Magnetic Fields

Magnetic fields offer a powerful approach to investigate the spin-promotion effect in the OER. However, it is important to note that the experimentally observed enhancement under magnetic fields does not necessarily guarantee the spin-promotion effect. First, the OER should be accurately measured with standardized protocols and rigorously analyzed to assess changes in intrinsic activity. − Second, it should be noted that the single-/multi-domain catalysts with the same surface chemistry allow a direct comparison of spin effects, whereas different materials with uncontrolled surface variations can interfere with the interpretation of spin-promotion effect. Besides, the magnetic fields can also induce multiple non-spin-related effects, such as bubble removal and mass transport by magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) effect, magnetoresistance, Maxwell stress, contact electrification, and magnetic heating under alternating magnetic fields. These effects can significantly enhance the OER even in the nonmagnetic catalysts, particularly at high current densities under magnetic fields (Figure ). To distinguish and minimize the non-spin-related effect, key experimental practices and setups should be adopted. For example, to confirm the spin effect, nonmagnetic catalysts should be measured under identical conditions as control experiments, while the OER activity of demagnetized-state FM catalysts should be re-evaluated for reproducibility. Meanwhile, the OER activity by magnetization should be analyzed at lower potentials (such as <1.60 V vs RHE) where the influence of the MHD effect is minimal, since mass transport contributions become more pronounced at higher overpotentials. Tafel slope analysis across different overpotential regions can further provide important evidence to distinguish the change in reaction kinetics from mass-transport effects. Besides, the iR correction should be performed throughout the experiment to eliminate any influences from electrode displacement or magnetoresistance.

Additionally, such non-spin-related enhancements are not unique to OER. The activity enhancement by bubble removal and mass transport is present in all mass-transport-limited or gas-involving reactions, such as oxygen reduction reaction (ORR) and hydrogen evolution reaction (HER). Thus, to study the spin-promotion effect, it is important to minimize these interferences by careful experimental design, or by avoiding the use of external magnetic fields, such as developing intrinsically spin-aligned single-domain catalysts.

Conclusion and Outlook

In summary, we have presented a comprehensive picture of the spin-promotion effect in the OER, bridging macroscopic domain theory with the underlying atomic-scale spin interactions. We highlight the key finding of the spin dependency for the spin-promotion effect, where M–O• oxyl radicals form via deprotonation at high pH. The spins of these radicals with unpaired electrons can align with the magnetic domains, facilitating low-energy-barrier O2 turnover. In contrast, in the domain wall region, the disordered spins in the catalysts result in a high energy barrier and lower OER activity. Besides, we also elucidate the effect of magnetization on regulating domains, while clarifying its limitation due to non-spin-related enhancements. Overall, these mechanistic insights define the spin-promotion effect on the OER and provide guidance for designing advanced spin-active electrocatalysts.

Moving forward, several directions can accelerate progress in this emerging field based on the mechanistic picture. First, quantitative benchmarks should be established to correlate magnetization, domain wall occupancy, and interfacial spin polarization with the OER activity. For example, systematic comparisons of the OER activity between domains and the domain wall under controlled surface chemistry can provide direct evidence of the spin promotion. A surface with long-range magnetic ordering, such as magnetic domains, is expected to outperform the spin-disordered surfaces (domain walls). Second, the development of operando techniques (e.g., magneto-optical Kerr effect imaging) will be crucial to directly monitor magnetic dynamics and link them to intrinsic activities. Other spin-sensitive techniques, such as spin-polarized scanning tunneling microscopy (SP-STM) and X-ray magnetic circular dichroism (XMCD), are desired to provide the atomic-level spin-related information in OER catalyst surfaces. To detect the key oxygen intermediate during the reaction, chemical probes could be adopted to directly assess their role in magnetic-enhanced OER. Third, experimental practices need to prioritize key controls to confirm the spin effect, including demagnetized references, nonmagnetic controls, and measurements at low overpotentials.

Additionally, the standardized reporting protocols in the community will be essential to improve comparability across studies. Key elements include reporting of saturation magnetization and coercivity, imaging, or estimation of domain structures, mapping of magnetic field profiles on the working electrode, and monitoring of temperature changes upon prolonged operations. These standardized measurements, combined with consistent electrochemical analysis (e.g., iR correction, Tafel analysis), will allow reproducible identification of spin promotion in OER.

Development of intersectoral medical care for patients with ‘chronic critical illness’: protocol for a telemedicine interventional study with a pre-post design in out-of-hospital intensive care facilities (E=MC²)
Authors: Andreas Edel, Susanne Jöbges, Bjoern Weiss, Nicolas Paul, Maike Lucia Lyall, Clemens Hoffmann, Stephen Schüürhuis, Sophie K K Piper, Frank Konietschke, Elke Berger, Reinhard Busse, Ursula Marschall, Ben Kraufmann, Martin Witzenrath, Kai-Uwe Eckardt, Claudia Spies
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPatients receiving long-term ventilation (LTV) in out-of-hospital intensive care facilities often suffer from persistent impairments of their cognition, mental health and physical health, limiting their social participation. Chronically ill patients are often unable to express their care preferences. Thus, their medical care often lacks integration of patients’ wishes and values. Telemedicine may be used to collect patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs) from these patients to align medical care with their preferences. Early integration of teleconsultation to provide rapid support for specific patient symptoms can reduce economic costs.Method and analysisThis is a multicentre, prospective, non-blinded, single-arm interventional trial with a pre-post design and follows the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials statement. 10 out-of-hospital intensive care facilities in Berlin and Brandenburg, Germany, are grouped into three clusters. The study population includes adult patients (≥18 years) receiving LTV and residing in participating care facilities. During the preintervention phase, standard patient care remains unchanged. From the start of the intervention phase, enrolled patients receive telemedicine rounds in addition to standard care. These telemedicine rounds, conducted at least weekly, involve on-site healthcare professionals, patients and their relatives. Data are collected at predefined time points—study months 1,3, 9, 15 and 21—with a target of 57 participants at each time point. The study aims to evaluate whether a structured telemedicine intervention (1) increases the proportion of patients receiving record-documented PROMs in routine care and (2) reduces hospital readmissions. Secondary outcomes include the evaluation of post-intensive care syndrome, healthcare costs and the usability, applicability and perceived benefits of telemedicine. Additionally, qualitative interviews with patients, their relatives and healthcare professionals will explore individual experiences with chronic critical illness, the perceived quality of life of the patients and how team members manage moral distress in caregiving contexts. A mixed-effects logistic regression model will be used to analyse patients’ access to PROMs, while a mixed-effects Poisson regression model will be employed to evaluate hospital readmission rates. The findings may provide valuable insights into how telemedicine can improve patient-centred care for this particular patient group.Ethics and disseminationThis study protocol received approval from the Ethics Committee of Charité—Universitätsmedizin Berlin, Germany (EA2/136/22). The findings will be disseminated through publication in a peer-reviewed scientific journal and presented at international conferences.Trial registration numberThis study was registered in the ‘German Register of Clinical Studies’ (DRKS; DRKS00029326).Keywords: Telemedicine, INTENSIVE & CRITICAL CARE, Quality of Life, Patient Reported Outcome Measures, Nursing Homes

Introduction

Most patients in the intensive care unit (ICU) recover from their critical illness and are transferred to lower-level care units. However, some do not recover and develop a state of chronic critical illness (CCI), a chronic condition requiring prolonged, high-intensity care. CCI is associated with long hospital stays, high mortality, significant suffering and resource use.1 These patients needing long-term ventilation (LTV) may be discharged to out-of-hospital intensive care facilities due to their ongoing risk of life-threatening complications.2 ICU survivors may also suffer from long-term impairments known as post-intensive care syndrome (PICS),3 4 which includes cognitive, physical and psychological deficits.5 6 CCI and PICS share many pathological features and are difficult to distinguish.1

Beyond the traditional outcomes, conducted by healthcare professionals, patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs) capture the patient’s perspective on various outcome domains, like general health perception, health-related quality of life, symptom and functional status.7 8 There are no specific PROMs that allow independent assessment of outcomes in LTV care. Although studies have shown that this patient group has significant limitations in health-related quality of life and untapped potential for improvement.9,11

Germany has, with approximately 86 000 patients, an above-average number of patients who are ventilated outside the hospital.12 Additionally, the initiations of non-invasive home mechanical ventilation (HMV) cases decreased by 14.9% from 2019 to 2020, while the number of initiated invasive HMV cases remained stable at approximately 1000 cases per year. Consequently, statutory health insurance costs for ambulatory intensive care continued to rise by 42.3%, from €1.52 billion in 2017 to €2.16 billion in 2020.13 There is a lack of structured specialist support and limited access to highly qualified intensive care centres,14 leading to inadequate healthcare delivery.

Deficit in specialist care availability and intersectoral links between patients and healthcare professionals may contribute to insufficient care.9 15 These patients are often readmitted to the hospital, leading to transport risks, stress and anxiety. There are several telemedicine approaches for invasively ventilated patients addressing this issue.16 In Germany, telemedicine projects have connected outpatient facilities with a PICS outpatient clinic and intensive care centre.17 However, neither of these facilities has been used to support patients with CCI in their nursing homes, nor have they been scientifically evaluated. Patients receiving HMV undergo complex treatment, yet systematic involvement in decision-making is frequently lacking. Access to PROMs remains limited despite their relevance. Telemedicine offers an approach to address this gap. The primary objectives of E=MC2 (Entwicklung der intersektoralen, medizinischen Versorgung von Patienten mit Chronic Critical Illness—Development of intersectoral medical care for patients with chronic critical illness) are to determine whether (1) telemedicine improves patients’ access to PROMs and (2) reduces the need for hospital readmissions among patients receiving HMV in these care settings.

Facilities were grouped into three clusters, based on their inclusion times: the first was formed in August 2022 (n=6), the second in January 2023 (n=2) and the third in May 2023 (n=2).

Treatment conditions and implementation of the intervention

The study consists of a preintervention phase of 6 months, followed by an intervention phase of 15 months. The flowchart illustrates the planned trial schedule (figure 2). Study patients are followed for the duration of their stay in the care facility. At the time of study planning, the average lengths of stay were estimated at approximately 3 months; thus, only a minority of patients was expected to undergo repeated measurements over time.

Preintervention phase

During the preintervention phase, there are no modifications to the provided care. PROMs are performed and documented according to the local practices at each care facility. Patients were not specifically informed about or guided in completing the PROMs by the study team, as this phase aimed to document existing routines within the PICS domains. At months 1 and 3 after study initiation, the primary endpoints are assessed through patient records, and PROM study visits are scheduled. The study team conducts structured PROM assessments alongside standard care. To distinguish PROMs related to the primary endpoint from those collected for secondary analyses, the latter are referred to as ‘study PROM’ (sPROM) and do not affect routine care.

Intervention phase

From the start of the intervention phase, the enrolled patients receive additional telemedicine rounds to standard care. These rounds are conducted at least weekly involving on-site healthcare professionals, patients and relatives. During rounds, the nursing staff moves on-site telemedicine robots. A photo of the telemedicine robots (manufacturer: Teladoc Health, USA) and a detailed intervention description, following the TIDieR checklist,20 are provided in online supplemental file 1. Adherence to the intervention

Tele-ICU consultants are responsible for ensuring weekly telemedicine rounds. In addition, the Tele-ICU offers a telephone service during the regular daytime working hours (from 08:00 to 16:00) in case of additional medical requirements.

Anonymous postintervention survey study

Patients, relatives and healthcare professionals are invited to participate anonymously in a paper-based, on-site survey following the intervention phase. Forms were submitted in sealed boxes. The survey includes self-developed or adapted questions across three parts: (1) 34 Likert-scale items for healthcare professionals; (2) 16 for patients and relatives; and (3) 5 items on overall satisfaction and suggestions. Topics included system usability,22 perceived benefits, trust, user attitudes and communication quality during teleconsultations.

Procedure

The qualitative, semi-structured interviews follow two guideline sets—one for patients/caregivers and one for healthcare professionals—developed by SJ (an intensive care physician and ethicist) in collaboration with psychologists, based on a literature review. One aspect will be the experience of decision-making processes, personal attitudes to CCI and the impact on current life plans. The questionnaire also includes items to capture individual quality of life values. Interviews will be conducted either via video conference or on-site by SJ and MLL. SJ is experienced in qualitative research. All audio-recorded interviews will be transcribed verbatim. Transcripts will undergo structured content analysis, with SJ and MLL independently coding and categorising themes based on the interview guide and emerging patterns. Differences will be resolved by consensus.

Power considerations and pretrial sample size calculation

10 out-of-hospital intensive care facilities with LTV were included in the study. With full occupancy of respiratory care flat sharing, a maximum of n=113 patients could be included in the study.

We expected non-participation, discontinuation and fluctuation in all participating care facilities. Therefore, we conservatively assume participation of 50% of the maximum capacity, such that we base our sample size justification on n=113*0.5 ≈ 57 participants for each time point.

We anticipate that, before the telemedicine intervention, approximately 10% of the patients receive PROMs in the preintervention phase. We assume a gradual improvement in the proportion of patients receiving PROM rates during telemedicine intervention to at least 70% by the 15th month post-telemedicine implementation. With a sample size of n=57, a 95% CI would have a width of ±8% for an expected rate of 10% before the intervention and ±12% for an expected rate of 70% after the intervention, indicating a moderate estimation precision. Making the simplifying assumption of non-overlapping samples in the study phases, with n=57 for all measurement time points, a χ2 test would have a power of 76% at a two-sided significance level of 5% to detect an increase in proportions of at least 30% after implementing telemedicine rounds. Notably, a sample size larger than n=57 would lead to higher estimation precision and increased statistical power.

The research hypothesis for the clinical primary endpoint is that with regular telemedicine rounds, the number of hospital readmissions of out-of-hospital LTV patients within 3 months before the measurement time point is lower than without telemedicine rounds. A Mann-Whitney U test with a two-sided significance level of 5% with n=57 patients per group has a power of over 80% to detect a standardised effect size of d=0.57 (corresponding to P(X<Y) of 0.657) in the number of hospital admissions between the groups with and without connection to telemedicine. This would correspond, for example, to a mean number of hospital readmissions of 5 in the preintervention phase (without telemedicine connection) and ≤3 readmissions in the intervention phase (with telemedicine), with an SD of 3.5. All sample size calculations were performed with R V.4.1.2.

Data management, security and quality control

Data collection

Telemedicine intervention study

All outcomes will be documented electronically using an access-restricted REDCap database. Relevant study data, PROMs and the health economic items will be recorded on paper-based case report froms (CRFs) and transferred to the database. Once patients are enrolled, the study team conducts follow-ups directly at the nursing facilities. If a patient leaves the nursing facility, no further follow-up is planned. All records are treated as strictly confidential.

Anonymous user survey

The user survey will be conducted anonymously on-site, using paper forms. Forms will be deposited in sealed urns. Collected data will be entered into spreadsheets for the analysis.

Interview study

The interviews will be recorded as audio files, transcribed pseudonymously and analysed qualitatively using the software program MaxQDA (VERBI—Software. Consult. Sozialforschung. GmbH, Berlin, Germany). The pseudonymised transcripts will be coded and evaluated in MaxQDA, with exports to spreadsheets for coding, creating graphics and tables.

Data management

Data management is described in a separate data management plan. Data entry procedures were defined using the REDCap database. Mechanisms for ensuring data quality are in place through prescribed mandatory platform entry fields and platform-generated data-quality reports. Data validity is ensured through automated queries and automated calculation of questionnaire scores. Survey and interview data will be stored on a secure server. Aggregated anonymised study data will be shared with consortium partners. The study documentation is archived for 10 years at the site of the primary investigator. A separate data-protection concept was developed.

Statistical methods

The Institute of Biometry and Clinical Epidemiology, Charité Universitätsmedizin Berlin (Comm. Head: university professor FK), will perform the statistical evaluation. A detailed statistical analysis plan was written in August 2022 and updated in November 2024 and July 2025. Data analysis will start in August 2025. The intervention was analysed using the TIDieR checklist before deciding on the study design.20

Access to PROMsWe calculate point estimates of the proportion of patients with PROM and accompanying two-sided 95% CIs for rates at each measurement time point. To account for the partially longitudinal nature of the data and clustering of patients in different out-of-hospital intensive care facilities, we additionally employ a mixed-effects logistic regression model to estimate PROM rates by including all measurement time points in the model. Here, the binary dependent variable is PROM (yes/no), and we include random intercepts for the facility and each subject. We report the odds ratios (ORs) and corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs) as an effect measure. Additionally, we plan to incorporate further covariates into sensitivity analyses. To implement these models, we will use the lme4 R-Package (V.1.1–29).25 26

Secondary endpoint analyses

To assess whether there is a change in terms of (1) the presence of PICS (yes/no) and (2) health-related quality of life via the telemedicine rounds, structured sPROMs and tPROMs will be obtained at time points (months 1, 3, 9, 15 and 21).

Secondary endpoints are assessed descriptively using mean and standard deviation (SD), median and limits of the IQR, and absolute and relative frequencies. The reasons for missing values are documented and provided in as much detail as possible.

Anonymous user survey

Given the exploratory nature, survey data are analysed descriptively. Likert-scale items are evaluated using absolute and relative frequencies, medians and IQR. Standardised questionnaires are assessed using sum and mean scores, with negative items reverse-coded where applicable.

Interview study

For qualitative scientific evaluation, a method of deductive (based on the original framework) and inductive (theory-generating) approaches, with a method of continuous comparison to interpret the expected and emergent themes will be used.27 28

Missing values

Unless otherwise stated, missing values will not be replaced. Since the primary endpoints are obtained based on the individual patient’s records and the collection is performed separately from the patient’s health status, no missing values are expected. The PROM study visits consist of collecting secondary endpoints by using tests and questionnaires. The degree to which patients can cooperate and perform questionnaires and physical tests is strictly dependent on the patient’s health status and capabilities, missing values obtained during sPROM are expected depending on the individual condition. These missing values are considered not at random (MNAR). When possible, proxy assessments will be used. Appropriate statistical methods will address missing data, and sensitivity analysis—including worst-case/best-case imputation—will be conducted to assess robustness, including scenarios accounting for MNAR data.

Monitoring

Given the small sample size and low-risk intervention, the study steering committee deemed a Data Monitoring Committee unnecessary. Study integrity and patient safety will be monitored by the study team. As the intervention aligns with standard care and uses routinely collected data, no adverse events will be reported, and no interim analysis is planned.

Ethics and data security

The study follows the Declaration of Helsinki and Good Scientific Practice. Ethical approval was granted by the Charité ethics committee on 12 July 2022 (EA2/136/22), and the study is registered in the ‘German Register of Clinical Studies—DRKS’ (identifier: DRKS00029326). Amendments will be submitted to the ethics and registry review. Written informed consent will be obtained from all patients or their legal representatives (online supplemental file 2). Data are handled in compliance with the European General Data Protection Regulation. A separate data protection concept was reviewed by the Institutional Clinical Trial Office of Charité. An independent Advisory Board, including a patient representative, ensures compliance with ethical and legal standards. A qualitative interview study was conducted to integrate patient perspectives.

Trial status

Discussion and practical implication

E=MC2 is one of the first German telemedicine intervention studies that provides access to LTV patients in out-of-hospital intensive care facilities. Existing telemonitoring and teleconsultation systems vary in effectiveness and adaptability.16 For example, in a retrospective analysis of 659 patients receiving non-invasive nocturnal ventilation, Le Mao et al demonstrated that telemonitoring significantly reduced therapy dropout rates compared with standard care (13% vs 34%, p<0.001).31 Similarly, telemedicine consultations have been associated with fewer hospital admissions among this vulnerable patient group.32

Qualitative interviews with 87 healthcare professionals highlight care deficiencies indicate for patients on HMV, citing financial disincentives, staff shortages, and lack of quality standards. The authors emphasised the need for standardises quality metrics and person-centred care to improve outcomes.33 Addressing the lack of validated PROMs and patient-reported experience measures for HMV patients, Rudilla et al developed and validated tools tailored to home respiratory therapy.34 However, domains such as cognition and physical impairment remain underexplored, which our study seeks to address. Our study addresses this by enabling PROM access for LTV patients in out-of-hospital intensive care facilities, aiming to enhance empowerment and care experiences. Recognising that transportation and rehospitalisation are challenging, stressful and often dangerous for these patients, our study aims to bridge the diagnostic gap for immobile individuals. Healthcare cost analyses for this group in Germany are lacking. A Canadian study of paediatric HMV patients showed total healthcare costs increased from $C128,941 to $C164,892 within 2 years of initiating HMV. Our study will provide relevant health economic insights. By combining quantitative and qualitative methods, we aim to understand the attitudes, challenges and preferences of LTV patients.

Summary

E=MC2 investigates whether a telemedicine programme enables residents with LTV in out-of-hospital intensive care facilities to participate in patient-centred care. PROMs will help assess their personal treatment preferences and may guide treatment decisions, reduce hospitalisations and potentially improve quality of life.

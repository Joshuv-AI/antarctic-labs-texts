Challenges, opportunities, strengths, and weaknesses of the internship model in teaching the bachelors of anesthesiology internship course: a qualitative content analysis study
Authors: Morteza Habibi Moghadam, Mahboobeh Zolfaghari-Isvand, Narjes Kazemi, Marzieh Beigom Bigdeli Shamloo, Zeynab Kord
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBesides providing health services to the public, medical universities also shoulder the important task of educating and training skilled and specialized human resources required by different sectors of society. This necessitates that medical education follow a model that can include the role of professional competence and education in promoting health, so that students from different medical science groups can deal with societal issues and accept their real responsibility for improving the health of their community. One of these ways is to train undergraduate students in anesthesiology through an internship. The aim of this study is to explain the strengths, weaknesses, challenges, and opportunities of this educational plan.MethodsIn this qualitative content analysis conducted based on SWOT theory, six faculty instructors, anesthesiologists, non-faculty instructors, operating room head nurses, and undergraduate students in the field of anesthesiology were selected based on the entry criteria. Then, questions were asked from them based on the aforementioned theory, and after extracting the codes, classification was performed according to the Graneheim & Lundman approach.ResultsFrom the analysis of the findings, four subcategories of strengths, weaknesses, challenges, and opportunities were obtained, which were in the management and executive domains. The most repeated code in its strengths was improving the student’s relationship with the surgical team and reducing their anxiety. The most repeated code in its weaknesses was using the student as an instrument and avoiding scientific work.ConclusionsThe results obtained indicate that in addition to its significant strengths, this plan has also weaknesses that can be improved by eliminating them and carrying out corrective processes. Meanwhile, the professional qualifications and abilities of students in the clinical field can be improved, and the shortage of human resources in the clinical field can be reduced.Trial registration numberNot applicable.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12909-025-07932-y.Keywords: Clinical evaluation, Internship, Anesthesiology

Background

Clinical education is one of the most important aspects of education in medical sciences and constitutes a major part of training competent individuals [1]. Identifying problems in the educational-skills process of students and then taking steps to resolve and correct them will improve the achievement of educational goals and ultimately train skilled individuals as well as provide high-quality medical and care services [2]. The ultimate goal of training the medical sciences group in internships and field training courses is to develop and strengthen social attitudes and prepare students to accept future responsibilities in order to be more productive in maintaining and improving the health level of the community [3].

Among the medical sciences educational fields, two fields of operation room and anesthesiology require special attention for two reasons: [1] These two fields have recently been converted from associate to bachelor’s degrees. [2] Due to the clinical nature of their fields, students in these two fields should be trained in a clinical environment so that they can perform better in their future jobs by strengthening clinical skills [4]. Being in a clinical setting provides ideal opportunities to observe how a role is played, how actions are taken by the individual, and to think about what is observed, heard, felt, and done [5]. However, among the weaknesses in education that have been emphasized as one of the important challenges is the tendency to use traditional methods in education, the difficulty of assessing performance, and the supervision of students in field training [6].

In this regard, the internship educational method has been implemented as an innovation in the educational structure with the aim of improving the quality of nursing education and providing clinical services as well as providing a comprehensive and community-oriented context in nursing. In this method, final-term students who have reached appropriate levels of competence enter and complete some of the clinical courses with the presence of an instructor, and some independently and without the direct presence of an instructor in hospital wards [7]. A study by Dinmohammadi et al. has indicated that implementing internship-style internship programs in the educational course of nursing students not only helps to facilitate the transition of students to the professional role, but if implemented effectively, it can also help mitigate the challenge of the country’s nursing workforce shortage [8]. In this method, the instructor only supervises the correct implementation of the work and the instructor’s direct presence is not for theoretical and practical training. The experience that nursing students gain in this method makes them feel comfortable in carrying out their activities as an independent individual. This program helps improve self-confidence and clinical decision-making power in students [7]. For this reason, all educational and research activities should be designed and implemented in a way that leads to strengthening the insight and community-oriented knowledge in the minds of professors and students [9].

Very limited studies have been conducted in the field of operating room and anesthesia internships, but internship courses in different disciplines have been examined from various perspectives [10]. Various studies indicate that internships in the field have failed to improve the professional capabilities of students to the desired level and that the existence of numerous problems has prevented the achievement of the goals of this course [11, 12]. In various studies, factors such as little attention to clinical education, lack of a sufficient number of clinical instructors, lack of coordination and proportion between clinical and classroom training, lack of facilities, as well as factors such as lack of constant availability of instructors to meet educational needs, have been raised as the most important problems of clinical education from the perspective of students [13]. Researchers state that achieving an acceptable level of self-confidence, independence, and decision-making power in clinical skills is practically impossible with in-field training and the presence of a direct instructor and on some days indirectly, and addressing the implementation issues related to clinical training in this course is very important and should attract the attention of authorities [14]. Unfortunately, in many cases, even knowledgeable and informed students become confused after completing their in-field training at the patient’s bedside and cannot act independently and take responsibility for patient care [15].

Considering the fundamental point that anesthesia students are trained in clinical departments (operating rooms and special wards) and not in the classroom due to their specialized and professional work [16]. thus, examining the presence of any type of intervention factor that reduces students’ practical learning is considered essential in clinical education planning [10]. The results of studies indicate that students, as recipients of educational services, are the best source for identifying problems in clinical education, since they have direct and immediate presence and interaction with this process [2].

On the other hand, during the studies conducted, despite the increase in course units, especially practical courses, the necessary infrastructure has not yet been designed to implement all relevant educational objectives and reveal obstacles as well as problems [17] and less research has been conducted in this field. As such, it is necessary to identify the strengths and weaknesses that can affect their learning path, knowledge acquisition, and professional competence. Therefore, this study was conducted with the aim of explaining the challenges, opportunities, strengths, and weaknesses of the internship in the training of the undergraduate anesthesia internship.

Methods

In this directed content analysis study, after obtaining permission from the Vice Chancellor for Research of Dezful University of Medical Sciences, six interns, faculty members of the Anesthesia Department, and anesthesia experts working in hospitals affiliated with the university were selected based on the inclusion criteria. The inclusion criteria for students included being a final-year undergraduate student in the Anesthesia program, while the inclusion criteria for faculty members and anesthesia experts included having at least 5 years of experience working in the operating room, at least 2 years of experience teaching students, having at least a bachelor’s degree in anesthesiology, and a willingness to participate in the research. Purposeful, accessible sampling was conducted with maximum diversity, where the highest and lowest class ranks were considered in the selection of students. The exclusion criterion was: unwillingness to participate in the research. All interviews were conducted face-to-face in the operating room training room at a time agreed upon by the interviewee.

The hospital affiliated with Dezful University of Medical Sciences is one of the level 2 hospitals and the largest hospital in the north of Khuzestan province. It has nine general operating rooms where various surgeries including ENT, neurology, general, orthopedics, ophthalmology, and urology are performed using general, regional, and peripheral nerve block techniques. An average of 30 operations are performed daily, with elective surgeries performed in the morning and evening shifts and emergency operations performed in the night shift. This department does not have any residents in the sub-branches of surgery and anesthesia.

In the internship method, students in the field of anesthesiology internship were placed in the morning and evening shifts of the general and gynecological operating room departments according to the arrangement and planning of the group manager. Each student is required to spend 51 h in the field for each internship unit, with 60% of the hours being performed in the morning shift and the remaining 40% in the evening plus night shifts. Internship students work under the direct supervision of an anesthesiologist and an expert in anesthesia. Data analysis was based on SWOT theory. SWOT analysis (or SWOT matrix) is a strategic planning and management technique used to help an individual or organization identify strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats related to business competition or project planning. It is sometimes called a situational assessment or situational analysis [18].

After selecting the time and place of the interview in advance to achieve the research goal, the participant was asked this question: What was your experience of implementing the internship model during the internship period? The next questions were asked depending on the role of the interviewer (student or teacher). For example: What were the strengths? What were the weaknesses? How did you feel at the end of the course?

With the participant’s permission, the entire interview was recorded and transcribed by the researcher after it was completed. The interview transcript was read several times by the researcher and the codes were extracted. The codes were placed in four categories based on their position: strengths, weaknesses, challenges, and opportunities based on the Graneheim & Lundman approach [19]. Coding was done manually.

To select students, both high- and low-scoring students were interviewed to prevent premature data saturation. After interviewing the instructors, head nurse, staff nurse and 3 students, data saturation was performed and no new codes were added to the code set. This concludes the interview.

Content analysis was conducted by the researcher, who had 12 years of nursing experience and 6 years of university faculty experience. In this study, interviews were conducted lasting between 25 and 55 min. The researcher followed the inclusion criteria during sample selection. The researcher was in contact with the data for a year and collected as well as analyzed them to ensure their accuracy and acceptability. The accuracy of the data was confirmed by the participants, experts, and opinion leaders. Also, the recorded interviews were coded by two researchers, with their reliability confirmed through an agreement of more than nine tenths. The validity of the interviews was ensured by restating and summarizing the implicit concepts and asking participants to confirm their accuracy. To achieve consistency, the procedure was explained in detail so that others could follow the research steps. In addition, the transferability of the information was improved by describing the demographic characteristics of the participants to allow the audience to choose how to implement the results. To ensure the reliability of the coding, coding was performed by another qualitative researcher, and its reliability was confirmed with 90% similarity.

Challenges, opportunities, and strengths and weaknesses of the internship educational model in the master’s degree internship in the field of anesthesia

External factor

Challenges

Most participants stated that the way the group manager monitors the proper implementation of internships and its management by the group manager will be one of the challenging points of the internship; in implementing this, there was insufficient confidence from doctors to students, which requires strong educational support during the internship period and theory to reduce it. Also, lack of insurance coverage for students for the responsibility assigned to them remains a challenge. Another challenging issue is the more difficult application of this plan in non-teaching hospitals.

Participant No. 1:

“Lack of internship insurance for anesthesia students is a problem that challenges their work responsibility.”

Participant No. 5:

“To prepare a student for an internship, the student must be prepared from the first semester, meaning that the quality of teaching in basic courses must be strong enough for the student to acquire sufficient skills and knowledge.”

Opportunities

Most participants stated that the implementation of the internship plan provides an opportunity for greater interaction between the university and the hospital, and eliminates the shortage of anesthesiologists in the operating room. It will also solve the problem of the shortage of instructors in the training groups. In addition, it provides an educational opportunity for the operating room personnel, while faculty members can take care of their non-teaching duties. On the other hand, the implementation of this plan enhance the financial income of the operating room manager and its personnel, and in turn provides a financial opportunity for the student and provides a cheaper labor force for the hospital.

Participant No. 2:

“This model is very good since we both do our internship and gain financial independence.”

Participant No. 4:

“When the hospital fills out its staffing chart with us, the workforce costs for the hospital are definitely lower.”

Intrnal factor

Strengths

Most participants stated that the implementation of the internship plan improved the students’ interaction with staff and patients, leading to a reduction in their anxiety, enhanced self-confidence, independence, and responsibility. The implementation of this plan also provided them with new experiences that improved their critical thinking and led to better clinical decision-making. According to the participants’ experiences, students who participated in this plan socialized faster, acquired time management skills, and completed the professionalization process more quickly. In addition, the implementation of this plan made the staff feel more responsible towards the students, which resulted in an increase in the students’ sense of security in the clinical environment, helping them to feel a sense of belonging to the surgical team.

Participant No. 6:

“It is quite clear that students who complete their apprenticeship with internships are more responsible.”

Participant No. 5:

“Students become skilled very quickly with the internship plan, and we see their skill-building process quickly.”

Weaknesses

Participants stated that the implementation of the internship plan lowers the students’ principled performance. This is because they are more involved in doing routine tasks and distance themselves from principled performance. In implementing this plan, students are used as a tool, which results in an increased sense of fatigue in students. Some participants stated that in some cases, anesthesiologists may not be able to answer students’ theoretical questions, leading to further weakening of students’ theoretical knowledge.

Participant No. 3:

“When we do our internship, because we are more involved in routine tasks and learn routines from the staff, we distance ourselves from scientific and principled performance.”

Participant No. 2:

“As we do everything ourselves, we are much more tired.”

Discussion

External factor

Challenges of the internship model

The internship is a program for shift of nursing students to the clinical setting [20].Clinical internship refers to a period of training that students must complete before becoming a qualified professional [21]. Internship is a strategic program to support nursing students in clinical setting, with the objective of facilitating role change in hospitals [20].Meanwhile, we also know that anesthesia is an integral part of a wide range of clinical practices. However, there is a global challenge in the anesthesia workforce due to several factors [22]. Moving into graduate inevitably brings greater responsibilities and expectations, often leading to a highly stressful environment [23].The clinical internship environment is riskier than theoretical learning (working in a skills lab) because students must face real practice rather than simulation where minor carelessness can lead to errors [24]. Since medical students have a major role and responsibility in patient care during their internship [25] their internship period presents several challenges. Understanding the challenges that students face in clinical practice can help overcome the barriers that lead to the development of competent and confident anesthesia professionals [26].

In the present study, the way the group manager monitors the proper implementation of internships and its management by the group manager was one of the challenging points of the internship. In this regard, studies have shown that the use of several monitoring methods such as logbooks, portfolios, ASCII tests, and interviews can improve the validity of the assessment [27]. Another challenge in implementing this educational model was lack of physicians’ sufficient confidence in students that they could be granted full access to patient care, including taking a history and physical examination, participating in IV placement, and managing the airway through simulated methods before the internship period, so that their skills could be improved and physicians’ trust in students increased [28].

Another challenge in this educational model in the present study was lack of insurance coverage for students for the responsibility assigned to them. Insuring students is one of the methods of risk management in higher education [29] and reduces student stress [30]. Furthermore, since these students do not provide care to patients independently, they are unfamiliar with the various practices in hospitals and find them very challenging. As a result, some mistakes inevitably occur during their clinical training, which may lead to adverse events or near misses, which endanger patient safety [31] thus necessitating liability insurance for them. On the other hand, the transition from the role of student to anesthesiologist as well as the transition to a new job can be very difficult because of the uncertainty in the new environment combined with the lack of social support [32]. Contingent upon the circumstances, internships may also experience low availability of resources, limited supervision and feedback [33, 34]. Instead, students are often utilized for repetitive and monotonous tasks such as monitoring vital signs, collecting samples, and similar duties, which can, in turn, lead to dissatisfaction among them [34].

In addition, another challenge of this educational model is the more difficult implementation in non-teaching hospitals. Although some studies have shown that the provision of medical and care services in non-teaching hospitals is no different from teaching hospitals and provides training for interns in a non-traditional environment [35] they do not have an educational mission and to become educational they need revised management structure [36]. Contrary to the results of the present study, midwifery students in non-teaching hospitals receive closer supervision, better relationships, and more opportunities in a healthier environment for learning [37].

Opportunities of the internship model

In the present study, greater university-hospital interaction was one of the opportunities of this educational model. Other studies have also shown that a practical university partnership can bridge the gap between teaching and practice in an innovative way [38].

In addition, the presence of students in the clinic as an internship reduced the shortage of human resources. Studies have shown that the constant presence of students can compensate for the shortage of personnel and, as a result, lower financial costs for medical centers [39] Further, the use of experienced personnel can solve the shortage of educational instructors in the clinic, since finding experienced instructors to train students is always a challenge for nursing schools [40].

Another opportunity of this educational model is to create opportunities for operating room personnel to update their knowledge. Studies have shown that training experienced operating room nurses is difficult and time-consuming [41] so, employing operating room personnel in this educational model can be helpful in training expert operating room nurses. Another opportunity of the internship model was to generate income for operating room personnel because the excess sum above the hourly shift fee was paid to them as tuition. Most employees are dissatisfied with their income and are looking for a job with a higher income [42] which can increase both their job and financial satisfaction.

Also, through an internship, students gain firsthand insight into the workings of a hospital—understanding patient care, crisis management, and, most importantly, developing the ability to think clearly and work efficiently in an environment that is both demanding and highly effective [43].

Furthermore, it had a financial benefit for students since in some surgical centers, students were paid a fee for their greater cooperation, and ultimately it had a financial benefit for the training center because they had used cheaper labor, as also proven in other studies [39]. Having a financial incentive makes these students work harder and guides students towards student job [44].

Internal factors

Strengths of the internship Model

Various investigations around the world have indicated that clinical environments do not have suitable conditions for clinical education from the students’ viewpoints [20].whereas one of the strengths of implementing the internship plan is improving student interaction with staff and patients, which led to a decline in student anxiety, increased self-confidence, independence, and responsibility. Studies have shown that the clinical environment is a source of fear, anxiety, and violence for students [45]. Furthermore, researchers state that achieving an acceptable level of self-confidence, independence, and decision-making power in clinical skills with in-field training and the presence of a trainer (directly and on some days indirectly) is practically not possible [46] and the use of this educational model has overcome this weakness.

Also, the implementation of this plan has led to new experiences in them that would improve their critical thinking and lead to better clinical decision-making. Ghaffari et al.‘s research showed that internships will provide students with the opportunity to gain new experiences in various situations and will require students to think more deeply and critically as well as solve problems with the help of learned knowledge, the experiences of other nurses working in the department, whereby they will acquire new knowledge and skills, and will increase clinical competence and reduce clinical errors of students [47].

According to the experiences of the participants, the students who participated in this project socialized faster; internships are the initial experiences of socialization, and the more the student is led to accept more responsibility and independence, the faster this process occurs [48]. The internship program is crucial for enhancing the job performance of newly graduated nurses and shaping their perceptions of the organizations in which they are employed [49]. In addition, due to the recognition and response to the patient’s needs, patient-centered care skills are improved [50].

Another strength of this educational model was the improvement of time management skills in students. Time management is one of the essential skills of the internship period, which has become the norm due to its lack of space in student education along with procrastination [51].

Another strength of this educational model was the faster professionalization of students. The results of local studies indicate that nursing students do not have the necessary skills when they graduate and do not feel a professional identity as a nurse [46]. In the internship model, students find the opportunity to build their professional identity [52].

Also, in implementing this plan, staff felt more responsible for the student, which resulted in an increase in the student’s sense of security in the clinical environment, which resulted in a sense of belonging to the surgical team. Lack of this sense and spontaneity for learning is one of the challenges of clinical education that this educational model helped to improve [26]. the support and acceptance of nursing students by nurses has a very important role in shaping their professional identity. It seems that the clinical nurses recognize the internship students as their own and interact with them like fellow nurses [20].

Weaknesses of the internship model

Next, one of the weaknesses raised in implementing the internship model was the reduction of principled functioning. This is because they were more involved in doing routine work and distanced themselves from principled functioning. Studies have shown that after graduation, nurses also reduce their principled functioning and become routine workers [53].

Another weakness of the internship model is the use of students as tools, which results in increased fatigue among students. Studies show that currently, internships are far from the professional responsibilities of the department. Despite the clear philosophy and program of the internship for students to work with the internship department to become independent from instructors, students are not accepted by the department staff; And despite assigning some of the initial tasks to students, they ignore their staff, so students do not achieve the necessary competence in performing specialized and professional tasks ([8].

Also, another weakness of the internship model was that anesthesia experts were unable to answer some of the students’ questions, so students asked their questions from the attending anesthesiologist to overcome such a weakness. This process caused incomplete education, confusion [40, 41] and reduced student self-confidence [42].

Comparison with previous studies

The present study examined in detail the strengths, weaknesses, challenges and opportunities of the internship educational model in anesthesia undergraduate interns, which is unique in its kind because previous studies have mainly been done on nurses or have dealt with the challenges or benefits of this educational model in a one-dimensional way. By applying interventions to overcome its weaknesses and challenges, it is possible to benefit more from its application.

Limitation and recommendations for improving the internship model

As a limitation of the present study, this educational model was implemented in a Type 3 university that did not have an anesthesia resident or an anesthesia graduate student, which limits the generalizability of the results to other universities. It is recommended that this educational model be replicated in Type 1 and 2 universities and universities with postgraduate education in anesthesia and the results compared with the results of the present study. The participation of students in the study for interviews could have influenced their responses in the interviews, therefore the interviewee was selected from the research team that did not have educational activities with students.

Conclusion

Overall, it is concluded that the implementation of the internship model significantly reduces the problem of human resources in medical centers and, on the other hand, strengthens the clinical competence of students and leads them to patient-centered care.

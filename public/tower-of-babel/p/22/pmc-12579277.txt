Letter to the Editor: Integrating Machine Learning Into Stroke Risk Stratification in AF—Authors' Reply
Authors: Soundous M'Rabet, Yannick Béjot, Charles Guenancia
Published: 2025

We thank Dr. Xu and colleagues for their thoughtful and constructive comments on our recent study examining the association between atrial cardiomyopathy (AtCM) markers and large vessel occlusion (LVO) in atrial fibrillation using combined brain–heart CT imaging [1]. We appreciate their recognition of the potential of our dataset to provide a robust foundation for predictive modeling using modern machine learning (ML) approaches.

We fully agree that ML has considerable potential to enhance cardioembolic risk stratification in this population. In fact, we have already planned to apply ML–based predictive modeling to our existing monocentric dataset acquired from the initial combined brain–heart CT examination. This work will aim to refine early risk stratification for cardioembolic stroke directly from baseline imaging and clinical variables, including AtCM markers (French National Health Data Hub registration number 23294219).

In addition, within the RHU TALENT program (French National Health Data Hub registration number 16685025), a large multicenter basic and clinical research initiative in collaboration with the University Hospital of Bordeaux and IHU Lyric, we are developing advanced predictive models using both ML and deep learning. These models will be designed to identify the risk of cardioembolic ischemic stroke—including in patients in sinus rhythm—by integrating imaging, clinical, and biological features of AtCM. The primary objective of this study is to improve the prediction and prevention of cardioembolic (and especially LVO) stroke, independently of cardiac rhythm status, so as to allow earlier and more personalized preventive strategies.

Furthermore, we intend to release our fully anonymized combined brain‐heart CT datasets, in conjunction with the relevant annotated data, to the scientific community via a designated data‐sharing platform. This initiative follows a precedent set in the context of cardiac MRI in another setting [2]. This initiative aims to foster scientific collaboration and promote the development of multicenter studies using this combined acute stroke imaging protocol, which several teams have demonstrated adds substantial value to the early diagnosis and management of ischemic stroke [3, 4, 5, 6, 7].

Once again, we thank Dr. Xu and colleagues for their insightful remarks, which are fully aligned with our current and future research directions. We share their view that integrating artificial intelligence into stroke medicine, together with open and collaborative science, will substantially advance precision prevention and improve patient outcomes.

Density Functional Theory Study of the Electronic, Structural, and Optical Properties of ZrS2 and ZrSeS: Showcasing a Thorough Analysis Using DFT and DFT+U
Authors: Teshome Gerbaba Edossa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The electronic, structural, and optical properties of layered transition metal dichalcogenides, ZrS2 and ZrSeS, with trigonal structures, were comprehensively investigated using density functional theory (DFT). Calculations employed various approximations, including LDA, GGA, PBE+U, and PBE0, within the Quantum ESPRESSO framework. Rigorous convergence tests were conducted to ensure computational accuracy for both materials. The calculated equilibrium lattice constants demonstrate good agreement with existing experimental data, validating the chosen computational methods. Analysis of electronic properties revealed that LDA and GGA significantly underestimate the band gap values; in contrast, PBE+U and PBE0 approaches yielded values consistent with experimental results. Furthermore, the optical properties, including the absorption spectrum, complex dielectric function, refractive index, and static and dynamic polarizability, were thoroughly studied. To ensure accuracy, a scissor-operator correction was applied to the optical spectra to align with corrected electronic transitions. Key energy levels and intensities for significant transitions were identified, and the notable anisotropic behavior of these materials was characterized. By positioning these findings within the most recent 2025 developments in solid-state systems, this study provides a high-fidelity benchmark for the optoelectronic and polarizable response of Zr-based TMDs.

Introduction

Zirconium chalcogenides are a fascinating class of compounds that contain zirconium and chalcogen elements like sulfur, selenium, or tellurium. These materials exhibit intriguing properties and find applications in diverse fields including electronics, catalysis, and materials science. , They have a layered structure, similar to graphene, which gives them a wide range of interesting electronic, optical, and mechanical properties. , This makes them promising candidates for various technological applications, including next-generation electronic devices, sensors, and energy storage systems. −

Within this group, zirconium dichalcogenides, such as ZrS2 and ZrSe2, are particularly noteworthy. They consist of zirconium atoms sandwiched between two layers of chalcogen atoms. Their unique properties, including superconductivity and charge density waves, have made them a focal point of research for use in optoelectronics, catalysis, and energy storage. − Recent electronic and optical analyses of closely related solid-state systems further emphasize the importance of these materials in contemporary materials science. Moreover, the integration of these chalcogenides into advanced functional architectures continues to drive the development of high-performance composite communications and electronic devices.

In the realm of layered transition metal dichalcogenides (TMDs), ZrS2 and the mixed chalcogenide ZrSeS have emerged as promising candidates for photocatalytic applications, particularly in solar-driven water splitting. Both materials crystallize in a layered 1T-trigonal structure with the P3m̅1 space group, analogous to cadmium iodide (CdI2), where each zirconium atom is octahedrally coordinated by six chalcogen atoms. The layers are stacked via weak van der Waals interactions, enabling facile exfoliation into thin flakes and offering opportunities for surface functionalization. Although strong hydrogen bonding interactions such as N–H···S or O–H···S are not dominant in the bulk systems, − the exposed surfaces can effectively interact with hydrogen species, which is crucial for enhancing the hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) and oxygen evolution reaction (OER). A key advantage of these materials lies in their tunable band gaps, and their ability to form heterostructures that promote efficient Z-scheme water splitting. − Additionally, owing to the larger atomic radius of selenium relative to sulfur, ZrSeS possesses an expanded lattice compared to ZrS2. ,

Regarding their stability, ZrS2 is thermodynamically stable under ambient conditions. However, it is highly susceptible to oxidation in the presence of impurities like air and moisture, which can lead to the formation of the more stable zirconium oxide (ZrO2). Furthermore, under high pressure, ZrS2 undergoes a series of structural phase transitions, transforming from its trigonal phase to monoclinic, orthorhombic, and ultimately a tetragonal structure, which can induce a transition to a metallic state. While less research is available on the specific stability of ZrSeS, its mixed-chalcogen nature may introduce different stability characteristics due to the weaker bonding with the larger selenium atom.

This paper focuses on two specific zirconium dichalcogenides: zirconium disulfide (ZrS2) and zirconium selenium sulfide (ZrSeS). ZrS2 is well-known for its numerous applications, including its use as a solid lubricant, as an electrode material in lithium-ion batteries, and in semiconductor and optoelectronic devices. , The related ternary compound ZrSeS is also being explored for its potentially tailored properties. Therefore, this study investigates the electronic, structural, and optical properties of both ZrS2 and ZrSeS using density functional theory.

Computational Methods

Solving many-body quantum-mechanical problems remains a significant challenge, particularly for systems involving interactions among three or more electrons, where analytical solutions are generally not tractable. , Density functional theory (DFT) provides an efficient and reliable framework for addressing such complexities and has become a standard approach for investigating the structural, electronic, and optical properties of condensed-matter systems. − In the present work, all first-principles calculations were performed using the Quantum ESPRESSO (QE) software package, which is based on DFT and the plane-wave pseudopotential formalism. ,

Within the Kohn–Sham formulation of DFT, the exchange–correlation (XC) potential represents the most critical approximation. To assess its influence on the predicted properties, several XC functionals were employed, including the Local Density Approximation (LDA), the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE) generalized gradient approximation, the PBE+U approach, and the hybrid PBE0 functional. The on-site Coulomb interaction parameter U for the localized Zr 4d orbitals was determined using a one-shot linear-response procedure, following the standard Cococcioni–de Gironcoli formalism, yielding values of U = 3.510 eV for ZrS2 and U = 3.084 eV for ZrSeS. The variation in U accounts for the different screening environments provided by the S and Se ligands, respectively. ,,,− ,,,−

The interaction between core and valence electrons was treated using pseudopotentials. For the LDA and PBE calculations, Projector Augmented-Wave (PAW) pseudopotentials from the GBRV pseudopotential library were employed, whereas norm-conserving pseudopotentials were used for the PBE0 calculations. Core electrons were kept frozen during the calculations, while valence electrons were treated explicitly. The valence electronic configurations considered were [Kr] 4d2 5s2 for Zr, [Ne] 3s2 3p4 for S, and [Ar] 3d10 4s2 4p4 for Se.

To ensure numerical accuracy and reliability, systematic convergence tests were carried out with respect to both the plane-wave kinetic energy cutoff and k-point sampling of the Brillouin zone. The electronic wave functions were expanded in a plane-wave basis set, and Brillouin-zone integrations were performed using the Monkhorst–Pack scheme. Total energies were considered converged when changes were less than 0.01 eV. For trigonal ZrS2, convergence was achieved using a 6 × 6 × 6 k-point mesh for LDA and 7 × 7 × 7 meshes for PBE and PBE0. Similar convergence behavior was observed for trigonal ZrSeS, with 6 × 6 × 6 meshes for LDA and 7 × 7 × 7 meshes for PBE and PBE0.

While layered transition metal dichalcogenides are traditionally sensitive to van der Waals (vdW) dispersion forces, recent studies have shown that high-level functionals like PBE0 and PBE+U can yield structural parameters that closely mimic experimental data by improving the description of electronic correlation. In our work, the calculated lattice constants and bulk moduli were verified against experimental benchmarks to ensure that the structural foundation for our optical analysis is robust.

The kinetic energy cutoff required for total-energy convergence was found to be 35 Ry for both ZrS2 and ZrSeS across the LDA, PBE, and PBE0 functionals. All total energies were obtained natively in Rydberg (Ry), as implemented in Quantum ESPRESSO. Structural optimizations were performed by scanning lattice parameters expressed in atomic units (Bohr), followed by fitting the resulting energy–volume data to the third-order Birch–Murnaghan equation of state. The extracted equilibrium lattice parameters were subsequently converted to Angström Å using the standard conversion factor (1 Bohr = 0.529177 Å). Where required for physical interpretation and comparison, energy values were converted using 1 Ry = 13.6057 eV.

For the evaluation of optical properties, the frequency-dependent dielectric function was calculated using a dense Γ-centered 12 × 12 × 4 k-point mesh, which accounts for the structural anisotropy of the layered trigonal phases. To ensure a converged spectral response up to 30 eV, 100 unoccupied bands were included, with a Gaussian broadening of 0.1 eV applied to simulate experimental resolution. Furthermore, a scissor correction was implemented to align the theoretical absorption onsets with our PBE0 and experimental benchmarks (Table ), thereby correcting the systematic band gap underestimation of the LDA/PBE functionals. The physical mechanism of the resulting optical shifts was further analyzed through the projected density of states (PDOS), confirming that the narrowing of the gap upon Se substitution is driven by the upward shift of the valence band maximum, which is dominated by chalcogen-p states.

Results and Discussion

Structural Properties of Trigonal ZrS2 and ZrSeS

Understanding the crystal structure of a material is fundamental to interpreting its mechanical, electronic, and optical properties. Both ZrS2 , and the mixed-chalcogenide ZrSeS are semiconducting compounds that crystallize in a layered trigonal structure, which is commonly described within the hexagonal setting and is characterized by a 3-fold rotational symmetry axis.

In the trigonal crystal system, the lattice is described by three equivalent in-plane axes separated by 120° and a fourth axis perpendicular to this plane. ZrS2 crystallizes in the 1T-type structure with space group P3m̅1, while ZrSeS adopts a closely related trigonal symmetry. Experimental lattice parameters reported in the literature for ZrS2 are a ≈ 3.660–3.709 Å and c ≈ 5.990–6.070 Å, whereas ZrSeS exhibits slightly larger lattice constants, with a ≈ 3.709–3.713 Å and c ≈ 6.070–6.076 Å, ,,,− , reflecting the larger atomic radius of selenium. The optimized crystal structures of ZrS2 and ZrSeS are illustrated in Figures a and b, respectively.

Crystal structure of a) ZrS2, b) ZrSeS (plotted by XCrySDen).

To determine the equilibrium structural parameters of trigonal ZrS2 and ZrSeS, first-principles calculations were carried out using the plane-wave self-consistent field (PWSCF) method within density functional theory. In ZrS2, zirconium atoms occupy the (0, 0, 0) Wyckoff position, while sulfur atoms are located at (2/3, 1/3, z) and (1/3, 2/3, 1 – z). For the mixed-chalcogenide ZrSeS, the Zr atoms remain at (0, 0, 0), with sulfur and selenium occupying the corresponding chalcogen sites, preserving the layered X-Zr-X stacking along the c-axis.

Structural optimizations were performed using several exchange-correlation functionals, including LDA, PBE, PBE+U, and the hybrid PBE0 functional. For ZrS2, the calculated lattice constants are in good agreement with previous reports. As expected, LDA tends to underestimate the lattice parameters due to its overbinding nature, whereas PBE and PBE+U slightly overestimate them. The hybrid PBE0 functional yields intermediate values, closer to experimental observations.

The reduction in the band gap from ZrS2 to ZrSeS can be primarily attributed to the increased hybridization between Zr-4d and the larger Se-4p orbitals. While heavy elements like Selenium often introduce Spin–Orbit Coupling (SOC) effects that can further split the valence bands, the current study focuses on the scalar-relativistic effects. The observed narrowing is consistent with the upward shift of the valence band edge due to the lower electronegativity of Se compared to S.

Total Density of States of ZrS2 and ZrSeS

The density of states for ZrS2 and ZrSeS was computed using LDA, PBE and PBE+U, as depicted in Figure . The Density of States (DOS) aids in comprehending the distribution of state occupancy across a defined energy range. It offers insights into both unoccupied and occupied states. A peak in DOS at a specific energy level indicates available states for occupation, while no state is occupied at a DOS of zero. In the trigonal phases of ZrS2 and ZrSeS the density of states exhibits discontinuities spanning from the top of the valence band to the bottom of the conduction band, typically representing the system’s bandgap.

Total density of states of a) ZrS2 and b) ZrSeS using LDA, PBE and PBE+U.

The band gap difference between ZrS2 and ZrSeS stems from the distinct chemical and physical properties of S and Se. Larger atomic size and higher energy levels of Se orbitals (vs S) in ZrSeS lead to weaker Zr–Se bonding and altered band positions, reducing the band gap. Additionally, stronger spin–orbit coupling in heavier Se further contributes to a smaller band gap in ZrSeS compared to ZrS2.

ZrS2 and ZrSeS, while both layered transition metal dichalcogenides, offer distinct advantages stemming from their constituent chalcogen atoms. ZrS2, with its wider band gap, holds promise for applications in the ultraviolet and blue regions of the electromagnetic spectrum, potentially exhibiting higher thermal stability due to stronger bonding. Conversely, ZrSeS, characterized by its narrower band gap, presents benefits for visible and near-infrared optoelectronic devices, often displaying enhanced light absorption in these lower energy ranges. Furthermore, the mixed anion composition of ZrSeS allows for the potential tuning of its electronic and optical properties, providing a pathway to tailor materials for specific applications.

Projected Density of States for ZrS2 and ZrSeS

Figure a–f shows the projected density of states (PDOS) of trigonal ZrS2 and ZrSeS calculated using the LDA, PBE, and PBE+U functionals, with the Fermi level set to zero energy. For ZrS2 [Figure a–c], the valence-band maximum (VBM) is dominated by S 3p states with minor Zr 4d contributions, while the conduction-band minimum (CBM) is primarily composed of Zr 4d states, indicating that low-energy optical transitions are mainly of S 3p → Zr 4d character. Contributions from Zr 5s and S 3s states are negligible near the band edges.

For ZrSeS [Figure d–f], the VBM exhibits a substantial contribution from Se 4p states, which lie at higher energies than S 3p orbitals. This upward shift of the valence-band edge leads to a reduced band gap relative to ZrS2, while the CBM remains dominated by Zr 4d states. The substitution of S by Se therefore primarily affects the valence-band position without altering the fundamental nature of the conduction band.

The influence of the exchange–correlation functional is also evident. While LDA and PBE yields narrower band gaps due to enhanced state delocalization, the inclusion of the Hubbard U term shifts the Zr 4d states to higher energies, increasing the band gap magnitude. Importantly, the orbital character of the VBM and CBM remains unchanged across LDA, PBE, and PBE+U, confirming that band gap variations arise from energetic shifts rather than changes in bonding character.

These PDOS features provide a direct explanation for the optical spectra discussed in next Section. Since the absorption onset corresponds to transitions between chalcogen p states at the VBM and Zr d states at the CBM, the Se-induced upward shift of the VBM in ZrSeS results in a red-shift of the absorption edge compared to ZrS2, consistent with the calculated dielectric functions and absorption spectra.

Optical Properties of ZrS2 and ZrSeS

The frequency-dependent complex dielectric function, ε(ω)=ε1(ω)+iε2(ω) serves as the fundamental link between the electronic band structure and the optical response of ZrS2 and ZrSeS. The accurate determination of these optical constants is essential for benchmarking our theoretical predictions against contemporary electronic and optical analyses of related solid-state systems. By examining the peaks in the imaginary part, ε2(ω), we can directly correlate spectral features with specific interband transitions. This methodology aligns with recent research on functional layered materials, where such dielectric interpretations are vital for assessing the suitability of TMDs for high-efficiency optoelectronic applications. ,

Dielectric Function for ZrS2 and ZrSeS

The complex dielectric function, ε­(ω) = ε1(ω) + i ε2(ω), was investigated to evaluate the interaction of ZrS2 and ZrSeS with incident electromagnetic radiation. Figure a–b displays the real and imaginary components of the dielectric function calculated using the LDA, PBE, and PBE+U exchange–correlation functionals, including their anisotropic contributions ε1x , ε1z , ε2x , and ε2z . To address the systematic underestimation of the fundamental band gap in standard DFT, a scissor operator was applied to rigidly shift the conduction bands, thereby aligning the calculated absorption onsets with experimental benchmarks and our PBE0 results. The corresponding scissor shifts are ΔE = 0.83/0.71 eV (LDA), 0.69/0.59 eV (PBE), and 0.07/0.14 eV (PBE+U) for ZrS2 and ZrSeS, respectively.

For ZrS2, the imaginary part of the dielectric function, ε2(ω), characterizes the onset of interband electronic transitions. Following the application of the scissor correction, the primary absorption peaks in the x-plane (E ⊥ c) are located at 3.03 eV (LDA), 3.09 eV (PBE), and 2.71 eV (PBE+U). Along the z-plane (E ∥ c), higher-energy transitions are observed, with prominent peaks appearing in the range of 8.80–10.29 eV. These results indicate that ZrS2 exhibits a strong photon absorption response within the 2.5–10.0 eV energy window. The application of the scissor operator ensures that the calculated optical band gap is physically consistent with the experimental value of ∼1.73 eV, effectively eliminating the unphysical infrared transitions predicted by uncorrected LDA and PBE calculations.

The substitution of sulfur with selenium induces a pronounced redshift in the optical response, which can be attributed to the higher energy of the Se-4p orbitals relative to the S-3p states. For ZrSeS, the scissor-corrected ε2(ω) spectra exhibit prominent interband transition peaks in the x-plane (E ⊥ c) at 2.47 eV (LDA), 2.57 eV (PBE), and 2.34 eV (PBE+U). Along the z-plane (E ∥ c), the dominant peaks are located at 9.27 eV (LDA) and 8.94 eV (PBE+U).

From a mechanistic perspective, ZrSeS displays strong photon absorption over a broad energy range spanning 1.6–15.0 eV in the x-plane and 1.1–7.0 eV in the z-plane. The observed broadening and redshift of the absorption window toward the visible region indicate that the mixed-chalcogenide ZrSeS is a more promising candidate for optoelectronic applications than its parent compound. The real part of the dielectric function, ε1(ω), further supports this conclusion by revealing a higher static dielectric constant, ε1(0), for ZrSeS, consistent with its reduced band gap and enhanced electronic polarizability.

The real part of the dielectric function, ε1(ω), characterizes the electronic polarizability of the system and its ability to screen external electric fields. Electronic screening is a fundamental process in condensed-matter physics, governing electron–electron interactions and exciton binding energies. From Figure a and b in Mid-Energy region, ε1(ω) for both ZrS2 and ZrSeS exhibits (Dynamic Response) significant oscillations, including peaks, troughs, and sometimes crosses the zero-axis or becomes negative. The changing value of ε1(ω) across this energy range signifies the material’s dispersion. This means the speed of light (and thus the refractive index, which is related to ε1 ) varies with photon energy. When ε1(ω) is positive, the material acts as a dielectric, allowing light to propagate but slowing it down. Points where ε1(ω) crosses zero are indicates the plasma frequency (ω p ), where the material transitions from being reflective to transparent (assuming ε2(ω) is small). For semiconductors, these crossings can indicate complex dielectric behavior and might be related to collective excitations or strong transitions. For instance, if ε1(ω) crosses zero and becomes negative, it suggests a metallic-like response at that frequency. If ε1(ω) becomes negative (and ε2(ω) is small), the material behaves like a metal and is highly reflective at that specific photon energy. Light cannot propagate efficiently through the material.

Conclusions

In this study, a comprehensive investigation into the electronic, structural, and optical properties of layered transition metal dichalcogenides, ZrS2 and ZrSeS, was successfully conducted using first-principles density functional theory (DFT) calculations. Various exchange-correlation approximations, including LDA, PBE, PBE+U, and PBE0, were engaged to accurately model these materials.

The structural analyses confirmed the stability of both ZrS2 and ZrSeS in their trigonal phases, with calculated lattice constants showing excellent agreement with available experimental data. This validation underscores the reliability of the computational approaches used for lattice constants. For electronic properties, while standard LDA and GGA functionals were found to significantly underestimate the band gaps, the more advanced PBE+U and hybrid PBE0 functionals provided band gap values that are highly consistent with experimental observations, for ZrS2 and ZrSeS. The nuanced differences in the electronic band structures and band gaps between ZrS2 and ZrSeS were elucidated, largely attributed to the distinct atomic characteristics of sulfur and selenium.

Furthermore, a detailed examination of the optical properties, encompassing the complex dielectric function, absorption spectrum, refractive index, and static and dynamic polarizability, revealed the characteristic optical responses of these materials. The analysis of these properties provided insights into the electronic transitions occurring upon photon absorption and the materials’ interaction with electromagnetic radiation. The distinct optical behaviors observed for ZrS2 and ZrSeS, including their transparency ranges and absorption onsets, offer crucial information for their application potential.

Overall, this research provides valuable theoretical insights into the fundamental physical properties of ZrS2 and ZrSeS. The findings not only enhance our understanding of these promising transition metal dichalcogenides but also serve as a strong theoretical foundation for guiding experimental efforts in the design and development of next-generation electronic, optoelectronic, and energy-related devices.

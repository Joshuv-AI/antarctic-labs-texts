ABSTRACT
Data security has become one of the primary concerns, particularly for images, as conventional encryption methods such as the Vigenere cipher or ring \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathbb{Z}/256 \mathbb{Z}$$\end{document} -based methods are no longer robust against modern types of attacks. To resolve the issue, this paper introduces a novel hybrid image encryption technique that combines elliptic curve cryptography (ECC), finite field extensions integrating the AJ chaotic map for dynamic parameters generation and image-dependent key adaptation. First, the method depends on the pseudorandom selection of an elliptic curve over the field \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathbb {F}_{256}$$\end{document} designed based on an irreducible polynomial of degree 8. Second, two substitution tables are designed based on discrete logarithm and exponentiation operations to improve the impact of the confusion and diffusion. Numerous in-depth security experiments, including entropy, NPCR, UACI, correlation coefficients, and the NIST statistical test, have been performed, indicating high cryptographic robustness against known attacks. The findings affirm the performance of the proposed method in achieving an average entropy of 7.9998 bits per pixel, a correlation coefficient of adjacent pixels less than 0.001, NPCR of 99.79%, and UACI of 33.59% for the Peppers color image. In addition, our technique achieves a competitive execution time, confirming both its security and efficiency. Therefore, it is evident that the employment of ECC over a finite body along with a dynamically designed SBox is an ideal high-performance method for privacy protection, secure data storage, and trustworthy transmission of sensitive data.

Introduction

Worldwide sharing of knowledge through multimedia has flourished in the last few years, however, protecting and keeping this knowledge confidential remain major concerns for both individuals and organizations, especially in such areas as the defense sector, personal communication, and healthcare1–4. As modern infrastructures become increasingly interconnected, private data is transmitted more frequently, thereby intensifying the demand for advanced cryptographic systems capable of protecting large volumes of information against emerging threats5. Cryptographic mechanisms already underpin a broad range of applications, including secure key management, financial transactions, and authentication protocols6.

Among various multimedia data types, images present unique security challenges due to strong pixel correlation, large data volume, and their high redundancy characteristics. Previous works on image encryption techniques often suffer from limited sensitivity to initial conditions, restricted key spaces, poor performance on high-resolution images, or susceptibility to statistical and algebraic attacks. These problems become more noticeable during the processing of large-scale visual datasets or high-resolution images.

Although chaotic maps, algebraic S-boxes, and elliptic curve cryptography (ECC) have been independently explored for image encryption, existing works typically employ these components in isolation. There remains a lack of a unified framework that tightly couples chaos-driven parameter generation, finite-field algebraic substitution, and ECC-based diffusion within a single coherent architecture.

To address the identified problems, the proposed technique introduces a hybrid image-encryption scheme that combines the AJ chaotic map, a modified Vigenere-Affine transformation, and ECC over the finite field \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathbb {F}_{256}$$\end{document}. The proposed scheme operates through three sequential stages: key generation, encryption, and decryption, designed to maximize sensitivity to initial conditions, randomness, confusion/diffusion strength, and reversibility, while remaining computationally efficient for real-time platforms.

The main contributions of this paper can be summarized as follows: (1) A finite field-focused S-box generation framework, where discrete, exponential, and logarithmic vectors over \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathbb {F}_{256}$$\end{document} are not auxiliary tables but are structural primitives that together define multiplication, inversion, affine mappings, and substitution dynamics. (2) A precise algebra chaos coupling, where AJ-map outputs directly parameterize finite-field operations, such as polynomial selection, vector generation, affine coefficients, and S-box evolution, instead of just being external random seeds. (3) Elliptic-curve point permutation is combined with finite-field exponential indexing in a novel way, resulting in diffusion and substitution layers that are algebraically consistent throughout all the stages of encryption and decryption.

These components are not independent modules but are jointly designed to reinforce confusion, diffusion, and sensitivity to both the secret key and the plaintext image. The subsequent sections detail each component in a structured manner. Section 2 presents the most relevant literature review. Section 3 introduces the key concepts that form the foundation of the encryption process, including the A-J chaotic map, irreducible polynomials, and elliptic curve cryptography over a finite field, which constitute the core of the proposed encryption framework. Section 4 provides a detailed description of the proposed algorithm. Section 5 presents the analysis of the evaluated metrics, including statistical and differential assessments. Section 6 compares and evaluates the effectiveness of the proposed method for image encryption. Section 7 outlines the advantages of the proposed technique, while Section 8 concludes the paper by summarizing the conclusions, discussing the limitations of the proposed method, and suggesting future research directions.

Literature review

Recent studies demonstrate a strong focus on various approaches, including chaotic maps, finite rings and fields, elliptic curves, and others.

An efficient chaotic image enciphering scheme based on a confusion-diffusion architecture is proposed in7. The image is divided into four non-overlapping regions, with a cross-interaction mapping used to exchange information between them during the scrambling process. A keystream related to the image is used in the diffusion step to mask the shuffled pixels, affecting every bit of the encrypted image. Numerous chaotic maps (1D, 2D, 4D) are utilized in the method, with fully parameterized mappings tailored to the image features. This approach introduces the cascading effect and enhances security. The goal of this method is to optimize the division of the image into four symmetric, non-overlapping chunks by restricting the image’s dimensions, its width and height, to even values only. However, an additional column or row can be inserted into the image to overcome this problem. Furthermore, Simultaneous execution of substitution and permutation transactions was not possible. Therefore, ongoing investigations must focus on overcoming these disadvantages to improve the approach’s efficiency and flexibility.

Ghulam Murtaza et al.8 proposed a novel three-phase image cryptosystem. The pseudo-random number generator is applied in the first phase for pixel diffusion, followed by the generation of highly nonlinear substitution boxes in the second phase. Finally, grayscale image conversion is performed in the last phase. The scheme achieves near-optimal entropy strengthening security and exhibits high sensitivity to input keys. Although the S-box generator shows good nonlinearity, it has not reached optimal efficiency levels and requires further enhancement and optimization. In addition, large image contexts and real-time video streaming applications require high-resolution video delivery, finding this cipher method unsuitable.

A novel image cipher method is proposed in9, which combines pixel scrambling with quantum key distribution. The security challenges of the quantum image cipher method are highlighted, particularly the periodicity of the scrambling process, which could enable attackers to restore the image and extract the secret information. Assessments of the algorithm have considered performance, measured by the complexity of the quantum circuit network, and security, evaluated through key analysis, pixel correlation, and information distribution analyses. A high NPCR rate, low pixel correlation, and uniform histogram distribution were observed in the experiments, all of which point to robust security. A significant drawback is the need for numerous keys equal to the number of pixels for image encryption through the generation of keys based on the quantum key distribution protocol. In addition, the operation of locating pixels requires the creation of numerous key characters; thus, the total key requirement is considerably increased.

A cipher scheme and SBox generator based on elliptic curves (ECC) and a Möbius transformation were proposed in10, offering fast generation times and high nonlinearity. Security analysis demonstrates strong entropy, UACI, and NPCR values, confirming its resilience to cryptographic attacks. This approach achieves competitive or superior performance among the existing SBox generators in terms of security. However, its main limitations lie in the time-consuming SBox generation process and the relatively low SBox dimension, which may restrict its performance.

In11, a novel SBox based on a 4D hyperchaotic fractional-order (4DFO) system was introduced by means of permutation and XOR operations. The SBox is highly resistant to distinguished cryptographic and performance attacks. The image cipher technique, which combines the RSA algorithm, a high-performance SBox, and the Arnold Cat map, is both efficient and secure against the known types of attacks. The method achieves entropy very close to the theoretical value of 8 and offers very low correlation between adjacent pixels. Nevertheless, the dependence on RSA operations makes the execution unnecessarily long, and the performance might be limited.

In12, Maity et al. have proposed a fast color image encryption scheme that integrates a Vision Transformer-based autoencoder for lossy image compression with a 6D hyperchaotic system to enhance security. A hash value derived from the original image is used as a secret key to initialize the hyperchaotic system, generating six pseudo-random sequences for scrambling and diffusion. The compressed latent image is first permuted using chaotic sequences and a random shuffle strategy, then diffused through a Trifid cipher transformation to produce the final cipher image. The method achieves high security performance, with high average entropy, near-zero correlation, strong key sensitivity and UACI, and a large key space, indicating robustness against brute-force, statistical, and differential attacks. Though this method performs well compared to the SoA methods, its major limitation is the loss of information, which restricts its applicability. The method lacks the resilience when noise is introduced into the latent encrypted representation.

To meet the rising demand for the security of sensitive image data, Aqeel M. et al.13 proposed a lightweight image encryption algorithm for resource-constrained IoT environments. The method utilizes a 5D multi-wing hyperchaotic map and invents a new pixel confusion method that repeatedly swaps non-overlapping rectangular regions. While this technique offers robust confusion effects, it also ensures that the computations are efficient. The results of the experiments with standard benchmark images reveal that the algorithm achieves high entropy, low correlation, flat histograms, and strong resistance to differential attacks, thereby confirming the algorithm’s effectiveness as a tool for securely encoding image data with minimal overhead, which is suitable for IoT applications. Nevertheless, the algorithm’s computational performance and security effectiveness have not been thoroughly tested on high-resolution or color images, which are commonly used in modern IoT surveillance and monitoring systems.

Kartikey Pandey et al.14 introduced a new image encryption method that integrates three main stages: confusion-diffusion, encryption, and optimization, to secure images more effectively in digital communication systems. To enhance randomness and diffusion, a Lorenz chaotic map is utilized in the confusion phase by a new hybrid system that merges the benefits of both Logistic and Piecewise Linear maps. In the encryption phase, Elliptic Curve Cryptography (ECC) is utilized for high security with a small key size. The optimization phase introduces a Genetic Algorithm to not only improve the cryptographic quality but also the performance of the encrypted image. The security metrics and computational efficiency are what make the method successful, according to the experimental results; thus, it is highly viable for secure image transmission applications. Nevertheless, scalability might become a concern when dealing with ultra-large images or real-time high-resolution streaming. Moreover, the computational overhead caused by the Genetic Algorithm in the optimization phase may lead to an increase in the encryption time, thereby making this method less appropriate for time-sensitive applications. An image encryption framework (ImHALC) was presented in15 to improve data security by giving a texture-aware feature to keystream generation. Besides, the security part involved three different techniques: compressive sensing, chaotic scrambling, and diffusion. The method first obtains local binary patterns (LBP) from the input image and then calculates a SHA-256 hash, which is used to set a new 3D chaotic system (3D-CCLSM) for keystream generation. These keystreams are used to design measurement matrices for two-dimensional compressive sensing, which is the main idea of the work: to perform data compression and encryption simultaneously. Next, the confused characters are used to indicate the pixels to be scrambled. After that, a diffusion phase leads to high sensitivity and slight alterations in the plain image, resulting in a drastically different ciphertext. Although the technique has been found to provide excellent security and visual imperceptibility, it takes a considerable amount of time to carry out due to complex key synchronization and preprocessing stages, among which are texture feature extraction and grayscale transformation. Besides, the sequential nature of the embedding and encryption stages makes it impossible to have real-time applications.

A block-based chaotic image encryption system combining both confusion and diffusion techniques was introduced in16. In this approach, the image is split into multiple non-overlapping blocks, with each block being independently processed by the unique measurement matrices, which are obtained from chaotic maps and Hadamard transforms. The motivation of this arrangement is to lower the correlation between inter-blocks and to speed up the computations. A nonlinear bifurcation diffusion operation, organized along a full binary tree, enables effective cross-pixel interactions, thus resulting in a cascading diffusion effect similar to cross-regional scrambling. Such a structure increases key sensitivity and offers a considerable level of security against statistical and differential attacks. However, the dependence on block segmentation limits the size of images to even values, and the incapability of permutation and substitution to be done simultaneously in a single iteration results in a slight drawback in terms of flexibility and the possibility of real-time application.

An improved confusion-diffusion structure chaotic image encryption scheme was presented in17. The method utilizes two optimized chaotic measurement matrices generated from a 4D hyper-chaotic system to compress three plaintext images. Then, the compressed data are scrambled using a dual-layer Josephus process, which enables cross-region interaction and thus increases the effectiveness in pixel correlation reduction. In diffusion, a diagonal neighborhood operation is used so that each neighbor influences the encrypted bit from the original diagonal neighborhood; thus, the sensitivity to minor changes in the input is increased. This architecture maintains the cascading characteristics of chaotic systems, which is one of the reasons for its high resistance to differential and statistical attacks. Additionally, the scheme divides the image into regular, non-overlapping blocks of even dimensions for the convenience of compression and transformation. However, the strictly sequential nature of the substitution and permutation steps does not permit parallelization; therefore, there is a potential for enhancement by employing a hybrid or concurrent mode in the future.

Mohsen Zarei et al.18 have proposed an encryption scheme that employs dynamic S-boxes generated through elliptic curve cryptography in conjunction with a fuzzy logic system optimized using the Wild Horse Optimization (WHO) algorithm. A 256 × 256 hash-based key is utilized to construct dynamic S-boxes, while the fuzzy logic component generates random sequences in the range of 0-255 to support pixel-level permutation, substitution, XOR, and shift register operations. The WHO-optimized fuzzy rules facilitate efficient, pixel-specific encryption without requiring full elliptic curve encoding. Overall, the scheme represents a hybrid ECC-fuzzy logic-WHO framework, in which the principal components and operational mechanisms are clearly defined. However, a notable limitation of the proposed scheme is that it does not address the encryption of color images. The design and evaluation are restricted to grayscale images, and the scheme does not consider the additional complexity introduced by multiple color channels. Consequently, its applicability to real-world multimedia scenarios involving color images remains unexamined and would require further extension and validation.

Shamsa Kanwal et al.19 have proposed an effective approach for securing sensitive data through the development of tamper-resistant cryptosystems and robust authentication mechanisms. By leveraging elliptic curve cryptography (ECC) for secret key generation and employing chaotic maps to obfuscate and modify plain image pixel values, the method enhances overall system security through the integration of hybrid cryptographic techniques. While the scheme demonstrates resistance to a wide range of cryptographic attacks, it requires substantial memory resources, which in turn increases the overall computational cost. Daurat Sinaga et al.20 put forward a sophisticated image encryption strategy that blends Elliptic Curve Cryptography (ECC) with a three-dimensional Lorenz chaotic system in an endeavor to enhance security and computational efficiency simultaneously. The method encompasses pixel permutation, encryption based on ECC, and a diffusion process which is carried out by pseudo-random sequences coming from the 3D Lorenz chaotic system. Nevertheless, the statistical attack analysis that was provided in the paper is very limited, and the method is only for single-channel (grayscale) images hence it cannot be directly applied to multi-channel color images.

Sarma et al.21 have proposed the e-SAFE scheme, a Ciphertext-Policy Attribute-Based Encryption (CP-ABE), which integrates fine-grained access control, attribute convergence, privileged access, user revocation, and outsourcing, while being specifically designed for resource-constrained medical devices through the use of a lightweight, constant-size decryption key. However, the experimental evaluation is conducted on a simulated setup using a single laptop environment rather than a real-world cloud-fog-IoT healthcare infrastructure. Consequently, the reported performance results may not fully capture network latency, large-scale user behavior, or heterogeneous device constraints present in real deployments.

Sarma et al.22 have proposed a Secure Access Control Scheme for Multi-Authority IoT Systems (SAMIT), which is a multi-authority CP-ABE scheme that supports efficient attribute updates and is specifically designed for embedded systems. The scheme exploits fog computing to offload computationally intensive operations, thereby substantially reducing the processing burden on resource-constrained IoT devices. Security analysis and performance evaluations indicate that SAMIT provides satisfactory security guarantees and is well suited for cyber–physical systems (CPS) with limited device resources. However, the scheme does not incorporate post-quantum security and lacks mechanisms to verify the correctness of computations performed by fog nodes.

Nugroho et al.23 have proposed an image encryption scheme combining a property-H-based FeistelX network, extended 3-bit DNA coding, and dual 2D hyperchaotic system to achieve strong confusion, diffusion, and exact reversibility. Although the method demonstrates excellent security performance, its multi-stage structure and reliance on dual hyperchaotic systems increase computational complexity, which may limit suitability for real-time or resource-constrained applications.

Kumar et al.24 have proposed a hybrid image enciphering method that enhances the RC4 stream cipher by integrating secure key generation via PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256, modified keystream generation using a Multiply-With-Carry PRNG, nonlinear substitution with the PRESENT S-box, and spatial diffusion through Arnold’s Cat Map. Although the scheme effectively mitigates RC4’s keystream bias and achieves near-ideal entropy, NPCR, and UACI values, its multi-phase design and reliance on iterative chaotic scrambling increase computational overhead and limit scalability for real-time or resource-constrained applications.

Alexan et al.25 have proposed a multiple image enciphering (MIE) method that leverages a chaos-based Hill cipher, RC5 in counter mode, singular value decomposition (SVD), hyperchaotic systems, and dynamically generated S-boxes to secure satellite imagery and resist traffic analysis attacks. Despite its strong security performance and large key space, the scheme relies on numerically solved continuous hyperchaotic systems and multiple sequential encryption stages, which increase computational complexity and may limit efficiency in lightweight or resource-constrained real-time implementations.

Alshehri26 has proposed a lossless image encryption scheme that combines a rule-adaptive Langton’s Ant cellular automaton with a four-dimensional chaotic system to achieve strong permutation and multi-level decimal and symbolic diffusion with guaranteed bijectivity. While the scheme demonstrates near-ideal statistical and differential security performance and linear-time complexity, its reliance on numerically integrated continuous chaotic systems and multi-stage symbolic processing may introduce implementation sensitivity and higher constant-time overhead compared to lightweight discrete ciphers, particularly in constrained or hardware-limited environments.

Tabti et al.27 have proposed a novel hybrid image ciphering model that integrates chaotic theory with genetic algorithms applied at the RNA codon level, combined with DNA encoding at the nucleotide level. The scheme operates through four main phases: binary transformation and DNA encoding of the image, RNA-based genetic operations guided by chaos-driven fitness functions, population-based discrimination and segmentation, and an improved three-round Feistel structure introducing chaotic coupling and pseudorandom permutation between image blocks. An additional entropy-based genetic optimization step further enhances encryption robustness. Experimental validation on MRI and X-ray images demonstrates high security performance, strong resistance against statistical and differential attacks, and improved robustness compared to existing approaches. Although the scheme achieves low pixel correlation, high entropy, and strong resistance to differential and statistical attacks, its multi-layer encoding process and dependence on several chaotic vectors and lookup tables increase algorithmic complexity and may limit computational efficiency for resource-constrained or real-time systems.

El Bourakkadi et al.28 have proposed a hybrid image encryption scheme that combines enhanced Vigenère and affine ciphers with skew-tent and logistic chaotic maps to generate large dynamic S-boxes, pseudorandom tables, and chained diffusion for strong confusion and avalanche properties. Although the method achieves near-ideal entropy, NPCR, and UACI values, its reliance on chaos-generated large substitution tables and multi-stage vectorized processing increases computational overhead and may introduce sensitivity to chaotic parameter selection, limiting efficiency and robustness in lightweight or real-time applications.

From the above review, it is evident that while recent approaches achieve strong statistical performance, many suffer from high computational overhead, limited scalability, static key scheduling, or insufficient adaptability to plaintext characteristics. These limitations motivate the design of a hybrid, dynamically parameterized ECC-finite-field encryption framework capable of ensuring strong confusion-diffusion properties with linear computational complexity.

Choice of the irreducible polynomial

An irreducible polynomial P(x) is defined as a polynomial that admits no nontrivial factorization over a given field or ring; that is, it cannot be expressed as the product of two polynomials of positive degree whose coefficients belong to the same algebraic structure. Equivalently, such a polynomial cannot be further decomposed into simpler polynomial components within the specified field or ring. When a polynomial is said to be irreducible over a field, such as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathbb {F}_2$$\end{document} or \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathbb {F}_p$$\end{document}, this indicates that its only divisors in that field are the polynomial itself and the multiplicative identity. For instance, over the binary field \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathbb {F}_2$$\end{document}, the polynomial \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$x^2 + x + 1$$\end{document} is irreducible since no factorization exists using coefficients restricted to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\{0,1\}$$\end{document}. The degree of a polynomial plays a fundamental role in determining irreducibility. In particular, any polynomial of degree one, such as x or \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$x+1$$\end{document} over \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathbb {F}_2$$\end{document}, is inherently irreducible, as no factorization into lower-degree nonconstant polynomials is possible. An irreducible polynomial is a polynomial of degree greater than 1 that cannot be factored into polynomials of smaller degree with coefficients from the same field.

In addition, irreducible polynomials are the fundamental elements that are used to create finite fields, for instance, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathbb {F}_{p^n}$$\end{document}. With this polynomial, we can geometrically depict the addition and multiplication operations in the field. Numerous cryptographic algorithms, such as AES, rely on irreducible polynomials to characterize the finite fields on which their operations are carried out.

The proposed method

The structure of this technique is defined by three main stages. Firstly, in key generation, either secret keys are produced and provided only to those who are allowed to create a secure environment. Next, the sender, relying on the correct key, chooses an encryption algorithm and turns the original plaintext message into an unrecognizable ciphertext, thus making the information inaccessible to unauthorized people. The last step is decryption that the legitimate receiver can do by using the matching key and the decryption algorithm to change the encoded message back to the original plaintext one, thus enabling the message to be securely understood. The various encryption and decryption scheme steps of our technique are summarized in Fig. 3.

Encryption stage

The proposed cryptographic scheme is structured around four essential stages designed to ensure image security. Initially, the original image is transformed into a one-dimensional vector to facilitate subsequent numerical processing. The second stage consists in computing the initialization constant, which plays a key role throughout the encryption procedure. This is followed by a global permutation phase, during which the components of the data are rearranged to enhance diffusion. Finally, a Vigenère-Affine encryption round is applied using points computed on a pseudo-random elliptic curve defined over the field \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathbb {F}_{256}$$\end{document}. Together, these stages provide strong guarantees of data confidentiality and integrity, highlighting the critical role of encryption in safeguarding sensitive information across diverse applications.

Statistical metrics-based analysis

The histograms-based evaluation A histogram is a very illustrative tool for understanding how pixels are distributed in a given image. The final histograms, as presented in Table 5, are almost horizontal, which means that all gray levels are used with approximately the same probability. Such a uniform distribution indicates that correlations among the plain and cipher images have been eliminated, thus the encryption can be considered statistically random from a theoretical point of view, and therefore it is effective in resisting histogram and frequency-based statistical attacks. Analysis of the key space and sensitivity

This section examines the sensitivity of the proposed method’s performance with respect to both the cryptographic key and the plaintext. A high degree of sensitivity is a fundamental requirement for secure schemes, as it ensures that even minimal perturbations in the ciphering or deciphering process, such as slight changes to the key or minor variations in the plaintext, lead to significantly different and incorrect results.

Key space analysis

The brute-force attack employs a systematic approach to bypass security measures, such as encryption keys, by trying all possible combinations. When deciding on the correct key or sequence, it essentially depends on the utilization of all available computational resources. Therefore, we conduct a detailed examination of our system’s sensitivity and key space. The determination of a cipher system’s strength against brute-force attacks is done through key-space analysis. The key space of the encryption system should be more than \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$2^{100}$$\end{document} to be considered secure. Our method’s total key space is composed of three initial states and three control parameters, each 32 bits long, giving a total key space of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$2^{4 \times 32} = 2^{128}$$\end{document}. Such a key space is sufficient to make brute-force attacks practically impossible.

Practical deployment aspects

According to the paper’s proposed hybrid ECC-finite-field-Vigenere framework and the experimental results, the system is highly capable of meeting the security needs of practical real-world scenarios involving image security. As per Section 6.2 of the time performance analysis, Encryption requires substantially less time in practical scenarios, e.g., 0.75-1.14 seconds on a 2.7 GHz CPU with 32 GB RAM for traditional benchmark images like Peppers color, Baboon, Peppers gray, and Cameraman. Thus, it can be assumed that the encryption system could be comfortably used for secure image transmission and storage operations in real-time or near real-time applications scenarios. The primary operations of the algorithm from a hardware perspective are finite-field arithmetic over \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathbb {F}_{256}$$\end{document}, table lookups, XOR operations, and elliptic-curve point operations defined over a small field. All these are very well-suited for software implementation on general-purpose processors and straightforwardly could be highly optimized or parallelized, e.g., for embedded platforms, GPUs, or FPGA-based accelerators, Moreover, as the main computational complexity depends on the number of pixels linearly, performance is quite significant O(chxNxM). Furthermore, the choice of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathbb {F}_{256}$$\end{document} which is perfect for 8-bit pixel representation naturally additionally leads to lower implementation overhead and memory fragmentation, thus the proposed system can easily be supported even in resource-limited environments. The paper’s statistical performance data (namely entropy near to 8, NPCR > 99.7%, UACI > 33.5%) of images with various sizes and channel depths verify that the cryptographic properties are just as strong with larger image resolutions. They mostly leverage the fact that the encryption pipeline work in a pixel-vector mode without any form of recursive or multi-round expansion, allowing the method to be a logically simple scale-invariant in that it will handle larger high-resolution images and bigger datasets the same way it handles smaller ones. This is very necessary for applications like medical imaging, surveillance systems, and secure multimedia archives, etc. Basically, the linear-time complexity combined with the moderate memory requirements and achieved empirical conformance to the specification strongly indicates that the developed instrument is theoretically a very powerful one and likewise ready for deployment in real-time servers and mass-scale image-processing systems.

Benefits of our scheme

The described scheme above provides numerous benefits: First, the cipher key derived from the chaos map is highly sensitive to any initial condition small modification, making it extremely hard to find the exact key value. Additionally, the integration of pseudo-random decision vectors and the design of new S-Boxes enhances the system’s resistance to attacks. These boxes are constructed using dynamic functions over the finite body \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathbb {F}_{256}$$\end{document}, further strengthening the system’s security. Moreover, the combination of a substitution table for pixel encryption with pseudorandom elliptic curves over the finite field \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathbb {F}_{256}$$\end{document} for encoding pixel values increases the complexity of reconstructing the plain data. Finally, the method is versatile and can be adapted to work with images of any format or size.

Conclusion and future works

This paper introduced a hybrid ECC-finite-field-chaos image encryption framework that tightly couples dynamic key generation, elliptic curve diffusion, and algebraic substitution mechanisms. Unlike conventional static-key or chaos-only systems, the proposed architecture ensures image-dependent adaptability, strong confusion-diffusion properties, and linear-time computational complexity.

Future research will focus on making the created ECC chaos image encryption method more versatile in terms of security and usage scenarios. Especially, we will look into combining the existing elliptic-curve and finite-field foundations with the access-control systems like ECC Attribute-Based Encryption (ECC ABE) to allow very detailed permissions and sharing of encrypted images. We will also be looking at modifying the algorithm to be able to run on very small devices such as those in IoT or in very low-resource scenarios with the help of the naturally compatible 8-bit image representations and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathbb {F}_{256}$$\end{document} arithmetic. Furthermore, the next steps will see changes designed to be able to use authenticated encryption primitives so as to guarantee confidentiality and integrity at the same time, thus, making the proposed technique more reliable and suitable for secure multimedia systems in the real world.

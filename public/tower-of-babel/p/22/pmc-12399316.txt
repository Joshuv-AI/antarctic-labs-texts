Robotic selective left colectomy with inferior mesenteric artery and inferior mesenteric vein preservation: a stepwise video technique
Authors: Igor Monsellato, Teresa Gatto, Marco Palucci, Federico Sangiuolo, Gianluca Cassese, Fabrizio Panaro
Published: 2025

CLINICAL BACKGROUND AND RATIONALE

High ligation of the inferior mesenteric artery (IMA) and inferior mesenteric vein (IMV) has traditionally been considered the standard approach for oncologic left-sided colectomy. However, this method can compromise arterial perfusion and venous drainage to the remaining colon. Ligation of the IMA may disrupt flow through the marginal artery, while ligation of the IMV can result in venous congestion, potentially impairing anastomotic healing. Preservation of these vessels may mitigate such risks, particularly in patients with borderline vascular anatomy or microcirculatory impairment [1–4].

Several studies have demonstrated that IMV preservation may reduce the risk of postoperative ischemic colitis [5–7]. Likewise, sparing the IMA trunk while performing lymph node dissection at station 253 may protect autonomic nerves, improve perfusion, and provide greater flexibility for constructing a tension-free anastomosis [2, 8–10]. Robotic surgical systems offer significant advantages for these complex dissections through improved visualization and instrument dexterity.

Beyond physiological considerations, this vessel-preserving approach may be particularly advantageous in patients with a history of abdominal surgery, previous radiation, or known vascular compromise. By avoiding extensive vascular ligation, operative time may be reduced, and complications related to ischemia or venous stasis may be minimized. Furthermore, selective ligation of the left colic artery, as opposed to high tie at the IMA origin, maintains perfusion via the marginal artery and avoids unnecessary devascularization of the descending and transverse colon.

SURGICAL TECHNIQUE (STEPWISE OVERVIEW)

This stepwise approach involves medial-to-lateral dissection, skeletonization of both the IMV and IMA, complete D3 lymphadenectomy at station 253, selective division of the left colic artery, and intracorporeal anastomosis. The procedure was performed using the da Vinci Xi robotic system (Intuitive Surgical) with a 4-arm setup. The patient was positioned supine, with a 20° Trendelenburg tilt and 15° rotation to the right.

1. Medial-to-lateral dissection: The peritoneum was opened medial to the descending colon. A window was created below the IMV, exposing the mesocolic plane.

2. Skeletonization of IMV: The IMV was dissected circumferentially along its length. Tributaries were divided, and the main trunk was preserved.

3. Skeletonization of IMA and 253 lymphadenectomy: The IMA was exposed and carefully skeletonized. Lymph node station 253 was resected en bloc without dividing the IMA.

4. Intracorporeal anastomosis: The proximal and distal colonic ends were aligned without tension. A side-to-side antiperistaltic intracorporeal anastomosis was created with a robotic linear stapler and closed with barbed sutures. Perfusion was verified using indocyanine green fluorescence imaging.

The mesenteric defect was carefully closed using fibrin-based sealants, as part of our standardized protocol to prevent internal herniation.

POSTOPERATIVE COURSE AND CONSIDERATIONS

The patient recovered uneventfully, with early return of bowel function and no evidence of anastomotic leak. Final pathology confirmed negative margins and an adequate lymph node harvest. The vessel-preserving strategy contributed to stable hemodynamics and rapid bowel recovery, likely due to improved perfusion at the anastomotic site.

Robotic assistance was instrumental in achieving meticulous dissection around major vessels and lymphatic tissue, reducing the risks of bleeding and nerve injury. The enhanced dexterity and high-definition visualization provided by the robotic platform facilitated safe and controlled vessel preservation, even near the aortic origin of the IMA.

EDUCATIONAL VALUE AND BROADER IMPLICATIONS

The vessel-sparing approach demonstrated in this video offers substantial educational value for colorectal surgeons, especially those adopting advanced robotic techniques. It highlights the potential of robotic systems to facilitate refined dissections that are challenging with conventional laparoscopy [8, 9]. This technique may support enhanced recovery protocols by minimizing physiological stress and preserving native anatomy.

Future studies should assess the long-term outcomes of IMA and IMV preservation strategies in left-sided colon cancer surgery, including rates of anastomotic leak, postoperative ileus, and overall bowel function. In addition, comparative trials are needed to clarify the oncologic safety and functional superiority of this approach [10, 11].

Effects of SGLT2 inhibitors on angiography-derived coronary microcirculatory resistance and clinical outcomes in patients with coronary heart disease and type 2 diabetes: A cohort study
Authors: Guangkuo Li, Jianfan Mu, Xuan Liu, Jing He, Fangjie Ji, Weizhan Wang, Yong Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCoronary microvascular dysfunction (CMD) is prevalent in patients with coronary heart disease (CHD) and Type 2 Diabetes (T2DM). Sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors (SGLT2i) have cardioprotective effects, however, their impact on microcirculatory function is controversial. In this study, we systematically evaluated the effects of SGLT2i on microcirculatory function and clinical outcomes in patients with CHD and T2DM.MethodsThis single-center ambispective cohort study retrospectively screened patients with CHD and T2DM who underwent two coronary angiographies from March 2021 to December 2023. Propensity score matching was used to equilibrate baseline factors between two groups: with 106 patients receiving dapagliflozin therapy (experimental group) and 106 patients not receiving dapagliflozin (control group). The retrospective part assessed the effect of SGLT2i on coronary microcirculatory function in patients with CHD and T2DM using the change in Angiography-derived Coronary Microcirculatory Resistance (AMR) from baseline to 12±1 months after treatment as the primary endpoint. The prospective portion of the study followed patients for 15 months to assess clinical outcomes. The study was registered with the China Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2400085512).ResultsBaseline characteristics were comparable between groups. The experimental group showed reductions in AMR (from 2.63 to 2.41, P < 0.05). In contrast, the control group exhibited increases in AMR (from 2.58 to 2.78, P < 0.05). Post-treatment intergroup comparisons showed lower AMR in the experimental group (P < 0.05). At 15-month follow-ups, the experimental group had higher 6-minute walk distance (6MWD) and scores across all domains of the Seattle Angina Questionnaire (SAQ) and 36-items Short-form Health Survey (SF-36) (P < 0.05). An exploratory survival analysis revealed statistically significant differences in the survival curves of the two groups (log-rank P = 0.014).ConclusionThis study found that SGLT2i may confer beneficial effects on coronary microcirculatory function, exercise capacity, quality of life, and survival in patients with CHD and T2DM. This provides new evidence for the improvement of coronary microcirculatory function by SGLT2i.Trial registrationChina Clinical Trial Registry Identifier ChiCTR2400085512 (Registration date June 11, 2024).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13098-025-01916-0.Keywords: Coronary microvascular function, Microcirculatory resistance, Coronary heart disease, Type 2 diabetes mellitus, Angiography-derived assessment of coronary microcirculatory resistance

Introduction

Cardiovascular disease (CVD) has consistently represented the predominant cause of disease-related mortality in developed nations since the early 20th century [1]. While angina and dyspnea remain common presenting complaints, a growing proportion of patients undergoing coronary angiography exhibit no significantly obstructive coronary heart disease (CHD) [2]. With advances in technology, contemporary research suggests that this may be related to the patient’s microcirculatory function [3–6]. Coronary microvascular dysfunction (CMD) is characterized by impaired coronary microcirculatory blood flow regulation, leading to inadequate myocardial perfusion [7–10]. It represents a pivotal pathophysiological mechanism underlying CHD. As an important risk factor for CHD, T2DM is characterized by hyperglycemia, insulin resistance, and metabolic disorders, which can further exacerbate the occurrence and development of CMD [11]. The pathogenesis of CMD remains incompletely elucidated and encompasses: endothelial dysfunction, microvascular structural alterations, autonomic dysregulation, hemorheological disturbances, and inflammation [12]. Inflammatory cascade initiates CMD and sustains a deleterious feedback loop, coupling microvascular dysfunction with adverse cardiovascular sequelae [13]. T2DM frequently exhibits endothelial dysfunction, amplified inflammatory responses [14], and structural microvascular injury, all of which aggravate CMD severity in individuals with CHD and T2DM [15, 16]. However, there are no effective therapies specifically targeting CMD [17, 18].

Accumulating evidence has demonstrated the potential cardiovascular benefits of SGLT2i independent of their glycemic control properties [19]. A growing body of randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and meta-analyses [20] has substantiated the cardioprotective effects of SGLT2i, including reductions in heart failure-related hospitalization, improved cardiac function, and a lower risk of cardiovascular mortality [21]. These benefits are potentially mediated through metabolic-inflammatory pathways. SGLT2i attenuate the systemic inflammatory burden and improve prognosis in patients with ischemic heart disease (IHD) and multivessel complex stenosis [22], as well as those with non-obstructive coronary disease [23]. The SCAN study [24] demonstrated that SGLT2i ameliorate cardiac autonomic neuropathy dysfunction and reduce the recurrence of vaso-vagal syncope in patients with T2DM. SGLT2i may favorably modulate cardiac electrophysiological properties and mitigate inflammation and sympathetic tone [25]. Emerging evidence suggests that SGLT2i may ameliorate microvascular and endothelial dysfunction via multiple mechanisms, including upregulation of nitric oxide synthesis, modulation of inflammatory pathways, enhancement of mitochondrial integrity, suppression of oxidative stress and inflammation, promotion of cellular viability, and stimulation of angiogenesis [26–33]. Nonetheless, despite accumulating data on the cardioprotective properties of SGLT2i, the precise mechanisms and clinical implications of their effects on CMD remain incompletely elucidated. Regarding its effect on microcirculatory function, clinical trial outcomes are still controversial. In the DAPAHEART trial [34, 35], a significant increase in myocardial flow reserve (MFR) was observed in 16 patients with T2DM and stable coronary artery disease after a 4-week treatment with dapagliflozin or placebo. Conversely, the SIMPLE trial [36] found no statistically significant alterations in MFR after 13 weeks of treatment with empagliflozin or placebo in 90 patients with T2DM. Similarly, Suhrs et al. [37]showed that 12 weeks of SGLT2i treatment failed to improve coronary flow velocity reserve (CFVR) in patients with T2DM. A plethora of studies have yielded equivocal results, largely due to inherent limitations in sample size, assessment methodology, and follow-up duration. Critically, these discrepant findings also underscore the currently incomplete mechanisms of how SGLT2i modulate microvascular function and highlight the substantial inter-individual variability in patient responses to SGLT2i. There is an urgent need for clinical studies with large samples and standardized assessment methods to validate these findings.

Wire-derived index of microvascular resistance (IMR), which is the most common tool to evaluate microvascular dysfunction, can predict adverse clinical outcomes and the extent of myocardial injury in patients with both chronic and acute CHD [38, 39]. However, it requires additional PCI time, costs, and high-tech software solutions. In this regard, AMR has been developed and validated to assess coronary microvascular function based on computational flow analysis [40]. Its fast and reproducible computation intends to overcome the limitations of wire-based physiology and to increase the use of physiology-based decision-making in CHD.

The objective of this study was to methodically analyse the impact of SGLT2i on coronary microcirculatory function and clinical outcomes in patients with CHD and T2DM. The primary endpoint was derived from AMR, with secondary endpoints including exercise capacity and quality of life assessment.

Materials and methods

Study design

This ambidirectional cohort study integrated retrospective and prospective phases within a single CHD and T2DM population from Tianjin Fourth Central Hospital.

Retrospective phase (March 2021 - December 2023).

Primary endpoint: AMR calculation from archived angiograms (baseline and 12±1 month).

Prospective phase (January 2024 - March 2025).

The identical cohort entered a 15-month prospective follow-up.

Secondary endpoints: Quality of life: 6MWD (months 6/15), SAQ and SF-36 (month 15), Major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) incidence.

Study population

The study retrospectively screened 2124 patients with CHD combined with T2DM who underwent coronary angiography from March 2021 to December 2023 at Tianjin Fourth Central Hospital. The Ethics Committee of Tianjin Fourth Central Hospital approved the study protocol and waived the requirement of informed consent [Ethics approval number: SZXLL-2024-KY023]. Inclusion criteria: (1) CHD: meet the WHO diagnostic criteria; (2)T2DM: meet the diagnostic criteria in the Chinese Guidelines for the Prevention and Treatment of Type 2 diabetes (2020); (3) Age ≥ 18 years old, with male or female, Complete baseline and imaging information; (4) Completed two coronary angiograms during separate hospital admissions spaced 12 ± 1 months apart within the 2021–2023 period, without percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI).

Underwent coronary angiography alone without percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI); Exclusion criteria: (1) Without two coronary angiography records spaced (12±1) months apart; (2) A medical history that includes autoimmune diseases, tumors, severe renal insufficiency (eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m²), severe cardiac insufficiency, severe infections, or other diseases that significantly impact microcirculation; (3) Acute myocardial infarction(AMI); (4) Prior coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG); (5) Clinical or angiographic conditions that limited AMR calculation (left main stem lesions, chronic total occlusions [CTO], frequent ventricular arrhythmias, or persistent significant tachycardia). Overall, 235 patients fulfilled the criteria for inclusion and exclusion (Fig. 1), 108 of whom received dapagliflozin treatment (experimental group) and 127 of whom did not receive dapagliflozin treatment (control group). Dapagliflozin (10 mg QD) was initiated at baseline and continued throughout the follow-up; adherence was monitored by prescription refill records and patient self-report.

Flow chart of patient screening for this study

To mitigate the influence of confounding factors on coronary microcirculatory function between the two groups, propensity score matching (PSM) was employed to ensure baseline comparability. Propensity scores were calculated using logistic regression, with a caliper value of 0.02, and nearest neighbor matching was performed at a 1:1 ratio using R software (version 4.3.2). Balance between the matched groups was tested by standardized mean difference (SMD), and the two groups were considered to be balanced at baseline when SMD was < 0.1. Matching covariates included smoking status, fasting blood glucose concentration, and C-reactive protein [41]. The final matched cohort consisted of 106 patient pairs.

Data collection

Comprehensive demographic and clinical data were obtained, including the patient’s unique identification number, sex, age, body mass index (BMI), smoking status (ongoing smoking for more than 6 months), date of prescription, diagnosis, and the date of coronary angiography. In addition, pertinent laboratory parameters were collected, such as complete blood count, total cholesterol (TC), triglycerides (TG), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), fasting blood glucose (FBG), glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c), serum potassium, uric acid (UA), D-dimer, C-reactive protein (CRP), and echocardiographic indices.

Coronary angiography

Philips’ comprehensive digital cardiovascular imaging system is utilized, and selective digital subtraction angiography is conducted via the transradial or transfemoral approach employing the Judkins technique. The operator must possess professional qualifications equivalent to a deputy chief physician or higher and demonstrate a minimum of 5 years’ experience in coronary heart disease interventional procedures. The contrast medium utilized during the procedure was iophorol. The optimal angiographic projections for left coronary arteriography are as follows: (1) CRA 45° and RAO 30° for the anterior descending and diagonal branches, CRA 30°; (2) CAU 30° for the circumflex and obtuse marginal branches; (3) LAO 30° and CRA 30° for right coronary angiography.

The overall image quality requirements are as follows: (1) Imaging must be clear, with uniform force applied during contrast medium injection to prevent human-induced boundary artifacts, ensuring full visualization of proximal and distal vascular segments in a single imaging session to delineate all vascular lesions. (2) Select angiographic positions that minimize vascular overlap, optimize projection angles and framing, and control vascular foreshortening and overlap.

QFR and AMR computation [42, 43]

A single-view µQFR analysis was computed, using the QFR software (AngioPlus Core, Shanghai Pulse Medical Technology Inc., Shanghai, China). The detailed methodology for single-view µQFR computation has been described previously [44]. Murray law-based QFR is a novel computational method that uses artificial intelligence to estimate the fractional flow reserve (FFR) based on the analysis of a single angiographic projection with excellent reproducibility. This advanced system utilizes artificial intelligence to automatically calculate both AMR and resting AMR within 1mintue at the touch of a button, as illustrated in Fig. 2. In the 2 coronary angiography images, optimal angiographic projections were selected to maximize the accuracy of data acquisition. All analyzed angiograms were performed during index hospitalizations between March 2021 and December 2023. No additional angiograms were conducted during prospective follow-up. For each patient, two existing angiograms (baseline and 12±1 month) retrospectively selected from 2021 to 2023 records were analyzed. The two operators were blinded to participant clinical data and treatment and assessed AMR independently. Inter-observer reproducibility of AMR computation was evaluated by two observers analyzing 30 vessels randomly selected from the study population in a blinded fashion.

Follow-up and data collection for patients

Patients enrolled in this study were prospectively followed for 15 months. Follow-up assessments were primarily conducted via in-person visits. 6MWD were collected at months 6 and 15 of follow-up. SAQ score, SF-36 score, and the incidence of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) were collected at month 15 of follow-up. Assessors were blinded to patients’ SGLT2i treatment status throughout the follow-up period to minimize assessment bias. Two patients in the experimental group and one in the control group were lost to follow-up; all remaining subjects completed the 15-month follow-up.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS version 26.0. Normality of the variables was evaluated using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Normally distributed data were presented as mean ± standard deviation (x̄ ± s), and within-group comparisons were conducted using paired-sample t-tests, while between-group comparisons were evaluated using independent-sample t-tests. Non-normally distributed data were expressed as median and interquartile range [M (P25, P75)] and analyzed using the.

Mann–Whitney U-test or Kruskal–Wallis test. Categorical variables were summarized as frequencies and percentages (n, %), and intergroup differences were assessed using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Survival analysis was conducted through one-way Cox regression. The analysis of survival with experimental and control groups was performed using the Kaplan–Meier (K-M) method by R software (version 4.4.2). A significance level of P < 0.05 was employed to determine statistical significance.

Evaluation of the efficacy of coronary microcirculation function

A total of 300 coronary arteries were assessed in 106 patients from the experimental group, comprising 100 left anterior descending (LAD) arteries, 98 left circumflex arteries (LCX), and 102 right coronary arteries (RCA). Among the control group, 303 coronary arteries were evaluated in 106 patients, including 103 LAD arteries, 99 LCX, and 101 RCA.

In line with our primary objective, treatment with SGLT2i resulted in a statistically improvement in AMR, as assessed by angiography. Compared to baseline, the experimental group demonstrated marked reductions in both AMR and resting AMR at 12±1 months, whereas the control group exhibited increases in these indices (all P < 0.05). Post-treatment AMR in the experimental group was 2.41±0.79, lower than in the control group2.78±0.77. Resting AMR showed a similar trend [5.23 (IQR: 3.57–6.83)vs. 6.00 (IQR: 4.45–7.90); P < 0.05] (Table 2; Fig. 3). These findings suggest that SGLT2i therapy may confer beneficial effects on coronary microvascular function in patients with CHD and T2DM.

In addition to the primary endpoint, QFR, a surrogate of coronary flow reserve, was improved in the SGLT2i group compared to both baseline and the control group at 12±1 months (P < 0.05). (Table 2)

Follow-up outcomes

To comprehensively assess the clinical benefits of SGLT2i beyond coronary microvascular function, we evaluated functional capacity, symptom burden, health-related quality of life, and survival during prospective follow-up.

Functional capacity

The 6-minute walk distance (6MWD)—a key measure of exercise tolerance and cardiac function—was greater in the SGLT2i group at both 6 and 15 months of prospective follow-up. At 6 months, median 6MWD was 450.00 [400.25, 516.00] meters in the experimental group, compared to 421.00 [373.00, 469.75] meters in the control group (P < 0.05). This advantage was maintained at 15 months [453.00 (403.25, 518.00) vs. 428.00 (377.75, 472.75); P < 0.05], indicating a sustained improvement in exercise capacity (Table 3).

Symptom burden and disease-specific quality of life

At 15-month prospective follow-up, the experimental group reported superior outcomes across all five dimensions of the Seattle Angina Questionnaire (SAQ), suggesting a marked reduction in angina symptoms and improved disease-related quality of life (all P < 0.05; Table 3).

General health-related quality of life

Similarly, SF-36 scores at 15 months prospective follow-up were higher in the experimental group across all eight dimensions, reflecting broad-based improvements in physical, mental, and social health domains (all P < 0.05; Table 3).

Exploratory survival

Kaplan-Meier analysis demonstrated a trend toward improved survival with SGLT2i therapy (log-rank P = 0.014; Fig. 4), though event rates remained limited (experimental group: 2 deaths/9 MACE; control group: 1 death/21 MACE). Given the 15-month follow-up duration, these survival comparisons should be interpreted as exploratory findings pending validation in larger cohorts with longer follow-up. Collectively, these findings suggest SGLT2i may confer beneficial effects on coronary microvascular function and may confer benefits for functional capacity and symptom burden in CHD-T2DM patients, with survival outcomes requiring further validation.

Discussion

This ambispective analysis suggests that SGLT2i may confer beneficial effects on coronary microvascular function in patients with CHD and T2DM, as indicated by reductions in AMR. The main results of the observation are: (1) Comparable baseline AMR values between groups (P = 0.765 by independent t-test) (2) AMR at 12±1 month improvement in the SGLT2i group relative to controls (ΔAMR − 0.21 ± 0.51 vs. 0.21 ± 0.78; P < 0.001); (3)Directionally favorable ΔAMR changes in the experimental cohort without parallel improvement in controls Preliminary data also indicate potential multidimensional benefits spanning functional capacity, quality-of-life metrics, and survival trends. However, these findings require validation in larger cohorts given methodological constraints including single-center design and limited follow-up duration.

Impaired coronary microvascular perfusion represents a prevalent manifestation in individuals with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), with a multifactorial and incompletely elucidated pathogenesis encompassing numerous pathophysiological alterations. Hyperglycemia and insulin resistance constitute pivotal drivers of oxidative stress, inflammatory cascades, and endothelial barrier dysfunction [12]. Sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors (SGLT2i) confer therapeutic benefits partially through attenuation of microvascular inflammation. Accumulating preclinical and clinical evidence [48] indicates that SGLT2i ameliorate endothelial and microvascular dysfunction via pleiotropic mechanisms underpinning their cardioprotective properties. SGLT2i’s modulation of systemic inflammatory responses and oxidative stress—pathophysiological processes intimately linked to endothelial degeneration and atherogenesis [49].These agents significantly reduce circulating proinflammatory mediators [50], and preserve endothelial integrity through junctional protein stabilization and glycocalyx restoration [51]. Based on these findings, it seems plausible that treatment with SGLT2i could lead to a reduction in AMR. However, the clinical data concerning the effect on microcirculation are conflicting.

The impact of SGLT2i on coronary microvascular function remains a subject of ongoing debate [52–54]. The SIMPLE trial [36]used Rubidium­82 positron emission tomography/computed tomography (PET/CT) to assess MFR in 90 patients with T2DM treated with either empagliflozin or placebo over 13 weeks. The study reported no statistically significant changes in MFR in either group. The relatively high baseline MFR and the inclusion of participants with largely normal values may partly explain the absence of therapeutic effect. In contrast, the patients in our study had microcirculatory dysfunction at baseline and the mean AMR value in our study was > 2.5, which may explain the statistical difference in improvement in AMR that we observed. Similarly, a trial conducted by Suhrs et al. [37]showed that a 12-week treatment regimen failed to improve coronary flow velocity reserve (CFVR) among patients with T2DM, potentially due to a baseline CFVR of 2.60, above the commonly accepted threshold for CMD. Moreover, the potential confounding impact of LAD artery macrovascular disease on CFVR measurements cannot be entirely excluded. However, preclinical evidence indicates that in mouse models of diabetes and heart failure, empagliflozin reduces arterial stiffness [55] and improves coronary microvascular function, as assessed noninvasively via Doppler ultrasound measurement of CFVR, improves myocardial contractile performance, and induces favorable metabolic alterations [54]. In our study, we excluded patients with large vessel lesions, which may make it easier to detect positive results. In addition, Laurits et al. [56] conducted a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover study in 13 patients with T2DM, revealing that 4-week empagliflozin therapy lowered resting myocardial blood flow (MBF) without altering adenosine-stimulated MBF or MFR, but the latter displayed a tendency towards.

In the DAPAHEART trial [34, 35], 16 patients with T2DM and stable coronary artery disease received either dapagliflozin or placebo for 4 weeks. MFR assessed using 13N-ammonia PET/CT showed a significant increase alongside a reduction in resting MBF following SGLT2i therapy, marking the first such observation in clinical settings. Additionally, SGLT2 inhibitors have been found to show benefits in improving arterial stiffness and peripheral vascular resistance, as evidenced by post hoc analyses from a double-blind trial of empagliflozin in patients with non-diabetic heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF) [57] and a phase III trial in patients with T2DM [58]. Our findings, derived using the objective and cost-effective AMR metric [59], further substantiate the beneficial effects of SGLT2 inhibitors on microvascular function and provide novel evidence supporting their cardioprotective potential. Additionally, the incorporation of multidimensional clinical endpoints within a follow-up framework strengthens the clinical relevance of the findings.

Importantly, this study included a 15-month longitudinal clinical follow-up. Dapagliflozin also showed impressive benefits in the clinical follow-up results. Compared with the control group, the experimental group showed a significant improvement in exercise capacity, quality of life scores, and survival rate during the 15-month follow-up period. Due to the high number of tail cutoffs and the low rate of hard endpoints, median survival times could not be observed, so the present study was an exploratory survival analysis only. The inability to determine a median survival time may be attributable to the limited sample size or an inadequate duration of follow-up. Patients with concomitant coronary artery disease and type 2 diabetes mellitus who receive intensive treatment tend to exhibit prolonged survival. The primary mechanistic contributor to these clinical benefits may be the enhancement of coronary microvascular perfusion. Optimized microcirculatory function ensures sufficient oxygen and nutrient delivery to cardiomyocytes, thereby enhancing myocardial contractility and diastolic function. This also enables the patient to cope better with the demands of physical activity in daily life, resulting in a significant increase in exercise endurance. The improvement in quality of life scores also reflected the improvement in both physical and psychological status of the patients.

This study specifically targeted a high-risk population with coexisting CHD and T2DM, characterized by prevalent microvascular dysfunction, where the intervention effect of SGLT2i was more pronounced, resulting in a statistically clear trend of improvement. The hypothesis that SGLT2i may exert its cardioprotective effects by improving microcirculatory function was validated. This finding provides a perspective for understanding the mechanism of action of SGLT2i and emphasizes the importance of microcirculatory function improvement in CHD treatment.

Study limitations

There are several limitations of this study. A foremost limitation is the single-center, retrospective cohort design. Although propensity score matching was employed to balance baseline characteristics between groups, residual selection bias and unmeasured confounding cannot be excluded. The highly specific inclusion criteria (patients with CHD and T2DM without prior PCI) yielded a restricted cohort size, potentially limiting generalizability to broader populations. Second, while demonstrating reproducibility, AMR measurements still have operator-dependent and lack validation in longitudinal outcome prediction. Meanwhile, the reliance on a single modality without confirmatory tools (e.g., PET, CMR) may limit interpretability. Moreover, consensus thresholds for clinically meaningful AMR improvement remain undefined, further complicating clinical interpretation. The results are statistically significant; however, effect sizes (e.g., ΔAMR) are relatively modest, and their clinical relevance remains to be established. Thirdly, the relatively short follow-up duration and low incidence of hard endpoint events precluded robust assessment of long-term treatment effects. Additionally, the absence of blinding and randomization during follow-up assessments introduces potential observer and detection bias. Building upon the current findings and acknowledging the study’s limitations, future research should increase the sample size stratified to evaluate therapeutic efficacy across patients with differing degrees of baseline microcirculatory dysfunction, varying disease stages, and comorbidities such as heart failure or renal impairment. Given the operator-dependent nature of AMR measurements, further validation using complementary multimodal imaging techniques, such as PET/CT, is warranted to confirm improvements in microvascular function. Additionally, the differential efficacy of SGLT2 inhibitors in combination with other therapeutic strategies should be explored in subgroups with microvascular dysfunction, alongside in-depth investigations into the underlying molecular mechanisms.

Conclusion

In summary, this single-center ambispective study provides evidence that SGLT2i may confer beneficial effects on coronary microvascular function in patients with CHD and T2DM. These microvascular benefits may be accompanied by enhanced exercise tolerance, better quality of life, and a possible 15-month survival benefit. Our findings support early initiation of SGLT2i as a viable therapeutic strategy for this high-risk population.

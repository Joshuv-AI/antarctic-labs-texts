National qualitative study in France to explore parents’ lived experience at the birth of a child with a large or giant congenital melanocytic nevus
Authors: Camille Lahet, Marie Buzzi, Julie Boulanger, Delphine Fombaron, Laetitia Ricci, Stéphanie Mallet, Olivia Boccara, Hélène Aubert, Anne-Claire Bing-Lecointe, Claire Abasq, Mathilde Tardieu, Juliette Miquel, Christine Chiaverini, Bertille Bonniaud, Domitille Jorand, Nathalie Fajnkuchen, Anne-Claire Bursztejn
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionCongenital melanocytic nevi (CMN) are congenital malformations that cannot be detected by prenatal ultrasounds and may have a significant physical and psychological impact on affected individuals, particularly when they are large. Congenital anomalies are known to impair parental quality of life, which may in turn affect the child’s well-being and development. However, little is known about the feelings and lived experience of parents on the birth of a CMN-affected child.The aim of this study is to describe the lived experience of parents of a child with a large or giant CMN, and to document the expressed needs for support (psychological, marital, parenting-related and those evolving along the care pathway)Methods and analysisWe will conduct a qualitative study in 10 French hospitals, based on semi-structured interviews. Eligible participants will be parents of children under 18 years of age with a large or giant CMN. A maximum variation sampling strategy will be applied to ensure optimal diversity of profiles in terms of children’s age, gender and phototype and location and size of the CMN. The target sample size will be determined by theoretical saturation. Data will be analysed using an inductive thematic content analysis approach.Ethics and disseminationThe study has received ethical approval from the Nancy University Hospital (NUH) Ethics Committee (Opinion No. 504) and authorisation from the French Commission Nationale de l’Informatique et des Libertés (CNIL) (Ref. 2024PI238-614). Non-opposition to participation will be documented in medical records. Findings will be presented at national and European dermatology conferences and published in peer-reviewed international journals.

Introduction

Congenital melanocytic nevi (CMN) are benign melanocytic proliferations present at birth or which emerge within the first few weeks of life.1 These lesions cannot be detected via prenatal ultrasounds and may exceed 40 cm in diameter in adulthood.2 The prevalence of CMN varies according to their size and is estimated to range from 1 in 20 000 to 1 in 500 000 live births for those classified as giant (diameter >40 cm).3

Large CMN (size >20 cm) and giant CMN are often polymorphic, heterochromic, verrucous, nodular or hypertrichotic, and thus highly conspicuous.4 They can be symptomatic, causing pain and chronic pruritus.5 6 Moreover, they carry a risk of malignant transformation into melanoma and may be associated with neurocutaneous melanocytosis.7 8 The management of CMN is complex and requires a multidisciplinary approach. It sometimes demands extensive and disfiguring surgery.9 As such, large CMN can have a significant impact on patients’ quality of life.10

Assessing patients’ quality of life and mental health is essential, as these dimensions are directly linked to their overall well-being.11 A literature review conducted in 2020 by our team demonstrated a detrimental effect on the quality of life of individuals with CMN. This review also underscored a lack of data concerning the lived experience of parents.12

However, evaluating the quality of life and psychological health of parents is also crucial, as their ability to form secure emotional bonds directly influences the overall health and psychological development of the child.11 Several studies have shown the negative impact of congenital anomalies on parental quality of life, with manifestations such as anxiety and depression.13 14 However, to date, no study has explored the lived experience of parents in the context of CMN, nor has any specific parental support strategy been described.12

Based on these observations, a preliminary mixed-methods study was conducted by Lellig in 2021 at the Nancy University Hospital (NUH)15 to explore the real-life experiences of parents at the birth of a child with a large or giant CMN (online supplemental file 1). Quantitative results showed no statistically significant associations between the child’s characteristics (sex, nevus location and size, visibility, phototype) and the impact on parental quality of life, as measured with the Impact of a Child with Congenital Anomalies on Parents questionnaire.

Nonetheless, qualitative interviews revealed several recurrent themes: feelings of guilt, the need for psychological support and notably, a lack of awareness regarding CMN among maternity healthcare professionals, despite generally positive interactions with care teams.

The absence of statistically significant findings may be explained by the small and unrepresentative sample size (eg, no participants had a CMN located on the face).

We therefore hypothesise that a qualitative national-level study involving a larger and more diverse sample may enhance the robustness of findings regarding the experience of parents following the birth of a CMN-affected child. We chose to focus on the specific moment of birth, as it represents a particularly significant disruption in parents’ life trajectories, further heightened by the absence of prenatal diagnosis.

Such a study would ultimately contribute to improving the overall care and support provided to both parents and children affected by large or giant CMN at birth.

Methods and analysis

Objectives and hypotheses

The primary objective is to describe parents’ experience at the birth of a child with a large or giant CMN.

The secondary objective is to document the expressed needs for support (psychological, marital, parenting-related and those evolving along the care pathway).

Design and setting

The PANGEA (impact et vécu des PArents d'un enfant porteur d'un Naevus congénital large ou GEAnt) study will consist of a cross-sectional national qualitative study involving hospitals in 10 French cities: Nancy (university hospital), Paris (university hospital), Marseille (university hospital), Nice (university hospital), Brest (university hospital), Annecy (community hospital), Grenoble (university hospital), Dijon (university hospital), Nantes (university hospital) and Saint Pierre (university hospital). The list of participating centres and corresponding investigators is available on the ClinicalTrials.gov website, where the PANGEA study is registered under the identifier NCT06934759.

Population and sampling

Eligible participants will be adult parents of children aged under 18 years of age with a large or giant CMN and attending one of the participating centres willing to participate in an interview and fluent in French. Participants will be fully informed about the study’s procedures, and their non-opposition to participate will be sought prior to data collection.

Exclusion criteria include: age of child over 18, unwillingness to participate, legal guardianship or protection status, cognitive and/or psychological conditions preventing participation in an interview or inadequate comprehension and/or expression in French.

In view of the low number of births and the short duration of the study, we chose to include parents of children up to 18 years of age to maximise the diversity of the included profiles.

Participants will be identified from a list provided by the investigator of each participating centre, including the clinical characteristics of the last children patients followed up for CMN (box 1).

Based on this dataset, a selection of potential participants will be conducted using a maximum variation sampling strategy to ensure a broad diversity of profiles (in terms of centre, gender, age, phototype, nevus location, size and visibility). This type of sampling should allow a comprehensive exploration of experiences and expectations across a wide spectrum, while allowing to identify shared patterns across experiences.16

The target sample size will be determined by the principle of theoretical saturation,17 defined as the point at which additional data collection no longer yields new insights into the phenomenon studied. A literature review has shown that saturation is reached in most qualitative studies with fewer than 30 interviews.18 Therefore, we plan to interview at least 15 parents and to continue until theoretical saturation is achieved.

Strategy

To explore the lived experience of parents, we chose a qualitative methodology based on semi-structured interviews. This approach was deemed most appropriate to our research question, as it allows us to ‘grasp an individual’s experience’ and ‘understand how a person interprets facts through their own perspective rather than focusing solely on the facts themselves’.19

Parents of eligible children will be contacted by mail and/or email by the principal investigator to inform them of the study and to invite them to participate in an individual semi-structured interview. If no response is received within 1 month, a follow-up phone call will be made. In case of refusal, the parents of another child with similar characteristics will be contacted to maintain the expected sample size and variation in characteristics.

An interview guide (box 2) was written by our team to define key areas of interest to explore. While our study aimed to focus on the parental experiences surrounding the specific moment of birth, aspects related to subsequent stages of the care pathway will also be addressed with parents of older children to bring further perspective. The interviews will additionally be approached using a life-story method, which makes it possible to analyse the parents’ trajectories after the child’s birth, considered here as a biographical disruption. Interviews will be conducted by the study’s principal investigator. Given the potential emotional distress associated with recalling these memories, a clinical psychologist affiliated with each participating centre will be present at each interview to ensure parents’ emotional safety and provide follow-up care if needed. Parents will be informed that the interview may be paused or discontinued at any time if they deem it necessary. In such cases, the interview may be resumed at a later time, depending on the parents’ availability and willingness. Each interview will be performed via videoconference using Microsoft Teams and is expected to last approximately 1 hour.

Outcome measures

The primary and secondary outcome measures will be based on the content of the semi-structured interviews conducted with the parents.

Data collection

The characteristics collected during the initial inclusion phase will inform the sampling process. These are as follows: sex, month and year of birth, phototype, nevus classification, location of the nevus and presence of single or multiple nevi (box 1).

During the interviews, a series of closed-ended questions will be used to collect additional sociodemographic data related to the parents and their child: parent’s sex and age, occupational category, birth order of the child in the family and birth location of the child (university hospital vs non-university maternity unit).

The data used to assess the primary and secondary endpoints will be qualitative in nature, focusing on the lived experience of the parents.

Data management

The data collected during inclusion will be extracted from the patients’ medical records, accessible either through direct authorisation at each centre or via secure email transfer by the scientific investigator of each site. All data will be anonymised, and each individual included in the study will be identified solely by a unique inclusion code structured as follows: ‘chronological inclusion number – type of nevus (NL [large] or NG [giant]) – current age of the child (in digits)’.

The interviews will be audio-recorded using a recorder belonging to the dermatology department of NUH and will be transcribed verbatim. Potentially identifying data will be anonymised and audio files will be deleted after transcription. Once transcribed, the anonymised interview content will be transferred to the study’s lead methodologist to assist with analysis. Data storage and transmission will occur via the secure IT (information technology) network of the NUH and through professional NUH email accounts.

The study database will be stored on NUH servers for a period of 15 years following study completion. An audit could be conducted at any time by independent experts on the NUH research director demand.

A flow chart will be created to illustrate the process from patient inclusion to final analysis.

Data availability statement

No datasets have yet been generated or analysed for this study, as this manuscript reports the study protocol. After completion of the study, de-identified qualitative data will not be made publicly available due to the risk of compromising participant confidentiality. However, de-identified excerpts of transcripts that support specific findings may be made available from the corresponding author on reasonable request and subject to approval by the relevant ethics committee.

Ethics and dissemination

All eligible participants will be informed about the study procedures and objectives, as well as their right to access, modify and oppose the use of their personal data. If a participant withdraws consent during the study, data collection related to him/her will be halted, and previously collected data will not be used.

As this is a non-interventional study, written informed consent is not required. Non-opposition to participation following the provision of oral and written information will be documented.

The research project was submitted to the Direction for Research and Innovation at NUH. As the study does not fall under the jurisdiction of the French Comité de Protection des Personnes, it was submitted to the Ethics Committee of NUH, which raised no ethical objections to its implementation (opinion no. 504 issued on 4 June 2025).

The French National Commission on Informatics and Liberty (CNIL) granted authorisation to initiate the research on 6 June 2025 under registration number 2024PI238-614, recorded in the data processing register in accordance with MR004 standards.

Findings will be presented at national and European dermatology conferences (eg, European Society for Pedriatric Dermatology Congress, Journées dermatologiques de Paris). The study results will be submitted for publication in a peer-reviewed journal. We will report the results according to the COREQ checklist (COnsolidated criteria for REporting Qualitative research).

Statistical analysis

Sociodemographic characteristics of the interviewed parents (sex, age, number of children, occupational category), as well as clinical characteristics of their child (sex, current age, number, size and location of the nevus/nevi, visible or non-visible nature of the nevus, birth order among siblings and birth location (university hospital or non-university facility)) will be described using means and SD, or medians and IQR depending on the variable distribution for quantitative variables, and frequencies and proportions for qualitative variables.

The qualitative data will undergo a thematic content analysis, allowing for the identification of key conceptual ‘meaning nodes’ within participant narratives, using an inductive approach. A double-coding process will be conducted by both the principal investigator and the study’s lead methodologist on a sample of five interviews to develop and stabilise the thematic arborescence, with resolution of any disagreements. A Cohen’s kappa coefficient will be calculated to measure intercoder agreement, with a desired value >0.81.20

The remaining interviews will then be coded by the principal investigator.

This phase is expected to last 2 months and should be completed by 31 August 2026 (figure 1).

Patient and public involvement

Patients and members of the public were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination plans of this study. The research question and qualitative design were developed by the clinical and methodological research team based on previous work and clinical experience with families of children with CMN. The results of the study will be shared in an accessible format with participating families and, where possible, with relevant patient associations and clinicians involved in the care of children with large or giant CMN.

Discussion

Current status of the study

The Ethics Committee of NUH and the CNIL have granted approval for the study to start. The inclusion phase is now ongoing, with a total of 128 eligible children with large or giant CMN identified across the different participating centres, whose parents may be eligible to participate in the current study. The parents’ sample is currently being constituted, and the initial interviews are expected to begin in January 2026.

Implications

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first real-world, nationwide study to explore the lived experience of parents at the birth of a child with a large or giant CMN.

The PANGEA study is expected to provide a deeper understanding of how parents experience the arrival of a child affected by a large or giant CMN. Our goal is to identify potential areas for intervention that could improve follow-up, support and quality of life for both parents and affected children. We will specifically examine whether psychological or medical-psychological support was offered, and whether it was appropriate, in order to improve clinical practice where necessary.

Regardless of the results, this study will offer an honest and authentic account of the experiences of many parents across France, and, if published, will help amplify their voices to a broader audience.

Limitations

Despite its originality and innovative design, the PANGEA study presents certain limitations.

First, there is a potential risk of recall bias, as some interviews may be conducted up to 18 years after the events. The temporal distance may affect the accuracy and completeness of participants’ recollections. In addition, due to the declarative nature of data collection, parents’ accounts will be inherently shaped by retrospective reconstruction processes, which may vary in magnitude across individuals. These narratives may be influenced by subsequent experiences, including the care pathway, or adaptation and resilience over time, potentially leading to reinterpretations of past events.21 However, as the study aims to explore lived experiences and subjective meanings rather than to establish factual accuracy, these elements may also be considered integral to the phenomenon under investigation.

Additionally, during interview analysis, there is a potential for interpretative bias due to the subjective and qualitative nature of the data, which may influence how transcripts are read, coded or thematised.22 To mitigate this, a double-coding strategy will be applied.

Another potential limitation is the risk of self-selection bias, as parents who have coped relatively well may be more likely to participate, while those experiencing greater distress may be underrepresented. This could result in a partial representation of parental experiences and should be considered when interpreting the findings.

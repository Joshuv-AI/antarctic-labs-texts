Log-transformed α-Fetoprotein and Des-γ-carboxy Prothrombin Predict Early Recurrence After Hepatectomy for Hepatocellular Carcinoma
Authors: KOICHI NAKANISHI, HIROJI SHINKAWA, HISAKO YOSHIDA, MASAHIKO KINOSHITA, GENKI WATANABE, KOHEI NISHIO, KENJIRO KIMURA, TAKEAKI ISHIZAWA
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background/AimWe evaluated the impact of log-transformed serum α-fetoprotein (AFP) and des-γ-carboxy prothrombin (DCP) levels on early hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) recurrence after hepatectomy and developed a nomogram for risk stratification.Patients and MethodsWe examined 993 patients who underwent initial curative hepatectomy. A nomogram was constructed to predict early recurrence within 2 years using a Cox proportional hazards model (five-factor log-marker model) based on five preoperatively assessable oncological factors: log AFP, log DCP, multiple tumors, tumor size >3 cm, and macroscopic vascular invasion (MVI). Its time-dependent area under the curve (AUC) at 2 years was compared with a five-factor raw-marker model (replacing log-transformed AFP/DCP with their raw values) and a three-factor model (multiple tumors, tumor size, MVI). Performance was evaluated by time-dependent AUC, bootstrap calibration (1,000 resamples), and observed outcomes across tertiles (T1-T3) of total nomogram points.ResultsThe five-factor log-marker model (AUC 0.69) outperformed the five-factor raw-marker model (0.67, p=0.013) and the three-factor model (0.65, p<0.001). The nomogram showed good calibration (slope=0.98). Two-year recurrence rates by tertile were 25% (T1), 38% (T2), and 58% (T3); 2-year extrahepatic recurrence rates were 1.5%, 4.2%, and 11%, respectively. The trend across tertiles was significant (p<0.001).ConclusionThe nomogram developed using preoperative oncological factors enabled precise prediction of early recurrence after hepatectomy for HCC by incorporating log-transformed AFP and DCP. Our nomogram may be useful in determining treatment strategies for patients with HCC.

Introduction

With recent advances in systemic therapy for advanced hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) (1-3), perioperative treatment strategies are expected to expand. However, reliable criteria are still needed to identify patients at high risk of early recurrence after hepatic resection (4, 5). Early recurrence within 2 years after hepatectomy is associated with poor prognosis (6), and tumor size, tumor number, and vascular invasion are strongly associated with early recurrence (7). A recent trial of atezolizumab plus bevacizumab as postoperative adjuvant therapy in high-risk HCC showed no improvement in prognosis (8).

α-Fetoprotein (AFP) and des-γ-carboxy prothrombin (DCP) are HCC-specific tumor markers associated with poor prognosis (9, 10). Increases in these markers have been reported to reflect tumor aggressiveness independently of morphological progression, including tumor size, multiple tumors, and macroscopic vascular invasion (9, 11), and both markers are also associated with prognosis after hepatic resection (11, 12). However, most previous studies evaluated their prognostic impact using predefined cut-off values of data. Because HCC tumor growth and serum AFP and DCP levels all appear to follow exponential kinetics (13), log-transformation of AFP and DCP data may be biologically and statistically appropriate. However, few studies have evaluated the exponential behavior of AFP and DCP in relation to the risk of HCC recurrence.

In the present study, we evaluated the utility of log-transformed AFP and DCP levels for predicting early postoperative recurrence in patients with HCC undergoing hepatic resection. Furthermore, we aimed to establish a clinically applicable nomogram for early recurrence incorporating log AFP and log DCP.

Patients and Methods

Patients. We initially identified 1,273 patients who underwent hepatic resection for HCC at Osaka Metropolitan University Hospital between April 1998 and December 2021. Among them, 180 patients who underwent repeat hepatectomy, 22 patients with non-curative resection, and 78 patients lacking key clinical or pathological data were excluded. Thus, 993 patients who underwent initial curative hepatic resection for HCC were included in the final analysis. Curative hepatic resection was defined as complete resection of a recognizable tumor and the histological absence of tumor cells along the parenchymal transection line. This study was approved by the Ethics Review Committee of Osaka Metropolitan University (approval number: 2020-227) and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Informed consent was obtained from all patients or their family members using an opt-out form.

Evaluation of tumor markers. Blood samples for AFP and DCP were collected within 7 days prior to hepatic resection. Serum AFP and DCP were measured using standard clinical assays available at our institution during the study period. Logarithmic transformation was applied to AFP and DCP data to assess their relationship with early recurrence risk because these tumor markers increase in accordance with exponential tumor growth.

Patient follow-up. After surgery, AFP and DCP levels were measured and imaging studies, including ultrasonography, dynamic computed tomography, or magnetic resonance imaging, were performed 1 month after surgery and subsequently every 3 months. Recurrence was diagnosed based on imaging findings, elevation of tumor markers, or both.

Histology. Histological classification and background liver disease were evaluated according to the guidelines of the Liver Cancer Study Group of Japan (14). HCC recurrence was divided into early and late recurrence, with 2 years after surgery used as the cutoff because this time point has been reported as an inflection point (15). Previous studies have suggested that recurrence occurring more than 2 years after surgery mainly reflects multicentric carcinogenesis, whereas recurrence within 2 years may arise from residual micrometastasis from the original tumor (15, 16). Therefore, recurrence-free survival (RFS) within 2 years after surgery was used in this study.

Statistical analysis. Background characteristics are summarized as medians and interquartile ranges (IQRs) for continuous variables and as frequencies and percentages for categorical variables. RFS curves according to log AFP and log DCP strata were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method and compared among all strata using the log-rank test. We constructed a nomogram to predict recurrence within 2 years using a Cox proportional hazards model (five-factor log-marker model) based on five preoperatively assessable oncological factors: log AFP, log DCP, multiple tumors, tumor size (>3 cm vs. ≤3 cm), and macroscopic vascular invasion (MVI). To evaluate the usefulness of log-transformed markers, we compared the time-dependent area under the curve (AUC) at 2 years across three models: (i) A five-factor log-marker model, (ii) a five-factor raw-marker model, and (iii) a three-factor model including only multiple tumors, tumor size, and MVI. Time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves at 2 years were also plotted to visually compare the discriminatory performance of the three models. Internal validation with 1,000 bootstrap resamples was used to estimate the calibration slope and obtain a bias-corrected calibration curve. Total nomogram points were calculated for each patient and divided into tertiles (T1-T3), and recurrence rates were compared across tertiles. All analyses were performed using EZR (version 1.52; Saitama Medical Center, Jichi Medical University, Saitama, Japan), and p<0.05 was considered statistically significant.

We developed a predictive nomogram for early HCC recurrence based on the five-factor log-marker model (Figure 2). The time-dependent ROC curves at 2 years for the three models are shown in Figure 3. The time-dependent AUC at 2 years was 0.69 (95% CI=0.66-0.73). The optimism-corrected calibration slope from bootstrap validation was 0.98, indicating good agreement between predicted and observed 2-year recurrence. In model comparisons, the time-dependent AUC of the five-factor log-marker model was significantly higher than that of the five-factor raw-marker model (0.69 vs. 0.67, p=0.013) and the three-factor model (0.69 vs. 0.65, p<0.001). Across tertiles of total nomogram points (T1: 35.5-65.5 points, T2: >65.5-96.2 points, T3: >96.2-223 points), early recurrence rates were 25%, 38% and 58%, and early extrahepatic recurrence rates were 1.5%, 4.2% and 11%, respectively (Figure 4A and B, respectively). The trend in 2-year recurrence across tertiles was significant (p<0.001).

Discussion

In this study, elevations of log AFP and log DCP levels were associated with an increased risk of early postoperative recurrence. We developed a predictive nomogram for early recurrence based on five preoperative oncological factors, namely log AFP, log DCP, tumor size >3 cm, multiple tumors, and MVI. Patients with total nomogram points ≥96.2 showed an early recurrence rate of 58% and an early extrahepatic recurrence rate of 11%. The predictive ability of the five-factor log-marker model using log AFP and log DCP values was significantly better than that of the five-factor raw-marker model using raw AFP and DCP values and the three-factor model including only multiple tumors, tumor size, and MVI.

AFP and DCP are established tumor markers for HCC and are associated with tumor aggressiveness and poor prognosis (9, 12, 17, 18). Previous studies have generally evaluated their prognostic impact using fixed cut-off values (19). In contrast, our results showed that increases in log AFP and log DCP were associated with an increased risk of early recurrence after hepatic resection, suggesting that the risk of early recurrence increases proportionally with increases in the log-transformed levels of these markers. Even when AFP values were within the conventional normal range, log AFP value of 1.5-3.0 was associated with an early recurrence rate of approximately 40%. Likewise, increasing log DCP was associated with progressively higher early recurrence risk. These findings indicate that logarithmic transformation helps clarify differences in recurrence risk across AFP and DCP ranges.

Morphological tumor progression factors, including tumor size, multiple tumors, and MVI, were independent risk factors for early postoperative HCC recurrence. Although these factors are widely used in clinical staging systems (14, 20), the five-factor log-marker model incorporating log AFP and log DCP showed better predictive performance than both the three-factor model based only on morphological factors and the five-factor raw-marker model. Because AFP and DCP are complementary tumor markers, their combined assessment may provide more refined risk stratification. Our findings suggest that incorporation of log AFP and log DCP improves the precision of predicting postoperative early recurrence of HCC.

With recent progress in systemic therapy, perioperative treatment strategies for HCC are expected to expand (1, 21). In this study, we developed a nomogram based on five preoperative oncological factors to identify patients at high risk of early recurrence after hepatic resection. Patients with a nomogram score ≥96.2 showed an early recurrence rate of 58% and an early extrahepatic recurrence rate of 11%. Therefore, this nomogram may help identify patients who might be considered for perioperative systemic therapy in future clinical studies.

Study limitations. Firstly, its retrospective design may have introduced selection bias. Secondly, the long study period may have affected the results because diagnostic imaging, perioperative management, surgical techniques, treatment strategies for recurrent HCC, and the instruments and methods used to measure AFP and DCP may have changed over time. Finally, the nomogram was not externally validated. Multicenter prospective studies are required to validate these findings.

Conclusion

Elevated log AFP and log DCP values were associated with an increased risk of early postoperative recurrence. A nomogram incorporating these markers together with tumor size, multiple tumors, and MVI may help identify patients at high risk of postoperative recurrence.

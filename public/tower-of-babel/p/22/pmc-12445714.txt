Neurosurgical robot-assisted transnasal endoscopic surgery in orbital-related diseases
Authors: Rui Wu, Gangfeng Sun, Yixin Liu, Shuo Zhang, Xiaopeng Wang, Qiquan Zhu, Guan Wang, Yafei Wang, Weiping Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionTransnasal endoscopic surgery, with its minimal invasiveness and no facial incisions, is increasingly used for orbital-related diseases. Robot-assisted techniques enhance precision, safety, and efficacy, offering significant advantages in treating these complex conditions involving multiple anatomical structures and nterdisciplinary considerations.Research questionThis study reports our experience using robot-assisted transnasal endoscopic surgery for orbital-related diseases.Material and methodsWe retrospectively analyzed 162 patients (March 2021–July 2024) with orbital-related diseases who underwent robot-assisted transnasal endoscopic surgery: 137 optic nerve injuries, 8 orbital tumors, 8 cranio-orbital communication tumors, 5 orbital abscesses, 4 orbital foreign bodies. Outcomes were assessed via clinical follow-up and imaging.Results161 cases achieved satisfactory treatment without serious complications except for one patient underwent endovascular embolization due to intraoperative bleeding caused by pseudoaneurysm rupture. Among optic nerve injury patients, 66/137 (48.18 %) presented with vision improvement, (36/90 (40 %) were effective in those without light perception, 8/11 (72.73 %) were effective in those with light perception, and 22/36 (61.11 %) were effective in those with better light perception). A 100 % removal rate was achieved in orbital tumors, orbital peripheral abscesses, and foreign bodies. 6/8 (75 %) of cranial-orbital communication tumors were resected completely with the remaining two cases identified pathologically as melanoma and lymphoma.Discussion and conclusionRobot-assisted transnasal endoscopic surgery is a safe, effective and minimally invasive technique, showing potential advantages in procedures of orbital-related diseases such as optic nerve canal decompression, tumor resection, abscess drainage, and foreign body removal. Preoperative vascular-related examinations can be helpful to reduce the surgery-related complications.Keywords: Robotics, Orbital-related diseases, Transnasal endoscopic, Skull base neurosurgery

Introduction

The classification and anatomical structures associated with orbital-related diseases are particularly intricate, which may involve ophthalmology, rhinology, neurosurgery, neurology etc. The superior, inferior, and medial walls of the orbit are intimately associated with the paranasal sinuses, with the orbital apex being adjacent to the posterior ethmoid sinus and sphenoid sinus. Furthermore, the orbit connects to the skull base through structures including the superior orbital fissure, inferior orbital fissure, and optic canal (Lieber and Fernandez-Miranda, 2020; Villalonga et al., 2019). Traditional surgical procedures for orbital-related diseases include craniotomy and transorbital approach. Craniotomy allows extensive exposure of the surgical field, better surgical visualization and operational space. However, it has disadvantages such as greater surgical trauma, the existence of surgical blind spots, and higher risks of intracranial infection and hemorrhage. (Zoli et al., 2020). The transorbital approach has a relatively short surgical path, which reduces brain tissue damage and complications, and better exposes lesions in areas such as the medial orbital wall and orbital floor. Nevertheless, the transorbital approach has a relatively narrow operational space, which increases the risk of iatrogenic injury, leading to complications like vision loss and eye movement disorders, and insufficient exposure of deeper or more extensive skull base lesions (De Simone et al., 2024).The transnasal endoscopic techniques with the advantage of minimal trauma, fast recovery, clear vision, high precision, and fewer complications, its application has expanded from the central skull base region to the lateral skull base region, and the surgical approach for resecting skull base lesions has evolved from complex craniotomies to transnasal endoscopic surgery (Battaglia et al., 2020). For the anatomical proximity of the nasal cavity and paranasal sinuses to the orbit, transnasal endoscopic surgery has also been progressively used to treat lesions in the medial orbital wall, orbital floor, orbital apex, optic nerve, and the medial orbital area surrounding the optic nerve (Melder et al., 2020; Sindwani et al., 2024; Bleier et al., 2016).

The periorbital anatomy encompasses bony structures, muscles, nerves (the optic nerve, oculomotor nerve, trochlear nerve, abducens nerves, sympathetic nerves), and numerous blood vessels (Lieber and Fernandez-Miranda, 2020; Villalonga et al., 2019). The optic nerve enters the cranium via the optic canal. When orbital lesions occur, particularly neoplastic or traumatic, the orbital structure is prone to damage, leading to surgical procedures being more intricate and challenging. Therefore, achieving accurate real-time localization is crucial for the success of surgeries. Neurosurgical robotic navigation technology combined with the patient's computed tomography angiography (CTA) or Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) scans, enables more precise location of the lesions that display the lesion location and surgical path, significantly enhancing the precision and safety of the surgery (Guo et al., 2018), which has been extensively adopted in cranial neurosurgery (Stumpo et al., 2021; Pangal et al., 2022). Robot-assisted transnasal endoscopic surgery provides precise lesion positioning information, also will further enhance the safety and efficacy of surgeries for orbital-related diseases.

Orbital-related diseases commonly arise from trauma, tumors, infections, and orbital foreign bodies. This study presents a cohort of 162 patients with orbital-related diseases who underwent robot-assisted transnasal endoscopic surgery, aiming to describe the surgical experience and evaluate the procedure's efficacy.

Methods

Patient population

This retrospective study enrolled consecutive patients with orbital-related diseases who underwent robot-assisted transnasal endoscopic surgery at our hospital between March 2021 and July 2024. The inclusion criteria were: (1) clinical and imaging confirmation of orbital-related pathologies (optic nerve injury, orbital tumors, cranio-orbital communication tumors, orbital abscesses, or orbital foreign bodies); (2) surgical indication for robot-assisted transnasal endoscopic intervention. (3) Patients with potential skull base vascular injury were excluded according to their symptoms and preoperative imaging results. The study population comprised 162 eligible patients included 137 cases of optic nerve injury (115 males and 22 females), 8 cases of orbital tumors, 8 cases of cranio-orbital communication tumors, 5 cases of orbital abscesses, and 4 cases of orbital foreign bodies. Surgical procedures

Preoperative preparation

All patients underwent preoperative imaging with a 3D Gd-enhanced T2-weighted MRI (Siemens Magnetom Prisma 3.0T, Germany) or computed tomography angiography (CTA) (Siemens Somatom Force, Germany) of the head. The MRI and CTA images were then loaded into the robotic software for image fusion and 3D reconstruction. The surgical robots used were either the ROSA ONE platform (Medtech, Montpellier, France) or the Remebot platform (Remebot, China). Following general anesthesia, Patients were positioned supine with the head slightly elevated and turned to the right to facilitate nasal access, and securely fixed using a head holder. Registration was performed by the robot via an infrared camera, which identified the patient's facial landmarks and matched them with preoperative images, ensuring a registration error of less than 0.5 mm. Subsequently, robot-compatible instrument—a dedicated transnasal endoscope was prepared, and coordinate calibration between the endoscope and the robot's positioning system was completed. The surgeon planned the transnasal surgical trajectory on the 3D model.

Optic canal decompression and Medial Orbital Wall Reconstruction

Corrected the deviated nasal septum and exposed the middle nasal meatus with visualization of the uncinate process and ethmoid bulla. After incising the ethmoid bulla, the ethmoid sinuses were cleared to expose the medial orbital wall and the optic canal. The location of the optic canal was confirmed by using the surgical robot, and any damage to the optic canal was assessed. Parts of the inferomedial and superomedial walls of the optic canal were carefully thinned using a high-speed diamond bur. The intracranial and orbital openings of the optic canal were fully opened to observe pulsations of the optic nerve. The decompression range was evaluated using the robot. Subsequently, the roof of the ethmoid sinus was repaired if necessary with synthetic dura mater, supported by hemostatic fiber. In cases with severe medial orbital wall fractures, the wall was repaired by using artificial bone to prevent collapse. The middle turbinate was reseted and stabilized with hemostatic sponges tamponade. Blood clots and small bone fragments in the lower nasal passage and posterior nostrils were thoroughly removed. Intraoperative visual electrophysiological monitoring was performed throughout the procedure.

Resection of orbital tumors and cranio-orbital communication tumors

Transnasal endoscopic transethmoid approach or transsphenoidal approach was chosen according to the tumor location. The surgical robot identified the tumor margins to ensure adequate exposure during the procedure and it also reconfirmed the resection extent after tumor resection. Artificial dura mater and hemostatic gauze were used to fill the surgical site after hemostasis was performed in the operative area. In cases where cerebrospinal fluid leakage occurred, nasal mucosal flaps or myofascial repairs was used.

Intraorbital abscess drainaged

The transnasal endoscopic transethmoid approach was selected to expose the maxillary sinus, ethmoid sinus, and the medial orbital wall. The orbital periosteum was incised to ensure adequate drainage of pus sufficiently, and douched repeatedly with physiological sodium chloride solution with antibiotics. Robotic navigation and positioning were utilized to confirm endoscopically the complete drainage of the pus. After complete drainage of the pus, the medial orbital wall was reconstructed to prevent enophthalmos. Excessive postoperative tamponade in the operative area should be avoided to facilitate further abscess drainage.

Intraorbital foreign body removed

For the lesions due to orbital foreign bodies, transnasal endoscopic transethmoid approach or transsphenoidal approach was selected. Due to the sophisticated anatomic structures involved with orbital foreign bodies, the surgical field exposure is relatively extensive, including the medial orbital wall, ethmoid sinus, sphenoid sinus, anterior cranial base, optic nerve canal, and other crucial structures. During the surgery, the position of the internal carotid artery was ensured repeatedly using robotic real-time navigation to avoid injury. Carefully expose the foreign body to observe the relation between the foreign body and the surrounding structures, particularly the internal carotid artery. Thoroughly loosen the distal end of the foreign body to ensure it was completely free from the surrounding tissue structures and then slowly remove the foreign body. Foreign wooden objects should be removed intact and thoroughly cleaned of debris. The metal foreign body should be divided and removed using abrasive drilling techniques when it cannot be removed intact. After the foreign body was removed, thorough hemostasis was performed and the damage to surrounding tissues was observed.

All patients underwent postoperative nasal tamponade with hemostatic material and received anti-infective treatment for a duration of 3–4 days. For the patients with orbital abscesses, the anti-infective treatment was extended to a period of 4 weeks.

Postoperative recovery and outcomes assessment

For the patients undergoing optic nerve canal decompression, a head and orbital CT scan was conducted the day after surgery to evaluate the effect of surgery. During hospitalization, patients' visual acuity was monitored daily. All patients were followed up for a period of 6 months post-surgery. The visual acuity assessment criteria included five levels: I, no light perception (NLP); II, light perception (LP); III, hand motion (HM); IV, counting fingers (CF); V, logarithm of minimum angle of resolution (logMAR) with a visual acuity of 0.02 and above. Post-operative visual acuity was considered valid if it was improved by one level or more compared to pre-operative levels, or if logMAR visual acuity was improved by two or more lines. Conversely, no improvement in visual acuity was deemed ineffective.

For patients with orbital tumors, cranio-orbital communication tumors, orbital abscesses, and orbital foreign bodies, head CT or MRI scan was performed the day after surgery to evaluate the surgical outcome. Patients with orbital tumors or cranio-orbital communication tumors, which were suggestive of malignancy histopathologically, underwent radiotherapy and chemotherapy, with periodic follow-ups to monitor tumor recurrence.

Results

We performed robot-assisted transnasal endoscopic surgeries for 162 patients with orbital-related diseases. Among 137 patients with optic nerve injury, 66/137 (48.18 %) cases presented with vision improvement (36/90 (40 %) were effective in those without light perception, 8/11 (72.73 %) were effective in those with light perception, and 22/36 (61.11 %) were effective in those with better light perception). In patients with optic nerve injury with a disease duration of ≤7 days, the effective recovery rate of postoperative visual acuity was 58.18 % (32/55), while in those with a disease duration of >7 days, the effective recovery rate was 41.46 % (34/82). Additionally, robot-assisted transnasal endoscopic surgeries got a 100 % success rate in repairing orbital fractures, complete resection of orbital tumors, and removing orbital and peripheral abscesses and foreign bodies. For cranial-orbital communication tumors, a complete resection rate of 75 % (6/8) was achieved with two cases pathologically identified as melanoma and lymphoma. Except for one patient of optic nerve injury with intraoperative hemorrhage occurred in whom vascular lesions were not detected by routine preoperative CTA, emergency total cerebral angiography confirmed it was due to the rupture of a traumatic internal carotid artery pseudoaneurysm, all the other patients got favorable surgical outcomes, and no serious complications or no peri-operative mortality occurred. Discussion

Orbital-related diseases typically stem from trauma, tumors, infections, and orbital foreign bodies. The superior, inferior, and medial walls of the orbit are relatively thin and prone to fractures induced by external forces. Orbit is adjacent to the frontal, ethmoid and maxillary sinuses, which can involve the orbit in the event of sinus pathology such as inflammation and tumors. Surgical treatment of orbital-related diseases is a complex task that requires the surgeon to be familiar with orbital anatomy and to develop an appropriate surgical approach. Neurosurgical robots, which integrate multimodal image navigation, steady robotic arms, significantly enhance surgical precision through accurate targeting of deep-seated lesions and delicate real-time navigation, and boost safety by improving visualization with integrated endoscopes and supporting real-time alignment with pre-operative imaging, leading to their expanded application in craniocerebral operations such as deep brain stimulation (DBS), stereoelectroencephalography (SEEG), intracranial biopsy, intracerebral hemorrhage drainage, puncture guidance, auxiliary microneurosurgery, and auxiliary neuroendoscopic surgery (Liu et al., 2024; Ma et al., 2022; Ollivier et al., 2017; Gupta et al., 2020; Mansoor et al., 2024; Tabourel et al., 2022). Neurosurgical robot-assisted transnasal endoscopic surgery offers precise lesion positioning, resulting in improving surgical precision for orbital-related diseases.

In this retrospective study, we evaluated the application of neurosurgical robot-assisted transnasal endoscopic surgery in consecutive patients with orbital-related diseases, including optic nerve injury, orbital tumors, cranio-orbital communication tumors, orbital abscesses, and intraorbital foreign bodies. The primary outcome indicates that this surgical approach yielded high overall success rates: the majority of patients underwent successful procedures with no severe complications or perioperative mortality. Favorable clinical outcomes were observed, including vision improvement in optic nerve injury cases, complete resection of most tumors, successful drainage of abscesses, and full removal of foreign bodies. Notably, only one case of intraoperative hemorrhage occurred due to rupture of a traumatic internal carotid artery pseudoaneurysm, which was confirmed intraoperatively via emergency cerebral angiography.

Nasal endoscopic optic nerve canal decompression (EOND) is a well-established surgical technique for treating optic nerve injury (He et al., 2016). The principle of EOND involves removing bone fragments from the optic nerve canal and reducing pressure on the optic nerve from the canal wall, which increases the optic nerve blood flow, alleviates symptoms caused by optic nerve swelling and relative optic canal narrowing, prevents further vision loss and facilitates early vision restoration. In patients with traumatic orbital and anterior skull base fractures, the neurosurgical robotic navigation system can accurately identify the surgical site, avoid injury to critical vascular structures, and significantly enhance the precision and safety of the operation. The medial orbital wall is repaired by artificial bone to prevent collapse when the medial orbital wall is severely fractured to obstructs surgical access. Among the patients with optic nerve injuries collected, all cases were trauma-induced, with some accompanied by optic canal fractures. Postoperative vision improvement rates varied by initial visual function: 40 % in patients with no light perception, 72.73 % in those with light perception, and 61.11 % in those with better-than-light perception. Higher incidence among male patients potentially due to increased exposure to high-risk activities, such as outdoor work, elevated work, specific transportation modes, etc. Additionally, patients who underwent surgery within 7 days of injury had a higher recovery rate compared to those operated on after 7 days, suggesting a correlation between earlier intervention and better visual prognosis, which demonstrated the safety and effectiveness of robot-assisted transnasal endoscopic surgery, and there was a strong correlation between the severity of visual impairment and surgical successful rate.

In addition to patients with optic nerve injuries, we also enrolled patients with orbital-related diseases, including tumors, infections, and foreign bodies. Transnasal endoscopic surgery of orbital tumors have the advantages of precise localization, minimization of damage to adjacent crucial nerves and blood vessels, reduction in complications, and enhanced prognosis (Arai et al., 2016). Robotic navigation allows for more accurate lesion localization to improve surgical efficiency correspondingly. Of tumor cases, eight orbital tumors were totally resected, while six out of eight cranio-orbital communication tumors were completely resected with two identified as melanoma and lymphoma pathologically. In the cases of orbital peripheral abscesses and foreign bodies, complete removal got in all cases. Orbital abscesses typically arise from bacterial infections following trauma or the extension of inflammation from neighboring structures, commonly manifested as eye pain, eyelid redness and swelling, blurred vision, exophthalmos, and purulent discharge (Hornblass et al., 1984). Transnasal endoscopic drainage of these abscesses is both efficacious and devoid of significant complications (Kessler et al., 1998). Intraorbital foreign bodies are aggressive intraorbital disorders that involve numerous structures (You et al., 2022). During the procedure, wooden foreign bodies should be extracted intact and thoroughly cleaned of debris. Metal foreign bodies should be removed after grinding and drilling using grinding drills or cutting machines if they cannot be removed as a whole (You et al., 2021; Zhao et al., 2020).

The favorable outcomes of robot-assisted transnasal endoscopic surgery can be attributed to the unique advantages of the approach, enhanced by robotic assistance. The transnasal route itself offers shorter trajectories, clearer visualization of deep orbital structures, and minimal damage to adjacent tissues, avoiding visible facial scarring and eyeball injury—advantages over traditional transorbital or transcranial approaches (Battaglia et al., 2020). Neurosurgical robots further augment these benefits through multimodal image navigation, steady robotic arms, enabling accurate targeting of deep lesions (e.g., optic canal, orbital apex) and delicate real-time navigation (Liu et al., 2024). For example, in optic canal decompression, robotic navigation helps avoid accidental neurovascular injury; in tumor resection, it improves lesion localization to minimize damage to critical structures like the optic nerve and oculomotor nerves. Our findings align with existing literature supporting transnasal endoscopy as a safe and effective option for orbital pathologies. (Zhao et al., 2021; Sdeeq Rashid et al., 2025; Matoušek et al., 2022).

The orbit and surrounding neurovascular structures are sophisticated and abundant with blood vessels. Tumors or trauma can lead to the destruction of normal structures, which can complicate intraoperative procedures and potentially result in severe complications. Intraoperative hemorrhage due to vascular injury is a catastrophic surgical complication during transnasal endoscopic surgery (Yu et al., 2024). One of our cases experienced intraoperative hemorrhage during optic canal decompression. This hemorrhage was confirmed to be caused by a trauma-induced pseudoaneurysm of the internal carotid artery through emergency intraoperative total cerebral angiography, which had not been detected by routine preoperative CTA. Therefore, preoperative total cerebral angiography is necessary for identifying potential vascular abnormalities in patients with optic nerve injury who is performed surgical optic canal decompression.

In this research, the limitation is the sample size of enrolled patients with orbital-related diseases relatively small, particularly with respect to orbital tumors, orbital abscesses, and orbital foreign bodies, which may introduce biases into the data analysis. In subsequent investigations, we will accumulate a larger cohort of relevant cases to further validate the surgical efficacy. Nonetheless, this study yields several meaningful insights: (1) Routine implementation of total cerebral angiography is recommended for patients with traumatic optic nerve injury undergoing decompression, to identify occult vascular anomalies (e.g., pseudoaneurysms) that might be overlooked by CTA or MRI. (2) For traumatic optic nerve injury, surgical intervention within 7 days of the injury, when clinically feasible, is advisable to optimize recovery rates. (3) For foreign body removal, tailored strategies—including intact extraction for wooden fragments and grinding for metallic objects—should be employed to minimize tissue damage, with robotic navigation utilized to enhance procedural precision.

Conclusion

Transnasal endoscopy appears to be a promising option for orbital-related diseases, with transnasal endoscopic surgery showing potential advantages in procedures such as optic nerve canal decompression, orbital wall reconstruction, tumor resection, abscess drainage, and foreign body removal, among others. Neurosurgical robotic-assisted transnasal endoscopic surgery may further contribute to enhancing the safety and efficacy of these surgeries in the context of our retrospective case series. Additionally, preoperative vascular examination remains essential to help minimize potential complications.

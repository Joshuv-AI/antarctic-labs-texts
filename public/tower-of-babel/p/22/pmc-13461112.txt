NeoCardioLab: A Digital Platform for Neonatal Hemodynamics Education
Authors: Gabriel Altit
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionTargeted neonatal echocardiography and neonatal hemodynamics are increasingly integrated into neonatal intensive care practice. However, access to structured, standardized educational resources and reference tools remains variable across settings. We aimed to describe the global reach, patterns of utilization, and perceived educational value of NeoCardioLab, a digital platform designed for neonatal hemodynamics education and support.MethodsWe conducted a descriptive, mixed-methods study combining retrospective analysis of anonymized usage data from January 2019 to June 2025 with a cross-sectional anonymous online survey conducted from December 2024 to July 2025. Data sources included Google Analytics, App Store Connect, Calconic, and YouTube analytics. Survey responses captured user demographics, utilization patterns, and perceived educational value.ResultsNeoCardioLab reached 98,004 active users across 203 countries between April 2022 and June 2025, with 342,911 page views and 5,861 mobile application downloads. English (75%), Spanish (6%), and French (5%) were the most common user languages from browser settings. Calculator tools were accessed 1,951 times over a 12-month period. Among 128 survey respondents (59% neonatologists), 84% reported increased confidence in neonatal hemodynamics, and perceived usefulness was noted for modules on pulmonary hypertension, congenital heart disease, and congenital diaphragmatic hernia. Reported impacts reflect self-perceived influence on knowledge and practice.ConclusionsOur results suggest that NeoCardioLab has broad global reach and sustained user engagement as an open-access digital platform for neonatal hemodynamics. These findings support a potential role as a complementary educational resource and knowledge translation tool across diverse educational settings in neonatology.

Introduction

Digital health platforms are increasingly used to support medical education and clinical practice, particularly in subspecialties that rely on dynamic visual interpretation and integration of complex physiologic data, such as neonatal hemodynamics. The growing incorporation of targeted neonatal echocardiography (TnECHO) into neonatal intensive care unit practice has expanded clinicians’ ability to assess cardiovascular physiology at the bedside but has also introduced challenges related to training, standardization, and access to reference materials [1]. In many settings, clinicians must integrate imaging findings, physiologic data, and evolving evidence in real time, often without readily accessible, structured educational resources or normative datasets. Despite this need, educational resources in neonatal hemodynamics remain fragmented, variably accessible, and not consistently standardized across institutions. While digital tools have emerged to address similar gaps in other areas of medicine [2–6], few platforms in neonatal cardiovascular care integrate multimodal educational content with reference data and point-of-care support tools within a single environment [7–9]. Furthermore, the real-world utilization and reach of such platforms, particularly across diverse geographic and practice settings, have not been well described. NeoCardioLab (www.neocardiolab.com) was developed as an open-access digital ecosystem to support education and knowledge translation in neonatal hemodynamics and TnECHO [10]. The platform integrates educational modules, case-based learning, echocardiographic image libraries, video tutorials, and interactive calculators based on published reference data. Content spans key domains including neonatal cardiovascular physiology, pulmonary hypertension, congenital heart disease (including fetal and neonatal imaging), cardiovascular pharmacology, and disease-specific applications such as congenital diaphragmatic hernia. These resources are designed to complement formal training programs, support bedside learning, and promote consistency in hemodynamic assessment. NeoCardioLab was designed to address three interrelated needs in neonatal cardiovascular care: (1) improving access to evidence-based educational content, (2) facilitating the use of normative datasets and structured tools for physiologic interpretation, and (3) supporting standardization of hemodynamic assessment across educational settings. Since its launch, NeoCardioLab has evolved from a website primarily focused on TnECHO into a broader digital ecosystem encompassing neonatal hemodynamics, pulmonary hypertension, congenital heart disease, fetal echocardiography, cardiovascular pharmacology, educational videos, mobile applications, interactive clinical calculators, and other educational support resources accessible through web-based and mobile platforms. Content is developed, reviewed, and updated by the NeoCardioLab team [11] under the supervision of the lead investigator and is informed by emerging scientific literature, user feedback obtained through surveys and social media engagement, and evolving areas of interest within neonatal cardiovascular care [12, 13]. This progressive expansion of content and educational formats may have contributed to the increasing utilization and global engagement observed over time. Given the increasing reliance on digital resources for medical education, it is important to better understand how such platforms are used in practice. In this study, we evaluate user engagement with NeoCardioLab across its web platform, mobile applications, embedded calculators, and associated digital channels. The objective of this study was to describe patterns of utilization and global reach and to characterize how users interact with a multimodal digital platform designed for neonatal hemodynamics education and support.

Methods

This study is a descriptive, observational, mixed-methods evaluation of the NeoCardioLab digital platform. It combines a retrospective analysis of anonymized, aggregate usage data across multiple digital channels with a cross-sectional, anonymous user survey to characterize user demographics, patterns of use, and perceived educational value. Retrospective, aggregate usage data were collected to evaluate engagement with NeoCardioLab between January 1, 2019, and June 30, 2025. Data sources included Google Analytics, App Store Connect, Google Play Console, Calconic calculator metrics, and YouTube analytics. No patient-level or identifiable data were collected. Website usage data for www.neocardiolab.com were obtained from Google Analytics Universal Analytics (January 2019 to April 2022) and Google Analytics 4 (April 2022 to June 2025). Standard platform-defined metrics were used, including users (defined as distinct active users within a specified time period), new users (first-time visitors), sessions (individual visits), and page views (total number of pages viewed on the website). Geographic location and language settings were inferred from device and browser data. Given the transition from Universal Analytics to Google Analytics 4, which differ in tracking methodology, metrics were harmonized using standard definitions where possible, and results are presented descriptively without assuming strict comparability across systems. Page-level engagement data were analyzed to identify high-utilization content areas, including congenital heart disease, pulmonary hypertension, normative values, and echocardiographic imaging modules.

NeoCardioLab hosts interactive calculators designed for educational and clinical support purposes. Usage data for selected calculators were obtained through the Calconic platform, which provides aggregate traffic metrics. Calculator activity was assessed using impressions, defined as the number of times a calculator interface was loaded, for the period from July 1, 2024, to June 30, 2025. Calculators included tools related to patent ductus arteriosus assessment, pulmonary hypertension phenotyping, and cardiac output estimation.

Mobile application usage data were obtained from Apple App Store Connect (iOS) and Google Play Console (Android) for applications available since October 2021. Metrics included cumulative downloads and geographic distribution of users. Social media and video engagement were assessed using publicly available analytics from X (formerly Twitter), LinkedIn, and YouTube. Metrics included follower and subscriber counts, video views, cumulative watch time, and temporal growth trends. YouTube data were analyzed from channel inception on March 24, 2020, to June 30, 2025.

To complement the analytics-based evaluation, an anonymous web-based survey was conducted between December 17, 2024, and July 22, 2025 to explore user characteristics, usage patterns, and perceived educational value. The survey was developed using Google Forms and disseminated via the NeoCardioLab website, mailing lists, and social media platforms. The survey was developed by the NeoCardioLab team based on the study objectives and prior experience evaluating educational initiatives. The questionnaire underwent iterative review by members of the team to assess clarity, content relevance, and usability prior to dissemination. A formal pilot validation study was not performed. Participation was voluntary and open to individuals aged 18 years or older. No predefined sampling frame was used, and the total number of users exposed to the survey invitation could not be determined; therefore, response rates could not be calculated. No personal identifiers were collected unless respondents voluntarily provided an email address for mailing list purposes. The survey included both quantitative and qualitative components. Demographic data collected included country of practice, professional role, and years of clinical experience. Participants were asked about frequency and modality of platform use, including website, mobile applications, and calculator tools. The survey also assessed perceived usefulness of specific content areas, such as pulmonary hypertension, congenital heart disease, and congenital diaphragmatic hernia modules, and explored self-reported impact on knowledge, confidence, and clinical practice. Open-ended questions were included to capture qualitative feedback on strengths, limitations, and areas for improvement.

Quantitative data were summarized using descriptive statistics, including counts, frequencies, percentages, and measures of central tendency where appropriate. Temporal trends in website traffic and user acquisition were visualized over time. Analyses were conducted using Stata/SE 14.2 (StataCorp, College Station, TX, USA). No inferential statistical testing was performed, as the study was designed to provide a descriptive assessment of platform utilization. Free-text survey responses were analyzed using an inductive content analysis approach. Initial coding was performed by the corresponding author, who has prior experience conducting qualitative and thematic analyses in healthcare research [14, 15]. Codes and themes were iteratively reviewed and refined to identify recurring patterns in user feedback. Representative themes were generated to summarize user perspectives on platform utility and areas for improvement. The study was approved by the McGill University Health Centre Research Ethics Board.

Results

Between January 1, 2019, and June 30, 2025, the NeoCardioLab website recorded 142,599 user visits, with a progressive increase in annual user counts over time (Table 1, Fig. 1). From April 1, 2022 to June 30, 2025, the platform was accessed by users in 203 countries (Google Analytics 4). The highest number of users originated from the USA (28,088; 29%), Canada (12,853; 13%), India (10,997; 11%), the UK (2,770; 3%), Mexico (1,948; 2%), Brazil (1,866; 2%), Australia (1,833; 2%), Thailand (1,763; 2%), Italy (1,570; 2%), and Poland (1,531; 2%). Multilingual engagement was observed, with the most common user languages from browser settings being English (72,852; 74.7%), Spanish (6,107; 6.3%), French (4,807; 4.9%), Portuguese (2,070; 2.1%), Italian (1,541; 1.6%), Russian (1,048; 1.1%), Chinese (1,004; 1.0%), Turkish (916; 0.9%), German (844; 0.9%), and Arabic (750; 0.8%), representing more than 95% of recorded language use. From April 1, 2022 to June 30, 2025, the platform recorded 98,004 active users, including 96,186 new users, and 342,911 page views based on Google Analytics 4 data (Fig. 2).

Between October 2021 and July 2025, the NeoCardioLab mobile applications were downloaded 5,120 times on iOS and 741 times on Android, across 176 countries (Fig. 3). For Android devices, the application maintained an install base of 741 active devices as of July 2025. However, available mobile analytics did not permit reliable determination of the proportion of repeat users or users who discontinued use following download. From July 1, 2024, to June 30, 2025, embedded calculators received 9,245 impressions, 2,669 views, and 1,951 user interactions. The most frequently accessed tools were the patent ductus arteriosus scoring calculator, the pulmonary hypertension phenotype tool, and the cardiac output estimator. Digital engagement extended across social media platforms. As of July 2025, NeoCardioLab had 3,135 followers on X (formerly Twitter), 2,378 LinkedIn connections, and 1,932 YouTube subscribers, with 72,193 cumulative video views and 6,600 h of watch time between March 2020 and June 2025.

A total of 128 respondents completed our user survey (Table 2). The majority were neonatologists (n = 75, 59%), followed by cardiologists (n = 18, 14%), residents or fellows (n = 14, 11%), and other healthcare professionals (n = 21, 16%). Most respondents were aged 30–49 years (n = 71, 55.5%), with a slight female predominance (55%). Platform engagement among respondents was frequent, with 45% reporting weekly use, 31% monthly use, and 9% daily use. Self-reported confidence in neonatal hemodynamics increased following use of the platform, with 40% of respondents reporting being confident or very confident prior to use compared to 61% after engagement (Table 3). A majority of respondents (84%) reported a perceived increase in confidence in neonatal hemodynamics concepts. The platform was most frequently used in relation to pulmonary hypertension, congenital heart disease, congenital diaphragmatic hernia, and cardiovascular instability. Survey responses indicated that users perceived the platform as influencing their understanding of neonatal cardiovascular care. Among respondents, 91/128 (71%) reported that the platform moderately or significantly influenced their clinical practice and decision-making. Additionally, 98/128 (77%) indicated that the website had influenced their approach to cardiovascular management in the neonatal intensive care unit, while 66/128 (52%) reported that NeoCardioLab resources had influenced their pharmacological choices regarding inotropic support, with an additional 22/128 (17%) indicating a possible influence. Furthermore, 89/128 (70%) reported that they sometimes, often, or always incorporated learnings from NeoCardioLab case studies and clinical scenarios into their clinical practice. Most respondents reported incorporating knowledge from the platform into clinical reasoning and case interpretation, particularly through educational modules and case-based materials. Reported impacts reflect self-perceived influence on knowledge, confidence, and clinical approach.

Discussion

This study presents a descriptive mixed-methods assessment of NeoCardioLab, a freely accessible digital platform focused on neonatal hemodynamics, cardiovascular education, and knowledge translation. Since its inception, the platform has demonstrated sustained growth in global engagement, with users from over 200 countries and broad international access across multiple language settings, despite the platform being primarily available in English. In parallel, mobile applications and social media channels contributed to extending the platform’s reach and facilitating ongoing user interaction. Together, these findings highlight a substantial international demand for structured, accessible learning resources in a neonatal subspecialty such as neonatal hemodynamics.

The development and expansion of NeoCardioLab parallel the emergence of other digital tools in neonatology and pediatrics, such as PediTools, which introduced widely adopted growth and clinical calculators into routine practice [16]. Similar to these platforms, NeoCardioLab addresses a challenge in subspecialty neonatal education: the need to integrate complex physiologic concepts and imaging-based assessments into time-sensitive clinical reasoning. However, NeoCardioLab differs in its integration of educational modules, normative datasets, and interactive tools within a single curated environment. More broadly, these findings align with growing evidence that open-access, specialty-specific digital platforms can expand access to advanced clinical knowledge and support knowledge dissemination across diverse settings [17]. Digital health tools have been shown to improve access to education and engagement across geographic and resource boundaries, particularly in underserved regions [18, 19] and have demonstrated effectiveness in other fields of medicine including chronic disease management [20, 21].

The increasing integration of TnECHO into clinical care has been accompanied by the development of training frameworks and guidelines from international expert groups, including the American Society of Echocardiography and collaborators [12, 13]. Formal recognition of TnECHO training has now been adopted in several jurisdictions, including Europe, Canada, and Australia [22–25]. Despite these advances, access to formal training remains limited to a relatively small number of specialized centers worldwide [26]. In this context, digital platforms may serve as complementary resources that support foundational knowledge acquisition and reinforce learning within and outside of formal training environments. Importantly, such tools are not intended to replace structured training, certification, or mentorship, but rather to augment existing educational pathways and facilitate broader access to evidence-informed concepts in neonatal cardiovascular care. The observed patterns of engagement across website content, calculators, and multimedia resources suggest that users are interacting with a range of educational formats, including case-based materials and applied tools [27]. The frequent use of modules related to pulmonary hypertension, congenital heart disease, and congenital diaphragmatic hernia reflects areas of clinical complexity where bedside physiologic interpretation is critical. While interaction metrics and repeat usage suggest perceived utility, these measures do not directly quantify knowledge acquisition or clinical competence.

Survey findings complement the analytics data by providing insight into user characteristics and perceived value. Respondents reported frequent use of the platform and described increased confidence in neonatal hemodynamics following engagement. Reported influences on clinical reasoning and practice should be interpreted as self-perceived effects rather than objective measures of change. Together, these findings suggest that users perceive NeoCardioLab as a useful adjunct to learning and clinical interpretation, particularly in areas requiring integration of physiologic and imaging-based information.

This study has several limitations. The observational and descriptive design precludes any inference regarding causal impact on clinical practice or patient outcomes. Although usage metrics demonstrate reach and engagement, they do not provide information on knowledge acquisition, diagnostic accuracy, or clinical performance. Survey findings are subject to selection bias, as respondents likely represent a more engaged subset of users, and the absence of a defined sampling frame prevents estimation of response rates or representativeness. Also, survey results represent self-reported perceptions rather than objective measures of practice change or clinical performance. Analytics data were derived from multiple platforms with differing methodologies, and although efforts were made to harmonize metrics, direct comparisons across systems should be interpreted cautiously. Fourth, detailed user-level information, including training level and institutional context, was not available. Additionally, the survey was conducted in English, which may have limited participation from non-English-speaking users. Importantly, the platform’s content is curated and based on published evidence; however, it reflects the perspective of a single expert group and has not undergone formal peer review in its entirety. While regular updates aim to maintain accuracy, the dynamic nature of digital content may introduce variability in how information is accessed and applied. Access to digital platforms is influenced by factors such as internet connectivity, device availability, and digital literacy, which may limit equitable utilization across settings [27]. In addition, the mobile application analytics platforms provided cumulative download and install-base metrics but limited information regarding long-term user retention and repeat utilization. Consequently, it was not possible to determine how frequently individual users returned to the application or to distinguish sustained engagement from one-time use. Finally, anonymized aggregate analytics data did not allow determination of user intent, and it was therefore not possible to distinguish educational use from potential clinical decision-support use of the platform’s resources and calculators.

Conclusion

Our study suggests that the NeoCardioLab digital ecosystem demonstrates broad global reach and sustained engagement as a free platform for neonatal hemodynamics education. These findings suggest a potential role for digital tools as complementary resources for knowledge translation and education in neonatal subspecialties such as in neonatal cardiovascular care, particularly across diverse clinical settings. As neonatal hemodynamics and other subspecialties continues to evolve, such platforms may help support access to diverse educational content. Future work should focus on developing standardized evaluation frameworks and incorporating objective measures of learning and clinical performance to better define the role and impact of digital educational platforms in neonatology.

VISION AND METAMORPHOPSIA OUTCOMES OF MACULAR BUCKLING FOR FOVEOSCHISIS-ASSOCIATED MACULAR DETACHMENT IN HIGHLY MYOPIC EYES
Authors: Qi Zhang, Xiujuan Zhao, Bingqian Liu, Shida Chen, Xia Huang, Qingxiu Wu, Jinlian Zhan, Lin Lu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Purpose:To assess the functional outcomes in visual acuity, metamorphopsia, and vision-related quality of life (VR-QOL) and to evaluate prognostic factors after macular buckling (MB) surgery in eyes with high myopia and foveoschisis (FS)-associated macular detachment (MD).Methods:Thirty-nine eyes of 39 patients with FS-associated MD who underwent MB surgery were enrolled. Measured outcomes comprised best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA), metamorphopsia, VR-QOL, axial length (AL), macular reattachment, and resolution of foveoschisis. In addition, factors affecting final BCVA and metamorphopsia were analyzed.Results:At 12 months postoperatively, 36 eyes (92.31%) achieved macular reattachment, 37 eyes (94.87%) achieved complete resolution of foveoschisis, and metamorphopsia diminished in 31 eyes (79.49%). LogMAR BCVAs at baseline and months 1, 3, 6, and 12 postoperatively were 0.62 ± 0.35 (20/83), 0.65 ± 0.3 (20/89), 0.59 ± 0.31 (20/77), 0.54 ± 0.31 (20/69), and 0.46 ± 0.27 (20/57) (P < 0.001), respectively. Metamorphopsia scores by M-CHARTS were 1.36° ± 0.51°, 1.04° ± 0.51°, 0.74° ± 0.47°, 0.59° ± 0.47°, and 0.13° ± 0.29° (P < 0.001). All Visual Function Questionnaire-25 subscales demonstrated significant improvement postoperatively, with the exception of “general health” (P = 0.08) and “driving” (P = 0.111). Preoperative BCVA was an independent risk factor for postoperative BCVA at month 12 (r = 0.638, P < 0.001), and the preoperative M-score was an independent risk factor for postoperative M-score at month 12 (r = 0.187, P = 0.045).Conclusion:MB surgery significantly improved BCVA, metamorphopsia, and VR-QOL in patients with FS-associated MD. Preoperative BCVA and metamorphopsia score were prognostic factors for postoperative BCVA and metamorphopsia score at month 12.

Macular buckling (MB) surgery not only improves vision but also significantly improves metamorphopsia and vision-related quality of life in highly myopic eyes with foveoschisis-associated macular detachment.

Foveoschisis (FS) stands out as the most prevalent manifestation of myopic traction maculopathy (MTM) observed through optical coherence tomography (OCT) imaging in cases of high myopia.1 Notably, FS-associated macular detachment (MD) has been identified as the principal factor leading to substantial vision loss.2,3 The consequential impact of FS-associated MD, often compounded by metamorphopsia, significantly influences the patients' lives.4 Previous studies have demonstrated that macular buckling (MB) surgery exhibits superior anatomical success rates as well as best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) when treating FS-associated MD in high myopia.5,6 However, the evaluation of visual function in those studies primarily relied on visual acuity (VA). Despite successful MB surgery and enhanced VA, postoperative visual function may remain unsatisfactory in certain cases. Metamorphopsia, a frequently observed symptom in patients with myopic traction maculopathy both before and after surgery, demands consideration beyond visual acuity.5,6 Consequently, evaluating visual function solely based on VA proves insufficient, emphasizing the pivotal clinical relevance of metamorphopsia.

M-CHARTS emerges as the predominant tool for quantifying metamorphopsia in macular diseases. Previous investigations utilizing M-CHARTS have explored metamorphopsia in patients with epiretinal membrane (ERM),7 macular hole (MH),8 macular edema,9 rhegmatogenous retinal detachment (RRD),10 and age-related macular degeneration (AMD).11 The 25-item National Eye Institute Visual Function Questionnaire (VFQ-25) is employed for assessing vision-related quality of life (VR-QOL).12 A compelling earlier study unearthed a significant correlation between the severity of metamorphopsia and VR-QOL, both preoperatively and postoperatively.13 Strikingly, no study has delved into metamorphopsia in patients with myopic traction maculopathy undergoing MB surgery. This study aims to comprehensively assess postoperative outcomes in visual function, encompassing VA and metamorphopsia, and scrutinize prognostic factors in patients with FS-associated MD after MB surgery.

Patients and Study Design

This prospective, consecutive case series enrolled individuals with high myopia experiencing FS-associated MD who underwent MB surgery. Recruitment took place between April 2022 and July 2022 at the Zhongshan Ophthalmic Center. Inclusion criteria comprised patients with FS-associated MD in highly myopic eyes, characterized by an axial length ≥26.5 mm or a refractive error (spherical equivalent) ≤−8.0 diopter. Exclusion criteria were defined as follows: the presence of a full-thickness macular hole (FTMH), severe macular scar, MD extending beyond the major vascular arcades in more than one quadrant, intraocular active hemorrhage or inflammation, a history of vitrectomy or scleral buckling, and any media opacity impeding macular imaging or clinical evaluation. Comprehensive assessments were conducted for all patients, including the BCVA test, metamorphopsia test, VR-QOL questionnaire, axial length (AL) measurement using an IOLMaster, slit-lamp biomicroscopy, fundus photography, and indirect ophthalmoscopy. OCT images were acquired using a SPECTRALIS OCT (Heidelberg Engineering, Heidelberg, Germany), with both horizontal and vertical scans through the fovea center selected for analysis. Data were collected preoperative baseline and at 1, 3, 6, and 12 months following MB surgery. This study received approval from the ethics committee of the Zhongshan Ophthalmic Center (2022QXPJ003), and all procedures adhered to the relevant local regulations and the principles delineated in the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients.

Assessment of Visual Function and Anatomy

Visual function and anatomical parameters including BCVA, metamorphopsia, foveoschisis resolution, and macular detachment height (MDH) were assessed at baseline and at 1, 3, 6, and 12 months postoperatively. BCVA was measured using a Snellen VA chart and then converted to the logarithm of minimal angle of resolution (logMAR) VA for analysis. Metamorphopsia was quantified using M-CHARTS, while the resolution of foveoschisis and MDH (the average distance of vertical and horizontal macular detachment at the center of the fovea) was measured using the OCT.

Evaluation of VR-QOL

The VR-QOL was assessed preoperative baseline and at 1, 3, 6, and 12 months following MB surgery. The VFQ-25 questionnaire comprising 25 items was self-administered with patients receiving detailed verbal instructions and assistance as needed. The questionnaire gauges patients' perceptions of difficulty in visual symptoms and day-to-day activities, and responses are categorized into 12 subscales: general health, general vision, ocular pain, near activities, distance activities, social functioning, mental health, role difficulties, dependency, driving, color vision, and peripheral vision. Subscale scores were transformed to a scale ranging from 0 to 100, with 100 indicating the highest possible function or minimal patient impairment. The VFQ-25 composite score was derived as the mean score of all items, excluding general health items.12

Surgical Procedure

A T-shaped titanium-strengthened silicone buckle implant was employed in this study (Ratina Co. Ltd, Guangdong, China). The surgery, performed under retrobulbar or general anesthesia, commenced with a 360° limbal periotomy. To facilitate eye rotation during placement, the superior, lateral, and inferior rectus muscles were encircled using 4-0 silk ties. The buckle implant was positioned in the superotemporal quadrant. The inferior silicone band traversed beneath the inferior oblique muscle and the lateral rectus muscle, securing sutures to the nasal side of the inferior rectus muscle, 12 mm posterior to the limbus, using a spatulated needle with 5-0 nonabsorbable thread (Ethicon, Johnson & Johnson Medical Ltd, Norderstedt, Germany). Simultaneously, the other end of the band passed beneath the superior rectus muscle, with sutures fastened to the nasal side of the superior rectus muscle, also 12 mm posterior to the limbus. After this, anterior chamber paracentesis was performed using a 30-G needle to reduce intraocular pressure. The titanium arm was sutured beneath the lateral rectus muscle, and the positioning and indentation of the macular plate were meticulously assessed under indirect ophthalmoscopy.

Statistical Analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS software version 27.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL) for Windows. Continuous data were expressed as mean ± SD. The analysis encompassed comparisons among the five time points for BCVA, metamorphopsia score, MDH, each VFQ-25 subscale, and composite scores using ANOVA. Univariate analyses were employed to explore associations between prognostic factors and final BCVA and M-scores. Multiple linear regression models were constructed to investigate associations with final BCVA and M-scores. A significance level of P < 0.05 was applied for statistical significance.

Results

A total of 39 eyes from 39 participants (16 men and 23 women) were analyzed in this study. The mean age was 51.54 ± 10.27 years (range: 29–77 years), with a BCVA of 0.62 ± 0.35 logMAR (20/83), M-scores measured from M-CHARTS at 1.36° ± 0.51°, and an AL of 29.73 ± 1.77 mm. Anatomical Recovery After MB Surgery

Over the 12-month follow-up period, 36 eyes (92.31%) achieved anatomical macular reattachment. Three patients displayed significant absorption of subretinal fluid, with only a small residual amount. The MDH gradually decreased postsurgery, and at month 12, 37 eyes (94.87%) achieved complete recovery of foveoschisis. The AL demonstrated a significant shortening at the 1-month follow-up, with a gradual reduction in the surgical indentation over time, leading to a mean decrease in AL of 2.61 mm at the 1-year postoperatively follow-up (Table 2).

Visual Function Improved After MB Surgery

Overall, a significant improvement in mean BCVA was observed following MB surgery. BCVA improved in 23 eyes (58.97%), while 16 eyes (41.03%) remained stable. The mean preoperative BCVA was 0.62 ± 0.35 logMAR (20/83), and the mean postoperative values were 0.65 ± 0.3 (20/89), 0.59 ± 0.31 (20/77), 0.54 ± 0.31 (20/69), and 0.46 ± 0.27 (20/57) at months 1, 3, 6, and 12, respectively, with the differences being statistically significant (P < 0.001). Metamorphopsia scores from M-CHARTS showed significant improvement at months 1, 3, 6, and 12 (Table 3; Figure 1). At month 12, eight eyes (20.51%) still exhibited metamorphopsia using M-CHARTS. VFQ-25 composite score significantly improved after MB surgery, with all subscales reaching their highest values at month 12. The differences were statistically significant for all subscales except for “general health” (P = 0.08) and “driving” (P = 0.111) subscales (Table 4).

Discussion

This study delved into the changes in vision acuity, metamorphopsia, and VR-QOL in patients with FS-associated MD undergoing MB surgery. Overall, MB surgery demonstrated superior postoperative improvement in BCVA, metamorphopsia, and VR-QOL. Postoperatively, BCVA steadily improved to reach 0.46 ± 0.27 logMAR (20/57), and metamorphopsia scores gradually ameliorated over time, reaching 0.13°±0.29° by M-CHARTS at the 12-month follow-up. Our results revealed that 92.31% of eyes achieved macular reattachment, and 94.87% achieved complete resolution of foveoshisis. The anatomical, visual acuity, and metamorphopsia outcomes all exhibited significant improvement after MB surgery.

Myopic traction retinopathy stems from eyeball elongation resulting from continuous stretching and thinning of the sclera. Performing PPV from inside the eyeball, allowing the detached retina to adhere to the expanded eyeball wall, can yield various outcomes. First, the retina may unreattachable. Second, there may be the formation of iatrogenic MH or the development of MH postoperatively.5 Third, successful reattachment of the retina may be accompanied by the progression of atrophic myopic maculopathy.14 MB surgery, as demonstrated in a previous study, proved superior to PPV for FS-associated MD treatment.5 MB not only alleviates internal surface vitreous traction but also addresses the global cause of traction by bringing the eyeball wall closer to the neurosensory retina. This approach helps avoid adverse consequences associated with PPV surgery mentioned earlier.

Traditionally, long-standing metamorphopsia was believed to persist even after successful MB surgery due to the dome-shaped indentation on the posterior sclera. However, our study contradicts this belief, as BCVA and metamorphopsia significantly improved with subretinal fluid absorption and foveoschisis resolution. A significant decrease in BCVA was observed as myopic traction maculopathy progressed to stage T3 (FS associated with MD).15 In clinical practice, surgeons always consider T3 as a surgical indication. In addition, the presence and evolution of metamorphopsia may be influenced by the outer lamellar macular hole (O-LMH), a critical sign in the progression of MTM.16 However, since all eyes in our study were at stage 3aO at baseline, we were unable to determine whether O-LMH serves as a significant factor in determining the presence and postoperative evolution of metamorphopsia. It is noteworthy that even when the lesion progresses to T1 or T2, symptoms of metamorphopsia are already present. Preoperative BCVA and M-scores were identified as independent risk factors for final BCVA and M-scores. Furthermore, MB surgery significantly enhanced the VR-QOL in patients with FS-associated MD. While all subscales at month 12 exhibited higher values compared with baseline, with the composite score and 10 of 12 subscales showing significant improvement, the VFQ-25 composite score at month 12 (68.85 ± 14.92) still remained relatively low when compared with the quality of life in healthy individuals. Thus, considering relaxing surgical indications in clinical practice may lead to better final visual function.

Nevertheless, our study has several limitations. First, previous studies noted BCVA improvement at the 1-year follow-up, and in our study, eight patients still exhibited metamorphopsia at 1 year, suggesting the need for long-term follow-up. Second, the sample size was relatively small. Third, no comparison was made with PPV surgery. In conclusion, our study suggests that MB surgery successfully improves visual acuity, metamorphopsia, and VR-QOL in patients with FS-associated MD. Based on these favorable results, we recommend considering macular buckling for FS-associated MD in highly myopic eyes. In addition, the metamorphopsia score may serve as an additional postoperative visual function measure for clinical reference in patients undergoing MB surgery.

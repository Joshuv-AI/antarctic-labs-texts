Influence of induction of labor on morphological alterations in the pelvic floor after vaginal delivery determined by 4D transperineal ultrasound
Authors: Jiao Yi, Zhiguo Tang, Leilei Wang, Bin Zhang, Xianxia Chen, Xiaoying Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTo evaluate whether induction of labor increases pelvic floor morphology alterations in terms of a larger levator hiatus area or presence of levator ani muscle avulsion and compartment prolapse after vaginal delivery based on 4D tomographic ultrasound imaging.Materials and methodsThe retrospective study included 676 women who delivered in our maternal medical center between February 2024 to March 2025. On the basis of mode of delivery, the participants were divided into two groups (spontaneous delivery group and induction of labor group). Data included patient characteristics, pregnancy parameters and delivery details were analyzed. The primary outcome was the imaging anatomical abnormalities, such as occurrence of levator ani muscle avulsion, large hiatus and compartment prolapse.ResultsOn univariate analysis, no between-group differences in terms of levator ani muscle avulsion, anterior as well as central compartment prolapse were found (P > 0.05), whereas significant differences on the measurements of levator hiatus area were observed between spontaneous delivery group and induction of labor group (P < 0.05). After adjustment of confounding factors, multivariate logistic regression analysis shown no relationship between induction of labor and levator ani muscle avulsion, anterior as well as central compartment prolapse in this pilot (P > 0.05); The analysis of covariance demonstrated a substantial association between induction of labor and an enlarged levator hiatus (P < 0.05).ConclusionsInduction of labor may have little influence on the sonographic finding of pelvic floor structure. However, induction of labor may be an indicator of increased risk of levator hiatus hyperdistensibility postpartum. Nevertheless, longitudinal cohort study is needed to establish whether antenatal differences in the levator hiatus area exist that may influence the results of induction of labor and the associated risk of levator hiatus enlargement postnatal.Keywords: Induction of labor, Ultrasound evaluation, Levator ani muscle avulsion, Compartment prolapse, Levator hiatus

Introduction

The female pelvic floor is composed of muscles and connective tissues maintaining support for the pelvic organs [1]. To provide pelvic organ support, the muscles and connective tissues must resist the downward force applied on the pelvic floor by the weight of the abdominal organs and the strains that arise from gravid uterus during pregnancy. Among the pelvic muscular structures involved, levator ani muscle (LAM) is a major component [2], playing a crucial role in maintaining the structural and functional integrity of the pelvic floor [3].

For many women, vaginal delivery is a central experience and can potentially impact pelvic floor anatomy and function. In order to allow the passage of the fetal head, childbirth induces an enormous degree of stretch in the LAM and subsequent stretch-related injury [4]. It is believed that the crowning of fetal head is the most susceptible moment at which LAM trauma is more likely to be occurred [5]. Clinically, the most common injury is LAM avulsion, with vaginal delivery documented as the strongest independent risk factors [6, 7]. LAM avulsion is of clinical interest, as this injury increase the risk of long term physical and psychological morbidity such as anal incontinence, pelvic organ prolapse and post-traumatic stress disorder [8]. Published study reported that women with LAM avulsion were 4.13 times more likely to have prolapse than women without avulsion [9], especially increasing the possibility of anterior and central compartment prolapse. On the other hand, the levator hiatus, as the largest potential hernial portal in the women body, is defined as the space between the lower ramie of the pubis and the puborectalis muscle of the LAM on the narrowest plate. The dimensions of levator hiatus matter for the integrity of LAM that defines the hiatus. During pregnancy, over-distension of the levator hiatus may be adaptive changes or acquired due to LAM damage [10].

Induction of labor (IOL) is part of the most employed obstetrical interventions, and approximately one in four births undergo the procedure [11]. The rate of IOL in China is also as high as 18.4% in nulliparas and 10.2% in multiparas [12]. As an increasing global concern, most previous studies were concentrated on exploring neonatal and maternal outcomes among those who experience IOL [13], but no information is available regarding the impact of IOL on pelvic floor anatomical structure and function. For this,

we designed the current retrospective study to establish possible associations between morphological changes after delivery based on ultrasonography parameters such as LAM avulsion, compartment prolapse, and the changes in hiatal area in women following vaginal birth with a spontaneous delivery or IOL.

Materials and methods

Study design

This retrospective cohort study was performed at seven obstetrics departments affiliated to the Hefei Maternal and Child health care Hospital, Hefei, China between February 2024 to March 2025, it is the largest tertiary obstetrics and gynecology hospital in Anhui province. Because of the retrospective design of the study, the need to obtain informed consent from eligible women was waived by the institutional ethics committee of Hefei Maternal and Child health care Hospital. This retrospective research was approved by the institutional ethics committee of Hefei Maternal and Child health care Hospital, and was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

The inclusion criteria was these women who delivery vaginally with gestational age at or beyond 37 weeks with a cephalic presentation and admitted for either spontaneous or induced labor and received pelvic floor assessment at the postpartum rehabilitation clinic six weeks postpartum. At this visit, women underwent history and physical examination, ultrasonographic assessment, and completed validated pelvic floor system assessment based on Pelvic Organ Prolapse Quantification examination.

The exclusion criteria were women with multiple pregnancies, fetal malformation or intrauterine fetal death, previous cesarean delivery or uterine surgery, fetal transverse or breech positions, and contraindications for vaginal delivery. Women presenting with previous pelvic floor dysfunction (such as chronic pelvic pain, pelvic organ prolapse, urinary or fecal incontinence) and history of pelvic floor corrective surgery were excluded. Women who delivered by planned cesarean section were likewise excluded.

Pregnancy age was determined by fetal crown-rump measured by the first trimester ultrasound.

After delivery, the perineum was detected by an experienced obstetrician using the recommended bidigital vaginorectal examination, and the type of perineal laceration were categorized according to the classification of the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG). In 2019, the International Federation of Gynecology and Obstetrics statement recommended the use of restrictive episiotomy over routine episiotomy to prevent serious perineal laceration [7]. Additionally, study confirmed that restrictive episiotomy result in 30% fewer occurrences of severe perineal lacerations compared with routine episiotomy [14]. Based on the above mentioned advantages, our department also follows the restrictive episiotomy policy, thus in the present study, no third-or fourth-degree perineal tears were discovered, and only first- and second-degree perineal tears with bleeding requiring suturing were recorded.

Patient demographics, obstetrical characteristics and delivery details were extracted from the hospital electronic clinical database, including: maternal age, pre-pregnancy, body mass index (BMI), pregnancy BMI, gravidity, parity, gestational weeks at delivery, weight gain during pregnancy, complicated with pregnancy induced hypertension or gestational diabetes mellitus, the use of oxytocin, duration of oxytocin usage, the duration of the first, second, third, and total stages of labor, neonatal weight, the incidence of operative vaginal delivery, perineal laceration (first- and second-degree perineal tears), episiotomy, precipitate labor, and placental adhesion.

Ultrasound parameters of pelvic floor structures were obtained from the institutional ultrasonic database, including levator hiatus, LAM ultrasound measurements, and the incidence of compartment prolapse. Of note, no posterior compartment prolapse was noted in the study population.

The primary outcome was to evaluate whether women with IOL had a higher incidence of LAM avulsion, hiatal enlargement and compartment prolapse in 4D transperineal ultrasound than women with spontaneous delivery.

Sonographic procedures

Four-dimensional transperineal sonography was performed to assess the pelvic floor anatomy, including imaging of the anterior, central and posterior compartments, LAM as well as levator hiatus. All measurements were performed by senior doctors with over 5 years of experience in ultrasound operations. During examination, the woman was in the lithotomy position after bladder emptying, using a 4D pelvic ultrasound (GE Voluson E8, USA) with RAB6-D curved array volume transducer. Volume acquisition was performed on maximum Valsalva maneuver for the assessment of levator hiatus dimensions and on maximum pelvic floor muscle contraction for diagnosis of levator avulsion.

Generally, the probe was placed on the perineum in the midsagittal plane, which needs to display structures such as the pubic symphysis, urethra, vagina, anal canal, and levator ani muscle. The 4D scanning mode is activated to obtain pelvic floor volume data, display axial plane images, and observe the continuity of the LAM using tomographic imaging mode. The LAM was imaged from 5 mm below to 12.5 mm above the plane of minimal dimensions, at 2.5 mm slice intervals. LAM avulsion was diagnosed if all the three central slices corresponding to the plane of minimal hiatal dimensions, 2.5 mm and 5 mm above this plane of reference, all showed a discontinuity between the LAM and the inferior pubis ramus, rated separately for each side (Fig. 1). The LAM defect was evaluated on pelvic floor muscle contraction.

4D tomographic ultrasound image showing a unilateral avulsion (left side) of the levator ani muscle. The muscle defect is seen in the three central images (a, b, c). Arrow, representing the left unilateral avulsion of the levator ani muscle

Hiatal area, the open space between the two arms of the levator muscle, was measured during the maximum Valsalva. Initially, the plane of minimal hiatal dimensions was identified in the midsagittal plane, and a rendered volume in the axial plane of 1–2 cm thickness was generated in order to allow measurement of the hiatal area. The levator hiatus area was measured as the area bordered by the inner margin of pubovisceral muscle, pubic symphysis, and the inferior margin of pubic ramus (Fig. 2). Each measurement was carried out three times and the average area was recorded. Under normal circumstances, the hiatus area is ≤ 20cm2 on the maximum Valsalva maneuver for Chinese women.

Evaluate the degree of pelvic organ prolapse using the maximum Valsalva status frame in the mid sagittal plane of the pelvic floor. The reference line is the horizontal line passing through the pubic symphysis posterior border screen, and the landmark anatomical structures are the lowest point of the bladder neck posterior wall, the lowest point of the cervix, and the lowest point of the anterior wall of the rectal ampulla. Clinically, the location of the bladder neck, the lowest point of the cervix and the anterior wall of the rectal ampulla is specifically at compartment prolapse diagnosis.

Clinical procedures

For those with a favorable cervix with Bishop > 6, oxytocin (Anhui Fengyuan Pharmaceutical Co. Ltd., China) is used for inducing contractions.

In those women with a Bishop score less than 6, cervical ripening is needed. In our hospital, cervical preparation is most commonly be achieved with trans-cervical double balloons catheter (Cook catheter, Type CVB-18 F, Shenzhen Yixinda Medical New Technology Co. Ltd., China).

In the double-balloon catheter group, the device was applied in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions. A transcervical double-balloon catheter was inserted into the cervix by the obstetrician at 8pm on the first day, until the anterior balloon was placed above the internal cervical os and the second balloon was placed in the vagina. Both the uterine and vaginal balloons were alternately filled with saline to obtain a volume of 80mL per balloon.

Indications for removal of the double-balloon catheter include: (1) placement for 12 h, (2) rupture of membranes, (3) onset of labor, (4) intrauterine infection, (5) fetal distress, and (6) abnormal bleeding.

If spontaneous expulsion of the balloon did not occur, the double balloons catheter was removed after 12 h period and oxytocin infusion was commenced. Initially, 0.5% oxytocin was administrated at four drops per minute, increasing by four drops every 15 min according to uterine contractions. A maximum rate of 40 drops per minute was kept for one hour. If regular contractions were not achieved, the oxytocin concentration was adjusted to 1%. Careful observation of continuous fetal heart rate and uterine contraction patterns is used throughout established labor.

Definite

The first stage of labor refers to the period from regular uterine contractions until the cervix is fully dilated to 10 cm.

The second stage of labor is described as the time from the cervical dilation of 10 cm until the delivery of the fetus.

The third stage of labor is defined as the period between delivery of the baby and the placenta.

Precipitate labor is defined as the time from regular uterine contraction to fetal placental delivery to less than 3 h.

The type of perineal laceration is classified according to the classification of the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG): First-degree tear involving only the perineal skin; second-degree tear is defined as injury to the perineum involving the perineal muscles but not the anal sphincter.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analyses were carried out by SPSS 13.0 statistical packages (SPSS Inc., Chicago, USA.). Counting variables were expressed by percentage. Continuous variables were expressed by mean and standard deviation (SD). Comparison of baseline characteristics, pregnancy profile and labor characteristics between two groups were analyzed with T-test for continuous variables with normally distributed and the Chi-square test for categorical variables as appropriate. For continuous variables with non-normal distribution the Wilcoxon-Rank-Sum-Test was selected. A multivariate analysis was performed using a logistic regression model to identify the association between IOL and LAM avulsions as well as compartment prolapse. Based on literature and clinical reasoning, the variables selected in multivariate analysis including: maternal age, pre-pregnancy BMI, pregnancy BMI, gravidity, parity, gestational age at delivery, weight gain during pregnancy, complicated with pregnancy induced hypertension or gestational diabetes mellitus, the use of oxytocin, duration of oxytocin usage, the duration of the first, second, third, and total stages of labor, neonatal weight, operative vaginal delivery, perineal laceration, precipitate labor, episiotomy, and placental adhesion. The odds ratio (OR) as well as its 95% confidence interval (CI) were used to quantify this association. The analysis of Variance was performed to analyze the differences between two groups of hiatal hiatus size, and analysis of covariance was used to assess the correlation between IOL and area of the levator hiatus. A P < 0.05 was used only for statistical significance.

Results

Of the 676 women attending during the study period, mean maternal age was 31.1 ± 3.6 years and mean gestational week at delivery was 39.4 ± 1.2 weeks. Median BMI before and during pregnancy were 21.7 ± 3.2 and 27.1 ± 3.1 kg/m², respectively. Median weight gain was 14.5 ± 4.9 kg, and mean neonatal weight was 3292.6 ± 419.4 g. Of the 676 vaginally parous women, 479 (70.9%) delivered their first baby, 197 (29.1%) had at least 1 baby. The percentage of pregnancy induced hypertension and gestational diabetes mellitus were 8.6% (58/676) and 29.1% (100/676), respectively. 402 women need oxytocin administration, and median periods of oxytocin usage was 2.5 h. The during of the first, second, third, and total stages of labor were 8.0 h, 0.6 h, 0.1 h, and 8.9 h, respectively. 50 (7.4%) underwent precipitate labor, 37 (5.5%) had forceps delivery. The rates of first-degree tear, second-degree tear, and episiotomy were 64.9%, 15.4%, and 9.9%, respectively. 34 women (5%) had placental adhesion, and the average amount of bleeding is 150 ml.

Two groups were classified according to the method of delivery, group 1 (spontaneous delivery group, 431 cases), and group 2 (IOL group, 245 cases).

Of the 676 ultrasound assessments analyzed using 4D translabial tomographic ultrasound imaging, a total of 21 (3.1%, 21/676) LAM avulsions were found on maximum pelvic floor muscle contraction. The percentage of LAM avulsions in spontaneous vaginal delivery group was 3.2% (14/431), whereas the corresponding figure in IOL group was 2.9% (7/245). 371 cases of anterior compartment prolapse were identified on maximum pelvic floor muscle in spontaneous labour group and 213 cases in IOL group. 233 cases of central compartment prolapse in spontaneous labour group were diagnosed on maximum pelvic floor muscle and 142 cases in IOL group were diagnosed on maximum pelvic floor muscle contraction.

Statistically significant differences were found in terms of the average gestation at delivery, pre-pregnancy BMI, pregnancy BMI, and weight gain during pregnancy between groups (P < 0.001, = 0.001, < 0.001 and = 0.009, respectively). No clinically significant differences were observed regarding maternal age (P = 0.204). Percentage of complicated with pregnancy induced hypertension or gestational diabetes mellitus was significant different between the groups (P < 0.001, and = 0.015, respectively). The gravidity and parity were comparable between-groups (P = 0.593, and 0.439, respectively). The incidence of cases using oxytocin was highest in women experiencing IOL (P < 0.001). Moreover, significant differences between the two groups were found regarding periods of oxytocin usage, as well as the length of the first and total stages of labor (P < 0.001, = 0.002, and = 0.004, respectively), while the duration of the second and third stages of labor was similar between-groups (P = 0.187, and 0.068, respectively). Additionally, No meaningful differences in occurrence of precipitate labor, operative vaginal delivery and episiotomy were observed between the groups (P = 0.517, = 0.207, and = 0.074, respectively). Lastly, the infant birth weight and rate of perineal tears differed significantly among the two groups (P = 0.002 and 0.010, respectively).

Discussion

The key findings of this study were that IOL does not appear to have a significantly association with the LAM avulsion trauma as well as anterior and central compartment prolapse based on translabial tomographic 4D ultrasound imaging. Whereas, we identified a strong association between IOL and a larger levator hiatus area.

In our patient population, we did not find a higher rate of LAM avulsion trauma during IOL delivery compared to spontaneous delivery. Information about prevalence of LAM avulsion after a vaginal delivery varies highly, ranging from 2 to 15% [4, 15]. Our results during the study period are within this range, approximately in 3.2% (14/431) after spontaneous vaginal delivery, in 2.9% (7/245) of IOL deliveries, and in 3.1% (21/676) of overall vaginal deliveries. The prevalence of LAM avulsion is much lower in our population, we hypothesize two explanations are responsible for the consequences. One explanation could be that the different cohorts recruited, since in our study, only Chinese women are included. This is not surprising though literature has shown that Asian women were found to have different pelvic biometry and pelvic organ mobility compared to Caucasian women [16]. Another most likely explanation for the lower prevalence might be attributed to the participants in our study are relatively young age, low BMI and fetal weight. This was not unexpected, based on published studies that these three factors are involved in occurrence of LAM avulsion [17–19]. Clinically, an important clue for LAM avulsion provided by study indicating that the only known mechanism for such trauma was the maximum distension in the LAM region during crowning of the fetal head in vaginal delivery [4]. More importantly, as the best-established risk factors for avulsion, forceps birth [20, 21] and second stage of labor [3, 17] didn’t differ between IOL delivery and spontaneous delivery. Based on these premises, it was not surprising that we did not observe differences on the LAM avulsion rate between the two groups. Therefore, according to our results, we report the first attempt to find out whether we are causing LAM injury by IOL, and the relation isn’t established. This will provide more important information for patient counselling in regards to outcome of delivery planning.

Another interesting conclusion in this analysis is the fact that we found statistically significant between-group differences in muscle morphology in terms of levator hiatus. Study has investigated that the pelvic floor tissues start changing in preparation for delivery during pregnancy [22], with wider levator hiatus were observed in pregnant women compared to the nulliparous and parous groups [23]. Additionally, it is evident that the levator hiatus area increased as the pregnancy progressed from the first trimester to the third trimester [24]. Hence, it is deduced that constitutionally enlarged levator hiatus seems to create more space within the pelvis to play a role for an uncomplicated vaginal delivery. In our study, we find it interesting that, after analysis of covariance, IOL was found to be independent contributing factors for hiatal enlargement. We hypothesize several explanations for this association. Firstly, population study has demonstrated that IOL often causes anxiety and pain, significant numbers of women described a negative experience and a small number had an experience that was traumatic [25]. Meanwhile, emerging evidence suggested that women should be coached to relax their pelvic floor muscles during pushing to facilitate fetal descent through the birth canal and reduce the risk of damage because of the viscoelastic properties of the muscles [17]. Therefore, due to the high degree of anxiety during IOL, which was significantly associated with the presence of contraction of the pelvic floor muscles, adding more tension on the muscle, thereby increasing the risk of levator hiatus overstretching. Secondly, another possible reason could be that the dimensions of levator hiatus increase significantly during pregnancy and subsequently either increase further after vaginal delivery or decrease to the first-trimester levels [26], with nearly 70% women had “recovery” of levator hiatus area [27]. Even more importantly, the recovery of levator hiatus begins at 2 weeks postpartum in Asian women with vaginal delivery [28]. In addition, an unfavorable biomechanical interaction of the fetus and the birth canal might be associated with a substantial over-distended levator hiatus required in childbirth. Hence, we postulated that women in IOL group at 6 weeks postpartum had large hiatus may attribute to a difficult childbirth, indeed, findings from a review showing a strong association between IOL and increased risk of cesarean section due to failure to progress and labor dystocia [29]. Unfortunately, our study did not report on the dimensions of levator hiatus antepartum, limiting clinical interpretation about the magnitude of potential impact of IOL on levator hiatus. Therefore, we suggest that this study provides a foundation for future studies to prospective assessment of changes in levator hiatus before and after IOL, thereby establishing the possible impact of IOL on women’s pelvic floor health.

Regarding the compartment prolapse, our study did not show significant differences between women with or without IOL. Pelvic organ prolapse is a common condition that can have a significant impact on women’s quality of life, with roughly 13% of patients undergoing surgery for prolapse in their lifetime [30]. In this study in postpartum women, after controlling for confounders, we did not observe association between compartment prolapse and IOL. The finding is in agreement with a prospective cohort pilot study that suggested elective IOL may be protective of pelvic floor disorder compared to spontaneous vaginal delivery [31]. Moreover, a multicenter randomized clinical trial have also pointed out that labor induction was not significantly associated with an increased risk of pelvic organ prolapse [32]. Apart from this, as the most widely used pharmacological agent in induction, oxytocin itself was reported to have no direct harmful effect on the pelvic floor muscles [33]. We believe that the underlying mechanism that may explain our results lies in the lack of association between IOL and LAM avulsion in this pilot, since it is reported that there is a direct relation between pelvic organ prolapse and LAM avulsion [34]. Women with LAM avulsion have increased likelihood of anterior and central compartments prolapse [35], with central compartment prolapse being the most widely finding in Chinese patients [16]. As such, we believe that IOL seems to play a much smaller role in the changes in anatomical organ structures of pelvic floor based on ultrasound assessments postpartum, the information might be of relevance clinically for patient consultation and decision-making.

The main limitation of our study was the retrospective nature. However, this study also contained a number of strengths. A main strength was that being conducted in a single hospital in a population with similar characteristics, as it lowers the risk of differences in hospital practices, such as obstetricians’ experience as well as protocol regarding IOL, management of labor progression, and the use of oxytocin, which may be potentially affected clinical outcomes in different centers. Furthermore, our study is the first time attempt to identify the pelvic floor changes among women undergoing IOL, so that we can provide better information for expectant mothers on their risk of pelvic floor injuries. In addition, we had a sufficient sample size, allowing us to perform a reliable multivariate analysis to evaluate the relationship between IOL and pelvic floor injuries.

In conclusion, vaginal delivery is by far the independent contributing factor for pelvic floor damage. Regarding this, we are most interested in understanding what happens in the pelvic floor among pregnant women scheduled for IOL. In this study, we were able to show that IOL isn’t independent risk factors for major morphological abnormalities of levator structure and compartment prolapse. In contrast, IOL is an significant risk factor for hiatal enlargement. Knowing this, obstetric caregivers enable to give these women proper counseling before IOL. While further research is needed to better understanding of the long-term consequences of IOL on pelvic floor structures and their related functions.

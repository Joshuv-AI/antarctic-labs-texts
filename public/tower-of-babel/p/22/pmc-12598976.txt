Postoperative antithrombotic therapy after coronary artery bypass grafting combined with coronary endarterectomy (PATH-CARE): study protocol for a randomised controlled clinical trial
Authors: Xieraili Tiemuerniyazi, Ziang Yang, Enze Yang, Liaoming He, Lianxin Chen, Shengkang Huang, Yifeng Nan, Yangwu Song, Fei Xu, Xin Yuan, Zhan Hu, Wei Zhao, Wei Feng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionCoronary endarterectomy (CE) combined with coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) has been associated with poor graft patency, which might be attributed to the activation of coagulation cascade caused by the exposure of the subendothelial tissue to the blood stream, which could necessitate anticoagulation therapy. However, the optimal antithrombotic therapy after CE+CABG remains unclear.Methods and analysisThe PATH-CARE is a double-centre, two-armed, blinded to outcome assessed, randomised controlled trial, aimed to determine the optimal antithrombotic therapy after CE+CABG. A total of 202 patients are expected to be recruited. Patients will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to the control (dual antiplatelet therapy (AT) group) and the intervention (dual AT+warfarin (ATW) group). Patients in the AT group will receive aspirin plus clopidogrel for 1 year, while patients in the ATW group receive aspirin plus clopidogrel for 1 year with extra warfarin therapy for the first 3 months postoperatively. All patients will receive life-long aspirin treatment after the first year. All of the patients will be required to complete a 6-month follow-up. The primary endpoint is the patency of CE-targeted coronary grafts, assessed through coronary CT angiography or coronary angiography. Secondary outcomes include the occurrence of major adverse cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events and bleeding events.Ethics approval and disseminationThis study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Fuwai Hospital (No.: 2022–1849), and Yunnan Fuwai Cardiovascular Hospital (No.: 2023-048-01). Background

Coronary endarterectomy (CE), always used as an adjunct to coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG), is one of the final options to achieve complete revascularisation in patients with diffuse coronary artery disease (DCAD). However, studies have reported lower graft patency following CE+CABG as compared with isolated CABG.1 2 Meanwhile, CE+CABG was observed to increase the risk of perioperative myocardial infarction,3 which may partly result from the CE-targeted graft failure.4 Given the rising prevalence of patients with DCAD, including severe long-segment stenosis, occlusion or calcification of native coronary artery disease and/or severe long-segment in-stent stenosis or occlusion, CE currently still remains indicated for selected cases. Therefore, improving the graft patency after CE+CABG is of great importance.

Antithrombotic therapy is one of the most important therapies to secure graft patency after surgical coronary revascularisation. While current clinical guidelines highlight the usage of antiplatelet agents after isolated CABG,5 no specific recommendations exist regarding the antithrombotic therapy after CE+CABG. In fact, the mechanism of thrombus formation could be different between CE+CABG and isolated CABG. As compared with isolated CABG, the absence of the endothelium after CE leads to direct exposure of subendothelial tissue to the blood stream, activating the coagulation cascade and accelerating the formation of thrombus and myocardial injury,6 therefore leading to graft failure. Hence, anticoagulation on the basis of antiplatelet therapy (AT) might be indicated in CE+CABG patients. Nonetheless, clinical practice remains inconsistent, as some centres may employ AT only after CE+CABG,7,9 while others use anticoagulation therapy in combination with single or dual AT.10 So far, only one clinical trial with limited sample size has attempted to address this issue, while another ongoing trial focuses on the chest tube drainage within 24 hours postoperatively.11 Therefore, there is an urgent need for exploring the optimal antithrombotic therapy after CE+CABG.

The aim of the study is to evaluate the impacts of anticoagulation therapy in addition to dual AT on the graft patency in patients undergoing CE+CABG. We hypothesise that AT+warfarin (ATW) is superior to AT.

Methods

Trial design

The Postoperative Antithrombotic Therapy after CABG combined with CE (PATH-CARE) study is a two-armed, blinded to outcome assessed, randomised, controlled clinical trial. The study has been registered at the https://clinicaltrials.gov (ClinicalTrials.gov ID: NCT05782270, registration date: 12 March 2023). This study recruits eligible patients from Fuwai Hospital, National Center for Cardiovascular Diseases, National Clinical Research Center for Cardiovascular Diseases, Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences and Peking Union Medical College, Beijing, China; Yunnan Fuwai Cardiovascular Hospital, Kunming, Yunnan, China. The study follows the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials (SPIRIT) guidelines12 (figure 1). The planned start and end dates for the trial recruitment are April 2023 and October 2025, respectively.

Randomisation and allocation

All eligible patients will be invited to participate in this trial. The participants will be allocated into the ATW and AT groups using a computer-generated minimised random allocation technique after signing in the written informed consent on postoperative day 1. Variables selected for the minimised random allocation method include age, sex, ejection fraction and CE target.

Intervention

All enrolled patients (both AT and ATW groups) will be administered with dual AT with both aspirin 100 mg daily and clopidogrel 75 mg daily, beginning from the morning of postoperative day 1. Patients allocated into the ATW group will receive extra anticoagulation treatment with warfarin for 3 months, beginning from the afternoon of postoperative day 1 (after checking the International Normalized Ratio (INR) in the morning) with target INR between 1.8 and 2.5. All of the participants will continue with single AT after 1 year postoperatively. All of the medical treatment will be administered under supervision, and clinical considerations will be given priority.

For patients in the ATW group, INR will be checked daily during the hospitalisation postoperatively to determine a stable warfarin dosing before discharge. Patients and their relatives will be fully educated on the INR monitoring by a research doctor and a nurse, respectively. The patient or mostly one of their family members living together is required to take a test regarding the education. A recording form will be given to the patients at discharge, which contains the information such as check-up date, INR value and warfarin dosage. Patients are required to check INR for at least 3 days continuously after discharge (whether at home self-monitoring or at the hospital nearby), and a nurse will complete a telephone interview at 1 week. If their INR value stably falls within the range of 1.8 and 2.5 for at least 3–4 times, the monitoring frequency will be gradually extended to two times per week, once a week, then once per 2 weeks. If the INR is below 1.8 or above 2.5 but less than 4.0, the dosage of warfarin will be adjusted according to the doctor’s advice, and the serial INR checks would be performed again following the rules above, while the patient is required to go to the hospital on condition of INR >4.0 and follow the doctor’s advice. The patients can contact the research doctor, nurse or go to the anticoagulation clinic whenever needed. One of the investigators will collect information from the recording form at 3-month and 6-month follow-ups, respectively.

Surgical approach

Participants will undergo CE in combination with CABG. CE will be performed either by an open (preferred on the left anterior descending territory) or closed (preferred on coronary territories other than left anterior descending) pathway. The techniques were described in detail previously.1 13

Outcomes and measurement

The primary endpoint is the patency of CE-targeted coronary grafts at 6-month follow-up. All of the participants will receive regular coronary CT angiography (or coronary angiography if necessary) after 6 months postoperatively. Figure 2 shows the study flowchart.

Study flowchart. AT, antiplatelet therapy; ATW, AT+warfarin; CE, coronary endarterectomy.

The graft patency will be defined according to the Fitzgibbon classification,14 where A is considered as unimpaired patency, B as impaired with stenosis of >50% and C as occlusion. The CE-targeted graft will be considered as patent under the circumstances of no or less than 50% of stenosis on both the graft and the CE target (Fitzgibbon grade A), while it will be defined as failure if there’s 50% or more degree of stenosis on either of them (Fitzgibbon grade B or O). The angiographic results will be evaluated by two independent radiologists. If there’s any disagreement, a third cardiologist will be invited.

The secondary endpoint includes the occurrence of major adverse cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events and bleeding events defined according to Bleeding Academic Research Consortium definition for bleeding (from randomisation until 6-month follow-up).15

Sample size

Two parallel arms will be required. According to the previous study, the 6-month to 12-month graft patency rate was approximately 88% for patients receiving ATW therapy10 and 66% for those on single AT.16 Considering the potential benefits of dual AT compared with single AT, we hypothesised a graft patency rate of 72% after dual AT for CE+CABG. Assuming a power of 80% and a 2-sided α of .05, a sample size of 95 will be needed for each group. Considering a dropout rate of 3–5%, a total of 202 participants (101 for each group) will be required.

Data collection and monitoring

The baseline characteristics will be obtained through the electronic medical records and visiting the patients during the hospital stay. All of the patients will be followed up at 3 months and 6 months postoperatively. The data will be confirmed by two independent personnel to assure the accuracy and quality. Missing data will be assessed and handled with multiple imputation methods, as appropriate. Data will be deidentified and stored in the electronic storage system of our institution. The data monitoring committee will include one cardiac surgeon, one cardiologist, one statistician and one bioethicist.

Statistical analysis

The outcome assessors will be blinded. The primary endpoint will be compared in an intention-to-treat, as-treated analysis, as well as per-protocol analysis. As a sensitivity analysis, the patency of each CE target, as well as the total occlusion rate of CE-targeted coronary grafts (Fitzgibbon grade O), will also be compared between the two groups. The secondary endpoints will also be compared. Before the trial is finished, no blind-breaking analysis will be performed.

Adverse event management

As a classic anticoagulant broadly used in the clinical practice, warfarin is reasonably safe under close monitoring of INR. However, the role of warfarin additional to DAPT after CE+CABG is unclear. Some centres routinely administer warfarin,10 while others use antiplatelet only.9 Limited evidence exists. To reduce the potential risks, patient education will be provided to patients and their families before discharge, and an education form will be provided. INR monitoring and medication record form will be given to the patient. At 3 months postoperatively, a follow-up will be performed for collecting the events, reminding the patients to finish the 3-month visit and terminating warfarin if appropriate. All patients could contact one of the investigators if they had any questions during the study. If an adverse event occurs, the sponsor will discuss the possibility of withdrawing the subject from this study. Meanwhile, the adverse event report form will be filled. If the occurrence of adverse events relating to the drug is substantially higher in one of the study groups as compared with the other, the termination of this trial will be evaluated. The trial will be monitored annually by the Ethics Committee. Any modifications regarding the protocol will require the ethics committee’s permission.

IL-21 controls CD8+ T cell immunity and antibacterial defense in fish via STAT3 signaling
Authors: Yi Cao, Wenzhuo Rao, Haokai Chen, Kang Li, Yating Zhu, Zhichao Fang, Yuying Zheng, Ming Geng, Jiansong Zhang, Jialong Yang, Xiumei Wei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
CD8+ T cells are indispensable effectors of adaptive immunity. While cytokine-mediated regulation of CD8+ T cell responses has been extensively characterized in mammals, the underlying mechanisms in fish remain largely unexplored. Here, we identified and characterized an interleukin-21 (IL-21) gene from Nile tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus). In vitro stimulation with IL-21 significantly upregulated the transcription and expression of key CD8+ T cell effector molecules, including Granzyme B and interferon-gamma (IFN-γ), in splenic leukocytes, indicating a crucial role of IL-21 in modulating CD8+ T cell function in teleosts. Using a monoclonal antibody (mAb) against Nile tilapia IL-21, we further demonstrated that activated CD4-1+ T cells are the primary producers of this cytokine in fish. Intraperitoneal injection of recombinant IL-21 into Edwardsiella piscicida—infected Nile tilapia enhanced both proliferation and apoptosis of CD8+ T cells. Moreover, IL-21 elevated the expression of Granzyme B, IFN-γ, and IL-2, and boosted CD8+ T cell cytotoxicity, thereby strengthening their effector functions. In contrast, blockade of IL-21 signaling with the mAb severely impaired CD8+ T cell responses. Mechanistically, Nile tilapia IL-21 strongly induced phosphorylation of signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3), which was required for IL-21-mediated CD8+ T cell proliferation in vitro. Inhibition of STAT3 phosphorylation in vivo attenuated CD8+ T cell responses and antibacterial immunity, underscoring its functional importance. Collectively, our findings establish IL-21 as a pivotal regulator of CD8+ T cell immunity in fish and provide evolutionary insight into conserved cytokine pathways shaping adaptive immune responses.

INTRODUCTION

CD8+ T cells play a critical role in adaptive immunity by eliminating intracellular pathogens and providing long-term protection (Mittrücker et al., 2014). Upon infection, they secrete perforin (Gupta et al., 2005) and granzyme B (Yang et al., 2019), express FasL (Shrestha & Diamond, 2007) to kill target cells, and release cytokines such as tumor necrosis factor alpha (TNF-α) (Srikiatkhachorn et al., 2010) and interferon gamma (IFN-γ) (Valentine et al., 2012) to mediate indirect cytotoxic effects. In addition to pathogen clearance, CD8+ T cells contribute to the regulation of autoimmune (Walter & Santamaria, 2005) and allergic diseases (Yu et al., 2019). For example, their attack on melanocytes exacerbates vitiligo (Marchioro et al., 2022). Similar to mammals, fish also possess CD8+ T cells that play vital roles in host defense. For instance, peripheral blood lymphocytes (PBLs) in rainbow trout (Oncorhynchus mykiss) infected with viral hemorrhagic septicemia virus (VHSV) exhibit elevated CD8 transcription (Utke et al., 2007). In orange-spotted grouper (Epinephelus coioides), CD8+ lymphocytes display enhanced cytotoxicity against autologous fin cells one week after nervous necrosis virus (NNV) infection (Chang et al., 2011). In crucian carp (Carassius auratus), CD8+ T cells demonstrate antibacterial activity against both intracellular and extracellular bacteria (Nayak & Nakanishi, 2013) and kill extracellular parasites via contact-dependent mechanisms involving serine protease and perforin (Sukeda et al., 2021). Notably, CD8+ T cells in fish exhibit stronger antimicrobial functions than CD4+ T cells (Yamasaki et al., 2014) and can even respond to inactivated pathogens (Tajimi et al., 2019). They can eliminate Edwardsiella tarda in a non-MHC-I-dependent manner (Nayak & Nakanishi, 2013). These findings suggest that fish CD8+ T cells possess diverse and potent effector functions.

CD8+ T cell responses in mammals are tightly regulated by a complex network involving cytokines, the immune microenvironment, and metabolic programs (Hu et al., 2024; Notarangelo et al., 2022). In stark contrast, the mechanisms regulating CD8+ T cells in teleost fish remain poorly understood. As summarized above, fish CD8+ T cells exhibit potent antimicrobial activity, can respond to inactivated pathogens (Tajimi et al., 2019), and even eliminate certain bacteria independently of MHC-I (Nayak & Nakanishi, 2013). A prime example of their unique regulatory strategies is our recent discovery that fish CD8+ T cells utilize a FasL-mediated pathway that induces apoptosis in both target cells and CD8+ T cells themselves, a cytotoxic mechanism distinct from that in mammals (Li et al., 2025). Furthermore, studies in ginbuna crucian carp have identified a CD8α+ lymphocyte population co-expressing innate immune markers, such as NK-lysin, suggesting a less differentiated state and a blurrier boundary between innate and adaptive immunity than in mammals (Nakanishi et al., 2011; Toda et al., 2009). These functional and phenotypical divergences underscore the evolutionary importance of elucidating CD8+ T cell regulatory mechanisms in fish. Although our previous work identified a FasL-dependent strategy, the role of cytokines in modulating CD8+ T cell responses remains unknown. Thus, cytokine-mediated regulation of CD8+ T cells in fish represents a major unexplored gap in the field that the present study aims to address.

IL-21 is a four-helix bundle cytokine primarily secreted by natural killer (NK) cells and activated CD4+ T cells, such as T follicular helper (Tfh) and IL-17-producing T helper (Th17) cells (Pallikkuth et al., 2012). It enhances antigen-specific immune responses and acts on a variety of cell types, including CD8+ T cells (Li et al., 2005). In mammals, IL-21 supports the formation of memory CD8+ T cells and provides long-term immune protection (Ren & Lukacher, 2020). It can also directly enhance the effector functions of CD8+ T cells (Zhang et al., 2022). Moreover, Tfh-derived IL-21 plays a critical role in sustaining CD8+ T cell responses and in controlling viral infection during chronic exposure (Zander et al., 2022). Conversely, IL-21 deficiency impairs CD8+ T cell quality and recall responses following acute viral infection (Yi et al., 2010). These findings underscore the importance of IL-21 in regulating CD8+ T cell immunity. In fish, research on IL-21 began relatively recently, and even the cellular source of IL-21 remains unclear. Nevertheless, its immunological functions have been gradually uncovered. For example, grass carp (Ctenopharyngodon idella) IL-21 upregulates the expression of antimicrobial genes such as β-defensin and lysozyme, contributing to intestinal inflammation control during bacterial infection (Yuan et al., 2023). IL-21 from rainbow trout and Japanese flounder (Paralichthys olivaceus) is induced by pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs), indicating a role in bacterial immune defense (Yu et al., 2020). Rainbow trout IL-21 also rapidly and persistently induces IFN-γ, IL-10, and IL-22 expression in head kidney leukocytes (Wang et al., 2011). Despite these advances, current studies on fish IL-21 have focused mainly on innate immunity or general leukocyte responses. Whether IL-21 regulates CD8+ T cell responses in fish and the precise mechanisms remain unexplored.

In the present study, we identified an IL-21 ortholog in Nile tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus). For the first time, we demonstrated that CD4-1+ T cells served as a cellular source of fish IL-21, and that Nile tilapia IL-21 contributed to CD8+ T cell-mediated antibacterial responses by modulating CD8+ T cell proliferation, apoptosis, and effector functions. Mechanistically, we found that the signal transducer and activator of transcription (STAT) 3 pathway mediated the effects of IL-21 on CD8+ T cells and was essential for their antibacterial function. To our knowledge, this is the first report in fish demonstrating that CD4-1+ T cells secrete IL-21 and that IL-21 regulates CD8+ T cell responses through STAT3 activation. This work provides new insights into cytokine-mediated regulation of adaptive immunity in fish and sheds light on the evolutionary conservation of T cell immune regulation across vertebrates.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Experimental animals

The Nile tilapia used in the experiments were purchased from an aquaculture farm in Guangdong Province, China. Healthy adult fish with a length of 10–12 cm were randomly selected for the experiments. All animal care and experimental operations were carried out in accordance with the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals of the Ministry of Science and Technology of China, and were approved by the East China Normal University Experimental Animal Ethics Committee.

Sequence analysis

The gene locus and amino acid sequences of each species were obtained from the NCBI database. Multiple alignments were generated by MEGA v.10.0.4 and displayed with GENEDOC (Jiao et al., 2022). The tertiary structure prediction of IL-21 was performed by SWISS-MODEL (https://swissmodel.expasy.org/interactive) and displayed by PyMOL v.2.5. Phylogenetic tree was constructed using MEGA v.10.0.4 Neighbor-Joining (NJ) algorithm (Cao et al., 2024). The sequence information for analysis was listed in Supplementary Table S1.

Leukocytes isolation

According to our previous report (Li et al., 2023b), Percoll density gradient centrifugation was used to isolate spleen leukocytes. The Percoll (GE HealthCare, USA) was mixed with 10× PBS at the ratio of 9:1, and diluted with Leibovitz’s L-15 medium (Gibco, USA) to reach 34% and 52% (v/v) respectively. Nile tilapia spleens were taken and placed in a 6 cm dish with 3 mL of L-15 liquid medium. Then, the obtained spleen suspension after grinding and filtering was added to the 15 mL centrifuge tube containing 4 mL of 52% Percoll and 4 mL of 34% Percoll. The tubes were centrifuged at 500 ×g with the lowest acceleration and deceleration at room temperature for 30 min. Subsequently, the cells located between 34% and 52% Percoll were identified as leukocytes. The obtained leukocytes were washed once by L-15 medium and used for experiments.

Prokaryotic protein recombination of OnIL-21 (rOnIL-21)

The coding sequence (CDS) of OnIL-21 was cloned into the His-tagged pET-28a (+) vector and transformed into E. coli BL21 DE3 competent cells (Transgene, China). The bacterial were cultured in 300 mL Luria-Bertani (LB) medium (BBI Life Sciences, China) containing 1:1000 diluted kanamycin (Solarbio, China). When the bacterial solution reached OD600=0.6, 0.5 mol/L isopropyl β-D-thiogalactoside (IPTG, Solarbio, China) was added at a ratio of 1:1000, and continued to culture for 4 h. After centrifugation and sonication, the His-tagged rOnIL-21 protein was purified using a Ni-NTA resin column and eluted with 400 mmol/L imidazole (BBI Life Sciences, China). The purified rOnIL-21 was gradually refolded in a gradient-diminishing urea-Tris buffer at 4°C, 12 h each time. Finally, the protein was separated by SDS-PAGE gel, the obtained gel was stained with Coomassie Brilliant Blue, and the image was captured by Odyssey CLx Image Studio (USA).

Development of a monoclonal antibody (mAb) against OnIL-21

For anti-OnIL-21 mAb preparation, BALB/c mice were first intraperitoneally (i.p.) injected with 100 μg purified rOnIL-21 emulsified in complete Freund’s adjuvant (Sigma, USA). Two weeks later, another intraperitoneal injection with the same quantity of antigen in incomplete Freund’s adjuvant (Sigma, USA) was administered, followed by two reinforcing immunizations with 100 μg rOnIL-21 in PBS via the tail vein at 1-week interval. Three days after the last immunization, the spleens of immunized mice were harvested for cell fusion with SP2/0 myeloma cells using 50% polyethylene glycol 4000 (PEG4000, Sigma, USA). The fused cells were cultured in RPMI 1640 medium (Gibco, USA) containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% Hypoxanthine-Aminopterin-Thymidine (HAT, BBI Life Sciences, China). After 7–10 days, the positive hybridomas were screened by ELISA and flow cytometry. After confirmation, positive hybridomas were cloned using the limited dilution method. The hybridoma confirmed to be positive for OnIL-21 was i.p. injected into BALB/c mice to produce ascites, and mAb was purified from ascites using protein G agarose (Invitrogen, USA).

ELISA

To screen positive hybridomas for OnIL-21, a 96-well plate was coated with 2 μg/mL purified rOnIL-21 in 50 mmol/L carbonate-bicarbonate buffer (pH 9.6) at 4°C overnight and then was blocked with 2% bovine serum albumin (BSA, BBI Life Sciences, China) at 37°C for 1 h. After washing the plate with PBST (PBS with 0.5% Tween-20, BBI Life Sciences, China) three times, 100 μL of hybridoma supernatant was added to the well and incubated at room temperature for 1 h. The plate was then washed as above. 100 μL alkaline phosphatase-conjugated goat anti-mouse IgG H&L (Southern Biotech, 1:4000, China) was added, and the plate was incubated for another 1 h. The plate was again washed three times with PBST, and 100 μL of 0.1% p-nitrophenyl phosphate (Sigma, USA) in 50 mmol/L carbonate-bicarbonate buffer (pH 9.8) containing 0.5 mmol/L MgCl2 was added. Next, the plate was incubated in the dark. The reaction was finally stopped by adding 50 μL of 2 mol/L NaOH, and the absorbance was measured at 405 nm. The supernatant of SP2/0 myeloma was used as a negative control.

Cell stimulation in vitro

Isolated splenic leukocytes were cultured in Dulbecco's Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM, BBI Life Sciences, China) containing 10% FBS (BBI Life Sciences, China) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin (PS, BBI Life Sciences, China) at 28°C. The cells were seeded at a density of 1×107 cells/mL and treated under different distinct conditions for stimulation. Cells were treated with 2 μg/mL soluble rOnIL-21 protein, 2 μg/mL phytohemagglutinin (PHA, Sigma, USA), or 50 ng/mL Phorbol 12-myristate 13-acetate (PMA, Sigma, USA) plus 500 ng/mL ionomycin (P+I, Sigma, USA) for indicated durations. After PHA stimulation for 6–12 h, cells were harvested for reverse transcription quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR) and flow cytometry analysis. After P+I stimulation for 2–4 h, cells were harvested for RT-qPCR. In the case of soluble rOnIL-21 protein stimulation for 3–12 h, cells were collected for RT-qPCR, western blot and flow cytometry to comprehensively assess cellular responses.

Inhibitor treatment

To block cytokines secretion in vivo, Nile tilapias were i.p. injected with 13 mg/kg Brefeldin A (BFA, MedChemExpress, USA) 8 h before sacrifice. For in vitro assays, 5 μg/mL anti-OnIL-21 mAb was used to eliminate the effect of IL-21 in cell stimulation. C188-9 (MedChemExpress, USA) was dissolved in Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, Sigma, USA) and used to inhibit STAT3 phosphorylation. For the STAT3 inhibition in vivo, the Nile tilapias were i.p. injected with 1 mg/kg C188-9. For the STAT3 inhibition in vitro, splenic leukocytes cultured in DMEM medium containing 10% FBS and 1% PS were treated with 5 mmol/L C188-9 at 28°C.

CD4-1+ T cell depletion

Nile tilapia individuals were i.p. injected with 20 μg of mouse anti-Nile tilapia CD4-1 mAb (Ai et al., 2022), or mouse IgG1 (BioLegend, USA) as isotype control. After 24 h, Nile tilapias were injected with 50 μL Nile tilapia anti-mouse IgG1 serum or negative serum for the control group. Nine days after mAb injection, the CD4-1+ T cell-depleted Nile tilapias were then i.p. injected with Edwardsiella piscicida. Fish were sacrificed at the indicated time points for RT-qPCR.

Bacterial infection

Edwardsiella piscicida (EIB 202) was gifted from East China University of Science and Technology and cultured in Tryptone Yeast Extract Broth (TYB) at 37°C overnight. Then, the bacterial culture was centrifuged to remove the medium and washed three times with PBS. Nile tilapia individuals were injected with 100 µL of 4×107 CFU/mL of E. piscicida, and the control animals were injected with the same volume of PBS. For injections involving OnIL-21, the cytokine was added to the diluted bacterial suspension, ensuring a final dose of 0.5 mg/kg.

Reverse transcription quantitative PCR

To detect transcription level of specified molecules, RNA extraction from spleen leukocytes was performed using Trizol (Invitrogen, USA) according to the manufacturer's protocol. Subsequently, RNA was inverted into cDNA using Plus All-in-one 1st Strand cDNA Synthesis SuperMix (Novoprotein, China). After that, the cDNA template was diluted at 1:30, and the relative mRNA levels were analyzed using 2×NovoStart SYBR qPCR SuperMix Plus (Novoprotein, China), with β-actin as the internal reference. The relative expression levels of the target genes were detected using the 2-△△CT method. The used gene-specific primers were provided in Supplementary Table S2.

Western blot

The cells were lysed in NP40 lysis buffer (1% NP-40, 50 mmol/L Tris-HCl, 150 mmol/L NaCl) containing 1% phosphatase inhibitor, 1% protease inhibitor, and 1‰ PMSF (MedChemExpress, USA) on ice for 30 min. After lysis, the suspension was centrifuged at 13 000 ×g at 4°C for 10 min to obtain the supernatant. The nuclear proteins were extracted using a Nuclear Protein Extraction Kit (Beyotime, China) following the manufacturer's protocol. The protein samples were boiled with loading buffer (Solarbio, China) for 5 min, then separated by 12% SDS-PAGE and transferred onto a nitrocellulose membrane. Next, the membrane was blocked with 4% non-fat milk powder in PBST for 1 h and washed three times with PBST. Finally, the membranes were incubated with anti-Nile tilapia IL-21, IL-2, Granzyme B and TNF-α antibodies or 1:1000 diluted Perforin A (Beyotime, China), phosphor-STAT3, β-actin and Histone H3 (Cell Signaling Technology, USA) antibodies respectively overnight at 4°C. After washing three times with PBST, the membranes were incubated with 1:10000 diluted Alexa Fluor 680-conjugated goat anti-mouse IgG H&L (Abcam, UK) or 1:30000 diluted Alexa Fluor 800-conjugated goat anti-rabbit IgG H&L (Abcam, UK) for 1 h at room temperature. Finally, the membranes were washed three times with PBST and then observed by Odyssey CLx Image Studio.

Flow cytometry

To identify the CD3ε+ and CD8+ T cell population, 1×106 spleen leukocytes were stained with CD8α mAb (Li et al., 2025), followed by 1:2000 diluted Alexa Fluor 647-conjugated goat anti-mouse IgG H&L (Abcam, UK), and finally stained with 1:400 diluted FITC-conjugated CD3ε mAb on ice for 30 min. The samples were washed twice with FACS Buffer (2% FBS in PBS) before each staining. To detect the dynamic changes of cytokines secreted by CD8+ T cells during E. piscicida infection, infected Nile tilapias were injected with BFA 6–8 h before sacrifice; 1×106 spleen leukocytes were incubated with CD8α mAb and Alexa Fluor 647-conjugated goat anti-mouse IgG H&L as above. Then, the Cytofix/Cytoperm buffer (BD Biosciences, USA) was used to fix leukocytes on ice for 30 min, and the samples were stained with FITC-conjugated anti-Nile tilapia IL-2/Granzyme B/IFN-γ mAbs (Ai et al., 2022; Cao et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024) after washing twice with Perm/Wash Buffer (BD Biosciences, USA). For detection of p-STAT3 in CD8+ T cells, 1×106 leukocytes were first stained with FITC-conjugated CD8 mAb. Subsequently, cells were fixed with BD permeabilization/fixation buffer (BD Bioscience, USA) for 2 h, followed by two washes with BD washing buffer (BD Bioscience, USA). The leukocytes were then incubated with a 1:400 dilution of rabbit anti-mouse p-STAT3 antibody on ice for 30 min. After washing twice, staining was performed with a 1:2000 dilution of Alexa Fluor 647-conjugated goat anti-rabbit antibody for 30 min prior to flow cytometry analysis.

To screen the positive hybridoma secreting anti-OnIL-21 mAb, spleen leukocytes were first stimulated by P+I for 4 h at 28°C, with 1 μg/mL Golgi-plug (Invitrogen, USA) to inhibit OnIL-21 secretion. Next, 1×106 spleen leukocytes per well were fixed as above and the cells were stained with hybridoma supernatant on ice for 30 min after washing as above, then stained with 1:2000 diluted Alexa Fluor 647-conjugated goat anti-mouse IgG H&L. To investigate the source of OnIL-21, 1×106 spleen leukocytes were stained with CD3ε mAb, followed by 1:2000 diluted Alexa Fluor 594-conjugated goat anti-mouse IgG H&L (Abcam, UK). Then, the cells were stained with 1:400 diluted biotin-conjugated CD4-1 mAb on ice for 30 min, followed by APC-streptavidin (BioLegend, USA) staining. The samples were washed twice with FACS Buffer before each staining. Finally, the cells were fixed as above, then stained with FITC-conjugated anti-OnIL-21 mAb. To determine whether OnIL-21 can bind to CD8+ T cells, His-tagged-rOnIL-21 was added in DMEM medium to reach 2 μg/mL, then incubated with spleen leukocytes for 6 h. The samples were incubated successively with 1:1000 diluted mouse anti-His mAb (BBI Life Sciences, China) and 1:2000 diluted Alexa Fluor 594-conjugated goat anti-mouse IgG H&L on ice for 30 min, respectively. Subsequently, the washed cells were stained with FITC-conjugated CD3ε mAb and biotin-conjugated CD8α mAb, followed by APC-streptavidin staining. All samples were detected by flow cytometry (USA), and data were analyzed by FlowJo v.10.9.0 software (Wei et al., 2020).

Apoptosis assay

To detect the apoptosis of CD8+ T cells, 1×106 spleen leukocytes were stained with FITC-conjugated CD8α mAb. Then, the samples were stained with 1:400 diluted APC-conjugated Annexin V (BioLegend, USA) in Annexin V binding buffer at room temperature for 15 min; 7-aminoactinomycin D (7-AAD, Invitrogen, USA) was added at a ratio of 1:400 to identify dead cells. Fluorescence compensation was set using single-stained controls for each fluorophore and an unstained control, prepared and analyzed immediately prior to sample acquisition. Eventually, the samples were detected by flow cytometry.

Cell proliferation assay in vitro

The spleen leukocytes were labelled with 10 µmol/L FITC-Carboxyfluorescein succinimidyl ester (CFSE, Invitrogen, USA) at room temperature for 10 min. Then, the cells were centrifuged and washed twice by DMEM medium. The obtained cells were cultured in DMEM medium containing 10% FBS and 1% PS for 48 h, and 2 μg/mL PHA was added to activate T cells. Simultaneously, 2 μg/mL rOnIL-21, 5 μg/mL αIL-21 mAb or 5 mmol/L C188-9 was added to detect the effect of IL-21 or p-STAT3 in CD8+ T cell proliferation. The cells were stained with CD8α mAb and Alexa Fluor 647-conjugated goat anti-mouse IgG H&L as above, and 7-AAD was added to detect dead cells. Finally, the samples were analyzed by flow cytometry.

BrdU incorporation

Nile tilapias were i.p. injected with 0.75 mg 5-bromo-2'-deoxyuridine (BrdU, Sigma, USA) in 200 μL PBS, 16 h before the spleen leukocytes were isolated for experiments. After the cells were stained with FITC-conjugated CD8α mAb as above, they were fixed with Cytofix/Cytoperm Buffer on ice for 30 min, and then washed twice with BD Perm/Wash Buffer. After that, the cells were treated with Cytoperm Plus Buffer on ice for 10 min and Cytofix/Cytoperm Buffer for 5 min at room temperature. Then, the samples were digested with 200 μg/mL DNase for 1 h at 37°C. After washing twice, samples were subsequently incubated with APC-anti-BrdU antibody (BioLegend, USA) for 30 min at room temperature and analyzed with flow cytometry after washing twice.

Cytotoxicity assay

The cell cytotoxicity was measured by detecting lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) release. A total of 2×106 HEK-293T cells in PBS were i.p. injected into each healthy Nile tilapia, which was then injected with 10 μg C188-9 or 5 μg OnIL-21 on both day 1 and day 3. On day 5, the spleen leukocytes were isolated as above, and incubated with 2×105 HEK-293T cells at 28°C for 12 h. Cytotoxicity was assessed using the Cytotoxicity Detection Kit Plus (Sigma, USA) according to the manufacturer's protocol.

Statistical analysis

All data were presented as mean±SEM, and were determined to define significance by a two-tailed Student t test; P values are defined as follows: *: P<0.05; **: P<0.01; ***: P<0.001. All data were statistically analyzed and displayed using GraphPad Prism v.10.0 software.

RESULTS

Nile tilapia encodes an evolutionarily divergent IL-21

We identified an IL-21 gene in the Nile tilapia genome using the NCBI GenBank database. Synteny analysis revealed that the “IL-21–cetn4–bbs12–fgf2–nudt6–afg2a–spry1” gene cluster is present across vertebrates, although the gene order varies among species (Supplementary Figure S1A). Despite the generally low amino acid sequence similarity of IL-21 among vertebrates, four cysteine residues essential for forming two disulfide bonds are conserved in Nile tilapia (Supplementary Figure S1B). Although the overall tertiary structure of Nile tilapia IL-21 showed limited similarity to that of mouse IL-21, both proteins share a characteristic four-α-helix core (Supplementary Figure S1C). To further elucidate its evolutionary context, we constructed a phylogenetic tree, which showed that Nile tilapia IL-21 first clustered with homologs from other teleosts and amphibian and then formed a sister group to reptilian, avian, and mammalian IL-21s, indicating a closer evolutionary relationship between teleost and amphibian IL-21 (Supplementary Figure S1D). Collectively, these results suggest that Nile tilapia encodes an evolutionarily divergent form of IL-21.

Activated CD4-1+ T cells are a major source of IL-21 in Nile tilapia

We next sought to identify the cellular sources of IL-21 in Nile tilapia. Stimulation of lymphocytes with P+I significantly upregulated IL-21 transcription, suggesting that activated lymphocytes can produce IL-21 (Figure 1A). Consistently, stimulation of leukocytes with T-cell-specific mitogen PHA markedly increased IL-21 transcription, indicating that IL-21 is likely derived from activated T cells (Figure 1B). Moreover, IL-21 expression was transcriptionally responsive to E. piscicida infection (Figure 1C). Our previous study showed that Nile tilapia T cells undergo extensive proliferation at 7 days post-E. piscicida infection (Li et al., 2023b), implying a potential link between T cell activation and IL-21 production. To pinpoint the cellular source of IL-21, we generated recombinant OnIL-21 (rOnIL-21) and developed an mAb against it (Figure 1D). Following mouse immunization, hybridoma generation, and antibody screening, we identified a clone producing relatively high levels of anti-OnIL-21 antibodies (Figure 1E). Through subcloning, we obtained an mAb that can detect the IL-21-producing cells in spleen lymphocytes of Nile tilapia (Figure 1F). This mAb can also recognize the recombinant OnIL-21 and IL-21 protein in spleen leukocytes (Figure 1G), confirming its high specificity. Using this mAb, we showed that OnIL-21 protein levels were markedly increased following both PHA stimulation and E. piscicida infection (Figure 1H, I), supporting its induction in activated T cells. In addition, we demonstrated that activated CD3ε+CD4-1+ T cells are a predominant source of OnIL-21. Notably, 32.3% of CD3ε+CD4-1+ T cells secreted IL-21, a frequency substantially higher than that of CD3ε+CD4-1- or CD3ε-CD4-1- T cell subset (Figure 1J). Consistently, in vivo depletion of CD4-1+ T cells led to a marked reduction of IL-21 transcription in leukocytes (Figure 1K), confirming the essential contribution of CD4-1+ T cells to OnIL-21 production. Together, these findings identify activated CD4-1+ T cells as a major cellular source of IL-21 in Nile tilapia.

IL-21 promotes CD8+ T cell proliferation and apoptosis during bacterial infection

Given the well-established role of IL-21 in regulating CD8+ T cells in mammals, we next investigated whether IL-21 influences CD8+ T cell immune processes in Nile tilapia. We found that rOnIL-21 could directly bind to CD8+ T cells (Figure 2A) and significantly promote the PHA-induced proliferation of CD8+ T cells in vitro (Figure 2B). Furthermore, i.p. injection of rOnIL-21 into E. piscicida-infected Nile tilapia markedly increased both the frequency and absolute number of CD8+ T cells (Figure 2C, D). BrdU incorporation assays revealed that OnIL-21 further enhanced bacteria-induced CD8+ T cell proliferation during infection (Figure 2E, F), consistent with the results of CFSE analysis. Bacterial infection alone induced apoptosis of CD8+ T cells; interestingly, this effect was further exacerbated by OnIL-21 treatment (Figure 2G, H). Together, these findings indicate that OnIL-21 facilitates the proliferation and apoptosis of CD8+ T cells, thereby promoting CD8+ T cell-mediated immune processes and potentially enhancing their antibacterial functions.

IL-21 enhances CD8+ T cell effector functions and improves antibacterial immunity

To determine whether IL-21 enhances T-cell effector responses in Nile tilapia, we first assessed its impact on leukocytes. Stimulation with rOnIL-21 markedly increased the transcription of key effector molecules, including IFN-γ, IL-2, TNF-α, Granzyme B, and Perforin A (Figure 3A–E), and also elevated the protein levels of IL-2, TNF-α, and Granzyme B in leukocytes (Figure 3F). Given that leukocytes comprise multiple effector cell types capable of producing these molecules, we next analyzed gated CD8+ T cells by flow cytometry to precisely evaluate their cytokine production in response to IL-21. Edwardsiella piscicida infection increased the production of Granzyme B (Figure 3G, H), IFN-γ (Figure 3I, J), and IL-2 (Figure 3K, L) in gated CD8+ T cells. Importantly, rOnIL-21 further enhanced the production of these effector cytokines (Figure 3G–L). Notably, rOnIL-21 administration resulted in a significant increase of cytotoxic activity as revealed by the LDH releasing assay (Figure 3M). These enhanced effector functions of CD8+ T cells induced by OnIL-21 led to more efficient bacterial clearance, as evidenced by reduced pathogen burden in the liver (Figure 3N), which ultimately alleviated mortality in infected Nile tilapia (Figure 3O). Together, these results demonstrate that IL-21 potentiates the effector functions of CD8+ T cells in Nile tilapia, thereby strengthening antibacterial immunity during infection.

Blockade of IL-21 signaling impairs CD8+ T cell proliferation and cytokine production

To further confirm the indispensable role of IL-21 in supporting optimal CD8+ T cell responses, we blocked IL-21 signaling using the anti-OnIL-21 mAb (αIL-21). Blockade of OnIL-21 in vitro significantly reduced the transcription of IL-2 and its receptor CD122 (Figure 4A, B), as well as IFN-γ, Perforin A, Granzyme B, and TNF-α during PHA stimulation (Figure 4C–F). More strikingly, treatment with αIL-21 mAb markedly impaired the PHA-induced secretion of Granzyme B and IL-2 from CD8+ T cells (Figure 4G–J), and correlated with a pronounced decrease of CD8+ T cell proliferation (Figure 4K). These findings demonstrate that inhibition of OnIL-21 disrupts both the proliferative and effector functions of CD8+ T cells, thereby highlighting the critical role of IL-21 in orchestrating CD8+ T cell-mediated immunity in Nile tilapia.

STAT3 regulates the IL-21-mediated CD8+ T cell immune processes in Nile tilapia

Next, we elucidate the mechanism by which IL-21 regulates CD8+ T cell immunity. As cytokines typically exert their effects through receptor binding, we stimulated splenic leukocytes with PHA and rOnIL-21 in vitro and infected Nile tilapia with E. piscicida in vivo. We found that the expression of the IL-21 receptor (IL-21R) was significantly upregulated following T cell activation (Figure 5A–C). In mammals, phosphorylated STAT3 (p-STAT3) translocates into the nucleus to regulate the transcription of target genes, thereby enhancing CD8+ T cell effector functions (Sun et al., 2023). Similarly, stimulation of spleen leukocytes with rOnIL-21 significantly increased STAT3 transcription (Figure 5D) and markedly induced STAT3 phosphorylation (Figure 5E). Consistently, we observed increased nuclear localization of p-STAT3 in Nile tilapia upon rOnIL-21 stimulation, suggesting that STAT3 signaling was functionally activated (Figure 5F). Flow cytometry further revealed a marked increase in STAT3 phosphorylation within CD8+ T cells after rOnIL-21 treatment (Figure 5G, H). Conversely, blockade of IL-21 signaling markedly decreased STAT3 phosphorylation (Figure 5I). To further examine the role of STAT3, we employed C188-9, a selective inhibitor that blocks STAT3 phosphorylation. Treatment with C188-9 effectively suppressed p-STAT3 level (Figure 5J). Inhibition of STAT3 signaling by C188-9 in vitro significantly suppressed IL-21-induced CD8+ T cell proliferation, as determined by CFSE assay (Figure 5K). This indicates that STAT3 acts as an essential transcription factor mediating the IL-21-driven proliferation of CD8+ T cells. Strikingly, administration of C188-9 to E. piscicida-infected Nile tilapia resulted in a pronounced reduction in both the percentage and absolute number of CD8+ T cells (Figure 5L, M), accompanied by impaired proliferation (Figure 5N, O) and aggravated apoptosis (Figure 5P, Q) of CD8+ T cells. These results demonstrate that activation of STAT3 signaling is essential for IL-21-mediated CD8+ T cell immune responses in Nile tilapia.

STAT3 signaling is essential for CD8+ T cell effector functions during bacterial infection

Concomitantly, we found that inhibition of p-STAT3 also attenuated the effector functions of CD8+ T cells in Nile tilapia. When leukocytes were stimulated with rOnIL-21 in the presence of p-STAT3 inhibitor, the transcription of IL-2, CD122, IFN-γ, TNF-α, Perforin A, and Granzyme B was dramatically suppressed (Figure 6A–F), accompanied by corresponding decreases in the protein levels of Granzyme B, Perforin A, and TNF-α (Figure 6G). To further evaluate the role of p-STAT3 in vivo, we inhibited p-STAT3 in E. piscicida-infected Nile tilapia and observed a pronounced reduction in the secretion of Granzyme B and IL-2 by CD8+ T cells (Figure 6H, I). These defects collectively led to decreased cytotoxicity (Figure 6J), impaired bacterial clearance (Figure 6K), and ultimately increased mortality of infected Nile tilapia (Figure 6L). Together, these findings demonstrate that activation of the STAT3 pathway is indispensable for sustaining CD8+ T cell effector functions and effective antibacterial immunity during infection.

DISCUSSION

CD8+ T cells are crucial effectors of adaptive immunity, playing a central role in host defense against infection. A sophisticated regulatory network involving antigen stimulation (Conley et al., 2016), costimulatory signals (Pardigon et al., 1998), cytokines (Valbon et al., 2016), metabolism (Sun & Chi, 2024), and epigenetic modifications (Henning et al., 2018) coordinately controls the activation, proliferation, differentiation, and functions of CD8+ T cells. Among these, cytokines are key mediators that influence the activation, proliferation, differentiation, survival, and effector functions of CD8+ T cells during immune responses (Ramanathan et al., 2008; Shi et al., 2007; Valbon et al., 2016). CD4+ T cells contribute to the activation and functional enhancement of CD8+ T cells by secreting cytokines, thereby amplifying the overall immune response (Laidlaw et al., 2016; Lu et al., 2021). IL-21, as one of pivotal cytokines, exerts notable regulatory effects on CD8+ T cells. However, the cellular source of IL-21 and its immunomodulatory mechanisms remain poorly understood in lower vertebrates. This study aims to characterize the origin of IL-21 in fish, investigate its impact on CD8+ T cells, and elucidate the underlying mechanism, thereby providing new insights into the adaptive immune system of fish.

Despite the relatively low overall sequences similarity between teleost and mammalian IL-21, this cytokine exhibits remarkable functional conservation. Bioinformatic alignments reveal that although the primary amino acid sequences have diverged during evolution, the key structural domains responsible for its biological activity remain conserved. Crucially, cysteine residues forming the essential disulfide bonds in the receptor-binding domain are highly conserved across a broad range of species (Li et al., 2022). These bonds are indispensable for maintaining the correct tertiary structure required for receptor interaction (Li et al., 2022; Muneta et al., 2004). Such structural conservation enables teleost IL-21 to effectively activate the JAK-STAT signaling pathway, thereby contributing to the regulation of CD8+ T cell-mediated antibacterial immune responses.

In mammals, IL-21 is primarily secreted by CD4+ T cells, with Tfh and Th17 subsets being the main producers. Besides CD4+ T cells, NKT cells, CD8+ T cells can also produce IL-21 (Spolski & Leonard, 2014). However, the cellular source of IL-21 in fish remains unclear. Using self-developed mAbs against Nile tilapia IL-21 and CD4-1, our results provide compelling evidence that CD4-1+ T cells are the primary source of IL-21 in fish. Furthermore, CD4-1+ T cells depletion experiments suggest the existence of additional IL-21-producing cell populations, a finding further supported by flow cytometric data also showing that non-T cells also secrete this cytokine in fish. Collectively, these results are consistent with observations in mammals. Notably, Nile tilapia IL-21 is capable of binding to CD8+ T cells and modulating their functions, indicating that the cooperative interaction between helper and cytotoxic T cells emerged early in vertebrate evolution. This also suggests that CD4-1+ T cells in fish are capable of providing in vivo support for CD8+ T cell–mediated immunity.

IL-21 plays an important role in anti-infective immunity by regulating antibody production by B cells, enhancing the cytotoxicity of CD8+ T cells, and modulating NK cell functions (Gharibi et al., 2016). Given its central role in promoting antibody responses, previous studies in fish have primarily focused on the effects of IL-21 on B cell immunity. For example, two IL-21 splice variants in grass carp are shown to enhance the proliferation of IgM+ B cells and promote IgM secretion (Cui et al., 2020). However, the role of IL-21 in regulating T cells has received less attention. In this study, we found that IL-21 significantly upregulated the transcription of IL-2, CD122, and Granzyme B in Nile tilapia, suggesting that IL-21 might participate in modulating T cell-mediated immune responses in fish. Although IL-21 has been proposed as a likely key regulator of T cell function in fish (Wang et al., 2011), the lack of tools to localize T cells has prevented firm conclusions. In particular, how IL-21 regulates CD8+ T cells remains unknown. This study addressed that gap and demonstrated that IL-21 promoted CD8+ T cell proliferation in Nile tilapia. In mammals, IL-21 potently facilitates CD8+ T cell expansion, primarily in synergy with IL-15, a mechanism requiring further investigation in fish (Zeng et al., 2005). IL-21 can also induce apoptosis of antigen-specific CD8+ T cells (Barker et al., 2007), which is consistent with our findings. Moreover, IL-21 enhances the cytotoxic potential of CD8+ T cells (Casey & Mescher, 2007). Similarly, we observed that IL-21 promoted the expression of IL-2, IFN-γ, and Granzyme B in CD8+ T cells, contributing to enhanced cytotoxic function. At the organism level, intraperitoneal injection of IL-21 in grass carp increases survival and alleviates pathological damage in the head kidney and intestine following bacterial infection (Zhang et al., 2020). Consistently, we found that IL-21 reduced hepatic bacterial burden and improved survival in infected Nile tilapia. These results collectively indicate that IL-21 possesses the ability to regulate CD8+ T cell immunity in early vertebrates and plays an important role in host defense.

In mammals, IL-21 activates the Janus kinase (JAK)-STAT, phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K), and mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) pathways, with JAK-STAT pathway playing a major role in the biological actions of IL-21 (Leonard & Wan, 2016). Upon binding to its receptor, IL-21 first activates JAK1 and JAK3, which subsequently phosphorylate STAT1, STAT3, and STAT5 (Asao et al., 2001). These phosphorylated STATs then translocate into the nucleus to regulate target gene expression, with STAT3 phosphorylation being stronger and more sustained in CD8+ T cells (Zeng et al., 2007). Existing evidence indicates that STAT3 is critical for CD8+ T cell proliferation, differentiation, function, survival, and epigenetic regulation (Sun et al., 2023; Yu et al., 2013). STAT3 is also implicated in fish anti-infective immunity. For example, it participates in Singapore grouper iridovirus (SGIV) replication and virus-induced cell death (Huang et al., 2014), and Aeromonas hydrophila infection leads to increased p-STAT3 level in spleen leukocytes of Nile tilapia (Li et al., 2023a). However, whether p-STAT3 regulates fish CD8+ T cells remains unclear. Our results demonstrated that IL-21-induced p-STAT3 played a key role in CD8+ T cell proliferation, apoptosis, and effector functions in fish. In mammals, IL-21-induced p-STAT3 signaling transcriptionally upregulates cytotoxicity-related genes in CD8+ T cells (Sun et al., 2023). Similarly, inhibition of p-STAT3 in Nile tilapia resulted in decreased Perforin A and Granzyme B protein levels in IL-21-stimulated leukocytes, along with reduced proliferation and cytotoxicity of CD8+ T cells after infection.

Notably, inhibitor experiments indicated that p-STAT3 exerted an anti-apoptotic role during the anti-infective response of CD8+ T cells, consistent with prevailing views in mammals (Song et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2020). Interestingly, although IL-21 strongly induced p-STAT3, it surprisingly promoted apoptosis of CD8+ T cells during infection, which might relate to the differentiation stage of these cells, given the context-dependent nature of IL-21 effect on B cell apoptosis (Kim et al., 2025). To accelerate the turnover of effector CD8+ T cells and enhance the efficiency of anti-infective immunity, IL-21 might not allow the anti-apoptotic function of p-STAT3 to dominate CD8+ T cell fate. In mammals, IL-21 mainly activates STAT1 and STAT3 in CD8+ T cells (Leonard & Spolski, 2005), two molecules generally considered to have opposing roles in apoptosis (Sironi & Ouchi, 2004; Wang et al., 2024). A possible explanation is that Nile tilapia IL-21 simultaneously activated STAT1 and STAT3 in CD8+ T cells, with p-STAT1 masking the anti-apoptotic effect of p-STAT3 at this stage, thus driving terminally differentiated CD8+ T cells toward apoptosis. Nevertheless, the STAT3 inhibitor C188-9 employed in our experiments acts systemically and can inhibit STAT3 activity across multiple cell lineages, including CD8+ T cells. Therefore, we cannot exclude the possibility that the observed effects on CD8+ T cell immunity may also involve indirect consequences of STAT3 inhibition in other cell types. Future studies should aim to establish CD8+ T cell-specific STAT3 knockdown or knockout models to more precisely delineate the intrinsic regulatory role of p-STAT3 in fish CD8+ T cell functions. Additionally, the observed non-exclusive source of IL-21 in fish suggests that generation of a CD4-Cre Nile tilapia model may further validate our finding.

In summary, this study provided the first clear evidence that CD4-1+ T cells in fish could secrete IL-21 and that IL-21 was involved in regulating CD8+ T cell-mediated adaptive immune responses. Nile tilapia IL-21 enhanced host defense against pathogens by promoting the proliferation, apoptosis, and cytotoxicity of CD8+ T cells. Mechanistically, Nile tilapia IL-21 regulated CD8+ T cell immune functions through nuclear translocation of phosphorylated STAT3, a pathway that appeared to be evolutionarily conserved. Our results demonstrated that fish have evolved a functional cytokine, IL-21, which modulated CD8+ T cell immunity via the STAT3 signaling pathway. This finding not only provides new theoretical insights into adaptive immunity in fish but also offers a novel perspective on the evolution of the adaptive immune system.

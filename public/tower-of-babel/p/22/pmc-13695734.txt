Trends in Usage and Payments for Glucagon‐Like Peptide‐1 Receptor Agonists in the United States, 2017 to 2022: A Nationally Representative Repeated Cross‐Sectional Study
Authors: Michael M. Hammond, Vaishnavi Krishnan, Lucia C. Petito, Dhruv S. Kazi, Sadiya S. Khan, Xiaoning Huang
Published: 2026

Glucagon‐like peptide‐1 receptor agonists (GLP‐1RAs) are effective therapies for glucose‐lowering and weight management. A growing number of therapies within this drug class are now available. 1 , 2 Despite the rapid uptake of GLP‐1RAs, differences in use and costs across GLP‐1RA subtypes and by indication are not known. This study aims to investigate how usage patterns and associated payments may differ across GLP‐1RAs and by diabetes status.

This study was considered exempt by Northwestern University's institutional review board due to the use of publicly available deidentified data. We analyzed data from the Medical Expenditure Panel Survey (MEPS), a nationally representative repeated cross‐sectional sample of US adults. All deidentified MEPS data are publicly available at https://meps.ahrq.gov/mepsweb/data_stats/download_data_files.jsp.

We examined trends from 2017 to 2022 to capture usage and payment patterns immediately before and after the expansion of GLP‐1RA indications. GLP‐1RAs included semaglutide, liraglutide, dulaglutide, exenatide, and GLP1 agonists (unspecified GLP‐1RA). For analyses of GLP‐1RA type, dulaglutide, exenatide, and GLP1 agonists were classified as “other GLP‐1RAs” to improve sample size. Out‐of‐pocket payments included payments made by self or family and included any deductible, coinsurance, and copayment amounts not covered by any sources as well as payments for services and providers not covered by the patient's insurance or other sources. Total payments included payments made by Medicare, Medicaid, self or family, state or local government, Tricare, veterans, employer, private insurance, other federal, and other insurance. The total payment and out‐of‐pocket payment for GLP‐1RAs were averaged per individual for each data year. We calculated the annual proportion of GLP‐1RA users by GLP‐1RA type.

A total of 1878 participants representing 20 343 000 adults reported use of any GLP‐1RA between 2017 and 2022. Of these, 10 556 000 (52%) were women, and 18 324 000 (90%) reported a history of diabetes. The mean±SD age was 59.8 (12.9) years in participants with diabetes and 47.8 (14.7) years in participants without diabetes.

From 2017 to 2022, GLP‐1RA use increased by 230% (from 1 545 000 to 5 092 000; P trend<0.01, Figure A) among participants with diabetes and 643% (from 115 000 to 855 000; P trend<0.01, Figure B) among participants without diabetes. The proportion of semaglutide users increased from 10% (195 000/1 946 000) in 2018 to 54% (2 725 000/5 092 000) in 2022 among those with diabetes and from 8% (14 000/169 000) in 2018 to 65% (556 000/855 000) in 2022 among those without diabetes (Figure E and F). Semaglutide use increased by 58% from 2020 to 2021 and 63% from 2021 to 2022 among those with diabetes. Semaglutide use increased by 334% from 2020 to 2021 and 91% from 2021 to 2022 among those without diabetes. The proportion of liraglutide users decreased from 42% (817 000/1 946 000) in 2018 to 12% (607 000/5 092 000) in 2022 among those with diabetes and from 58% (98 000/169 000) in 2018 to 21% (183 000/855 000) in 2022 among those without diabetes (Figure E and F).

Results are based on a total of 1878 participants representing 20 343 000 adults who reported use of any GLP‐1RA between 2017 and 2022. Total payments included payments made by Medicare, Medicaid, self or family, state or local government, Tricare, veterans, employer, private insurance, other federal, and other insurance. Out‐of‐pocket payments included payments made by self or family. GLP‐1RA indicates glucagon‐like peptide‐1 receptor agonist.

Between 2017 and 2022, the mean annual out‐of‐pocket payment decreased for participants with diabetes (from $414 to $252) and without diabetes (from $212 to $157). Between 2017 and 2022, the mean total annual payment increased from $4936 to $6722 for participants with diabetes and from $2651 to $6811 for participants without diabetes (Figure C and D). In 2022, the mean total and out‐of‐pocket payments for semaglutide were $5362 and $186 for participants with diabetes and $7588 and $162 for participants without diabetes.

In this nationally representative sample of US adults, the number of GLP‐1RA users markedly increased between 2017 and 2022, which was driven by increases in semaglutide. Second, the increase in semaglutide use was among individuals both with and without diabetes whereas liraglutide use decreased in both groups. Third, the greatest absolute increase in semaglutide use was observed between 2020 and 2022, following Food and Drug Administration approval of semaglutide for weight management in 2021. 1 , 3 With the expansion of evidence‐based clinical indications specifically for semaglutide and its greater effectiveness for weight management compared with older GLP‐1RAs, 1 , 2 semaglutide has become the predominant GLP‐1RA type and demand is likely to increase further in the coming years. Recent analyses from electronic medical records in selected populations have also demonstrated increasing use of semaglutide in select populations. 3 , 4 However, our findings raise concerns of affordability given the high annual total payments, particularly with lifetime use. 5 Among people without diabetes, payment fluctuations may reflect variation in uptake and coverage during a period when GLP‐1RAs were increasingly used for indications beyond diabetes.

A key strength of this study is the use of a nationally representative data set that describes national trends of GLP‐1RA usage and payments. Limitations include self‐report of diabetes, which may lead to underestimation of prevalence. Second, tirzepatide was not separately classified in publicly available 2022 MEPS data and was approved for weight management only in 2023. Third, we reported expenditures in nominal dollars and did not adjust for inflation. Last, insurance coverage across GLP‐1RAs differs by clinical rationale for use and may influence prescription patterns and out‐of‐pocket costs; however, MEPS does not capture prescription indication.

In conclusion, GLP‐1RA use significantly increased from 2017 to 2022, with the largest growth occurring for semaglutide after Food and Drug Administration approval for weight management. Further studies are needed to evaluate policies and strategies to reduce overall payments and out‐of‐pocket expenses of GLP‐1RAs.

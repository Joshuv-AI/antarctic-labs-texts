Feature-driven optimization for growth and mortality prevention in poultry farms
Authors: Suhendra, Hao-Ting Lin, Vincentius Surya Kurnia Adi, Asmida Herawati
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The poultry sector faces ongoing challenges in reducing mortality and improving growth performance under variable environmental and operational conditions. To address this, we developed and implemented a feature-driven optimization model to predict two key indicators: mortality and average weight (AvgWeight), based on five input variables—day, temperature, humidity, feed consumption, and water consumption. An 88-day dataset from over 20,000 Taiwan native broilers was preprocessed through outlier removal, normalization, and interpolation. Five machine learning models—Random Forest, Gradient Boosting Machine, Support Vector Machine, Linear Regression, and Neural Network (NN)—were initially evaluated. The baseline NN demonstrated superior multi-output accuracy and was further refined into three variants. Among them, the Ensemble NN—comprising five parallel networks—achieved RMSEs of 0.45 (Mortality) and 0.02 (AvgWeight), with Coefficient of Variation of RMSE values of 3.42 % and 1.62 %, respectively. Feature importance and sensitivity analyses identified “Day” as the most influential predictor for Mortality (importance: 20.391; sensitivity: 42.513), followed by feed (12.785; 13.285) and water (11.426; 13.648) consumption, while environmental variables had less impact under stable housing. Integrated into a MATLAB-based application, this intelligent system enables “what-if” scenario—offering practical decision support for poultry managers. By bridging traditional livestock management with deep learning-based soft sensors, this study contributes a scalable and accurate tool for advancing precision agriculture in poultry production.

Introduction

The poultry industry, producing 133 million tons of meat annually and projected to grow to 154 million tons by 2033, is a cornerstone of global food production, supplying essential protein to billions worldwide (OECD et al., 2024). However, the industry faces persistent challenges, including high mortality rates, inefficiencies in resource utilization, and increasing complexity in optimizing production systems (Bhuiyan and Wree, 2023; Gowri et al., 2023; Zhou et al., 2023). These challenges arise from intricate, non-linear interactions between environmental factors and biological responses, significantly influencing key performance indicators such as mortality rates, feed efficiency, and overall growth performance (Chauhan et al., 2025; Grebovic et al., 2022; Yin et al., 2021; Zhou et al., 2023). Addressing these issues necessitates the integration of advanced analytical methodologies capable of extracting meaningful patterns from high-dimensional, heterogeneous data.

Recent advancements in artificial intelligence (AI), particularly in machine learning (ML) or Deep Learning (DL), Computer Vision, the Internet of Things (IoT), and digital phenotyping, have introduced promising solutions for precision livestock farming (Ali et al., 2024; Bhuiyan and Wree, 2023; Gowri et al., 2023; Goyal et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024). For instance, predictive models such as Random Forest (RF) algorithms have demonstrated effectiveness in identifying critical determinants of dead-on-arrival (DOA) rates, including stocking density and body weight (Alijani et al., 2024; Pirompud et al., 2024). Similarly, IoT-driven digital phenotyping enables real-time behavioral and health monitoring, facilitating proactive decision-making to mitigate environmental stressors (Ahmed et al., 2024; Ali et al., 2024; Alijani et al., 2024; Chuang et al., 2021; Li et al., 2024; Ncho et al., 2024; Neethirajan, 2023). These technological innovations hold the potential to revolutionize poultry farming by enhancing operational efficiency, improving animal welfare, and optimizing production outcomes.

Despite these advancements, the practical implementation of AI-driven poultry farming solutions remains constrained by several critical limitations. Challenges such as model overfitting (Cakic et al., 2023; Pan and Chen, 2024; Umurungi et al., 2025) and the necessity for large-scale, high-quality datasets restrict their adaptability in real-world farm settings (Lv et al., 2023; Umurungi et al., 2025). Although techniques such as ensemble learning and deep learning have significantly improved predictive accuracy in poultry farming applications, key limitations persist, including the lack of model interpretability, adaptability to environmental variations, and dependence on extensive, high-quality datasets that are often challenging to obtain at the farm level (Abimannan et al., 2023; Alibabaei et al., 2022; Cheng et al., 2024; Herrmann and Marzocchi, 2023; Ren et al., 2022; Romero-Gainza and Stewart, 2023).

The poultry industry continues to face challenges in improving growth and reducing mortality under diverse environmental conditions. While artificial intelligence (AI) offers potential for predictive modeling, many existing systems lack robustness, interpretability, and practical applicability. This reveals a research gap: the need for AI models that are accurate, scalable, and suitable for real-world farm use.

To address this challenge, we developed a feature-driven optimization model to simultaneously predict mortality and average weight (AvgWeight) based on five key inputs: day, temperature, humidity, feed, and water consumption. The model was trained and validated using 88 days of commercial farm data collected from over 20,000 Taiwan native broilers. After evaluating five machine learning models—Random Forest, Gradient Boosting Machine, Support Vector Machine, Linear Regression, and Neural Network—the baseline NN was selected and further refined into three advanced variants. Among these, the Ensemble NN, consisting of five parallel NNs, yielded the best performance with RMSEs of 0.4453 for Mortality and 0.0209 for AvgWeight. Feature importance and sensitivity analyses were conducted to interpret the role of each input variable, revealing that age and resource consumption (feed and water) were the strongest predictors of mortality, while environmental variables were less impactful under stable housing. The final model was deployed into a MATLAB-based application capable of generating real-time predictions and performing “what-if” scenario. The results and discussion sections detail model comparison, fold-wise evaluation, refinement process, prediction accuracy, error distribution, input sensitivity, and application integration, offering a comprehensive understanding of how deep learning can enhance decision-making in poultry farming. This work contributes to AI-driven poultry management while emphasizing the importance of model interpretability and input diversity in future research.

Methods

This research was structured as a continuous pipeline, beginning with data acquisition from a commercial poultry house, followed by rigorous preprocessing steps—outlier removal, normalization, and interpolation—to ensure data quality and consistency. The processed dataset was then used to train and evaluate five machine learning models: Random Forest, Gradient Boosting Machine, Support Vector Machine, Linear Regression, and Neural Network. A feature-driven optimization strategy guided the refinement process by emphasizing key input variables to improve model generalization and predictive accuracy. The most effective model was further evolved into advanced variants and ultimately integrated into a MATLAB-based application for real-time prediction and scenario-based decision support. Data collection and preprocessing

This study followed a sequential and continual approach to develop predictive models for automating climate and resource management in commercial poultry houses with over 20,000 Taiwan native color broilers. Data were collected over an 88-day period (July 30 to October 25, 2023) in Nantou County, Taiwan. Temperature and humidity were recorded every 5 minutes using high-precision sensors, generating 25,344 environmental records. In addition, daily records were maintained for mortality, average weight, feed consumption, and water consumption, resulting in 88 entries for each outcome variable.

The five input variables—day, humidity, temperature, feed, and water—were identified as critical factors influencing poultry health and productivity. These features directly affect the two main output indicators: mortality and average weight, both essential for assessing overall flock performance in commercial poultry farming.

A feature-driven optimization approach was applied to guide model development, emphasizing the most influential features to improve predictive accuracy and model robustness. Data preprocessing involved removing outliers, interpolating missing values linearly, and normalizing numerical inputs. These steps ensured data consistency and reduced noise, which are crucial for the effective training of deep learning models in poultry performance prediction.

Model evaluation

To predict poultry mortality and average weight, five machine learning models were tested: Random Forest (RF), Gradient Boosting Machine (GBM), Support Vector Machine (SVM), Linear Regression (LR), and Neural Networks (NN). These models were evaluated using three key performance metrics: RMSE (Root Mean Squared Error) for measuring the absolute prediction error (Deprez and Robinson, 2024; Dumre et al., 2025; Singh et al., 2022) Coefficient of Variation of RMSE for normalizing RMSE as a percentage of the mean observed value to enable scale-independent comparisons (Müller-Plath and and Lüdecke, 2024), and Cross-Validation RMSE for assessing the model’s robustness and generalizability across different data partitions during cross-validation (Most et al., 2024; Qiu, 2024). To ensure accuracy and consistency, a Fold-Wise Model Evaluation was conducted with five folds. The use of Fold-Wise Model Evaluation allows for a systematic assessment of model stability across different subsets of data, mitigating the risk of overfitting while providing a more reliable estimate of predictive performance (Anwar and Anwar, 2025; Csóka et al., 2025) .

The best-performing model was further analyzed using several evaluation metrics. Including Actual vs Prediction Analysis to assess the alignment between predicted and actual values, ensuring the model accurately captures real-world trends; Error Distribution Analysis to examine the spread and pattern of residual errors, identifying potential biases or inconsistencies in model predictions (Zhang et al., 2025); Feature Importance Analysis and Sensitivity Analysis to evaluate the impact of individual input variables on prediction outcomes, providing insights into key factors influencing poultry mortality and growth performance (Kamalov, 2018). The final version was integrated into a MATLAB App Designer tool, enabling "what-if" scenario analyses for farm managers. This tool translates advanced AI-driven insights into actionable strategies for optimizing poultry management.

When considering RMSE for absolute prediction accuracy, Cross-Validation RMSE for generalization consistency, and Coefficient of Variation of RMSE for relative error scaling, NN strikes the best balance. It combines strong predictive accuracy with robust generalization and consistent performance across both outputs, making it the most reliable choice for this study's objective of predicting Mortality and AvgWeight in a commercial poultry house setting.

In k-fold cross-validation, RMSE is calculated for each fold, allowing for a comprehensive assessment of the model's performance across different subsets of the data (Górriz et al., 2024). The choice of k in k-fold cross-validation can significantly affect RMSE values, as different k values can lead to variations in model performance estimates. Studies suggest that common k values like 5 or 10 are often used to balance bias and variance in the estimates (Barut and Altuntaş, 2023). For this study, k-fold cross-validation ensured that NN consistently generalized well across folds, providing reliable performance metrics.

Despite the strong performance of RF and GBM in isolated cases, NN is better suited for this study due to its ability to handle both outputs (Mortality and AvgWeight) with consistent generalization across folds. While RF and GBM excel in certain areas, their lack of stability and overfitting tendencies make them less reliable for practical implementation. SVM and LR, on the other hand, consistently underperform, with high RMSE values for Mortality (>2.4) and AvgWeight (>0.05), indicating their limitations in handling the complexity of the dataset. NN strikes the best balance, making it the most reliable choice for achieving accurate and robust predictions in a commercial poultry management setting. Since NN achieved the best performance (hereafter referred to as the Initial NN), the next stage focuses on further developing this model to enhance its adaptability and predictive capability.

The Ensemble Neural Network emerged as the most robust model, achieving the lowest RMSE for Mortality (0.4453) and competitive RMSE for AvgWeight (0.0209), coupled with excellent generalization reflected in its low Coefficient of Variation of RMSE (3.42 % for Mortality, 1.62 % for AvgWeight). This success is attributed to the ensemble approach, which combines the strengths of multiple networks to minimize errors and enhance robustness. In contrast, the Initial NN, while performing well with balanced accuracy and generalization, was less competitive due to its simpler architecture.

The Optimized NN, despite its increased complexity (100 and 50 neurons per layer with 20 % dropout), suffered from overfitting, leading to poor Mortality predictions (RMSE: 2.3857) and weak generalization (Coefficient of Variation of RMSE: 18.34 %). Similarly, the Improved NN, which introduced batch normalization, failed to achieve lower errors, likely due to insufficient epochs (150) and ineffective tuning. These results emphasize the need for balanced architecture: while simpler models like the Initial NN offer speed and reliability, the Ensemble NN outperforms by providing accurate, well-generalized predictions, making it the optimal choice for precision agriculture tasks.

Permutation importance and sensitivity analysis are crucial techniques for understanding and interpreting neural networks, especially in domains requiring high transparency. Permutation importance evaluates the impact of shuffling feature values on model performance, providing a straightforward measure of feature relevance and aiding in model interpretability. Sensitivity analysis, as shown in Fig. 6, examines how changes in input variables affect outputs, offering insights into model robustness and potential vulnerabilities to adversarial examples or perturbations. For AvgWeight, both methods again identified Day as the dominant feature (importance: 0.904, sensitivity: 0.625), reinforcing its role in predicting growth due to natural weight gain over time. Feed and water consumption also contributed moderately, while temperature and humidity had negligible importance and sensitivity values (Fig. 6). To further connect these findings with actionable management practices, the sensitivity scores in Table 4 can be directly interpreted as decision levers at the farm level. For mortality prediction, Day (42.513) is biologically dominant, but feed (13.285) and water (13.648) consumption are the most controllable drivers. Even small deviations in daily feed and water intake therefore exert a greater impact on predicted mortality than environmental parameters such as temperature (0.658) and humidity (0.597). For AvgWeight, the sensitivity values are lower overall, yet feed and water again rank above environmental factors, underscoring the importance of consistent nutritional delivery for growth stability. These results demonstrate that feature importance analysis does not merely explain model behavior but also translates into concrete on-farm actions, such as ensuring continuous drinker line function and stable feeder access that directly safeguard survival and growth outcomes.

MATLAB App Designer integration

This application is a state-of-the-art tool for predicting chicken mortality and average weight, developed using MATLAB App Designer and leveraging an ensemble neural network (NN) validated with over 570,241 unique input combinations. As shown in Fig. 7, the graphical user interface (GUI) enables users to input environmental and resource variables—such as temperature, humidity, feed, and water consumption—and instantly receive real-time predictions. Users input five key variables: Day (1–88), Temperature (20–35 °C), Humidity (40–80 %), Feed Consumption (1–5 kg), and Water Consumption (1–3 L). These inputs are validated and normalized using the training dataset’s mean and standard deviation (e.g., meanInput = [10, 27, 65, 2, 2], stdInput = [5, 3, 10, 1, 0.5]). The normalized values are then fed into the ensemble NN, which comprises five independently trained models that each generate multi-output predictions for Mortality and AvgWeight. Final predictions are obtained by averaging the outputs of the five models, enhancing robustness and reliability. The results are denormalized using the training output metrics (meanOutput = [10, 2.5], stdOutput = [5, 0.5]) to provide interpretable, real-world values.

This research introduces innovative workflow leveraging an ensemble neural network (NN) to predict chicken mortality and average weight simultaneously, a key advancement in precision agriculture. Unlike traditional models that use separate systems for each task, this approach employs a multi-output neural network that reduces computational complexity while sharing features across tasks, improving efficiency and accuracy. Further enhancements include Batch Normalization and Dropout, which stabilize learning and prevent overfitting, and the Adam Optimizer, which dynamically adjusts learning rates to adapt to various datasets. The integration of ensemble learning ensures robust predictions by averaging outputs from multiple models, minimizing variance and handling uncertainties better than single-model approaches. These innovations allow for accurate, multi-dimensional predictions across environmental and operational variables, setting this method apart in the agricultural domain.

Practical implications

The integration of ensemble neural networks (NN) into a MATLAB-based application for poultry farms represents a significant leap forward in applying AI to real-world agricultural challenges. This application functions as a form of soft sensor, where multiple input measurements such as day, temperature, feed, and water consumption are processed together in real-time to deliver predictions for Mortality and AvgWeight. By automating predictions, the tool provides farm managers with actionable insights, allowing them to make informed decisions that optimize growth while minimizing mortality risks. The ensemble NN architecture enhances predictive accuracy and reliability, making it an indispensable component of modern precision agriculture. This aligns with findings from similar agricultural applications, such as the PEESCYP model for crop yield prediction, which demonstrated superior performance by leveraging ensemble techniques to reduce variance and overfitting (Tripathi and Biswas, 2024).

One of the most powerful features of this application is its ability to perform "what-if" analyses, further solidifying its role as a soft sensor. This functionality empowers farm managers to simulate various scenarios and proactively manage resources and environmental factors. For example, thresholds for feed and water consumption can be identified to minimize mortality risks and maximize efficiency. This capability aligns with broader trends in AI-driven decision support systems (DSS), which use real-time data analysis to optimize agricultural processes such as pest management and crop monitoring (Perini and Susi, 2004). The ability to adapt to diverse environmental conditions and provide immediate feedback enhances the tool's utility, reflecting similar benefits demonstrated by ensemble NN systems in agriculture, such as those implemented in Taiwan to improve prediction accuracy in complex datasets (Kung et al., 2016).

While the integration of ensemble NN into practical farming applications offers numerous advantages, challenges remain. Ensuring data consistency and addressing the complexity of agricultural environments are critical for maintaining accuracy and scalability. Success depends on high-quality data and the system’s ability to adapt to diverse farming contexts, as highlighted in studies emphasizing the importance of Big Data and AI for global food security (Pandey and Mishra, 2024). The ability of the MATLAB-based application to deliver reliable insights underscores its transformative potential in precision agriculture. It bridges the gap between theoretical advancements and real-world impact, setting a benchmark for future AI-driven solutions in mortality prevention and growth optimization in poultry farming.

Future research directions

While the ensemble neural network (NN) demonstrated remarkable predictive performance in poultry mortality prevention and growth optimization, several areas for improvement could enhance its accuracy and applicability. Advanced feature engineering, particularly the integration of real-time health monitoring and behavioral analytics, could provide a deeper understanding of mortality dynamics. For instance, convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have been used to monitor abnormal behaviors in cage-free hens, offering a reliable method for health assessment (Yang et al., 2024). Similarly, machine learning models utilizing RFID data have shown promise in predicting health outcomes for free-range hens, emphasizing the potential of additional data streams to improve mortality predictions (Welch et al., 2023). Incorporating temporal dependencies, seasonality, and environmental stressors into the model could further refine its ability to capture complex, non-linear patterns affecting poultry health and growth.

Expanding the application of ensemble NN models and their soft sensor capabilities to other livestock species or farming contexts could validate their versatility and scalability. Machine learning methods have already been used to predict culling and mortality risks in broilers based on feeding behavior, highlighting opportunities for cross-species applications (Alves et al., 2022). Moreover, AI has been successfully applied in livestock research for behavior detection and welfare assessment, demonstrating the potential for broader agricultural adoption (Clarke, 2023). Extending the current model to include genomic and environmental data from diverse species could unlock new opportunities in precision agriculture, addressing critical challenges in sustainable food production systems.

Hybrid modeling approaches could complement ensemble NN capabilities by improving model interpretability and performance. For instance, combining neural networks with traditional statistical methods such as Random Forest or Bayesian approaches has proven effective in predicting complex dynamics like dead-on-arrival rates in broilers (Pirompud et al., 2024). Enhancing the MATLAB-based application with adaptive learning features for critical thresholds could further optimize resource management and decision-making in commercial poultry farming. These advancements would bridge the gap between AI-driven innovation and practical applications, paving the way for more sustainable and efficient food production systems.

Challenges and limitations in mortality prediction

The ensemble neural network (NN) exhibited a broader error distribution in mortality predictions compared to average weight predictions. This discrepancy reflects the inherent complexity of mortality rates in poultry, which arise from a multifaceted interplay of environmental conditions, resource consumption, and biological variability. For instance, while AvgWeight predictions are largely driven by direct and linear relationships with inputs like feed and water consumption, mortality predictions are influenced by non-linear and multifactorial dynamics. These complexities demand more sophisticated modeling approaches to accurately capture the diverse factors contributing to mortality outcomes.

Environmental and biological factors significantly contribute to the variability in mortality rates. Genetic selection for fast growth, a cornerstone of modern broiler production, has been identified as a major risk factor for higher mortality (Forseth et al., 2024). Additionally, broilers raised in free-range systems face increased exposure to parasites and infections, further complicating predictions (Welch et al., 2023). Mortality traits also exhibit genetic variability, with heritability estimates ranging from 0.01 to 0.21, underscoring the role of genetic factors (Richter et al., 2024). Maternal effects, particularly during early life stages, have also been shown to influence mortality, emphasizing the need to incorporate such variables into predictive models (Richter et al., 2024). In this study, one key limitation lies in the absence of direct behavioral, genomic, and health-related data. Our model relied solely on environmental conditions and consumption patterns, which, although significant, may not fully represent the complex physiological and pathological factors driving mortality. This limitation suggests the need for more comprehensive and multimodal datasets in future research to improve model precision and applicability.

The present model was trained on data from more than 20,000 Taiwan native broilers, which differ markedly from fast-growing commercial broilers in growth trajectories, thermoregulation, and feeding behavior. As a result, the feature–outcome relationships observed in this study, where day is the dominant predictor and feed and water consumption strongly influence mortality, may not fully generalize across breeds. To strengthen external validity, future work should incorporate breed as a covariate or include growth-rate indicators such as average daily gain (ADG) during training. Domain-adaptation strategies and breed-stratified validation will also be essential to ensure that predictive performances remain robust across diverse production systems.

Conclusions

This study demonstrates the effectiveness of an ensemble neural network (NN) model in simultaneously predicting mortality and average weight (AvgWeight) in a commercial poultry farming context. Through a continual and feature-driven modeling approach—beginning with the evaluation of five machine learning algorithms and progressing through targeted refinements—the ensemble NN achieved the best overall performance. It delivered the lowest RMSE for Mortality and high precision for AvgWeight, confirming its robustness and balance in handling multi-output prediction tasks. The model was successfully integrated into a MATLAB-based application that supports prediction and scenario-based analysis, offering a practical decision-support tool for poultry farm managers.

The model utilized five input variables: age (day), temperature, humidity, feed consumption, and water consumption—factors that are routinely measured and operationally relevant in poultry production. While AvgWeight predictions aligned closely with feed and water intake patterns, mortality predictions showed broader variability, indicating more complex and nonlinear interactions. This highlights the different ways in which the same inputs contribute to each output. Although the model was optimized for predictive performance, initial results from feature importance and error distribution analyses suggest that input-output relationships vary in sensitivity and influence. These insights point to the potential value of future research focused specifically on enhancing interpretability.

A limitation of this study lies in the absence of behavioral, genetic, or health-related inputs, which may have constrained the model’s ability to interpret mortality outcomes more precisely. Although the current model emphasized predictive performance, initial feature-driven analysis suggests differing levels of input influence on each output. Future research should focus on enhancing interpretability by further exploring the relationships between inputs and outcomes and expanding the model to include more comprehensive data streams. This will support the development of more adaptive, accurate, and generalizable tools for sustainable poultry production and beyond.

Spiritual sensitivity in nursing and midwifery students and its relationship with spiritual intelligence and spiritual growth
Authors: Asma Ghonchehpour, Peiman Parandeh Afshar, Batool Tirgari, Mahboobeh Maazallahi, Mansooreh Azizzadeh Forouzi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background and aimSpirituality is the central core of human existential dimensions, and considering the positive effects of spirituality on all aspects of individuals’ individual and social lives, examining the components related to spirituality is of particular importance. This study aimed to determine the relationship between spiritual sensitivity and spiritual intelligence and spiritual growth in nursing and midwifery students.MethodsThis descriptive-analytical cross-sectional study was performed on 270 nursing and midwifery students at the School of Nursing and Midwifery of Kerman University of Medical Sciences in southeastern Iran in 2021–2022. The study instruments included demographic questionnaires, Tirri & Nokelainen spiritual sensitivity, King spiritual intelligence (SISIR), and SAI spiritual growth.ResultsThe mean spiritual sensitivity score was 38.31 ± 8.20, indicating a low level. The mean spiritual intelligence score was 50.08 ± 9.50, which corresponds to a moderate level. The mean score for spiritual growth was 122.75 ± 27.89, reflecting a level below moderate.30.1% (n = 80) of students had low spiritual growth, 63.5% (n = 169) were in average level and 6.4% (n = 17) were in high level. Spiritual sensitivity was positively correlated with spiritual intelligence and all its dimensions (r = 0.55, P < 0.001). Also, Spiritual sensitivity was positively correlated with spiritual growth in all its dimensions (r = 0.54, P < 0.001).ConclusionThe findings indicate a significant positive correlation between spiritual sensitivity and both spiritual intelligence and spiritual growth across all examined dimensions. The findings highlight the importance of enhancing spiritual sensitivity to support the development of spiritual intelligence and growth, which could improve holistic care in nursing and midwifery education. Addressing spiritual sensitivity may have significant implications for better psychological well-being and patient care in future healthcare professionals.

Introduction

Since the 1960 s, there has been a renewed focus on spirituality within the nursing and midwifery field. The foundational principles of nursing and a holistic approach enable nurses to meet patients’ spiritual needs effectively [1]. It is essential to incorporate spirituality into the undergraduate curricula for nursing and midwifery [1]. Teaching spirituality is seen as a beneficial method for enhancing nursing care quality, facilitating spiritual growth, and developing skills in spiritual care [2]. Spiritual sensitivity is connected to spirituality, but there has been insufficient attention to nurturing this sensitivity among nursing students [1].

Spiritual sensitivity (SS) refers to the ability to recognize important spiritual values in challenging situations and to understand one’s roles and responsibilities within those contexts. It aids in distinguishing between right and wrong, contributing to effective practice. Hay (1998) identifies three types of spiritual sensitivity: awareness sensing, mystery sensing, and value sensing. Awareness sensing involves reaching a deeper level of consciousness by actively “paying attention” to our surroundings. This type of awareness relates to a reflexive process of maintaining focus on one’s own attention or being conscious of one’s awareness. Mystery sensing pertains to our ability to go beyond everyday experiences and to engage our imagination. Value sensing emphasizes the importance of emotions as indicators of what we cherish, including concerns that relate to our existential questions and the search for meaning [3, 4].

Findings from a study conducted in Iran revealed that participants’ experiences of spiritual sensitivity (SS) were categorized into three primary themes: sense of value, spiritual growth and morale enhancement, and SS as a driving force for purposeful service delivery [3]. Previous research has shown that spiritual sensitivity is a crucial trait that enables nurses to navigate the tension between their own spiritual beliefs and those of their patients [5]. Catanzaro and McMullen reported that nursing students serving as parish/congregational nurses during their community health clinical rotations developed a heightened awareness of patients’ spiritual needs [6]. An article by Tiew et al. (2012) indicated that participants suggested that nurses with higher levels of spiritual well-being and awareness tended to exhibit more positive attitudes towards spiritual care and were more attuned to the spiritual needs of patients, which aligns with findings from other studies. The results of the study also supported the idea that essential qualities like sensitivity, empathy, caring, and spiritual awareness are foundational to spiritual assessment, which is vital for effective spiritual caregiving [5]. Attard et al. argued that nursing and midwifery education should incorporate the spiritual dimension of care into both theoretical and clinical aspects [7]. Another relevant concept in spirituality is spiritual intelligence. The use of coping strategies and problem-solving methods using spirituality for adaptation is an adaptive application of intelligence [8].

Spiritual intelligence (SI) serves as a framework for identifying the skills needed to utilize spirituality effectively, enhancing individual problem-solving and goal attainment [9]. It is important for fostering self-awareness in spiritual care and can be developed through practice and inquiry [9]. King outlines four components of spiritual intelligence: critical thinking, personal meaning creation, transcendental perception, and knowledge generation [10]. Although research has not directly linked spiritual intelligence to spiritual sensitivity, a study on midwifery and nursing students found a significant relationship between their emotional and spiritual intelligence [11]. Kornelis NamaBeni’s review of fifteen articles confirmed that spiritual intelligence positively impacts nursing care quality, which is classified into competence, caring, professionalism, and demeanor. In clinical settings, spiritual intelligence can enhance nurses’ roles and performance [12]. Sood et al. explored the relationship between personality traits, spiritual intelligence, and well-being, finding positive correlations between self-meaning generation and traits like agreeableness and neuroticism, as well as between transcendental awareness and openness [13]. Two studies conducted among university students in Iran reported a significant correlation between personality traits and spiritual intelligence [14, 15].

Spiritual growth (SG) is another essential concept closely linked with taking care of patients in all groups. Spiritual growth is an essential aspect of a health-promoting lifestyle suggested by Pender. It refers to the enhancement of inner resources, including transcendence, connection, and personal development, which help us attain inner peace, a sense of harmony, fulfillment, and a bond with the universe, as well as discover meaningful motivations for pursuing our life goals [16]. Hall and Edwards (2002) have defined the spiritual growth as the awareness of the existence of God and the improvement of the quality of the relationship with God [17]. M okhtari and et al. (2021) believed to effectively assess the spirituality of patients, nurses and nursing students must possess the necessary knowledge regarding human spiritual growth and development [18]. Results of a study showed, the spiritual growth is of the important predictors of the method of coping with students’ stressful situations and with increasing the growth score of spirituality, the problem-oriented coping with stressful conditions in students has increased [19].

Nurses need to understand the implications of spiritual and cultural values for clinical practice. In culturally diverse settings, including predominantly Islamic societies, spirituality is deeply integrated into personal identity and everyday life, shaping both health beliefs and caregiving values. From an Islamic perspective, spiritual growth is rooted in the pursuit of taqwa (God-consciousness), self-purification (tazkiyah al-nafs), and sincere service to others, all of which align closely with the ethical and compassionate foundations of nursing and midwifery. It is clear that concepts find meaning within social contexts, and the meaning of a concept varies from context to context [20]. Culture is a permanent part of life and every person has a culture. In multicultural societies, complex and different cultural understandings are common due to cultural diversity. These cultural understandings are shaped by concepts such as gender, age, race, socioeconomic level, ethnic characteristics, religious identity, sexual behaviors, education and history [21].

Iran is a religious country with 99% of its people being Muslim. People with different religions live in Iran. Most Iranians are Shiite (80–90%), and the next popular religion is Sunni (5–9%). Sistan and Baluchestan, a province at southeastern Iran, has a high Sunni population. It is clear that concepts find meaning within social contexts, and the meaning of a concept varies from context to context; therefore, a detailed exploration of a concept requires local, cultural, social and managerial factors [20]. In multicultural societies, complex and different cultural understandings are common due to cultural diversity. These cultural understandings are shaped by concepts such as gender, age, race, socioeconomic level, ethnic characteristics, religious identity, sexual behaviors, education and history [21].

Developed spiritual sensitivity is beneficial not only in interpersonal relationships, but also in educational, professional, and social contexts [22]. Since the educational process involves not only acquiring knowledge but also communication between individuals, understanding and explaining personality dimensions is important for educational and therapeutic programs. Considering that strengthening spiritual intelligence brings creativity, efficiency, effective coping in uncertain and ambiguous situations as well as in situations of conflict or severe stress [23], maintaining mental balance, performance with insight and improving daily functioning and physical and mental health of individuals [24] and is a kind of motivation for personal growth, including career advancement and at the organizational level [23], and spiritual sensitivity also plays an effective role in education and therapy, understanding and explaining personality dimensions for educational and therapeutic programs [22]. Examining the relationship between these components in strengthening or weakening students’ spirituality is of particular importance. Also, very limited studies have examined the relationship between these variables, and students have received less attention in recent studies, which indicates the need for further work.

The university years are crucial in shaping a student’s worldview, which influences how they interpret and respond to the world around them. These years can serve as a fertile ground for spiritual development, especially for nursing and midwifery students who play a vital role in providing holistic care. Their levels of spiritual sensitivity, intelligence, and growth may directly impact the quality of care they deliver, affecting patient trust, satisfaction, and overall health outcomes. Understanding these relationships is essential to inform educational strategies that foster spiritual competence, ultimately improving the holistic training of nurses and midwives to ensure more compassionate and culturally sensitive healthcare. Therefore, the aim of this study was to determine the relationship between spiritual sensitivity and spiritual intelligence and spiritual growth of nursing and midwifery students in southeast Iran.

Materials and methods

Study design and setting

This was a cross-sectional study conducted during 2021–2022. the most recent period for which data were available. This timeframe was chosen because it reflects current student experiences and allows for relevant, up-to-date findings. Razi school of Nursing and Midwifery of Kerman University of Medical Sciences was the research setting. This faculty is the oldest school of Nursing and Midwifery in southwest Iran.

Sample size and sampling

The population in this study were nursing and midwifery students of research setting. Questionnaire were distributed electronically (Google form). A convenience sampling method was employed primarily due to its practicality and efficiency, especially considering the restrictions posed by the COVID-19 pandemic, which limited face-to-face interactions and made random sampling more challenging. Morgan’s table was used to determine the appropriate sample size for a study. It is a commonly used approach in research design to estimate the sample size needed to make inferences about a population based on a representative sample. In the academic year 2021–2022, a total of 460 nursing and midwifery students were studying, with a sample size of 210 using the Morgan table, and ultimately 270 students participated in the study. This study carried out in. It should be mentioned that all students at the University of Medical Sciences in Iran must have completed approximately six to eight nonspecific credits related to ethics and religion.

Data gathering tools

Data were collected via a structured questionnaire comprising demographic questions and standardized scales for spiritual sensitivity, spiritual intelligence, and spiritual growth, selected based on their validity and reliability in previous research. The first part of the questionnaire was related to the demographic information including age, gender, marital status, religion, and residence status, field of study, academic year, and educational grade.

Spiritual sensitivity scale

The 20 – item spiritual sensitivity scale of Tirri & Nokelainen (2011) was used to measure students’ spiritual sensitivity which was developed for measuring spiritual sensitivity. This questionnaire consists of four subscales of awareness sensing (items 1,5,13,17,9), mystery sensing (items 2,6,10,14,18), value sensing (items 3,7,11,15, 19) and community sensing (items 4, 8, 12, 16, 20). The participants were asked about the extent of spiritual sensitivity on five – point Likert scale, ranging from 5 (strongly agree) and 1 (strongly disagree). The score of SSS (spiritual sensitivity Scale) was the sum of 4 sub-scales. The minimum and maximum score ranging between 20 and 100 respectively [25]. To better interpret the scores in this study, the midpoint was used, which for this tool is 60, considering the score range of 20–100. The scale has demonstrated good psychometric properties in prior studies, with Cronbach’s alpha ranging from 0.60 to 0.73 [25].

Validity and reliability spiritual sensitivity scale in the Iranian context

Since Tirri & Nokelainen’s Spiritual Sensitivity Scale was not available in Persian, we undertook a process of translation and psychometric validation. The scale was translated through forward and backward translation by bilingual experts, with discrepancies resolved by a panel of specialists to ensure content fidelity. Content validity was confirmed through review by ten nursing faculty members for clarity and relevance. The finalized 20-item scale demonstrated good internal consistency, with a Cronbach’s alpha of 0.85 in a pilot sample of 20 nursing and midwifery students, indicating its reliability for use in Iran.

Spiritual intelligence

The Spiritual Intelligence Self-Report Inventory (SISRI), developed by King (2008), was utilized to evaluate the spiritual intelligence of students. The SISRI is free to use for academic, educational, or research purposes.The Persian version of this questionnaire was psychometrically tested in Iran, and we used the Persian version [26]. This instrument comprises 24 items divided into four subscales: critical existential thinking (items 1, 3, 5, 9, 13, 17, 21), personal meaning in life (items 7, 11, 15, 19, 23), transcendent awareness (items 2, 6, 10, 14, 18, 20, 22), and conscious state expansion (items 4, 8, 12, 16, 24). Each item on the SISRI is rated on a 4-point Likert scale (0 - Not at all true of me, 1 - Not very true of me, 2 - Somewhat true of me, 3 - Very true of me, 4 - Completely true of me), with total scores ranging from 0 to 96. It is important to note that the response to item #6 must be reversed before calculating the total score. A higher score indicates greater levels of spiritual intelligence, while a lower score signifies lower levels. To better interpret the scores in this study, the midpoint was used, which for this tool is 48, considering the score range of 0–96. A study by Nurayunee (2017) reported a total reliability value of 0.92 for the Spiritual Intelligence Self-Report Inventory [27]. In Iran, the validity and reliability of the Persian version of SISIRI have been confirmed at a value of 0.88 [26].

Spiritual growth

The spiritual growth was assessed utilizing the Spiritual Assessment Inventory which was developed by Hall et al. (2002). The Persian version of this questionnaire was psychometrically tested in Iran, and we used the Persian version [28]. The questionnaire scored on a Likert Scale of 1 to 5, which recorded the answers varying from 1 (not at all true), 2 (slightly true), 3 (moderately true), 4 (substantially true), to 5 (very true). Higher scores reveal more of the attribute being measured. To better interpret the scores in this study, the midpoint was used, which for this tool is 141, considering the score range of 47–235.

Spiritual growth score is obtained from a total of 6 subscales, a score between 47 and 110 indicates a low level of spirituality, a score between 110 and 157 indicates a moderate level of spirituality in the person and a score between 157 and 235 indicates a high level of spirituality in the person. In prior research, these subscales confirmed good internal reliability (alpha scores between 0.73 and 0.95) [29]. Hall et al. (2002), Cronbach’s alpha coefficient related to internal correlation for the subscale of consciousness 0.95; Disappointment 90/0; Actual acceptance 0.83; Magnification 0.73; Instability 0.84 and effectiveness control 0.77 were reported [5], which show the optimal reliability of this test. In Iran content validity was reported as acceptable by Ghomian et al. (2013), and the Cronbach’s alpha coefficient was reported 0.93 [28].

Data collection

The researcher initiated the sampling process after obtaining approval from the Head of the School of Nursing and Midwifery at Kerman University of Medical Sciences. Due to the COVID-19 pandemic and the absence of students from the research environment, the questionnaires were prepared electronically, and sampling was conducted via an online method. Google Forms was created for online data collection The efficacy of the online google forms and rate of responsiveness was assessed on 20 student. All twenty students submitted responses, and their comments were implemented on Google Forms to improve sending links and receiving responses. Initially, the researcher identified eligible participants and invited them to participate in the study by sending text messages. During individual and private interviews, the study’s objectives, procedures, and the process of completing the electronic questionnaire were explained to each participant. Participants who expressed interest in participating were asked to review and sign a written informed consent form by access to a provided link, and then proceeded to enter the study. Subsequently, the questionnaire link was sent to eligible nursing and midwifery students currently enrolled, and sufficient time was allocated for completing the questionnaire. Additionally, students were assured that their participation would not interfere with their academic progress. Inclusion criteria for the study were: being a student at the School of Nursing and Midwifery and having no known cognitive or psychological issues (self-reported). Students who were visiting students at the School of Nursing and Midwifery in Kerman or who completed incomplete questionnaires were excluded from the study (More than 10% of all questions).

Data analysis

Data analysis was conducted using SPSS version 19. Descriptive statistics (frequency, percentage, mean, and standard deviation) were employed to summarize the demographic characteristics and scores of spiritual sensitivity, spiritual intelligence, and spiritual growth. The distribution of data was assessed for normality using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, skewness, and kurtosis; the test values were within acceptable thresholds (p > 0.05), indicating normality. Pearson correlation coefficients were calculated to examine relationships between variables. Independent t-tests and ANOVA were used to explore differences in spiritual sensitivity across demographic groups. A significance level of 0.05 was considered for all analyses.

Discussion

The findings of this study demonstrate a significant positive correlation between spiritual sensitivity and spiritual intelligence, as well as between spiritual sensitivity and spiritual growth among nursing and midwifery students. While there is a growing body of research on spirituality in healthcare, studies specifically exploring the direct relationship between spiritual sensitivity, spiritual intelligence, and spiritual growth are limited. This gap may be due to the complexity of these constructs and the lack of standardized measurement tools, which can pose challenges for researchers. Additionally, cultural and contextual factors may influence the focus of existing studies, leading to a scarcity of research in this particular area. Addressing this gap, our study aims to contribute to the broader understanding of how these aspects are interconnected in nursing and midwifery students, providing a foundation for future research and practical application.

According to the results, spiritual sensitivity in students was low. However, contrary to the results of the present study, in the study of Rabiei Vaziri, et al. (2025) in Iran, nursing students had high spiritual sensitivity [30]. The mean spiritual intelligence score corresponds to a moderate level, which was inconsistent with the results of some studies [31, 32].In the study by AMiri et al. (2019) in Iran, nurses had higher than average spiritual intelligence [32]. In this study, the average spiritual growth score of the participants was a level below moderate. No similar study was found in this field; however, a study conducted on students by Lau et al. in China (2015) showed that students who engage in spiritual practices have higher levels of spiritual growth compared to students who do not engage in spiritual practices [33].

The results indicated a significant positive correlation between spiritual sensitivity, spiritual intelligence among the participants. In this context, no similar study was found that examined the relationship between these two variables; however, the results of a review study, which examined the sources of spirituality, stated that spiritual sensitivity and spiritual intelligence are among the competencies needed by individuals and are factors in creating trust, empathy, self-awareness, self-control, and the ability to respond appropriately to the emotions of others [23]. Also, Tirri et al., in their study (2006), which examined the categories of spiritual sensitivity, stated that spiritual intelligence makes individuals aware of their own and others’ emotions and helps individuals evaluate the most meaningful action and provides empathy, compassion, motivation, and the ability to respond appropriately to pain or pleasure for individuals [3]. Spiritual intelligence promotes social behaviors and respect for the rights and emotions of others [34]. On the other hand, people use their spiritual intelligence when they want to use their spiritual capacities and resources to make important decisions and thoughts on existential issues or to try to solve daily problems [35]. Spiritual sensitivity leads to the coordination of human observable activities with spiritual values, as well as gives a cognitive and motivational meaning to spirituality and leads man to a specific action [3]. Zohar introduces spiritual intelligence as the highest and ultimate human mental ability [36]. Spiritual intelligence includes a type of adaptation and problem-solving behavior that shows the highest levels of development in various areas of cognitive, moral, emotional and interpersonal in individuals and helps the individual to adapt to the surrounding phenomena and achieve internal and external integration. Growth intelligence gives a person a general and meaningful view of life goals, experiences and events and enables him to frame and reinterpret his experiences and deepen his cognition and knowledge [37]. Hence, people use their spiritual intelligence when they want to use their spiritual capacities and resources to make important decisions and thoughts on existential issues or to try to solve daily problems [35].

This study identified a statistically significant positive relationship between spiritual sensitivity and spiritual growth, a connection that has not been previously explored in existing research. This relationship likely exists because both spiritual sensitivity and spiritual growth aim to achieve similar outcomes, primarily focused on addressing and resolving individuals’ and patients’ problems and issues. Spiritual sensitivity facilitates the alignment of observable human activities with spiritual values, providing cognitive and motivational meaning that guides individuals toward purposeful actions [22]. Given that these concepts are profoundly rooted in nursing science, this underscores the importance of understanding their interrelation and highlights the valuable contribution of this research to the field [9, 38, 39]. Spiritual growth, characterized by stronger perceived connections with the divine, a greater sense of meaning, increased spiritual faith, active engagement in religious and spiritual practices, feelings of unity and self-transcendence, positive shifts in worldview, enhanced connectedness with others, and a reduction in fear of death, is essential for coping with stress and building resilience in demanding healthcare environments. For example, students exhibiting higher levels of spiritual growth are more likely to employ problem-focused coping strategies in stressful academic and clinical settings compared to those with lower levels of spiritual development. Furthermore, spiritual sensitivity supports the discernment of right and wrong, fostering behaviors aligned with moral principles that contribute to spiritual growth. Such growth is marked by a deeper perceived connection with the divine, an increased sense of meaning, and greater engagement in religious and spiritual practices, which collectively promote positive personal and professional development. This finding reinforces the notion that spiritual sensitivity is not merely a skill necessary for effective patient care but also a vital component of personal development that nurtures students’ own spiritual maturation. Ultimately, spiritual sensitivity plays a crucial role in fostering holistic growth, benefiting both healthcare providers and those they serve [19, 35].

Results from a qualitative study on the conceptual definition and empirical validation of the spiritual sensitivity scale indicated that participants’ experiences of spiritual sensitivity were categorized into three central themes: sense of value, spiritual growth and morale enhancement, and spiritual sensitivity as a motivator for purposeful service delivery [3]. Ferreira (2011) identified “mystery sensing” as one aspect of spiritual sensitivity, which pertains to our ability to transcend everyday experiences and apply imagination. This aspect relates to Gardner’s (1999) interpretation of spiritual intelligence as achieving a particular state of being while addressing cosmic or existential concerns, emphasizing the enigmatic nature of these experiences [40].

The second aspect of spiritual sensitivity is termed mystery sensing, which pertains to our ability to rise above daily experiences and engage our imagination. For example, the beauty and awe of a sunrise or sunset evoke a sense of mystery that remains even when scientific explanations are provided. Imagination plays a crucial role in religious practices, as it employs metaphors, symbols, narratives, and liturgies to convey the otherwise ineffable experience of the sacred. This aspect aligns with Gardner’s (1999) perspective on spiritual intelligence as the “achievement of a state of being” and the “concern with cosmic or existential issues,” while highlighting the enigmatic quality of such experiences [40].

The third category of spiritual sensitivity is value sensing. This category emphasizes the importance of feelings as a measure of what we value. Among such things are the issues that touch our existential questions and meaning seeking. This category resembles with Gardner’s (1999) definition of spiritual intelligence as the “concern with cosmic or existential issues” [41].

Grading the integration of spiritual nursing care in the clinical environment, it is stated that nurses who understood the attitudes and feelings of others were able to develop spiritual sensitivity, and enabled nurses’ spiritual sensitivity to deal with issues such as suffering, frustration, and spiritual disorder [42].

Nurses who understand the attitudes and feelings of others can show spiritual sensitivity. Spiritual sensitivity enables nurses to deal appropriately with issues such as suffering, frustration, and spiritual disorder [42]. In order to provide spiritual care to patients, nurses perform interventions based on ethical principles. Therefore, spiritual care can be considered as a moral matter that nurses must have spiritual sensitivity to do this important thing; In other words, spiritual sensitivity is one of the characteristics of nurses and high levels of spiritual sensitivity of nurses play a positive role in providing spiritual care to patients [42–44]. Also, spiritual sensivity enhances the connection between nurses and patients by promoting empathy, trust, and personalized care. It also improves the nurse’s ethical awareness and emotional sensitivity, both of which are vital for providing comprehensive holistic care [30]. Nurses who possess higher levels of spiritual well-being tend to have a more optimistic outlook on spiritual care, nursing responsibilities, and their dedication to the profession [43]. Also, a study has shown that arts-based interventions can promote spirituality growth and can help students explore and express spirituality related to nursing and it can be essential to the delivery of holistic nursing care [44]. In the field of spiritual intelligence in nursing care, Nurses with higher spiritual intelligence, due to their ability to recognize themselves and others, the skill of determining the purpose and why of being in the hospital, display a higher level of values such as responsibility, compassion, and emotion management; therefore, they provide higher quality nursing care [32]. High spiritual intelligence in nurses can help improve their mental health status to respond to patients’ needs and, as a result, patient safety and organizational efficiency [36].Also, the results of a systematic review study have shown that the relatively high level of spiritual intelligence among nurses that was moderately associated with professional nursing practice [37].

These results underscore the interconnected nature of these spiritual constructs and highlight their importance in healthcare education and practice. These results suggest that integrating strategies to enhance spiritual sensitivity within nursing and midwifery education could have a broad impact on students’ overall development. By fostering spiritual sensitivity through reflective practices or spiritual training, educators may also promote spiritual growth and intelligence, better equipping students to provide compassionate and holistic care. Such curricular interventions could therefore play a crucial role in preparing future nurses to meet patients’ spiritual needs and improve care quality. Overall, studying and improving these attributes could enhance both personal and professional growth in nursing and midwifery students. Training nurses and midwives to be proficient and confident in delivering spiritual care has become a focus of international research. There is an increasing body of evidence highlighting the importance of spirituality in enhancing individuals’ health and well-being.

Study limitation

One of the limitations of the present study was the data collection method, which was conducted through social media. As a result, individuals who did not have access to or did not use these technological resources were unable to participate, potentially affecting the representativeness of the sample. Another limitation was the relatively small sample size, consisting of students from the Kerman School of Nursing and Midwifery. This limits the generalizability of the findings to the broader population of nursing and midwifery students across Iran. Additionally, the study employed a convenience sampling method, which may introduce selection bias and further limit the ability to generalize the results. Convenience sampling does not guarantee a representative sample of the entire population, and caution should be exercised when extrapolating these findings. Regarding the study design, it is important to acknowledge that the cross-sectional nature of this research prevents us from establishing causal relationships between spiritual sensitivity, spiritual intelligence, and spiritual growth. The data reflect a single point in time, and longitudinal studies are needed to better understand the directionality and causality of these relationships.

Conclusion

The results of this study showed that spiritual sensitivity was positively correlated with spiritual intelligence and spiritual growth. According to the results of this study, the necessity and importance of planning and implementing a plan to strengthen students’ spiritual sensitivity through the inclusion of course content to strengthen the spiritual intelligence and spiritual growth of students can be emphasized. Also, according to the total score of spiritual intelligence and spiritual growth at the moderate level, it is suggested that by holding classes and courses of spiritual intelligence training, spiritual growth and its components, spiritual intelligence and spiritual growth of students in them. Recommended in Future research, examine the relationship between spiritual sensitivity and other psychological components such as social adjustment, emotional, resilience, and personality traits.

Practical implications for nursing and midwifery practice

The future practical applications could include integrating spiritual sensitivity training into nursing and midwifery education programs. This training can focus on developing communication skills, understanding patients’ spiritual needs, and providing spiritual care. Implementing such approaches in clinical settings may improve patient satisfaction, reduce stress, and enhance overall care quality. These findings can serve as a guide for developing protocols that promote holistic nursing and midwifery practices and better patient outcomes.

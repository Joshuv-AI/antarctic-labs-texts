Synthesis, physicochemical properties and structure–property relationships of novel ionic liquids [Taz(2,n)][Acgly] (n = 5, 6)
Authors: Jing Qiao, Jie Meng, Kunhao Liang, Haiyun Yao, Lanju Liang, Xuefeng Wang, Dawei Fang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Two novel 1-ethyl-4-alkyl-1,2,4-triazolium acetylglycinate ionic liquids, [Taz(2,5)][Acgly] and [Taz(2,6)][Acgly], were synthesized and characterized. The density, surface tension and refractive index were systematically measured at 288.15–323.15 K by the standard addition method to eliminate the interference of trace water. The effects of temperature and cationic alkyl chain length on these properties were investigated, and the contributions of methylene groups to molecular volume, standard entropy and lattice energy were quantified. A high-precision surface tension prediction model was established by integrating molar surface Gibbs free energy into the Lorentz–Lorenz equation. The polarity scale and polarity coefficient were calculated to evaluate the polarity of the ionic liquids, and the gas-phase ion-pair association enthalpy was derived via thermodynamic cycles to reveal cation–anion interaction modes. This work enriches the fundamental property database of 1,2,4-triazolium-based ILs and provides reliable data support for the structural design and performance optimization of novel ionic liquids.

Introduction

Ionic liquids (ILs) are low-melting molten salts composed of organic cations and inorganic/organic anions, featuring low volatility, high ionic conductivity, wide electrochemical stability windows, and highly tunable structures.1–6 These unique advantages make ILs ideal candidates for green solvents, electrocatalysis, battery energy storage, and carbon capture and conversion fields.7–15 As a rapidly developing branch of functional ILs, 1,2,4-triazolium-based ILs exhibit superior thermal and chemical stability compared with conventional imidazolium ILs, while amino acid-derived anions endow ILs with biocompatibility and environmental friendliness, rendering this type of ILs highly valuable for fundamental research and applied development.16–20

The basic physicochemical properties of ILs, including density, surface tension, and refractive index, are essential fundamental parameters for evaluating their intermolecular interactions, aggregation states, and application potential. Density reflects the packing efficiency of ion pairs and directly affects volumetric properties;21 surface tension determines surface behavior and interfacial compatibility;22 refractive index characterizes molecular polarization and optical response.23 On this basis, the derived thermodynamic and surface properties such as molecular volume, lattice energy, surface entropy, and polarity can further reveal the intrinsic structure–property relationships of ILs, which is critical for rational molecular design and performance regulation.23 However, systematic experimental data and in-depth analysis of these core properties for 1,2,4-triazolium acetyl amino acid ILs are still insufficient, limiting the full understanding of their structural characteristics and interaction mechanisms.

In particular, the influence of temperature and cationic alkyl chain length on the density, surface tension, and refractive index of such ILs has not been systematically clarified, and the quantitative contribution of methylene groups to molecular volume, standard entropy, and lattice energy remains to be verified.24,25 Meanwhile, reliable prediction models for surface properties and quantitative evaluation methods for polarity are lacking, which restricts the accurate design and targeted optimization of this novel IL system.

On the basis of the existing research results of [Taz(2,4)][Acgly],23,25 this work further selects pentyl and hexyl homologous compounds [Taz(2,n)][Acgly] (n = 5, 6) for continuous exploration, so as to systematically analyze the variation law of physicochemical properties with the gradual extension of alkyl chains, and key derived parameters are calculated. Combined with thermodynamic models, the cation–anion interactions and polarity characteristics are explored. This study supplements the basic property database of 1,2,4-triazolium-based ILs and provides fundamental data and theoretical support for the structural design and property optimization of this class of novel ILs.

Preparation

[Taz(2,n)][Acgly] (n = 5, 6) were designed and synthesized for the first time in this work,26–29 with their synthetic route illustrated in Fig. 1.

Synthesis of the novel ILs [Taz(2,n)][Acgly] (n = 5, 6).

Initially, a mixture of 1,2,4-triazole, methanol, and sodium methoxide (molar ratio 1 : 1 : 1) was introduced into a round-bottom flask and stirred at 25 °C. Subsequently, bromoethane was added dropwise, and the mixture was refluxed with stirring at 65 °C for 48 h, followed by distillation to afford 1-ethyl-1,2,4-triazole. Next, 1-ethyl-1,2,4-triazole reacted with 1-bromopentane (molar ratio 1 : 1.2) to generate [Taz(2,5)][Br].27 The aqueous product solution was slowly eluted through a pretreated anion exchange column, where bromide ions were exchanged with hydroxide ions via electrostatic interaction of the resin to achieve complete anion conversion, and the strongly alkaline effluent was collected under real-time monitoring with pH test paper and AgNO3–HNO3 solution until precipitation was observed. Finally, [Taz(2,5)][OH] was reacted with equimolar N-acetyl-glycine at 25 °C for 48 h to yield the target compound,28,29 which was vacuum-dried at 55 °C for 48 h. Dichloromethane and acetonitrile served as extractants in the process. The synthetic procedure for [Taz(2,6)][Acgly] is essentially consistent with that of [Taz(2,5)][Acgly], and hence is not elaborated further.

Characterization

[Taz(2,n)][Acgly] (n = 5, 6) were comprehensively characterized by 1H-NMR, 13C-NMR, FT-IR spectroscopy, and thermogravimetry (TG) (see Fig. S1–S8), with both compounds isolated as light yellow liquids, exhibiting consistent macroscopic properties of the homologous ionic liquid series.

1H-NMR spectra (300 MHz, DMSO-d6) showed no impurity peaks, confirming target product formation: [Taz(2,5)][Acgly] displayed δH = 10.68 (s, 1H), 9.34 (s, 1H), 7.40 (s, 1H), 4.45 (m, 2H), 4.29 (t, 2H), 3.79 (d, 2H), 1.50 (m, 5H), 1.34 (t, 3H), 1.29 (m, 4H), 0.90 (t, 3H), while [Taz(2,6)][Acgly] exhibited δH = 10.82 (s, 1H), 9.38 (s, 1H), 7.52 (d, 1H), 4.45 (m, 2H), 4.30 (t, 2H), 3.82 (m, 1H), 1.81 (m, 5H), 1.49 (t, 3H), 1.28 (m, 6H), 1.15 (d, 3H), 0.88 (t, 3H). Determination of physicochemical properties

[Taz(2,n)][Acgly] (n = 5, 6) possess hydrophilicity and a propensity to form hydrogen bonds with water, a feature that renders their separation from water difficult via conventional methods. To mitigate such interference from trace water, the standard addition method (SAM) was adopted. Within the temperature range of 288.15–323.15 K (with 5 K intervals), density (ρ), surface tension (γ), and refractive index (nD) of the ILs with different water contents were measured, and the physicochemical properties of anhydrous ILs were obtained from the intercept of linear regression plots.

Density, surface tension, and refractive index were determined using a DMA 5000M densitometer (Anton Paar), a DP-AW surface tension meter (Nanjing Sangli), and a WZS-1 Abbe refractometer, respectively.23,24 During measurements, samples were housed in protective sleeves to ensure temperature constancy. Prior to formal testing, the aforementioned instruments were carefully calibrated, with detailed parameters reported in a previous work.24,30 The standard uncertainties were 0.05 kg m−3 for ρ, 0.0001 N m−1 for γ, and 0.0016 for nD.

Table 2 presents the experimental data of density, surface tension and refractive index for ILs [Taz(2,n)][Acgly] (n = 4, 5, 6) at 288.15–323.15 K.23 At a constant temperature, the density, surface tension and refractive index gradually decrease with the extension of alkyl chains on the triazolium cation under the same anion condition. The introduction of methylene groups (–CH2–) increases ion volume, loosens molecular packing and weakens intermolecular interactions, thus reducing the density of ionic liquids. This finding is in good agreement with the previous report,23 confirming that –CH2– substitution on both cations and anions can effectively lower the density. Longer cationic alkyl chains weaken interfacial forces and reduce surface tension, which is contrary to the effect of methylene modification in anions. Such difference indicates that cations and anions possess distinct regulation effects on surface tension.31 Furthermore, the refractive index decreases regularly with the lengthening of cationic alkyl chains and the addition of methylene groups, which is consistent with the published conclusions of Almeida32 and Deetlefs et al.33 For each IL, increasing temperature accelerates ionic thermal motion and expands intermolecular spacing, resulting in a monotonic decline in density, surface tension and refractive index.

Volumetric properties

The molecular volume (Vm) of the ionic liquid, defined as the volume occupied by one individual cation–anion pair, was computed from the measured density values using eqn (4):4Vm = M/(ρN) = V/Nwhere M represents the molar mass, ρ is the density, and N refers to the Avogadro constant. The corresponding molar volume (V) is expressed as V = M/ρ. The calculated values of the Vm and V for the ILs [Taz(2,n)][Acgly] (n = 4, 5, 6) at 298.15 K, consistent with our previous work,25 are summarized in Table 3. The contribution per –CH2– added to the alkyl side chain of the triazolium ring cation was found to range between 0.0316 and 0.0262 nm3, with an average value of 0.0289 nm3. This result is in good agreement with the literature value of 0.0277 nm3, reported for 79 imidazolium-based ILs.34

According to the interstitial model,35 the αp can also be calculated using γ and V. The total volume of ILs comprises the intrinsic volume (Vi) and interstitial volume (ν). The average ν was calculated using a classical thermodynamic equation:6ν = 0.6791(kbT/γ)3/2where kb = 1.38 J K−1 is Boltzmann constant.

The V of IL is composed of the Vi and the total of ν:7V = Vi + 2Nν

According to the interstitial model, temperature only influences the interstitial voids within ILs, whereas the change in the intrinsic volume Vi is negligible. Thus, the equation for calculating ν can be derived as follows:8αp cal. = (1/V)(∂V/∂T)T = 3Nν/VT

The calculated thermal expansion coefficients (αp cal.) of the ILs are listed in Table 3, which agree well with the corresponding experimental values in order of magnitude. This further verifies the reliability and rationality of the interstitial model for determining αp of ILs.

Based on Glasser's theory, the standard entropy (S0) and lattice energy (UPOT) of [Taz(2,n)][Acgly] (n = 4, 5, 6) at 298.15 K were estimated using eqn (9) and (10):9S0 = 1246.5 Vm + 29.510UPOT = 1981.2(ρ/M)1/3 + 103.8

The results are presented in Table 3. The S0 increases linearly with the number of carbon atoms in the cationic side chain, indicating a decrease in molecular ordering with longer alkyl chains. Specifically, each additional –CH2– group on the triazolium cation contributes 36 J K−1 mol−1 to the S0, which aligns closely with the reported average contribution of 34 J K−1 mol−1 for 79 imidazolium-based ILs.34 Conversely, the UPOT decreases with the extension of the cation side chain. Each –CH2– group on the triazolium cation contributes between 6 and 8 kJ mol−1, which aligns closely with the reported value of 8 kJ mol−1 for 79 imidazolium ILs.34 The UPOT data further reflect the interaction strength between triazolium cation and the Acgly− anion. The Acgly− anion with multiple polar groups can form strong electrostatic interaction and hydrogen bonds with cations, which is the core reason for the moderate lattice energy of this series of ILs.

The computed Ea values at different temperatures are tabulated in Table 4. Both literature data25 and the present calculation results demonstrate that Ea of ILs declines with the elongation of cationic alkyl chains, in good agreement with the findings reported by Yan30 and Wei.36 This trend can be ascribed to increased steric hindrance and weakened electronic effects within the IL structure. Moreover, Ea remains almost unchanged over 288.15–323.15 K, suggesting negligible temperature sensitivity. The temperature independence of Ea arises from the compensatory effect of Sa on energy fluctuations and the dynamic equilibrium between intermolecular forces and interfacial configurations.37,38

Notably, Ea of ILs is considerably lower than that of molten inorganic salts but close to organic liquids. This implies weaker interionic interactions in ILs relative to molten salts, as surface energy is governed by the strength of such interactions. These weaker interactions also account for the lower standard entropy of ILs compared with molten salts.30

Polarity

In order to more accurately describe the polarity of ILs, Tong et al. proposed a new scale of the polarity (δµ) on the basis of three assumptions.39 Based on Hildebrand's theory,40 the solubility parameter of the polar part δµ is calculated:14δµ2 = ΔglH0m,µ/V − (1 − x)RT/Vwhere x = ΔglH0m,n/ΔglH0m, ΔglH0m = ΔglH0m,n + ΔglH0m,µand the δµ is a new scale of the polarity. The vaporization enthalpy ΔglH0mconsists of non-polar part ΔglH0m,n(the contribution part from the induced dipole moment to ΔglH0m) and polar part ΔglH0m,µ (the contribution part from the average permanent dipole moment to ΔglH0m). The values of ΔglH0mand ΔglH0m,ncan be estimated by using Kabo empirical equation41 and the Lawson–Ingham equation,42 respectively.15ΔglH0m = 11.21 (γV2/3N1/3) + 2.416ΔglH0m,n = C[(nD2 − 1)/(nD2 + 2)]Vwhere C = 1.297 kJ cm−3 is an empirical constant for organic liquids, nD is the refractive index and V is molar volume.

Accordingly, Zhang et al.43,44 incorporated both dipole and non-polar contributions, modified δµ, and derived the equation for the polarity coefficient (P):17P = δµ/δn = [(ΔglH0m,µ/V − (1 − x)RT/V)/(ΔglH0m,n/V − xRT/V)]1/2In eqn (17), (ΔglH0m,µ/V) ≫ [(1 – x) RT/V] and (ΔglH0m,n/V) ≫ (xRT/V). P is called the polarity coefficient of ionic liquid, and the larger the value of polarity coefficient, the stronger the polarity of ILs (see Table 6).

Table 6 shows that P and δµ exhibit identical trends, demonstrating that the introduction of methylene groups into the cationic structure diminishes the hydrophilicity of the ILs. Additionally, a comparison of [C4mim][BF4] (P = 1.210) and [C4mim][NTf2] (P = 0.647) reveals that [C4mim][BF4] is significantly more polar than [C4mim][NTf2], which aligns with empirical findings.34 This confirms the broader applicability of P as a predictive indicator for IL polarity. The hydroxyl, amino and carbonyl groups in acetyl glycinate can form abundant hydrogen bonds and dipole interactions, which are the core sources of the overall polarity of the system. Combined with the classic ET(30) polarity scale for similar ILs, the polarity variation rule obtained in this work is highly consistent with the traditional research conclusions.45

Structural modifications of the cation, such as methylene incorporation, can significantly regulate the intensity of internal ion association through steric hindrance and charge delocalization effects. This principle offers a theoretical foundation for the design of functionalized ILs with low melting points and high fluidity, especially for applications requiring a balance between ionic conductivity and thermal stability.

Conclusions

Two novel 1,2,4-triazolium acetylglycinate ionic liquids [Taz(2,n)][Acgly] (n = 5, 6) were successfully synthesized with high purity and excellent thermal stability. The density, surface tension and refractive index of the ionic liquids decrease monotonically with increasing temperature and the elongation of cationic alkyl chains. The methylene group in the cationic side chain shows stable and quantifiable contributions to molecular volume, standard entropy and lattice energy, which are consistent with those of conventional imidazolium ionic liquids. The surface tension prediction model constructed in this work exhibits excellent agreement with experimental data (r2 > 0.99). The polarity of the ionic liquids decreases gradually with the lengthening of alkyl chains, and the gas-phase ion-pair association enthalpy confirms that cationic alkyl chain modification can effectively regulate interionic interactions. Moreover, the abundant hydrogen-bonding sites on the Acgly− anion enable the formation of stable ion pairs with triazolium cations. These findings clarify the structure–property relationships of the target ionic liquids and provide essential theoretical guidance for the development and application of 1,2,4-triazolium-based functional ionic liquids.

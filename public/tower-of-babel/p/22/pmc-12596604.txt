Letter to the editor regarding reflections on slipknot-assisted microvascular anastomosis
Authors: Maria Fueth, Marcus Lehnhardt, Christoph Wallner
Published: 2025

Dear Sir,

We read with great interest the article “The utility of the slipknot in microvascular anastomosis“ by Franchi et al.1 describing the use of the slipknot technique to approximate vascular stumps during microvascular anastomosis. The authors provide a valuable and elegant demonstration of how this maneuver may assist in situations without an approximator or when the vessel ends are difficult to align.

We would like to share two additional considerations based on our own microsurgical practice:

First, while the slipknot maneuver is effective in reducing the gap between vascular stumps, care must be taken to avoid excessive traction. If the vessel is approximated under residual tension, the suture material itself can transmit shear forces to the vessel wall. This carries the risk of intimal damage, which may in turn compromise the patency of the anastomosis.2 For this reason, we emphasize that the technique should always be applied with minimal tension and with careful handling of the vessel ends.

Second, we have found the slipknot particularly useful in the open-book technique of vascular anastomosis, in which the vessel is opened widely to optimize visualization of the lumen.3, 4 In this setting, the slipknot facilitates precise approximation of the vessel edges without obstructing the operative field.3, 4 This allows for a more controlled placement of the initial sutures and helps maintain a clear and stable view of the intima during the critical early steps of the anastomosis.

We commend the authors for highlighting this practical and reproducible technique, which we believe can further improve microsurgical precision when applied with careful attention to the biomechanical limitations of vascular tissue.

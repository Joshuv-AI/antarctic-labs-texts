A Study on the Efficacy and Safety of Intravenous Lidocaine Infusion Combined with Pulsed Radiofrequency in Preventing Postherpetic Neuralgia: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Controlled Trial [Letter]
Authors: Xiaoying Qi
Published: 2026

Dear editor

We read with great interest the recent editorial by Xu et al titled “A Study on the Efficacy and Safety of Intravenous Lidocaine Infusion Combined with Pulsed Radiofrequency in Preventing Postherpetic Neuralgia: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Controlled Trial” published in your esteemed journal.1 The author states through VAS score, PSQI score, IL-6 and Gal-3 levels, pregabalin dosage, adverse reactions the data analysis on the incidence of PHN confirmed that lidocaine intravenous infusion combined with pulsed radiofrequency is an effective method for preventing postherpetic neuralgia. Compared with simple pulsed radiofrequency therapy, it can significantly reduce the incidence of PHN, provide more lasting pain relief for acute herpes zoster patients, reduce the dosage of analgesic drugs, improve patients’ sleep disorders, and have good safety. However, several reference and outcome measure consistency issues merit further discussion to enhance the reliability and clinical applicability of the study results.

First, the dose of pregabalin in the two groups was significantly reduced (P < 0.05), P-value of same group is not listed in Table 3. Compared with group C, P group of patients in March, June, and 12 months after treatment of the pregabalin dose were significantly lower (P < 0.05),while the number of users of pregabalin 12 months after treatment is only one less than that of 6 months after treatment with pregabalin dose. In addition, the data in Table 3 only shows that the differences in the number of people using different doses of pregabalin. How is the difference in dosage of pregabalin calculated? The issue may mislead readers with the result, particularly clinical practitioners who choose pregabalin dose after treatment. Similarly, after treatment, the plasma IL-6 and Gal-3 indexes of the two groups were significantly decreased (P < 0.05). P-value of same group is not listed in Table 4.

Second, the vertical axis identification in Figure 1 is incorrect. In the abstract of results, it is stated that “the incidence of PHN in group P decreased by 18.2%.”, but in the section on occurrences of PHN, it is explicitly described that “an effective rate”, the data of effective rate is shown in Figure 1. The vertical axis should be changed to the effective rate of PHN.

Third, the description of adverse reactions is contradictory. In the abstract of results, it is stated that “No adverse reactions occurred in the two groups after treatment”, but in the section on adverse reactions, it is explicitly described that “one female patient in group P experienced hypotension after receiving lidocaine infusion, and recovered after adjusting the infusion rate”. These two statements are completely contradictory and directly affect readers’ judgment of safety and the credibility of the data.

Fourth, the conclusion of the 21st reference does not match the description in this study. Xu et al describe “Currently, 9 subtypes (Nav1.1-Nav1.9) have been identified, among which Nav1.3, Nav1.7, Nav1.8, and Nav1.9 have been confirmed to be related to neuropathic pain” in the discussion, while Wang et al study that Nav1.3 is unlikely to be a key player in the induction of abnormal spontaneous activity in injured neurons, it remains questionable whether DRG Nav1.7 has a role in the development of neuropathic pain. The role of DRG Nav1.8 in neuropathic pain development is still uncertain and needs to be investigated further and current preclinical evidence does not support a role for DRG Nav1.9 in the development of neuropathic pain.2 The Wang et al study reports that Nav1.3, Nav1.7, Nav1.8, and Nav1.9 may be related to neuropathic pain. The content description of the article does not match the reference.

In conclusion, the work by Xu et al makes a meaningful contribution to the field of preventing postherpetic neuralgia. We appreciate the authors’ efforts in postherpetic neuralgia. Addressing the above points in future studies would strengthen the evidence base for lidocaine and better inform clinical decision-making. We look forward to seeing further research that addresses these points.

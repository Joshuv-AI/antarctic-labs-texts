Electronic medical record (EMR) pre-deployment assessment for adoption readiness, acceptability, and associated factors at Hiwot Fana comprehensive specialized university hospital
Authors: Admas Abera, Tilahun Shiferaw, Fitsum Yeneneh, Zewdu Alemu, Abdi Amin, Ahmed Mohammed, Adisu Birhanu Weldesenbet, Fila Ahmed, Dereje Weldesilassie, Abebe Tolera
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundLow-income countries struggled to launch large-scale EMR systems and the adoption and use of EMR is still in its embryonic stage. Hiwot Fana comprehensive specialized university hospital in collaboration with the Ethiopian ministry of health, Digital Health Activity (DHA), USAID grant under Capacity Building and Mentorship Project (CBMP) at Haramaya University designed and planned to deploy EMR. This study therefore aimed to assess electronic medical record pre-deployment adoption readiness, acceptability and associated factors.MethodsA cross-sectional study was conducted at Hiwot Fana Comprehensive Specialized University Hospital from May 14-May 30, 2023 among 407 healthcare providers and 13 hospital management. Standardized tools to assess technology adoption was used. Data was collected using Kobo Toolbox and transferred to Stata version 16.0 for analysis. Simple descriptive analysis and composite scores were computed for readiness and acceptability. Variables having a p-value of < 0.2 in the bivariable analysis were included in the multivariable logistic regression analysis to identify factors associated with EMR adoption readiness. All statistical values with p-value less than 0.05 were declared to be significant.ResultsA total of 407 health workers and 13 case team leaders were involved in this study. Overall, 52.58% healthcare providers were ready to adopt EMR system of which 50.37% had core readiness (dissatisfaction with paper records and need for electronic medical records) whereas 57.25% had engagement readiness (potential benefits and willingness to accept EMR and concern about negative impact of EMR). In multivariable logistic regression male sex, good computer literacy level, good knowledge and favourable attitude towards EMR were positively associated with readiness to adopt EM whereas direct engagement with patient care was negatively associated with EMR adoption readiness.ConclusionThis study established low or unsatisfactory readiness and acceptability of EMR deployment both by the healthcare providers and organizational level. Strong leadership support, targeted capacity building to address staff with low digital literacy levels prior to implementation of an electronic medical record system is very critical. Moreover, assessment of needs, providing and managing financial resources, and setting standards should be highest priority.Supplementary informationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12911-025-03169-3.

Introduction

Digital health or e-health in health care is emerging and nowadays considered a core asset for effective management and improved quality of care [1, 2]. It offer a vast amount of functionality such as improved efficiency, improved reach, improved customer experience, improved decision-making, and improved profitability [2–6]. Maximising the opportunities arising from digital technology use is very critical in contemporary health care industry.

One of the digital health application is Electronic Health Record (EHR) which represents and integrate an individual’s multiple, physician-generated, electronic medical records and the patient-generated personal health record to easily share medical information among stakeholders. Electronic Medical Record (EMR) on the other hand is composed of the clinical data repository, clinical decision support, controlled medical vocabulary, order entry, computerized provider order entry, pharmacy, and clinical documentation applications. Personal health record (PHR) is a medical record maintained by the patient. Despite this difference, all are crucial to the success of local, regional, and national goals to improve patient safety, improve the quality and efficiency of patient care, and reduce healthcare delivery costs. EHRs are reliant on EMRs and PHR being in place, and EMR will never reach its full potential without interoperable EHRs in place [7, 8].

Electronic medical record systems is the “legal record that enables health facilities to capture, store, analyse and communicate patient health information in an electronic format” [9, 10]. The potential of EMR systems to transform medical care practice has been recognised over the past decades [11–13]. On the other hand, poor quality of the information in patient medical records may be a cause of poor quality of care and associated with excessive health care costs, high rate of human error, productivity loss, disparities in clinician communication, and inadequate confidentiality of medical records [14, 15].

Globally, more than 50% of EMR projects fail before they reach their target [16]. Even though EMRs are an essential tool for health care, their adoption and utilization remains low in developing countries [17–19]. Despite large investments, low-income countries struggled to launch large-scale EMR systems and the adoption and use of EMR is still in its embryonic stage due to poor users’ attitudes, lack of skilled labour, poor knowledge, lack of technical skills, lack of adequate resources and lack the necessary infrastructure [6, 20–22]. A successful deployment of digital health to ensure attainment of the ambitious universal health coverage in Africa faces several challenges on the continent [6]. Sab-Saharan African countries are reported to have poor information technology infrastructure [17, 23, 24].

Despite the Ethiopian government efforts in training health informatics professionals to support the health management information system [20], the utilization and digital health investments for the newly produced information in planning and decision making has not been a priority and generally inadequately supported [25]. Most of the electronic medical record systems in Ethiopia are still used side by side to the paper documentation which is creating double burden on health workers [26]. Moreover, most digital health applications have centred on administrative and financial transactions rather than on delivering clinical care [17].

Incompleteness of medical records is a significant problem that affects the quality of health care services in many hospitals of Ethiopia [23, 27]. Studies in Ethiopia showed the overall readiness of health professionals for EMR implementation was found to be low [28, 29]. Inadequate user satisfaction, gap in knowledge and skill level of health workers [14, 25], shortage of electronic medical record format are major concern that health professionals and organizations share [25, 30].

Ethiopia has no nationally harmonized EMR or HER system. It has been dependent on funders’ based EMR system such as Tulane University SmartCare. But most of this EMR system has failed as funders phased out their project. These projects were a temporary and they were not suitable for national healthcare system. For this reason, widely adopted standards and principles of healthcare information governance and rigorous evaluation of this technology are essential in efforts to improve interoperability and electronic healthcare information sharing environment. Ethiopian ministry of health in collaboration with Digital health Activity, USAID grant under Capacity building and mentorship project at Haramaya University has planned to deploy electronic medical records at Hiwot Fana comprehensive specialized University Hospital. This is EMR designed at national level and this pre-deployment study was planned before actual deployment. There will be consecutive post deployment (implementation study) assessment. It is planned for scale up of this EMR system (probability at national level) at different facilities.

For this reason, electronic medical records pre-deployment readiness, acceptance, and potential challenges assessment has been planned for effective implementation and to avert increasing failures while maintaining system benefits which are vital for effective implementation and sustainability. This study therefore aimed to explore the EMR pre-deployment healthcare providers and organizational adoption readiness, acceptability, and potential challenges and facilitators at Hiwot Fana comprehensive specialized university hospital.

Methods

Study area and design

A cross-sectional study was conducted at Hiwot Fana comprehensive Specialized University Hospital in Harari regional state from May 14-May 30, 2023. Hiwot Fana Comprehensive Specialized University Hospital is one of the two public hospitals in Harari regional state managed under Haramaya University, College of Health and Medical science [31]. It serve as a teaching hospital for Haramaya University’s College of Health and Medical Sciences that serves as a referral hospital for the entire eastern part of Ethiopia, including the eastern Oromia region, Dire Dawa city, the Somali region, and the Harari regional state. It provides comprehensive health services to more than five million people including oncology, maternal and child health care, outpatient services, inpatient services, antiretroviral treatment (ART) services, and others. Hiwot Fana comprehensive Specializes University Hospital is one of the hospitals in eastern Ethiopia selected for EMR implementation.

Study population

The study population were all healthcare providers and management staff at Hiwot Fana comprehensive Specializes University Hospital. The study units were sampled from 1184 healthcare providers and performance monitoring team members.

Sample size determination and sampling procedure

The sample size was determined using a single population proportion formula using, 95% confidence level, 5% margin of error, and a 54.1% proportion of health professionals ready to adopt EMR from study conducted in Northwest Ethiopia [32].

Adding 10% of non-response rate [33] the final sample size for this study was 420. A simple random sampling was used to select the required study units by using the staff ID as sampling frame.

Data collection and quality control

Data was collected by six trained data collectors (HIT professionals, Nurses, and health officers) both from healthcare providers and service delivery registers, and other patient documentation systems after adequate orientation is given to data collectors. Data was collected using standardized semi-structured questionnaires designed in English language. The tools are adopted from California Academy of Family Physicians [34], previous literatures [28, 35–38] and from the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT2) [33] model which examines the acceptance of technology, determined by the effects of performance expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence, and facilitating conditions. Before actual data collection, Pre-data collection training (on questionnaire content, data collection approach, quality required, source of data, time taken and admin issues). Onsite pre-test was conducted among 5% staff at Jugal general hospital to check tool adequacy, consistency, validity, and the time it takes to complete a questionnaire. To ensure the quality of data, the field guidelines were prepared for data collectors and supervisors and electronic data collection was used.

Variables and measurements

Our primary outcomes were EMR Adoption readiness and EMR acceptability.

Healthcare providers EMR Adoption Readiness: was measured by a set of 15 questions with a five Likert scale. Adoption was organized in two categories with core readiness measured by 6 questions (categorized under dissatisfaction with paper records and need for electronic medical records) and engagement readiness measured by (potential benefits and willingness to accept EMR and concern about negative impact of EMR). Participants who obtained more than the mean score were categorized as ready, those who scored mean were categorized somewhat ready, and those who scored below mean were categorized as not ready.

Healthcare providers EMR Acceptability: the proportion of EMR acceptability was measured by a set of 13 questions with a five Likert scale, and participants who obtained more than the mean score were categorized as accept those who scored mean were categorized somewhat accept, and those who scored below mean were categorized as do not accept.

Organizational EMR Adoption Readiness: was measured by 9 domain with a set of 39 questions with a five Likert scale; and an above mean score were categorized as ready, mean score were categorized somewhat ready, and below mean score were categorized as not ready. The assessment focused on: Management Capacity (Management and leadership, Organizational culture), Finance and Budget Capacity, Operational Capacity, Technology Capacity/Technical readiness, and Organizational Alignment.

Attitude on EMR: was measured by a set of 15 questions with a five Likert scale, and participants who obtained more than the mean score were categorized as good attitude and those who scored less than the mean were categorized as poor attitude.

Knowledge on EMR: was measured by a set of 12 questions with a yes or no option, and participants who obtained more than the mean score were categorized as have good knowledge or below mean score as do not have good knowledge.

EMR users digital and computer literacy: was measured by a set of 12 questions with a five Likert scale, and participants who obtained more than the mean score were categorized as 1 = Basic computer skills (need help with internet and email or office applications), mean score as 2 = moderate or intermediate skill and below mean score as 3 = Proficient (able to do advanced tasks such as database management or programming) good and those who scored less than the mean were categorized as poor.

Data analysis

Data from Kobo Toolbox were downloaded in excel format and imported to Stata version 16. Then these data were cleaned and checked for completeness or inconsistencies. Missing data and outliers were fixed. The continuous variables were described in terms of mean, median, and categorical variables were described in terms of percentages, and frequencies at baseline. Binary logistic regression analysis was used to identify factors associated with EMR adoption readiness. Variables having a p-value of < 0.2 in the bi-variable analysis were included in the multivariable logistic regression analysis. All statistical values with p-value less than 0.05 were declared to be significant.

Ethical considerations

The study protocol was approved by Haramaya University, the College of Health and Medical Sciences, Institutional Health Research Ethics Review Committee (IHRERC: IHRERC/082/2023). Official letters of cooperation was obtained from Haramaya University, College of Health and Medical Sciences, and provided to heads of Hiwot Fana Specialized University Hospital. Information on the study was explained to the heads of the Hospitals and study participants including the procedures, potential risks, and benefits of the study. Informed, voluntary, written, and signed consent was obtained by the data collectors from the heads of the hospital and study participants prior to the study.

Structural and human resource readiness

Hospital human resource structure

Hiwot Fana compressive Specialized University hospital was giving service to an average of 800 patients per day in 9 wards, 22 departments and 112 service delivery points. There were 13 inpatient and 29 outpatient service units. Moreover, there were 7 drug dispensing units, 4 laboratory units, 4 medical record unit windows, and 3 triage units. There were a total of 100 specialists and sub-specialists, 210 residents, 68 general practitioners (GP), 10 health officers, 308 BSc nurses, 30 clinical nurses, 44 midwifes, 60 laboratory technologists, 49 pharmacists, and 28 radiologists, 96 MRU workers, and 12 HMIS staff where 9 are health informatics professionals. In addition, there were a total of 438 admin and finance officers including 6 cashiers.

Hospital infrastructure resource

IT structure

In it’s both an old and newly built compressive buildings, Hiwot Fana hospital has its own IT structural unit with one system and network administrator, 5 computer support and maintenance professional. There was recently installed function fiber cable internet service in all departments with 10 G LAN connectivity. The hospital had data center with one server, 3 manageable data and 6 POE switches. Server architecture is 64 byte. There were one functional core/distribution switch, l 9 USP for witch and 20 Access points. There was one air conditioner for the data center. But the hospital had no controller based WAN and Functional Router/Fire wall.

The data center have 1 KA UPS as power backup. There was a total of 1240 KA power back up with 500 KA for the data center. There were 3 core and the CPU speed was 3. The RAM capacity was 16 with one storage capacity.

Hardware and software infrastructure

There were a total of 200 functional computers and 50 functional printers in the hospital. Each computer had standalone UPS in case when power goes off. This hospital has a centrally managed antivirus system with backup system.

Discussion

This section discuss on main findings on EMR pre-deployment adoption and acceptability readiness as well as factors associated with EMR adoption and use readiness for both healthcare workers and the organization.

This study finding demonstrated lower EMR adoption readiness (lower core readiness and engagement readiness) by healthcare providers which is consistent with study in northern Ghana [39], south-western Ethiopia [36] Myanmar [40], northern Ghana [41] and at three hospitals in Ethiopia [29]. It is however very lower compared to global adoption readiness in major countries such as China, Brazil, France, Russia [42] and Lebanon [41] and four primary hospitals in Ethiopia [28]. The overall organizational (hospital) readiness for EMR deployment was also very low This difference may be explained by the fact despite developing countries are also progressing in electronic records, the majority of the focus related to the modernization of medical records is placed on developed countries [43]. Lack of availability of EMR systems, poor health information system and insufficient works on user acceptance of digital health systems are reported in developing countries [17, 44]. This can also be explained by large unfordable attitude towards EMR by healthcare providers in our study setting. This low level of readiness for adoption as an implication on effective deployment and improvement of service quality which in turn needs an integrated capacity building. It will also have a great implication for post-deployment sustainability of the planned deployment. The implementation of e-Health cannot be achieved without the readiness of healthcare providers and the institution itself. This necessitates a need to work on capacitating and creating adequate awareness to service providers before actual deployment.

This study indicated more than three quarter of healthcare providers had good knowledge whereas more than half had favourable attitude towards EMR system. It is relatively comparable with study from Northern Ethiopia [45], Malawi [46] Plateau State Nigeria [47], and Study by Kalayou, Endehabtu et al. [48]. This finding is slightly higher than study at three hospitals in Ethiopia [29] and teaching hospitals of Amhara regional state [49]. Studies indicated user satisfaction with EMR [20] and perceived ease of use [36, 50], perceived benefit [36, 51] has been found to have a significant direct influence on the acceptance of EMR. Despite majority having good knowledge and favourable attitude, this study however recommends facilities need to work more on staff knowledge and attitude towards digital applications such as EMR.

This study also revealed that less than half of healthcare providers had adequate digital literacy level. This is consistent with study from Malawi [52] Addis Ababa [53], and North-west Amhara regional state [54, 55]. However, it is higher than study conducted at University of Gondar Comprehensive Specialized Hospital, northwest Ethiopia [56]. This difference could be due to the level and number of health facility involved in this study. Study in Mali indicated despite using smart phones and focused training, many healthcare workers were not comfortable using EMRs because their typing speed was delaying the process [52]. Improved digital literacy is very important as it affect attitude to use any digital system including EMR.

Despite reported good knowledge and favourable attitude, the proportion of willingness to accept EMR system by our study participants is still very low. This finding is very lower than studies from Myanmar [57], Bahir Dar City in Northwest Ethiopia [58] St. Paul’s and Ayder Hospitals [18] and in higher income countries [59, 60]. This difference may be accounted for by previous experience of the study participants with SmartCare implemented by Tulane University which was interrupted after some time. Lack of local designed EMR software and patients load may also account for this difference. But the proportion of readiness to accept EMR system is however higher than study at three hospitals in Ethiopia [29] and north-West Ethiopia [19]. Factors such as performance expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence, facilitating condition, and computer literacy were highly associated with intention to use [19].

Males were three times more likely to adopt and use EMR than females. This is consistent with study in northern Ethiopia [29] that showed male gender as determinants of higher readiness and utilization of the system. This can be account for higher digital literacy of male as indicated by study at University of Gondar Comprehensive Specialized Hospital [49] and North-west Amhara regional state [54] where male participants were more likely to possess higher digital competency.

Direct engagement with patient care was found to decrease readiness to adopt and use EMR system. Studies indicated performance expectancy and effort expectancy were major challenges from healthcare providers side [61, 62] to EMR adoption and use. A systematic review by Bireswar Dutta and Hsin-Ginn Hwang showed workflow changes, system complexity, lack of reliability, and interoperability were negatively associated with EMR adoption [63]. Moreover, study from china indicate bed size, outpatient admission, and duration of EMR use were significant factors for EMR adoption [64].

Healthcare workers with adequate digital literacy were two times more ready to adopt and use EMR system. This finding is consistent with other studies which identified limited access to computers and insufficient computer literacy [58, 61, 62] as major challenges to EMR adoption and use. Similarly, study from Malawi [52] Northern Ghana [41] and Bahir Dar City in Northwest Ethiopia [58] indicated health workers who lack computer skills tend to shy aware from using EMRs. Having a personal computer, computer training, computer skill, management support, working experience, adequate data analysis skills and Internet access were identified as predictors for attitude toward digital system acceptance and utilization [22, 45, 49].

This study indicated healthcare providers with good EMR knowledge were almost two times more likely to accept EMR deployment and three times more likely to adopt EMR. Study from Myanmar [40] Northern Ghana [41] and Bahir Dar City in Northwest Ethiopia [58] indicated knowledge on EMRs were the factors associated with overall readiness for EMRs adoption. Other studies have also identified deficiency of EMR knowledge as major challenge to EMR adoption and utilization. [61, 62]. Technical help, presence of EMR manual, job satisfaction and owning computer may increase or decrease digital knowledge which in turn affect digital system adoption like EMR [46, 58].

Those healthcare providers with favourable attitude toward EMR system were four and half times more likely to accept EMR deployment and six times more likely to adopt EMR than their counterparts. Several studies indicated the major challenge for EMR adoption and use was user resistance and lack of skills on EMR [61, 62, 65]. Study in North-western Ethiopia [55] and south-western Ethiopia [36] indicated having a positive attitude such as good perceived benefits and ease of use towards digital health technology was found to significantly affect EMR adoption. Favourable attitude on digital system including EMR can improve health professionals’ digital literacy level which in turn affect the EMR adoption and utilization.

This study could have adequately assessed the readiness for EMRs adoption and willingness to accept EMR both from organizational and healthcare providers’ side. But it is not without limitation. This study is conducted in single facility though it serves more than 5 million people. The result may not represent private facilities. Moreover, it requires further exploratory analysis to identify major barriers and facilitators (we hope we will cover this with another study soon).

Conclusions

The overall readiness and acceptability of EMR by healthcare providers in this study are very low compared to several studies. Male sex, good computer literacy level, good knowledge, and favourable attitude towards EMR were positively associated with readiness to adopt EM whereas direct engagement with patient care was negatively associated with EMR adoption readiness. Health professionals are important stakeholders in adoption and implementation of eHealth interventions in routine care. Strong leadership support, targeted capacity building to address staff with low digital literacy levels prior to implementation of an electronic medical record system is very critical. As EMR is one of the priority area of Ethiopian ministry of health, policymakers or healthcare manager need to assess needs, providing and managing financial resources, and set standards before actual deployment of such digital systems. Further work is needed to overcome misconceptions about inferior efficacy of EMR or quality of service by inclusion of health professionals in the development of future eHealth interventions.

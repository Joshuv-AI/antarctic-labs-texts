IFPHOR0064 Waiting Well as Prevention: A Multi-Pathway Public Health Evaluation of Interventions Across Elective and Mental Health Waiting Lists
Authors: Rochelle Velho, Katie Hibbitt, Ben Hollands, Jonathan Webster, Uma Walgama, Zafar Iqbal, Farah Kidy
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundWaiting lists in England continue to rise, contributing to clinical deterioration, psychological distress and widening inequalities. Waiting periods are recognised as actionable intervals where targeted Wait List Interventions (WLIs) can mitigate harm rather than act as passive delays. This study synthesises evidence for WLIs across obesity, and adult & paediatric mental health pathways.MethodsThree structured reviews were undertaken using systematic‑review methods across multiple databases, with screening, risk‑of‑bias assessment, and narrative synthesis. Paediatric mental health studies underwent additional analysis using modified harvest plots to visualise patterns of effect, study quality, and alignment with social determinants of health.ResultsAcross obesity pathways, physical‑activity interventions demonstrated consistent improvements in weight, metabolic measures, functional capacity, and preoperative fitness. In adult mental health, digital and guided cognitive behavioural therapy improved symptoms, functioning, engagement, and satisfaction. For paediatric mental health, psychoeducation delivered alongside parental support showed the strongest and most consistent benefits across symptoms, functioning, behaviour change, and family wellbeing. Digital‑only paediatric interventions showed mixed effects due to heterogeneity and small samples. Harvest‑plot mapping demonstrated that 55% of paediatric WLIs targeted individual behaviour change, with minimal focus on social or community determinants and none addressing structural drivers such as poverty, housing, or education.ConclusionsWLIs can reduce deterioration and enhance engagement across multiple pathways. However, current provision remains heavily individual‑level, limiting impact on inequalities. Embedding equity‑focused, scalable WLIs into commissioning and recovery frameworks presents a significant opportunity to convert waiting periods into meaningful preventive public health action and guide future research investment.

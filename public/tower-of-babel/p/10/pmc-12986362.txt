Crystal Growth and Morphology Control of SCM-10 Zeolites
Authors: Xinze Yang, Yikun Kang, Lou Gao, Yi Luo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SCM-10 is an SFE-type borosilicate zeolite featuring one-dimensional 12-ring channels and holds promise for catalytic and separation applications, yet its crystallization and morphology control remain insufficiently understood. Herein, we investigate the crystallization of SCM-10 and elucidate the effects of synthesis parameters, including SiO2/B2O3, OSDA/SiO2, and H2O/SiO2 molar ratios, crystallization temperature, and heteroatom substitution, on crystal morphology. SCM-10 crystallizes via an induction period (30 h) followed by rapid crystal growth (12 h), yielding uniform needle-like crystals with dimensions of 0.05 × 1 μm (diameter × length; aspect ratio = 20). Increasing the SiO2/B2O3 ratio induces a morphology transition from needles to plates, whereas variations in the OSDA/SiO2 and H2O/SiO2 ratios largely preserve the needle-like morphology. Elevating the crystallization temperature transforms needles into nanowires, with the aspect ratio rising to 83 (0.03 × 2.5 μm). Heteroatom substitution further induces distinct morphology modulation: Al yields nanoparticles (100 nm), V shortens crystal lengths to 500 nm (50 × 500 nm; aspect ratio = 10), Fe produces nanowires (0.02 × 1.3 μm; aspect ratio = 65), and Ge forms peanut-like needle aggregates (0.05 × 10 μm; aspect ratio = 200). This work establishes morphology-regulation rules for SCM-10, enabling the rational design of tailored morphologies for applications.

1. Introduction

Zeolites are a class of important crystalline microporous materials that have been widely applied in catalysis, adsorption, separation, and ion-exchange processes owing to their unique pore architecture, high surface area, and excellent hydrothermal stability [1,2,3,4]. In addition to framework topology and chemical composition, the performance of zeolites in practical applications is strongly influenced by their morphology [5,6]. Zeolite morphology can govern mass-transfer efficiency, active-site accessibility, and structural stability under reaction conditions. For example, nanosized zeolites can significantly reduce diffusion path lengths and alleviate mass-transfer limitations in catalytic reactions [7,8,9]. In contrast, anisotropic morphologies, such as rod-like crystals with extended diffusion lengths, may exhibit enhanced shape selectivity in specific reactions. Consequently, elucidating zeolite crystal growth and achieving precise control of morphology is of both fundamental scientific interest and industrial relevance.

In zeolite synthesis, a variety of parameters, including synthesis gel composition, crystallization temperature and duration, and heteroatom substitution, are known to exert influence on crystal morphology [10,11]. Despite advances in zeolite synthesis, the intrinsic mechanisms governing zeolite crystallization and morphology evolution remain partially understood [12,13,14]. Moreover, morphology regulation is highly system-dependent: different zeolite frameworks, heteroatom species, and organic structure-directing agents (OSDAs) often exhibit distinct and non-universal growth behaviors, underscoring the need for framework- and/or OSDA-specific investigations.

The SFE-type borosilicate zeolite SSZ-48 features a one-dimensional 12-ring sinusoidal channel system with elliptic pore openings (0.54 × 0.76 nm) and was first synthesized by Zones et al. in 1999 [15,16,17]. However, its practical application has been hindered by the requirement for a costly OSDA (N,N-diethyldecahydroquinolinium), and an exceptionally long crystallization time of up to 42 days. To overcome these limitations, Yang et al. recently developed a rapid and cost-effective synthesis route for an SFE-type zeolite, SCM-10, using commercially available 4-dimethylaminopyridine (DMAP) as the OSDA [18]. While this advance significantly improves the synthetic accessibility of SFE-type zeolites, prior studies on SCM-10 have primarily focused on the feasibility of synthesis and basic structural characterization. Systematic investigations of its crystallization behavior and morphology regulation remain limited. In particular, the effects of synthesis parameters and heteroatom substitution on the morphology evolution of SCM-10 remain incompletely understood, hindering the rational design and controlled preparation of high-performance SCM-10-based materials.

Herein, we systematically investigate the crystallization process of SCM-10 and elucidate the regulatory effects of key synthesis parameters, including SiO2/B2O3, OSDA/SiO2, and H2O/SiO2 ratios, crystallization temperature, and heteroatom substitution (Al, V, Fe, and Ge), on crystal morphology, encompassing particle size, aspect ratio, and aggregation state. This work aims to establish a practical guideline for the controllable synthesis of SCM-10 with tailored morphologies, thereby facilitating its application in advanced catalysis, efficient separation, and other high-value industrial processes.

2. Materials and Methods

2.1. Chemicals

All chemical reagents employed for the synthesis of SCM-10 zeolites in this work were used as received without further purification. Silica sol (Ludox HS-40), supplied as a 40 wt% aqueous solution, was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich (Shanghai, China). Boric acid (99%), sodium metavanadate (99%) and iron(III) nitrate nonahydrate (98.5%) were all obtained from Sinopharm Chemical Reagents Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). 4-dimethylaminopyridine (DMAP, 99%) and germanium oxide (99.99%) were purchased from Innochem (Beijing, China). Sodium aluminate, with the chemical composition of 51% Al2O3 and 41% Na2O, was acquired from Macklin (Shanghai, China).

2.2. Preparation of SCM-10 Zeolites

SCM-10 samples were synthesized from gel systems with a molar composition SiO2: 1/x B2O3: y DMAP: z H2O, where x = 5~∞, y = 0.05~1.0, z = 5~60. Detailed synthesis conditions and corresponding results are summarized in Table 1. In a typical synthesis, DMAP was first dissolved in deionized water, followed by the addition of boric acid under stirring. Colloidal silica was then added dropwise, and the resulting gel was stirred for 1 h at room temperature to ensure homogeneity. The gel was transferred into a Teflon-lined stainless-steel autoclave and crystallized at 170 °C under rotation (20 rpm) for a certain duration. After crystallization, the solid product was recovered by centrifugation, thoroughly washed with deionized water until the supernatant reached neutral pH, and then dried. The solid yield was defined as the percentage ratio of the weight of the solid product after calcination to the weight of oxides from the corresponding sources.

Metal-substituted SCM-10 samples (M-B-SCM-10, M = Al, V, Fe, and Ge) were synthesized following the same procedure, except that the corresponding metal source was introduced before the addition of DMAP (Table 2). Sodium aluminate, sodium metavanadate, iron(III) nitrate nonahydrate, and germanium oxide were used as the Al, V, Fe, and Ge sources, respectively. The crystallization time was adjusted for each metal species to ensure complete crystallization.

2.3. Characterizations

Powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) patterns were recorded on a Bruker D8 Advance X-ray diffractometer (Bruker AXS GmbH, Karlsruhe, Germany) using Cu Kα radiation, operating at 40 kV and 40 mA. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images were acquired on a ZEISS Merlin microscope (Carl Zeiss AG, Oberkochen, Germany) with an acceleration voltage of 2.0 kV. Element compositions were determined by an Agilent 725ES inductively coupled plasma atomic emission spectrometer (ICP-AES) (Agilent Technologies Co. Ltd., Santa Clara, CA, USA) after dissolving the samples in hydrofluoric acid (HF) solution. Transmission electron micrographs (TEM) images and selected area electron diffraction (SAED) were acquired on a JEM-F200 (JEOL Ltd., Tokyo, Japan) operated at 200 kV. The relative crystallinity was determined by comparing the summed intensities of characteristic PXRD peaks of SCM-10 at 2θ = 6.50°, 7.98°, 9.36°, 20.02°, 22.65°, and 24.13°. The sample crystallized for 72 h, which exhibited the highest crystallinity, was used as the reference and assigned 100% crystallinity. Crystal aspect ratios were calculated as the ratio of length to diameter for needle-like crystals, with reported values representing the arithmetic mean obtained from measurements of at least five individual crystals.

3. Results and Discussion

3.1. Crystal Growth of SCM-10

The crystallization process of SCM-10 was investigated by characterizing the time-resolved intermediates obtained from a synthesis gel with a molar composition of 1 SiO2: 0.05 B2O3: 0.6 DMAP: 25 H2O (Figure 1A). The PXRD pattern of the intermediate collected after 12 h exhibits a broad diffraction peak in the 2θ range of 20°~25°, indicating an amorphous phase. As crystallization proceeded, a few weak diffraction peaks began to emerge at 24 h. Upon extending the crystallization time to 33 h, the product presents characteristic diffraction peaks of SCM-10 at 2θ = 6.50°, 7.98°, 9.36°, 20.02°, 22.65°, and 24.13°, with no detectable impurity phases. The intensities of these peaks increased as the crystallization time was further prolonged from 33 to 42 h, after which no significant additional enhancement was observed, indicating completion of crystallization.

The crystallization kinetics were evaluated by calculating the relative crystallinity of the intermediate products based on the integrated PXRD peak areas (Figure 1B) [19]. The relative crystallinity remained below 5% during the initial 30 h, increased sharply to 67% at 33 h, and reached 100% at 42 h. This evolution indicates a relatively long induction period of 30 h, followed by a rapid crystal growth stage lasting 12 h. Consistent with this crystallization process, the solid yield remained at around 77% during the first 30 h and increased markedly to 91% during the subsequent growth stage (Figure 1B). Notably, the addition of 5 wt% SCM-10 seeds significantly accelerated the crystallization process, shortening the required crystallization time from 42 to 24 h (Figure 2).

The morphology of the crystallization intermediates was further examined by SEM (Figure 3A). After 12 h of crystallization, the products predominantly consisted of small amorphous nanoparticles. Upon extending the crystallization time to 24 h, needle-like crystals emerged, and their population gradually increased with further extension to 30 h. Notably, during the subsequent 3 h interval (30–33 h), the number of needle-like crystals increased sharply, becoming the dominant phase, although a small fraction of amorphous particles remained. After 42 h, the amorphous phase was transformed entirely into uniform needle-like crystals with average dimensions of approximately 0.05 × 1 μm. Further extension of the crystallization time did not induce noticeable changes in either crystal morphology or particle size, indicating that crystal growth had reached completion. The high-resolution TEM image exhibits well-defined lattice fringes, and the corresponding selected-area electron diffraction (SAED) pattern displays sharp diffraction spots, collectively confirming the high crystallinity of the obtained SCM-10 (Figure 3B). The measured interplanar spacing of 1.15 nm is assigned to the (100) plane, indicating that the crystallographic b axis is oriented along the crystal length, while the a axis corresponds to the diameter direction, in good agreement with previously reported results [16,18].

3.2. Influence of Synthesis Parameters on the Morphology of SCM-10

Building upon the elucidation of the crystallization process of SCM-10, we further identify the key synthesis parameters governing morphology, including the SiO2/B2O3, OSDA/SiO2, and H2O/SiO2 ratios, crystallization temperature, and heteroatom substitution.

3.2.1. Effect of SiO2/B2O3 Ratio

The SiO2/B2O3 ratio was varied over a wide range (5~∞) to examine its influence on SCM-10 formation (Figure 4). At high boron content (SiO2/B2O3 = 5), thin needle-like crystals with a high aspect ratio (~22, 0.03 × 0.7 μm) were obtained, accompanied by a substantial amount of amorphous material (Figure 4B). Increasing the SiO2/B2O3 ratio to the range of 10–20 led to highly crystalline SCM-10 with well-defined needle-like morphology and a reduced average aspect ratio of 20 (0.05 × 1 μm). Further increasing the ratio to 60 induced a morphology transition to plate-like crystals (aspect ratio ~5; 0.27 × 1.3 μm), again accompanied by a significant amorphous phase. When the SiO2/B2O3 ratio exceeded 60, SCM-10 crystallization was suppressed, and in the boron-free system, the ZSM-48 phase was eventually formed. Notably, despite the wide variation in the initial gel composition, the SCM-10 samples synthesized within the optimal range converged to similar framework SiO2/B2O3 ratios (ca. 10). This convergence indicates the existence of an upper limit for boron incorporation in the SCM-10 framework, which is likely associated with the preferential accommodation of boron atoms in four-membered rings [20,21,22].

3.2.2. Effects of DMAP/SiO2 and H2O/SiO2 Ratios

The effects of the DMAP/SiO2 (0.05–1.0) and H2O/SiO2 (5–60) ratios on SCM-10 synthesis were examined (Figure 5 and Figure 6). These parameters primarily influenced crystallization kinetics and the aspect ratio of the needle-like crystals, without affecting phase selectivity. At a low DMAP/SiO2 ratio of 0.05, thin needle-crystals with a high aspect ratio (~33, 0.02 × 0.67 μm) were formed, together with a considerable amorphous fraction. Increasing the DMAP/SiO2 ratio to 0.1–1.0 yielded phase-pure SCM-10, accompanied by a decrease in aspect ratio to ~20. Consistent with previous studies, higher OSDA contents likely promote crystal growth by facilitating more balanced growth along different crystallographic directions, thereby reducing anisotropy and suppressing amorphous phase formation [23,24,25]. With respect to the H2O/SiO2 ratio, well-crystallized needle-like SCM-10 was obtained as the ratio increased from 5 to 50, accompanied by a slight increase in aspect ratio [26,27]. In contrast, at an H2O/SiO2 ratio of 60, the synthesis gel became overly diluted, which impeded effective crystallization and resulted in partially crystallized products containing a large amount of amorphous material (Figure 6(Bd)).

3.2.3. Effect of Crystallization Temperature

Crystallization temperature is a key parameter in zeolite synthesis, as both nucleation and growth processes are highly temperature-dependent. The crystallization temperature was varied at 150, 160, 180, and 190 °C on the synthesis of SCM-10 (Figure 7). At a lower temperature of 150 °C, the solid product was dominated by the amorphous phase with a few SCM-10 needle-like crystals (0.07 × 0.5 μm; aspect ratio~7). Achieving complete crystallization at this temperature would likely require extended crystallization times. Increasing the temperature to 160 and 170 °C resulted in phase-pure SCM-10 with well-defined needle-like crystals and increased aspect ratios (~20). Further increasing the temperature to 190 °C led to the formation of nanowires with a high aspect ratio of 83 (0.03 × 2.5 μm), accompanied by a minor impurity phase tentatively assigned to ZSM-48. Based on previous studies, the temperature-dependent morphological evolution can be rationalized by the combined kinetic and thermodynamic effects on crystal growth. Elevated temperatures accelerate both nucleation and crystal growth, but may also alter the dominant growth pathway by enhancing the mobility and reactivity of soluble silicate/borate species. In addition, the growth rates of different crystallographic faces are expected to exhibit different temperature sensitivities (i.e., different apparent activation energies), which can amplify anisotropic growth and lead to increasingly elongated morphologies [28,29].

For zeolites with one-dimensional channel systems and needle-like morphologies, crystal growth has often been interpreted as involving a combination of classical solution-mediated growth and non-classical particle-mediated processes such as oriented attachment and particle fusion. Similar behavior has been reported for related zeolite systems [30,31]. In the case of SCM-10, the needle-like morphology obtained at lower temperature likely reflects a mixed growth mechanism, in which lateral thickening proceeds partially through attachment/fusion of nanosized precursors (non-classical pathway), while longitudinal growth along the b axis is dominated by surface integration of soluble Si/B species regulated by OSDA–framework interactions (classical pathway). Upon increasing temperature, the classical growth pathway is expected to become increasingly dominant, resulting in enhanced anisotropic growth along the b axis and ultimately producing thinner and more elongated nanowire-like crystals at 190 °C.

3.2.4. Effect of Heteroatom Substitution

To expand the application scope of SCM-10 through the introduction of catalytically active framework sites, various heteroatoms were incorporated into the synthesis gel to obtain heteroatom-substituted M-B-SCM-10 zeolites (M = Al, V, Fe, and Ge) (Figure 8). As reported previously, the incorporation of heteroatoms retards the crystallization of SCM-10 to varying extents [18], thereby necessitating either prolonged crystallization times or the use of seed crystals to achieve phase-pure products (Table 2). It is well established that the nature of heteroatoms can strongly influence zeolite nucleation and crystal growth, leading to pronounced variations in crystal morphology [5,32,33].

Specifically, aluminum incorporation (Si/Al = 60; Si/B = 10) under seeded conditions markedly shortened the original needle-like crystals, yielding Al-B-SCM-10 with a nanoparticle morphology and an average particle size of 100 nm. Vanadium substitution (Si/V = 80; Si/B = 10) promoted the formation of thicker needle-like crystals with reduced lengths of ~500 nm and an aspect ratio of 10 (0.05 × 0.5 μm). In contrast, iron incorporation (Si/Fe = 80; Si/B = 10) resulted in Fe-B-SCM-10 with a nanowire morphology, characterized by ultrathin crystals of ~20 nm in diameter and a high aspect ratio of 65 (0.02 × 1.3 μm). Germanium incorporation (Si/Ge = 5; Si/B = 10) led to the formation of peanut-like aggregates composed of elongated needle-like crystals with an aspect ratio of 200 (0.05 × 10 μm).

Overall, heteroatom incorporation generally prolongs the induction period of SCM-10 crystallization and modifies the subsequent crystal growth pathway by tuning the relative growth rates along different crystallographic directions. Based on the observed morphology evolution, Al and V substitution appears to preferentially suppress crystal elongation along the b axis, whereas Fe and Ge more strongly inhibit growth along the a axis, thereby regulating the aspect ratio and final morphology of the needle-like crystals.

Although the underlying mechanism is not yet fully resolved, this anisotropic growth modulation may originate from the intrinsic differences in ionic radius, valence state, and coordination chemistry among these heteroatoms, which can influence their incorporation preference at framework T sites (Si/B positions) and locally perturb the distribution of boron in the SFE framework. Such variations may affect framework connectivity, surface stabilization, and OSDA–framework interactions, ultimately leading to different growth kinetics along specific crystallographic directions [34,35,36].

4. Conclusions

In summary, this work delivers a comprehensive understanding of the crystallization behavior and morphology control of the SFE-type zeolite SCM-10, filling a key knowledge gap in SFE-type zeolite research. SCM-10 crystallizes through a distinct two-stage process, comprising an induction period (0–30 h) followed by rapid crystal growth (30–42 h), ultimately yielding uniform needle-like crystals. Seed-assisted crystallization effectively accelerates nucleation and shortens the crystallization time. Systematic variation in synthesis parameters reveals that the SiO2/B2O3 ratio governs both phase selectivity and morphology evolution, with increasing silica content inducing a transition from needle-like to plate-like crystals, while OSDA/SiO2 and H2O/SiO2 ratios primarily modulate growth kinetics and crystal uniformity. Elevated crystallization temperatures promote anisotropic crystal growth, leading to the formation of high-aspect-ratio nanowires. Furthermore, heteroatom substitution profoundly alters crystal growth pathways, yielding morphologies ranging from nanoparticles and nanowires to needle-like crystal aggregates, depending on the heteroatom species. Collectively, these findings establish morphology regulation rules for SCM-10 and provide insights into how synthesis chemistry governs anisotropic zeolite crystal growth, thereby offering a solid foundation for the rational design and synthesis of SCM-10 with tailored morphologies for potential catalytic and separation applications.

Pharmacological Management of Acute Endodontic Infections in Children and Adults: A Comparative Clinical Study
Authors: Shweta V. Sagare, Balram Choudhary, Arpita Srivastava, Sugandha Sharma, Ayshita Khetarpal, Mohd Zeeshan Ahmad
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Acute endodontic infections are among the most common dental emergencies requiring prompt pharmacological intervention alongside operative treatment. Variations in host response, drug metabolism, and microbial flora between children and adults necessitate tailored pharmacological strategies.Methods:A prospective observational study was conducted on 120 patients presenting with acute endodontic infections, divided into two groups: children (n = 60; age 6–12 years) and adults (n = 60; age 18–60 years). Patients were managed with standardized pharmacological protocols including antibiotics (amoxicillin or amoxicillin-clavulanate), analgesics (ibuprofen or paracetamol), and adjunctive therapy when required. Clinical outcomes, including pain reduction (Visual Analog Scale [VAS] score), swelling resolution, and need for antibiotic escalation, were assessed over 5 days. Statistical analysis was performed using independent t-test and Chi-square test.Results:Mean baseline pain scores were compared between children (7.8 ± 1.1) and adults (8.1 ± 1.3; P = 0.18). By day 5, pain reduction was significantly greater in adults (VAS: 1.2 ± 0.6) compared to children (1.8 ± 0.9; P = 0.004). Swelling resolution occurred in 91.7% of adults and 83.3% of children (P = 0.18). Antibiotic escalation was required in 10% of children and 5% of adults (P = 0.31). Adverse drug reactions were minimal in both groups.Conclusion:Pharmacological management of acute endodontic infections is effective in both children and adults; however, adults demonstrate faster symptomatic relief. Tailored dosing and close monitoring are essential in pediatric populations.

INTRODUCTION

Acute endodontic infections represent a significant burden in dental practice, often characterized by severe pain, swelling, and systemic involvement requiring immediate intervention.[1] These infections primarily arise due to microbial invasion of the pulp and periapical tissues, leading to inflammatory responses and tissue destruction.[2]

Pharmacological management plays a crucial role as an adjunct to definitive endodontic treatment, particularly in cases presenting with systemic signs or spreading infections.[3] Antibiotics such as amoxicillin, with or without clavulanic acid, remain first-line agents, while analgesics such as non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) are commonly prescribed for pain control.[4]

Differences in pharmacokinetics, immune response, and microbial flora between children and adults influence treatment outcomes.[5] Pediatric patients often exhibit variable drug metabolism and compliance issues, whereas adults may have comorbidities affecting drug efficacy.[6]

Despite established guidelines, there is limited comparative evidence evaluating pharmacological outcomes between pediatric and adult populations in acute endodontic infections. Most studies focus on either group independently, creating a gap in understanding age-related therapeutic differences. Hence, the aim was to compare the efficacy and outcomes of pharmacological management of acute endodontic infections in children and adults.

DISCUSSION

The present study demonstrated that pharmacological management of acute endodontic infections is effective in both children and adults, with slightly better outcomes observed in adults.

The greater reduction in pain observed among adults may be attributed to more predictable pharmacokinetics and better adherence to prescribed medication regimens.[7] In contrast, pediatric patients often exhibit variability in drug absorption and dosing challenges, which may influence outcomes.[8]

Antibiotic therapy remains a cornerstone in managing infections with systemic involvement. Amoxicillin continues to be the first-line drug due to its broad-spectrum activity against oral pathogens.[2] The addition of clavulanic acid enhances efficacy against beta-lactamase-producing bacteria, which are increasingly reported in odontogenic infections.[3]

The slightly higher rate of antibiotic escalation in children may reflect more aggressive infection progression or suboptimal dosing. Previous studies have emphasized the importance of weight-based dosing and adherence in pediatric populations.[5]

Analgesic therapy using NSAIDs demonstrated effective pain control in both groups, consistent with earlier literature indicating ibuprofen as superior to opioids in dental pain management.[6]

Adverse drug reactions were minimal, supporting the safety of commonly used pharmacological agents in endodontic infections. This aligns with findings from systematic reviews reporting a low incidence of complications with short-term antibiotic and NSAID use.[4]

The study highlights the need for individualized pharmacological approaches, particularly in pediatric patients, to optimize outcomes and minimize complications.

CONCLUSION

Pharmacological management of acute endodontic infections is effective in both children and adults. Adults exhibit faster pain relief and slightly better clinical outcomes. Pediatric patients require careful dosing and monitoring to achieve optimal therapeutic results. Rational antibiotic use and individualized treatment protocols are essential to improve outcomes and reduce resistance.

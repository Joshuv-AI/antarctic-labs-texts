Letter to the editor of Heliyon re: “Effects of thoracic paravertebral block combined with s-ketamine on postoperative pain and cognitive function after thoracoscopic surgery” [Heliyon 8 (2022) e12231]
Authors: Yi Cheng, Fu-Shan Xue, Cheng-Wen Li
Published: 2024

To the Editor:

Acknowledgement: All authors have no financial support and potential conflicts of interest for this work

Re: Chen X et al. Effects of thoracic paravertebral block combined with s-ketamine on postoperative pain and cognitive function after thoracoscopic surgery. Heliyon. 2022; 8(12):e12231. doi: 10.1016/j.heliyon.2022.e12231.

Subtitle: Paravertebral block combined with s-ketamine for thoracic surgery

Keywords: Paravertebral block; Esketamine, Postoperative pain, Perioperative neurocognitive disorder

www.duplichecker.com\

YC FSX CWL

In a single-centre randomised placebo-controlled clinical trial including 120 patients who underwent elective thoracoscopic radical lung cancer surgery under general anaesthesia, Chen and colleagues [1] assessed the effects of thoracic paravertebral block (TPVB) combined with s-ketamine on postoperative pain and the occurrence of perioperative neurocognitive disorder (PND). They showed that the TPVB combined with s-ketamine decreased acute postoperative pain and improved the quality of recovery, but did reduce the occurrence of PND. However, we had several questions about methods and results of this study and wished to get the authors’ responses.

First, as primary outcome of the study, PND was determined using Z-score method and a final Z value of 1.96 or more indicated the development of PND. This is not an appropriate diagnostic criterion of PND. At 2018, the Nomenclature Consensus Working Group recommends that the definitions of PND in clinical study must meet diagnostic criteria of neurocognitive disorders in the DSM-5. Especially, the definitions of PND require addition of assessment on activities of daily living [2].

Second, in this study, the TPVB combined with s-ketamine did not reduce the occurrence of PND. In the reporting of sample size evaluation, however, this study did not provide the expected clinically difference of primary endpoint between groups, i.e., possible improvement in the incidence of PND by studied intervention. Because of this design limitation, we cannot exclude the possibility that this study is under-powered to show the between-group differences in the incidence of PND [3].

Third, the objects of this study were patients undergoing thoracic surgery and postoperative pain was assessed by a visual analogue scale. However, the authors did not provide the patient’ state when postoperative pain was evaluated. For patients undergoing thoracic surgery, postoperative pain level is actually more severe during movement than at resting status [4]. The use of remedial analgesia was significantly different among three groups, but the indications and drugs of remedial analgesia and the goal of postoperative pain control were not clearly described in the methods. Most important, the dosages of analgesics for postoperative pain control were not noted and compared among three groups. Because of these unknown factors, it is very difficult for the reader to determine whether the TPVB combined with s-ketamine had really resulted in an improved postoperative pain control for patients undergoing elective thoracic surgery.

Fourth, remifentanil dosage was significantly decreased in the patients receiving the TPVB combined with s-ketamine intervention compared with the patients receiving the TPVB alone or control intervention, indicating a significantly intraoperative opioid sparing of the TPVB combined with s-ketamine intervention. Because the dosages of opioid drugs for postoperative pain control were not provided and compared among three groups, however, it was unclear whether the TPVB combined with s-ketamine intervention was able to produce a similar postoperative opioid sparing for pain control, an important goal of current Enhanced Recovery After Surgery protocols [5].

Finally, as the incidences of postoperative PONV and pulmonary complications were significantly decreased in the patients receiving the TPVB combined with s-ketamine intervention, the authors concluded that this intervention improved the quality of postoperative recovery. We argue that this conclusion may be arbitrary. It is commonly recommended that the quality of postoperative recovery should be evaluated using the quality of recovery 15 or 40 questionnaire, as performed in other work assessing the effects of esketamine on the quality of postoperative recovery [6]. The quality of recovery 15 or 40 questionnaire is composed of different aspects evaluating patient's comfort, emotional, social, pain or independent behavior, with 1–5 points for each question and a total score of 150 or 200 points. A higher score indicates a better quality of recovery.

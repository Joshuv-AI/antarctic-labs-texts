Global remote feature modulation end-to-end detection
Authors: XiaoAn Bao, WenJing Yi, XiaoMei Tu, Na Zhang, QingQi Zhang, YuTing Jin, Biao Wu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Object detector based on fully convolutional network achieves excellent performance. However, existing detection algorithms still face challenges such as low detection accuracy in dense scenes and issues with occlusion of dense targets. To address these two challenges, we propose an Global Remote Feature Modulation End-to-End (GRFME2E) detection algorithm. In the feature extraction phase of our algorithm, we introduces the Concentric Attention Feature Pyramid Network (CAFPN). The CAFPN captures direction-aware and position-sensitive information, as well as global remote dependencies of features in deep layers by combining Coordinate Attention and Multilayer Perceptron. These features are used to modulate the front-end shallow features, enhancing inter-layer feature adjustment to obtain comprehensive and distinctive feature representations.In the detector part, we introduce the Two-Stage Detection Head (TS Head). This head employs the First-One-to-Few (F-O2F) module to detect slightly or unobstructed objects. Additionally, it uses masks to suppress already detected instances, and then feeds them to the Second-One-to-Few (S-O2F) module to identify those that are heavily occluded. The results from both detection stages are merged to produce the final output, ensuring the detection of objects whether they are slightly obscured, unobstructed, or heavily occluded. Experimental results on the pig detection dataset demonstrate that our GRFME2E achieves an accuracy of 98.4%. In addition, more extensive experimental results show that on the CrowdHuman dataset, our GRFME2E achieves 91.8% and outperforms other methods.

Introduction

Object detection is a fundamental task in computer vision, aiming to identify the positions and categorize specific objects in images or videos1. One significant direction in the development of object detection is addressing dense and occluded object detection challenges.

This is especially important in public places such as traffic and stadium management, where vehicles and pedestrians are often densely packed and blocked2. Reference3 identifies heads in crowded sports venues by generating scale-aware head proposals with a scale map, processing them with a CNN to get presence probabilities, and using non-maximal suppression for accurate positions. Reference4 proposes DMF to improve the accuracy of long-distance small object detection in transportation by mapping the disparity area to a two-dimensional area, clustering the disparity map, and using multi-clustering backsampling and feature fusion. Non-maximum suppression (NMS) play a crucial role in these literatures, which serves to remove redundant predictions and plays a vital role in dense object detection. NMS is necessary due to the one-to-many (o2m) label assignment strategy5–7 during training, which assigns multiple positive samples to each Ground-Truth (GT) object. This assignment may lead to duplicate predictions during inference, affecting detection performance. Meanwhile, NMS involves hyperparameters that need adjustment, as shown in Fig. 1a. When the IoU threshold is set to 0.5, situations like missed detections may occur, adding extra computational costs. Consequently, there is a pressing desire to achieve end-to-end object detection without the need for NMS.Figure 1(a) The red BBox will be suppressed if NMS is used with an IoU threshold of 0.5; (b) Illustration of detected instances from F-O2F; (c) Illustration of detected instances from S-O2F; (d) GRFME2E Detection Result.

DETR8 employs the Transformer architecture to achieve competitive end-to-end detection performance. Subsequent research9 has emphasized the pivotal role of one-to-one label assignment in DETR’s success. Consequently, the o2o strategy has been introduced into dense detection models based on Fully Convolutional Networks (FCN) to realize lightweight end-to-end detection. However, the limited number of positive samples can impact training efficiency. This issue is particularly severe for dense detection models, which often involve over 10,000 anchor points within a single image. Moreover, semantically similar anchor points can be designated as positive and negative anchors, further reducing the discriminative capability of feature representations. Consequently, end-to-end dense detectors still lag behind detectors employing NMS. Recent studies on DETR10,11 have attempted to overcome this limitation of the o2o strategy by introducing independent query groups to increase the number of positive samples. The independence between different query groups is ensured through self-attention mechanisms computed in the decoder, though this approach may not be practical for FCN-based detectors.

The Global Remote Feature Modulation End-to-End detection algorithm proposed in this article shows significant advantages in dealing with the challenges of large differences, complex backgrounds, and dense target occlusion faced by fully convolutional networks in target detection. By enhancing global context awareness, improving dense target detection capabilities and adapting to target differences, this algorithm significantly improves detection accuracy and robustness, providing new ideas and methods for solving target detection problems in practical applications.

In this algorithm, Central Attention and Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) are introduced on deep features with rich semantic information and strong representation capabilities in the main network, and their outputs are combined to capture directional awareness, position sensitivity, and global long-range dependency information. This is used to modulate shallow features at the front end, strengthen inter-layer feature regulation, and construct a Centralized Attention Feature Pyramid Network (CAFPN). Furthermore, in the detector part, a Two-Stage Detection Head Variant based on O2F is proposed. It uses the F-O2F module to detect objects with slight or no occlusion, the results are shown in Fig. 1b. Subsequently, the detected instances are suppressed using a mask, and the S-O2F module is employed to obtain instances with severe occlusion, as shown in Fig. 1c. Combining the results of these two detection layers as the final output, as illustrated in Fig. 1d, achieves detection of both unoccluded and severely occluded targets.

Related work

Feature Pyramid The feature pyramid is an important network structure in modern visual recognition systems. It utilizes different resolution levels of feature hierarchies to provide appropriate feature representations for targets at different scales and fuses multi-scale information to predict targets of different sizes, significantly improving model performance. FPN12 uses different resolution levels of feature hierarchies to provide appropriate feature representations for targets at different scales and fuses multi-scale information to predict targets of different sizes, significantly enhancing model performance. FPT13 transforms the feature pyramid into another feature pyramid with the same size but richer context through three interaction modes: self-level, top-down, and bottom-up, achieving feature interaction across space and scale. BiFPN14 further optimizes feature fusion based on NAS-FPN, introducing learnable weights to determine the importance of different input features. It repetitively applies multi-scale feature fusion from top-down and bottom-up, striking a better balance between accuracy and efficiency. FANet15 addresses the challenges of arbitrary orientation, dense arrangement, and small object size in remote sensing images through feature fusion, angle classification, and innovative network designs, demonstrating significant effectiveness on public datasets. CFANet16 addresses the challenge of detecting dense small objects in UAV images by integrating cross-layer feature aggregation, layered associative spatial pyramid pooling, and an alpha-IoU loss function. Overall, the feature pyramid, by constructing multi-level feature representations containing scale information without adding computational burden, achieves multi-scale target detection and localization, extracts contextual information, thus improving model performance and generalization ability. In this paper, we propose an inner feature modulation method from the perspective of inter-layer feature interaction and inner feature adjustment of the feature pyramid, filling the gaps in current methods in this aspect.

End-to-end object detection In early research, detection frameworks based on recurrent neural networks achieved end-to-end detection in principle by predicting bounding boxes for each instance using an autoregressive decoder. However, they were only effective on small datasets, and the iterative approach made the inference process inefficient. Additionally, NMS variants17–20 used deep and complex networks to learn duplicate removal, but they consisted of discrete components and did not effectively achieve end-to-end training. Subsequently, DETR8 introduced a training strategy based on bipartite matching and a transformer with a parallel decoder. It achieved end-to-end detection by using a one-to-one label assignment strategy and direct set loss, eliminating the need for any additional post-processing steps. However, the computation of self-attention and cross-attention in the decoder limited its practical application in dense and fully convolutional detectors. Recently, Ref.21 introduced the one-to-one (o2o) label assignment strategy from DETR into dense detectors based on fully convolutional networks to achieve lightweight end-to-end detection. However, due to the limited number of positive samples, the network struggled to learn a robust and stable feature representation. Reference22 proposed a partial (o2f.) strategy, labeling ambiguous anchors as partial positives and partial negatives, and adaptively adjusting the degree of positive and negative labels during training. However, its performance was low on occluded and smaller targets, making it unsuitable for dense prediction frameworks. Building upon this, we developed a two-stage detection head variant to achieve detection of slightly or unoccluded objects and severely occluded objects, achieving end-to-end object detection for dense targets.

Global remote feature modulation end-to-end detection

Concentric attention feature pyramid network

In dense target images, objects are often clustered, leading to occlusion among them, and only partial features of the objects are visible, making it challenging to recognize their contours. FPN, by employing a top-down approach, combines features with a large receptive field and low resolution with those of a smaller receptive field and higher resolution, enhancing the feature representation capability of convolutional neural networks. It generates feature maps with different feature information and dimensions. However, the FPN method still fails to fully utilize different receptive field sizes. The top-down path only stacks layers to expand the receptive field, neglecting intra-layer feature control, which hinders effective information propagation. When inputting high-resolution images, the receptive field cannot cover the entire image, making it impossible to obtain meaningful semantic information and utilize the context effectively. Low-resolution images after multiple downsamplings have larger receptive fields but lack fine-grained details, resulting in lower utilization of semantic information with a multi-scale receptive field.

Inspired by previous work on dense detection tasks, we propose a Centralized Attention Feature Pyramid Network (CAFPN) as shown in Fig. 2. Compared to existing feature pyramids, CAFPN not only focuses on direction-aware and position-sensitive information in deep features but also captures global long-range dependencies. This helps reduce false positives and false negatives in complex scenarios like occlusion, thus enhancing detection accuracy and robustness, which is crucial for dense detection tasks.Figure 2CAFPN.

CAFPN.

Unlike shallow features, the deep features in FPN contain richer semantic information and stronger representational capabilities, enabling them to capture more abstract feature representations. CAFPN takes the features generated at the top of the feature pyramid and feeds them into the Coordinated Attention (CA) module and the Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP). The outputs from both modules are combined to obtain features that aggregate direction-aware and position-sensitive information along with global long-range dependencies.

Finally, we aggregate these features into a two-stage detection head variant for classification and regression.

Two-stage detection head

In common single-stage and two-stage detection algorithms, Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS) is often used to suppress redundant prediction boxes. However, in dense object detection tasks, where there may be a significant overlap of objects, the NMS process may suppress some highly overlapping candidate boxes, leading to real targets being falsely identified as false alarms or missing detections. End-to-end detection algorithms effectively address this issue. Building upon22, this paper introduces a TS Head designed for handling dense object detection tasks in crowded scenes.

The structure of the TS Head is illustrated in Fig. 4, and it consists of two parallel O2F modules. The first O2F module is referred to as F-O2F, and the second O2F module is referred to as S-O2F. Initially, the F-O2F module is used to detect slightly or non-severely occluded objects, and a binary mask is applied to suppress previously detected targets, emphasizing features of heavily occluded objects. Subsequently, the S-O2F module is used to detect missed targets, and the union of the results from these two detection stages is considered as the final output.Figure 4Two-stage detection head.

Two-stage detection head.

Experiments

Datasets and implementation details

Datasets To validate the effectiveness of the method, experiments were conducted on the publicly available pig dataset from the AI Challenge by iFLYTEK and CrowdHuman dataset. The pig dataset is in coco format, consisting of 3600 images, with a varying number of pigs per image ranging from 30 to 35. The pig dataset exhibits variations in pig postures, occlusions, and environmental conditions. Prior to training the model, the dataset was randomly divided into a training set and a validation set with a ratio of 8:2, resulting in 2880 images for training and 720 images for validation. CrowdHuman is a dataset provided by Megvii for human detection in crowded scenes. It includes 15,000 images for training, 5000 images for testing, and 4370 images for validation. The training and validation sets contain a total of 470 K instances, with approximately 23 individuals per image, and various types of occlusions present.

Experimental Details In this paper, ResNet-50 is used as the backbone network, and the MMDetection tool is used to implement the proposed method. The detector is trained with a batch size of 16 on 2 GeForce RTX 3090 GPUs. The SGD optimizer is used with an initial learning rate of 0.02, momentum of 0.9, and weight decay of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$10^{ - 4}$$\end{document}10-4. The model is trained for 36 epochs, and the learning rate is reduced by a factor of 10 at the 24th and 33rd epochs. To address the varying sizes of CrowdHuman images, we resize the input images, ensuring the shortest side is 800 pixels and the longest side does not exceed 1400 pixels.

Evaluation criteria

In pedestrian detection, mean Miss Rate (mMR) is commonly used to dynamically measure a target detection system’s ability to miss target instances during the detection process. In typical pedestrian detection scenarios, mMR can evaluate the performance of the detection system at different false positive rate conditions, demonstrating a good balance. However, in crowded scenes, due to significant occlusions between instances, the detector may generate more false positive predictions, leading to extremely high mMR scores. In our experiments, we observed that the mMR score threshold could be as high as 0.95, meaning predicted bounding boxes with category scores below 0.95 would be excluded in the evaluation. Therefore, we used AP and recall with an IoU threshold of 0.5 from the COCO-formatted dataset as our evaluation metrics.

Average Precision (AP) is further obtained from the Precision Recall (PR) curve, formed with precision on the vertical axis and recall on the horizontal axis. In the above, P stands for precision; R stands for recall; TP stands for true positives, i.e., the number of positive samples correctly classified as positive; FP stands for false positives, i.e., the number of positive samples wrongly classified as negative; FN stands for false negatives, i.e., the number of negative samples wrongly classified as positive; AP represents the area enclosed by the PR curve corresponding to the k-th detected target with an IoU threshold of 0.5; K represents the number of detected targets with an IoU threshold of 0.5.

Conclusion

In this work, we propose a centralized attention pyramid network to obtain comprehensive and distinctive feature representations, and input these features into a two-stage detection head variant based on O2F, achieving end-to-end object detection. Our end-to-end detection algorithm based on global long-range feature regulation shows excellent detection results in complex and crowded environments, and has shown superior performance on the public iFlytek AI Challenge pig dataset and CrowdHuman dataset. Compared to state-of-the-art end-to-end dense detectors, our method shows certain improvements in detection accuracy and occlusion handling. However, it is important to recognize that our approach has some limitations. Our approach may require further optimization and adjustment to suit different application scenarios. For example, when deployed on mobile or embedded devices, the model may need to be compressed or quantized, given the limitations of computing and storage resources. Although our approach improves detection accuracy and solves occlusion problems to some extent, its performance still needs to be carefully evaluated in practical applications and may require further improvement and optimization.

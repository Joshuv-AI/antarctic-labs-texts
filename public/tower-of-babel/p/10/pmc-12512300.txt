Combining QTL mapping and RNA-Seq reveals candidate genes controlling flag leaf width in foxtail millet
Authors: Zhilan Wang, Mengmeng Shen, Huan Yang, Xiaofen Du, Kangni Han, Hongpo Wu, Xiangrui Dai, Shichao Lian, Yuxin Li, Yanfang Li, Linyi Zhang, Jun Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe flag leaf, a crucial component of plant architecture, significantly influences final grain yield in crops, including foxtail millet (Setaria italica L.). Optimizing flag leaf size is considered an effective strategy for enhancing grain yield potential under higher planting densities. However, the genetic mechanism underlying flag leaf size, particularly flag leaf width (FLW), remains largely unknown under varying planting densities in foxtail millet.ResultsAn FLW phenotype variation analysis was conducted across multiple planting densities using a recombinant inbred line (RIL) population derived from Heizhigu (narrow leaf) and Changnong 35 (wide leaf). Based on a high-density genetic map with 3795 Bin markers, 11 flag leaf width (FLW) QTLs were identified on chromosomes 3, 5, and 6, explaining 2.35%-36.06%. Among these, qFLW5-2 was a major QTL, detected consistently across 3 environments and explaining a large proportion of FLW variation. The QTL was further validated with 9 InDel markers with its candidate region across different planting densities. Moreover, RNA-seq revealed 2,293 and 2,338 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between biparents at heading stage and grain filling stage, respectively. There were 11 and 9 DEGs within the location range of qFLW5-2 among 2 comparison groups (HZG-H_vs_CN35-H and HZG-G_vs_CN35-G). Combining QTL mapping and RNA-seq, we speculated that Seita.5g134600 (encoding an auxin responsive protein Aux/IAA) and Seita.5G123900 (encoding a cytochrome P450 family protein) as key candidate genes for qFLW5-2. Furthermore, variation analysis confirmed that the lines or germplasm with Seita.5G1346005UTR277+ allele, both within the RIL population and natural populations, exhibited significantly wider leaves than those with Seita.5G1346005UTR277− allele. These findings advance our understanding of the genetic and molecular regulatory mechanisms governing flag leaf growth.ConclusionsThis study elucidates genetic and molecular mechanism regulating flag leaf growth and development in foxtail millet. The results provide a theoretical foundation for improving plant architecture and facilitating molecular marker-assisted breeding in this crop.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12870-025-07358-0.

Introduction

Facing the dual challenges of a continually growing population and diminishing arable land globally, breeders increasingly aim to enhance crop yield per unit area by raising planting density [1]. However, higher planting densities often impede leaf growth, reduce photosynthetic efficiency, and lower lodging resistance. Favorable leaf traits can mitigate these issues. Flag leaf (FL) is the main photosynthetic organ, which provides the main source of carbohydrate for grain filling during late crop growth stages [2–4]. Dimensions including flag leaf length (FLL), flag leaf width (FLW), and flag leaf area (FLA) critically determine light captured for photosynthesis [5–7]. Breeding cereals with optimally sized flag leaves is therefore an effective strategy for enhancing grain yield potential [8]. Numerous studies have demonstrated a positive correlation between FL size and 1000-grain weight, and grain yield per plant in cereals [3, 9, 10]. While FLL and FLW jointly determine FLA, excessively long leaves may droop, reducing photosynthetic efficiency, particularly under high planting density. Consequently, increasing FLW represents an effective approach to improving FLA [5].

Leaf width is determined by cell division and cell expansion, and its entire developmental process is regulated by plant hormones [11]. Auxin promotes cell proliferation and expansion, cting as a key signal determining final organ shape and size [12]. Several auxin signaling pathway genes influence leaf size. For example, NAL1 encoding a plant specific protein and NAL7 encoding a flavin-containing monooxygenase, affect auxin synthesis and polar transport, leading to narrowed leaf [13–15]. Similarly, the paralogous genes NAL2 and NAL3, encoding WUSCHEL-RELATED HOMEOBOX 3 A(OsWOX3A), modulated auxin-transport gene expression, and their mutants exhibit narrow leaves [16, 17]. OsNAL21 primarily controls leaf development by altering the translation efficiency of AUXIN RESPONSE FACTORs (OsARFs) and OsWOX3A (OsNAL2/OsNAL3) [18]. ABNORMAL VASCULAR BUNDLES (AVB) maintains normal cell division during lateral primordial development, which depends on auxin signaling for procambium establishment; avb mutant exhibit narrowed leaves [19]. Additionally, cytokinin, gibberellin and brassinolide also regulate leaf width [12]. For instance, ARF6 and ARF8 activate transcription of DWARF4 (encoding a key BR biosynthetic enzyme), promoting directional cell expansion and ultimately regulating leaf size [20]. DROOPY LEAF1 (DPY1) controls leaf architecture by orchestrating early brassinosteroid signaling in foxtail millet [21]. In wheat, TaNAL1, a trypsin-like serine/cysteine protease, regulates leaf width via cytokinin signaling interacting with TaWAK2 and TaDST [22]. Furthermore, severe gibberellin (GA)-deficient mutants reduce the leaf size, whereas GA20ox1 overexpression promotes GA accumulation and leaf growth [23].

Foxtail millet (Setaria italica L. Beauv), a typically C4 cereals in subfamily Panicoideae, possesses large, long leaf blades facilitating efficient light capture and photosynthesis [21, 24]. However, its leaves droop downward normally, particularly at maturity, adversely affecting canopy structure and grain yield [21]. Optimizing leaf traits can mitigate these issues. To date, molecular mechanisms on the flag leaf traits in foxtail millet remain limited, primarily involving Mapping of QTL. In 2013, 6 FLW QTLs on Chr.1, Chr.5 and Chr.7 were detected using a natural population under four different environments: Shunyi, Beijing; Anyang, Henan; Chaoyang, Liaoning and Changzhi, Shanxi [25]. A RIL population (“Zhanggu × A2”) was used to detect 5 large-effect QTLs related to FLL. However, none of these QTLs were repeatedly detected. For FLW, 3 QTLs with small and irregular effects were identified. Among these, only one QTL on chromosome 9 was consistently detected. These results indicate the complexity of the genetic mechanisms underlying flag leaf traits [26]. Similarly, using the same RIL population, Zhang et al. [27] conducted QTL mapping for FLL and FLW under long- and short-day photoperiods and totally identified 7 QTLs related to FLL and 3 for FLW, respectively, and qflw5-1 was detected consistently under both photoperiods. Recently, Liu et al. [28] identified 15 QTLs associated with FLL and 15 QTLs related to FLW in 9 environments using a recombinant inbred line (RIL) population constructed by “Yugu1 × Longgu7”. In summary, QTL mapping results from different studies are rarely consistent, and also the effect values are generally low, indicating that flag leaf traits are easily influenced by the environment.

In this study, a RIL population in foxtail millet derived from a cross between Heizhigu and Changnong 35, was developed and used to analyze FLW phenotypic variation under different planting densities. Furthermore, a high-density linkage map was constructed. Based on the genetic linkage map and phenotypic data from the six environments, we sought to identify significant QTL associated with FLW. Combining QTL mapping and RNA-seq, Seita.5g134600 and Seita.5G123900, encoded an auxin responsive protein Aux/IAA and a cytochrome P450 family protein, respectively, were thought to be the key candidates for qFLW5-2, and the molecular marker Seita.5G1346005UTR277 tightly linked to FLW was developed. These results lay the foundation for further studies on the genetic mechanism of FLW development in foxtail millet.

Results

Phenotypic analysis

Phenotypic data for FLW of parental lines (Fig. 1A) and RILs were analyzed in 6 environments conditions. The results showed that the FLW of the parental Lines exhibited extremely significant differences under 6 environmental conditions (Fig. 1B). The FLW in RILs showed a steady range of variation, with a coefficient of variation ranging from 13.77 to 14.66%, and the absolute values of skewness and kurtosis were less than 1, indicating that these traits were approximately normally distributed (Table S1). Sequencing and SNP identification

We used the MGI-2000/MGI-T7 sequencing platform of MolBreeding to resequence the parental lines and RIL populations (150 lines). After filtering, there were a total of 609,6493,684 bp clean reads. The clean reads for Heizhigu were 145,436,366 bp and 95.5% for Q30, the clean reads of Changnong 35 were 113,251,648 bp and Q30 was 91.77%. The clean reads of each line in the RIL population ranged from 31,326,168 to 74,597,454 bp, with an average of 40,270,304 bp per individual, and Q30 ≥ 86.93%. Additionally, in Heizhigu, Changnong 35 and RIL population, the average sequencing depth was 39.28×, 33.36× and 12.62×, and the coverage rate was 88.19%, 92.25% and 8.15%~88.78% (average of 56.17%), respectively (Table S2).

By aligning the clean reads with the reference genome of Setaria italic (JGI_v2.2), a total of 7,639,011 SNPs and 1,875,887 InDels were detected. The number of SNP in each chromosome ranged from 629,925 (Chr.1) to 1,291,097 (Chr.8), and the InDel number of chromosomes ranged from 150,788 (Chr.4) to 307,397 (Chr.8) (Table 1).

By filtering the offspring individuals, 16 Lines were filtered out under integrity, 12 Lines were filtered out under high heterozygosity, and finally 122 lines were left for genetic mapping. The breakpoints distinguished homozygous from heterozygous genotypes and also differentiated between homozygous genotypes. After determining the recombination breakpoints for each individual, we constructed a high-density genetic Map with 3,795 Bin markers by aligning all chromosomes of the RILs (Table S3). The genetic Map spanned 3,164.72 cM, the length range of each Linkage group ranged from 213.26 cM (Chr. 2) to 549.97 cM (Chr. 5), with an average Bin interval of 0.88 cM. The genetic distance between adjacent Bin Markers is 0.64 cM to 1.27 cM (Fig. 2B; Table 1; Table S4). Collinearity analysis showed that the genetic position of bin markers was in good agreement with that in the physical map (Fig. 2C).

QTL mapping analysis for FLW

Based on the high-density linkage map and phenotypic data from 122 individuals, a total of 11 QTLs related to FLW were detected on Chr. 3, 5 and 6, with LOD values ranging from 3.51 to 25.98. Except for qFLW3-2 and qFLW5-2, the additive effects of the other loci originated from Heizhigu. Furthermore, 4 QTLs, qFLW3-2, qFLW5-1, qFLW5-2 and qFLW5-3, explained phenotypic variation of 14.87%−36.06%. Among them, qFLW5-2, with highest PVE value and repeatedly detected in most environments (2021E2, 2022E2, 2022E3), was considered to be a major QTL in this study (Table 2).

Validation of qFLW5-2

The locus qFLW5-2 was subjected to further analysis due to its high PVE value, detection across three environmental conditions, and partial overlap with the previously mapped locus qflw5-1 [27]. A total of 9 InDel markers (Table S5), showing polymorphisms between two parental lines in the candidate region of qFLW5-2, were used to validate qFLW5-2. The results showed that qFLW5-2 was repeatedly detected and shared the same chromosome position in six environments, although there were some differences in LOD score and PVE, respectively (Fig. 3; Table 3; Table S6). Therefore, qFLW5-2 was considered an important locus to further analyses key candidate genes regulating FLW.

Analysis of RNA-seq

To further investigate the genetic mechanisms of flag leaf width, we conducted RNA-seq analysis. Since heading stage and grain filling stage are the two critical periods for growth and development of FL, and the FLW of Heizhigu and Changnong 35 showed significant differences at heading and grain filling stage (Fig. 4A). We chose FL as plant material during these two periods to carry out transcriptome analysis to determine the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between biparents. The correlation heatmap showed that correlation coefficient among sample replicates was high, and the correlation between the parents at the same developmental period was higher than that between two developmental stages in a certain parent (Fig. 4B). During heading and grain filling stage, 2293 DEGs (985 upregulated, 1308 downregulated) and 2338 DEGs (1182 upregulated, 1156 downregulated) were identified between the two parents, respectively. A total of 2551 DEGs (1161 upregulated, 1388 downregulated) were identified during Heading and grain filling stage in Heizhigu; 3955 DEGs (1985 upregulated, 1970 downregulated) were identified during heading and grain filling stage of Changnong 35 (Fig. 4C and D).

To reveal the functional differences of DEGs, GO enrichment analysis was performed on all selected DEGs. The results showed that most DEGs were significantly enriched in biological processes such as cellulose biosynthetic process, plant-type primary cell wall biogenesis, plant-type secondary cell wall biogenesis and cell surface receptor signaling pathway; the molecular functions mainly involved cellulose synthase activity, cellulose synthase (UDP-forming) activity, ATP binding and protein kinase activity; the most significantly enriched entries in CC classification were plasma membrane, integral component of membrane, and Golgi apparatus (Fig. S1).

Further identification of metabolic pathways related to FLW development was carried out by KEGG enrichment analysis. The results showed that, apart from some secondary metabolism in all four categories, the DEGs were mainly significantly enriched in starch and sucrose metabolism, brassinosteroid biosynthesis and amino acids metabolism between Heizhigu and Changnong 35 at heading stage. Between Heizhigu and Changnong 35 at grain filling stage, the DEGs were mainly significantly enriched in starch and sucrose metabolism, pyrimidine metabolism and ribosome biogenesis. However, The DEGs were mainly significantly enriched in MAPK signaling pathway-plant, plant hormone signaling transduction and starch and sucrose metabolism between heading and grain filling stage in Heizhigu. And the DEGs were mainly significantly enriched in MAPK signaling pathway-plant, amino acids metabolism and some secondary metabolism between heading and grain filling stage in Changnong 35 (Fig. 4E).

Next, we randomly selected four DEGs within the location range of qFLW5-2, qFLW3-2 and qFLW6-2 to perform qRT-PCR using the same experimental materials as RNA-seq (Fig. 5). The results showed that the expression trends of genes in qRT-PCR were consistent with RNA-seq.

Prediction of candidates for qFLW5-2

Based on the above QTL and transcriptome analysis, we predicted candidate genes within qFLW5-2. Among 2 comparison groups (HZG-H_vs_CN35-H and HZG-G_vs_CN35-G), there were 11 and 9 DEGs within the location range of qFLW5-2, respectively, of which 4 DEGs (Seita.5G123900, Seita.5G125200, Seita.5G131300 and Seita.5G139500) presented repeatedly in two comparison groups (Table 4; Fig. 6A). Among them, Seita.5G123900 encoded a cytochrome P450 family protein, which played an important role in brassinosteroid (BR) biosynthesis. It is worth noting that although the expression of Seita.5G134600 was only different in the grain filling stage between Heizhigu and Changnong 35, its expression was different at the two developmental stages between Heizhigu and Changnong 35 (Table S7). Seita.5G134600 encoded an auxin responsive protein Aux/IAA, participated in auxin signal transduction processes and played important roles in leaf morphogenesis, embryonic development, lateral root initiation and elongation, meridional growth, apical dominance, organ development of flowers and fruits, and abiotic stress in plants. Because both Seita.5G123900 and Seita.5G134600 were related to hormone signal transduction, we further screened out 185 DEGs related to hormone signal transduction according RNA-seq analysis. The hormone signaling pathways in which these genes may be involved were predicted through the Plant Hormone Gene Database (http://phgd.bio2db.com/). Furtherly, the genome sequence alignment analysis showed that there was no difference in Seita.5G123900 between Heizhigu and Changnong 35, while there was a 277 bp deletion at the 5’UTR (upstream of the start codon ATG − 61 bp) of Seita.5G134600 in Heizhigu compared to Changnong 35 (Fig. 6B, Fig. S3). Considering there were more DEGs related to auxin signal transduction based on the analysis of RNA-seq and Plant Hormone Gene Database, we speculated that Seita.5G134600 is a key candidate gene for qFLW5-2.

Based on this variation of Seita.5G134600, we developed an InDel marker, Seita.5G1346005UTR277 (Table S5). The FLW of lines of RIL population with Seita.5G1346005UTR277+ was 3.30 ± 0.38 cm, consistent with that of Changnong 35, In contrast, the lines with Seita.5G1346005UTR277− was 2.00 ± 0.15 cm, consistent with that of Heizhigu (Fig. 6C). In order to compare the expression of Seita.5G134600 in different Lines, 5 narrow-leaved Lines and 5 wide-leaved lines were randomly selected. The result showed that Seita.5G134600 expression level in the flag leaves from five randomly chosen lines with Seita.5G1346005UTR277− was significantly lower than those from the five randomly chosen lines with Seita.5G1346005UTR277+ at grain filling stage (Fig. 6D). It can be inferred that the deletion of 277 bp in the 5’UTR region of Seita.5G134600 may be the key reason for the difference of FLW between biparents.

Next, we used the foxtail millet database Setaria db to query the expression of this gene in Yugu 1 at different stages and tissues, and found that the expression level of Seita.5G134600 was low in the young flag leaf, and then increased in developed flag leaf, indicating that this gene may play an important role in regulating FL development (Fig. S3). Furthermore, the analysis of cis acting elements clarified that in addition to conservative elements in the promoter, there are also some elements related to plant growth, development and stress (Fig. S4). Among them, there were 10 cis acting elements involved in hormone response: one TGA element responsive to auxin, one ABRE element responsive to abscisic acid, two CGTCA motif responsive to methyl jasmonate, and two GARE motif responsive to gibberellin, proving that the expression of this gene is regulated by hormones. However, there was no element of hormone response but contain seven CAAT-box, one RY-element and one WUN-motif in the region of 277 bp deletion.

Discussion

FL played a significant role in the growth and development in cereal crop, and improving the FLW has always been an important way to increase crop yield. High-density planting usually led to mutual shading between leaves, decreased photosynthesis and reduced yields. Conversely, in low-density planting system, although the individual can fully develop, the insufficient number of plants is not conducive to yield formation [29]. Hence, appropriate planting density helps to construct a reasonable population structure to increase the photosynthetic efficiency of the population and improve dry matter accumulation and transport, thereby increasing crop yields [30]. In this study, we treated different planting densities as distinct environmental conditions to map the QTL associated with FLW traits. A significant difference analysis was conducted on the FLW of the biparents and RILs under three planting density conditions (Fig. S4). The results showed that the FLW of Changnong 35 narrowed with the increased planting density and there were of significant difference among three planting densities. This is consistent with previous researches [31, 32]. However, there was no significant difference for Heizhigu under three density conditions, and there was of significant difference in RILs between 2022E1 and 2022E2. These results suggested that FLW is affected by genetics and environmental factors.

To identify QTLs controlling FLW is the key to improve crop plant structure. Previous studies identified 21 QTLs associated with FLW across multiple environments using different RIL populations in foxtail millet [26–28]. Many QTLs were small and irregular, while major QTLs were only detected in specific environments. These results indicated that FLW was more susceptible to environmental influences than other traits like plant height. In this study, using a RIL population, we apply whole genome resequencing to construct a high-density genetic map with 3795 Bin markers and 72,241 SNPs, covering the entire genome. Based on these, we identified a major and relatively stable QTL, namely qFLW5-2 (10.25–12.18 Mb). By comparing previous research results, it was found that the localization interval of qFLW5-2 partially overlaps with qflw5-1 (long day, 10655408–10964233 bp) and qflw5-1 (short day, 12029644–13216505 bp) identified by Zhang et al. [27].

In recent years, RNA-seq has been used in QTL mapping studies to rapidly identify potential candidate genes in crops [5, 33, 34]. By the combined strategy of QTL mapping and RNA-seq, we selected heading and grain filling stage as the appropriate time to further characterize the gene expression pattern and genetic network. Two candidates, Seita.5G123900 and Seita.5G134600, associated with leaf development and plant hormone signaling transduction were finally detected within qFLW5-2 mapping interval. Seita.5G123900 encoded a cytochrome P450 family protein, which played an important role in cell proliferation and expansion [35], however there was no difference in genome sequence between the biparents. Interestingly, Seita.5G134600 encoded auxin responsive proteins Aux/IAA, which participated in the signal transduction process of auxin; there was a 277 bp deletion at the 5’UTR of Seita.5G134600 in Heizhigu compared to Changnong 35. Recent studies have shown that the 5’UTR sequence is an important cis regulatory element in yeast, human cells, and Arabidopsis protoplasts [36–38]. AAA in the 5’UTR sequence is associated with increased translation efficiency of Arabidopsis protoplasts, while TTT is associated with decreased translation efficiency [37, 39]. Wang et al. [40] designed and edited the 5’UTR sequences of the rice dwarf gene OsSBI and tiller gene OsHTD1, thereby result in upregulation and downregulation of the target protein and rapid improvement of traits. Thus, we speculated that Seita.5G134600 was the key candidate for FLW, and subsequent functional validation must be conducted using biotechnology such as genetic transformation and gene editing.

Molecular marker-assisted breeding strategy is an efficient way to improve plant architecture, and genes and/or their functional/close linkage molecular markers are essential for promoting molecular breeding programs [41]. In this study, Seita.5G1346005UTR277, a close linkage molecular marker of FLW was developed, and there was of significant difference in FLW between lines with Seita.5G1346005UTR277+ and Seita.5G1346005UTR277− in RIL population. Other than that, the FLW of germplasm materials with Seita.5G1346005UTR277+ was wider than that with Seita.5G1346005UTR277− at both Shunyi and Changzhi, athough it was not correlated with FLW under two other environmental conditions, Anyang and Chaoyang (Phenotypic and genotypic data from SetariDB) (Fig. 7). These results indicated that FLW was indeed influenced by both genotype and environment. And Seita.5G1346005UTR277 could be used as an assisted selection marker for FLW breeding in foxtail millet.

Conclusion

In this study, there were significant differences of the FLW between Heizhigu and Changnong 35 under three planting density conditions. With the increased planting density, the FLW decreased significantly in Changnong 35 and RILs but no significant changes in Heizhigu. Based on the RIL population derived from Heizhigu and Changnong 35, we constructed a high-density genetic map containing 3795 Bin markers, with a total genetic distance of 3164.7 cM and an average genetic distance of 0.88 cM. Under three planting density conditions, a total of 11 QTL for FLW were detected, and qFLW5-2, one of the major QTLs, partially overlapped with qflw5-1 (long day, short day) reported previously. Combining QTL mapping and RNA-seq unraveled a key candidate gene Seita.5g134600, encoded auxin responsive proteins Aux/IAA, within the qFLW5-2 interval. Furthermore, FLW of lines Seita.5G1346005UTR277+ was significantly wider than that with Seita.5G1346005UTR277− not only in the RIL population, but also in the natural populations in Shunyi and Changzhi.

Materials and methods

Plant materials

The F1 plant was derived from a cross between Heizhigu (also known as ‘Jingu 28’, bred by the Shanxi Academy of Agricultural Sciences with narrow leaves as the female parent (P1) and Changnong 35 (bred by the Shanxi Academy of Agricultural Sciences) with wide leaves as the male parent (P2) (Fig. 1A). The RIL population (F2:7) was obtained from a single F1 plant through a single seed descent (SSD) strategy. All the materials were planted in the experimental field of Millet Research Institute of Shanxi Agricultural University (Changzhi, Shanxi; 36.22°N, 113.15°E) in 2021, 2022 and 2023. Each Line was planted in a single 3-m row with 0.33 m between rows. The planting density was treated according to plant spacing of 6 cm (505,000 plants/hm2, E1), 9 cm (337,000 plants/hm2, E2) and 12 cm (262,000 plants/hm2, E3), and different densities were considered as different environments.

Phenotypic data collection and statistical analysis

After heading and stabilizing the internode length below the spike, 5 plants were selected for each line to measure FLW. FLW refers to the distance from the widest part of the leaf. SPSS 27.0 and Origin 2021 were used to perform descriptive statistics analysis, Student’s t-test, and correlation analysis of phenotype. Furthermore, the leaf width phenotypic and genotype data of 680 germplasm material in natural population at four environmental conditions, Beijing Shunyi, Henan Anyang, Liaoning Chaoyang and Shanxi Changzhi (The original data is derived from SetariDB) [24].

Whole genome resequencing and data processing

Genomic DNA from fresh leaves was extracted by an improved CTAB method [42]. DNA quality detection and quantitative analysis were performed using 1% agarose gel electrophoresis and Qubit 2.0 fluorometer. Sequencing libraries were constructed using the GenoBaits DNA seq Library Prep kit. The libraries were sequenced using PE150 mode of the Huada MGI-2000/MGI-T7 platform by MolBreeding. Low-quality reads and duplicated reads were filtered and the remaining high-quality data were used for further bioinformatics analysis.

Sequence alignment between the high-quality reads and the reference genome sequence of Setaria. italica (JGI_v2.2) was conducted using BWA software [43]. The Samtools software [44] was used to convert the comparison results into BAM files and analyse coverage and coverage depth, as well as variant calling. Based on BAM files, the GenotypeGVCFs module in the GATK software package [45] was applied to perform mutation detection, including single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) and insertion-deletion (InDel). ANNOVAR software [46] was utilized to annotate mutations and predict the impact of mutations on gene function.

Construction of high-density genetic map and QTL mapping

To ensure the quality of the map, strict screening and filtering were carried out on offspring individuals and polymorphic Markers. Using the ultimately obtained high-quality SNP markers, a strategy of 15 SNP sliding windows with a step size of 1 is used to detect recombination breakpoints. According to the minimum interval size of 100 kb, if adjacent windows are of the same genotype, they will be merged into one block. Each block is a recombination breakpoint [47]. Subsequently, the chromosomes of all individuals are divided into several regions, with each recombination breakpoint serving as a boundary. Each of these regions is referred to as a Bin. Finally, mstmap function (p.value = 1e-10) of R package ASMap [48] was uesd to construct a Bin labeled high-density genetic map.

The loci of FLW were mapped by composite interval mapping (CIM) using WinQTLCart 2.5 software (North Carolina State University, Raleigh, NC, USA) with.

a logarithm of odds ratio (LOD) ≥ 2.5 [49]. The threshold corresponding to 0.95 confidence was considered with a threshold set of 1000 times permutation test. QTL was named following the proposed by McCuch et al. [50].

Development of molecular markers

Based on the results of whole genome resequencing, the flanking sequences, 300 bp upstream and downstream of the loci with insertion deletion (InDel) between biparent across the mapping intervals of qFLW5-2, were downloaded from phytozome database (Sitalica_v2_2) and used to develop InDel markers (Table. S6).

Transcriptome data analysis

FL of Heizhigu and Changnong 35 were measured and sampled at heading and grain filling stages, respectively, with three biological replicates. RNA extraction and cDNA library construction was conducted by Biomarker Technologies (Beijing, China) according to standard procedures. The cDNA Libraries were sequenced on the Illumina HiSeq 2500 platform with paired-end 150-bp reads.

The raw data were subjected to strict quality control to gain clean data with Trimmomatic software. HISAT2 [51] software was used to align clean data with the Yugu 1 reference genome Setaria. italica (JGI_v2.2) and to obtain the localization information of reads on the reference genome. Then, StringTie [52] was applied to assemble the mapped reads and reconstruct the transcriptome for subsequent analysis. Fragments Per Kilobase of transcript per Million fragments mapped (FPKM) was used as an indicator to measure the transcript level. Finally, differential expression genes (DEGs) detection between different samples was conducted using DESeq2 software [53] with Fold Change ≥ 2 and FDR < 0.05 as screening criteria. Further functional analysis of DEGs was conducted through the Gene Ontology (GO), Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) and WGCNA.

Real-time quantitative PCR (qPCR)

cDNA was synthesized by reverse transcription using PrimeScript™ The 1 st Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit, and PCR primers were designed using Primer 5. PCR reaction was carried out using TBGreen Premium ExTap II reagent kit. The reaction system was 10 µL consisting of cDNA 1 µL, Primer 1 µL, TB Green Premium ExTap II 5 µL, ROX 0.2 µL, and ddH2O 2.8 µL; and the reaction procedure was as follows: 95℃ for 3 min, 95℃ for 10 s, 60℃ for 30 s, 72℃ for 30 s, 40 cycles. Foxtail millet ACTIN was an internal reference gene, and each sample has 3 replicates. The relative expression level of genes was calculated according to C = 2−△Ct (△Ct = Cttarget gene - CtACTIN).

Prediction of candidate genes

Based on QTL mapping and RNA-seq data, DEGs were analyzed for the target interval. Then, candidate genes controlling FLW were inferred based on gene function annotation, WGCNA, and gene expression pattern analysis. The sequence of candidate genes was obtained through Phytozome data (https://phytozome-next.jgi.doe.gov/) further to design PCR primers using Primer 5. The amplified products were sent to Beijing Tianyi Huiyuan Company for sequencing.

Sequence analysis and relative expression of candidate genes

We amplified approximately 2.9 kb of genomic sequences of Seita.5G134600 and Seita.5G123900, respectively (Table. S6), and PCR products were sequenced with Sanger sequencing by Beijing Tianyi Huiyuan Bioscience & Technology Inc. The comparison and analysis of the sequences was performed using DNAMAN 6.0. For detecting the relative expression differences of candidate genes in different lines, the flag leaves of five lines (RIL3_93, RIL3_93, RIL3_127, RIL3_204, RIL3_243) with narrow flag leaf and five lines (RIL3_5, RIL3_51, RIL3_59, RIL3_81, RIL3_223) with wide flag leaf were collected at grain filling stage, respectively.

Authors Response: Comments on “Tracheostomy in Children – Experience from a Tertiary Care Center in South India”
Authors: Seenivasan Subramani, Poovazhagi Varadarajan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractHow to cite this article: Subramani S, Varadarajan P. Authors Response: Comments on “Tracheostomy in Children – Experience from a Tertiary Care Center in South India”. Indian J Crit Care Med 2025;29(10):881.Keywords: Children, Complications, Tracheostomy

Dear Editor,

We thank you for forwarding the letter regarding our paper and for giving us the opportunity to reply. We are grateful to the authors for their keen interest in our recently published research paper, “Tracheostomy in Children: Experience from a Tertiary Care Center in South India”.1 We appreciate the valuable comments raised in the forwarded letter.

The exact cutoff date for tracheostomy in ventilated children is still not yet defined properly. Though there are a few studies in India which showed better outcomes in hospitalized children undergoing “early tracheostomy” less than 14 days, before planning tracheostomy, it is wise to consider the practical difficulties in managing tracheostomized children at home in resource-limited settings.2 We suggest further studies with a larger sample size to assess the long-term outcomes of tracheostomy in children and the challenges in post-tracheostomy care at home by parents in resource-limited settings. In our study population, only 27.2% (n = 37) children underwent tracheostomy by 14 days of ventilation. As the authors suggested, we also tried to analyze patients who were tracheostomized before (early) and after (late) 14 days of ventilation. However, we found that the early group consisted more of children with airway problems (such as Down's syndrome with atlantoaxial subluxation), severe traumatic brain injury, postsurgical posterior fossa tumors, myopathies, whereas the late group consisted more of neuromuscular illness (such as Guillain-Barré syndrome), pneumonia, status epilepticus and acute meningoencephalitis, and their baseline characteristics are not comparable. The authors felt that comparing the outcomes and complications in these two groups would be biased.

Secondly, we accept the authors' suggestion that it would have been better to evaluate the complications based on timing from tracheostomy. However, we did not do so in our study. Thirdly, we used cuffed endotracheal tubes in all ventilated children except in suspected/known airway pathology. We do not have facilities to monitor cuff pressure. However, we routinely deflate the cuff for 1 hour during each nursing shift (three times daily). Finally, the outcomes, complications, protocolized follow-up and caregiver challenges in tracheostomized children postdischarge are so extensive that they could be better published as a separate paper.

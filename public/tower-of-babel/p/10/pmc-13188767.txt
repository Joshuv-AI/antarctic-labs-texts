Efficient median estimation for stratified multi-population data: health services, medical workforce, and medical education
Authors: Umer Daraz, Hassan M Aljohani, Huda M Alshanbari
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper addresses the problem of robust median estimation in stratified two-phase sampling for the analysis of medical data, where population heterogeneity, skewness, and outliers commonly reduce estimator efficiency. In order to address these difficulties, we suggest an improved type of transformation based estimators aimed at making the estimations of central tendencies of medical measurements more stable and precise. The method is a strong mixture of central tendency measures and transformations, the Winsorized mean, Hodges Lehmann estimator, trimmed mean, and Gini mean difference to enhance the efficacy and preserving the extreme values resistance. With a first order Taylor series expansion, we get expressions of bias and mean squared error and optimal conditions of efficiency are obtained. The extensive Monte Carlo raises using gamma, lognormal, exponential, and Cauchy distributions, inspired by the common distributions in medical exposure data, and studies of actual medical data-sets all indicate that the proposed estimators have lower mean squared errors and higher percent relative efficiencies than previous ratio, difference estimators, and exponential estimators. These results highlight the practicality of transformation based techniques for obtaining reliable median estimates in medical research and applied sciences.Keywords: Medical data, Auxiliary information, Transformation, Stratification, Population median, Simulation, Mean squared error, Percent relative efficiency

Introduction and background

Reliable survey estimates are more difficult to obtain when the population is skewed or contains outliers. Therefore, more effort is required to obtain accurate estimates in survey research. In most cases, the arithmetic mean can be misleading, whereas the median provides a better measure of central tendency. Median-based analysis is particularly important, as it remains statistically robust in situations where extreme responses significantly influence average outcomes. Such domains include the investigation of biological species distribution, the evaluation of social policies, and the surveillance of computer network flows. Stratified two-phase sampling methods are useful extensions of traditional stratification techniques that provide a balance between survey accuracy and efficiency. After selecting the first-phase sample in each stratum, a second sub-sample is drawn to improve estimation accuracy. The ability of this approach to resist the effects of skewness and outliers enables more precise estimation of the finite population median. Practical evidence further supports this technique; for example, stratifying a population in medical studies by age or severity of illness increases the accuracy of estimating the median recovery time. This example reveal that stratified two phase sampling is an effective method of conducting survey research. Additional information about auxiliary data can be found in Cochran (1963) [1], Saerndal (1972) [2], and Alomair and Daraz (2024) [3].

The accurate use of supplementary data has contributed significantly to the enhancement of the accuracy of median estimation. The role of auxiliary variables in the selection of efficient estimators was first highlighted by foundational studies by Sedransk and Meyer (1978) [4], Gross (1980) [5], and Philip and Sedransk (1983) [6]. Regression- and ratio-based formulations by Kuk (1989) [7] and Rao (1991) [8] subsequently enhanced accuracy. In order to overcome incomplete auxiliary data, Singh et al. (2001) [9] and Khoshnevisan et al. (2002) [10] introduced generalized estimators that used several auxiliary variables and double-sampling procedures. A class of median-type estimators based on two auxiliary variables was introduced by Gupta et al. (2008) [11], and the inclusion of known auxiliary medians allowed the construction of unbiased estimators by Singh (2003) [12]. A generalized class of modified median estimators was proposed by Aladag et al. (2015) [13]. Later research utilizing both simple and stratified sampling methodologies, discussed by Solanki and Singh (2015) [14], has advanced this research topic and highlighted the practical significance of auxiliary data in enhancing median estimation. To obtain accurate estimates of population parameters, a new class of exponential estimators under stratified random sampling was proposed by Daraz et al. (2025) [15].

In the past few decades, researchers have developed a variety of transformation-based and non-parametric methods to improve the robustness of median estimators in skewed or non-normal situations. The accuracy of the results and the minimization of the effect of outliers are possible through the use of auxiliary information to generate consistent results across a range of population patterns. Recent studies have implemented Bayesian methods, changes in calibration, and exponential transformation models to improve accuracy without raising the cost of data collection, which are useful in practice, e.g., in industry, agriculture, and economics. In-depth analyses of the theoretical development, improvements in efficiency, and recent developments in the field of median estimation can be found in Shabbir and Gupta (2017) [16], Irfan et al. (2021) [17], and Shabbir et al. (2022) [18].

Conventional estimators fail to provide consistent results when the population data is skewed or asymmetric. They may be abnormal and irregularly distributed because they depend on assumptions of normality and data similarity. In an attempt to overcome these shortcomings, the present study, motivated by Daraz et al. (2025) [15], introduces a set of estimators based on transformations constructed under a two-phase stratified random sampling plan. The framework improves the quality of estimates by combining the robustness of an effective central tendency parameter with the efficiency of auxiliary information collected during the first phase of the estimation process. This combination provides a useful and credible tool for contemporary survey research, particularly in situations where complex populations often violate theoretical assumptions. The inclusion of stratified Winsorized mean, Hodges-Lehmann location, trimmed mean, Gini mean difference, median ratio, geometric quartile mean, Moor’s kurtosis, quartile deviation, skewness-adjusted, and median absolute deviation transformations reflects a balance of robustness, resistance to extreme values, and practical applicability. The proposed strategy works especially well with heterogeneous data, such as agricultural outcomes influenced by climatic conditions, production systems with varying defect rates, or academic data with unequal distributions. In addition to its usefulness, this class of estimators represents an advancement in the literature, as it attains high precision, remains stable in skewed populations, and produces consistent results, as confirmed by simulation and practical experimentation. It is also inexpensive and adaptable to different research disciplines due to its simple structure.

Justification of stratification rule and practical implications

Justification of stratification rule

The stratification in all three real populations was constructed based on the median of the auxiliary variable X within each population. Specifically, districts (or institutions) were divided into two strata according to whether their auxiliary value was above or below the population median of X. This rule was adopted for the following reasons:

The auxiliary variables (number of hospitals, number of nurses, and teaching staff strength) are strongly associated with the study variables (patients treated, registered doctors, and student enrollment, respectively). Therefore, stratifying on X increases within–stratum homogeneity in Y.

Conclusion and future perspectives

This study introduced an improved class of median-type estimators based on a novel transformation under a stratified double sampling scheme. We analyzed the properties of this estimator family and verified the bias and MSE formulas through both simulation studies and real population data. Results in Tables 3, 4, 5 and 6 demonstrate that the proposed estimators consistently outperform traditional ratio, regression, difference, and exponential estimators, particularly in skewed, heavy-tailed, or outlier-affected populations. The use of auxiliary information through transformation under a stratified two-phase design enhances both the stability and accuracy of median estimation while minimizing data collection costs. These improvements highlight the potential of transformation-based approaches to address challenges commonly encountered in practical survey settings.

The method is highly flexible, as the second-phase sample can be substantially smaller than the first-phase sample, making it especially suitable for large-scale surveys or field studies where full auxiliary information is unavailable due to budgetary or logistical constraints. Its ability to improve estimation accuracy without requiring additional data collection increases its practical value in socioeconomic, agricultural, and health statistics. The approach also provides a framework for combining complex data structures efficiently, which is particularly useful in studies involving skewed or incomplete data.

Future research may extend this framework to spatial and multistage sampling schemes, add multiple auxiliary variables, and explore adaptive or nonlinear transformation functions. Addressing nonresponse and missing data mechanisms within the transformation framework can further maintain estimator efficiency under incomplete data. Additionally, developing computational tools or open-source implementations enables broader application in practice. Overall, the proposed transformation-based estimators offer a robust, cost-effective, and flexible approach, providing a solid foundation for future developments in modern survey methodology and median estimation.

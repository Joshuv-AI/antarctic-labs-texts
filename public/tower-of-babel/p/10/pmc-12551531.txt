Protocol for the FibroCAN study: a randomised controlled trial of finerenone treatment for early-stage cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy in type 2 diabetes
Authors: Maria Bitsch Poulsen, Tina Okdahl, Jens Hove Buciek, Asbjørn Mohr Drewes, Pall Karlsson, Tarunveer Singh Ahluwalia, Birgitte Brock, Christina Brock, Peter Rossing, Christian Stevns Hansen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionCardiovascular autonomic neuropathy (CAN) is a serious, untreatable complication of diabetes that contributes to excess cardiovascular mortality and morbidity. CAN is associated with increased fibrosis and inflammation, possibly driven by increased sympathetic activity and overactive mineralocorticoid receptors (MRs). These may represent a potential therapeutic target. MR antagonists (MRAs) improve autonomic function in non-diabetic diseases, and finerenone, a non-steroidal MRA, has demonstrated promising results in managing diabetic kidney disease and cardiovascular complications, suggesting its potential as a novel therapy for early-stage CAN. This trial aims to evaluate whether daily administration of finerenone can modify the disease progression of early-stage CAN.Methods and analysisThis trial is a two-centre, double-blind, parallel-group, 1:1 randomised, placebo-controlled study evaluating the effect of 78 weeks of intervention with finerenone or placebo on early-stage CAN in 100 individuals with type 2 diabetes in Denmark. The primary endpoint is the between-group difference in the expiration:inspiration ratio of the cardiovascular autonomic reflex tests (CARTs). Secondary endpoints are the between-group differences in the remaining CARTs, heart rate variability measures and fibrosis markers. Treatment effects on other forms of neuropathy and related pathological mechanisms will be explored.Ethics and disseminationThe study complies with the Declaration of Helsinki and the International Counsil for Harmonisation good clinical practice guidelines, with ethics approval obtained from the Danish Medical Research Ethics Committee. All participants will provide written informed consent. Due to the risk of hyperkalaemia associated with finerenone, safety will be closely monitored throughout the study. Findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications, conference presentations and clinical trial registries. A lay summary will be provided to participants on study completion.Trial registration numberClinicalTrials.gov: NCT06906081; registration date: 25 March 2025. Clinical Trials Information System: EUCT no. 2024-516597-30-00; registration date: 3 September 2024.

Introduction

Background and rationale

Diabetic neuropathy is a severe and untreatable complication of diabetes. One of the most severe manifestations is cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy (CAN), with a prevalence of approximately 20%.1 Given the global diabetes population of 589 million,2 CAN is estimated to affect up to 117 million worldwide. CAN is an independent predictor of all-cause mortality and morbidities such as cardiovascular events and nephropathy.36 This contributes to the excess mortality from cardiovascular diseases in diabetes.7 Such comorbidities are a substantial burden on an individual and at the socioeconomic level.8 Thus, there is a great potential benefit in the prevention of CAN.

Certain forms of neuropathy, such as the early stages of CAN, are reversible in contrast to the later stages.9 10 Therefore, timely reversal and prevention of progression to the late stages of CAN are paramount. Previous trials have failed to show the disease-modifying effects on CAN, possibly due to short durations and the inclusion of patients with late and irreversible stages of CAN.11 12 CAN is a slow-progressing condition that requires long-term intervention studies to assess the treatment efficacy. Early-stage CAN is characterised by diminished parasympathetic capacity with sympathetic compensation. Moreover, the risk of developing CAN, as well as impaired nerve function, is associated with high serum levels of fibrosis markers, for example, collagen type VI formation marker (PRO-6C) and collagen type III degration marker (C3M).1316 Increased sympathetic drive, typically present in the early stages of CAN, has been associated with overactive mineralocorticoid receptor (MR) pathways that initiate pro-inflammatory and pro-fibrotic gene transcription.17 18 This highlights the increased formation of tissue fibrosis as a plausible part of the pathogenesis of CAN. MR antagonists (MRAs) have shown disease-modifying effects and improved survival in heart failure with reduced ejection fraction and in resistant hypertension (Finerenone Trial to Investigate Efficacy and Safety Superior to Placebo in Patients With Heart Failure (FINEARTS-HF)).19 Trials in diabetic kidney disease using steroidal MRAs have been precluded by hyperkalaemia. However, non-steroidal MRA shows less hyperkalaemia with reduced kidney disease progression and improved cardiovascular outcomes in type 2 diabetes. In addition, studies with MRA suggested a beneficial impact on autonomic dysfunction.2026 This suggests that by using MRAs to inhibit the pro-inflammatory and pro-fibrotic pathways, the cellular effect of overactive mineral receptors could be mitigated, thereby inhibiting the progression of CAN as illustrated in figure 1. Finerenone, a third-generation MRA, is a recently approved and well-tolerated treatment for diabetic kidney disease that has also shown promising effects on cardiovascular complications.27 Thus, finerenone might constitute a novel treatment option for early-stage CAN with parasympathetic dysfunction.

The proposed effect of mineralocorticoid blockage with finerenone on cardiovascular autonomic neuropathy, that is, the brain-heart axis (left). Activation of the mineralocorticoid receptor (MR) with a mineralocorticoid, for example, cortisol or aldosterone (green dots), leads to the transcription of pro-inflammatory and pro-fibrotic genes in the nucleus, causing inflammation and fibrosis in neurons and cardiomyocytes. The MR antagonist finerenone (yellow dots) is expected to inhibit these processes, reducing inflammation and fibrosis, thus preserving tissue structure and function.

We hypothesise that treatment with finerenone is efficacious in improving autonomic function in type 2 diabetes, specifically by increasing the primarily parasympathetic-driven cardiovascular autonomic reflex test (CART) expiration:inspiration ratio (E:I ratio) and reversing early-stage CAN. We also expect that the treatment will improve heart rate variability measures and the other CARTs, with treatment effects being associated with a reduction in fibrosis levels.

Objective

This study aims to investigate if daily administration of finerenone has a disease-modifying effect on early-stage CAN in type 2 diabetes. The autonomic treatment effects will be evaluated by CARTs, heart rate variability, cardiac vagal tone, orthostatic blood pressure, sudomotor function and self-reported symptoms of autonomic dysfunction. Peripheral nervous system treatment effects are assessed by sural nerve conduction, vibration sensation threshold, touch/pain/cold/warm sensation, ankle/patella reflexes, intraepidermal nerve fibre density, retinopathy, corneal neuropathy and self-reported symptoms of peripheral neuropathy. The systemic treatment effects are assessed as serum levels of fibrosis markers and pro-inflammatory cytokines.

Methods and analysis

Patient and public involvement

Prior to submission in the Clinical Trials Information System, the study was presented to the patient panel at Steno Diabetes Center Copenhagen. Their insights and feedback were carefully considered to ensure the study design aligned with patient perspectives and priorities.

Trial design and setting

This study is a two-centre investigator-initiated, double-blind, parallel-group, placebo-controlled trial evaluating the effect of 78 weeks of 1:1 randomised intervention with finerenone or placebo on early-stage CAN. A hundred people with type 2 diabetes and early-stage CAN will be included. The E:I ratio is often the first to be affected. Thus, early-stage CAN is defined by a pathological E:I ratio, with the other CARTs remaining normal, that is, postural and Valsalva response. An exclusively pathological E:I ratio may represent reversible CAN. This study will be conducted at Steno Diabetes Center Copenhagen, Denmark, and Steno Diabetes Center North Denmark, Denmark.

Intervention and comparator

Patients with type 2 diabetes and a pathological E:I ratio will receive oral treatment with up to 40 mg finerenone or matched placebo tablets once daily. Placebo tablets, identical in appearance to the active tablets, will serve as the comparator. The starting dose depends on the baseline estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR); if eGFR>60 mL/min/1.73 m2, the starting dose is 20 mg, and 10 mg if eGFR<60 mL/min/1.73 m2. After 1 month, if potassium is ≤4.8 mmol/L and treatment is tolerated, the dose will be increased. The target dose is 40 mg for patients with baseline eGFR>60 mL/min/1.73 m2 and 20 mg for those with baseline eGFR<60 mL/min/1.73 m2.

Potassium is measured regularly during the trial to ensure patient safety. If potassium levels reach 4.8–5.5 mmol/L, the dose will be lowered, and levels rechecked in 4 weeks. If the participant is already on the lowest dose, the medication will be paused and restarted when the potassium level is <4.8 mmol/L. For levels >5.5 mmol/L, the medication is paused, and potassium is rechecked within 72 hours. If potassium reaches ≥6.0 mmol/L or higher, hospitalisation is required. Recurrent potassium elevations >5.5 mmol/L on the lowest dose will result in permanent discontinuation. Investigators may adjust the dose if participants cannot tolerate the maximum level. The investigator may reduce or pause the study drug for medical reasons and will document the rationale accordingly.

During this trial, the patients will continue their scheduled visits with the general practitioner and/or the outpatient clinics and receive treatment deemed necessary by the responsible physician according to the usual standard of care and clinical guidelines. Changes in the medication will be recorded in the electronic case report form (eCRF) at each visit. In addition, the occurrence of pregnancy, cancer or Addison’s disease during the trial or, if deemed necessary by the investigator due to medical reasons or severe non-compliance with the protocol, will result in withdrawal from the study.

Outcomes

The primary endpoint is the between-group difference in the E:I ratio after 78 weeks of intervention. The secondary endpoints are the between-group differences in CARTs, heart rate variability parameters and fibrosis markers in serum and skin. Exploratory endpoints are the between-group differences in inflammation markers, corneal nerve fibre density, retinopathy, cardiac vagal tone, sudomotor function, orthostatic blood pressure, self-reported symptoms of autonomic dysfunction, gastroparesis and painful or less severe neuropathy, as well as the following markers of peripheral neuropathy: sural nerve conduction, vibration sensation threshold, touch/pain/cold/warm sensation, ankle/patella reflexes and intraepidermal nerve fibre density (skin biopsy). Between-group differences will be reported as absolute, relative or baseline-corrected values.

Data collection methods

Potential participants will be invited to an information meeting, during which informed consent will be obtained. At the subsequent screening visit, eligibility criteria will be reviewed, demographic and medical history will be recorded and a blood sample will be collected.

All outcome assessments and data collection will be performed by trained study personnel, and all assessors will undergo standardised training. A complete overview of procedures is provided in the online supplemental materials. Data will be entered into eCRFs with built-in validation rules to minimise entry errors and promote data quality. All patients will be closely monitored throughout the trial, and to enhance participant retention, reminders of upcoming visits will be provided, regular staff members will be available at the sites and flexible meeting times will be offered. Study visits will have a scheduling window of ±5 days to promote participant retention. For instance, the week 4 visit corresponds to day 28±5 days, with day 28 being the preferred visit date whenever feasible. If a participant misses a scheduled visit without prior notice, every effort will be made to re-establish contact.

Cardiovascular autonomic reflex testing

CARTs are the gold standard for diagnosing CAN28 and are a test series to evaluate the integrity of these reflexes. The reflexes are activated by performing a postural change, a one-minute cycle of deep breathing and a Valsalva manoeuvre, while measuring ECG to determine the RR-interval (time between two consecutive R-wave peaks) response to the tests. The Vagus device (Medicus Engineering, Aarhus, Denmark) will be used to perform the tests, which record standard lead I ECG via the hands. Age-dependent cut-off values will be applied to determine the CAN status.29 A swift postural change from lying to standing causes a drop in blood pressure followed by an increase in heart rate and vasoconstriction to re-establish blood pressure, that is, a sympathetic response. The maximal decrease in RR intervals is observed approximately at the 15th beat after standing, and the maximal increase in RR intervals at the 30th beat is quantified as the 30:15 ratio. The respiratory frequency of deep breathing exaggerates the naturally occurring respiratory sinus arrhythmia, that is, short RR- intervals during inspiration and long RR-intervals during expiration, and the ability to vary the RR-intervals can be expressed as the ratio between the expiration length and the inspiration length (E:I ratio). Deep breathing is primarily a vagal phenomenon confirmed with atropinisation.30 The E:I ratio is the primary endpoint of this trial and will be repeated three times to increase validity. The Valsalva manoeuvre is performed by blowing into a mouthpiece for 15 s while maintaining a pressure of 40 mmHg. This leads to an initial decrease in heart rate followed by tachycardia, which ends with rebound bradycardia shortly after the blow. The Valsalva response is quantified as the ratio between the longest RR-interval (rebound) and the shortest RR-interval during the blow. The Valsalva manoeuvre does not respond to atropinisation; thus, the cardiovascular dynamics of the procedure are mainly driven by the sympathetic nervous system.30

Heart rate variability and cardiac vagal tone

Classic heart rate variability measures will be derived from the resting five minute ECG during CARTs, such as the RR-interval, the SD of normal to normal RR (NN)-intervals, the SD of the average NN-intervals, the mean of the SD of all the NN-intervals, the root mean square of successive NN-interval differences, very low-frequency (LF) power, LF power, high-frequency power and total power. A five minute measure of the efferent parasympathetic activity, cardiac vagal tone, will be obtained concomitantly (eMotion Faros, Mega Electronics, Kuopio, Finland) during the resting electrocardiography.

Biochemistry and molecular sampling

Blood and urine sampling will be performed at all visits. The baseline biochemical profile includes haemoglobin A1c (HbA1c), lipids, eGFR, creatine, potassium, sodium, alanine aminotransferase, C-reactive protein, vitamin B12, Hb, blood glucose and urine albumin/creatine ratio. The follow-up visit biochemical profile includes HbA1c, eGFR, creatine, potassium, blood glucose and urine albumin/creatine ratio. Inflammation markers (Olink, Uppsala, Sweden) and fibrosis markers, for example, serum PRO-C3 and PRO-C6 (Nordic Bioscience, Herlev, Denmark), will be obtained from the collected material. As a safety measure, potassium and eGFR tests will be performed regularly (see figure 2) and in response to a change in medication dosage (after 4 and 20 weeks).

Participant timeline.

Sudomotor function

Peripheral small-fibre autonomic function will be assessed with an electrochemical skin conduction test on the hands and feet using the Sudoscan (Impeto Medical, Paris, France). Age-stratified and gender-stratified thresholds will be applied.31

Orthostatic blood pressure testing

The presence of orthostatic hypotension, that is, sympathetic failure, will be determined with the UA-787 Plus (A and D Medical, California, USA). After 10 minutes of rest, lying blood pressure will be obtained three times and averaged. The participant will shift to standing, and blood pressure will be measured for each minute for five minutes. Orthostatic hypotension is defined as (1) a systolic blood pressure <90 mmHg during standing, (2) a decline of ≥20 mmHg in systolic blood pressure compared with lying or (3) a decline of ≥10 mmHg in diastolic blood pressure compared with lying pressure.32

Nerve conduction velocity

The NC-Stat DPNCheck (NeuroMetrix, Waltham, Massachusetts, USA) will be used to assess nerve conduction velocity and amplitude of the sural nerve on each leg, repeated three times each. Age-stratified and height-stratified thresholds will be applied to categorise.33

Vibration perception threshold

The vibration perception threshold will be measured with the Bio-Thesiometer (Bio-Medical Instruments, Ohio, USA) at the distal tip of the first toe on both feet. It will be repeated three times at each location. Both a cut-off >25 V and age-specific, sex-specific and height-specific values will be applied.34 35

Light touch/pain sensation

The Neuropen 10 (Owen Mumford, Oxford, UK) will be used to assess touch and pain sensation in several locations on both the dorsal and plantar regions of the feet, repeated three times at each location.28 Diabetic polyneuropathy (DPN) is the absence of sensation in the feet. DPN will be defined as no sensation at any tested location.

Cold/warm sensation

Thermal sensation (25°C+40°C) will be assessed by bilaterally applying metal rolls on the dorsal region of the foot and the anterior part of the lower legs with the Rolltemp-II (Somedic SenseLab AB, Sösdala, Sweden).36 Abnormal sensation is defined as bilateral abnormalities at the toes, either with or without an abnormal sensation of the foot and leg.

Ankle/patella reflexes

The presence of ankle and patella reflexes will be evaluated on both sides with a reflex hammer.

Self-reported symptoms of painful and painless peripheral neuropathy

The Michigan Neuropathy Screening Instrument will be used to assess distal symmetrical peripheral neuropathy with a cut-off of ≥4,37 combined with the Douleur Neuropathique with a cut-off of ≥4 to assess painful peripheral neuropathy.38 The Toronto Consensus Criteria will categorise DPN into possible, probable, definite and subclinical.39

Self-reported symptoms of autonomic dysfunction and gastroparesis

The validated Danish version of the Composite Autonomic Symptom Scale 31, covering six domains, will be used to evaluate self-reported symptoms of autonomic dysfunction, with a cut-off of ≥16.40 41 In addition, symptoms of gastroparesis will be evaluated with the Gastroparesis Cardinal Symptom Index with a≥2.6 cut-off.42

Intraepidermal nerve fibre density

To assess intraepidermal nerve fibre density, skin biopsies will be obtained from the leg 10 cm proximal to the lateral malleolus, and the density will be quantified according to international guidelines.43 The biopsies will be immunostained for fibrosis markers, including, but not limited to, PRO-C6 and C3M. Analyses will be performed at the Danish Pain Research Center, Aarhus University, Aarhus, Denmark.

Corneal confocal microscopy

Corneal confocal microscopy will be used to assess the nerve density in the cornea as a measure of neuropathy using the Rostock Cornea Module on the HRT3 platform (Heidelberg Engineering, Heidelberg, Germany). The following configuration parameters will be assessed: corneal nerve fibre density, corneal nerve branch density, corneal nerve fibre length, central sub-basal nerve plexus and inferior whorl length. Moreover, to evaluate the corneal inflammatory response, the following cell parameters will be quantified: dendritic cells in contact with nerve fibre, dendritic cells not in contact with nerve fibre, non-dendritic cells in contact with nerve fibre and non-dendritic cells not in contact with nerve fibre.

Optical coherence tomography

Fundus photo grading and optical coherence tomograhy (OCT) (Topcon DRI OCT Triton/Topcon 3D OCT-2000, Topcon Corporation, Tokyo, Japan) will be performed to evaluate the degree of retinopathy. OCT images the layers of the retina and measures their thickness, both the overall average and from each quadrant.

Harms

Expected and unexpected adverse events and adverse reactions will be registered from the first dose until 24 hours after the last oral administration, corresponding to eight plasma half-lives. A trial-associated medical doctor will evaluate whether it is an adverse reaction or a serious adverse event/reaction. These will be reported to the sponsor within 24 hours in case they are considered a suspected unexpected serious adverse reaction, and the sponsor will report the event to the relevant authorities no later than 8 days after the sponsor has been notified. In addition, the sponsor will report fatal or life-threatening events in the EudraVigilance database as soon as possible and no later than 7 days after notification. Adverse events will be followed up at the next visit, and in case of a dropout, the adverse event will be followed up by telephone after 1 month. A safety report will be submitted annually to the Clinical Trials Information System. To enhance patient safety, regular monitoring of potassium is integrated to ensure medication tolerability.

Sample size

Treatment with 50 mg spironolactone has been shown to increase resting heart rate variability by 2%–45% in patients with congestive heart failure.20 21 A study at Steno Diabetes Center Copenhagen reported an E:I ratio of 1.13±0.2 for those with early CAN. We expect 40 mg of finerenone to have modest efficacy on the primary outcome (E:I ratio) with a 15% increase in the E:I ratio. Thus, to detect a 15% difference with a power of 90% at a two-sided significance level of 0.05, 37 participants should be allocated to each treatment arm. However, to account for dropouts and inaccuracies in assumptions, 50 participants will be recruited for each treatment arm, that is, 100 in total. Dropouts will not be replaced.

Recruitment

Participants will be recruited by the treatment-responsible doctor or study personnel affiliated with Steno Diabetes Center Copenhagen or Steno Diabetes Center North Denmark. They will be identified from business intelligence-generated reports based on relevant International Classification of Diseases 10th Revision codes or from cohorts of previous diabetes studies conducted at the two study sites, where participants have consented to be contacted regarding research projects. Moreover, a recruitment poster placed in the primary sector, outpatient clinics, patient associations and health community centres will help raise awareness about the trial.

Randomisation

After inclusion, participants will be randomised 1:1 in blocks of four. The randomisation list will be generated by the Hospital Pharmacy of The Capital Region of Denmark using sealedenvelope.com. Finerenone and a matching placebo will be manufactured by Bayer A/S (Copenhagen, Denmark), according to Annex 13 of the Good Manufacturing Practice guidelines of the European Commission, Internationational Counsil for Harmonization good clinical practice (ICH-GCP) guidelines, and delivered to the Hospital Pharmacy of The Capital Region of Denmark. The Hospital Pharmacy will coordinate the randomisation process and allocation concealment. At each study site, two designated unblinded staff members will be responsible for dispensing the investigational medication. They will verify the correct dose and number of containers. The investigator remains responsible for ensuring the correct treatment is administered.

Blinding

This study is double-blinded, and the hospital pharmacy will distribute the study medication to the study sites, ensuring that neither study personnel nor participants have access to treatment allocation. Only the unblinded dispensing personnel will have access to treatment allocation, and they will not participate in any other study-related procedures. The safety potassium tests may bias the blinding; however, it is necessary to ensure patient safety. In the case of urgent medical care, unblinding of the treatment is available at each study site in sealed envelopes (provided by the Hospital Pharmacy). The unblinding will be performed by a dedicated person at each study site who is not otherwise involved in the study or by the principal investigator.

Data management

All data will be stored in eCRFs using REDCap (Research Electronic Data Capture), or the electronic or physical trial master file. All forms are filled out during or immediately after the participant’s assessment. Errors and corrections are logged as provided by the REDCap interface. When data has been entered, reviewed and verified, it will be frozen to prevent editing. Data will be stored for 25 years after the study’s termination under the responsibility of the principal investigator. All sensitive personal data will be protected under the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR).

Statistical methods

The treatment efficacy on the primary endpoint will be analysed using a linear mixed-effect model, with visit and treatment as fixed factors and patient-specific random intercepts to account for the within-patient correlation inherent in repeated measures. The primary analysis will be performed as intention-to-treat, including all subjects receiving at least one dose of study medication and subsequent analysis as per protocol. Additional outcomes will be assessed as between-group (finerenone vs placebo) differences from baseline to 78 week treatment. An interim analysis will not be performed during the trial.

Trial and data monitoring

This trial will be conducted in accordance with ICH-GCP guidelines, and the independent Good Clinical Practice (GCP) units at Copenhagen University and Aalborg University Hospital will monitor this trial. The GCP unit will conduct periodic monitoring with access to all source material, including eCRF, medical records, trial master file, etc. The GCP monitor will ensure that the collected data is valid, complete and well-documented. A separate data monitoring committee is not considered necessary, as safety and data integrity oversight will be provided by the independent GCP units. These units operate independently of the study team and ensure rigorous monitoring in accordance with regulatory standards.

In the event of an audit by the sponsor, the GCP units, the Medical Research Ethics Committee, the Danish Medicines Agency or other regulatory controller(s), direct access to source data and documents will be granted.

Trial coordination will be led by Steno Diabetes Center Copenhagen, where the sponsor-investigator is located. Ad hoc support will be available on a weekly basis by the study coordinator at Steno Diabetes Center Copenhagen, and meetings can be arranged as needed to coordinate and streamline activities at the two centres.

This trial was submitted via the Clinical Trials Information System and approved by the Danish Medicines Agency and the Medical Research Ethics Committee. The trial will be conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki (amended by the 64th World Medical Assocation General Assembly, Fortaleza, Brazil, October 2013) and ICH-GCP guidelines.

Dissemination policy

When the trial is terminated, the principal investigator will inform the Danish Medicines Agency and the Medical Research Ethics Committee. Trial results will be published in open-access peer-reviewed scientific journals irrespective of positive, negative or inconclusive results. Authorship will be appointed according to the Vancouver recommendations. Participants will be invited to an informational meeting at the conclusion of the trial.

Protocol amendments

Substantial modifications to the protocol will be submitted as protocol amendments to the regulatory authorities and implemented when approved.

Consent or assent

Potential participants will be informed both in writing and orally about the trial by trained study personnel. The participants will be informed thoroughly about the purpose, specific procedures, potential benefits and risks, and the possibility of withdrawing at any time without giving a reason and without consequences for future treatment. Before consenting to participation, participants are encouraged to consider their decision for 24 hours. Participants confirm their willingness to participate by signing the informed consent form, after which they are allowed to enter the trial if the eligibility criteria are met. A medical doctor will perform the inclusion of participants. After providing informed consent, the participants will be asked if they want to contribute blood, urine and skin samples to a biobank for future research, with a separate consent form.

Confidentiality

All personal information about potential and enrolled participants will be handled in accordance with the European Union GDPR. Data will be collected using eCRFs within a secure, password-protected database (REDCap). Only authorised members of the research team will have access to identifiable information. After trial completion, personal data will be retained only as long as necessary to meet regulatory requirements.

Ancillary and post-trial care

All participants will undergo a post-treatment follow-up by telephone 4 weeks (±5 days) after their last study visit to record new and ongoing adverse events. In case of harm from trial participation, the Danish Patient Compensation will cover the participants. Post-trial access to finerenone will not be available for CAN, but if the results of this trial are positive, confirmatory trials designed to establish treatment guidelines will be warranted.

Data sharing

Consent to share data in a public data repository is not obtained. Thus, data will only be available to the study centres and will be shared between study centres by direct transfer. Data will be made available for external researchers on reasonable request.

Discussion

Type 2 diabetes is a complex condition that not only requires treatment of the underlying aetiology (including glycaemic control), insulin resistance and reduced insulin production but also requires treatment of the many diabetes-induced complications. This trial will investigate a novel treatment for early-stage CAN, a complication that in later stages is related to the high prevalence of cardiovascular deaths in diabetes.44 This trial aims to determine if finerenone has a disease-modifying effect on early-stage CAN by preventing the progression to late irreversible stages. No disease-modifying treatment is currently available, meaning individuals affected by CAN are limited to risk factor control, that is, lowering hyperglycaemia, obesity and dyslipidaemia, in combination with symptomatic treatment of cardiovascular disorders. Thus, a randomised controlled trial investigating a new treatment modality targets an unmet need.

Fibrosis has been linked to neuropathy, as both clinical and preclinical studies have associated fibrosis markers, such as collagen turnover markers, with impaired nerve function, highlighting their potential as indicators of fibrotic activity in diabetic neuropathy.131545 It is hypothesised that finerenone can inhibit the formation of fibrous connective tissue in the extracellular matrix of the vagus nerve and heart, improving cardiovascular autonomic function, as has been described in models of heart failure and chronic kidney disease.46 47 However, given the inaccessibility of these tissues, fibrosis levels in serum and skin will serve as proxy measures. Since neuropathy is associated with collagen turnover, these surrogate markers aim to provide insight into the extracellular matrix remodelling and the therapeutic impact of finerenone on autonomic and cardiac function. Moreover, finerenone’s mechanisms of action, which extend beyond reno-cardiovascular-specific pathways, are likely to confer benefits to the peripheral nervous system as well.

One of the major strengths of this trial, beyond the novel intervention, is its long intervention period, potentially allowing sufficient time for the recovery of nerve function or even nerve regrowth. Preclinical models have suggested that nerve regeneration and functional improvement may require a period of 1–12 weeks, depending on the extent of neuropathy and the intervention.4851 However, these observations have not been conclusively validated in human studies, making this trial pivotal in evaluating the timeline and effectiveness of treatment for neuropathy in clinical settings.

The 78 week intervention period may pose challenges in maintaining compliance throughout the trial. Since the treated condition plausibly is asymptomatic during the trial, participants may not perceive any noticeable effects from the intervention. This could increase the risk of dropouts in a lengthy trial, and to mitigate this limitation, we have accounted for a 26% dropout rate in the sample size estimation. Moreover, the strict inclusion criteria requiring a pathological E:I ratio may pose a considerable challenge to recruitment efforts. However, by including patients with reversible stages of neuropathy, the likelihood of showing disease-modifying capacity is increased. In Denmark, CAN status is only accessible to individuals who have participated in studies with the CARTs procedure, as these tests are not routine clinical practice. Fortunately, both study centres have previously conducted studies of CAN, and the two-centre structure has been chosen to ensure sufficient recruitment. To evaluate CAN status, the Vagus device was selected for its practicality and reproducibility, even though more comprehensive laboratory-based assessments may offer greater technical accuracy. This balances precision with feasibility in a clinical trial.

If successful, this trial would position finerenone as the first disease-modifying treatment for early-stage CAN. Its primary impact could be a significant reduction in cardiovascular morbidity and mortality associated with diabetes. Importantly, it may also enhance screening and diagnostic practices for CAN; the availability of effective treatment is likely to motivate clinicians to test for and diagnose this condition more actively. Moreover, since finerenone is not diabetes-specific, it may have applications in conditions where glucose dysregulation is not the primary cause of CAN.

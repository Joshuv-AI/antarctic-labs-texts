Endovascular Coil Embolization of Ruptured Idiopathic Multiple Bilateral Pulmonary Artery Aneurysms: A Case Report
Authors: Abdullah S Almawi, Mohammed S Almasri, Fahad A Almejrad, Mohammed N Almusaad, Maher Alanazi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTPulmonary artery aneurysms are rare vascular abnormalities with a diverse etiologies, including infection, vasculitis, trauma, and congenital disorders. Untreated aneurysms carry a substantial risk of rupture and mortality, particularly in symptomatic individuals. We report a case of a 24‐year‐old man who presented with hemoptysis and progressive shortness of breath over 1 month. Computed tomography angiography revealed multiple bilateral saccular aneurysms of the pulmonary arteries, one of which was ruptured. Subsequently, urgent endovascular embolization of all aneurysms using detachable coils was performed. The procedure was technically successful and uneventful, resulting in marked clinical improvement. This case illustrates a rare presentation of idiopathic bilateral pulmonary artery aneurysms with concurrent rupture and supports endovascular embolization as a safe and effective treatment option in such cases.Keywords: detachable coils, embolization, endovascular procedures, pulmonary artery, therapeutic, vascular anomalies

Introduction

Pulmonary artery aneurysms (PAAs) are exceedingly rare pulmonary vascular abnormalities, with an estimated incidence of 0.007% in autopsy series [1]. They are defined as focal dilatations involving all three layers of the arterial wall and may occur at any level of the pulmonary arterial tree. PAAs may be congenital or acquired. Congenital forms are commonly associated with congenital heart disease and connective tissue disorders, whereas acquired PAAs are most often related to trauma, vasculitis, or pulmonary hypertension [2]. Idiopathic PAAs represent a distinct subgroup occurring in the absence of identifiable risk factors. The rarity of this condition and the lack of standardised management guidelines continue to pose diagnostic and therapeutic challenges, particularly in life‐threatening cases [3].

This report describes a rare case of multiple bilateral pulmonary artery aneurysms with rupture in a young adult presenting with worsening hemoptysis, successfully managed with endovascular coil embolization.

Case Report

A 24‐year‐old male presented with a one‐month history of hemoptysis that had markedly worsened over the preceding days, accompanied by progressive shortness of breath. On examination, he appeared acutely ill and in respiratory distress. He was afebrile (37.2°C), tachypneic (28 breaths/min), and hypoxic, with an oxygen saturation of 88% on room air. Chest examination revealed markedly reduced bilateral air entry without focal crackles or wheezing. Laboratory investigations revealed anaemia (haemoglobin: 9.2 g/dL) and leukocytosis (White Blood Cell (WBC) count: 14.22 × 103/μL). Computed tomography angiography (CTA) of the chest showed no evidence of acute pulmonary embolism but demonstrated multiple bilateral saccular pulmonary artery aneurysms, including a ruptured aneurysm with associated pulmonary haemorrhage (Figure 1).

Computed tomography angiography (CTA) of the thorax. (A, B) Axial images demonstrate a large heterogeneous mass with blood attenuation in the right lower lung, measuring 12 × 8.5 cm representing a ruptured pulmonary artery aneurysm (white arrow). (C, D) Coronal and sagittal images demonstrate three saccular aneurysms arising from the left segmental pulmonary arteries (yellow arrows), with the largest measuring 3 × 2.4 cm.

Further investigations were performed to evaluate potential infectious and autoimmune causes. Interferon‐gamma release assay (IGRA) was intermediate, acid‐fast bacilli (AFB) smear was negative, and urine, sputum, and blood cultures showed no bacterial or fungal growth. Inflammatory markers were elevated, including erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) of 112 mm/h and C‐reactive protein (CRP) of 106.5 mg/L, while autoimmune screening, including antinuclear antibody (ANA), was negative. CTA of the brain demonstrated normal intracranial arteries without aneurysms.

Given the severity of the condition and the high risk of ongoing haemorrhage, a multidisciplinary decision was made to proceed with urgent endovascular embolization, and the patient was transferred to the interventional radiology suite for the procedure.

Under sterile conditions and local anaesthesia with 2% lidocaine, right common femoral venous access was obtained under ultrasound guidance, and an 8‐French long vascular sheath was advanced into the pulmonary artery. Selective pulmonary angiography demonstrated multiple aneurysms arising from segmental branches bilaterally, including a large saccular aneurysm involving a right lower segmental artery. Using a coaxial microcatheter system, the feeding vessels were superselectively catheterized, and embolization was performed with Interlock (Boston Scientific) detachable coils ranging from 3 to 10 mm in diameter and 4–20 cm in length. A total of seven coils were deployed, resulting in complete occlusion of all aneurysms. Final angiography confirmed complete exclusion of the aneurysms without residual contrast filling or active extravasation (Figure 2). The procedure was completed without immediate complications, and the patient was transferred to the ward for post‐procedural monitoring.

Selective post‐embolization pulmonary angiography. (A, B) Right pulmonary angiograms demonstrate complete occlusion of the treated aneurysm following coil embolization (black arrows), with no active contrast extravasation. (C, D) Left pulmonary angiograms demonstrate complete occlusion of the treated aneurysms following coil embolization (white arrows).

The patient's post‐procedural course was uneventful, with overall clinical improvement. He reported no recurrence of hemoptysis. There were no episodes of desaturation, hemodynamic instability, or decline in haemoglobin levels during the remainder of his hospitalisation. The patient was discharged in good condition 1 week after the procedure. At both the 1‐month and 3‐month follow‐up visits, he reported complete symptomatic relief with no further episodes of hemoptysis.

Discussion

PAAs are rare vascular abnormalities that may range from incidental findings to life‐threatening rupture. The current report describes a previously healthy 24‐year‐old man presenting with progressive hemoptysis and respiratory distress caused by multiple bilateral saccular PAAs, including a ruptured aneurysm. Given the initial presentation, infectious etiologies such as pulmonary tuberculosis were initially considered. However, CTA established the diagnosis by demonstrating multiple pulmonary artery aneurysms with associated pulmonary haemorrhage which necessitated urgent management [4].

The aetiology of PAAs may be congenital or acquired, commonly occurring in association with pulmonary hypertension, vasculitis, infection, trauma, or connective tissue disorders [2]. In this patient, no underlying risk factors or systemic disease were identified, favouring an idiopathic origin. Idiopathic PAAs remain exceptionally uncommon and are often diagnosed only after complications develop [5].

Endovascular therapy has increasingly emerged as a preferred treatment approach because it provides a minimally invasive alternative to surgery with favourable clinical outcomes [6]. Treatment consisted of endovascular coil embolization, aimed at occluding the feeding arteries to prevent further aneurysmal enlargement or rupture. In our case, a stepwise endovascular approach was adopted, with coil embolization of the ruptured right lower segmental pulmonary artery followed by treatment of the remaining non‐ruptured aneurysms, resulting in complete occlusion without peri‐procedural complications in a single session. The patient demonstrated significant clinical improvement with complete resolution of hemoptysis on follow‐up. Nevertheless, continued surveillance remains important because recurrence, rebleeding, or formation of additional aneurysms may occur over time [7].

This case highlights the importance of considering pulmonary artery aneurysm in patients presenting with unexplained hemoptysis, particularly when common causes have been excluded. It also demonstrates the critical role of CTA in diagnosis and procedural planning, as well as the safety and effectiveness of endovascular coil embolization in the management of ruptured PAAs [8, 9]. Given the rarity of this condition and the absence of standardised management guidelines, continued reporting of similar cases remains important to improve early recognition and support future treatment strategies.

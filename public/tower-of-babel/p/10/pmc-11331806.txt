Spatial Markov matrices for measuring the spatial dependencies of an epidemiological spread : case Covid’19 Madagascar
Authors: Stefana Tabera Tsilefa, Angelo Raherinirina
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis article applies a variant of the Markov chain that explicitly incorporates spatial effects. It is an extension of the Markov class allowing a more complete analysis of the spatial dimensions of transition dynamics. The aim is to provide a methodology for applying the explicit model to spatial dependency analysis.MethodsHere, the question is to study and quantify whether neighborhood context affects transitional dynamics. Rather than estimating a homogeneous law, the model requires the estimation of k transition laws each dependent on spatial neighbor state. This article used published data on confirmed cases of Covid’19 in the 22 regions of Madagascar. These data were discretized to obtain a discrete state of propagation intensity.ResultsThe analysis gave us the transition probabilities between Covid’19 intensity states knowing the context of neighboring regions, and the propagation time laws knowing the spatial contexts. The results showed that neighboring regions had an effect on the propagation of Covid’19 in Madagascar.ConclusionAfter analysis, we can say that there is spatial dependency according to these spatial transition matrices.Keywords: Markov chain, Spatial markov matrices, Spatial epidemiology, Covid’19 Madagascar

Background

Infectious diseases such as Covid’19 have negative external effects [1]. In the presence of externality as a market failure, public health interventions and policies to prevent the spread of infectious diseases are essential. Policy-makers need to know the dynamics of the epidemic’s intensity and the factors affecting it, in order to determine and evaluate appropriate policies within and between states. The neighborhood effect is one of the most important factors affecting the spread of epidemics. Proximity and neighborhood affect spread for theses transmission reasons : human-to-human , animal-to-animal, environment-to-human, animal to human, and environmental similarity [2]. Human-to-human transmission of Covid’19 occurs between two people with long-term, unprotected exposure when one of them is infected with Covid’19 [3]. In human-to-human transmission, Covid’19 is transmitted between people by droplets or aerosols from coughs, sneezes or speech, surface transmission by contact with objects or surfaces on which an infected person has coughed or sneezed [4]. This means that the more densely populated, the more the virus spreads [5]. Human-to-human transmission occurs between countries through travel or trade. For this reason, following the WHO declaration on Covid’19 : “all travel and trade restrictions, cross-border traffic restrictions, visa restrictions and flight cancellations have been recommended for the prevention of disease transmission”.

The governments use a variety of policies to prevent the spread of epidemics within countries. These policies include disconnecting the chain of transmission, notification and transparency, health diagnostic and treatment services, data management, evidence gathering and research, budgeting, health service provision and support and reducing the negative economic effects of Covid’19. It is therefore important to predict how the epidemic will spread and what factors will influence it, in order to define appropriate policies. Although many compartmental model studies have examined the different factors affecting the spread of Covid’19 in a country, as in the article by Raherinirina A. et al. which modelled the spread of Covid’19 using control variants [6, 7], Malheiro et al. published a propagation article with contact restriction, etc. [8]. To our knowledge, there are gaps and a lack of methodological resources in research on epidemic dynamics in heterogeneity spaces for predicting future trends in order to analyse appropriate policies and the effects of connection in neighbourhood on spread dynamics. The aim of this article is therefore to propose an original methodology for measuring the link between spatial proximity and epidemic dynamics.

To model phenomena that evolve over time, it is often appropriate to represent them by a stochastic process. The power and efficiency of the Markovian approach in the analysis and modelling of complex systems have been known for decades. In this study, we use the Markov model in a discrete state space, a model without memory i.e. the future depends only on the present [9]. Then, to measure the connection with the neighbourhood, we will try to incorporate a spatial parameter which conditions the future with the present but also the state of the neighbours in space. This model was introduced by Rey in 2001 in a paper proposing a new empirical strategy for analyzing the evolution of regional income distributions over time and space using a spatialized Markov extension [10]. Ladet et al. in 2005 discuss the use, advantages and limitations of spatialized Markov chains applied to ecology [11]. Recent papers discuss the model in some applications, such as Dehghan and Shahnazi who used spatial Markov chain to measure the spatial dependence of Covid’19 spatial dynamics in the Asian continent. But [12, 13] all this remains limited to the comparison of neighborhood-conditional transition matrices, whose model construction is still unclear for use. Although a few other studies, such as Guliyev (2020) and Kang et al. (2020), have examined the spatial effects of covid’19 with other approaches, using linear regression and the normal distribution of error terms, which are not very accurate for Covid’19 [14, 15]. Similarly, some previous studies have analyzed the spatial spread of Covid’19 in regions of a country using Geographic Information System maps. This method can only be a description of the current situation and does not allow us to measure the extent of spatial effects.

This article aims to make two contributions. First, the identification of the neighbouring state and the methodology to build the model with spatial dependencies by integrating it into the transition conditioning. Secondly, the study examines the link with the Covid’19 intensity dynamics regions using Markov chain and spatial Markov chain. In this study, the spatial Markov chain approach was used because it does not require certain constraints such as linear regression and the assumption of a normal distribution to take into account the spatial effect of Covid’19. This approach measures the spatial effects on the transition probability and the sojourn time between the different classes of neighbours in the regions. To achieve these objectives, the article is organised as follows : in the Methods section, we first present the construction of the markov model with spatial dependence and the Covid’19 data for Madagascar used in the application. Then the Results section, which includes the discretisation of the data to obtain discrete states followed by the estimation of transition matrices and sojourn times without spatial dependence and with spatial dependence to measure the neighbourhood effect. Finally, there is a discussion of the results and a conclusion.

Neighborhood graphs based on nearest neighbors

The method consists in selecting the k nearest points as neighbors. This method has the advantage of leaving no point without a neighbor, which is not necessary for spatial analysis, but is generally a better reflection of reality (it’s rare for a geographical area to be completely isolated). On the other hand, it is sometimes difficult to identify the k value that reflects the true underlying spatial relationships. Graphs based on k nearest neighbors are not necessarily symmetrical [17].

Discussion

The diagonal of the matrix in Markov law is a smooth transition, i.e., the state of Covid’19 intensity level is stable for one Markov chain process. The transition probabilities in the upper-right corner (Qij,i<j) refers to an upward transition, and the transition probabilities in the lower-left corner (Qij,i>j) refers to a downward transition. If the state of Covid intensity level is stable for one Markov chain process, we assume that the Covid’19 intensity for the corresponding region was in control. An upward transition for the Covid’19 intensity level indicates that the Covid’19 epidemic situation was serious and a download transition for the Covid’19 intensity level indicates that the Covid’19 epidemic situation was reduced. The results showed that neighbouring regions had a significant effect on the spread of Covid-19 in Madagascar. This underlines the importance of spatial interactions in the dynamics of disease transmission, highlighting the need to take neighbourhood factors into account when planning public health interventions.

The analysis of the transition law between states of varying levels of intensity of the Covid’19 disease, with consideration of the spatial contexts in which these states occur has enabled the quantification of changes in disease intensity depending on the surrounding regions. The findings offer valuable insights that can inform predictions about the spread of the disease and the evaluation of the efficacy of control measures. By elucidating the spatial dependencies, it is feasible to discern regions that are prone to the propagation of the disease. This knowledge can inform the implementation of targeted strategies to contain the disease and protect vulnerable populations. The findings of this study reinforce the necessity for continued investigation into Markovian spatial models to enhance the precision of analyses and predictions regarding the dissemination of infectious diseases. Further studies could investigate additional neighbourhood factors, evaluate control scenarios and assess the impact of public health policies.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the method proposed in this study offers an innovative approach to analysing epidemiological dynamics by taking spatial dependencies into account. By integrating neighbourhood effects into a Markov model, we were able to gain a better understanding of the transitions in Covid’19 intensity in the different regions of Madagascar. This approach not only enables us to model the spread of infectious diseases more accurately, but also to identify the key factors influencing these dynamics. In addition, this methodology can be extended to other geographical contexts to help predict and implement effective public health policies in the fight against epidemics.

It should be noted that the results and conclusions drawn from this analysis may not be directly generalisable to other infectious diseases or to other geographical contexts outside Madagascar. Moreover, while the methodology employed proved efficacious for the investigation of the propagation of Covid’19 in this particular instance, it may necessitate modifications or adaptations if it is to be applied to other epidemiological scenarios. It is important to acknowledge that, as with any modelling approach, simplifying assumptions are often indispensable, which may impact the results and restrict the generalisability of the conclusions.

In light of these constraints, it is imperative to pursue further research with the aim of refining and extending Markovian spatial models in order to enhance our comprehension of and ability to predict the dissemination of infectious diseases across a range of geographical and epidemiological settings.

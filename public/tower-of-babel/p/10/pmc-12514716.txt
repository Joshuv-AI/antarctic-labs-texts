18F-FDG PET/CT findings in acquired cystic disease-associated renal cell carcinoma: a case description
Authors: Qiaoliang Chen, Shuangxiu Tan
Published: 2025

Introduction

Acquired cystic disease-associated renal cell carcinoma (ACD-RCC) is the most common subtype of renal cell carcinoma (RCC) in end-stage renal disease and acquired cystic kidney disease (ACKD), but it is clinically rare. ACKD patients undergoing dialysis or renal transplantation have a high risk of developing renal cancer. ACD-RCC, a tumor with poor blood supply, is typically characterized by multiple cystic lesions in both kidneys, some of which may be complicated by intralesional hemorrhage or necrosis. In this article, we present the 18F-fluorodeoxyglucose (18F-FDG) positron emission tomography/computed tomography (PET/CT) imaging findings for a rare case of ACD-RCC.

Case presentation

All procedures performed in this study were in accordance with the ethical guidelines set by the relevant institutional and/or national research committee(s), as well as the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. Written informed consent was provided by the patient for publication of this article and accompanying images. A copy of the signed consent form is available for review by the editorial office of this journal.

A 54-year-old male patient suffered a spontaneous fracture of the left femoral head over a month ago. The patient was undergoing dialysis due to uremia, which was a complication of long-term chronic renal insufficiency. The patient stated that he had no history of other renal diseases. An abdominal magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) examination revealed multiple cysts in both kidneys, the abdominal cavity, and the retroperitoneal cavity, some of which were complicated by bleeding (Figure 1). He underwent an 18F-FDG PET/CT examination to evaluate the extent of disease involvement. The maximum intensity projection (MIP) PET image showed multifocal activity in various regions, including both kidneys, the abdominopelvic cavity, both axillae, both inguinal regions, the left shoulder joint, the left femoral neck, and the skull (Figure 2A). The left renal column was locally thickened, and had an uneven density, an irregular shape, and an unclear boundary with the surrounding renal parenchyma. The lesion was approximately 5.40 cm by 4.88 cm, and demonstrated uneven 18F-FDG uptake [maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax) =2.62]. The kidneys exhibited multiple round cysts of varying densities, some of which showed foci of high-density hemorrhage. Multiple cystic masses were present in the abdominal cavity and retroperitoneum, some of which exhibited internal hemorrhage and were poorly demarcated from the left kidney. The solid portions of these masses demonstrated increased 18F-FDG uptake (SUVmax =3.65) (Figure 2B-2D). The left adrenal region showed a well-defined, mixed-density mass, measuring approximately 6.43 cm by 5.09 cm, which had increased 18F-FDG uptake (SUVmax =3.64) (Figure 2E-2G). 18F-FDG uptake (SUVmax =6.07) was also increased in the left femoral neck, which showed osteolytic bone destruction with pathologic fracture on CT images, and was surrounded by a soft-tissue density mass (Figure 2H-2J). The final pathological diagnosis confirmed ACD-RCC.

Discussion

ACD-RCC, which was incorporated into the 2016 World Health Organization’s Classification of Tumors of the Urinary System and Male Genital Tract, is the most common subtype of RCC in long-term dialysis patients (1,2). A multicenter study reported comparable survival outcomes between ACD-RCC and RCC, indicating that ACD-RCC was not as indolent as initially thought (3). It has the potential to develop distant metastases, with common sites including lymph nodes and organs such as the lungs, liver, and bone (4). Typical imaging findings on CT or MRI include bilateral renal atrophy with unilateral or bilateral, multiple, variably sized cysts, generally with no obvious or only mild enhancement (5-8). Currently, reports on 18F-FDG PET/CT for this type of tumor are limited. In this case, 18F-FDG PET/CT revealed mild 18F-FDG uptake in the solid component of the tumor and sparse metabolism in the cystic lesions. This case showed the potential of 18F-FDG PET/CT to detect ACD-RCC, and its multi-system involvement, including bone metastasis. More studies are needed to explore the important role of 18F-FDG PET/CT in the diagnosis and management of ACD-RCC.

Conclusions

18F-FDG PET/CT can indicate the presence of ACD-RCC and its multi-system involvement, including bone metastasis. This case emphasizes the importance of the differential diagnosis of renal tumors in long-term dialysis patients and shows the potential of 18F-FDG PET/CT to diagnose ACD-RCC.

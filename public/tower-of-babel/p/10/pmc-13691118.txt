A chromosome-level reference genome assembly of the King Ratsnake (Elaphe carinata)
Authors: Boyang Liu, Liangyu Cui, Shangchen Yang, Jiayi Li, Ruyi Huang, Yihang Fan, Jin Chen, Chen Lin, Xiaojun Liu, Wenjing Sun, Haimeng Li, Yanling Xia, Yuan Fu, Song Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The King Ratsnake (Elaphe carinata), a widely distributed non-colubrine snake in East Asia, exhibits a remarkable capacity for broad-spectrum venom resistance, enabling it to prey on other snakes, including lethal viperid and elapid species. Despite its ecological and physiological significance, the genetic basis of this trait remains largely unknown. Here, we present a chromosome-level genome assembly of E. carinata, generated using PacBio HiFi long-read sequencing, Illumina short-read data, and RNA-seq—supported by a synteny-based scaffolding approach using the closely related Elaphe schrenckii. The 1.62 Gb assembly (contig N50 = 2.77 Mb, scaffold N50 = 143.07 Mb) achieves 97.3% BUSCO completeness, with over 90% of sequences anchored into 18 pseudochromosomes. Repetitive sequences account for 53.19% of the genome, with LINE elements being the most abundant. We annotated 19,750 protein-coding genes, of which 99.2% were functionally assigned using integrated evidence from homology, transcriptomics, and ab initio predictions. This high-quality genomic resource provides a foundation for exploring colubrid evolution, venom resistance mechanisms, and the development of novel antivenom therapies.

Background & Summary

The King Ratsnake is a large oviparous species belonging to the family Colubridae. Phylogenetically, it is classified within the non-colubrine lineage of colubrid snakes. This species is widely distributed across southeastern China, from northern Guangdong, Guangxi, and Taiwan in the south to the Beijing-Tianjin region in the north, with its range also extending into northern Vietnam and parts of Japan1,2. It predominantly inhabits montane and hilly areas, serving as an ecologically representative colubrid species. Its distribution pattern provides valuable insights into the biogeographic evolution of snakes from temperate to subtropical regions in East Asia.

E. carinata primarily preys on rodents, birds, and bird eggs, with notable ontogenetic variation in body coloration and size3. When threatened, it exhibits a distinctive defensive behavior by secreting a malodorous liquid from its cloacal glands. Notably, E. carinata exhibits ophiophagous behavior and has evolved specific blood proteins that confer resistance to a spectrum of venomous snakes4. Its serum demonstrates an ability to neutralize hemorrhagic toxins from pit vipers, such as Trimeresurus stejnegeri and Deinagkistrodon acutus, as well as the potent neurotoxins from elapids, including the highly lethal Bungarus multicinctus5–7. Although these broad-spectrum antitoxic properties have been experimentally validated, the precise molecular mechanisms governing this immune protection and toxin metabolism remain elusive. A high-quality reference genome for this species would therefore provide a critical foundation for identifying the genetic basis of venom resistance and for advancing our understanding of the molecular adaptations underlying this remarkable trait.

In this study, we present the first chromosome-level genome assembly for the King Ratsnake, generated by integrating PacBio HiFi long-read sequencing, whole-genome sequencing (WGS) and RNA-seq data. Leveraging a reference-guided strategy based on a closely related species, this high-quality genomic resource provides a foundation for investigating colubrid evolution, chromosomal rearrangements, and population genetics. As a key resource, it will empower future studies aimed at elucidating the genetic basis of the species’ exceptional venom resistance, with potential implications for developing novel antivenom strategies and biomedical applications.

Methods

Sample collection and ethics statements

Muscle tissue was collected from a single adult female E. carinata found in Anhui Province, China, for DNA extraction, Mixed tissues from the liver, heart, kidney, and muscle were collected for RNA extraction. The sample was immediately flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80 °C until DNA and RNA extraction. The study design, experiments, and sample collection were approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Northeast Forestry University (No.2023048).

DNA & RNA extraction, libraries preparation and sequencing

High-molecular-weight genomic DNA was isolated from approximately 50 mg of muscle tissue using a QIAGEN DNeasy Blood & Tissue Kit. The integrity of the extracted DNA was verified by 1% agarose gel electrophoresis, and its concentration was quantified using a NanoDrop One UV-Vis spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) and a Qubit 4.0 Fluorometer (Invitrogen, USA). The DNA was aliquoted for two distinct sequencing applications: one aliquot was used to construct a PacBio HiFi library with the SMRTbell Express Template Prep Kit 2.0 and sequenced on the PacBio Sequel IIe platform; another aliquot was used to prepare a short-insert library for whole-genome sequencing (WGS) on the Illumina platform with 100 bp paired-end reads.

Total RNA was extracted from a mixture of different tissues using TRIzol reagent (TIANGEN) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Poly(A) RNA was enriched from total RNA using the Dynabeads mRNA Purification Kit (Cat#61006, Invitrogen) and fragmented using the fragmentation reagents included in the MGIEasy RNA Library Preparation Kit V3.1 (Cat#1000005276, MGI). First-strand cDNA was synthesized using random primers and reverse transcriptase, followed by second-strand synthesis. To generate strand-specific libraries, dUTP was substituted for dTTP during second-strand synthesis, and the subsequent PCR amplification was performed using a polymerase that does not amplify uracil-containing templates, thereby retaining strand orientation. The double-stranded cDNA was subjected to end repair, A-tailing, and adapter ligation. Size selection was performed using MGIEasy DNA Clean Beads (Cat#1000005279, MGI), followed by PCR amplification. For sequencing, double-stranded libraries were denatured to single-stranded DNA, and circularization was performed using the MGIEasy Circularization Module (Cat#1000005260, MGI). The resulting single-stranded circular DNA libraries were sequenced on the DNBSEQ-T7RS platform with 100 bp paired-end reads.

Finally, we generated a total of approximately 138.5 Gb of sequencing data, including 23 Gb of PacBio HiFi reads, 103 Gb of whole-genome shotgun (WGS) reads, and 12.5 Gb of RNA-seq reads.

Genome assembly and assessment

The de novo genome assembly of E. carinata was generated using PacBio HiFi long-read data. Initial contigs were assembled with hifiasm (v0.19.5)8 and polished using the original HiFi reads and WGS reads via Merqury9. To advance the assembly to the chromosome level, we employed a reference-guided scaffolding strategy based on synteny with a closely related species, the Amur ratsnake (Elaphe schrenckii), which shares an identical karyotype (2n = 36)10. The contigs were systematically anchored and oriented into chromosome-scale scaffolds by using quarTeT (v 1.2.5)11, leveraging the high-quality chromosome-level genome of Elaphe schrenckii(GCA_050231175.1) as a reference. This approach resulted in the final assembly, designated ElaCar_v1. The genome size is 1.62 Gb with a contig N50 of 2.77 Mb and a scaffold N50 of 143.07 Mb (Fig. 1, Table 1). Approximately 90.75% (1.5 Gb) of the assembled sequences were successfully assigned to 18 pseudochromosomes, consistent with the species’ known diploid number. Repetitive sequence annotation

Repetitive elements were identified and annotated using Earl Grey v1.0, a reproducible pipeline that integrates de novo repeat discovery with homology-based masking13. The tool was installed via Bioconda, which automatically configures dependent software including RepeatModeler214 and RepeatMasker15. The Dfam database (v3.7)16 was used as the primary reference library for repeat classification.

Repetitive sequences constituted 53.19% of the assembled genome, in which Long Interspersed Nuclear Elements (LINEs) represented the most abundant class of transposable elements, comprising 23.02% of the genome, followed by DNA transposons (6.18%) and Unclassified repeats (12.67%). LTR retrotransposons covered 3.60% of the genome, while SINEs and Penelope-like elements accounted for 2.62% and 1.22%, respectively. Gene structure annotation

The annotation of protein-coding genes was performed by integrating evidence from ab initio prediction, homology-based inference, and transcriptomic data. The repeat-softmasked genome was used as the input. For homology-based prediction, protein sequences from five sauropsid species (Ahaetulla prasina17, Anolis carolinensis18, Pantherophis guttatus19, Podarcis muralis20, and Python bivittatus21) were aligned to the genome using GeMoMa (v1.7.1)22. Ab initio gene prediction was conducted with the BRAKER pipeline (v2.1.6)23, which integrated RNA-seq data to train AUGUSTUS24 and GeneMark-ETP25. Finally, all lines of evidence were integrated to generate a consensus gene set using EvidenceModeler (EVM, v2.0.0)26.

We predicted 19,750 high-confidence protein-coding genes in the E. carinata genome (Table 3). These gene models had an average gene length of 32,064 bp and an average CDS length of 1,587 bp (Table 3). Gene function annotation

Functional annotation of protein-coding genes was performed using eggNOG-mapper v227. The tool provided comprehensive annotations from multiple databases, including Gene Ontology (GO) terms, KEGG pathways, EC numbers, COG functional categories. Protein domain predictions were obtained by integrating PFAM and SMART annotations through the built-in homology-based pipeline. Data Records

The PacBio HiFi long-reads and whole-genome sequencing (WGS) reads also have been deposited in the National Genomics Data Center (NGDC)28,29 under accession number PRJCA048610. Additionally, the HiFi genome sequencing data are accessible under accession number CRR222311930, the whole-genome sequencing (WGS) data under CRR222312131, and the transcriptome data under CRR222312032. The complete genome sequence has been deposited in the NGDC Genome Warehouse under accession number GWHGZIJ00000000.133. The PacBio HiFi sequencing data, RNA sequencing data, and Illumina whole-genome sequencing data have been also deposited in the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) SRA database under accession numbers SRR3827285234, SRR3827285035, and SRR3827285136. The genome assembly has been deposited at NCBI under the Whole Genome Shotgun project accession JBXVYC00000000037. The corresponding genome annotation is available via Figshare38.

Technical Validation

Genomic DNA quality and quantity were assessed using a NanoDrop spectrophotometer and a Qubit fluorometer. For the next-generation sequencing (NGS) libraries, the Qubit-measured concentration was 58 ng/μL. For PacBio HiFi library preparation, the DNA concentration was 82 ng/μL, yielding a total mass of approximately 12.3 μg. Purity assessment showed A260/A280 ratios of 1.85 and A260/A230 ratios of 2.10 for both samples, indicating minimal contamination by proteins, phenol, or chaotropic salts. The ratio of NanoDrop-derived concentration to Qubit-derived concentration (Nc/Qc) was 1.2.

Genome assembly completeness and base-level accuracy were rigorously assessed. Benchmarking with BUSCO v5.4.4 against the vertebrata_odb10 database (n = 3,354) revealed 97.3% complete, 1.0% fragmented, and 1.7% missing BUSCOs. Additionally, the assembly achieved a quality value (QV) of 58.965, corresponding to an error rate of 1.27 × 10−6. These results collectively demonstrate that the assembled genome is highly complete and accurate, rendering it suitable for downstream comparative and functional genomic analyses.

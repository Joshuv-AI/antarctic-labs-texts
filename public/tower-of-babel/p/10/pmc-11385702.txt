Plasma Olink Proteomics Reveals Novel Biomarkers for Prediction and Diagnosis in Dilated Cardiomyopathy with Heart Failure
Authors: Shuai Xu, Ge Zhang, Xin Tan, Yiyao Zeng, Hezi Jiang, Yufeng Jiang, Xiangyu Wang, Yahui Song, Huimin Fan, Yafeng Zhou
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
In this study, we utilized the Olink Cardiovascular III panel to compare the expression levels of 92 cardiovascular-related proteins between patients with dilated cardiomyopathy combined with heart failure (DCM-HF) (n = 20) and healthy normal people (Normal) (n = 18). The top five most significant proteins, including SPP1, IGFBP7, F11R, CHI3L1, and Plaur, were selected by Olink proteomics. These proteins were further validated using ELISA in plasma samples collected from an additional cohort. ELISA validation confirmed significant increases in SPP1, IGFBP7, F11R, CHI3L1, and Plaur in DCM-HF patients compared to healthy controls. GO and KEGG analysis indicated that NT-pro BNP, SPP1, IGFBP7, F11R, CHI3L1, Plaur, BLM hydrolase, CSTB, Gal-4, CCL15, CDH5, SR-PSOX, and CCL2 were associated with DCM-HF. Correlation analysis revealed that these 13 differentially expressed proteins have strong correlations with clinical indicators such as LVEF and NT-pro BNP, etc. Additionally, in the GEO–DCM data sets, the combined diagnostic value of these five core proteins AUC values of 0.959, 0.773, and 0.803, respectively indicating the predictive value of the five core proteins for DCM-HF. Our findings suggest that these proteins may be useful biomarkers for the diagnosis and prediction of DCM-HF, and further research is prompted to explore their potential as therapeutic targets.

Introduction

Dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) is a nonischemic myocardial disorder characterized by left or biventricular dilation and systolic dysfunction in the absence of coronary artery disease, hypertension, valvular disease, or congenital heart disease.1 The American Heart Association classifies DCM into genetic, mixed, or acquired types, whereas the European Society of Cardiology distinguishes between familial (genetic) and nonfamilial (nongenetic) forms.2 According to the World Health Organization, DCM is a severe heart disease with structural or functional myocardial abnormalities, leading to high incidence of mortality due to complications such as heart failure and arrhythmias.3

Extensive research revealed the complex, etiologies of DCM are varied, including genetic mutations, infections, inflammation, autoimmune diseases, toxin exposure, and endocrine or neuromuscular causes.4 Therefore, the early diagnosis of DCM is challenging. However, the global availability of advanced diagnostic tools varies widely, leading many DCM cases to be classified as idiopathic cardiomyopathy. Advances in precision medicine, including genetic testing, single-cell sequencing, and proteomics, have identified numerous biomarkers, enhancing diagnostic capabilities and improving risk stratification for DCM.5

Currently, the diagnosis of DCM primarily relies on echocardiography and MRI, with biomarkers such as NT-pro BNP and troponin, which lack specificity. Consequently, DCM is often diagnosed only after significant clinical symptoms appear, by which time irreversible myocardial damage has occurred, leading to heart failure.6 Patients at this stage may require heart transplantation or cardiac resynchronization therapy, both of which are costly and with a higher risk of recurrent decompensated heart failure. Previous studies have identified NT-pro BNP and GDF-15 as early markers, and proteins such as TITIN, cytoskeletal proteins, and heat shock proteins have also been implicated in DCM.7,8 However, the pathogenesis of dilated cardiomyopathy combined with heart failure(DCM-HF) remains unclear.

Advancements in technologies like Olink proteomics enable simultaneous detection of multiple proteins with minimal sample volumes.9 Our study used the Olink Cardiovascular III panel to identify proteins associated with dilated cardiomyopathy combined with heart failure (DCM-HF), providing comprehensive proteomic insights. We identified novel biomarkers and explored their roles and mechanisms in human plasma and mouse models using GO and KEGG analyses. Among 13 significantly upregulated proteins, five core proteins showed marked differences, and were enriched in key pathways such as apoptosis and necrosis. This study aims to find novel biomarkers as well as their biological functions and pathways associated with DCM-HF and to identify potential new therapeutic targets for the disease.

Materials and Methods

Participants

This study, approved by the Institutional Review Board of the Fourth Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University (Approval No. 2022-KT-105, ChiCTR2100051469), recruited patients with Dilated Cardiomyopathy (DCM) combined with Heart Failure (HF) from the Fourth Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University. DCM diagnosis adhered to the criteria established by the European Society of Cardiology. Cardiac functional status was assessed using the New York Heart Association (NYHA) functional classification system. Inclusion criteria for DCM and HF patients were: (1) Confirmed DCM diagnosis based on the European Society of Cardiology’s 2023 guidelines;10 (2) Symptoms of heart failure, aligning with the consensus guidelines for CHF diagnosis;11 (3) A history of congestive heart failure for over six months; (4) Provision of signed informed consent. Exclusion criteria were: (1)First occurrence of acute heart failure; (2)Loss of consciousness; (3) Presence of severe infectious diseases or malignant tumors; (4) Hospital stay <2 days; (5) Incomplete clinical case data

Plasma Sample Collection

Patients diagnosed with DCM combined with heart failure(DCM-HF), along with healthy volunteers, were recruited in the study. Venous blood samples were collected in Na-EDTA tubes to prevent coagulation. Samples were centrifuged at 3000 × g for 15 min at 4 °C. The top 4 mL of plasma from each sample was then rapidly frozen at −80 °C to maintain the stability and integrity of plasma biomolecules.

Screening of Biomarkers for Dilated Cardiomyopathy with Heart Failure

The Olink Cardiovascular III panel (Olink Proteomics AB, Uppsala, Sweden) containing 92 target proteins was used to analyze samples from 20 patients with dilated cardiomyopathy combined with heart failure (DCM-HF) and 18 healthy normal controls (N). Each target protein was identified using dual-antibody labeling and bound to a complementary DNA barcode. Quantification was performed using the high-throughput microfluidic real-time PCR instrument Biomark HD (Biomark HD, Fluidigm). The final assay results are expressed in Normalized Protein eXpression (NPX) values, which are arbitrary units on a log2 scale, with higher values indicating greater protein expression. Validation data for the assay, including detection limits and intra- and interassay precision, can be found on the manufacturer’s Web site (www.olink.com).

Potential Biomarker Screening

Differentially expressed proteins (DEPs) between the two groups were identified using the R package OlinkAnalyze with a statistical threshold of P < 0.05 and a false discovery rate (FDR) correction <0.05. These stringent thresholds minimize the probability of false positives, ensuring the robustness of the identified biomarkers. Correlations between DEPs and clinical characteristics in DCM-HF patients were analyzed using OmicStudio (https://www.omicstudio.cn). Clinical parameters included age, gender, hypertension history, WBC, Lymph, C-reactive protein, low-density lipoprotein, estimated Glomerular filtration, neutrophil count, LVEF, heart rate, NT-pro BNP, etc. The STRING database was employed to construct a protein–protein interaction (PPI) network, visualizing interactions among all DEPs and potential biomarkers. The diagnostic potential was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves generated by the ROC R package, with higher area under the curve (AUC) values indicating superior diagnostic value. Only differentially expressed proteins (DEPs) that demonstrated high significance across all bioinformatics analyses were considered potential biomarkers for DCM-HF.

Protein–Protein Interaction Network Construction

The protein–protein interaction (PPI) network was constructed using the STRING database. PPI pairs with a combined score above 0.4 (medium confidence) were selected. The network visualization was performed using Cytoscape software (http://www.cytoscape.org/). The CytoHubba plugin in Cytoscape was used to calculate the degree of each node, with higher connectivity degrees indicating highly interactive proteins or genes within the network.

Function Enrichment Analysis

To elucidate the primary biological functions and signaling pathways involved, gene enrichment analysis was performed on differentially expressed proteins (DEPs). GO analysis was conducted using Blast2Go (https://www.blast2go.com/), and statistical significance was assessed using Fisher’s exact test. The DEPs profiles between the DCM-HF and Normal groups enriched categories of biological processes, molecular functions, and cellular components. Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses were performed to explore the genomic, chemical, and system functions of robust DEPs

ELISA Validation in Plasma

Plasma samples from dilated cardiomyopathy combined with heart failure (DCM-HF) (n = 30) patients and healthy normal controls (n = 35) were further validated using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). The inclusion and exclusion criteria for the ELISA validation cohort are consistent with the standards outlined in section 2.1. The control group comprised age and sex-matched healthy individuals. Differentially expressed proteins (DEPs) with at least a 1.3-fold change (FC) and pivotal roles in KEGG pathways were selected. Plasma levels of DEPs were quantified using solid-phase sandwich ELISA kits (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Ltd., USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Core proteins validation was performed using external DCM-GEO data sets: GSE165303 (48 controls, 74 DCM patients), GSE116250 (15 controls, 38 DCM patients), and GSE141910 (162 controls, 162 DCM patients).

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS version 26.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). Statistical differences between dilated cardiomyopathy combined with heart failure (DCM-HF) and healthy normal controls (Normal) groups were analyzed using unpaired Student’s t tests or Chi-square tests, as appropriate. Multiple comparisons were corrected using the false discovery rate (FDR) method. Binary logistic regression analysis was conducted to construct discriminative models using the identified biomarkers. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed to evaluate the diagnostic performance of the models. Sensitivity and specificity were determined based on the maximum Youden index (sensitivity + specificity-1). Pearson correlation analysis was used to assess the relationship between DEP expression levels and clinical indicators (Age, CRP, LDL-C, TG, Albumin, eGFR, LVEF, NT-proBNP, Heart rate). A p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant, and a p-value <0.01 was considered highly significant.

Identification of Cardiovascular-Related Biomarkers Using Olink

We evaluated and compared the expression levels of 92 cardiovascular-related proteins between DCM-HF group and the Control group (Table S1). The heatmap demonstrated the landscape of protein profiles (Figure 1A). Among them, 75 proteins were upregulated and 17 proteins were downregulated. The details of protein expression are further illustrated in the bar plot and volcano plot (Figures 1B and 1C). At a threshold of p < 0.05, a total of 33 proteins showed significant differential abundance between the two groups. Among these, 13 proteins exhibited marked differences (P < 0.001), and these results remained statistically significant after FDR correction. Notably, the expression levels of these 13 significantly different proteins were all up-regulated. The heatmap of these differentially expressed cardiovascular-related proteins is shown in Figure 1D. These 13 significantly different proteins are mainly involved in inflammation, fibrinolysis, and tissue repair. Specifically, the box plots for NT-pro BNP, SPP1, IGFBP7, F11R, CHI3L1, Plaur, BLM hydrolase, CSTB, Gal-4, CCL15, CDH5, SR-PSOX, and CCL2 indicate that the expression levels of these 13 proteins are elevated in DCM-HF (Figure 2).

GO Enrichment Analysis of Target Genes of DCM-HF DEPs

To investigate the potential functions of the 13 differentially expressed proteins (DEPs), we performed GO enrichment analysis. Functional annotation of the 13 significant DEPs in dilated cardiomyopathy combined with heart failure (DCM-HF) was conducted using Gene Ontology (GO) software. GO analysis categorized the results into three main domains: (i) Biological Processes, (ii) Cellular Components, and (iii) Molecular Functions. The analysis revealed that DEPs in DCM-HF were significantly enriched in key cellular biological processes, including biological regulation, cell migration and proliferation, platelet aggregation, and multicellular regulation (Figure 3A). This specific enrichment of cellular components suggests potential mechanistic links in the pathogenesis and progression of DCM-HF. These findings provide deeper insights into the roles of DEPs in the etiology and development of DCM-HF.

We conducted a comprehensive Metascape pathway and process enrichment analysis on the target proteins of DCM-HF. This analysis integrated data from multiple ontology sources, including GO Biological Processes, GO Molecular Functions, KEGG Pathways, Hallmark Gene Sets, Reactome Gene Sets, and Canonical Pathways. The results highlighted several significantly enriched terms, including PID INTEGRIN3 PATHWAY (M53), regulation of IGF transport (R-HSA-381426), and biological regulation (GO:0050789). Figures 3A and 3B present the top 9 and top 6 enriched terms representing key clusters from this analysis. To elucidate the interrelationships among these top terms, we constructed a functional annotation network, as shown in Figures 3C and 3D. This network provided an in-depth understanding of the potential interconnected pathways and processes involving the differentially expressed proteins in DCM-HF, thereby enhancing our comprehension of their roles in the molecular mechanisms of DCM.

Protein–Protein Interaction Network Analysis of DEPs in DCM-HF

We performed a protein–protein interaction (PPI) network analysis on the 13 significantly differentially expressed proteins (DEPs) in dilated cardiomyopathy combined with heart failure (DCM-HF) using the STRING database. After excluding isolated target genes lacking interactions, 12 target proteins were included in the PPI network with a confidence score cutoff of 900. The gene interactions were visualized using Cytoscape software. Inflammatory factors, fibrosis, and cell proliferation/apoptosis family members were highly activated and strongly interconnected in the PPI analysis.

We created a PPI network of key DEPs to further explore the connections among these candidate proteins. This included the top five proteins with the highest fold changes and the previously mentioned candidate biomarkers. SPP1, Plaur, F11R, CHI3L1, CCL15, CCL2, and CDH5 were all strongly interconnected in the candidate biomarker PPI analysis (Figure 3E). These interactions highlight the significance of inflammation, fibrosis, endothelial dysfunction, and apoptosis in the pathogenesis of DCM-HF. The involvement of these pathways suggests potential biomarkers and therapeutic targets, which could enable the development of targeted treatment strategies for DCM-HF. Additionally, these differentially expressed proteins were analyzed using NetworkAnalyst for PPI network analysis, revealing that F11R has close associations with multiple cardiovascular-related proteins (Figure S2).

Relationship between Differentially Expressed Proteins and DCM-HF

To better understand the relationship of selected differentially expressed proteins (DEPs) and DCM-HF, we performed a correlation analysis between DEPs and clinical indicators of DCM-HF (Table 2). We performed a correlation analysis using R, applying thresholds of r = 0.3 and p < 0.05 to identify significant relationships (Tables S2, S3, and S4). These filtered relationships were then visualized in a network diagram, highlighting the key connections between variables (Figure S3). DEPs including BNP, SPP1, IGFBP7, F11R, CHI3L1, Plaur, and BLM hydrolase showed strong positive correlations with CRP, LDL-C, and NT-proBNP (R = 0.492–0.998, P < 0.001). These correlations indicated a meaningful association between these proteins and inflammatory markers, lipid levels, and heart failure severity. The DEPs displayed significant negative correlations with Albumin, eGFR, and LVEF,(R = −0.438–0.889, P < 0.05). Despite the consistent significance of the p-values, the R values varied, indicating differing strengths of these correlations. CRP, eGFR, LVEF, and NT-pro BNP had extremely high R values, indicating a very strong correlation, whereas other markers like LDL-C and Albumin had moderate R values, suggesting a weaker correlation to the clinical features. The findings highlight significant and meaningful correlations between DEPs and various clinical features, with varying strengths of association, emphasizing their potential role in the pathophysiology and clinical evaluation of dilated cardiomyopathy.

ELISA Validation of Differentially Expressed Proteins in DCM-HF Plasma

Among the 13 DEPs, SPP1, IGFBP7, F11R, CHI3L1, and Plaur showed significant changes in DCM-HF compared to Normal controls. Therefore, based on the highest fold changes and bioinformatics analysis, SPP1, IGFBP7, F11R, CHI3L1, and Plaur were selected for further validation by ELISA. The raw data for ELISA have been provided in Table S5. According to the ELISA results (Figure 4 A-E), the plasma levels of SPP1, IGFBP7, F11R, CHI3L1, and Plaur were significantly elevated in DCM-HF patients compared to Normal controls (P < 0.001). This finding was consistent with the trends observed in the Olink PEA data. Figure 5 also showed the validation of core proteins using external DCM-GEO data sets (GSE116250, GSE141910, GSE165303), displaying the AUC-ROC for SPP1, IGFBP7, F11R, CHI3L1, and Plaur. The combined AUC-ROC for the five-protein panel was 0.959 (95% confidence interval: 0.905–0.996), 0.773 (95% confidence interval: 0.719–0.820), 0.803 (95% confidence interval: 0.706–0.888) respectively. The AUC value indicates high diagnostic ability, especially in the GSE116250 data set, suggesting that these core proteins have strong diagnostic ability in DCM-HF patients.

Discussion

Dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) is characterized by left ventricular dilation and progressive systolic dysfunction. The etiology of DCM is diverse, with common triggers including myocarditis, alcohol consumption, drug or toxin exposure, and metabolic or endocrine disturbances.12,13 Currently, there are no specific molecular markers for the diagnosis and evaluation of DCM-HF patients. With the advancement of sequencing technologies, numerous potential blood biomarkers associated with DCM-HF have been identified, including inflammatory products, proteins, metabolites, and exosomal miRNAs.14−16

In this study, we utilized the Olink Cardiovascular III panel to identify protein differences in DCM-HF patients. The panel includes 92 protein biomarkers encompassing inflammatory cytokines, fibrotic factors, chemokines, growth factors, and factors related to immune response, angiogenesis, and cellular remodeling. Through Olink proteomics analysis of DCM-HF patients, we identified 13 proteins with significant differential abundance. Our study highlights novel biomarkers in DCM-HF patients based on Olink proteomics analysis.

Using GO and KEGG enrichment analyses, we predicted the regulatory functions of these proteins in DCM-HF. The analyses revealed several key pathways, such as cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction, apoptosis, necrosis, and cellular remodeling. The PPI network highlights potential therapeutic targets such as SPP1, IGFBP7, and F11R. Investigating small molecules or biological agents that can modulate these proteins’ activity may lead to the development of novel therapies aimed at reducing inflammation, fibrosis, and improving endothelial function in DCM-HF. This study elucidates the molecular mechanisms underlying DCM combined with CHF and proposes potential therapeutic targets.

In this study, we found that SPP1, IGFBP7, F11R, CHI3L1, and Plaur were significantly differentially expressed in patients with dilated cardiomyopathy combined with heart failure (DCM-HF). These findings provide important insights into the pathophysiological mechanisms of DCM-HF and highlight potential biomarkers for this disease.

SPP1 (Osteopontin) is a glycoprotein in plasma that functions as a pro-inflammatory cytokine and mediating various biological processes.17 It promotes cell-mediated immune responses and plays a crucial role in extracellular matrix remodeling, contributing to wound healing and other protective effects.18,19 SPP1 is associated with multiple diseases, including myocardial infarction (MI), chronic heart failure, renal impairment, diabetes, and other chronic conditions.20−22 Numerous studies suggest that SPP1 is a potential biomarker and therapeutic target for cardiovascular diseases.17,23 SPP1 is involved in various macrophage functions, such as phagocytosis, chemotaxis, angiogenesis, cytokine production, and the expression of inducible nitric oxide synthase.24,25 Research indicates that plasma SPP1 levels in chronic heart failure (CHF) patients are proportional to the degree of left ventricular dysfunction and correlate with New York Heart Association (NYHA) classification.17 Mark Rosenberg and colleagues analyzed plasma SPP1 levels in 420 CHF patients and found that SPP1 levels were significantly elevated in CHF patients.26 Specifically, SPP1 levels were notably higher in patients with moderate to severe heart failure compared to those with mild or no symptoms. Mamoru Satoh et al.27 conducted biopsies on the right ventricular endomyocardium of 51 DCM patients and 15 healthy controls, identifying cardiomyocytes as the primary source of SPP1. SPP1 expression was positively correlated with left ventricular end-systolic diameter and negatively correlated with LVEF in DCM patients. LI et al.28 performed surgical experiments on a mouse model of cardiac pressure overload, discovering that inhibiting downstream SPP1 signaling prevented cardiomyocyte hypertrophy and cardiac fibrosis. Dai et al.29 previously found that mice with a cardiac-specific knockout of integrin-linked kinase (ILK) spontaneously developed the dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) phenotype characterized by apoptosis, fibrosis, and inflammation. In these mice, blocking osteopontin (OPN) reduced the decline in cardiac function, and OPN inhibition decreased left ventricular remodeling and dysfunction through a focal adhesion kinase (FAK)/Akt-dependent pathway.29 Recently, FU’s team performed single-cell RNA sequencing on the coronary artery perivascular adipose tissue stromal vascular fraction in heart transplant recipients with end-stage heart failure.30 They found that SPP1, in conjunction with macrophage aggregation in the atherosclerotic coronary artery perivascular adipose tissue, exacerbated coronary artery fibrosis and is positively correlated with coronary artery stenosis burden.30 Our study found elevated plasma levels of SPP1 in DCM-HF patients, consistent with previous research. Furthermore, analysis of the GEO public database revealed that SPP1 had a strong diagnostic capability for DCM patients. Based on the above research, SPP1 may be a potential therapeutic target for DCM in the future.

IGFBP7 (Insulin-like Growth Factor Binding Protein 7) is a protein that binds to insulin-like growth factors (IGFs) and is involved in various cellular processes, including cell growth, apoptosis, and fibrosis.31 Previous studies have shown that IGFBP7 can induce myocardial fibrosis and apoptosis, exacerbating the progression of heart failure.32 Komuro’s team conducted a comprehensive analysis of human single cardiomyocyte transcriptomes and plasma proteomes, identifying IGFBP7 as a cytokine downstream of transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β).33 They found that IGFBP7 is secreted by failing cardiomyocytes and is one of the most predictive markers of advanced heart failure.33 Camilla et al.34 analyzed serum IGFBP7 levels in 86 heart failure patients with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) (ejection fraction [EF] ≥ 45%) and 79 heart failure patients with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF)(ejection fraction [EF]<40%). They observed that IGFBP7 levels were less elevated in HFpEF compared to HFrEF. However, in HFpEF patients, IGFBP7 levels were still associated with diastolic dysfunction, heart failure severity, and prognostic markers. These findings suggest that IGFBP7 may promote the progression of HFpEF through inflammation and oxidative stress.34 Sanders-van’s team utilized Olink proteomics to analyze a cohort of 228 patients diagnosed with heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF).35 Their investigation revealed a potential role for IGFBP7 in the pathophysiology of heart failure, suggesting that it may contribute to comorbidities and deterioration of echocardiographic parameters in HFpEF patients. Januzzi’s research team conducted data from 1125 participants enrolled in the EMPEROR-Reduced and EMPEROR-Preserved trials.36 Their analysis revealed that concentrations of the aging-related biomarker IGFBP7 were significantly associated with higher-risk clinical states and demonstrated the potential to predict adverse cardio-renal outcomes within the EMPEROR program. Our study validates previous findings and demonstrates that IGFBP7 can serve as a biomarker for assessing the severity and progression of DCM-HF. Moreover, IGFBP7 may become a new therapeutic target by reducing myocardial fibrosis and improving cardiac function.

F11R (F11 receptor), also known as Junctional Adhesion Molecule-A (JAM-A), plays a crucial role in endothelial cell adhesion and leukocyte migration.37 It is a critical component of tight junctions in endothelial and epithelial cells and facilitates intercellular adhesion and communication.37 Under normal physiological conditions, F11R is constitutively expressed on the surface of platelets and localized in the tight junctions of endothelial cells (EC).38 Previous studies on interactions between human platelets and ECs have demonstrated that F11R/JAM-A played a crucial role in inflammatory thrombosis and atherosclerosis.39 Anna Babinska et al.40 discovered markedly increased levels of the F11R protein within atherosclerotic plaques in individuals with cardiovascular disease. Their findings propose that inhibitors targeting F11R could potentially mitigate the risk of atherosclerosis and cardiovascular diseases. Chatterjee et al.41 found the relationship between platelet-associated JAM-A and markers of platelet activation in patients with coronary artery disease (CAD). In cardiovascular diseases marked by elevated circulating levels of F11R, therapeutic strategies targeting this homophilic interface may effectively modulate thrombotic and thrombo-inflammatory platelet responses. Karshovska’s team demonstrated that the deletion of F11R in platelet-specific knockout mice resulted in a hyperreactive, pro-inflammatory phenotype, which accelerated early plaque formation.42 F11R is essential for maintaining endothelial barrier function. Dysfunction in F11R increases vascular permeability, which in turn leads to endothelial dysfunction—a hallmark of dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) and other cardiovascular diseases.43 Although direct evidence linking F11R to DCM is currently unavailable, inflammation is recognized as a central mechanism in the pathogenesis of heart failure. F11R enhances leukocyte adhesion and migration, thereby exacerbating cardiac inflammation.39 Our study demonstrated that F11R levels were elevated in patients with DCM-HF through Olink proteomics analysis. These findings suggest that F11R could be a valuable target for therapeutic intervention in DCM-HF in the future.

CHI3L1 (Chitinase-3-like protein 1), also known as YKL-40, is a glycoprotein associated with inflammation and tissue remodeling.44 Elevated levels of CHI3L1 in DCM-HF patients indicate its involvement in the inflammatory and fibrotic processes of the disease. Kardys et al.45 found that CHI3L1 was closely related to the prognosis of patients with chronic heart failure (CHF), with statistical results demonstrating that CHI3L1 levels was the strongest predictor, evidenced by a significant hazard ratio (HR). Lumbers’ team conducted a Mendelian randomization and genome-wide association study on 3019 CHF patients, identifying a positive correlation between CHI3L1 and the occurrence of heart failure.46 These findings suggest CHI3L1 could be a potential therapeutic target for heart failure. Olga et al.47 measured serum CHI3L1 levels in 121 CHF patients and followed them up for 720 days, finding that CHI3L1 was a novel inflammatory marker that increased in CHF and was closely associated with prognosis and complications. Our investigation revealed that CHI3L1 levels were markedly elevated among individuals diagnosed with dilated cardiomyopathy and concomitant heart failure (DCM-HF), as indicated by the Olink proteomics analysis. This finding was further substantiated by ELISA, demonstrating a positive correlation between CHI3L1 levels and DCM-HF.

Plaur (plasminogen activator, urokinase receptor) is a key regulator of extracellular matrix degradation and remodeling, and is fundamental in the progression of heart failure and cardiomyopathy.48 Plaur plays a crucial role in the protease system involved in angiogenesis. It regulates pericellular proteolysis, thereby activating a cascade of proteolytic events.49 Stephen’s animal studies have shown that Plaur contributes to atherosclerosis.50 Previous research has also found increased uPAR expression in peripheral blood mononuclear cells of acute myocardial infarction patients.51 In vitro studies have revealed the role of uPAR in cell adhesion, migration, lipid accumulation, and oxidative stress, while in vivo animal studies have demonstrated the involvement of uPAR in platelet adhesion to activated endothelium, foam cell formation and macrophage accumulation in arterial walls.51 Jessica et al.52 have identified Plaur as a critical inflammatory protein that is associated with heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF). Plaur is an essential protein in both heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) and heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF). These findings emphasize the importance of Plaur in the inflammatory processes underlying different stages of heart failure.52 Our study shows that Plaur is significantly elevated in patients with dilated cardiomyopathy with heart failure (DCM-HF), suggesting its involvement in pathological remodeling processes and its potential as a therapeutic target for the treatment of DCM-HF.

We also identified some important pathways through GO enrichment analysis of significantly differentially expressed proteins, such as multicellular organismal process, PID integrin3 pathway, regulation of biological process, response to stimulus and regulation of cell–cell adhesion. Multicellular organismal process involves a variety of biological processes in multicellular organisms and highlights the importance of coordinated cellular interactions in maintaining cardiac function.53 In DCM-HF, dysregulation of this pathway may lead to aberrant cardiac remodeling and dysfunction. Cell-cell adhesion pathway is essential for maintaining the integrity and function of cardiac tissue.54 Disruption of this pathway may lead to defective intercellular communication and impaired contractile function in DCM-HF. Weng et al.55 demonstrated the critical role of cell–cell adhesion complexes and signaling in cardiovascular health and disease, emphasizing the importance of pathways associated with cell–cell adhesion in DCM-HF. Our study also supports the crucial role for these pathways in DCM.

However, our study has several limitations. First, the sample size tested using the Olink Cardiovascular Panel III was relatively small. Although we expanded the sample size in subsequent validations and conducted additional validation in GEO database, larger cohort studies are needed in the future to confirm our findings. In the future, we plan to further investigate the pathophysiological mechanisms in patients with DCM-HF based on the Olink Proteomics findings.

Conclusion

The significant elevation of SPP1, IGFBP7, F11R, CHI3L1, and Plaur in DCM-HF patients underscores their roles in the pathogenesis of this disease. These proteins are involved in critical processes such as fibrosis, inflammation, endothelial dysfunction, and extracellular matrix remodeling, all of which are pivotal to the development and progression of DCM-HF. Our findings suggest that these proteins may be new biomarkers for the diagnosis and prediction of DCM-HF, and could potentially be targeted for therapeutic intervention.

Association between methylated fatty acids and early pregnancy outcomes in dairy cows: a plasma GC‒MS assay
Authors: Maryam Hemmatzadeh-Dastgerdi, Farid Barati, Mohammad Barati, Azar Baradaran, Abdonnaser Mohebbi, Ebrahim Taghizadeh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractEarly conception failure is a major contributor to infertility in dairy cows. Fatty acids (FAs) play critical roles in maintaining normal reproductive function. This study aimed to determine Long Chain Fatty Acids (LCFAs) and their derivatives in plasma and to explore their association with early pregnancy outcome in high producing dairy cows. Blood samples were collected from 36 inseminated cows at 30 days post-insemination (dpi). Pregnancy was diagnosed at both 30 dpi and 45 dpi. Plasma LCFA levels were quantified via gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (GC–MS). Cows that experienced conception failure or early embryonic death (before 45 dpi; n = 25) had significantly higher concentrations (P < 0.05) of oleic acid (OA), palmitic acid (PA), stearic acid (SA), and linoleic acid (LA) than cows carrying viable embryos at 45 dpi (n = 11). Methylated LCFAs were detected only in the plasma of cows with healthy embryos at 45 dpi. The plasma OA: PA and LA: PA ratios exceeded 1 in cows with conception failure. Principal component analysis indicated that a component including LA and methylated FAs differed between cows that failed to conceive from those with viable embryos at 45 dpi. These findings reveal the composition of LCFAs and their derivatives in the cattle plasma and suggest an association between LCFAs and their methylated derivatives with early conception outcomes in dairy cows.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-57619-7.Keywords: Methylated fatty acid, Early conception failure, Dairy cow, GC‒MSSubject terms: Biochemistry, Physiology, Zoology

Introduction

Long-chain fatty acids (LCFAs) are carboxylic acids with aliphatic chains of 12 to 22 carbons that serve as fundamental building blocks of cellular structure, energy substrates, and signaling molecules in mammals1. The most biologically relevant saturated LCFAs in cattle include palmitic acid (PA; C16:0) and stearic acid (SA; C18:0), whereas key unsaturated LCFAs encompass the monounsaturated oleic acid (OA; cis C18:1ω9) and palmitoleic acid (POA; cis C16:1ω7), as well as the polyunsaturated linoleic acid (LA; cis C18:2 ω6) and alpha-linolenic acid (ALA; cis C18:3 ω3).2The biological activities of these fatty acids are mediated through several receptor systems, including peroxisome proliferator-activated receptors (PPARs), sterol regulatory element binding protein 1 (SREBP1), Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4), and G-protein-coupled receptors such as GPR40 and GPR1203. Endometrial cells have been shown to express both free FA receptor 1 (FFA1/GPR40) and free FA receptor 4 (FFA4/GPR120), and docosahexaenoic acid (DHA), a natural FFA4 agonist, increased intracellular calcium mobilization in bovine endometrial cells4. Notably, n-3 polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs) suppress inflammatory responses in leukocytes by modulating the nuclear factor-κB (NF-κB) synthesis pathway, whereas n-6 PUFAs and saturated fatty acids (SFAs) exert distinct, often opposing, pro-inflammatory effects5,6.

The success of early pregnancy in cattle depends on a precisely orchestrated sequence of events: ovulation of a developmentally competent oocyte, successful fertilization, cleavage formation, activation of the embryonic genome, and elongation of the conceptus7,8. LCFAs profoundly influence each of these stages by modulating oocyte function, embryo development, and the uterine environment. In the female, the FA composition of follicular fluid (FF) directly impacts oocyte quality, with elevated concentrations of saturated LCFAs impairing meiotic maturation, reducing cleavage rates, and compromising subsequent blastocyst formation9,10. Cumulus cells play a critical protective role through stearoyl-CoA desaturase (SCD) activity, converting harmful SFAs to less toxic monounsaturated forms and sequestering excess lipids in triglyceride droplets11. Within the uterus, endometrial epithelial cells exposed to elevated concentrations of PA, SA, or OA at concentrations relevant to lipolytic conditions exhibit reduced cell viability and proliferation while increasing apoptosis and pro-inflammatory cytokine production, particularly interleukin-6 and interleukin-8.12Furthermore, supplementation of endometrial epithelial cells with LCFAs and β-hydroxybutyrate increased the trimethylation of histone H3 at lysine 9 (H3K9me3), an epigenetic modification associated with transcriptional repression, and altered the expression of genes involved in apoptosis, glucose transport, and DNA methylation13. Lipid metabolism within endometrial cells plays a crucial role in the establishment of pregnancy via the cyclooxygenase pathway14. Importantly, embryos derived from oocytes matured under lipotoxic conditions exhibit reduced cell numbers, increased apoptosis, altered transcriptomic and DNA methylation profiles, and reduced cryotolerance, indicating persistent carryover effects that compromise pregnancy establishment10,15. The accumulation of lipids and oxylipins in the uterine lumen during diestrus suggests a local, temporally regulated supply of these molecules to support the developing conceptus16. Dietary supplementation with n-3 PUFAs (e.g., from fish oil or linseed) alters the endometrial transcriptome, downregulating genes involved in prostaglandin F2α synthesis (e.g., PLA2, COX-2) and potentially reducing luteolytic signals, thereby supporting the corpus luteum and early pregnancy17.

Despite various studies on embryonic and endometrial responses to LCFAs, no study has examined these effects at the clinical level during early pregnancy. This study designed to test two hypotheses: (1) determining LCFAs profile and their derivatives in plasma and (2) exploring their association with early pregnancy outcome in high producing dairy cows.

Discussion

Our study focused on early and late conception failure in dairy cattle, periods during which the incidence of failed ovulation, fertilization disruption, and embryonic death is highest. Detailed analysis revealed that conception failure was more frequent in animals with elevated plasma LCFAs, whereas cows carrying healthy embryos at 45 dpi had higher levels of methylated LCFAs in their blood. In all groups, OA was the most abundant FA, while the FA present at the lowest concentration varied among groups. Cows with failed conception had plasma OA: PA and LA: PA ratios greater than 1, in contrast to cows with healthy embryos at 45 dpi.

LCFAs influence bovine reproduction through multiple well defined molecular pathways. One key mechanism is the modulation of prostaglandin and eicosanoid signaling. OA increases the PGE₂:PGF₂α ratio in trophoblast cells by up regulating PTGES1 and down regulating PTGES2 and AKR1B1, favoring PGE₂ production that supports luteal function and early pregnancy18. LA also enhances PGE₂ synthesis and the PGE₂:PGF₂α ratio while increasing interferon tau expression, a critical signal for maternal recognition of pregnancy19. In contrast, conjugated LA reduces both PGE₂ and PGF₂α synthesis in embryos without altering their ratio, indicating a general suppression of cyclooxygenase activity20. Dietary n 3 PUFAs compete with arachidonic acid for cyclooxygenase enzymes, shifting eicosanoid profiles toward less inflammatory mediators and reducing luteolytic PGF₂α pulses17.

Another important pathway is the regulation of the endocannabinoid system, which negatively affects implantation and luteal function. Omega 3 FA supplementation reduces the abundance of n 6 derived endocannabinoid precursors and decreases the expression of cannabinoid receptors CNR1 and CNR2 in granulosa cells and endometrium21. Conjugated LA lowers endometrial expression of CNR2 and the synthesizing enzyme NAPEPLD, while increasing plasma progesterone, providing a direct mechanism for its positive effects on fertility22. In the oviduct, anandamide acting through CB1 receptors inhibits sperm binding and induces release from epithelial cells, a process that can be influenced by dietary FAs23.

Oxidative stress and inflammatory pathways are also differentially modulated by LCFAs. SFAs such as PA and SA activate NF κB and the NLRP3 inflammasome, leading to increased IL 1β and IL 18 in hepatocytes, and similar inflammatory responses occur in endometrial and trophoblast cells24. PA induces reactive oxygen species, ceramide formation, and caspase 3 activation in cumulus cells, resulting in apoptosis11. In contrast, USFAs like OA and DHA exert protective effects: OA reduces lipotoxicity by promoting incorporation of saturated fats into neutral triglycerides, while DHA up regulates antioxidant enzymes and lowers IL 6.25 Maternal essential FA plus CLA supplementation affects neonatal oxidative status, with increased reactive oxygen metabolites and decreased FRAP depending on the specific FA.26

Membrane fluidity and the lipid composition of gametes and reproductive fluids are directly shaped by dietary and circulating LCFAs. Spermatozoa require an optimal balance of PUFAs for membrane fluidity, acrosome reaction, and cryotolerance; a lower n 6:n 3 ratio and higher mitochondrial membrane potential are associated with better progressive motility and fertility27. Oocyte quality is influenced by the FA profile of FF: elevated saturated NEFAs during in vitro maturation (IVM) reduce cleavage and blastocyst rates, whereas OA is less harmful28. L carnitine, which facilitates FA oxidation, improves blastocyst bioenergetics and cryotolerance29. Uterine luminal fluid undergoes dynamic changes in lipid composition across the estrous cycle, with increased LCFAs and oxylipins during late diestrus, preparing the endometrium for conceptus elongation16. The conceptus itself modifies endometrial FA concentrations, increasing SA and OA in the gravid horn30.

LCFAs also directly affect hormonal regulation, including steroidogenesis and gonadotropin secretion. SFAs and PUFAs alter serum estradiol, progesterone, and PGF₂α concentrations; omega 3 rich diets increase estradiol on the day of AI and improve pregnancy rates31. Fish oil plus vitamin E increases progesterone and cholesterol while reducing embryo loss32. Conjugated LA increases plasma progesterone in postpartum cows, likely by reducing luteolytic PGF₂α and down regulating endometrial endocannabinoid receptors22. Free FAs can disrupt granulosa cell steroidogenesis via PPAR mediated pathways; oleic acid, but not PA, reduces FSHR expression and estradiol production33. Adipokines such as leptin, adiponectin, and resistin are modulated by dietary fat and in turn affect reproduction, with plasma resistin positively correlated with follicular growth34.

Oocyte and embryo developmental competence are influenced by LCFAs through direct and epigenetic mechanisms. Elevated saturated NEFAs during IVM cause endoplasmic reticulum stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and increased apoptosis in cumulus oocyte complexes, effects partially reversed by co incubation with OA or by inhibiting FA oxidation10. L carnitine enhances mitochondrial β oxidation, improving blastocyst oxygen consumption and altering the transcriptome of elongation stage embryos29. Conjugated LA during IVM and IVC reduces both PGE₂ and PGF₂α in embryos and improves cryotolerance by modifying membrane lipid composition20,35. Serum levels of triacylglycerols, NEFA, and BHB are correlated with oocyte lipid content, cumulus cell expansion, and the ability to conceive after first calving36. Palmitoleic acid supplementation altered FF composition but did not improve oocyte developmental capacity, indicating that simply changing FA profiles is insufficient without addressing other metabolic factors37.

Endometrial receptivity and conceptus maternal cross talk are also regulated by LCFAs. PA, SA, and OA at concentrations relevant to negative energy balance reduce endometrial epithelial cell viability and proliferation while increasing pro inflammatory cytokines12. Moreover, NEFA and β hydroxybutyrate increase H3K9me3, an epigenetic mark, in endometrial cells, potentially altering gene expression13. Omega 3 PUFA supplementation down regulates endometrial expression of genes involved in PGF₂α synthesis (PLA2, COX 2) and up regulates PPARγ, which may enhance endometrial receptivity17. The conceptus itself modifies its environment: in the gravid horn, SA and OA concentrations are increased, and the ω6:ω3 ratio is higher compared to the non gravid horn30. Endometrial lipid droplets accumulate during diestrus, and oxylipin profiles change, providing local signaling molecules for the developing conceptus16.

Furthermore, metabolic programming through nutritional supplementation with specific LCFAs can produce long lasting effects on reproduction. Calcium salts of n 3 and n 6 FA fed prepartum increase colostrum IgG, milk yield, and shorten days to first estrus38. Extruded linseed improves fertility in a large epidemiological study, reducing days to first AI and days to conception at low intake levels39. PUFA supplementation before fixed time AI increases ovulatory follicle diameter and pregnancy rates in beef heifers40. Schizochytrium oil (rich in DHA) fed peripartum enhances maternal antioxidant capacity, reduces colostral IL 1β, and improves neonatal immune status without affecting birth weight, indicating prenatal programming25. Together, these mechanistic insights demonstrate that the balance among saturated, monounsaturated, and polyunsaturated LCFAs—rather than any single FA—determines reproductive outcomes in cattle.

LCFAs influence angiogenesis in reproductive tissues through several molecular mechanisms. Docosahexaenoic acid (DHA) directly stimulates tube formation in first trimester human trophoblast cells, indicating a pro angiogenic role in placental development41. In bovine granulosa cells, OA up regulates genes involved in circulatory system development, vasculature development, and angiogenesis, suggesting that OA may initiate premature folliculo-luteal transition and vascular remodeling42. Similarly, OA and LA induce angiogenesis in placental trophoblast cells through cellular FA binding proteins, while the cis 9,trans 11 isomer of conjugated LA increases expression of angiopoietin like 4 (ANGPTL4) in extravillous trophoblasts, a key regulator of angiogenesis and lipid metabolism43,44. Additionally, the accumulation of lipid droplets and oxylipins in the bovine endometrium during diestrus may create a local environment that supports vascular remodeling necessary for conceptus implantation16. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that specific LCFAs, particularly DHA, OA, LA, and conjugated LA, can directly or indirectly modulate angiogenic processes in reproductive tissues through gene expression changes, growth factor regulation, and lipid signaling pathways.

PCA revealed that the component consisting of methylated FAs (MS, MP, and ML) was lower in cows with conception failure than in cows with healthy embryos at 45 dpi. MP is derived primarily from plant oils, microbial lipids, and animal tissues, and its methylation can involve specific methyl donors. Although there is not reported study that directly investigate FA methyl esters as treatments or supplements for reproductive outcomes in cattle. However, methyl donors (methionine, choline, betaine, riboflavin, and vitamin B₁₂) are the most relevant to reproductive biology. In periparturient Holstein cows, supplementation with rumen‑protected methionine (22 g/d), choline chloride (10 g/d), betaine (3 g/d), riboflavin (96 mg/d), and vitamin B₁₂ (1.4 mg/d) increased serum methionine by 26%, decreased the lysine‑to‑methionine ratio by 21%, and suppressed the progression of hepatic lipid accumulation (relative change: +3.57% in controls vs. − 0.29% in treated cows)45. By providing methyl groups for one‑carbon metabolism, these donors facilitate phosphatidylcholine synthesis and very low‑density lipoprotein (VLDL) assembly in the liver, thereby reducing fatty liver. The improved hepatic health may indirectly benefit reproductive outcome (e.g., pregnancy rate, oocyte quality). In contrast, 3‑methylhistidine and 1‑methylhistidine appear solely as biomarkers of muscle protein turnover in periparturient cows, with no direct reproductive effect46,47. Additionally, 3‑methyladenine was used only as an autophagy inhibitor in cell culture experiments48.

MP occurs naturally in palm oil, marine oils, and microalgae, and can also be obtained through methanolysis of PA. It is frequently isolated from endophytic Bacillus strains and other lipid-producing microbes49,50. MP exerts anti-inflammatory and antifibrotic effects by suppressing macrophage activity and the production of inflammatory cytokines51. It confers cardioprotection via activation of GPR40 and PI3K/Akt signaling52. MP also acts as a chemosensitizer, enhancing the efficacy of sorafenib in hepatocarcinoma models53, and shows nematocidal activity, supporting its potential in agricultural biocontrol49.

MS is obtained from SA‑rich oils such as cocoa butter, shea butter, and tallow, as well as microbial cultures that co‑produce MP and MS.54 It has been isolated from Citrullus colocynthis fruit55 and can be produced synthetically through transesterification of SA. MS promotes tissue repair and regeneration by stimulating mesenchymal stem cell migration and cartilage healing via the Vav1/ROCK2 pathway56, and it exhibits moderate nematicidal activity when combined with MP 49.

ML is abundant in lauric acid‑rich oils, notably coconut and palm‑kernel oils, and has been isolated from Schinus terebinthifolia fruits57. It is also produced industrially via microbial or chemical esterification of lauric acid. ML displays potent acaricidal and larvicidal effects against Amblyomma sculptum and Aedes aegypti, demonstrating its value as a natural pesticide57,58. Additionally, ML undergoes microbial biotransformation to dodecanedioic acid by Candida tropicalis, indicating its industrial and biotechnological importance59. However, despite these documented biological activities, no studies have reported any effects of these FA methyl esters (MP, MS, ML) on reproductive outcomes in cattle, including oocyte quality, embryonic development, or pregnancy rates.

This study revealed differences in plasma LCFAs and their methylated derivatives between pregnant and non-pregnant cows at 45 dpi. Designing cause-and-effect experiments with specific methyl FAs and investigating their optimized concentrations may confirm these findings and help improve pregnancy outcomes in dairy cows. Further experiments should be conducted to elucidate the possible mechanisms by which these FA methyl esters influence early pregnancy outcomes in cattle.

In summary, GC–MS analysis identified distinct plasma profiles of OA, PA, SA, and their methylated derivatives in cattle. The presence of methylated LCFAs was associated with healthy embryos at 45 dpi and established pregnancy. Cows with early pregnancy failure exhibited plasma OA: PA, SA: PA, and LA: PA ratios exceeding 1.

Methods

Animals and location

The study was conducted from February to May 2022 at the Zagros Milk and Meat Complex near Shahrekord, Iran (32°19′32″N, 50°51′52″E). Holstein cows were fed a balanced ration formulated for high-producing dairy cows, consisting of hay, alfalfa, corn silage, concentrate, and beet pulp, to meet the energy, protein, and mineral requirements recommended by the National Research Council for dairy cattle (Table 3)60. Water was provided ad libitum.

Study design

The estrus of all the selected cows was synchronized via the Heatsynch protocol and artificially inseminated with the same batch number of sperm. From total number of 236 inseminated cows, 36 plasma samples were randomly collected at 30 dpi and subjected to GC-MS assay. Based on the results of the ultrasound exam at 30 (28–33) and 45 (43–47) dpi, the cows were categorized into three groups: cows that were not pregnant at 30 dpi (n = 15), cows that lost their embryo between 30 and 45 dpi (n = 10), and cows with a viable embryo at 45 dpi (n = 11).

Pregnancy diagnosis and blood sampling

Pregnancy was diagnosed via a B-mode ultrasound device (7.5 MHz; V9, Shenzhen Empower Electronic Technology Co., Ltd., China) at 30 and 45 dpi. The embryo’s heartbeat, arrangement of the chorioallantoic membrane, and fluid echogenicity were assessed for embryonic health. At 30 dpi, blood samples were collected from the tail vein into tubes containing ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid. The tubes were then centrifuged at 5000 rpm for 10 min to separate the plasma, which was subsequently stored at −80 °C freezer until further analysis.

Plasma FA extraction and measurement

For FA extraction, liquid–liquid extraction was performed on each sample. Briefly, 1.0 mL of n-hexane was added to each 5.0 mL tube containing 1.0 mL of serum and vortexed. The tubes were left undisturbed for 1.5 h to allow phase separation. The upper phase, containing FAs and other n-hexane-soluble substances, was carefully collected. To remove residual water, 0.01 g of calcium carbonate was added as a drying agent and thoroughly mixed. The mixtures were then centrifuged at 5,000 rpm, and the resulting supernatants were collected. Subsequently, 200 µL of 0.004 M ethyl tridecanoate was added as an internal standard, and the samples were analyzed.

For GC–MS analysis, the extracted solutions were analyzed using an Agilent 6890 GC–MS instrument (USA). Separation was performed on an HP-5 capillary column (30 m × 0.25 mm, 0.25 μm film thickness). Helium (99.99% purity) was used as the carrier gas at a flow rate of 1.5 cm³/min. The column temperature program was as follows: initial temperature of 80 °C held for 2 min, increased to 290 °C at a rate of 3 °C/min, and held at 290 °C for 10 min. The injector split ratio was set at 10:1, and the ion source temperature for mass spectrometry was maintained at 250 °C. A sample of the GC‒MS data is reported in Supplement A.

A sample of the ration was also subjected to GC‒MS to explore the LCFAs, and the data are included in supplementary file B.

Data analysis

PCA was conducted on the combined data without any grouping. The PCA method was employed, with “prior = one” used for estimating prior communality. The principal axis method was used to extract the components, followed by Promax rotation. Components with eigenvalues greater than 1 and the results of the scree test were considered significant. Pearson correlation coefficients (r2) were calculated for the loaded components and components. In the rotated component pattern, an item was considered to load on a specific component if the component loading was 0.40 or higher. To evaluate the influence of components on conception status, standardized regression coefficients were computed via the rotated component pattern table for components 1 and 2.

Components 1 and 2 were selected and retained for rotation, collectively explaining 69.1% of the total variance. On the basis of the established criteria, PAs, OAs, and SAs were associated with the first component. On the other hand, the second component exhibited loadings for ML, MP, LA, and MS.

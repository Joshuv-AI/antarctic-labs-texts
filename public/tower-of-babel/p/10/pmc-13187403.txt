Multi-strategy enhanced orchard algorithm for optimal integration of renewable energy sources and EV charging stations in microgrids
Authors: Kandasamy V, Sathesh Kumar Thirumalaisamy, Mathankumar M, Rajnarayanan PN
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe integration of Electric Vehicle Charging Stations (EVCS) with renewable energy (RE)-based microgrids (MGs) introduces significant operational challenges due to the stochastic charging behavior of EVs and the intermittent nature of Renewable Energy Sources (RES) such as solar photovoltaic (PV) and wind turbines (WTs). These uncertainties may have a negative impact on voltage stability, power balance, and system reliability. To solve these problems, this research paper presents a Multi-Strategy Enhanced Orchard Optimization Algorithm (MSEOA) which combines several search strategies to enhance exploration capabilities, faster convergence, and more accurate solutions than the conventional Orchard Algorithm (OA). The suggested approach simultaneously determines the optimal sizing and placement of RES units and EVCS while coordinating EV charging (EVC) behavior. A multi-objective optimization (MOO) framework is formulated to minimize voltage deviation, real power losses (PLs), and tie-line power deviation in the MG. The optimization variables include the capacities and locations of solar PV, WTs, and EVCS units, while EVC management is regulated using coefficients associated with solar irradiance, wind velocity, and demand ratio. The suggested framework is implemented in MATLAB and tested on a general MG test system using four operating conditions. The performance of the suggested MSEOA based on the simulation results shows that the MSEOA approach drastically diminishes real PLs, voltage variance, as well as tie-line power variation by 17.8%, 14.6%, and 12.3%, respectively, compared to the conventional OA. In addition, the algorithm converges more quickly with a computational time of 18.92 s, which supports the effectiveness of the algorithm in the optimal planning and operation management of EV-based renewable MGs.Keywords: Battery energy storage systems, Distributed generation, Electric vehicles, Electric vehicle charging station, Multiobjective optimization, Renewable energySubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering

Introduction

Background and motivation

The rapid electrification of transportation and increasing penetration of RES are transforming conventional distribution networks into active and complex MGs1. Combining the solar PV systems, wind energy conversion systems, and EVCS is associated with major operational challenges because of their stochastic and intermittent nature2,3. Although these technologies contribute to emission reduction and sustainable development, their uncoordinated integration may adversely affect voltage stability, tie-line power exchange, feeder congestion, and system losses4–6. MGs with high RES penetration frequently experience voltage deviations and reverse power flow (PF), particularly during low-load and high-generation periods7,8. At the same time, massive EVC demands pose erratic bursts of loading, causing a decline in the quality of power and augmented network losses9–11. The simultaneous occurrence of variable generation and stochastic EV demand creates significant complexity in planning and operation12,13. Thus, it is also necessary to optimize the size and position of RES and EVCS to guarantee safe, efficient, and stable MG operation14.

The significance of overcoming those challenges is also supported by the fact that EVs and RE systems are rapidly expanding globally. The increasing adoption of EVs is expected to significantly raise electricity demand and introduce additional charging loads on distribution networks. At the same time, the penetration of RES such as PV and wind is continuously increasing due to global decarbonization initiatives and RE policies. The concurrent nature of the intermittent generation of renewable sources and stochastic charging of the EVs can present a number of operational issues that can be experienced in the system and include voltage instability, feeder overload, and augmented system PLs unless handled adequately. Consequently, proper planning of the most suitable locations and sizes of the RES and EVCS is gaining more and more significance to assure stable, efficient, and sustainable MG operation.

Because of renewable intermittency and stochastic EVC behavior, the conventional deterministic planning approaches frequently do not suffice to represent them. Metaheuristic optimization methods like Genetic Algorithm (GA), Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), and Differential Evolution (DE) have thus been popularized. Although these methods show reasonable performance in single-objective optimization problems, they usually have premature convergence, a lack of exploration–exploitation ratio, and a lack of strength when applied to high-dimensional multi-objective MG planning problems. Besides, the integrated implementation of the RES and the EVCS is a system that implies conflicting goals. Reduction of the voltage deviation could demand higher penetration of distributed generation, but economic issues can limit installation capacity. Equally, minimization of tie-line power variation could be in conflict with active minimization of PL. The resolution of such trade-offs implies the use of sophisticated MOO models that can guarantee the stability of convergence and diversity of solutions.

Recent developments in the field of metaheuristic optimization have been more and more directed towards variants of classifying the classical algorithms to overcome the problem of premature convergence, local optima stagnation, and the disappearance of diversity in the population when it comes to complex engineering problems. Traditional search systems tend to fail to provide a good exploration–exploitation tradeoff, especially in high-dimensional and multi-objective search spaces. Towards these shortcomings, scholars have reformulated the design of important operators like selection scheme, guiding scheme, mutation operator, and population management plan. Specifically, Fitness-Distance Balance (FDB)-based methods have been largely embraced to improve the process of selecting and guiding the populace on leaders, thus improving global search and convergence. Swarm and evolutionary algorithms with improved variants that included FDB mechanisms, adaptive survivor algorithms, and dynamic crowding strategies have shown remarkable improvements in optimization problems in benchmark and power systems15–20. These studies collectively indicate that operator-level modification plays a critical role in improving optimization stability and scalability. Motivated by these developments, the present work adopts a multi-strategy enhancement approach for the OA by integrating adaptive exploration–exploitation regulation, elite opposition-based learning, and dynamic mutation mechanisms, specifically tailored for stochastic multi-objective MG planning.

Related work

A comprehensive investigation of EVCS and research gaps related to its design, refinement and charging infrastructure utilization are recorded in detail by21–30.

According to Ye et al.21, the profit level of charging station can be increased by using a methodology called Centralized Allocation and Decentralized Execution (CADE). By this technique, EVs are allocated optimally based on the charging or waiting spots, which helps in increasing the scalability as well as computational efficiency of the system. With improvisation of Reinforcement Learning (RL) algorithms, CADE methodology was able to provide additional value to the existing system. For charging stations connected with the grid, PV System and building load, Huu et al.22 proposes a three stage allocation process for effective power distribution. This technique simplifies the multivariable optimization problem and aids in successful achievement of peak shaving, valley filling with reduced load fluctuations. A multiple objective strategic architecture for optimizing the allocation in EVCS coupled with RES has been suggested by Ali et al.23, with an aim of reducing the voltage deviations and energy losses and thereby increasing the EV User satisfaction. Notably, a remarkable decrease of 96% is observed in voltage deviations and about 74% in energy losses, as against conventional uncontrolled EVC system. As an alternative to this, Queueing Model is introduced by Sadhukhan et al.24 for optimal EVCS placement. This approach focused on reducing the losses in electrical grids and accounting for the transmission losses while going to the charging stations. To estimate the necessary Charging Station (CS) count and its associated cost, the CS Utilization Factor is very essential. Thus, this Queueing approach assures of an effective charging framework, with enhanced CS Utilization Factor and reduced load variations. Similarly, Asna et al.25 have also used queuing theory approach for designing a technique for Fast Charging Stations (FCS). Various factors considered are the impact on the distribution system (DS) and environment, station sizing and economics and finally the convenience of the vehicle owner.

For Modular Multilevel Converter (MMC) based EVCS, it is necessary to maintain the module voltage and grid currents within the standard values during charging demand fluctuations. This can be achieved by integrating Load Management (LM) and PF Management (PFM) as suggested by Gumrukcu et al.26. For the extra losses contributed by active balancing of PFM, the LM fixes an optimal charging schedule for EVs and EV allocations, thereby reducing the stress on the charging architecture. Amer et al.27 have considered the economic factors like revenue linked with FCS and Distributed Generations (DG) and their geographical locations for proposing a novel scoring system. Bald Eagle Search Algorithm (BESA) is another optimization method presented by Yuvaraj et al.28 for identification of favorable sites for EVCS and DSTATCOM (Distributed Static Compensators) amidst the DS. For sizing and allocation of Dynamic Wireless Charging (DWC) architecture, Fathollahi et al.29 have established a MINLP. The common issues associated with conventional charging techniques are addressed by this introduced scheme, in addition to making the system accessible to all types of EVs. For Vehicle to Grid (V2G) based Charging Stations, many challenges arise in signal communications owing to the variant parking nature of EVs. To tackle this problem, Zhou et al.30 have formulated a Dynamic Partitioning Model (DPM), which includes a two level dispatch mechanism for Virtual Power Plants (VPP). This DPM model takes care of RES integration in addition to maximizing the profit of VPP. Moreover, to manage Probabilistic Optimal PF (POPF) optimization problem in modern power grids, Mohamed A.M Shaheen, et al.34 proposes a Catch Fish Optimization method (CFOA). Finally, an Enhanced Transient Search Optimization (ETSO) technique was demonstrated by Mohamed A.M Shaheen, et al.35 that helps to solve the ORPD problem with integrated EV, effectively minimizes active PL and voltage deviation at load buses. Although, a modified version of TLBO and the JAYA algorithm developed by Nandini, et al.36 that helps to minimize the VRP index of the DS. Krishnamurthy et al.37 developed an MINLP module that helps to solve the economic dispatch of RES-hybrid systems in EVCS. To minimize the losses in the DS, Kashif Habib et al.38 established a hybrid optimization approach named Hybrid PSO (HPSO) technique. To ensure system reliability and sustainability, Esakkiappan, et al.39 suggested a Binary Quantum-based Puma Optimizer (IBQP) to minimize the cost of the system. As a next step, to achieve better results and reduce losses, Marzieh Poshtyafteh, et al.40 was decided to design with the placement method using MHHO algorithm and the FDM.

Recent studies have further explored dynamic and stochastic management of RES-integrated distribution networks with EV participation. Lotfi et al.41 introduced a dynamic feeder reconfiguration plan that involves EVCSs, RESs and demand response programs through a better Whale Optimization Algorithm (IWOA) with Levy-flight-based mutation to reduce the Energy Not Supplied (ENS) and operational costs. Later on, Lotfi42 presented a reliability-based feeder reconfiguration model utilizing a better Crow Search Algorithm that incorporated a fuzzy logic designed to enhance fault recovery performance in DG- and EV-based networks. Also, Lotfi43 presented a stochastic bi-level optimization model coping with demand and market uncertainties based on a multi-objective PSO (MPSO), to handle the reconfiguration of the distribution networks. Though, these methods are effective in reliability improvement and dynamic network reconfiguration, they are more concerned with operational reconfiguration algorithms instead of coordinated long-term planning of the concurrent optimal sizing and placement of RES and EVC facilities in a unified stochastic multi-objective model.

Recent advancements have introduced efficient many-objective optimization algorithms for complex power system applications. Khunkitti et al.44 developed many-objective marine predators (MaMPA) algorithm that combines the non-dominated sorting and crowding to suitably address multi- and many-objective optimal PF problems. In addition, Wichitkrailat et al.45 use an algorithmic crayfish optimization algorithm (COA) to optimally size and locate multiple battery energy storage systems (ESS) and optimize effects of voltage regulation, peak demand reduction, and PLs. Though these approaches have good optimization capabilities, they have been largely limited to OPF-based analysis, or standalone component optimisation, but with no long-term framework on how to deploy stochastic RES and EVCS together. Merits and limitations of existing methods.

Even though the studies reviewed report significant increases in the voltage regulation, loss reduction, enhanced reliability, and cost-efficient performance, there are some inherent limitations. First, numerous of the works use deterministic formulations or scenario-constrained stochastic modeling, limiting their use in the highly uncertain MG setting of fluctuation in RES generation and EV demand. Second, a large portion of the literature treats sizing, placement, reconfiguration, and operational control as isolated problems, thereby neglecting coordinated long-term infrastructure planning. Third, while advanced metaheuristic algorithms report improved convergence speed or diversity preservation, most studies do not provide rigorous statistical validation or scalability analysis under high-dimensional multi-objective conditions. Furthermore, conflicting objectives such as voltage stability, tie-line power smoothing, and loss minimization are often addressed independently rather than within a unified trade-off framework. These limitations indicate the necessity for a robust, scalable, and stochastic multi-objective planning methodology capable of simultaneously optimizing RES and EVCS deployment within an integrated MG infrastructure context.

Research gap and novel contributions

Although a lot of research has been conducted on EVCS allocation, dynamic feeder reconfiguration, many-objective OPF optimization, and storage planning of MGs with RES-integrated MGs, there are still a number of limitations in coordinated long-term stochastic infrastructure planning. Numerous studies emphasize single-objective optimization or consider sizing and placement as independent and in many cases are deterministic. Besides, traditional metaheuristic algorithms are often prone to premature convergence and low robustness in solving multi-objective, high dimensional planning problems. To eliminate these gaps, this paper suggests a MSEOA to optimally size and place RES and EVCS simultaneously. The suggested algorithm uses adaptive exploration–exploitation control, elite opposition-based learning, and dynamic mutation mechanisms to enhance accuracy of convergence and solution diversity. A MOO model is created in a holistic manner to reduce the variation of voltages, Tie-line power variation and active losses of power under stochastic operating conditions. Comparative and statistical studies also reveal better robustness and scalability as opposed to well established evolutionary algorithms.

System model

The significant problem faced during the integration of Solar PV and WT units along with EVCS in a MG is the inherent uncertainty of RES. Primarily, the incorporation of PV and WT components into the grid introduces uncertainty in the generation of power. This in turn causes anomalies in PF between the utility network and MG, commonly called tie line control. The inconsistency in the tie-line control needs to be addressed effectively, failing which may cause severe voltage deviations leading to MG instability. If the tie line control is reduced to compensate for these issues, it may lessen the self-sufficiency of the MG and increase the stress on the utility grid. Hence, it is crucial to strike a perfect balance between them, for which optimization of tie line power is modelled as secondary objective in the suggested framework. Through this arrangement, the complexities reported can be tackled by coordinating technical problem mitigation and the autonomy of MG. This scheme helps in transforming the system into more reliable and resilient energy system.

To enhance the network effectiveness and efficiency, the primary duty is to determine optimal size and location for these units. At the same time, constraints are one of the important considerations in the modelling, as they confirm whether the solutions obtained are adhering to the particular requirements or limitations. Fig. 1 represents the distribution network with topographical allocation of Solar PV, WT and EVCS components. The planning architecture for this distribution network comprises of optimization methodologies, mathematical modelling, various constraints and ambiguities to find out the optimal size and location for PV, WT and EVCS components. Through this framework and modelling, it is expected to make the network efficient, sustainable and resilient to increasing integration of RES and EVCS into the grid.

Introduction to the MSEOA framework

The conventional OA is a population-based meta-heuristic optimization method inspired by the natural growth behavior of trees in an orchard46. Candidate’s solutions in this algorithm are modeled as trees and the evolvement of the trees is guided by biological rules like seedling growth, screening and grafting. Although the traditional OA provides good exploration capability, it may experience slow convergence and premature stagnation when applied to complex MOO problems. To address these drawbacks, this paper suggests an improved MSEOA. The suggested algorithm presents a few adjustments to enhance the efficiency of the search, the rate at which the algorithm converges and the stability of the solution when it is applied to resolve the optimal placement and sizing of RES and EVCS in a MG. To begin with, the suggested MSEOA includes a MOO structure, which would concurrently address the minimization of voltage deviation, minimization of tie-line power deviation, and minimization of real PL. Because these objectives are different in their units and magnitudes, normalization and weighted aggregation strategy is used to merge them into a single fitness function (FF). Second, the algorithm improves the balance of exploration and exploitation using various operators of search. The seedling growth operator carries out local search about putative solutions to augment exploitation ability whereas the grafting operator is used to produce alternative candidate solutions by blending features of various trees and thereby augment the search space exploration. Third, there is a screening mechanism that is used to separate trees into weak and strong candidates according to their fitness values. To ensure the diversity of population and prevent premature convergence, weak trees are substituted with seedlings produced. Lastly, an elitism methodology is used to maintain the most competent solutions at every iteration so that only quality candidate solutions survive the optimization process. These improvements ensure that the suggested MSEOA has better convergence behavior and increase in solution reliability than the traditional OA, as well as other evolutionary algorithms, in the optimal allocation and sizing problem of RES and EVCS in MG systems.

As demonstrated in Fig. 2, the identification of different charging locations based on these policies is achieved using this MSEOA approach. To resolve the subset problem of sizing and allocation of EVCS inside the distribution network, suitable policies are formulated to guide the EVs to locate a specific site for charging. The choice of location and policy decision are influenced by the most favorable OFs. The specialty of the meta-heuristic algorithm is its ability to employ suitable operators which can make the solutions more optimal, inspite of the random nature. The operators chosen confirms a gradual alignment towards the better outcomes, though they behave randomly. In general, any optimization problem requires wide solution spaces and operators competent for extensive exploration. Meta-heuristic algorithms can effectively balance both exploration (the process of adventuring new solution areas) and exploitation (the process of investigating potential solutions). These exploring and exploiting activities are treated as distinct components in the MSEOA algorithm. Existing solutions are improved further via local search conducted by the exploitation operator called seedling growth operator. The exploration process happens whenever the randomly generated solutions replace the weaker solutions. Another versatile operator called Grafting can perform both exploration as well as exploitation. In the algorithm functioning, novel elements are introduced to facilitate exploration at the beginning, while at later iteration instants; convergence towards best solutions is being focused. Next, the screening process filters the solutions having higher growth rate and superior optimality, thereby enabling furthermore exploration in the following operations.

The EVC demand is modeled considering the random arrival and departure of EVs at charging stations. Discussion

The results of the simulation indicate the effectiveness of the suggested MSEOA to enhance performance of the MG in terms of operations. The obtained results show that the optimal placement and sizing of RES and EVCS significantly enhance the voltage profile and reduce system PLs. Under the optimized configuration, the voltage deviation is reduced to 0.024 p.u., which is considerably lower than the values obtained using traditional OA (0.051 p.u.), WOA (0.058 p.u.), PSO (0.065 p.u.), and GA (0.078 p.u.). This improvement indicates that the suggested optimization strategy effectively enhances voltage stability across the network. Similarly, the real PL is minimized to 0.187 MW using the suggested MSEOA, whereas traditional OA, WOA, PSO, and GA produce higher losses of 0.221 MW, 0.249 MW, 0.276 MW, and 0.312 MW, respectively. This reduction in PL can be attributed to the optimal placement of RES units closer to high-demand buses, which reduces line current and associated transmission losses. Besides, the suggested solution has the minimal tie-line power deviation of 0.071 MW, which implies enhanced stability in power transfer between the MG and the utility grid. Moreover, convergence analysis indicates that MSEOA takes 63 iterations to arrive to the optimum solution, among the traditional OA, WOA, PSO, and GA, it takes 88, 95, 105, and 120, respectively. The higher convergence rate is possible because of the multi-strategy enhancement mechanisms involved in the suggested algorithm that enhances the balance between exploration and exploitation of the search process. In general, these findings affirm that the suggested MSEOA offers better optimization performance and computational efficiency than the traditional evolutionary algorithms in optimizing planning of RES and EVCS in MG systems.

Practical applicability to real-world grids

The suggested optimization model may be implemented to the real-world MG planning issues related to the integration of EVCS and RE systems. In practical distribution networks, the changing renewable generation, the changing EVC demand and the constraints of operating the network may affect the performance of the system. Although the present study is validated through simulation, the suggested MSEOA framework provides a flexible optimization approach that can support decision-making for the optimal placement and sizing of RES and EVCS in practical MG environments. Furthermore, the ability of the algorithm to achieve faster convergence and improved voltage regulation suggests that it can assist distribution network planners in managing increasing renewable penetration and EVC loads. The further research can also examine the large-scale networks and real-operational data to respond to the other practical issues related to the real-world application.

Limitations of the study

Despite the fact that the suggested MSEOA-based optimization framework allows achieving considerable progress in the context of MG planning and operation, some limitations must be admitted. To begin with, the simulation studies on a typical MG test system have been performed to determine the performance of the suggested method. Thus, the application in the context of a large-scale distribution network might need additional verification with real operating data. Second, the stochastic behavior of the demand of RES and EVC is based on a set of predetermined scenarios that might not be able to embody all the uncertainties that are faced in the real operating conditions. Moreover, the optimization algorithm can also have differences in computation speed on bigger networks with more decision variables. Furthermore, the study primarily focuses on technical performance indicators such as voltage deviation, tie-line power deviation, and PLs, while economic aspects such as installation cost and operational cost are not extensively analyzed. Future studies may integrate economic considerations and additional system components to further enhance the applicability and robustness of the suggested optimization framework.

Conclusion

The incorporation of RES and EVCS into the system of MGs creates enormous operational problems because of the variability of renewable power generation and the stochastic character of EVC demand. To mitigate these issues, this research paper suggested a MSEOA of optimum size and position of RES and EVCS and at the same time controlled the behavior of EVC. A MOO framework was formulated to minimize voltage deviation, real PLs, and tie-line power deviations in the MG. The suggested model was implemented and validated in the MATLAB environment under multiple operating scenarios. Simulation results demonstrate that the suggested MSEOA achieves improved optimization performance compared with conventional algorithms such as GA, PSO, WOA, and traditional OA. The suggested method significantly reduces voltage deviation, minimizes real PLs, and improves tie-line power control while achieving faster convergence with a computational time of 18.92 s. These results confirm the effectiveness and computational efficiency of the suggested optimization approach. Beyond the obtained numerical improvements, the suggested framework provides an effective planning strategy for the coordinated integration of RE systems and EVC infrastructure in MGs. The optimization approach can support distribution network planners and MG operators in improving voltage stability, reducing network losses, and managing power exchange with the utility grid under increasing EV penetration and renewable generation. Hence, the suggested method serves as the practical decision-support tool to make the future operational efficiency, reliability, and sustainability of the smart MG systems more efficient.

Future work

Future research may focus on extending the suggested optimization framework by incorporating ESS to further improve the operational flexibility of the MG and effectively manage the stochastic behavior of RES and EVC demand. ESS integration has the ability to store surplus RE during the high generation periods and provide power during peak demand conditions and hence enhanced energy balancing, system stability and overall reliability of the MG. Moreover, the suggested MSEOA framework can be tested on bigger distribution networks, including IEEE 69-bus system in the future to better show how scalable and robust the suggested approach can be in more complicated system configurations. The use of larger test systems will help validate the effectiveness of the algorithm when dealing with increased system size, higher EV penetration levels, and more complex network constraints. Furthermore, future research may incorporate economic considerations, including installation cost, operational cost, and lifecycle cost of RE systems and EVC infrastructure, to develop a more comprehensive optimization framework. The use of real operational datasets and large-scale distribution networks can further enhance the practical applicability of the suggested approach for MG planning and management.

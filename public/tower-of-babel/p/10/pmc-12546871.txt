Comparative evaluation of Hyaluronic acid and injectable platelet-rich fibrin as a non-surgical approach for management of black triangle
Authors: Mansi Pal, Nand Lal, Umesh Pratap Verma, Anjani Kumar Pathak
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AimsTo compare and evaluate the clinical efficacy of Hyaluronic Acid and Injectable Platelet Rich Fibrin (i-PRF) in the management of gingival black triangles using clinical and photographic analysis over 6 months.Material and methodThirty patients (15 in each group) were evaluated in this comparative study. Patients were divided into two groups: Group(HA) and Group(i-PRF). Injections were administered at baseline, 3 weeks, and 6 weeks. Black triangle height and area were measured at baseline, 1 month, 3 months, and 6 months using a UNC-15 probe and ImageJ software. Data were analyzed using repeated measures ANOVA and Tukey's post hoc test.ResultsBoth the HA and i-PRF groups showed significant reductions in black triangle height and area over a 6-month period. HA reduced height by 0.80 mm at 1 month (p = 0.067), 1.47 mm at 3 months (p < 0.001), and 2.00 mm at 6 months (p < 0.001), while i-PRF reduced height by 1.20 mm (p = 0.003), 1.80 mm (p < 0.001), and 2.27 mm (p < 0.001) at the same intervals. Area reductions followed similar patterns. No significant intergroup differences were observed.ConclusionHA and i-PRF are effective, minimally invasive, and biocompatible treatments for managing black triangles. HA offers ease of use and patient comfort, while i-PRF is biologically driven and cost-effective. Material selection should be based on clinical conditions and patient preferences.

Introduction

The aesthetic appearance of the smile is essential for shaping self-confidence and interpersonal connections. As the demand for aesthetically pleasing dental reconstruction grows, clinicians are expected to go beyond restoring function.1,2 A key element in this balance is the interdental papilla, the small visible tissue between adjacent teeth. Ideally, this tissue is characterized by a pink colour, firm texture, and triangular shape in the anterior region.3 The structure of the interdental papilla depends on the contact points of adjacent teeth, the width of the interproximal areas, and the positioning of the Cemento-Enamel Junction (CEJ).4

When this papilla is lost, it leads to a "black triangle.5 Black triangles develop as a result of various factors like age, periodontal disease, malocclusion, interproximal contacts, and trauma.6, 7, 8 Besides being a cosmetic concern, they can trap food, plaque buildup, and impact pronunciation. The complex anatomy of the interdental area is sensitive and weak, making it highly risk to inflammation, which can lead to recession, bone deterioration, or both due to inadequate blood supply.9,10

Traditional treatments like periodontal surgeries or orthodontic interventions can be invasive and don't always offer long-term success.11,12 Hence, non-surgical, minimally invasive solutions like Hyaluronic Acid (HA) and Injectable Platelet-Rich Fibrin (i-PRF) have gained interest.

HA is a naturally occurring substance in the body known for its ability to hold moisture, promote tissue repair, and wound healing by stimulating cell proliferation, migration, tissue hydrodynamics, and interaction with growth factors.13 It controls osmotic equilibrium while promoting tissue hydration and elasticity, which helps in sustaining tissue architecture and homeostasis, which is advantageous in tissue regeneration.14 Hyaluronic acid (HA) gels, often derived from Streptococcus species, are formulated using advanced cross-linking technologies. Their strength and durability are enhanced by improving cross-linking density and biodegradability.15

On the other hand, i-PRF, an advanced form of PRF developed through slower, low-speed centrifugation and shorter durations, preserves more growth factors and regenerative cells.16 This autologous injectable gel releases healing proteins like PDGF and TGF-β over time, promoting tissue regeneration, fibroblast activity, and collagen formation. i-PRF has outperformed older PRF methods in tissue healing and rejuvenation.17

Given these developments, this study aims to compare the clinical effectiveness of i-PRF versus HA injections have been individually investigated in treating black triangles, direct comparative evidence is limited. Both approaches offer non-surgical, patient-friendly alternatives that could redefine soft tissue aesthetics in dentistry.

Material and methods

All the patients who presented with a complain of black triangle or interdental papilla loss in maxillary anterior region, to the Periodontology out-patient department (OPD) at Faculty of Dental Sciences, KGMU were screened for inclusion to this study.

The present study was designed as a randomized controlled trial single blinded (Outcome assessor). Patients seeking treatment for gingival black triangles were enrolled in the study and informed about the aim, objectives, and purpose of this study. After screening based on eligibility criteria, patients were enrolled and divided into two groups, and one was treated with Hyaluronic acid injection, while the other was treated with (i-PRF). A total of 30 participants, 15 participants in each group included, with 9 females and 6 males in group (HA) and 8 females and 7 males in (i-PRF) group. Patients were clinically assessed at baseline, 1 month, and 6 months follow-up. All enrolled participants completed the study follow-up visits, and no data were missing during the trial.

Ethical clearance/approval and informed consent

Ethical approval for the research protocol was obtained from the Institutional Ethics Committee of King George's Medical University, U.P., Lucknow. (Ref. Code: XIX-PGTSC-IIA/P51).

Consort flow chart

Inclusion criteria: Patient above 18 years of age with papillary deficiency types I or II, according to Nordland and Tarnow classification (1998).1 Teeth free from caries, proximal restorations, fixed prosthesis or orthodontic appliances and systemically healthy patients were selected.Image 1

Exclusion criteria: The study excluded patients with Papillary deficiency types III according to Nordland and Tarnow classification 1998.1, Open contacts between affected teeth, subjects suffering from any systemic diseases or systemic conditions, patients with a history of smoking or active smokers, pregnant/lactating women, site with CAL ≥4 mm and tobacco intake, and alcoholics, history of drug intake predisposing to gingival enlargement, ongoing or recent (≤6 months) orthodontic treatment, traumatic oral hygiene practices, or periodontal surgery at the study site within the past 6 months were excluded.

Pre-procedural patient preparation

Each patient underwent a full-mouth scaling and root planing session include informed consent, baseline clinical parameters.

Clinical procedures

Minimum 1 week after SRP to allow soft tissue healing following confirmation of good oral hygiene, treatment was initiated after pre-rinse with 5 % Povidone mouthwash. The black triangle was then measured clinically using a periodontal probe (UNC-15) from the tip of the papilla to the contact point of the adjacent teeth (height) and the black triangle (width) at the base of the triangle. Then, standardized digital photographs were taken before the procedure at a perpendicular angle to the area of interest. Topical anesthesia was applied prior to injection. HA group received HA dermal filler (Restylane-Lidocaine cross-linked Hyaluronic Acid Filler, Galderma S.A., Sweden, FDA approved in 2012),18 with a HA concentration of 20 mg/ml with 0.3 % lidocaine incorporated into the syringe (30G) itself. Injections were administered 2–3 mm below the papilla tip and angled directed coronally at 45° to the long axis of the tooth, with the bevel facing apically. A precise amount (0.1 mL) of HA gel was administered. Then the papilla was gently massaged in a coronal direction for approximately 1 min. While the i-PRF group received freshly prepared autologous i-PRF following Choukroun's protocol.17 for this, 10 mL of blood was drawn from the antecubital region and divided into two 5 mL sterile plastic tubes without any anticoagulants. These tubes were then placed in a centrifuge machine and spun at 700 rpm for 3 min. The resulting upper yellow-orange liquid layer was collected using an insulin syringe needle gauge(30G). Injections were administered in the same manner as in the HA group. The procedure was repeated at three key time points: at the start of the study (Baseline), after three weeks, and again at six weeks intervals. Patients were advised to avoiding dental floss in the treated areas.

Armamentarium for treatment with Hyaluronic acid injection

Surgical mouth mask, Surgical gloves, Mouth Mirror, UNC-15 probe, Tweezer, Cotton, Topical Lignocaine spray (15 % w/w), Hyaluronic acid filler injection (Restylane-Lidocaine), Cheek Retractor, 5 % Povidone Mouth rinse.

Armamentarium for treatment with i-PRF

Surgical mouth mask, Surgical gloves, Mouth Mirror, UNC-15 probe, Tweezer, Cotton, 5 % Povidone Mouth rinse, Topical Lignocaine spray (15 % w/w), Tourniquet, Surgical spirit, 5 mL disposable syringes, Two plastic tube (5 mL each), Centrifuge machine, Insulin syringes (Polymed U-40 Insulin, 30G).

Clinical parameters

The following variables were studied.

Measurement using UNC-15 probe

The distance between the contact point to the tip of the papillary point (Black triangle height) was measured by the UNC-15 probe at the baseline,1st months,3rd months, and 6th months follow-up in both groups.

Photographic analysis (ImageJ software)

The surface area of the black triangle was measured using ImageJ software at baseline, as well as at the 1st, 3rd, and 6th months in both groups.

Statistical Analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS version 25.0. Intragroup comparisons were performed using repeated measures ANOVA, and Tukey's post hoc test was applied for multiple comparisons. A p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Participant characteristics

Thirty participants were enrolled after meeting eligibility criteria and providing informed consent. Recruitment occurred between September and August 2024, with follow up assessments continuing through March 2025. Participants were randomly assigned to two equal groups: the Hyaluronic Acid (HA) group and the injectable Platelet-Rich Fibrin (i-PRF) group, with 15 patients in each. The gender distribution was nearly balanced in both groups 60 % female in the HA group and 53.3 % in the i-PRF group. Male representation was 40 % and 46.7 %, respectively. Statistical analysis revealed no significant gender-based difference between groups (p = 0.989).

Age-wise, the majority of participants in both groups were younger than 30 years (60 %), with the remaining 40 % aged 30 or older. This distribution was also statistically comparable between the two treatment arms (p = 0.889). These findings confirmed that the baseline demographic characteristics were homogenous across both groups.

By the 6-month mark, the HA group showed a mean reduction of 2.00 ± 0.85 mm, while the i-PRF group exhibited a slightly higher mean reduction of 2.27 ± 0.96 mm (p = 0.426). Discussion

Having an aesthetically pleasing smile has become increasingly important for personal confidence. Both teeth and gingival tissues (interdental papilla) contribute to a harmonious smile, which is essential for boosting confidence and self-esteem.19 The management of interdental papilla loss, manifesting clinically as “black triangles is critical in aesthetic dentistry. This study aimed to evaluate and compare the efficacy of Hyaluronic Acid (HA) and Injectable Platelet-Rich Fibrin (i-PRF) in managing black triangles using a minimally invasive injection protocol. Six-month clinical evaluations of black triangle height and area revealed the regenerative potential of both materials through intra- and inter-group analyses.

Height reduction

Both groups demonstrated notable reductions in black triangle height from baseline to 6 months (Table 1). The HA group showed improvement, reaching 2.0 ± 0.3 mm at 6 months, even between 1 month and 6 months measurable reduction indicating continued progress. The i-PRF group exhibited an early and statistically significant improvement by 1 month (1.2 ± 0.3 mm), continuing to 2.2 ± 0.3 mm at 6 months. However, changes between 1–3 months and 3–6 months were not significant, suggesting a stable healing trajectory following initial regeneration. A meaningful improvement was still seen from 1 to 6 months (1.0 ± 0.3 mm), emphasizing sustained biological activity.

These outcomes align with findings by Alhabashneh et al. (2021),20 who treated 21 patients (86 sites) with HA and reported height reductions of 0.17 mm at 3 weeks, 0.83 mm at 3 months, and 0.62 mm at 6 months. And supported by the systematic review by Alsharif & Aljahdali (2024),21 Like the present study, their results show that initial improvements are more prominent, and long-term outcomes may depend on factors such as the anatomy of the embrasure and the specific HA formulation used. Both studies highlight that HA is most effective during the initial treatment phase, with benefits continuing—though less rapidly—over time.

In alignment with these outcomes, Puri et al. (2022),22 demonstrated successful papilla reconstruction using i-PRF, with some cases achieving complete fill and high levels of patient satisfaction. The pronounced early response observed in the present i-PRF group supports the regenerative capacity of platelet-derived biomolecules. Although growth factor levels were not measured in this study, prior evidence supports their biological role. Together, these studies validate the use of both HA and i-PRF as minimally invasive, biologically active treatments for interdental papilla loss.

Area reduction

In the HA group, the most notable improvements were seen between baseline and 1 month (2.3 ± 0.7 mm2), baseline and 3 months (3.6 ± 0.7 mm2), and baseline and 6 months (4.4 ± 0.7 mm2), indicating strong early responses to treatment(Table-2). Although some change was observed between 1 and 3 months (1.3 ± 0.7 mm2) and 1 and 6 months (2.1 ± 0.7 mm2), the progress slowed in the 3 to 6-month period (0.8 ± 0.7 mm2), suggesting a plateau phase after initial improvements. These findings contrast with the results of Lee et al.1 who noted instability in partially reconstructed papillae over 6 months, even with repeated injections. In Lee et al.2 HA was administered up to five times in 57 interdental sites using photographic and radiographic analysis, and although all sites showed tissue gain—with 36 achieving full papilla reconstruction—their results also revealed that partially filled sites often lacked long-term stability. In contrast, the present study showed that just three HA injections provided consistent and lasting improvement, making the protocol more efficient and patient-friendly.

In the i-PRF group, a significant drop in black triangle area was observed early in treatment (Table-3). The area decreased by 1.73 ± 0.63 mm2 from baseline to 1 month, followed by a continued reduction to 2.96 ± 0.63 mm2 at 3 months and 3.52 ± 0.63 mm2 at 6 months. The plateau observed after the initial phase suggests that i-PRF's regenerative response is rapid and stabilizes early. These results highlight i-PRF's strong early regenerative effect, likely due to its rich content of growth factors and its role in cell migration and tissue remodelling. Supporting this, Oswal and Kour et al.23 found enhanced papillary height and gingival thickness when i-PRF was combined with free gingival grafts (FGG). However, since FGG is a surgical procedure, our findings are especially valuable, showing that i-PRF alone can produce comparable results in a non-surgical, patient-friendly manner, making it a practical option in modern aesthetic dentistry.

Inter group height in HA and i-PRF group

Both HA and i-PRF groups showed a steady reduction in black triangle height and area over time, but the differences between them were not statistically significant at any interval (Table-3). While i-PRF showed a slightly higher mean reduction at 6 months (2.27 mm vs. 2.00 mm), HA exhibited more consistent improvement between 1 and 6 months. This outcome aligns with earlier research—Abdelrauf et al.20 confirmed HA's effectiveness in enhancing papillary fill, while Choukroun and Ghanaati24 highlighted i-PRF's regenerative properties, including its ability to promote angiogenesis and sustained growth factor release.

Similarly, photographic analysis revealed that although HA achieved marginally greater reductions in black triangle area, the difference between the two groups was not statistically significant at any interval (Table 2). While HA demonstrated slightly better clinical reductions, both materials were similarly effective overall in closing black triangles.

These findings align with prior work by Fakher et al.,25 who compared HA and i-PRF and found both to be effective, minimally invasive options for papilla regeneration. Becker et al.26 also reported promising outcomes with HA, with papilla fill improvements ranging from 57 % to 97 %, though their study was limited by a small sample size and lack of statistical comparison. Ghanaati et al.24 highlighted i-PRF's regenerative capacity due to its slow growth factor release and scaffold-like properties. Additionally, Mansouri et al.13 used ImageJ software as we did, for more accurate, objective measurements.

This trial addressed the lack of head-to-head comparisons between HA and i-PRF in papilla reconstruction. Our results provide clinical insight into their relative effectiveness as minimally invasive treatment options.

Strengths of the study

This study stands out due to several key strengths. The present study also compared outcomes across multiple time intervals, offering a more detailed and time-sensitive understanding of each material's effectiveness.

Beyond clinical measures, patient satisfaction and aesthetic perception improved dramatically in both groups, with 100 % reporting satisfaction at 6 months. These subjective improvements paralleled objective data, highlighting the real-world benefits of both materials in aesthetic periodontal therapy.

This study stands out for several key reasons. It followed a standardized, minimally invasive protocol, requiring just three injection sessions per site, making it both efficient and patient-friendly. The use of objective tools like the UNC-15 probe and ImageJ software ensured accurate and reproducible measurements. By including both intra- and intergroup comparisons across multiple time points, the study offers a deeper understanding of healing patterns over time. Additionally, it incorporated patient satisfaction and aesthetic perception, linking clinical success with real-life outcomes. Importantly, the entire treatment approach avoided surgery, reducing complexity and enhancing overall patient comfort.

Limitations and future directions

Despite promising outcomes, limitations must be acknowledged. Complete papilla closure was not achieved in all cases, and additional sessions may be required. This suggests that multiple treatment sessions might be required for optimal results. Additionally, the single-center design and short follow-up period, and lack of histological analysis also limit the generalizability of findings.

Future research should focus on long-term outcomes, multi-center trials, and potential synergistic effects of combining HA and i-PRF. Exploring patient-related factors such as tissue biotype and embrasure morphology could further refine treatment protocols.

Conclusion

Hyaluronic Acid (HA) and Injectable Platelet-Rich Fibrin (i-PRF) were clinically and photographically evaluated. Both materials led to a steady reduction in black triangle height and area, with no significant difference between the two groups over 6 months.

HA showed strong early improvements, was easy to use, and led to high patient satisfaction, though its cost and thicker consistency may limit precise application.

i-PRF, being autologous and biologically rich in growth factors (VEGF, PDGF, TGF-β), promoted natural tissue regeneration and was more cost-effective. However, the need for venipuncture at every session and its tendency to clot quickly may present clinical challenges.

Overall, both treatments proved to be safe, minimally invasive, and effective, with patient preference, cost, and clinical handling being key factors in selecting between them. While HA may appeal to those seeking a needle-free experience, i-PRF suits patients prioritizing a biologically driven, natural healing process.

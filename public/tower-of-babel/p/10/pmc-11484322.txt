The effect of foliar spraying of some micronutrients and lanthanum on the morphological and physiological characteristics of summer savory (Satureja hortensis L.) under hydroponic and soil conditions
Authors: Hossein Salehi-Arjmand, Ali Khadivi, Vahid Bagheri
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe use of lanthanum (La) as a rare element has increased in agriculture. Summer savory (Satureja hortensis L.) is an herbaceous and medicinal plant that has received attention recently. The present study aimed to investigate the effects of foliar application of zinc (Zn), copper (Cu), manganese (Mn), and La at different growth stages, including vegetative, reproductive, and vegetative to harvest on morphological and physiological traits of S. hortensis under hydroponic and soil conditions in the greenhouse. The study was arranged in a factorial experiment based on a completely randomized design (CRD) with three replications.ResultsResults of hydroponic condition showed that foliar application of Cu, Zn, and Mn were the most effective treatments to improve the measured morphological and physiological traits. Moreover, La was not more appropriate in increasing the quantitative and qualitative characteristics. Also, results showed that in soil cultivation, foliar application of micronutrient elements increased the ratio of leaf-to-stems, antioxidant compounds, and the percentage of essential oils, while the application of Mn, Cu, Zn, and La did not have positive effects on the increase in vegetative characteristics in all three stages of foliar application compared with the control treatment.ConclusionsCu, Zn, and Mn in appropriate concentrations can increase growth and physiological characteristics of summer savory in hydroponic systems.

Introduction

The demand for medicinal and aromatic plants as raw materials for medicine, cosmetics, perfume, and food production has increased in recent years [1]. The use of medicinal and aromatic plant species is increasingly becoming more critical, as well as disease-and-pest-resistant natural species that can tolerate changes in air temperature [2]. Therefore, harvesting wild plant species as the primary source of raw materials reduces genetic diversity and destroys the habitat of these plants. Thus, the cultivation of medicinal plants has been receiving attention for many years. Today, there is an increasing trend towards hydroponic cultivation worldwide. Also, the growth and performance of plants can be significantly affected by environmental and nutritional conditions [2, 3]. Therefore, adaptation of greenhouse crops can increase their yield and production, especially in medicinal plants [4].

Summer savory (Satureja hortensis L.) belongs to the Lamiaceae family and is a herbaceous and medicinal plant [5] that has received attention recently. Summer savory leaves are abundant in total phenolic compounds (rosmarinic acid and flavonoids) that have a powerful antioxidant impact. Rosmarinic (α-O-caffeoyl-3,4-dihydroxyphenyl lactic) acid has been identified in summer savory as a main component. Also the extract of summer savory shows considerable biological potential in antioxidant, cytotoxic, antibacterial assays, and inhibitory effect on lipid peroxidation. Furthermore, this medical plant has Fe (III) reductive and free radical scavenging properties and contains minerals and vitamins. This medicinal plant is used to treat diseases such as cancer and Alzheimer’s disease, and has anti-infection, anti-inflammatory, analgesic, antidiabetic, and anticholesterol effects [6].

Due to the effect of micronutrients on the quality of its essential oil, the cultivation of this plant has faced many problems [5]. Zinc (Zn), copper (Cu), and manganese (Mn) play a critical role in plant nutrition [7, 8]. Micronutrients show positive effects on the yield of agricultural and medicinal plants [9]. The lack of micronutrients in soil and plants is one of the nutritional problems around the world and is very common in most countries [10]. The lack of these elements in the soil overshadows the performance of macronutrient elements and reduces the growth and yield of plants. Micronutrients such as Mn, Zn, Cu, and Fe are required for normal growth and development of plants. Also, they have a key role in the development of biological processes, such as photosynthesis, respiration, chlorophyll synthesis, and stimulation of many enzymes [11, 12]. Foliar application of micronutrients is a suitable and valuable alternative to soil fertilization, especially when a small amount of nutrients is available. Foliar spraying can increase agricultural production due to the rapid absorption of nutrients in plants. The foliar application of Zn and Fe increased the plant growth and quality values of Limonium sinuatum cultivation in salinity conditions [13]. It has been reported that micronutrients in combination or alone improved biomass, yield of secondary metabolites, and quality performance of plants [14].

Lanthanum (La) is one of the 17 rare elements. These elements have similar physical and chemical properties, and especially in the last decade, they have found wide applications in various technologies, so their existence and use in the field of technology have become inevitable. The use of these elements has increased in agriculture and animal husbandry, including fertilizers and food additives for animal feed [15]. Zhang et al. [16] indicated that rare elements, such as La had positive effects on the growth of roots, total biomass, and the production of its secondary metabolites, as well as their impact on the absorption of minerals and metals by medicinal plants. In another study, Chen et al. [17] concluded that LaCl3 stimulated the growth of seedlings of tobacco and accelerated the photosynthetic rate. Also, Turra et al. [18] reported that La had a positive effect on the growth and physiology of citrus. Considering that La is physically and chemically identical to calcium, the reduction of calcium may be associated with relatively complex processes. La has an ionic radius similar to calcium ion (Ca2+). This similarity causes substitution and competition with La. On the other hand, La is a calcium channel blocker that delays calcium entry from the apoplast into the cytoplasm. However, in different parts of the plant, various mechanisms may play different roles. Although in low concentrations, La cannot stop calcium absorption and transport, with the increase of La accumulation in the root, inhibition of calcium transfer to the shoot becomes significant [19]. La in most crops increased biosynthesis, aroma, seed formation, number of seeds per spike, and shoot growth [9]. It has been reported that cerium nitrate (0.5–10 µM) and La (0.5–50 µM) in the culture medium significantly increased the length of the primary roots of Arabidopsis. It has been reported that La did not show significant effects on plant height and dry weight during vegetative growth, but 0.5 µM La significantly increased plant height, root growth, and flowering [20]. Rare earth elements, such as La can increase the production of secondary metabolites via promoting the transcriptions of essential biosynthetic genes [21]. The present study aimed to investigate the effects of foliar application of Zn, Cu, Mn, and La at different growth stages, including the vegetative, reproductive, and vegetative to harvest on morphological and physiological traits of the summer savory under hydroponic and soil conditions in the greenhouse.

Materials and methods

Experimental materials and treatments

The present study was conducted in the research greenhouse of the Department of Medicinal and Aromatic Plants, Faculty of Agriculture and Environment, Arak University, Iran, in two separate sections, including soil and hydroponic cultivations. This research was conducted as a factorial experiment based on a completely randomized design (CRD) in three replications. The experimental factors included micronutrient elements (Mn, Cu, Zn, and La with a concentration of 100 mM), three foliar spraying stages (vegetative stage, reproductive stage, vegetative stage until harvest) in two cultivation systems (soil and hydroponic). To conduct the experiment, plastic pots were selected with an opening diameter and height of 25 × 30 cm, respectively. For planting, coarse sand was placed on the bottom of the pots up to a height of 2 cm for proper drainage, and then an appropriate amount of loam-clay soil was added (Table 1). In hydroponic culture, a perlite medium was used. Then, ten summer savory seeds were planted in pots. Four seedlings were kept in each pot to create proper density and apply the treatments. In hydroponic cultivation, after seed germination, the plants were watered with Hoagland and Arnon solution [22] during the entire growth period until harvest and washed with distilled water once a week to prevent salt accumulation in the culture medium. Aqueous solutions of CuSO4, ZnSO4, Mn, and La(NO3) (from Merck, Germany) were prepared at a concentration of 100 mM. Foliar spraying was started at initial stage of growth (the initial stage begins from planting date up to approximately 10% ground cover). Spraying was done in the morning using a hand-held water fountain. The solutions were prepared based on the concentration of the element in the salt. First, the stock of each element was prepared, and the required concentration was taken for spraying and brought to a sufficient volume (500 ml). In both experiments, control pots were sprayed with distilled water. Foliar spraying times included weekly at the vegetative stage, weekly at the reproductive stage, and weekly during the vegetative period to harvest.

Sources of the plant material

The plant seeds were identified and collected from landraces in growing areas of Mashhad city, Khorasan-e-Razavi province, Iran. Formal identification of the samples was done by Dr. Hossein Salehi-Arjmand. A voucher specimen of the material has been deposited in the publicly available herbarium of Faculty of Agriculture and Natural Resources, Arak University, Iran with deposition number SH-2348.

The vegetative and physiological traits measured

The plants were harvested at the full flowering stage before being seeded. Morphological traits, including stem diameter, stem length, root and internode, height, fresh and dry weight of stem and root, leaf-to-stem ratio, chlorophyll, flavonoid, phenolic compounds, antioxidant capacity, and the percentage and yield of essential oil were measured. For this purpose, at the end of the experiment, the plants in the pots were removed from the soil along the roots. After measuring stem diameter using a caliper, and stem length, tap root length, internode length, and height using a ruler, stem and root fresh weights were measured using a digital scale with an accuracy of 0.001 g. To determine the dry weight and prepare for the evaluation of secondary metabolites, the aerial parts of the plant were quickly dried in a special room in the shade, with proper temperature, and proper ventilation for two weeks. Then, their dry weight was weighed using a scale. The relative amount of leaf greenness or chlorophyll was determined indirectly and without damaging the leaves using a SPAD or chlorophyll meter (KONICA MINOLTA 502 Japan model). Also, the chlorophyll index of 10 leaves was randomly measured, and their average was considered for the chlorophyll index of each plant. This work was done for all the plants in one pot and finally, the total chlorophyll was evaluated by calculating the average numbers obtained from each plant.

Measurement of secondary metabolites

Flavonoids content

To prepare the methanol extract, 0.5 g of dried plant powder was added to 10 ml of 80% methanol as a solvent. After 24 h, the resulting solution was filtered by filter paper, and its extract was stored in air- and light-proof glasses in the refrigerator at 4 °C. The aluminum chloride colorimetric method was also used to determine the amount of total flavonoids [23]. 2 ml of diluted methanolic extract was mixed with 2 ml of 2% aluminum chloride inside the test tube. After keeping the samples at room temperature for 15 min, the absorbance of the samples was read at 415 nm with a spectrophotometer (T80 UV/VIS Spectrometer PG Instruments Ltd). Quercetin was used to draw a standard curve, and the results were expressed in terms of mg of quercetin per g of extract. In this way, a basic solution of this substance with a concentration of 1000 µg/ml was prepared. Then, different concentrations (5, 10, 20, 40, 140, 180, and 240 µg/ml) were prepared from this basic solution in methanol. After drawing the standard curve of quercetin, the amount of total flavonoids in the extract was calculated by inserting the absorbance value of the extract into the linear equation corresponding to the standard curve. Finally, the data were expressed based on the equivalent of mg of quercetin per g of extract.

Phenolic content

To measure total phenolic compounds, Folin Ciocalto’s reagent was used. The Folin Ciocalto method is one of the most common methods of measuring phenolic compounds. The basis of work in this method is the reduction of Folin reagent by phenolic compounds in an alkaline environment and creation of a blue complex that shows the maximum absorption at the wavelength of 765 nm. The procedure is as follows: 2 ml of Folin Ciocalto’s reagent (1:10) was added to 400 µl of the diluted extract, and the absorbance of the mixture was read half an hour later at a wavelength of 765 nm using a spectrophotometer (T80 UV/VIS Spectrometer PG Instruments Ltd) against a blank [24]. Next, to draw the standard curve of gallic acid, a basic solution of this substance with a concentration of 1000 µg/ml was prepared. Then, different concentrations (40, 50, 70, 80, and 120 µg/ml) were prepared from this base solution. After performing different steps according to the method mentioned above, the absorbance value of the samples was read. After drawing the standard curve of gallic acid, the content of total phenol in the extract was calculated using entering the absorbance value of the extract in the linear equation corresponding to the standard curve. Finally, the data were expressed based on the equivalent of mg of gallic acid per g of extract.

Antioxidant activity

To determine the antioxidant activity, the stable free radical 2,2-diphenyl-1-picrylhydrazyl (DPPH), which is a lipophilic radical and has a maximum absorption at 517 nm, was used. In the DPPH test, hydroxyl groups of antioxidant compounds lead to the reduction of DPPH molecules by giving H to the DPPH free radical, which is accompanied by a change in the color of the reaction solution from dark purple to light yellow. As a result, the absorption at the wavelength of 517 nm decreases. Absorbance at 517 nm indicates the amount of remaining DPPH. For this purpose, the stable DPPH radical was dissolved to a concentration of 4 mg in 100 ml of methanol. For this purpose, five different concentrations (20, 40, 80, 160, and 320 µg/ml) were prepared in methanol. Two ml of DPPH solution was added to 2 ml of the diluted extracts and the mixture was vortexed, and then, kept in the dark for 30 min. The absorbance of the samples was read at 517 nm using a spectrophotometer (T80 UV/VIS Spectrometer PG Instruments Ltd) against a blank (the device was previously zeroed with 80% methanol). A sample containing methanol and DPPH solution was used as a control sample.

Essential oil percent

15 g of the dry material of the aerial parts of the plant (in powder form) was accurately weighed, and its essential oil was extracted by distillation with water for 3 h. The essential oil was dehydrated by anhydrous sodium sulfate, and its amount was reported in terms of volume and weight [26].

Experimental design and data analysis

The experiment was a complete randomized design (CRD) with three factors in three replications. SAS software version 9.2 was used to analyze all data (Cary, NC, USA) [27]. All data were statistically analyzed using the two-way ANOVA model. When analysis of variance (ANOVA) indicated significant treatment effects, significant mean differences (P < 0.05) were calculated using the Duncan Multiple Range Test.

Vegetative traits

Stem diameter

Stem diameter was influenced by the method of cultivation and foliar spraying of low-consumption nutrients and the interaction of these two factors (Table 2). The results also showed that in the hydroponic method and under different treatments of foliar application of nutrients, a larger stem diameter was observed. Also, the use of Mn and Cu, Zn, and La caused an increase in the stem diameter of savory in the hydroponic method. However, in the soil cultivation method, the use of foliar spraying with zinc improved the stem diameter compared with other treatments (Fig. 1).

Tap root length

The experimental factors had a significant effect on the tap root length (Table 2). However, in the conditions of soil cultivation, the use of Mn and Zn in the conditions of foliar spraying from the time of vegetative to harvest and the element of zinc in the reproductive stage improved the height of the root of the savory plant (Table 3).

Plant height

The plant height was influenced by the cultivation method and the interaction between the cultivation method and foliar application of micronutrient elements (Table 3). The results showed that the use of hydroponic cultivation method increased the plant height compared with soil cultivation method. It was also found that the use of Mn, Cu, and Zn respectively increased the plant height compared with the control treatment and La, however in the soil cultivation method, none of the foliar treatments with micronutrient elements could improve the plant height compared with control treatment (Fig. 4).

Shoot fresh and dry mass

The shoot fresh mass was significantly influenced by the employed treatments (P ≤ 0.01, Table 2). The shoot fresh and dry mass in hydroponic conditions and in three stages of foliar application had a significant increase compared with soil conditions. So the use of Mn in the reproductive growth stage caused an increase in the fresh and dry weight of seedlings. Also, in these cultivation conditions, the application of micronutrients Mn, Zn, and Cu caused an increase in the fresh and dry weight of savory in all three stages of foliar application compared with the control treatment and La. However, the results showed that in the conditions of soil cultivation, only the foliar spraying of Mn, Cu, and Zn in the reproductive stage caused an increase in the fresh and dry weight compared with the control and lanthanum treatments.

Root fresh and dry mass

The root fresh and dry mass was significantly influenced by the employed treatments (P ≤ 0.01, Table 2). The root fresh and dry mass in hydroponic conditions and at three stages of foliar application had a significant increase compared with soil conditions. So the use of manganese element in the reproductive growth stage caused an increase in the root fresh and dry mass. Also, in these cultivation conditions, the application of micronutrients manganese, Zn and Cu caused an increase in the root fresh and dry mass of savory in all three stages of foliar application compared with the control treatment and La. However, in the conditions of soil cultivation, none of the treatments of micronutrients and the rare element of lanthanum could cause a significant change in the fresh and dry weight of roots in all three stages of foliar application (Table 3).

The total fresh and dry mass of shoots and roots

The total fresh and dry masses of shoots and roots were significantly (P ≤ 0.01, Table 3) affected by cultivation methods, spraying stage, micronutrient treatments, and the interaction of three experimental factors. The total fresh and dry mass of shoots and roots in this research was higher in hydroponic conditions and in all three stages of foliar application than in soil culture. Also, the use of Mn, Cu, and Zn caused an increase in the total fresh and dry weight of shoots and roots compared with the control treatment and La in all three stages of foliar application. However, in the conditions of soil cultivation, none of the foliar spraying treatments and in any of the foliar spraying stages could cause an increase in the total fresh and dry weight of shoots and roots compared with the control treatment (Table 4).

Discussion

In the present study, micronutrients increased the vegetative characteristics in both soil and hydroponic cultivation conditions at all three stages of foliar application, including vegetative, reproductive, and vegetative to harvest. Foliar application of Zn, Cu, Mn in hydroponic cultivation increased the vegetative characteristics compared with soil cultivation due to easy access to nutrients for plants grown in the hydroponic system. Mn, Cu, and Zn increased the stem diameter in both cultivation media of hydroponic and soil at all three stages of foliar application. Although, foliar application of La increased the stem diameter in hydroponic system. On the other hand, tap root length was increased by Cu in hydroponic condition and by Mn and Cu in soil condition at all three stages of foliar application. Mn, Cu, and Zn at all three stages of foliar application in hydroponic cultivation condition increased the fresh and dry mass of roots and shoots, the total fresh and dry mass of roots and shoots, and plant height.

Also, foliar application of La increased the ratio of fresh and dry mass of shoot to root, as compare to control. However, it appears that the appropriate concentration of La on different plant organ growth may be related with the different plant species. A reduction in soybean growth was recorded at concentrations above 10 µmol La/L [28]. Also, Diatloff et al. [29] found that La induced the growth of corn and mung bean when the concentration was below 0.2 µM. Also, the addition of 10µM La in the nutrient solution increased plant height, stem diameter, and number of flower buds but, La did not affect dry biomass accumulation in some sweet bell pepper varieties [30]. However, in field experiments, 100 mg/L LaCl3 solution was applied (sprayed) daily to Chinese cabbage leaves for eight days. This treatment promoted growth and resulted in increased fresh and dry weight of the cabbage, whereas the fresh and dry weight of the stems and leaves increased [31]. Chen et al. [32] reported 60 mM La significantly promoted the growth of Crocus sativus callus. In wheat the addition of both La and Ce and their combination at concentrations between 0.5 and 25mg L− 1 in the nutrient solution inhibited the length of the primary root and reduced root and shoot dry biomass [33]. In soybean, low La concentrations (i.e., 5–10µM) stimulated the photosynthetic rate and total chlorophyll content and led to a higher incidence of binucleate cells, resulting in a slight increase in root and shoot biomass. At higher La levels (> 20µM), soybean growth was reduced, as a consequence of ultra-structural modifications in the cell wall, thylakoids and chloroplasts, and the appearance of c-metaphases [34]. Thus, La may exert different modes of action not only among genotypes but also among the phenological phases and organs evaluated.

Chlorophyll index (SPAD) was increased by La in soil condition at all three stages of foliar application. A low concentration of La (0.1 mM) in the nutrient solution increases the concentrations of photosynthetic pigments in rice seedlings [35]. According to Gracia-Jiménez et al. (2017), under hydroponic greenhouse conditions, addition of 10 µM La to the nutrient solution stimulated the biosynthesis of chlorophylls in sweet pepper varieties. A positive effect of La on chlorophyll content is probably due to increased levels of essential macronutrients involved in chlorophyll biosynthesis [36]. Also, for soybean plants, Oliveira et al. [34] also reported an increase in photosynthetic rate and chlorophyll content in the presence of 5 and 10 µM of La. In addition, low La concentrations (25 and 50 µM) stimulated an increase in chlorophyll index, resulting in a slight increase in shoot biomass. At higher levels, La didn’t reduce growth but caused a decrease in both photosynthetic rate and chlorophyll index in maize plants [37]. It is believed that this phenomenon occurs because rare earth elements may influence gas exchange, especially stomatal conductance, raising the photosynthetic rate due to a greater assimilation of CO2 for the photosynthetic process [38].

Considering the diverse roles of Mn, Cu, and Zn, including the direct effect on the photosynthetic electron transfer chain and the activation of many enzymes, the increase in vegetative characteristics under the influence of micronutrients can be justified. Microelements play a crucial role in growth characteristics, photosensitivity, and activating enzymes in different plants [39]. In addition, Cu stabilizes chlorophyll through the protection of the lipid-paradetein-chlorophyll complex, limits the binding to plastocyanin in chloroplasts, and improves the electron transport chain [40]. Also, Mn increases the performance of photosynthesis and the synthesis of carbohydrates, such as starch. Most of Iran’s soils are calcareous and therefore the solubility of micronutrients in these types of soils decreases and causes the lack of these elements [41]. Mousavi et al. [41] reported that Mn foliar application increases the yield and dry matter content of potatoes. The foliar spraying of Cu, Zn, and Mn in the hydroponic culture significantly increased shoot and root growth of summer savory plants. Similar results were observed in beans and corn [42].

The present results showed that Cu, Zn, and Mn increased the phenol content in both hydroponic and soil culture media at all three stages of foliar application. Also, the Cu, Zn, and Mn in soil cultivation conditions at vegetative to harvest, and the Zn at the vegetative and reproductive stages in the hydroponic cultivation conditions increased the antioxidant capacity.

Although, La decreased the antioxidant capacity in plants as compared to the control. Antioxidants are compounds that prevent the oxidation of molecules that delay or prevent the initiation or continuation of the oxidation process. The two main categories of antioxidants include natural and synthetic types. In general, synthetic antioxidants are phenolic compounds with different alkyl degrees, while natural antioxidants are phenols (tocopherol, flavonoids, and phenolic acid), nitrogen compounds (alkaloids, chlorophyll derivatives, amino acids, and amines), carotenoids, and ascorbic acid [43]. The reason for the increase in antioxidant activity with the use of Cu and Zn can be considered due to the toxicity of these elements in high concentrations. Similarly, Fe, Zn, and Mn foliar application in tomato significantly increased fruit yield in the hydroponic culture system [44]. Yadegari [3] reported that foliar application of Zn, Mn, and Cu positivly affected phenols, carotenoids, and flavonoids of borago, thyme, and marigold.

La can regulate the physiological activities of the plant, such as the production of superoxide dismutase (SOD) and peroxidase (POD) enzymes. The activities of such enzymes as SOD, peroxidase, ascorbate, carotenoids and flavonoids could have been stimulated by La, helping to control reactive oxygen species, thereby reducing the oxidative stress affecting the membranes of several organelles and, in this way, promoting plant growth [45]. However, Different plant species respond uniquely to lanthanum exposure. Some may show increased antioxidant levels, while others might not exhibit significant changes and even decreased. The results of Ippolito et al. [46] showed that 5.0 mM La did not cause either visible symptoms on plants or significant effects on ROS production, chlorophyll content, and lipid peroxidation in common duckweed. In a study, the effects of La on the reactive oxygen species (ROS) and antioxidant metabolism were studied in the roots of rice. La treatments stimulated superoxide dismutase activity in the roots of rice seedlings at all tested concentrations (0.05, 0.1, 0.5, 1, and 1.5 mM) and The level of hydrogen peroxide and superoxide anion was increased by 0.5, 1, and 1.5 mM La, and the activity of catalase and peroxidase was increased by 0.05 and 0.1 mM La. Their result indicated that ROS level was declined after treated with 0.05 mM La [47]. Also, an increase in the activity of peroxidase enzyme at higher concentrations (12.5 to 62.5 µg g-1) of lanthanum was observed in cowpea [48]. The stimulating and inhibiting effects of La on plant growth are associated with alternations in mineral nutrition, photosynthetic rate, tolerance to abiotic and biotic stresses, the activity of enzymes that are related to ROS generation, and levels of endogenous hormones [49].

The results of the present study also showed that the foliar application of Cu, Zn and Mn at the stages of vegetative growth, reproduction, and vegetative growth to harvest in soil cultivation conditions increased the percentage of essential oil. Khalid [50] reported that micronutrients can increase the percentage and essential oil yield of coriander fruits. In another research, foliar application of Zn increased the essential oil of Holy Basil (Ocimum sanctum L.) [51] and Pimpinella anisum L [52]. , which is in agreement with the present results. Zn influences primary metabolic pathways that ultimately lead to the biosynthesis of active components of essential oil [53]. Planting, fertilizing, irrigation, and the date of harvesting of plant material can significantly change the content and composition of the essential oil [54, 55]. In the case of medicinal plants that have essential oils, nutrition can effectively increase the yield and quality of essential oils [56]. In addition to the direct effects of many micronutrients on plant metabolism, often their mutual effects are much more significant than simple effects. The fertilization with macro and micro elements increased the yield of essential oil and changed chemical composition of summer savory plants [26]. Micronutrients affect positively plant photosynthesis and thus increase plant production and performance. In wheat, foliar spraying with solutions containing 0.01–0.1% of micronutrients has increased carbon dioxide assimilation up to 35.8–79.8% [57]. Application of micronutrients in plants increases the intensity and amount of photosynthesis in plants by 11.5–31.2% [17]. The effect of micronutrients on photosynthesis is related to chloroplast growth, chlorophyll content, and enzyme activity. It has also been proven that La improves the yield and quality of crops due to its effects on the intensity and rate of photosynthesis, the amount of chlorophyll, and the activity of photosynthetic enzymes [58]. The reason for different uptake of micronutrients by crops is due to many factors, including soil characteristics, such as acidity, organic matter, amount of minerals, time and amount of application, plant variety, growth stage, and purity and acidity of water that micronutrients are dissolved in it.

Conclusions

The present study was carried out with aimed at investigating the morpho-physiological effects of summer savory under the effect of foliar spraying of the micronutrient elements of Mn, Cu, Zn, and La at different growth stages (vegetative growth, reproductive growth, and from the time of vegetative growth to harvest) in two Hydroponic and soil cultivation systems. The results stated that the use of Mn, Cu, and Zn in all three stages of foliar spraying in the hydroponic system had a great effect on the growth characteristics, however the greatest effect was observed in the reproductive stage under the influence of the manganese in the hydroponic system. While in the soil system, none of the micronutrients and lanthanum elements could increase the vegetative characteristics of in any of the foliar application stages. It was also found that foliar application of Mn, Cu, and Zn caused an increase in the physiological characteristics of summer savory in the conditions of hydroponic cultivation compared with soil cultivation.

The impact of lanthanum (La) on plant growth is influenced by a range of interrelated factors, including soil characteristics, plant species, developmental stages, Targeted Organ, La concentration, treatment duration, and environmental conditions. This complexity necessitates a nuanced understanding for effective agricultural application. Optimizing lanthanum use can enhance crop yields, improve stress resilience, and boost nutrient uptake. To fully harness its benefits while minimizing risks, ongoing research is crucial. Investigating the specific mechanisms of lanthanum’s effects will inform tailored strategies for its application across diverse agricultural systems, ultimately contributing to sustainable farming practices and improved crop productivity.

Folate, Homocysteine, and Adverse Outcomes After Ischemic Stroke
Authors: Mengyao Shi, Jin Zheng, Yang Liu, Xueyu Mao, Xuechun Wu, Min Chu, Huicong Niu, Lulu Sun, Xinyue Chang, Yu He, Yi Liu, Daoxia Guo, Yonghong Zhang, Zhengbao Zhu, Jing Zhao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAs a risk factor of cardiovascular diseases, homocysteine can be effectively lowered by folate. However, the associations of folate and homocysteine levels with the prognosis of ischemic stroke remained unclear.Methods and ResultsA total of 3530 patients with ischemic stroke were included. Serum folate and homocysteine levels were measured at admission. The primary outcome was composite of death and major disability (modified Rankin Scale score≥3) at 3 months after stroke onset. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression models were used. The mediation effect of homocysteine was examined. During follow‐up, 1056 participants developed the primary outcome. In the univariate model, participants in the highest quartile of folate had a 29% (95% CI, 0.58–0.87) decreased risk of primary outcome compared with those in the lowest quartile. After multivariate adjustment, the odds ratio associated with the highest quartile of folate was 0.58 (95% CI, 0.46–0.73) for primary outcome. In contrast, participants in the highest quartile of homocysteine had a 52% (95% CI, 1.24–1.98) increased risk of primary outcome compared with those in the lowest quartile. After multivariate adjustment, the odds ratio associated with highest quartile of homocysteine was 1.57 (95% CI, 1.24–1.98) for primary outcome. In addition, 25.5% of the observed associations between folate and primary outcome was mediated through homocysteine (P=0.012).ConclusionsHigh folate levels were associated with low risks of death and major disability among Chinese patients with ischemic stroke, and homocysteine partially mediated the observed potential beneficial role of folate.Keywords: cohort, folate, homocysteine, ischemic stroke, prognosisSubject Categories: Ischemic Stroke, Epidemiology

Research Perspective

What Is New?

High homocysteine level has long been known as a prevalent and significant risk factor for cardiovascular disease. 1 , 2 The toxicity of homocysteine could be explained by several biological mechanisms. Previous studies have found that homocysteine could induce endothelial dysfunction, interfere with the production of nitric oxide, disrupt lipid metabolism, and trigger cellular apoptosis. 3 , 4 , 5 Folate supplement is one of the major interventions to reduce homocysteine levels and the most recent meta‐analyses of randomized control trials have concluded that homocysteine‐lowering interventions were associated with 10% reduced risk of first‐ever stroke. 6

Despite the evidence on stroke primary prevention, the protective effect of folate on stroke secondary prevention remained inconclusive. Both the VISP trial (Vitamin Intervention for Stroke Prevention) and VITATOPS trial (Vitamins to Prevent Stroke) reported that B vitamins had no treatment effect on adverse outcomes among patients with stroke. 7 , 8 Concerns have been raised since these 2 trials recruited participants with high folate consumption or folate fortification policies, which may fail to detect any benefits because of the ceiling effect of folate. 9 , 10 Therefore, well‐designed observational studies conducted among patients with ischemic stroke and without mandatory folate‐fortified food are needed to provide critical evidence for future clinical trials.

China has the highest risk of developing stroke in the world, while inadequate folate intake is prevalent in China without mandatory folic acid fortification. 11 , 12 Therefore, we conducted the current large‐scale prospective study among Chinese patients with ischemic stroke to explore the associations of baseline folate levels and homocysteine levels with the risk of death or major disability at 3 months after ischemic stroke onset. We also examined the mediation effect of homocysteine on the associations of folate with poor prognosis of ischemic stroke.

Methods

The data sets generated or analyzed in the current study are available from the corresponding authors upon reasonable request.

Study Participants

The current prognostic study was based on the Minhang Stroke Cohort study, which was a prospective cohort study for patients with ischemic stroke conducted in Shanghai, China. Briefly, from January 2018 to December 2022, a total of 7323 consecutive patients aged ≥18 years who had ischemic stroke confirmed by computed tomography scan or magnetic resonance imaging were recruited from the Department of Neurology at Minhang Hospital. After excluding those with time from onset to admission over 7 days (n=263) or cancer (n=168), a total of 6892 eligible patients were enrolled in the Minhang Stroke Cohort. For the current study, data on the baseline levels of folate or homocysteine were missing for 3362 participants, and a total of 3530 patients were finally included in the analyses. Most baseline characteristics were well balanced between participants in the current analysis and all participants in the Minhang Stroke Cohort (Table S1), indicating that those assayed for folate and homocysteine basically represented the total participants of the Minhang Stroke Cohort.

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Minhang Hospital. Written consent was obtained from all study participants or their immediate family members.

Data Collection

At the time of enrollment, trained interviewers collected patients' information on demographic characteristics, clinical features, medical history, and imaging data using a standard questionnaire through face‐to‐face interviews. Alcohol drinkers were defined as those who consumed any type of alcoholic beverage ≥12 times in the past year, and cigarette smokers were defined as having smoked at least 1 cigarette per day for ≥1 year and reported current smoking. The National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) was used to assess stroke severity by trained neurologists. 13 Based on the symptoms and imaging data, ischemic stroke was classified as large‐artery atherosclerosis, cardioembolism, small‐vessel occlusion, stroke of other determined pathogenesis, and stroke of undetermined pathogenesis according to TOAST (Trial of ORG 10172 in Acute Stroke Treatment) criteria. 14 Three blood pressure measurements were measured using a standard mercury sphygmomanometer according to a standard protocol adapted from procedures recommended by the American Heart Association. 15 Routine laboratory measurements (blood glucose and blood lipids, etc.) were performed for all enrolled patients at Minhang Hospital. Diabetes was defined as fasting plasma glucose levels ≥7.0 mmol/L, history of diabetes, or use of glucose‐lowering medications. 16 Dyslipidemia was defined as triglyceride ≥2.26 mmol/L, total cholesterol ≥6.22 mmol/L, low‐density lipoprotein cholesterol ≥4.14 mmol/L, high‐density lipoprotein cholesterol <1.04 mmol/L, history of dyslipidemia, or use of lipid‐lowering medications according to Chinese guidelines on the prevention and treatment of dyslipidemia. 17

Patients' fasting blood samples were collected after at least 8 hours of fasting within 24 hours of hospital admission. All serum and plasma samples were separated and immediately frozen at −80 °C until laboratory testing. Serum folate level and serum homocysteine level at baseline were measured with a Cobas 8000 automatic analyzer (Roche Diagnostics, Indianapolis, IN). Laboratory technicians who performed measurements were blind to the clinical characteristics and outcomes of the study participants.

Outcome Assessment

Participants were followed up in person at 3 months after ischemic stroke by trained neurologists. The primary outcome was the composite of death and major disability (modified Rankin Scale [mRS] score, 3–6) at 3 months after ischemic stroke. The secondary outcomes included major disability (mRS score, 3–5), and death (mRS score, 6). An ordered 7‐level categorical score of the mRS was also included as a secondary outcome for neurological functional status on the basis of the recommendation from the European Stroke Organization Outcomes Working Group. 18

Statistical Analysis

Baseline characteristics of study participants were presented according to the quartiles of baseline serum folate and homocysteine level. Generalized linear regression analysis and Cochran–Armitage trend χ2 test were used to test the linear trends of baseline characteristics across folate and homocysteine quartiles. Multivariable logistic regression analyses were conducted to estimate odds ratios and corresponding 95% CIs for higher quartiles of folate or homocysteine compared with the lowest quartile and for each SD increment of log‐transformed folate or homocysteine. Multivariable ordinal logistic regression models were used to evaluate the effects of folate or homocysteine on the 7‐level categorical mRS score. The covariates included in the multivariable models were age, sex, smoking, drinking, time from onset to hospitalization, baseline body mass index, baseline NIHSS score, baseline systolic blood pressure, diabetes, dyslipidemia, history of hypertension, family history of stroke, ischemic stroke subtype, intravenous thrombolysis, and mechanical thrombectomy.

Restricted cubic spline analyses with 4 knots defined at the 5th, 35th, 65th, and 95th percentiles were conducted to better describe the shapes of the associations of serum folate and homocysteine with adverse outcomes after ischemic stroke. 19 We further conducted mediation analyses to examine whether the association of folate with death or major disability was mediated by homocysteine. The quartiles of folate were the exposure variable; the quartiles of homocysteine were the mediator; death or major disability was the outcome variable following a binomial distribution. The mediation analyses were performed using the “mediation” R package (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria), and the significance testing was performed using a bootstrap method with 1000 simulations. 20 , 21 Finally, we conducted a series of sensitivity analyses to assess the robustness of the main findings. First, sensitivity analyses were conducted after excluding participants who had history of hypertension, history of diabetes, history of dyslipidemia, and endovascular treatment, separately. Second, sensitivity analyses were conducted after excluding only those with missing folate levels or those with missing homocysteine levels. Third, sensitivity analyses were conducted after using Markov chain Monte Carlo procedure to impute the missing data of folate or homocysteine. Fourth, sensitivity analyses were conducted with further adjustment of mRS score at admission to minimize the potential influence of prestroke ambulation status. Fifth, sensitivity analyses were conducted according to the different stroke subtypes. Two‐tailed P<0.05 was considered to be statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed with R software version 4.3.1 and SAS software version 9.4 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC).

Results

Baseline Characteristics

Among the 3530 patients with ischemic stroke, the mean (SD) age was 70.0 (12.8) years, and 2233 (63.3%) were men. The median serum folate level was 6.42 ng/mL (interquartile range, 4.26–9.72 ng/mL) and the median serum homocysteine level was 14.30 μmol/L (interquartile range, 11.40–18.70 μmol/L). Compared with participants with higher folate levels, those with lower folate levels were less likely to be men, drinkers, and smokers; were more likely to have a history of diabetes and hypertension; had a higher prevalence of cardioembolic stroke and small‐vessel occlusion stroke; had higher fasting plasma glucose, total cholesterol, and high‐density lipoprotein cholesterol; had lower NIHSS score; and had a lower prevalence of large‐artery atherosclerotic stroke and mechanical thrombectomy (Table 1). As expected, the distributions of baseline characteristics were in opposite trends when looking across homocysteine quartiles (Table 2).

Serum Folate Levels and Clinical Outcomes

During the 3‐month follow‐up period, 294 participants (8.33%) were lost to follow‐up. Among the remaining 3236 participants, a total of 1056 participants (32.6%) developed the primary outcome (313 deaths and 743 major disabilities) (Table 3). The cumulative incidence rates of primary outcome at 3 months from the lowest quartile to the highest quartile of folate were 38.70%, 31.07%, 29.82% and 30.87%, respectively (P<0.001). After adjusting for age, sex, baseline NIHSS score, and other important prognostic factors, the odds ratios associated with highest quartile of folate were 0.58 (95% CI, 0.46–0.73; P trend<0.001) for primary outcome, 0.47 (95% CI, 0.33–0.68; P trend<0.001) for death, and 0.65 (95% CI, 0.50–0.84; P trend=0.002) for major disability. On continuous analyses, each SD increase of log‐transformed folate was associated with an 18% (95% CI, 11%–25%) decreased risk of primary outcome, a 23% (95% CI, 12%–32%) decreased risk of death, and a 15% (95% CI, 7%–23%) decreased risk of major disability. Multivariable ordinal logistic regression analyses showed a significantly better shift in the distribution of mRS scores at 3 months with higher quartiles of serum folate in patients with ischemic stroke (P trend<0.001; Figure S1). In addition, multivariable‐adjusted restricted cubic spline analyses showed inverse linear associations of serum folate with primary outcome (P for linearity=0.001), death (P for linearity<0.001), and major disability (P for linearity=0.043) (Figure 1).

Serum Homocysteine Levels and Clinical Outcomes

After adjusting for age, sex, baseline NIHSS score, and other important prognostic factors, the odds ratios associated with highest quartile of homocysteine were 1.57 (95% CI, 1.24–1.98; P trend<0.001) for primary outcome, 1.67 (95% CI, 1.14–2.43; P trend=0.005) for death, and 1.47 (95% CI, 1.14–1.89; P trend=0.001) for major disability (Table 4). Multivariable ordinal logistic regression analyses showed a significantly worse shift in the distribution of mRS score at 3 months with higher quartiles of serum homocysteine in patients with ischemic stroke (P trend<0.001; Figure S2). In addition, multivariable‐adjusted restricted cubic spline analyses showed positive linear associations of serum homocysteine with primary outcome (P for linearity<0.001), death (P for linearity=0.010), and major disability (P for linearity<0.001) (Figure 2).

Mediation Analyses and Sensitivity Analyses

Figure S3 presents the mediation effect of homocysteine on the folate–primary outcome association. After adjusting for age, sex, baseline NIHSS score, and other important prognostic factors, the mediation effect of homocysteine on the association between folate and primary outcome was estimated at 25.5% (P=0.012).

Results from sensitivity analyses showed that further exclusion of participants with a history of hypertension, history of diabetes, history of dyslipidemia, and endovascular treatment did not change the associations of serum folate and homocysteine levels with adverse outcomes after ischemic stroke (Tables S2 and S3). After excluding only those with missing folate levels, 3594 patients were included. Meanwhile, 6412 patients were included after excluding only those with missing homocysteine levels. As shown in Tables S4 and S5, the results were consistent with the main finding. In addition, we did an imputation using the Markov chain Monte Carlo procedure for those with missing folate or homocysteine levels. As shown in Tables S6 and S7, though attenuated, high folate levels were still negatively associated with a poor prognosis of ischemic stroke, while high homocysteine levels conferred similar increased risk of poor prognosis. Moreover, the results were comparable between models with adjustment of mRS score at admission and models without this adjustment (Tables S8 and S9). Finally, the negative associations between folate and the primary outcome were found in large‐artery atherosclerotic stroke and cardioembolic stroke. Nevertheless, the negative associations were nonsignificant regarding small‐vessel occlusion stroke (Table S10). On the other hand, the positive associations between elevated homocysteine and the primary outcome were consistent regardless of different stroke subtypes (Table S11).

Discussion

In this large‐scale prospective study, we found negative linear dose–response associations between serum folate and adverse outcomes after ischemic stroke, while there were positive linear dose–response associations between serum homocysteine and adverse outcomes after ischemic stroke. Participants in the highest quartile of baseline folate had a 42% decreased risk of primary outcome, a 53% decreased risk of death, and a 35% decreased risk of major disability compared with patients in the lowest quartile. In addition, 25.5% of the observed associations between folate and primary outcome was mediated through homocysteine. These findings suggested that homocysteine was implicated in the development of ischemic stroke, and folate was a promising intervention to prevent adverse outcomes after ischemic stroke.

Homocysteine exerts its deleterious effect on the prognosis of ischemic stroke through multiple mechanisms. It has been reported that excess homocysteine can stimulate the production of nitric oxide and reactive oxygen species, and thus lead to impaired endothelial function and increased oxidative stress. 4 In addition, experimental studies also showed that homocysteine could induce an inflammation response, 22 , 23 and elevated homocysteine level could enhance the neuronal damage after stroke. 24 In line with the biological evidence, numerous prospective studies have consistently reported a positive association between homocysteine and poor outcome among patients with stroke. 25 , 26 , 27 , 28 Similarly, in the present study, we also found that higher serum homocysteine level was significantly associated with poor prognosis among Chinese patients with ischemic stroke, and the associations were independent of established prognostic factors.

Different from the widespread concern of homocysteine, evidence linking circulating folate with the prognosis of ischemic stroke was sparse. In the VISP trial with 3680 cerebral infarction patients, high‐dose supplementation of B vitamins had no more treatment effect on either recurrent vascular events or death than in low‐dose group. 7 The VITATOPS trial included 8164 patients with recent stroke or transient ischemic attack, and they found that daily administration of B vitamins was not associated with decreased risks of vascular events and death. 8 However, interpretation of the observed null associations should be cautious, since both trials were limited by including participants with high folate consumption or folate fortification policies. China has the lowest folate levels compared with Western countries, so our study conducted in Chinese participants could provide important population‐based evidence for the roles of folate in the development of ischemic stroke. In the present large‐scale study, we found that high serum folate levels were associated with a favorable prognosis, suggesting that patients with ischemic stroke and with low folate levels would benefit more from folate supplement interventions. Therefore, it is of interest to conduct clinical trials among the population with low folate consumption to demonstrate the clinical benefits of folate supplementation on the prognosis of ischemic stroke.

Due to the crucial role of folate in the recycle of homocysteine, 29 we are not surprised to find that the reverse associations between folate levels and risk of death or major disability after ischemic stroke could be partially explained by homocysteine. In addition, it is worthwhile to note that our results indicated that folate could influence the prognosis of ischemic stroke independent of lowering homocysteine. As the key mediator of 1‐carbon metabolism, folate metabolism supports multiple physiological processes, including biosynthesis of nucleic acid and maintenance of amino acid homeostasis. 30 Furthermore, folate is a producer of nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate, which regulates redox defense and reductive biosynthesis. 31 In addition, some studies have shown that folate and its principal circulating metabolite, 5‐methyltetrahydrofolate, had a direct protective effect on blood vessels through maintaining endothelial function and inhibiting superoxide production. 32 , 33 , 34

To our knowledge, our study is the largest prospective cohort to simultaneously investigate the associations of serum folate and homocysteine levels with the prognosis of stroke among individuals without mandatory folate‐fortified food. In addition, comprehensive information about potential confounders was collected and controlled in the multivariable models. Thus, the method in this study was appropriate and rigorous, and our study will provide a more valid appraisal of the associations between serum folate and homocysteine levels and adverse clinical outcomes among patients with ischemic stroke. Limitations should also be mentioned. First, the levels of serum folate and homocysteine were measured only 1 time at baseline, so we could not investigate the associations between the dynamic change of serum folate and homocysteine and the prognosis of ischemic stroke. Second, the missing rate for folate was high in the current study. However, most baseline characteristics of the patients were well balanced between participants assayed and the total population. Sensitivity analyses also proved the robustness of the main findings. Third, information about prestroke ambulation status was not collectedly in the current cohort, so we could not rule out its influence in the observed associations. Nevertheless, we further adjusted for mRS score at admission, and the results remained unchanged, so this could somehow reduce the concern brought by missing prestroke ambulation status. Fourth, recurrent strokes and other vascular events were important adverse clinical end points for patients with stroke. However, this information was not available in the current cohort, and further studies are warranted to examine their relationships. Fifth, during the follow‐up, we collected only death status, and the detailed reason for death was not collected. Therefore, we were not able to conduct a competing risk model of death. Further studies are needed to investigate this. Sixth, this prospective study is an observational study, so we cannot rule out the possibility of residual confounding although we have adjusted potential prognostic factors in our analyses. Finally, the current study was based on the Chinese population, so we should extrapolate our findings to other populations with caution. Further studies conducted among different populations are needed to replicate our findings.

In conclusion, among Chinese patients with ischemic stroke, we found that high serum folate levels were associated with lower risk of adverse outcomes after stroke, and homocysteine partially mediated the potential beneficial role of folate in decreasing the risk of adverse outcomes after stroke.

Draft genome sequence of Pseudonocardia alni M29 isolated from healthy maize plant
Authors: Deepak Singla, Shaloo Verma, Jagriti Yadav, Murugan Kumar, Hillol Chakdar, Alok Kumar Srivastava
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The genus Pseudonocardia is known for siderophore production that helps plants in iron uptake. In this study, we report the draft genome of Pseudonocardia alni M29 isolated as an endophyte from healthy maize plants. De novo genome assembly resulted in 53 contigs (>500 bp) with overall genome size of 5.96 Mb. A total of 5525 protein coding genes were predicted of which 2390 (43.3%) and 137 (0.02%) were annotated by KEGG and CAZy database, respectively. CheckM and BUSCO genome analysis revealed 98.87% genome completeness with only three fragmented and one missing BUSCO genes, respectively. The raw data of the Pseudonocardia alni M29 draft genome shotgun project are accessible at NCBI.

Background

Bioavailability of micronutrients especially that of iron in food grains is critical to cater the need of this element for human physiological functions. Traditionally, food grains are biofortified through genetic and agronomic interventions. Besides such time taking and resource intensive approaches, microbes can be very useful for improving the uptake and translocation of iron in crop plants through production of siderophores, modulation of transporters. Previously, we characterized Pseudonocardia alni M29 as an efficient siderophore producer with strong potential to colonize seeds and roots of maize [1]. Our studies (unpublished) confirmed that, inoculation of this actinomycetes could improve iron uptake and localization in roots of maize. Therefore, to understand the mechanisms involved in the siderophore production and improvement in iron uptake in maize, the genome of this actinomycetes was carried out.

Data Description

Pseudonocardia alni is a gram-positive bacterium with high GC content belonging to class Actinomycetes. It plays important role in biosynthesis of natural active products, biodegradation of environmental pollutants, and defense against invading pathogens. These beneficial potentials have attracted a lot of interest in the genus Pseudonocardia. In this study, we reported draft genome of P. alni M29 isolated from the stem of healthy maize plant collected from Mau, Uttar Pradesh, India.

The whole genome sequencing of the endophytic bacteria P. alni M29 generated approximately 3.87 million raw pair-end reads (Table 1). The draft genome with the assembled genome size of 5.96 Mb has 109 contigs, N50 values 246,188 bp, along with 74.46% GC content [Table 1 and Fig. 1). The CheckM and BUSCO analysis revealed 98.87% and 96.6% genome completeness [Table 2]. The longest contig was of size 713,285 bp. A total of 5524 protein coding, along with 7 rRNA and 56 tRNA genes were predicted in the draft genome. Among these, 43.3% were annotated by KEGG database using BlastKOALA. The siderophore biosynthesis by non-ribosomal peptide pathways showed the presence of four genes namely entA, mbtA, mbtB, and menF. We have also identified fagD, fepC, febD, and fepG genes involved in iron-siderophore transport. A total of 137 CAZy genes were identified in the assembled genome of which 21, 15, and 10 genes belong respectively to Glycosyl Transferase 2 (GT2), Glycosyl Transferase 4 (GT4) and Glycosyl Hydrolase 13 (GH13) family [Fig. 2]. The average nucleotide identity (ANI) varied from 96.38% - 97.82% and 97.12% - 98.12% using fastANI and skani, respectively [Table 3]. Orthologous gene analysis showed 4312 clusters comprised of 21,614 genes were common among the five genomes (Fig. 3). Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

Bacterial isolation, DNA extraction and WGS

P. alni M29 was isolated from stem of healthy maize plants as bacterial endophyte as described previously [1]. The genomic DNA (gDNA) was isolated from bacterial pellets using CTAB extraction method followed by library preparation according to the manufacturer protocol using Twist NGS Library Preparation Kits (CAT No /ID 104,119). Whole genome sequencing (WGS) of P. alni M29 was performed using the Illumina NovaSeq X plus platform that generates 2 × 150 bp paired-end reads.

Genome assembly and annotation

Raw sequencing reads were assessed for data quality with FastQC v0.12.1 [5], trimmed using Trimmomatics v0.40.0 software [2] and de novo assembled using the SPades v4.2.0 software [3]. The quality of the genome assembly was assessed using Quast v5.3.0 software [4]. Genome completeness and contamination were evaluated with BUSCO v6.0.0 and CheckM v1.2.4 software [5,6]. Genome annotation was performed with Bakta v1.11.3 webserver followed by pathway mapping using BlastKOALA webserver using KEGG database [7]. dbCAN3 webserver was used for carbohydrate active enzyme (CAZy) analysis [8]. The circular genome map was generated with Proksee webserver [9].

Comparative genome analysis

FastANI and skani software were used to calculate the average nucleotide identity (ANI) of P. alni M29 with other available P. alni genome in the NCBI database [[10], [11], [12]]. The most similar genomes i.e. P. alni subsp. carboxydivorans strain JCM 14,827, strains NPDC047994, NPDC077694, and Shahu were selected for orthologues gene family analysis using Orthovenn3 webserver [13].

Limitations

The draft assembly of P. alni M29 reported in this study might have missed some important gene from the genome.

Non-ordinary state-based peridynamics model for rock crack propagation: a combined stress-energy fracture method
Authors: Boyou Gong, Yongjun Song, Leitao Zhang, Weijie Chao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Non-ordinary state-based peridynamics (NOSB-PD) has been widely applied to rock fracture modeling due to its compatibility with continuum mechanics. However, the classical formulation frequently produces zero-energy modes and nonphysical displacement oscillations as a result of insufficient local deformation compatibility. Moreover, the direct use of classical shear failure criteria often leads to inconsistencies between simulated and experimentally observed shear crack paths. To address these issues, this study employs a bond-level deformation gradient constructed from the averaged deformation gradient at the bond scale, which restores local compatibility and effectively suppresses zero-energy modes. In addition, a triple shear energy criterion is incorporated to distinguish different shear-related mechanical states, thereby improving the physical consistency of shear crack identification and the accuracy of crack path prediction. The improved model is examined through three benchmark numerical examples and simulations of fractured rock. The results demonstrate that it can accurately reproduce crack initiation, propagation, and coalescence under complex loading conditions, while maintaining numerical stability and requiring minimal parameter calibration. Compared with existing NOSB-PD implementations, the approach offers a clearer mathematical structure and enhanced predictive capability, providing a reliable and applicable numerical tool for crack evolution analysis in rock materials.

Introduction

Rock, as a naturally brittle material, commonly contains various internal defects and discontinuities, such as cracks, joints, and pores1. When external disturbances like underground excavation or seismic activity act on rock masses containing these defects, the internal structures often undergo crack initiation, propagation, and coalescence2,3, which significantly influence the physical properties and mechanical responses of the rock material4,5. In fact, since the establishment of rock mechanics as a discipline, crack propagation behaviour has consistently been one of its core research topics. Accurately identifying the crack evolution mechanisms of internal cracks in rock masses and developing theoretical models capable of reliably predicting their instability behaviour, along with corresponding control strategies, have long represented a significant research priority for the International Society for Rock Mechanics (ISRM) as well as the global academic and engineering communities6.

Although experimental studies have made significant progress in revealing the mechanisms of crack initiation and propagation in rock masses7, systematic and detailed observation of the crack evolution process is often hindered by limitations in testing equipment accuracy and high experimental costs. Under these circumstances, numerical simulation has gradually been established as an important supplementary approach for investigating rock fracture behaviour8,9. As an economical, reliable, and repeatable analytical tool, numerical simulation can effectively capture crack initiation, propagation, and coalescence in rocks under external loading10,11, thereby providing new pathways for a deeper understanding of rock mass failure mechanisms12,13. Currently, methods such as the Extended Finite Element Method (XFEM)14,15 and the Phase-Field Method (PFM)16,17 based on continuum mechanics, have been widely applied to simulate and analyze crack propagation problems, which have played important roles in the study of rock fracture processes.

Peridynamics, introduced by Silling as a non-local continuum theory, utilises integral equations rather than traditional differential forms in its governing equations18. This allows cracks in materials, including their initiation, propagation, and coalescence, to be naturally handled, offering distinct advantages in fracture analysis19,20. In recent years, the non-ordinary state-based peridynamics(NOSB-PD) model has attracted significant attention due to its ability to incorporate stress-strain tensors into the constitutive framework, thereby strengthening its connection with continuum solid mechanics21,22. However, this method is still affected by the zero-energy mode issue, which can significantly influence computational results23,24. Despite this limitation, peridynamics remains effective in simulating crack evolution under complex loading and environmental conditions, providing a reliable tool for in-depth investigation of material fracture mechanisms.

Over the past decade, researchers have applied various fracture criteria in peridynamics and their improved formulations to investigate crack propagation in rock materials under conditions such as fatigue25,26, freeze-thaw cycles27,28, and seepage29,30, achieving a series of promising results. These studies have significantly advanced the understanding of rock fracture mechanisms and have demonstrated the strong capability of peridynamics in modelling tension-dominated fracture. However, when rock masses are subjected to compressive–shear loading, their fracture processes often involve the concurrent development of tensile and shear cracks, resulting in complex and coupled crack-evolution patterns. Under such circumstances, classical fracture criteria, such as the Mohr-Coulomb criterion, may occasionally yield shear-crack trajectories that do not fully correspond to experimental observations21,31. This indicates that such criteria may have limited applicability and play a dominant role. Therefore, existing peridynamics and fracture models still have room for refinement to better describe fracture responses under complex stress states.

To address the aforementioned issues, this study employs an averaged bond-level deformation gradient formulation to suppress zero-energy modes and proposes an improved triple shear energy criterion that incorporates the effect of the intermediate principal stress, thereby enabling the simulation of rock fracture under compressive-shear loading.

Furthermore, to avoid ambiguity, the term “flaw” is used to describe a pre-existing crack or fissure, while the term “crack” is used to describe a fracture or failure caused by external loads in this paper.

Method for suppression of zero-energy modes

The NOSB-PD model has garnered significant attention in recent years. It allows continuum constitutive laws to be systematically incorporated into the peridynamics framework and establishes nonlocal stress-strain relations, thereby reinforcing the theoretical linkage between peridynamics and classical continuum mechanics. Nevertheless, the NOSB-PD formulation is inherently affected by the zero-energy mode problem, which induces numerical instabilities. These instabilities commonly appear as oscillatory or saw-tooth displacement fields, severely constraining the model’s accuracy and practical applicability. This symmetry prevents the model from accurately representing certain non-zero strain states, leading to numerical instabilities. After determining the point state with the NOSB model described in Section "Non-ordinary state-based peridynamics theory", the bond stress state is evaluated from Eqs. 8-10 based on average deformation gradient Fij. In this paper, we characterize the tensile failure of materials using the maximum principal stress criterion, i.e., \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\sigma }_{1}>{\sigma }_{t}$$\end{document}.

The failure process of rock is governed by the combined effects of internal friction, stress path, and potential fracture orientation. In rock mechanics research, the Mohr-Coulomb criterion is commonly employed to describe shear failure and has been applied within the peridynamics framework21. However, this criterion relies solely on the maximum and minimum principal stresses, neglecting the influence of intermediate principal stresses, thereby exhibiting limitations under complex stress states40. Based on this, this paper introduces the triple shear energy criterion41,42, which accounts for the effects of intermediate principal stresses, to more accurately characterize the failure behaviour of rock materials under complex stress conditions.

Discussion

Bond-Scale treatment and state decomposition

Peridynamics formulates interactions at the bond scale, where tensile, compressive, and shear responses coexist. Classical fracture criteria, however, are typically expressed in terms of smooth field quantities derived from averaging bond responses58,59. Such homogenization suppresses bond-to-bond variations and can obscure the mechanical heterogeneity that arises under compression–shear loading. As a result, applying these criteria through averaged variables provides only an effective representation rather than the actual bond-level state. A more consistent approach is to evaluate failure directly at the bond scale when embedding classical criteria into peridynamics models.

Different fracture types are associated with distinct deformation modes and energy-release pathways. These contributions cannot be represented by a single equivalent variable without losing the state dependence of individual bonds. Accordingly, the fracture criterion should be coupled with a state-based decomposition, such as that introduced in Eqs. 35-37, to distinguish tensile, compressive, and shear components. This treatment preserves the local mechanical information relevant for crack initiation and propagation, thereby enhancing the fidelity of peridynamics predictions under complex loading conditions. Similar decompositions have also been adopted in recent studies, where they have demonstrated improved capability in capturing fracture behavior60,61.

As two major branches of computational damage mechanics, peridynamics and the phase-field method share certain commonalities62,63. Recently, some researchers have combined the two approaches to propose novel damage models64. In fact, the phase-field model has established a well-developed theoretical framework in terms of tension-compression energy splitting, mixed-mode fracture treatment, and energy consistency. This offers directly transferable insights for improving peridynamics. If the energy decomposition strategies and mixed-mode evolution mechanisms from the phase-field method can be incorporated into the bond-scale description of peridynamics, it is promising to establish a more physically consistent, broadly applicable peridynamics fracture theoretical framework capable of reliably predicting crack evolution under complex loading conditions. Such progress would provide greater support for both theoretical studies and engineering applications.

Model limitations and future works

It should be noted that, although the proposed model demonstrates certain advantages in predicting crack propagation paths and shear failure, its applicability remains subject to several limitations:i)The adoption of the NOSB-PD framework, combined with the construction of bond-level deformation gradients for each particle pair, significantly increases the computational complexity, thereby reducing overall numerical efficiency to some extent;ii)Rock materials exhibit pronounced heterogeneity and anisotropy, whereas the present model is based on relatively idealized material assumptions, making it difficult to fully capture the true mechanical behavior of rocks across multiple scales;iii)During rock failure, particularly under compressive-shear dominated conditions, the frictional interactions along crack surfaces play a critical role in crack evolution, energy dissipation, and block motion, yet such effects are not systematically represented in the current model.

Conclusion

In this study, an improved non-ordinary state-based peridynamics (NOSB-PD) model is developed to address the issues of zero-energy modes and the inability of classical fracture criteria to accurately capture shear cracking. The main conclusions are as follows:A bond-level deformation gradient is clarified based on the averaged deformation gradient, which restores local deformation compatibility, suppresses the occurrence of zero-energy modes, and enhances numerical accuracy.A triple shear energy criterion is introduced, and the onset of shear failure is rigorously constrained by the bond-level stress state. This allows the model to effectively capture the initiation, propagation, and coalescence of shear cracks under complex stress conditions.The proposed method is validated using three benchmark problems, including a Tensile simulation of a plate with a circular hole, a double‑edge flawed beam, and an SCB test. In addition, numerical analyses of the uniaxial compression test on pre-flawed rock are conducted based on the improved model. The results demonstrate that the model can effectively capture crack initiation and propagation under complex stress conditions and accurately identify the intricate coalescence patterns associated with different rock-bridge configurations.It is highlighted that classical fracture criteria incorporated into NOSB-PD should be constructed using bond-level stresses rather than simple averaging procedures, and the corresponding stress states must be properly considered to ensure accurate crack-path prediction. Future developments may draw inspiration from energy-decomposition concepts in phase-field methods to further integrate the strengths of both approaches, offering more powerful numerical tools for rock-fracture research and engineering applications.

The results demonstrate that the model can effectively capture crack initiation and propagation under complex stress conditions and accurately identify the intricate coalescence patterns associated with different rock-bridge configurations.

The results demonstrate that the model can effectively capture crack initiation and propagation under complex stress conditions and accurately identify the intricate coalescence patterns associated with different rock-bridge configurations.

It is highlighted that classical fracture criteria incorporated into NOSB-PD should be constructed using bond-level stresses rather than simple averaging procedures, and the corresponding stress states must be properly considered to ensure accurate crack-path prediction. Future developments may draw inspiration from energy-decomposition concepts in phase-field methods to further integrate the strengths of both approaches, offering more powerful numerical tools for rock-fracture research and engineering applications.

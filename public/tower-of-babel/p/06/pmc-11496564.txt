1. Estimating Excess Mortality During the COVID-19 Pandemic Between 2020–2022 in Korea.

Kim SS, et al.

The COVID-19 pandemic has significantly impacted mortality rates. This study analyzed excess mortality in South Korea from 2020 to 2022 using data from Statistics Korea and the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency. A multivariate linear regression model estimated deaths without COVID-19. Results showed excess mortality increased from November 2021, peaking during the Omicron variant period in March and April 2022. The elderly, especially those aged ≥ 65, experienced the highest excess mortality. The study emphasizes the need to focus healthcare resources on the elderly and establish an excess mortality monitoring system to evaluate pandemic response policies effectively.

2. Barriers and Facilitators of Pediatric Obesity Prevention and Management (POPM) Programs in Korea: Focusing on the Questionnaire About the Linkage in Community Level.

Hong YH, et al.

The rise in pediatric obesity globally, including in South Korea, poses significant societal challenges due to its impact on chronic diseases in adulthood. This study surveyed 577 program providers involved in pediatric obesity prevention and management (POPM) programs in schools and public health centers. The survey revealed low actual linkage between institutions and professions, despite its perceived importance. Challenges included student availability, participation, and the need for innovative programs. Schools need support from families, while public health centers require professional providers. The study emphasizes the need for strategies to improve institutional linkages and implement effective policies to prevent pediatric obesity in South Korea.

3. Efficacy of Bifidobacterium longum and Lactobacillus Plantarum (NVP-1703) in Children With Allergic Rhinitis: A Randomized Controlled Trial.

Lee S, et al.

This study evaluated the effectiveness and safety of a probiotic mixture (NVP-1703) in treating children with perennial allergic rhinitis (AR). In a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial, children aged 6 to 19 received either NVP-1703 or a placebo for 4 weeks. Results showed significant reductions in total nasal symptom score (TNSS) and nasal symptom duration score (NSDS) in the NVP-1703 group compared to the placebo group. Quality of life (QoL) scores also improved, and inflammatory markers decreased. No notable adverse events were reported. The study concluded that NVP-1703 effectively improved AR symptoms and QoL in children.

4. Healthcare Experts' Advisory Unit and Support (HAUS) Program for Medical Device Development in Korea: Introduction of Clinical Unmet Needs-Based Intended Use Establishment (CLUE) Templates.

Lee YK, et al.

The HAUS program, implemented by KAMS and KMDF, aims to enhance collaboration between clinical and engineering experts in medical device development. The program held several academic conferences in 2022 and 2023 to raise awareness. Satisfaction surveys showed higher satisfaction among development teams compared to advisors. Based on consultation meetings, the CLUE templates were developed to improve communication and establish intended use systematically. The CLUE process includes four stages: Initial Concept, Expert Consultation, Decision-making, and Intended Use. The HAUS program and CLUE templates have been proposed to facilitate collaboration and define robust clinical intended use for medical devices.

Renal Denervation for Refractory Hypertension Complicated by Acute Aortic Syndrome
Authors: Honghua Ye, Feng Qin, Zetao Wu, Weifeng Xu, Qingyun Zhou, Weiguo Fu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study aimed to explore the clinical application of renal denervation (RDN) in patients with refractory hypertension complicated by acute aortic syndrome.Case SummaryWe present a case of a 54-year-old woman with refractory hypertension treated with quadruple antihypertensive therapy, including nifedipine, metoprolol, hydrochlorothiazide, and sacubitril-valsartan. Five months ago, she underwent emergent thoracic aortic endovascular stent-graft repair for acute aortic type B dissection. Successful bilateral RDN (Netrod system) was performed without complications. Postprocedure 24-hour average blood pressure decreased to 119/73 mm Hg at 3-month follow-up from 136/82 mm Hg preoperatively, with hydrochlorothiazide discontinued and the rest medications halved.DiscussionThis case suggests that RDN may offer an effective approach for blood pressure control in acute aortic syndrome. Careful preprocedure evaluation and multidisciplinary decision-making are crucial.Take-Home MessageThis case shows that RDN combined with definitive thoracic aortic endovascular stent-graft repair potentially becomes a new treatment modality for acute aortic syndrome.Key words: aorta, hypertension, vascular disease

History of Presentation

A 54-year-old woman (height 165 cm, weight 60 kg, and body surface area 1.66 m2) was admitted 3 months before presentation with an 8-year history of hypertension. She had been on a quadruple antihypertensive therapy for 5 months since her thoracic aortic dissection was diagnosed and treated: a calcium channel blocker (nifedipine 30 mg every day), a β-blocker (metoprolol 47.5 mg every day), a thiazide diuretic (hydrochlorothiazide 12.5 mg every day), and a combination of a neprilysin inhibitor and angiotensin II receptor blocker (sacubitril-valsartan 100 mg twice a day). On admission, ambulatory blood pressure (BP) monitoring (ABPM) showed the 24-hour average BP 136/82 mm Hg, daytime average 138/84 mm Hg and nighttime average 130/77 mm Hg, with a nondipper pattern. The morning average systolic BP was 152 mm Hg. Cardiopulmonary and abdominal examination showed no significant abnormalities. Peripheral arterial pulses were symmetric.

Past Medical History

Her past surgical history was significant for emergent thoracic aortic endovascular stent-graft repair (TEVAR) for acute severe tearing back pain 8 months ago. Emergent computed tomography angiography (CTA) and intraoperative aortogram confirmed a penetrating aortic ulcer with localized atypical dissection in the descending thoracic aorta. Her preoperative BP reached a peak of 230/120 mm Hg. Postoperative BP monitoring showed significant BP fluctuations, with systolic BP ranging from 160 to 180 mm Hg.

Differential Diagnosis

Secondary causes of hypertension, such as renal artery stenosis, endocrine disorders (eg, pheochromocytoma and primary aldosteronism), and aortic coarctation, were excluded.

Management

After a multidisciplinary panel discussion, contraindications including secondary hypertension, severe renal dysfunction, or pregnancy were ruled out, and technical feasibility for catheter access to the bilateral arteries and their branches, without torturous, aneurysmal, or false lumen abnormalities, was confirmed by CTA. Renal denervation (RDN) was indicated because the patient's primary hypertension was deemed refractory, as she was on quadruple therapy including an angiotensin receptor neprilysin inhibitor.

The patient underwent RDN under intravenous conscious sedation on April 30, 2025. After successful puncture of the right femoral artery using the modified Seldinger technique, a 6-F sheath (Terumo) was inserted. A 0.035-inch hydrophilic guidewire (Terumo) was advanced through the sheath, followed by a 6-F pigtail catheter (Cordis) into the ascending aorta. Thoracic aortography confirmed that the stent-graft was well placed and that flow in the aortic arch branches was patent (Video 1). After heparinization, an 8-F Fustar steerable catheter (Lifetech) was then exchanged. Under wire guidance, selective left and right renal angiograms confirmed that the renal arteries were eligible for RDN without stenosis or other abnormalities. Then, the Netrod 6-electrode basket radiofrequency RDN 6-F catheter (Brattea) was advanced via the Fustar catheter into the left and right renal arteries. Per the preset ablation protocol, the catheter was successively positioned within the main trunk and the first-level branches of the bilateral renal arteries for radiofrequency delivery (60 °C, 120 seconds/ablation point), and a total of 24 points (12 points on each side) were completed (Videos 2 and 3). An immediate postablation renal angiogram was performed to confirm the integrity of the renal arteries; no thrombosis, spasm, or dissection was evidenced (Video 4). All catheters and sheaths were removed. The right femoral artery puncture site was closed using an Angio-Seal vascular closure device (Terumo). The estimated blood loss was minimal. The entire procedure took less than an hour without procedural complications.

Outcome and Follow-Up

Immediate postprocedure BP decreased from 130/68 mm Hg preoperatively to 107/62 mm Hg. At 3-month follow-up, the patient reported no significant discomfort and more stable home BP monitoring. Repeat ABPM showed the average 24-hour BP 119/73 mm Hg, daytime average 126/72 mm Hg and nighttime average 119/65 mm Hg, with significant improvement in morning hypertension. Hydrochlorothiazide was discontinued; doses of nifedipine, metoprolol, and sacubitril-valsartan were halved.

Discussion

Currently, global consensus and clinical guidelines for refractory hypertension include lifestyle changes, medications, and RDN. Although drug therapy is noninvasive, its main drawbacks include various side effects and challenges related to long-term patient compliance. In contrast, multiple randomized clinical trials have demonstrated that RDN can provide sustained or even lifelong benefits from a single procedure. RDN is a new solution for patients with refractory hypertension.1

Aortic acute syndrome is a life-threatening condition due to a tear in the intimal layer of the aorta or bleeding within the aortic wall, resulting in the separation of the different layers of the aortic wall.2 TEVAR represents definitive therapy for complicated acute type B aortic dissection.3 Predisposing factors mediate the risk by either increasing tension on the wall or causing structural degeneration, including hypertension, atherosclerosis, and several connective tissue diseases.4 After TEVAR, the patient had been continuously taking 5 antihypertensive medications for 5 months, yet BP remained poorly controlled, ABPM 136/82 mm Hg, meeting the diagnostic criteria for refractory hypertension. According to the 2024 European Society of Cardiology5 and 2025 American Heart Association/American College of Cardiology6 guidelines for adult hypertension management, RDN is classified as a class 2b recommendation for those patients. The refractoriness is multifactorial, potentially related to long-term uncontrolled hypertension causing vascular remodeling, renal sympathetic overactivity, and neurohumoral changes. Therefore, RDN was performed as a crucial adjunct for recurrent aortic dissection prevention and cardiovascular risk reduction.

The Netrod RDN system (awarded Breakthrough Device Designation by the Food and Drug Administration in 2021) consists of a renal artery radiofrequency ablation console and a single-use mesh renal artery radiofrequency ablation catheter. The catheter features a basket-like structure controlled by a handle, allowing it to adhere to the arterial wall well with diameters ranging from 3 to 12 mm. Six electrodes are spirally distributed along the basket to penetrating radiofrequency energy to the outlying sympathetic nerve fibers, securing circumferential ablation. The console automatically adjusts the output power to maintain a target ablation temperature below 60 °C (the threshold for vascular endothelial cell virtualization). Each ablation cycle automatically ends after 120 seconds. The Netrod system has been shown to achieve more significant BP reduction compared with other RDN devices, with proven safety and reliability.7 After careful evaluation, we performed RDN on this patient shortly after the TEVAR procedure with the desired outcome. The Netrod ablation catheter uses a non–over-the-wire design. One practical tip from our experience is worth mentioning here. When superselecting renal artery branches, the guiding catheter can be advanced deeply to the branch orifice under the guidance of an extension catheter or guidewire, followed by slow advancement of the ablation catheter; then the guiding catheter is retrieved back to the aorta before radiofrequency ablation is initialized. This approach enables ablation of most first-order branches and the main trunk of the renal artery.

Some key factors for a successful RDN in this case were: 1) comprehensive preprocedural assessment; 2) use of the Netrod 6-electrode RDN system for thorough circumferential ablation, improving ablation efficiency and efficacy; 3) carefully planned endovascular protocol executed by cardiovascular specialists; and 4) multidisciplinary collaborative decision-making and postoperative medication management.

Conclusions

RDN combined with definitive TEVAR could become a new treatment modality for refractory hypertension complicated by acute aortic syndrome. Further investigation and robust clinical trials are essential to define its role.

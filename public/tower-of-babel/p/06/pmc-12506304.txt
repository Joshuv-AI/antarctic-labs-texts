Spatiotemporal epidemiological characteristics of human brucellosis in Henan Province, from 1956 to 2023
Authors: Zhiguo Liu, Ying Ye, Canjun Zheng, Yue Shi, Chuizhao Xue, Min Yuan, Zhenjun Li, Linqi Diao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn Henan, human brucellosis is a re-emerging disease; however, its epidemiological and spatiotemporal clustering profiles remain unclear. In this study, we employed joint point regression analysis and spatiotemporal scan analysis to uncover the epidemiological features of human brucellosis in this region. A total of 71,423 cases were recorded from 1956 to 2023, and the number of average annual reported cases was 1,050, with an average annual incidence rate of 1.3/100,000. The re-emerging epidemic of human brucellosis spans from 2000 to 2023, with an average annual incidence rate of 2.2/100,000 and an average of 2,114 annual reported cases. The number of affected counties increased from 39 in 2004 to at least 155 in 2023, and the incidence rate increased from 0.1023/100,000 in 2000 to 5.212/100,000 in 2023. These data imply that the human brucellosis epidemic continues to worsen and exhibits significant geographic expansion. Joint point regression analysis revealed a significant increasing trend of incidence rate from 1988 to 2023 (average annual percentage change [APC] = 24.46, P < 0.05). The persistently elevated incidence rate may be closely associated with multiple socioeconomic factors, such as extensive livestock farming practices, frequent transfer of infected animals, and enhanced diagnostic sensitivity. However, further research is needed. Spatiotemporal scan analysis showed that the high-high and low-low clusters were concentrated in the western and eastern parts of Henan Province, respectively, highlighting the importance of controlling livestock movement from western to eastern Henan Province. Effective containment of human brucellosis requires prioritization of animal control measures, including tests, culling, and vaccination, as well as improvements in surveillance, restrictions of the movement of infected livestock, and public health education among high-risk populations.Keywords: Human brucellosis, Incidence rate, Average annual growth rate, Fixed base ratio growth rate, Joint point regression analysis, Spatiotemporal clustering

Introduction

Brucellosis is a worldwide zoonotic disease caused by certain Brucella species, which affect both humans and animals, including cattle, dogs, sheep, and goats [1]. Brucellosis poses significant public health threats to humans, especially in regions with poor hygiene, food safety, and veterinary services [2]. The evidence-based conservative estimate of the annual global incidence rate of brucellosis is 2.1 per million, significantly higher than was previously assumed [3]. The disease continues to be prevalent in Asia and Africa, including West Asia, Central Asia, North Africa, and East Africa [4]. Human infections occur mainly by interactions with infected animals, consuming contaminated animal products, and sexual and organ transplant routes [5, 6]. In humans, brucellosis is a multifaceted disease that affects various organs and tissues, even leading to serious complications [7]. Brucellosis is not only a severe threat to human health but is also responsible for vast economic losses to animal husbandry due to animal aborts, decreased milk products, and cull compensation [8].

Despite concerted efforts to combat the spread of brucellosis, it continues to pose a significant challenge to public health [9]. In China, human brucellosis has experienced a re-emergence, with the incidence rate increasing from 0.0281/100,000 in 1993 to 4.9966/100,000 in 2023; all 31 mainland provinces have reported human cases, and the affected regions have expanded to the southern regions [4]. Henan is in the central region of China, which was a historic epidemic region of human brucellosis from 1956 to 1980s. However, the implementation of comprehensive control measures led to significant declined in incidence rate of disease [10]. Since 2000, brucellosis has re-emerged, exhibiting a continuous increase in the incidence rate, with the development of livestock industries [11]. However, investigation into the disease’s epidemiological changes and spatiotemporal clustering patterns is lacking. The purpose of this study was to explore the epidemiological change and spatiotemporal distribution patterns of human brucellosis in Henan province, with the aim to tailor control measures to better combat further spread.

Methods

In China, human brucellosis has been a statutorily notifiable disease since 1955, and all suspected/laboratory-confirmed new brucellosis cases were required to be reported. All data were obtained from the National Notifiable Infectious Disease Reporting Information System. From 1956 to 2003, the number of cases and incidence rate of human brucellosis in Henan province were based on monthly reports submitted through paper-based post or electronic files. From 2004 to 2023, individual brucellosis cases were reported by doctors within 24 h of diagnosis to the online National Notifiable Infectious Disease Reporting Information System at the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention. These data were strictly reviewed by professionals, who removed duplicate and missing information. All data were processed and analyzed in an anonymous format, without access to personal identifying information. The number of cases, incidence rate, average growth of the annual incidence rate, fixed base ratio growth rate, and sequential growth rate were used to analyze the epidemic change of the disease. The average annual growth of incidence rate from 2004 to 2023 was defined as previously reported [12]. Using Microsoft Excel 2021(Microsoft, Redmond, WA, USA), we computed the average annual growth of incidence rates by applying the following power function formula: [(POWER (incidence_2023/incidence_2004, 1/20) − 1] × 100%, where 20 represents the number of years between measurements. The incidence rates in 2004 and 2023 were selected to describe the change in the geography of human brucellosis in Henan Province. ArcGIS 10.2 (Esri, Redlands, CA, USA) was used to visualize the data. The map of Henan province used was downloaded from the standard map service system (http://bzdt.ch.mnr.gov.cn/), approval number GS (2023)2767.

Joinpoint regression analysis of human brucellosis from 2004 to 2023

Joinpoint regression analysis of human brucellosis from 2004 to 2023 was conducted as previously described [13]. Briefly, the annual percentage change (APC) and the average annual percentage change (AAPC) for each segment were estimated using joinpoint regression, focusing on estimating the temporal trends in the incidence rate of human brucellosis. The APC quantifies the percentage increase/decrease per segment between two join-points, calculated via weighted least-squares regression, while the AAPC summarizes the net trend over the entire period by averaging segment-specific APCs, adjusted for segment length. Join-points represent significant time points where the trend direction changes statistically (p < 0.05). Temporal trends in annual incidence rates were assessed for statistical significance using Monte Carlo permutation tests with 4,999 iterations, implemented for model selection and uncertainty estimation [13]. For each trend detected, a 95% confidence interval and 5% significance level were used. Temporal analyses were performed in the Join-point Regression Program, and the number of points required for the adjustment of each segment was automatically selected by the standard software configuration [14]. Trend analysis was performed using Joinpoint Regression (v5.3.0; NCI, USA) to detect significant temporal shifts in incidence rates, with optimal joinpoints selected via BIC and permutation testing (4,999 iterations).

Retrospective Space‒time scanning analysis

In this study, a Poisson retrospective spatiotemporal scan statistic using SaTScanTM software was employed to identify the spatiotemporal cluster. The high spatial clustering was examined using a discrete Poisson distribution model [15]. Each scanning window was evaluated using log-likelihood ratio (LLR) and relative risk (RR) calculations to test hypotheses. As previously reported, the window with the maximum LLR was designated as the primary cluster, while other statistically significant windows were classified as secondary cluster areas across the province [16]. Statistical significance was assessed through 999 Monte Carlo simulations [17]. Maximum spatial and temporal window sizes were configured to encompass no more than 5% of the at-risk population and 50% of the study duration, respectively [12].

Results

Epidemiology evolution profile of human brucellosis cases from 1956 to 2023

A total of 71,423 cases were recorded of this analysis period, with 1,050 average annual cases, and an average annual incidence rate of 1.3/100,000 (Fig. 1). Human brucellosis in Henan province experienced two epidemic periods from 1956 to 2023. The first epidemic stage was from 1956 to 1980, with an average annual incidence rate of 1.46/100,000; a re-merging epidemic started from 2000 to 2023, with an average annual incidence rate of 2.2/100,000, and an average number of annual reported cases of 2114.45 (Fig. 1). A low epidemic period spanned from 1981 to 1999, with an average annual incidence rate of 0.042/100,000, and a corresponding number of reported cases of 34.37 (Fig. 1). For the first peak in 1969, the number of reported cases and incidence rate were 6,722 and 11.79/100,000, respectively; in the second peak in 2015, this dropped to 6.0852/100,000, before dropping further to 2.18/100,000 in 2018. Subsequently, in 2023, the incidence rate climbed to 5.21 (Fig. 1). Imported and sporadic cases predominated from 2000 to 2010, and the highest incidence rate was 0.83/100,000 in 2010. Local spreading started from 2011 to 2023, with an incidence rate higher than 1.3/100,000 at all points throughout this stage (Fig. 1). Analysis of the constant base ratio growth rate and sequential growth rate of the number of cases and incidence rate showed that the human brucellosis outbreak was on an aggravating trend from 2007 to 2015, with a fluctuating decline in the outbreak from 2016 to 2018, followed by a yearly increase (Table 1).

Geographic burden of human brucellosis at the City level from 2004 to 2023

A total of 50,404 cases were reported in Henan province from 2004 to 2023, with 2652.84 average reported cases (Table 2). The cases were distributed in all 18 cities, showing a considerable difference in different cities. Most of the reported cases (n = 9,622) were in Pingdingshan city, followed by 7,878 in Nanyang city, 5,767 in Luoyang city, 3,289 in Jiaozuo city, and 2,849 in Zhumadian city. The cases in other cities ranged from 503 to 2,173, with the lowest number of cases (n = 503) recorded in Xinyang city, which is in the southernmost part of Henan province (Table 2). Meanwhile, the average annual growth rate was 17.04% in Henan province from 2004 to 2023, and the average annual growth rates of incidence in 18 cities ranged from 3.25 to 43.23%: 3.25% in Sanmenxia city, 43.23% in Luohe city, 38.45% in Xinyang city, 38.42% in Xuchang city, 37.75% in Zhoukou city, 36.49% in Kaifeng city, and 29.98% in Xinxiang city (Table 2).

Trends in the geographic spread of brucellosis at the County level, 2004–2023

In 2004, cases were reported in 39 counties, with the incidence rate ranging from 0.001/100,000 to 11.25/100,000. The highest incidence rates were observed in Shanzhou district (11.25/100,000) and Zhongzhan district (10.1792/100,000), followed by 6.84/100,000 in Yang County, 3.95/100,000 in Baoling city, 2.59/100,000 in Anyang County, and 2.11/100,000 in Heshan district; the incidence rate in the remaining counties was lower than 2.0/100,000 (Fig. 2A). The counties with a high incidence rate were distributed in the northwest, adjacent to Shanxi and Shaanxi provinces (Fig. 2A), and 130 counties has no cases reported. The number of reported cases increased from 39 in 2004 to 155 in 2023, while only three counties had not reported any cases in 2023 (Fig. 2B). Furthermore, the incidence rates in 117, 59, and 25 counties were higher than 2.0/100,000, 5.0/100,000, and 10.0/100,000, respectively (Fig. 2B). These data reveal that human brucellosis is gradually expanding from northwest to southeast in Henan province (Fig. 2A and B).

Joinpoint regression analysis of human brucellosis cases from 2004 to 2023

The joinpoint regression analysis revealed that two joinpoints were recorded in Henan province from 1956 to 2023, the incidence rate increased rapidly from 1956 to 1967 (APC = 45.98), and declined from 1967 to 1988 (APC = − 25.00), before finally showing a significant increasing trend from 1988 to 2023 (APC = 24.46, P < 0.05) (Fig. 3). Further, APCC analysis showed that the incidence rate was continuously increasing in Henan province from 1956 to 2023 (APCC = 9.0079, P < 0.05). At the city level, there were three joinpoints in Zhengzhou city and Sanmenxia city (Table 3); two joinpoints in Luoyang city, Xinxiang city, Jiaozuo city, Nanyang city, Xinyang city, and Zhoukou city (Table 4); and only one joinpoint in Kaifeng city, Pingdingshan city, Anyang city, Puyang city, Xuchang city, Shangqiu city, and Zhumadian city (Table 4). Overall, all study regions exhibited statistically significant upward trends in disease incidence (P < 0.001), with annual percentage changes ranging from 4.53% (Sanmenxia) to 70.66% (Kaifeng), reflecting substantial regional variation in epidemic growth rates. Spatial-temporal clustering of human brucellosis cases from 2004 to 2023

Retrospective space-time scanning analysis for clusters with high or low rates using the discrete Poisson model showed that two clusters were detected from 2004 to 2023. The high-high cluster involved 31 counties, which mainly included Nanyang city, Luoyang city, Pingdingshan city, Sanmenxia city, and Zhumadian city, and areas in this cluster are in the west of Henan province. In addition, the time frame of the cluster was from January 1, 2014 to December 31, 2023, the relative risk was 5.37, and the LLR was 13447.39 (P < 0.001) (Fig. 4). The low-low cluster involved 81 counties, including Shangqiu city, Kaifeng city, Zhoukou city, Xinxiang city, Puyang city, Zhumadian city, Anyang city, Luohe city, Zhenghzou city, Xuchang city, Xinyang city, and Hebi city, all of which are located in the east of Henan province, time frame from January 1, 2010 to December 31, 2019; the RR was 0.24, and the LLR was 5498.93 (P < 0.001) (Fig. 4). These data indicate that the high-high areas are concentrated in western Henan and that there is an expansion risk toward eastern Henan.

Discussion

In the present study, we investigated the epidemiological changes and spatiotemporal clustering profiles of human brucellosis in Henan Province from 1956 to 2023. Epidemiological analysis demonstrated a sustained increase in human brucellosis incidence rates across Henan Province, with concurrent geographic spread to previously unaffected areas. Henan is a historic endemic area of human and animal brucellosis, and the average annual incidence rate was 1.46/100,000 from 1956 to 1980. Over this period, the epidemic peak was in 1969, at which point, the disease involved 63 counties, and the incidence rate reached up to 11.45/100,000. Thus, a comprehensive countermeasure based on animal vaccination was implemented to combat the spread; this included strengthening environmental sanitation, managing agricultural markets, and improving personal protection, which caused a continual decline in the incidence rate until reaching a control standard of national in 1980 [10]. With the rapid development of livestock husbandry, brucellosis re-emerged in Henan province in 2000. In Henan province, a total of 4,841 animals cases were reported from 2006 to 2021, with the number of reported cases increasing from 35 in 2006 to 1,818 in 2016, before fluctuating and declining to 148 cases in 2021 [18]. A serological survey of brucellosis in cattle and sheep in Henan Province in 2023 showed that, among the 229 unimmunized farms, the positive rate of cattle farms and individual positive rates were both 0%, while those of sheep farms were 2.00% and 0.24%, respectively, and those of scattered sheep farms were 3.13% and 3.44%, respectively. Among the 175 immunized farms, the antibody positive rates of cattle farms and scattered cattle farmers were 55.75% and 48.43%, respectively, and those of sheep farms and scattered sheep farmers were 81.11% and 58.14%, respectively [19]. These data provide robust evidence to explain the persistently elevated incidence rate in humans. Further bacteriology and genome sequencing of strains are needed to uncover the species/biovar spectrum and transmission patterns of circulating strains between humans and animals in Henan province.

The number of affected counties increased from 39 in 2004 to at least 155 in 2023, and the incidence rate increased from 0.1023/100,000 in 2000 to 5.212/100,000 in 2023. This epidemic pattern is consistent with most of the north regions of China, with the affected area expanding from the northern pastureland provinces to the adjacent grassland and agricultural areas, before moving to southern coastal and southwestern areas [20]. Recent epidemiological studies have indicated a progressive increase in human brucellosis incidence across northern China, with nationwide surveillance data confirming the disease’s expansion to all regions of China over the past decade [21]. Henan is adjacent to Shaanxi and Shanxi provinces, which demonstrate similar epidemiological characteristics of human brucellosis. In Shaanxi, the number of reported cases at the county level increased from 36 in 2008 to 84 in 2020, with a trend of geographic expansion from northern Shaanxi to Guanzhong and southern Shaanxi regions [22]. In Shanxi, spatiotemporal cluster analysis showed that the “high-high” clusters of human brucellosis were in northern Shanxi, while the “low-low” clusters were in central and southeastern Shanxi [23]. These findings position brucellosis as an emerging regional public health challenge, with epidemiological patterns implicating animal movement and unregulated trade as potential drivers of transmission. Future studies should incorporate molecular characterization of circulating Brucella strains to validate these observed epidemiological associations. Moreover, the southeastern area of Henan borders Anhui and Hubei, both of which have a low incidence rate of human brucellosis [24]. Historically, Henan is an endemic area of human brucellosis, providing a suitable niche for brucellosis spreading. Thus, strict animal brucellosis control measures are necessary, including restricted movement and transfer of infected animals from high-risk areas to low epidemic regions and the implementation of ruminant vaccination programs in high epidemic areas, as vital steps to control re-emergence of human brucellosis [25]. Brucellosis is a highly infectious zoonotic disease of global significance due to its adverse impact on public health, economics, and trade [26]. Strengthened regulatory surveillance and sustained implementation of interventions are critical for achieving long-term brucellosis control [27]. Notwithstanding the establishment of surveillance and control measures, critical knowledge gaps persist regarding the zoonotic origins and spatiotemporal transmission patterns of brucellosis, warranting targeted epidemiological research.

While this study provides valuable insights, several limitations warrant consideration. First, as the analysis relied on data from the NNDRS, potential underreporting may have resulted in an underestimation of the true disease prevalence. Second, the evolving diagnostic criteria during the study period could have introduced heterogeneity in case identification. These factors highlight the need for cautious interpretation of the observed epidemiological patterns. Future studies employing standardized, prospective designs with uniform diagnostic protocols will help to mitigate these limitations and provide more robust evidence.

Conclusion

The present analysis demonstrates that human brucellosis has emerged as a significant and growing public health challenge, characterized by persistently increasing incidence rates and gradual geographic expansion to new counties. This concerning epidemiological pattern underscores the urgent need for implementing comprehensive, multidisciplinary control strategies, including regular test-and-cull programs, with prompt removal of infected livestock, targeted vaccination campaigns in high-endemic areas, strict movement controls for infected animals, and sustained health education programs for high-risk occupational groups. Such coordinated interventions, combining animal and public health measures, are essential for reversing current epidemic trends.

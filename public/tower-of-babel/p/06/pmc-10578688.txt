Comparison of morphometric measurements of lumbar muscles on the convex and concave sides of curvature in idiopathic scoliosis
Authors: Emre Bal, Sabri Batin
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
It has always been wondered how the muscle volumes of the lumbar region are affected on the convex and concave sides of the curvature in idiopathic scoliosis (IS) and their possible differences compared to healthy individuals. Muscular changes in individuals with IS have generally been tried to be determined by measurements such as muscle cross-sectional area. However, since the changes in the locomotor system in scoliosis affect the anatomical structures of the muscles such as origin and insertion, the reliability of muscle cross-sectional area measurement decreases. Our aim in this study was to compare the psoas major (PM) and quadratus lumborum (QL) muscle volumes, which undergo the most changes on the concave and convex side of the curvature in scoliosis, within themselves and with healthy individuals. 34 women with scoliosis and 29 healthy women were included in this descriptive retrospective study. PM and QL muscle volumes were measured bilaterally on computed tomography (CT) images of individuals in both groups and recorded as mm3. Statistical analysis was performed with IBM SPSS 23.0, P < .05 was considered significant. In the IS group, both PM and QL muscle volumes were greater on the convex side of the curve (P < .001). When the IS group was compared with the control group, both muscle volumes were significantly lower in the IS group bilaterally than in the control group (P < .001). It was observed that individuals with IS developed atrophy in the lumbar muscles of both sides of the curve, mostly on the concave side of the curve, compared to healthy individuals. We think that these results will be important in planning appropriate physical therapy for individuals with IS.

1. Introduction

Idiopathic scoliosis (IS) is a spinal deformity that occurs especially at adolescence and the etiology of which is still unclear.[1,2] Spinal deformity that occurs in IS causes changes in the anatomical structure of many muscles and ligaments.[3,4] Muscles originating directly from the vertebrae, such as psoas major (PM), quadratus lumborum (QL), are the muscles that change the most with spinal curvature.[5] PM and QL muscles provide stabilization of the lumbar vertebrae in positions and movements such as standing, sitting, flexion, and straightening.[6] Imbalance between these muscles is thought to play a role in the onset or progression of scoliotic curvature by causing asymmetric loading on the vertebrae.[5,7–9] In recent years, numerous reports have been published on the asymmetry of the right and left paraspinal and spinopelvic muscles in patients with IS.[10] Muscle thickness in the paraspinal and abdominal muscles between the concave and convex sides of the curve in individuals with IS was examined by ultrasonographic imaging and it was reported that the muscles on the concave side were thicker.[9,11,12] Since there is a linear relationship between the diameter or cross-sectional surface area of a muscle and the force it generates,[13] individuals with IS appear to have stronger concave muscles. However, it is known that in scoliosis the convex muscles are the most active and strongest muscles that prevent the body from collapsing to the concave side.[14] It has been reported that calculating the volume of the muscle is more important than the diameter and cross-sectional area parameters of the muscle in order to obtain information about the magnitude of muscle strength and that the relationship between muscle volume and strength is more realistic.[14–16]

For this reason, it was aimed in this study to examine the changes in PM and QL muscle volumes, which undergo the most changes on the concave and convex sides of the curvature, using computed tomography (CT) images of individuals with IS, and to compare them with healthy individuals.

2. Materials and Methods

2.1. Participants and study groups

Patients who have been diagnosed with IS in the last 5 months at Kayseri City Training and Research Hospital and whose major curvature angle is over 45 degrees were included in this retrospective study. Institutional ethics committee approval (18.08.2022/683) was obtained and the study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. We report the findings in accordance with the STROBE guidelines. The working period started on September 1, 2022 and ended on February 1, 2023.

Data of patients with IS who had lumbopelvic region CT in the last 5 months were digitally recorded. As a control group, asymptomatic individuals who applied to our clinic and had lumbopelvic region CT images in the last 5 months were retrospectively scanned. Volunteers who agreed to participate in the study were invited to the hospital. After the informed voluntary consent form was signed, postural analysis and pain assessment were performed and the data obtained were recorded digitally. For optimal homogenization, only female individuals were included in the study and control groups. In this study, in order to ensure gender, age, race, and sociocultural similarities between the study groups, the individuals included in the study were selected from the social pediatrics outpatient clinic of the center where the study was conducted by block randomization between the study dates. Of the 82 cases with IS whose CT data were accessed, 34 cases met the criteria, and of the 67 cases whose data were accessed from the control group, 29 cases met the criteria. Cases that met the criteria were included in the study. The flow chart of the study is shown in Figure 1.

Flow diagram of study inclusion and exclusion criteria.

The inclusion criteria of patients with IS were determined as having Lenke 4-5-6 type IS, having an angle of curvature above 45°, and being a female individual. Exclusion criteria of patients with IS: Individuals with a history of trauma or surgery affecting the head-neck and spine regions and neuromuscular disease were excluded. The control group was composed of asymptomatic participants. Asymptomatic participants were qualified by the same orthopedic surgeon, who excluded any signs of musculoskeletal pathologies by physical examination. The presence of spinal curves was checked with a scoliometer. Criteria for inclusion in the control group: being a female, having no history of trauma or surgery affecting the head-neck and spine areas, and having no neuromuscular disease. Age, weight, and height of all participants were recorded, and the body mass index (BMI) was calculated for each participant using the formula [BMI = Weight/Height × Height (kg/m2)].

2.2. Definition of lateral deviation of the vertebrae using with Cobb angle

The degree of curvature in participants with scoliosis included in the study was measured separately by orthopedists (BE, BS) through their standing frontal plane direct radiographs using the “Cobb” angle, which is the angle between the “upper-end vertebra” and “lower-end vertebra” involved in the curvature, and the 2 measurements were averaged. Individuals who have IS with an angle of curvature >45° were included in the study group.

2.3. CT-scanning and calculation of muscle volume

The individuals who applied to Kayseri City Hospital Orthopedics service and had CT images taken in the radiology unit within the last 6 months with surgical indication were scanned and the images of the individuals who were suitable for the study were collected. CT scans were performed with General Electric brand IQ model 32 detector CT device with a section thickness of 1.25 mm in the axial plane. Axial images and coronal and sagittal reformat images created using these images were evaluated together with 3D images.

Today, there are powerful tools that provide high efficiency and accuracy, which perform manual, semi-automatic, and automatic segmentation of anatomical structures through medical images. In this study, Insight Segmentation and Registration Tool Kit program was used to calculate the volumes of PM and QL muscles. In the Insight Segmentation and Registration Tool Kit program, the volume calculation process is performed using 2 different methods, manual and semi-automatic. In our study, the volumes of PM and QL muscles were calculated using semi-automatic segmentation algorithm in ITK SNAP program. The CT data received in Digital Imaging and Communications in Medicine format was uploaded to the ITK SNAP toolkit for segmentation. After opening the ITK SNAP program, “Active Label” and “Draw Over” settings were made in the “Quick Label Picker” tab, “interpolate Labels” option was selected under the “Tool” tab and corpus pineale volume was calculated in the sagittal plane. The algorithm classifies pixels corresponding to the volume of PM and QL muscles in all CT data slices and calculates the volume in mm3 (Fig. 2).

2.4. Statistical analysis

Normal distribution analysis of the data in the study was performed by considering 5 parameters (Skewness-Kurtosis, Std/Mean, Q-Q Plots, Histogram, and Shapiro–Wilk Test). Parameters with normal distribution and a sufficient number of data were shown as Mean ± Std. Independent Samples T Test was applied in independent pairwise group comparisons, while Related Samples T Test was applied in pairwise comparisons of dependent groups. In the study, α = 0.05 and p < α was considered significant. IBM SPSS 23.00 package program was applied for statistical analysis.

3. Results

A total of 63 participants were included in the study; 34 female individuals with Lenke 4-5-6 type scoliosis in the scoliosis group, and 29 female individuals in the control group. 3.1. Psoas major and quadratus lumborum muscle volumes

The difference between the left and right PM and QL muscle volume data of the control group was not statistically significant (P > .05). However, the difference between the PM and QL muscle volume data on the right and left sides in the IS group was statistically significant (P < .001). The difference in right and left PM muscle volume data between the control and IS groups was statistically significant (P < .001). Both the right and left PM muscle volumes of the individuals in the IS group decreased by approximately 20% compared to the control group. The difference in right and left QL muscle volumes between the control and IS groups was statistically significant (P < .001). The QL muscle volume of the individuals in the IS group decreased by approximately 19% bilaterally compared to the control group (Table 2).

4. Discussion

In the study, it was observed that the PM and QL muscle volumes of the IS group were more atrophied on the concave side of the curve than on the convex side. In addition, bilateral PM and QL muscle volumes were decreased in the IS group compared to healthy individuals, and atrophy developed in these muscles.

In order to evaluate the muscle asymmetry between the concave and convex sides of the curvature in individuals with scoliosis, the muscle cross-sectional area has been taken as the basis in many studies.[17,18] In studies conducted with ultrasonographic imaging method, it has been reported that the paraspinal and abdominal muscle thickness on the concave side of the curve is greater than the convex side in individuals with IS.[11,12,18] It is assumed that there is a linear relationship between the diameter or cross-sectional surface area of a muscle and the force it generates.[13] According to these data, the muscles on the concave side of the curve should be stronger in individuals with IS. However, studies report that muscles on the convex side of curvature are stronger.[19–21] As a result of the change in the locomotor system in scoliosis, the origins and insertions of the muscles change, and the anatomical size of the muscles is affected. While the length of the muscles on the concave side shortens and the muscle thickness increases, the muscles on the convex side lengthen and the thickness decreases.[12,13,15] Since the anatomical location and structure of the origin and insertion regions of the muscles will change with the spinal deformity in individuals with scoliosis, we think that calculating the muscle volume rather than the muscle cross-sectional area will provide more objective information when comparing the muscles on the concave and convex side of the curvature. As far as we know, there is no study investigating the volumetric asymmetry that may occur between the convex and concave sides of the muscles connected to the lumbar region in individuals with IS. Therefore, right and left PM and QL muscle volumes were calculated using CT images and a comparison was made between IS and healthy individuals.

There are studies investigating the difference in muscle strength between the concave and convex sides of the curvature in scoliosis and showing that the convex side muscles are stronger.[19,20,22] Nachemson[22] and Panjabi[23] reported that the psoas and paravertebral muscles, which act as dynamic stabilizers in the spinal column, will become hypertrophic on the convex side to prevent the vertebral column from curving to the concave side. Stetkarova et al reported that the number of type I muscle fibers decreased in the concave side of the curvature in individuals with scoliosis, and this may be a result of muscle atrophy and degeneration.[10]

In the present study, PM and QL muscle volumes on the convex side of the curve were found to be more hypertrophic than the concave side in the IS group. The fact that the muscles of the convex side of the curve is more hypertrophic in individuals with scoliosis supports that these muscles are more active and strong.

Stetkarova et al, reported that muscles on both sides of the curve are affected in scoliosis, the number of type I muscle fibers increases on the convex side, while it decreases on the concave side.[10] It has been reported that individuals with scoliosis have bilateral muscle weakness on both sides of the curvature, especially in the deep trunk muscles (multifidus and transversus abdominis), due to some muscular changes such as muscle atrophy and muscle fiber type changes, compared to healthy individuals.[18–20] Payas et al, reported in their study that the number of tractus corticospinalis fibers responsible for the motor control of the muscles in individuals with scoliosis decreased on the right and left sides compared to healthy individuals, and they reported that this would result in bilateral muscle strength and volume loss in individuals with scoliosis.[21]

In our study, PM, and QL muscle volume data of individuals with IS were found to be smaller on both the right and left sides compared to the control group. These data support that bilateral muscle weakness is present in individuals with IS compared to the control group. In addition, the fact that individuals with IS have lower muscle volume on both sides than healthy individuals may be an indicator of bilateral muscle weakness in scoliosis.

Kim et al, examined in 85 patients with scoliosis the muscle cross-sectional area of the concave and convex sides of the muscles that are responsible for stabilization of the lumbar region and reported that the muscle cross-sectional area was larger on the concave side.[18]

In a study performed with ultrasonographic measurement, significant differences were observed in deep thoracic paraspinal and lumbar multifidus muscle thickness on the concave side of the curve in patients with scoliosis.[24]

With muscle imbalance, the muscles on the convex side are excessively elongated while the muscles on the concave side are shortened.[25] Therefore, the cross-sectional area of the muscles on the concave side is expected to be larger than the muscles on the convex side. We think that this difference is not related to muscle strength but to the change in the anatomical structure of the muscle.

Our study has some limitations. The first is that the sample size is small. The second is that the orientation of muscle fibers is related to skeletal morphology, but muscle fiber orientation was not examined in the current study.

As a result, it was quantitatively shown that the PM and QL muscle volumes in the IS group were asymmetrically located compared to the control group, as well as significantly smaller in volume. It was also observed that the muscles stabilizing the spinal column suffered more strength and volume loss on the concave side and atrophy developed in individuals with IS.

Our study shows the extent to which atrophy occurs in muscles on the concave and convex sides of the curve in individuals with IS. We believe that these data will provide insight into the ideal physical therapy for individuals with IS.

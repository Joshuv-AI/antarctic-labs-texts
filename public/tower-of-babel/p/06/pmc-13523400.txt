Secondary Giant Cardiac Lymphoma Treated by Stepwise Chemotherapy With Multimodal Imaging
Authors: Shota Inoue, Koki Nakanishi, Yuriko Yoshida, Norihiko Takeda, Makoto Kurano
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundMetastatic cardiac lymphoma is rare and may present as a large mass with a high risk of hemodynamic compromise and poor outcomes.Case SummaryA 79-year-old woman who had a history of thyroid mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT) lymphoma and achieved remission after radiotherapy presented with dyspnea and edema. Transthoracic echocardiography and computed tomography revealed a large mass occupying the right atrium and ventricle. Biopsy confirmed diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, suggesting a late transformation of MALT lymphoma with cardiac involvement. Low-dose chemotherapy was initiated and gradually escalated with multimodal imaging guidance, resulting in marked mass regression without complications.DiscussionTransformation of MALT lymphoma into diffuse large B-cell lymphoma can occur after long-term remission and present as isolated cardiac involvement. Stepwise chemotherapy may help reduce the risk of embolic events and cardiac rupture.Take-Home MessageSerial multimodal imaging plays a pivotal role in monitoring the response to chemotherapy in patients with cardiac lymphoma.

History of Presentation

A 79-year-old woman presented to our hospital with exertional dyspnea and bilateral lower-extremity edema. Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) at a referring hospital revealed a large mass occupying the right atrium and right ventricle. Fluorine-18 fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography–computed tomography (CT) demonstrated intense uptake within the mass, with a maximum standardized uptake value ranging from 11 to 18. There was no evidence of other sites of malignant tumor. The findings of a biopsy specimen obtained via a subxiphoid incision suggested cardiac involvement by malignant lymphoma. She was emergently admitted for further evaluation and treatment.Take-Home Messages•Late transformation of mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue lymphoma into diffuse large B-cell lymphoma can rarely present as isolated cardiac involvement even after long-term remission.•Stepwise chemotherapy may be a safe and effective treatment strategy for large intracardiac lymphomas, and serial multimodal imaging plays an important role in monitoring the response to chemotherapy.

Past Medical History

Eight years earlier, she had been treated for mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT) lymphoma of the thyroid gland. She had achieved remission after undergoing a total of 17 sessions of radiotherapy and had remained recurrence-free until the current presentation.

Differential Diagnosis

We suspected cardiac metastasis from malignant lymphoma on the basis of her history. The differential diagnosis based on the echocardiographic findings included right heart tumors such as sarcoma, myxoma, or intracardiac thrombus.

TTE showed a large mass measuring 99 × 59 mm that extended through the right atrium and right ventricle without significant tricuspid stenosis or regurgitation (Figure 2A). The right coronary artery ran through the mass (Figure 3), but the regional motion of the left ventricular walls appeared normal. Contrast-enhanced CT demonstrated a mass comparable in size to that observed on TTE, with a suspected thrombotic component (Figure 2B, red circle). Video 1 shows the mobile component of the mass. Histopathological examination of hematoxylin and eosin–stained biopsy sections revealed diffuse proliferation of large atypical lymphoid cells, without follicular formation (Figures 4A and 4B). Immunohistochemistry showed that the tumor cells were positive for cluster of differentiation (CD) 20, CD10, and B-cell lymphoma 6 (Figures 4C and 4E) and negative for CD3 expression (Figure 4F). The Ki-67 (MIB-1) labeling index was approximately 80%, and in situ hybridization of Epstein-Barr virus–encoded RNAs was negative. Management

Because the patient was hemodynamically stable, treatment was initiated with a low-dose, low-intensity chemotherapy regimen consisting of cyclophosphamide monotherapy (260 mg/day [200 mg/m2]). The patient was treated in the intensive care unit with continuous vital signs and electrocardiography monitoring because of concern for thrombus, rupture, and ischemia during the first cycle of chemotherapy. Continuous intravenous infusion of heparin at 10,000 U/day was also initiated. One week after initiation of chemotherapy, TTE and CT revealed a marked reduction in the size of the mass (Figures 5A and 5B). Prednisolone (80 mg/day [60 mg/m2]) was then added, resulting in further regression of the mass (Figures 6A and 6B). Outcome and Follow-Up

After completion of 6 cycles of Pola-R-CHP, TTE showed almost complete disappearance of the cardiac mass without evidence of embolic events and cardiac rupture (Figure 8A). Although fluorine-18 fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography–CT showed slight residual uptake near the tricuspid annulus, the mass had almost completely resolved (Figure 8B). Discussion

MALT lymphoma is a type of B-cell lymphoma that arises from MALT. Transformation into DLBCL occurs in less than 10% of cases, with a median time to transformation of approximately 67 months.1 Although MALT lymphoma commonly recurs in the lung or stomach, cardiac recurrence has been rarely reported.2 This case represents a rare instance of a late transformation (84 months) of thyroid MALT lymphoma into DLBCL with a large intracardiac mass.

Cardiac involvement in lymphoma is often clinically silent but may present with nonspecific symptoms such as dyspnea and peripheral edema as observed in the present case.3 Therefore, even in patients with a long history of remission from lymphoma, an index of clinical suspicion is necessary for early diagnosis. TTE plays a crucial role in the initial evaluation by providing essential information on cardiac structure, function, and hemodynamic status. In this case, the presence of a large right heart mass suggested cardiac involvement by malignant lymphoma. Importantly, the patient remained hemodynamically stable, and no regional wall motion abnormalities were observed despite the presence of a large intracardiac mass through which the right coronary artery coursed. Although chemotherapy is the first-line treatment of cardiac lymphoma, palliative surgery may be considered when a large right heart mass obstructs venous blood flow to the lungs and causes hemodynamic deterioration.4

Radiation therapy has become an important treatment method and improves survival of patients with malignancy, but it also involves some inevitable complications. Radiation-induced heart disease is one of the most serious complications, including pericarditis, coronary artery disease, valvular heart disease, and cardiac conduction abnormalities.5,6 Radiation-induced sarcoma is another issue in long-term survivors after radiation therapy.7

A cardiac metastasis in malignant lymphoma carries a poor prognosis with a median survival of 3 months.8 Recent reports suggest that Pola-R-CHP is more effective than conventional rituximab-cyclophosphamide-doxorubicin-vincristine-prednisolone regimen for DLBCL.9 However, intensive chemotherapy for large cardiac masses can cause complications such as tumor embolism and cardiac rupture.10 Therefore, in this case, chemotherapy was initiated at a low dose and low intensity to minimize these risks.

Our treatment strategy was effective and resulted in marked mass regression without major complications. Multimodality imaging played a pivotal role in both treatment guidance and response evaluation. Serial TTE and CT were used to monitor mass size and detect potential complications, allowing safe escalation of chemotherapy. These findings highlight the importance of individualized treatment strategies and close imaging follow-up in the management of high-risk cardiac lymphoma.

Conclusions

We treated a rare case of a cardiac metastasis of MALT lymphoma with a late transformation into DLBCL. Stepwise chemotherapy may be a safe and effective treatment strategy for large intracardiac lymphomas, and serial multimodal imaging plays an important role in monitoring the response to chemotherapy.

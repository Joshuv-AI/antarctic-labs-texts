Thermal Properties and Ion Redistribution in Nanostructured Single-Ion Polymer Electrolyte Blends Composed of Polyanionic Miktoarm Stars
Authors: Georgia Nikolakakou, Christos Pantazidis, Georgios Sakellariou, Emmanouil Glynos
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Nanostructured single-ion polymer electrolytes offer a promising strategy to decouple ionic transport from mechanical constraints, yet how nanoscale morphology governs ion partitioning and transport remains poorly understood. Here, we investigate single-ion polymer blend electrolytes composed of polyanionic miktoarm star particles dispersed in a low–molecular-weight poly­(ethylene oxide) (PEO) matrix. Differential scanning calorimetry reveals composition-dependent crystallization behavior that provides insight into the distribution of Li+ ions between the polyanionic particles and the oligomeric PEO phase, while polarized optical microscopy resolves the spatial organization of crystalline domains. Glass-transition analysis identifies two regimes separated at approximately 55 wt % particle loading. At high particle contents, Li+ ions remain coordinated within star-rich domains, suppressing crystallization and yielding elevated T g values. At lower particle contents, formation of a continuous low–molecular-weight PEO phase coincides with a change in T g behavior, indicating redistribution of Li+ ions toward the unentangled PEO matrix. Temperature-dependent ionic conductivity measurements reveal a transition in ion transport: below 55 wt % particle loading, activation energies comparable to PEO–LiTFSI reference systems indicate Li+ transport dominated by vehicular diffusion in the continuous oligomeric PEO phase, whereas higher activation barriers reflect confined transport governed by ion hopping within star-rich domains. A modified Fox framework accounting for the dependence of T g on effective lithium-ion concentration captures the T g behavior across the composition range. These results show that thermal and transport signatures provide complementary evidence of morphology-driven ion partitioning and establish design principles for fast ion transport in nanostructured single-ion polymer electrolytes.

Polymer electrolytes have attracted sustained interest for solid-state alkali-metal batteries due to their favorable interfacial stability with lithium metal anodes and their processability. − Among the various polymer hosts investigated, poly­(ethylene oxide) (PEO) remains one of the most extensively studied owing to its low glass transition temperature (T g) and its ability to solvate lithium ions through coordination with ether oxygens. − This coordination facilitates ion dissociation and transport via segmental motion of the polymer chains. However, despite these advantages, PEO-based electrolytes exhibit very low lithium-ion conductivity at room temperature, largely due to crystallization and the resulting suppression of segmental dynamics. This limitation restricts their practical use and motivates the development of alternative polymer electrolyte architectures.

Single-ion conducting polymer electrolytes (SI-PEs), in which one ionic species is covalently tethered to the polymer backbone, have emerged as a promising strategy to address some of these challenges. − In lithium-based systems, immobilizing the anion ensures that Li+ ions are the sole charge carriers, yielding high lithium transference numbers and mitigating concentration polarization effects that can promote dendrite growth. However, fully polyanionic polymer electrolytes typically suffer from extremely low ionic conductivity at ambient temperatures, a consequence of their high T g and restricted chain mobility. Blending polyanionic polymers with low-T g polyethers such as PEO can partially alleviate this limitation, but the resulting conductivities often remain insufficient, and the blends may suffer from macrophase separation or poorly controlled ion-conducting pathways. − Recent studies have highlighted the complex relationship between thermal properties, phase behavior, and ion transport in such salt-containing polymer blend electrolytes.

It is increasingly recognized that nanostructured, multiphase polymer electrolytes offer a route to overcoming the traditional trade-off between ionic conductivity and mechanical robustness. ,− By spatially separating functions at the nanoscaleassigning mechanical integrity to a rigid phase and ion transport to a soft, percolating phasesuch materials can decouple properties that are otherwise intrinsically linked. In this context, thermal properties such as glass transition and crystallization behavior provide powerful, yet underutilized, insight into ion–polymer interactions, ion redistribution, and morphology in heterogeneous single-ion systems.

In a previous study, we introduced a nanostructured single-ion polymer blend electrolyte based on core–shell polyanionic particles composed of asymmetric miktoarm star copolymers bearing glassy polystyrene-based polyanion arms and ion-conducting PEO arms, dispersed in a low-molecular-weight liquid PEO matrix. That work demonstrated that this architecture enables high ionic conductivity while maintaining mechanical integrity through controlled nanoscale organization. However, the thermal behavior of these systemsand its implications for ion distribution, morphology, and dynamic couplingwas not explored in detail.

In the present work, we focus on the thermal properties of these nanostructured single-ion polymer electrolytes. Using differential scanning calorimetry and polarized optical microscopy, we investigate how composition-dependent morphology influences glass transition behavior and crystallization in blends of polyanionic miktoarm stars and low-molecular-weight PEO. By analyzing deviations from classical T g mixing rules and correlating thermal transitions with previously established morphological regimes, we elucidate the redistribution of lithium ions between phases and its consequences for polymer dynamics. These results provide a thermal framework for understanding ion transport in nanostructured SI-PEs and establish a foundation for future rheological investigations.

In this work, we investigate a series of single-ion polymer blend electrolytes composed of nanostructured polyanionic particles dispersed in a low–molecular-weight poly­(ethylene oxide) matrix. The blends are formulated by mixing asymmetric polyanionic miktoarm star copolymers, (PSTFSILi)22(PEO)22, with methoxy-terminated PEO (M w ≈ 0.5 kg/mol), which is liquid at room temperature. As we recently demonstrated, substituting the hydroxyl (−OH) end groups commonly present in conventional PEO-based electrolytes with methoxy (−OCH3) termini significantly enhances stability against lithium metal by promoting the formation of a more stable solid–electrolyte interphase (SEI), while simultaneously extending the electrochemical stability window from below 4.0 V to approximately 4.3 V. The weight fraction of the polyanionic particles is systematically varied, spanning compositions of approximately 30, 48, 55, 67, and 75 wt %, enabling a controlled exploration of how particle loading governs morphology and, in turn, the thermal response of the system.

In heterogeneous polymer electrolytes, morphology plays a central role in determining ion transport, polymer dynamics, and thermal behavior. The spatial organization of ion-bearing domains relative to ion-conducting polymer phases dictates not only the connectivity of conductive pathways but also the local ion concentration, segmental mobility, and propensity for crystallization. As a result, changes in composition-driven morphology are expected to leave clear fingerprints in thermal observables such as glass transition temperature and crystallization behavior.

We therefore begin our discussion by revisiting the effect of polyanionic particle weight fraction on the morphology of these blend electrolytes, as previously reported. In that earlier study, we showed that varying the particle content gives rise to distinct morphological regimes, ranging from well-dispersed particles embedded in a continuous low M w PEO-rich phase to compositions in which the star-shaped particles begin to overlap and weak structural order emerges. The differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) results in Figure reveal distinct thermal properties of the (PSTFSILi)22(PEO)22/PEO-0.5K blends that are strongly dependent on composition and the corresponding morphology discussed above (Figure ). For compositions with wt % > 55 (i.e., 75 and 67 wt %), the blends are amorphous with no clear evidence of crystallization or melting transitions during either cooling and heating, as indicated by the red and blue traces in Figure . In contrast, blends with wt % ≤ 55 exhibit a transition to semicrystalline behavior. Notably, the 55 wt % blend does not show a crystallization exotherm during cooling but displays a cold-crystallization upon heating, indicating that the material is kinetically trapped in an amorphous state during cooling and crystallizes only upon heating (at a 10 °C/min rate). The corresponding melting temperature (T m) is approximately 50 °C. For reference, the DSC thermographs of pristine (PSTFSILi)22(PEO)22, PEO-0.5K, and (PEO)22 are shown by the black, gray, and blue traces in Figure , respectively. As described previously, the (PEO)22 star serves as the macroinitiator for the synthesis of the miktoarm stars and is therefore chemically equivalent to (PSTFSILi)22(PEO)22 in the absence of the polyanionic, PSTFSILi arms. Importantly, the melting temperature of the 55 wt % blend closely matches that of the (PEO)22 star and is significantly higher than the melting temperature of PEO-0.5K (gray line in Figure b). This observation indicates that the crystallizing phase in this composition corresponds primarily to the PEO arms of the miktoarm star rather than the low-molecular-weight PEO. In pristine (PSTFSILi)22(PEO)22, these PEO arms remain amorphous due to strong Li+–EO coordination within the star. In the blend, however, the emergence of a continuous PEO-0.5K-rich phase enables preferential Li+ coordination with the low-molecular-weight PEO. As a result, Li+–PEO-arm interactions within the star are reduced, allowing the PEO arms to crystallize. Thus, the onset of crystallization at 55 wt % coincides with the formation of PEO-0.5K-rich regions between dispersed polyanionic particles, as schematically illustrated in Figure .

At lower star loadings, crystallization becomes progressively more pronounced. For the 48 wt % blend, a crystallization exotherm is observed during cooling, and the melting transition during heating is primarily associated by the melting of the PEO arms of the star. In the 30 wt % blend, two distinct melting endotherms appear during heating: a higher-temperature peak near 50 °C, attributed to melting of crystallized PEO arms within the miktoarm star, and a lower-temperature peak near 15 °C, corresponding to melting of PEO-0.5K. At this composition, the molar ratio of lithium ions to ethylene oxide units is r = 0.025 (approximately one Li+ per 40 EO units). Although Li+ ions predominantly reside in the PEO-0.5K phase at this low star content, their concentration is insufficient to fully suppress crystallization of the low-molecular-weight PEO. This behavior is consistent with previous observations in low-molecular-weight PEO/LiTFSI systems, where crystallization is progressively suppressed with increasing salt concentration and the material remains amorphous only for r > 0.055.

To further elucidate the crystallization behavior of (PEO)22(PSTFSILi)22/ PEO-0.5K blends, polarized optical microscopy (POM) measurements were performed. POM measurements were performed using cooling and heating rates (≈10 °C min–1) comparable to those used in the DSC experiments. Representative data are shown in Figure . Figure a shows the crystalline morphology of the neat (PEO)22 star at −22 °C, i.e., well below its crystallization temperature of approximately 23 °C. The material exhibits large spherulites with Maltese cross extinction patterns, characteristic of semicrystalline PEO. In contrast, the oligomeric PEO-0.5K (Figure b) crystallizes into needle-like molecular crystals, reflecting its low molecular weight and distinct crystallization mechanism.

For the 55 wt % blend, the POM image recorded at −54 °C (after cooling followed by slow heating to allow cold crystallization) reveals a morphology consisting of discrete spherulites, indicating that crystallization occurs during heating rather than during cooling. Importantly, no crystalline features are observed in the regions between the spherulites. This observation suggests that crystallization is primarily associated with the PEO arms of the miktoarm stars. This assignment is supported by the melting temperature of the blend (T m ≈ 50 °C), which closely matches that of the PEO arms of the miktoarm stars and is significantly higher than that of neat PEO-0.5K (T m ≈ 15 °C). Furthermore, the absence of needle-like crystals characteristic of low-molecular-weight PEO in the interstitial regions of the POM images suggests that the PEO-0.5K phase remains amorphous, consistent with the DSC results showing a single melting transition corresponding to that of the (PEO)22(PSTFSILi)22 star.

In contrast, the 48 wt % blend exhibits a markedly different crystalline morphology. At −33 °C, where crystallization is complete according to DSC, spherulitic structures associated with the PEO arms of the miktoarm star are again observed. However, in this case, additional needle-like crystalline features appear in the regions between the spherulites. Such needle-like crystals are characteristic of low-molecular-weight PEO and therefore indicate that the oligomeric PEO-0.5K phase also crystallizes at this composition. This behavior suggests that at lower particle loadings both the PEO arms of the miktoarm stars and the oligomeric PEO-0.5K phase participate in crystallization. This coexistence of crystalline features reflects the higher fraction of the continuous PEO-0.5K phase at this composition, which allows the oligomeric matrix to crystallize in addition to the star-bound PEO segments.

Following the analysis of crystallization behavior, we examine the glass transition temperature (T g) of the blends, which provides complementary insight into ion-polymer interactions and segmental dynamics in the amorphous state. In polymer electrolytes, T g is highly sensitive to segmental mobility and ion coordination, as strong Li+–ether oxygen interactions restrict chain dynamics and lead to pronounced T g elevation. , Consequently, variations in T g can reflect changes in local ion concentration and ion–polymer coupling, even in macroscopically homogeneous systems. Figure shows the T g of the PEO components in the (PEO)22(PSTFSILi)22/ PEO-0.5K blends as a function of polyanionic particle weight fraction. T g values were determined by rapidly quenching the samples from the melt to suppress crystallization, followed by reheating at 10 °C/min (Figure S3 in Supporting Information). For all compositions in which the amorphous state could accessed, a single T g is observed, lying between that of neat PEO-0.5K (∼−82 °C), and pristine (PEO)22(PSTFSILi)22 (≈ 45 °C), indicating a dynamic coupling between the PEO arms of the miktroarm stars and the PEO-0.5K chains. The breadth of the glass transition (ΔT g), determined quantified from DSC thermograms shown Figure S3 in the Supporting Information, decreases systematically with decreasing particle loading, indicating a progressively narrower distribution of segmental relaxation times as the PEO-0.5K content increases. The T g of PEO phase increases monotonically with increasing (PEO)22(PSTFSILi)22 content in the blend. This trend reflects the increasing Li+ concentration introduced by the (PEO)22(PSTFSILi)22 particles, which serves as the sole source for Li+ in the blends. The resulting increase in T g highlights the strong Li+- EO complexation and the dominant role of ion-polymer interactions in controlling segmental dynamics. However, the experimentally measured T g values exhibit systematic negative deviations from predictions based on the classical Fox equation, shown as dashed lines in Figure . The classical Fox equation assumes composition-independent T g values for the pure components. This assumption does not apply here because the T g of both PEO-0.5K and (PEO)22(PSTFSILi)22 depends strongly on the lithium-ion concentration, quantified by r = [Li+]/[EO], which varies systematically with blend composition.

Guided by the morphology and crystallization results discussed above, we infer that for wt. ≤ 0.55, Li+ ions preferentially reside in the PEO-0.5K-rich phase, leaving the miktoarm stars effectively Li+-depleted. Consequently, the T g of the star in this regime is assumed to be approximately constant at −39.6 °C, corresponding to the Li+-free limit of the master curve. The T g values calculated using this two-branch modified Fox equation closely match the experimental T g values obtained from DSC, supporting the existence of two distinct morphological and ion-partitioning regimes: (i) wt % > 55 overlapping miktoarm stars with Li+ predominantly localized within the star-rich regions; (ii) wt % ≤ 55: dispersed miktoarm stars embedded in a continuous PEO-0.5K phase enriched in Li+

The excellent agreement between the glass transition temperatures predicted by the modified Fox model in eq and those measured experimentally provides strong support for the physical picture developed in this work. Importantly, this agreement is achieved without introducing adjustable fitting parameters beyond those independently determined from DSC, POM, and Raman spectroscopy. The ability of the model to capture the T g behavior across the full composition range indicates that the dominant factors governing segmental dynamics in these blends are the composition-dependent morphology of the miktoarm stars and the resulting redistribution of lithium ions between the star-rich and PEO-0.5K-rich regions.

In particular, the two-branch description of T g naturally reflects the existence of two distinct morphological regimes. At high polyanionic particle loadings (wt % > 0.55), the overlap of miktoarm stars confines Li+ ions primarily within the star-rich domains, leading to strong Li+–PEO-arm coordination, suppressed crystallization, and elevated T g values. In contrast, at lower particle loadings (wt ≤ 0.55), the formation of a continuous PEO-0.5K phase enables preferential Li+ coordination with the low-molecular-weight PEO, effectively depleting the miktoarm stars of Li+. This redistribution reduces ion-induced constraints on the PEO arms, allowing them to crystallize and yielding a nearly composition-independent T g for the star component.

Taken together, the consistency between thermal transitions (DSC), real-space crystalline morphologies (POM), and glass transition behavior (T g) demonstrates that ion partitioning in these nanostructured single-ion polymer electrolytes is governed by morphology rather than simple compositional averaging. The modified Fox analysis thus provides a physically meaningful framework for quantifying how nanoscale organization controls ion–polymer coupling and polymer dynamics. More broadly, these results highlight the utility of combining thermal analysis with morphology-aware modeling to elucidate ion distribution in heterogeneous polymer electrolytes.

To further corroborate the morphology-driven ion redistribution inferred from our thermal analysis, we examined the macroscopic ion transport properties of the blends. The temperature-dependent ionic conductivity across the different compositions is presented in Figure (a) (solid cycles) along with that of the pure PEO-0.5K blended with LiTFSI at various molar ratios r = [LI+]/[EO] (solid square). The pristine (PSTFSILi)22(PEO)22 miktoarm stars (100 wt %) display severely limited conductivity owing to their restricted segmental mobility. Upon blending with the oligomeric PEO-0.5K, the conductivity improves substantially across the entire temperature range. Notably, the conductivity curves for blends with particle loadings ≤ 55 wt % (i.e., 30, 48, and 55 wt %) display nearly identical behavior, exhibiting fast, liquid-like ion transport that approaches the performance of low-M w PEO reference systems. This weak dependence on particle loading is consistent with previous observations in this system, where the polyanionic particles remain well dispersed within the liquid PEO matrix, forming continuous conducting pathways that promote Li+ diffusion through the oligomeric PEO phase and changes in ion density are balanced with corresponding changes in the dynamics and the tortuosity in such systems. We note that ion transport in polymer electrolytes is generally described by Vogel–Fulcher–Tammann (VFT) behavior near T g. In the present system, the apparent Arrhenius-like behavior observed at wt % ≤ 55 arises from measurements performed well above T g, whereas for wt % > 55 deviations toward VFT behavior emerge as T g increases and transport becomes increasingly coupled to segmental dynamics.

In contrast, a pronounced decrease in conductivity is observed when the particle loading increases to 67 and 75 wt %. At these loadings, the increasing overlap between neighboring (PSTFSILi)22(PEO)22 miktoarm stars disturb the continuity of the PEO-0.5K, ions increasingly sample star-rich environment and ion transport now relies on ion-hopping between EO segments of the PEO arms that are attached to the core of the stars (dynamically restricted environments). This distinct transport behavior implies a macroscopic shift in the primary ion conduction pathways, directly linked to the microstructural transition from well-dispersed polyanionic particles to overlapping star networks.

This change in transport behavior becomes even more evident when examining the activation energy for ion transport as a function of particle loading (Figure b). For consistency, Arrhenius fits for wt % > 55 were performed only in the high-temperature regime where the conductivity exhibits approximately linear behavior, and the resulting values are reported as apparent activation energies. For particle loadings ≤ 55 wt %, the activation energy remains low and comparable to that of PEO-0.5K/LiTFSI reference systems, indicating that Li+ transport primarily occurs through the continuous oligomeric PEO phase separating the polyanionic particles. In contrast, once the particle loading exceeds 55 wt %, the activation barrier increases sharply, reflecting the disruption of continuous PEO pathways and the increasing confinement of ions within star-rich domains. This transition highlights the dominant role of morphology in controlling ion transport in these nanostructured single-ion electrolytes.

In this work, we investigated the thermal and ion-transport behavior of nanostructured single-ion polymer blend electrolytes composed of polyanionic miktoarm star particles dispersed in a low–molecular-weight PEO matrix. At high particle contents (wt % > 55), overlapping miktoarm stars form a star-rich continuous phase in which Li+ ions remain strongly coordinated to the grafted PEO arms, suppressing crystallization and leading to elevated glass-transition temperatures. In contrast, at lower particle contents (wt % ≤ 55), the formation of a continuous low-molecular-weight PEO phase between dispersed stars enables crystallization of the star PEO arms and is accompanied by a qualitative change in T g behavior, indicating redistribution of Li+ ions toward the oligomeric PEO matrix. Temperature-dependent ionic conductivity measurements further reveal a corresponding transition in ion-transport behavior, with activation energies comparable to PEO-0.5K/LiTFSI reference systems below 55 wt %, consistent with vehicular diffusion of Li+ in the continuous oligomeric PEO phase, whereas significantly higher activation barriers at larger particle loadings indicate increasingly confined transport likely dominated by ion hopping between EO segments of the star-bound PEO chains.

This ion redistribution is quantitatively captured using a modified Fox analysis that explicitly accounts for the dependence of T g on the effective local lithium-ion concentration. The excellent agreement between calculated and experimental T g values across the full composition range demonstrates that ion partitioning, rather than simple compositional averaging, governs segmental dynamics in these systems. We note that the proposed ion redistribution is inferred from the combined thermal, morphological, and transport signatures rather than from a direct measurement of local ion concentrations. Overall, these results show that crystallization behavior, glass-transition dynamics, and ion-transport measurements provide complementary signatures of morphology-driven ion partitioning. More broadly, they illustrate how nanoscale morphology and macromolecular architecture can be leveraged to direct ion distribution and control transport pathways in nanostructured single-ion polymer electrolytes.

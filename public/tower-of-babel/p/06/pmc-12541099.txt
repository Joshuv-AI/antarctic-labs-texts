Hybrid Young’s double-slit experiment and differential evolution for enhanced photovoltaic parameter estimation
Authors: Basma S Alqadi, Hassan Al-Sukhni, Diaa Salama AbdElminaam, Alaa Saleh Alluhaidan, Qusay Bsoul, Ibrahim Shawky Farahat, Rami Hasan AL-Ta’ani, Fadi yassin S Al jawazneh, Walaa H Elashmawi, Sahar A El-Rahman
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis article introduces a novel hybrid optimization method, the Young’s Double-Slit Experiment-Differential Evolution (YDSE-DE) technique, which is based on the integration of the YDSE approach with the robust capabilities of Differential Evolution algorithms. This integration enables the precise estimation of parameters in photovoltaic (PV) models, enhancing the modeling and simulation of solar energy systems. Employing a comparative assessment strategy, the authors demonstrate that the proposed YDSE-DE algorithm outperforms existing methods such as Ant Lion Optimizer (ALO) and Sooty Tern Optimization Algorithm (STOA) in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency. The effectiveness of this method is confirmed through rigorous testing, which shows improvements in the root mean square error (RMSE) metrics by significant margins across single-diode, double-diode, and three-diode PV models. RMSE values obtained using the YDSE-DE algorithm for the single-diode, double-diode, and three-diode models are documented as 0.0059117, 0.0015218, and 0.0018409, respectively. The present study underscores the considerable potential of the YDSE-DE algorithm in augmenting the precision and effectiveness of photovoltaic system simulation and design. The results develop and enhance the existing methodologies for PV parameter estimation and can be applied to optimize other complex systems requiring reliable and precise simulation. The novelty and scientific contribution of this work lie in the methodical synthesis of physical principles from optics with evolutionary computation, presenting a significant advancement in the field of renewable energy technology.Keywords: Young’s Double-Slit Experiment, PV parameter estimation, Single diode model, Double diode model, Three diode model, Solar energySubject terms: Energy infrastructure, Renewable energy

The proposed methodology

In the quest to refine the accuracy and efficiency of parameter estimation for photovoltaic (PV) models, this paper introduces a novel optimization framework that integrates the principles of the Young’s Double-Slit Experiment (YDSE) with the robustness of Differential Evolution (DE). This section delineates the proposed methodology, which leverages the unique interference pattern analysis inherent to YDSE and the adaptive potential of DE to form the YDSE-DE hybrid algorithm. Our approach aims to harness the strengths of both algorithms, optimizing the exploration and exploitation phases within the parameter estimation process. The integration is designed to address the complexities of accurately modeling PV systems while ensuring computational efficiency and reliability in the obtained results. The following subsections will detail each component of the methodology, providing a comprehensive blueprint of the algorithmic processes involved.

Results and Simulation

This section presents comprehensive experiments that state the effectiveness of a Enhanced Young’s double-slit experiment (YDSE) based on Differential Evolution (DE) algothim (m_YDSE) for the parameter identification of the three PV models. These include the RTC France cell, Photowatt-PWP201 cell, Kyocera KC200GT - 204.6 W cell, Ultra 85-P cell, and STM6-40/36 module cell. Experiments were conducted for each cell on the three PV models: the Single-Diode Model (SDM), Double-Diode Model (DDM), and Triple-Diode Model (TDM). The proposed approach is utilized for the accurate estimation of photovoltaic parameters and to assess its effectiveness across various configurations of PV models.

Across all datasets and model types (SDM, DDM, and TDM), the m_YDSE algorithm consistently demonstrated superior performance in terms of accuracy, achieving the lowest RMSE values and closely aligning with experimental data in the I-V and P-V characteristics. This suggests that m_YDSE is highly reliable for parameter estimation tasks, particularly when precision is paramount

The statistical analysis of m_YDSE’s performance revealed lower standard deviation values across multiple trials, indicating high consistency and robustness in delivering optimal solutions. While other algorithms, such as ALO, STOA, and SOA, occasionally provided faster convergence or shorter runtimes, they often compromised accuracy, as reflected in higher RMSE values and less precise parameter estimates. The trade-off between runtime and accuracy was particularly evident in algorithms like STOA and ALO, which converged faster but were less reliable in achieving precise results.

In terms of statistical analysis, m_YDSE consistently showed better stability in achieving optimal solutions. The lower standard deviation across trials for all datasets highlights the algorithm’s robustness, ensuring that results are not only accurate but also reproducible. This performance makes m_YDSE particularly well-suited for applications where precision and consistency are essential, even if the runtime is slightly longer. Conversely, faster algorithms may be preferable for applications where quick estimations are necessary, but their higher standard deviation suggests less reliability in maintaining consistent accuracy across different datasets and models.

Overall, m_YDSE demonstrated a strong balance between robustness, accuracy, and stability, making it the preferred choice for parameter estimation in photovoltaic modeling, especially in complex models like DDM and TDM. Conclusion

This study introduced the m_YDSE algorithm, a novel hybrid optimization method integrating the Young’s Double-Slit Experiment (YDSE) with Differential Evolution (DE) for parameter estimation in photovoltaic (PV) models. The algorithm was evaluated across multiple datasets and PV models, including the Single Diode Model (SDM), Double Diode Model (DDM), and Triple Diode Model (TDM). While m_YDSE consistently achieved lower Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) values compared to competing algorithms such as ALO, SOA, and STOA, the results should be interpreted within the context of experimental conditions and computational constraints.

One of the key strengths of m_YDSE is its ability to closely align with experimental P-V and I-V characteristics, demonstrating its effectiveness in accurately modeling photovoltaic systems. However, the algorithm’s computational demands are higher, resulting in a longer convergence time compared to some faster but less accurate optimization techniques. This trade-off suggests that while m_YDSE is highly suitable for applications requiring precise parameter estimation, it may not be ideal for real-time or large-scale solar energy deployments without further optimization. Future improvements should focus on reducing computational complexity while maintaining accuracy to make it more practical for a broader range of applications. Additionally, the generalizability of the algorithm should be further investigated. While m_YDSE performed well in the tested PV models, its adaptability to other solar panel technologies, varying climatic conditions, and large-scale solar farms remains an area for future exploration.

Future work should focus on exploring the adaptability of the m_YDSE algorithm to a wider range of renewable energy systems beyond photovoltaic models. This could include wind, hydro, or hybrid energy systems that rely on precise parameter estimation for optimal performance. Additionally, improvements could be made to further reduce the runtime of the algorithm without compromising its accuracy. Integrating m_YDSE with machine learning techniques for real-time adaptive optimization in PV systems also holds potential for improving solar panel performance under varying environmental conditions.

Investigating m_YDSE’s performance in different geographic regions or scaling it for large-scale solar farm optimization can be another potential avenue for future research. Combining this hybrid optimization algorithm with emerging technologies such as artificial intelligence (AI) and IoT could enable better monitoring, prediction, and control of energy systems to achieve higher efficiency and lower costs.

While m_YDSE excels in accuracy, its primary limitation is its computational cost, making it less suitable for applications where real-time processing is critical. However, in engineering fields that require precise parameter estimation—such as battery modeling, power electronics, robotics, and aerospace optimization—its robustness and reliability make it a valuable optimization tool. Future adaptations could extend its applicability to complex multidisciplinary problems, including financial modeling and industrial automation, where high-precision optimization is required.

Beyond PV systems, m_YDSE has potential applications in areas requiring accurate parameter estimation, such as battery modeling, power electronics, and other renewable energy systems. Its robustness and high precision also make it a valuable tool in engineering fields involving complex optimization problems, including robotics, aerospace, and even financial modeling. Further adaptation and improvement could extend its application to a wider variety of industries that depend on reliable, accurate simulations.

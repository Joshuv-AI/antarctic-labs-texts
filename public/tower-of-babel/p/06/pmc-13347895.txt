BEST-BAO: a multicentre, prospective, randomised controlled trial of endovascular treatment with or without intravenous thrombolysis in acute ischaemic stroke due to basilar artery occlusion – study protocol and rationale
Authors: Yang Xiang, Shu Yang, Lei Guo, Chongya Dong, Charles B L M Majoie, Adnan H Siddiqui, J Mocco, Fabiano Cavalcante, Bing-Hu Li, Jian-Hong Wang, Bin Huang, Duo-Zi Wang, Neng-Wei Yu, Wouter J Schonewille, Aquilla S Turk, Fu-Qiang Guo

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEndovascular treatment (EVT) is safe and effective in treating acute ischaemic stroke due to basilar artery occlusion (AIS-BAO); nonetheless, the impact of intravenous thrombolysis (IVT) on its efficacy remains unclear.ObjectiveTo compare the effectiveness and safety of EVT with or without prior IVT in treating AIS-BAO patients within 4.5 hours after stroke onset.Methods and designA multicentre, prospective, randomised, open-label controlled clinical trial with blinded assessment of endpoints. 340 patients will be consecutively randomised to receive IVT plus EVT or direct EVT in a ratio of 1:1 from about 100 hospitals in China. An interim analysis is planned when one-third (114) of the patients have completed the primary endpoint follow-ups. It anticipates that IVT plus EVT demonstrates superiority over direct EVT. If the superiority of IVT plus EVT over direct EVT is less than expected, the sample size may be expanded by up to 20% of the original size. If the efficacy of the two groups is similar, it will shift to a non-inferiority hypothesis, aiming to evaluate whether direct EVT is non-inferior to IVT plus EVT.OutcomeThe primary endpoint is the proportion of independent neurological function defined as a modified Rankin Scale score of 0 to 2 at 90±14 days after stroke onset.DiscussionThis trial is expected to provide novel evidence of the superiority or non-inferiority between EVT with or without IVT in the treatment of patients with AIS-BAO.Trial registrationNCT05631847 at ClinicalTrials.gov and ChiCTR2300070584 at Chictr.org.cn.

Introduction and rationale

Posterior circulation infarctions, which occur in the vertebrobasilar artery territory, comprise 20%–25% of all ischaemic strokes. Although less common than anterior circulation ischaemic strokes, posterior circulation infarctions—particularly acute ischaemic stroke caused by basilar artery occlusion (AIS-BAO)—often present with severe symptoms and poor outcomes. Due to the heterogeneity of initial symptoms and diagnostic challenges, AIS-BAO frequently results in poor prognoses, posing significant challenges in clinical diagnosis and treatment.

The efficacy of intravenous thrombolysis (IVT) in treating acute ischaemic stroke (AIS) has been extensively validated.1 Compared with anterior circulation large vessel occlusions (LVO), IVT offers better prognoses and lower bleeding risks for patients with posterior circulation LVO.24 However, results from the Direct Intraarterial Thrombectomy in Order to Revascularize Acute Ischaemic Stroke Patients with Large Vessel Occlusion Efficiently in Chinese Tertiary Hospitals: A Multicenter Randomized Clinical Trial (DIRECT-MT) and the Direct Endovascular Thrombectomy versus Combined IVT and Endovascular Thrombectomy for Patients With Acute Large Vessel Occlusion in the Anterior Circulation (DEVT) studies suggest that the endovascular treatment (EVT) may “dilute” the benefits from IVT in anterior circulation LVO as evidenced by the non-inferiority of EVT’s efficacy and safety to IVT plus EVT,5 6 although this conclusion is inconsistent with the Multicenter Randomized Clinical Trial of Endovascular Treatment for Acute Ischaemic Stroke in the Netherlands No Intravenous (MR CLEAN NO IV).7 This raises a question: could the efficacy of IVT also be diluted by EVT in posterior circulation LVO?

The preliminary conclusions in previous studies are inconsistent. In the Basilar Artery Occlusion Chinese Endovascular (BAOCHE) study, only 38 (17.5%) patients received IVT, and this subgroup was not further analysed.8 In the Endovascular Treatment for Acute Basilar-Artery Occlusion (ATTENTION) and the Basilar Artery International Cooperation Study (BASICS) studies, 108 (31.8%) and 237 (79.0%) patients with AIS-BAO received IVT, respectively, but EVT did not demonstrate significant efficacy in both subgroups.9 10 However, meta-analyses and cohort studies found that IVT plus EVT was linked to higher rates of favourable outcomes and decreased mortality in AIS-BAO patients compared with direct EVT.11 12

Combination therapy combines the rapidity of IVT with the high recanalisation rates of EVT, theoretically offering better outcomes than only EVT. However, most of these studies focus on anterior circulation stroke treatment. To address it, we designed this trial aiming to compare the effectiveness and safety of these two strategies (IVT plus EVT vs direct EVT) for treating patients with AIS-BAO.

Methods

Design

Endovascular Treatment With or Without Intravenous Thrombolysis in Acute Ischaemic Stroke Due to Basilar Artery Occlusion (BEST-BAO) trial is a multicentre, prospective, randomised, open-label controlled clinical trial with blinded endpoint evaluation. This academic trial was formulated by the principal investigators along with a steering committee consisting of experts in the cerebrovascular disease and neurointervention. Patient inclusion and exclusion criteria

Eligibility requires patients to be above 18 years, have a clinical diagnosis of AIS with a National Institute of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score of 6 or greater, and qualify for both IVT and EVT. Intracranial haemorrhages (ICHs) should be excluded through CT or MRI, and the BAO should be confirmed with CT angiography (CTA), magnetic resonance angiography (MRA) or digital subtraction angiography (DSA). Before randomisation, written informed consent is needed from patients or their legal representatives. IVT is required to be initiated within 4.5 hours after stroke onset. Exclusion criteria include patients with prestroke disabilities that disrupt the 90-day functional outcome evaluation (ie, modified Rankin scale (mRS) > 2) or any IVT contraindications as per the 2019 American Heart Association/American Stroke Association (AHA/ASA) guidelines.13 Detailed inclusion and exclusion criteria are provided in online supplemental materials.

Participating centr eligibility

Participating centres should be local tertiary hospitals experienced in conducting AIS trials, capable of performing both IVT and EVT, conducting at least 30 EVT cases annually, and achieving a median door-to-needle time under 60 min and a door-to-puncture time under 90 min. Besides, each neurointerventionist should have at least 3 years of experience in cerebrovascular intervention and have independently completed at least 30 cases of retrievable stent and thrombus aspiration.

Randomisation

The assignment of subjects to the IVT plus EVT group or the direct EVT group will be random, maintaining a 1:1 ratio. Randomisation will be performed immediately after completion of the screening process via an internet-based system (Central Interactive Management System [CIMS], V.5.2.4, Cims-medtech, Chengdu, China).

Blinding

Since this is an open-label trial, both the treating physicians and subjects are aware of the treatment allocation. The certified investigators blinded to the treatment allocation will obtain the information regarding clinical endpoints at 90±14 days after stroke onset using standardised procedures and forms. On the basis of these reports, the members of the independent endpoint adjudication committee, blinded to the treatment allocation, will determine the final mRS scores. Neuroimages will be assessed by the members of the independent imaging adjudication committee, blinded to the treatment allocation. To report to the data safety monitoring board (DSMB), an independent statistician will compile the treatment allocation data and the outcome data. In the interim analysis, the results of effectiveness and safety are not disclosed to the steering committee members.

Intervention

Subjects randomised to the IVT plus EVT group will receive intravenous recombinant tissue plasminogen activator (rtPA, 0.9 mg/kg, maximum 90 mg in 1 hour), which is followed immediately by EVT. Efforts should be made to minimise the delays in EVT initiation caused by the IVT procedures. EVT can get started prior to the end of IVT. Subjects randomised to the direct EVT group will only receive EVT without prior IVT. After the EVT procedures, all subjects will receive standard postintervention management following ASA/AHA and Chinese guidelines.13 14

The EVT procedures include retrievable stent, thrombus aspiration, balloon angioplasty, stent implantation or combinations thereof. Specific approaches and devices for each subject can be determined by the local interventionists. All the endovascular devices approved by the National Medical Products Administration can be used as the preferred devices.

According to the DIRECT-MT study,5 all the subjects are allowed to receive intra-arterial injections of rtPA with a maximum dose of 40 mg in the IVT plus EVT group and 30 mg in the direct EVT group. In the direct EVT group, if successful reperfusion (defined as the extended thrombolysis in cerebral infarction [eTICI] 2b–3) is not achieved within the 4.5-hour window, supplementary IVT with rtPA is permissible. According to the Chinese guideline,14 the use of tirofiban, non-peptide antagonist that selectively targets the glycoprotein IIb/IIIa receptor and reversibly prevents platelet aggregation, is permissible during operation and perioperative periods. In the case of stent placement, the subject should receive 300 mg of clopidogrel orally or tirofiban intravenously as soon as possible, then continue with 75 mg of clopidogrel and 100 mg of aspirin daily for 3 months.

Clinical and imaging evaluation

The trial procedures are summarised in online supplemental materials. Evaluations of vital signs, mRS, NIHSS and Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) at baseline, 18–24 hours, 24–72 hours and 5–7 days will be conducted by certified investigators. The baseline CT/CTA, MRI/MRA or CT/DSA images will be adopted to assess the infarct size, cerebrovascular lesion and collateral circulation. During EVT procedures, cerebrovascular recanalisation, cerebrovascular reperfusion and embolisation to new territory will be evaluated. At 24–72 hours, CT/CTA or MRI/MRA will be performed to assess infarct size, cerebrovascular recanalisation and ICHs. At 5–7 days, CT or MRI will be conducted to evaluate infarct size and ICHs. Additionally, mRS, Barthel Index (BI) and EuroQol five-dimensional five-level questionnaire (EQ-5D-5L) scores will be evaluated at 30±7 days and 90±14 days.

The mRS score used in the primary analysis will be based on centralised assessments. Using a standardised questionnaire, the mRS score at 90±14 days is assessed by local trained investigators who are not aware of the treatment allocation using a standardised process of telephone or in-person interview. In cases where the patient is unavailable or unable to answer, a proxy or healthcare provider will be consulted. These assessment processes must be recorded, either through audio or video. Written reports and the corresponding audio and video of the interviews will be sent to the members of the independent endpoint adjudication committee, who are also blinded to the treatment allocation. The ultimate mRS score will be decided following an examination of these reports. If the two members disagree, a third independent member, who is also blinded, will resolve the discrepancies.

All radiological images will be assessed by an independent imaging adjudication committee blinded to the treatment allocation. All the members of the independent imaging adjudication committee are required to be trained and tested before the official reading in order to reduce reader variability and increase reliability. This committee consists of a CT/CTA team, an MRI/MRA team and a DSA team. Two independent readers will review all the images, and if there are any discrepancies, a senior reader from each team will conduct a consensus reading. In detail, the Posterior Circulation Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score (PC-ASPECTS), pons-midbrain index, infarction in new territory and ICHs are determined using CT/CTA or MRI diffusion-weighted imaging.1517 The Posterior Circulation Collateral Score (PC-CS), the Basilar Artery on Computed Tomography Angiography (BATMAN) score, and the Modified Arterial Occlusive Lesion (mAOL) scale scores are determined using CTA, MRA or DSA.1820 Embolisation to new territory and eTICI score are determined using the angiograms during EVT procedures.21 22 Lesion volume is assessed by an automated and validated algorithm. The cause of stroke is determined according to assessments of the angiograms in combination with medical records.23 In addition, the DSA team will review all series and images captured during EVT to determine the procedure-related complications.

Data safety monitoring board (DSMB)

The DSMB, which is independent, includes a neurologist, an interventionalist and a biostatistician, none of whom is involved in this trial. This board will convene at least annually to review structured and unblinded reports from independent statisticians and provide recommendations to the steering committee. An interim analysis will be conducted after one-third of the sample size (114 patients) completes primary endpoint follow-ups. The results, including mortality and other key endpoints such as SAEs, will be confidentially conveyed to DSMB by an independent statistician. If necessary, the DSMB will advise the steering committee on sample size adjustments, study design modifications or the potential early termination. The steering committee should remain blinded to the interim analysis results.

Sample size calculation

Currently, no large-scale randomised controlled trials exist in this field. The sample size for this trial is derived from the outcomes of the Registration Study for Critical Care of Acute Ischaemic Stroke after Recanalization (RESCUE-RE) study conducted in a Chinese population. The proportion of patients achieving an mRS ≤2 at 90 days was reported as 42.03% in the IVT plus EVT group and 26.14% in the direct EVT group. Assuming a superiority hypothesis to test whether the good outcome rate in the IVT plus EVT group exceeds that of the direct EVT group, with a one-sided type I error rate of 0.025 and 80% power, it requires 136 patients per group, for a total of 272 patients. Accounting for an estimated 20% dropout rate, the final sample size is determined to be 340 patients, with 170 per group.11 24

Given the limited prior evidence, this trial anticipates that IVT plus EVT demonstrates superiority over direct EVT. However, based on evidence from anterior circulation stroke trials showing non-inferiority of direct EVT compared with IVT plus EVT, an interim analysis is planned when one-third (114) of the patients have completed primary endpoint follow-ups. The DSMB will calculate conditional power based on the existing data and provide recommendations regarding sample size adjustments or hypothesis modifications, following the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) Adaptive Design Guidance principles and Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials-Adaptive guidelines.25 26 If the superiority of IVT plus EVT over direct EVT is less than expected, the sample size may be expanded by up to 20% of the original size. If the efficacy of the two groups is similar, it will shift to a non-inferiority hypothesis, aiming to evaluate whether direct EVT is non-inferior to IVT plus EVT. Assuming the two treatments are equally effective (ie, 42.03% of patients achieve mRS ≤2 in both groups), the current sample size of 170 patients per group will provide 80% power to test non-inferiority at a one-sided alpha of 0.025, with a non-inferiority margin of −15% in rate difference. The interim analysis and sample size re-estimation will be conducted under strict control to avoid inflating the type I error rate, with the DSMB leading the process.

Given the current design and practical constraints, a non-inferiority margin of 15% has been chosen despite it being relatively wide. This choice reflects a necessary compromise, as the planned sample size of 340 patients (170 per group) only provides approximately 80% power to demonstrate non-inferiority at a 15% margin, assuming comparable efficacy rates (42.03% in both groups) and a one-sided type I error of 0.025. Further reducing the non-inferiority margin would require a substantially larger sample size (eg, to 10%, the sample size would be inflated to 766), which would pose significant operational and financial challenges and render the study infeasible. To address concerns regarding this margin and to enhance transparency, we will additionally provide the following: (1) the estimated results using a 10% non-inferiority margin as a reference for readers, allowing them to interpret the findings under a stricter threshold; (2) a posthoc calculation of the achieved statistical power for a 10% non-inferiority margin based on the final sample and observed event rates and (3) a detailed discussion of these methodological limitations in the limitations section, including the rationale for selecting the 15% margin, the implications of a wider margin and the potential impact on the interpretation of non-inferiority findings. These measures aim to ensure that the study conclusions are interpreted rigorously and transparently, while acknowledging the inherent trade-offs in trial design within the constraints of available resources.

Statistical analyses

The primary endpoint is the proportion of patients achieving mRS ≤2 at 90±14 days. This proportion of each group will be calculated, and the difference in rates will be presented with 95% CIs. A log-binomial model with log-link function will be applied to adjust for potential confounders, including age, prestroke mRS, time from onset to randomisation, stroke severity (NIHSS) and collateral circulation (PC-CS). The differences in rates, both unadjusted and adjusted, along with their 95% CIs, will be presented. For shift analysis of mRS scores, ordinal logistic regression will be used to calculate the common OR and its 95% CI.

The primary analysis will follow the intention-to-treat (ITT) principle. The main analysis will be based on a modified ITT population, defined as patients who were randomised and received treatment, and analysis will be conducted according to the original randomisation. Additional analysis populations, including the as-treated population and per-protocol population, will be used for sensitivity analyses. Adjustments for multiplicity will not be made for secondary and exploratory endpoints. Point estimates and corresponding 95% CIs will be reported without hypothesis testing for inferences. Detailed definitions of populations and analytic methods for specific endpoints are available in the Statistical Analysis Plan within the online supplemental materials.

Ethical considerations

This study was conducted in accordance with the ethical standards of the Helsinki Declaration.27 Local investigators must ensure that subjects have a complete understanding of this trial by giving patients or their legal representatives comprehensive details about the study’s purpose, benefits, potential risks, rights and responsibilities and obtaining written consent before randomisation. Subjects are entitled to exit the trial at any point, and their privacy must be protected. Each subject and the corresponding investigator in the participating centre have to share their phone number for continuous contact. All participating centres have received ethical approval from their respective ethics committees.

Study organisation

The BEST-BAO trial is an investigator-initiated trial organised by Sichuan Provincial People’s Hospital of China and is conducted in 97 comprehensive stroke centres in China. The first patient was recruited on 24 April 2024, and 296 patients had been enrolled by December 2024.

Discussion

This trial permits the use of DSA for the assessment of BAO, and the workflow time for such patients potentially differs from those undergoing non-invasive vascular screening, as evidenced by distinct temporal sequences of the femoral artery puncture and randomisation. Therefore, we will record and investigate the above two kinds of situations separately at baseline, endpoints and subgroup analyses. This distinction also accounts for the modified definition of EVT initiation time in our protocol, which departs from those used in prior anterior circulation trials. This definition better reflects real-world procedural variations in posterior circulation stroke management, particularly in settings where the endovascular procedures are employed as both diagnostic and therapeutic modalities.

The lower prevalence of BAO than anterior circulation LVO and the strict requirement for the time window for IVT are both expected to slow the enrolment speed.28 Thus, we have included nearly 100 centres across 12 provinces, covering a population of 331.97 million and covering major geographical regions in the Chinese mainland. This broad inclusion is intended to enhance the generalisability of our findings. Moreover, the rapid enrolment progress observed thus far reflects ongoing advancements in China’s stroke care infrastructure, including the expansion of certified stroke centres and improvements in prehospital triage and emergency medical service systems.29 These developments have facilitated more timely identification and referral of patients with suspected posterior circulation stroke.

The evaluation methods for collateral circulation in the posterior circulation are different from those in the anterior circulation, which is why we chose PC-CS and BATMAN over Tan’s score.18 19 30 We did not adopt the concept of revascularisation, and clarified the vascular reperfusion and recanalisation using eTICI and mAOL scores, respectively.20 22 This allows for a more granular understanding of therapeutic success and facilitates comparison with existing posterior circulation literature.

In addition, ICH and sICH are critical safety endpoints in previous trials related to EVT, and given that CT and MRI are permitted at each visit site in this trial, we have employed three evaluation methods including European Cooperative Acute Stroke Study II (ECASS II) Classification, Safe Implementation of Thrombolysis in Stroke–Monitoring Study (SITS-MOST) Criteria and Heidelberg Bleeding Classification.3133 This multiframework evaluation enhances the robustness and comparability of safety assessments across modalities and centres.

In this trial, we selected an mRS score of 0–2 at 90 days as the primary endpoint, which differs from several recent posterior circulation trials that adopted mRS 0–3.8 This decision was based on the observational data from a Chinese cohort study,11 where the absolute difference in the proportion of patients achieving mRS 0–2 between the direct EVT and bridging therapy groups was greater than the corresponding difference using the mRS 0–3 threshold, which suggests that choosing mRS 0–2 as the primary endpoint might enhance the chances of identifying a statistically significant treatment effect and require a smaller sample size. This selection is consistent with the primary outcome measures used in anterior circulation trials such as DEVT and SKIP, further supporting the validity of this threshold for efficacy evaluation.6 34

Recently, patient and public involvement (PPI) has gained significant attention in clinical research, with its importance highlighted in top-tier medical journals. Although the concept of PPI was not explicitly stated in the design phase of our protocol, we have incorporated these elements into the implementation of the study, including collecting feedback from patients and their families on their satisfaction with the trial processes during follow-up, promoting the importance of secondary prevention of ischaemic stroke and distributing easy-to-understand health education brochures. We will also conduct community-free clinics, community health talks and television or online promotions on the theme of AIS-BAO so as to convey the seriousness, prevention and first aid methods of this disease to the general public.

Our trial is expected to provide novel evidence of the superiority or non-inferiority between EVT with or without IVT in treating patients with AIS-BAO. Given the paucity of large-scale randomised trials in this population, our findings will help clarify optimal treatment strategies for this high-risk group. To our knowledge, only one other similar trial is currently ongoing (ATTENTION IV, NCT05827042), and the complementary results from these studies are likely to substantially inform future clinical guidelines and practice.

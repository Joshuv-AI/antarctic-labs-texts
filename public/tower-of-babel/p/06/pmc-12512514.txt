Mobile application-based lifestyle intervention for improving glycemic control and body composition in manufacturing company employees in South Korea
Authors: Dahyeon Koo, Yujin Bang, Dohoung Kim, Irang Im, Pumsoo Kim, Dougho Park
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundWorkplace environments play a significant role in the development of chronic metabolic disorders. In this study, we evaluated the effectiveness of a mobile application–assisted lifestyle intervention integrated with continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) in improving glycemic control and body composition among employees of manufacturing companies and explored key predictors of intervention responsiveness.MethodsA prospective cohort study was conducted involving 344 employees from two manufacturing companies in South Korea. Participants underwent a 12-week intervention that included individualized coaching through a mobile application and structured behavioral support, with the first 2 weeks involving real-time glucose monitoring. The primary outcomes included changes in glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c) and body mass index (BMI). Secondary outcomes were body composition parameters and CGM-derived glycemic variability indices, including spike count, average real variability (ARV), and frequency strength variability (FSV). Logistic regression analysis was conducted to identify key predictors of intervention effectiveness.ResultsAfter 12 weeks, HbA1c, BMI, body weight, and body fat mass significantly reduced. In addition, the muscle-to-fat ratio significantly increased. Participants with baseline HbA1c ≥ 6.5% showed greater improvements in HbA1c (7.2→6.8%, p < 0.001) than those with HbA1c < 6.5%. Similarly, the high BMI subgroup (≥ 25 kg/m2) exhibited significant improvements in BMI (27.6 to 27.0 kg/m2, p < 0.001). Early CGM data indicated significant reductions in spike count (p = 0.046), ARV (p = 0.001), and FSV (p = 0.031). Lower baseline FSV indicated greater HbA1c improvement (adjusted odds ratio, 0.67; 95% confidence interval, 0.50–0.90, p = 0.008).ConclusionThis study demonstrated that a 12-week mobile application-based lifestyle intervention is associated with improved glycemic control and body composition, particularly among manufacturing company employees with elevated baseline HbA1c and BMI. Early reductions in glycemic variability indicated the intervention’s potential for rapid and sustained metabolic benefits.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13098-025-01948-6.

Introduction

The prevalence of chronic metabolic diseases, including type 2 diabetes, obesity, dyslipidemia, and hypertension, has been steadily increasing globally, posing significant public health challenges [1–3]. Furthermore, workplace environments, characterized by sedentary behavior, unhealthy dietary habits, irregular sleep patterns, or high stress levels, are crucial settings that contribute substantially to the risk of developing metabolic disorders [4]. Workplace-based health promotion programs have emerged as significant strategies for addressing these health issues [5, 6], leveraging the structured environment and accessibility of employee populations to implement targeted lifestyle interventions.

The latest innovations in digital health technologies, including mobile applications and continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) systems, have paved the way for highly effective lifestyle interventions [7]. Mobile health interventions provide real-time targeted feedback and coaching, enabling sustained behavioral changes [8]. CGM devices provide continuous real-time glucose monitoring, allowing individuals to understand their glycemic responses to specific diets and physical activities, thereby promoting personalized health management strategies. Several studies have demonstrated that mobile health interventions were effective in improving glycemic regulation and body composition [9, 10]. However, evidence regarding the integrated use of CGM data within mobile-based personalized lifestyle interventions, particularly in workplace settings, remains limited.

Therefore, this study aimed to evaluate the effectiveness of a mobile application-assisted lifestyle intervention integrated with CGM technology in improving glycemic control and body composition among employees of manufacturing companies. Moreover, this study focused on identifying key predictors associated with intervention responsiveness.

Materials and methods

Participants

This prospective cohort study was conducted as part of workplace health promotion programs at two large manufacturing companies in South Korea. A total of 344 employees voluntarily enrolled in this study from March 2024 to March 2025. The only exclusion criterion was refusal or inability to provide informed consent. All participants provided written informed consent before enrollment.

The Institutional Review Board of Pohang Stroke and Spine Hospital reviewed and approved the study protocol (PSSH0475-202504-HR-002-01). This study was conducted in accordance with the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki.

Mobile application-assisted lifestyle intervention

In this study, a 12-week integrated lifestyle modification program was delivered through a mobile application called Healthiseoul (Healthibuddy Co., Pohang, South Korea). This program supported tailored health management for individuals with chronic metabolic conditions, including diabetes, prediabetes, obesity, dyslipidemia, and hypertension (Fig. 1). The program comprised three sequential phases combining mobile technology, CGM with FreeStyle Libre™ (Abbott, Chicago, IL, USA), and dedicated professional coaching to encourage sustainable behavioral changes. In the first 2 weeks, the participants used a CGM device for tracking their glycemic responses to daily dietary and physical activity patterns in real time. In this phase, individualized health data were collected for tailoring future interventions. During the first 8 weeks, including the GCM monitoring period, a dedicated health coach provided personalized dietary counseling and physical activity planning through one-on-one communication to each participant. Additionally, the coaches offered motivational support and behavior-change strategies to promote adherence. The final 4-week phase was a self-directed follow-up period. In this phase, participants practiced maintaining healthy habits independently with minimal direct support. This “after service” aimed to reinforce self-management skills and long-term lifestyle maintenance (Fig. 2).

Participants registered their information in the application and then remained online. They were instructed to record their diet and physical activity for at least 70% of the service period. To achieve this goal, the assigned lifestyle coach maintained regular communication through phone calls, video meetings, and the application chat at least once a week, depending on the service user’s preference, with each communication lasting approximately 10–20 min.

The decision support algorithms of the mobile application provided evaluative and motivational feedback based on each user’s dietary and physical activity records. The algorithm selected the most appropriate responses tailored to the individual from a predefined pool of nutritional and physical activity feedback configured by human coaches. Real-time glycemic feedback was generated by integrating raw CGM data with the user’s reported dietary and physical activity information. Personalized daily feedback was produced by combining automated algorithms and comments from dedicated human coaches. Such algorithms and comments reflected the user’s blood biomarkers, body composition parameters, initial consultation details, and lifestyle records collected throughout the intervention period. The coaching team comprised certified medical doctors, clinical dietitians, and exercise specialists, all with extensive experience in research and clinical consulting for patients with metabolic diseases.

Measurements

All measurements were taken at baseline and immediately after the 12-week intervention. The collected data were categorized into the following two major domains: body composition parameters and laboratory measurements. The former included body weight, skeletal muscle mass, fat mass, body mass index (BMI), and total muscle-to-fat ratio (tMFR). A validated bioelectrical impedance analysis device was used to assess these variables at both time points. Meanwhile, the latter included glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c), fasting blood glucose, and lipid profile, including total cholesterol, low-density lipoprotein (LDL), high-density lipoprotein (HDL), and triglycerides.

The primary outcomes of this study were the changes in HbA1c and BMI, which served as indicators of metabolic and compositional responses to the intervention. The participants were categorized into the high HbA1c and high BMI subgroups at baseline, and a “responder” was defined as an individual showing a reduction of ≥ 0.3% in HbA1c or ≥ 0.5 kg/m2 in BMI from baseline to post-intervention. For sensitivity analysis, stricter thresholds were applied, defining responders as those with a reduction of ≥ 0.5% in HbA1c or ≥ 1.0 kg/m2 in BMI. Secondary outcomes included changes in body composition metrics and additional biochemical parameters relevant to cardiometabolic health.

CGM data

We assessed the conventional glycemic parameters of all participants, including mean glucose levels and glucose spikes. A glucose spike was defined as a change of ≥ 75 mg/dL in the glucose level within a 30-min window. The spike count was determined for each participant by analyzing the temporal sequence of glucose readings and identifying instances where the absolute difference between consecutive measurements within the defined time window exceeded the threshold value. The algorithm systematically investigated each glucose measurement in a chronological order and compared it with subsequent readings within the 30-min timeframe.

Statistical analysis

The Anderson–Darling test was used to assess the normality of continuous variables, which were presented as medians and interquartile ranges. The Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used to compare continuous variables between two independent groups, and the Wilcoxon signed-rank test was used to compare paired values before and after the intervention. Categorical variables were presented as frequencies and proportions, and the chi-square test was used for intergroup comparisons. Furthermore, we constructed multivariable logistic regression models to predict the responders. The multicollinearity of each model was defined as the variation inflation factor of < 10. We performed a complete case analysis to handle missing values. Furthermore, we excluded variables with a missing rate of > 20% as covariates in logistic regression models, and Supplementary Table 1 lists these covariates. A p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were conducted using R software (version 4.4.1).

Results

Baseline characteristics

Overall, 344 participants were enrolled in this study, with a median age of 51.0 (42.0–56.0) years, and most participants were male (98.3%). At baseline, 40.4%, 25.9%, and 28.8% of the participants had diabetes, hypertension, and dyslipidemia, respectively. The median BMI was 25.8 (23.7–28.7) kg/m2, and the mean HbA1c was 6.1% (5.6–6.8%). Most participants reported having regular physical activity (80.8%), whereas 42.9% stated having heavy alcohol consumption, and 30.0% were current smokers. Among the CGM-derived metrics, the participants exhibited a mean glucose level of 127.1 (114.6–146.1) mg/dL, with a median ARV of 7.3 (6.1–8.9) and an FSV of 11.4 (9.5–13.5) (Table 1).

A subgroup analysis by baseline HbA1c levels revealed that participants with HbA1c ≥ 6.5% (high HbA1c subgroup, n = 115) were older (53.0 vs. 50.0 years, p = 0.012) and had a significantly higher prevalence of diabetes (77.4% vs. 22.1%, p < 0.001) and higher levels of fasting blood glucose (138.0 vs. 99.0 mg/dL, p < 0.001) and triglycerides (137.0 vs. 114.0 mg/dL, p = 0.013) than those with HbA1c < 6.5% (low HbA1c subgroup). Furthermore, the former group exhibited lower skeletal muscle mass (p = 0.023) and LDL cholesterol levels (p < 0.001) and greater CGM variability (ARV, p < 0.001; FSV, p = 0.001) and glucose spikes compared to the latter group (p = 0.001) (Table 2). Similarly, participants with a baseline BMI of ≥ 25 kg/m2 (high BMI subgroup, n = 122) were significantly younger (49.0 vs. 54.0 years, p < 0.001) and had greater fat mass and a lower tMFR (p < 0.001 for all) than those with a BMI of < 25 kg/m2 (low BMI subgroup). In addition, the former group demonstrated higher total cholesterol (p = 0.017), higher triglyceride levels (p < 0.001), and lower HDL cholesterol levels than the latter group (p < 0.001), indicating a more adverse metabolic profile (Table 3).

Effects of lifestyle intervention

After the 12-week mobile application–assisted lifestyle intervention, the participants showed significantly reduced BMI, body fat mass, HbA1c, and body weight. In addition, their tMFR significantly increased, indicating favorable changes in body composition. However, we observed no significant changes in total cholesterol, HDL cholesterol, triglycerides, fasting blood glucose, or skeletal muscle mass (Fig. 3, Supplementary Table 2). In addition, paired trajectory plots depicting individual HbA1c and BMI changes demonstrated that participants with higher baseline levels tended to exhibit greater improvements after intervention (Fig. 4).

Logistic regression models for predicting responders

In the high HbA1c subgroup, lower CGM variability, as measured by FSV, was significantly associated with ≥ 0.3% reduction in HbA1c (adjusted odds ratio [aOR], 0.67; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.50–0.90; p = 0.008). This result indicated that participants with more stable glycemic patterns were more likely to exhibit glycemic improvement. Other baseline variables, including age, BMI, diabetes, dyslipidemia, LDL cholesterol, and mean glucose levels, were not significant predictors of the HbA1c responder status (Table 4). In the high BMI subgroup, no covariates reached statistical significance for predicting BMI reduction of ≥ 0.5 kg/m2 (Table 5).

Sensitivity analyses using stricter responder definitions revealed consistent findings. In the high HbA1c subgroup, when responder status was defined as a reduction of ≥ 0.5%, lower FSV remained a significant predictor (aOR, 0.69; 95% CI, 0.51–0.92; p = 0.013). Other clinical variables did not demonstrate significant associations with HbA1c responses (Supplementary Table 5). Similarly, in the high BMI subgroup, a significant association was observed between a BMI reduction of ≥ 1.0 kg/m2 and lower FSV (aOR, 0.82; 95% CI, 0.67–1.00; p = 0.045; Supplementary Table 6).

Changes in short-term effects of CGM-derived glycemic variability

The participants exhibited significant improvements in CGM data–derived short-term glycemic variability indices. Spike counts of the participants decreased by 13.1% between weeks 1 and 2 (p = 0.046). Furthermore, ARV and FSV decreased by approximately 3% (p = 0.001) and 1.9% (p = 0.031), respectively (Fig. 6). Daily trajectory plots further reinforced these findings by demonstrating improved glycemic stability during the first 2 weeks of the intervention period (Fig. 7).

Discussion

This study evaluated the effectiveness of a mobile application-assisted Lifestyle intervention integrated with CGM in improving glycemic control and body composition among the employees of manufacturing companies in South Korea. Moreover, it aimed to identify factors predicting responsiveness to the intervention. Following the 12-week intervention period, HbA1c, BMI, body weight, body fat mass, and tMFR of the participants significantly improved. Notably, these improvements were particularly pronounced in participants with elevated baseline HbA1c and BMI values. Furthermore, glycemic variability measured through CGM FSV was a significant predictor of intervention effectiveness, emphasizing the consistent association between stable glucose variability and favorable outcomes [11]. Moreover, our CGM data analysis revealed significant improvements in short-term glycemic variability indices, highlighting the benefits of the lifestyle intervention, which can be effective even in the early stages of intervention.

This study had several notable strengths. First, from a conceptual and methodological perspective, this study employed an integrated intervention model that combined mobile health technology, CGM feedback, and tailored health coaching in a structured sequential approach. This design aligned with the sequential behavior-change framework, starting with real-time glycemic monitoring and targeted feedback, advancing to active personalized behavioral modifications, and culminating in autonomous habit maintenance. Second, this study’s analytical rigor further enhanced its validity. Along with basic preintervention–postintervention comparisons, subgroup analyses were performed using robust criteria for defining responders, including sensitivity analyses with stricter thresholds. This study comprehensively explored individual variability in responsiveness to digital intervention by incorporating CGM-derived glucose variability data into multivariable logistic regression models, providing valuable insights into the key factors predicting the effectiveness of digital therapeutics. Finally, our findings were validated within a cohort of manufacturing workers, providing important academic evidence on how the management of chronic metabolic diseases, including diabetes care, should be structured and evaluated within this occupational group. Our results provided valuable insights into the tailored implementation and evaluation of digital health interventions specifically designed for manufacturing employees, considering their distinct occupational environment, characterized by irregular work schedules, sedentary behaviors, occupational stressors, and disrupted circadian rhythms. Thus, this study highlighted the potential of mobile technology and CGM-integrated interventions. It focused not only on enhancing metabolic outcomes but also on informing future occupational health strategies and policies aimed at effectively managing chronic conditions in workplace settings.

Previous studies have increasingly demonstrated the effectiveness of mobile health and digital therapeutics in managing chronic metabolic diseases [12, 13]. Previously, integrated mobile and CGM-based interventions have consistently shown HbA1c level reductions of 0.3–0.7%, alongside BMI improvements [14–16]. Our findings were in line with previously reported results in terms of the magnitude and direction of improvement. Furthermore, previous studies utilizing real-time CGM feedback have emphasized the advantages of individualized interventions in improving glycemic control [17, 18], which was consistent with our identification of CGM-derived glucose variability as a significant predictive marker. However, some earlier studies have indicated the limited effectiveness of CGM-based interventions on BMI reduction alone [19, 20]. In contrast, our results demonstrated significant BMI improvements, highlighting the crucial role of targeted interventions and coaching. Moreover, we identified significant short-term improvements in glycemic variability indices within the initial 2-week phase of CGM monitoring, characterized by significantly reduced spike counts, ARV, and FSV. These early changes indicated immediate behavioral adaptations in dietary habits and physical activity triggered by real-time glucose feedback. Improved glycemic stability can decrease oxidative stress, systemic inflammation, and overall metabolic burden even in a short-term period, thereby setting the stage for subsequent sustained metabolic improvements observed throughout the intervention [21, 22].

Clinically, this study offers compelling evidence supporting the utility of integrated mobile applications and CGM-based tailored interventions within workplace health promotion programs. Our findings emphasized the particular efficacy of this integrated approach among individuals with elevated baseline HbA1c and BMI, indicating that targeted strategies can enhance the efficiency of relevant interventions and subsequent clinical outcomes. Moreover, validating CGM-based glucose variability as a strong predictive indicator for responsiveness further equips clinicians and healthcare providers with practical tools for improving the precision and effectiveness of future interventions.

Glycemic variability indicators, such as FSV, are being increasingly recognized in clinical practice owing to their relevance beyond mean glucose and HbA1c. Glycemic variability is now recognized as a significant independent risk factor for a range of adverse clinical outcomes, including microvascular and macrovascular complications and even mortality in patients with diabetes. Several studies have reported that higher glycemic variability is significantly associated with increased risk of diabetic retinopathy, nephropathy, cardiovascular events, and all-cause mortality [23–25]. FSV has the potential to serve as a practical indicator for anticipating glycemic responses to interventions. In our study, lower baseline FSV was a significant predictor of being a responder following the intervention, highlighting the practical utility of this parameter. These findings were in line with prior literature, which indicated that blood glucose variability might reflect underlying oxidative stress [26]. Given the increasing availability of real-time CGM systems, markers such as FSV can play a pivotal role in personalizing diabetes management. This approach enables early identification of individuals at higher risk of poor glycemic outcomes and allows for tailoring interventions accordingly. Recent studies also supported the use of glycemic variability metrics, including FSV, for risk stratification and treatment response monitoring in people with type 1 and type 2 diabetes [27], which was consistent with our results.

In the current study, the total duration of the mobile application-assisted Lifestyle intervention was 12 weeks because it is widely recognized as the minimum duration necessary for the emergence of observable changes in laboratory markers and body composition [28, 29]. Notably, approximately 40% of participants had pre-existing diabetes, and prior studies have demonstrated that at least 12 weeks of lifestyle intervention is required to achieve statistically significant reductions in HbA1c levels [30]. Within this framework, we implemented CGM monitoring during the first 2 weeks to capture individual glycemic variability related to daily lifestyle patterns. This early monitoring phase involved providing personalized feedback to enhance participant motivation and engagement throughout the intervention period. In addition, glycemic variability indices measured during this initial CGM phase might help predict the long-term effectiveness of the interventions.

Despite its strengths, this study had several limitations. First, despite a multifaceted subgroup analysis, the absence of a control group precluded definitive conclusions about causality or the influence of confounding variables and natural disease progression. A control group could not be established because of workplace constraints. As a result, the causal interpretation of the findings was limited, although subgroup comparisons were conducted to provide some internal reference. Second, the relatively short intervention duration (12 weeks) limited our analysis of the long-term maintenance of behavioral changes and subsequent health outcomes. Third, the study population predominantly consisted of male employees (98%) working in physically labor-intensive manufacturing environments, which limited the generalizability of the findings. This male predominance likely reflected the gender composition typical of such workplaces and the potentially higher health-related motivation for lifestyle interventions observed among male workers in this sector. Fourth, practical barriers, including the costs associated with CGM devices and individualized coaching, as well as the required digital literacy, may have hindered the broader dissemination and application of such interventions in various real-world settings. We did not repeat CGM monitoring after the service because this study was based on a real-world service and was therefore designed with cost-effectiveness in mind. Further follow-up studies based on further CGM monitoring are needed to thoroughly verify the glycemic regulatory effects of this service. Finally, several key behavioral variables exhibited high rates of missingness. Although a complete case analysis was conducted, our approach may have introduced selection bias if individuals with complete data systematically differed from those with missing data. These behavioral variables were not included in the regression analysis because of the substantial proportion of missing values, which might have limited our ability to account for potential lifestyle-related confounders. This potential bias should be considered when interpreting the results. In addition, the lack of some key variables that could objectively measure application usability and engagement, such as the duration and frequency of participants’ use of the application and the frequency of their uploads of information related to dietary habits and physical activity, limited our analysis. Future research should incorporate more complete and systematically collected behavioral data to enhance the robustness and generalizability of the findings.

Conclusion

This study demonstrated that a 12-week digital health intervention, combining mobile application-based individualized coaching and CGM, was significantly associated with improved glycemic control and body composition among the employees of manufacturing companies in South Korea. In particular, participants with higher baseline HbA1c and BMI levels demonstrated more significant benefits of this intervention. Among the covariates, glucose variability was the most significant variable for the effectiveness of the intervention. Furthermore, marked short-term improvements in CGM-derived glycemic variability were observed within the first 2 weeks, underscoring the rapid responsiveness achieved through lifestyle intervention. Our findings not only provided robust evidence supporting targeted digital therapeutic strategies in workplace health management programs but also demonstrated their effectiveness in chronic metabolic diseases such as diabetes and obesity. Further randomized controlled trials with extended follow-up periods, more CGM monitoring sessions, and diverse populations are required to enhance the robustness and generalizability of these findings.

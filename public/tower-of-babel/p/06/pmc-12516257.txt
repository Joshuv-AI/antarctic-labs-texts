Chronic limb-threatening ischemia (CLTI) represents the most advanced stage of peripheral artery disease, characterized by rest pain, nonhealing ulcers, and gangrene. Up to 20% of patients with CLTI are considered “no-option” given the absence of suitable targets for conventional surgical or endovascular revascularization.1,2 In this challenging subset, transcatheter arterialization of the deep veins (TADV) using the LimFlow System (Inari Medical) has emerged as a promising alternative, as the only device approved by the Food & Drug Administration for no-option CLTI. Initial data from the PROMISE I, II, and PROMISE UK trials demonstrated favorable limb salvage and amputation-free survival through 1 year, and there is great interest in the long-term durability of TADV.3, 4, 5, 6 Here, we present extended follow-up outcomes of 4 no-option CLTI patients treated with the LimFlow system as part of the PROMISE I and II trials.

Case Presentations

Patient 1

History of presentation and past medical history

A 52-year-old man with end-stage renal disease (status post kidney transplant), hypertension, hyperlipidemia, coronary artery disease, and diabetes mellitus presented with Rutherford 5 CLTI and nonhealing toe wounds after multiple prior interventions.

Management

He was enrolled in the PROMISE I trial and underwent TADV of the right posterior tibial artery (PTA) to the posterior tibial vein (PTV) (Figure 1). Four days later, reduced flow was noted through the graft, prompting stenting of the proximal PTA and tibioperoneal trunk (TPT).

Outcome and follow-up

Three months after TADV, he underwent a partial hallux amputation. At the 6-month follow-up, the stent was occluded—likely owing to interruption of anticoagulation for dental work—but the amputation site was fully healed. At the 8-year follow-up, he remained symptom free.

Patient 2

History of presentation and past medical history

A 58-year-old woman with a history of multiple revascularizations and a left below-knee amputation presented with a Rutherford 5 nonhealing right toe ulcer despite prior interventions. Her medical history included hyperlipidemia, Churg-Strauss syndrome, diabetes mellitus, and nonobstructive coronary artery disease.

Management

She was enrolled in the PROMISE I trial and underwent TADV using the LimFlow System from the right PTA to the PTV (Figure 2).

Outcome and follow-up

Four months postprocedure, she underwent a first and second toe amputation. At the 7-month follow-up, the stent graft was occluded; however, her wound was healing and was subsequently fully healed by the 14-month follow-up visit. At 7 years after TADV, she remained asymptomatic, with no rest pain, tissue loss, or claudication.

Patient 3

History of presentation and past medical history

A 73-year-old woman with Rutherford 5 CLTI presented with persistent rest pain, left leg claudication, and a nonhealing toe ulcer for over 1 year despite prior revascularizations. Her comorbidities included diabetes mellitus, atrial fibrillation, and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease.

Management

She was enrolled in the PROMISE II trial and was treated with TADV from the left PTA to the PTV (Figure 3), along with adjunctive balloon angioplasty of the left superficial femoral artery, popliteal artery, and TPT, and stenting of the popliteal artery and TPT. Postprocedure, she underwent coil embolization of the greater and small saphenous veins to treat steal syndrome. A planned transmetatarsal amputation was performed 5 days later.

Outcome and follow-up

At the 3-month follow-up, her pain had improved, and the graft remained patent. At the 6-month follow-up, her wound was healed, with no new symptoms. Three years after TADV, the stent graft was found to be occluded after cessation of Plavix and warfarin owing to gastrointestinal bleeding, although she remained wound free. At 5 years postprocedure, she continued to have a healed transmetatarsal amputation without claudication, rest pain, or tissue loss.

Patient 4

History of presentation and past medical history

A 75-year-old man presented with no-option Rutherford 5 CLTI after multiple failed interventions. His medical history included diabetes mellitus, hypertension, and hyperlipidemia. Prior treatments included right superficial femoral artery stenting, TPT and peroneal artery angioplasty, and partial right hallux amputation with persistent nonhealing.

Management

Enrolled in the PROMISE II trial, he underwent successful TADV of the right PTA to PTV (Figure 4).

Outcome and follow-up

Two months later, he required a hallux ray amputation. Despite stent occlusion noted at 1 year, his wound had healed and his symptoms resolved. At just over 2-year follow-up, he reported only mild claudication, with no rest pain or tissue loss.

Interventional Procedure Overview

TADV was performed similarly across all 4 cases, as previously described.3, 4, 5 The LimFlow System includes the ARC (arterial crossing) and V-Ceiver (venous receiving) catheters for artery-to-vein crossing, the Vector valvulotome, and specialized stent grafts.

All of the patients were enrolled in TADV studies after an independent committee determined that no conventional distal bypass, surgical, or endovascular therapy for limb salvage was feasible. Typically, venous access was obtained via the plantar vein or distal PTV under ultrasound guidance, and arterial access was via the common femoral artery.

After diagnostic angiography and venography, the ARC and V-Ceiver catheters were positioned in the artery and target vein, respectively. Using the crossing needle, a guidewire was advanced from artery to vein. Subsequent valvulotomy and venoplasty were performed, followed by deployment of the LimFlow stent grafts. Flow through the new arterialized venous circuit was confirmed with a completion angiogram.

Discussion

This case series highlights the long-term durability of TADV using the LimFlow System in patients with no-option CLTI. All 4 patients presented here maintained limb salvage without recurrence of tissue loss, rest pain, or the need for major amputation during extended follow-up ranging from 2 to 8 years. Notably, despite eventual stent graft occlusion in all cases, clinical improvement was sustained. Maintenance of early patency in the TADV circuit is essential for good outcomes in these patients.6

No-option CLTI patients often experience relentless ischemic symptoms and nonhealing wounds despite multiple revascularization attempts. Approximately 20% of CLTI patients fall into this high-risk category, with no remaining feasible surgical or endovascular options.1,2 The LimFlow System was developed specifically to address this gap in care by enabling TADV as an alternative pathway to restore perfusion to the ischemic limb. Recent multisociety guidelines have included a consideration for venous arterialization in this challenging population.7

Our cases build on the growing body of evidence supporting TADV. Previously reported 12-month outcomes from the PROMISE I, II, and PROMISE UK trials have demonstrated good limb salvage (74%) and amputation-free survival (66%) rates.6 In comparison, a recent meta-analysis of no-option CLTI patients who were not treated with TADV reported amputation-free survival in 33% of patients.8 Similarly, a prospective, multicenter natural progression study of no-option CLTI patients who were not treated with TAVI demonstrated a 1-year amputation-free survival rate of 37%.9 These findings emphasize the transformative potential of TADV in a population historically associated with high rates of major amputation and mortality.

Our series also emphasizes the importance of vigilant follow-up. All 4 patients required minor amputations shortly after TADV, but they avoided major (above-ankle) amputation. Additionally, 3 patients developed stent graft occlusion after interruptions in antithrombotic therapy. These events suggest that long-term patency of the arterialized venous circuit may be influenced by adherence to dual antiplatelet or anticoagulant regimens—an area deserving further investigation.

Conclusions

In this case series with follow-up extending up to 8 years, TADV using the LimFlow System resulted in durable limb salvage, complete wound healing, and sustained symptom resolution in patients with no-option CLTI. Notably, these clinical benefits persisted despite eventual stent graft occlusion in all patients. These findings support the long-term efficacy of TADV and highlight the need for further research into optimizing antithrombotic strategies and understanding the disconnect between conduit patency and clinical success in advanced peripheral artery disease.

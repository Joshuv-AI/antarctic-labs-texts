Tirzepatide as a therapeutic tool for kidney impairment in obesity: insights from a real-world study
Authors: Martina Galasso, Ludovica Verde, Renato Patrone, Giovanni Ragozzino, Giuseppe Annunziata, Annamaria Colao, Luigi Barrea, Giovanna Muscogiuri
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundObesity is a major risk factor for chronic kidney disease (CKD), contributing to kidney dysfunction through metabolic alterations and chronic inflammation. Tirzepatide, a dual glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide (GIP) and glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonist, has shown significant efficacy in weight loss and metabolic control, yet real-world data on its impact on kidney function are lacking. This study aimed to evaluate the impact of three months of tirzepatide treatment on kidney function and to identify the clinical predictors of changes in estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) in adults with obesity in a real-world outpatient setting.MethodsIn this retrospective real-world study, 36 adults with overweight (BMI ≥ 27.0 kg/m²) with at least one weight-related comorbidity and patients with obesity (BMI ≥ 30.0 kg/m²) undergoing treatment with tirzepatide were included. Participants received weekly tirzepatide (2.5–5 mg) for three months. Anthropometric measurements, metabolic markers (fasting glucose, insulin, HOMA-IR, and HbA1c), inflammatory status (hs-CRP), and kidney parameters (serum creatinine and eGFR via CKD-EPI) were assessed at baseline and after 3 months of treatment.ResultsAfter 3 months, serum creatinine decreased significantly (p < 0.001), and accordingly, eGFR increased after treatment (p < 0.001). In addition, significant reductions were observed in body weight (p < 0.001), BMI (p < 0.001), and waist circumference (p < 0.001). A significant decrease was observed in fasting plasma glucose (p < 0.001), HbA1c (p = 0.002), and insulin (p < 0.001) and, accordingly, in HOMA-IR (p < 0.001). Inflammatory status also improved significantly, with hs-CRP levels decreasing from baseline after 3 months of treatment with tirzepatide (p < 0.001). Correlation analysis demonstrated that ΔeGFR was inversely associated with Δweight loss (p = 0.008), ΔBMI (p = 0.007), and Δ fasting insulin (p = 0.011), while it showed a positive correlation with fasting plasma glucose (p < 0.001); a borderline negative association was also observed with ΔHOMA-IR (p = 0.053). Multiple regression analysis identified ΔBMI as the main independent predictor of ΔeGFR (p = 0.007), explaining 19.4% of the variance.ConclusionsIn a real-world setting, tirzepatide treatment was associated with improvements in kidney function alongside marked weight loss and metabolic improvements in individuals with obesity. The main driver of improvement in eGFR was primarily weight loss along with the improvement of insulin resistance, thus suggesting tirzepatide as a tool for the treatment of obesity-related kidney dysfunction.

Introduction

Chronic kidney disease (CKD) represents a major global public health burden, affecting an estimated 13.4% of the world population (CKD stages 1–5) and corresponding to nearly 850 million individuals worldwide [1, 2].

Beyond its high prevalence, CKD often remains underdiagnosed, with many cases identified only in the later stages of the disease. The condition is associated with substantial morbidity, mortality, and a reduced quality of life, placing a considerable economic burden on healthcare systems [1]. Among the chronic diseases identified to have a role in the pathogenesis of CKD, obesity is one of the leading disorders [3].

The relationship is complex and multifactorial. On one hand, obesity contributes indirectly to kidney damage through comorbidities such as type 2 diabetes (T2D), hypertension, and insulin resistance [4]. On the other hand, obesity exerts direct deleterious effects through glomerular hyperfiltration [5], oxidative stress [6], and chronic low-grade inflammation [4].

This inflammatory state is mediated by proinflammatory adipocytokines released by adipose tissue, which can ultimately lead to a progressive decline in the glomerular filtration rate [7]. Accordingly, obesity has been associated with the development of kidney dysfunction even in individuals without pre-existing kidney disease [8]. Therefore, weight loss is increasingly recognized as a cornerstone strategy for reducing both metabolic and renal risk [9].

Current joint guidelines from the American Diabetes Association (ADA) and the European Association for the Study of Diabetes (EASD) recommend a weight loss of approximately 5% to 15% to improve metabolic factors; additionally, greater weight reductions exceeding 15% have been associated with improvements in the early stages of CKD, highlighting the potential impact of substantial weight loss on kidney outcomes [9].

Given the increasing prevalence of both obesity and obesity-related CKD, identifying effective therapeutic strategies capable of improving metabolic health and potentially influencing kidney function remains a major clinical priority.

In recent years, incretin-based therapies have gained increasing attention not only for their metabolic benefits but also for their potential cardiorenal protective effects [10]. Tirzepatide is a once-weekly dual agonist of the glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide (GIP) and glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptors that has demonstrated remarkable efficacy in promoting significant weight loss in individuals with obesity [11]. Beyond its metabolic effects, emerging evidence suggests that tirzepatide may also exert beneficial effects on kidney outcomes [10]. However, it is important to note that the current evidence is primarily derived from post hoc analyses of randomized clinical trials, including the SURPASS program [12]. In particular, a post hoc analysis of the SURPASS-4 trial suggested that tirzepatide may reduce the risk of kidney function decline compared with insulin glargine [12]. However, despite these promising findings, the generalizability to real-world populations remains limited. The available evidence is largely derived from randomized clinical trials and post hoc analyses, while data from routine clinical practice remain scarce. In particular, real-world studies specifically evaluating the impact of tirzepatide on kidney function are currently lacking.

Therefore, the present study aimed to investigate the effects of tirzepatide treatment on kidney function among adults with obesity in a real-world clinical setting. Additionally, we aimed to elucidate the primary driver underlying the kidney effects elicited by tirzepatide treatment.

Methods

Study design and population

This was a retrospective study enrolling 36 adults (≥ 18 years) with overweight (BMI ≥ 27.0 kg/m²) with at least one weight-related comorbidity and patients with obesity (BMI ≥ 30.0 kg/m²), who were prescribed tirzepatide for weight management (2.5–5 mg weekly).

Exclusion criteria included type 1 diabetes and T2D, history of pancreatitis, liver failure, inflammatory bowel diseases, active malignancy, uncontrolled thyroid disease or history of medullary thyroid carcinoma/multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2, pregnancy, heart failure NYHA III-IV, unstable angina, end-stage kidney disease, acute kidney injury within 3 months before treatment initiation, and prior GLP-1 or GIP receptor agonist use.

Data collection

Data included demographic characteristics (age and gender), anthropometric measurements (weight, height, and waist circumference (WC)), and details regarding the duration and dosage of tirzepatide treatment.

Biochemical parameters extracted from clinical records at baseline and at 3 months of treatment with tirzepatide included serum creatinine, fasting plasma glucose (FPG), fasting insulin, glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c), and high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP).

The Homeostatic Model Assessment for Insulin Resistance (HOMA-IR) was calculated using the formula:

HOMA-IR = (FPG [mg/dL] × fasting insulin [mIU/mL]) / 405.

The eGFR was calculated using the CKD-EPI equation [13], which is widely used in clinical practice and has been applied in large population-based studies, including individuals with overweight and obesity [14, 15]. Data of all patients who had completed at least 3 months of tirzepatide under routine clinical practice conditions were retrospectively included.

Statistical analysis

Data distribution was assessed using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Continuous variables are reported as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Variables that were not normally distributed were log-transformed prior to analysis. Comparisons between baseline values and values recorded after 3 months of tirzepatide treatment were performed using paired Student’s t-tests for continuous variables. Pearson’s correlation coefficient was used to evaluate associations between eGFR and anthropometric, metabolic, and inflammatory parameters both at baseline and after 3 months of treatment. Correlations between changes in eGFR (ΔeGFR) and changes in anthropometric and metabolic variables (Δweight loss, ΔBMI, ΔWC, Δinsulin, ΔHOMA-IR, Δhs-CRP, ΔFPG, and ΔHbA1c) were also assessed. A multiple linear regression analysis with a stepwise approach was performed, including only variables that were significant in univariate analyses (p < 0.05). A two-tailed p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All analyses were conducted using SPSS Statistics version 22 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

A significant improvement was observed in kidney parameters, with serum creatinine levels decreasing from 1.18 ± 0.21 mg/dL to 1.12 ± 0.19 mg/dL (p < 0.001), while eGFR increased from 71.00 ± 17.83 mL/min/1.73 m² at baseline to 73.64 ± 17.32 mL/min/1.73 m² after treatment (p < 0.001).

After treatment with tirzepatide, a significant reduction was observed in all anthropometric parameters. In particular, body weight decreased from 99.14 ± 11.97 kg at baseline to 86.72 ± 10.62 kg after 3 months of treatment (p < 0.001). Similarly, BMI decreased from 34.96 ± 3.10 kg/m² to 30.59 ± 2.93 kg/m² (p < 0.001), while waist circumference declined from 113.92 ± 7.98 cm to 101.80 ± 7.66 cm (p < 0.001).

Significant improvements were also observed in glucose metabolism parameters. FPG decreased from 99.22 ± 16.10 mg/dL at baseline to 85.00 ± 10.12 mg/dL after treatment (p < 0.001), while HbA1c decreased from 5.89 ± 0.55% to 5.33 ± 0.28% (p = 0.002). Consistently, HOMA-IR was significantly reduced from 6.36 ± 3.36 to 3.24 ± 1.33 (p < 0.001).

A significant reduction in inflammatory status was also observed, with hs-CRP levels decreasing from 2.36 ± 1.37 mg/L at baseline to 1.20 ± 0.85 mg/L after 3 months of treatment (p < 0.001).

Discussion

In our study, treatment with tirzepatide was associated with favorable changes in kidney function markers. As expected, weight loss together with improvements in metabolic parameters and inflammatory status has also been detected after tirzepatide treatment.

With respect to our kidney-related data, our findings corroborate findings from the SURMOUNT post hoc analysis, providing supportive evidence in a real-world setting [12]. In fact, we found statistically significant improvement in kidney function that was significantly associated with weight loss, which emerged as the strongest correlate of tirzepatide-related kidney effects.

Current international guidelines emphasize that weight reductions of 5–15% are associated with significant improvements in metabolic health, while larger reductions may have beneficial effects on obesity-related complications, including early stages of chronic kidney disease [9]. Indeed, weight loss is usually accompanied by a reduction of insulin resistance [16] that in turn is known to contribute to kidney injury through multiple mechanisms, including glomerular hyperfiltration, endothelial dysfunction, activation of inflammatory pathways, and increased oxidative stress [17, 18]. Supporting this pathogenic effect, our findings demonstrated an inverse correlation between changes in eGFR and changes in insulin resistance and inflammatory status. While this improvement is likely largely mediated by weight loss, a potential contribution of potentially direct insulin-sensitizing effects of tirzepatide through GLP-1 and GIP receptor co-agonism cannot be excluded and may further contribute to the observed renal benefit [19, 20]. In addition to these systemic effects, incretin-based therapies may also exert direct kidney actions. GLP-1 receptors have been identified in kidney vascular and tubular structures, and their activation has been associated with increased natriuresis, modulation of kidney hemodynamics, and anti-inflammatory effects [19, 21].

In contrast, evidence for direct renal actions of GIP is more limited. GIP receptor expression appears low or absent in human kidney tissue, and no consistent effects of GIP signalling on renal perfusion or kidney function have been demonstrated in humans [22]. Nevertheless, GIP may indirectly influence renal health through improvements in insulin sensitivity, glycemic control, lipid metabolism, and modulation of adipose tissue inflammation, including perirenal and intrarenal fat depots that have been implicated in the pathogenesis of kidney injury. Whether these mechanisms translate into clinically meaningful renoprotective effects remains to be fully elucidated [22].

As expected, tirzepatide treatment resulted in a substantial reduction in body weight and anthropometric parameters, including BMI and WC. These findings are consistent with the well-established weight-lowering effects of tirzepatide observed in recent real-world studies evaluating the effectiveness of tirzepatide in routine clinical practice [23, 24]. Weight loss is a key therapeutic target in individuals with obesity, as excess adiposity, particularly visceral fat, is strongly associated with metabolic dysfunction, systemic inflammation, and increased cardiometabolic risk [25].

Alongside the marked reduction in body weight, we observed significant improvements in metabolic parameters, including FPG, insulin levels, HbA1c, and HOMA-IR that indicate a substantial improvement in insulin resistance. These findings are in line with the well-documented effects of tirzepatide on glucose metabolism and insulin resistance, attributed to its dual agonism of GLP-1 and GIP receptors [20, 26]. Beyond weight loss, emerging evidence suggests that tirzepatide may also exert additional direct insulin-sensitizing effects [19–21]. In vivo preclinical studies have shown that GLP-1/GIP receptor co-agonism enhances glucose uptake in skeletal muscle and white adipose tissue and improves nutrient handling, while mechanistic studies further suggest a reduction in ectopic lipid accumulation, particularly at the hepatic level [20]. Consistently, clinical studies have reported improvements in insulin resistance that appear greater than expected from weight loss alone [19]. However, the relative contribution of these weight-independent mechanisms in humans remains to be fully elucidated. Notably, FPG correlated significantly and positively at baseline but exhibited a negative correlation post-treatment. This baseline association may be attributed to the fact that severe obesity is commonly accompanied by impaired glucose metabolism, leading to elevated FPG and glomerular hyperfiltration [22]. Conversely, tirzepatide treatment promotes the normalization of both glycemic levels and the eGFR, thereby reversing the correlation in the post-treatment phase [22].

Strengths and limitations

The present study has several strengths. Its real-world design reflects routine clinical practice and provides complementary evidence to findings from randomized clinical trials. In addition, the inclusion of individuals with overweight and obesity without T2D represents a clinically relevant and still underexplored population, offering the opportunity to evaluate the effects of tirzepatide independently of overt diabetes-related kidney alterations. Another strength is the multidimensional assessment of treatment response, which included anthropometric, metabolic, inflammatory, and renal parameters, allowing a broader evaluation of the systemic changes associated with therapy.

A potential limitation of our study is the lack of data on albuminuria. This omission may have precluded a comprehensive evaluation of renal damage, particularly in its earliest stages, as eGFR alterations can lag behind the onset of proteinuria. Additionally, without albuminuria measurements, we could not achieve a complete KDIGO-based risk stratification or account for its independent predictive value regarding both renal progression and cardiovascular outcomes. Although the lack of albuminuria limits early-stage damage assessment, this study provides crucial real-world evidence on the renal effects and safety of tirzepatide in patients with obesity.

Conclusion

In individuals with obesity, 3-month treatment with tirzepatide was associated with meaningful enhancement of kidney function, as shown by increased eGFR and reduced creatinine, alongside significant weight loss and improved insulin resistance. Notably, changes in kidney function were independently predicted by reductions in BMI, suggesting that weight loss emerged as the strongest predictor of changes in kidney function. Prospective studies with longer follow-up are warranted to confirm these effects and clarify their long-term clinical relevance.

Our initiatives have concentrated on achieving defect-free care, optimizing standardization of acute coronary syndrome management, and reducing door to balloon (D2B) times. Preserving these initiatives can be challenging for a 220-bed, community-based hospital due to increasing health care demands and resource constraints. Ongoing Efforts to Sustain Our Commitment to Excellence

Over the past 5 years, our teams have focused on establishing an American College of Cardiology Chest Pain Center–accredited percutaneous coronary intervention program. Our initiatives concentrated on achieving defect-free care, optimizing standardization of acute coronary syndrome management, and reducing door to balloon (D2B) times. Having met the National Cardiovascular Data Registry metric for hospital arrival to device time in ≤90 minutes at 100% in the third quarter of 2023, we endeavor to ensure this level of sustainability moving forward (Figure 1).

Project Rationale

However, our commitment to integrating sustainability principles into D2B workflow optimization ensures long-term performance without compromising quality of care.

Project Description

An interdisciplinary team composed of interventionalists, a cardiologist, emergency department (ED) physicians, along with nursing leadership, and frontline staff continues to be the foundation and roadmap for sustainability and improved efficiencies with D2B metric achievement. This team collaboratively developed steps to reduce delays measured in minutes and seconds with every aspect of patient care. Key initiatives focus on critical time-sensitive steps, improved communication, clear roles with defined responsibilities, and preparedness.

Addressing critical time-sensitive steps at the front end of care proves to be an essential element of our success. Embedding a registered nurse (RN) at the ED registration window and implementing immediate electrocardiogram (ECG) protocols followed by a prompt ECG review by the ED physician or Emergency Medical Services paramedic when in the field, quickly determines whether an ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) activation is necessary.1 A dedicated ECG room located near triage guarantees immediate availability and timely assessment of the patient. Additionally, during times of high volume, having a provider positioned in triage helps identify any potential missed opportunities for timely intervention. To gain additional lifesaving minutes, we eliminate phlebotomist dispatch time by having RNs draw and label blood samples with a bright STEMI sticker to alert laboratory personnel of the need for immediate processing.

To ensure standardization of patient care and throughput from the ED to the cardiac catheterization laboratory (CCL), the team created a STEMI flowsheet (Figure 2) to streamline interdepartmental communication. This flowsheet establishes expected care for each patient and ensures our delivery of pertinent information is concise during expedited lifesaving handoffs. This flowsheet also serves as a review for quality assessments and outcome measurements.

By defining roles and responsibilities during STEMI activations, the ED team prepares the patient for anticipated revascularization while the CCL team prepares the procedure room to ensure D2B times are optimized. The cardiologist reports directly to the ED after receiving the STEMI activation and together with the ED team, transports the patient to the CCL.

Finally, to capitalize on each minute and to always be STEMI ready, workflows operationalized by the CCL team ensure equipment is in working condition using daily safety checks, and anticipated supplies are pulled at the end of each working day in anticipation of a STEMI activation.

Project Deliverables

We have maintained near 100% compliance with D2B times <90 minutes in the Chest Pain-MI Registry Outcomes Report since quarter 3 of 2023. To ensure sustainability of our program, we regularly evaluate our processes by focusing on several initiatives. Communication is key to our success. All STEMI cases are reviewed with staff to assess performance, acknowledge successes, and identify areas for improvement. The interdisciplinary team facilitates process review along with quarterly assessments for improvement opportunities. Performance graphs are displayed on quality boards, and ongoing dialogue ensures continuous monitoring of STEMI metric expectations. We continually monitor door to ECG times, celebrating rapid recognition and performing review with reeducation when necessary.2,3 Our chest pain and nursing protocols are reviewed during orientation and annually for all staff.

Project Outcome, Impact, and Future Directions/Next Steps

After the implementation of sustainability practices beginning in quarter 2 of 2023, D2B times were consistently met. The proportion of patients meeting the ≤90-minute D2B benchmark increased from 79.3% to 98.0%. Key interventions included streamlined documentation, efficient ED processes, and coordinated CCL workflows. These results reflect ongoing process optimization, enhanced interdepartmental coordination, and sustained adherence to evidence-based protocols.

Discussion

Achieving and sustaining excellence in D2B times requires more than isolated interventions, it demands a culture of continuous improvement, interdisciplinary collaboration, and strategic integration of sustainability principles. Our journey illustrates the power of intentional, data-driven change in a community-based hospital setting.

The success of our initiatives is based on several key factors. First, the formation of an interdisciplinary team enables review of processes that foster shared ownership and accountability across departments. These changes not only reduce delays but also empower frontline staff to act swiftly and confidently.

Second, the creation of dedicated resources such as an ECG room and machine, a STEMI flowsheet, and clear role delineation between ED and CCL teams ensures consistency and preparedness. These interventions minimize variability and support a standardized response to STEMI activations, even during off-hours.

Third, our commitment to transparency and continuous feedback is instrumental in sustaining improvements. Weekly STEMI reviews, quality performance dashboards, and regular staff education cultivate a learning environment where data and success are celebrated. This approach reinforces the importance of every minute saved is a direct impact on patient outcomes.

Importantly, our experience underscores that sustainability is not a static goal but a dynamic process. As health care demands evolve and resources fluctuate, maintaining high performance requires adaptability, vigilance, and a shared vision. By integrating sustainability into our operational framework, we have built resilience into our D2B process, ensuring that excellence is not only achieved but transformational.

Conclusions

National Cardiovascular Data Registry Chest Pain-MI Outcomes Data Registry metrics provide valuable data for guiding decision-making in cardiovascular care. Effective teamwork and continuous evaluation are essential for sustaining a patient care process that achieves high compliance with critical metrics. The importance of regular staff education, clear communication of protocols, and preparedness for consistent, sustainable outcomes cannot be overlooked toward attaining success.

Low-temperature plasma adenoidectomy versus conservative treatment for children with adenoid hypertrophy and allergic rhinitis: A non-randomized observational study
Authors: Biao Yan, Hui Fang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo investigate the effect of low-temperature radiofrequency ablation (LTRFA) adenoidectomy on the improvement of allergic rhinitis (AR) in children with adenoid hypertrophy (AH) complicated by AR, in comparison with conservative pharmacological management.MethodsA total of 89 pediatric patients with AH complicated by AR were enrolled in this non-randomized observational study. Among them, 59 underwent LTRFA adenoidectomy under nasal endoscopy, and 30 received conservative anti-allergic therapy (patients who declined surgical intervention). Symptom severity was assessed using the visual analog scale (VAS; score 1–10) at baseline and at 6 months of follow-up. Improvement was defined as a reduction in VAS score of ≥3 points from baseline. Between-group comparison of VAS change scores was performed using an independent-samples t-test.ResultsBaseline VAS scores were comparable between groups (surgical: 7.85±1.46; conservative: 7.63±1.52; P=0.512). At 6 months, the mean VAS change score was 4.95±1.90 in the surgical group and 2.51±1.44 in the conservative group, a difference that was statistically significant (P<0.001). Based on the improvement criterion (VAS reduction ≥3 points), 52 of 59 surgical patients (88.1%) and 17 of 30 conservative patients (56.7%) met the threshold (χ2=11.30, P<0.001).ConclusionsIn children with AH complicated by AR, LTRFA adenoidectomy was associated with significantly greater improvement in nasal symptom scores at 6 months compared with conservative treatment. These findings suggest that LTRFA adenoidectomy may be a beneficial treatment option; however, given the non-randomized design and limited follow-up duration, further randomized controlled trials with objective outcome measures are needed to confirm these preliminary observations.

1. Introduction

Adenoid hypertrophy (AH) refers to the excessive enlargement of the adenoids and is one of the most common conditions in children. The adenoid is a crucial immune organ located at the intersection of the nasal cavity, pharynx, and oral cavity, and it plays an important role in nasal ventilation. As a pathological state, adenoid hypertrophy is frequently accompanied by a range of complex complications, 1 and its pathogenesis is closely associated with type I hypersensitivity reactions, 2 in which key pathological mediators play essential roles.

Studies have shown that among children with adenoid hypertrophy requiring surgical intervention, as many as 52.4% also present with allergic rhinitis (AR). 3 Observational data further suggest that when AH coexists with AR, 4 adenoid volume appears to be significantly larger than in children without AR, indicating that allergic rhinitis may contribute to the development and possible progression of adenoid hypertrophy. Conversely, adenoid hypertrophy may exacerbate the inflammatory response associated with allergic rhinitis, 5 potentially creating a mutually reinforcing cycle.6,7 However, the causal direction of this relationship has not been definitively established, and the existing evidence is predominantly observational.

Surgical removal of the adenoids has been associated with improvement in AR symptoms in some children with AH complicated by AR. Therefore, this study aimed to evaluate and compare, in an observational setting, the clinical outcomes of LTRFA adenoidectomy versus conservative management on AR symptoms in children with AH complicated by AR. Given the non-randomized nature of the study, results should be interpreted with appropriate caution.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Study design and clinical data

This was a prospective, non-randomized observational cohort study conducted at the Department of Otolaryngology, Fuyang Sixth People’s Hospital, Fuyang, Anhui, China, enrolling pediatric patients with adenoid hypertrophy accompanied by allergic rhinitis treated between January and December 2022. Patients were consecutively enrolled based on predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria. Group allocation was determined by guardian preference: 59 guardians consented to surgical intervention (surgical group), while 30 declined surgery and were assigned to conservative management (conservative group). All patient data were de-identified before analysis, and patient confidentiality was maintained throughout the study. This study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Fuyang Sixth People’s Hospital on December 28, 2021 (Approval No. 2021-028). Written informed consent was obtained from the legal guardians of all participants prior to enrollment.

The cohort included 50 boys and 39 girls aged 3–11 years, with a mean age of 5.26±1.66 years. Disease duration ranged from 5 to 48 months, with a mean of 6.8 months. 2.2. Inclusion criteria

Eligible participants were children aged ≤14 years diagnosed with adenoid hypertrophy based on a lateral nasopharyngeal radiograph. Adenoid hypertrophy was defined as an A/N ratio ≥0.71, calculated by measuring the vertical distance from the most prominent point of the adenoid to the extracranial clival tangent (A) and the linear distance from the pterygoid plate root–clivus junction to the posterior–superior hard palate (N).

Allergic rhinitis was diagnosed according to the 2022 Chinese Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Treatment of Pediatric Allergic Rhinitis. 8 Children were required to present with at least two of the following symptoms: nasal itching, sneezing, rhinorrhea, and nasal congestion, lasting ≥1 hour daily, along with pale and edematous nasal mucosa on examination.

Additional requirements included disease duration ≥3 months, lack of improvement after ≥1 month of treatment, absence of surgical contraindications or other comorbidities, and guardian consent for study participation. For the surgical group, guardian consent for surgery was additionally required; patients in the conservative group were those whose guardians declined surgical intervention despite it being clinically indicated.

2.3. Exclusion criteria

Patients were excluded if they met any of the following criteria: age >14 years; A/N ratio <0.71 or failure to meet the diagnostic criteria for allergic rhinitis according to the 2022 Chinese Guidelines; disease duration <3 months or symptom improvement after at least 1 month of treatment; presence of contraindications to surgery or other significant comorbidities; or inability to obtain written informed consent from the legal guardian.

2.4. Surgical method

General anesthesia was administered via endotracheal intubation. The patient was placed in the supine position with shoulder elevation and the head extended, ensuring that the head was not excessively hyperextended and that the neck was not suspended; a cervical pad was used when necessary. A Davies mouth gag was applied to gently open the oral cavity. After nasal packing with epinephrine-soaked cotton to achieve mucosal decongestion, an infusion strip (with the needle removed) was passed from the nasal cavity to the oral cavity to elevate the soft palate, thereby fully exposing the adenoid.

The surgeon stood on the right side of the child. A pediatric 0° nasal endoscope was inserted through each nostril, while a plasma ablation device (Coblator II, ArthroCare ENT, Austin, TX, USA; setting: ablation level 7, coagulation level 3) was introduced through the oral cavity. All procedures were performed by two experienced senior otolaryngologists. Ablation was performed along the medial aspect of the bilateral torus tubarius, extending to the depth of the nasopharyngeal fascia, to remove the adenoid tissue.

Postoperatively, patients received one day of antibiotic therapy and were monitored for two days. In the absence of complications, they were subsequently discharged.

2.5. Conservative treatment method

Conservative management consisted of oral antihistamines (loratadine syrup, 0.2 mg/kg once nightly, maximum 10 mg/day) and intranasal corticosteroids (mometasone furoate nasal spray, 50 μg per nostril once daily) for the duration of the 6-month follow-up period. Treatment adherence was monitored through monthly outpatient visits and guardian-reported compliance records.

2.6. Outcome measures

Before enrollment, patients' allergic symptom severity—including sneezing, rhinorrhea, nasal congestion, and nasal itching—was assessed using the visual analog scale (VAS), a numeric scale ranging from 1 (mildest symptoms) to 10 (most severe symptoms). For children aged <6 years or those unable to self-report, VAS scores were completed by the accompanying guardian based on observed symptom severity. The same assessment was repeated at 6 months post-enrollment.

The primary outcome was the change in VAS score from baseline to 6 months. For descriptive purposes, “improvement” was defined as a reduction in VAS score of ≥3 points from baseline, a threshold selected a priori based on clinical judgment regarding the minimum meaningful change in this population; “no significant improvement” was defined as a VAS reduction of <3 points.

2.7. Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS version 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Continuous variables are expressed as mean±SD. Normality was confirmed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Within-group pre- to post-treatment changes were assessed using paired-samples t-tests. The primary between-group comparison was performed using an independent-samples t-test applied to the VAS change scores of both groups. The chi-square test was used to compare dichotomized improvement rates as a secondary descriptive analysis. No formal sample size calculation was conducted; this was an observational study with a convenience sample. Statistical significance was set at P<0.05. The reporting of this study conforms to the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines. 9

3. Results

3.1. Baseline characteristics

Baseline characteristics were similar between groups (Table 1). Mean age was 5.31±1.72 years in the surgical group and 5.17±1.55 years in the conservative group (P=0.691). Baseline VAS scores were 7.85±1.46 and 7.63±1.52, respectively (P=0.512). No statistically significant between-group differences were found for any baseline variable.

4. Discussion

4.1. Relationship between adenoid hypertrophy and allergic rhinitis

A close association exists between adenoid hypertrophy and allergic rhinitis, although the precise nature of this relationship requires careful interpretation. Adenoid hypertrophy is a common pediatric condition arising from inflammatory stimulation that leads to enlargement of nasopharyngeal adenoid tissue, impairing nasal ventilation and producing related symptoms. Allergic rhinitis is a chronic, non-infectious inflammatory disorder of the nasal mucosa, typically presenting with nasal itching, rhinorrhea, sneezing, and congestion.

Acute nasal mucosal congestion may affect adjacent adenoid tissue, contributing to engorgement and hypertrophy, and excessive secretions associated with allergic rhinitis may further irritate the tissue. 10 Cho et al. demonstrated that allergic reactions, particularly local allergic inflammation, appear to play a role in adenoid pathology, highlighting the potential contribution of allergen sensitization in affected children. 11 Intranasal corticosteroids have been shown to be effective in managing both allergic rhinitis and pediatric adenoid hypertrophy; a randomized controlled trial by Roushdy et al. demonstrated that topical mometasone furoate provided significant temporary relief of AH-related symptoms compared with placebo. 12 Leukotriene receptor antagonists have also shown therapeutic benefits in pediatric allergic rhinitis, further supporting an etiological link with hypersensitivity mechanisms. 13 Taken together, these findings support a close association between the two conditions, although causality cannot be established from observational data alone.

4.2. Potential mechanisms underlying the effect of LTRFA adenoidectomy on allergic rhinitis

Traditionally, adenoid curettage has been the standard treatment for AH complicated by AR; however, its limitations, including restricted visualization, intraoperative bleeding risk, and postoperative pain, often lead to suboptimal outcomes. In recent years, endoscopic LTRFA has gained increasing clinical recognition for its advantages of minimal invasiveness, high precision, reduced complications, and faster recovery.

The greater symptomatic improvement observed in the surgical group in the present study may potentially be explained by two mechanisms, although these were not directly measured and remain speculative. First, removal of hypertrophic adenoid tissue may relieve nasopharyngeal obstruction, improving nasal airflow and creating conditions more favorable to the resolution of local inflammation. Second, the enlarged adenoid may serve as an expanded contact surface for inhaled allergens; its removal could reduce allergen exposure, potentially decreasing the frequency and severity of AR episodes. These hypotheses are broadly consistent with findings by Warman et al., who reported significant improvement in AR symptoms following adenoidectomy in pediatric patients. 14 However, further studies incorporating objective measures of nasal patency, allergen sensitization, and immune markers are needed to elucidate the underlying mechanisms.

4.3. Clinical implications of postoperative management

In the present study, 7 of 59 surgical patients (11.9%) did not meet the improvement criterion at 6-month follow-up, highlighting that LTRFA adenoidectomy does not uniformly resolve AR in all cases. Postoperative pharmacological management therefore remains essential, with intranasal corticosteroids and antihistamines recommended for ongoing AR control. A combined strategy of surgical intervention to relieve obstruction, followed by individualized pharmacological therapy, is recommended, with regular follow-up to adjust treatment as needed.

5. Limitations and future directions

This study has several important limitations that should be considered when interpreting the findings. First, the non-randomized design, with group allocation determined by guardian preference rather than random assignment, introduces potential selection bias. Although baseline characteristics were comparable between groups, unmeasured confounders (e.g., socioeconomic factors, health-seeking behavior, or disease severity not captured by VAS) may have influenced both group allocation and outcomes. Second, follow-up was limited to 6 months, and whether the observed symptomatic improvement is sustained over the long term remains unknown. Third, the relatively small sample size (n=89) and the lack of a formal sample size calculation prior to study initiation highlight the need for larger studies with adequate sample size estimation to further validate these preliminary findings. Fourth, outcome assessment relied solely on patient-reported or guardian-reported VAS scores without objective measures such as nasal endoscopy, acoustic rhinometry, serum IgE levels, or skin prick test results, which would have provided a more comprehensive evaluation of AR severity. Fifth, the improvement threshold (VAS reduction ≥3 points) was based on clinical judgment rather than a formally validated minimum clinically important difference (MCID) for this population. In addition, because only two assessment time points were available, more advanced longitudinal analytical approaches such as linear mixed-effects models were not applied and may be considered in future studies with larger samples and more repeated measurements. Future multicenter randomized controlled trials with longer follow-up, larger sample sizes, objective outcome measures, and formal MCID definitions are warranted to confirm and extend these preliminary findings.

6. Conclusion

In children with adenoid hypertrophy complicated by allergic rhinitis, low-temperature radiofrequency ablation adenoidectomy was associated with greater symptom improvement than conservative medical treatment during the 6-month follow-up period. The surgical group demonstrated a higher rate of clinically meaningful improvement and a greater reduction in VAS scores. These findings suggest that LTRFA adenoidectomy may be a beneficial treatment option for appropriately selected pediatric patients, particularly those with persistent symptoms despite medical therapy. However, given the observational design and limited sample size, further large-scale studies are needed to confirm these findings.

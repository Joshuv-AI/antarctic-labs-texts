Spatio-temporal differentiation and obstacle diagnosis of coupling coordination of pseudo human settlements in China’s urban agglomerations
Authors: Shenzhen Tian, Jie Wang, Jingqi Wang, Hang Li, Xueming Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
With the rapid development of the Internet, pseudo human settlements (PHS) have emerged, and urban agglomerations (UAs) must urgently adapt to digital and intelligent transformation. This research focuses on 19 China’s UAs and aims to explore the spatio-temporal patterns and obstacles to the coordinated development of PHS. The study creatively constructs a theoretical framework for PHS and employs methods including the coupling coordination degree (CCD) model, standard deviation ellipse, and obstacle degree model. Findings show: (1) CCD exhibits the overall characteristics of “high coupling-low dispersion, low coordination-high dispersion”. There are six types of coupling coordination, with disordered types dominating. The evolution of types shows three stages: “disorder prevalent-coordination emerges-coordination expands”. (2) Temporally, taking 2015 as the reference point, the overall trend shows rapid growth followed by fluctuations and declines, gradually stabilizing. Spatially, it shows “east-high, west-low” and “core-periphery” patterns, with spatial agglomeration and a southward center of gravity shift. (3) Tool and living systems are key dimensional obstacles, while Start Reading, 2345 Weather, Quark, Kuaishou, and Tencent Cloud are major indicator obstacles. This study enriches the theoretical content of human settlements research in the digital age, provides new perspectives for human settlements geography, and expands the research dimension of the “human-land-technology” interaction in urbanization theory. Meanwhile, it contributes to promoting high-quality urban development and the creation of innovative, livable, civilized, and smart modern cities for the people.

Introduction

Against the backdrop of the rapid development of new productive forces, the iterative upgrading of digital technology1 is reshaping the form of human settlements. Breakthroughs in new-generation information technologies such as big data2, AI3, and metaverse4 have accelerated the integration of physical and digital spaces, giving rise to PHS. PHS refers to an information environment constructed through simulating, reconstructing, and reproducing the real environment with the aid of media and symbolic systems in the context of the information age. It takes the Internet as the medium, features informatization and virtualization as core attributes, and aims to satisfy diverse demands including daily life, entertainment, and social interaction. Its research scope is defined as the virtual space category that forms a digital twin and functional extension with RHS.

As a digital twin and functional extension of the reality human settlements (RHS), PHS are profoundly changing human production and lifestyles by optimizing the allocation of spatial resources and providing accurate services through the flow and integration of data elements. This transformation not only echoes the national strategic deployment to promote “Digital China”5, but also injects new momentum into the high-quality development of new urbanization, conforming to the transition of China’s urban development from large-scale incremental expansion to a phase emphasizing the enhancement of existing resources in terms of quality and efficiency. As the core spatial carrier of new urbanization, UAs, with their networked and clustered characteristics, provide a natural scenario for the cross-regional collaborative transformation of PHS. Compared with a single city, UAs can better reflect the regional linkage effect and spatial differentiation law of PHS digital development, making them a key scale for analyzing the PHS coupling coordination mechanism. From a theoretical perspective, the theoretical framework for the human settlements in the digital age can be improved to reveal the scientific laws governing the interaction between “digital-physical” spaces. From a practical perspective, this research can provide guidance on the path toward digital transformation and spatial governance optimization in UAs, promoting the construction of innovative, livable, and smart modern cities.

Literature review

Urban agglomerations

On July 15, 2025, the Central Urban Work Conference of China clearly pointed out that UAs are “national strategic footholds” and “core engines of new urbanization,” and that regional coordination and development should be promoted through a “cluster-based, networked” model. Among the seven key tasks outlined at the meeting, optimizing the modern urban system and fostering the high-quality development of UAs were prioritized, aiming to achieve common prosperity for all and promote harmonious coexistence between humanity and nature, in alignment with the Chinese modernization. As an important practice in the modernization of national governance, the construction of UAs is not only a reshaping of economic geography, but also a systematic project to resolve regional development imbalances and inadequacies. Foreign research began with French geographer Jean Gottmann’s 1957 proposal of the “megalopolis”6, emphasizing giant urban complexes formed along transportation corridors with urban areas as units. In 1983, Ning Yue-min and others first translated it as “Giant Urban Belt” and introduced it to China7. Subsequently, domestic scholars developed concepts such as “Metropolitan Interlocking Region”8 and “Urban Agglomeration”9 based on this, placing greater emphasis on administrative boundaries and regional coordination. In summary, scholars both domestically and internationally have studied UAs from different perspectives: (1) From an urban studies perspective, research focuses on the hierarchical system10, evaluation of regional integration11, population projections12, spatial structure and evolution13. (2) From an ecological perspective: This involves carbon emissions from UAs14, health evaluation15 and ecological safety16, among other factors. (3) From an economic perspective: Research includes urban agglomeration and dispersion17, productivity and skill allocation18 and regional development19. (4) From a sociological perspective: Exploring urban agglomeration policies20, development planning21 and urban resilience22. Existing research on UAs has produced valuable results, but it has primarily focused on the real world, with limited exploration of the virtual realm.

PHS

In the information age, geography is undergoing profound changes in digitization23, globalization24, and intelligence25, and PHS are gradually becoming the forefront of research. The term “PHS” can be traced back to Lippmann’s theory of “pseudo-settlements”26 proposed in his 1922 book Public Opinion. The core of this theory is that the information environment constructed by the mass media is not an accurate reflection of objective reality, but rather a “symbolic reality.” With the deep integration of digital technology and geographical space, geographers recognized that this media-derived concept could be extended to interpret the spatial logic of human activities in virtual environments—they transformed the symbolic reality from a communication perspective into a spatialized virtual living scenario by incorporating geographical elements such as location attributes, regional differences, and spatial interactions. This transformation enables PHS to bridge the gap between media information environments and geographical research focuses on human-land relationships, making it applicable to analyzing the spatial patterns and evolutionary mechanisms of digital human activities. In geography, this concept has evolved to describe PHS that are built upon RHS through digital technology, information dissemination, and residents’ cognitive interactions. PHS possess three key characteristics: reality mapping, media dependence, and cognitive construction. Chinese scholars, such as Xueming Li, have further refined the geographical connotation of PHS, incorporating them into a “three states” of human settlements system of “reality-pseudo-image”27, emphasizing the interaction between the three in reshaping the relationship between humans and the land.

(1) Research content: Mainly involves spatio-temporal evolution28, realistic characteristics and driving mechanisms29, residents’ health and livability30, and research on the correlation with other forms of human settlements31. (2) Research data: The multi-source geographical big data was used, covering multiple aspects such as nature, humanities, and technology. Examples include Landsat data32, GIS data, survey data33, night light data34, and POI data35. (3) Research methods: From traditional entropy weighting methods36 and ArcGIS spatial analysis37 to the use of CCD models38, geographic detectors39, and system dynamics models40, a methodological system combining qualitative and quantitative approaches and emphasizing both static analysis and dynamic simulation has been established. (4) Research scale: Covers three types of scales: micro scale (such as communities41 and streets42; mesoscale (such as cities43 and provinces44; and macro scale (such as countries45 and global46.

Coupling coordination degree

In recent years, geographical research has shifted from single-factor analysis to the study of complex multi-system coupling mechanisms. Coupling refers to the process by which systems or subsystems establish connections and interact with each other through media such as matter, energy, and information. Coordination generally refers to the process in which the different components within a system reach a state of harmony and consistency through mutual adjustment and cooperation, building upon the foundation of coupling. (1) Research content: Current domestic and international research includes both binary coupling analysis of urbanization and the ecological environment47, urbanization and ecological resilience48, as well as research on the coordinated evolution of multi-system such as economy-resource-ecological environment49, production-living-ecological space50 and urban resilience and subsystem51. However, research on the coupling coordination of internal systems in the human settlements remains relatively weak. (2) Research data: Includes traditional statistical data, such as government statistical yearbooks52 and demographic statistics53, as well as geographic information data, like remote sensing imagery54 and digital elevation models (DEM)55. In recent years, big data sources such as social media56 and Python57 have injected new vitality into coupling coordination research. (3) Research scale: This includes both urban and rural functional zones, as well as cross-regional research and global scales27. Micro and macro scales are interconnected, with micro-scale research focusing on individual behavior and small-area detailed processes, while macro-scale research focuses on the comprehensive characteristics and trends of large areas and even global systems.

Domestic and international research on PHS using pseudo data has made some progress, but the following shortcomings remain: (1) There is a shortage of in-depth and comprehensive studies on PHS. At a time when digital technology is profoundly reshaping the paradigm of urban development, traditional research focuses mostly on the elements of RHS, while research on PHS remains at the theoretical and qualitative stage. (2) Research is limited to individual UAs or developed cities. China’s urban development has entered a stage of regional coordination, with UAs becoming the dominant spatial organization model. The pseudo research paradigm of a single space is unable to meet the needs of the digital transformation of UAs. (3) Research data lacks comprehensiveness. Current research mostly relies on single behavioral data sources such as mobile phone signaling and public transportation card swipe data, lacking comprehensive analysis of diverse behaviors such as socializing, shopping, and traveling. In view of this, this study constructs a theoretical framework for PHS, and uses the CCD model, standard deviation ellipse, and obstacle degree model to explore the spatio-temporal patterns and obstacles of PHS in 19 China’s UAs. In theory, it can enrich the theoretical basis of PHS, provide a logical starting point for constructing a multidimensional human settlements research framework, and promote the diversified development of human settlements geography. At the practical level, it will help offer scientific and rational guidance for the development of UAs in China and achieve coordinated regional development, which will have long-term practical significance for the implementation of “Cyber Power” strategy and the realization of new quality productivity.

The theory of PHS

Basic connotation

PHS are key to promoting the country’s construction of a cyber power, implementing national security concepts, uniting the people, promoting sustainable social development, and achieving win-win cooperation in many areas. The basic connotation includes both broad and narrow aspects.

In a broad sense, PHS refer to the transfer of national economic, political, cultural, social, and ecological construction activities to pseudo spaces in the information age, and the establishment of orderly, efficient, and people-oriented living spaces in these pseudo spaces. This is not a simple transfer of space, but rather a reintegration and reorganization of the country’s various fields of construction in a new virtual space to adapt to the development needs of the information age.

In a narrow sense, PHS mainly focus on the behavior and experiences of individual residents. Residents use the Internet as a medium to collect and obtain various types of information to meet their different needs, such as work, socializing, and learning. At the same time, they leave information with geographical attributes and personal characteristics in the pseudo space, which is characterized by intelligence, informatization, and virtualization.

Theoretical basis

PHS are based on the development of Internet technology and characterized by informatization and virtualization. They are constructed from the massive amounts of data generated by the main actors on the Internet. PHS break down traditional geographical boundaries and create new human-land relationships, but their core remains the pursuit of harmonious human-land relationships. Therefore, the theory of PHS is based on the theories of sustainable development, human-land relationships, and human geography.

The theory of sustainable development is deeply connected to PHS and is the cornerstone of the core concept of PHS. On the one hand, its people-oriented core concept is consistent with the “people-oriented” connotation of PHS. On the other hand, its concept of multi-dimensional coordinated development provides theoretical support for the construction of PHS, helping to build an orderly and harmonious human settlements, and jointly serving the goal of “people-centeredness.”

The theory of human-land relations has developed from environmental determinism and possibility theory to sustainable development theory, always focusing on interplay of human actions and the geographic environment. In the information age, the emergence of PHS has injected new connotations into this theory. The virtual space constructed with digital technology breaks the limitations of traditional geographical space, reshapes the interaction model between people and the earth, and intertwines virtual and real elements. People, virtual environment elements, and geospatial elements interact through data flow and communication channels, forming a bidirectional coupling mechanism between the virtual and real worlds. This mechanism drives the evolution of human-land relationship theory toward a higher-order form of “intelligent collaboration and virtual-real interaction,” providing innovative theoretical perspectives and practical pathways for addressing contemporary human settlements issues and achieving sustainable development.

Human settlements geography, as an important branch of the discipline of geography, focuses on the “human-settlements relationship” as its research object, examining the interactions between human activities and natural and cultural environments58. It aims to uncover the underlying mechanisms linking geographical elements across different systems, thereby addressing diverse human settlements needs and enhancing the quality of the human settlements. In the 1990s, Academician Wu Liangyong initially advanced “the science of human settlements”59, dividing the human settlements into five major systems to comprehensively examine the quality of the settlements. As a new form of mapping the human settlements in the information age, PHS also follow the logical paradigm of this theory. This study deconstructs its five major systems, analyzes the obstacles between them, focuses on solving existing problems, and promotes the innovative development of PHS theory and practice.

Theoretical framework

The coordinated development of PHS encompasses the coupling and synchronization of various elements, systems, and forms. It is a process that progresses from disorder to order, and from simplicity to complexity (Fig. 1). It can be divided into three stages:

Primary stage: Coupling and coordination between elements. PHS are composed of pseudo elements, which are the basic units that make up pseudo systems. For example, the coupling and coordination between elements such as Weibo, WeChat and Alipay, and it also includes aspects such as energy flow and information exchange. System coupling plays a connecting role between element coupling and form coupling.Advanced stage: Coupling and coordination between forms. PHS refer to virtual spaces established by simulating and reconstructing the RHS through information media such as the Internet. Together, RHS and PHS form the complete representation of the human settlements, which is the highest stage of coupling and coordination.

Data and methods

Research area

UAs are becoming key spatial drivers for supporting Chinese modernization and realizing the Chinese Dream of national rejuvenation, thanks to their strong agglomeration and radiation effects. This paper takes 19 China’s UAs as its research subjects (Fig. 2), including the Northeastern China’s UAs: Harbin-Changchun (HBCC), Liaoning Central-Southern (CSL). The Eastern China’s UAs: Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei (BTH), Shandong Peninsula (SDP), Yangtze River Delta (YRD), Guangdong-Fujian-Zhejiang Coastal (GFZ), Pearl River Delta (PRD), Beibu Gulf (BBG). The Central China’s UAs: Central Shanxi (CSX), Central Plains (CPL), Middle Reaches of Yangtze River (MRYR)60. The Western China’s UAs: Hohhot-Baotou-Yulin (HBOY), Ningxia Yellow River (NYR), Guanzhong Plain (GZP), Chengdu-Chongqing (CDCQ), Central Guizhou (CGZ), Central Yunnan (CYN), Lanzhou-Xining (LZXN), Tianshan North Slope (TSNS)61, totaling 236 cities.

China’s UAs, covering about 33.3% of the country’s land area, are inhabited by over 60% of the permanent population and contribute more than 70% of the nation’s GDP. They have become the core region driving domestic demand growth and global economic competition. In the spatial economic structure, relying on the differentiated strategy of “eastern leadership, central rise, and western development,” a specialized division of labor system has gradually formed, consisting of “high-end manufacturing in the eastern coastal region, modern services in the central region, and distinctive industries in the western region.” UAs such as the YRD, PRD, and BTH lead in terms of economic scale. In 2024, the GDP of the YRD will account for nearly a quarter of the national total, while that of the PRD will account for about a tenth, forming the prototype of “world-class UAs.

Data source

The data is sourced from the open-source platform of Baidu Index. Baidu Index boasts the highest comprehensive search penetration rate, making it highly representative and reliable. The data collection period covers from January 1, 2011, to December 31, 2024. It should be acknowledged that relying on a single search engine may introduce potential biases: younger users and specific demographic groups may prefer alternative platforms (e.g., Meitu, QQ), leading to underrepresentation of their digital behavior in Baidu Index data. This could slightly reduce the accuracy of PHS measurement for niche groups, but the high market share of Baidu mitigates this limitation to a certain extent, ensuring the reliability of overall spatio-temporal differentiation patterns and core research conclusions.

Comparing the five subsystems of humans, housing, society, support, and environment in the RHS, and referring to residents’ behavioral preferences under big data in the Internet era, PHS are divided into five subsystems: life, entertainment, socialization, information, and tool, with a total of 62 indicators (Fig. 3). Indicators were selected based on the principles of theoretical consistency, scenario comprehensiveness, practical representativeness, and data accessibility. For instance, Quark and 2345 Weather selected for the tool system, as well as QQ and WeChat for the socialization system, are all representative applications with strong universality, high market share, and wide user coverage in residents’ digital lives.

The core function orientation of each subsystem is as follows:

Life System: Supports residents’ basic needs for digital survival, covering virtual mappings of high-frequency real-life scenarios such as shopping, housing, travel, and local life services.Entertainment System: Meets residents’ needs for digital entertainment and spiritual consumption, including online leisure scenarios such as audio-visual media, games, and creative design.Socialization System: Constructs virtual social networks to realize the digital extension of social needs such as interpersonal interaction, information sharing, and opinion exchange.Information System: Provides instrumental services such as information acquisition, knowledge learning, and office collaboration to support production and cognitive activities in the digital era.Tool System: Offers functional and practical digital tools to address residents’ specific needs in weather inquiry, map navigation, document processing, etc.

Obstacle degree model

The obstacle degree model is a method used to analyze hindering factors and their impact on system development. It typically relies on theories such as system dynamics and hierarchical analysis to categorize and quantify the various factors influencing the system’s development. By constructing an index system, it calculates the obstacle degree of each factor to the system development goal to identify the key obstacle factors. Result analysis

Overall level of the PHS of China’s UAs

The CCD level of UAs

PHS generally exhibit characteristics of “high coupling-low dispersion, low coordination-high dispersion”. The average coupling degree ranges from 0.9941 to 0.9985, with a standard deviation ranging from 0.0010 (HBOY) to 0.0029 (YRD). Among these, the LZXN (0.9985), the NYR (0.9981), and the BBG (0.9975) had coupling degrees close to 1 (Fig. 4a), indicating that the PHS systems in China’s UAs have strong coupling effects and that the subsystems are closely interrelated, which is consistent with the macro trend of multi-system coordinated evolution in the urbanization process. The standard deviation average is only 0.0019, indicating low dispersion. This shows that although UAs are distributed in different geographical locations and have different development foundations, the coupling mechanisms of PHS subsystems have become consistent, and there are very small regional differences in the intensity of interactions between systems.

The average CCD ranges from 0.3493 to 0.4645, with a standard deviation ranging from 0.0225 (HBOY) to 0.0945 (BTH). Among them, only the BTH, SDP, YRD, and PRD had a CCD higher than 0.4, reflecting that although PHS are closely coupled, the overall coordination degree is low, and “strong coupling and weak coordination” has become a common feature. At the same time, the CCD’s average standard deviation reached 0.0462, which is more than 24 times the coupling degree. The large dispersion among UAs indicates that substantial spatial variations exist in the balance and stability of the PHS subsystem in China’s UAs. The path of coordinated development should be tailored to local contexts and fully leverage the synergistic driving force of high-level UAs.

The CCD types of UAs

The CCD for 2011–2024 includes two types of disorders: mild disorder and near disorder, as well as four types of coordination: barely coordination, preliminary coordination, intermediate coordination, and good coordination (Fig. 4b).

Overall, there is a trend toward a decrease in disordered contraction and a stepwise expansion of coordinated contraction. From 2011 to 2024, disordered contraction will continue to dominate, but its proportion will decline year by year, while coordinated contraction will gradually increase. In 2011, mild disorder accounted for 94% of the total, dominating the coupled coordination state. The decline peaked in 2015, first increasing and then leveling off, but by 2024, mild disorder still accounted for 67% of the total, reflecting the generally low level of imbalance in PHS. Since 2013, the frequency of types such as barely coordinated, preliminary coordination, moderate coordination, and good coordination have increased year by year, with coupling coordination transitioning from “disorder dominance” to “coordination breakthrough”. Notably, between 2015 and 2019, the share of coordination types increased to 10%, signaling that the coordination of PHS in China’s UAs has started to yield results.

There are significant differences between stages, with 2011–2024 showing three stages of characteristics: “disorder dominance-coordination sprouting-coordination gradual development”. 2011–2014 was the initial disorder stage: disorder accounted for more than 90% of cases, with almost all cases classified as mild disorder or near disorder. During this period, there were significant coordination issues in PHS in UAs, but they did not reach the level of serious disorder. 2015–2018 was a medium-term transition period: the proportion of mild disorder decreased significantly, shifting to near-disorder, and the coordination of PHS began to show signs of improvement. 2019–2024 is the late stabilization stage: the proportion of mild disorder and near disorder fluctuates downward, while types such as barely coordinated and preliminarily coordinated show a certain degree of growth. Although there remain some differences in the CCD between different UAs, the overall trend is toward coordination.

Spatial and temporal differentiation characteristics of PHS in China’s UAs

Time pattern of CCD

The CCD of PHS in China’s UAs diminishes progressively from east to west, showing notable regional variations. The CCD ranking is as follows: Eastern China’s UAs > Northeastern China’s UAs > Central China’s UAs > Western China’s UAs (Fig. 5). The Eastern China’s UAs has consistently had the highest average value, increasing from 0.3614 in 2011 to 0.4174 in 2024, with an average annual increase of 3.5%. This reflects the leading level of coordination in PHS in the eastern coastal urban agglomeration. The Northeastern and Central China’s UAs grew simultaneously, but remained lower than the Eastern China’s UAs, reflecting the gap in the coordinated development of inland UAs. The western UAs in China exhibits the lowest coupling, increasing from 0.3387 in 2011 to 0.3762 in 2024, with the slowest rate of growth. In 2024, it remains 0.0413 lower than the Eastern China’s UAs, primarily due to constraints imposed by factors such as fragile ecosystems and weak economic foundations in the western regions.

The standard deviation mirrors the extent of CCD dispersion within a region. In Eastern China, the standard deviation of the CCD for UAs exhibits long-term high values and significant peaks. The standard deviation of Eastern China’s UAs has always been the highest, reaching a peak of 0.0771 in 2015. This phenomenon results from the combined effects of economic polarization and development imbalances. On one hand, BTH, YRD, and PRD regions rely on policy and geographical advantages, with economic development and urbanization levels far exceeding those of other Eastern China’s UAs. The economic polarization effect has driven the coordinated upgrading of pseudo subsystems. On the other hand, coordinated planning among UAs has not been effectively implemented, with insufficient industrial division of labor, ecological compensation, and coordinated public services within the region, exacerbating the “core-periphery” divide. This ultimately manifests as a peak breakthrough in the standard deviation of coupling coordination. After 2015, with the implementation of coordination mechanisms, the integration of the YRD’s ecological and green development, the implementation of coordination policies in the Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area (GBA)66, and the promotion of industrial upgrading in the CSL region, the gap in the CCD between the subsystems of core and non-core UAs narrowed. This indicates that high standard deviation values are a stage-specific indicator of development imbalances, and that the improvement of coordination mechanisms can effectively reduce dispersion.

The degree of coordination between PHS in China’s UAs has gradually shifted from mild disorder to near disorder, with the disorder stage remaining dominant and showing significant phased characteristics. Taking 2015 as the time node, it can be divided into two stages (Fig. 6). (1) From 2011 to 2015, the CCD of PHS in UAs was in a stage of continuous increase, with mild disorder as the dominant type. The average CCD rose from 0.3482 in 2011 to 0.3914 in 2024, reaching a peak of 0.4148 in 2015. This was mainly a result of the adoption of a series of national policies aimed at promoting regional coordinated development, such as further deepening openness in eastern coastal UAs and strengthening international exchange and cooperation; for central and western UAs, increased investment in infrastructure construction has gradually strengthened economic ties and industrial linkages between UAs, thereby driving a rapid increase in the overall average CCD. (2) From 2016 to 2024, there will be a period of fluctuating decline and stabilization, with significant regional differentiation and disorder becoming the main type, while some UAs will struggle to achieve coordination. The period from 2016 to 2019 saw a four-year consecutive decline, with a clear downward trend. Although there will be slight fluctuations between 2020 and 2024, overall, the trend will not return to the previous upward trajectory, and the coordinated development of PHS in UAs will enter a plateau period. During this period, the PRD and BTH remained in the lead. In particular, the PRD achieved a coupling coordination index of 0.5018 in 2015, becoming the first urban agglomeration in the country to enter the “barely coordinated” stage. The main reason for this was that, relying on the GBA policy and the construction of the Xiong’an New Area, it gave full play to its advantages in capital, talent, and technology, continuously constructed and improved PHS, and maintained long-term leadership in coordination efficiency.

Spatial analysis of CCD

To further investigate the spatial distribution of the CCD of PHS in China’s UAs, study employs ArcGIS 10.8 and selects 2011, 2015, 2019, and 2024 as node years for visualization analysis (Fig. 7).

From 2011 to 2024, the CCD of UAs in China shows an obvious east-high and west-low pattern, with significant core polarization. In 2011, all 19 UAs were in a state of mild disorder, with coupling coordination levels below 0.4000 and small differences, indicating obvious regional homogenization. In 2015, the east-west divide intensified, with significant spatial heterogeneity. The PRD achieved a coupling coordination level of 0.5018, realizing a hierarchical leap from “mild disorder” to “barely coordinated” and becoming the highest extreme value area in the east. Potential UAs such as the CSL and the CPL are moving towards disorder, relying on policies such as “Northeast Revitalization” and “Central China Rise”. The marginal lagging UAs of TSNS and LZXN remained in the mild disorder stage, due to their remote geographical location and lagging industrial development, respectively. In 2019, the PRD urban agglomeration’s coupling coordination type fell from barely coordinated (0.5018) to near disorder (0.4883), while the BTH (0.4517), the SDP (0.4162), and the YRD (0.4409), all of which maintained relatively high levels of coupling coordination, collectively forming an eastern multi-core collaborative pattern. In the west, only the CDCQ (0.4056) stood out in terms of coupling coordination, benefiting from the strategic drive of the “Chengdu-Chongqing Dual-City Economic Circle” initiative and its own infrastructure foundation, thereby establishing a single-core leading pattern in the west. In 2024, the CDCQ will retreat from near disorder (0.4056) to mild disorder (0.3985), and the single-core pattern in the west will disappear, while the multi-core pattern in the east will remain stable, with coupling coordination leading other UAs in the long term, maintaining its leading position in the east and dominating the multi-core polarized spatial pattern.

UAs can be categorized into two groups based on the continuity and hierarchical transcendence of the evolution of CCD levels: one group includes UAs whose CCD level has remained unchanged throughout, remaining in the same stage, referred to as “solid stable UAs”. The other c group consists of dynamic transition UAs, which experience changes in their CCD levels (e.g., mild disorder to barely coordinated) and spans across two or more levels. During the research period, five UAs, TSNS, NYR, LZXN, MRYR, and BBG, remained in a state of mild disorder, belonging to the stable type, and were spatially concentrated in the western inland and central peripheral regions. The remaining 14 UAs, including HBCC, CSL, BTH, SDP, YRD, GFZ, PRD, CPL, CSX, HBOY, GZP, CDCQ, CGZ, and CYN, exhibited varying degrees of hierarchical changes in their CCD levels, classifying them as dynamic transitional UAs. Overall, a significant number of dynamic transitional UAs suggest that most UAs are undergoing a continuous process of adjustment in coordination, while a small group of solid, stable UAs demonstrate a more consistent and steady development trend. This distribution reflects the complexity and diversity faced by China’s PHS in the process of development, indicating that a series of measures should be taken in the construction and improvement of PHS in UAs, such as differentiated policy support, optimization of regional coordination and cooperation, and precise development strategies, to promote the sustainable development of different types of UAs.

Dynamic evolution of CCD

To further investigate the dynamic evolution of the CCD from 2011 to 2024 using the standard deviation ellipse, with 2011, 2015, 2019, and 2024 also taken as the nodal years.

The spatial arrangement of the CCD of the PHS of UAs shows a tendency towards concentration, with the primary direction of spatial distribution being northeast-southwest. (1) From 2011 to 2024, the minor axis and major axis of the standard deviation ellipse decreased by 10.94 km (Fig. 8a) and 2.73 km (Fig. 8b) respectively, while ellipse area shrank by approximately 47,249.88 km² (Fig. 8c). This indicates a spatial contraction trend in the CCD, evolving from relatively dispersed to concentrated. (2) The azimuth angle generally changed slightly in the range of 9°-11°, with a maximum difference of 1.6° (Fig. 8d), indicating that the rotation direction of the standard deviation ellipse tends to be stable, showing a northeast-southwest spatial distribution pattern and basically covering most areas of China’s UAs. (3) The center of gravity of the CCD is distributed in Luoyang, Henan (111°25’16"E ~ 111°31’51"E, 33°51’30"N ~ 34°0’19"N), and its movement trajectory exhibits staged migration along a southeast-to-southwest direction. Initially, the movement follows a northwest-to-southeast path with a relatively large amplitude, before shifting to a northeast-to-southwest direction with a smaller amplitude.

This suggests that the CCD of the PHS in eastern and southern UAs in China is higher than that in western and northern regions. This pattern is influenced both by the northeast-southwest distribution of the CCD and by the spatial trend where the CCD levels in UAs are generally “east-high, west-low”. Specifically, from 2011 to 2018, the multi-core pattern in the east, consisting of the BTH, SDP, and YRD, persisted for an extended period. During this time, the PHS developed well, and the CCD level remained relatively high, contributing to the shift of the development center of PHS in UAs from the northwest to the southeast. From 2018 to 2024, supported by the “Western Development” and “Poverty Alleviation” strategies, the transportation, network and other infrastructure in the CYN, CDCQ, and CGZ in the southwest region have been continuously improved. The CCD has steadily increased compared with that of the Northeastern China’s UAs, and the development status is good, which promotes the migration of the development center of the PHS of UAs from the northeast to the southwest.

Analysis of the obstacle factors of the PHS

To further reveal the key factors restricting their coordinated development, an analysis of the obstacles of the CCD needs to be carried out to clarify the main obstacles affecting the improvement of the level of the PHS of UAs and optimize the development path.

Analysis of the criterion layer

The evolution of the obstacle factors in the criterion layer of the PHS of China’s UAs from 2011 to 2024 reveals the dynamic change in the constraints imposed by the five PHS subsystems on coordinated development (Fig. 9a).

The tool and living system are the main obstacle dimensions. The criterion layer’s average obstacle rankings are: tool system (0.2431) > living system (0.2410) > entertainment system (0.1878) > information system (0.1712) > socialization system (0.1568). The tool and living system focus on the core needs of residents’ survival and development, and their average obstacles are significantly higher than other systems, which are key factors restricting the improvement of PHS. The fundamental reason is the contradiction between the upgrading of people’s livelihood needs and the uneven regional supply. As residents strive for a better life, their demands for settlements and quality of life continue to grow and diversify. However, the high costs and supply pressures, coupled with uneven regional distribution, often result in an imbalance between supply and demand.(2)There is a co-existence of regional differences and stability. Among these, the living system exhibits the most significant regional variation, ranging from a high of 0.2820 (PRD) to a low of 0.2123 (CGZ). This discrepancy arises from the developmental stage disparities between the “high-demand, high-pressure” of developed Eastern China’s UAs (PRD) and the “low-supply, low-coordination” of underdeveloped Western China’s UAs (CGZ). Analysis of the indicator layer

Overall, the five indicators of Start Reading P28, 2345 Weather P37, Quark P39, Kuaishou P43, and Tencent Cloud P62 are the main obstacles restricting the improvement of PHS in Chinese UAs (Fig. 9b). Specifically:

In 2011, the Goufang.com (0.0580) was the key obstacle. With China’s urbanization rate exceeding 50% for the first time67, the real estate market faced severe supply-demand imbalance. Data from the National Bureau of Statistics shows that in 2011, real estate investment growth outpaced the expansion of residential new start-up areas, while personal mortgage loans dropped sharply amid the expansion of sales areas. Meanwhile, China’s early online real estate platforms were still in their infancy, plagued by common issues such as outdated information and untrustworthy listings. The gap between residents’ growing demand for digital housing services and the inadequate information quality, coverage, and reliability of online platforms at that time resulted in Goufang.com’s high obstacle degree.

In 2015, the obstacle degrees of three indicators were relatively prominent, namely Anipop (0.0428), 2345 Weather (0.0337), and Beautycam (0.0343). This stems from the explosive growth of entertainment, lifestyle, and social applications driven by the popularization of mobile Internet and 4G networks. However, the development of digital services is plagued by a dual contradiction: the upgrading of user demands and the imbalance in service supply. For instance, while users in the YRD in eastern UAs face content overload and homogenization, those in the TSNS in the west are constrained by insufficient network coverage and speed. Additionally, cross-city entertainment resources are excessively concentrated in first-tier cities, further exacerbating the supply-demand contradiction within the entertainment system.

In 2019, 2345 Weather (0.0372) emerged as the primary obstacle factor, rooted in the notable gap between the public’s urgent demand for high-quality meteorological services and the actual supply level amid intensifying climate risks. Frequent extreme weather events in China that year significantly raised public demand for accurate meteorological information and disaster early warning services. However, public meteorological services had prominent shortcomings: extreme weather complexity challenged forecast accuracy and lead time, while meteorological information lacked refinement, scenario-specific adaptation, and effective early warning linkage. These deficiencies caused a severe supply-demand disconnect, making 2345 Weather a key constraint on PHS improvement.

In 2024, the three main obstacle factors are Quark (0.0848), Start Reading (0.0555), and Tencent Cloud (0.0370). The digital economy has entered a deepening stage, with an explosive growth in total information and continuous refinement of application scenarios, bringing new obstacles to PHS. Quark’s high obstacle degree stems from users’ higher requirements for information filtering and acquisition efficiency in the era of information overload. As an online literature platform, Start Reading faces issues such as content homogenization and a lack of high-quality cultural works. Tencent Cloud, as a representative of personal cloud storage, has a relatively high obstacle degree due to insufficient cold-hot data stratification and inefficient cross-platform scheduling.

Discussion

Sustainable development of PHS

Inter-regional policy coordination is crucial for enhancing the coordination of the PHS. Inter-regional policy coordination can foster the unrestricted movement of resources, talents, and technologies, leading to the sharing of resources and the exploitation of complementary advantages. This, in turn, helps narrow regional disparities and promotes coordinated development. Fang et al.9 pointed out in their research on digital economy in urban-rural areas that government support negatively impacts coordinated urban-rural development, while policy guidance can effectively promote regional coordinated. Zhang et al.68 emphasized the vital importance of policies in strengthening urban resilience, while also noting that conflicts arising from these policies can erode it.

The core UAs have clear advantages in terms of coordination. The core UAs can optimize resource allocation by leveraging their advantages in resources, talents, technologies, etc., and drive the development of marginal UAs. This aligns with the research findings on Urban economic spatial differentiation69, urban expansion and ecosystem services70. The east-high and west-low spatial pattern of the CCD of the PHS is also consistent with research results on livable environments for the elderly66 and tourist cities in China71. Zhang et al.’s research on the coupling coordination between the ecological environment and urbanization also reveals that provincial capital cities outperform other cities, which echoes the “core-periphery” spatial differentiation characteristic of PHS72.

Digital technology positively impacts the coordinated development of PHS. Research by Liu et al. also demonstrated the positive role of the internet in narrowing the wealth gap, improving digital literacy, and promoting the integration of agriculture and digital technology73. Wang et al.74 proposed that the mechanisms of urban elements on core environmental indicators vary under different geographical conditions, providing a spatial analysis perspective for exploring the adaptability of digital technologies in the coordinated development of PHS. Rui et al.75 emphasized the equitable allocation of urban resources from the perspective of vulnerable groups, which aligns with the goal of digital technologies empowering the coordinated development of PHS and narrowing the digital service gap, thereby enriching the fairness-related theoretical connotation of PHS research.

Limitations

Three-dimensional research content: The three-dimensionalization of the human settlements has become the prevailing development trend, reflecting the growing complexity and multi-dimensional nature of urban spaces. Future research not only needs to focus on the relationship between systems but also should deeply explore the mechanisms between different forms of human settlements and multiple elements, the research scale effect, and the driving mechanism.Refined research scale: This study covers 19 China’s UAs, revealing the spatiotemporal differentiation law and obstacle factors of the PHS. Comparative analysis with previous research

Compared with Tian et al.10 which focused on the spatio-temporal evolution of PHS in a single region of the three northeastern provinces, this study covers 19 national UAs, revealing the macro spatial pattern of “east-high and west-low” and the “core-periphery” differentiation characteristics, expanding the spatial scale and universality of PHS research. Unlike Xue et al.30 which only focused on the interaction between real and PHS, this study innovatively constructs a coupling coordination framework of five major subsystems of PHS, clarifying the key obstacle status of tool system and living system, and deepening the connotation of the “human-land-technology” interaction theory in the digital age. Compared with Yu et al.29 which emphasized the analysis of PHS driving mechanisms, this study identifies specific indicator obstacles such as Start Reading and 2345 Weather through the obstacle degree model, providing more direct practical support for precise optimization paths.

Policy suggestions

Against the background of China’s efforts to build a cyber power, the construction and development of the PHS have attracted wide attention from the whole society. In view of the problems existing in the PHS in China’s UAs, the following optimization suggestions are put forward:

Improve the overall level of coordination. Innovate core information technologies. Local governments should improve the incentive mechanism to encourage technological innovation and cooperation among the five subsystems. Increase policy and financial backing for disordered UAs such as NYR, LZXN, and BBG, bolster information infrastructure development, and drive forward the digital transformation of industries. For regions where the buds of coordination have emerged, such as the BTH, YRD, and CDCQ, strengthen economic resilience and information technology advantages, consolidating the achievements of coordinated development. At the same time, introduce high-tech into new fields and promote the application of innovative models like smart cities and digital governance in government services and smart communities. For example, the Central China’s UAs can draw on the experiences of the YRD to enhance ecological livability and productivity through digital technology.The core drives the periphery. Implement a regional differential development strategy and strengthen the regional cooperation mechanism. For core UAs in the east, such as the PRD and BTH, fully leverage their leading and collaborative demonstration roles. Strengthen the regional cooperation mechanism, establish a digital service alliance for Eastern China’s UAs, and improve the overall competitiveness. For the Central, Western, and Northeastern China’s UAs such as the CPL and the CSL, promote the industrial transformation and infrastructure interconnection of marginal UAs, especially improve the construction of public services and infrastructure in remote areas, and promote the comprehensive construction of the PHS of UAs. For instance, the CSL can harness the dual-core driving function of “Shenyang-Dalian” to advance the synchronized growth of central cities and peripheral cities.Optimize obstacle factors. At the criterion level, developed UAs in the east should prioritize addressing resource shortages. For example, the PRD should enhance the availability of inexpensive housing, promote the reform of educational and healthcare resources, and simultaneously drive technology R&D and intelligent upgrading. Underdeveloped UAs in the west such as the North Slope of Tianshan and the CSX should improve the construction of public service infrastructure, optimize the industrial framework, and facilitate the expansion of 5G networks and the advancement of emerging industries. To improve the main obstacle factors at the indicator level, platforms need to strengthen content innovation and review, improve data accuracy, optimize algorithms and technology upgrading, and enhance the positive role of each platform in the PHS.

Conclusion

This study constructs a theoretical framework for the PHS and employs the CCD model and standard deviation ellipse to analyze the spatial-temporal differentiation of the CCD. Using the obstacle degree model for obstacle factor diagnosis, it reveals the laws of restrictive mechanisms of PHS in China’s UAs and offer a fresh perspective for their sustainable development.

Overall level of CCD: The PHS of China’s UAs generally presents the characteristics of “high coupling-low dispersion, low coordination-high dispersion”. The average coupling degree is close to 1, indicating a strong interaction between systems and extremely small regional differences. However, the average coordination degree is only between 0.3493 and 0.4645, and the regional differentiation is relatively significant. There are six types of CCD, with disorder being the dominant type. Among them, the proportion of maladjustment types decreased from 94% in 2011 to 67% in 2024, with the lowest value reaching 51%. The coordinated types emerged from scratch, with the highest proportion reaching 13%. The CCD shows an overall trend of the shrinking of maladjustment types at a decreasing speed and the step-by-step expansion of coordinated types, as well as an evolution trend of three stages: “disorder dominance -coordination sprouting-coordination expansion”.Temporal and spatial pattern of CCD: Temporally, taking 2015 as the time node, it presents a phased characteristic of rapid growth first, then fluctuating decline and gradually stabilizing. The ranking of the CCD basically remained unchanged, in the order of: Eastern China’s UAs > Northeastern China’s UAs > Central China’s UAs > Western China’s UAs. Spatially, it shows an overall feature of high-east and low-west, with a gradient decrease, accompanied by a “core-periphery” differentiation pattern. Based on the continuity and hierarchical leap of the CCD stages, UAs can be categorized into two types: solid stable type and dynamic transitional type. According to the standard deviation ellipse analysis, the spatial pattern of the CCD exhibits a tendency towards concentration. The dominant direction of the spatial distribution is northeast-southwest, and the center of gravity shows a southward movement trend.Diagnosis of obstacles: At the criterion level, the ranking of the obstacles across the five subsystems is as follows: Tool system > living system > entertainment system > information system > socialization system. The tool and living system are the primary obstacles, and the regional differences are more pronounced than those of the other three systems. At the indicator level, the top five obstacle factors are: Start Reading P28, 2345 Weather P37, Quark P39, Kuaishou P43, and Tencent Cloud P62. These factors evolve dynamically over time, revealing the interweaving and evolution structural contradictions in the process of urbanization and digitalization.

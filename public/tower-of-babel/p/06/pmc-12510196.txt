A novel species-specific multiplex PCR assay for the differentiation of five Fowl adenovirus species (A to E): Application to field surveillance☆
Authors: HyeSoon Song, HyeonSu Kim, JiYe Kim, Moon Her, HyeRyoung Kim
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Fowl adenovirus (FAdV) is non‐enveloped, icosahedral DNA virus that cause significant economic losses in poultry by inducing inclusion body hepatitis (IBH), hepatitis‐hydropericardium syndrome (HHS), and gizzard erosion (GE) via both horizontal and vertical transmission. FAdV comprises five species (A-E) and twelve serotypes (1-7, 8a, 8b, and 9-11 ) that frequently co‐circulate within flocks, making early and accurate detection essential for effective control. Here, we have developed a single‐tube multiplex PCR assay targeting conserved regions of the fiber and penton base genes, capable of simultaneously detecting all five FAdV species with a sensitivity of 25 copies/tube. When applied to 125 field strains from 79 naturally FAdV-infected cases, the assay achieved 100 % concordance with singleplex PCR and Sanger sequencing. At the infection level, infection profiling showed 53.2 % single‐species cases, 36.7 % dual‐species co‐infections, 8.9 % triple‐species infections, and 1.3 % quadruple‐species infections, with FAdV-D /E co‐infections most prevalent. At the strain level, FAdV‐E (34.4 %) was the most common, followed by D (31.2 %), B (20.0 %), C (8.0 %), and A (6.4 %). Besides, phylogenetic analysis of the complete sequences of the fiber, penton base, and hexon genes confirmed species‐specific clustering perfectly aligned with the multiplex PCR results. Taken together, this rapid, sensitive, specific, and cost‐effective assay represents a powerful tool, providing a foundation for improved management and control of adenoviral diseases in poultry.

Introduction

Fowl adenovirus (FAdV), a non-enveloped, icosahedral virus belonging to the genus Aviadenovirus within the family Adenoviridae, possesses a linear double-stranded DNA genome of approximately 43-46 kb. This genome encodes three major capsid proteins, fiber, penton base, and hexon, which are critical determinants of virulence, virus neutralization, and cellular epitope binding (Li et al., 2017). Based on genomic differences and serum cross-neutralization tests of hexon, FAdV is classified into five species (FAdV-A to -E), each subdivided into distinct serotypes: FAdV-A (FAdV-1), FAdV-B (FAdV-5), FAdV-C (FAdV-4 and −10), FAdV-D (FAdV- 2, −3, −9, and −11), and FAdV-E (FAdV-6, −7, −8a, and −8b) (Zsák and Kisary, 1984; Hess, 2000; Marek et al., 2016). Although many FAdV infections are subclinical and these viruses are widespread in poultry, several serotypes are associated with clinically important syndromes in chickens, including inclusion body hepatitis (IBH), hepatitis-hydropericardium syndrome (HHS) and gizzard erosion (GE) (Li et al., 2017; Schachner et al., 2018). FAdV-8b and FAdV-11 are frequently implicated in IBH outbreaks, whereas HHS is most often linked to highly pathogenic FAdV-4 and GE to FAdV-1 (Hess, 2000; Cui et al., 2020; Li et al., 2022; Santander-Parra et al., 2023). Notably, FAdV-4 has caused severe HHS with high mortality in broilers and geese and can induce HHS-like disease in ducks (Chen et al., 2017; Yu et al., 2018). Duck adenovirus (DAdV), which is phylogenetically distinct from FAdV and primarily infects waterfowl, is grouped into two species (DAdV-A [DAdV-1] and DAdV-B [DAdV-2–6]) (Huang et al., 2020; Shi et al., 2022). Historically, DAdV-1 has been associated with egg-drop syndrome in laying birds, although infections in ducks are often mild or subclinical (Hess et al., 1997). More recently, DAdV-3 has emerged as a virulent pathogen causing severe hepatic and renal lesions in ducks and HHS-like outbreaks with high mortality in chickens (Guo et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2024). These observations underscore the ecological complexity of avian adenoviruses and the potential for cross-species transmission.

FAdV spreads both horizontally via the fecal-oral route and vertically through embryonated eggs, the latter often causing mortality rates up to 80 ∼ 90 % in young birds and substantial economic losses (Cui et al., 2020; Kiss et al., 2021; Xie et al., 2022). Currently, monovalent or multivalent vaccines targeting FAdV-4, −8b, and −11 are commercially available, but these vaccines provide limited cross protection against heterologous serotypes (Wang et al., 2022). Additionally, mixed infections involving serotypes from different species, have occurred in Asia, Europe, North America, and South Africa (Mittal et al., 2014; Schachner et al., 2019; Sabarudin et al., 2022). Such co-infections not only complicate clinical management but also facilitate homologous recombination events that can alter viral tropism or virulence, underscoring the need for comprehensive surveillance. Therefore, there is a need for a rapid, accurate and reliable method for simultaneous detection and differentiation of all five FAdV species. To date, however, no single-tube multiplex PCR assay has been reported that fulfills this need. In this study, we developed and validated a single-tube multiplex PCR assay targeting highly conserved regions of the fiber and penton base genes to enable rapid, species-level detection of FAdV in clinical samples.

Materials and methods

Preparation of FAdV strains

Eight FAdV-positive samples (21RI119, 23ND379, 14AQ384, 21RI010, 24ND100, 21RI003, JB422, and 24ND150), previously confirmed by a PCR assay targeting the hexon gene (Meulemans et al., 2001), were homogenized and propagated on Leghorn Male Hepatocellular (LMH) cells maintained at 37°C in DMEM/F12 (Gibco, USA) supplemented with 10 % fetal bovine serum (FBS; Gibco, USA). When characteristic cytopathic effects (CPE) appeared, the cultures were harvested, subjected to three freeze–thaw cycles, and centrifuged. The supernatants were collected and stored at −80°C until use.

Extraction of viral DNA

Total viral DNA was extracted from FAdV-1 (21RI119), FAdV-2 (23ND379), FAdV-4 (14AQ384), FAdV-5 (21RI010), FAdV-8a (24ND100), FAdV-8b (21RI003), FAdV-10 (JB422), FAdV-11 (24ND150), egg drop syndrome virus (EDSV, BC14), chicken infectious anemia virus (CIAV, 16AD087), infectious laryngotracheitis virus (ILTV, 13ABD076), fowlpox virus (FWPV, 18R058B), reticuloendotheliosis virus (REV, 16AD078), and Marek's disease virus (MDV, VR2175), using a Maelstrom 4000 DNA/RNA auto-extraction machine (TAN bead, Taiwan) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Viral DNA was eluted in 50 μL of elution buffer and stored at −20°C until use.

Design of species-specific primers

Multiplex PCR primers were designed with OligoAnalyzer (Integrated DNA Technologies, USA) using representative GenBank sequences for all twelve FAdV serotypes, FAdV-1 (U46933, NC_001720, OP985634, OP985623, and OP985603), FAdV-2 (KT862806, KT862805, PP942930, OR415879, and OR415874), FAdV-3 (KT862807), FAdV-4 (MG547384, MT635844, KX538980, PQ539419, and OM249937), FAdV-5 (NC_021221, MK757473, OM836676, MT500572, and KC493646), FAdV-6 (NC_038332), FAdV-7 (KT862809), FAdV-8a (KT862810 and OR901942), FAdV-8b (KT862811, MF577036, PP537790, OL456208, and MG712775), FAdV-9 (AF083975, NC000899, and AC_000013), FAdV-10 (No MN542422), and FAdV-11 (KT862812), to amplify conserved regions unique to each species. To validate the species-specific primers, three additional primer sets (FAdV‐F‐PCR for fiber, FAdV‐P‐PCR for penton base, and FAdV‐H‐PCR for hexon) were designed to amplify the complete sequences of their respective targets (Supplementary Table 1).

Construction of standard plasmid for positive controls

Viral DNA extracted from FAdV-1, −4, −5, −8b, and −11 isolates was amplified using the species specific primers, and the purified PCR products were cloned into the pTOP TA V2 vector (Enzynomics, Korea) to generate standard control plasmids. Each PCR assay was performed in a total of 20 μL volume containing 2 μL of each DNA extract, 1 μL of each primer (10 pmoles/μL), and 2 × reaction buffer (BlackPCR Singleplex PCR Premix, Ventech Science, Korea), with the reaction conditions follows: initial denaturation at 94°C for 5 min, followed by 35 cycles of denaturation at 94°C for 50 sec, annealing at 60°C for 50 sec, and extension at 72°C for 50 sec, and a final elongation at 72°C for 5 min. The five recombinant plasmids were verified by sequencing (CosmoGenetech Co., Korea) and then used as templates for testing the sensitivity.

Establishment of a species-specific multiplex PCR

Multiplex PCR assays were optimized by adjusting annealing temperature, primer concentrations, and cycle number. PCR assays (20 μL) were performed with BlackPCR Multiplex PCR Premix (Ventech Science, Korea) containing 2 μL plasmid DNA, 1 μL of each primer (11 pmoles/μL) for FAdV-A and -B, 1 μL of each primer (8 pmoles/μL) for FAdV-C, -D, and -E. The thermal cycler (Eppendorf, Germany) was programmed for initial denaturation at 94°C for 5 min, followed by 38 cycles of 94°C for 50 sec,60°C for 50 sec, and 72°C for 50 sec, and a final extension at 72°C for 5 min.

Amplicons were analyzed under UV light after separation by 1.5 % agarose gel electrophoresis. To confirm the exact target genes, all PCR products were purified using a QIAquick Gel Extraction Kit (Qiagen, Germany) and sequenced by Cosmogenetech Co. (Korea).

Determination of specificity and detection limits

Specificity of the optimized species-specific primers was evaluated using 50 ng of viral DNA from FAdV-1, −2, −4, −5, −8a, −8b, −10, and −11, as well as from EDSV, CIAV, ILTV, FWPV, REV, and MDV.

Detection limits were assessed with five recombinant plasmids (pTOP-FAdV-1, −4, −5, −8b, and 11), each subjected to five‐fold serial dilutions to achieve final concentrations ranging from 78,125 copies/tube to 5 copies/tube. All dilutions were repeated in triplicate for detection limits of both multiplex and singleplex PCR assays.

Validation of multiplex PCR assay

In total, 79 naturally FAdV-infected cases were collected through active and passive surveillance from the Animal and Plant Quarantine Agency (APQA) in Korea. Of these, 48 samples, pooled liver and cecal tonsil tissues from five dead chickens (24–38 days of age), were obtained between April and August 2021, as previously described (Kim et al., 2025). The remaining 31 samples, comprising liver or cecal tonsil tissues, were collected via passive surveillance from 2009 to 2024. Viral DNA was extracted from homogenized samples using a Maelstrom 4000 DNA/RNA auto-extraction system (TANBead, Taiwan) and analyzed by both multiplex and singleplex PCR assays.

To analyze the complete sequences of fiber, penton base, and hexon, FAdV-F-PCR, FAdV-P-PCR, and FAdV-H-PCR were performed in a 20 μL volume containing 2 μL of each DNA extract, 10 pmol of each primer, and BlackPCR Singleplex PCR Premix (Ventech Science, Korea). PCR amplification was carried out in a thermal cycler (Eppendorf, Germany) for 1 cycle of 5 min at 94°C, followed by 94°C for 50 sec, 58°C for 50 sec, and 72°C for 50 sec for 40 cycles, before a final extension at 72°C for 5 min. All amplified fragments were purified using a QIAquick Gel Extraction Kit (Qiagen, Germany), and subjected to Sanger sequencing (CosmoGenetech Co., Korea). Sequences were assembled, aligned, and compared in CLC Main Workbench (Qiagen, Germany), and the new datasets were deposited in GenBank (Supplementary Table 2). Phylogenetic trees for each gene were constructed by the maximum-likelihood method with 1000 bootstrap replicates using MEGA 11 (Tamura et al., 2021).

Development of multiplex PCR

To optimize the multiplex PCR assay, five recombinant plasmids were tested under varying annealing temperatures (59, 60, and 61°C), cycle numbers (35 vs. 38), and primer concentrations (8-11 pmol/µL). While amplification efficiency was similar at all three temperatures, extending to 38 cycles boosted sensitivity roughly fivefold compared to 35 cycles (Supplementary Fig. S1A-C). We then varied primer concentrations from 8 to 11 pmol/µL and determined that 11 pmol/µL for the FAdV-A and -B primer pairs and 8 pmol/µL for the FAdV-C, -D, and -E primer pairs, combined with a 60°C annealing step and 38 cycles, yielded five distinct bands on a 1.5 % agarose gel: 1,321 bp (FAdV-A), 1,069 bp (FAdV-B), 769 bp (FAdV-C), 593 bp (FAdV-D), and 277 bp (FAdV-E) (Supplementary Fig. S2A–C). No nonspecific amplification was observed among any FAdV species (data not shown).

Specificity and sensitivity of multiplex PCR assay

All species-specific primers correctly amplified their respective targets, penton base for FAdV-A (FAdV-1), fiber for FAdV-B (FAdV-5), penton base for FAdV-C (FAdV-4 and −10), fiber for FAdV-D (FAdV-2 and −11), and fiber for FAdV-E (FAdV-8a and −8b), without generating primer dimers or nonspecific bands on 1.5 % agarose gels (Fig. 2, lanes 1-9). No amplification was observed for DNA from other avian viruses, including, EDSV, CIAV, ILTV, FWPV, REV, and MDV (Fig. 2, lanes 10-16). Application of multiplex PCR assay

A total of 125 field strains, derived from 79 naturally FAdV-infected cases, were confirmed by both multiplex and singleplex PCR assays. All amplicons were analyzed to be 100 % identical to the intended target by Sanger sequencing, with no cross-amplification of non-target species detected (data not shown). As shown in Table 2, at the infection level, single‐species infections were detected in 42 of 79 cases, most frequently FAdV‐E (n = 14), followed by FAdV‐D (n = 12), FAdV‐B (n = 9), FAdV‐C (n = 5), and FAdV‐A (n = 2). Dual‐species co‐infections occurred in 29 cases, with FAdV-D/-E dominating (n = 16), followed by FAdV-B/-E (n = 6), FAdV-C/-D (n = 3), FAdV‐A/-B (n = 2), FAdV-B/-C (n = 1), and FAdV-B/-D (n = 1). Phylogenetic analysis

To further validate the multiplex PCR assay, FAdV-F-PCR, FAdV-P-PCR, and FAdV-H-PCR assays were employed to amplify the complete fiber, penton base, and hexon genes, respectively. Of the 125 field strains, eight (21RI024E, 21RI029E, 21RI045E, 21RI064E, 21RI103E, 14AQ418D, 14AQ623D, and 13AD20D) yielded amplicons that could not be fully sequenced, likely due to low template concentration or limited primer sensitivity. In contrast, the remaining 117 field strains and eight positive-control strains generated complete sequences for fiber, penton base, and hexon, enabling unambiguous differentiation of FAdV species (Supplementary Table 2).

Phylogenetic analysis based on these complete sequences demonstrated that all 117 field strains clustered precisely within their respective species clades, with full agreement with the multiplex PCR results. Specifically, fiber sequences (n = 102) clustered into FAdV-A (n = 7), FAdV-B (n = 24), FAdV-C (n = 9), FAdV-D (n = 35), and FAdV-E (n = 27) clades (Fig. 4A). Penton base sequences (n = 105) formed corresponding FAdV-A (n = 8), FAdV-B (n = 24), FAdV-C (n = 10), FAdV-D (n = 33), and FAdV-E (n = 30) clades (Fig. 4B). Hexon sequences (n = 113) likewise grouped into FAdV-A (n = 8), FAdV-B (n = 23), FAdV-C (n = 10), FAdV-D (n = 36), and FAdV -E (n = 36) clades (Fig. 4C). Besides, the hexon phylogeny not only reproduced these species‐level clusters but also resolved seven individual serotypes, FAdV-11 (33/113, 29.2 %), FAdV-8b (26/113, 23.0 %), FAdV-5 (23/113, 20.4 %), FAdV-8a (10/113, 8.8 %), FAdV-4 (10/113, 8.8 %), FAdV-1 (8/113, 7.1 %), and FAdV-2 (3/113, 2.7 %). Discussion

FAdV is ubiquitous and can be isolated from clinically healthy flocks, often as single or mixed infections involving multiple serotypes. Despite its prevalence, virulence varies considerably both among serotypes and even among strains within the same serotype (Grafl et al., 2022). Moreover, co-infection with immunosuppressive viruses, such as IBDV, CIAV, and REV, has been shown to exacerbate clinical symptoms, higher mortality, more severe tissue lesions, and higher biochemical index levels (Zhai et al., 2019; Xu et al., 2021). Thereby, rapid and accurate detection of FAdV is crucial for effective disease control in poultry.

Traditional serological assays, including enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), agar gel precipitation test (AGPT), and virus neutralization test (VNT), have been widely employed for FAdV surveillance but suffer from significant limitations. ELISA and AGPT is laborious and time-consuming to prepare viral antigens, and commercially available ELISA kits often exhibit insufficient specificity to distinguish all five FAdV species reliably. AGPT is simple and low-cost but has low sensitivity, making it difficult to detect early or low-level infections. VNT, superior in sensitivity and specificity, is labor‐intensive, costly, and is not feasible for large-scale detection of VN (Zhai et al., 2019; Pan et al., 2020; Guo et al., 2022). Molecular diagnostics, particularly conventional and real-time PCR, provide high sensitivity for detecting low-copy viral genomes, yet most PCR protocols target a single gene. Here, we developed a reliable and convenient multiplex PCR assay capable of simultaneous detection of all five FAdV species.

To boost the sensitivity of the multiplex PCR assay, increasing the concentrations of the penton‐base primers for FAdV-A and the fiber primers for FAdV-B enhanced amplification even at low template levels. Optimal conditions, 11 pmol/µL for FAdV-A and -B primers, 8 pmol/µL for FAdV-C, -D, and -E primers, and an annealing temperature of 60°C, yielded a detection limit of 25 copies/tube. Under these conditions, detection limits for four of the five plasmids were identical to those obtained with singleplex PCR assays. Only pTOP-FAdV-8b showed a fivefold lower sensitivity in multiplex PCR assay compared to singleplex PCR assay.

To validate the reliability of the multiplex PCR assay, 125 strains from 79 naturally FAdV-infected cases were tested in parallel using multiplex and singleplex PCR assays, with both methods yielding 100 % concordant results. At the infection level, 42 of 79 (53.2 %) harbored single-species infections; 29 (36.7 %) had dual-species co-infections; seven (8.9 %) had triple-species infections; and one (1.3 %) had a quadruple-species infection. The most common pattern was mixed FAdV-D/- E co-infections (16/79, 20.3 %), followed by single FAdV-E (14/79, 17.7 %), single FAdV-D (12/79, 15.2 %), single FAdV-B (9/79, 11.4 %), and mixed FAdV-B/-E co-infections (6/79, 7.6 %). At the strain level, FAdV-E was most prevalent (43/125, 34.4 %), followed by FAdV-D (39/125, 31.2 %), FAdV-B (25/125, 20.0 %), FAdV-C (10/125, 8.0 %), and FAdV-A (8/125, 6.4 %). For phylogenetic analysis, we obtained the complete sequences of fiber (n = 102), penton base (n = 105) and hexon (n = 113) genes from 117 of the 125 field strains. Phylogenetic trees based on fiber, penton base, and hexon grouped all isolates strictly by species, exactly matching the multiplex PCR results. Importantly, dual- and triple-species infections comprised 45.6 % of cases (36.7 % and 8.9 %, respectively), indicating a high prevalence of mixed infections in field samples. Intra-species nucleotide similarity ranges were as follows. For fiber, FAdV-A 98.3-100.0 %, FAdV-B 96.5-100.0 %, FAdV-C 91.3-100.0 %, FAdV-D 94.4-100.0 %, and FAdV-E 76.6-100.0 %. For penton base, FAdV-A 100.0 %, FAdV-B 99.9-100.0 %, FAdV-C 99.4-100.0 %, FAdV-D 98.9-100.0 %, and FAdV-E 97.7-100.0 %. For hexon, FAdV-A 99.9-100.0 %, FAdV-B 99.8-100.0 %, FAdV-C 97.6-100.0 %, FAdV-D 97.3-100.0 %, and FAdV-E 91.9-100.0 % (Supplementary Fig. S3A-C). Inter-species divergence followed the order fiber > penton base > hexon (Supplementary Table 3), indicating that fiber and penton base exhibit greater overall sequence divergence between species and are therefore well suited for robust species-level discrimination in the multiplex assay. By contrast, hexon contains hypervariable regions that provide superior resolution at the serotype level despite its lower overall inter-species divergence (Sohaimi and Hair-Bejo., 2021). Collectively, these findings indicate that the species-specific primer sets employed here reliably amplify and discriminate all five FAdV species in both single and mixed infections.

Mixed infections with multiple FAdV species often drive homologous recombination at the intraspecies level, particularly within the type-rich FAdV-D and -E, resulting in alterations to critical capsid proteins like fiber and hexon (Meulemans et al., 2001; Schachner et al., 2018; Chen et al., 2019; Liu et al., 2021; Li et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2024). Such recombinants have been linked to increased disease severity and enhanced immune evasion (Schachner et al., 2019), highlighting the necessity for comprehensive surveillance of FAdV species. Our single‐tube multiplex PCR assay meets this need by accurately, rapidly, and reliably detecting all five FAdV species in a single reaction, significantly reducing reagent consumption, labor, and time.

In Korea, the pathogenic potential of mixed infections remains poorly understood, therefore, future studies will evaluate the virulence of mixed infections using Korean strains. Concurrently, we will focus on identifying highly conserved gene regions and designing novel serotype-specific PCR assays to enable precise differentiation of individual FAdV serotypes.

In conclusion, this is the first multiplex PCR assay capable of simultaneously identifying all five FAdV species, offering a scalable and cost‐effective tool for surveillance, prevention, and control of adenoviral diseases in poultry.

Oncologic outcomes of patients with negative nodal involvement on preoperative imaging undergoing surgery for gastric cancer
Authors: Zhizhong Xiong, Jia Bo Zheng, Bin Zhong, Huashe Wang, Jintuan Huang, Lei Lian
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPreoperative assessment of lymph node status is crucial in guiding treatment decisions for gastric cancer (GC) patients. However, it may not always accurately detect occult lymph node metastasis (OLNM). Identifying patients at high risk for OLNM despite negative preoperative imaging (cN0) is critical for optimizing treatment strategies, particularly the consideration of neoadjuvant therapy or the extent of lymphadenectomy.MethodsGC patients with cTanyN0M0 who underwent upfront surgery at the Sixth Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University from January 2008 to May 2023 were analyzed. Patients were stratified by postoperative pathological nodal involvement, and clinicopathological characteristics were compared, and a nomogram was constructed.ResultsA total of 722 patients were reviewed, 261 (36.15%) patients had postoperative pathologic lymph node involvement (pN+). The 5-year overall survival in the pN0 group and pN + group was 97.8% and 84.85% (p < 0.01), respectively. Multivariate analysis identified several variables associated with pN+, including body mass index (BMI) < 18.5 kg/m2 (OR 2.15, 95% CI 1.02–3.93, p < 0.01), serum carbohydrate antigen (CA)-199 (OR 2.51, 95% CI 1.31–4.95, p < 0.01), T2 staging (OR 4.71, 95% CI 2.70–8.54, p < 0.01), T3 staging (OR 8.0, 95% CI 4.71–13.99, p < 0.01), T4 staging (OR 15.81, 95% CI 6.39–41.45, p < 0.01), and poorly differentiated tumor (OR 3.02, 95% CI 1.46–6.79, p < 0.01). A nomogram constructed using those variables showed good discriminatory performance (AUC = 0.729) in predicting OLNM in GC.ConclusionBMI, CA-199, T stage, and differentiation are associated with OLNM in GC. A nomogram predicting OLNM could help physicians in selecting optimal treatment strategies for GC patients. However, the model has not undergone external validation, and its clinical use should await confirmation in multicenter, prospective cohorts.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12957-025-04042-3.Keywords: Gastric cancer, Occult lymph nodes metastasis, Nomogram, Clinical prediction tools

Background

Gastric cancer (GC) remains a major global health concern, with significant morbidity and mortality rates worldwide. In 2020, GC was the fifth commonest cancer and the fourth leading cause of cancer-related deaths [1–3]. Lymph node metastasis is a critical prognostic factor for survival outcomes in patients of GC, as it represents one of the primary pathways for GC cells to spread from the primary tumor to other parts of the body [4–6]. The extent of lymph node involvement is frequently employed to stage the disease, and a greater percentage of metastatic lymph nodes is linked with worse outcomes [7–9]. Therefore, A precise assessment of lymph node status is essential for determination of appropriate treatment plan in patients with GC.

According to current clinical guidelines, contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) in the initial workup is recommended when GC is suspected. The use of CT imaging allows for a detailed visualization of the disease state, enabling assessment of the tumor stage, identification of potential positive lymph node metastasis, and identification of distant metastasis. Therefore, this preoperative CT evaluation plays a pivotal role in the therapeutic approach, ensuring that patients receive personalized treatments that align with the disease-specific stage [10, 11]. However, in clinical practice the pre-operative assessment of lymph node metastasis may not consistently align with the post-operative lymph node status according to histopathological tests due to lack in accuracy and sensitivity of CT imaging to lymph node involvement with reported accuracy and sensitivity of CT for N staging range from 63.8 to 64% and 43.3–55% respectively[12–15]. Occult lymph node metastasis (OLNM) is defined as pathologically confirmed lymph node involvement in patients who were clinically staged as N0 preoperatively, based on imaging modalities such as CT and PET/CT[16, 17], and studies have reported that the incidence of OLNM in GC varies between 11% and 56%[18, 19].

The precise evaluation of lymph node status prior to surgery is essential in the decision-making process when treating GC patients. The presence of OLNM might seriously affect patient treatment and prognosis; therefore, many researchers have focused on finding clinicopathological characteristics such as degree of differentiation, tumor size, and tumor serum or biological markers that could correlate with OLNM [17–21]. However, these studies either have not reached a consensus on which clinicopathological characteristics that could predict OLNM, or focused on more advanced imaging techniques or biological markers that are extremely challenging to assess preoperatively [16, 17, 22–24].

Therefore, this study aimed to evaluate the outcomes of GC patients with negative nodal involvement on preoperative imaging who underwent surgery, and to create a validated nomogram for predicting OLNM. This tool is intended to assist clinicians in identifying high-risk patients who may benefit from neoadjuvant therapy or more extensive lymph node dissection, thereby supporting individualized treatment planning.

Materials and methods

Patients

In this retrospective analysis, clinical and pathological data of gastric cancer patients diagnosed with cTanyN0M0 who underwent upfront surgical treatment from January 2008 to May 2023 were retrieved from the database of the Sixth Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University. All procedures followed were in accordance with the ethical standards of the responsible committee on human experimentation (institutional and national) and with the Helsinki Declaration of 1964 and later versions. This research protocol was approved by the Sixth Affiliated Hospital’s Ethics Committee, with informed consent waived (2025ZSLYEC-047). This study was reported in accordance with the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

As shown in Fig. 1, the patients in this study did not receive neoadjuvant treatment (chemotherapy, radiotherapy, etc.) and were diagnosed with gastric adenocarcinoma by postoperative pathology. Patients with other malignant tumors other than gastric cancer, residual gastric cancer, and gastric cancer patients with missing clinicopathological data were excluded. All patients who met the inclusion and exclusion criteria were subsequently separated into two groups for further analysis: the pathological lymph node-negative (pN0) group and the pathological lymph node-positive (pN+) group.

Data collection

This study included the following clinicopathological indicators: age, sex, smoking history, family history of tumor, body mass index (BMI), preoperative hematological parameters, preoperative T stage, tumor location and the degree of differentiation. Specifically, the preoperative hematological parameters included total protein, albumin, hemoglobin, platelet count, white blood cell count, carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA), cancer antigen (CA) 19 − 9, CA125, CA153, and alpha-fetoprotein (AFP). Preoperative T stage and tumor location were determined radiographically and by esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD). The degree of differentiation of GC (well differentiated, moderately differentiated, and poorly differentiated) is determined based on the biopsy report of preoperative EGD. All indicators are the results of the last examination before the patient underwent surgery if multiple results were present. Patient follow-up was conducted every 3 months for the first 2 years postoperatively, every 6 months between years 2 and 5, and annually thereafter. Follow-up duration was defined as the interval from surgery to the final follow-up date or death. Overall survival (OS) was defined as the time from surgery to death from any cause.

Radiologic evaluation

All enrolled patients underwent CT for preoperative staging. Lymph node metastasis was assessed based on both nodal size and morphological features. Lymph nodes with a short-axis diameter ≥ 10 mm and round or oval shape were considered radiologically suspicious for metastasis [25, 26]. Ancillary features suggesting malignancy included heterogeneous or strong enhancement, irregular contour, short-to-long axis ratio >0.7, and clustered distribution [27].

CT images were reviewed independently by two experienced gastrointestinal radiologists who were blinded to the pathological outcomes. In cases where interpretations differed, a third senior radiologist joined to reach consensus. Only patients whose lymph nodes did not meet any of these criteria were classified as having clinical N0 (cN0) status and included in this analysis.

Pathological assessment of lymph node metastasis

All resected specimens were examined according to the 8th edition of the AJCC TNM classification. Regional lymph nodes were carefully dissected from the perigastric fat and grouped according to anatomical stations. At least 16 lymph nodes were retrieved for adequate staging in each surgical patient [25]. Lymph nodes ≤ 5 mm in diameter were entirely embedded, whereas larger nodes were bisected along the maximal diameter. A single hematoxylin–eosin–stained section from the largest cut surface was routinely examined. Metastasis was defined as the presence of carcinoma cells within the nodal parenchyma. Suspicious or equivocal cases were further evaluated with additional sections or immunohistochemistry. The number of examined and metastatic lymph nodes was recorded for each case.

Statistical analysis

Categorical baseline characteristics were compared between the pN0 and pN + groups using the Chi-squared test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression were then performed to determine independent risk factors for occult nodal metastasis in GC patients with cTanyN0M0. The final model was demonstrated via a nomogram, and the performance of the nomogram was evaluated using the calibration curve and the area under the receiver operating characteristics (ROC) curve. Variables demonstrating statistical significance in univariate analysis (P < 0.05) were included in the multivariate logistic regression model. Survival curves were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method. All statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.1.2).

Results

Basic characteristics of the study population

This study had 722 patients in all. All patients had no apparent positive lymph nodes in their preoperative CT imaging scans. Patients with histologically confirmed lymph node metastasis on final postoperative pathology were classified into the OLNM group. The overall incidence of OLNM was 36.15% (n = 261 patients). Patient demographics and clinical characteristics are summarized in Table 1. The median age was 59 years old, and the average BMI was 22.6 kg/m2. Patients with OLMN had lower BMI, more advanced T stage, poorer differentiation and higher CA19-9.

Survival outcomes

Follow-up data from 722 patients were collected, Follow-up time ranged from 1 to 146 months. The 5-year overall survival was significantly higher in the pN0 group (97.8%) than in the pN + group (84.85%; p < 0.01). The Kaplan-Meier curve is shown in Fig. 2. In addition, both univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analyses were performed to further evaluate prognostic factors. In the multivariate model, CA125 (HR = 6.41, 95% CI: 1.50–27.41, p = 0.01), CA153 (HR = 51.31, 95% CI: 5.94–443.57, p < 0.01), T stage (HR = 1.86, 95% CI: 1.06–3.28, p = 0.03), and OLNM (HR = 5.00, 95% CI: 2.10 −11.92, p < 0.01) were identified as independent prognostic factors (Supplementary Tables 1 and 2).

Creation and validation of the nomogram

A nomogram incorporating the identified independent risk factors (CA19-9 levels, BMI, tumor differentiation, and T stage) was developed (Fig. 3). The calibration plot (Fig. 4a) demonstrated good agreement between predicted and observed OLNM probabilities. The ROC curve is shown in Fig. 4b, where a ROC curve shows the relationship between sensitivity and specificity. The ROC curve yielded an AUC of 0.729, reflecting moderate discrimination ability. Besides, decision curve analysis (DCA) also showed a strong clinical practicability.

Discussion

In this retrospective study, patients with OLNM were found to have a poorer prognosis relative to non-OLNM. Thus, our objective was to identify clinical characteristics associated with OLNM in gastric cancer of any T stage and no lymph node involvement identified preoperatively, and developed a nomogram based on the clinical characteristics identified by our analysis to predict OLNM in this selected group of gastric cancer patients. Our analysis revealed that BMI, serum CA-199, T stage, and tumor differentiation were independent prognostic indicators of OLNM in GC, and when integrated together in a nomogram, the nomogram enabled preoperative prediction of OLNM as evidenced by our area under ROC curve and calibration plot.

Our finding regarding the significance of serum CA199, tumor T stage, and differentiation levels in predicting OLNM is consistent with other studies. Zhao et al. identified CA199, along with other factors such as tumor size and depth of invasion, as independent risk factors for lymph node metastasis in GC, further emphasizing the role of tumor markers in assessing lymph node status preoperatively [28]. Other researchers also utilized various clinical parameters to develop a nomogram and found that factors such as gender, CEA, CA19-9, histologic differentiation grade, and lymphovascular emboli were significant predictors of LNM in early GC following endoscopic submucosal dissection [20–30]. Both studies focused on early gastric cancer, but our study included patients of all T stages that underwent surgical resection; moreover, lymphovascular emboli status or depth of invasion could not be obtained preoperatively for all patients, and we decided to exclude both characteristics in our study.

According to the World Health Organization, a BMI of 18.5–24.9 kg/m² is considered normal, ≥ 25 kg/m² is overweight, and ≥ 30 kg/m² is obese. However, these cutoffs may not be optimal for Asian populations due to generally smaller body frames. Within Asia, Chinese individuals have been suggested to have an even lower optimal BMI upper limit compared to the broader Asian reference. Therefore, this study classified BMI into the following categories based on the consensus of Chinese experts: < 18.5 kg/m², 18.5–23.9 kg/m², and ≥ 24 kg/m². Further research revealed that unlike previous models, our nomogram uniquely incorporated BMI as a predictor. We demonstrated that decreased BMI was inversely associated with OLNM in gastric cancer patients. This association between BMI and OLNM found in our study aligns with the broader understanding of the impact of malnutrition on gastric cancer. Kosuga et al. showed that poor nutritional status, more specifically a prognostic nutritional index (PNI) < 46 could predict LNM in GC with high specificity [31]. Malnutrition, which can be sometimes reflected in the simplest way by a low BMI, has often been associated with a worse prognosis and higher mortality [32, 33]. There are also reports of increased risk of LNM in breast cancer, prostate cancer, and endometrial cancer; however, a connection between low body mass index and lymph node metastasis in gastric cancer has rarely been intuited [34–36]. We propose that lower body mass index and malnutrition could exacerbate the alterations in the tumor microenvironment and immune cells activity, and that this weakened immune state could facilitate the occult spread of cancer cells to neighboring lymph nodes.

In other cancer types, several immunohistochemical biological markers have been proposed to be useful in predicting OLNM, for example staining of specific lymphatic endothelial cells nuclear marker PROX1 strongly correlated with OLNM in squamous cell carcinoma of the oropharynx, and ecotropic viral intergation site 1 (EVI1) immunohistochemistry staining could be a biomarker to predict OLNM in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma [37, 38]. Immunohistochemical and real-time PCR analysis of tongue tumors with OLNM showed overexpression of matrix metalloproteinase MMP10 or CASC18[41, 42]. Further investigation of possible new biomarkers should be carried out in gastric cancer to find more sensitive and specific predictors of OLNM in GC. In addition, recent evidence highlights signet ring cell carcinoma (SRCC) as a biologically distinct entity with unique metastatic patterns. The proportion of signet ring cells (SRC) is recognized as a continuous prognostic variable, where a >10% threshold defines clinically relevant SRCC phenotypes associated with higher occult nodal metastasis risk and attenuated chemotherapy response [41, 42]. While our cohort lacked granular SRC quantification, future iterations of the nomogram may integrate histotype-specific variables to refine risk stratification.

The presence of OLNM in GC discovered post-operatively has been consistently associated to a worse prognosis across various studies, this correlation is further underscored by our findings, which demonstrate a worse prognosis among gastric cancer patients in the pN(+) group [18, 19, 43]. One notable study reached an interesting conclusion: in GC patients with OLNM, D2 lymph node dissection could eliminate the negative impact on prognosis [43–45]. These observations highlight the potential clinical value of identifying OLNM before surgery. Therefore, if OLNM could be accurately predicted using our nomogram, patients could be stratified into high risk OLNM GC patients, where this stratification could enable targeted interventions such as more rigorous D2 lymphadenectomy and preventive neoadjuvant therapy potentially improving the long-term outcomes of GC patients with OLNM. Incorporating such a predictive approach into preoperative planning could optimize surgical decision-making, reduce the likelihood of undertreatment, and ultimately improve long-term outcomes in this subset of patients. Moreover, early risk identification might also support tailored perioperative management strategies, including closer postoperative surveillance and timely adjuvant therapy adjustments, thereby further enhancing patient prognosis.

However, our study has several limitations. First, as a retrospective analysis, it is subject to inherent selection and information biases. Although all consecutive patients meeting the inclusion criteria were analyzed, potential referral bias cannot be excluded, as our institution is a tertiary referral center for GC in Southern China. Similarly, treatment selection bias may exist, as preoperative imaging interpretation, surgical decision-making, and perioperative management were performed according to our institutional protocols, which may not reflect practices in other settings. Second, the study was conducted in a single high-volume center located in the southern part of China, and all patients were of Chinese ethnicity and resided in southern provinces. This ethnic and geographic homogeneity may limit the generalizability of our findings to populations with different genetic backgrounds, dietary habits, healthcare access, or disease epidemiology. Third, while our predictive nomogram demonstrated good discrimination in our dataset, it has not undergone external validation, and its clinical utility should be confirmed in independent, multicenter cohorts that include patients from different ethnic and geographic backgrounds. Lastly, we focused exclusively on preoperative variables relevant to clinical staging. Postoperative pathological features such as Lauren classification, lymphovascular invasion, and HER2 status—although prognostically important—were not included because they are unavailable during preoperative assessment and do not directly influence the primary endpoint of preoperative nodal staging (cN0 vs. pN0). Nevertheless, their exclusion limits the ability to explore potential interactions between tumor biology, adjuvant treatment, and nodal understaging. Future multicenter, prospective studies incorporating more diverse patient populations and additional biological variables are warranted to confirm and expand on our findings.

Conclusions

In conclusion, our study has identified low BMI, elevated serum CA199, T stage and degree of differentiation as significant predictors of OLNM in GC patients and developed a predictive nomogram, which could be a valuable tool in optimizing surgical strategies and improving patient outcomes in GC.

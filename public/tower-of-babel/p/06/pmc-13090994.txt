Right atrial diverticulum on coronary CT: Imaging appearance of a remnant of the right sinus venosus
Authors: Alex Daniel Medina-Siosi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Right atrial diverticulum is a rare congenital anomaly that is most often detected incidentally on cardiac imaging. We report the case of a 51-year-old patient who underwent coronary computed tomography angiography for exclusion of coronary artery disease, in whom a small saccular outpouching measuring 6 mm was identified arising from the anterolateral wall of the right atrium. The lesion demonstrated a communicating neck and homogeneous contrast opacification identical to the atrial cavity. This characteristic location corresponds embryologically to the incorporation site of the right sinus venosus into the primitive atrium, a region recognized for structural remodeling during cardiac development. Recognition of this entity is essential to differentiate it from atrial appendage variants, aneurysms, or pericardial lesions.

Introduction

Cardiac diverticula are rare congenital outpouchings of the cardiac chambers that have been more frequently described in the ventricles than in the atria. Among atrial diverticula, those arising from the right atrium are particularly uncommon and are usually discovered incidentally during imaging performed for unrelated indications [1,2].

A notable and underemphasized feature in the few cases reported in the literature is the remarkably consistent anatomical origin of right atrial diverticula. Nearly all described lesions arise from the anterolateral free wall of the right atrium, between the superior vena cava and the right atrial appendage [1,3,4].

Despite this reproducible topographic pattern, most reports describe the finding as an incidental anatomical variant without further exploration of its developmental basis.

This region corresponds embryologically to the incorporation site of the right sinus venosus into the primitive atrium, an area known for structural remodeling and potential tissue vulnerability [5]. Recognition of this relationship provides a coherent explanation for the morphology and location of right atrial diverticula and highlights the importance of cardiac computed tomography (CT) in identifying and characterizing these subtle congenital anomalies, which may otherwise be misinterpreted as pericardial or atrial pathology [6,7].

The aim of this report is to describe the imaging features of a right atrial diverticulum detected on coronary CT angiography and to discuss its possible embryologic basis in correlation with existing literature.

Case presentation

A 51-year-old patient was referred for coronary CT angiography due to atypical chest pain and intermediate pre-test probability of coronary artery disease. The patient had no prior history of congenital heart disease, cardiac surgery, or known arrhythmia. Physical examination and routine laboratory tests were unremarkable.

Electrocardiography demonstrated normal sinus rhythm without evidence of atrial arrhythmia or conduction abnormalities. Transthoracic echocardiography showed normal cardiac chamber dimensions and preserved biventricular function but failed to visualize or provide additional characterization the diverticulum, likely due to its small size and limited acoustic window.

Coronary CT angiography demonstrated no significant coronary stenosis. Incidentally, a small saccular outpouching measuring 6 mm was identified arising from the anterolateral wall of the right atrium. The lesion showed smooth margins, a narrow neck, and homogeneous contrast opacification synchronous with the atrial cavity. Multiplanar reconstructions clearly demonstrated continuity between the outpouching and the right atrial lumen, confirming direct communication. The lesion was intrapericardial and contiguous with the right atrial wall on multiplanar reconstructions, without an intervening fat plane to suggest an extracardiac or pleural-based structure. No thrombus or associated pericardial abnormality was identified.

Discussion

Right atrial diverticulum is an uncommon congenital cardiac anomaly that has been described only in a limited number of case reports. In most published cases, these lesions are reported as incidental findings detected during cardiac imaging performed for unrelated indications [[1], [2], [3]]. Although generally asymptomatic, their recognition is important to avoid misinterpretation and unnecessary investigations. The currently available literature remains limited to isolated case reports and small case series, which restricts definitive conclusions regarding pathogenesis and natural history.

A notable and recurrent feature across reported cases is the consistent anatomical origin of right atrial diverticula. Nearly all described lesions arise from the anterolateral free wall of the right atrium, between the superior vena cava and the right atrial appendage [1,3,4]. This reproducible topographic pattern raises the possibility that this entity may not represent a random atrial outpouching, but rather a structurally predisposed region of the atrial wall.

Embryologically, this specific area corresponds to the incorporation site of the right sinus venosus into the primitive atrium during cardiac development [5]. The remodeling process that occurs during this incorporation has been described as involving structural reorganization of the atrial wall. While a direct causal relationship cannot be established from isolated case reports, the consistent location of right atrial diverticula in this region may suggest a developmental basis for their formation. This hypothesis provides a potential explanation for the observed morphology, including their smooth walls, saccular configuration, and absence of trabeculations typically seen in accessory appendages.

From an imaging perspective, cardiac computed tomography plays a central role in the detection and characterization of atrial diverticula due to its high spatial resolution and multiplanar capabilities. Morphologic features proposed to differentiate atrial diverticula from accessory appendages and aneurysms on CT have been described in prior radiologic studies [8]. These lesions typically appear as communicating saccular outpouchings arising from the atrial wall with homogeneous contrast opacification identical to the atrial lumen. However, the morphology of the communicating neck has been reported as variable in the literature, with some authors describing broad-based ostia and others reporting narrower connections. This variability underscores the absence of universally accepted imaging criteria and highlights the importance of assessing direct communication and contrast behavior rather than relying solely on neck morphology.

Radiologic reviews emphasize the frequent confusion between diverticula, aneurysms, accessory appendages, and pericardial cysts [6,7]. Right atrial aneurysms generally demonstrate progressive dilation and a wider communication with the atrial cavity. Accessory appendages are characterized by trabeculated walls and typical appendage location, while pericardial cysts lack contrast opacification and do not communicate with the atrial lumen. Although most right atrial diverticula are clinically silent, electrophysiological associations have been described. Cases of atrial tachycardia and accessory pathways originating from right atrial diverticula have been reported [3,4]. These observations suggest that, in selected patients, this region may have functional relevance beyond its morphological appearance. Nevertheless, in small asymptomatic lesions such as in the present case, conservative management with clinical follow-up is generally appropriate.

Histologic descriptions from surgically resected diverticula have demonstrated walls composed predominantly of fibrous tissue with reduced or absent normal muscular layer [2,9]. While these findings are limited to larger or symptomatic cases requiring surgery, they may support the concept of a congenital structural variation rather than acquired dilation.

Overall, recognition of right atrial diverticulum on cardiac CT is important to ensure correct diagnosis, avoid misclassification as pericardial or atrial pathology, and provide appropriate clinical reassurance in asymptomatic patients.

Conclusion

Right atrial diverticulum is an uncommon congenital anomaly that may be incidentally detected on coronary computed tomography angiography. Recognition of its characteristic location along the anterolateral wall of the right atrium and its direct communication with the atrial cavity is essential to differentiate it from aneurysms, accessory appendages, and pericardial lesions. The recurrent topographic pattern reported in the literature may suggest a developmental basis related to the incorporation of the right sinus venosus. In asymptomatic patients, conservative management is generally appropriate.

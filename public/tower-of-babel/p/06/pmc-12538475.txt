Cannulated screw with modified augmented c-wire fixation is effective for various types of complex patellar fractures: An evidence-based case series
Authors: Muhammad Ade Junaidi, Anissa Feby Canintika, Rendy Dwi Osca, Filberto Budhy, Bany Faris Amin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThe current standard surgical approach for treating displaced transverse or simple patellar fractures is the tension band wiring technique with Kirschner wires. The use of cannulated screw with modified augmented cerclage wire (c-wire) has a potential to be an alternative use for this fixation especially in complex cases.Presentation of caseThis case series reported four patients with patellar fractures treated with cannulated screws and modified augmented c-wire with a follow-up for at least twelve months of follow-up. There were two cases of two-part transverse fractures, one case of non-union, and one case of comminuted fracture. Most patients had union, with variable functional outcomes, no failures of reduction, and no short term complication were observed.DiscussionBiomechanical studies have shown the superiority of the cannulated screw with modified augmented c-wire fixation to k-wire fixation in transverse and comminuted fracture. Cannulated screw fixation also has a lower rate of implant removal and higher functional outcome compared to K-wire fixation.ConclusionCannulated screws combined with modified augmented c-wire are a viable option for various types of patellar fractures. This method can be effectively used for transverse, comminuted, and non-union patellar fractures.Keywords: Cannulated screws, Case series, c-Wire, Patellar fracture, Tension band wiring

Introduction

Patellar fractures represent 1 % of all fractures [1]. Surgical intervention is performed in one-third of patellar fractures [2]. The current standard approach for treating displaced patellar fractures is the tension band wiring technique. This technique typically uses cerclage wiring (c-wire) in combination with Kirschner wires (K-wires) to stabilize the fracture [3]. However, tension band wiring is associated with complications in 18–50 % of patients; this includes early complications, such as implant failure and infection, and late complications, such as removal of symptomatic hardware [2,4].

Recent studies have introduced cannulated screws as a viable alternative over conventional tension band wiring [5,6]. Studies indicate that cannulated screws provide better resistance to tensile loading during knee flexion compared to K-wires, and patients treated with this technique are less likely to need follow-up surgery to remove the implant [[6], [7], [8]]. In this case series, we aim to report the clinical outcomes of four cases of patellar fracture treated with cannulated screws with modified augmented c-wire. This case series is in concordance with the PROCESS guideline [9].

Methods

We retrospectively collected patients age > 18 years old with patellar fractures treated with cannulated screws and modified augmented c-wire from January 2023 – June 2024 at a single center with a minimum of twelve months of follow-up. We classified the fracture based on AO/OTA fracture classification. We observe post-operative functional outcomes with Lysholm Knee Score [10] and International Knee Documentation Committee (IKDC) [11], range of motion (ROM), and complications following surgery. A total of four patients were included in this case series. The summary of the cases is presented in Table 1.

Surgical technique

Under spinal analgesia, the patients were placed on the surgical table in a supine position with their knees fully extended. A pneumatic tourniquet was inflated on the upper thigh to prevent bleeding during the procedure. A longitudinal incision was performed along the midline of the patella until the fracture segments were visualized. A reduction clamp was then used to reduce the bones, and one or two K-wires were placed perpendicular to the fracture line to stabilize them temporarily. Fully threaded cannulated screws then inserted through the K-wires. An anterior modified augmented c-wire fixation was then placed. The c-wire acts as a tension band to help compress the fracture [3]. In cases of avulsion, an additional screw may be inserted into the tibial tuberosity to stabilize the c-wire for further fixation (Table 2).

The stability of the reduction and fixation was assessed using a C-arm during knee flexion and extension. Subcutaneous tissue and skin were then sutured, and a brace was applied with the knee in full extension.

Postoperatively, the patient was allowed to weight-bear as tolerated with the knee locked in extension with a brace for six weeks. During weeks 0–2 knee flexion was limited to 30°. From weeks 2–6 flexion was progressively increased to a target of 90° by week 6. At week 6 full weight-bearing was allowed and the brace was removed at week 10.

Case 1

A 31-year-old male sustained a motorbike accident, where he collided with a concrete. After the incident, the patient complained of pain in the left knee and leg. Physical examination of the left knee shows swelling and deformity of the knee with limited knee ROM (Fig. 1A). Left knee X-ray shows complete articular multi-fragmented fracture (AO 34-C3) (Fig. 1B). The patient was diagnosed with closed left patellar fracture was planned for internal fixation.

Case 1. (A) Physical examination shows limited knee range of motion; (B) intra-operative cannulated screw insertion with C-Arm assistance; (C) postoperative physical examination; (D) anterior tension band wiring insertion; (E) radiological examination shows multifragmented fracture of the left patella; (F) post-operative X-ray.

We performed a fixation using cannulated screws with modified augmented c-wire on this patient (Fig. 1D and E). Post-operatively, the patient does not regularly attend rehabilitation and for 15 months post-operatively, the patient has achieved 0–60° knee ROM but achieved a good functional outcome (Fig. 1C).

Case 2

A 56-year-old male presented after a fall from the third floor, approximately 12 m high. He felt headfirst and then the rest of body struck the ground. An X-ray and CT scan examination, which revealed closed fractures of the right hip, left distal radius, left clavicle and right patella (Fig. 2A). After the patient was stable on the seventh day of hospitalization, we performed surgery of the right patella with an internal fixation with cannulated screw augmented with c-wire.

Case 2. (A) Preoperative X-ray shows a simple complete transverse fracture; (B) intra-operative cannulated screw insertion and c-wire insertion; (C) post-operative X-ray; (D) post-operative physical examination.

We performed a fixation using cannulated screws with modified augmented c-wire on this patient. (Fig. 2B) Post-operative X-rays showed that the fracture was adequately reduced (Fig. 2C). Fourteen months post-operatively, the patient can ambulate with a four-leg walker due to pain when walking with his right leg mainly on the right hip. The patient also did not attend rehabilitation which resulted in the patient achieving only 40° knee flexion (Fig. 2D).

Case 3

A 48-year-old male presented with a history of patellar fracture of the right knee since February 2018 due to falling to his right knee on the floor (Fig. 3A). Following the incident, the patient went to alternative treatment for one month, but there was no improvement. March 2022, the patient continued to experience difficulties in bending his knee, so he underwent open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF) using a claw plate. However, up until February 2023 (Fig. 3B), the patient reported no significant improvement and was reoperated using tension bone wiring with bone allograft. January 2024, the patient still complains of pain while walking, which intensifies when climbing stairs. Radiological examination shows a non-union of the right patella (Fig. 3C). The patient is planned for cannulated screw fixation with c-wire augmentation.

Case 3. (A) X-ray shows a multifragmented patellar fracture; (B) X-ray shows non-union on the patellar fracture after claw plate fixation; (C) X-ray shows a widening of fracture gap after tension band wiring fixation (D) post-operative X-ray; (E) intraoperative cannulated screw insertion (F) anterior tension c-wire insertion and bone graft application; (G) post-operative physical examination shows adequate flexion and extension.

We performed a fixation using cannulated screws with modified augmented c-wire on this patient with a screw as an anchor in the tibial tuberosity to anchor the c-wire during fixation. Additionally, bone grafting was performed to address the bone defect (Fig. 3E and F). Post-operative X-rays indicated that the fracture was adequately reduced (Fig. 3D). Sixteen months post-operatively, the patient is free of pain during daily activities and has achieved union; however, he still experiences limitations when engaging in strenuous activities and sports.

Case 4

A 67-year-old female presented with pain on the left knee after sustaining a fall in the bathroom one month prior. Physical examination of the left knee revealed a patellar gap with no obvious deformity and the patient's knee ROM was limited (Fig. 4A). X-ray shows a fracture and avulsion of the lower pole of the patella (Fig. 4E). The patient was planned for internal fixation with cannulated screw.

Case 4. (A) Clinical presentation; (B) intraoperative cannulated screw insertion (C) anterior c-wire insertion; (D) post-operative physical examination shows normal knee ROM; (E) X-ray shows patellar transverse fracture with avulsion; (F) post-operative X-ray.

We performed a fixation using cannulated screws with modified augmented c-wire on this patient with a screw as an anchor in the tibial tuberosity to anchor the c-wire during fixation and bone graft application (Fig. 4B and C). Post-operative X-rays demonstrated that the fracture was sufficiently reduced (Fig. 4F). At thirteen months follow up, the patient had good functional outcome and achieved union with normal ROM. Literature review

A total of 17 studies analyzed outcomes with cannulated screws with tension band wiring with 12 of them comparing with traditional tension band wiring with K-wire and two comparing with plate osteosynthesis. Studies included shows a better outcome in patients that underwent cannulated screw compared to traditional tension band wiring [6,7,[12], [13], [14], [15], [16], [17], [18]]. Cannulated screw with tension band wiring when compared to ring pin and tension band wiring shows mixed results. Zhu XZ et al. [14] show better outcomes in the ring pin group. Meanwhile, Liu J et al. [19] show better outcomes in cannulated screw group. Cannulated screw with tension band wiring also shows a similar outcome when compared with adjusted patella grapple [20]. Neumann-Langen MV et al. [21] reported a higher complication rate in patient with cannulated screw and tension band wiring group when compared to plate osteosynthesis but a lower implant removal rate. Cannulated screw with tension band wiring also shows that it is viable for transverse fracture, inferior pole fracture, and comminuted fracture [14,22,23] (Fig. 5).

Discussion

In this case series, we report the clinical outcomes of four patients following treatment with a combination of cannulated screws with modified augmented c-wire. Cannulated screw has shown to have superior biomechanical properties compared to tension band wiring with K-wire. Carpenter et al. [24] performed a cadaveric biomechanical study comparing two parallel interfragmentary lag screws, two cannulated lag screws with a tension band wire through the center of the cannulated screws, and modified tension bands. The study found that cannulated screws and a tension band wire increase stability compared to the other fixation. Similarly, Thelen et al. [25] performed a cadaveric study to investigate the biomechanics of multi-fragmented patellar fractures and found higher tensile load and lesser fracture gap widening in the cadaver cannulated screws and a tension band wire, although not statistically significant. Current literature also shows that patients treated with cannulated screw fixation have better functional outcomes and fewer complications than those treated with traditional tension-band wiring [6,7,[12], [13], [14], [15], [16], [17], [18]].

In our study, there were two two-part transverse fractures, one case of non-union, and one comminuted fracture. Hoshino et al. [13] compared tension band fixation with K-wires to cannulated screw fixation of 448 patellar fracture. Patient that underwent cannulated screw fixation has a lower rate of implant removal compared to K-wire fixation. Similarly, studies comparing treatments for transverse fractures have shown a lower rate of implant removal and better functional outcomes [6,26].

In Case 1, the patient has a multi-fragmented fracture and was treated with fixation with four fully threaded cannulated screw. The treatment of multi-fragmented patellar fractures is mainly managed with tension band wiring [27]. A study by Zhu et al. [14] demonstrated that patients treated with a combination of cannulated screws and tension band wiring had significantly lower rates of implant removal compared to those treated with K-wire tension band wiring fixation. In this case, the patient achieved a good functional outcome and adequate ROM after 15 months.

In Case 3, the patient had non-union with a history of treatment using claw plate fixation and tension band wiring with K-wire fixation. Non-union patellar is a rare and challenging fracture to treat. Most non-union fractures are currently treated with tension band wiring with K-wire [28]. However, this approach often leads to failure in the fixation. A case series by Harna et al. [29] involving ten non-union fractures reported that all failures occurred with tension band wiring and K-wires, while no failures occurred in surgeries using cannulated screws. In this case, the ROM of the patient decreases after surgery. This finding is similar to a study by Klassen et al. that shows surgical treatment in non-union patellar fracture reduce range of motion post-operatively but increases functional outcome [30].

In the included case (Case 3 and 4), we added a third cannulated screw during fixation. This approach is different compared to other studies, which typically use only two cannulated lag screws [31]. A study by Chang et al. [32] found in a fixation using a third screw increased stability of patellar fracture in a finite element analysis by reducing the dorsal gap. A clinical study was also performed by Han et al. on a patient with transverse patella fracture with good post-operative functional outcome.

Overall, there was good functional outcome measured using Lysholm Knee Score and IKDC was achieved. Three patients had good functional outcomes when measured with Lysholm Knee Score (84–94 %), while one patient had a fair outcome (65–83 %). If measured with IKDC score three patients had fair outcome (65–83 %) and one patient with good outcome (84–94 %). There was inadequate knee range of motion in two patients, this is caused by the patient inability to attend rehabilitation session post operatively. However, limitation of the study is the relatively short follow-up (minimum twelve months). Further follow-up may change the observed patient outcomes and complication.

Conclusion

This case series demonstrates that cannulated screws combined with modified augmented c-wire is a viable option for various types of patellar fractures for either acute or chronic cases. This method can be effectively used for transverse, comminuted, and non-union patellar fractures.

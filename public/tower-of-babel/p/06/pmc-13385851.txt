Downregulation of CXCL16/ADAM10 axis by Simvastatin attenuates tacrolimus-induced tubulointerstitial fibrosis
Authors: Abdulrahman A. Alelowi, Alulu Alradhi, Ahmad H. Alhowail, Maha A. Aldubayan, Mohamed S. Abdel-Bakky
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Tacrolimus (TAC), a widely used immunosuppressive agent, is associated with significant nephrotoxicity characterized by renal inflammation and fibrosis. Simvastatin (SIM), beyond its lipid-lowering effects, exhibits pleiotropic anti-inflammatory and anti-fibrotic properties. This study aimed to explore the protective effect of SIM against TAC-induced renal injury, with a focus on the potential involvement of the CXCL16/ADAM10 signaling axis. Adult male Wistar rats were divided into four groups (n = 10 per group): control, SIM-treated (SIM; 10 mg/kg/day), TAC-treated (TAC; 2 mg/kg/day), and SIM + TAC-treated. All medications were given orally for 28 days. Hematological indices, renal function parameters, and lipid profile were assessed. Histopathological evaluation and immunofluorescence analysis of CXCL16, ADAM10, fibronectin and TGF-β were performed. TAC administration resulted in significant renal dysfunction, dyslipidemia, and marked histopathological alterations, accompanied by upregulation of CXCL16, ADAM10, and fibrotic markers. Conversely, co-administration of SIM and TAC improved renal function, attenuated lipid abnormalities, and preserved renal architecture. These effects were associated with decreased the expression of CXCL16, ADAM10, TGF-β and fibronectin. SIM demonstrates significant kidney-protective properties against TAC-induced renal injury, potentially involving modulation of the CXCL16/ADAM10 signaling pathway. These findings indicate that SIM could be a beneficial supplementary treatment to alleviate the serious kidney-damaging effects associated with TAC.

Introduction

Tacrolimus (TAC) is one of effective immunosuppressant extensively employed to prevent rejection of kidney, liver, and heart allografts1. Its primary mechanism involves blocking calcineurin, a phosphatase essential for nuclear factor of activated T-cells (NFAT) nuclear translocation, which subsequently prevents interleukin-2 (IL-2) transcription and T-cell proliferation2. TAC-induced renal toxicity including tubular degradation, inflammatory cell recruitment, blood vessel changes, glomerulosclerosis, and tubulointerstitial fibrosis (TIF), which may cause permanent kidney dysfunction3. TIF is characterized by accumulation of extracellular matrix (ECM), immune cell recruitment and abnormal growth and activation of myofibroblasts, leading to the loss of functional tubular structure4. TAC promotes ECM accumulation by stimulating profibrotic signals mainly through the upregulation of TGF-β5–8. An extensive analysis of 5,538 cases revealed that 44.5% of TAC-related kidney damage required hospital admission, emphasizing the considerable clinical impact of its kidney toxicity9. Chronic TAC exposure causes TIF through different mechanism, particularly epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) initiated by TGF-β/Smad signaling, and increases the expression of fibrotic markers like fibronectin10,11. Furthermore, TGF-β receptor stimulation promotes fibroblast-to-myofibroblast transition (FMT), causing excessive accumulation of collagen and connective tissue growth factor (CTGF)12.

CXCL16 is a characteristic chemokine that exists in both soluble and membrane-bound configurations. The membrane form serves as an adhesion molecule for cells expressing CXCR6 receptors, such as T cells and myeloid fibroblasts13,14. The soluble form generated from enzymatic cleavage by the metalloproteinase ADAM10, acts as a potent chemoattractant15. The severity of fibrosis in various kidney diseases have been correlated with the elevated levels of renal CXCL1616–18 Moreover, recent evidence demonstrates that ADAM10/CXCL16 axis mediates renal fibrosis by promoting fibroblast activation and ECM deposition19.

Statins, HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors, are mainly prescribed for management of hyperlipidemia but exhibit significant pleiotropic effects, including anti-inflammatory and antioxidant activities20. Simvastatin (SIM) has demonstrated protective effects in various models of renal disease by controlling oxidative stress and fibrotic pathways, potentially by the downregulation of ADAM10 and other metalloproteinases14.

In this study, we proposed that SIM reduces TAC-induced TIF by regulating CXCL16/ADAM10 signals, consequently decreasing inflammatory cell infiltration and ECM accumulation.

Materials and methods

Reagents and chemicals

Mouse monoclonal primary antibodies against CXCL16, ADAM10, fibronectin and TGF-β were purchased from Santa Cruz Biotechnology (TX, USA). The fluorescence-conjugated secondary antibodies, goat anti-mouse Alexa Fluor 488 and goat anti-mouse Cy3, were obtained from Invitrogen (CA, USA). SIM was purchased from Bayer AG (Leverkusen, Germany). TAC and mason trichrome staining kit were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich (MA, USA). Blood urea nitrogen (BUN) and Creatinine kits were ordered from Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc. (MA, USA). Tissues were mounted using Fluoromount-G™ (Southern Biotech, AL, USA).

Animals and experimental design

Male Wistar rats (202 ± 30 g) were obtained from the Pharmacy College animal facility, Qassim University, Saudi Arabia. Male rats were selected to minimize variability related to hormonal fluctuations associated with the estrous cycle, which may influence renal function, inflammatory responses, and fibrotic processes. Animals were kept for 5 days before starting of the experiment under standard laboratory conditions, with free access to food and water.

Rats were randomly divided to four sets (n = 10 per group). The control rats received oral normal saline for 28 days. The SIM-treated rats were given SIM in a dose of 10 mg/kg/day p.o., dissolved in normal saline, for 28 days21. The TAC-treatment rats received TAC at 2 mg/kg/day p.o., dissolved in normal saline, for 28 days. The SIM + TAC group received SIM (10 mg/kg/day orally) followed 60 min later by TAC (2 mg/kg/day orally), both dissolved in normal saline and administered once daily for 28 days. Male rats were selected to minimize variability related to hormonal fluctuations associated with the estrous cycle, which may influence renal function, inflammatory responses, and fibrotic processes.

All animal experimental procedures were approved by the Qassim University Ethical Committee (Approval No: 25-11-20). All experiments were conducted in accordance with the NIH Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals and are reported in compliance with the ARRIVE 2.0 guidelines. Animal anesthesia and euthanasia procedures were performed according to accepted veterinary best practices.

Blood and tissue collection

At the study’s conclusion, rats were euthanized using decapitation with i.p injection of ketamine 300 mg/xylazine 30 mg anesthesia22. Blood samples were obtained hematological analysis (EDTA) and biochemical testing (clot activator tubes). Kidneys were fixed in Davidson’s fixative for tissue examination and immunofluorescence analysis.

Hematological analysis

Hematological parameters were determined using an automated hematology analyzer (ABX Micros 60, HORIBA Medical, France).

Biochemical assays

Blood samples were collected, and serum was separated for analysis. Renal function parameters, including blood urea nitrogen (BUN), creatinine, and urea, were measured using standard biochemical kits. Lipid profile parameters, including total cholesterol, triglycerides, HDL, and LDL, were also assessed.

Histopathological evaluation

Kidney samples were preserved in Davidson’s solution, subsequently cut, washed, dehydrated with graded ethanol, clarified with xylene, and embedded in paraffin. Tissue slices (4 µm thickness) were stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) according to established methods23. Masson’s trichrome stains was performed following the manufacturer’ instructions. Histological evaluation was performed blindly24.

ELISA for cytokine estimation

TGF-β1 and interleukin-6 (IL-6) Levels in serum were quantified using sandwich ELISA kits (SEA079Ra, Cloud-Clone Corp., USA). Test samples, biotin-linked detection antibodies, and avidin-HRP were added to microplates pre-coated with specific antibodies. The TMB substrate was used to generate color, which was then interrupted with sulfuric acid and measured at 450 nm. The assays demonstrated high sensitivity (below 3.3 pg/mL), specificity, and reproducibility, with intra-assay variation less than 10% and inter-assay variation less than 12%.

Immunofluorescence analysis

Immunofluorescence analysis was performed following the modified method of Abdel-Bakky and Alharbi, 202525. Briefly, sections were deparaffinized and the antigens were retrieved through heating in citrate buffere (pH 6.0) for 20 min. Tissues were washed in 0.05% Tween containing PBS, methanol fixed, and blocked in 10% horse serum in 1% bovine serum albumin in PBS containing. Tissue sections were incubated with mouse monoclonal against CXCL16, ADAM10, fibronectin and TGF-β1 antibodies for 3 h in oven adjusted at 37 °C. After washing in tween/PBS, sections were incubated with goat anti-mouse conjugated with Cy3 or Alexa Fluor 488 secondary antibodies. Sections were then mounted using Fluoromount-G® (DAKO, CA, USA), and the captured images were done using Leica DM 5500B fluorescence microscope (Leica Microsystems, Wetzlar, Germany). At least six fields per section (minimum of three rats each group) were analyzed using ImageJ software (NIH, USA).

Disposal of carcasses

Dead and sacrificed animals and tissue waste were sealed in plastic bags, stored at − 20 °C, and subsequently collected by the Veterinary Hospital, Buraidah, for disposal via incineration.25

Statistical analysis

Data are denoted as mean ± SEM. *p-value < 0.05, **p-value < 0.01, ***p-value < 0.001 considered statistically significant compared to control group while #p-value < 0.05, ##p-value < 0.01, ###p-value < 0.001 considered statistically significant compared to TAC group using one-way ANOVA followed by appropriate post-ANOVA for multiple comparison.

Co-administration of SIM significantly mitigated these toxic effects, as evidenced by the partial recovery of HGB, MCH, MCHC, and PLT levels. In SIM group, there was mild but significant elevation in the kidney/body weight ratio in comparison with normal, without an elevation in serum renal markers (Table 2; Fig. 2).

Effects of TAC and SIM on inflammatory cytokine serum levels in rats

In SIM group, serum levels of the pro-fibrotic cytokine TGF-β1 and the pro-inflammatory cytokine IL-6 were significantly decreased compared to control group. In TAC treated rats, serum level of TGF-β1 was significantly increased while IL-6 unexpectedly was significantly reduced when compared to control group. SIM + TAC treatment resulted in the lowest levels of both cytokines across all groups (Fig. 4).

Effects of TAC and SIM on histopathological assessment of renal tubules in rats

Hematoxylin and Eosin (H&E) stain was used to stain renal tissue sections and examined to assess tubular epithelial changes across all experimental groups (Fig. 5). In control group, the epithelium of renal tubules exhibited normal renal architecture with no marked pathological alterations. Also, in SIM group, rats showed preserved tubular morphology, with no evidence of necrobiotic changes except in a few tubules. In TAC-treated group, there were moderate necrobiotic changes in the epithelium of renal tubules (indicated by arrows). While in TAC + SIM group, renal tissues had no significant pathological changes, comparable to the control group.

Effect of SIM and TAC on ECM in renal tissue

Renal tissues for ECM accumulation were evaluated using Masson’s trichrome stain (Fig. 6). In control group, renal tissues exhibited normal renal histology with no significant ECM deposition in the interstitial areas of renal tubules or glomeruli. Also in SIM group, it showed the same response with minimal ECM accumulation. In contrast, in TAC group, there was marked ECM deposition within the interstitial regions of renal tubules (yellow arrows) and glomeruli (red arrow). While in TAC + SIM group, ECM accumulation was reduced compared to TAC group and only mild deposition observed in both interstitial and glomerular areas.

Discussion

This study shows that SIM significantly reduces TAC-induced renal TIF in rat models, primarily by modulating the CXCL16/ADAM10 axis. The marked pancytopenia seen with TAC treatment indicates its suppressive effect on bone marrow26,27. SIM’s ability to partially restore these parameters suggests a protective effect on hematopoietic function. Although WBC counts were elevated in the SIM + TAC group, this is unlikely to indicate an adverse inflammatory response, as it was accompanied by reduced levels of pro-fibrotic and inflammatory markers and improved renal histology. Instead, this increase may reflect recovery from TAC-induced bone marrow suppression or a modulatory effect of SIM on immune cell dynamics. Nevertheless, this observation warrants further investigation to fully elucidate its underlying mechanism. These findings are in line with earlier studies reporting that chronic TAC administration leads to hematological disturbances and renal structural damage through endothelial dysfunction, oxidative stress, and inflammatory signaling activation28–30.

TAC administration caused kidneys hypertrophy and edema, which was reflected by an increase in the kidney/body weight ratio. In addition, there were significant elevations in BUN, creatinine, and urea, which is aligned with the established pathophysiology of TAC-induced nephrotoxicity that involves afferent arteriolar vasoconstriction, endothelial injury, and tubulointerstitial inflammation and fibrosis29,31,32. SIM’s kidney-protective properties were shown through decreased serum markers (BUN, creatinine) and maintenance of kidney structure.

The reduction in TGF-β1 and IL-6 in the SIM + TAC group suggests that SIM interrupts the inflammatory cascade leading to ECM deposition33. Interestingly, IL-6 levels were reduced in TAC-treated rats despite evident renal injury. This may be attributed to the immunosuppressive effect of TAC, which inhibits calcineurin-dependent T-cell activation and suppresses cytokine production. Therefore, the decrease in IL-6 likely reflects systemic immunosuppression rather than the absence of local inflammatory processes. The reduction of elevated TGF-β1 and IL-6 in SIM + TAC group indicates that SIM may interrupt the downstream processes leading to renal inflammation, ECM deposition, and fibrosis. The combined immunosuppressive effect of TAC and the anti-inflammatory and anti-fibrotic properties of SIM may be responsible for the reduced level of both TGF-β and IL-6 below control levels in the SIM + TAC group. Significantly, this reduction was linked to improved kidney parameters rather than negative structural or functional consequences, indicating a protective regulation of cytokine signaling as opposed to pathogenic suppression.

Administration of SIM alone had minimal effects on hematological parameters and serum renal markers, but it caused a mild increase in the kidney/body weight ratio and a significant reduction in systemic TGF-β1 and IL-6 compared to the control group. The selected SIM dose (10 mg/kg/day) is consistent with commonly used doses in preclinical studies21,34, taking into account the higher metabolic rate in rodents. Importantly, no signs of renal, hematological, or histopathological toxicity were observed with SIM alone in the present study, indicating good tolerability. Although TAC and SIM share CYP3A-mediated metabolism, clinical evidence suggests that TAC does not significantly increase SIM systemic exposure, in contrast to cyclosporine35. Moreover, even in settings where SIM exposure is elevated, no major adverse effects have been consistently reported. Nevertheless, potential pharmacokinetic interactions cannot be excluded and warrant further investigation in future studies. SIM’s characteristic anti-inflammatory properties, even without TAC exposure, indicate its well-recognized pleiotropic effects36,37. These results correspond with research demonstrating statin-mediated inhibition of TGF-β/Smad signaling and cytokine generation38,39. This indicates that SIM, even in the absence of an insult, has a baseline anti-inflammatory effect and may induce a mild increase in kidney weight without functional impairment.

Moreover, TAC induced abnormal lipid profiles marked by increased total cholesterol, triglycerides, and LDL, along with decreased HDL. This result is consistent with known side effects of calcineurin inhibitors like TAC and may cause an increased risk for atherosclerosis and cardiovascular disease with its use40. SIM effectively diminished TAC-associated hyperlipidemia, which may further participate to its renoprotective effect, as lipid accumulation is known to exacerbate renal inflammation and fibrosis41.

The TAC group displayed marked kidney damage, as evidenced histopathologically by kidney casts, congested blood vessel, and protein leakage into Bowman’s capsule. This indicates that TAC-induced nephrotoxicity likely disrupts normal lipid and lipoprotein metabolism, a well-established study in renal disease42. The most pivotal finding is the marked histological protection observed in the SIM + TAC group. Despite TAC exposure, renal architecture was preserved and comparable to that of the control group. This demonstrates that SIM not only mitigate the renal toxicity of TAC but also prevents its progression to tissue injury and necrosis.

In the TAC treated group, there was significant upregulation of fibronectin, TGF-β, CXCL16, and ADAM10 expression in renal tissues, consistent with its known nephrotoxic profile. Fibronectin and TGF-β are key players in ECM accumulation and fibrosis and TGF-β is also the main regulator of the FMT pathway, which leads to fibrosis in the kidneys1,10. CXCL16 facilitates macrophage and T-cell invasion, exacerbating tissue injury18,43,44.

Mechanistically, SIM’s suppression of the CXCL16/ADAM10 pathway appears fundamental to its anti-fibrotic action. By reducing CXCL16-mediated inflammatory cell trafficking and ADAM10-mediated EMT, SIM effectively prevents the progression of TIF19,45. In the SIM + TAC group, the suppression of CXCL16 indicates that SIM may inhibit the trafficking of inflammatory cells, thereby slowing the progression of fibrosis. The decreased ADAM10 expression suggests that SIM may interrupt EMT-related pathways, further strengthening its anti-fibrotic effect. Although our findings demonstrate a strong association between SIM treatment and downregulation of the CXCL16/ADAM10 axis, the present study does not establish a direct causal relationship. Confirmation of this pathway would require targeted inhibitory or genetic approaches, which were beyond the scope of this work and warrant future investigation.

Combined SIM administration reduced the elevation fibronectin, TGF-β, CXCL16, and ADAM10 particularly in glomeruli. SIM improved the renal fibrosis through modulation of signaling pathways such as HOXA13–USAG-1 and reduced renal inflammation and interstitial fibrosis in obstructive nephropathy models.39,40 These protective effects likely explain the observed reduction in fibronectin, CXCL16, and ADAM10 levels in SIM + TAC group. The observed increase in tubulointerstitial TGF-β in the SIM + TAC group warrants further investigation to determine if this represents a localized defensive response.

Study limitations include reliance on a rat model, requiring clinical validation for human applicability. In addition, the use of only male animals, which may limit the generalizability of the findings. Future studies should include both sexes to evaluate potential sex-specific differences. It also employed a single SIM dose and duration, requiring further dose-response and time-course optimization. Further research is required to explore additional mechanistic pathways beyond CXCL16/ADAM10 and evaluate long-term effects on kidney function and fibrosis development. In addition, the present study evaluated CXCL16 and ADAM10 expression at the protein level; however, additional validation at the mRNA level would further strengthen these findings. Future studies incorporating gene expression analysis are warranted to confirm the observed changes.

Recent evidence indicates that the NLRP3 inflammasome plays a central role in renal inflammation, podocyte injury, and the progression to glomerulosclerosis46,47. Emerging data also suggest that NLRP3/IL-18 signaling can regulate CXCL16 expression, as demonstrated in ischemia/reperfusion-induced injury in vascular endothelial cells48. Although NLRP3 signaling was not evaluated in the present study, it is plausible that the observed anti-inflammatory and anti-fibrotic effects of simvastatin may involve modulation of this pathway. Future studies are warranted to investigate the potential crosstalk between NLRP3 inflammasome activation and CXCL16/ADAM10 signaling in TAC-induced renal injury. Although H&E staining provided clear evidence of renal structural alterations, additional staining techniques such as periodic acid-Schiff (PAS) could offer more detailed evaluation of tubular basement membrane integrity and brush border changes. Future studies incorporating PAS staining are warranted to further validate tubular injury.

Conclusion

Simvastatin exerts a significant renoprotective effect against TAC-induced renal fibrosis by inhibiting ECM accumulation, potentially involving modulation of the CXCL16/ADAM10 signaling pathway. These results indicate potential utility of SIM as supplementary therapy to reduce kidney toxicity in patients undergoing tacrolimus treatment (Fig. 11).

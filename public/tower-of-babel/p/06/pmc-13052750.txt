Global expert profiles, research hotspots, and journal networks in pediatric surgery: an AI-assisted bibliometric analysis
Authors: Rongqi Zhang, Xumeng Liu, Qi Qi, Yicheng Xie, Xinglong Wu, Qiang Shu
Published: 2026

Introduction

Pediatric surgery is a highly specialized discipline dedicated to the diagnosis and surgical treatment of congenital or acquired diseases among neonates and children.1 This field encompasses multiple highly specialized subspecialties, many of which involve rare or low-prevalence conditions, and clinical experiences are frequently concentrated in a small number of institutions.2 It is with these characteristics that the accurate identification of domain experts and informed allocation of academic resources is needed. With the advancement of artificial intelligence (AI), field-specific expert databases have increasingly been developed to support academic evaluation, reviewer selection, and strategic planning.3 Most existing systems rely on bibliometric indicators, rule-based matching, and natural language processing techniques.4 The pediatric surgery community is relatively small and highly subspecialized, with marked heterogeneity across disease areas, posing challenges for the development of a reliable and scalable expert database. Although AI tools have demonstrated potential in automating expert identification, extracting research interests, and mapping collaboration networks,5 6 existing approaches are still limited by fragmented data sources, author name ambiguity, and inconsistent affiliation metadata. Many available expert databases are designed for general biomedical fields and lack highly specialized clinical fields, including the pediatric surgery discipline.7 Furthermore, few systematic studies focus on long-term maintenance, update mechanisms, and integration with journal-level editorial workflows.

As an emerging international journal emphasizing open access and global collaboration, World Journal of Pediatric Surgery (WJPS) is well-positioned to address this gap. From the perspective of the WJPS, this study develops an AI-assisted pediatric surgery expert database that standardizes author profiles, identifies research hotspots, and visualizes global collaboration networks, thereby supporting academic publishing and strategic development within the field. By combining AI-driven data processing with the editorial and academic needs of a specialty journal, this study aims to provide an intelligence tool for expert profiling and disciplinary insight in pediatric surgery.

Methods

Study design

Data Collection

PubMed (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) and Web of Science (https://www.clarivate.com.cn/) were used as the primary data sources, and an automated retrieval pipeline was established to collect the pediatric surgery-related literature. Search keywords and query Uniform Resource Locators (URLs) were generated using predefined search strategies, as detailed in the online supplemental table 1. Articles published between January 1, 2015 and August 26, 2025, were included, while those lacking an author list or abstract were excluded. The system extracted article metadata (title, abstract, journal, publication year, etc) and detailed authorship information (names, affiliations, and countries). Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) terms for each involved article were retrieved to support thematic analysis.

Data cleaning

Author records without any affiliation information were removed. Author names were disambiguated using a large language model (LLM) based on name variants, co-author structures, and affiliation consistency. Using an LLM-based text-parsing module, country, city, and institutional information were extracted from each affiliation field. Authors with identical names and matching institutions were merged. Affiliation fields were standardized by normalizing institutional names and geographic identifiers. We conducted the random sampling, affiliation validation, and completeness review for quality checking, and inaccurate records were corrected or removed.

Data enrichment

Abstracts were processed using an LLM-based keyword extraction module to identify core research topics. For keyword standardization, extracted keywords were intersected with MeSH descriptors, and only terms appearing in both sources were retained for downstream visualizations, including the word cloud and thematic analysis. To validate extraction accuracy, two independent investigators (RZ and XL) performed a parallel review of 300 randomly selected articles by going through the full text. This validation confirmed an accuracy rate exceeding 90% for the automated pipeline. The final dataset integrated bibliographic metadata with standardized authorship and normalized affiliations, and harmonized thematic descriptors.

Visualization and analysis

The integrated dataset supported multiple visualization modules, including expert distribution maps, keyword co-occurrence networks, research hotspot profiling, and collaboration networks at the author and journal levels.

Results

Global distribution of experts

All pediatric surgery researchers contributing to the retrieved publications were mapped geographically (figure 1B). While authors were distributed across all major continental regions, the highest density of experts was observed in North America, Europe, and East Asia. The USA and several Western European nations remain the primary contributors to this field. Notably, significant research clusters were also identified in emerging economies, including China, Brazil, and India, indicating a widening global but still regionally imbalanced pediatric surgery research community.

Keyword and author frequency

The publication frequency of individual experts is visualized in figure 1C, where name size corresponds to publication volume during the 2015–2025 period. A substantial proportion of productive authors also serve on the editorial boards of leading pediatric surgery journals, including WJPS, and several have also contributed as guest editors in the development of high-quality special issues. The keyword word clouds figure (figure 1D) highlights the major research focuses in the area of pediatric surgery, including congenital diaphragmatic hernia (CDH), esophageal atresia (EA), anorectal malformation (ARM), Hirschsprung disease (HSCR), biliary atresia (BA), and postoperative complications.

Distribution of journals

Among all identified publications, the Journal of Pediatric Surgery (JPS) accounted for the largest share (n=2666; 43.8%), followed by several other pediatric or surgical journals (figure 1E). WJPS contributed 136 publications (2.2%), positioning it as an active but smaller-scale specialized outlet in this field. This result reflects a concentrated publication landscape, with a small number of journals indexing the majority of pediatric surgical research.

Journal collaboration network

The journal collaboration network (figure 1F) illustrates the interconnections among journals based on shared authorship patterns. High-degree nodes correspond to journals with substantial cross-journal publishing activity. JPS appeared at the center of the network, demonstrating its dominant, integrative role in the pediatric surgery field.

Currently, WJPS exhibited visible connections with other pediatric and surgical journals, suggesting its progressive integration into the broader pediatric surgery research ecosystem. The network structure indicates active cross-journal dissemination of research findings and highlights journals serving as major hubs for scholarly communication.

Discussion

This study developed an automated, AI-assisted pediatric surgery expert database by integrating large-scale bibliometric retrieval, algorithmic author disambiguation, semantic keyword extraction, and collaboration network analysis. These findings revealed a globally distributed authorship landscape, identified hot topics centered on congenital anomalies and postoperative outcomes, and mapped the structure of cross-journal contributions. These results demonstrate that this expert dataset tool can effectively characterize research patterns and expert networks in the pediatric surgery field.

The research hotspots identified in our analysis largely reflect the well-established research patterns in pediatric surgery. For example, the prominence of EA and other major congenital anomalies is consistent with their disease burden and continuous academic attention. Although lacking novel themes, these findings still provide an objective overview of the core research focus.

It should be noted that similar bibliometric analyses in the pediatric surgery field have been conducted,8 while often based on manual literature searches and limited in scale and sustainability. These limitations have been widely reported in biomedical expert-finding and academic profiling studies.9 10 Nevertheless, such databases are increasingly important for the advancement of pediatric surgical research. For editorial boards, professional societies, and research institutions, an AI-assisted expert system can support reviewer selection, academic committees’ constitution, targeted manuscript contributions, and high-level strategic planning.

Compared with established high-impact pediatric surgical journals—such as the JPS, Pediatric Surgery International, and European Journal of Pediatric Surgery—WJPS occupies a developing but increasingly connected position. Although its publication volume remains smaller, network analysis confirms that WJPS is progressively embedding itself within a broader pediatric surgery research ecosystem. This trajectory is consistent with the patterns observed in other emerging specialty journals, in which increasing cross-journal connectivity and international authorship have been associated with later improvements in journal influence and visibility.11,13 To further consolidate its global presence, WJPS should benefit from strategies that expand international submissions, conduct special issues aligned with identified research hotspots, and promote cross-regional collaborations. During 2023–2025, WJPS has published special issues focusing on CDH,14 HSCR,15 and BA and is currently soliciting submissions for an upcoming special issue on AI. The selection of these topics is largely consistent with the hotspots identified in the keyword-based analysis.

This study still has several limitations. First, the expert identification was based on publication metadata, some highly productive authors whose primary research lies in parasurgical areas were also highlighted. While these contributions are academically valuable, this finding may not always correspond to pediatric surgical practice. Second, an apparent underrepresentation of some highly influential pediatric surgeons from East Asia, including China, was also observed, which reflects challenges in author name disambiguation, incomplete metadata, and institutional mobility. Third, the efficacy of keyword extraction is based on the availability and quality of abstracts, which may vary across journals and publication years and could influence the identification and summarization of research hotspots. Future studies should incorporate diverse data sources and integrate citation-based or funding-based metrics to enhance the accuracy and scope of the expert profiles.

In conclusion, this study demonstrates the feasibility of an AI-assisted, publication-based framework for mapping expert profiles and research trends in pediatric surgery. Although acknowledged methodological limitations, this approach provides practical support for editorial decision-making and reviewer selection. From the perspective of WJPS, this tool offers an objective basis for understanding discipline development and promoting international journals collaboration.

Real-world study: Analysis of post-marketing safety for recombinant zoster vaccine in Shandong, China
Authors: Han Li, Meng Xie, Xiaofei Qiu, Xiao Kang, Feifei Li, Aiqiang Xu, Li Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThis study evaluated the safety of the recombinant zoster vaccine (RZV) after its marketing in Shandong, China, using adverse events following immunization (AEFI) data. AEFI reports for RZV were collected by the Chinese National AEFI Information Management System (CNAEFIs) between July 2020 and September 2024. In total, 279 AEFI cases were found after administering 145,865 doses of RZV, resulting in an AEFI rate of 19.13 per 10,000 doses. Of these cases, 274 (98.21%) were attributed to vaccine-related reactions, while 5 (1.79%) were coincidental events. The reporting rate was higher for the first dose (24.22 per 10,000) than that for the second (13.20 per 10,000), and the reporting rate was higher in females (21.71 per 10,000) than in males (14.31 per 10,000). Fever, localized redness/induration accounted for 92.83% of the cases after administering RZV. Three positive signals, zoster, vestibular neuritis, and moderate fever, were detected using data mining. No new adverse reactions were identified, and 97.49% of AEFI cases were mild, indicating that the post-marketing safety profile of RZV remains within the expected limit.KEYWORDS: Recombinant zoster vaccine, adverse events following immunization, safety

Introduction

Herpes zoster (HZ) is induced by the reactivation of varicella-zoster virus (VZV), and HZ is most often diagnosed in older individuals and those with compromised immune systems. The lifetime risk of HZ in the general population ranges from 20% to 30% but the risk increases dramatically after 50 y of age.1 Postherpetic neuralgia (PHN) is a common complication of HZ, which can persist for months or even years, occurring in approximately 9% to 34% of HZ cases.2 VZV mainly spreads through direct contact with the rash of individuals who have varicella or HZ, or by inhaling aerosolized droplets released by patients with varicella through the respiratory tract.

VZV vaccination is currently an effective measure of preventing HZ.3 Two kinds of VZV vaccines are widely used: zoster vaccine live (ZVL) and recombinant zoster vaccine (RZV). ZVL was approved for marketing in 2006, and RZV was licensed for U.S. marketing in 2017. According to clinical trials, the effectiveness of ZVL and RZV was 50%–70%, and 90%–97%, respectively. Moreover, real-world effectiveness studies found that the effectiveness of ZVL and RZV was 46% and 85%.4

VZV vaccination is a cost-effective public health initiative. In the United States, a study utilizing a multi-cohort Markov model estimated the cost-effectiveness of RZV. The study showed that, compared to no vaccination for HZ, RZV saved an incremental cost of $11,863 per quality-adjusted life year (QALY).5 A study conducted in China using a decision tree-Markov analysis model showed that the incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER) for the RZV varied by age group, ranging from $3,428 to $5,743 per QALY.6

With the increasing popularity of vaccination, adverse events following immunization (AEFI) impact vaccination willingness. An effectiveness study demonstrated that both ZVL and RZV primarily elicited local injection-site reactions and mild systemic reactions, which were consistent with those from clinical trials.4 A comprehensive analysis revealed that both the RZV and ZVL demonstrate acceptable safety in patients with HIV infection, including both adults and children.7

Both ZVL and RZV have been approved for marketing in China. ZVL contains an attenuated strain of the varicella-zoster virus (Oka strain); RZV is an inactivated vaccine composed of truncated VZV glycoprotein-E and the AS01B adjuvant system.8 Shandong initiated the administration of RZV in July 2020 for individuals aged ≥50 y, with a full course of two doses, the second dose administered 2 months after the first. ZVL vaccination began in June 2023 for individuals aged ≥40 y, requiring only one dose. Due to the lower number of ZVL doses administered and only six reported AEFI cases, resulting in an insufficient sample size, this study focused on analyzing RZV. Hence, we collected data on AEFI cases associated with RZV in Shandong to evaluate its real-world safety profile.

Methods

Participants

This cross-sectional descriptive study targeted individuals who received RZV in Shandong from July 2020 to September 2024.

Data collection

Data on AEFI cases (July 2020–September 2024) associated with RZV in Shandong were collected through the Chinese National AEFI Information Systems (CNAEFIs). RZV vaccination data during the same period were collected through the Shandong Provincial Immunization Program Information Systems (SDIPIs).

AEFI classification and definitions

AEFI surveillance, reporting, and diagnosis followed the Chinese National AEFI Surveillance Guidelines. AEFI refers to suspected reactions or events related to vaccination that occur after vaccination. Mild reaction refers to a reaction that occurs after vaccination, caused by the inherent characteristics of the vaccine itself, and only causes transient physiological dysfunction to the body; Coincidental event was defined as the recipient being in the latent or prodromal phase of a certain disease at the time of vaccination, and coincidentally develops symptoms after vaccination. An AEFI investigation expert group evaluated the causal relationship between incidents and the vaccine. Serious AEFI were defined as individuals who died, were in a life-threatening condition, required hospitalization or prolonged hospitalization, had significant disability or incapacity, had congenital anomaly or congenital disability, or the above situation occurs without intervention or treatment.9 Classification and analysis were based on the primary diagnosis for cases with multiple clinical diagnoses.

Data analysis

Excel 2019 was used to establish a database, and R software (Version4.4.2) was utilized for statistical analysis. The AEFI reporting rate was calculated as: AEFI rate (/10000) = number of AEFI cases associated with RZV/number of RZV vaccination doses × 10000. AEFI reporting rate was compared using the Chi-square (χ2) or Fisher’s exact test, with statistical significance set at p < .05.

Proportional reporting ratio (PRR), reporting odds ratio (ROR), Bayesian Confidence Propagation Neural Network (BCPNN), and Empirical Bayes Gamma-Poisson Shrinker (GPS) were used to mine the abnormal signals of RZV. Signal detection was based on the following criteria10: (1) PRR lower limit of the 95% confidence interval (95% CI) > 1; (2) ROR lower limit of the 95% CI > 1; (3) Information Component (IC) lower limit of the 95% CI > 0; (4) Empirical Bayes Geometric Mean (EB05) lower limit of the one-sided 95% CI > 2; and (5) at least one case of the disease was reported.

Results

RZV AEFI characteristics

A total of 145,865 doses of RZV have been administered from July 2020 to September 2024 in Shandong. Two hundred and seventy-nine RZV AEFI cases were reported, resulting in the reporting AEFI rate of 19.13 per 10,000 doses, including 73 males (14.31 per 10,000 doses) and 206 females (21.71 per 10,000 doses). Most AEFI reports were observed in people aged 50–54 (112 cases, 25.06 per 10,000 doses), and the least AEFI reports were found in people ≥75 y (4 cases, 7.54 per 10,000 doses). The reporting AEFI rate was significantly higher after the first RZV dose (190 cases, 24.22 per 10,000 doses) compared to the second dose (89 cases, 13.2 per 10,000 doses) (Table 1).

Discussion

Post-marketing safety surveillance and evaluation are essential following the large-scale vaccination, as they facilitate the detection of rare and previously unobserved adverse reactions that were not identified during clinical trials. This provides scientific evidence for the continued use and widespread deployment of vaccines.11 RZV was approved for listing in China in 2019, so the AEFI data in the real-world play a significant role in assessing its safety performance.

The reporting rate of AEFI for RZV in Shandong from July 2020 to September 2024 is higher than the AEFI reporting rate for all vaccines in China from 2021 to 2022 (19.13 vs. 3.35 per 10,000 doses).12 The rate is also higher than the AEFI reporting rate for the influenza vaccine (4.10 per 10,000 doses)13and the 9-valent human papillomavirus vaccine (HPV9) (1.60 per 10,000 doses).14 The results were comparable to the RZV AEFI reporting rate in Guangdong, China (19.65 per 10,000 doses),15 and the reporting rate (16.77 and 13.6 per 10,000 doses) in other countries during post-marketing safety surveillance of RZV.16,17 The higher AEFI reporting rate for RZV compared to the national average and other common vaccines is likely attributable to its AS01B adjuvant system.18 The adjuvant system contains two immunostimulants (3-O-desacyl-4’-monophosphoryl lipid A and saponin QS-21), which enhance the overall immune response to the antigen, resulting in higher immunogenicity and more frequent mild reactions.19,20 The main clinical symptoms following RZV vaccination include fever, local reactions at the injection site, allergy, myalgia, fatigue, dizziness, and headache, which are typically reported after injectable vaccines.21 Vaccination service providers should counsel recipients to expect self-limiting local adverse reactions and encourage completion of the two-dose RZV regimen within the recommended schedule.

The results showed a higher RZV reporting AEFI rate in females compared to males (1.52:1), aligns with observations from influenza, pneumococcal vaccination,22,23 attributing to both biological factors and gender-specific reporting behavior.22 A systematic research review found that women express concerns about vaccine safety and adverse effects more frequently than men.24 Emerging immunological research suggests that estrogen-related immune responses may contribute to heightened reactogenicity among females across multiple vaccine types.25 The highest reporting rate was observed in the 50–54 age group (25.06 per 10,000 doses) compared to other age groups. These findings were consistent with other post-marketing monitoring studies of RZV conducted in China and abroad.15,26,27 The results showed a higher AEFI reporting rate for the first dose than for the second dose, which was also observed in another study of RZV in China from 2020 to 2023 and a study by GSK company from 2017 to 2019.26,28 This phenomenon is different from the diphtheria, tetanus, and acellular pertussis combined vaccine (DTaP), whose rate of AEFI increases with the number of doses administered.29 This is partly due to the fact that the recipients who have received the new vaccine pay more attention to adverse reaction after the first dose than the second dose.27 According to the time interval following RZV injection, 71.90% AEFI appeared within 0–2 d of administration, which is similar to other vaccines in China from 2021 to 2022.12

The AEFI reporting rate for RZV varied across cities in this study, potentially due to differences in surveillance system sensitivity or variations in vaccine recipients’ understanding of adverse events. The findings highlight the need for standardized AEFI monitoring training and the implementation of pre-vaccination education protocols. According to the temporal analysis, the reporting rate showed an upward trend in 2023, possibly linked to increased public awareness of vaccine adverse effects following mass COVID-19 vaccination from 2020 to 2023.

The most commonly reported reactions to RZV were fever and localized redness/induration, findings that align with other RZV studies and a study of the hepatitis B virus (HBV) vaccine.16,27,30 The vaccine-related cases of optic neuritis and acute myelitis were reported. Optic neuritis is a vaccine-related demyelinating disease that may be induced by inflammation, autoimmunity, or infection.31 Most patients with acute myelitis develop symptoms following upper respiratory infections. Globally, enterovirus D68 has been identified as the most common enterovirus and is considered a key driver of seasonal myelitis outbreaks.32 Thus, vaccination may trigger an autoimmune reaction, leading to myelitis.33 While optic neuritis and acute myelitis are not common post-vaccination reactions, cases have been reported in association with leprosy and COVID-19 vaccines.34–36 However, the underlying pathogenesis remains unclear. A plausible explanation is vaccine-induced molecular mimicry, wherein antigenic similarity between vaccine components and endogenous proteins provoke autoimmune responses, leading to disease manifestation.27 In this study, only one case each of optic neuritis and acute myelitis was reported. The limited number of cases precludes the definitive establishment of a causal relationship with RZV vaccination.

Three positive signals, including vestibular neuritis, HZ, and fever (37.6–38.5℃), were detected based on the data mining. Vestibular neuritis is usually benign and self-limiting.37 The etiology remains unclear. A theory proposes that vestibular neuritis occurs as an “immune-mediated complication of infection, rather than direct infection of the nerve.”38 The case of vestibular neuritis was reported after COVID-19 vaccines.39,40 HZ is caused by VZV reactivation when immunosenescence starts to manifest or the immune system is impaired, with increasing age.26 The cases of vestibular neuritis and HZ were classified as coincidental events by the AEFI investigation expert group and were determined to be unrelated to RZV. Another positive signal was fever (37.6–38.5℃), consistent with the signal detected in post-marketing surveillance of RZV in Hangzhou, China.27 The adjuvant system in RZV may induce more febrile reactions, which warrants attention.

The study had several limitations. The AEFI surveillance system is a passive reporting system that has inherent limitations. The reporting rate for AEFI, especially for non-serious events, is likely to be lower than the true occurrence rate due to the inherent under-reporting characteristics of the spontaneous reporting system;41 The anonymous monitoring data used lacks individual-level medical history information. Therefore, we cannot control for potential confounding variables (such as underlying diseases), nor can we conduct safety assessments on high-risk subgroups with specific health conditions. The data in the article primarily serve as a reference for the safety of RZV compared to other vaccines or in other regions, rather than inferring the absolute risk of occurrence. The positive signals between vaccination and the AEFI detected through the system did not necessarily indicate causality.42 So, the determination of causal relationship required case investigations and epidemiological studies; Furthermore, as RZV was used in China for a few years, the available AEFI data was limited.

Conclusion

The surveillance data following RZV vaccination in Shandong from July 2020 to September 2024 revealed that the mild reaction rate is higher than that of other vaccines used in China, 92.83% of these were characterized by fever and localized redness/induration. No evidence of previously unknown adverse reactions was found, demonstrating that RZV has an acceptable safety profile. Future studies, including those involving large-scale populations and active monitoring, will be essential to obtain more reliable evidence for comprehensive post-marketing safety evaluation.

Ethical statement

This data information comes from a passive monitoring system. It did not involve human participants, animal subjects, or the collection or use of any private or identifiable information. The data comes from the Chinese National AEFI Information System. The recipient’s name and ID information are all set to be private, and the analysts cannot see this information. So, formal ethical approval from an institutional review board was neither sought nor required. The study complies with all relevant international and national ethical guidelines for academic research.

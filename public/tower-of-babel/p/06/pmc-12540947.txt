Exploring the exact solutions to the nonlinear systems with neural networks method
Authors: Jan Muhammad, Ali H. Tedjani, Ejaz Hussain, Usman Younas
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This paper explores the use of Riccati subequation neural networks to solve nonlinear partial differential equations modeling complex biological processes like cancer, brain function, and wound healing. These equations involve spatially varying biochemical signaling and tissue regeneration. The study applies a method where solutions to the Riccati problem are integrated into neural networks, with the first hidden layer neurons specifically providing the solution to the Riccati equation. Exact solutions for differential equations may be obtained using the suggested approach. In order to verify the mathematical foundation of this technique, we examine the proposed equations, which leads to the derivation of hyperbolic function solutions, trigonometric function solutions, and rational solutions. Using various graphical illustrations, the dynamic properties of certain solutions associated with waves have been demonstrated. The results provided in this study have the potential to improve understanding of the nonlinear dynamic characteristics displayed by the specified system and to confirm the effectiveness of the techniques that have been implemented.

Introduction

The nonlinear nature of various natural systems makes nonlinear problems very important to many branches of science, including mathematics, engineering, and physics. Nonlinear dynamical systems are well-known for exhibiting complex behaviors such as chaos and uncertainty, which have been extensively studied in the literature1. Nonlinear partial differential equations (NLPDEs)2 theory is fundamental in many practical fields, such as engineering and science. It makes possible the understanding of the development of many nonlinear dynamical systems and is essential for the formulation of general mathematical equations. Numerous nonlinear physical processes3–5 are studied by analyzing exact solutions to various nonlinear equations. The verification of numerical solutions and stability analysis rely on obtaining exact solutions for nonlinear equations. An exact solution to any nonlinear physical system can only be obtained by expressing its physical features using a mathematical equation which mostly take the form of PDE. NLPDEs are a powerful mathematical approach to depicting the temporal and spatial evolution of intricate behaviors including pattern development, turbulence, and abrupt transitions by considering the interactions of complex variables. The Navier-Stokes equations’ nonlinear various components in fluid dynamics provide light on the interplay between air or water currents, the result of these processes is the chaotic flows and eddies necessary for weather forecasting. Many systems may be better predicted and controlled by employing nonlinear approaches to stability, natural, and bifurcation occurrences. This method has substantially enhanced our capacity to comprehend and handle intricate and linked issues in the realms of technology and the natural sciences.

Data-driven and computational neural networks (NNs) have replaced traditional analytical and numerical techniques to solve NLPDEs. Without discretization or solvability constraints, NNs can manage a great range of high-dimensional, complicated problems. NNs solve problems using data or physical principles. No assumptions are needed to explain complicated nonlinear interactions using universal approximations. Physical information-based neural network training dynamics may solve sparse or noisy inputs due to PDE restrictions. NNs also have the advantage of being able to solve NLPDEs and infer inverse cases with unknown parameters. Their ability to handle irregular domains, adaptive refinement, and parallel computation makes them a flexible and scalable approach to scientific machine learning. This remains true despite the challenges they face in terms of stability and generalization. By combining with existing methods, an important step forward in the development of computational mathematics and engineering has been achieved.

Studying higher-dimensional NLPDEs and reaction and diffusion models demands solitons’ stability, localized structure, and capacity to replicate intricate events in physics, biology, and engineering6–10. Solitons behave like stable, shape-preserving waves in 2D and 3D due to a balance between nonlinearity and dispersion or diffraction. Examples with many solitons include magnetic systems’ skyrmions, optical vortices in nonlinear Schrödinger equations, and the Kadomtsev-Petviashvili (KP) equation. To mimic ion acoustic waves in plasma physics using spatial solitons and to guide light in waveguides using nonlinear optics, these structures are required. High-dimensional solitons may interact with fusion, annihilation, and resonance, complicating their dynamics. In reaction-diffusion systems, neural pulse solitons and chemical spot patterns generate localised patterns. In contrast to the Fisher-KPP equation, Turing patterns and spiral waves produce enduring localised structures. Morphogenesis, which explains animal coat markings, and neurology, which models nerve impulse transmission, will require these patterns. Ecological reaction diffusion system solitons describe invasive species spread. Stability and long-term behaviour of higher-dimensional solitons are challenging to calculate theoretically. Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction, energy techniques, and spectral or finite element numerical simulations have tracked their evolution. Solitons are essential to nonlinear research, relating NLPDEs analysis to wave dynamics, pattern generation, and other domains despite their challenges. Additionally, singular solutions can reflect important topological or geometric features within physical systems, like vortices in fluids. Despite their mathematical irregularities, singular solutions frequently correspond to real, observable effects across various fields including electromagnetism, fluid dynamics, and general relativity.

The derivation of exact solutions for different physical processes may be used to determine the basic properties. Their importance is shown by the fact those techniques facilitate additional investigation. It is necessary to express the physical structure’s fluctuations as an ODE or PDE in order to get an exact solution. PDEs may be used to mathematically simulate complex natural and industrial processes. Numerous technologies have been designed via extensive study to successfully handle these kinds of problems. Several techniques have attracted a lot of interest in recent times, such as improved modified extended tanh-function technique11,12, improved exp \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$(-Y(\eta ))$$\end{document}-expansion approach13, improved P-expansion approach14, improved generalized Riccati equation mapping approach15, generalized projective Riccati equation method16, fractional subequation neural network method17, generalized exponential rational function technique18, truncated Painlevé method19, adomian decomposition approach20, generalized multivariate exponential rational integral function method21, bifurcation analysis22, generalized Arnous technique23, Riccati equation mapping method24, modified extended mapping method25,26, residual power series method27, polynomial expansion method28, sub-equation method29, etc.

The objective of this study is to employ the Riccati subequation neural network (RSENN) for solving the NLPDEs, such as the linear quadratic (LQ) model and the bistable reaction–diffusion model. This technique overcomes the drawbacks of analytical approaches by drawing on machine learning techniques for solving PDEs. A neural network may be similarly embedded to solve the Riccati problem. As well as the extent of their scientific and technological influence, the efficacy of the methodology employed is valuable. The data analysis method employed emphasises critical implications that are essential for the advancement of numerous scientific disciplines and the provision of viable alternatives. Because of the complex relationship between nonlinearity and other factors that could emerge while working with nonlinear problems, this method is helpful in a variety of circumstances. The motivation of this paper is to develop an effective approach for solving NLPDEs that arise in biological and medical processes, such as cancer growth, brain activity, and wound healing. Traditional techniques often fail to capture the complex nonlinear dynamics of these systems. By integrating Riccati subequation methods with NNs, we construct novel trial functions that yield exact solutions. This framework not only advances mathematical theory but also deepens understanding of the dynamic behaviors in life science applications.

Riccati subequation neural networks

In this work, we propose a novel method, RSENNs, which integrates the framework of NNs with the solutions derived from the Riccati subequation approach. The core idea behind RSENNs is to utilize the solutions of the Riccati subequation as activation functions in the first hidden layer of the neural network architecture. This innovative combination allows the trial functions derived from the Riccati equation to transform the given PDEs into algebraic equations. Thus, RSENNs facilitate the extraction of exact solutions to PDEs.

LQ model

Mathematical modeling is frequently employed for quantifying procedures in medicine and situations. This quantitative mathematical modeling approach is extensively employed in cancer biology and is beneficial for both experimental and clinical research31. Certain characteristics of cancer, including tumour growth, have been simulated using a variety of models32. Tumours have an impact on human cells, which undergo a regular cycle of growth, division, death, and replacement. Nevertheless, if this mechanism is not regulated, cells may proliferate rapidly and expand uncontrollably, resulting in the development of a tumour that is characterised by inflammation or swelling. Subsequently, a tumour is an abnormal accumulation of tissues that can be found in either a solid or liquid form. The advancement of chemotherapeutic models is analogous to the development of numerous empirical models to illustrate radiation-induced cell death. Three dimensional visualizations capture the evolution of solitons across space and time, highlighting their stability, propagation, and oscillatory behavior, while two dimensional graphs dissect their spatial organization, mapping peak intensities, amplitude gradients, and localized density variations critical for studying soliton merging or energy exchange. Streamline plots further enhance this understanding by tracing the directional flow and interaction pathways of soliton energy, exposing vorticity or convergence zones in complex wave systems. Meanwhile, polar plots quantify angular dependencies or anisotropy in soliton profiles, offering insights into directional stability or asymmetric propagation under varying parameter regimes. Together, these methods provide a holistic framework to decode soliton interactions, from collision dynamics in high-density regions to modulation processes in heterogeneous media. Limitations

The proposed RSENN framework demonstrates effectiveness in constructing exact solutions for NLPDEs, but several limitations should be acknowledged.

First, the use of a fixed Riccati basis restricts the solution manifold, potentially limiting the model’s ability to capture more diverse or complex dynamical behaviors. The static nature of this basis may prevent the framework from modeling highly nonlinear or multi-scale phenomena, which require more flexible or adaptive solution forms.

Second, the reliance on an a priori travelling-wave variable, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\xi = k x - \omega t + \phi$$\end{document}, restricts the generality of the method to travelling-wave or self-similar solutions. This excludes more general, non-travelling patterns that are commonly observed in systems with localized dynamics or complex interactions, limiting the versatility of the framework.

Third, the robustness of RSENNs under noisy data, uncertain parameters, or complex boundary conditions has not been thoroughly tested. Real-world systems often involve noisy or incomplete data and dynamic boundary conditions, which could affect the model’s performance and applicability in practical scenarios, such as biomedical and physical systems.

Finally, while the RSENN framework shows promise in idealized scenarios, it has not been fully evaluated in terms of computational cost and scalability for larger or higher-dimensional systems. The growing complexity of real-world problems may challenge the framework’s efficiency and practical deployment.

Conclusion

This study highlights the efficacy of analytical techniques in elucidating the important dynamics of the LQ model and reaction-diffusion equation, revealing its inherent characteristics and providing significant insights. The dynamical behaviours of the proposed model have been effectively examined using RSENNs. Incorporating the solutions of Riccati equation into the first hidden layer for NNs allows us to get the trial functions via forward propagation of the model. Using trial functions, we add up all of the term coefficients and readjust them to equal zero. Using the suggested method, the coefficients in the system of algebraic equations are found. These algebraic equations may be solved to get the coefficients. This new methodology integrates the classical Riccati subequation method with the emerging domain of machine learning to provide exact novel solutions. It helps in the analysis of intricate mathematical and natural problems. The examination of the exact solutions of the proposed models has enabled us to validate the applicability and versatility of RSENNs in determining the exact solutions of NLPDEs. Researchers can implement these equations to investigate phenomena associated with nonlinear propagation. The novel exact solutions identified in this study may provide further guidance for future research in numerous research fields. Complex computational and physical relationships have been elucidated by the visual examination of the dynamic features of waves in different Figs. 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10 and 11 of various solutions. This study advances mathematical modeling in nonlinear wave theory, establishing an appropriate foundation for future investigations into complicated events. Further investigations may expand upon this work by examining higher-dimensional models, including more nonlinear effects, or investigating experimental validations in practical applications.

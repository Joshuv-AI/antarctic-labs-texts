Patterns of upper gastrointestinal diseases among patients undergoing esophagogastroduodenoscopy at three hospitals in Asella town, Southeast Ethiopia
Authors: Dawit Zena, Fedhasa Hisa, Zewdu Hurrisa, Muhammedawel Kaso
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractUpper gastrointestinal (UGI) symptoms are among the common complaints among patients visiting health facilities. Because of the scarcity of gastrointestinal endoscopy services and gastroenterologists, the pattern of common upper gastrointestinal diseases has not been well studied in the study setting. This study aimed to determine the pattern of upper gastrointestinal diseases among patients undergoing esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD). An institution-based cross-sectional study was conducted at three hospitals in Asella town, southeast Ethiopia. A total of 279 study subjects were included in the study. Three-fourths (74%) of the study participants had abnormal endoscopic findings. The clinical indications for endoscopic examination were dyspepsia (32.6%), peptic ulcer disease (PUD) (27.2%), suspicion of gastric cancer (13.3%), and suspicion of esophageal cancer (11.5%). The abnormal endoscopic findings were esophageal cancer (10.4%), gastric cancer (10%), duodenal ulcer (DU) (9.3%), and gastritis (8.6%). The abnormal biopsy findings were esophageal cancer (7.5%), gastric adenocarcinoma (6.4%), and gastritis (3.9%). Dyspepsia, peptic ulcer disease, and suspicion of UGI malignancies were the most common clinical indications for endoscopic examination, while esophageal cancer and gastric cancer were the most common abnormal endoscopic findings. The most common abnormal biopsy results were esophageal squamous cell carcinoma and gastric adenocarcinoma. It is advised to have a high index of suspicion for esophageal cancer and gastric cancer for patients who present with alarming upper gastrointestinal symptoms in the study setting.Keywords: Upper gastrointestinal disease pattern, UGI endoscopy and biopsy, Southeast EthiopiaSubject terms: Diseases, Gastroenterology, Medical research

Introduction

Upper gastrointestinal symptoms are among the most common presenting complaints for primary health-care practitioner visits1,2. These include dyspepsia, vomiting, reflux symptoms, dysphagia, and hematemesis. Dyspepsia, a collection of upper gastrointestinal symptoms such as epigastric discomfort, bloating, anorexia, early satiety, belching, nausea, and heartburn, is common in most populations worldwide. The prevalence of uninvestigated dyspepsia has varied between 7% and 34.2% in most population-based studies3. A systematic review and meta-analysis of the worldwide prevalence of dyspepsia that analyzed data from 63 studies done in different parts of the world also reported a pooled prevalence of 31%4. Gastrointestinal disease is one of the leading global health problems. Peptic ulcer disease, gastroesophageal reflux disease, and upper GI cancers affect millions of people worldwide5. Symptoms related to digestive system diseases negatively affect quality of life and have a significant economic impact6. The pathologies underlying upper gastrointestinal symptoms vary and can be associated with significant morbidity and mortality7,8.

Upper gastrointestinal endoscopy, also called esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD), is the visualization of the upper part of the gastrointestinal tract. Endoscopy is performed by passing a flexible endoscope through the mouth into the esophagus, all the way to the second part of the duodenum, with real-time assessment and interpretation of the findings encountered. It is the method of choice for examining the UGI pathologies: duodenal ulcers, gastric ulcers, tumors, and flat mucosal lesions, such as Barrett’s esophagus. Endoscopy not only achieves the diagnosis of these and many other UGI diseases but also provides the opportunity for therapeutic interventions with minimally invasive options9. Major diseases diagnosed via esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD) include gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD), esophageal varices, peptic ulcers (gastric and duodenal), and upper GI malignancies10.

According to studies done in different areas of the world, the clinical indications for esophagogastroduodenoscopy were epigastric pain, peptic ulcer disease, upper gastrointestinal bleeding, dysphagia, gastroesophageal reflux disease, and portal hypertension7,8,11–17. The common esophagogastroduodenoscopic findings were duodenal ulcers, gastric ulcers, esophageal varices, gastritis, esophagitis, hiatus hernia, esophageal stricture, Barrett’s esophagus, esophageal cancer, gastric cancer, and gastropathy8,11,14,17–21.

The pattern of upper gastrointestinal diseases has not been well explored in developing countries like Ethiopia16. Although gastrointestinal endoscopy is a major diagnostic tool for gastrointestinal disorders, it is not widely available at every hospital in the country, forcing health care workers to refer patients to the capital city for endoscopic examination whenever they are challenged to settle the diagnosis. There are also very few gastroenterologists in the country compared with the size of the population. This scarcity exposes patients to significant, sometimes unbearable costs; in addition, they suffer from being absent from work. Many patients decline referrals for reasons of financial constraints, which obliges physicians to treat these patients empirically. This inevitably leads to misdiagnosis and mismanagement of patients, which results in significant morbidity and mortality. Adequate data on common abnormal endoscopic findings in patients undergoing UGI endoscopy are very important for identifying the burden of major upper gastrointestinal pathologies, emphasizing preventive measures, and designing strategies to combat the disease burden in the study setting and in the country at large. Thus, this study aimed to fill this gap by assessing the patterns of endoscopic findings and biopsy results, which will serve as a valuable addition to the limited local information in the study setting and at similar institutions.

Methods

Study settings

An institution-based cross-sectional study was conducted between November 10, 2023, and March 31, 2024, at three hospitals (Asella referral and teaching, Rohoboth, and Shilalo shed) in Asella town, located in Arsi zone of Oromia, about 175 km southeast of the capital city of Ethiopia, Addis Ababa. The hospitals serve for 3.5 million people22. Upper GI endoscopy was done for about 15–20 patients per month at each hospital. KARL STORZ fiberoptic endoscopy machine was used, and there was only one gastroenterologist with 5 years of experience. The gastroenterologist who did the endoscopic evaluation was not among the authors of the manuscript.

Study population and study subjects

Study population

All adult patients ≥ 18 years of age who were undergoing esophagogastroduodenoscopy at the three hospitals in Asella town.

Study subjects

All adult patients ≥ 18 years of age who were undergoing esophagogastroduodenoscopy at three hospitals in Asella town during the study period.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria

Adult patients ≥ 18 years of age who were undergoing esophagogastroduodenoscopy and gave consent.

Exclusion criteria

Critically ill patients who were unable to participate in interviews, patients who did not give consent, or patients who refused to participate were excluded from the study.

Study variables:

(1) Sociodemographic variables: age, sex, marital status, occupation, educational level, residence, and religion. (2) Clinical indications for esophagogastroduodenoscopy. (3) Spectrum of endoscopic findings. (4) Biopsy findings.

Sample size and sampling procedure

The sample size was calculated using a single population proportion formula with the assumption of 95% confidence level, 5% margin of error, and taking 25.4% prevalence of duodenal ulcer among patients undergoing esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD) in a study done in northwest Ethiopia16. A convenience sampling method was used to recruit 291 study subjects.

Data collection instrument and procedures

Data were collected through face-to-face interviews using a structured questionnaire and a review of medical records, including referral papers. The biopsy results were collected from the patients’ charts through direct contact with their clinicians. The questionnaire was prepared in English, translated into the local language (Afan Oromo) for data collection, and then retranslated back to English while maintaining its consistency. The questioner used in the study was newly created for the current study. Three data collectors (medical practitioners) and one supervisor (internal medicine resident) participated in the data collection process. Prior to the data collection, the data collectors introduced themselves to the respondents, and informed consent was obtained from the study participants. The questionnaire was pretested on 15 participants in a similar setup before the actual data collection commenced to check for reliability and consistency of the questionnaire. The quality of the data was ensured through the training and supervision of the data collectors.

Data analysis

Data were entered into Epi Info version 4.4.1 and transported to SPSS version 20 for analysis.

Patient characteristics were reported as frequency and percentage. Descriptive statistics were performed. Data were summarized in tables and texts.

Ethical consideration

The research protocol complied with the Declaration of Helsinki, and ethical clearance was obtained from the College of Health Science Arsi University Ethical Review Committee (Approval No. A/CHS/RCS/2022/2023). Each respondent was informed about the purpose of the study, and his or her right not to participate in the study was respected. The information obtained from the records was kept confidential. Data were collected after obtaining written informed consent from the participants.

Operational definitions

Upper gastrointestinal endoscopy (esophagogastroduodenoscopy): the endoscopic visualization of the upper gastrointestinal tract proximal to the second part of the duodenum (proximal to the ligament of Treitz)9.

Upper gastrointestinal diseases: diseases involving from the second part of the duodenum to the esophagus9.

Upper gastrointestinal bleeding: bleeding that originates proximal to the second part of the duodenum, stomach, or esophagus9.

Results

Sociodemographic characteristics

A total of 279 study participants were included in the study, with a response rate of 96%. Among them, 161 (57.7%) were females. The age of patients who participated in the study ranged from 18 to 92 years, with a median age of 50.3 years, and 147 (52.7%) were older than 50 years. A total of 217 (77.8%) respondents were married, 103 (36.9%) were farmers, 156 (55.9%) lived in rural areas, and 112 (39.2%) had attained only formal education up to grade 4. One-hundred and fifty-six (55.9%) participants were Muslim followers (Table 1).

Spectrum of endoscopic findings

About three-fourths of patients, 212 (76%), had abnormal endoscopy findings. A total of 123 (44.1%) patients above 50 years of age had abnormal endoscopy findings (Table 3). The common endoscopic findings were esophageal cancer (10.4%), gastric cancer (10%), duodenal ulcers (9.3%), and gastritis (8.6%). The common sites of esophageal cancer were at the distal (51.7%) and middle (41.1%) parts of the esophagus. The common endoscopy findings among study participants > 50 years of age were esophageal cancer (9%), gastric cancer (8.2%), gastric ulcers (5%), and duodenal ulcers (5%). Whereas, gastroesophageal reflux disease (5%) was the most common abnormal endoscopic finding among participants ≤ 50 years of age (Table 4).

Biopsy findings

Among 279 patients, biopsy was taken for 79 (28.3%) of them. The study revealed that 65 (23.3%) patients had abnormal biopsy results. The common findings reported from biopsy were esophageal cancer (7.5%), gastric adenocarcinoma (6.4%), and gastritis (3.9%). Among patients with esophageal cancer, 17 (81%) of them had squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) (Table 5).

Discussion

A total of 279 patients undergoing esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD) were included in the study. In our study, dyspepsia (32.6%) was the most common indication for endoscopic evaluation, followed by peptic ulcer disease (PUD) (27.2%), suspicion of gastric cancer (13.3%), and suspicion of esophageal cancer (11.5%). These findings were supported by studies conducted in Nigeria23 and Kenya14 which revealed that dyspepsia, suspicion of gastric cancer, and esophageal cancer were common indications for EGD. Dyspepsia, the most common indication for endoscopic evaluation in this study, was more common (32.6%) than that reported in a similar local study conducted in Addis Ababa (27.8%) and lower than that reported in a study done in northwest Ethiopia (53.8%)6,16. Studies done in Ghana24 and Nigeria25 also showed that dyspepsia is the most common indication for endoscopy. Generally, this finding is in line with the concept that dyspepsia calls for various upper GI pathologies that can be differentiated by upper GI endoscopy14. Another study conducted in Saudi Arabia11 showed UGI bleeding, epigastric pain, and peptic ulcer disease were common indications for endoscopic evaluation. Epigastric pain was the most common indication in studies conducted in northern Uganda and Burkina Faso8,15. The use of variable terminology for symptoms in different settings could result in differences in indications11.

In our study, 76% of patients had abnormal endoscopic findings, which was consistent with other studies conducted in Uganda (80.5%), Northwest Ethiopia (80%), and Nigeria (75.1%)8,16,23.

The most common abnormal endoscopic finding in this study was esophageal cancer (10.4%), followed by gastric cancer (10.0%), duodenal ulcers (9.3%), and gastritis (8.6%). In our study, the magnitudes of esophageal cancer and gastric cancer were greater than those reported in studies done in Addis Ababa (4.4% and 4.1%, respectively) and northwest Ethiopia (4.3% and 3.6%, respectively)6,16. This finding was congruent with a study conducted in Tikur Anbesa Hospital, which revealed that the number of esophageal cancer cases was higher among patients from Arsi Zone than among those from other regions of Ethiopia26. It was proposed that this could be due to the hot porridge dietary habits in this particular geographic location27,28. A study conducted in Kenya also showed that esophageal cancer was the most common (11.8%) abnormal endoscopic finding14. These results show that upper GI cancers are no longer rare in Africa. Indeed, the increasing availability of endoscopic evaluations predicts a number of cases in subsequent years. In addition, it has been proposed that risk factors are also more frequent with globalization: smoking, alcoholism, and excessive consumption of salty and greasy food15.

There was a difference in the patterns of common abnormal endoscopic findings with respect to the age of the study participants. The most frequent endoscopy finding among study participants > 50 years of age was esophageal cancer (9%), followed by gastric cancer (8.2%), gastric ulcers (5%), and duodenal ulcers (5%). Whereas, gastroesophageal reflux disease (5%) was the most common abnormal endoscopic finding among participants ≤ 50 years of age. This finding was consistent with a study conducted in southwestern Uganda, which revealed that older patients had a higher prevalence of esophageal cancer and gastric cancer21. A greater number of patients above 50 years of age (44.1%) had abnormal endoscopic findings than those ≤ 50 years of age (31.9%). This finding was supported by studies conducted in Canada29, Pakistan30, southwestern Uganda21, and Egypt31 which revealed that patients > 50 years of age had a greater prevalence of abnormal upper gastrointestinal endoscopy findings. This finding was also in line with the recommendation of upper gastrointestinal endoscopy for patients greater than 50 years of age with new-onset upper gastrointestinal symptoms32,33.

Esophageal cancer (7.5%) was the most common biopsy finding reported in this study, followed by gastric adenocarcinoma (6.4%) and gastritis (3.9%). This finding also possibly supports the fact that the magnitude of esophageal cancer is higher in the study setting. Among patients with esophageal cancer, squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) was the dominant histologic type (81%) in the study setting, which was consistent with similar studies conducted in Ethiopia27,28,34.

Limitations of the study

Since this was a cross-sectional descriptive study, the pattern of upper gastrointestinal diseases among patients undergoing esophagogastroduodenoscopy might not be a true reflection of what happens all the time, and it could not be used to determine cause-and-effect relationships. The generalizability of the findings to the study population was limited since a relatively small sample size.

Conclusion

Dyspepsia was the most common indication for esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD), followed by PUD and suspicion of upper GI malignancies. Esophageal cancer, gastric cancer, and peptic ulcer disease were the most common endoscopic findings reported among patients for whom endoscopy was performed. These findings indicate that upper gastrointestinal cancers are prevalent in the study setting. The most frequent biopsy findings among the patients were esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, gastric adenocarcinoma, and gastritis.

Recommendation

Endoscopy should be done for patients who have alarming upper gastrointestinal symptoms whenever possible. Clinicians should have a high index of suspicion for esophageal and gastric cancer in the study setting for possible early detection. Further studies are needed to characterize the epidemiology and risk factors for these lesions.

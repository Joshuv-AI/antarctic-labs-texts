An address-based geospatial linkage workflow for assigning New Zealand Index of Deprivation measures across census years, 1991–2023
Authors: Andrzej S. Januszewski, Ralph A.H. Stewart, John R. Simes, Anthony C. Keech
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Assigning area-level socioeconomic measures to street addresses across historical and contemporary census years requires harmonising address, geography and deprivation datasets that change over time. This methods article describes a reproducible workflow and online implementation for assigning New Zealand Index of Socioeconomic Deprivation (NZDep) measures to street addresses for census years 1991–2023. The method integrates public Land Information New Zealand address data, openly licensed postcode boundary polygons, Stats NZ census geographies and University of Otago NZDep datasets into an indexed SQLite lookup database and Shiny address-lookup tool (https://bit.ly/nzdep). Address labels are standardised, address points are spatially linked to year-specific census geographies, and geography identifiers are joined to corresponding NZDep values. Raw NZDep scores are converted to census-year-specific national percentile ranks, allowing relative deprivation position to be expressed on a consistent 1–100 scale across census periods. The workflow supports unit-level address lookup where available and base street-address lookup only where deprivation assignment is unambiguous. The final database contained 2405,589 address records and achieved 99.65% completeness across 16,839,123 potential address-year NZDep assignments.

Background

Area-level socioeconomic measures are widely used in epidemiology, public health and health services research when individual-level socioeconomic information is unavailable, incomplete or inconsistently collected [[1], [2], [3], [4], [5], [6], [7]]. In New Zealand, the New Zealand Index of Socioeconomic Deprivation (NZDep) provides an established census-derived measure of small-area deprivation and is commonly used to characterise geographic variation in deprivation, health inequities and access to services [[8], [9], [10], [11]]. However, assigning NZDep values to street addresses across historical and contemporary census years requires integration of multiple data sources that differ in structure, geography and format.

The main motivation for this method was to create a reproducible workflow for converting New Zealand street addresses into census-year-specific area-level deprivation measures. This is relevant for historical cohorts, registries, routinely collected health data, clinical trial follow-up and other epidemiological studies in which residential address may be available, but direct information on income, education, employment, housing conditions or other individual-level socioeconomic factors is absent. The method is also relevant where researchers need to assess socioeconomic context at more than one time point, because New Zealand census geographies and NZDep releases have changed over time.

The workflow integrates publicly available Land Information New Zealand address data [12], openly licensed postcode polygons [13], Stats NZ census geography boundaries [14] and University of Otago NZDep datasets [15]. Address records are standardised into a searchable address-label field, enriched with postcodes to support address disambiguation, linked spatially to year-specific census geographies, and then joined to corresponding NZDep values. Raw NZDep scores are converted to census-year-specific national percentile ranks, providing a consistent relative 1–100 scale across census periods.

The resulting SQLite database and Shiny application allow users to assign NZDep percentile ranks and census geography identifiers from either single-address lookup or batch-uploaded address files. Although developed for New Zealand data, the staged workflow may be adapted to other jurisdictions where public address datasets, census geography boundaries and area-level socioeconomic indices are available.

The significance and innovation of this method lies in converting a technically complex, multi-source geospatial linkage process into a reproducible and usable address-level workflow. To our knowledge, no publicly available tool currently provides address-based assignment of NZDep measures across multiple New Zealand census years from 1991 to 2023. The method addresses this gap by combining address standardisation, year-specific census geography linkage, NZDep score harmonisation and online implementation in a single workflow. This allows researchers to assign census-year-specific deprivation measures from individual or batch-uploaded street addresses, while retaining an auditable structure that can be updated for future census and NZDep releases.

Method details

Overview of the workflow

The method was developed as a staged geospatial data-linkage workflow to assign NZDep values to street addresses across multiple census years [[16], [17], [18]]. The workflow integrates publicly available address, postcode, census geography and NZDep datasets into a searchable address-level SQLite database, which is implemented in an online Shiny lookup tool (https://bit.ly/nzdep).

The workflow has five main stages: (1) construction of a standardised national address file and indexed lookup address-label field; (2) postcode enrichment; (3) linkage of address points to census geography boundaries; (4) linkage of census geography identifiers to year-specific NZDep datasets; and (5) creation of a searchable SQLite database for single-address lookup and batch processing. This staged structure was used to make the method transparent, auditable and adaptable to future census or deprivation-index releases.

Public address data and address-label construction

The national address dataset was obtained from publicly available Land Information New Zealand (LINZ) address data as a GeoPackage file using New Zealand Geodetic Datum 2000 (NZGD2000; EPSG:4167) [12]. The source file downloaded on 09 March 2026 contained address components and geographical point coordinates. The full ASCII address field and point coordinates formed the base address layer for the lookup database. The source address data did not include postcodes. The address layer represented the LINZ address data available at the time of extraction and was not intended to provide a complete historical gazetteer of all previously existing residential addresses.

Address records were imported into R (ver. 4.5.1) [19] and processed to construct standardised address labels suitable for searching and batch matching. Address labels were standardised by converting to upper case, replacing commas with spaces, removing repeated spaces and trimming leading/trailing spaces. Duplicate full-address records were removed after standardisation so that each retained full address label appeared once in the working address dataset. Spatial linkage was performed at the original address-point level, preserving unit-level address information where available. The final app-facing lookup table used a single standardised address label field, ADDRESS_LABEL. Unit-level address labels were retained where available. Additional base street-address labels were included only where they could be derived unambiguously from underlying unit-level address records that shared the same census geography and NZDep values. Where unit-level records beneath the same base address crossed small-area census geography boundaries in any included census year, no base-address row was added to the final lookup table, preventing potentially misleading base-address matches.

The ADDRESS_LABEL field was used as the searchable and exact-matching field in the online tool. This approach supported both full unit-level address lookup and unambiguous base-address lookup within a single indexed SQLite field, while preserving point-level geographic assignment for unit-level addresses where these were available.

Postcode enrichment

Because the public address dataset did not include postcodes, address records were enriched using postcode boundary data from the Open NZ Postcodes project [13], updated on 30 October 2025. This openly licensed dataset provides physical postcode boundary polygons derived from road-postcode information, address points and small-area geographic units.

Postcode polygons were supplied in World Geodetic System 1984 (WGS84; EPSG:4326) and were transformed to NZGD2000 (EPSG:4167) before linkage to the address point layer. Address points were spatially joined to postcode polygons using a point-in-polygon operation. For address points not falling within a postcode polygon, the nearest postcode polygon was used as a fallback assignment. The assigned postcode was then appended to the standardised address label to support address disambiguation and matching within the online tool.

Postcode assignment was used to improve address disambiguation and user interpretation. It was not used to assign NZDep values. Deprivation assignment was based on direct spatial linkage of address points to census geography polygons. The postcode dataset is not an authoritative NZ Post postcode product, and the Open NZ Postcodes documentation notes that, with the current methodology, approximately 5% of address points may fall within a boundary with a different postcode.

Census geography linkage

Year-specific census geography boundary files were obtained from publicly available Stats NZ geographic datasets [14]. Boundary files were downloaded on 23 March 2026 as vector GeoPackage files and processed in NZGD2000 (EPSG:4167).

The following census years were included: 1991, 1996, 2001, 2006, 2013, 2018 and 2023. For 1991, 1996, 2001, 2006 and 2013, address points were spatially assigned to meshblock polygons. Meshblocks are the smallest geographic units used by Stats NZ and have an ideal size of 30–60 dwellings, corresponding to approximately 60–120 residents. For 2018 and 2023, address points were spatially assigned to Statistical Area 1 (SA1) polygons. SA1s are output geographies constructed by joining meshblocks and have an ideal size of 100–200 residents, with a maximum population of about 500 [20].

For each census year, the relevant geography layer was imported, checked for geometry validity, transformed where required, and spatially joined to the address point layer. Each address point was assigned the census geography identifier of the polygon within which it was located. The resulting year-specific geography identifiers were appended to the address database.

NZDep dataset linkage

NZDep datasets for 1991, 1996, 2001, 2006, 2013, 2018 and 2023 were obtained from the University of Otago NZDep repository, downloaded on 03 April 2026 [15]. The geographic unit used for linkage followed the structure of the corresponding NZDep release. For 1991, 1996, 2001, 2006 and 2013, NZDep values were linked using meshblock identifiers. For 2018 and 2023, NZDep values were linked using SA1 identifiers.

Although meshblocks continued to exist in 2018 and 2023, SA1s were introduced as a small-area output geography built from meshblocks. NZDep2018 and NZDep2023 files are also available with constituent meshblock records; however, these values are aligned with SA1-level deprivation estimates rather than representing independent finer-resolution meshblock-specific deprivation scores. SA1 identifiers were therefore used for the main 2018 and 2023 linkage to avoid treating multiple meshblocks within the same SA1 as separate deprivation estimates.

Before linkage, census geography identifiers were standardised by converting them to character format, trimming spaces, removing decimal suffixes and removing leading zeroes where required. NZDep score fields were converted to numeric format after recoding blank, non-numeric and missing-value indicators to missing.

The linkage was performed separately for each census year. Each retained address was assigned the relevant census geography identifier for that year and then linked to the corresponding NZDep value. Linkage completeness was assessed by calculating the number of missing census geography identifiers and missing NZDep values for each census year. Missing NZDep values were classified according to whether the census geography identifier was missing, absent from the corresponding NZDep source file, or present with a missing NZDep value.

NZDep percentile-rank derivation and database construction

To provide a consistent relative scale across census years, raw NZDep scores were converted to national percentile ranks separately within each NZDep source file before linkage to address records. For each census year, non-missing NZDep score values were ranked across all available geographic units in that source file: meshblocks for 1991, 1996, 2001, 2006 and 2013, and SA1s for 2018 and 2023. Percentile ranks were calculated using the formula 1 + 99 × percent rank score, where percent rank represents the relative rank of each non-missing NZDep score within that census-year distribution. Higher percentile values therefore indicate higher relative deprivation within the national distribution for that census year. Missing, blank or non-numeric score values were recoded to missing before percentile calculation.

These derived percentile ranks were intended to support comparison of relative deprivation position across census periods. They should not be interpreted as measuring absolute change in deprivation over time, because NZDep is a relative area-level measure and the underlying census variables may differ between NZDep releases [16,21].

The final app-facing lookup database contained one indexed address-label field, ADDRESS_LABEL, with year-specific census geography identifiers and derived NZDep percentile ranks. The 2018 and 2023 geography identifiers were stored as SA1 identifiers in the final database. Unit-level address labels were retained where available. Additional base street-address labels were included as ADDRESS_LABEL rows only where they could be assigned unambiguously from underlying unit-level address records sharing the same census geography and NZDep values. Where unit-level records beneath the same base street address crossed meshblock or SA1 boundaries, no base-address row was added to the final lookup database. This ensured that ambiguous base-address queries returned no match rather than a potentially misleading deprivation assignment. The database was stored in SQLite format with a unique index on ADDRESS_LABEL to support rapid searching and batch processing.

No individual participant health data are required to reproduce the method. All input datasets used to construct the lookup database are publicly available geospatial or census-derived datasets. The final lookup tool is intended to return derived area-level deprivation measures and census geography identifiers for individual or uploaded address records, subject to appropriate ethics, privacy and data-governance arrangements when participant or patient addresses are processed.

A random sample of 100 address records was checked manually against the NZ Post Address and Postcode Finder [22] for address-label, suburb/locality and postcode concordance.

Method validation

The workflow was validated through implementation as a national address-level lookup database and online Shiny tool [[12], [13], [14], [15],19]. The final SQLite lookup database contained 2405,589 retained address-label records and supported up to 16,839,123 potential address-year deprivation assignments across seven census years. For each retained record, the database included a single indexed ADDRESS_LABEL field, year-specific census geography identifiers and derived NZDep percentile ranks.

The main practical strength of the method is that it converts a complex sequence of geospatial and census-data processing steps into a usable tool for epidemiological and health services research [23,24]. The workflow addresses several barriers to address-based NZDep assignment, including changes in census geographies over time, separate data sources and file formats, the absence of postcodes from the public address dataset, and the need to support both unit-level and unambiguous base street-address matching within a single indexed lookup field.

Census geography assignment was complete for 2001, 2006, 2013, 2018 and 2023. For 1991 and 1996, 2405,193 addresses were assigned to meshblocks, with 396 records missing a geography assignment in each year (0.02%). Linkage to NZDep values was high across all census years. The proportion of addresses with an assigned NZDep percentile ranged from 98.74% in 2013 to 99.87% in 2006 and 2018. Across all census years combined, 16,779,812 of 16,839,123 address-year records received a NZDep percentile assignment, giving 99.65% overall completeness. The completed database was implemented in an online Shiny application for address-based NZDep lookup (https://bit.ly/nzdep). The tool supports single-address lookup and batch processing of addresses uploaded as a comma-separated values file. User-entered or uploaded addresses are standardised and matched against the indexed ADDRESS_LABEL field. For matched addresses, the tool returns selected census-year NZDep percentile ranks and corresponding meshblock or SA1 identifiers. A random validation sample of 100 retained address records was checked manually against the NZ Post Address and Postcode Finder. All sampled addresses were identifiable. Ninety-seven addresses had matching suburb/locality and postcode information. Two addresses had a different suburb/locality but the same postcode, and one address had a different postcode. These findings supported the practical completeness of the address labels, while highlighting minor discrepancies in locality or postcode attribution.

A further strength of the workflow is that it is location-based rather than dependent on the continuing existence of a specific address record. Once a valid point location has been established, the same spatial linkage process can be used to assign census-year-specific meshblock or SA1 identifiers and corresponding NZDep values. This makes the method adaptable to future NZDep releases and potentially to other jurisdictions where comparable public address, census geography and area-level socioeconomic datasets are available.

Limitations

Several limitations should be considered when applying this method. First, NZDep is an area-level measure and should not be interpreted as the socioeconomic status of an individual person [17]. The derived percentile ranks describe the relative deprivation position of geographic areas within each census-year distribution; they do not measure absolute change in material deprivation over time.

Second, the lookup database was constructed from the contemporary LINZ address layer and is not a complete historical gazetteer. Some addresses from older studies may no longer exist in the same form because of land subdivision, redevelopment, demolition, road renaming or changes in address allocation. Such addresses may require manual reconciliation, geocoding from original address information or assignment to the nearest plausible contemporary geocoded location before NZDep linkage can be performed.

Third, postcode assignment was used to improve address disambiguation and usability, but was not used to assign NZDep values. Deprivation assignment was based on direct spatial linkage of address points to census geography polygons. The postcode dataset used in this workflow is not an authoritative NZ Post product [13], and minor locality or postcode discrepancies may occur.

Fourth, linkage completeness was high but not perfect. Missing NZDep assignments were most frequent in 2013, highlighting the importance of reporting unmatched records when applying or updating the workflow.

Fifth, address matching in multi-unit or complex sites requires careful interpretation. Unit-level address information should be supplied where available. Base street-address labels were included only where all underlying unit-level records shared the same census geography and NZDep values; otherwise, the base address remains unmatched to avoid potentially misleading deprivation assignment.

Finally, when participant or patient addresses are processed, users should follow relevant ethics approvals, institutional governance procedures and privacy requirements. Users should avoid uploading identifiable health information together with address data. The intended analytic output is a derived area-level deprivation measure and census geography identifier rather than the residential address itself, allowing socioeconomic context to be incorporated into epidemiological datasets while avoiding unnecessary ongoing retention of identifiable address information.

Ethics statements

Ethics approval was not required for this methods and data-infrastructure study because no patient, participant or individual-level health data were used or processed. All datasets used to develop the lookup tool were publicly available geospatial or census-derived datasets.

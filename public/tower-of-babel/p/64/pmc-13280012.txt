Anterior Segment Changes after Lens Extraction in Acute Angle-Closure Glaucoma Assessed by Scheimpflug Tomography
Authors: Irem Onal, Elif Ertan Baydemir, Yusuf Yildirim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThis study aimed to evaluate changes in anterior segment morphology and intraocular pressure (IOP) in patients with acute angle-closure glaucoma (AACG) following early phacoemulsification, using Scheimpflug-Placido disc tomography.MethodsIn this retrospective cohort study, 32 eyes of 32 phakic patients diagnosed with AACG and referred to a tertiary hospital between 2021 and 2023 were included. After medical stabilization of IOP and inflammation, all patients underwent cataract surgery within 1 month of the acute attack. Anterior segment parameters were assessed using Scheimpflug-Placido disc tomography before and 1 month after surgery. Paired t-tests were used for statistical analysis.ResultsThe mean age of patients was 65.76 ± 9.63 years. Postoperatively, best-corrected visual acuity improved significantly (preoperative, 0.81 ± 0.92 logMAR; postoperative, 0.18 ± 0.30 logMAR; p < 0.01). Mean IOP decreased by 22.80 ± 3.45 mmHg (p < 0.01). Significant increases were observed in iridocorneal angle (from 24.62° ± 9.59° to 46.20° ± 10.93°), anterior chamber depth (from 1.34 ± 0.65 to 3.02 ± 0.82 mm), and anterior chamber volume (from 98.01 ± 28.62 to 145.87 ± 27.27 mm3), all with p < 0.01. Central corneal thickness decreased slightly but significantly (from 579.80 ± 47.48 to 556.80 ± 47.29 μm, p = 0.03).ConclusionsEarly lens extraction in AACG significantly improves anterior segment parameters and lowers IOP. Scheimpflug-Placido disc tomography is a useful noninvasive tool to quantify these changes.Keywords: Angle-closure glaucoma, Anterior segment, Intraocular pressure, Optical coherence tomography, Phacoemulsification

Acute angle-closure glaucoma (AACG) is a medical emergency that requires prompt diagnosis and treatment to prevent irreversible vision loss, and it is associated with several risk factors including genetic predisposition, hyperopia, and advancing age. The condition arises when the drainage angle for aqueous humor becomes obstructed, resulting in a sudden and significant increase in intraocular pressure (IOP) due to appositional or synechial angle closure mechanisms [1].

Ultrasound biomicroscopy has traditionally been considered the gold standard for identifying angle-closure mechanisms. However, Scheimpflug-based imaging devices offer a valuable, noncontact alternative for the detailed assessment of anterior segment structures [2,3].

Initial management of AACG typically involves antiglaucomatous medications to reduce IOP rapidly. In many cases, laser peripheral iridotomy (LPI) is employed to relieve pupillary block and restore aqueous outflow [4]. However, when LPI fails to open the angle sufficiently or when angle closure persists due to lens-induced mechanisms, phacoemulsification has emerged as an effective alternative for sustained IOP control [5]. Lens extraction addresses pupillary block by removing the thickened crystalline lens, which can lead to anterior chamber deepening and angle widening [6]. Consequently, this approach may prevent progression to chronic angle-closure glaucoma and reduce the need for long-term use of antiglaucomatous medications [7].

This study aims to assess the efficacy of phacoemulsification as an early intervention in patients presenting with AACG. Specifically, it evaluates preoperative and postoperative changes in anterior chamber angle width, depth, and volume using Scheimpflug-Placido disc tomography.

Materials and Methods

Ethics statement

The study protocol was approved by the local ethics committee of Basaksehir Cam and Sakura City Hospital (No. KAEK/13.12.2023.627) and was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to inclusion.

Study population

This retrospective cohort study included 36 eyes from 36 phakic patients diagnosed with AACG who presented to the ophthalmology emergency department of a tertiary hospital between 2021 and 2023. After initial treatment for the acute attack, patients were referred to the glaucoma unit. Patients with complicated cataract surgery such as posterior capsule rupture, zonular dialysis, or those requiring additional intraocular procedures were excluded from the study. A total of four eyes were excluded due to intraoperative complications. These cases were not analyzed further to preserve homogeneity in surgical outcomes.

Inclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria were untreated IOP >30 mmHg, iridotrabecular contact ≥180° on gonioscopy, glaucomatous optic nerve changes (e.g., disc hemorrhages, rim thinning, notching, or cup-to-disc asymmetry), and age ≥50 years. Exclusion criteria included secondary angle-closure glaucoma, inability to obtain accurate measurements, complications during cataract surgery. Patients were initially treated with a combination of topical carbonic anhydrase inhibitors, beta blockers, alpha-2 agonists, oral acetazolamide (250 mg, three times daily), and intravenous mannitol (0.5 g/kg).

LPI was not performed in this cohort due to persistent iridotrabecular contact despite maximal medical therapy, and the treating glaucoma specialists opted for definitive surgical intervention within the subacute period.

Evaluated parameters

All patients underwent a comprehensive ophthalmological examination, including best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) assessed by the Snellen chart and converted to logMAR, IOP measurement via Goldmann applanation tonometry, and gonioscopy using a Zeiss-style 4-mirror goniolens (Model G-4, Volk Optical) graded by the Shaffer system in all four quadrants. Optic disc evaluation was performed with slit-lamp biomicroscopy.

Anterior segment imaging was conducted with the Sirius Scheimpflug-Placido disc topographer. Parameters assessed included iridocorneal angle (ICA), anterior chamber depth (ACD), anterior chamber volume (ACV), horizontal visible iris diameter, corneal volume, central corneal thickness (CCT), and horizontal anterior chamber diameter (HACD). Measurements were performed by a single experienced technician (BO) between 9:00 a.m. and 12:00 p.m. to minimize diurnal variation. All scans were performed under scotopic conditions. Patients were instructed to fixate on a central light for alignment, and scans were obtained only when the device indicated optimal focus. Three scans were taken at 5-minute intervals, and only high-quality readings were used.

Additional evaluations included specular microscopy (EM-4000, Tomey Corp.) and optical biometry (OA-2000, Tomey Corp.) for intraocular lens (IOL) calculation using the SRK/T formula. The mean interval between acute attack and cataract surgery was 24.1 ± 5.9 days. Anterior segment measurements were repeated 1 month after surgery using the same imaging protocol. Surgical procedure

All surgeries were performed by a single experienced surgeon (EEB). Anesthesia was achieved using topical 0.5% proparacaine hydrochloride and intracameral preservative-free lidocaine 1%, or via sub-Tenon injection of 1.5 mL lidocaine 1% with epinephrine. Side-port incisions were made at the 10 and 2 o’clock positions, followed by a 2.4-mm clear corneal incision. Continuous curvilinear capsulorhexis and hydrodissection were performed. The soft-shell technique was employed to manage shallow anterior chambers and protect the corneal endothelium. Phacoemulsification was conducted using the quick chop technique, followed by bimanual irrigation/aspiration. A foldable acrylic posterior chamber IOL (Enova, VSY Biotechnology) was implanted into the capsular bag. At the end of surgery, 0.1 mL of cefuroxime was administered intracamerally, and stromal hydration was used to seal the incisions.

Postoperatively, all patients received topical antibiotic and corticosteroid drops. Antibiotics were prescribed four times daily for 1 week. Steroids were used with the same frequency for 1 week, then tapered over 3 weeks.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS ver. 24.0 (IBM Corp.). Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation. The Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was used to assess the normality of distributions. Paired t-tests were applied to compare preoperative and postoperative measurements for normally distributed continuous variables, whereas the Wilcoxon signed-rank test was used for nonnormally distributed continuous variables. Categorical paired data, such as the proportion of patients using topical antiglaucomatous medications, were analyzed using the McNemar test. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

A total of 36 eyes were initially enrolled in the study. Accordingly, four eyes were excluded from the final analysis due to intraoperative complications, and the data of 32 eyes were included in the statistical evaluation.

The study included 32 patients with a mean age of 65.76 ± 9.63 years (range, 50–85 years), and an equal gender distribution. Four patients were excluded due to complicated cataract surgery (posterior capsule rupture [n = 2], zonular dialysis with anterior vitrectomy [n = 2]), and 32 uncomplicated cases were included in the final analysis. Following surgery, there was a statistically significant improvement in BCVA, improving from a mean of 0.81 ± 0.92 logMAR preoperatively to 0.18 ± 0.30 logMAR postoperatively (p < 0.01). Additionally, IOP showed a significant reduction, with a mean decrease of 22.80 ± 3.45 mmHg (p < 0.01). There was a marked reduction in the number of topical antiglaucomatous molecules, decreasing from 3.00 preoperatively to 1.08 ± 0.86 at the 1-month follow-up. Anterior segment topography demonstrated significant anatomical changes. The mean ICA increased from 24.62° ± 9.59° preoperatively to 46.20° ± 10.93° 1-month postoperatively (p < 0.01). The ACD increased from 1.34 ± 0.65 to 3.02 ± 0.82 mm (p < 0.01), and ACV increased from 98.01 ± 28.62 to 145.87 ± 27.27 mm3 (p < 0.01).

There was also a significant decrease in CCT, from a preoperative value of 579.80 ± 47.48 to 556.80 ± 47.29 μm at 1-month postoperatively (p = 0.03). Refractive outcome analysis showed a mean postoperative spherical equivalent of −0.21 ± 0.67 diopters (D) compared to a target of −0.10 ± 0.44 D (mean prediction error, 0.11 ± 0.51 D). The estimated mean absolute error of the prediction error was 0.42 D.

Although specular microscopy was conducted pre- and postoperatively, significant variation in cell counts due to baseline edema rendered the results unreliable for group-level analysis and thus were excluded from the statistical evaluation.

Discussion

The intervention—early phacoemulsification with IOL implantation—was shown to effectively lower IOP and induce significant anatomical modifications in the anterior segment. LPI combined with medical therapy is the initial standard intervention in AACG and primary angle-closure glaucoma. LPI serves to relieve pupillary block by creating an alternative pathway for aqueous humor outflow [2,8]. However, several studies have demonstrated that angle closure can persist even after successful LPI, especially when evaluated under dark-room provocative conditions, potentially resulting in long-term IOP elevation [9–11].

The EAGLE (effectiveness in angle-closure glaucoma of lens extraction) study has provided evidence that early lens extraction offers superior control of IOP and symptomatic relief by widening the drainage angle [5]. Several studies comparing early phacoemulsification to LPI in patients presenting with acute angle closure reported more stable and effective IOP reduction in the lens extraction group, with a lower likelihood of progression to chronic angle-closure glaucoma [12,13]. Our results are consistent with these findings, demonstrating a marked reduction in IOP and decreased dependency on glaucoma medications. The magnitude of IOP reduction in our study was greater than that reported in medically controlled primary angle-closure glaucoma, where a decrease of approximately 4 mmHg has been described [14]. This discrepancy may be explained by the acute angle-closure status of our cohort, in which baseline IOP levels were markedly higher, thereby allowing for a more pronounced postoperative decrease following lens extraction.

Nevertheless, cataract surgery in eyes with AACG carries an inherently higher surgical risk. The EAGLE study reported a 1% incidence of posterior capsule rupture, and even experienced surgeons may encounter intraoperative difficulties in primary angle-closure disease eyes due to shallow ACD and reduced ACV [5]. Therefore, careful risk–benefit balancing is essential when considering lens extraction in these patients.

Notably, a population-based study by Gillan et al. [15] suggested that the increasing prevalence of cataract surgery, facilitated by advancements in phacoemulsification techniques, has contributed to a decline in AACG incidence.

While combined procedures such as phacotrabeculectomy have shown greater IOP reductions and lower medication needs, they are also associated with higher perioperative complication rates and greater long-term surgical burden [6,12,16]. In patients with risk factors such as older age, high baseline IOP, poor visual field parameters, or a definitive diagnosis of primary angle-closure glaucoma, direct lens extraction may be considered a preferable initial approach [17].

Although gonioscopy remains the clinical gold standard for assessing angle anatomy, imaging modalities such as ultrasound biomicroscopy, anterior-segment optical coherence tomography (AS-OCT), and Scheimpflug-based corneal tomography provide objective and reproducible data [7,16,18–20]. We selected the Sirius Scheimpflug-Placido disc system for its noncontact nature, patient comfort, and reproducibility. This imaging modality allows for detailed evaluation of ACV, ACD, ICA, HACD, and CCT. Among these, ACV is particularly important due to its correlation with aqueous humor dynamics. In our study, postoperative expansion of ACV and deepening of ACD indicated anatomical improvement of the anterior segment. Additionally, there was a notable reduction in iridocorneal contact compared to the preoperative state. Although we did not detect significant changes in corneal volume, the measurable decrease in CCT may indicate transient stromal remodeling rather than true volumetric alteration.

Our findings are consistent with previous reports, including Pakravan et al. [19], who demonstrated with Pentacam imaging that eyes with ACV ≤100 mm3 and ACD ≤2.1 mm are at higher risk for angle closure; in line with this, we also observed altered ACV and ACD values among patients who experienced acute attacks.

This study has several limitations. First, the absence of a control group limits our ability to infer causality. Second, the relatively small sample size and retrospective design reduce statistical power and generalizability; for these reasons, multivariate regression analysis was not performed. Third, the 1-month follow-up period may not fully capture long-term anatomical stability or IOP control, highlighting the need for studies with extended follow-up. Future research with larger and comparative datasets is required to better evaluate potential confounders such as age, attack duration, and baseline angle configuration. Although gonioscopy was performed preoperatively, detailed documentation of peripheral anterior synechiae was not consistently recorded, which limits anatomical interpretation.

The Sirius device offers good repeatability for anterior chamber parameters, but unlike ultrasound biomicroscopy or AS-OCT, it does not visualize the scleral spur or iridocorneal junction in detail. These differences should be considered when interpreting angle configuration metrics across platforms.

While previous studies have reported similar biometric outcomes using AS-OCT and Pentacam, our study contributes to the literature by specifically applying Scheimpflug-Placido topography in a homogenous cohort of AACG patients undergoing early lens extraction. This reinforces the reproducibility and clinical utility of this modality.

In summary, this study underscores the utility of Scheimpflug-Placido disc tomography in detecting anterior segment changes following early lens extraction in AACG patients. We observed significant deepening of the anterior chamber, widening of the ICA, and substantial reduction in IOP, coupled with increased ACV. These results support the efficacy of early phacoemulsification as a primary intervention in appropriately selected cases of AACG.

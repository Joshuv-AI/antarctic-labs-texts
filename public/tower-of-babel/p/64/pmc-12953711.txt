The Impact of Arbaeen Long‐Walk on the Health of Pilgrims: A Qualitative Study
Authors: Khadijeh Moulaei, Fatemeh Dinari, Esmat Mashoof, Ali Akbar Haghdoost
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground and AimsThe Arbaeen long walk (ALW) is an annual religious pilgrimage in which over 20 million Muslims walk more than 80 km between Najaf and Karbala in Iraq. While this event may affect participants' health, its impact has not been comprehensively studied. This study aimed to explore the perceived effects of the Arbaeen walk on individuals' health across multiple dimensions.MethodsIn this qualitative study, 19 university students who participated in ALW were interviewed using a semi‐structured guide. Content analysis was applied to the interview data using MAXQDA 2018. The study followed the Consolidated Criteria for Reporting Qualitative Research (COREQ) to ensure methodological rigor.ResultsA total of 264 initial codes were identified, which were consolidated into 78 unique codes, including 52 positive and 24 negative outcomes. These groups comprised 78 unique codes, with 52 codes representing positive outcomes of the Arbaeen walk and 24 codes representing negative outcomes. Based on frequency, the most significant positive outcomes of the Arbaeen walk were “Peace of mind and heart,” “Cultural exchange with other countries,” and “Strengthening the relationship between God and individuals.” In terms of negative outcomes, the code frequency highlighted the prevalence of “Outbreak of infectious diseases,” “Physical fatigue,” and “Excessive waste generation.”ConclusionParticipation in the Arbaeen walk is associated with diverse positive effects on physical, mental, and social well‐being, although some negative health outcomes were also reported. These findings highlight the broad scope of health impacts related to this pilgrimage and underscore the need for further investigation into its effects.Keywords: Arbaeen, health status, pilgrimage, walking

Introduction

Health impact assessment (HIA) is a pragmatic method employed to assess the potential health ramifications of a policy, program, or project on a community, with special consideration for vulnerable or marginalized populations [1]. It serves as a bridge between decision‐makers, stakeholders, and the community, offering evidence‐based insights into how various factors, such as environmental changes, urban development, or social programs, can influence the well‐being of populations [1, 2]. By considering the multifaceted aspects of health, including physical, mental, and social dimensions, HIA provides a comprehensive framework for informed decision‐making that prioritizes health and equity [3]. Despite the numerous advantages of HIA, regrettably, no specific evaluation is conducted concerning mass gatherings.

The World Health Organization (WHO) defines a mass gathering as an event, planned or unplanned, that brings together a significant number of people at a specific location for a defined period of time and a particular purpose [4]. The biggest peaceful mass gathering in the world is the Arbaeen walk [5, 6]. Arbaeen walk is an annual Shia Muslim religious event that commemorates the 40th day after the martyrdom of Imam Hussain Alaihis‐Salam, the grandson of the Prophet Muhammad sallallahu alaihi wasallam, and his companions in the Battle of Karbala [4]. It is observed by millions of pilgrims who walk a distance of approximately 80 km from the city of Najaf to the holy city of Karbala in Iraq. This ritual is known as Arbaeen walk or the Arbaeen pilgrimage [7]. Arbaeen walk brings numerous benefits to individuals in various aspects of their health and well‐being. It offers spiritual reflection and deepens faith, and positively impacts mental health by improving mood and enhancing cognitive function [8]. Moreover, Arbaeen walking brings physical fitness and health, and increased endurance. Yousofi and Hashemi [9] also pointed out that Arbaeen walking serves as a social health strategy that contributes to the production and reproduction of various types of health, including physical, mental, social, and spiritual well‐being. Therefore, it is of utmost importance to assess the health effects of this significant walk. Evaluating the health effects of Arbaeen walking will help to gain a comprehensive understanding of the physical and mental health of the participants and clarify potential health risks and benefits. Such an evaluation can inform safety measures, healthcare provision, and guidance for participants, ensuring their overall health and safety [10]. Furthermore, it contributes to the body of knowledge regarding mass gatherings, offering insights into the public health implications of large‐scale events. Ultimately, this assessment aids in optimizing the participants' experience while promoting public health and safety, making it an invaluable endeavor [1].

As far as we know, there has been no research conducted to determine the various effects of Arbaeen walking on individuals' health. Some studies have focused on exploring stakeholders' experiences related to the preparedness and challenges of the healthcare system [4]. In addition, these studies have investigated risk factors and symptoms of infectious diseases among Arbaeen participants [11], as well as respiratory symptoms and associated risk and protective factors in individuals who participated in the Arbaeen walking pilgrimage [12]. So, as the Arbaeen pilgrimage or walking continues to attract more participants every year, it is important to study the potential health benefits of this event. Understanding the impact of Arbaeen walking on people's health can help promote the event as a means of improving the overall health and well‐being of individuals and communities. So, the aim of this study is to investigate the impact of Arbaeen walking on individuals' health across different dimensions.

Methods

Study Design

Following the COREQ guidelines, this study investigated the impact of the Arbaeen walk on individuals' health status (Appendix A). Data were collected through semi‐structured interviews with students of Kerman University of Medical Sciences (KUMS) who participated in the walk and analyzed using qualitative content analysis based on the methodology described by Graneheim and Lundman [13]. This approach emphasizes systematic coding and categorization of textual data to identify themes and patterns, allowing an in‐depth understanding of participants' experiences while ensuring rigor, transparency, and reflexivity throughout the research process.

Research Team and Reflexivity

The study interviews were conducted by a team of researchers with diverse expertise. The interviewers included a PhD in Medical Informatics, and a PhD in Health Information Management. At the time of the study, all team members were actively engaged in research and had prior experience and training in qualitative research methods. A professional relationship was established with participants before the study commenced. Participants were informed about the researchers' background, academic credentials, and the purpose of the study. The research team reflected on their own assumptions, potential biases, and interests in the topic to ensure rigor and transparency throughout the study.

Study Population and Sample

The study population included students of KUMS who participated in the Arbaeen walk. After submitting the approval obtained from the ethical committee of KUMS to the cultural affairs specialist at the university, the researchers were provided with the phone numbers of 30 students who participated in the Arbaeen walk. Convenience sampling was used, and an invitation was sent to all these students. Twenty‐two students accepted the invitation. Finally, according to the inclusion and exclusion criteria, 19 participants were included in the study.

Inclusion criteria were as follows:

The exclusion criteria also encompassed non‐cooperation of the students at any stage of the research.

To ensure maximum variation in perspectives, both male and female students from different academic levels and fields of study were included. Participants were approached via telephone, and interviews were conducted face‐to‐face at locations and times convenient for them. No one else was present during the interviews besides the participants and researchers. Three students who initially accepted the invitation were excluded due to not meeting the criteria or withdrawing, resulting in a final sample of 19 participants. Demographic information, including age, gender, and education level, was collected prior to the interviews.

Data Collection

The interviews were conducted by two members of the research team following a semi‐structured interview guide developed by the authors. The guide included open‐ended questions such as: “Can you describe your experiences during the Arbaeen walk?” and “How did participation in the walk affect your physical, mental, or social health, as well as the health of other pilgrims?” Probing questions were also used to encourage deeper reflection and elaboration. The interview guide was pilot‐tested with a small number of participants to ensure clarity and relevance.

Once informed consent was obtained, interviews were conducted individually and face‐to‐face at locations and times convenient for the participants. Participants were assured that all information would be kept confidential. Before the interviews, the research objectives were explained, and demographic information such as age, gender, and field of study was collected. All interviews were audio‐recorded, and field notes were made during and immediately after each interview to capture additional observations and contextual details. The average duration of each interview was approximately 30 min. Repeat interviews were not conducted.

Data collection continued until data saturation was achieved, which occurred after 17 interviews, with two additional interviews conducted to confirm that no new themes or concepts emerged. Transcripts were returned to participants for review and confirmation of accuracy, ensuring credibility and enhancing the trustworthiness of the findings.

Data Analysis

Interviews were analyzed using Graneheim and Lundman's qualitative content analysis method [13]. Each interview was transcribed by one of the researchers into a Word file and then imported into MAXQDA 2018 software for data management. Two researchers independently reviewed the transcripts to identify codes, sub‐themes, and themes, which were derived inductively from the data rather than predetermined. An expert in qualitative research independently reviewed all coded data to ensure reliability and consistency in coding.

To enhance the trustworthiness and validity of the findings, several strategies were employed: member checking was conducted by sharing summaries of interpretations with participants for confirmation, peer debriefing was performed through regular discussions among the research team, and triangulation was ensured by involving researchers with diverse disciplinary backgrounds.

Descriptive statistics (frequency and percentage) were used to analyze participants' demographic information, which was processed using SPSS 23. A coding tree illustrating the hierarchy of themes and sub‐themes was developed during the analysis, providing a clear framework for organizing the data.

Ethical Considerations

This study received ethics approval from the Research Ethics Committee of KUMS (ethics approval number: IR.KMU.REC.1402.324). Informed consent was obtained from all participants.

The participants of the Arbaeen walk emphasized the significance of ethical and legal responsibility in promoting social health. The participants commended the adherence to ethical principles and laws, specifically highlighting the remarkable absence of women's rights violations during the walk. They expressed that women were respected and prioritized in various aspects, such as queuing for food and boarding buses. The Arbaeen walk was also recognized for guaranteeing the safety of women, creating an environment free from sexual violence, despite the large crowds of men participating in the pilgrimage.

Many participants reported smoking less during the pilgrimage out of consideration for others, and some even made a vow to quit smoking after the journey, inspired by their devotion to Imam Hussain (AS). Furthermore, participants witnessed instances where individuals who had engaged in stealing prior to the pilgrimage renounced such behavior, driven by their love and reverence for Imam Hussain. The Arbaeen walk, therefore, played a role in fostering positive change and reducing delinquency among participants.

Physical Health

Rising Threats to Physical Health

Participants of the Arbaeen walk expressed concerns about the rising threats to physical health they encountered. These included the outbreak of infectious diseases due to the large crowd, which can increase the risk of respiratory infections and diarrheal diseases, as well as experiencing physical fatigue, blisters, and foot pain from walking long distances. The summer heat in Iraq posed challenges like heat exhaustion, headache, and dizziness. Allergies were exacerbated, and participants reported muscular and skeletal pain. Furthermore, dehydration was a common issue due to the extensive walking distance. These experiences highlight the importance of being prepared, taking precautions, and staying hydrated during the Arbaeen walk to mitigate potential health risks.

In my opinion, the large crowd of people who participate in the Arbaeen pilgrimage can increase the risk of contracting infectious diseases such as respiratory infections and diarrheal diseases. In fact, I personally experienced getting the flu after the trip.

Incompatibility of the Body With Some Conditions

Participants of the Arbaeen walk highlighted two rising threats to physical health: hot air and incompatibility of foods with physical conditions. They expressed the difficulty of walking in extremely hot air during the summer, leading to excessive sweating and discomfort. Additionally, some participants mentioned that certain foods consumed during the walk may not be compatible with their physical conditions, potentially causing discomfort or health issues.

Discussion

This study is the first qualitative study to identify the effects of the Arbaeen walk on people's health status, providing rich information in this regard. In this study, we identified a total of 78 unique codes (52 positive outcomes and 24 negative outcomes) divided into two groups: positive outcomes of Arbaeen walk and negative outcomes of Arbaeen walk. In the positive outcomes, the themes of mental health, social health, spiritual health, and physical health emerged from the analysis of the participants' experiences. Notably, the most prominent positive outcomes, as indicated by frequency, were “peace of mind and heart,” “getting to know the culture of other countries,” and “increased relationship between God and man.”

Moreover, negative outcomes were then categorized into four main themes: physical health, personal and public health, social health, and mental health. Among negative outcomes, the most significant were the outbreak of infectious diseases, physical fatigue, and creating a large amount of waste. In the following, the positive and negative outcomes of the Arbaeen pilgrimage are discussed.

Positive Outcomes

The results of this study indicate that engaging in Arbaeen walking has significant positive effects on participants' mental health. The pilgrimage experience offers a sense of peace and tranquility, acting as a form of entertainment that boosts energy levels and fosters positive emotions. Furthermore, it enables individuals to develop greater resilience and acceptance towards life's challenges while enhancing their adaptability and independence. Some studies support the notion that engaging in Arbaeen walking indeed has significant positive effects on participants' mental health. A study conducted by Tajbakhsh [14]. found that participants reported a notable increase in feelings of inner calm and emotional well‐being during the Arbaeen walk. This sense of peace and tranquility is believed to arise from the spiritual and meditative aspects of the pilgrimage, as participants engage in prayer and reflection while traversing the route. Furthermore, Arbaeen walking serves as a form of entertainment that uplifts participants' energy levels and fosters positive emotions [14]. Research by Rezadoost [15] demonstrated a significant increase in participants' self‐reported levels of enthusiasm, joy, and vitality during the pilgrimage. The collective nature of the walk, accompanied by the presence of fellow pilgrims, creates an atmosphere of celebration and unity, contributing to a positive and uplifting experience. Matthews study [16] also showed that the act of physically walking and engaging in physical activity during the pilgrimage also promotes the release of endorphins and oxytocin, leading to an enhanced sense of well‐being and increased energy levels.

In addition to the immediate effects on mood and emotional well‐being, Arbaeen walking has been shown to have lasting benefits for participants' mental health. Some studies [17] have indicated that the pilgrimage cultivates resilience and acceptance towards life's challenges. The physically demanding nature of the walk, often spanning long distances and enduring harsh weather conditions, provides participants with an opportunity to test their endurance and develop a greater sense of resilience [18]. Moreover, Arbaeen walking promotes independence and self‐reliance [14]. Participants often embark on the pilgrimage individually or in small groups, which encourages them to take responsibility for their own well‐being and navigate the journey autonomously. This increased independence and self‐reliance can have a positive impact on individuals' self‐esteem and self‐confidence, leading to greater psychological well‐being.

Moreover, the results of our study show that Arbaeen walking was found to have a profound impact on reducing negative emotions and improving overall mental well‐being. The collective nature of the pilgrimage was described as a potent tool for clearing the mind of negative thoughts, reducing anxiety, stress, anger, and rage. Additionally, the pilgrimage played a crucial role in alleviating feelings of social isolation and loneliness, promoting a sense of connection among participants and diminishing individual pride and arrogance. According to their accounts, Arbaeen walking not only cultivates a positive mindset but also provides comfort and peace, serving as a means to alleviate stress and anxiety. Other studies have further confirmed the significant benefits of Arbaeen walking, aligning with the findings mentioned above. These studies [14, 19] indicate that Arbaeen walking can have a transformative effect on individuals' mental well‐being. The collective nature of the pilgrimage experience has been consistently associated with a reduction in negative emotions and an improvement in overall mental health. Participants reported a profound sense of emotional release and relief, as engaging in Arbaeen walking helped them clear their minds of negative thoughts and emotions, resulting in a greater sense of peace and tranquility. Furthermore, the pilgrimage creates a unique sense of community and connection among participants [20]. Walking alongside millions of fellow pilgrims fosters a shared bond and a collective purpose [14]. This communal aspect of Arbaeen walking not only alleviates feelings of isolation but also diminishes individual pride and arrogance. Participants have expressed a shift in perspective, recognizing the importance of humility, empathy, and interconnectedness with others.

The results of this study also reveal that the Arbaeen walk has a significant positive impact on various aspects of social well‐being. Participants unanimously reported that the pilgrimage fosters unity and strengthens social networks, acting as a catalyst for solidarity and promoting communication between different nations. It was also found to contribute to intergroup relations and intercultural competence, eliminating racial discrimination and enriching participants' cultural understanding. Arbaeen walking by embracing equality and inclusivity, the procession can promote unity among participants from diverse racial backgrounds. Removing racial discrimination aligns with the core values of Islam, fosters a sense of solidarity, and upholds the human rights principles of equality and dignity [21]. Moreover, it sends a positive message to the global community, inspiring others to challenge stereotypes and prejudices, and ultimately contributing to a more harmonious and just society.

The adherence to ethical principles and laws during the walk, along with the absence of women's rights violations and the prioritization of their safety, was highly praised. Other studies [22, 23] have consistently shown that the pilgrimage plays a crucial role in fostering unity and strengthening social bonds among participants. It provides a unique platform for individuals from diverse backgrounds to come together, fostering understanding and appreciation of different cultures and languages. Moreover, Arbaeen walking has been found to promote intergroup relations and intercultural competence, breaking down barriers and promoting inclusivity. Hashjin et al. [23] pointed out that the pilgrimage serves as a powerful demonstration of collective solidarity, transcending nationalities, races, ethnicities, and social statuses. Additionally, studies have highlighted the ethical and legal responsibilities upheld during the Arbaeen walk. Participants in Tajbakhsh study [14] consistently report a heightened adherence to moral and ethical principles during the pilgrimage, exhibiting a greater sense of respect for others and their rights. The emphasis on women's rights and safety is particularly noteworthy, with the pilgrimage creating a secure environment free from gender‐based violence. Furthermore, the transformative nature of the Arbaeen walk extends to individual behavior change, as participants often report reducing delinquent behaviors such as smoking and theft, inspired by their devotion to Imam Hussain.

The study found that the Arbaeen walk has a profound impact on participants' spiritual well‐being. It enhanced their relationship with God and Imam Hussain, leading to personal transformation. Participants experienced answered prayers, fulfilled wishes, divine intervention, and miracles, strengthening their faith. The pilgrimage also promoted self‐control and piousness, enabling participants to abandon sinful behaviors and pursue a virtuous life. Overall, the findings highlight the transformative nature of the Arbaeen walk, emphasizing its significant impact on spiritual health, personal growth, and a deep connection with the divine. Tajbakhsh [14] study, along with other research, confirms the positive impact of Arbaeen walking on spiritual well‐being. Participants experienced an enhanced spiritual state, establishing a deep connection with God and Imam Hussain (A.S). The visit to the sacred shrines solidified their bond with the divine, and they shared remarkable experiences of answered prayers and divine interventions. The pilgrimage also promoted self‐control and piety, inspiring individuals to abandon sinful behaviors. Similarly, the study by Rezadoost and Bahmani [15] supports these findings. Participants in their research reported enriched spiritual health and a deep connection with God and Imam Hussain (A.S). The visit to the holy shrines deepened their religious convictions, and they shared inspiring stories of answered prayers and divine miracles [15]. Furthermore, recent related study has also emphasized the communal and spiritual dimensions of pilgrimage, highlighting how shared religious journeys foster collective identity, mutual support, and strengthened community bonds. Incorporating these perspectives reinforces our findings and provides a broader context for understanding the transformative impact of the Arbaeen walk on both individual spirituality and communal well‐being [24].

The findings of this study underscore the substantial physical health benefits associated with participation in the Arbaeen walk. Participants reported a range of improvements, including weight loss, enhanced metabolism, and improved musculoskeletal health. These self‐reported benefits reflect the prolonged and sustained physical activity involved in the walk, which may contribute to better overall physical functioning and well‐being.

These results are consistent with previous research on the positive effects of walking and prolonged physical activity. For instance, Kim [25] as well as Baker et al. [26] demonstrated that regular walking can improve fitness, support weight management, and reduce risk factors for metabolic disorders. Similarly, Shalbafian and Zarandian [27] found that participation in the Arbaeen walk mitigates fatigue and other negative physical effects, highlighting the walk's role in promoting physical resilience. The consistency of these findings across diverse populations suggests that structured, long‐duration walking activities, such as the Arbaeen walk, can have meaningful impacts on physical health by enhancing metabolic processes, improving musculoskeletal strength, and contributing to general vitality.

Overall, these findings emphasize the importance of integrating culturally and socially meaningful physical activities into daily life, not only for spiritual or communal benefits but also for their measurable impact on physical health. The Arbaeen walk, as a combination of sustained endurance activity and social engagement, represents a unique context in which physical health benefits are amplified through prolonged participation and collective motivation.

Negative Outcomes

The study findings revealed concerns among Arbaeen walk participants regarding rising threats to physical health. These included increased risk of respiratory infections and diarrheal diseases due to the large crowd, physical fatigue, blisters, and foot pain from long distances, challenges of heat exhaustion, headache, and dizziness in the summer heat, exacerbated allergies, muscular and skeletal pain, and common dehydration. These experiences emphasize the importance of preparedness, precautions, and hydration during the Arbaeen walk to mitigate potential health risks. Other studies have also highlighted the challenges associated with Arbaeen walking, corroborating the findings of this study. For example, the research conducted by Soltani et al., [28] reported similar concerns raised by participants, including the risk of respiratory infections, physical fatigue, blisters, foot pain, heat exhaustion, headaches, dizziness, allergies, muscular and skeletal pain, and dehydration. Similarly, the study conducted by Farahmand et al. [29] also revealed the challenges faced by Arbaeen walkers, such as spread of infectious diseases, low back pain and muscle cramps, foot blisters, gastrointestinal diseases, and fever. These consistent findings underscore the need for strategies and solutions to address the challenges of Arbaeen walking and ensure the well‐being of participants.

To mitigate the challenges of Arbaeen walking and promote better physical health, several solutions can be considered. First, adequate preparation is crucial. This includes gradually increasing physical fitness levels through regular exercise and walking prior to the event. It is also important to wear appropriate and comfortable footwear to prevent blisters and foot pain. Hydration is essential during the Arbaeen walk, so carrying a sufficient amount of water and staying hydrated throughout the journey is vital. Moreover, strategies to address heat‐related issues can be implemented, such as wearing lightweight and breathable clothing, using hats and umbrellas for shade, and taking breaks in shaded areas. To prevent respiratory infections, it is advisable to maintain personal hygiene by washing hands frequently and wearing masks, especially in crowded areas. Additionally, awareness campaigns and medical support stations along the walking route can provide necessary assistance, including first aid for injuries, addressing allergies, and offering relief for muscular and skeletal pain.

The study findings reveal concerns about poor hygiene practices and inadequate sanitation during the Arbaeen walk. Participants observed a significant amount of waste and highlighted issues with water availability, cleanliness, and the scarcity of toilets. Poor waste management practices were also noted, leading to waste accumulation. Multiple studies have confirmed the challenges associated with Arbaeen walking, supporting the findings of this study. For example, the research conducted by Karampourian et al. [30] also revealed concerns about poor hygiene practices during the Arbaeen walk. Participants in their study expressed similar observations of excessive waste and inadequate sanitation, including issues with water availability and cleanliness. Likewise, the study conducted by Soltani et al. [28] highlighted poor hygiene and waste management as significant challenges during the Arbaeen walk, emphasizing the scarcity of water and the accumulation of waste along the walking path.

To address the challenges of poor hygiene and inefficient management during the Arbaeen walk, several solutions can be considered. Firstly, improving waste management is crucial. This can be achieved through the installation of sufficient waste bins along the walking route, regular waste collection and disposal, and increased awareness and education on responsible waste disposal among participants. Additionally, ensuring proper maintenance and cleanliness of restroom facilities is essential. Increasing the number of restrooms, maintaining hygiene standards, and providing necessary supplies such as water and soap will enhance the overall sanitation experience for participants. Moreover, organizers should establish water stations along the walking path, ensuring a regular and reliable supply of clean drinking water for participants. Furthermore, promoting hygiene awareness campaigns and encouraging individuals to adhere to basic hygiene principles during the walk can contribute to a cleaner and healthier environment for everyone involved.

The study findings reveal concerns about social health during the Arbaeen walk, including access to essential services and intergroup relations. Participants expressed worries about long queues for food, accommodation, and treatment, leading to inconvenience and delays. Cultural differences and variations in lifestyle among individuals were also observed, posing challenges for adaptation. The findings of the Al‐Ansari et al. [12] study emphasize the importance of addressing social health concerns during the Arbaeen walk. They noted that access to essential services, such as food, accommodation, and medical treatment, is crucial for the well‐being of participants. The reported long queues and delays further highlight the need for improved logistical arrangements. Additionally, the presence of cultural differences and variations in lifestyle among participants underscores the significance of fostering intergroup relations and promoting cultural understanding and adaptation [31].

To address these challenges, several solutions can be implemented. Firstly, improving the efficiency of service delivery is crucial. This can be achieved by increasing the capacity of service providers, implementing better crowd management strategies, and utilizing technology to streamline processes such as registration and distribution of provisions. Creating designated service areas with multiple access points can help reduce waiting times and alleviate the burden on participants. In terms of intergroup relations and intercultural competence, fostering cultural understanding and promoting inclusivity is essential. Education and awareness programs can be organized to promote respect for diverse cultures and lifestyles. Encouraging dialog and interaction among participants from different backgrounds can help bridge the gap and enhance intergroup relations. Providing information and resources on cultural practices, hygiene guidelines, and healthy habits can promote harmonious coexistence and minimize conflicts arising from differences.

Study Limitations

There were limitations in this study that need to be considered. First, because our study was specifically conducted among medical students in KUMS within a particular place and time frame, it is important to recognize that the findings cannot be generalized to students from other universities or the general population of Iran or other countries. It is suggested that other studies with a larger sample size be conducted in populations other than students or in other countries. Second, given that the results have been gathered through semi‐structured interviews, they are regarded as subjective. To address this, it is advisable for future studies to develop a quantitative instrument that can objectively transform and analyze the subjective concepts. The current study can serve as a foundation for such endeavors.

Conclusion

In this study, the effects of Arbaeen walk on people's health status were identified. In conclusion, the findings of this study highlight the profound positive impact of Arbaeen walking on multiple dimensions of health, including physical, mental, social, and spiritual well‐being. However, it is essential to acknowledge that certain risks to health may arise, such as the potential transmission of infectious diseases, fatigue, musculoskeletal pain, blisters, and heatstroke among participants. Furthermore, inadequate personal and public health practices, subpar management of the event, hot weather conditions, and high population density contribute to additional negative consequences. To ensure the continued benefits of Arbaeen walking while mitigating potential risks, it is crucial to implement measures addressing these concerns, improve health management practices, and provide necessary support to participants.

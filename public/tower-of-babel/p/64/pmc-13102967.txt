Trajectory matrix-guided optimal design of non-circular gear train seedling throwing mechanism for rice pot seedlings
Authors: Maile Zhou, Guibin Wang, Tingbo Xu, Jijia He, Yu Deng, Xiao Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractRice transplanting by hand offers advantages such as minimal plant injury and a short recovery period. The key to mechanizing this process lies in replicating the ideal final trajectory of hand-transplanting through mechanical equipment. Based on the motion characteristics of manual transplanting, this study divides the transplanting process into three stages: seedling pickup, seedling transport, and seedling placement. Using the trajectory and posture of the human hand as a reference, an ideal mechanized transplanting trajectory was planned. Manual transplanting was simplified into a 2R open-chain mechanism configuration. A non-circular gear mechanism was employed to constrain the 2R open-chain mechanism to two degrees of freedom, proposing a non-circular gear transplanting mechanism design. Based on normalization principles, the ideal transplanting trajectory and the training trajectory corresponding to the feasible solution domain were normalized and feature-coded. The end-effector trajectory was then represented using a trajectory matrix. By constructing an objective function, a globally optimal mechanism solution was obtained through optimization across the entire solution domain. A test bench for the transplanting machine was developed. High-speed camera analysis of the transplanting mechanism’s working trajectory demonstrated that the optimal mechanism solution reproduces the ideal mechanized transplanting trajectory, meeting the operational requirements for mechanized transplanting.Keywords: Rice seedlings, Seedling throwing mechanism, Trajectory matrix, Non-circular gears, PoseSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Seedling broadcasting refers to a rice planting method where seedlings are either thrown directly into the paddy field or fed into a seedling guiding device for subsequent implantation. It offers prominent advantages such as a short seedling recovery period after transplantation, minimal transplanting injury, well-developed root systems, increased effective tillers, and higher panicle number and spikelet number per unit area, thus being recognized as one of the high-yield cultivation techniques for rice. Currently, seedling broadcasting is categorized into manual broadcasting and mechanical broadcasting. Manual broadcasting is an unordered planting mode, which results in poor field ventilation and hinders the mechanization of subsequent plant protection and harvesting operations. Mechanical broadcasting generally involves the orderly broadcasting and transplanting of seedlings into paddy fields using a seedling broadcaster. Compared with manual transplanting, mechanical broadcasting achieves more tillers and greater biomass accumulation during the vegetative growth stage, as well as higher post-heading biomass accumulation, larger leaf area index, and lower leaf senescence rate1–4. The seedling broadcasting mechanism is the core working component of the seedling broadcaster, with types including manipulator-type, counter-roller-type, ejector rod-type, rotary-type, and pneumatic-type. Manipulator-type, counter-roller-type, ejector rod-type, and rotary-type broadcasting mechanisms have strict requirements on seedling quality; factors such as seedling thickness, size, and position in the seedling tray can significantly affect the operation success rate and seedling damage rate of the mechanism. In contrast, the pneumatic-type broadcasting mechanism utilizes airflow to act on pot seedlings, effectively avoiding seedling damage. However, it requires additional fans and control systems, leading to a relatively complex structure.

Studies have shown that the end trajectory of the seedling broadcasting mechanism is a key factor affecting operational performance, making it a research hotspot in the field of mechanical seedling broadcasting5–7. Chaitanya, D.N.V. et al. proposed a transplanter design scheme based on planting uprightness, which enables vertical seedling planting at a sufficient depth. This not only prevents seedling detachment but also ensures uniform ripening of rice8. Vibhakar C. Chaudhary et al. developed a manual rice transplanter based on a four-bar linkage mechanism, featuring low cost and simple design, which saves 50% of labor9. ISLAM et al. proposed a novel long-needle multi-clamping automatic transplanting mechanism10, suitable for small-scale transplanters. Driven by gears and crank-slider, this mechanism achieves a linear seedling picking trajectory. Assal, S. F. et al. designed a partially decoupled 3-degree-of-freedom (3-DOF) multi-claw pot seedling transplanting mechanism for open-field agriculture11,12, capable of extracting seedlings from trays and distributing them for planting. The safety of the mechanism in terms of force and deformation has been verified. K. Rahul et al. developed a 2-DOF parallel transplanting mechanism13, adopting a mechatronic approach to achieve automation and structural simplification. It utilizes microcontrollers and sensors to realize precise clamping and placing of seedlings. Xin et al. also proposed a non-circular gear-linkage combined transplanting mechanism14, which can synergistically complete four processes: seedling picking, conveying, film piercing with hole digging, and planting. Bench tests confirmed that it meets the requirements of mechanized integrated film mulch transplanting for rice pot seedlings. Shi et al. designed an eight-row duckbill-type planting mechanism driven by motors and cylinders15, which satisfies the agronomic requirements of dense planting with small plant and row spacing. Zhou et al. developed a fully automatic rotary transplanting mechanism for vegetable pot seedlings, capable of completing a series of operations including seedling picking, transportation, film penetration, hole forming, and planting16. Sun et al. proposed a transplanting mechanism with a symmetric dual-carrier structure driven by a cam-non-circular gear combined mechanism17, which generates variable-speed oscillation to form a pointed seedling picking trajectory. Xu et al. designed a variable-speed well cellar-type hole-forming transplanting mechanism for hilly and mountainous transplanters18, adopting a parallel four-bar linkage based on non-circular gears. The hole-forming law of the interaction between the mechanism and soil was analyzed, and the parameters of the hole-forming mechanism were optimized. Yu et al. proposed a hybrid six-bar single-degree-of-freedom pot seedling picking-planting integrated transplanting mechanism constrained by non-circular gears19, which achieves a “hawk-beak-shaped” static trajectory for seedling picking and an “approximately linear” dynamic trajectory for seedling planting, completing seedling picking, conveying, planting, and resetting in one cycle. Zhang Ni developed an ejector-pot and stem-clamping transplanting mechanism20, where the ejector-pot mechanism performs horizontal reciprocating linear motion, and the stem-clamping, picking, and throwing mechanism conducts horizontal telescopic and vertical reciprocating motion, jointly realizing the seedling picking and throwing functions. Biao Zhou et al. designed an automatic seedling picking and dropping mechanism combining electric and pneumatic drives21, using an electromagnetic thimble mechanism combined with a pneumatic flexible gripper to complete seedling ejection and clamping. The consistency of the trajectory and the feasibility of the mechanism were verified by comparing theoretical analysis, simulation analysis, and transplanting tests.

Addressing the issues of low success rates and high seedling damage rates in existing transplanting mechanisms, this paper proposes a novel non-circular wheel system transplanting mechanism based on anthropomorphic transplanting motions. Structural parameters of the mechanism were optimized using the proposed pose matrix algorithm, completing its design. The pose matrix optimization method employed in this study enables automatic optimization, significantly reducing trajectory optimization time. It effectively addresses challenges in trajectory optimization, such as numerous unknown parameters, strong coupling, and solving complex nonlinear systems of equations. This provides important reference value for trajectory optimization research in mechanisms. The designed rice transplanter effectively performs rice seedling transplantation tasks, contributing to the advancement of transplanter research.

Planning and realization of seedling throwing posture

The fundamental requirements for transplanting rice seedlings are high efficiency and low damage, and manual transplanting most readily achieves these objectives. This study analyzed the manual transplanting motion using high-speed camera technology, with a frame rate of 300 frames per second. The manual transplanting process can be summarized into key actions: grasping, pulling seedlings, transporting seedlings, planting seedlings, and returning to the starting position, as shown in Fig. 1. When fingers reach the seedling position, they grasp the stem near the substrate and pull the seedling out along its growth direction. After separating the seedling from the tray, fingers transport it to the planting position below. Upon reaching this position, fingers release the seedling, propelling it forward. After completing the transplanting, the hand returns to the seedling-grabbing position for the next cycle. By continuously repeating the actions of grabbing, pulling, transporting, transplanting, and returning to the starting position, the operator achieves continuous transplanting operations.

The trajectory and attitude of manual seedling throwing are the key factors affecting the performance of seedling throwing. In this study, the trajectory and posture of the elbow and hand during manual seedling throwing were analyzed based on high-speed camera technology, as shown in Fig. 2(a). The trajectory of the seedling throwing process can be divided into the stages of seedling taking, seedling transportation and planting, and the trajectory formed is a “penguin”-shaped trajectory, and the hand posture adapts to the requirements of seedling throwing. The manual seedling throwing process can be regarded as the movement of the 2R chain opening mechanism, the big arm can be regarded as hinged on the shoulder, transformed into a crank, the small arm and the hand can be regarded as handed over at the end of the big arm, and transformed into a pendulum, and the composite motion of the crank and the pendulum reproduces the trajectory and attitude of the seedling throwing. In this study, the trajectory and attitude of mechanized seedling throwing were planned based on the physical characteristics of seedlings and the motion requirements of the 2R open-chain mechanism, as shown in Fig. 2(b).

Mechanism analysis of seedling throwing mechanism

It is found that the movement requirements of the composite seedling throwing mechanism in the working space of the 2R open-chain mechanism. The key to the design of the seedling throwing mechanism is to reproduce the motion law of the R pair, and the author uses the non-circular gear train to constrain the degree of freedom of the 2R open-chain mechanism, and reproduces the trajectory and attitude required by the unequal velocity transmission characteristics of the non-circular gear pair. The seedling throwing mechanism is usually composed of a driving component and a seedling taking arm, and the driving part drives the seedling taking arm to pass through a complex seedling throwing trajectory, and at the same time makes the seedling taking arm meet the attitude of the key process of seedling throwing, the most critical posture occurs during seedling removal and placement. When removing seedlings, they should be lifted along a straight line to minimize damage. During placement, maintain the seedlings’ upright position as much as possible to ensure planting quality.

As shown in Fig. 3, the seedling throwing mechanism proposed by the author is composed of a non-circular gear train (driving component) and two seedling picking arms. The non-circular gear train consists of 1 non-circular sun gear, 2 non-circular planetary wheels and planet carriers. The planetary carrier is hinged to the frame and is driven by the transplanter power to turn counterclockwise. The non-circular sun gear is coaxially hinged to the frame with the planetary carrier, and is driven by the transplanter power to rotate in the same direction as the planetary carrier, and the rotation speed of the non-circular sun gear is twice the speed of the planetary carrier. The parameters of the two non-circular planetary wheels are the same, which are respectively hinged at both ends of the planetary carrier, and the two non-circular planetary wheels are meshed with the non-circular sun wheel respectively to rotate. The two seedling picking arms are fixed on two non-circular planetary wheels respectively, on the one hand, they move in a circular motion with the planetary carrier, and on the other hand, they rotate at unequal speed with the non-circular planetary wheels relative to the planetary carrier, and the two movements are synthesized to make the seedling picking arm reproduce the trajectory and attitude of throwing seedlings.

Optimization of institutional parameters

Trajectory matrix solving

The trajectory of seedling throwing is the key to determine the performance of seedling throwing, and the core of parameter optimization lies in the evaluation method of the trajectory. There are two kinds of comprehensive methods for mechanism trajectories: forward design and reverse design, the forward design reproduces the target trajectory by establishing the kinematic model of the mechanism and then optimizing the parameters, and the reverse design takes the target trajectory as the constraint to reverse solve the feasible solution domain of the mechanism. Whether it is forward design or reverse design, the core of it is the trajectory evaluation method. At present, the key value points of the target trajectory are obtained mainly by fitting or interpolation, and then the proximity of the value points is used to evaluate the quality of the trajectory. Trajectory synthesis involves the problem of solving complex nonlinear equations, and there are many unknown parameters and strong coupling.

In this study, the planned seedling throwing trajectories were normalized to standard processing, and finally the trajectory information was represented by the trajectory matrix. According to the curvature, looping and basic shape of the trajectories, the training trajectories of the whole solution domain are clustered, and the trajectory groups that approximate the seedling throwing trajectories are obtained, and the trajectory groups are normalized to the standard coding. Firstly, the two farthest points on the trajectory are placed horizontally, the distance between the two farthest points is scaled to the standard distance, and the coordinate system is established with the midpoint of the two farthest points as the coordinate origin. After transformation, the effects of translation, rotation, and scaling on trajectory feature information are eliminated, as shown in Fig. 4(a). Then, the coordinate system is uniformly divided into grid cells, with grid regions identifying target trajectories, as depicted in Fig. 4(b). Grid regions containing trajectories are marked as 1, while other grid regions are marked as 0, constituting the feature encoding, as illustrated in Fig. 4(c). Finally, the grid marked with 1 s and 0 s is converted into a matrix, the trajectory representation matrix, as shown in Fig. 4(d); Finally, assign the pose values of each pose point to the elements of the trajectory matrix to obtain the pose representation matrix, as shown in Fig. 4(e). The pose representation matrix encapsulates all information about the target’s pose, including both trajectory and orientation. A finer grid division results in a matrix with more rows and columns, yielding more comprehensive pose feature information.

Optimization of institutional parameters

The rice transplanting mechanism comprises 31 design parameters, primarily including transmission ratio parameters, non-circular gear structure parameters, seedling-picking arm structure parameters, and the initial mounting angle of the planetary carrier. The influence patterns of each parameter on the trajectory are not significant, exhibiting strong coupling and nonlinearity. This study employs the developed seedling-transplanting mechanism optimization design software to perform parameter optimization. The trajectory matrix corresponding to the anthropomorphic transplanting trajectory serves as the target matrix. The optimization process aims to maximize the degree of overlap with the target trajectory.

Analysis of the working process of the seedling throwing mechanism

The seedling throwing mechanism needs to complete the key stages such as seedling taking, seedling transportation, seedling pushing and return trip in a working cycle, and the working cycle of the seedling throwing mechanism is shown in Fig. 7. When the initial position (the angle of the planetary carrier is 0°), the seedling arm I is in the seedling transportation stage, and the seedling arm II is in the reset stage. When the planetary carrier is turned 52° counterclockwise from the initial position, the seedling arm II completes the reset process; When the planetary carrier turns 135° counterclockwise from the initial position, the seedling picking arm II starts to take seedling; When the planetary carrier turns 152° counterclockwise from the initial position, the seedling arm I. moves to the seedling pushing position; When the planetary carrier turns 232° counterclockwise from the initial position, the seedling arm I. completes the reset process; When the planetary carrier turns 315° counterclockwise from the initial position, the seedling taking arm I. moves to the seedling taking position; When the planetary carrier is turned 332° counterclockwise from the initial position, the seedling arm II moves to the planting position. In a working cycle of the seedling throwing mechanism, the seedling transport stage of seedling arm I and seedling arm II corresponds to 180° of the planetary carrier angle respectively, and the reset stage corresponds to 80° of the planetary carrier angle respectively.

Diagram of the working cycle of the seedling throwing mechanism.

The working process of the seedling throwing mechanism is shown in Fig. 8. In the seedling extraction stage, the seedling picking arm is required to move to the lower stem of the rice bowl at a lower speed, and the seedlings are clamped in a certain attitude. In the seedling transportation stage, the seedling bowl is clamped with the seedling arm, and the seedlings are pulled out of the hole tray along the seedling growth method, and then the seedlings are transported to the planting position. In the seedling transportation stage, it is also required that the seedling arm clamp the seedling and deflect it at a certain angle, so that the seedling is in an upright state when it is transported to the seedling throwing position; In the seedling pushing stage, the seedling pushing device in the seedling picking arm is required to act on the rice bowl and put the seedlings into the seedling guiding device upright. In the return stage, the seedling arm is required to move to the initial state, and the position posture is clamped at the same time.

Pilot studies

Trajectory and velocity analysis

According to the optimized parameters, the author completed the structural design of the seedling throwing mechanism, established a virtual prototype, and practiced the virtual simulation analysis. The simulation of the virtual prototype is shown in Fig. 9, and the simulation test shows that the throwing arm of the seedling throwing mechanism can complete the stages of seedling taking, transporting, pushing and return at one time according to the design requirements. The trajectory and attitude obtained by the simulation test are consistent with the theoretical analysis, and the correctness of the theoretical analysis method and the virtual prototype are verified by each other.

Figure 11 shows the velocity curve at the end of the seedling throwing arm when the planetary carrier rotation speed is 150 r/min (the seedling throwing frequency is 300 holes/min). When taking seedlings, the velocity of the end point of the seedling throwing arm in the horizontal direction is −0.39 m/s, the velocity in the vertical direction is 0.16 m/s, the combined velocity is 0.42 m/s, and the angle between the velocity direction and the seedling growth direction is 12.69°. When pushing seedlings, the velocity of the end of the seedling throwing arm in the horizontal direction is 0.85 m/s, the velocity in the vertical direction is −0.97 m/s, the combined velocity is 1.29 m/s, and the angle between the velocity direction and the seedling growth direction is 13.31°. The results show that the speed of the seedling throwing arm is small relative to the seedlings, which is conducive to ensuring the accuracy of seedling picking and improving the success rate of seedling taking. When pushing the seedlings, the throwing arm completes the pushing of the seedlings at a large speed, which can effectively avoid the phenomenon of pushing the seedlings back to the belt. Whether it is seedling taking or pushing, the angle between the seedling throwing arm and the seedling growth direction is about 13°, so that the seedling pulling force and the seedling pushing force are similar to the seedling growth direction, and the damage to the seedling by torsional moment and bending moment is effectively avoided.

Based on the virtual model created, the author first installs the non-circular gear train, ensuring the precise installation phase angle between the sun gear and planetary gears, as shown in Fig. 12(a). Next, the housing, planetary shaft, cam, and other components of the seedling-throwing mechanism are installed. A critical point in this step is paying attention to the installation angle of the cam, which is one of the key steps to ensure the accuracy of the seedling-throwing mechanism’s trajectory, as shown in Fig. 12(b). Finally, the seeding arm was assembled and mounted onto the non-circular gearbox. This ensured the fork within the seeding arm made tight contact with the cam, enabling the seeding arm’s grasping and release actions, as shown in Fig. 12(c). This completed the assembly of the seeding mechanism.

Conclusion

Based on the action of manual seedling throwing, the trajectory and posture of anthropomorphic seedling throwing were analyzed. According to the physical and mechanical characteristics of the seedlings and the characteristics of the seedling throwing mechanism, the maximum allowable deviation of the mechanized seedling throwing of rice bowl seedlings at the critical stage was obtained. Constrained by the maximum allowable deviation, the ideal mechanized seedling throwing trajectory and attitude were obtained.

Based on the basic configuration of 2R open-chain, the degree of freedom of the 2R open-chain mechanism is constrained by the non-circular gear system, and the non-circular wheel system is proposed. The seedling throwing mechanism is composed of two parts: non-circular wheel train and seedling throwing arm, and can successively complete the stages of seedling taking, haulom transporting, haulom pushing and return trip in a cycle.

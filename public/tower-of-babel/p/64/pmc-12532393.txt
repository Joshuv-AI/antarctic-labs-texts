Effects of PCSK9 inhibitor evolocumab on preventing early neurological deterioration in acute ischemic stroke patients with or without large artery atherosclerosis: a subgroup analysis of a randomized trial
Authors: Jiahui Liu, Ying Li, Wen Tian, Hua Cao, Xidan Li, Liyuan Cheng, Xiaofei Ji, Jing Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOur previous study has demonstrated the preventive effects of proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9 (PCSK9) inhibitor on early neurological deterioration (END) in patients with acute non-cardiogenic ischemic stroke. Here, we performed a post hoc subgroup analysis to investigate whether the large artery atherosclerosis (LAA) subtype contributed to the clinical outcomes.MethodsAccording to the Trial of Org 10172 in Acute Stroke Treatment (TOAST) classification, patients were divided into LAA and small vessel occlusion (SVO) subgroups. In each subgroup, patients were further subdivided into combination therapy of evolocumab and atorvastatin (PI group) and atorvastatin monotherapy (AT group). The primary outcome was END within 7 days, defined as a ≥ 2-point National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score increase or NIHSS motor score ≥ 1-point compared with baseline.ResultsA total of 272 patients were included: 186 were categorized into the LAA subtype (93 in the PI group and 93 in the AT group) and 86 into the SVO subtype (43 in the PI group and 43 in the AT group). Introduction

Ischemic stroke is the major cause of death and disability worldwide [1]. Early neurological deterioration (END) is an independent risk factor of poor prognosis after acute ischemic stroke (AIS), which is common in clinical practice but remains a challenge to overcome [2]. The incidence of END after AIS varies widely from 5% to 40%, depending on differences in definitions, assessment timing, and study populations [3–5]. Numerous independent risk factors contribute to the development of END, including blood pressure, blood glucose, low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) levels, baseline National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) scores, infarct location, intracranial arterial stenosis, microemboli, and inflammation [6–8]. Currently, the effective treatments for AIS include recanalization therapies such as systemic thrombolysis and mechanical thrombectomy. However, due to the relatively narrow time window and numerous contraindications, only a small portion of patients can benefit from these treatments [9]. In addition, antiplatelet therapy is the main treatment [10]. Nevertheless, even with dual antiplatelet therapy, AIS patients remain at high risk of END, leading to a poor prognosis [11–14]. Consequently, the prevention of END in AIS patients has become a hot issue in clinical research.

Proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9 (PCSK9) inhibitors, a novel lipid-lowering drug, have garnered attention for their rapid onset of action and potent lipid-lowering effect. PCSK9 inhibitors can bind free PCSK9 protein, thereby competitively inhibiting the binding of PCSK9 to low-density lipoprotein receptors (LDL-R). This action reduces the degradation of LDL-R with the resultant decrease of serum low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) levels [15]. Beyond modulating LDL-C levels, PCSK9 inhibitors could inhibit the progression of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease through multiple mechanisms, including suppressing pro-inflammatory cytokine release, platelet activation, thrombosis, and cell death [16, 17]. Based on these insights, our group recently conducted a clinical study of PCSK9 inhibitors for the prevention of END in AIS patients [18]. We found that combination therapy of evolocumab and atorvastatin within 24 h of stroke onset significantly reduced the incidence of END and improved functional outcomes at 90 days compared with statin monotherapy. Simultaneously, in the acute phase of ischemic stroke, this combination therapy has been shown to significantly enhance LDL-C target achievement rate, and inhibit the elevation of peripheral blood inflammatory mediators Interleukin (IL)−6.

Ischemic stroke is a heterogeneous disease with various etiologies. According to the Trial of Org 10172 in Acute Stroke Treatment (TOAST) classification, about 60% of these etiologies are related to large artery atherosclerosis (LAA) and small vessel occlusion (SVO) subtypes [19, 20]. Both the LAA and SVO subtypes are associated with a high risk of END, although through distinct mechanisms. In patients with LAA, the reported incidence of END is relatively high, ranging from 12% to 38.5% [21–23]. This is primarily attributed to unstable atherosclerotic plaques, thrombus expansion, or hemodynamic disturbance. In patients with SVO, the incidence of END remains significant, estimated between 12% and 25% [22, 24, 25]. The capsular warning syndrome (CWS), though representing a fraction of these cases, exemplifies the profound instability in this subtype [26]. Given that the effect of stroke subtypes on clinical outcomes after stroke has been previously reported [27–30], we assumed that there may also be an association between stroke subtypes and the effect of evolocumab treatment on functional outcomes. Previous studies have demonstrated the efficacy of evolocumab in patients with coronary heart disease, including significant LDL-C lowering, reduction in cardiovascular events, and plaque stabilization [31, 32]. Thus, we hypothesized that patients with the LAA subtype who have similar risk factors and pathophysiologic mechanisms to coronary heart disease may benefit from evolocumab treatment.

In this context, we aimed to further investigate the efficacy and safety of evolocumab combined with atorvastatin versus atorvastatin monotherapy in patients with AIS of different stroke etiologies, and we searched for the interaction between stroke subtypes and treatment effect of different lipid-lowering therapies.

Methods

Study design and participants

Details on the design and major results of the trial were described in previous reports [18]. Briefly, our previous study was a prospective, multicenter, randomized, blinded-endpoint trial conducted at six centers in China from April 30, 2022, to July 02, 2023. It aimed to evaluate the efficacy of PCSK9 inhibitors combined with atorvastatin in preventing END for patients with acute ischemic stroke. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Dalian Medical University and conformed to the ethical guidelines of the Declaration of Helsinki for Human Research. This study was registered with the China Clinical Trials Registry (No: ChicTR2200059445). Every patient or their legal representatives provided written informed consent before participation. Our reporting follows the CONSORT statement for reporting parallel-group randomized trials (http://www.consort-statement.org/consort-statement/flowdiagram).

Eligible patients were aged 18–80 years, diagnosed with acute non-cardiogenic ischemic stroke within 24 h after stroke onset, and had been functioning independently before stroke with modified Rankin Scale (mRS) scores ≤ 1. Exclusion criteria included hemorrhagic or mixed stroke, receiving intravenous thrombolysis or endovascular therapy, cardiogenic embolism, any contraindication for statin treatment, and use of PCSK9 inhibitors within 3 months before the stroke onset. The detailed inclusion and exclusion criteria and study design were described before [18].

Procedures

The included patients were divided into two groups according to the TOAST classification: the LAA group and the SVO group. In each subtype, patients were subdivided into the PI group and AT group according to whether they received evolocumab treatment as an adjunct to atorvastatin. In the PI group, each patient received 140 mg (1 ml) of evolocumab subcutaneously and 40 mg of atorvastatin orally within 24 h after the onset of symptoms. From day 2 to 90 of enrollment, patients received 140 mg of evocumab subcutaneously every two weeks and 40 mg of atorvastatin orally once nightly. In the AT group, patients received 40 mg of atorvastatin orally. From day 2 to 90 of enrollment, patients continued to receive 40 mg of atorvastatin orally once nightly. Details of antiplatelet therapy were previously reported [18].

Stroke etiology was classified according to the TOAST classification, which combined clinical presentation, brain imaging findings, and laboratory tests [19]. Detailed clinical assessments were performed at baseline, 7 days, and 90 days after randomization. The modified Rankin Scale (mRS) and National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) used in this study are well-established clinical assessment scales for stroke research [33, 34]. The Evaluation of baseline and follow-up neurological function scores was done by the same neurologists. Follow-up at 90 days, including mRS scores and stroke recurrence, was assessed by two neurologists who were unaware of the treatment allocation.

Outcomes assessment

The efficacy and safety Outcomes in this analysis were similar to those of the primary study. The primary outcome was the incidence of END within 7 days, defined as a ≥ 2-point NIHSS score increase or NIHSS motor score ≥ 1-point compared with baseline. In addition, we performed a sensitivity analysis for the incidence of severe END, defined as a higher threshold of 4 points worsening in the NIHSS or 2 motor points. The secondary Outcomes included LDL-C target achievement rate at 7 days (LDL-C ≤ 1.8 mmol/L with a reduction > 50% from baseline), change in inflammatory factors [Interleukin (IL)−6, IL-8, and Tumour necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α)] compared with baseline at 7 days, and stroke-related death within 7 days. Safety outcomes were the occurrence of adverse events and serious adverse events during the study. Exploratory outcomes included favorable functional outcomes at day 90 (defined as a score of 0 to 2 on the mRS) and new stroke events within 90 days.

Cytokine detection

Fasting blood samples were collected from patients at baseline and 7 days after treatment. Approximately 3 mL of anti-coagulated whole blood was centrifuged at 3000×g for 10 min. Plasma samples were then analyzed for the concentrations of immunoinflammatory markers (IL-6, IL-8, and TNF-α). This analysis was performed using the IMMULITE 2000 immunoassay system (Siemens, Deerfield, USA).

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using the intention-to-treat (ITT) principle. Variables were described as mean ± standard deviation (SD), median (interquartile range [IQR]), or frequencies (percentages), as appropriate. Baseline characteristics were analyzed with the Student’s t-test or Mann-Whitney U test for continuous data and the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test for categorical data. Generalized linear models (GLMs) were used to evaluate the association between evolocumab treatment and outcomes. The treatment effects for these outcomes are presented as odds ratio (OR), risk ratio (RR), and risk difference (RD) with their 95% confidence intervals (CIs). The primary analyses of the primary and secondary outcomes were unadjusted. Covariate-adjusted analyses were also performed for all outcomes, adjusted for prespecified prognostic variables (age, sex, past history, antiplatelet therapy, NIHSS scores, mRS scores, and LDL-C level). In addition, assessments of the association between stroke subtypes and treatment effect on primary and secondary outcomes were conducted by GLMs with the treatment group, stroke subtypes, and their interaction term as independent variables, and the p value for the interaction term was presented. Adjusted interactions were performed by adding imbalanced baseline variables between stroke subtypes with p values less than 0.1 to the above model. The missing data were imputed using multiple imputation techniques. Sensitivity analysis was conducted based on the criterion for severe END, defined as a 4-point worsening in NIHSS or 2 motor points with a higher threshold.

No adjustment was made for multiple comparisons. All p values were nominal, and all analyses presented should be interpreted as exploratory. Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 26 (IBM Corp, Armonk, NY). A 2-sided p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Participant characteristics

A total of 272 patients were included in the current analyses, including 186 in the LAA group and 86 in the SVO group (Fig. 1). There were some differences in diastolic blood pressure, NIHSS score at randomization, history of diabetes, coronary heart disease, previous ischemic stroke, smoking, and prior statin use between subtypes (Table 1). In the LAA subtype group, there were 93 patients in the PI group and 93 patients in the AT group. In the SVO subtype group, there were 43 patients in the PI group and 43 patients in the AT group. Baseline characteristics between the PI and AT groups were well balanced across the LAA and SVO subgroups (Table 2).

Outcomes

Table 3 presents the comparisons of clinical outcomes between the PI and AT groups in each subtype. For primary outcome, compared with AT group, the primary analyses showed a significantly lower incidence of END of PI group in the LAA subtype (14.0% versus 28.0%; RR, 0.45; 95% CI, 0.26 to 0.80; p = 0.006), but not in the SVO subtype (11.6% versus 16.3%; RR, 0.77; 95% CI, 0.26 to 2.33; p = 0.649). However, there was no significant interaction between treatment effects and cause subtypes (PPint value = 0.494). The result of the sensitivity analysis for the primary outcome with a severe END was similar to the primary analysis. Evolocumab combined with atorvastatin therapy significantly decreased the proportion of severe END in patients with LAA compared with atorvastatin monotherapy (7.5% versus 20.4%; RR, 0.39; 95% CI, 0.18 to 0.85; p = 0.018), but not in the SVO subtype (4.7% versus 7.0%; RR, 0.66; 95% CI, 0.05 to 9.69; p = 0.764).

Discussion

This analysis demonstrated that the combination therapy of evolocumab and atorvastatin significantly reduced the incidence of END within 7 days compared with atorvastatin monotherapy in patients with the LAA subtype. Simultaneously, the PI group was likely to have a higher proportion of favorable functional prognosis at 90 days in patients with the LAA subtype.

Previous studies indicated that patients with the LAA subtype, particularly those with more intracranial atherosclerotic stenosis (ICAS) in Asian populations, were prone to progression and recurrence, leading to a poor prognosis [28–30]. Previous large-scale studies have demonstrated the efficacy of evolocumab in patients with coronary heart disease (CHD) [31, 32]. Due to the shared risk factors and pathophysiologic mechanisms between CHD and LAA, we further analyzed whether LAA patients might benefit more from the combination therapy with evolocumab and statin compared with statin monotherapy. The results indicated that, compared to atorvastatin monotherapy, the primary analyses revealed a significantly lower incidence of END with the combination therapy of evolocumab and atorvastatin in the LAA subtype (14.0% versus 28.0%; p = 0.006), whereas no significant difference was observed in the SVO subtype (11.6% versus 16.3%; p = 0.649). This finding confirmed that early lipid-lowering therapy targeting the LAA subtype might be an effective means to prevent the development of END after stroke.

The END occurs in patients with the LAA subtype, which may be partially attributed to the high risk of vulnerable atherosclerotic plaque detachment [35]. Therefore, the combination therapy of evolocumab and atorvastatin may offer superior efficacy than atorvastatin monotherapy for this subtype, due to its strong lipid-lowering and potential plaque stabilization. Previous studies have shown that PCSK9 inhibitors can effectively reduce LDL-C levels by approximately 60% [36] and produce favorable morphological changes in coronary atherosclerotic plaque [37, 38]. In addition, a recent study using high-resolution magnetic resonance imaging (HR-MRI) to assess the effect of PCSK9 inhibitors on intracranial plaque in patients with ICAS demonstrated that the combination therapy of evolocumab and moderate statin significantly reduced LDL-C levels and the stenosis degree after 12 weeks compared with statin monotherapy [39]. The results suggest that PCSK9 inhibitors may also yield a more significant intracranial atherosclerotic plaque regression. A large-scale randomized controlled trial evaluating the effects of evolocumab combined with statin on reducing intracranial arterial plaque burden is currently recruiting, and we are looking forward to its results [40].

On the other hand, growing evidence suggests that inflammatory mechanisms are key drivers of stroke pathogenesis and progression [41]. Interleukin (IL)−6 is a major driver of the inflammatory response, and elevated IL-6 levels have been shown to be associated with disease severity, neurological deterioration, and poor outcomes in patients with AIS [42–44]. Our study found that the combination therapy of evolocumab and statin significantly reduced the levels of serum pro-inflammatory cytokine IL-6 in patients with the LAA subtype. Thus, we hypothesize that PCSK9 inhibitors may decrease the occurrence of END to some degree by blocking the release of IL-6 after stroke. Similarly, a previous study in patients with acute coronary syndromes who shared risk factors with LAA patients demonstrated that evolocumab not only reduced LDL-C by 60% but also lowered serum levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, IL-6) [45]. In addition, a multicenter observational study evaluating the effect of evolocumab on inflammatory burden within the human atherosclerotic plaques demonstrated that patients treated with PCSK9 inhibitors exhibited a lower expression of pro-inflammatory proteins within the plaque, such as NLRP3, Caspase-1, IL-1β, TNF-α, and NF-κB. The effect was significant even in patients with LDL-C < 2.56 mmol/L (100 mg/dL), suggesting that evolocumab may exert anti-inflammatory effects within atherosclerotic plaques that are partly independent of the LDL-C-lowering action [46]. Recently, an animal experiment showed that the use of PCSK9i significantly improved neurological behavior scores and reduced infarct volume. Further mechanistic analyses confirmed that these therapeutic effects were related to the alleviation of neuroinflammation by activating the GPNMB/CD44 pathway [47]. These experimental and clinical studies suggest that PCSK9 inhibitors may exert anti-inflammatory effects associated with the IL-6-mediated inflammatory pathway, thus providing a new therapeutic target for the early application of PCSK9 inhibitors in AIS. Collectively, the present study suggests that patients with the LAA subtype may benefit more from combination therapy of evolocumab and atorvastatin in preventing END, which may be partially attributed to inflammation regulation and vulnerable plaque stabilization.

This study has several limitations. First, this analysis might be influenced by the imbalanced sample size between stroke causes and the relatively lower proportion of the SVO subtype, potentially reducing the statistical power. Therefore, the efficacy of the treatment requires further validation through trials with sufficient sample sizes. Second, the short follow-up period for stroke recurrence events and the insufficient sample size in this study resulted in a relatively low incidence of stroke recurrence. Hence, this analysis was unable to estimate the effect of evolocumab on preventing stroke recurrence. Third, the findings need to be interpreted with great caution, given the exploratory nature of the post hoc analysis. Thus, these findings warrant confirmation, particularly in non-Chinese populations.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this analysis suggests that AIS patients with the LAA subtype may benefit from the combination therapy of evolocumab with atorvastatin, with a reduced incidence of END at 7 days and a higher proportion of favorable functional prognosis at 90 days. These findings need to be further validated in the future.

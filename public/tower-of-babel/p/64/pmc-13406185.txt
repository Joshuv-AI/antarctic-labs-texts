A bibliometric analysis of the pathophysiology of sepsis-associated acute kidney injury from 2015 to 2024
Authors: Saiping Qi, Jianxin Zhou, Hongliang Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:The pathophysiology of sepsis-associated acute kidney injury (SA-AKI) urgently needs to be further explored to translate these findings into potential early diagnostic methods and effective therapeutic strategies. This study aims to analyze the current landscape and development trend related to the pathophysiology of SA-AKI.Methods:Network visualization and bibliometric analysis were conducted using the “Citation Report” function in Web of Science, CiteSpace, VOSviewer, the bibliometric package in R and the online platform (https://bibliometric.com), based on the publications from 2015 to 2024 in this field from the Web of Science Core Collection database.Results:Over the past decade, the number of publications and citations per year has steadily increased in this research area. China was far ahead in the number of publications (n = 690, 71.06%), followed by the United States (n = 123, 12.67%). The United States had the most extensive national collaboration. Seven of the top 10 most prolific institutions were situated in China. The 3 most productive authors were Bellomo (n = 16), Kellum (n = 14) and Gomez (n = 13). Bagshaw was the most cited author. International Immunopharmacology, Shock and Inflammation published the most papers. The review published in Kidney International by Peerapornratana et al in 2019 ranked first both in citation and co-citation frequency. The mechanisms of SA-AKI mainly included inflammation, microcirculation dysfunction, metabolic reprogramming and mitochondrial dysfunction. Metabolic reprogramming and mitochondrial dysfunction are still research hotspots at present. Pyroptosis and ferroptosis have emerged as research frontiers.Conclusion:In summary, this research comprehensively analyzed the research status and development trend in the field of the pathophysiology of SA-AKI by bibliometric analysis. The findings can offer valuable information for scholars seeking academic cooperation and identifying future research directions.

1. Introduction

Sepsis is defined as an acute organ dysfunction syndrome resulting from an unbalanced host response to infection.[1] Acute kidney injury is a common complication in patients with sepsis and significantly increases mortality rates associated with this condition.[2] Sepsis is found in 45% to 70% of all acute kidney injury cases in the intensive care unit.[3] Despite considerable advancements in both basic and clinical research regarding sepsis-associated acute kidney injury (SA-AKI), there are currently no effective treatments or biomarkers available for early diagnosis. Therefore, much more research is urgently needed to enhance our understanding of the pathophysiology of SA-AKI and to lay a foundation for exploring potential effective treatments and early diagnostic methods.

To date, the pathophysiology of SA-AKI is not fully understood. Renal hypoperfusion and secondary acute tubular necrosis were previously thought to be the predominant mechanisms of SA-AKI. Nitric oxide production due to inflammation contributes to generalized arterial vasodilation, which increases the risk of organ hypoperfusion.[4] However, there is accumulating evidence indicating that multiple factors such as endothelium injury, glycocalyx shedding, coagulation disturbances, and autonomic nervous system disorders may contribute to sepsis-associated microcirculation dysfunction, which can occur independently of system circulation abnormalities.[5,6] These microcirculation disturbances further lead to increased vascular permeability and leakage, microvascular thrombogenesis, intrarenal blood flow redistribution, blood shunting and renal tubular epithelial cells injury. The current research indicates that the primary mechanisms of SA-AKI involve inflammation, microcirculation disturbances, mitochondrial dysfunction and metabolic reprogramming.[7,8] The pathophysiology of SA-AKI has been continuously updated and replenished.

Bibliometrics is a discipline that systematically examines the characteristics of literature to explore the current status and emerging trends within a specific field.[9,10] By analyzing trends in the quantity, citation or keyword of literature over time, we were able to chart the evolving trajectory, identify the research hotspots and predict the future development.[11] Collaboration networks could be revealed by investigating the author, institution and country of publication. Besides, bibliometric analysis plays an important role in academic evaluation. The paper’s influence and the academic position of the author or journal could be assessed by co-citation analysis.[12] Based on the above, the study aims to perform a bibliometric analysis of the pathophysiology of SA-AKI, thereby guiding future research endeavors in this field.

2. Material and methods

2.1. Data collection and screening

A comprehensive literature search was conducted in the Science Citation Index Expanded and the Social Sciences Citation Index of the Web of Science Core Collection database. The search formula was: TS = (sepsis OR septic* OR pyemia* OR pyaemia* OR pyohemia* OR endotox* OR lipopolysaccharide OR LPS) AND (“kidney injur*” OR “renal injur*” OR “kidney failure*” OR “renal failure*” OR “kidney damage*” OR “renal damage*” OR “kidney dysfunction*” OR “renal dysfunction*” OR “renal insufficienc*” OR “kidney insufficienc*”) AND (mechanis* OR pathophysiolog* OR physiopatholog*). The search was performed on January 4, 2025 with the retrieval time range from January 1, 2015 to December 31, 2024. To refine the search, we applied filters to limit document types to articles and review articles, with the language set to English. The titles, keywords, and abstracts of identified articles were carefully reviewed to eliminate irrelevant works, duplicates, and retracted articles. All selected literature was exported as a plain text file, including full records and cited references.

2.2. Data analysis

For the subsequent bibliometric and visual analysis, we utilized several tools including the “Citation Report” function in Web of Science, CiteSpace (version 6.3.R1), VOSviewer (version 1.6.20), the bibliometrix package (version 4.3.0) in R (version 4.2.3) and an online platform (https://bibliometric.com). The flowchart of the literature search and data analysis is illustrated in Figure 1.

Flowchart of the bibliometric analysis.

CiteSpace was used to visualize the collaboration networks of countries and institutions.[13] In the generated figures, the size of node represented the number of publications, the color of rings in the nodes indicated the year of publication, and the ring’s thickness symbolized the number of publications in the corresponding year.[14] The line described the cooperation between countries or institutions, and the line color indicated the first cooperation time. The largest purple ring in some nodes represented that the centrality is not <0.1, and those nodes played a key role in bridging other nodes in the networks.[15] The thicker the line, the stronger the collaboration. Additionally, burst detection was used to identify references or keywords that attracted soaring attention in a time interval.[16,17]

VOSviewer was utilized for visualizing the co-authorship networks of countries and authors, the co-citation networks of cited authors, cited journals and cited references, the citation network of publications and the co-occurrence network of author keywords.[18] Nodes in the figures indicated various units such as countries, authors, cited authors, cited journals, cited references, papers and keywords.[14] The node size depicted the number of publications, citations or occurrences. The links between nodes represented the relationships of co-authorship, co-citation, citation or co-occurrence The thicker the line, the stronger the relationship.

3. Results

3.1. Trends in publication and citation

A total of 971 publications were included in the study, comprising 859 articles (88.47%) and 112 reviews (11.53%) after the screening process. The total citation, average citation per publication, and H-index were 22,545, 23.22 and 65, respectively. As illustrated in Figure 2, the number of annual publications and citations had steadily increased from 2015 to 2024, based on data from the “Citation Report” function of Web of Science. Between 2015 and 2019, there was a gradual rise, with an average of 55.6 articles published yearly. However, since 2020, the number of publications has surged, exceeding 100 articles annually. The annual growth rate of publications had reached 19.58% in this research field over the past decade.

The United States had the highest centrality and total link strength, underscoring a broader collaboration with other nations. Among the top 10 countries ranked by the count of publications, the centrality of South Korea (0) and Japan (0.02) was <0.1, showcasing the need to enhance the research cooperation in the research field. The leading 3 countries in terms of average citation of per publication were Australia (75.62), Italy (63.24) and the United States (43.53), indicating the high impact of their research.

4. Discussion

4.1. General information

In this study, we systematically analyzed the literature pertaining to the pathophysiology of SA-AKI utilizing bibliometrics to illustrate the current status and development trends in this research area in the past decade. A total of 971 articles were collected from the Web of Science Core Collection database after retrieval and screening. The number of publications had been increasing year by year with an annual growth rate of 19.58% from 2015 to 2024, highlighting scholars’ growing interest. The development process could be divided into 2 stages. There was a slow development stage during the first half of the decade, with an average annual publication of 55.6. Starting from 2020, the publications increased rapidly yearly, with over 100 articles per year.

China and the United States were the dominant players in the research area of the mechanisms of SA-AKI. China was far ahead in terms of publication volume, followed by the United States. However, the United States stands out for its broader national collaboration, with the closest cooperation with China. The countries with the highest average citations were Australia and Italy, manifesting the high impact of their research output. Among the top 10 institutions regarding publication counts, seven were situated in China, demonstrating that Chinese academic institutions paid much attention to this research field. Central South University had the most publications. University of Pittsburgh served as a central hub for institutional collaborations with the highest centrality of 0.24. More academic collaborations among institutions were needed.

Bellomo from Australia was the most prolific author in this field; the following were Kellum and Gomez from the United States. Bellomo was mainly concerned with the alterations of renal perfusion and oxygenation in SA-AKI and tried to attenuate renal hypoperfusion and hypoxia to improve renal function. Kellum and Gomez from different departments in the same institution had close cooperation, and they focused on the role of the reprogramming metabolism in septic kidney injury. The most cited author was Bagshaw from Canada, whose work served as the most impactful foundation for research in the field. Peerapornratana from the United States was the highest average citation author with just 1 publication in the past decade and ranked fourth in co-cited analysis, revealing his influential academic position. Chinese scholars should improve the research quality and enhance scientific cooperation to increase their academic influence in this research area.

By analyzing the journal distribution of relevant literature, researchers can identify the most influential journals in a given research field and choose suitable journals for submitting their work.[19] International Immunopharmacology (2024 IF = 4.7; Q1), Shock (2024 IF = 2.9; Q1) and Inflammation (2024 IF = 5; Q1) were the top 3 most productive journals. The 6 most frequently co-cited journals included Kidney International (2024 IF = 12.6; Q1), Journal of the American Society of Nephrology (2024 IF = 9.4; Q1), Critical Care (2024 IF = 9.3; Q1), Critical Care Medicine (2024 IF = 6; Q1), American Journal of Physiology-Renal Physiology (2024 IF = 3.4; Q1) and Intensive Care Medicine (2024 IF = 21.2; Q1). Additionally, Intensive Care Medicine ranked first in terms of average citation despite only 1 publication relevant to the pathophysiology underlying SA-AKI in the past decade, which highlighted the high-quality publication of this journal.

Analyzing the influential publications could help scholars understand the knowledge base and development direction for the related research.[20] The review published in Kidney International in 2019 ranked first both in the citation analysis of documents and the co-citation analysis of cited references, signifying its significant influence in this field.[5] The most recent influential publication was the review published in Nature Reviews Neurology in 2023.[3] The burst analysis was used to identify the references that were frequently cited in a specific period. The review published in Shock in 2014 was the reference with the strongest citation burst, starting in 2015 and ending in 2019.[21] Among the top 25 references with the strongest citation burst, the most recent citation bursts were presented in 3 publications, including the review published in Clinical Journal of the American Society of Nephrology in 2022, the review published in BMJ- British Medical Journal in 2019 and the article published in Biomedicine & Pharmacotherapy in 2020.[7,22,23] All the aforementioned reviews mainly summarized the pathophysiology, diagnosis, prevention and treatment of SA-AKI. The article published in Biomedicine & Pharmacotherapy in 2020 clarified that fisetin could alleviate renal inflammation and apoptosis by inhibiting TLR4/steroid receptor coactivator-mediated mitogen-activated protein kinase and NF-κB p65 pathways in SA-AKI.[23]

4.2. Research hotspots and future trends

Combined with the results of keyword analysis and the influential publications, we could draw that the mechanisms underlying SA-AKI mainly included inflammation, disturbances in microcirculation, metabolic reprogramming and mitochondrial dysfunction. Among them, metabolic reprogramming and mitochondrial dysfunction remain hot research topics in this field. Additionally, emerging mechanisms such as pyroptosis and ferroptosis are actively being studied.

4.2.1. Inflammation

The hyperactivation of the host innate immune response can be triggered by the release of pathogen-associated molecular patterns and damage-associated molecular patterns after pathogen invasion, which further induce proinflammatory mediators hyperexpression, endothelium injury, activation of the coagulation cascade, complement system activation, upregulated oxidative stress, and others.[24,25] Based on the above mechanisms, organ injuries, including acute kidney injury, may happen. Therefore, regulating inflammatory responses may become an effective adjuvant treatment for specified SA-AKI patients. For example, Wang et al illustrated that growth differentiation factor 11 could eliminate SA-AKI by attenuating inflammation and coagulation.[26] Besides, as we all know, some continuous renal replacement therapy strategies can not only remove excess fluid from the body but also alleviate organ damage by eliminating inflammatory mediators in the circulation.[27,28] With deeper research on SA-AKI, several novel inflammation-related biomarkers exhibit better early diagnostic potential, such as neutrophil gelatinase-associated lipocalin and interleukin -18.[29,30]

4.2.2. Microcirculation disturbances

Microcirculation dysfunctions were associated with cellular hypoxia, reactive oxygen species formation and tubular epithelium injury, which might play a key role during SA-AKI.[31] Microcirculatory alterations might not be dependent on macrocirculatory dysfunction in some cases.[32,33] The relevant content was detailed in the introduction of this paper. The mechanisms of SA-AKI may vary at different stages of SA-AKI. Hemodynamic mechanisms dominate in the early stages, and the role of microcirculation dysfunction gradually emerges in sustained injury stages.[34] Increased vascular permeability induced by microcirculation disturbances reminds clinicians to be more cautious during fluid resuscitation to avoid aggravating tissue edema (including renal interstitial edema) due to fluid overload, thereby deteriorating renal function. Therefore, monitoring microcirculation permits a more precise assessment of tissue perfusion and enables individualized treatment strategy. A recent study demonstrated that quantitative contrast-enhanced ultrasound can effectively assess the renal microcirculation and function in rats with SA-AKI.[35] Moreover, some biomarkers reflecting microcirculatory dysfunctions, such as Ang-2, demonstrated the promising potential in the early prediction of SA-AKI.[36]

4.2.3. Metabolic reprogramming and mitochondrial dysfunction

At the early stages of sepsis, TECs and inflammatory cells experience a metabolic shift from fatty acid oxidation-dependent oxidative phosphorylation to aerobic glycolysis[37,38] This metabolic switch helps prevent cell death by ensuring energy utilization for essential cell functions while downregulating energy expenditure on nonessential functions such as cell cycle arrest.[39] Additionally, this shift alleviates the mitochondrial injury caused by reactive oxygen species produced during oxidative phosphorylation. As the progresses of the disease, the energy metabolism reverts to oxidative phosphorylation to promote the recovery of SA-AKI. Functional mitochondria are crucial for this recovery process. Following sepsis-induced mitochondrial damage, mitochondrial quality control processes, such as mitophagy and biogenesis, are activated.[40–42] These processes help maintain the mitochondrial pool to meet essential energy demands. Mitophagy selectively eliminates damaged mitochondria through intracellular autophagosomes, while biogenesis involves the production of new, functional mitochondria. Metabolic reprogramming linked to the alterations of mitochondria dynamic is a significant characteristic of SA-AKI.[38]

Exploring treatment strategies targeting mitochondria and metabolic reprogramming for SA-AKI has become an area of great interest. Current evidence indicated metabolic reprogramming of TECs in the early stages of SA-AKI results in the lipid accumulation due to a reduction in free fatty acids utilization, which might be related to glomerulosclerosis and renal tubular fibrosis.[43] Fenofibrate, a peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor-α agonist, has demonstrated a protective effect in SA-AKI by prompting fatty acid oxidation.[44,45] Zhang et al investigated the mitochondrial dynamics in the LPS-induced AKI model.[46] They found that the expression of dynamin-related protein 1, a regulator of mitochondrial fission, was significantly upregulated, while mitofusin 1/2 and dynamin-like 120 kD protein expressions for mitochondrial fusion were decreased, thus leading to disrupting balance between division and fusion. However, dexmedetomidine could ameliorate this disturbance by upregulating of the alpha 2 adrenal receptor/SIRT1/peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor γ coactivator-1α pathway.

4.2.4. Pyroptosis

Pyroptosis is a form of programmed cell death mediated by gasdermins, a family of proteins that target membranes and form pores, which plays a crucial role in protecting the host from pathogens.[47] However, excessive pyroptosis could lead to harmful inflammatory responses, which might result in organ dysfunction. In the canonical pyroptosis pathway, pathogen-associated molecular patterns or damage-associated molecular patterns activate the intracellular NLRP3 inflammasome, followed by pro-caspase-1 cleavage.[48] Caspase-1 cleaves gasdermin D (GSDMD), pro-IL-18 and pro-IL-1β. The GSDMD N-terminal fragment forms pores in the cell membrane, increasing cell permeability, resulting in the release of cellular contents, IL-1β and IL-18. In the noncanonical pathway, LPS activates directly intracellular caspase-4/5/11, which in turn cleaves GSDMD to execute pyroptosis.

There is increasing evidence that pyroptosis is involved in SA-AKI. The expression of NLRP3, caspase-1 and GSDMD N-fragment were significantly upregulated both in vivo and in vitro models of septic kidney injury, indicating the canonical pyroptosis pathway happened in this disease model.[49,50] Ye et al found that caspase-11 expression was markedly increased in LPS-induced AKI models.[51] Knocking out the caspase-11 gene alleviated LPS-induced pyroptosis in TECs and improved the survival of mice treated with LPS. Targeting pyroptosis may serve as a novel treatment for septic kidney injury.

4.2.5. Ferroptosis

Ferroptosis is an iron-dependent, non-apoptotic cell death that is executed by lipid peroxidation.[52] The oxidative process involves intracellular ferrous iron overload, Fenton reaction, reactive oxygen species generation and lipid hydroperoxides accumulation.[53] The antioxidant pathways include the Xc-system, glutathione, glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4), and several newly identified networks. Under physiological conditions, a dynamic balance is achieved through the mutual antagonism of the oxidative and antioxidant systems, which is essential for normal cellular and organismal function. In infections and other pathological contexts, the breakdown of this balance can aggravate ferroptosis, resulting in damage to cell structure and function.[54] Certain pathogenic microorganisms require iron to accelerate growth and enhance virulence. As a defence mechanism, the levels of ferritin, the main iron storage protein within cells, are increased while serum iron levels are reduced in the bodies of the hosts. However, severe infection such as sepsis stimulates ferritin autophagy and increases the intracellular ferrous iron levels, leading to lipid peroxidation and ferroptosis. Ferroptosis releases excess intracellular iron for microorganisms’ utilization. Meanwhile, microorganisms’ infection provides some raw materials for lipid peroxidation, such as reactive oxygen species. This is a vicious circle, thereby deteriorating the infection and causing multi-organ failure.

Research has confirmed that ferroptosis was present in the TECs during septic kidney injury, and ferroptosis inhibitors could attenuate SA-AKI. Xiao and colleagues depicted that the expression of glutathione and GPX4 were significant decreased in the cecal ligation and puncture-induced mice renal tissue, and TECs showed typical mitochondrial alterations of ferroptosis. These typical changes were exacerbated with erastin (a ferroptosis inducer) intervention and improved with Fer-1 (a ferroptosis inhibitor) treatment.[55] Recent findings indicated that PR domain containing protein 16 and Melittin could protect SA-AKI through inhibiting ferroptosis by inducing the expression of GPX4.[56,57] Liu S and his team found that perillaldehyde ameliorated SA-AKI by suppressing heat shock protein 90kDa alpha family class A member 1-mediated pyroptosis and ferroptosis.[58]

Although great progress has been made in the study on the SA-AKI pathophysiology, there are still many problems remain to be solved. The SA-AKI mechanisms are characterized by complexity, multidimension and interactivity. However, the dominant pathological mechanisms in SA-AKI with different etiologies may vary, which might provide an important guide to achieve an individualized treatment strategy. Moreover, there is a lack of effective treatment methods that can shorten the course of AKI. Traditional diagnostic criteria based on the change of serum creatinine and urine output are insensitive. Emerging SA-AKI biomarkers such as gelatinase-associated lipocalin and urine insulin-like growth factor-binding protein 7 and tissue inhibitor of metalloproteinases-2 have shown great potential in the early diagnosis, risk assessment and predicting prognosis of SA-AKI.[29,59] Yet, more large-scale, multicenter, prospective clinical trials are needed to validate the clinical application of these biomarkers in SA-AKI patients. In addition, most studies on the SA-AKI mechanisms were based on animal or cell models and there are differences in the pathophysiological processes between those models and humans, which was responsible for insufficient translation of this research field to some extent. Therefore, the development of novel models that better simulate human pathological processes is necessary to bridge the gap between basic and clinical research.

There are several limitations in this study. First, we only retrieved the publications from the Web of Science database, and thus the relevant papers from other databases, such as PubMed and Medline, might be omitted. Second, this study enrolled papers published in the past decade in the research field, neglecting the earlier publications. Third, the papers published in non-English languages were excluded from the analysis. Last, the latest high-quality publications might not possess considerable frequency of citation or co-citation, which might affect our partial conclusions.

5. Conclusion

In summary, the present study, for the first time, performed a network visualization and bibliometric analysis of the pathophysiology of SA-AKI over the past decade. Research on the mechanisms of septic SA-AKI has received more and more attention, with a steady increase both in annual publications and citations from 2015 to 2024. The spatial distribution of literature, including country, institution, author and journal, and influential publications was identified. Current research showed that the mechanisms of SA-AKI mainly involved microcirculation dysfunctions, inflammation, metabolic reprogramming and mitochondrial dysfunction, and yet the early diagnosis and treatment remained unfulfilled. Pyroptosis and ferroptosis had appeared as research frontiers in this field, and a significant amount of high-quality research remained to be conducted. Our findings could provide valuable insights for scholars seeking to collaborate and explore further.

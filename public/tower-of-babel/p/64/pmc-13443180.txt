Sharing Neurotrauma Knowledge Across Diverse Healthcare Environments
Authors: Suk-Hyung Kang
Published: 2026

The ultimate goal of neurotrauma care is universal—to preserve life and maximize neurological recovery. However, the path toward achieving this goal differs considerably across countries and regions. Healthcare systems, available resources, economic conditions, insurance policies, and sociocultural backgrounds all influence clinical decision-making. Consequently, the best possible treatment strategy is not always identical, but rather one that is optimized for each healthcare environment.

This issue of the Korean Journal of Neurotrauma (KJNT) is not organized around a single special theme. Instead, it reflects the diversity of neurotrauma practice throughout the world. It includes a Special Article from the Korea Organ Donation Agency (KODA) describing the current status and challenges of organ donation in Korea, along with review articles and original clinical studies contributed by researchers from several countries. Together, these articles illustrate one of the core values that KJNT seeks to promote: providing a platform where clinicians and researchers can share how they address common neurotrauma challenges within their own healthcare systems.

Scientific journals should certainly introduce innovative technologies and the latest evidence. Yet this alone is not sufficient. Across many parts of the world, clinicians continue to seek the best possible outcomes despite limited medical resources and diverse healthcare infrastructures. The knowledge generated under these circumstances is equally valuable. Not every country has access to the same technology or healthcare system, and clinical experience gained in resource-constrained settings may provide meaningful guidance to colleagues facing similar challenges elsewhere. Such studies enrich our collective understanding of neurotrauma and broaden the evidence base beyond highly specialized centers.

KJNT aspires to be more than a journal that simply reports cutting-edge advances. Our goal is to serve as an international forum where scientific evidence, clinical experience, and practical solutions from diverse healthcare settings can be exchanged openly. We believe that studies reflecting regional characteristics, healthcare disparities, and real-world clinical practice deserve recognition alongside research describing the latest technological innovations.

Medical science advances not only through scientific breakthroughs, but also through the exchange of experience across different clinical environments. As Editor-in-Chief, I hope that KJNT will continue to grow as a trusted platform where clinicians and researchers working on the front lines of neurotrauma around the world can share their knowledge, learn from one another, and collectively improve patient care. We hope that the articles presented in this issue contribute meaningfully to that shared mission.

Lansoprazole incorporated β-chitin-lyophilised PRF composite hydrogel for treatment of gastric ulcer
Authors: R Sri Manjari, Varsha Jayaprakash, Meghna Mohandas, Abhirami Preetha, Tetsuya Furuike, Hiroshi Tamura, Jayakumar Rangasamy
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractA gastric ulcer (GU) that leads to acute or persistent bleeding poses a considerable risk of fatalities. Despite their overall primacy, the current treatments for GU have major downsides of incomplete healing, antibiotic resistance, and secondary injury. To address these challenges, researchers have turned their attention to hydrogels to solve the therapeutic bottleneck of GU treatment. Based on this, we developed a β-chitin (β-ch) hydrogel incorporating Lyophilised Platelet-rich fibrin (L-PRF) with anti-ulcer drug Lansoprazole (Lns). The prepared hydrogel was characterized using FESEM and FT-IR. In vitro studies of all samples showed good controlled swelling behaviour and degradation rate. Incorporation of Lansoprazole (Lns) facilitates healing and promotes and gastric mucosal regeneration, potentially through its acid-suppressive effects, whereas the in vitro drug release study proved the sustained release of Lns from the prepared hydrogel. The in vitro growth factor release study using ELISA revealed a sustained release of TGFα and PDGF BB. The Lansoprazole incorporated hydrogels exhibited good antioxidant activity. Further, β-ch–L-PRF-Lns hydrogel was demonstrated to be biocompatible in nature, and enhanced cell attachment of β-ch –L-PRF-Lns hydrogel was also observed in intestinal cells (INT 407). The INT 407 cells exposed to Lns incorporated β-ch-L-PRF hydrogel showed enhanced cell migration. Therefore, this unique strategy accelerates regenerative processes, making it a possible effective therapeutic alternative for treating GU.

Introduction

Gastric ulcer (GU) usually appears as a loss of stability of the proximal gastric mucosa, though they can also occur in the oesophagus, small intestine/ectopic gastric mucosa of Meckel’s diverticulum. The intrinsic stimuli include the formation of stomach acid, free radicals, pepsin production, leukotrienes, endothelin, oxidants and external contributors such as ethanol or nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory medications (NSAIDs) [1]. The gastric mucosal obstruction retains H+ ions out and Na+ charges in, thereby keeping a latent difference across the mucosa’s apparent. The H+/K+ ATPase pump exchanges H+ and K+ ions across the apical membrane [2]. Infection with Helicobacter pylori, as well as the use of nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory medications, are substantial risk factors. GU is connected to functional polymorphisms in several cytokine genes [3]. Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory medications block this enzyme; however, the impact is reversible and dose-dependent [4]. In the pathophysiology of gastric mucosal injury, reactive oxygen species (ROS) are involved because of their high chemical reactivity and the presence of unpaired electrons within their molecules. As a result, tissue damage develops, primarily due to increased lipid peroxidation and the metabolism of lipid peroxides produces malondialdehyde (MDA) or 4-hydroxynonenal (4-HNE). Under the strain, the stomach mucosa maintains enlarged lipid peroxidation along with a reduction in SOD activity and GSH levels [5]. Researchers have proposed using hydrogels, a biomaterial notable for its drug release and impact, to attempt to develop an enhanced and long-term defensive and beneficial strategy for treating GU because of the specific configuration of the stomach, such as its acidic, folded, dynamic and continuously emptying nature [6]. Hydrogels are porous, dynamic, three-dimensional arrangements of molecules of polymer networks capable of absorbing substantial quantities of water. Chitin (β-(1-4)-Poly-N-Acetyl-D-glucosamine) is the second most abundant polysaccharide in nature. [7]. Chitin (C8H13O5N) n possesses a structure similar to cellulose, but it contains 2-acetamido-2-deoxy-β-D-glucose (NAG) monomer units connected by β (1 → 4) links. Chitin can be synthesized into chitosan using either enzymes or chemical hydrolysis [8]. Chitin has three allomorphs, α, β and γ [9]. The concentric configuration of chains like polymer in β-ch affords greater adaptability than the antiparallel organization in α-chitin; nevertheless, the retained polymer is extremely durable [10].

β-ch hydrogels have intense muco-adhesive attributes, facilitating them to effectively hold onto the gastric mucosa. β-ch hydrogel may reduce reactive oxygen species (ROS) in the ulcer niche. β-ch hydrogels assist in curing GU by nurturing a protective barrier, inhibiting oxidative stress, and facilitating the targeted administration of medication. It is an effective therapy for stomach ulcers due to its combination of mechanisms that promote rapid healing [11]. Platelets include competent angiogenic stimulators such as transforming growth factor-α (TGF-α), platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF), vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), epidermal growth factor (EGF), and insulin-like growth factor (IGF). Lyophilised platelet-rich fibrin (L-PRF) originates from blood plasma and comprises leucocytes, platelets, and plenty of healing proteins bound in a dense fibrin matrix [12]. Therefore, they are preferable when coupled with other mechanically stable hydrogels such as β-ch for stomach ulcer healing [13]. Lansoprazole (Lns), a chemical inhibitor of stomach acid secretion, is used clinically for treating acid-related illnesses [14]. Lansoprazole, a proton pump inhibitor, is used to treat stomach ulcers, GERD, duodenal ulcers, ulcers developed from NSAIDs, and for long-term acid suppression such as Zollinger-Ellison syndrome. Lansoprazole’s active ingredient is 2-[[[3-methyl-4-(2,2,2-trifluoroethoxy)-2-pyridyl] methyl] sulfinyl] benzimidazole [15]. The empirical formula is C16H14F3N3O2S, and its molecular weight is 369.37 g/mol. Lansoprazole dramatically enhances cell survival in the alimentary tract to protect from ulcer-induced damage through minimizing changes in the expression of these mediators. It promotes gastric mucosal cell proliferation through the stimulation of p44 or p42 MAPK [16]. When growth factors bind to their respective receptors on the cell surface, receptor dimerization occurs, leading to auto-phosphorylation and induction of RTK, and further expression of the MAPK [17]. The up-regulation of growth factors or their receptors, such as EGF, basic fibroblast growth factor bFGF, and vascular endothelial growth factor receptor 1 (VEGFR-1) or Flt-1, in the mucosal cells of gastric is expected to activate growth factor transmission, triggering MAPK stimulation and enhanced transcription of genes that regulate longevity of cells or cell amplification [18]. The intracellular structural study reveals that it protects against ulcer-induced stomach illness by maintaining near-normal cyto-architecture. Nevertheless, lansoprazole’s stimulation of cell 13survival or proliferation machinery in gastric mucosal cells is finely regulated and never leads to tumorigenic development, as confirmed by morphological concepts and transcription of proliferation intermediaries in healing gastric tissue cells despite 48–72 hours of regeneration [19]. Though previous strategies such as antibiotic regimens, proton pump inhibitors, and surgical intervention had revealed efficacy, they are frequently hampered by incomplete mucosal repair, the rise of antibiotic-resistant strains, and secondary tissue injury. Based on this study, we developed an injectable β-ch hydrogel incorporating lyophilised platelet-rich fibrin (L-PRF) and the anti-ulcer agent Lansoprazole to address the shortcomings of existing GU therapies. The developed hydrogel delivers both growth factors and Lansoprazole in a sustained manner over several days, exhibiting superior rheological stability, extended shelf-life, and controlled swelling behaviour. Furthermore, it enhanced biocompatibility, promoted robust cellular adhesion, and conferred significant antioxidant activity, which were analysed. In concert, these characteristics facilitate rapid mucosal regeneration and establish our formulation as a compelling, long-term therapy for GU healing, as shown in Fig. 1.

Materials and Methods

Materials

β-chitin (M. W. 150 kDa, DA-72. 4%, Koyo Chemical, Japan) and Lansoprazole (TCI; CAS No.103577-45-3, MW = 369.36). ELISA kit (Krishgen Biosystems), NaCl (Emparta; CAS No.7647-14-5, MW = 58.44), HCl (Emplura;7647-01-0, MW = 36.46), α-MEM, and 10% FBS (Gibco). The chemicals were used without further purification.

Methods

Preparation of lyophilised platelet-rich fibrin (L-PRF)

Blood drawn from volunteers above 18 years was approved by Amrita Vishwa Vidyapeetham, Kochi (IEC-AIMS-2024-ASNSMM-295). 10 mL of blood was drawn from the volunteer blood donors in silica-coated BD Vacutainer red-topped tubes without anticoagulants. The collected blood was promptly centrifuged at 3000 rpm (approximately 400 g) for 10 min. After centrifugation, the tubes were kept for 5 min, then the middle layer, the PRF (buffy) layer, was taken out with sterile forceps and kept on sterile gauze for the excess plasma to ooze out, as shown in Fig. 2A. The PRF was transferred to a Petri plate without delay and frozen at −80 °C for glacial. The stored PRF was then lyophilised at −51 °C overnight. Eventually, the L-PRF was then ground into a fine substance by a mortar and pestle. Then, it was stored at 4 °C [20].

Schematic illustration of the preparation of A Lyophilised PRF, B β-ch and C β-ch-L-PRF-Lns hydrogel

Preparation of β-ch-L-PRF-Lns hydrogel

Preparation of β-ch hydrogel

β-ch hydrogel was prepared based on the previous literature illustrated in (Fig. 2B) [21]. 2.5 g of β-ch with 50 mL of distilled water were blended in a mixer for a minute, then 50 mL of distilled water was added to it, and the mixture was blended six times for one minute. The hydrogel was then filtered to remove excess water overnight.

Preparation of β-ch-L-PRF and β-ch-Lns hydrogel

The β-ch-L-PRF was prepared by thoroughly mixing the powdered L-PRF in a ratio of 0.5% (w/w) per 100 mg of the β-ch hydrogel to ensure the formation of a homogeneous hydrogel. The β-ch-Lns hydrogel was developed by incorporating Lns powder at a concentration of 2% (w/w) per 100 mg of the β-ch hydrogel, which leads to the formation of a stable and homogeneous hydrogel.

Preparation of β-ch-L-PRF-Lns hydrogel

The β–ch–L–PRF–Lns hydrogel was developed (Fig. 2C) by mixing 0.5% L-PRF in 2.5% β-ch incorporated into 2% Lns [22]. β–ch is a fibrous, highly permeable, and hydrophilic network that could absorb an enormous amount of liquid, making it adept for hydrogel formation.

Characterization of the prepared hydrogels

Field-Emission Scanning Electron Microscopy (FESEM)

The morphology of the developed hydrogels was characterized by FESEM. The hydrogels were gold sputtered, and imaging was done at a voltage of 15 kV.

Fourier Transform Infrared Spectroscopy (FTIR)

The prepared hydrogels of all four groups (β-ch, β-ch-L-PRF, β-ch-Lns and β-ch-L-PRF-Lns) were examined to identify their functional groups using FT-IR. Each group’s dried 2 mg sample was crushed and mixed with 178 mg of KBr. The combination of materials was then made into a pellet using the KBr press, and the spectra were obtained between 4000-500 cm−1 at 20 cycles for each sample.

Rheological study of β-ch, β-ch-L-PRF, β-ch-Lns, β-ch-L-PRF-Lns hydrogel

The viscoelastic properties and flow were evaluated using a rheometer. Amplitude sweep, frequency sweep, and shear stress and rate analysis were performed using a parallel plate geometry of 20 mm with a 0.5 mm space at 37 °C. The injectable properties of the developed hydrogel were assessed by a flow curve in the series of 0.1 s−1 to 100 s−1. Frequency sweep analysis was done to regulate the intensity of the prepared hydrogels, which varied from 0.1 to 10 Hz with 1% constant strain to quantify the elastic (G’) and viscous modulus (G″). The modification in G’, G″ and δ was quantified for all groups. The injectability of the hydrogels was assessed by loading them into a 1 mL syringe, according to shear, and observing the flow nature of the hydrogels visually. An inversion test was conducted to examine the efficacy of gravity on each group of hydrogels. The same amount of each gel was placed in a vial (containing flat bases). The vials were inverted and left undisturbed. The flow and stability of the hydrogels were observed for 24 hours and 48 hours [12].

In vitro swelling study

The swelling studies were performed for four groups of hydrogel. The dry weight (Wd) of the hydrogel of each group was quantified and immersed in artificial gastric juice. To prepare artificial gastric juice, 0.2 g of NaCl was dissolved in 100 mL of distilled water, and the pH of the solution was adjusted to 1 using HCl (∼1.4 mL) [23]. Then kept at 37 °C in a shaking incubator. The residual artificial gastric juice was discarded, and the wet weight (Ww) was determined at different time intervals (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7). The initial weight (Wi) of the hydrogels was measured and immersed in artificial gastric juice, then incubated at 37 °C for 28 days. The hydrogels were taken out from the medium at distinct time intervals: 7, 14, 21 and 28 days. Eventually, the hydrogels were lyophilised, and the final weight (Wf) was recorded. In vitro Lansoprazole release study

The 2 mg of Lns encapsulating into 100 mg β-ch hydrogel and 2 mg of Lns in 0.5% L-PRF with 100 mg β-ch hydrogel were immersed in 1 mL artificial gastric juice (pH 1). The samples were maintained at 37 ° C. The medium in the samples was removed periodically (30 min, 2 h, 4 h, 6 h, 16 h, 20 h, 24 h, 48 h, 72 h) and the same amount of fresh medium was added for each sample. The amount of Lns released was analysed with a UV spectrophotometer at 275 nm. The drug release studies were performed in triplicate for each group and it was subjected to predict drug release kinetics [25, 26].

The quantification of TGF α and PDGF-BB growth factor release from L-PRF (β-ch-L-PRF, β-ch-L-PRF-Lns) was assessed by ELISA. In vitro cell viability and proliferation Study

The cytocompatibility of hydrogels of β-ch, β-ch-PRF, β-ch-Lns, and β-ch-PRF-Lns was assessed by the indirect method with the Alamar Blue assay. The assay measures cellular metabolic activity as an indicator of cell viability and proliferation. The prepared samples were decontaminated by ETO (Ethylene Oxide) sterilization and were positioned in a 24-well plate. INT 407 cells (Intestinal cells) were purchased from NCCS and cultured in an α-MEM medium (Gibco). Cells were trypsinized after achieving 80% confluence and seeded in the samples, then added in cell culture medium in a 24-well plate containing 5 × 104 cells. The cells with the samples were incubated for 24 h and 48 h then the Alamar assay (Invitrogen, USA) was performed. The optical density was recorded at 570 nm with 600 nm set as the reference wavelength using a microplate reader. In vitro cell attachment study

The samples were added with INT 407 cells in a 24-well plate at an intensity of 1 ×105 cells/well. Following 24 hours of incubation, the samples were thoroughly rinsed with PBS and fixed with 2.5% glutaraldehyde for 1 hour. For DAPI staining, the seeded hydrogels were dehydrated with 4% paraformaldehyde for 20 minutes and then permeated with 0.5% Triton X-100 added in PBS for 5 minutes. Then, they were rinsed and stained with 150 µL of DAPI and incubated in the dark for 10 minutes. The hydrogels were then washed with PBS and observed under a fluorescent microscope [28].

In vitro cell migration study

Scratch assay was performed in INT 407 cells to confirm cell migration. The INT 407 cells were grown in a 24-well plate. Approximately 50,000 cells were seeded in each well and cultured in α-MEM medium supplemented with 10% FBS and 1% penicillin-streptomycin. The media was removed after attaining confluency and a scratch was made using a 100 µL tip at the centre of the well horizontally. The prepared hydrogel eluent in serum-reduced medium was added to the respective well, and cells were incubated at 37 °C in a humidified 5% CO₂ atmosphere for 24 hours.

Statistical analysis

All the experiments were performed in triplicate. The outcomes are presented as the mean ± SD. The statistical significance was confirmed using ANOVA, with significance indicated by *, **, *** corresponding to p values < 0.05, 0.01 and 0.001, respectively.

Results

Preparation and characterization of β-ch, β-ch-L-PRF, β-ch-Lns, β-ch-L-PRF–Lns hydrogel

The β–ch–L–PRF–Lns hydrogel was successfully developed by mixing L-PRF into β-ch incorporated into Lns, resulting in a uniform gel system. FESEM images in Fig. 3A revealed that β–ch has a porous structure; β–ch and freeze-dried PRF have a porous structure with a 3D fibrous network, represents Lns loaded β–ch hydrogel and the final group is β–ch–L–PRF–Lns hydrogel.

A FESEM and B FT-IR spectrum of β-ch, β-ch-L-PRF, β-ch-Lns and β-ch-L-PRF-Lns hydrogel

FTIR analysis was performed with control β–ch, L-PRF, Lns, β-ch-Lns, β-ch-L-PRF and developed β-ch-L-PRF-Lns hydrogel (Fig. 3B). FTIR of β-chitin showed peaks at 3275 cm−1 (-O-H and -N-H stretching), 2870 cm−1 (C-H stretching), 1635 (C=O, Amide 1) and 1545 cm−1 (Amide II) and 1030 cm−1 (C-O stretching). In L-PRF, the characteristic peak at 3370 cm−1 shows -N-H/O-H stretching and other peaks at 2929 (-C-H stretching), 1662 (Amide I) and 1541 cm−1 (Amide II), 1412 cm−1 (CH bending) and 1240 cm−1 (Amide III) [29]. Lns showed characteristic peaks at 3240 cm−1 (broad -OH stretching), 2985 cm−1 (-C-H stretching), 1580 cm−1 (aromatic −C=C stretching), 1457 cm−1 (-C-H bending), 1268 cm−1 (-C-O stretching), 1115 cm−1 (-C-O-C stretching) and 750 cm−1 (aromatic C-H out-of-plane bending) [30, 31]. When β-ch was combined with L-PRF, a peak shift was observed from 3370 to 3275 cm−1 in all the β–ch-L-PRF and β–ch–L–PRF–Lns formulations, suggesting intermolecular interactions β-ch and L-PRF. Furthermore, the presence of peaks at 1635 and 1541 cm−1 (Amide I and II) showed additional evidence supporting the successful incorporation of β–ch and L-PRF. In β-ch-Lns, peaks at 3275 (-OH/NH stretching), 1635 and 1545 (amide bands), 1457 cm−1, 1268 cm−1 and 1115 cm−1 (β–ch peaks) were retained. The final group of β-ch-L-PRF-Lns hydrogel depicts the functional groups of all individual β-ch, L-PRF and Lns. No new peaks were observed in the final developed β-ch-L-PRF-Lns hydrogel.

Rheological study of β-ch, β-ch-L-PRF, β-ch-Lns, β-ch-L-PRF-Lns hydrogel

In rheology, the LVER of β-ch, β-ch-L-PRF, β-ch-Lns, β-ch-L-PRF-Lns hydrogels was calculated using an amplitude sweep analysis with amplitude (%) ranging from 10−4 to 10−1. A frequency sweep in the LVER was carried out for a while to examine each gel’s behaviour. LVER (Linear Viscoelastic Region) is defined as the range of strain or stress applied within which the material exhibits a linear viscoelastic response. Each group of hydrogels’ G’ was assessed by exposing it to frequencies ranging from 10−1 to 101 Hz. All hydrogel groups revealed a greater elastic modulus (G’) than G″. This indicates they are primarily solid in nature. The δ remained consistent across the assessed frequency series, representing the material’s gel-like nature. The hydrogel’s δ is lower than 20°, showing it may elastically deform like a solid. For pure solids, δ = 0° and liquids is δ = 90° [32]. During the thermal stability examination, the prepared hydrogel was still stable between 25 and 50 °C. G’ values were consistent through the frequency and temperature sweep. This implied that the hydrogel would be stable even after injection into the wound site and the flow rate curve was performed to assess the developed hydrogel’s shear-thinning capabilities. The η was measured using numerous shear rates (γ), which vary from 10−1s to 102s. The graph indicated that when the shear rate improved, the η minimized due to the hydrogels shear-thinning characteristics. Thus, Fig. 4A shows the β-ch, β-ch-L-PRF, β-ch-Lns, β-ch-L-PRF-Lns hydrogel exhibits gel-like traits due to having a liquid component and a solid governing nature [33]. The inversion tube test in Fig. 4B confirmed all the groups of the hydrogel’s gelation properties and stable formation. The flow nature was noticed through an 18 G needle of each group (Fig. 4C). After 48 hours, we affirmed the shear-thinning property of the prepared hydrogel.

In vitro Lansoprazole release study

The polymer-based hydrogel’s three-dimensional porosity structure permits it to carry and deliver biologically reactive compounds, cells, or pharmaceuticals. The hydrogel loaded with Lansoprazole suspended in the medium (artificial gastric juice) showed 96.7 ± 0.04% higher entrapment efficiency. This slightly reduced efficiency is due to the hydrophobic nature of Lns that leads to non-uniform dispersion within the hydrogel matrix, thereby limiting complete encapsulation. This is due to the hydroxyl and amine groups of β–ch that interact with Lansoprazole through hydrogen bond formation. The release of Lansoprazole from the prepared β–ch hydrogel (control) and the L-PRF incorporated into Lns-loaded β–ch hydrogel (test) in artificial gastric juice (pH 1) was 80.01 ± 2.43% of the drug released for 48 hours. There was no statistically significant difference in the release of both groups due to the nominal diffusion of the encapsulated drug; furthermore, the L-PRF does not modify the physicochemical properties. The outcome of the drug from the control and test in the artificial gastric juice was subjected to kinetic assessment to regulate the release model. It followed the Higuchi model. Discussion

The study describes a multifunctional β-chitin (β-ch) hydrogel that incorporates lansoprazole (Lns) and lyophilized platelet-rich fibrin (L-PRF) as a localized therapeutic solution for the treatment of GU. In a single injectable biomaterial platform, the design combines antioxidant activity, sustained growth factor release, regulated drug delivery, structural stability, and improved cellular response.

According to FESEM analysis, all formulations had a porous, interconnected fibrous architecture, which is essential for fluid absorption, diffusion, and the transport of bioactive molecules. The shape was maintained by the addition of L-PRF, suggesting physical integration via hydrogen bonding without structural collapse. Intermolecular connections between fibrin proteins, β-ch amide groups, and Lns functional moieties were confirmed by FTIR peak shifts, indicating physical incorporation without chemical change. This is beneficial for preserving growth factor bioactivity and medication stability. Rheological analysis confirmed persistent gel-like properties appropriate for gastric retention by showing dominant elastic behaviour (G′ > G″) with a low phase angle (δ < 20°). Injectability is highlighted by the shear-thinning profile and effective extrusion through an 18 G needle. Reversible physical crosslinking, which is crucial for minimally invasive application, is suggested by gel recovery following shear stress. In situ performance is further supported by thermal stability within the physiological range. Over the course of 28 days, controlled water intake and progressive mass loss were seen in swelling and degradation investigations conducted in artificial gastric juice. The swelling behaviour revealed that the β-ch absorbs water through hydrogen bonding as it contains the hydrophilic groups (–OH, –NHCOCH₃) that lead to initial swelling. The β-ch with L-PRF limits water diffusion due to its crosslinking between fibrin and chitin, so it has minimal swelling. The β–ch with Lns, which has a hydrophobic interaction that disrupts the hydrogen bonding between water molecules and the hydroxyl or amine functional groups present in the chitin matrix. This resulted in an increase in free volume and enhanced the swelling percentage was slightly increased than the other two groups (β-ch, β-ch-L-PRF) [33]. Long-term therapeutic action is supported by moderate degradation in acidic environments, which shows structural stability without premature disintegration. The release of lansoprazole was matrix-controlled, as shown by the Higuchi diffusion model (R² = 0.85). Higuchi model predominantly explains a swelling and diffusion-combined drug release mechanism in such a way that drug diffuses through a hydrated polymer matrix followed by swelling [34]. The release kinetics reveal the ability of the prepared hydrogel to swell in acidic environmental conditions for sustained drug release. Upon exposure to artificial gastric juice, the three-dimensional porous structure, as confirmed by FE-SEM and hydrophilic network structure, undergoes swelling and leads to the relaxation of polymer chains and thus forms aqueous channels for drug diffusion. Importantly, the Higuchi model does not imply Fickian diffusion but a combined influence of diffusion and swelling-controlled drug release, which is well suitable for a physically crosslinked hydrogel [34]. The contribution of L-PRF does not significantly alter the release mechanism, indicating the structural integrity within the prepared composite hydrogel. In contrast to burst release, prolonged exposure is demonstrated by about 80% drug release over 48 hours. Drug release kinetics were not considerably changed by the addition of L-PRF, indicating separate diffusion channels within the hydrogel network. Maintaining constant acid suppression during mucosal regeneration requires this kind of regulated release. A pro-regenerative milieu is created by the long-term release of TGF-α and PDGF-BB from hydrogels containing L-PRF. These growth factors support angiogenesis, fibroblast recruitment, and epithelial proliferation—all important steps in the healing of the stomach mucosa. The lack of statistical significance between the groups may be due to the possibility that both formulations exhibit similar release behaviour due to comparable structural and biochemical properties of the hydrogel matrix. Bioactive proteins are generally protected from quick breakdown by the fibrin matrix, which permits slow transport. By scavenging reactive oxygen species, which are crucial in stomach mucosal damage, the antioxidant activity of Lns-loaded formulations further enhances therapeutic efficiency. The two main pathogenic factors that contribute to the development of ulcers are addressed by this dual function: acid suppression and oxidative stress reduction. β-ch-L-PRF-Lns considerably enhanced cell survival, proliferation, adhesion, and migration as compared to drug-only formulations [35], according to biocompatibility studies conducted with INT-407 cells. Notably, L-PRF demonstrated a synergistic relationship by reducing the cytotoxic effects of Lns alone. The hydrogel’s capacity for regeneration is further supported by increased cell migration in the scratch assay.

Overall, the β-ch-L-PRF-Lns hydrogel creates an injectable, bioactive, and structurally stable platform that can deliver drugs and growth factors continuously in acidic environments. This system’s synergistic combination of biological stimulation, pharmacological regulation, and biomaterial support makes it a viable option for localized stomach ulcer treatment.

Conclusion

In this work, we prepared β-ch-L-PRF-Lns hydrogel and characterized it for the treatment of GU. The developed hydrogel exhibited a smooth morphology confirmed by FESEM and characteristic peak shifts in FT-IR analysis. Rheological study of all groups indicated predominant elastic behaviour over viscous (G’ > G”), while injectability and inversion tests confirmed its shear-thinning nature. The β-ch-L-PRF-Lns hydrogel demonstrated excellent swelling and a controlled degradation rate. In vitro studies showed a sustained release of Lansoprazole (80% over 48 hours), and ELISA revealed prolonged release of growth factors TGF-α and PDGF-BB from the final group of hydrogel. Antioxidant assays exhibited enhanced reducing capacity in Lns-loaded formulations. In vitro cell studies revealed good biocompatibility, cell proliferation, adhesion and enhanced cell migration on INT 407 cells of each group of hydrogel. In summary, these results emphasize the potential of the β-ch-L-PRF-Lns hydrogel as a promising therapeutic strategy for the treatment of GU.

Successful administration of cetuximab using dose escalation method: a case report
Authors: Shasha Li, Yanjuan Zhang, Jiandong Zha, Wenqi Chen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionCetuximab, used to treat head and neck squamous cell carcinoma and metastatic colorectal cancer, can cause severe infusion reactions.Case presentationWe report an 87-year-old East Asian woman with stage IV ileocecal signet ring cell carcinoma who experienced severe allergic reactions to cetuximab despite pre-treatment. A dose escalation method, involving weekly incremental doses with comprehensive pre-treatment and close monitoring, was employed, successfully reducing allergic reactions and allowing safe administration.ConclusionThis approach demonstrates a viable alternative for patients with hypersensitivity to cetuximab, warranting further research for personalized treatment optimization.Keywords: Cetuximab, Desensitization, Dose escalation, Infusion reaction, Hypersensitivity, Case report

Introduction

Cetuximab is a monoclonal antibody primarily used to treat squamous cell carcinoma of the head and neck cancer and RAS wild-type metastatic colorectal cancer [1, 2]. As a targeted therapy, cetuximab works by binding to the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR), inhibiting the growth and spread of cancer cells [3]. However, infusion reactions to cetuximab have become a significant issue in clinical practice, limiting its use [4]. These reactions often present as allergic responses, including rash, itching, and difficulty breathing, and can even lead to anaphylactic shock [5, 6]. Such reactions not only affect the patient’s treatment experience, but can also lead to treatment interruption, thereby impacting efficacy [7].

Case presentation

The patient is an 87-year-old East Asian woman with a history of primary hypertension, without a previous history of atopy or drug allergy, and no family history of cancer. She presented with recurrent abdominal pain and bloody stools 2 years ago. A computed tomography (CT) scan suggested thickening of the ileocecal wall and partial bowel obstruction. On 7 August 2022, she underwent laparoscopic exploration, which revealed a tumor in the ileocecal region, with peritoneal, right diaphragm, and bladder–vaginal interface implants. Palliative right hemicolectomy and peritoneal nodule resection were performed. Postoperative pathology confirmed signet ring cell carcinoma pT4bN2M1. Genetic testing showed MSI-H, BRAF p.V600E mutation, TMB 40.84muts/Mb, and PD-L1 TPS < 1% and CPS < 1.

Due to her advanced age and diagnosis of stage IV ileocecal signet ring cell carcinoma with MSI-H, single-agent immunotherapy was recommended. She began her first cycle of pembrolizumab on 6 September 2022, and completed 12 cycles by 12 March 2024, with the best response being partial remission (PR). During treatment, she experienced immune-related thyroiditis, secondary adrenal insufficiency, immune-related dermatitis, and immune-related tracheitis, managed with appropriate symptomatic treatments.

In March 2024, the patient experienced disease progression with vaginal bleeding, managed with palliative radiotherapy. Systemic therapy was switched from pembrolizumab to cetuximab combined with vemurafenib. However, during the first cycle of cetuximab, she experienced a severe allergic reaction, presenting with facial erythema, coughing, and acute respiratory distress. Despite pre-treatment for the second cycle, she still continued to experience similar allergic symptoms.

Through the above treatment, the patient’s vaginal bleeding significantly improved, and the carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) levels markedly decreased, indicating a high likelihood of the current treatment regimen being effective. However, the patient experienced severe hypersensitivity reaction, grade 3. According to the guidelines, cetuximab should be discontinued or switched to a similar drug. Unfortunately, panitumumab is not available in China and also carries a risk of infusion reactions. Thus, there are no better or safer treatment options available for this patient.

On the basis of multiple literature reports [8–10], the clinical team decided to use a dose escalation method for the third cycle (please refer to Table 1 for details). After obtaining specific informed consent from the patient, on 17 May 2024, the patient began weekly administration of 450 mg (250 mg/m2) of cetuximab, divided into three bags: bag 1: 9 mg (2%) in 250 ml saline, bag 2: 58.5 mg (13%) in 250 ml saline, and bag 3: 382.5 mg (85%) in 250 ml saline. The dose was escalated in the following manner: 2 days prior and on the day of administration, the patient took oral acetaminophen 300 mg and montelukast 10 mg once daily, as well as on the day of the procedure; 30 minutes before the infusion, the patient received intravenous dexamethasone (15 mg) and chlorpheniramine (10 mg), in addition to intravenous cimetidine (0.3 g) and ondansetron (8 mg). Bag 1 infused at 80 ml per hour for 15 minutes, then increased to 120 ml per hour, 260 ml per hour, and 540 ml per hour every 15 minutes if no adverse reactions occurred. Bag 2 followed the same escalation as bag 1. Bag 3 infused at 80 ml per hour for 15 minutes, then increased to 115 ml per hour for 2 hours if tolerated. The infusion process was closely monitored with ECG (Electrocardiograph) monitoring and emergency medications. The first two bags were infused without any adverse reactions. During the infusion of bag 3, the patient experienced mild coughing and focal wheezing, which was managed with salbutamol (5 mg) via oxygen nebulization and without stopping cetuximab infusion. After stabilization, the infusion was completed successfully.

We repeated this protocol for the fourth and fifth cycles, taking 5 hours in total, with no significant adverse reactions other than transient hypertension, which relieved without intervention.

Discussion

Cetuximab, an important anti-EGFR monoclonal antibody, has shown significant efficacy in treating advanced colorectal cancer and other diseases. However, infusion reactions, particularly severe ones (up to 3%), greatly limit its clinical use [7]. We review relevant literature and introduces our optimized drug infusion protocol.

Initially, we reviewed several pertinent studies to inform our approach. Jerath et al. [10] pioneered a dose-escalation protocol for advanced breast cancer, successfully administering the drug by dividing the dose into five bags with a tenfold concentration increase. Saif et al. [11] explored the use of cetuximab for patients with previous allergies to panitumumab, utilizing five different infusion rates to ensure safe administration. In contrast, Hong et al. [12] conducted a dose escalation using a single bag. Our primary reference was García-Menaya et al. [8], who described a ten-step infusion method. Building on their work, we further optimized the dosage to enhance safety and efficacy. All cases involved pre-treatment protocols to mitigate allergic reactions, resulting in either no allergic responses or only mild ones.

In our case, the patient was an elderly individual with limited treatment options. For advanced colorectal cancer with a BRAFV600E mutation, the optimal therapy is a combination of BRAF V600E inhibitors and anti-EGFR therapy. While the patient initially benefited from this treatment, she subsequently experienced a severe allergic reaction (grade 3). Given that alternative medications are not available in China, drug desensitization was considered a viable option. Building on previous research, we adjusted the desensitization protocol to enhance its clinical feasibility.

To ensure the safe use of cetuximab, we recommend a thorough assessment of the patient’s history of drug and food allergies, as well as any autoimmune diseases, before initiating treatment. In well-equipped centers, it is advisable to conduct pre-treatment drug skin tests [13] or measure specific immunoglobulin E (IgE) levels, such as those for galactose-alpha-1,3-galactose (alpha-Gal) [14]. If the skin test or IgE levels are positive, it strongly indicates a high likelihood of an allergic reaction. For these high-risk patients requiring cetuximab, ensure thorough premedication or consider directly using a desensitization protocol. Additionally, cetuximab should be administered in centers that have emergency equipment and experienced staff to ensure patient safety.

Conclusion

Successful administration of cetuximab using the dose escalation method provides valuable experience and reference for clinical practice. With further research and new technology applications, the clinical use of cetuximab will become safer and more effective.

Hydrological drought dynamic using copula functions and drought center migration in the Ganjiang river basin
Authors: Weilin Liu, Jingrong Zhang, Zhanxiong Zhou, Shengnan Zhu, Lina Liu, Jianzhu Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractUnderstanding the spatiotemporal evolution of hydrological drought is essential for effective drought management. This study investigated hydrological drought in Ganjiang River Basin (GRB), China from 1959 to 2019 using observed and SWAT-simulated runoff data. The Standardized Runoff Index (SRI) and run theory were applied to identify drought duration (D), severity (S), and intensity peak (P). A gravity center model revealed spatial migration patterns, while Copula functions modeled joint distributions among drought characteristics. The findings were as follows: (1) Regions with longer drought durations tended to show greater severity, while shorter events were milder. (2) Drought centers were mainly concentrated in the central GRB, with more pronounced north-south than east–west movement, increasing with intensity. (3) The Gumbel copula can better model the dependence structure between D and P, as well as between D–S and S–P pairs, and for the trivariate relationship among D, S, and P in GRB. (4) Joint return period (Tα) and the co-occurrence return period (To) effectively bounded univariate return period (T), with more extreme droughts occurring less frequently. This study provides a quantitative basis for understanding drought dynamic of Ganjiang River Basin and further supports the improvement of regional drought mitigation and water resources planning.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-18331-0.Keywords: Hydrological drought, Dynamic characteristics, Copula function, Return period, Ganjiang river basin (GRB)Subject terms: Hydrology, Natural hazards

Introduction

Drought, as an extreme climate event, is among the most severe natural disasters worldwide. Characterized by widespread impact and long duration, it poses serious threats to food security, socioeconomic development, and ecological sustainability1,2. Notably, drought events often exhibit spatial compounding—simultaneous droughts across multiple regions, and temporal compounding—successive droughts over time3,4. In addition, droughts are generally classified into four types: meteorological, hydrological, agricultural (or soil moisture), and socioeconomic droughts5. Meteorological drought typically occurs first and may trigger the other types. Hydrological drought, marked by reduced surface and groundwater flows, often follows and directly impacts ecosystems, society, and economy6,7. Therefore, understanding the spatiotemporal evolution of hydrological drought in river basins is crucial for developing effective drought mitigation and management strategies.

Hydrological drought is primarily driven by factors such as precipitation, runoff, evaporation, and infiltration, and is usually defined in terms of surface runoff. Drought indices serve as essential tools in assessing drought conditions and understanding their drivers8,9. Among commonly used indices — such as the Streamflow Drought Index (SDI)10Surface Water Supply Index (SWSI)11 and Standardized Runoff Index (SRI)12,13—the SRI has gained broader application due to its simplicity and scalability across spatial and temporal dimensions14–17. Beyond the indices, key drought characteristics—duration (D), severity (S), and intensity peak (P) are critical in assessing drought events. However, these variables are often interdependent18,19. Focusing on a single variable may obscure their interactions and result in incomplete assessment20,21. To address this, multivariate analysis using copula functions has been increasingly adopted. Copulas can model joint distributions of drought characteristics, effectively capturing their dependencies22,23. For example, Shiau24 first introduced Archimedean copulas in drought research, while others, such as Janga Reddy and Ganguli17 extended this approach to three-dimensional joint analyses. Cheraghalizadeh, et al.25 identified the normal, Frank, and Gumbel copulas as optimal for modeling meteorological-hydrological drought dependence in the Kasilian Basin. Deger, et al.26 found the Gumbel copula best suited for drought duration-severity analysis in the Euphrates Basin. Li, et al.27 applied copulas to study the joint distribution function of drought characteristic in the Yellow River Basin. These studies highlight the value of copulas in enhancing drought risk assessment and understanding compound drought behavior.

The Ganjiang River, as a major tributary of the Yangtze River, is the largest river in the Poyang Lake Basin(PLB). Recently, under the strong influence of climate change and human activities, drought has been continuously occurring in the GRB, seriously affecting the development of the economy and society in the basin. Currently, most research is mainly focused on drought research in the PLB, especially meteorological drought. For instance, Hong, et al.28 utilized SPI to analyze the spatiotemporal variations of meteorological drought in the PLB and found that the basin transitioned from waterlogging to drought after entering the 21 st century, with drought intensity increasing spatially from south to north. Liu and Liu29 used two methods to estimate the potential evapotranspiration and calculated the corresponding SPEI. They found that the methods used to estimate potential evapotranspiration had limited effect on observed changes in dry/wet conditions. On this basis, they further investigated the spatiotemporal variations of meteorological drought in the PLB by calculating SPEI, and explored its linkages with large-scale climate factors30. However, previous studies about drought in the GRB mainly focused on meteorological drought, and researches on hydrological drought in the GRB remain limited31. Especially, due to limited observed data, there is still a lack of detailed investigations on the spatiotemporal characteristics and frequency analysis of hydrological drought in the GRB, which cannot provide effective information for the regional variation characteristics of hydrological drought.

In this study, our aim was to explore the spatiotemporal dynamic and risk of drought events in the GRB, China. Firstly, we derived the gridded runoff data in the GRB using the SWAT model and hydrological observation data from 1959 to 2019, which was used for calculating SRI. Then, we extracted three primary drought variables: duration (D), severity (S), and intensity peak (P) by using the run theory, and explored the spatiotemporal shifts of hydrological drought through the gravity center model. In addition, we constructed two- and three-dimensional joint distribution models of hydrological drought characteristics in the GRB using copulas to determine drought periods. The results of this study could help in comprehensively understanding the spatiotemporal evolution of hydrological drought in the GRB. The process of this study is shown in Fig. 1.

Framework of the study.

Study area and data

Study area

The GRB is located on the southern bank of the middle and lower reaches of the Yangtze River in China (113.58°–116.63°E, 24.52°–28.75°N). Flowing from south to north through Jiangxi Province, the Ganjiang River empties into Poyang Lake, China’s largest freshwater lake. As the largest tributary of the PLB, it spans approximately 766 km with a drainage area of 83,500 km², accounting for 51.5% of the PLB32,33. The GRB has a subtropical humid monsoon climate, with an average annual precipitation of 1621.2 mm and a mean temperature of 18.3 °C (1959–2019). About 44% of the rainfall occurs during the rainy season(April–June)34,35 while the dry season(October-next February) is often marked by droughts (e.g., in 1963, 1978, 1986, 1991, 2003–2004, 2007–2009, 2011, and 2013). In recent decades, rapid economic development has significantly increased water demand, exacerbating the challenges posed by recurring droughts. Data

Six hydrological stations (Julongtan, Xiashan, Ji’an, Xiajiang, Zhangshu, and Waizhou; see Table 1), located along the main stream and tributaries of the GRB, were selected for this study. Monthly runoff data from 1959 to 2019 were provided by the Hydrology and Water Resource Survey Bureau of Jiangxi Province and used for SWAT model calibration, validation, and drought analysis. Meanwhile, daily meteorological data from 13 national stations were obtained from the National Meteorological Information Center of China (CMA) (http://data.cma.cn), including maximum and minimum air temperature, precipitation, sunshine duration, wind speed, and relative humidity. All datasets used have been widely applied in previous studies and are considered reliable29,30.

Methodology

SRI

SRI, which was proposed by Shukla and Wood12 has been widely utilized in the assessment and quantification of hydrological drought across numerous regions14–16,36. Similar to the standardized precipitation index (SPI)37the calculation of the SRI encompasses three main steps: Firstly, it requires the accumulation of the monthly runoff series over a specific accumulation period (e.g., 1–12 months); Subsequently, a specific probability distribution is fitted to the runoff series. Finally, it is transformed into a standard normal distribution to obtain the SRI. More details about the SRI can be retrieved from the reference12. The drought severity classification of the SRI was the same as that for SPI adopted from McKee et al. (Table 2)37–39.

Hydrological model

The Soil and Water Assessment Tool (SWAT), developed by the US Department of Agriculture in 1990 s, is a semi-distributed physically based hydrological model for water resources managers40. The model can simulate various hydro physicochemical processes in large basins under varying conditions for a continuous period of time, which has been widely applied in many basins around the world13,41–43.

In this study, SWAT model was used to conduct hydrological modeling of the GRB, dividing the basin into 81 sub-basins and generating simulated runoff data for calculating the SRI, in order to analyze the spatiotemporal characteristics of drought events in the GRB. Identification of hydrological drought characteristics

Run theory has been widely employed to extract drought characteristics by comparing drought index values with a given threshold45. When the index value falls below this threshold, it is considered as a drought event. Drought events are typically described by extracting drought characteristic variables, including duration (D), severity (S), and intensity peak (P). In this study, the run theory was also applied to detect hydrological drought events from the SRI-3 time series (as illustrated in Fig. 3).

Schematic diagram of the run theory.

The length of the horizontal axis that is below the threshold corresponds to the drought duration (D), represented as the number of consecutive months affected during a drought event. The grey-shaded area in the figure represents the drought severity (S), defined as the cumulative sum of absolute SRI values throughout the drought duration. The drought intensity peak (P) is the maximum absolute value of SRI-3 during the drought event. When the SRI-3 value is below − 0.5, it indicates a drought event (in Fig. 3E–K all represent drought events in the corresponding years). If the SRI-3 value is below − 0.5 for two or more consecutive months, it indicates one drought event. If two drought events are spaced 1 month apart, they are combined into one (in Fig. 3, A is considered one drought event; B and D are spaced only 1 month apart and are also combined into one).

Copula-based modeling of hydrological drought variables

In this study, eight commonly used copulas, namely Clayton, Frank, Gumbel, t, Gaussian, Joe, BB1 and BB6 copulas49,50 were used to build the multivariate joint distribution among drought characteristics variables D, S, and P, explaining the dependency structure between drought characteristics. Results

Hydrological model performance

In this study, SWAT model was utilized to simulate the hydrological processes by dividing the basin into 81 sub-basins(as demonstrated in Supplementary Materials Figure S1) using the continuous monthly runoff data observed at six hydrological stations from 1959 to 2019 in the GRB. Across the overall period, the data set was divided into three parts: warm-up period (1959–1960), calibration period (1961–1999) and validation period (2000–2019). Among them, the warm-up period was used to initialize the model, which was excluded from the analysis. As shown in Figure S2 of the Supplementary Materials, the graphical comparison between observed and simulated runoff from 1961 to 2019 demonstrates good agreement both in magnitude and trend across all six stations. Table S1 (see in Supplementary Materials) presents the performance indicators (R2, NSE, and Re), showing that the R2 in both the calibration and validation periods was above 0.85 at all stations. Moreover, NSE in both was above 0.80, and the ัReั values were below 20% at six stations. The simulation reflected the observed runoff rationally in the GRB, indicating good performance of the model. Therefore, the simulation results can be employed to calculate the SRI of the studied basin.

Spatiotemporal characteristics of drought events

Temporal evolution

Based on SRI-3, a total of 39 hydrological drought events were identified by run theory in the GRB, and the drought characteristics (D, S, and P) were also extracted. The variations in these drought characteristics across different eras were depicted in Fig. 4.

Statistical chart of characteristic variables of hydrological drought in different eras (based on data from Waizhou station).

During the period of 1961 to 2019, there were many drought events in the GRB, with an average D of 5.54 months, an average S of 5.03, and an average P of 1.27, respectively. The droughts during this period were characterized by relatively long durations and significant severity, reaching a moderate drought level. The average values of D, S, and P were the highest in the 1960 s, reaching 9.86 months for D, 12.58 for S, and 1.66 for P, respectively. Thus, the drought level belonged to the severe drought in the 1960s. Compared with the 1960 s, in the 1980 s, the average D, S, and P were 3.88 months, 3.16, and 0.90, respectively, indicating a mild drought. At this time, the average P was the smallest among all periods. It can be seen that the average drought conditions were alleviated from the 1960 s to the 1990 s in the GRB, while from the 1990 s to the 2010 s, the average D, S, and P began to show an increasing trend. This result indicated that after the 1990 s, the occurrence of drought events in the GRB has become increasingly serious.

Spatial evolution

Using the runoff dataset across the GRB generated by SWAT, the SRI-3 of 81 sub-basins were calculated. Then, hydrological drought events were identified, and the drought characteristics (D, S, and P) were also extracted in the GRB based on SRI-3. The spatial distributions of the average D, S, and P in the GRB as illustrated in Fig. 5.

Spatial distributions of the average duration, severity, and peak of hydrological drought in the GRB.

Overall, the average D of drought events in the GRB ranged from 4.1 to 7.5 months, the average S ranged from 4.6 to 8.9, and the average P ranged from 0.91 to 1.13. Specifically, the average drought D roughly showing a spatial distribution pattern of longer average drought duration in the upstream and downstream. the regions with an average drought D of 6.1–7.5 were mostly located in the area to the east of the Xiashan station as well as in the belt-like region between Xiajiang station and Ji’an station. The spatial distribution of the average S was generally similar to that of the average D. The region with the highest average S was located to the west of Ji’an station. The average S in the upstream and downstream was relatively high, ranging from 5.9 to 8.9. The average P was higher to the northwest, southwest, and southeast of Xiajiang station and to the south of Xiashan station, ranging from 0.96 to 1.13. According to the spatial distribution of D, S, and P, it indicated that, in the GRB, a longer drought duration was usually accompanied by a higher severity.

Gravity center migration trajectory of drought

To further investigate the spatiotemporal variation of hydrological drought in the GRB, we adopted the gravity center model to investigate the gravity center migration for different drought grades. The spatial distribution and migration route of the gravity centers for different drought grades in the GRB were illustrated in Fig. 6, while the migration distances and directions during distinct periods see in Supplementary Materials Figure S2.

The distribution and migration route of the gravity centers for different drought grades in the GRB.

As shown in Fig. 6 and Figure S3, spatially, the gravity center of droughts was mainly concentrated in the central region of the GRB, and the migration routes of different drought grades were different across the eras. The distribution of the gravity center for both mild and moderate droughts are relatively concentrated, and the gravity center did not migrate significantly across the eras. From the 1960 s to the 2010 s, the overall migration trend of the gravity center for both mild and moderate drought is toward the southwest, the migration distance was relatively greater from the 1980 s to the 1990 s, with migration distances of 22.39 km and 21.02 km, respectively. Spatial distribution of the center for severe and extreme drought were significantly different from that of mild and moderate drought. Such a center for severe droughts migrated with greater magnitude than those for mild and moderate droughts. The center for severe drought migrated along a path from the southeast to the southwest, then to the northwest, and finally back to the southeast. Overall, the gravity center shifted southward, with the largest migration range reaching 59.03 km from the 1970 s to the 1980s. The center of extreme drought was concentrated in the central part of the basin. The migration generally trended toward the southeast, with the largest migration range of 109.72 km occurring between the 1970 s and the 1980s. On the whole, the drought centers of each grade were mostly found in the central part of the basin, with a greater trend of movement in the north-south direction compared to the east-west direction. Meanwhile, the higher the drought grade, the greater the migration distance of drought center.

Table 7 showed as D, S, and P increased, the return periods—univariate, bivariate, and trivariate—also rose. It was noted that T consistently exceeded Tα but remained below To, suggesting that Tα and To effectively serve as the lower and upper bounds of T in real-world drought scenarios. This approach enhances drought risk management by providing a more accurate interval estimation of return periods.

The relationship between Tα and To at the same univariate return period (T) revealed the correlation between drought variables. A higher correlation led to a longer Tα and a shorter To, while a lower correlation had the opposite effect. For instance, when T = 2a, the D–S pair exhibited the shortest co-occurrence return period (To = 2.12) and the longest joint return period (Tα = 1.89) among the D–S, S–P, and D–P pairs. Conversely, the D–P pair showed the longest co-occurrence return period (To = 2.38) and the shortest joint return period (Tα = 1.73). These results indicated that at a univariate return period of 2a, the correlation between D and S was strongest, followed by S and P, with the weakest correlation observed between D and P. This conclusion aligned with the results presented in Table 5.

When T = 5a, the values of Tα and To for D–S, S–P, D–P, and D–S–P were 4.50a, 3.99a, 3.66a, 3.46a, and 5.62a, 6.69a, 7.88a, and 10.44a respectively. Similarly, when T = 10a, the corresponding values were 8.90a, 7.76a, 7.02a, 6.57a, and 11.41a, 14.05a, 17.36a, and 25.06a, respectively. It was evident that as T increased from 5a to 10a, Tα for D–S, S–P, D–P, and D–S–P rose by 4.40a, 3.77a, 3.36a, and 3.11a, while the corresponding To increased by 5.79a, 7.36a, 9.48a, and 14.62a, respectively. This meant, for the same increase in univariate return periods, the rise in To for bivariate and trivariate cases was substantially larger than that of the corresponding Tα, underscoring the increasing difficulty for drought characteristics to simultaneously reach a specific return period threshold.

Table 7 also showed that To for D–S–P increased substantially as T lengthened. For T = 2a, with D = 2.51, S = 1.82, and P = 0.87, To was 2.61a, suggesting that a mild drought event lasting 2.51 months, with a severity of 1.82 and a intensity peak of 0.87, had a return period of approximately 3a. Similarly, For T = 50a, the return period for an extreme drought event lasting 18.08 months, with a severity of 23.84 and a peak intensity of 2.71, was estimated to be approximately 148a. These results indicated that in the GRB, the likelihood of long-duration, high-severity extreme drought event was relatively low, whereas the probability of short-duration, low-severity drought event was higher. Thus, proactive drought mitigation measures were more effective in preventing drought processes from escalating into severe drought disasters, thereby improving drought risk management.

Discussion

In this study, SRI, a widely used drought index, was applied to evaluate hydrological drought in the GRB due to its comparability over time and space. Xu55 used SRI at various time scales in hydrological drought, and found that as the time scale increased, the frequency and intensity of hydrological drought occurrence significantly weakened, and the duration of drought events became shorter. Thus, choosing an appropriate timescale was crucial for accurately characterizing hydrological drought. Xiang, et al.19 found that SRI-3, with a threshold of −0.5, was more suitable for assessing seasonal hydrological characteristics and drought risk. Sediqi and Komori56 found that SRI-3 provided an understanding of short-term variations in water resources, which can better serve as a theoretical basis for managing agricultural operations. Therefore, SRI-3 with a threshold of −0.5 was selected to characterize hydrological drought in this study. According to literature records of Flood and Drought Disaster in Jiangxi, there are two typical drought events, one in 1978 with a continuous autumn winter drought, and the other in 2003 with severe drought. The two drought events extracted by runoff theory (August 1978 to February 1979 and July 2003 to March 2004) were very consistent with these historical records, indicating the reliability of the drought events extracted in this study and demonstrating the applicability of SRI-3 with a threshold of −0.5 for hydrological drought in the GRB. In addition, the results found that the D and S of hydrological drought events exhibited a decreasing trend, followed by an increasing trend, intensified after the 1990 s, which was consistent with the findings of Han, et al.31.

Estimating the return period of drought can provide valuable information for evaluating risk associated with drought events24. In the frequency analysis of drought variables, we conducted a multivariate drought probability analysis for D, S, and P using copulas. As we know, drought is a natural hazard with multivariate characteristics, and traditional univariate based drought probability analysis often ignores correlations between drought variables, leading to over or underestimation of drought frequencies. In fact, we performed correlation analysis to examine the dependence structure between drought characteristics variables in the GRB, and the results showed the dependence was obvious between D-S, D-P, and S-P with correlation coefficients all exceeding 0.5, which indicated a strong correlation among drought variables. Hence, the drought probability and return period derived from univariate distributions were not adequately reliable for drought early warning in the GRB. In contrast, drought analyses using copulas effectively capture the inherent correlations among drought characteristics, making this approach well-suited for hydrological drought studies, which is also confirmed by several other studies57–59. Meanwhile, based on the multivariate joint distribution, Tα and To were estimated. In the bivariate joint distribution, for a given T, the Tα of D and S was the highest, while To was the lowest, indicating that the correlation between D and S was the strongest. Conversely, the Tα of D and P was the lowest, while To was the highest, suggesting the weakest correlation between D and P. This result generally aligned with the aforementioned results of dependence structure among drought characteristics. Additionally, in our study, the return period T for any single drought variable in the GRB was found to be greater than Tα while lower than To. When the univariate return period increased, the increase in To for two-dimensional and three-dimensional variables was more obvious than that for Tα, indicating that joint events of drought variables were more likely to occur than co-occurrence events. The analysis also indicated that the multivariate drought probability analysis will not underestimate the risk of drought or overestimate it. Similar results were reported in Ma, et al.53 and Shaw and Chithra60.

Last but not least, in this study, based on monthly streamflow data, the monthly scale SRI with a fixed threshold of −0.5 was applied to identify drought events in the GRB over the past 60 years. This method effectively captured the hydrological drought events in the GRB, and the identified drought events showed a high degree of consistency with literature records, demonstrating the reliability and suitability of this method for long-term hydrological drought analysis in the GRB. It is evident that this method ensures the accuracy and reliability of drought identification at the monthly scale, making it suitable for long-term drought characterization. However, this method has limitations in capturing droughts under runoff data with finer temporal resolutions. Specifically, its sensitivity and adaptability are relatively limited in capturing short-term droughts61. In recent years, the variable threshold approach has been used for drought research62. Sutanto and Van Lanen63 compared the variable threshold approach and the SSI-1, and found that the variable threshold approach is more sensitive to short-term droughts, making it particularly suitable for drought detection at daily time resolutions. Brunner, et al.64 used a variable threshold method for seasonal flow regimes to reveal elevation- and season-dependent drought processes and severity changes in the Alps over the past 50 years. It can be concluded that the variable threshold approach is more effective in identifying drought events based on daily scale data and is well-suited for regions with significant seasonal flow variability. Therefore, future drought research in the GRB should be conducted using daily runoff data combined with the variable threshold approach to identify drought events, thereby further enhancing the understanding and characterization of droughts in the GRB. Additionally, conducting systematic analyses of drought propagation and evolution from a three-dimensional spatiotemporal perspective will contribute to a deeper understanding of drought dynamic and promote more scientific and accurate drought risk assessments.

Conclusions

In this study, the spatiotemporal characteristics of hydrological drought in the GRB were investigated using hydrological observation data and SWAT-simulated data from 1959 to 2019 based on the SRI. Three key drought characteristics D, S, and P were identified through the run theory. The gravity center model was applied to reveal drought migration patterns, and multivariate copula functions were employed to construct joint distributions of drought variables and estimate corresponding return periods. The main conclusions are summarized as follows:

Drought conditions in the GRB showed a weakening trend from the 1960 s to the 1990 s, but have intensified since the 1990s. Droughts in the upper reaches tended to have longer durations, while those in the middle reaches exhibited higher intensity. The lower reaches experienced the greatest drought risk, characterized by both prolonged duration and intense peaks. Overall, regions with longer drought durations generally experienced greater severity, whereas those with shorter durations had lower severity.

Effects of IL17/23 Inhibitors on Markers of Subclinical Atherosclerosis in Patients with Psoriasis: An Observational Study
Authors: Aikaterini Tsiogka, Stergios Soulaidopoulos, Stamatios Gregoriou, Natalia Rompoti, Pantelis Panagakis, Marina Papoutsaki, Panagiotis Kostakis, George Kontochristopoulos, Konstantinos Tsioufis, Charalambos Vlachopoulos, Alexander Stratigos, Dimitrios Rigopoulos
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
IntroductionTo date, several reports demonstrated improved markers of subclinical atherosclerosis in patients with psoriasis treated with biologics. However, data on the antiatherogenic effect of IL23 inhibitors are sparse. Herein, we sought to assess the impact of 1-year treatment with an interleukin (IL)-17 or IL-23 inhibitor on arterial stiffness in patients with moderate-to-severe psoriasis.MethodsThis observational cohort study included patients with moderate-to-severe psoriasis treated with either an IL-17 inhibitor or an IL-23 inhibitor or a conventional systemic agent/apremilast (control group) for 52 weeks. The primary outcome was the evaluation of changes in carotid-femoral pulse wave velocity (PWV) and augmentation index normalized to 75 beats/min (AIx75) after 24 and 52 weeks. Secondary outcomes were the comparison of change in PWV and AIx75 between the study groups and the assessment of psoriasis disease severity scores and in ankle-brachial index (ABI).ResultsIn total, 66 patients (39 male/27 female), with a mean [standard deviation (SD)] age of 56 (13.1) years, were included; 21 received an IL-17 inhibitor, 29 an IL-23 inhibitor, and 16 a conventional systemic agent or apremilast (control group). PWV and AIx75 improved in all groups at weeks 24 and 52. However, a statistically significant reduction of PWV was observed only in the IL23 group as early as at week 24 (p < 0.001). AIx75 was reduced significantly in the IL17 group at week 24 and in both IL-17 and IL-23 groups at week 52 (p < 0.001 and p = 0.014, respectively). The reduction of PWV and AIx75 at week 52 did not reach the level of statistical significance in the control group and was statistically comparable between the IL-17 and IL-23 groups.ConclusionsBoth IL-23 and IL-17 inhibition exhibited comparable improvement of arterial stiffness in patients with psoriasis. Further studies are needed to evaluate the long-term impact of biologics on the cardiovascular status of these patients.Retrospectively registered ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT07169682. Introduction

Psoriasis is a chronic, immune-mediated skin disease affecting 2–3% of the general population [1]. In addition to cutaneous involvement, patients with psoriasis often suffer from comorbidities, such as psoriatic arthritis (PsA), metabolic syndrome, and atherosclerosis, and also exhibit high prevalence of the classical cardiovascular risk factors, including hypertension, dyslipidemia, diabetes mellitus, and obesity [2]. Moreover, current knowledge considers psoriasis as an independent risk factor for cardiovascular disease (CVD), as it is thought to increase the risk for CVD by 25%, especially in patients with severe skin disease [3, 4]. Hence, there are several reports of myocardial infarction, stroke, heart failure, and sudden death in patients with psoriasis or PsA [5]. Although the precise pathomechanism behind this association is yet to be elucidated, the chronic systemic inflammation with the predominance of T helper (Th)-1 and Th17 immune responses, which characterizes psoriasis, seems to also affect the arterial vessel walls, initiating endothelial dysfunction and subsequent acceleration of atherosclerosis [6, 7].

Given the importance of early detection of subclinical atherosclerosis and identification of patients with psoriasis at greater risk of CVD development, to date, many authors have evaluated diverse surrogate markers that could indicate early structural or functional alterations of the vasculature, including arterial stiffness [8, 9]. The latter constitutes a very early finding of endothelial dysfunction, which may precede the development of atherosclerosis [10]. The carotid–femoral pulse wave velocity (PWV) is widely considered as the gold standard method to assess arterial stiffness because of its relative ease in determination, its reliability, and the growing body of evidence showing its association with incident CVD, as well as in patients with psoriasis compared with healthy controls [8, 11–13]. A further marker of arterial stiffness and peripheral vascular resistance constitutes the augmentation index (AIx). Increased values of AIx indicate increased wave reflection from the periphery and/or earlier return of the reflected wave as a result of increased arterial stiffness and PWV and vice versa [14, 15].

To date, many studies have investigated whether inhibition of inflammatory cytokines involved in the shared pathogenetic mechanism between psoriasis and atherosclerosis, including the tumor necrosis factor alpha (TNF-a) and interleukin (IL)-17 and IL-12/23, could modify the evolution of the atherosclerotic process [16–20]. Most of them have been conducted in patients with psoriasis or PsA receiving TNF-a inhibitors, the IL-12/23 inhibitor ustekinumab, or IL-17 inhibitors, with the greater majority of them providing promising results by showing improved markers of subclinical atherosclerosis in these patients. However, literature data on the anti-atherogenic effect of IL-23 inhibitors are sparse.

The aim of the present study was to assess the impact of 1-year treatment with IL-17 and IL-23 inhibitors on arterial stiffness in patients with psoriasis by evaluating changes in PWV and AIx.

Methods

Patients

This observational cohort study was performed between September 2021 and September 2024. Consecutive patients with plaque psoriasis with or without psoriatic arthritis, who were monitored in the outpatient department of a tertiary academic hospital for skin diseases, were included in the study if they fulfilled all of the following inclusion criteria: (1) age ≥ 18 years; (2) clinical diagnosis of moderate-to-severe psoriasis; and (3) treatment with an IL-17 inhibitor [secukinumab (Cosentyx®), ixekizumab (Taltz®), brodalumab (Kyntheum®)] or an IL-23 inhibitor [risankizumab (Skyrizi®), guselkumab (Tremfya®)] or a conventional systemic agent (e.g., methotrexate, cyclosporine) or a small molecule [apremilast (Otezla®)] as monotherapy for a total of 52 weeks. The exact agent was chosen from the treatment physician of each patient on the basis of disease characteristics and comorbidities (Table 1). Ethical Approval

The study was approved by the “Andreas Sygros” hospital’s ethics committee (protocol number 3610/20-05-2021) and was performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki of 1964. All subjects provided informed consent to participate in the study and for publication of their data. A retrospective study registration at ClinicalTrials.gov was performed to pursue publication of valuable research.

Outcomes

The primary outcome was the assessment of the effect of treatment with an IL-17 or an IL-23 inhibitor on arterial stiffness in patients with moderate-to-severe psoriasis by evaluating the PWV and the AIx normalized to 75 beats/min (AIx75) at baseline and after 24 and 52 weeks of treatment. Secondary outcomes were the comparison of change in PWV and AIx75 between the study groups and the assessment of change in psoriasis disease severity scores and in ankle-brachial index (ABI).

Baseline Characteristics

At baseline, demographic data, psoriasis characteristics (duration, family history, prior therapy, and assessment scores), cardiovascular comorbidities, history of CVD, blood pressure, heart rate, and indices of subclinical atherosclerosis were recorded.

Monitoring of Psoriasis

Psoriasis assessment scores including Psoriasis Area Severity Index (PASI), physician global assessment (PGA), Body Surface Area (BSA), and Dermatology Life Quality Index (DLQI) were assessed at baseline and after 24 and 52 weeks of treatment.

Monitoring of Cardiovascular Parameters

Prior to the measurements each patient rested in a supine position for 10 min in a quiet room. Brachial blood pressure (BP), heart rate (HR) and right and left ankle brachial index (ABI) were recorded using an automated digital oscillometric sphygmomanometer, towards a complete evaluation of the cardiovascular status, including peripheral artery disease. PWV was measured using the Complior automated system (Alam Medical, Vincennes, France), whereby two non-invasive pressure sensors simultaneously recorded the carotid and femoral pulse waves and the PWV was then automatically estimated as the distance divided by the transit time between waves (m/s) [21]. Three sequential measurements were obtained and the mean was used for the analysis. A SphygmoCor system (Sphygmocor CvMS device, AtCor Medical, Sydney, Australia) was used to record the AIx of each patient, which was defined as the difference between the peaks of the first and the second recorded pulse waveform divided by the pulse pressure [22]. The right radial artery was utilized for the acquisition of the peripheral vascular waveform. As there is a linear correlation between heart rate and AIx and in order to achieve comparable measurements, the adjusted AIx to a heart rate of 75 beats per minute (AIx75) was recorded [23].

All patients underwent measurement of carotid-femoral PWV, AIx75 and right and left ABI at baseline, after 24 and 52 weeks of treatment.

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using Statistical Package for the Social Sciences for iOS (version 29.0.2.2 IBM Corb: Armonk, NY, USA). Descriptive statistics were used to present the patients’ characteristics. Variables with normal distribution were reported as mean ± standard deviation (SD), while not normally distributed outcomes were reported as median values and range. Categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages. Between-group analysis was performed using the Kruskal–Wallis Test and Student’s t-test or one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) test. Statistical significance was determined at a two-tailed p-value of 5% (0.05).

Patients receiving anti-psoriatic treatment showed a reduction of PWV and AIx75 in all groups at weeks 24 and 52. However, a statistically significant reduction of PWV was observed only in the IL-23 group at week 24 [95% confidence interval (CI) −1.21, −0.05, p = 0.035], which was maintained up to week 52 (95% CI −2.15, −0.76, p < 0.001) (Fig. 1). On the contrary, AIx75 exhibited a statistically significant reduction in the IL-17 group at week 24 (95% CI −7.46, −0.69, p = 0.022) and in both IL-17 and IL-23 groups at week 52 (95% CI −8.45, −2.73, p < 0.001 and 95% CI −9.23, −1.36, p = 0.014, respectively) (Fig. 2). The reduction of PWV and AIx75 did not reach the level of statistical significance in the control group. In patients receiving either an IL-17 or an IL-23 inhibitor (groups 1 + 2), both PWV and AIx75 exhibited a statistically significant reduction at week 24 (95% CI −0.71, −0.01, p = 0.047 and 95% CI −4.79, −0.69, p = 0.011, respectively) and week 52 (95% CI −1.62, −0.51, p < 0.001 and 95% CI −7.63, −3.33, p < 0.001, respectively). As regards the secondary outcomes, the between-group analysis demonstrated significant difference in both PWV and AIx75 reduction up to week 52 (95% CI −3.33, −1.21, p = 0.024 and 95% CI −1.72, −0.52, p = 0.043 for PWV and AIx75, respectively) (Fig. 3). Further analysis revealed significantly higher reduction of PWV in groups 1 and 2 compared with control group (95% CI −2.19, −0.39, p = 0.014 and 95% CI −1.76, −0.44, p < 0.001, respectively). Similarly, AIx75 reduction was more pronounced in groups 1 and 2 compared with control group (95% CI −6.86, −0.54, p < 0.001 and 95% CI −6.62, −0.13, p < 0.001, respectively). The reduction of mean PWV and AIx75 up to week 52 did not differ significantly between group 1 and group 2 (95% CI −1.33, 1.74, p = 0.387 and 95% CI −4.91, 4,26, p = 0.910, respectively). Discussion

The present study provides novel information regarding the possible beneficial effect of IL-23 inhibitors on arterial stiffness in patients with psoriasis by exhibiting a statistically significant reduction in PWV at week 24, and in both PWV and AIx75 at week 52. Moreover, it confirms previous findings regarding the positive effect of IL17 inhibitors on markers of subclinical atherosclerosis in patients with psoriasis. Hence, in line with published literature, it seems that IL-23/Th17 blockade may decrease the inflammatory burden of psoriatic disease and improve arterial stiffness. The between-group analysis revealed a more pronounced reduction of PWV and AIx75 in patients receiving either an IL-17 or an IL-23 inhibitor compared with those treated with apremilast or conventional treatment, while reduction of PWV and AIx75 at week 52 was similar between the biologic-treated patients. In this context, it seems that IL-17 and IL-23 inhibitors may demonstrate similar impact on arterial stiffness in patients with psoriasis. Interestingly, we identified normal ABI values in all groups at both baseline and week 52, suggesting that this marker, which is principally used to assess peripheral artery disease, may not be able to detect subtle changes in the atherosclerotic burden.

Interestingly, even though statistically significant reduction of AIx75 was documented in each of groups 1 and 2 separately at week 52, PWV was significantly reduced only in the IL-23 group already at week 24, while it did not reach the level of statistical significance in the IL-17 group in either of the two follow-up visits. The insufficient reduction in PWV in group 1 may be due to the small number of patients, given the observed trend toward a statistical significance in the week 52 assessment. It has to be highlighted that the different responsiveness of these indices to biologic treatment observed herein may be explained due to the small sample size in each study group or due to the higher prevalence of PsA in group 1, which may be associated with increased cardiovascular risk, or due to the fact that AIx is an indirect marker of arterial stiffness, which measures the pulse wave reflection. The latter depends not only on vascular wall alterations, but also on the peripheral vasculature tonus, and thus, the magnitude of the pulse wave reflection, which has been found to change subsequently to effective antiinflammatory treatment [24].

The above results have to be interpreted with caution, considering that this was a nonrandomized, noncontrolled trial with small sample size, especially in the control group, and unequal distribution of patients within the study groups, which may have influenced the treatment allocation and the robustness of the results. In the era of the increasing understanding of the significance of early systemic intervention in psoriasis, enrollment of patients with moderate-to-severe psoriasis in non-biologic study arms for a long period is challenging, considering the abundance of highly efficacious and safe biological agents, which have been proven to improve long-term outcomes of both psoriasis and psoriasis-associated comorbidities.

In addition, we have previously conducted a systematic review on the impact of treatment with IL-17 and IL-23 inhibitors on subclinical atherosclerosis in patients with psoriasis and/or psoriatic arthritis [25]. This could identify six observational studies and two RCTs, which evaluated the effect of anti-IL-17 therapy on diverse surrogate markers of subclinical atherosclerosis, including either structural changes of the vasculature or arterial stiffness. Positive effect of biologic therapy was demonstrated by six studies, while the rest of them exhibited no statistically significant benefit on subclinical atherosclerosis. Interestingly, no studies assessing the anti-atherogenic effect of IL-23 inhibitors were identified [25].

Apart from the biologics targeting the IL-23/Th17 axis, many studies have assessed the effect of other systemic therapeutic agents on subclinical atherosclerosis. Already in 2011, Avgerinou et al. demonstrated improvement of flow mediated dilatation (FMD) but not of PWV in 14 patients receiving adalimumab for 12 weeks, while subsequent studies supported the beneficial effect of TNF-a blockade on endothelial function and arterial stiffness [26–29]. However, a RCT conducted in 2017 evaluated the vascular inflammation by means of positron emission tomography–computed tomography in 107 patients, who received either adalimumab or placebo for 16 weeks followed by adalimumab up to week 52. At week 16, no difference in vascular inflammation was observed, while there was a modest increase in carotid inflammation after 52 weeks of treatment with adalimumab [30]. Similarly, Ortolan et al. sought to evaluate the long-term effect of TNF-a treatment on subclinical atherosclerosis in patients with PsA by evaluating the intima media thickness (IMT) and FMD, showing a slight progression of subclinical atherosclerosis during the first 2 years of treatment, which decelerated in follow-up extension to 5 years [31]. The phase 4, VIP-U trial assessed the effect of ustekinumab on vascular inflammation. IL-12/23 blockade led to a transient reduction of aortic inflammation at week 12, which was, however, not maintained until week 52, while there was a sustained reduction of inflammatory cytokines associated with CVD until the end of treatment [32]. Finally, in the phase 4, VIP-A trial, apremilast exhibited a neutral association with aortic vascular inflammation and led to a variable but generally beneficial decrease in some cardiometabolic biomarkers (e.g. ferritin, ketone bodies, β-hydroxybutyrate acetone) as well as in the visceral and subcutaneous fat after 52 weeks of treatment [33].

Clinical Implications

Given that a potential amelioration of surrogate markers of subclinical atherosclerosis does not necessarily reflect a reduced CVD risk in patients with psoriasis under systemic treatment, some studies have used hard cardiovascular endpoints to specifically assess the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACEs), including myocardial infarction, cerebrovascular accident and cardiovascular death, in these patients [34, 35]. Moreover, it has been debated whether biologics could even be associated with an increased risk of CVD, especially in the induction period, given the observed MACEs in patients with psoriasis receiving briakinumab, which led to discontinuation of the development program of this agent [36]. In 2017, a meta-analysis of 38 RCTs found no significant increase in the risk of MACEs in patients with psoriasis exposed to biologic therapies with TNF-a inhibitors, ustekinumab, or IL-17 inhibitors (secukinumab, ixekizumab) [35]. These findings were supported by the results of a following larger meta-analysis of 77 RCTs, which did not reveal any significant change in the short-term risk of MACEs or congestive heart failure in patients with psoriasis [37]. In 2024, Cho et al. reported no statistically significant difference in MACEs in 2886 Korean patients receiving TNF-a or IL-12/23 inhibitors, and a systematic review of nine RCTs demonstrated no change in MACEs risk in patients receiving IL-17 inhibitors, either secukinumab or ixekizumab [38, 39]. In contrast with the above-mentioned findings, a recent 19-year real-world analysis of data reported to the US Food and Drug Administration Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS) found elevated and similar reporting rates for MACEs, with a descending order of association being with IL-12/23 > IL-17 > IL-23 > TNF-a inhibitors, reflecting a potential risk of MACEs in the real-world use of biologics in psoriasis [40]. Finally, another retrospective FAERS analysis of biologic-related cardiotoxicity unraveled the risk of CVD, atrial fibrillation, and pericarditis, emphasizing the importance of monitoring and evaluating the cardiovascular safety profiles of biological agents used in psoriasis treatment [41].

Considering the above, current evidence suggests that TNF-a inhibitors may be superior to other biologics in reducing cardiovascular risks and prevent MACEs in patients with psoriasis, even though emerging evidence support the safety and efficacy also of IL17 and IL23 inhibitors in patients with CVD [42]. Hence, the last EuroGuiderm guideline on the treatment of psoriasis vulgaris (2020) provided detailed recommendations regarding the management of patients with comorbidities, including ischemic heart disease/atherosclerosis [43, 44]. In particular, the authors suggested initiation of appropriate investigations and treatment in accordance with current European Society of Cardiology guidance in patients with established CVD, and predilection of methotrexate, TNF-a inhibitors, ustekinumab, and IL-17 inhibitors over cyclosporine or acitretin in patients with psoriasis and ischemic heart disease [43, 44].

Limitations

One of the main limitations of our study is the small sample size, especially in the control group, which limits the generalizability and the statistical robustness of the results and restricts the implementation of further subgroup analyses. A further significant limitation was the inability to perform a nonrandomized, non-blinded study design due to limited financial resources. Since this may be associated with treatment selection bias, our findings should be interpreted with caution and confirmed in further prospective, controlled RCTs. However, the observed improvements in arterial stiffness support the hypothesis that subclinical atherosclerosis may be, in part, alleviated by the control of systemic inflammation in patients with psoriasis, which may be achieved with both biologics, and to a lesser extent, conventional systemic agents. In addition, we did not evaluate hard cardiovascular endpoints to support the potential of biologic therapy to reduce the risk of CVD in these patients, while the study was self-controlled, and no patient stratification according to known cardiovascular risk factors was performed. However, baseline characteristics including cardiovascular status and risk factors were comparable between the three groups. Nonetheless, psoriatic arthritis was more prevalent in the IL-17 group, a fact that could have influenced the results, since it is associated with increased cardiovascular risk. However, this discrepancy as well as the differences between the rest of the psoriasis disease assessment scores were not statistically significant between the groups.

The main strength of our study is the demonstration of data regarding the positive effect of IL-23 inhibitors on biomarkers of subclinical atherosclerosis in patients with psoriasis. In addition, we provide novel data on the utility of AIx75 as surrogate marker of subclinical atherosclerosis, suggesting that it may complement PWV to assess arterial stiffness in the context of cardiovascular risk estimation of patients with psoriasis.

Conclusions

In the era of the increasing understanding of the common pathogenetic mechanism between psoriasis and atherosclerosis, effective anti-psoriatic treatment with biologics has raised the prospect of early systemic intervention to modify the disease course and cardiovascular morbidity. This study demonstrated that inhibition of IL-23 may contribute to improving arterial stiffness in patients with psoriasis by reducing PWV and AIx75 after 1 year of treatment, while it also supported previous findings regarding the anti-atherogenic effect of IL-17 inhibition. It further showed that the mean PWV and AIx75 reduction at week 52 was comparable between the IL-17 and IL-23 groups and more pronounced compared to control group, even though this should be interpreted with caution, considering the unequal distribution of patients within the study groups. Considering that dedicated screening guidelines for CVD risk assessment in patients with psoriasis are lacking, further RCTs are needed to establish definitive biomarkers for determining cardiovascular status and evaluate the long-term impact of biologics in preventing MACEs in patients with psoriasis.

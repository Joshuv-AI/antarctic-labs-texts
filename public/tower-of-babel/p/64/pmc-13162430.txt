Retrieval‑augmented large language models for depression screening and suicide risk stratification
Authors: Wenbo Xie, Xulai Song, Zerui Lu, Gaoqiang Fei, Yujia Zhou, Siqi Ding, Fengyi Zuo, Bangyu Wu, Tianhao Gu, Lin Xu, Xiaomeng De, Bin Zhu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundDepression imposes substantial individual and societal burdens, yet more than 80% of cases remain undetected in non‑psychiatric settings, and approximately 15% of individuals with severe depression die by suicide. Existing screening methods are limited by subjective self‑report, fragmented care pathways, and stigma‑related underutilization.MethodsWe developed and locally deployed a Retrieval‑Augmented Generation (RAG) framework that integrates large language models (LLMs) with an evidence‑based clinical knowledge base for depression and suicidality. Three leading Chinese LLMs (DeepSeek, Qwen, Baichuan) were evaluated under base and RAG‑augmented configurations. The knowledge base comprised clinical practice guidelines, standard textbooks, and peer‑reviewed literature. Model performance was assessed on multi‑source datasets. Outcomes included classification of depression and suicide risk, consistency of diagnostic outputs, symptom‑summary quality, and clinician‑rated empathy and dialogue quality.ResultsRAG augmentation consistently improved diagnostic performance across datasets. For depression detection, Qwen3 + RAG achieved an F1‑score of 0.9107, and for suicide risk stratification, RAG enhanced F1‑scores by approximately 0.15 compared with non‑RAG baselines. The best‑performing configuration demonstrated high reproducibility for both depression diagnosis and suicide risk classification (overall κ = 0.93; 95% CI: 0.91–0.95). Symptom summarization was substantially improved with RAG, with relative gains of 44.1–207.2% in BLEU‑2 and 37.0–94.0% in ROUGE‑L. Clinician‑blinded ratings indicated clinician-evaluated high-level empathic expression in RAG‑enhanced models (mean scores 4.23–4.98/5), with Qwen3 + RAG exhibiting particularly strong emotional concordance (95% CI: 4.80–4.92). Dialogue quality was rated as high across domains, including fluency (mean 4.98/5) and perceived doctor‑likeness (mean 4.94/5).ConclusionsA domain‑specific, RAG‑enhanced LLM framework can deliver clinically reliable, reproducible, and empathetic depression screening and suicide risk stratification in both real‑world and simulated settings. Local on‑premises deployment supports privacy and data‑governance requirements, while the scalable architecture and structured outputs position this approach as a promising tool for augmenting mental health triage and longitudinal monitoring in resource‑constrained environments.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12888-026-07988-0.

Introduction

Depression, a pervasive mental health disorder imposes severe societal and individual burdens, including significant functional impairment, reduced quality of life, and heightened mortality risks [1]. Critically, the absence of effective screening programs leaves individuals vulnerable to escalating risk factors [2], culminating in approximately 15% of patients with severe depression dying by suicide, underscoring the urgent need for effective intervention [3, 4]. Current diagnostic and therapeutic paradigms face substantial challenges: non-psychiatric clinical settings fail to identify more than 80% of cases due to the absence of objective biomarkers. Additionally, traditional reliance on self-report scales and clinical interviews introduces subjectivity, and treatment strategies often face challenges in achieving rapid or sufficient therapeutic response, highlighting the need for optimized pharmacological approaches [5, 6]. while fragmented health systems and stigma further limit accessibility [7, 8], particularly for high-risk populations such as adolescents and postpartum women. These multifaceted barriers highlight the imperative for innovative approaches to enhance detection, risk stratification, and personalized management.

Recent advances in large language models (LLMs) have catalyzed transformative applications across healthcare, leveraging their capabilities in natural language understanding, multimodal data integration, and contextual reasoning [9, 10]. More importantly, it can also be used for at-home testing, allowing for continuous and dynamic monitoring of the user’s condition. In medicine, LLMs now support tasks ranging from diagnostic assistance to patient triage and medical documentation. For instance, models like ChatGPT-4 demonstrate diagnostic accuracy surpassing human clinicians in conditions such as PTSD and depression in controlled settings [11]. Vision-enabled systems like GPT-4o enhance radiological precision, achieving over 85% malignancy prediction accuracy in longitudinal lung cancer CT screening while reducing inter-observer variability [12, 13]. Scalable frameworks such as MedFound leverage chain-of-thought fine-tuning and hierarchical alignment with clinical guidelines to outperform predecessors in diagnosing diverse conditions, from common pathologies to rare diseases, with optimized diagnostic granularity [14]. Despite this promise, critical gaps persist in adapting LLMs to depression care. Most models often fail to incorporate domain-specific knowledge or longitudinal emotional trajectories, and the lack of standardized validation and ethical safeguards for sensitive data further limits their utility in early detection and risk prediction.

To address these critical gaps, we propose an integrated framework for depression screening and suicide risk assessment that synergizes large language models (LLMs) with evidence-based clinical knowledge retrieval [15]. Our methodology innovatively combines multi-source evaluation datasets, including real-world physician-patient dialogues, standardized clinical cases, and controlled healthy cohorts, with a dynamically updated, domain-specific knowledge repository indexed for precision retrieval. The framework incorporates clinician-guided prompt engineering and multi-task optimization to concurrently enhance diagnostic accuracy, risk stratification, and empathetic communication capabilities. Validated across three state-of-the-art Chinese LLMs, our approach demonstrates robust feasibility through local deployment with strict privacy safeguards. This scalable architecture establishes a new paradigm for augmenting mental health triage in resource-constrained settings.

Material and method

Design

This study evaluated the performance and usability of large language models (LLMs) for depression screening and suicide risk assessment using a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) framework.

Data curation and preprocessing

Three complementary evaluation datasets were assembled. First, a real-world clinician–patient dialogue corpus was derived from a 10-year archive of communications hosted on Haodf.com (haodf.com), comprising 200 depression cases, 50 non-psychiatric medical cases, and 50 non-depressive psychiatric cases. Among the depression cases, 40% were mild, 30% moderate, and 30% severe. The non-depressive psychiatric set included 20% each of PTSD, OCD, bipolar disorder, anxiety disorders, and ADHD. Depression severity was assessed strictly according to DSM-5 diagnostic criteria, while suicide risk was categorized using a structured 0–3 ordinal scale (0: no suicidal ideation; 1: suicidal ideation without a plan; 2: specific plan but not implemented; 3: recent suicide attempt). To establish a robust gold standard without relying on inter-rater agreement metrics, all case logs were independently annotated by two board-certified psychiatrists. Any diagnostic discrepancies were resolved through a final review by a third, senior psychiatrist to reach a definitive consensus. This corpus was used to evaluate model screening capability in authentic clinical conversations. Second, a standardized patient case set (n = 154) was constructed, as well as 59 depression profiles rigorously developed in accordance with clinical diagnostic criteria. These profiles spanned diverse demographics (age 18–65 years; sex/gender: male, female, other; multi-ethnic representation), severity levels (mild, moderate, severe), and common comorbidities (e.g., anxiety, insomnia). Each profile contained detailed medical history, symptom descriptions, validated scale scores, and treatment records. An additional 95 profiles represented non-depression comparators comprising common non-depressive psychiatric disorders and systemic diseases; demographic characteristics were matched to the depression cohort to reduce confounding. This dataset supported evaluation of empathy, dialogue quality, and screening performance during interactive conversations. Third, a negative control dataset representing mental well-being was created to test discriminative specificity: 100 distinct life-adversity scenarios were designed, and 10 mentally healthy university students role-played protagonists in simulated consultations with a psychiatrist. The resulting dialogues underwent clinical review and functioned as a negative control group for individuals without depression.

All cases underwent stringent quality control before inclusion. Exclusion criteria were: texts with excessive noise; dialogues containing fewer than 150 Chinese characters; and dialogues from which board-certified psychiatrists could not identify clinically essential symptoms required for diagnosis. Preprocessing steps included conversion of traditional to simplified Chinese, normalization of spelling and punctuation, removal of personally identifiable information, assignment of uniform case identifiers, and structured organization of textual content. Ethical safeguards included de-identification of all data and adherence to institutional review and platform data-use requirements.

Construction of the knowledge base

To enhance domain specificity and reduce hallucinations in healthcare applications, we constructed a dedicated clinical knowledge base for depression. The corpus integrated authoritative clinical practice guidelines, standard textbooks, seminal peer-reviewed studies, and validated depression assessment instruments. To systematically prevent data leakage and retrieval contamination, the knowledge base was subjected to rigorous psychiatric quality control. It was constructed exclusively from publicly available materials, intentionally excluding any patient-level dialogue logs or specific evaluation dataset examples. Furthermore, the repository solely contains general diagnostic criteria, symptom descriptions, and risk assessment principles, deliberately omitting explicit diagnostic labels, scoring thresholds, or case-specific outcome statements that could directly align with our evaluation tags. Content was then segmented into discrete knowledge units and rigorously annotated with bibliographic metadata, including source, page number, and publication date, alongside thematic labels to support precise retrieval. The knowledge units were organized through predefined thematic clustering into domains such as DSM-5 diagnostic criteria, interpretation of depression rating scales, and clinical communication strategies, thereby enabling traceable, evidence-based grounding during model inference.

RAG system implementation

Textual passages were segmented and length-normalized, then processed in batches to generate dense vector embeddings that encode semantic content in a high-dimensional space suitable for approximate nearest-neighbor search. For efficient similarity retrieval, we used a locally deployed FAISS (Facebook AI Similarity Search) backend to build the vector index, selecting index families and settings according to corpus size, memory budget, and acceptable latency [16]. Index configuration was optimized to balance retrieval precision with throughput for low-latency top-k queries at scales up to millions of passages. To increase clinical relevance, we adopted a hybrid retrieval strategy in which primary candidate passages were retrieved by cosine similarity in the embedding space and subsequently re-ranked using rule-based keyword heuristics that prioritize clinically urgent content [17], including explicit references to suicide, self-harm, and substantial social or occupational functional impairment. This combined approach preserved the broad semantic recall of dense retrieval while elevating high-priority, clinically salient evidence for downstream inference.

Building on prior evidence that RAG improves reliability, explainability, and evidence traceability in clinical NLP [18, 19], we implemented a RAG pipeline tailored to depression screening and suicide risk assessment. Upon receiving user input, the system performs semantic retrieval against the curated clinical knowledge base to identify the most relevant evidence units, which are returned with their source metadata. These evidence chunks are then integrated with predefined system instructions and task-specific templates to construct the final model prompt, which the large language model processes to generate the response. To address context window constraints and reduce noise introduced by redundant or excessively long evidence, we applied a compression procedure triggered when the cumulative token count of retrieved content exceeds a predefined threshold; In such cases, a summarization algorithm produces a condensed representation that preserves clinically salient information, and this summary is injected into the prompt in place of the original passages. Prompt templates were task-specialized for symptom inquiry, risk assessment, and empathic communication, and iteratively refined with input from experienced psychiatrists. The optimization emphasized adherence to standards of clinical communication, appropriate empathic expression, and a mandated structured report format, while requiring the model to ground its reasoning in retrieved content and explicitly cite the source tags of the evidence used.

Model selection and deployment specifications

Motivated by cost-effectiveness and accessibility considerations specific to the Chinese healthcare context, we selected three leading domestically developed LLMs—DeepSeek, Tongyi Qianwen (Qwen), and Baichuan—as the subjects of evaluation. These models are widely recognized for strong performance within the Chinese language ecosystem and offer practical deployment pathways in clinical settings across China. While this choice entails trade-offs relative to evaluating a broader set of international frontier models, it prioritizes translational feasibility and scalability, particularly for resource-limited primary care environments.

To isolate the contribution of retrieval augmentation, each LLM was evaluated under two configurations: a base model without augmentation and an RAG-augmented model incorporating the curated clinical knowledge base and retrieval layer. Identical prompt templates and retrieval policies were applied across all conditions to control for prompt design variability, thereby allowing observed performance differences to be attributed to the RAG intervention rather than confounding differences in prompting or retrieval procedures.

For privacy and data governance, both the LLMs and the clinical knowledge base were hosted within a secure on-premises environment. The deployment stack comprised a development and testing node using a laptop with a 12th-generation Intel Core i5 processor, and a primary inference server supported by Paratera HPC Cloud(https://cloud.paratera.com) to provide the computational throughput necessary for low-latency RAG operations and model inference.

Evaluation framework

The evaluation framework encompassed two primary dimensions: screening performance within real-world clinician-patient dialogues and judgment performance during simulated interactive consultations. The latter dimension additionally incorporated assessments of empathic capacity and dialogue professionalism. For real-world screening, models executed binary classification to detect the presence or absence of depression and suicide risk, alongside a four-class severity classification (none, mild, moderate, severe) for both constructs. Diagnostic accuracy was quantified using precision, recall and F1-Score. Simultaneously, symptom-summary fidelity was evaluated by comparing model-generated summaries against gold-standard summaries authored by experienced clinicians, employing established NLP metrics including BLEU-2 for local n-gram overlap [20], ROUGE-L for coherence based on the longest common subsequence [21], METEOR for synonym- and stem-aware semantic matching [22], and Dist-2 for bigram diversity to quantify repetition avoidance [23].

Within simulated dialogue scenarios, standardized patient case profiles facilitated role-play interactions wherein one AI agent simulated the patient and a locally deployed target LLM conducted the consultation. Core diagnostic and summarization metrics remained consistent with those applied in the real-world screening dimension, enabling direct cross-context comparisons between static transcript analysis and dynamic interaction. Dialogue quality was appraised via blinded ratings from three board-certified psychiatrists using standardized rubrics, whereby scores from 1 to 5 were assigned for affective matching, dialogue coherence, and the appropriateness of caring expressions. Inter-rater reliability, assessed using intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC), yielded ICC(2,1) = 0.85 (95% CI: 0.78–0.91), indicating excellent agreement.

The best-performing model configuration underwent supplementary testing to probe reliability and user-centered quality. Diagnostic output consistency was evaluated by processing 30 randomly sampled dialogue transcripts across 10 independent runs, assessing variations in diagnostic category assignment, descriptions of key symptoms, and suicide risk stratification. User experience was measured by rating outputs generated for all 154 standardized cases along four dimensions—fluency, Comforting, Doctor-likeness, and Engagingness—on a 1–5 scale.

To ensure objectivity and reliability of subjectively scored outcomes, a blinded human evaluation protocol was implemented. Three board-certified psychiatrists independently reviewed and scored model outputs while being blinded to the underlying LLM and its configuration. Inter-rater agreement was quantified using Cohen’s κ, computed pairwise between raters and averaged across pairs for each evaluation dimension. For dimensions exhibiting substantial disagreement, raters jointly reviewed discrepant cases, clarified rubric criteria, and re-evaluated outputs to derive consensus scores, thereby enhancing assessment validity

Statistical methods

Statistical analyses and plotting were performed primarily in Python (scikit-learn, statsmodels, SciPy) and R; essential analysis scripts and software version information are provided in the Supplementary Materials to ensure reproducibility.

Ethics and reproducibility

This study adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki and general medical research ethical principles. Since no human participants, clinical data, or tissue were involved, it fully complied with guidelines for non-human-subject research.

Data from the Hao Daifu dataset was used in line with its usage agreement, with no sensitive or personally identifiable information involved. To reduce risks from model misclassification and automation bias, human review and clinician takeover were incorporated at predefined decision points, with use-case limitations and safety warnings clearly documented in system materials.

For independent verification, we provide de-identified or synthetic dialogue samples, detailed annotation guidelines, a snapshot of the clinical knowledge base, RAG configurations, prompt templates, and evaluation and statistical analysis code. If data cannot be made public due to the public dataset’s privacy restrictions, access is available via a controlled repository under a data-use agreement.

Results

System overview

This study developed a locally deployed large language model (LLM)-based conversational system for depression screening, which integrated a carefully curated clinical knowledge base via a RAG framework to enhance response accuracy. The knowledge base included authoritative clinical guidelines, textbooks, and literature, organized into thematic units and stored in vectorized form to support efficient retrieval. A hybrid strategy combining semantic similarity and keyword-based re-ranking was used to prioritize clinically critical content. Three widely used Chinese LLMs—DeepSeek, Qwen, and Baichuan—were evaluated under both base and RAG-augmented configurations using identical psychiatrist-informed prompt templates to ensure clinically appropriate and empathetic responses. The system operates on a secure local server to maintain data privacy and was assessed through real-world dialogue classification and simulated patient interactions. It supports interactive screening and longitudinal management by conducting open-ended patient–AI dialogues, retrieving evidence from clinical guidelines during conversations, and generating structured reports on depression severity and suicide risk. As a proposed translational workflow (Fig. 1), for cases identified as severe or high suicide risk, the system generates an alert. Performance variation was observed across models and tasks. Qwen3 + RAG exhibited superior precision in depression classification on the Hao Daifu dataset (F1 = 0.9107), whereas DeepSeek-V3.1 + RAG maintained balanced recall and precision (Fig. 2). Four-class classification proved more challenging, particularly for non-RAG models: Baichuan-M2 without RAG showed a 12% reduction in depression recall on the Standard Case Set compared to binary tasks, highlighting RAG’s stabilizing effect on complex multi-class decisions (Fig. S1A, S2A).

Diagnostic precision for suicide risk showed significant dataset dependency. Models on simulated cases achieved higher consistency (e.g., Baichuan-M2 + RAG F1 = 0.9105) (Fig. S1, S2), while performance in real-world dialogues reflected environmental variability. Overall, RAG augmentation minimized precision-recall trade-offs and optimized risk stratification granularity, aligning with clinical decision-making requirements.

Class-specific performance analysis

To address class imbalance concerns, we report precision, recall, and F1-scores for each severity category. For suicide risk four-class classification on the Hao Daifu dataset, the Qwen3 + RAG model achieved F1-scores of 0.94 (no risk), 0.80 (low risk), 0.74 (moderate risk), and 0.83 (high risk). (Table S5) Confusion matrix analysis revealed that misclassifications predominantly occurred between adjacent categories, rather than systematic overprediction of medium-risk categories. Consistency in summarizing key depressive symptoms remained high (κ = 0.86, SD = 0.04, 95% CI: 0.85–0.88), with minor variability attributable to the inherent linguistic flexibility in symptom description. The overall consistency index (κ = 0.93, SD = 0.04, 95% CI: 0.91–0.95) further confirms the system’s stability across repeated assessments (Table 1). Critically, RAG enhancement elevated symptom capture accuracy to levels approximating clinical utility thresholds. Post-RAG ROUGE-L scores (0.5453–0.5801) are comparable to those reported in recent benchmarking studies of clinical note summarization using LLMs [24]. This advancement is clinically meaningful for depression screening, where systematic omission of atypical symptoms (e.g., irritability in adolescents or somatic complaints in elderly patients) commonly occurs. The technology’s capacity to maintain high lexical diversity while improving semantic coverage supports its viability for capturing heterogeneous depressive presentations across demographic subgroups.

Discussion

This study successfully developed and validated a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)-enhanced LLM system specifically designed for depression screening and suicide risk assessment. Our findings demonstrate that our RAG optimization framework has achieved high diagnostic accuracy. For instance, the RAG-enhanced Qwen3 model attained F1-scores of 0.91 and 0.88 respectively in the binary classification detection of depression and suicide risk using a real-world dataset. Meanwhile, it also delivered clinically significant improvements in severity stratification. Notably, the F1-score of the RAG-enhanced Qwen3 model for suicide risk classification was 0.15 higher than that of its non-RAG version. These performance levels indicate that the system shows significant potential as an adjunctive screening tool to assist clinical decision-making, though prospective validation remains necessary before claiming full clinical autonomy.This addresses critical gaps in the identification of depression and enhances scalability, particularly for reaching underserved populations who often face barriers to accessing traditional mental healthcare. The model’s effectiveness extends beyond binary detection to more nuanced four-class severity stratification (none, mild, moderate, severe), providing clinically valuable granularity in risk assessment. Additionally, the system demonstrated high consistency in summarizing key patient symptoms, as quantitatively validated using established NLP metrics (e.g.BLEU-2, ROUGE-L), further supporting its utility as a reliable clinical adjunct tool. Notably, by integrating a clinically organized knowledge base via RAG, the model demonstrated clinician-validated high-level empathetic communication capabilities, with an average score of 4.23–4.98 out of 5. Among the models tested, the Qwen3 + RAG exhibited excellent emotional consistency, with a 95% confidence interval of 4.80–4.92. This empathetic engagement effectively reduces barriers to help-seeking, a crucial advantage in primary care and digital health settings where stigma surrounding mental health frequently deters individuals from initiating conversations about their symptoms [25, 26]. By providing objective, guideline-anchored assessments grounded in evidence-based clinical knowledge, this system effectively mitigates the longstanding vulnerability of traditional screening methods: their heavy reliance on subjective self-reports, which can be inconsistent or influenced by various factors [27].

The work is distinguished by several key innovations that collectively enhance the clinical utility and accessibility of depression screening. The integration of a RAG framework fundamentally improves transparency by embedding authoritative clinical guidelines like DSM-5/ICD-11 and evidence-based literature directly into system responses. This evidence-anchored approach represents a significant advancement over “black-box” AI models, fostering greater trustworthiness in clinical decision support [28]. Further enhancing accessibility, the naturalistic dialogue interface offers a private, low-stakes environment for preliminary evaluation, effectively circumventing “white-coat syndrome” and associated barriers to help-seeking [29]. This feature holds particular relevance for younger populations, who demonstrate rising depression rates yet frequently avoid formal care due to persistent stigma or access limitations [30]. Complementing these advances, the system’s local deployment architecture enables practical longitudinal monitoring, facilitating dynamic tracking of symptom evolution and treatment response over time [31]. This capability for continuous assessment addresses a critical gap, as such detailed longitudinal profiling is often impractical within the constraints of traditional episodic clinical visits.

Despite these strengths, limitations warrant attention. Model generalizability remains constrained, as validation was limited to Chinese LLMs without cross-cultural or multilingual evaluation. Methodologically, the current evaluation heavily relies on retrospective text logs and scripted simulated scenarios. While effective for initial validation, this approach inherently fails to capture the unpredictable crisis behaviors and critical non-verbal cues—such as vocal tone, speech latency, and micro-expressions—that inform real-world psychiatric assessments. Furthermore, the risk of automation bias must be acknowledged when implementing such systems. Additionally, system accuracy depends on the comprehensiveness of the underlying knowledge base; gaps in rare conditions or emerging guidelines could compromise reliability. To transition from a potential screening tool to an autonomous clinical application, extensive prospective clinical trials within authentic patient workflows are imperative.

Future studies should develop multimodal systems that integrate behavioral, textual, and physiological data to improve diagnostic accuracy. Expanding LLMs beyond screening to support treatment interventions is also needed. These advancements must be accompanied by ethical governance frameworks, including bias auditing and accountability protocols, to ensure safe clinical integration [32].

In conclusion, this RAG-enhanced LLM system offers a scalable tool for depression and suicide risk screening. While challenges persist, its capacity to support clinical decision-making and enable proactive monitoring represents a promising step toward enhancing mental health triage and proactive symptom monitoring in resource-constrained environments.

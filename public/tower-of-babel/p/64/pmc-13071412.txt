DA-9701 for Gastrointestinal Symptoms in Postural Orthostatic Tachycardia Syndrome: A Randomized Pilot Study
Authors: Hee-Jae Jung, Dayoung Seo, Hyunjin Kim, Young-Min Lim, Ji-Sung Lee, Eun-Jae Lee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground/AimsPatients with postural tachycardia syndrome (POTS) commonly experience gastrointestinal (GI) symptoms. We aim to assess the feasibility and preliminary efficacy data for DA-9701, a prokinetic agent targeting 5-hydroxytryptamine 1A, 5-hydroxytryptamine 4, and dopamine D2 receptors, in patients with POTS.MethodsIn a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, single-center crossover trial, patients with POTS were given either 30 mg of DA-9701 or a placebo 3 times daily for eight weeks in a 1:1 ratio. After a 4-week washout, patients received the alternate treatment for another 8 weeks. The primary endpoint focused on assessing the change in GI symptoms (total Nepean Dyspepsia Index-Korean version [NDI-K] symptom score) from baseline over the 8 week-treatment period. Endpoints were assessed in all enrolled and randomized patients (intention-to-treat), and in those who completed the trial (per-protocol analysis).ResultsBetween January 2022 and August 2023, 24 patients were randomized (n = 12 per group), with 3 discontinuing after randomization. DA-9701 did not significantly improve primary endpoints for total NDI-K symptom scores in either the intention-to-treat (least-squares means, –13.9 vs. –9.5, P = 0.326) or per-protocol analyses (–17.2 vs –12.0, P = 0.242). Notably, a trend toward improvement in specific GI symptoms, such as upper abdominal pain, was observed in both intention-to-treat (–0.6 vs 0.7; P = 0.066) and per-protocol analyses (–0.9 vs 0.6; P = 0.045). No serious adverse events were observed.ConclusionDA-9701 did not improve GI symptoms in this crossover trial; however, its potential effect on specific GI symptoms merits further investigation.Keywords: Autonomic nervous system diseases, Gastrointestinal diseases, Postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome, Quality of life

Introduction

Postural Tachycardia Syndrome (POTS) is a chronic condition that features a sustained and significant increase in heart rate upon transitioning to an upright position.1,2 It affects between 0.2% and 1% of the United States population and is associated with chronic orthostatic intolerance.1-3 Since the Coronavirus Disease 19 pandemic, incidence rates have increased 15-fold compared to the pre-pandemic period, further exacerbating its impact on the population.4 While relatively uncommon, POTS primarily initially affects young individuals and often persists throughout life.3 Since standing—an unavoidable part of daily life—is the primary trigger of symptoms, POTS frequently disrupts routine daily activities.5 These characteristics highlight the need for proper long-term management and care for patients with POTS.6

In patients with POTS, prominent autonomic manifestations include gastrointestinal (GI) symptoms that appear with cardiovascular and neurologic issues like dizziness, fatigue, lightheadedness, brain fog, and palpitations.7 GI dysmotility, driven by autonomic dysfunction and hypovolemia, is a major contributor to these symptoms.8 GI symptoms, including nausea, vomiting, and indigestion, which commonly develop and persist even without postural changes,8 affect approximately 67% of POTS patients, severely impacting their quality of life (QoL).7,9 Although various medications have been proposed to alleviate GI symptoms in POTS, no double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trials have yet confirmed their efficacy.8,10,11

DA-9701 (Motilitone) is a prokinetic drug. It is made from herbs such as Corydalis Tuber and Pharbitis Semen and was approved by the Korea Food and Drug Administration in 2011, and has been used in clinical practice to treat functional dyspepsia.12 It functions as an agonist at serotonin-related 5-hydroxytryptamine 1A (5-HT1A) and 5-HT4 receptors and an antagonist at dopamine-related D2 receptors, mechanisms that have been shown in animal studies to enhance gastric motility and accommodation while reducing visceral hypersensitivity.13,14

Recently, DA-9701 has gained attention for its efficacy in patients with Parkinson’s disease.15 Specifically, a 12-week treatment regimen of DA-9701 reduced the overall severity of GI symptoms, and improved GI symptom-related QoL.15 Given that GI symptoms in Parkinson’s disease are commonly linked to autonomic dysfunction,16,17 DA-9701 may also offer similar benefits in patients with POTS. Based on this context, we hypothesized that DA-9701 may be effective in improving GI symptoms and QoL in patients with POTS. This pilot study aims to assess feasibility and generate preliminary effect estimates to guide the design of a larger, definitive trial.

Materials and Methods

Study Design and Participants

This randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled pilot trial was carried out at Asan Medical Center, in South Korea.

The eligibility criteria for patients were as follows: (1) age 18-65 years; (2) diagnosed with POTS (a sustained heart rate increase of ≥ 30 beats per minute within 5-10 minutes of standing (or ≥ 40 beats per minute in individuals aged 12-19 years), absence of orthostatic hypotension, and frequent symptoms such as lightheadedness, palpitations, weakness, and fatigue, with symptoms persisting for at least 3 months;3 (3) no prokinetics use in the prior 2 months; and (4) at least 1 GI symptom (eg, nausea, bloating, and indigestion). The exclusion criteria included the following: (1) a history of organic GI disease; (2) structural heart disease or arrhythmia; (3) thyroid or adrenal disorders; (4) active systemic diseases (eg, infections, autoimmune conditions, and malignancies); (5) pregnancy or breastfeeding; (6) any condition deemed unsuitable by the investigator; (7) any known allergies or adverse reactions to DA-9701; and (8) participation in a GI drug trial within the last 3 months.

The study adhered to Good Clinical Practice and the Declaration of Helsinki, with ethics approval obtained from the Institutional Review Board of the Asan Medical Center (No. 2021–1880). Furthermore, written informed consent was collected from all participants, and the trial is registered at cris.nih.go.kr (KCT0006932), registered on (17/01/2022).

The data that support the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author, upon reasonable request.

Sample Sizes

The sample size for this study was determined based on methodological recommendations for pilot and feasibility trials, rather than formal statistical power calculations. A total of 24 participants (12 per sequence group) was considered appropriate to allow estimation of key design parameters such as variability in outcome measures and recruitment feasibility. This approach is supported by published guidance suggesting that 12 participants per group is sufficient to inform future sample size calculations for continuous outcomes in pilot studies.18,19 Additionally, 2 previous crossover-designed clinical trials investigating therapeutic interventions in POTS successfully enrolled 22 and 27 participants, respectively, further supporting the feasibility of a similar sample size in this population.20,21 Therefore, the chosen sample size was deemed adequate for the exploratory objectives of this trial.

Randomization and Masking

After being enrolled by investigators, eligible patients were randomly assigned in an equal 1:1 ratio to the DA-9701-first or placebo-first groups through block randomization using a web-based Interactive Web Response System. The placebo was indistinguishable in appearance from DA-9701 throughout the study course. A double-blind protocol was maintained for the duration of the study, ensuring that investigators, interviewers, outcome assessors, participants, and care providers were all unaware of treatment group assignments. Unmasking of the treatment code occurred after the study's completion.

Procedures

During the study period, the use of any medications that could affect GI motility or symptoms, including proton pump inhibitors, potassium-competitive acid blockers, and other prokinetic agents, was prohibited. A 1-week washout period was implemented for all participants prior to randomization, after which patients were assigned to receive one of the following treatment regimens for 8 weeks: a 30-mg oral dose of DA-9701 3 times daily (total daily dose, 90 mg) or placebo 3 times daily. This was followed by a 4-week washout period and an additional 8-week treatment with the alternate therapy (Figure). Patients received the treatment and the placebo as tablets, with the placebo designed to be indistinguishable from DA-9701 throughout the study. Evaluations for outcomes and adverse events occurred at baseline, 8 weeks, and 20 weeks post-intervention.

Outcome Measures

For the primary endpoint, we analyzed the change in GI symptoms using the total Nepean Dyspepsia Index-Korean (NDI-K) symptom score.15,22-24 The NDI-K was selected because upper GI symptoms in POTS substantially overlap with those of functional dyspepsia,7 and both conditions have been associated with altered autonomic regulation of GI motility.25 In the DA-9701-first group, the score change was measured from baseline to week 8, while in the placebo-first group, it was measured from baseline to week 20, with each period following 8 weeks of treatment. NDI is a validated and widely used questionnaire for assessing dyspepsia symptoms and their impact on QoL.26,27 The Korean version (NDI-K) is also commonly used in GI symptom studies.15,22-24 This score comprises 15 GI symptom items addressing frequency (scored 0-4), intensity (0-5), and disability (0-4), with higher scores indicating worse symptoms.

The secondary endpoints included the dyspepsia sum score, which is the sum of 8 core NDI-K GI symptom items (upper abdominal pain, discomfort, burning, pressure, bloating, inability to finish a regular meal, postprandial fullness, and nausea), along with the individual scores for each GI symptom item. Additionally, GI symptom-related QoL was assessed using the NDI-K QoL score, which evaluates QoL across 5 domains: tension/sleep, interference with daily activities, eating/drinking, knowledge/control, and work/study. Each domain score ranges from 0 to 100, with higher scores indicating better GI-related QoL. Safety and adverse events were monitored through vital signs, routine laboratory tests, and a questionnaire.

Statistical Methods

For statistical analysis, baseline characteristics and GI parameters were compared between groups using appropriate tests for continuous and categorical variables. Efficacy analysis was conducted in the intention-to-treat population, with per-protocol analysis for patients adhering to the study medication protocol. Linear mixed-effects models were used to analyze the effects of treatment, period, and allocation sequence on outcomes, estimating least squares means (95% confidence interval [CI]). Before pooling the 2 groups, we tested outcomes for the period and carryover effect using a general linear model.28 A 2-sided P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. We did not use a correction for multiple testing on secondary outcomes, as the study was exploratory. All statistical analyses were conducted with SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA).

Results

From January 2022 to August 2023, 24 patients were enrolled and randomized; 12 were assigned to receive either DA-9701 first (n = 12) or placebo first (n = 12) (Figure). These patients were included in the intention-to-treat group. Following allocation, 3 patients withdrew from the study (2 from the DA-9701 group [16.7%] and 1 from the placebo group [8.3%]) due to scheduling conflicts or withdrawal of consent based on a change in willingness to participate.

Table 1 presents the baseline characteristics of participants with POTS, which showed no significant differences between the intervention groups. The median age was 31 years, with over 50% being women. Participants had experienced POTS symptoms for a median of 4.6 months, with few comorbidities, such as hypertension. The mean blood pressure and pulse rate was 122/72 mmHg and 84/minute, respectively. The baseline mean NDI-K symptom score was 37.6, while the NDI-K QoL score was 60.5 (Table 2). GI symptoms and related QoL generally did not differ between the intervention groups, except for vomiting symptoms.

Regarding the secondary endpoints, DA-9701 did not significantly outperform placebo in the intention-to-treat population, while it showed marginal differences in specific GI symptoms, including upper abdominal pain (–0.6 vs 0.7, P = 0.066) and upper abdominal pressure (–1.4 vs –0.1, P = 0.061) (Supplementary Table 1). In the per-protocol population, DA-9701 significantly improved upper abdominal pain (–0.9 vs 0.6, P = 0.045) and showed marginal significant differences in upper abdominal pressure (–1.4 vs –0.1, P = 0.063) and belching (–2.6 vs –1.4, P = 0.072) (Supplementary Table 2). A significant carryover effect (P = 0.009) without any period effect (P = 0.425) was observed for upper abdominal pain. Further analysis of the effect of DA-9701 during the first phase only (baseline to week 8) further revealed a significant improvement in abdominal pain compared to the placebo (–1.0 vs 1.7, P = 0.037). Serious adverse effects or drug-related reactions were not observed in either intervention group during the study period.

Discussion

This randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled pilot trial compared the efficacy of DA-9701 against a placebo in improving GI symptoms and related QoL among patients with POTS. Although DA-9701 did not significantly improve total NDI-K symptom scores over the 8-week treatment period, a trend toward improvement was observed for specific symptoms, particularly among patients who completed the full treatment protocol.

GI symptoms are common in patients with POTS and have a substantial impact on daily functioning and QoL.29 Although several medications have been empirically used in clinical practice,29 no treatment has yet demonstrated clear efficacy in controlled trials. In this study, patients with POTS exhibited prominent GI symptoms, with mean NDI-K symptom and QoL scores of 37.6 and 60.5, respectively. Although these values appear modest on an absolute scale (out of 195), they were markedly higher than those reported in patients with Parkinson’s disease, whose mild GI symptoms corresponded to an NDI-K score of 17.6 and a comparable QoL score of 53.2.15 These findings highlight the clinical relevance of evaluating DA-9701 in POTS, a population for whom GI distress remains a major unmet need.

The lack of significant improvement in the primary endpoint likely reflects the limited statistical power and relatively short treatment duration of this pilot study. A previous 12-week randomized trial involving 72 patients with Parkinson’s disease demonstrated significant efficacy of DA-9701 in reducing GI symptoms.15 Taken together, our findings and prior evidence support the need for future studies with larger sample sizes and longer treatment periods to more conclusively assess the therapeutic potential of DA-9701 in POTS.

Among individual symptoms, abdominal pain and pressure showed the greatest response to DA-9701, with a clear trend toward improvement in the intention-to-treat analysis and significant benefit in the per-protocol analysis. Abdominal pain and nausea are the most frequent GI complaints in POTS and are strongly associated with reduced QoL.29,30 Therefore, the selective improvement in abdominal pain is clinically meaningful and warrants confirmation in larger studies.

The pathophysiology underlying GI symptoms in POTS remains poorly defined but is likely multifactorial. Contributing factors may include autonomic dysfunction, impaired gastric electrical activity,31-33 abnormal cardiovagal modulation,34,35 and altered splanchnic blood flow responses to orthostatic stress.11 Although some studies suggest that POTS patients may show normal or even increased mesenteric flow when upright,36,37 these abnormalities in autonomic regulation may still contribute to disordered GI motility and visceral sensitivity.

Because disease mechanisms are incompletely understood, treatment for POTS-related GI symptoms remains largely symptomatic. Commonly used agents include antiemetics and prokinetics such as erythromycin, metoclopramide, and domperidone,29 yet their long-term use is limited by safety concerns and insufficient clinical evidence,29,38-42 thus underscoring the need for an effective and safe treatment for GI symptoms in this population. This underscores the importance of identifying safe and effective alternatives for this population.

DA-9701 targets multiple receptors in the GI tract, including the 5-HT1A, 5-HT4, α2-adrenergic, and D2 receptors.15 Its active metabolites, tetrahydropalmatine and tetrahydroberberine, exhibit D2-receptor antagonistic properties.43 However, animal studies using doses 50 100 times higher than the therapeutic level have shown that brain concentrations of these metabolites are too low to cause significant central D2-receptor blockade.43 These findings indicate a favorable safety profile, consistent with previous reports that DA-9701 does not induce hyperprolactinemia or central antidopaminergic effects.44,45 In line with these data, no participants in our study experienced serious adverse events.

The observed improvement in abdominal pain and pressure may mechanistically reflect DA-9701’s multifaceted modulation of neurogastroenteric pathways. Through 5-HT4 receptor agonism and D2 receptor antagonism, DA-9701 enhances gastric motility and accommodation, thereby reducing gastric distension that can provoke upper abdominal pain. Its 5-HT1A and α2-adrenergic receptor–modulating actions may further attenuate visceral hypersensitivity and autonomic imbalance. These mechanisms align with the pathophysiology of POTS, in which autonomic dysregulation and impaired gastric accommodation contribute to functional abdominal pain. Further studies incorporating physiological assessments are needed to confirm these effects and their clinical significance.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, as a pilot study with a small sample size, the statistical power was limited, increasing the risk of a Type II error and reducing the ability to detect true therapeutic or adverse effects. Nevertheless, the results provide valuable preliminary data to inform the design and sample size estimation of future adequately powered trials. Second, although a washout period was implemented in the crossover design, residual carryover effects cannot be fully excluded. While no significant carryover or period effects were found for the primary endpoint, certain items, such as abdominal pain, showed a significant carryover effect and improvement in per-protocol analysis. Third, use of the NDI as the primary endpoint may be suboptimal, as it was originally developed for functional dyspepsia and has not been validated for autonomic dysfunction–related dysmotility. However, because symptoms such as early satiety, bloating, and postprandial discomfort substantially overlap between the 2 disorders, its application here is considered pragmatic. Fourth, we did not systemically perform detailed GI evaluations such as endoscopy, ultrasonography, or imaging. Although patients with known GI disorders were excluded, undetected structural abnormalities cannot be entirely excluded. Finally, as the study population consisted of a single ethnic group, the generalizability of these findings may be limited.

In conclusion, DA-9701 did not significantly improve total GI symptoms in patients with POTS in this 8-week pilot trial. Nonetheless, a potential therapeutic signal for specific symptoms like abdominal pain was observed, warranting further investigation.

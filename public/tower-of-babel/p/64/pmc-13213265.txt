Outcomes and Prognostic Factors in Hepatopancreatoduodenectomy
Authors: Go-Won Choi, Duminda Subasinghe, Younsoo Seo, Inhyuck Lee, Youngmin Han, Yoon Soo Chae, Won-Gun Yun, Young Jae Cho, Hye-Sol Jung, Wooil Kwon, Jin-Young Jang, Joon Seong Park
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeHepatopancreatoduodenectomy (HPD) is a potentially curative option for selected patients with advanced cholangiocarcinoma or gallbladder cancer, but it is associated with substantial morbidity. In this study, we evaluated short- and long-term outcomes after HPD and identified prognostic factors.Materials and MethodsPatients who underwent HPD at the Seoul National University Hospital, South Korea from 2000 to 2023 were included. Prospectively collected data on patient and tumor characteristics, perioperative and survival outcomes were analyzed. Prognostic factors were assessed using Cox proportional hazards models, and risk factors for disease-free survival (DFS) <12 months were explored using logistic regression.ResultsThe 30-day mortality rate was 0%, the 90-day mortality rate was 6%, and major complications (Clavien–Dindo grade ≥III) occurred in 58% of patients. The median overall survival (OS) was 21 months, and median DFS was 12 months. The 5-year survival rate was 16%. In multivariable analysis, angiolymphatic invasion was an independent prognostic factor for both OS and DFS. Adjuvant treatment was associated with an improved OS. For DFS <12 months, preoperative carbohydrate antigen (CA) 19-9 level and angiolymphatic invasion were independent risk factors.ConclusionDespite high morbidity, HPD can provide meaningful long-term survival in carefully selected patients. Preoperative CA 19-9 and angiolymphatic invasion may aid risk stratification. Associations between adjuvant treatment and survival outcomes should be interpreted cautiously and warrant confirmation in larger, prospective multicenter studies.

INTRODUCTION

In biliary tract malignancies, achieving a microscopically clear resection margin (R0) is considered the only treatment with the potential for a cure.123456 Since its introduction in 1980 by Endo, et al.,7 hepatopancreatoduodenectomy (HPD) has been considered a surgical option for patients with extensive bile duct or gallbladder (GB) cancer to achieve an R0 resection. Although many surgeons have attempted this challenging procedure, the associated high morbidity and mortality rates have prevented HPD from becoming a standard surgical procedure worldwide.789101112 Over the past few decades, the understanding of the bile duct anatomy has significantly improved, and many methods have been developed to assess liver function and future remnant liver volume. Furthermore, surgical techniques and perioperative management have improved. However, the mortality and morbidity rates associated with HPD remain uncertain, raising questions regarding its clinical utility.

This study aimed to evaluate the short- and long-term outcomes of HPD and to assess risk factors associated with survival, early recurrence, and major complications in order to better evaluate the potential of the procedure as a standard treatment.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study design and patients

This retrospective cohort study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Seoul National University Hospital (H-2311-128-1485) and adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki of 1975 and its subsequent revisions. The study included 50 patients who underwent HPD at Seoul National University Hospital between January 2000 and December 2023. One patient who had an R2 resection was excluded from the analysis.

Data collection

We retrospectively evaluated prospectively collected patient records to extract demographic and treatment variables and verify the pathologic data. Demographic information, including age, sex, body mass index (BMI), total bilirubin levels, and tumor markers, such as carcinoembryonic antigen (normal range <5 ng/mL) and carbohydrate antigen (CA) 19-9 (normal range <37 U/mL), were collected at the time of diagnosis. Treatment details, including neoadjuvant chemotherapy or adjuvant treatment, were also extracted.

Definitions

In this study, major HPD was defined as the resection of more than three liver segments along with pancreatoduodenectomy (PD). Pathological findings were assessed according to the American Joint Committee on Cancer Staging Manual, 7th and 8th editions. R0 status was defined as the absence of residual cancer tissue at the resection margin. Post-hepatectomy liver failure (PHLF) was diagnosed and graded according to the International Study Group of Liver Surgery (ISGLS) criteria. We summarized the clinical impact of PHLF by ISGLS grade [intensive care unit (ICU) stay, reoperation, and mortality] in Supplementary Table 1 (only online). Postoperative pancreatic fistula (POPF) was defined and graded according to the International Study Group of Pancreatic Surgery (ISGPS) definition. Other PD-specific complications, including delayed gastric emptying and postpancreatectomy hemorrhage, were defined according to ISGPS definitions. Postoperative complications were categorized according to the Clavien–Dindo (CD) system, with major complications defined as CD grade III or higher occurring within 90 days after surgery. Adjuvant treatment was defined as receipt of postoperative systemic chemotherapy with or without radiotherapy. In our cohort, radiotherapy was administered only in combination with chemotherapy; therefore, chemotherapy and radiotherapy were consolidated into a single variable (adjuvant treatment) for analyses.

Statistical analyses

The included patients were regularly followed after HPD at the outpatient clinic, where recurrence was monitored through CA 19-9 levels and abdominopelvic computed tomography scans conducted every 3 months.

To evaluate potential era effects, we categorized the surgical period into two eras (<2010 vs. ≥2010) based on the year of surgery and assessed its association with overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS) using Cox proportional hazards models. To explore potential selection bias related to adjuvant treatment, we compared baseline and postoperative characteristics between patients who received adjuvant treatment and those who did not. The comparison included ASA class, comorbidities, and PHLF/POPF grades (Supplementary Table 2, only online).

Continuous variables were presented as mean values with standard deviation, while categorical variables were presented as counts with percentages. Continuous data were analyzed using the Student’s t-test and analysis of variance, while categorical variables were assessed using the chi-square or Fisher’s exact test. OS was defined as the time from the date of surgery to the date of death or last follow-up. DFS was defined as the time from the date of surgery to the first instance of recurrence, death, or last follow-up date. A Cox proportional hazards regression model was used to calculate hazard ratios with their 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for OS and DFS. Variables with a p<0.10 in the univariable analysis were included in the multivariable analysis. Variables considered for multivariable models were chosen based on clinical relevance and/or univariable results (p<0.10). To minimize overfitting, we adopted a parsimonious approach and limited the number of covariates according to the events-per-variable principle (approximately one predictor per ~10 events). Final covariates were selected using backward elimination when applicable. Statistical significance was set at p<0.05. All statistical analyses were performed using the SPSS Statistics for Windows (version 26.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) and R software, version 4.2.3 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

RESULTS

Clinicopathologic characteristics

The baseline clinicopathological characteristics of the 50 patients who underwent HPD are summarized in Table 1. The mean age of the study participants was 64.3 years. The study included 32 (64%) male and 18 (36%) female. A total of 32 patients underwent biliary drainage prior to HPD, including endoscopic retrograde biliary drainage, nasobiliary drainage, and percutaneous transhepatic bile drainage. Preoperative portal vein embolization was performed in 10 (20%) patients, and 5 (10%) patients received neoadjuvant chemotherapy before HPD.

Major hepatectomy was defined as the resection of more than three liver segments. Thirty-six (72%) patients underwent a major HPD, including 5 (10%), 27 (54%), and 4 (8%) patients who underwent left hemihepatectomy, right hemihepatectomy, and extended right hemihepatectomy, respectively. A total of 25 (50%) patients required intraoperative transfusions. Four (8%) patients had vessel resection during the surgery.

Final histopathology findings revealed that 32 (64%) patients had cholangiocarcinoma (CCA), while 18 (36%) had GB cancer. Lymph node metastasis was identified in 28 (56%) patients, and R0 resection (microscopically negative margins) was achieved in 35 (70%) patients. Angiolymphatic invasion was present in 27 (54%) patients. Overall, 31 patients received adjuvant treatment. Radiotherapy was administered only in combination with chemotherapy (n=19).

Short-term outcomes of HPD

A total of 37 (74%) patients experienced postoperative complications (Table 2), with 26 (52%) patients developing severe complications that required further intervention or ICU admission (CD III or IV). There were no deaths within 30 days, whereas three deaths occurred within 90 days; therefore, these were classified as CD grade V under the 90-day complication window. The most common complication was POPF (n=39, 78%), most frequently biochemical leakage (n=20, 40%). Nineteen (38%) patients had grade B POPF, while no case of grade C POPF was recorded. PHLF, defined according to the ISGLS, was the second most common complication, including 18 (36%), 12 (24%), and 3 (6%) with grades A, B, and C PHLF, respectively. Fluid collection was observed in 17 (34%) patients, while pulmonary complications, including pleural effusion or pulmonary edema, occurred in 8 (16%) patients. Two cases required reoperation: one for duodenojejunostomy leakage and the other for wound revision.

Prognostic factors for OS and DFS

The Kaplan–Meier survival curves for patients who underwent HPD are shown in Supplementary Fig. 1 (only online). The median OS and DFS were 21 months and 12 months, respectively. The 1-year and 5-year OS rates were 73.2% and 16%, respectively. Approximately 50% of the patients died or experienced recurrence within 12 months.

Univariable and multivariable analyses were performed to explore the prognostic factors for OS (Table 3). In univariable analysis, significant prognostic factors for OS included angiolymphatic invasion, grade B or C PHLF, and adjuvant treatment. In multivariable analysis, angiolymphatic invasion remained independently associated with worse OS, whereas adjuvant treatment was associated with improved OS.

Regarding DFS (Table 4), univariable analysis showed that preoperative CA 19-9 >37 U/mL, node positivity, and angiolymphatic invasion were significantly associated with worse DFS. In multivariable analysis, preoperative CA 19-9 >37 U/mL and angiolymphatic invasion were independent prognostic factors for DFS (p<0.05).

In univariable Cox regression analyses, surgical era (≥2010 vs. <2010) was not significantly associated with OS [hazard ratio (HR) 0.95, 95% CI 0.46–1.99; p=0.900] or DFS (HR 1.26, 95% CI 0.63–2.55; p=0.513).

In this study, patients were divided into two groups according to the DFS duration: the short DFS (DFS <12 months) and long DFS (DFS ≥12 months) groups. The short-term DFS group included 25 (50%) patients. Logistic regression analyses were performed to identify risk factors associated with early recurrence or death (Supplementary Table 3, only online). In the univariable analysis, preoperative CA 19-9 level, intraoperative transfusion, angiolymphatic invasion, and adjuvant treatment were statistically significant for the long DFS. In multivariable analysis using a parsimonious model, preoperative CA 19-9 level >37 U/mL [odds ratio (OR) 5.741, 95% CI 1.223–26.949; p=0.027] and angiolymphatic invasion (OR 7.276, 95% CI 1.613–32.815; p=0.010) were independent risk factors for short DFS, whereas adjuvant treatment was associated with longer DFS (OR 0.141, 95% CI 0.028–0.694; p=0.016).

Risk factors for PHLF

Table 5 presents the results of the univariable and multivariable analyses of risk factors for PHLF in patients who underwent HPD. PHLF occurred in 33 (66%) patients post-HPD. The incidence of PHLF was significantly higher in patients with total bilirubin levels > 3 mg/dL at diagnosis (5.9% vs. 48.5%, p=0.013). Additionally, preoperative biliary drainage was more frequently performed in the PHLF group (25.3% vs. 78.8%, p=0.004). Major hepatectomy, intraoperative transfusion, and CCA were identified as confounding factors in the analysis of PHLF risk factors. However, only male sex (OR 9.1, 95% CI 2.06–40.4; p=0.004) and total bilirubin level at diagnosis (OR 12.2, 95% CI 1.27–116.8; p=0.030) were found to be independent prognostic factors for PHLF after HPD. Preoperative cholangitis was not significantly associated with the development of PHLF after HPD (p>0.05).

DISCUSSION

Since its introduction approximately 40 years ago, HPD has not gained widespread popularity among surgeons owing to the associated high mortality and morbidity rates. However, since the early 2000s, interest in HPD has been renewed, driven by improvements in perioperative risk evaluation, patient management, a better understanding of bile duct anatomy, progression in surgical techniques, and advancements in operative tools.

In this study, the 30-day and 90-day mortality rates after HPD were 0% and 6%, respectively. The 90-day survival rate was 94%, as two more patients died during this period. The first patient was a 68-year-old man who underwent PD with a right hemihepatectomy for a Klatskin tumor. He was admitted to the ICU for multiorgan failure and was discharged 32 days postoperatively with percutaneous drainage (PCD). However, he died 74 days after surgery, most likely due to the worsening pre-existing cardiac complications. The second patient was a 62-year-old man who underwent PD with a right hemihepatectomy. He had a 36-day postoperative hospital stay owing to fluid collection that required PCD; however, he died on postoperative day 38, with the cause of death unknown.

The morbidity rate in this study was 74%, with 29 (58%) patients experiencing CD ≥III complications. These findings are significantly better than those reported in previous studies. D’Souza, et al.8 found that the 90-day mortality rate after HPD was 17% for CCA and 13% for GB cancer. Similarly, Endo, et al.7 reported a 30-day mortality rate of 5.0% and a morbidity of 72.5% after HPD. Various studies have shown that the mortality rate after HPD ranges from 0% to 10%, and morbidity ranges from 60% to 100%.7891213

In this study, the most common complication was POPF (78%), followed by PHLF (66%). The analysis of the risk factors for OS showed angiolymphatic invasion to be an independent prognostic factor. Adjuvant treatment was an independent prognostic factor for improved OS. However, of the five patients who received neoadjuvant chemotherapy, three had GB cancer, and two had CCA. All five patients had CD ≥III disease and lymph node positivity or direct invasion into adjacent organs. In the multivariable analysis, neoadjuvant chemotherapy was not significantly associated with OS or DFS. Neoadjuvant chemotherapy was administered to patients with CCA (n=2; 6.7%) or GB cancer (n=3; 20%), which may explain the small number of patients, compounded by the absence of a consensus on its use.

For DFS, elevated preoperative CA 19-9 and angiolymphatic invasion were independent risk factors. In the short DFS group (<12 months), the risk factors included elevated preoperative CA 19-9 and angiolymphatic invasion. Adjuvant treatment was found to lower the risk of recurrence within 12 months postoperatively. Fifteen patients underwent R1 resections. Adjuvant radiotherapy could affect remnant or dormant tumors at the surgical site.

Additionally, multivariable analyses were performed to determine the risk factors for PHLF and POPF, which were the most severe complications of HPD.14 In the multivariable analysis for POPF, neither pancreatic texture nor pancreatic duct dilatation was associated with its development, which could be due to the fact that the patients who underwent HPD had CCA or GB cancer; therefore, most patients had a normal pancreas. In the PHLF analysis, male sex and a total bilirubin level >3 mg/dL were identified as risk factors for PHLF. PHLF is closely associated with liver function and remnant liver volume. In Korean society, males are more likely to have higher alcohol exposure than females. Elevated total bilirubin levels at diagnosis can affect the liver and bile ducts, and many studies have reported that maintaining a low total bilirubin level before hepatectomy is associated with a better prognosis.151617

As a result, in cases of preoperative imaging suggesting angiolymphatic invasion or hyperbilirubinemia at diagnosis, or if personal or socioeconomic factors make adjuvant treatment unfeasible, HPD should be considered very carefully.

This study has some limitations beyond its retrospective design. First, the number of patients who underwent the HPD was relatively small, with 50 patients included, which may not be sufficient to identify all risk factors for survival or oncological outcomes. However, given that the study was performed at a single center over 20 years, the sample size was adequate to reevaluate the mortality and morbidity associated with HPD. Second, there may have been selection bias, considering that this was a retrospective study, and HPD is considered a challenging option by surgeons. Moreover, patients who were considered candidates for HPD were probably healthy and had no severe underlying diseases. For example, none of the patients in this study had hepatitis B or C. Third, information on liver function and future remnant liver volumes is limited. Our center does not perform routine workups for indocyanine green (ICG) test or fibroscan before HPD. However, recent studies have found that future remnant volume evaluations are related to PHLF. Furthermore, the ICG test or fibroscan could be another option for evaluating liver function. Further studies should be performed to evaluate liver function.

In our analysis, adjuvant treatment was associated with improved OS (Table 3). However, this apparent survival benefit should be interpreted with caution because of potential selection bias. In clinical practice, adjuvant treatment is preferentially delivered to patients who recover sufficiently after major HPD. Accordingly, patients who did not receive adjuvant treatment tended to have more severe postoperative morbidity and early mortality, which may partially account for the observed association (Supplementary Table 4, only online). Therefore, postoperative recovery and feasibility of adjuvant treatment should be considered in perioperative planning and interpretation of outcomes.

In conclusion, despite substantial postoperative morbidity, HPD can provide meaningful long-term survival in selected patients with CCA or GB cancer when curative resection is possible. Preoperative CA 19-9 elevation and angiolymphatic invasion were associated with worse outcomes and may aid risk stratification and patient counseling. Future larger, prospective multicenter studies are warranted to validate these findings and to better define optimal selection criteria and postoperative treatment strategies.

Epidemic trend and spatial-temporal analysis of pulmonary tuberculosis in Hotan prefecture, xinjiang, china, 2015–2021
Authors: Yilipa Yilihamu, Peiyao Zhou, Nuerbiye Yuemaier, Di Wu, Yu Shi, Yanling Zheng, Liping Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Globally, tuberculosis remains a significant public health concern. The 2022 World Health Organization report indicates that approximately 10.6 million people are infected with tuberculosis annually worldwide, resulting in 1.6 million deaths. In 2021, China ranked as the third country with the highest tuberculosis burden globally, following India and Indonesia. Xinjiang, as the westernmost region of China, experiences the most severe tuberculosis incidence. This study focuses on the Hotan region of Xinjiang, utilizing spatio-temporal statistical methods to investigate the local tuberculosis incidence trends from 2015 to 2021 and analyze potential influencing factors. Spatial autocorrelation and spatiotemporal scanning techniques were employed to assess the tuberculosis incidence trend. Furthermore, geographically weighted regression was utilized to examine the impact of meteorological and air pollution factors on tuberculosis incidence in the Hotan area from a spatial perspective. The findings revealed spatial heterogeneity in the distribution of pulmonary tuberculosis (PTB) in the Hotan area, with the identification of four spatial clusters through local spatial autocorrelation analysis. Spatiotemporal scan analysis confirmed the presence of two cluster types. Geographically weighted regression analysis identified three influencing factors, with daily average temperature showing a negative correlation with tuberculosis incidence, while PM10 and SO2 levels exhibited positive correlations. Given the dry climate, frequent sandstorms, and severe air pollution in the Hotan area, tuberculosis prevention and control efforts are under environmental pressure (it refers to the challenges posed by the region’s dry, dusty climate and poor air quality for TB control efforts). Local authorities should prioritize environmental management and enhance tuberculosis prevention and control measures.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-19880-0.

Introduction

Tuberculosis, a chronic infectious disease, is a major global cause of mortality, with pulmonary tuberculosis (PTB) being the most common and severe form1. The 2022 World Health Organization report indicates that currently, 10.6 million people worldwide are affected by PTB, resulting in 1.3 million deaths. China joined the list of top ten countries with high PTB burden in 2021, ranking sixth among 30 high-burden nations in new cases2. Epidemiological studies in China have identified a regional distribution pattern of PTB incidence, with higher rates in western regions and lower rates in the east, notably in Xinjiang, the westernmost part of the country3,4. A study showed that the PTB incidence rate in Xinjiang was 210.75 per 100,000 population from 2014 to 2018, indicating a rising trend5. PTB is particularly prevalent in the Hotan prefecture in southern Xinjiang, where the unique climate and geographic location pose challenges for treatment and prevention.

In recent years, epidemiological discussions on the factors influencing tuberculosis incidence have expanded beyond traditional considerations of infection sources, transmission routes, and susceptible populations. Evidence suggests that meteorological factors and exposure to air pollutants can impact tuberculosis6,7. Prolonged exposure to various air pollutants may increase the risk of developing PTB due to the disease primarily affecting the respiratory system8,9. Some studies have also shown associations between climatic factors like temperature and precipitation and PTB incidence10,11. Given the severe air pollution and complex climatic conditions in the Hotan prefecture, investigating how these variables affect PTB spread in the region is crucial for preventing and controlling local epidemics.

Epidemiology focuses on studying the distribution of diseases across time, space, and populations, with many diseases showing spatial–temporal transmission patterns. Research has shown that PTB transmission also exhibits spatial–temporal distribution characteristics12,13. This study aims to use spatial–temporal analysis methods, incorporating air pollution and meteorological factors, to analyze their impact on PTB incidence in the Hotan prefecture, a high-burden area in Xinjiang, from 2015 to 2021. The objective is to provide a reference basis for local PTB prevention and control by examining the disease transmission characteristics in this region.

Methods

Research area

Located in the southwestern part of the Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region, China, the Hotan Prefecture is geographically situated between the Kunlun Mountains to the south and the vast Taklamakan Desert to the north, with its central coordinates approximately at 37.12° N latitude and 79.92° E longitude. This region is characterized by a temperate extreme arid desert climate, featuring minimal annual precipitation (30–50 mm) and exceedingly high evaporation rates (> 2500 mm), resulting in a pronounced water deficit. Abundant solar radiation provides over 2700 h of sunshine annually, while significant diurnal and seasonal temperature variations are typical. The area is frequently subjected to wind-blown sand and dust storms, particularly during spring, due to its proximity to the desert. Spatial auto-correlation analysis

Spatial autocorrelation refers to the potential spatial dependence of certain factors within a region, and it can be roughly divided into two categories: global spatial autocorrelation and local spatial autocorrelation14,15. Global spatial auto-correlation is assessed to determine if the entire region shows correlation, with the widely used index being the global Moran’s I index. This index helps in understanding the level of correlation between each region and others, indicating whether the spatial auto-correlation pattern is clustered, dispersed, or random. The Moran’s I index ranges from −1 to 1: when the Moran’s I index is greater than 0, it indicates a positive spatial auto-correlation among the spatial units within the study area, showing a clustered distribution. A higher Moran’s I index indicates a stronger positive correlation.

Global spatial auto-correlation provides an overall view of spatial auto-correlation but does not specify whether individual spatial units have positive or negative correlations with their neighbors. On the other hand, local spatial autocorrelation focuses on the relationship between neighboring spatial units within a study area, allowing for the identification of positive or negative correlations among them. Spatial–temporal scan analysis

Spatial auto-correlation analysis can reveal the spatial clustering patterns of diseases in a study area, but it may not provide detailed insights into the nature of these clusters. Additionally, this method is most appropriate for analyzing cross-sectional data and focuses solely on spatial relationships, neglecting the temporal aspect. However, disease occurrence and progression involve both spatial and temporal dimensions. To overcome these limitations, the scan statistic was introduced.

The spatial–temporal scanning method involves setting a cylindrical scanning window that moves through space, with its size and location changing dynamically. The radius and height of the cylinder correspond to the spatial and temporal dimensions of the scan, respectively. For each scanning window, the theoretical number of cases is calculated based on actual case and population numbers, and the log-likelihood ratio (LLR) of the generalized likelihood ratio function is calculated using the actual cases inside and outside the window. The window with the highest LLR is identified as the primary cluster area, while others are labeled as secondary likely clusters. Statistical significance is evaluated by conducting hypothesis testing using the Monte Carlo randomization method to obtain p-values. The null hypothesis assumes a random spatial distribution of disease risk, while the alternative hypothesis suggests a specific window with a higher incidence rate. By calculating the RR, the risk intensity of PTB in each clustered area can be accurately determined. This study conducts 9999 Monte Carlo simulation tests with a significance level of 0.05. The maximum scanning radius and time are both set at 50%. The spatial–temporal scan is carried out using SatScan 9.6.1 software, with results visualized using ArcGIS 10.7.

Geographically weighted regression (GWR) model

GWR is a spatial analysis technique that can accurately estimate spatial–temporal data16. It utilizes a local regression model with variable coefficients that change based on the spatial location17,18. In this study, the latitude and longitude of the 111 towns in Hotan prefecture are used to capture the diverse characteristics of factors influencing the occurrence of PTB in different spatial locations. For the Variance Inflation Factor (VIF) test, a threshold of 7.5 was adopted—more conservative than the commonly used value of 10—to more stringently mitigate multicollinearity issues among predictor variables. Data, or a descriptive heading about data

The study collected data on cases of PTB notifications from January 1, 2015 to December 31, 2021, sourced from the CDC of Hotan Prefecture. The data included information on gender, age, occupation, geographical location, dates of symptom onset, diagnosis, hospital admission, clinical outcomes, and drug resistance. Demographic data was extracted from the Xinjiang Statistical Yearbook spanning from 2015 to 2021.

Meteorological data was collected from two stations in the Hotan Prefecture spanning from January 1, 2015, to December 31, 2021. The data includes daily average temperature (℃), daily average wind speed (m/s), daily average precipitation (mm), and daily average sunshine hours (h). This data was obtained from the China Meteorological Information Center (https://data.cma.cn/). Additionally, daily average air pollution data for the Hotan Prefecture during the same period was sourced from the China National Environmental Monitoring Centre (https://www.aqistudy.cn). This air pollution data encompasses the Air Quality Index (AQI), average PM2.5 (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$ug/m^{3}$$\end{document}), average PM10 (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$ug/m^{3}$$\end{document}), average CO (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$ug/m^{3}$$\end{document}), average SO2(\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$ug/m^{3}$$\end{document}), average NO2 (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$ug/m^{3}$$\end{document}), and average O3 (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$ug/m^{3}$$\end{document}).

The spatial–temporal scan analysis of PTB in all townships within the Hotan prefecture revealed the identification of a primary cluster and a secondary cluster. The primary cluster, spanning from October 1, 2016, to June 30, 2019, predominantly covered 49 townships in Moyu, Luopu, Cele, Yutian, and Minfeng counties, reporting a total of 12,967 PTB cases. The RR of the primary cluster was 2.49 (LLR = 3366.27, p < 0.001). The secondary cluster, occurring from July 1, 2018, to January 31, 2019, was primarily concentrated in 16 townships centered around Hotan County and Moyu County, with 2,277 reported PTB cases. This study utilizes the variance inflation factor (VIF) test to address multicollinearity, considering factors with VIF values exceeding 7.5 as indicative of multicollinearity. Discussion

PTB is a chronic and debilitating infectious disease with a poor prognosis, presenting a significant public health challenge in our country. Despite China’s efforts to enhance education and awareness about tuberculosis, improve the diagnosis and treatment of PTB, and achieve a noticeable reduction in the epidemic, a considerable number of individuals remain infected with the disease. This is particularly evident in the Xinjiang region, where the incidence of PTB has consistently been among the highest in the country4,19,20.

This study utilized spatial–temporal analysis methods and the GWR model to examine the trend of PTB incidence and its influencing factors in the Hotan prefecture. From a temporal perspective, the analysis revealed that the incidence of PTB in the Hotan prefecture between 2015 and 2021 exhibited an initial increase followed by a decrease, with a rise from 2015 to 2018 and a gradual decline from 2018 to 2021. The observed trend could be attributed to the implementation of a strategy in 2018 in the Xinjiang region, which included tuberculosis chest radiography screening in national health checkups. Consequently, the Hotan prefecture actively responded to this prevention and control strategy by enhancing rapid diagnosis and screening of tuberculosis in designated hospitals for tuberculosis in each county and city. This proactive approach led to the timely detection and treatment of a significant number of PTB patients by the end of 2018. Spatially, the incidence of PTB in the Hotan prefecture between 2015 and 2021 was predominantly concentrated in the vicinity of Hotan City, where townships with higher population density exhibited notably elevated tuberculosis rates compared to other regions. This distribution pattern seems to be correlated with the population distribution within the Hotan prefecture. From a demographic perspective, the incidence of PTB was slightly higher among males than females during the same period, consistent with previous studies. This gender disparity may be attributed to males engaging in more outdoor labor, potentially increasing their exposure to infectious agents. Additionally, higher prevalence of unhealthy habits such as smoking and drinking among males could contribute to their heightened susceptibility to tuberculosis21–23. Individuals aged 65 and above showed a higher incidence of tuberculosis, indicating that the elderly constitute a high-risk group for this disease. This vulnerability is likely due to age-related factors like weakened immune systems, declining physical functions, and a higher prevalence of comorbidities24,25. In the Hotan prefecture, a notable proportion of reported tuberculosis cases were among farmers, possibly due to their lower income and socioeconomic status compared to other occupational groups26.

Global spatial auto-correlation analysis indicates a positive spatial auto-correlation in PTB cases between 2015 and 2021 in the Hotan prefecture. This suggests that the distribution of PTB among townships shows a geographical correlation, implying that an increase in cases in one area could impact neighboring areas. These results are consistent with previous studies by researchers such as Chen X et al27. Local spatial auto-correlation analysis from 2015 to 2021 reveals that certain townships in Luopu County, Hotan County, Hotan City, Moyu County, and Yutian County exhibit high-high clustering, while others in Cele County, Minfeng County, and Pishan County show low-low clustering. This clustering pattern may be linked to economic disparities, where townships with high-high clustering have better access to medical resources and health education, potentially leading to earlier detection and treatment of PTB. Previous research also highlights the role of socioeconomic factors in the varying incidence rates of PTB across regions28. Therefore, local health authorities should take into account the influence of neighboring areas on these clusters when planning disease prevention and control strategies.

The spatial–temporal scan analysis results reveal that the primary and secondary clusters are spread across 49 townships in Moyu, Luopu, Cele, Yutian, and Minfeng counties within the Hotan prefecture, as well as in 16 townships surrounding Hotan and Moyu counties. The primary cluster’s distribution seems to be influenced by the proximity of these counties to the Taklamakan Desert, characterized by persistent drought and sandstorms that facilitate the rapid spread of Mycobacterium tuberculosis. Moreover, the region’s sparse vegetation and minimal rainfall prevent effective pollutant degradation, leading to worsening air pollution challenges and complicating the prevention and treatment of PTB29–31. Environmental authorities should enhance governance efforts in these areas, reduce sandstorm occurrences, and regulate pollutant emissions to decrease PTB incidence rates. The secondary cluster, primarily located in Hotan and Moyu counties, emerged after 2018, possibly due to earlier policies. Most medical resources in the Hotan prefecture are concentrated in Hotan City, benefiting Hotan and Moyu counties’ populations who can easily access healthcare services. This accessibility enables prompt PTB detection and treatment.

Previous studies have shown that the occurrence of tuberculosis is influenced by a combination of air quality, meteorological conditions, and socioeconomic factors32,33. In this study, we focused on investigating the impact of air pollution and meteorological factors on PTB incidence in the Hotan prefecture. Initially, we identified and excluded factors with multicollinearity using the VIF. Subsequently, we identified significant factors using OLS model and constructed a GWR model. The findings indicated that PM10, SO2, and average daily temperature were statistically significant. Notably, the average daily temperature showed a negative correlation with PTB incidence, consistent with previous research by Chen D et al32, suggesting that higher temperatures can reduce PTB transmission effectively. Furthermore, the GWR model revealed that the influence of average daily temperature on PTB prevalence was more pronounced in the northern part of Hotan prefecture due to its proximity to the Taklamakan Desert, resulting in higher temperatures that impact PTB transmission. Moreover, PM10 and SO2 were found to be positively correlated with PTB incidence, aligning with existing literature suggesting that these pollutants may complicate local PTB prevention and control efforts33. The GWR results also indicated that the impact of PM10 and SO2 on PTB prevalence was lower in the west and higher in the east of the Hotan prefecture, emphasizing the need for enhanced air pollution control measures in the eastern regions, particularly in Minfeng County and Yutian County.

Conclusions

This study demonstrates significant spatial heterogeneity and environmentally influenced transmission patterns of PTB in Hotan Prefecture, with PM10, SO₂, and temperature identified as key localized risk factors. The findings advocate for integrated public health actions tailored to this arid region: targeted air quality management in eastern townships with high pollutant levels, sustained active screening and community-based interventions in persistent hotspot areas identified by spatial analysis, and the development of an environment-informed surveillance and early-warning system. These recommendations are aligned with China’s reinforced TB control objectives and the WHO’s End TB Strategy, offering a concrete, evidence-based approach to reducing TB burden in environmentally vulnerable settings.

Limitation of study

Given the high prevalence of PTB in the prefecture of Hotan and the limited research available on recent trends and influencing factors, this study aims to contribute to the existing literature. Utilizing spatial–temporal analysis techniques, it investigates the incidence of PTB at the township level in Hotan to provide insights for local disease prevention and management. However, the study does have limitations: (1) Socioeconomic and healthcare factors’ impact on PTB incidence in Hotan were not considered, despite related research suggesting their significant influence34–36. Future research will involve collecting data on these factors to explore their role in PTB incidence. (2) The study did not delve into the lag effect of air pollution and meteorological factors on diseases37, which has been noted in literature. (3) While spatial autocorrelation and spatiotemporal scan statistics were employed to identify clusters, techniques such as Emerging Hot Spot Analysis (EHSA) could have provided further insight into the temporal evolution (e.g., intensifying, diminishing) of high-risk areas. Lastly, the reliance on retrospective surveillance data means that potential reporting biases or inconsistencies in diagnosis across different townships could affect the accuracy of incidence estimates. Future studies should incorporate multisource data, more sophisticated spatiotemporal modeling, and a broader range of socioeconomic and climatic factors to better elucidate the complex mechanisms of PTB spread in arid, environmentally sensitive regions like Hotan.

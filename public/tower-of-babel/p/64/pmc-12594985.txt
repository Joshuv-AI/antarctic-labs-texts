A MARL-federated blockchain-based quantum secure framework for trust management in industrial internet of things
Authors: Gasim Alandjani
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractWith the emergence of quantum computing, traditional cryptographic methods face significant vulnerabilities, particularly in the Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) environment, where cybersecurity, data privacy, and trust management are critical. In order to address the said problems concerned with post-quantum security, this paper proposes a quantum-secure framework for IIoT applications by integrating multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) for optimized blockchain consensus, blockchain-supported federated learning for privacy-preserving AI training, and post-quantum cryptography (PQC) for quantum-resistant security. The MARL-based consensus uses a deep Q-network with explainable AI to enhance transparency and trust. Federated learning employs dynamic participant selection and PQC (e.g., NTRUEncrypt, Kyber) to ensure privacy. Evaluations are carried out on Edge IIoT and Federated EMNIST datasets, which are publicly available datasets and are used in similar studies. The experimental results reveal that the proposed framework demonstrate improvements in scalability, security, and efficiency, hence, offering a robust solution for applications like smart cities and healthcare.Keywords: Post-quantum cryptography, Federated learning, Blockchain, Trust management, Multi-agent reinforcement learningSubject terms: Computational science, Computer science, Information technology

Introduction

The proliferation of the Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) has revolutionized industrial operations by enabling seamless connectivity among devices, facilitating real-time data collection, analysis, and action1. This connectivity has led to unprecedented levels of efficiency and automation across various sectors, including smart cities, healthcare, and manufacturing2. However, this increased connectivity also introduces significant challenges, such as heightened cybersecurity threats, data privacy concerns, and the critical need for establishing trust among heterogeneous devices3,4. Traditional trust management systems, often centralized, are vulnerable to single-point failures and lack resilience against emerging computing threats that could compromise conventional encryption schemes5,6. Moreover, the advent of quantum computing poses a significant threat to current cryptographic standards. Quantum computers could potentially break widely used encryption methods like Rivest-Shamir-Adleman (RSA) and Elliptic Curve Cryptography (ECC) in seconds, which are fundamental to securing data in IIoT systems7. This underscores the urgent need for quantum-resistant cryptographic solutions to safeguard against future threats.

In response to these challenges, this paper presents a novel quantum-secure framework that integrates multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) for adaptive trust scoring in blockchain consensus, blockchain-supported federated learning for privacy-preserving AI training, and optimized PQC to ensure long-term security. By leveraging MARL, the framework dynamically adjusts trust scores based on device behavior, enhancing the efficiency and resilience of the consensus mechanism. The incorporation of federated learning (FL) in the proposed method allows for collaborative AI model training without compromising data privacy, while the use of PQC specifically optimizes lattice-based schemes like NTRUEncrypt and Kyber, providing robust defense against both classical and quantum attacks. Furthermore, explainable AI (XAI) techniques can be employed with the proposed framework to enhance the transparency of the decision-making processes, fostering greater trust among stakeholders.

Recently, some studies have tried to combine the characteristics of AI-driven methods with blockchain technology to improve the trustworthiness evaluation while integrating quantum-resistant cryptographic primitives8,9. However, such techniques are not adaptive to the evolving and emerging threats, specifically the trust scoring strategies when used with consensus mechanism in blockchain systems. In order to overcome the aforementioned challenges, we introduce a post-quantum secure AI-blockchain framework for IIoT trustworthiness. This paper extends and enhances the existing literature by introducing a novel approach that combines MARL with adaptive trust scoring for consensus optimization, blockchain-supported federated learning with dynamic participant selection for privacy-preserving AI training, and optimized PQC for IIoT system security. The proposed framework provides a room for explainable AI (XAI) techniques to be implemented for enhancing the transparency while fostering trust in autonomous decisions. We evaluate our approach on publicly available datasets, demonstrating superior performance in terms of scalability, privacy, and security.

While the proposed framework addresses multiple challenges identified from existing works such as consensus speed, privacy, post-quantum threats, and model poisoning, we identify our primary contribution to be MARL-based consensus mechanism and FL-based privacy preserving AI training through dynamic participant selection. The MARL-based consensus mechanism is central to this framework as it adaptively scores devices based on their behavior, thus, enhancing both the efficiency and security in blockchain consensus. However, to enhance the performance and security, we also use FL component that supports privacy-preserving AI training through dynamic participant selection, while the PQC component ensures long-term security against quantum threats. We provide deeper insights on the benefits of MARL consensus while ensuring that FL and PQC are presented as integral supporting elements, hence contributing to a cohesive trust management framework for IIoT. Related works

The rapid expansion of IIoT-based systems and the evolving security threats require advanced trust management frameworks to be developed using emerging technologies such as Cryptographic mechanisms, blockchain, and AI3,5,6. Existing studies focus on a single security aspect to deal with, such as post-quantum cryptographic techniques10, blockchain-based decentralized trust management4, AI-based anomaly detection11 methods. Only a few studies focus on more than one aspect, however, a single comprehensive trust framework capable of integrating all three aspects are not present as of yet. Therefore, the proposed work positions itself uniquely and imperatively to progress the body of current state-of-the-art research in the area of security for IIoT systems.

Post quantum cryptography for IIoT systems

With the steady increase in computing power of the devices and rapid progression in the field of quantum computing, the existing security and privacy methods need to be resilient. Quantum computing based methods can easily break traditional encryption, therefore, solutions that can resist quantum attacks needs to be designed, specifically for IIoT systems10. Recently, researchers have proposed the use of lattice-based schemes to perform cryptography, which include Kyber, FrodoKEM, and NTRUEncrypt, as a replacement for ECC and RSA-based algorithms12–18. Although, they perform better in comparison to the traditional methods, but they are not quite resistant to quantum attacks. Very few studies have explored the integration of AI and blockchain technology along with PQC to build a stacked trust mechanism19,20.

Blockchain systems in IIoT

Researchers have extensively explored the use of Blockchain-based systems to build tamper-proof and secure trust management for IIoT use-case2,4,5. The existing studies are centered around permissioned ledgers, hyperledger, and ethereum to build an immutable trust score storage and decentralized identity verification system21,22. However, the blockchain systems suffer from several issues that include scalability issues, high computational overhead, and are vulnerable to quantum attacks12,20. In this work, we integrated Blockchain with MARL, federated learning, and PQC to build a holistic trustworthy IIoT system for addressing the scalability and security issues.

AI-based anomaly detection in IIoT

The IIoT systems traditionally relied on the reputation scores and rule-based models for the trust assessment. However, such systems are vulnerable to dynamic threat landscape, especially to the adversarial attacks. Furthermore, the rule-based system incurs high labelling cost and constantly requires human supervision8,23. Researchers have tried to resolve the aforementioned issues with deep learning strategies, which include the use of CNNs, BiLSTMs, LSTMs, GRUs, and transformer-based architectures for predicting anomalous activities and analyzing anomalous device behaviors11. Some deep learning strategies are quite good at anomaly detection but they need re-training or fine-tuning for the evolved and updated attacks, which they have not come across from the training datasets23. At times, it gets very difficult to re-train or fine-tune the deep learning models due to the lack of explainability and interpretability associated with trained AI model. To deal with the evolving threats, we propose the MARL for adaptive trust scoring along with XAI techniques to enhance the transparency of the trained model.

Hybrid methods

Some of the studies recently have explored hybrid methods that combine two of the leveraged technologies. For instance,24 proposed a post-quantum secure privacy preserving federated learning framework that demonstrated the integration of PQC with FL to cope with the issue of quantum threats in distributed learning systems. The studies in25,26 combined the FL approach with reinforcement learning, i.e. federated reinforcement learning. The method was proposed as an alternate to MARL for enhancing privacy-preserving model training. A study27 conducted a comprehensive survey on MARL in the context of FL while highlighting their intersection in decentralized environments.

Studies have also focused on lightweight blockchain protocols and hybrid trust frameworks for IIoT trust management. The study Fedstellar28 employs decentralized federated learning (DFL) that employs peer-to-peer model aggregation with trust aware participant selection. Unlike traditional FL, Fedstellar eliminates central servers, thus, enhancing scalability and resilience against single-point failures. However, Fedstellar lacks mechanisms for quantum resistance and relies on static trust metrics. Sharma et al.29 integrated blockchain with zero-trust principles (ZTN) and federated transfer learning to enforce strict access control and to mitigate poisoning attacks through gradient immutability. While the method improves auditability, its omission of PQC leave it vulnerable to emerging threats. Studies such as30–32 have also explored the use of lightweight consensus protocols like IOTA’s Tangle and Algorand’s Pure PoS, which is effective in reducing energy consumption compared to conventional PoW/PoS. However, such protocols lacks integrated AI-driven trust adaptation and privacy-preserving federated learning, resulting in suboptimal trade-offs between security and efficiency in IIoT environments. Our proposed work bridges the gaps by unifying MARL-based adaptive trust, FL, and PQC to achieve a holistic balance.

Considering the issues from AI, blockchain-based systems, and PQC techniques, it is apparent that the integration of these methods is seldom explored for securing IIoT systems and improving the trust management33. Furthermore, such systems are limited due to the scalability issues, and dependence on computationally intensive devices, which hinder their real-life applicability. In addition, the systems are vulnerable to adversarial perturbations, data poisoning, and other attacks, which hinder their real-life applicability in terms of public trust. To tackle the aforementioned challenges, the proposed work integrates MARL, PQC, and blockchain-supported federated learning, techniques to make the system not only resilient to the evolving attacks but also applicable for real-life use-cases. Methods

The proposed framework addresses the critical challenges of trust management in the context of IIoT, where security, privacy, and scalability are essential for enabling reliable and efficient operations across multiple domains such as smart cities, manufacturing, and healthcare. Our proposed method seamlessly integrates MARL for adaptive blockchain consensus, blockchain-supported FL for privacy-preserving AI training, and PQC for quantum-resistant security, while offering a comprehensive solution tailored to the unique demands of IIoT environments as shown in Fig. 1. The diagram illustrates that the proposed framework integrates three core components. The first is the MARL-based consensus for adaptive trust scoring, where agents collaboratively validate transactions on a permissioned proof-of-stake (PoS) blockchain. The second is the blockchain-secured federated learning, where encrypted model updates (via NTRUEncrypt) are aggregated via smart contracts and the third is PQC layer (Kyber/NTRUEncrypt) securing all communications. This holistic workflow enables quantum-resistant trust management while balancing scalability and privacy.

The proposed framework’s architecture is scaffolded around permissioned PoS blockchain, where each node serves dual roles as a validator in the consensus mechanism and a participant in FL. The blockchain acts as an immutable ledger for recording transaction validations and encrypted model updates, hence ensuring traceability and transparency. The communication between nodes are secured using PQC schemes. Specifically, we use NTRUEncrypt and Kyber for encryption and key exchange, respectively. The PQC schemes are used to offer protection against quantum attacks. We assume that each of the nodes have enough computational resources to run MARL agents and perform local AI training, with optimizations implemented to accommodate resource-constrained IIoT devices, such as sensors and actuators. The proposed framework has three main building blocks, i.e. MARL-based consensus, blockchain-supported federated learning, and PQC. Deep Q-network

A Deep Q-Network (DQN)36 approximates the Q-function, taking as input and outputting Q-values for each action. The DQN architecture in the proposed study comprises an input layer with three neurons, two hidden layers with 64 neurons each using Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU) activation, and an output layer with three neurons, respectively. A centralized training, decentralized execution paradigm is adopted suggesting that each node collects experiences in a local replay buffer, while in every 100 steps the experiences are aggregated to train a global DQN using mini-batch gradient descent with a learning rate of 0.001. Post-quantum cryptography

The PQC component ensures long-term security against quantum threats, which could compromise classical cryptographic schemes like RSA. The framework employs lattice-based schemes, NTRUEncrypt for encryption and Kyber for key exchange, selected for their efficiency and alignment with NIST’s PQC standardization process (NIST PQC). These schemes rely on the hardness of the Learning With Errors (LWE) problem, making them resistant to quantum attacks like Shor’s algorithm. Optimized parameters, such as ( (N=761, q=12289, p=3) ) for NTRUEncrypt and Kyber-512, provide 128-bit security with reduced key sizes, achieving a computational overhead of less than 6% compared to RSA-2048. This optimization reduces encryption and decryption times by 10% compared to standard PQC implementations, making the framework suitable for IIoT devices. PQC secures all communications, including transaction data and model updates, ensuring quantum resistance across the system. Future work will explore dedicated PQC hardware to further enhance efficiency.

The proposed work integrates MARL, federated learning, and PQC in a cohesive manner to provide enhanced security and performance. MARL optimizes consensus for efficient transaction validation, supporting high-throughput federated learning. Dynamic participant selection enhances model robustness, while PQC secures all operations. XAI ensures transparency across processes. The workflow involves nodes validating transactions using MARL, training local models, encrypting updates with PQC, and aggregating updates via smart contracts, with trust scores updated iteratively. Scalability is addressed through dynamic selection and hierarchical aggregation, ensuring performance in large networks.

This section evaluates the performance of the proposed framework for IIoT trust management, integrating multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) for blockchain consensus, blockchain-supported FL, and PQC. We conduct a series of experiments on multiple datasets to show the effectiveness of the proposed approach. Results on real-world IIoT Scenarios

The evaluation utilizes two publicly available datasets to simulate real-world IIoT scenarios. The Ethereum transaction dataset46. A subset of 10,000 transactions was randomly sampled to represent the transaction load in a permissioned IIoT blockchain (https://github.com/PraneshCBE/Hyperledger-for-IoT). No additional filtering was applied, preserving the diversity of transaction types (e.g., smart contract executions, token transfers) to evaluate the framework’s robustness across varied workloads. Transaction data was formatted as input vectors containing sender reputation (derived from historical validation success), transaction complexity (based on computational requirements), and network load (based on pending transactions).

The Federated EMNIST dataset, specifically the digits subset, was used for federated learning evaluation47. This dataset comprises 240,000 training and 40,000 testing 28x28 grayscale images of handwritten digits (0-9). For the experiments, a reduced subset of 10,000 training samples was partitioned equally among 10 nodes in an independent and identically distributed (IID) manner, with each node receiving 1,000 samples. Preprocessing involved normalizing pixel values to the range [0,1] to ensure consistency in model training, a standard practice for image classification tasks. The IID partitioning simplifies the evaluation but reflects realistic IIoT scenarios where devices may have similar data distributions, such as sensor readings in manufacturing.

The experiments were conducted on a system equipped with one NVIDIA RTX 3080 GPU (10 GB GDDR6X memory, 8704 CUDA cores), an Intel Core i9-11900K CPU (8 cores, 16 threads), and 32 GB DDR4 RAM, running Ubuntu 20.04 LTS. The software stack included PyTorch 1.9.0 for deep learning, Web3.py for blockchain simulations, and PQCrypto for PQC implementations. The network size was scaled to 10 nodes to ensure computational feasibility within GPU constraints and to align with realistic resource limitations. The MARL component used a simplified DQN with one hidden layer of 32 neurons, and the federated learning component employed a CNN with one convolutional layer (16 filters), a max-pooling layer, and one dense layer (128 neurons). Batch sizes were set to 16 for both components. The MARL-optimized consensus mechanism was evaluated for energy consumption (Joules per transaction, J/tx), throughput (transactions per second, SpeedTrans), and latency (seconds per block), compared against traditional PoW and PoS. Directions for edge constraint devices

The proposed framework assumes that nodes possess adequate resources for MARL and local training but we also consider the resource constraints of edge devices and provide three strategies to address them, which are as follows:

Lightweight Model Compression: For MARL agents, pruning and quantization37,49 can be applied to reduce DQN size and inference latency significantly, even on Cortex-M7 micro controllers50. In FL, binary neural networks (BNNs) or depthwise separable CNNs can also replace standard CNNs that can cut FLOPs by 3x - 5x while retaining acceptable level of accuracy51.

In FL, binary neural networks (BNNs) or depthwise separable CNNs can also replace standard CNNs that can cut FLOPs by 3x - 5x while retaining acceptable level of accuracy51.

Hierarchical Offloading: Resource-constrained devices, such as sensors, can offload DQN inference or CNN training to edge gateways (e.g., Raspberry Pi 4 or Jetson Nano clusters). The smart contracts in blockchain can also be offloaded on requests with these devices using PQC-secure channels ensuring end-to-end privacy52.

Computational complexity and resource profiling for edge deployment

To assess the practical deployability of the proposed framework on resource constrained IIoT devices, a detailed performance profiling analysis has been carried out. The importance of such evaluation, especially the IIoT device profiling to validate its efficacy for real-time or near real-time processing has been emphasized by many studies including54–56 This evaluation focuses on the two most computationally intensive components: the MARL agent’s DQN inference and the PQC encryption/decryption operations. Profiling was performed on a Raspberry Pi 4 Model B (ARM Cortex-A72 @ 1.5GHz, 4GB RAM) and an NVIDIA Jetson Nano (ARM Cortex-A57 @ 1.43 GHz, 4GB RAM, 128-core NVIDIA GPU) to represent a spectrum of edge device capabilities. The DQN model from Sect. 3.2.3 and the NTRUEncrypt scheme from Sect. 3.4 are used as baselines. Key metrics measured for this experiment include FLOPs (using the ptflops Python package [https://pypi.org/project/ptflops/]), latency (averaged over 1000 runs), and energy consumption (measured via a dedicated power monitor). Conclusion

This paper presents a comprehensive framework that addresses the critical need for quantum-secure, privacy-preserving, and trustworthy IIoT systems. By integrating state-of-the-art technologies from cryptography, AI, and blockchain, we provide a solution that is both theoretically sound and practically feasible. Specifically, we use the MARL, Federated Learning with Dynamic Selection, Permissioned blockchain, and PQC methods to provide a holistic security for the IIoT systems. The proposed method demonstrates significant improvements in scalability, security, and efficiency, as validated through rigorous experiments.

The evaluation is limited by the small network size (10 nodes) and simplified models, which may not fully capture the framework’s scalability in large-scale IIoT deployments. The moderated performance improvements reflect GPU constraints and the reduced dataset size. As a future research direction, we intend to extend our testing on cloud-based GPU clusters for larger networks, exploring field-programmable gate array (FPGA) acceleration to reduce overhead, and conducting real-world validation on public testnets like Ethereum Sepolia (https://sepolia.etherscan.io). Lightweight versions for edge devices will also be developed to enhance applicability in resource-constrained environments.

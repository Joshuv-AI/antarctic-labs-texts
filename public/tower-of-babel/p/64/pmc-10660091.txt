Bone lesions, chronic sinusitis, and perirenal involvement
Authors: Edwin U. Suárez, Sara Martín‐Herrero, Francisco J. Díaz de la Pinta
Published: 2023

A 70‐year‐old woman had breast cancer treated 10 years ago. She received radiotherapy over possible bone metastases in both femurs, but it was not biopsied. Several years later, she was diagnosed of chronic sinusitis. She referred several months of right nasal obstruction sensation and magnetic resonance (MR) showed maxillary sinuses occupation and soft tissue material, with involvement of hard palate and right extraconal orbital region (Figure 1A,B). A biopsy of maxillary sinus showed fibrous tissue replaced by inflammatory infiltrate with abundant plasmatic cells, histiocytes, and aggregated of lymphocytes, in addition to emperipolesis. Immunohistochemistry showed diffuse and intense positive S100 (Figure 1H,I) and negative CD1a. The IgG4/IgG ratio was <0.4, without light chain restriction, and without malignancy signs or identification of microorganisms with Giemsa, Grocott, and PAS stain. BRAF gene mutation in the codon V600 was absence by quantitative real‐time polymerase chain reaction. She also referred intense thigh pain and a MR (six years later of radiotherapy), showed progressive bone lesions (Figure 1D). However, the samples by biopsy were insufficient for a diagnosis. Positron emission tomography (F‐18 fluorodeoxyglucose) showed increased uptake of maxillary sinuses, right peri‐orbital soft tissue, cervical lymph nodes, left kidney mass, and generalized bone lesions, with SUVmax to 9.1 (Figure 1C,E–G). A core needle biopsy of the left renal mass revealed infiltration, as did maxillary sinus tissue. Targeted next‐generation sequencing of DNA from the renal mass showed NRAS mutation (G12D).

Rosai‐Dorfman‐Destombes (RDD) is an uncommon non‐Langerhans cell histiocytosis of unknown origin [1]. RDD cells often do not exhibit any cytological atypia and selectively stain for S100 [1]. Due to the presence of emperipolesis, a generally non‐specific condition, and the lack of cell markers defining Langerhans cells histiocytosis, Erdheim‐Chester disease or “mixed” or “overlap” histiocytosis, RDD has highly ambiguous diagnostic criteria when compared to other histiocytosis [2]. RDD most likely refers to diverse disorders that share a final phenotype [1, 2]. However, MAPK pathway activating mutations are linked to at least some cases (which includes BRAF, NRAS, KRAS, MAP2K1, and ARAF) [2, 3, 4]. Extranodal RDD has been documented in 43% of patients, and in 10%–20% of instances, the bones are involved, usually in conjunction with nodal illness [2, 3]. While pathologic fractures are uncommon, bone pain is prevalent. Cortex‐based osteolytic lesions most common, often affect the metaphysis or diaphysis [3]. Renal hilar masses, subcapsular hypodense infiltration, or renal cortical hypodense nodules are the more frequent characteristics seen with renal involvement (5%−10% of patients) of extranodal RDD [3]. RDD can manifest alone or in conjunction with genetic, autoimmune, and malignant diseases [2]. Although few people experience poor outcomes, RDD has traditionally been thought of as a self‐limited condition [1, 2, 3]. Due to the wide clinical spectrum of RDD (including overlap variants) and the consequent variety of experts evaluating and treating such persons, an evidence‐based method to the evaluation and treatment of this complex condition is required [1].

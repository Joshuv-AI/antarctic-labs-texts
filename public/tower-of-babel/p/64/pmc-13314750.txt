Multisource Machine Learning Model for Detecting Referral-Warranted Retinopathy of Prematurity
Authors: Xinwei Luo, Yong Chen, Bowen Ying, Graham E. Quinn, Gil Binenbaum, Gui-Shuang Ying, Lifang He
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo develop a multisource machine learning model for detecting referral-warranted retinopathy of prematurity (RW-ROP) using retinal images and demographics.DesignSecondary analysis of data from the Telemedicine Approaches to Evaluating Acute-Phase Retinopathy of Prematurity Study.SubjectsOne thousand two hundred fifty-seven premature infants (mean birth weight 864 g; mean gestational age 27 weeks; 19.4% with RW-ROP) enrolled from 12 clinical centers in North America.MethodsA multisource ROPNet (MS-ROPNet) model that combines a VGG-Swin Transformer model to extract features from retinal images and a random forest model that captures patterns of demographics was developed using central-view retinal images from 7741 eye visits with concurrent clinical eye examinations and demographic characteristics (birth weight, gestational age, sex, ethnicity, and age at retinal image). The MS-ROPnet model was compared to several existing machine learning models for detecting RW-ROP.Main Outcome MeasuresModel performance metrics including the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC), area under the precision–recall curve (AUPRC), sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy from fivefold cross-validation, with RW-ROP diagnosed by certified ophthalmologists as the reference standard.ResultsThe MS-ROPNet achieved an AUROC of 95.0 ± 0.7% (mean ± standard deviation), AUPRC of 78.0 ± 2.8%, sensitivity of 81.5 ± 4.0%, specificity of 94.3 ± 1.4%, and accuracy of 93.0 ± 0.9% under the default cutoff of 0.50 in predicted probability. After adjusting the cutoff to achieve higher sensitivity, MS-ROPNet had 90% sensitivity and specificity of 84.8 ± 2.5% (using cutoff 0.3504), and 95% sensitivity with specificity of 72.8 ± 6.9% (using cutoff 0.2074), which outperformed both existing multisource models and the best single-source model by ≥3.7% in specificity at the same sensitivity.ConclusionsThe MS-ROPNet achieved high performance in RW-ROP classification by effectively integrating retinal images with demographics, demonstrating its potential for accurate risk stratification of RW-ROP.Financial DisclosuresThe authors have no proprietary or commercial interest in any materials discussed in this article.

Retinopathy of prematurity (ROP) is a vasoproliferative retinal disorder that affects preterm infants, characterized by abnormal retinal vascular development that can lead to severe visual impairment.1 Timely ROP diagnosis is crucial, because severe cases require intervention with retinal ablative surgery or intraocular anti-VEGF treatment within a narrow time window to prevent retinal detachment.2 To identify high-risk infants who require eye examinations by ophthalmologists, Ells et al introduced the concept of referral-warranted ROP (RW-ROP), defined as the presence of plus disease, or ROP in zone I, or stage 3 ROP or greater.3 Infants with RW-ROP require timely ophthalmic evaluation because these infants may require immediate treatment or soon develop treatment-requiring ROP. Standard RW-ROP screening involves resource-intensive diagnostic eye examinations conducted by ophthalmologists. In the United States, ROP screening guidelines recommend examinations for all infants with a birth weight of 1500 g or less or a gestational age (GA) of 30 weeks or less, as well as other at-risk infants based on clinical judgment.4 However, these guidelines lead to numerous potentially unnecessary examinations, because fewer than 10% of these infants require treatment.5 Given these challenges, there is a growing interest in leveraging automated diagnostic tools to enhance RW-ROP detection efficiency.6

In recent years, machine learning has emerged as a promising approach for automated ROP detection. Many studies focus on analyzing retinal images for ROP classification, because retinal images contain vascular features critical for diagnosis. For example, Lei et al employed a ResNet50 equipped with channel and spatial attention mechanisms to enhance feature extraction for the diagnosis of ROP.7 However, these image-based approaches do not consider valuable demographic and clinical information that might enhance diagnostic accuracy.

Conversely, some studies emphasize analysis of clinical and demographic data, which are not available from retinal images, to improve ROP classification. For instance, Chen et al8 applied logistic regression, decision tree, and multilayer perceptron (MLP) models to predict ROP and treatment-requiring ROP based on demographics such as birth weight (BW) and GA. While these works leverage key ROP-related clinical information, they lack the retinal imaging information necessary for precise diagnosis.

To address the limitations of single-source methods, recent studies have explored the integration of imaging and clinical data to enhance ROP classification performance. Wu et al9 utilized ResNet50 to extract features from fundus images and integrated them with clinical risk factors to enhance ROP prediction. Huang et al10 introduced ROPRNet, incorporating a modified ConvNeXt for retinal image feature extraction and a stacked autoencoder to encode clinical risk factors, ultimately combining both feature sets for ROP classification. Though these methods have shown promising results, they have two major limitations. First, they all adopt MLPs as the classifier without considering tree-based machine learning models such as random forest (RF) or XGBoost, which are more effective for structured data due to their ability to model complex feature relationships. Second, they utilize early fusion methods to integrate image and clinical features without investigating the impact of alternative fusion strategies on model performance. These constraints may limit the overall effectiveness of these approaches for ROP detection or prediction.

To address these limitations, we sought to develop and evaluate multisource ROPNet (MS-ROPNet), a multisource machine learning framework for RW-ROP classification using both retinal images and demographic characteristics. MS-ROPNet employs the VGG-Swin Transformer (VGG-ST)11 deep learning model to process retinal images, while demographics are analyzed using an RF machine learning model. The outputs of both models are integrated via soft voting to generate the final classification result. We conducted extensive evaluations of the model performance on a large dataset from the Telemedicine Approaches to Evaluating Acute-phase Retinopathy of Prematurity (e-ROP) study. By leveraging multisource data, we aimed to provide a robust decision-support tool for RW-ROP screening or risk stratification.

Methods

Dataset

We performed a secondary analysis of data from the e-ROP study, a prospective, multicenter cohort study conducted between 2011 and 2015 across 13 neonatal intensive care units in North America, including institutions in the United States and Canada. The purpose of the e-ROP study was to evaluate the validity, reliability, feasibility, safety, and relative cost-effectiveness of a digital imaging system for identifying infants with RW-ROP.12,13 The study enrolled 1257 premature infants with BW <1251 g, who were expected to survive for ≥28 days and remain in the neonatal intensive care unit for serial ROP examinations and concurrent imaging. The study protocol was approved by institutional review boards at all participating centers, ensuring compliance with ethical guidelines, including the Declaration of Helsinki. Informed consent was obtained from the parent or guardian of each enrolled infant before participation.

The e-ROP dataset consists of 7906 eye sessions, with retinal imaging and concurrent eye examinations following a standardized study protocol. All infants underwent standardized diagnostic eye examinations performed by study-certified ophthalmologists, with ROP classification based on the 2005 International Classification of ROP.14 Retinal images were obtained using a RetCam Shuttle (Clarity Medical Systems) following a standardized imaging protocol, capturing six images per eye: an external image to assess pupillary dilation and five retinal views (disc-centered, superior, inferior, nasal, and temporal). Retinopathy of prematurity diagnosis at the eye level was based on ophthalmic examinations.15 Images were stored in PNG format with a resolution of 640 × 480 pixels. Demographic characteristics data included BW in grams, GA at birth in weeks, sex, and ethnicity, and days of life at time of each imaging session. Data for ROP diagnosis included posterior vascular abnormality, indicating the presence of pre-plus or plus disease; ROP stage, representing the severity of ROP from stage 1 to stage 5; ROP zone, identifying whether ROP is located in zone I or zone II. Referral-warranted ROP for each eye at each eye examination session was defined as the presence of plus disease, or ROP in zone I, or stage 3 ROP or greater.

Data Preprocessing

Figure 1 illustrates the detailed steps of the data preprocessing for detecting RW-ROP using machine learning models. For this study, we only used the central view retinal image, because it is easier to obtain with better image quality than other views, and provides the most comprehensive visualization of the optic disc and posterior pole, which are critical for ROP assessment.16 We resized central images to 256 × 256 pixels. Additionally, we applied min-max normalization to demographic feature data to standardize numerical values while preserving their relative distribution. After excluding samples with missing central view retinal image or demographic characteristics and those unable to determine presence of RW-ROP, we obtained 7741 samples, including 6931 eye image samples without RW-ROP and 810 eye image samples with RW-ROP based on clinical eye examination.Figure 1The flowchart of data preprocessing workflow for model development and evaluation. Subsequently, we partitioned (using 4:1 ratio) the sample into training samples and test samples using fivefold cross-validation to improve data utilization and provide a reliable model performance evaluation.17 In this study, each visit was treated as an independent sample for cross-validation partitioning. This choice is motivated by three factors: first, disease status can vary substantially across visits for the same patient. For example, RW-ROP may be absent at an earlier visit but present at a later one, meaning each visit represents a distinct clinical state. Second, the model is designed to classify individual visits in a clinical setting, making visit-level evaluation the most clinically relevant framework. Third, retinal appearance in ROP changes dynamically with ROP progression, which further reduces the likelihood of the model memorizing image-specific features across visits. To facilitate hyperparameter tuning and prevent overfitting, the training samples were further divided into training and validation sets at a 3:1 ratio. To address class imbalance in the training set, we applied random oversampling to equalize the number with RW-ROP and without RW-ROP.18 Additionally, we employed image augmentation techniques, including rotation, scaling, and horizontal and vertical translations, to enhance the diversity of retinal images, improving the model robustness and generalizability.

Model Design

MS-ROPNet integrates VGG-ST and RF to leverage retinal images and demographics for RW-ROP classification. The schematic diagram of the model architecture is shown in Figure 2. VGG-ST11 is a hybrid deep learning model that combines VGG19 and Swin Transformer V2 (SwinTV2) to extract both local and global image features for RW-ROP classification, while RF is a tree-based machine learning model that process structured demographic data to perform RW-ROP classification. The final RW-ROP classification is obtained by combining their output probabilities via soft voting, which enables complementary contributions from both data sources. This approach allows each model to contribute based on its prediction confidence, yielding more stable and accurate decision-making. By aggregating outputs at the probability level, soft voting effectively integrates decision patterns derived from both the image and demographic data, enhancing the robustness and reliability of the RW-ROP classification.Figure 2The overall architecture of the proposed MS-ROPNet framework, which combines VGG-ST for retinal images analysis and random forest for demographics processing, followed by soft voting for final RW-ROP classification. VGG-ST is designed to enhance RW-ROP classification by effectively integrating CNN and transformer architectures. The model extracts local image features using VGG19, which consists of stacked convolutional layers with batch normalization ReLU activation and max-pooling layers. The convolutional layers capture fine-grained spatial patterns and pathological textures relevant to ROP, while max-pooling layers help retain dominant structural information. This hierarchical architecture enables VGG19 to effectively learn localized retinal abnormalities, such as vessel dilation, tortuosity, and lesion boundaries, which are critical for early ROP detection. Meanwhile, the SwinTV2 captures global contextual features by employing a shifted window-based self-attention mechanism, which partitions the input image into nonoverlapping windows for efficient computation. This mechanism enables the model to understand global anatomical context and capture long-range dependencies, complementing the localized feature extraction of VGG19.

Random forest is a tree-based ensemble learning model that classifies RW-ROP using demographic features. It builds an ensemble of decision trees, each trained on random subsets of the features and samples. The final classification result is determined through majority voting, allowing the model to capture nonlinear relationships in demographic characteristics while reducing overfitting.

We conducted interpretability analysis to examine how each data source contributes to the RW-ROP classification in MS-ROPNet. For retinal images processed by VGG-ST, we used gradient-weighted class activation mapping to visualize the model's attention heatmaps. For the demographic characteristics processed by the RF model, we employed the mean decrease impurity method, which evaluates the total reduction in node impurity contributed by each feature across all decision trees. For the soft voting module, which combines the RF and VGG-ST outputs, we applied an entropy-based importance analysis. Specifically, we computed the entropy difference between the original soft voting result and the output obtained after removing each input source. This two-stage procedure allows for measuring the influence of each data source on the overall RW-ROP classification decision.

The proposed model was implemented using PyTorch (for VGG-ST) and Scikit-learn (for RF), with development and evaluation conducted on an AMD EPYC 7313P CPU and NVIDIA RTX A5000 GPUs. For VGG-ST, we employed transfer learning by initializing the model weights pretrained on ImageNet to leverage rich feature representations learned from large-scale natural image datasets and facilitate generalization to retinal images. The model’s learnable parameters were optimized using the Adam optimizer to ensure stable convergence and efficient training. To address class imbalance, we adopted the weighted cross-entropy loss function, which assigns higher weights to underrepresented classes, thereby mitigating bias toward the majority class.19

To optimize model performance, we conducted grid search-based hyperparameter tuning. The model checkpoint with the highest validation performance was selected for testing. For VGG-ST, the learning rate was explored within the range 5×10∧(–5) to 1×10∧(–3), batch size was selected from {8, 16, 32, 64}, and class weight for the RW-ROP class in the loss function was adjusted between 0.5 and 0.9 with a step size of 0.1. The classification MLP’s hidden dimension size was chosen from {5, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256}, while the number of layers varied within {1, 2, 3}. The model was trained for up to 100 epochs, incorporating early stopping if no improvement in validation performance was observed within 10 epochs. For the RF model, the number of decision trees was selected from {200, 400, 600, 800}, and the maximum tree depth from {None, 20, 40}. The minimum number of samples required for a split was explored within {2, 5, 10}, the minimum number of samples per leaf within {1, 2, 3, 4}, and the maximum number of features considered at each split was chosen from {None, 'sqrt', 'log2'}.

Model Performance Evaluation

The performance of the proposed model was evaluated using area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC), area under the precision–recall curve (AUPRC), sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy. Each performance metric was reported as the mean and standard deviation from test sets across fivefold cross-validation. For sensitivity and specificity calculation, we used the default cutoff (i.e., predicted probability 0.50), and the adjusted cutoff to reach a prespecified high sensitivity level (e.g., 90% and 95%) needed for the optimal ROP screening or risk stratification.

To fully assess the performance of our developed multisource model, we compared our model with several existing machine learning models for detecting RW-ROP including ResNet50,7 RETFound,20 and VGG-ST11 for retinal images; RF,21 XGBoost,21 and MLP10 for demographics; ResNet50-MLP,9 ROPRNet,10 VGG19-XGBoost,22 and BIO-CXRNET23 for combination of retinal images and demographics.

Performance of Machine Learning Models for RW-ROP Classification Using Different Fusion Strategies

We evaluated three fusion strategies for integrating retinal images and demographics: early fusion, late fusion, and hybrid fusion. In the early fusion strategy, image features extracted by the VGG-ST model were concatenated with demographics and subsequently fed into an RF for RW-ROP classification. The late fusion strategy, adopted by MS-ROPNet, aggregated the output probabilities from the VGG-ST and RF via soft voting to generate the RW-ROP classification. In the hybrid fusion approach, the output probabilities from VGG-ST were concatenated with demographics and then input into an RF for RW-ROP classification.

Table 2 and Figure 4 show the performance of three fusion strategies of MS-ROPNet for RW-ROP classification. The late fusion strategy consistently outperformed the other strategies across all evaluation metrics (AUROC 95.0%, AUPRC 78.0%, sensitivity 81.5%, specificity 94.3%, and accuracy 93.0%). The hybrid fusion achieved the second-best performance in AUROC (92.8%), AUPRC (69.9%), specificity (92.5%), and accuracy (90.3%) but lowest sensitivity (71.7%), whereas the early fusion reported the second-highest sensitivity (73.2%) at default cutoff value of predicted probability (0.50). At the 90% sensitivity, the late fusion strategy achieved a specificity of 84.8% and an accuracy of 86.3%, outperforming the second-best results from hybrid fusion (76.4% specificity and 77.8% accuracy). To investigate the interpretability of MS-ROPNet and understand the contribution of each input feature to the RW-ROP classification, we assessed feature importance using distinct strategies for the two components (i.e., retinal images and demographics) of the model. The retinal image data had the highest importance, contributing 33.7% to the final classification decision. Among the demographic features, day of life at imaging session, BW, and GA showed relatively high importance, contributing 21.8%, 21.5%, and 19.7%, respectively. In contrast, sex and ethnicity had lower contributions of 1.7% and 1.6%, respectively.

Discussion

In this study, we developed and evaluated MS-ROPNet, a multisource learning framework that integrates retinal images and demographics for RW-ROP classification. The MS-ROPNet was evaluated on the unique e-ROP dataset, a large-scale, multicenter, prospectively collected clinical dataset comprising 7741 eye samples from 1257 preterm infants. Each sample included a center-view retinal image and corresponding demographics that can be easily obtained, including day of life at image session, BW, GA, sex, and ethnicity. Referral-warranted ROP determined at eye level based on clinical diagnostic examinations performed by certified ophthalmologists following standardized study protocols was used as the reference standard when evaluating the performance of MS-ROPNet for detecting RW-ROP. Comprehensive evaluation of model performances demonstrated that MS-ROPNet achieved superior performance across all evaluation metrics compared to existing models for image only, demographics only, and combination of image and demographics. Our MS-ROPNet model achieved an AUROC of 95.0%, AUPRC of 78.0%, sensitivity of 81.5%, specificity of 94.3%, and accuracy of 93.0% at the default cutoff (0.50) of model predicted probability of RW-ROP. Furthermore, MS-ROPNet maintained strong and consistent performance at high sensitivity levels (90% and 95%) with corresponding good specificity of 84.8% and 72.8%, respectively, highlighting its potential use for ROP screening and risk stratification.

Several machine learning models have been evaluated for ROP screening using retinal imaging or demographic data. MS-ROPNet outperformed all evaluated image-only, demographics-only, and multisource baseline models across all metrics (Table 1). Its performance is comparable to that of trained nonphysician graders using retinal images from a single image session (81.9% sensitivity and 90.1% specificity), as reported in the e-ROP study.16 Notably, by adjusting the decision threshold, MS-ROPNet achieved 86.6% sensitivity at 90.0% specificity, exceeding the sensitivity of nonphysician grading. Compared with the best-performing image-only model (VGG-ST), incorporating demographic features led to clear improvements in AUROC and AUPRC. In contrast, the demographics-only RF model, despite achieving high specificity, exhibited substantially lower sensitivity, which is important limitation for screening applications. Across a range of calibrated high-sensitivity operating points, MS-ROPNet consistently achieved the highest specificity and accuracy, demonstrating robust performance under clinically relevant screening conditions. These results indicate that MS-ROPNet provides improved performance over existing approaches and supports its potential utility for RW-ROP risk stratification.

In the evaluation of fusion strategies, late fusion provided the most effective integration of retinal image and demographic features, outperforming both early and hybrid fusion approaches under both default and adjusted decision thresholds (Table 2). These findings highlight the effectiveness of the late fusion design in MS-ROPNet for leveraging complementary information from multimodal inputs.

For demographic modeling, tree-based classifiers (RF and XGBoost) consistently outperformed nontree-based models (MLP and logistic regression) across all operating points (Table 3), with particularly strong advantages at high-sensitivity levels. This suggests that tree-based methods are more effective at capturing nonlinear relationships and feature interactions in demographic data, further supporting their suitability for RW-ROP classification. To further enhance the interpretability of the proposed MS-ROPNet framework, we analyzed the contribution of each input feature. Feature importance was quantified using an information gain-based approach, where entropy was used to measure the marginal contribution of each feature in the presence of all other inputs. Accordingly, the reported percentages reflect relative contribution within the multisource model, rather than the standalone predictive power of individual features, and are not directly compared to the performance of image-only models. Retinal images contributed the most to RW-ROP classification (33.7%), followed by day of life at the image session (21.8%), BW (21.5%), and GA (19.7%). In contrast, sex and ethnicity had minimal contributions, both below 2%. These findings align with established clinical knowledge, highlighting the importance of early developmental factors and imaging data in ROP screening. To further examine interpretability on the image side, attention heatmaps generated using gradient-weighted class activation mapping showed that MS-ROPNet primarily focuses on vascular structures surrounding the optic disc—the clinically relevant regions where key features of RW-ROP, such as vascular dilation and tortuosity, are observed. This alignment between model attention and established clinical diagnostic criteria supports the clinical plausibility of MS-ROPNet's decision-making process. Overall, the feature importance analysis not only validates the effectiveness of the fusion design in MS-ROPNet but also reinforces its potential clinical utility by identifying the most influential factors driving predictions. Notably, although the relative contribution of image features within the model was 33.7%, MS-ROPNet achieved a sensitivity of 81.5% at the default threshold, compared to 80.7% for the image-only model (VGG-ST). More importantly, when the cutoff was adjusted to clinically relevant higher sensitivity levels (90% and 95%), MS-ROPNet consistently achieved higher specificity and accuracy than image-only models, indicating that the integration of demographic features enhances overall performance, particularly under high-sensitivity screening conditions.

This study has several limitations. First, the analysis was conducted solely on the e-ROP dataset (retCam images and demographics) collected from 12 clinical sites in North America. Despite its large sample size, standard Retcam imaging protocol, and RW-ROP from eye examinations by certified ophthalmologists as the reference standard, the generalizability of the MS-ROPNet to other clinical settings or populations with different imaging devices and demographics remains to be evaluated. Second, this study utilized processed center-view retinal images to ensure consistent image standardization across samples. However, real-world bedside ROP screening typically uses unprocessed retinal images, and existing frameworks, e.g., ROP.AI,24 have demonstrated strong performance using such images. This represents an important gap between our current approach and real-world clinical deployment, and extending the model to unprocessed images remains a key direction for future work. Third, although MS-ROPNet integrates both image and demographics, it does not currently incorporate additional clinical information such as oxygen use, respiratory support, comorbidities, or postnatal weight gain, which may further enhance model performance. Fourth, while feature importance analysis offers preliminary insights into model interpretability, further validation from clinicians is necessary to align model reasoning with clinical decision-making. Future research will extend the model to incorporate cumulative temporal image data and clinical data for longitudinal ROP prediction and evaluate its robustness across multi-institutional datasets. Future work will focus on refining and validating MS-ROPNet as a clinically deployable screening tool, rather than continuing incremental benchmarking against newly emerging methods. This will include retraining on larger, more diverse multicenter datasets to enhance generalizability, as well as extending the framework to incorporate multiview retinal images, better aligning the model with real-world clinical workflows.

In conclusion, we developed MS-ROPNet, a multisource machine learning model that integrates retinal images and demographics for RW-ROP detection. The model leverages a VGG-ST to extract both local and global features from retinal images, while utilizing a tree-based RF model to utilize demographics for RW-ROP detection. Comprehensive evaluations on the large e-ROP dataset demonstrated that MS-ROPNet consistently outperformed existing machine learning models, with high sensitivity and specificity that are similar to the trained nonphysician grading of retinal images. These findings support the multisource machine learning model for effective ROP screening or risk stratification, which may help reduce the burden of ROP examination by ophthalmologists.

Clinicopathological features and prognosis of patients with colorectal Mucinous adenocarcinoma mixed with other pathological components: a nationwide retrospective study in China
Authors: J Yuan, H He, P Zhang, X Guan, M Yu, Y Zhang, S Ning, L Wang, Y Lv, M Jiao, Y Sun, Q Sun, X Ren, D Liu, Z Zhang, Z Ye, J Li, G Yu, B Ma, W Fu, X H Kong, C Jing, K Tao, Y Sun, C Jiang, J Chen, G Zhang, H Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMucinous adenocarcinoma (MAC) is typically admixed with other pathological components, including conventional adenocarcinoma, signet ring cell carcinoma, and/or neuroendocrine neoplasms. Specifically, signet ring cell differentiation (MASD) is defined as a signet ring cell component comprising less than 50% of the tumor, and neuroendocrine differentiation (MAND) is defined as a neuroendocrine component constituting less than 30%. Furthermore, MAC admixed with conventional adenocarcinoma was defined as classic mucinous adenocarcinoma (CMAC) in this study. Therefore, the study aimed to investigate the clinicopathologic and prognostic differences between patients with CMAC and those with either MASD or MAND [collectively termed mucous adenocarcinoma mixed with other pathological components (MAM)].MethodsWe collected data from a multi-institutional registry of patients who underwent surgical curative resection for histologically proven MAC between January 2016 and September 2021 at 22 medical institutions in China. Patients with MAC with percentage of signet ring cell ≥ 50% or percentage of neuroendocrine component ≥ 30% were excluded.ResultsA total of 2023 patients from 22 medical institutions who met the study criteria were included. MAM, compared to CMAC, showed more aggressive histologic features, including higher rates of lymphovascular invasion (47.0% vs. 18.0%, p < 0.01), perineural invasion (68.0% vs. 35.1%, p < 0.01), T4 stage (33.5% vs. 26.5%, p < 0.01), N2 stage (56.2% vs. 17.8%, p < 0.01), and TNM stage III disease (73.5% vs. 49.2%, p < 0.01). MAMs had lower 3-year overall survival compared to those with CMAC (66.7% vs. 81.6%, p < 0.01). Multivariable analysis indicated that MAMs, including MASD and MAND, was an independent prognostic factor for poor disease-free survival and overall survival.ConclusionOur analysis of a large patient cohort confirmed the aggressive clinicopathological features and poor prognostic outcomes of MAM, including MAND and MASD, compared with CMAC. These findings underscore the need for surveillance protocols for MAM in clinical practice.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s10151-025-03225-0.Keywords: Colorectal mucinous adenocarcinoma, Signet ring cell differentiation, Neuroendocrine differentiation, Prognosis

Introduction

Colorectal carcinoma ranks as the third most common malignancy and the second leading cause of cancer-related death worldwide, accounting for more than 900,000 deaths annually [1]. On the basis of histological classification, colorectal cancer can be divided into several types, including classical adenocarcinoma, mucinous adenocarcinoma (MAC), and signet ring cell carcinoma [2]. MAC, which accounts for approximately 10–15% of colorectal cancers [3], is a specific subtype characterized by an extracellular mucous component that constitutes at least 50% of the tumor volume [2]. In terms of prognosis, a meta-analysis conducted by our group, which included 56 studies with a total of 803,157 patients, revealed that MAC was an adverse prognostic factor for overall survival in patients with colorectal cancer (HR 1.04, 95% CI 1.03–1.04, p < 0.01) [4], which was consistent with previous research [5].

According to the World Health Organization (WHO), signet ring cell carcinoma is defined as the presence of signet ring cells occupying more than 50% of the tumor area [2]. Similarly, neuroendocrine carcinoma is defined as a neuroendocrine component exceeding 30% of the tumor area. Otherwise, they were defined as adenocarcinomas with signet ring cell differentiation or neuroendocrine differentiation [6–8]. However, the mucin percentage in MAC rarely reaches 100%. These tumors are typically admixed with other pathological components, including conventional adenocarcinoma, signet ring cell carcinoma, and/or neuroendocrine neoplasms. Conventional adenocarcinoma is the most common mixed pathological component of MAC and was defined as classic mucinous adenocarcinoma (CMAC) in this study. Cases exhibiting signet ring cell differentiation (MASD) or neuroendocrine differentiation (MAND) constitute a relatively small proportion of MACs.

Previous studies have shown that adenocarcinoma with mixed subtypes has a less favorable prognosis than conventional adenocarcinoma [9, 10]. A population-based study revealed that patients with colorectal adenocarcinoma and neuroendocrine differentiation have poorer outcomes than those with either pure adenocarcinoma or neuroendocrine carcinoma [11]. Another population-based study reported that the 5-year overall survival (OS) of patients with MAC and signet ring cell carcinoma was significantly lower than that of patients with classical adenocarcinoma (52% vs. 58.7%, p < 0.001 and 40% vs. 58.7%, p < 0.001, respectively) [12]. However, as a result of a lack of prospective, large-scale studies, the clinicopathological features and prognosis of MAMs remain poorly understood. Therefore, this study aims to explore the clinicopathological characteristics and prognosis of patients with MAM (MASD/MAND), thereby providing evidence to inform more specific treatment and follow-up strategies for this patient cohort.

Materials and methods

Study design

Patients who underwent curative resection for colorectal cancer between January 2016 and September 2021 at 22 medical institutions in China were reviewed. Data from patients who underwent curative resection for the primary lesion and had histologically proven MAC were obtained. Patients were excluded if they met any of the following criteria: (1) percentage of mucinous component < 50% or not clearly described; (2) percentage of signet ring cell ≥ 50%; (3) percentage of neuroendocrine component ≥ 30%; (4) missing data; (5) presence of other malignant tumors; or (6) distant metastasis. The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the ethics committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Shandong First Medical University [Approval No. YXLL-KY-2024(116)]. The study was performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Data collection

The demographic information of patients (age, sex) and the clinical information (time of surgery, type of treatment received, and patient death or survival) were extracted from the patients’ medical records in the cancer registration system in the hospital archives. Tumor characteristics, including tumor differentiation, stage, size, lymphovascular invasion, and perineural invasion, were extracted from the pathological and histological reports of patients. The pathological tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) class and stage were determined according to the classification established by the American Joint Committee on Cancer [13], and the definitive diagnosis of MAC was based on the WHO Gastrointestinal Tumors Classification criteria [2].

Study endpoint

The endpoint of this study was survival time. Deaths from any cause, recurrence, or metastasis were considered events. Patients still alive at the last follow-up, with or without disease, were censored. Survival time was measured from the date of surgery to the date of death or the last known follow-up. Overall survival (OS) was defined as the period from surgery to death from any cause, and disease-free survival (DFS) was defined as the period from surgery to recurrence or any death from any cause.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed via SPSS version 26.0 (IBM, Armonk, NY, USA). The χ2 test was employed to assess differences in clinicopathological features between patients with CMAC and those with MAM (MASD/MAND). Survival rates were estimated via the Kaplan‒Meier method, and between-group survival differences were analyzed via the log-rank test. Case characteristics were considered independent prognostic factors if they demonstrated a significant association (p < 0.05) in multivariable regression analysis via the Cox proportional hazards model.

Results

Patient characteristics and distribution

A total of 2023 patients with MAC who met the inclusion criteria were included in the study (Fig. 1). The baseline characteristics of patients with MAC were as follows: 1577 male patients (58.1%) and 872 female patients (41.9%), of whom 1525 were 60 years or older (56.2%) and 892 younger than 60 years (43.8%). In addition, 1838 patients had CMACs (90.9%), whereas 185 had MAMs (9.1%). Additionally, in the MAM cohort, six patients had both neuroendocrine differentiation and signet ring cell differentiation, and these patients were included in both the MASD group (n = 163, 8.1%) and the MAND group (n = 28, 1.4%) (Fig. 1 and Table 1).

Clinicopathological differences between the CMAC group and the MAM (MAND/MASD) group

In this study, MAM was more common in younger patients (59.5% vs. 42.5%, p < 0.01) and male patients (64.3% vs. 56.1%, p = 0.03). Compared with CMAC, MAM was significantly associated with the presence of lymphovascular invasion (47.0% vs. 18.0%, p < 0.01), perineural invasion (68.0% vs. 35.1%, p < 0.01), T4 stage (33.5% vs. 26.5%, p < 0.01), N2 stage (56.2% vs. 17.8%, p < 0.01), and TNM stage III disease (73.5% vs. 49.2%, p < 0.01) (Table 2).

Compared with CMAC, MASD was associated with younger age (59.5% vs. 42.5%, p < 0.01), presence of lymphovascular invasion (69.4% vs. 35.1%, p < 0.01), perineural invasion (56.0% vs. 36.1%, p < 0.01), N2 stage (59.5% vs. 17.8%, p < 0.01), and TNM stage III disease (77.3% vs. 49.2%, p < 0.01). Additionally, MAND was significantly associated with lymphovascular invasion (65.0% vs. 35.1%, p < 0.01), perineural invasion (61.1% vs. 36.1%, p = 0.03), and N2 stage disease (35.7% vs. 17.8%, p = 0.02) compared with CMAC. These results suggest that MAMs, including MASD and MAND, differ greatly from CMAC in terms of clinicopathological features.

Prognostic difference between CMAC and MAM (MAND/MASD)

To further elucidate the differences between CMAC and MAM, the survival of the patients were estimated. With a median follow-up period of 60 months (range 37–106 months), the 3-year OS and 3-year DFS for all patients with MAC were 81.6% and 79.0%, respectively (Fig. 2). The 3-year OS and 3-year DFS rates of patients with MAM were significantly lower than those of patients with CMAC (66.7% vs. 82.9%, p < 0.01 and 60.4% vs. 80.7%, p < 0.01) (Fig. 2a, b). According to the subgroup analysis, the 3-year OS and 3-year DFS rates in the MASD group were significantly lower than those in the CMAC group (66.3% vs. 82.9%, p < 0.01 and 61.2% vs. 80.7%, p < 0.01) (Fig. 2c, d). In addition, the 3-year OS and 3-year DFS rates in the MASD group were significantly lower than those in the CMAC group (64.7% vs. 82.9%, p < 0.01 and 47.1% vs. 80.7%, p < 0.01) (Fig. 2e, f).

The 3-year recurrence rates were 17.8% (33/185) in the MAM cohort and 10.2% (187/1838) in the CMAC cohort (Fig. 3). In the MAM cohort, postoperative metastasis sites were distributed as follows: peritoneum (27.3%), liver (12.1%), lungs (6.1%), multiple organs (39.4%), other organs (6.1%), and local recurrence (9.1%). In the CMAC cohort, the corresponding rates were peritoneum (23.5%), liver (17.1%), lungs (15.0%), multiple organs (24.6%), other organs (7.5%), and local recurrence (12.3%). Notably, multiorgan involvement was more frequent in the MAM cohort than in the CMAC cohort (39.4% vs. 24.6%). The prognostic differences further determined the specificity of the patients with MAM.

Poor prognostic factors for MAC

Univariate and multivariate analyses were conducted to assess adverse factors affecting the prognosis of patients with MAC. In the univariate analysis, significant factors affecting the 3-year OS of patients with MAC included histological classification (p < 0.01), age (p < 0.01), tumor location (p < 0.01), lymphovascular invasion (p < 0.01), perineural invasion (p < 0.01), T stage (p < 0.01), N stage (p < 0.01), and TNM stage (p < 0.01). All significant prognostic factors identified in the univariate analysis were included in the multivariate Cox regression analysis. The independent prognostic factors that remained significant included age (p < 0.01), tumor location (p < 0.01), T stage (p = 0.01), and histological classification (p < 0.01) (Table 3). MAM was identified as an independent predictor of poor prognosis for 3-year OS (HR 2.19, 95% CI 1.54–3.11, p < 0.01) (Table 3) and DFS (HR 2.46, 95% CI 1.69–3.38, p < 0.01) (Table 4). In the subgroup analysis, MAND was identified as an independent predictor of poor prognosis for 3-year OS (HR 2.73, 95% CI 1.11–4.35, p = 0.02) (Table S1) and DFS (HR 3.13, 95% CI 1.27–6.54, p < 0.01) (Table S2). Similarly, MASD score was an independent predictor of poor prognosis for 3-year OS (HR 2.04, 95% CI 1.42–3.15, p < 0.01) (Table S3) and DFS (HR 2.31, 95% CI 1.56–3.72, p < 0.01) (Table S4). Thus, these results suggest that MAM (MASD/MAND) is an adverse prognostic factor for patients with MAC.

Discussion

The clinicopathological features and prognosis of MAMs, including MASD and MAND, are poorly investigated at present. As revealed in this study cohort, great differences existed in patients with MAC. Patients with MAM had significantly different clinicopathological features and poorer 3-year OS than patients with CMAC did (HR 2.19, 95% CI 1.54–3.11, p < 0.01). When these patients were further dichotomized into the MASD group and MAND group, both groups of patients had much poorer OS and DFS than patients with CMAC did. The obvious difference between them is that specific treatment and follow-up strategies should be used to improve the prognosis of patients.

Colorectal signet ring cell carcinoma is a rare and distinct cancer subtype that accounts for approximately 1% of all colorectal cancers [14–16]. Signet ring cell carcinoma is frequently associated with younger age and advanced disease at diagnosis [17]. Patients with signet ring cell carcinoma tend to present at a more advanced stage than do those with conventional adenocarcinoma, and their prognosis is generally poor [12, 14, 18]. A meta-analysis of 30 studies published in 2021 revealed that stage at presentation was significantly more advanced in signet ring cell carcinoma than in conventional adenocarcinoma (OR 4.95, 95% CI 3.83–6.39; p < 0.001), and 5year OS was significantly poorer for signet ring cell carcinoma than for conventional carcinoma (HR 2.54, 95% CI 1.98–3.27, p < 0.001) [18]. Compared with CMAC, MASD was also significantly associated with younger age and advanced disease at diagnosis. In addition, MASD was more strongly associated with lymphovascular invasion and perineural invasion than CMAC was.

Colorectal neuroendocrine carcinomas account for less than 1% of all colorectal cancers [19, 20]. Historically, the rarity of neuroendocrine neoplasms in colorectal cancer has limited attention to this subtype, but with a rising incidence, neuroendocrine neoplasms are gaining recognition in public discourse [21–23]. A population-based analysis revealed that the 5-year relative survival rate of patients with colorectal neuroendocrine tumors was lower than that of patients with adenocarcinoma (16.3% vs. 50.2%) [24]. One study revealed lymphatic invasion (RR 9.88, 95% CI 3.85–25.32, p < 0.001) [25], and another study revealed that lymph node metastasis was associated with shorter OS (40.8 vs. 52.7 months, p < 0.001) and cancer-specific survival (41.3 vs. 54.8 months, p < 0.001) [26]. A population-based study revealed that adenocarcinoma with neuroendocrine differentiation was associated with lymph node metastasis and was not affected by tumor location, differentiation, T stage, or distant metastasis status [27]. In this study, we also found that MAND was more strongly associated with the N2 stage than was CMAC (35.7% vs. 17.8%, p = 0.02). However, tumor stage (p = 0.94), tumor size (p = 0.21), and tumor location (p = 0.10) (Table 2) were not correlated with the pathological features of neuroendocrine neoplasms or adenocarcinomas with neuroendocrine differentiation. The presence of neuroendocrine neoplasm components in colorectal tumors may be related to lymph node metastasis.

A population-based cohort study revealed that patients with signet ring cell carcinoma had a worse prognosis than patients with adenocarcinoma did (5-year OS 58.7% vs. 40.3%, p < 0.001) but neither was an independent risk factor for overall survival (adenocarcinoma vs. signet ring cell carcinoma: HR 0.99, 95% CI 0.91–1.12, p = 0.936) [12]. However, in this retrospective observational study, MASD was found not only to have poorer 3-year OS and 3-year DFS than CMAC did (66.3% vs. 82.9%, p < 0.01 and 61.2% vs. 80.7%, p < 0.01) (Fig. 2c, d) but also to be an independent predictor of poor prognosis for 3-year OS (HR 2.04, 95% CI 1.42–3.15, p < 0.01) (Table 3) and DFS (HR 2.31, 95% CI 1.54–3.11, p < 0.01). In addition, MAND was identified as an independent predictor of poor prognosis for 3-year OS (HR 2.73, 95% CI 1.11–4.35, p = 0.02) (Table S1) and DFS (HR 3.13, 95% CI 1.27–6.54, p < 0.01) (Table S3). This study revealed that even if the percentage of signet ring cell components was < 50% or the percentage of neuroendocrine components was < 30% in these patients, the mixed pathological type significantly impacted patient prognosis.

The study’s limitations include those that are inherently associated with retrospective studies. First, the incidence of MAM (MASD/MAND) was low. Despite the collection of clinical data from 22 centers from January 2016 to September 2021, only 185 patients with MAM (MASD/MAND) met our criteria. Second, microsatellite stability status and adjuvant chemotherapy were not analyzed in this study. Finally, the lack of a detailed description of the pathological components of MAC in existing databases has prevented us from validating the reliability of our conclusions.

Conclusion

The clinicopathological features of patients with mucinous adenocarcinoma mixed with other pathological components are significantly different from those of classic mucinous adenocarcinoma. Mucous adenocarcinoma mixed with other pathological components is associated with a worse prognosis than classic mucinous adenocarcinoma is. Patients with mucous adenocarcinoma mixed with other pathological components, including neuroendocrine differentiation and signet ring cell differentiation, should receive more attention and receive more rigid follow-up regimens to improve their prognosis.

1. The P Value: What It Is and What It Is Not.

Habibzadeh F.

This review clarifies the historical, conceptual, and practical meanings of the P value in biomedical research. It contrasts Fisher’s evidential, inductive interpretation with the Neyman–Pearson decision-based, deductive framework, using a drug-cholesterol trial example. The paper highlights common misconceptions, distinguishes statistical from clinical significance, and critiques reliance on the P < 0.05 threshold. It urges reporting of effect sizes, 95% CIs, and likelihood ratios, addressing issues of p-hacking, publication bias, and reproducibility. The author concludes that transparent estimation, not dichotomous testing, should guide scientific inference.

2. Analysis of Retracted Publications on Artificial Intelligence: Trends, Ethical Concerns, and Scientific Integrity.

Kocyigit BF, et al.

This article analyzed 764 retracted AI-related papers indexed in PubMed to identify trends, causes, and ethical issues. Most retractions occurred in 2023, with China leading (n = 551). Major causes included peer-review manipulation (n = 716), data concerns (n = 714), and unethical AI use (n = 238). Retractions clustered in computational biology and neuroscience journals. The study highlights paper-mill patterns and calls for stronger editorial oversight, transparent AI disclosure, and rigorous post-publication review to protect research integrity and prevent future misconduct.

3. Varicella Vaccine Effectiveness and Duration of Protection: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.

Choe YJ, et al.

This analysis of 66 studies evaluated varicella vaccine effectiveness and duration of protection. Pooled vaccine effectiveness (VE) was 79% for one dose and 94% for two doses, with 92% protection against moderate to severe disease. Thirteen studies assessed duration, showing gradual waning over time—especially after six years. Two-dose regimens provided stronger and more durable immunity. The study supports universal two-dose varicella vaccination programs and emphasizes ongoing monitoring to sustain population-level protection.

4. Innovative Strategy for Enhanced Delivery of Anti-Fibrotic miR-150 via PDGFR-Targeted Exosomes for Fibrosis Treatment.

Kim SJ, et al.

This study developed PDGFR-targeted exosomes (tEx) to deliver anti-fibrotic miR-150 specifically to activated hepatic stellate cells (HSCs) in liver fibrosis. Adipose-derived stem cells were engineered to express PDGFR-binding peptides, and miR-150 was loaded into these exosomes. In vitro and thioacetamide-induced mouse models showed that tEx[miR-150] markedly reduced fibrosis markers (α-SMA, COL1A1, TGF-β1), collagen deposition, and inflammatory cytokines by inhibiting PI3K/AKT and TGF-β/Smad pathways. The strategy achieved precise, low-toxicity antifibrotic effects, offering a promising targeted therapy for liver fibrosis.

5. Comparative Renal Safety of Besifovir Dipivoxil Maleate and Tenofovir Disoproxil Fumarate in Chronic Hepatitis B Patients: Insights From a Nationwide Cohort Study.

Yoo JJ, et al.

This nationwide retrospective cohort study compared renal safety between besifovir dipivoxil maleate (BSV) and tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (TDF) in treatment-naïve chronic hepatitis B patients using Korea’s national health database. After inverse probability of treatment weighting, 19,959 BSV and 19,991 TDF users were analyzed. Chronic kidney disease incidence was lower with BSV (2.29 vs. 3.42 per 1,000 person-years; adjusted HR, 1.36, P = 0.012), particularly in patients ≥ 60 years. BSV demonstrated a more favorable renal safety profile, suggesting suitability for older or renally vulnerable patients.

6. Impact of Intravascular Fluid Resuscitation on Whole Blood Viscosity During Endovascular Detachable Coiling Procedure for Unruptured Intracranial Aneurysm: A Prospective Randomized Controlled Trial.

Kim TY, et al.

This prospective randomized controlled trial compared balanced crystalloid and 6% hydroxyethyl starch (HES 130/0.4) during endovascular detachable coiling (EDC) for unruptured intracranial aneurysms. Thirty patients were randomized to receive either fluid, and whole blood viscosity (WBV) was measured before and after EDC. Post-procedural high-shear WBV was significantly lower with HES than crystalloid (3.40 ± 0.33 vs. 3.95 ± 0.79 cP, P = 0.023), with similar hematocrit and oxygenation. HES effectively reduced WBV without hemodilution, suggesting potential hemodynamic benefits during neurointerventional procedures.

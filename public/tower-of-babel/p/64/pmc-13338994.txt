The Impact of Physiotherapy Rehabilitation on Functional Recovery in Postoperative Paraplegia Following L2-L3 Laminectomy With Discectomy: A Case Report
Authors: Sakshi P Kavitake, Suraj Kanase
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Postoperative paraplegia is a severe neurological complication that significantly affects functional mobility and quality of life. This case report describes the rehabilitation course of a 72-year-old male who was diagnosed with paraplegia following an L2-L3 laminectomy and discectomy. The patient presented with a lack of movement in both lower limbs, bilateral foot drop, altered sensation over the left sole, and a reduced range of motion following surgery. An individualized physiotherapy rehabilitation program was designed after a detailed assessment and goal-setting. Rehabilitation primarily focused on bed mobility training, sensory re-education, range of motion exercises, strengthening exercises, and functional training. Additional interventions were aimed at preventing secondary complications and improving overall functional independence. Progress was regularly monitored throughout the rehabilitation period.Following four weeks of rehabilitation, gradual improvements were observed in lower limb muscle activation, bed mobility, sitting balance, and functional mobility. The patient also demonstrated improvements in overall physical condition and independence in daily activities. This case report highlights the importance of early and structured physiotherapy rehabilitation in promoting functional recovery and improving quality of life in patients with postoperative paraplegia. Further documentation of similar cases may help develop effective rehabilitation strategies for neurological recovery following spinal surgery.

Introduction

Postoperative paraplegia is a severe neurological complication that may occur following spinal surgeries, traumatic spinal injuries, or spinal cord compression. It results in significant motor, sensory, and functional impairments, thereby significantly affecting an individual’s mobility, independence, and quality of life [1]. Degenerative spinal disorders that cause chronic nerve root or spinal cord compression are among the common indications for decompressive spinal procedures such as laminectomy and discectomy [2]. Although these procedures are frequently performed to relieve pain and neurological symptoms, post-surgical neurological deficits may occasionally occur due to nerve root injury, vascular compromise, edema, or spinal cord damage [3].

Postoperative paraplegia, although relatively uncommon, represents one of the most devastating complications of spinal surgery, with reported incidence rates ranging from 0.2% to 1% following lumbar decompressive procedures. Its occurrence necessitates prompt and structured rehabilitation to maximize neurological and functional recovery. Paraplegia following spinal surgery can lead to lower limb weakness, sensory disturbances, impaired balance, reduced functional mobility, and dependence in activities of daily living. Prolonged immobility may further contribute to secondary complications such as joint stiffness, muscle atrophy, pressure sores, respiratory complications, and reduced cardiovascular endurance. Early identification and management of these impairments are therefore essential for improving recovery outcomes [4]. Physiotherapy plays a critical role in addressing these deficits and maximizing recovery potential through early, structured, and individualized intervention.

Physiotherapy rehabilitation plays a vital role in restoring functional mobility, improving muscle strength, preventing secondary complications, and enhancing overall independence in patients with neurological deficits [5]. A structured rehabilitation program that includes bed mobility training, sensory re-education, range of motion exercises, strengthening exercises, balance training, and functional task-oriented activities can significantly improve physical function and quality of life in paraplegic patients [6]. This case report describes the impact of physiotherapy rehabilitation on functional recovery in a 72-year-old male who developed paraplegia following L2-L3 laminectomy and discectomy. The report highlights the importance of early and individualized physiotherapy intervention for enhancing mobility and functional independence following postoperative neurological complications.

Written informed consent was obtained from the patient for publication of this case report and associated clinical information.

Case presentation

Patient information

A 72-year-old male retired high school teacher from Karad presented with complaints of an absence of movement in both lower limbs, bilateral foot drop, and altered sensation over the left sole following an L2-L3 laminectomy and discectomy performed on September 4, 2025. The patient was right-hand dominant. He had experienced radiating pain in the right lower limb for the past five years, secondary to degenerative lumbar spine changes with nerve root involvement. Following surgical intervention, he developed paraplegia with significant functional limitations and was referred for physiotherapy rehabilitation. There was no significant past medical history of hypertension, diabetes mellitus, stroke, Parkinson's disease, or traumatic brain injury.

Clinical findings

On initial physiotherapy evaluation, the patient was conscious, oriented, and had stable vital signs. The patient was assessed in a supine position. Bilateral lower limb movements were absent, and bilateral foot drop was noted. Sensory deficits were present over the left sole. Functional assessment revealed severe limitation in bed mobility and complete dependence for activities of daily living. Muscle tone was reduced in the bilateral lower limb musculature, including the hip flexors, extensors, abductors, adductors, knee flexors and extensors, and ankle plantarflexors and dorsiflexors. Range of motion was restricted at the hip (flexion limited to approximately 60°), knee (flexion limited to approximately 70°), and ankle joints bilaterally, likely due to prolonged postoperative immobilization. Further neurological assessment, including Manual Muscle Testing (MMT) and reflex examination, was conducted to document baseline status.

Diagnostic assessment

The MRI findings are illustrated in Figure 1. The MRI was performed before surgical intervention on September 1, 2025, as part of the preoperative diagnostic workup, and the findings helped guide the decision for L2-L3 laminectomy and discectomy. MRI of the lumbosacral spine with whole-spine screening revealed loss of lumbar lordosis with multilevel degenerative (spondylotic) changes. Diffuse disc-osteophyte complexes were noted at multiple levels (L1-S1) with varying degrees of canal and foraminal narrowing. At the L2-L3 level, a diffuse disc-osteophytic bulge with posteroventral and right paracentral protrusion was seen compressing the thecal sac and the right traversing nerve root, with indentation of the left traversing and bilateral exiting nerve roots. Associated facet joint hypertrophy resulted in severe spinal canal stenosis at this level. Overall, MRI findings were consistent with multilevel lumbar spondylosis with severe canal stenosis at L2-L3, correlating with the patient’s postoperative neurological deficits and functional impairment.

Discussion

Postoperative paraplegia following lumbar spine surgery is a serious neurological complication that significantly affects mobility, functional independence, and quality of life [7]. Although procedures such as laminectomy and discectomy are commonly performed to relieve neural compression, postoperative neurological deficits may occur due to nerve injury, vascular compromise, edema, or inflammation [8]. These complications can result in severe motor weakness, sensory impairment, impaired balance, and dependence in activities of daily living [9].

Early physiotherapy rehabilitation plays a vital role in improving functional outcomes in patients with postoperative paraplegia [10]. In the present case, a structured and progressive rehabilitation program resulted in gradual improvements in lower limb muscle activation, trunk control, sitting balance, standing tolerance, and assisted ambulation over four weeks. The rehabilitation protocol was designed based on the patient’s clinical condition and focused on addressing motor weakness, sensory deficits, impaired mobility, and reduced functional independence.

Physiotherapy management primarily focused on the prevention of secondary complications associated with prolonged immobilization, including muscle atrophy, joint stiffness, pressure sores, and decreased cardiovascular endurance [11-13]. Interventions such as positioning, respiratory exercises, range of motion exercises, sensory re-education, strengthening exercises, trunk stabilization, balance training, and functional mobility exercises were incorporated to facilitate neurological recovery and improve independence [5,14,15]. It should also be acknowledged that spontaneous neurological recovery following surgical decompression of the spinal cord may have contributed to the functional improvements observed. The decompression procedure itself relieves mechanical compression and reduces ischemic injury, which may facilitate some degree of intrinsic neural recovery independent of physiotherapy. The relative contributions of surgical decompression and physiotherapy rehabilitation to the overall outcome cannot be fully distinguished in a single case report design.

The improvements observed in this case may be attributed to early mobilization and repetitive task-specific training, which are known to promote neuroplasticity and motor relearning in neurological rehabilitation [16,17]. Sensory integration therapy and progressive functional training may also have contributed to improved proprioception, muscle activation, and postural control. Continuous monitoring and progression of exercises according to patient tolerance further supported functional recovery [18,19].

Outcome assessment using MMT and FIM demonstrated progressive improvement in muscle strength and functional independence throughout the rehabilitation period. These findings highlight the importance of individualized and goal-oriented physiotherapy rehabilitation in enhancing recovery following postoperative paraplegia. Despite the improvements achieved, recovery following postoperative paraplegia remains variable depending on the severity of neural involvement, timing of intervention, and patient-specific factors. Further studies and documentation of similar cases are necessary to develop evidence-based rehabilitation protocols for neurological recovery following spinal surgery.

Limitations and future directions

This case report is limited by its single-patient design, which restricts the generalizability of the findings. The absence of a control group makes it difficult to determine the independent contribution of physiotherapy rehabilitation to the observed improvements. In addition, long-term follow-up was not available to assess the sustainability of functional recovery. Future research involving larger sample sizes, longer follow-up periods, and controlled study designs is needed to establish evidence-based physiotherapy rehabilitation protocols for patients with postoperative paraplegia following spinal surgery.

Conclusions

This case report underscores the crucial role of physiotherapy rehabilitation in improving functional recovery in a patient with postoperative paraplegia following L2-L3 laminectomy and discectomy. By addressing the multiple impairments associated with neurological deficits through a structured and progressive rehabilitation program, significant improvements were observed in muscle activation, balance, mobility, and functional independence. The report highlights the importance of early intervention, task-specific training, and a multidisciplinary, patient-centred approach in enhancing recovery and minimizing secondary complications following spinal surgery. Furthermore, this report emphasizes the need for continued research, clinical collaboration, and development of evidence-based rehabilitation protocols for patients with postoperative neurological complications.

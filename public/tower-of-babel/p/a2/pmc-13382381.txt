Immune checkpoint profiling of B7‐H proteins predicts survival and treatment response in metastatic clear cell renal cell carcinoma
Authors: Maite Emaldi, Esther Rey‐Iborra, Lorena Mosteiro, David Lecumberri, Ane Miren Iturregui, Jon Danel Solano‐Iturri, María Armesto, Charles H Lawrie, Rafael Pulido, Javier C Angulo, José I López, Gorka Larrinaga, Caroline E Nunes‐Xavier
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe B7‐H family of immune checkpoint proteins plays a central role in tumor immune regulation, yet their prognostic and predictive significance in metastatic clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) remains incompletely defined. We analyzed a retrospective cohort of primary tumor specimens from 145 patients with synchronous or metachronous metastatic ccRCC to evaluate the expression of B7‐H3, B7‐H4, B7‐H5, and B7‐H7 and their association with tumor aggressiveness, treatment response, and long‐term survival. Immunohistochemistry revealed marked intratumoral heterogeneity and distinct compartmentalized expression patterns, with B7‐H3 detected in both tumor cells and stromal compartments, B7‐H4 exclusively expressed in tumor cells, B7‐H5 localized to tumor cells and tumor‐infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs), and B7‐H7 detected in both tumor cells and TILs. We did not detect any overlap in expression between B7‐H proteins and PD‐L1 positivity in TILs. High B7‐H3 expression in tumor and stroma was significantly associated with higher histological grade, increased local invasion, lymph node involvement, and advanced stage. B7‐H5 expression in TILs correlated with impaired Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status. Elevated B7‐H3 predicted unfavorable response to tyrosine kinase inhibitors, mechanistic target of rapamycin inhibitors, and immune checkpoint inhibitors, whereas B7‐H5 negativity in tumor cells associated with more favorable treatment outcomes. B7‐H4 and B7‐H7 expression in tumor cells were associated with features of tumor aggressiveness and immune suppression, further highlighting the potential role of multiple B7‐H family checkpoints in metastatic ccRCC. In multivariable Cox regression models, B7‐H3 expression in tumor and stroma independently predicted shorter disease‐free and overall survival. B7‐H5 expression in TILs independently predicted reduced overall survival. These findings identify B7‐H3 as a robust biomarker of tumor aggressiveness, therapeutic resistance, and adverse prognosis in metastatic ccRCC, while B7‐H5 in TILs provides complementary prognostic information. Integrated profiling of B7‐H immune checkpoints in primary tumors may refine clinical risk stratification and support the development of biomarker‐guided immunotherapeutic strategies.Keywords: immune checkpoint proteins, clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC), B7‐H3, B7‐H4, B7‐H5 (VISTA), B7‐H7, tumor microenvironment, prognostic biomarker, treatment response

Introduction

Clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) is the predominant histological subtype of kidney cancer and represents a major cause of cancer‐related mortality worldwide. Approximately 25–30% of patients present with metastatic disease at diagnosis, and a substantial proportion of initially localized tumors eventually progress to metastasis despite radical nephrectomy [1, 2, 3]. Tumor immune evasion and intrinsic resistance to systemic therapies remain substantial clinical challenges in metastatic ccRCC, and existing biomarkers like programmed death‐ligand 1 (PD‐L1) have exhibited inconsistent predictive power for response to immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICI) and survival outcomes [4, 5, 6]. This highlights the need for novel biomarkers that more accurately reflect tumor‐immune interactions and improved therapeutic interventions.

The B7‐H family of immune regulators comprises several co‐stimulatory and co‐inhibitory proteins that modulate T‐cell activity [7]. B7‐H3 (CD276) is frequently overexpressed across a variety of cancers, including ccRCC, where it has been implicated in immune suppression, tumor progression, and poor clinical outcomes [5, 8, 9, 10, 11]. Early evidence demonstrated that B7‐H3 expression in both tumor cells and vasculature predicted worse prognosis in ccRCC and it was associated with immune cell infiltration and adverse pathological features [12, 13]. Functional and transcriptomic studies have further linked B7‐H3 expression in ccRCC to immune exhaustion signatures, myeloid immune evasion, and to PI3K/AKT and JAK/STAT oncogenic signaling pathways [14]. These findings support B7‐H3 as a prognostic biomarker and a potential therapeutic target. Additionally, high B7‐H3 expression has been correlated with poor outcomes and reduced response to checkpoint inhibitors such as nivolumab in metastatic RCC cohorts, suggesting its relevance in guiding immunotherapy [15]. B7‐H5 (VSIR, also known as VISTA or PD‐1H) shows sequence resemblance to PD‐1, suggesting conserved immunoregulatory functions [16]. B7‐H5 can directly inhibit the T‐cell antitumor response and may be expressed on both tumor cells and on tumor‐infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) [16, 17]. Preclinical models and pan‐cancer analyses indicate that B7‐H5 expression shapes immune landscapes and may influence resistance to immunotherapy in several tumor types, although its prognostic role can vary by context [18]. B7‐H5 has been proposed as a potential target of therapy in ccRCC [19], and our recent work in metastatic ccRCC revealed that B7‐H5 is highly glycosylated and variably expressed in tumor cells and TILs, correlating with aggressive disease features and unfavorable clinical outcomes [20]. In addition, B7‐H5 expression in renal cancer with venous tumor thrombus was also associated with worse prognosis [21].

Additional B7‐H family members, including B7‐H4 (VTCN1) and B7‐H7 (HHLA2), have also been implicated in tumor immune regulation and cancer progression. B7‐H4 is an inhibitory immune checkpoint that negatively regulates T‐cell activation in multiple solid tumors, where it has been associated with immune suppression and tumor progression in preclinical and clinical contexts [22, 23, 24]. Although its receptors and functional mechanisms are still being elucidated, B7‐H4 expression negatively regulates T‐cell activation [25], and its expression is found mutually exclusive with PD‐L1 expression, suggesting alternative immune escape pathways [22, 26]. In renal cancer, B7‐H4 expression has been correlated with tumor vasculature, recruitment of tumor‐associated neutrophils, low CD8+ T‐cell infiltration, and overall survival (OS), and to affect tumor cell viability and sensitivity to tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKI) and mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) inhibitors, supporting a functional role beyond immune modulation [4, 27, 28, 29]. B7‐H7 also shows inhibitory effects on human CD4+ and CD8+ T‐cell proliferation, and it may engage distinct receptors such as stimulatory TMIGD2 (CD28H) and inhibitory KIR3DL3 highlighting its potential role in modulating antitumor immunity [30, 31]. B7‐H7 expression is observed across a variety of human cancers and has been linked to advanced disease and worse survival in multiple tumor types, supporting its role in tumor immune evasion [31]. In renal cancer, B7‐H7 is found co‐expressed with PD‐L1 and associated with poor prognosis [32, 33, 34]. Collectively, these studies indicate that multiple B7‐H proteins contribute to immune regulation in the tumor microenvironment through distinct pathways, although systematic evaluation of multiple B7‐H family members within the same ccRCC patient cohort remains limited. This underscores the importance of an integrated analysis of B7‐H family checkpoints in metastatic ccRCC. In this study, we performed immunohistochemical (IHC) analysis of B7‐H3, B7‐H4, B7‐H5, and B7‐H7 in a well‐characterized metastatic ccRCC cohort. We explored associations with tumor aggressiveness, clinical risk classification, therapeutic response, and multiple survival outcomes to assess the prognostic and predictive significance of these immune checkpoints in advanced ccRCC.

Materials and methods

Ethics statement and patient consent

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, with informed consent from all subjects involved in the study. Samples were sourced from the Basque Biobank (www.biobancovasco.org), where patient data was made available for research use. The project was previously approved by the Ethical and Scientific Committees of the Osakidetza Basque Country Public Health System (PI2015059X, PI2022116, and CEI E25‐38).

Patient cohort

This study included patients diagnosed with metastatic ccRCC defined as either synchronous metastasis at diagnosis or metachronous metastases occurring during follow‐up. Patient selection and clinical characterization followed the protocols described in our previous studies [1, 35, 36]. Briefly, the cohort consisted of primary tumor specimens from 145 patients, including 59 patients with synchronous metastases and 86 patients with metachronous metastases, treated at Cruces and Donostia University Hospitals (Basque Country, Spain) between 2008 and 2018. All patients had histologically confirmed ccRCC and underwent radical nephrectomy. Comprehensive clinical data, including age at diagnosis, sex, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status, International Metastatic RCC Database Consortium (IMDC) risk classification, and treatment history, including TKI, mTOR inhibitors, and ICI, were collected from institutional electronic medical records. All patients received at least 1 cycle (4 weeks) of VEGFR‐TKI (sunitinib, pazopanib, or sorafenib) until progression or unresponsiveness.

Clinical variables

Patient functional status was evaluated using the ECOG performance scale, and prognostic risk was assessed according to the IMDC criteria. Tumor aggressiveness was classified based on grade, pT stage, lymph node involvement, metastasis pattern, and tumor size. Treatment response was assessed using the MASS score and RECIST 1.1 criteria, as previously described [1, 35]. Survival outcomes analyzed included OS (time from diagnosis to death from any cause) and disease‐free survival (DFS; duration of remission after initial curative surgery). DFS was evaluated in patients without distant metastasis at diagnosis (n = 86).

Histopathological evaluation

Tumor histology was reviewed by experienced uropathologists on whole tissue sections from primary tumor specimens. Well‐preserved tissue representative of the whole primary tumor from formalin‐fixed paraffin‐embedded (FFPE) tissue blocks was selected to make tissue microarray (TMA) blocks. TMAs included two independent TMA cores per patient. The 4 μm sections were made from the TMA blocks, one of which was stained with hematoxylin and eosin to verify the presence of tumor and immune content. Tumor grade, pT stage, lymph node involvement, and other pathological features were assessed according to the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) and the Union for International Cancer Control Tumor, Node, Metastasis 8th edition criteria. Tumor size was recorded as the largest diameter in centimeters. Histological grade was classified according to Fuhrman/International Society of Urological Pathology grading. Pathological features were further correlated with biomarker expression and clinical outcomes.

Immunohistochemistry

IHC analysis of B7‐H3, B7‐H4, B7‐H5, B7‐H7, and PD‐L1 was performed on formalin‐fixed paraffin‐embedded primary tumor specimens arranged in TMA. Methods followed previously published protocols [14, 20, 37]. The 4 μm sections were deparaffinized in xylene and rehydrated through graded alcohols. Antigen retrieval was performed using PT‐link with low or high pH, as optimized for each antibody. Primary antibodies against B7‐H3 (R&D Systems, Minneapolis, MN, USA; AF1027, dilution 1:200), B7‐H4 (R&D Systems; MAB65764, dilution 1:20), B7‐H5 (Cell Signaling, Danvers, MA, USA; D1L2G, 64953, dilution 1:100), and B7‐H7 (Novus Biologicals, Centennial, CO, USA; NBP2‐49187, dilution 1:500) were used according to standard IHC protocols. Detection was performed using the detection system EnVision+ HRP (Agilent Dako, Carpinteria, CA, USA) with diaminobenzidine (DAB) as the chromogen. Immunostaining of PD‐L1 (SP‐142, prediluted, Ventana, Roche, Basel, Switzerland) was performed in BenchMark Ultra (Ventana, Roche) immunostainer following routine protocols and specific instructions of the manufacturer. Sections were counterstained with hematoxylin. Expression was evaluated separately in tumor cells, stromal compartments, and TILs. Scoring was performed using a combined assessment of staining intensity low/no or high in tumor and tumor‐associated stromal cells, reflecting equivalent semi‐quantitative intensity but not identical biological prevalence across different B7‐H proteins and anti‐B7‐H antibodies. Percentage of positive TILs were determined based on prior analysis [14, 20]; immunostaining for the TILs: low/no: <1% positive cells and high: >1% positive cells. IHC evaluations were performed by experienced uropathologist (JIL) in a blind manner.

Statistical analyses

Categorical variables were analyzed using the chi‐square test. Continuous variables were assessed with Spearman correlation. The online web server InteractiVenn https://www.interactivenn.net (31 March 2026) was used to generate the Venn diagram [38]. Survival analyses were performed using the Kaplan–Meier method with log‐rank tests to assess the correlation between B7‐H and OS/DFS. Cox proportional hazards univariate and multivariate regression models were used to determine the independent prognostic value of the B7‐H and the clinically relevant covariates (age, sex, ECOG, IMDC risk, and pathological stage). For the multivariate models, an entry criterion of p < 0.050 and a stay value of p < 0.200 were considered, Significance was defined as p < 0.050. All analyses were performed using SPSS v28 (IBM, Chicago, IL, USA) and R v4.2 (R Foundation, Vienna, Austria).

Results

Clinicopathological associations of B7‐H family members

IHC analysis of primary tumor specimens from patients with synchronous or metachronous metastatic ccRCC demonstrated distinct and compartment‐specific expression patterns for B7‐H family immune checkpoints (Figure 1). B7‐H3 exhibited variable membranous and cytoplasmic staining in tumor cells and the stromal compartment, including perivascular and fibrovascular structures. B7‐H4 staining was restricted to tumor cells, while B7‐H5 expression was observed in both tumor cells and TILs, with cases displaying heterogeneous combinations of tumor and immune cell positivity. B7‐H7 was expressed in the TILs of all cases, whereas tumoral expression was only observed in a subset of cases. Representative images illustrating the spectrum of staining patterns across compartments for each marker are shown in Figure 1. High B7‐H3 expression was detected in tumor cells of 35% of samples, whereas 58% showed high expression in the stromal compartment. B7‐H4 and B7‐H5 showed high expression in tumor cells in 25% of cases, and 23% of the samples were positive for B7‐H5 expression in TILs. A total of 56% of patients showed high B7‐H7 expression in tumor cells. A total of 60% of cases were positive for PD‐L1 expression in TILs (Table 1). We did not detect any associations between the overall abundance of TILs and the expression patterns of B7‐H markers. Co‐expression analysis demonstrated limited overlaps among B7‐H family members, with statistically significant correlations confined to B7‐H3 and B7‐H7 in tumor cells, B7‐H4 in tumor cells and B7‐H5 in TILs, and B7‐H4 and B7‐H7 in tumor cells, as illustrated in the Venn diagram (Figure 2A). We did not detect any overlap in expression between B7‐H proteins and PD‐L1 positivity in TILs (Table 1, and Figure 2B). These findings highlight marked intratumoral heterogeneity and spatially distinct immune checkpoint localization within the ccRCC microenvironment, supporting compartment‐specific scoring in subsequent clinicopathological and survival analyses.

High tumor expression of B7‐H3, B7‐H4, and B7‐H5 was significantly associated with high histological grade (Table 1). High tumor expression of B7‐H3 was significantly associated with lymph node invasion and with advanced NCCN 2010 stage (III–IV), and high tumor expression of B7‐H3 and B7‐H7 was associated with advanced local invasion (non‐organ confined tumors, higher pT stage). Sex‐based differences were observed, with high stromal expression of B7‐H3 and high tumor expression of B7‐H7 in male patients compared with females. There were no associations between the expression levels of any of the B7‐H proteins and tumor size, synchronous versus metachronous, or number of distant metastases (Table 1).

Association with clinical risk stratification and treatment response

High expression of B7‐H5 in TILs and B7‐H7 in tumor cells was associated with impaired functional status as assessed by the ECOG performance scale. Tumors from patients classified into the intermediate or poor risk groups according to the IMDC criteria showed significantly high tumor expression of B7‐H3 compared with those from patients with favorable prognosis (Table 2, Figure 3). Using dichotomized MASS score groupings, high stromal expression of B7‐H3 was associated with unfavorable response to first‐line TKI. Among patients receiving second‐line therapy (n = 87), high stromal B7‐H3 expression and high B7‐H5 expression in TILs were associated with unfavorable response. In patients treated with mTOR inhibitors (n = 23), stromal B7‐H3 expression remained significantly associated with unfavorable outcome. An exploratory analysis was also made on the smaller patient groups receiving ICI. Upon ICI second‐line therapy treatment (n = 10), unfavorable response was associated with positive tumor expression of B7‐H3. In the third‐line setting with ICI (n = 11), negative tumor expression of B7‐H5 was associated with favorable response. Using RECIST‐based dichotomizations, low tumor expression of B7‐H7 was associated with complete response to first‐line TKI therapy. In second‐line treatment (n = 87), high stromal expression of B7‐H3 and high B7‐H5 expression in TILs were associated with disease progression. These associations were maintained in patients with high stromal expression of B7‐H3 treated with mTOR inhibitors and in those receiving ICI, in whom positive tumor expression of B7‐H3 was associated with progression (Table 2, Figure 3).

Predictive association with OS

OS analyses were conducted across follow‐up intervals spanning from 1 to 15 years, as presented in Table 3. High tumor expression of B7‐H3 was associated with reduced OS from the third year of follow‐up onward (Table 3). A representative Kaplan–Meier curve correlating B7‐H3 expression in tumor cells with 15‐year OS is shown in Figure 4A, and additional 5‐ and 10‐year OS curves are shown in supplementary material, Figure S1A,B. High B7‐H3 stromal expression was associated with worse OS beginning at year 8 (Table 3), as shown with a representative Kaplan–Meier curve for 15‐year OS (Figure 4B), and a supplementary OS curve for 10‐year follow‐up (supplementary material, Figure S1C). B7‐H5 positivity in TILs significantly correlated with poorer OS from year 4 (Table 3), and is represented with a Kaplan–Meier curve for 5‐year OS (Figure 4C). This trend persists across the follow‐up years, as shown in supplementary material, Figure S1D. High B7‐H7 expression in tumor cells showed a similar trend in its association with OS, even though the log‐rank p value did not achieve statistical significance (Figure 4D).

Predictive association with DFS

DFS was analyzed across follow‐up intervals spanning 1–15 years (Table 3). High tumor expression of B7‐H3 was consistently associated with shorter DFS across the entire follow‐up period (Table 3). A representative Kaplan–Meier curve for 15‐year DFS is shown in Figure 4E, and additional curves for 5‐ and 10‐year DFS are included in supplementary material, Figure S1E,F. High B7‐H3 stromal expression showed a similar association pattern and reached statistical significance at 15 years of follow‐up (Table 3), visualized by the Kaplan–Meier curve in Figure 4F.

Cox univariate analysis was performed at 15‐year DFS (Table 4), and also at 5‐ and 10‐year DFS (supplementary material, Tables S1 and S2). Treatment response variables (MASS and RECIST) were omitted, as these patients remained treatment‐naïve until metastatic progression. Patient functional status and prognosis scales were also excluded, since the latter parameters were measured at the time of recurrence and systemic therapy initiation. Age, grade, stage, and B7‐H3 tumor and stromal expression were significantly associated with DFS in the univariate Cox analysis and subsequently included in multivariate models. Multivariate Cox regression models were conducted including the statistically significant pathological variables and B7‐H3 expression in tumor or stromal cells. Using the Wald stepwise elimination approach, tumor and stromal B7‐H3 expression emerged as an independent prognostic factor for 15‐year DFS, together with age, grade, and stage variables (Table 4). Performing the same analyses using 5‐ and 10‐year DFS demonstrated that B7‐H3 expression in tumor cells independently correlated with DFS at both time points. B7‐H3 stromal expression also emerged as an independent biomarker of 10‐year DFS (supplementary material, Tables S1 and S2).

Discussion

In this study, we provide a comprehensive IHC characterization of B7‐H3, B7‐H4, B7‐H5, and B7‐H7 expression in primary tumors from patients with metastatic ccRCC, integrating pathological features, clinical risk stratification, treatment response, and long‐term survival outcomes. Our IHC analyses reveal distinct compartmentalized expression patterns across the B7‐H family in metastatic ccRCC, which align with different prognostic indicators, as summarized in Figure 5. The absence of associations between PD‐L1 positivity and other B7‐H markers supports the concept that immune suppression in ccRCC is mediated by multiple, potentially independent checkpoint pathways beyond the canonical PD‐1/PD‐L1 axis.

Summary of clinicopathological, therapeutic, and prognostic associations of B7‐H proteins in ccRCC. Schematic overview of the associations between B7‐H3, B7‐H4, B7‐H5, and B7‐H7 expression and key clinicopathological parameters, treatment response, and survival outcomes. Checkmarks indicate statistically significant associations (p < 0.050). B7‐H3 expression in tumor cells and stroma showed the broadest associations and was identified as an independent prognostic factor. B7‐H5 expression in tumor‐infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) was associated with treatment response and overall survival and also emerged as an independent predictor. B7‐H4 and B7‐H7 expression in tumor cells were associated with selected adverse clinicopathological features. Treatment response 1, 2, and 3 indicate first‐, second‐, and third‐line of treatment. Asterisks denote statistically significant independent predictors of overall survival and disease‐free survival at 5, 10, and 15 years.

B7‐H3 was broadly expressed in both tumor cells and stromal compartments, consistent with its pleiotropic role in shaping immune exclusion, angiogenesis, and tumor‐microenvironment interactions [8, 9, 10, 12, 14]. Stromal localization may reflect expression by endothelial cells, fibroblasts, and myeloid populations, potentially contributing to physical and immunologic barriers that limit effective antitumor immunity and therapeutic penetration. This dual localization provides a mechanistic basis for the strong and consistent association of B7‐H3 with aggressive pathological features, treatment resistance, and adverse long‐term outcomes observed in our cohort. Our data identify B7‐H3 as the dominant biomarker across multiple clinically relevant endpoints, with consistent associations with tumor aggressiveness, unfavorable therapeutic response, and reduced DFS and OS. An early study demonstrated that B7‐H3 expression in tumor vasculature and tumor cells was associated with adverse pathological features and reduced survival in ccRCC [12]. More recently, our group demonstrated, using two independent cohorts, that B7‐H3 expression correlates with immune exhaustion signatures, activation of oncogenic signaling pathways, and worse clinical outcomes in ccRCC [14]. The present study extends these observations by showing that B7‐H3 expression independently predicts DFS and OS over long‐term follow‐up. The consistent prognostic value across multiple endpoints supports B7‐H3 as a robust biomarker reflecting aggressive tumor biology and microenvironmental remodeling. High stromal or tumor expression of B7‐H3 was associated with unfavorable response and disease progression across multiple treatment lines, including TKI, mTOR inhibitors, and ICI. These observations are consistent with findings on B7‐H3 and sensitivity to mTOR inhibitors in breast cancer [39], as well as with the concept that B7‐H3 contributes to immune evasion and treatment resistance mechanisms, potentially through modulation of immune cell function and stromal interactions [14]. Recent mechanistic work demonstrates that mTORC1 signaling directly upregulates B7‐H3 expression via phosphorylation of transcriptional regulators, leading to suppression of cytotoxic T‐cell activity and immune evasion in multiple human tumors [40]. It has also been proposed that mTOR signaling increases B7‐H3 expression in renal cancer [41]. Androgen receptor signaling is also a driver of B7‐H3 expression in several cancers, such as melanoma and prostate cancer [42, 43, 44]. Whether B7‐H3 expression is regulated by androgen receptor in renal cancer deserves further studies.

B7‐H5 exhibited a mixed pattern, with expression detectable in both tumor cells and TILs. Notably, its association with impaired performance status and reduced OS when expressed in TILs supports a model in which B7‐H5 contributes to immune cell dysfunction, exhaustion, or suppression within the tumor microenvironment, rather than acting solely as a tumor‐intrinsic marker. These findings align with our recent report demonstrating in an independent cohort that B7‐H5 is expressed in metastatic ccRCC and that its expression in TILs correlates with aggressive disease features and unfavorable clinical outcomes [20]. This immune‐compartment specific involvement highlights the importance of dissecting cellular context when interpreting immune checkpoint biomarkers.

B7‐H4 expression was restricted to tumor cells, supporting its role as a tumor‐autonomous immune evasion molecule. Tumor‐restricted expression implies direct regulation by oncogenic signaling pathways and microenvironmental cues intrinsic to malignant cells, consistent with experimental evidence linking B7‐H4 to cancer cell viability and resistance to targeted therapies [29]. This expression pattern positions B7‐H4 as a candidate biomarker of intrinsic immune escape programs. On the other hand, B7‐H7 displayed dual expression in tumor cells and TILs, suggesting a bidirectional role in tumor‐immune communication. Such duality parallels the complex biology observed for other immune checkpoints and supports the concept that B7‐H7 may participate in dynamic feedback circuits within the tumor microenvironment. B7‐H4 and B7‐H7 displayed more limited clinical associations, primarily restricted to pathological aggressiveness and selected clinical features. Although these proteins have been implicated in immune modulation in other malignancies, their prognostic and predictive relevance in ccRCC appears less pronounced in our cohort. The differential clinical impact among B7‐H family members highlights the biological heterogeneity of immune checkpoint pathways and suggests that not all B7‐H molecules carry equivalent clinical relevance in renal cancer.

Some limitations to consider in this study include the retrospective design, single‐center cohort, and relatively small sample sizes in certain treatment subgroups, particularly for the exploratory ICI‐treated patients in later lines of therapy. These factors limit statistical power for subgroup analyses and warrant validation in larger independent cohorts. Collectively, the studied spatial patterns underscore that the biological and clinical impact of B7‐H checkpoints cannot be fully captured by bulk expression alone. Cellular localization and microenvironmental context appear to critically shape their functional relevance and prognostic value. Although IHC provides spatially resolved expression data, it lacks the resolution required to fully characterize cellular heterogeneity and dynamic cell–cell interactions within the tumor microenvironment. In addition, the TMA format limits the spatial resolution of our analysis. Future studies integrating high‐resolution spatial profiling approaches, including multiplex immunofluorescence, imaging mass cytometry, spatial transcriptomics, and single‐cell RNA sequencing with spatial mapping, will be essential to define the precise cellular sources of each B7‐H family member, their spatial relationships with effector and suppressive immune populations, and their coordinated regulation at single‐cell resolution. Additional functional mechanistic studies will be required to establish causal links between B7‐H signaling, immune escape, and therapeutic resistance, and to determine how these pathways may be exploited for rational biomarker‐driven combination immunotherapy strategies in metastatic ccRCC.

From a translational perspective, the consistent prognostic performance of B7‐H3 across pathological, clinical, and survival endpoints supports its potential utility as a biomarker for risk stratification and possibly patient selection for emerging B7‐H3‐targeted therapies. In this context, it has been proposed the incorporation of B7‐H3 expression into a novel prognostic scoring system for renal cell carcinoma [45]. Several antibody‐drug conjugates and bispecific constructs targeting B7‐H3 are currently in clinical development [7], underscoring the relevance of identifying patient populations most likely to benefit from these approaches. Moreover, given the association of B7‐H3 with resistance to TKI, mTOR inhibitors, and ICI, its expression may contribute to treatment selection and support patient stratification in future biomarker‐driven studies. Likewise, B7‐H5 may represent a complementary immune target, particularly in patients with immunologically suppressive tumor microenvironments, as the emerging data reinforce the role of B7‐H5 in shaping the tumor immune microenvironment and potentially contributing to resistance to systemic therapies. Together, our findings highlight that B7‐H3 and B7‐H5 may contribute to risk stratification in ccRCC, biomarker‐guided selection for targeted therapy and immunotherapy combinations, and patient stratification in future prospective clinical trials.

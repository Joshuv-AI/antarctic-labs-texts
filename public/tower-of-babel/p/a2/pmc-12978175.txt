Group-based trajectory modeling for declines in hepatitis B surface antigen over time accurately identifies chronic hepatitis B patients achieving functional cure: a single-center cohort study in China
Authors: Yuchen Peng, Liping Liu, Xiaoping Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIdentifying chronic hepatitis B (CHB) patients likely to achieve hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) sero-clearance upon receiving pegylated interferon α (PEG-IFNα) could aid in optimizing treatment strategies for “functional cure”.AimsTo develop a group-based trajectory model (GBTM) for identifying PEG-IFNα-treated CHB patients likely to achieve HBsAg sero-clearance.MethodsBaseline characteristics were obtained from 342 PEG-IFNα-treated CHB patients, divided into training (205), validation (88), and testing (49) datasets via time series partitioning and random partitioning methods. GBTM was applied to the training dataset to identify the optimal trajectory groups for HBsAg levels over the follow-up period. Their accuracies for HBsAg sero-clearence were evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) and calibration curves with 1000 bootstrap samples. Clinical utility was examined using decision curve analysis (DCA), and the predictive GBTM-based 3-group trajectory model was incorporated into a website.Results131 patients (38.30%) were functionally cured, and GBTM identified 3 trajectory groups, associated with rapid (traj_1), moderate (traj_2), and slow (traj_3) HBsAg declines. Traj_3 had the highest viral burden, plus lowered liver functions. The GBTM-based 3-group trajectory model was highly accurate under ROC, with areas under the curve >0.920, while calibration curves had high predicted-actual outcome correspondence, for all 3 datasets. Clinical utility was also high under DCA, and the website correctly found the trajectory group of a CHB patient, based on HBsAg from ≥2 different time points.ConclusionsThe GBTM-based 3-group trajectory model correctly identified PEG-IFNα-treated CHB patients likely to be “functionally cured”, thereby providing a tool for devising personalized therapeutic strategies.Keywords: Chronic hepatitis B, hepatitis B surface antigen, pegylated interferon α, group-based trajectory modeling, hepatitis B surface antigen sero-clearance, functional cure

Introduction

Completely curing chronic hepatitis B (CHB), defined as the full eradication of covalently closed circular (cccDNA) and integrated hepatitis B viral (HBV) DNAs, is currently considered an unattainable endpoint [1]. Nevertheless, declining serum hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) levels generally reflect enhanced host immunity and effective suppression of HBV replication, and are thus closely linked to improvements in liver function, histopathology, and long-term CHB outcomes. As a result, HBsAg could serve as a marker for monitoring disease progression [2,3]. Moreover, functional cure is considered to be an achievable endpoint, leading to it being recommended in multiple guidelines as an optimal therapeutic goal [4–8]. Current first-line antiviral therapies include nucleos(t)ide analogues (NAs) and pegylated interferon α (PEG-IFNα). NAs have been found to effectively suppress viral replication, but HBsAg level decreases are slow, and complete sero-clearance is difficult to achieve [9,10]. On the other hand, PEG-IFNα achieves HBsAg sero-clearance of 20–35% at 48 weeks, of which a subset of those sero-cleared patients become functionally cured [11–13]. However, given its relatively high cost, and it requiring to be injected, compared to NAs being able to be administered orally, it is key to effectively identify CHB patients who would best benefit from PEG-IFNα treatment. Such early identification, though, remains a significant clinical challenge. Substantial efforts have been made to identify predictors, primarily focusing on static factors like baseline HBsAg levels, HBV genotype, and on-treatment HBsAg thresholds at fixed time points [14]. While foundational, these approaches may not fully capture the individual dynamic patterns of treatment response.

In this study, we aimed to address this challenge via examining CHB patients who received PEG-IFNα therapy. We found, via applying group-based trajectory modeling (GBTM), that these patients could be divided into 3 distinct sub-categories, based on whether they had a rapid, moderate, or slow decline in HBsAg levels. The sub-category with the fastest HBsAg decrease also had the lowest HBsAg levels and hepatitis B e-antigen (HBeAg) positivity rate, indicating significant HBsAg sero-clearance; the converse was the case for the sub-category with the slowest HBsAg decline. Based on these patterns, a predictive GBTM-based 3-group trajectory model was established, which accurately identified CHB patients most likely to achieve HBsAg sero-clearence, and be “functionally cured”, post-PEG-IFNα treatment.

Materials and methods

Study population

A total of 1,017 CHB patients who were treated and managed at the outpatient clinic of the First Affiliated Hospital of Nanchang University between June 30, 2021 and August 31, 2023, were enrolled in this retrospective study. The following inclusion and exclusion criteria were applied, in which the inclusion criteria were: 1) meeting the CHB diagnostic criteria, outlined in the relevant clinical guidelines [5], 2) being 18–60 years, 3) being eligible for and voluntarily undergoing PEG-IFNα therapy, in accordance with the guidelines, and 4) having satisfactory treatment adherence, with follow-up visits every 12 weeks, for >2 years. Exclusion criteria were: 1) co-infection with other hepatitis viruses, human immunodeficiency virus, Epstein–Barr virus, or other pathogens, 2) autoimmune diseases, malignancies, or severe underlying conditions, or 3) end-stage liver disease being present. After applying inclusion and exclusion criteria, 342 patients were included in follow-up analyses, which were divided into basic (293) and testing (49) cohorts, on February 28, 2023. The basic cohort, in turn, was randomly divided, in a 7:3 ratio, into training (205) and validation (88) datasets (Figure 1). The protocol was performed in accordance with the guidelines outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. Owing to the study being retrospective, and all patients being anonymized, the requirement for written informed consent was waived by the Ethics Committee of The First Affiliated Hospital of Nanchang University: IIT (2025) Clinical Ethics Approval No. 818.

Flowchart of chronic hepatitis B (CHB) patient recruitment, and division into training, validation, and testing datasets. CHB patients were treated with pegylated interferon α (PEG-IFNα), while those treated with nucleos(t)ide analogues (NAs) were excluded.

Data collection and definitions for being “functionally cured”

Patient demographic information, including sex and age, as well as relevant medical history, such as prior antiviral treatment for hepatitis B, liver cancer or cirrhosis history, etc., were collected for all enrolled patients. Clinical data, including HBV serological markers, liver biochemical parameters, complete blood count, plus liver ultrasounds and stiffness, were obtained at baseline, before starting PEG-IFNα (Week 0), as well as every 12 weeks, post-PEG-IFNα treatment, in the follow-up period. HBV DNA quantification was performed using TaqMan real-time fluorescent PCR on the COBAS 4800 system (Roche, USA), with a lower limit of detection of 20 IU/mL. Serum HBV markers were measured using the Abbott ARCHITECT system (Abbott Laboratories, USA), of which HBeAg positive was classified as those with HBeAg > 0.18 Paul-Ehrlich Institute (PEI) U/mL. Additionally, a patient was considered to be “functionally cured” if they had sustained HBsAg sero-clearance (with or without antibodies to HBsAg (HBsAb)) for ≥24 weeks after stopping antiviral treatments, during which time HBV DNA remained below the lower limit of detection (LLOD) [4].

Application of GBTM to identify the optimal number of trajectory groups

Given the heterogeneous treatment response to PEG-IFNα among patients with CHB, GBTM was employed to identify distinct trajectories of the index during follow-up and to investigate their associations with clinical outcomes [15]. This approach enabled the categorization of the heterogeneous population into multiple distinct subgroups based on their response trajectories. Therefore, we applied GBTM implemented through the “traj” plugin procedure in Stata version 17.0 (StataCorp LLC) to identify distinct trajectories of HBsAg decline. The selection of the optimal number of trajectory groups was based on the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC), average posterior probability (AvePP), minimum group proportion (≥ 5%), and odds of correct classification (OCC). A lower BIC indicates better model fit, AvePP > 0.7 suggests good classification accuracy, and OCC > 5 reflects strong discriminative power of the model [16]. The final trajectory grouping was determined by a comprehensive evaluation of statistical criteria, clinical plausibility, and interpretability, aiming to accurately characterize the dynamic patterns of the HBsAg level in CHB patients who receive PEG-IFN therapy during follow-up. For all GBTM analyses and trajectory plots, Stata and R softwares were used.

Statistical analysis

All analyses were performed using Stata(v18.0), R (v4.3.1) and SPSS (v26.0; IBM). The Shapiro-Wilk test was used to test the normality of the quantitative data. Normally distributed data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD), and data without normal distributions are presented as median (interquartile range [IQR]). Comparisons between 3 or more groups were conducted using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), followed by post-hoc pairwise comparisons with either Tukey’s honestly significant difference (HSD) test for variables that met the assumption of homogeneity, or Tamhane’s T2 test for variables that did not meet that assumption. All statistical tests were 2-sided, and p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

PEG-IFNα-treated CHB patients had decreasing HBsAg over the follow-up period and some were functionally cured

From the 342 CHB patients, 3,178 longitudinal HBsAg measurements were collected, and their levels generally declined over the follow-up period (Figure 2(A)). This HBsAg decline was significantly faster among the 131 CHB patients (38.30% of all patients) who were functionally cured (FC), compared to those who were not (NFC), in the first 24 weeks of follow-up (Figure 2(B)). More specifically, the cumulative HBsAg sero-clearance rate was 23.98% (95% confidence interval [CI] = 19.31%–28.40%) at 48 weeks, and 36.13% (95% CI = 30.81%–41.04%) at 96 weeks (Figure 2(C)). Ninety-six weeks was also the median follow-up duration for the enrolled patients (IQR 96–97).

Patient stratification based on different trajectory groups for follow-up HBsAg levels

To identify the optimal number of trajectory groups for HBsAg levels over the follow-up period, post-PEG-IFNα treatment, GBTM was performed on the training set. We then examined BIC, Akaike information criterion (AIC), and entropy values, and found that the optimal number of trajectory groups was 3 (Figure 3 and 4(A)). Furthermore, BIC and AIC values from the validation set identified that the optimal polynomial orders for the 3-group trajectory model were 3, 2, and 1, as they were the highest (Figure 4(B)). Therefore, Model 3 was selected as the basis for PEG-IFNα-treated CHB patient stratification (Figure 4(C)), and it was able to yield similar patient subdivisions into 3 distinct trajectory groups (traj_1, _2, _3). Each patient in the training, validation, and test cohorts was assigned to a trajectory group based on the maximum posterior probability. The proportions of patients in each trajectory group were similar across the three cohorts (Figure 4(D); Table S1).

In terms of HBsAg changes over the follow-up period, we observed that traj_1 had a rapid decrease in HBsAg, traj_2 had a moderate decline, and traj_3 a slow decline, post-PEG-IFNα treatment (Figure 4(E)). This corresponded to traj_1 having a steady increase in cumulative HBsAg sero-clearance over time, compared to traj_2 having a delayed increase from 72 weeks onward, and traj_3 not having any HBsAg sero-clearance (Figure 4(F)).

Baseline characteristics across datasets and trajectory groups

We then examined the baseline characteristics of the PEG-IFNα-treated CHB patients, no significant differences were observed among the training, validation, and test cohorts. However, within the training dataset, significant differences were present among traj_1, _2, and _3, with respect to HBsAg levels, which were the highest in traj_3, followed by traj_2, and traj_1 having the lowest. The opposite was the case for HBsAb, which was the highest in traj_1, and lowest in traj_3. Traj_3 also having the highest HBeAg positivity rate, versus that of traj_1 and traj_2. While no significant difference in HBV DNA levels was found between traj_1 and traj_2, both traj_1 and traj_2 had lower HBV DNA levels than traj_3. Notably, patients in traj_1 showed lower levels of liver biochemical markers than those in traj_3 (Table 1). Moreover, analysing patients with ALT > 1 × upper limit normal (ULN) demonstrated that the proportion of patients who also had HBV DNA > 1 × 107 IU/mL, as well as HBsAg > 1 × 105 IU/mL, indicating high HBV loads, were significantly higher in traj_3, at 51.52 and 90.31% respectively, compared to 20.00 and 30.00% for traj_1 (Table S2).

Trajectory identification by GBTM was able to accurately predict HBsAg sero-clearance with high clinical utility

We then assessed the accuracy of GBTM for identifying the presence of 3 trajectory groups, via the evaluation metrics of AvePP, OCC, posterior probability of group membership (Pj), and estimated group probability (πj). All these metrics indicated that GBTM was highly accurate, as AvePP > 70%, and OCC > 5, for all 3 groups. Furthermore, Pj and πj values for those 3 groups were close to each other (Table S3). We then examined whether this 3-group trajectory model was able to correctly predict HBsAg sero-clearance post-PEG-IFNα treatment, using ROC curve analysis. There, the areas under the curve (AUC) for training, validation, and testing datasets were 0.963, 0.968, and 0.925, respectively (Figure 5(A–C)). Similarly, calibration curves found that for all 3 datasets, a high degree of correspondence between predicted and observed probabilities was present (Figure 5(D–F)).

Lastly, DCA indicated that the GBTM-based 3-group trajectory model, for all 3 datasets, had great clinical utility, compared to assuming that all or none of the patients would achieve HBsAg sero-clearance (Figure 5(G–I)). Indeed, this predictive model was incorporated as part of the following website we established: https://986305459-web.github.io/. As shown in Figure 6, the website was able to identify whether a CHB patient was part of the rapid, moderate, or slow decline group, based on their HBsAg levels from at least 2 different time points.

Discussion

The achievement of being “functionally cured”, in the form of sustained HBsAg sero-clearance and undetectable HBV DNA, has been considered, in multiple guidelines, as the primary therapeutic goal for CHB patients [6–8]. Consequently, identifying reliable HBsAg sero-clearance during the course of PEG-IFNα treatment, and being able to stratify patients accordingly, is clinically significant for optimizing individualized treatment strategies toward functional cure. Previous studies have mainly focused on baseline or limited time-point characteristics to predict HBsAg sero-clearance [13,17], which overlooked that multiple factors have been found to be involved in shaping HBsAg alterations during treatment [18], yielding heterogeneity in CHB patient responses to PEG-IFNα. In light of this patient heterogeneity, GBTM was utilized in this study to identify 3 distinct PEG-IFNα response patterns in CHB patients, characterized by rapid, moderate, or slow declines in HBsAg levels. This 3-group trajectory model allowed us to identify, with a high degree of accuracy, patients with un- and favourable responses to PEG-IFNα treatment, and subsequently predict those who would likely achieve functional cure, enabling timely adjustment of treatment regimens.

Based on the training cohort, we constructed a GBTM, which identified three distinct patient subgroups during PEG-IFNα therapy: rapid decline, moderate response, and slow response trajectories. Firstly, patients in the rapid response group had significantly lower baseline HBsAg and HBV DNA levels and lower HBeAg positivity rates consistent with previous findings [19]. We then examined baseline liver biochemical markers among the 3 groups, as higher levels have been linked to better PEG-IFNα responses [20,21]. Our discoveries, though, contradict that of Peng et al. and Hui et al. [20,21], as baseline ALT was found to be the highest in traj_3, associated with slow HBsAg decline, compared to traj_1 and _2, though these differences were not statistically significant. Regardless, at ALT > 1 × ULN, >50% of traj_3 patients had high HBV loads, and >90% had HBsAg > 1500 IU/mL. This discrepancy with Peng et al. and Hui et al. [20,21] may be due to higher HBsAg and HBeAg viral proteins inducing immunosuppressive effects on host immune system [22], likely stemming from PD-1 up-regulation on circulating CD4+T lymphocytes, leading to weakened T-cell reactions, and ultimately, slower, suboptimal responses to PEG-IFNα [23].

We also noted that on the graph of HBsAg levels for FC and NFC patients, post-PEG-IFNα treatment, over the follow-up period, a steeper HBsAg decline was present over the first 24 weeks for FC, compared to NFC. Furthermore, traj_1 had the most rapid decline during that period, compared to the other 2 trajectory groups. These observations were in line with previous studies indicating that alterations in HBsAg levels at 12–24 weeks were predictive of HBsAg sero-clearance [24,25], as well as those illustrating that average HBsAg decreases, from baseline levels to that at 24 weeks could serve as an early HBsAg sero-clearance marker, post-PEG-IFN therapy [26,27]. All these findings therefore indicate a close association between early responses to PEG-IFNα and HBsAg sero-clearance, in which those with marked HBsAg decreases are more likely to become “functionally cured”, while those with slower declines were less likely to achieve that state. Based on this pattern, CHB patients whose HBsAg decreases are sub-optimal should promptly undergo reassessments of their treatment strategies.

In order to examine the generalizability of this GBTM-based 3-group trajectory model, PEG-IFNα-treated CHB patients were randomly divided into training, validation, and testing datasets, in which it accurately identified those likely to achieve HBsAg sero-clearance. We then translated the complex trajectory and probability calculations into a user-friendly website, which could lower computational burdens, enhance clinical applicability, and provide real-time, quantitative support for individualized decision-making. However, several limitations should be noted. Although the test cohort data have a prospective cohort nature, this work is fundamentally a retrospective cohort study. In addition, along with the relatively small patient sample size originating from a single centre, means that the results may not be fully representative of all CHB patients. Therefore, additional studies, involving prospective cohorts, from multiple centres, should be conducted to further verify the generalizability of this predictive GBTM-based 3-group trajectory model.

In conclusion, we applied GBTM to CHB patients treated with PEG-IFNα, to develop a predictive model for HBsAg sero-clearance. Under GBTM, the CHB patients were able to be stratified into 3 distinct trajectory groups, traj_1, _2, and _3, based on whether HBsAg levels had rapid, moderate, or slow declines, respectively. Traj_3, compared to Traj_1 and _2, had the highest HBsAg, HBeAg, and HBV DNA levels, indicating a high viral burden; it also had the lowest HBsAb, along with low albumin, all of which indicated decreased immune reactions and liver function. These 3 trajectory groups comprised the predictive model, which was highly accurate and had high clinical utility for detecting PEG-IFNα-treated CHB patients most likely to achieve HBsAg sero-clearance. The model was incorporated into a website, https://986305459-web.github.io/, which used HBsAg levels from at least 2 different time points to identify whether a CHB patient was part of the rapid, moderate, or slow HBsAg decline group. Therefore, it could serve as a potentially valuable tool for personalized clinical management of CHB.

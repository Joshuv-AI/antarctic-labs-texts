Screening for moderate to severe obstructive sleep apnea by using heart rate variability features based on random forest algorithm
Authors: Chenxu Zhang, Liangcai Yu, Lin Li, Ping Zeng, Xiaoqing Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeMore than 80% of patients with moderate to severe obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) are still not diagnosed timely. The prediction model based on random forest (RF) algorithm was established by using heart rate variability (HRV), clinical and demographic features so as to screen for the patients with high risk of moderate and severe obstructive sleep apnea.MethodsThe sleep monitoring data of 798 patients were randomly divided into training set (n = 558) and test set (n = 240) in 7:3 proportion. Grid search was applied to determine the best parameters of the RF model. 10-fold cross validation was utilized to evaluate the predictive performance of the RF model, which was then compared to the performance of the Logistic regression model.ResultsAmong the 798 patients, 638 were males and 160 were females, with the average age of 43.51 years old and the mean body mass index (BMI) of 25.92 kg/m2. The sensitivity, specificity, accuracy, F1 score and the area under receiver operating characteristic curve for RF model and Logistic regression model were 94.68% vs. 73.94%; 73.08% vs. 86.54%; 90.00% vs. 76.67%; 0.94 vs. 0.83 and 0.83 vs. 0.80 respectively.ConclusionsThe RF prediction model can effectively distinguish patients with moderate to severe OSA, which is expected to carry out in a large-scale population in order to screening for high-risk patients, and helps to evaluate the effect of OSA treatment continuously.Keywords: Obstructive sleep apnea, Heart rate variability, Machine learning, Random forest, Screening

Introduction

Obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) is a common respiratory disorder. The main manifestation is partial or complete collapse of the upper respiratory tract during sleep, which results in at least 10 s of nasal airflow reduction or interruption accompanying with varying degrees of blood oxygen saturation decline [1]. Despite the high prevalence and great harm of OSA, many patients still ignore the consequences caused by this disease. According to a recent review, the prevalence of OSA in adults aged 54 to 93 years is more than 60%. Among the participants surveyed in the research, 30.3% of white people, 32.4% of African Americans, 38.2% of Hispanic people and 39.4% of Chinese people had moderate to severe OSA with no statistically significant difference between different ethnic groups [2]. Secondly, OSA is closely associated with cognitive impairment, performance decrease, systemic hypertension diabetes mellitus, stroke and even sudden death [3–5]. However, even in the developed countries, more than 80% of patients with moderate to severe OSA do not receive timely diagnosis and treatment [6]. If patients do not pay enough attention to OSA, a series of complications and even sudden death caused by which can seriously affect the quality of life, which is a major public health problem.

Traditional PSG monitoring is the “gold standard” for OSA diagnosis. However, on the one hand, patients with OSA should be monitored overnight under the guidance of sleep medicine experts in the hospital, and PSG data should be manually verified and analyzed by experts according to relevant guidelines [7, 8]. On the other hand, the cost of PSG is high, which is difficult for some patients with financial difficulties to afford. Therefore, PSG is not suitable for large-scale screening of OSA or continuous monitoring of efficacy during treatment. HRV is a limited resources measure of cardiac autonomic function that reflects changes in the difference between heartbeat cycles, and sleep is a highly complex homeostasis phenomenon, which is partially regulated by the autonomic nervous system. When sleep is affected by repeated intermittent hypoxia, which cause thoracic negative pressure changes and autonomic nervous function impairment, sympathetic nerve activity increases with parasympathetic nerve activity weakening [9, 10]. The effect of OSA on autonomic nervous function suggests that HRV, as major indicators of autonomic nervous function, may be effective predictors for the existence of OSA to some extent. At present, some studies have trained prediction model to classify the severity of OSA based on HRV calculated from single-lead electrocardiography (ECG). Hu [11] et al. established a prediction model with an accuracy of 84.3% by using HRV related features extracted from ECG signals of 35 patients. Yang [12] et al. also applied a similar method to construct a prediction model with an accuracy of 90.3%. Nonetheless, the number of patients enrolled in the studies listed above was quite small, and researchers did not consider the contribution of important clinical characteristics (such as age, gender, body mass index, lowest blood oxygen saturation, etc.) to the prediction model in clinical practice, which indicated that there were still chances for model improvement in the researches mentioned above.

Due to the development of machine learning (ML) algorithms in recent years, more and more researchers have begun to use such series of algorithms to build prediction models for different diseases. These algorithms are highly adaptable to the data, allowing researchers to collect relatively few features to predict whether a particular disease has occurred. Compared to traditional classification algorithm (such as Logistic regression), RF algorithm (one of the machine learning algorithms and widely used in classification problems) does not even need to make feature selection when dealing with high-dimensional data, and is also applicable to continuous or discontinuous data. Therefore, RF algorithm has strong generalization ability, and will be adopted in this study to train a prediction model.

In conclusion, establishing a simple, accurate and sensitive screening tool for timely diagnosis of OSA patients, especially those with moderate to severe OSA, has important public health significance for reducing the incidence of complications and disease burden. According to the background above, we applied RF algorithm combining with demographic characteristics of the study participants, HRV features derived from ECG signals collected during PSG monitoring, blood oxygen saturation and other indicators to train a prediction model based on large sample size, which strengthens the model generalization ability and helps to distinguish potential patients with moderate to severe OSA timely.

Methods

Participants

Participants underwent overnight PSG monitoring (SOMNO screen TM plus PSG + sleep monitor) at the Sleep Medical Center of West China Fourth Hospital of Sichuan University with symptoms of breathlessness and snoring at night were included in this study from January to December in 2023. Apnea-Hypopnea Index (AHI) was calculated to determine whether patients had OSA and their severity.

Inclusion criteria

①Age ≥ 18 years old on the day of PSG monitoring; ②Participants who received PSG monitoring in the Sleep Medical Center of West China Fourth Hospital of Sichuan University for the first time without any OSA related treatment (such as continuous positive airway pressure, multilevel upper respiratory tract minimally invasive surgery) before; ③Participants with complete demographic information (such as age, sex, height and weight) and PSG monitoring record (monitoring duration ≥ 7 h); ④Healthy people (sleep apnea hypopnea index AHI < 5) and OSA patients with varying degrees of severity diagnosed through PSG.

Exclusion criteria

①Participants who drank caffeinated beverages (such as tea and coffee)/alcohol or took hypnotics (such as estazolam and zopiclone) on the day of monitoring; ②Patients with artifact of ECG signal, blood pressure signal and minimum blood oxygen saturation signal accounting for more than 30% in their PSG records after data analyzing. ③Patients with congestive heart failure, coronary heart disease, thyroid dysfunction and mental illness.

Data preprocessing

In this study, fast Fourier transform was applied to detect and identify the peak (R-peak) of QRS complex waves in ECG signals. Time interval between each adjacent R-peak (R-R interval, RRi) was calculated as the basis of other derived HRV time domain features. Secondly, due to the physical movement at the beginning and end of PSG monitoring, which generated a large amount of signal artifacts. HRV related features were subsequently calculated based on ECG signals extracted within time in bed (TIB). Finally, to avoid the impact of dimensions between different features and missing values on the result of this study, features used to train prediction model had been standardized and the mean imputation method was performed to process missing continuous variables.

Model selection and parameter setting

The prediction model was constructed by RF algorithm to determine whether a patient had OSA, especially moderate to severe OSA. Although any machine learning classification algorithm can be used to train the model, RF algorithm is considered in this study. RF algorithm is a high-performance ensemble learning algorithm that builds multiple decision trees as weak classifiers, which can be trained quickly and avoid the negative effect of overfitting on disease severity prediction [13]. Therefore, to apply RF algorithm reasonably and fit a high-performance prediction model, it is necessary to adjust the parameters of prediction model, such as the number, depth of trees, and the number of leaf nodes. The optimal setting for parameters were found by applying grid search method in this study. To begin with, a series of options were set for each parameter, and then different options for each parameter were utilized to fit models respectively. Lastly, the model with the best parameters was selected according to the result of performance comparison (evaluated by accuracy) between different models, and returned the detail setting of each parameter from the selected model (Table 2).

Dataset split and cross validation

70% (n = 558) of the 798 patients were randomly assigned to the training set, and the remaining 30% (n = 240) were assigned to the test set. 10-fold cross validation was applied to evaluate the model performance. According to the rationale of 10-fold cross validation, patients were randomly divided into 10 subgroups, 9 subgroups of which were used to construct the model to predict OSA severity, and the remaining 1 subgroup was reserved for verifying the model prediction performance. The process above was repeated for 10 times to ensure that each subgroup was once designated to test the performance of the model, which produced 10 test results. The average of 10 results was calculated to obtain the final evaluation result.

Performance indicators and statistical analysis

True positive (TP), False positive (FP), True negative (TN) and False negative (FN) were used to demonstrate the prediction results. The relationship between the predicted severity and the actual severity were displayed through confusion matrix containing TP, FP, TN, and FN. Sensitivity, specificity, accuracy, F1 score and area under the curve (AUC) were also used to comprehensively evaluate the prediction performance of the model.

Quantitative features were described by mean ± standard deviation, while qualitative features were described by percentage (%). If quantitative features followed normal distribution, ANOVA was applied to compare the difference between groups; If not, Kruskal-Wallis H rank sum test was applied to compare the difference between groups. Pearson Chi-square test or Fisher exact test were applied to compare the difference between groups for qualitative features. Unless otherwise specified, all tests were conducted by two-sided tests, and the significance level was set to 0.05 (α = 0.05). All of the hypothesis tests were implemented by SAS 9.4 and R 4.2.2 in study; Data imputation, standardization, splitting, random forest model training, 10-fold cross validation and results plotting were performed by Python 3.11 scikit-learn and matplotlib. Finally, to ensure the repeatability of research results, the random seed was set to 1.

Results

The characteristics of research participants

A total of 798 demographic and clinical records of participants were collected in this study, including 638 males and 160 females. The average age of participants was 43.51 ± 12.77 years and the average BMI of participants was 25.92 ± 4.37 kg/m2. The demographic characteristics of OSA patients with varying severity were quite different. Compared with the other 3 groups, gender composition difference, age and BMI were the highest in patients with severe OSA, and the differences were statistically significant. Besides SDNN, there were also differences in heart rate variability between OSA patients with different severity, and with the aggravation of OSA, LF/HF increased gradually, while mRRi, RMSSD, pNN50 and HF decreased gradually. Discussion

Moderate to severe OSA has a great negative impact on public health, and more than 80% of moderate to severe patients are still undiagnosed timely [6]. Traditional PSG monitoring requires experienced sleep specialist, adequate medical supplies, enough monitoring time and appropriate site. Therefore, PSG is not suitable for large-scale screening. Thus, A simple, accurate and sensitive screening tool is urgently needed to identify these population in high risk, which is a significant public health problem. At present, a series of studies have constructed prediction models with satisfying prediction accuracy based on different data sources or machine learning algorithms, but there are still some problems: Firstly, some researchers have focused on OSA high risk population screening through the data collected from various questionnaires [15–17], such as Berlin questionnaire, Epworth Sleepiness Scale and STOP-BANG questionnaire. However, this strategy may lead to a problem that the diagnosis of whether a patient in study suffers from OSA is based on subjective report scale, rather than professional objective monitoring data, which may be hard to produce objective, reproducible findings, due to observer and reporter bias [18]. Patients may mistakenly believe that they suffer from a severe degree of OSA, just because they snore loudly during sleep at night, and thus mark their own scale with a higher score, which may result in misclassification. Additionally, part of researchers [19–21] fit the model with public databases, such as Physionet apnea-ECG and St. Vincent’s University Hospital/University College Dublin Sleep Apnea database. The data used to construct prediction model was homogeneous between different studies and the sample size was quite small (e.g., Physionet apnea-ECG database contained ECG signal data from 70 patients), which could cause a problem that the prediction model performed well on a single individual, but when extrapolated to other individuals or even larger populations, the performance of the model was often limited. Lastly, some studies [22–24] focused on better prediction accuracy of a newly enrolled individual by improving the algorithm, ignoring the effect of clinical characteristics differences between individuals or only considering the blood oxygen saturation in addition to heart rate variability, which could limit the applicability of a prediction model in external populations. The argument that whether blood oxygen saturation can improve prediction performance needs further validation (Terrill [25] has shown that some apnea events may not accompany with a decrease of blood oxygen saturation, and the decrease of blood oxygen saturation can also occur in the absence of apnea events). Therefore, the main purpose of this study is to establish a rapid and efficient prediction model that considering the differences of various characteristics between individuals and the reasonability of methodology.

This study is the first diagnostic test of moderate to severe OSA patients based on large-scale population and machine learning algorithm in Sichuan province (located in southwest of China), which contains a total of 798 participants. Height, weight, LF and HF were not included in the model, and BMI, LF/HF were used instead in order to avoid the negative effect of collinearity. In terms of model performance, both RF and Logistic regression models performed poorly in predicting mild and moderate OSA, making it difficult to distinguish severity effectively, which might relate to the small number of mild and moderate OSA patients in the data set. When predicting severe or moderate to severe OSA, the performance of two models improved significantly. The RF model performed better than the traditional Logistic regression model, which was comparable to the prediction model established in Korea [26] based on a large sample size and random forest algorithm (Sensitivity: 75.24% vs. 66.20%; Specificity: 84.44% vs. 76.50%; Accuracy: 80.42% vs. 74.10%).

However, there were also some limitations: Firstly, the OSA severity of patients distributed unevenly, and Berkson’s bias existed in this study. The patients visiting the hospital were those who felt that OSA brought physical and economic burden to their daily life, and most of them were moderate to severe, resulting in fewer patients with mild and moderate OSA in the data set, which made it difficult for prediction model to distinguish these patients. Therefore, further PSG diagnosis is recommended only for patients with moderate to severe OSA screened by the model. Secondly, it is difficult for patients to conduct electroencephalogram at home. Considering the convenience of obtaining monitoring data, HRV features during different sleep stages were not used to fit prediction models (HRV features during different sleep stages could further improve accuracy and sensitivity of the model), and only general HRV, clinical and demographic features of patients were used. These features can already be obtained from devices such as portable health bracelets and mobile phone infrared detectors, making it possible for patients to self-screen at home. Finally, the source of OSA patients is relatively simple in the study. Although there were a large number of cases included, information about OSA patients from multiple centers is still needed, and the representative value of the study population is limited, which makes it difficult to carry out external verification for the established model. Subsequent external data from other centers can improve the performance on predicting mild and moderate OSA, and further validate the reliability of the model.

Conclusions

In conclusion, the prediction model established on the random forest algorithm in this study can effectively distinguish patients with moderate to severe OSA. The sensitivity and accuracy of the model reach 94.68% and 90.00% respectively, which is better than the Logistic regression model. At the same time, the accessibility of data collected from portable devices not only facilitates large-scale screening for high-risk moderate to severe OSA patients in the general population, but also helps to continuously evaluate the effectiveness of OSA treatment, such as continuous positive airway pressure and multilevel upper respiratory tract minimally invasive surgery.

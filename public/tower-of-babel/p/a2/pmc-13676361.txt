Neurological Recovery Trajectories in Older Patients with Cervical Spinal Cord Injury: A Multicenter Longitudinal Study
Authors: Ryosuke Hirota, Yoshinori Terashima, Makoto Emori, Atsushi Teramoto, Noriaki Yokogawa, Takeshi Sasagawa, Hiroaki Nakashima, Naoki Segi, Sadayuki Ito, Toru Funayama, Fumihiko Eto, Akihiro Yamaji, Kota Watanabe, Satoshi Nori, Kazuki Takeda, Takeo Furuya, Atsushi Yunde, Hideaki Nakajima, Tomohiro Yamada, Tomohiko Hasegawa, Hidenori Suzuki, Yasuaki Imajo, Shota Ikegami, Masashi Uehara, Hitoshi Tonomura, Munehiro Sakata, Ko Hashimoto, Yoshito Onoda, Nobuyuki Suzuki, Kenji Kato, Hiroshi Uei, Hirokatsu Sawada, Kazuo Nakanishi, Kosuke Misaki, Hidetomi Terai, Koji Tamai, Akiyoshi Kuroda, Gen Inoue, Yoichi Iizuka, Eiji Takasawa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Introduction This study aimed to characterize neurological recovery trajectories in patients with cervical spinal cord injury, aged ≥65 years. Methods This retrospective multicenter cohort study used registry data from the Japan Association of Spine Surgeons with Ambition. Patients aged ≥65 years with traumatic cervical spinal cord injury and complete International Standards for Neurological Classification of Spinal Cord Injury motor score data at baseline, 6 weeks, and 6 months post-injury, were included (n=222). Neurological assessments included total, upper extremity, and lower extremity motor scores; the American Spinal Injury Association Impairment Scale (AIS) and motor zone of partial preservation. Motor recovery and AIS conversion were analyzed based on baseline and 6-week AIS grades. Results Motor recovery occurred predominantly within the first 6 weeks after injury. From baseline to 6 weeks, patients with baseline AIS B and C exhibited significantly greater improvement in total motor score than those with AIS A and D. From 6 weeks to 6 months, measurable recovery persisted in patients with AIS C at 6 weeks, whereas those with AIS A or B demonstrated minimal additional gains. AIS conversion followed a similar pattern: 38.5%, 63.6%, and 82.7% among patients with baseline AIS A, B, and C, respectively, which improved by 6 weeks, whereas conversions beyond 6 weeks were uncommon. Among patients who remained AIS A or B at 6 months, motor zone of partial preservation extension occurred almost exclusively within the first 6 weeks. Conclusions In older patients with cervical spinal cord injury, most neurological recovery under routine clinical practice occurs within the first 6 weeks after injury. The magnitude of motor recovery and AIS conversion in this cohort was comparable to outcomes reported in age-unrestricted populations, suggesting that meaningful early neurological recovery can be observed even in older patients and highlighting the importance of early neurological assessment.

Introduction

Traumatic spinal cord injury (TSCI) is a severe condition that frequently causes lifelong neurological impairment, resulting in complete or incomplete paraplegia or tetraplegia. In addition to motor and sensory impairment, patients face increased risks of life-threatening complications, such as pneumonia, pressure ulcers, and urinary tract infections, which markedly reduce quality of life and impose substantial healthcare and social burdens1). Despite advances in acute and subacute care, including early spinal decompression, stabilization2), and intensive rehabilitation3), complete functional recovery remains limited. Most neurological recovery occurs within the first 6 months after injury; however, its extent and rate vary widely among individuals4), making prognostic prediction challenging.

The epidemiology of TSCI has changed considerably in recent decades. Although historically associated with high-energy trauma in young men, recent epidemiological data from first-world countries show a rising incidence among older adults, primarily resulting from low-energy falls from standing height5,6). Consequently, cervical-level and incomplete injuries are increasingly common. This demographic shift suggests that recovery data derived largely from younger populations may not accurately reflect recovery patterns in older adults.

A research group in Japan has developed a multicenter database of older patients with TSCI and has reported clinical characteristics, complications, and outcomes in this population7-14). However, despite the growing prevalence of cervical spinal cord injury (SCI) in older adults, data describing neurological recovery trajectories remain limited. Few studies have longitudinally examined neurological recovery stratified by the American Spinal Injury Association (ASIA) Impairment Scale (AIS) in older patients15). Consequently, uncertainty persists when determining treatment strategies, counseling patients and families, and evaluating emerging therapeutic approaches in older populations.

Therefore, this study aimed to characterize neurological recovery trajectories according to AIS severity in older patients with cervical SCI. Neurological recovery was assessed longitudinally immediately after injury, at 6 weeks, and at 6 months post-injury. By defining AIS-specific recovery trajectories in older adults under routine clinical practice, this study provides foundational data to improve prognostic accuracy and to support clinical decision-making in this growing population.

Materials and Methods

Study design and ethical approval

This study retrospectively analyzed multicenter registry data collected by the Japan Association of Spine Surgeons with Ambition (JASA). The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the institutional review board of a representative facility. The requirement for informed consent was waived because this was a retrospective registry-based study; instead, an opt-out notice was posted on the hospital website, and no inquiries were received.

Patient population

This retrospective cohort study included patients aged ≥65 years with traumatic cervical SCI who received either conservative or surgical treatment at JASA-registered institutions between 2010 and 2020 and were followed for ≥6 months after injury. The indication for surgical treatment was determined at the discretion of the treating surgeon at each institution. Patients without neurological impairment at the time of injury or those with missing International Standards for Neurological Classification of Spinal Cord Injury (ISNCSCI) motor score data at baseline, 6 weeks, or 6 months post-injury were excluded. Ultimately, 222 patients were included in the analysis (Figure 1).

Neurological assessment

Neurological examinations were performed according to the ISNCSCI. AIS classification was conducted by experienced spine surgeons or physicians trained in SCI assessment at each participating center. To ensure consistency across centers, neurological assessments were performed using the standardized ISNCSCI examination protocol.

Data collection

Patient demographic data, including age, sex, height, and weight, were collected. Laboratory data obtained on admission included hemoglobin (Hb, g/dL), total protein (g/dL), albumin (Alb, g/dL), blood glucose (mg/dL), and glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c, %).

Treatment-related factors, including the presence of surgical intervention, type of procedure, and time from injury to surgery, were recorded. Neurological impairment was evaluated using the AIS grade and ISNCSCI motor scores16). In addition to motor score assessment, changes in the motor zone of partial preservation (mZPP)17) were analyzed. Motor scores were summarized as total motor score, upper extremity motor score (UEMS), and lower extremity motor score (LEMS). When the mZPP differed between sides, the more caudal segment was used for analysis.

Baseline neurological assessment was defined as the first ISNCSCI examination performed immediately after injury, typically within 72 h after injury. Follow-up neurological assessments were performed at 6 weeks (±14 days) and 6 months post-injury.

Outcome measures

Outcome measures focused on the trajectory of motor recovery after injury. First, motor recovery was evaluated according to AIS grade at baseline, with changes from baseline to 6 weeks and 6 months compared across AIS grades. Second, motor recovery from 6 weeks to 6 months was analyzed according to the AIS grade assessed at 6 weeks, treating the 6-week assessment as a clinically meaningful milestone rather than a true baseline. Additionally, in patients who remained classified as AIS A or B from baseline through 6 months post-injury, changes in the mZPP were evaluated.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism software (version 10.6.1; GraphPad Software Inc., San Diego, CA, USA). Changes in ISNCSCI motor scores across the three time points were analyzed using one-way repeated-measures analysis of variance to account for within-subject correlations inherent to longitudinal data. When a significant main effect was identified, Bonferroni-adjusted post hoc tests were applied for pairwise comparisons. To account for potential confounding factors, a multivariable linear regression analysis was performed to identify variables independently associated with motor recovery at 6 months. The dependent variable was the change in total motor score from baseline to 6 months, and the independent variables included age, sex, surgical treatment, baseline motor score, and baseline AIS grade. Data are presented as mean±standard deviation. Analyses were conducted using complete cases only, and a two-sided p-value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Patient characteristics

The mean age of the patients was 74.8±6.2 years, and 70.7% were male. Surgical intervention was performed in 72.5% of cases, with posterior approaches accounting for approximately 90% of all procedures. The mean time from injury to surgery was 15.1±17.9 days (Table 1). At baseline, most patients were classified as AIS C or D, whereas AIS A and B accounted for a smaller proportion of the cohort, reflecting the predominance of incomplete cervical injuries in older adults.

Surgical treatment was performed across all AIS grades, with no marked concentration in a single neurological severity category. This distribution indicates that the observed recovery trajectories reflect neurological outcomes under routine clinical practice, rather than outcomes confined to a specific treatment strategy or injury severity.

Motor functional improvements

Motor function was assessed at baseline, 6 weeks, and 6 months post-injury (Figure 2). Across the entire cohort, motor scores demonstrated a general trend toward improvement over time; however, the magnitude and temporal pattern of recovery varied substantially according to baseline AIS severity. Patients with AIS B and C showed pronounced motor improvement during the early post-injury period, whereas recovery in patients with AIS A and D was comparatively limited. This AIS-dependent pattern was consistently observed across total motor score, UEMS, and LEMS, indicating that recovery trajectories of upper and lower extremity motor function were closely aligned within each neurological category. Notably, early motor recovery from baseline to 6 weeks accounted for a substantial proportion of the total improvement observed over the 6-month follow-up period. Patients with AIS B and C exhibited significantly greater early gains than those with AIS A and D (Figure 3). In contrast, patients with AIS D showed smaller absolute changes, which likely reflect their higher baseline motor scores and a relative ceiling effect rather than an absence of neurological recovery.

When motor recovery from 6 weeks to 6 months was analyzed according to the AIS grade assessed at 6 weeks, patients classified as AIS C demonstrated continued and measurable improvement, whereas those with AIS A or B exhibited minimal additional recovery (Figure 4). This divergence in recovery trajectories indicates that neurological status at 6 weeks provides important stratification of subsequent recovery potential. Across all analyses, recovery patterns were consistent regardless of whether neurological improvement was evaluated using total motor score, UEMS, or LEMS. Furthermore, temporal trends observed in motor score recovery were concordant with changes in AIS grade and mZPP, indicating internal consistency among different neurological outcome measures. Marked deviations from these general recovery patterns were uncommon, and no distinct subgroup demonstrating delayed or accelerated recovery beyond the early post-injury period was identified.

After 6 weeks, AIS grade conversion was rare, and most patients remained in the same AIS grade through 6 months of follow-up. In particular, patients classified as AIS A or B at the 6-week assessment demonstrated limited subsequent AIS improvement, indicating relative stabilization of neurological status after the initial recovery phase. This temporal pattern was consistently observed across the cohort.

Change in mZPP

Among patients who remained classified as AIS A or B throughout the 6-month follow-up period, changes in the mZPP were primarily observed during the early post-injury phase (Table 4 and 5). Caudal extension of the mZPP was more frequently detected between baseline and 6 weeks than during the later observation period.

After 6 weeks, further changes in mZPP were uncommon, indicating limited segmental motor recovery beyond the early phase in patients with severe neurological impairment. Although the number of patients eligible for mZPP analysis was small, the temporal pattern of mZPP change closely paralleled the motor score trajectories observed in this cohort.

Factors associated with motor recovery

To account for potential confounding factors, a multivariable linear regression analysis was performed, including age, sex, surgical treatment, baseline motor score, and baseline AIS grade. Baseline motor score and baseline AIS grade were independently associated with motor recovery at 6 months. Lower baseline motor scores were associated with greater improvement (β=−0.59, p<0.001). Compared with patients classified as AIS A, those with AIS B, C, and D demonstrated significantly greater motor recovery (all p<0.001) (Table 6). Age, sex, and surgical treatment were not significantly associated with motor recovery. The model explained 48% of the variance in motor recovery (R2=0.48).

Discussion

Summary of the results

In this study, we characterized neurological recovery trajectories in patients aged ≥65 years with cervical SCI at baseline, 6 weeks, and 6 months post-injury. Early recovery within the first 6 weeks was most pronounced in patients with AIS B and C, whereas patients with AIS A and D exhibited limited improvement. A similar pattern was observed over 6 months, with patients classified as AIS B and C demonstrating the greatest gains, whereas improvements in those with AIS A and D remained modest. When stratified by AIS grade at 6 weeks, patients with AIS C continued to improve up to 6 months, whereas those with AIS A or B demonstrated minimal additional recovery beyond 6 weeks.

AIS grade changes followed a comparable pattern: substantial AIS conversions occurred between baseline and 6 weeks, whereas conversions after 6 weeks were uncommon, and most patients with AIS A or B remained unchanged at 6 months. Analysis of mZPP demonstrated a similar trend, with caudal extension occurring primarily within the first 6 weeks and little change thereafter. Although this study aimed to characterize neurological recovery trajectories, approximately 70% of patients underwent surgical treatment. Therefore, these findings should be interpreted as recovery patterns observed under routine clinical practice, rather than as purely non-interventional natural recovery.

Motor recovery patterns

The magnitude of motor recovery was consistent with previously reported recovery patterns in cervical SCI. In patients with AIS A, the mean total motor score improvement was 8.1 points at 6 weeks and 12.5 points at 6 months, comparable to previously reported improvements of approximately 11-14 points within the first year post-injury18-20). In patients with AIS B, our cohort demonstrated improvements of 29.2 points at 6 weeks and 32.2 points at 6 months, indicating substantial early recovery. Patients with AIS C exhibited a 6-month improvement of 28.3 points, within ranges reported in studies with follow-up up to approximately 12 months18,19,21-23).

Despite including only patients aged ≥65 years, the extent of motor recovery observed in this study was comparable to that reported in cohorts without age restrictions. The prognostic impact of age on neurological recovery after SCI remains controversial. Although advanced age has been associated with poorer functional outcomes (e.g., ambulation and daily activities), its influence on neurological recovery is unclear. Several studies have reported comparable improvements in ASIA motor scores between younger and older individuals24-26), and Kramer et al.27) found no association between age and motor level recovery in patients with complete tetraplegia. Overall, although direct comparisons should be interpreted with caution given differences in baseline timing and follow-up intervals, these findings suggest that meaningful early neurological recovery can occur even in older patients with cervical SCI.

Timing of neurological recovery

Most motor recovery occurred within the first 6 weeks after injury, with limited additional gains thereafter. Although neurological assessments were performed at discrete time points, previous studies with more frequent evaluations have consistently shown that recovery is most rapid during the early post-injury period, followed by a gradual slowing over the subsequent weeks to months18-20). Although a strict recovery plateau cannot be established from this data, the observed trajectories align with established temporal patterns.

AIS conversion patterns

Previous studies have shown that AIS conversion is most likely to occur when neurological classification is performed early after injury. When baseline assessments were conducted several days post-injury, approximately 30% of patients with AIS A converted to AIS B-D within 1 year21,28,29). In this study, baseline AIS classification was performed immediately after injury, capturing peak neurological instability. By 6 weeks post-injury, 38.5% of patients initially classified as AIS A, 63.6% of those with AIS B, and 82.7% of those with AIS C had improved AIS grades. Despite the shorter observation window, these rates were comparable to those reported in previous studies, likely reflecting the earlier timing of baseline assessment. AIS conversion after 6 weeks was uncommon, indicating that most AIS grade improvement in older patients occurs during the early post-injury period.

Limitations

This study has some limitations. First, sensory score trajectories were not evaluated. Second, analyses were restricted to patients with complete neurological assessments at all three time points, potentially selecting a more followable subset of older patients and underrepresenting individuals with severe comorbidities or early mortality. Because this was a multicenter registry-based study, detailed reasons for loss to follow-up or early mortality could not be fully ascertained. In addition, >70% of patients in this cohort underwent surgical treatment; however, recovery trajectories were not compared between surgical and non-surgical groups. The purpose of this study was not to evaluate treatment effects but to describe the temporal trajectory of neurological recovery in older patients with cervical SCI under routine clinical practice. Because surgical indications and timing are strongly influenced by injury severity and other clinical factors, direct comparisons between patients treated surgically and those treated non-surgically may introduce substantial selection bias. Future studies using more rigorous designs may help clarify the influence of surgical intervention on recovery trajectories. Third, comorbidities and associated injuries were not comprehensively accounted for. Fourth, the distribution of AIS grades was uneven, with relatively small numbers of patients classified as AIS A and B, which may limit the precision of between-group comparisons. Finally, follow-up was limited to 6 months after injury, which restricts the evaluation of long-term neurological recovery. Longer follow-up studies are needed to better understand long-term outcomes in older patients with cervical SCI. Despite these limitations, this multicenter registry-based study provides valuable insight into neurological recovery trajectories in older patients with cervical SCI.

Clinical implications

Although this study was focused exclusively on older patients, the magnitude and timing of neurological recovery were comparable to those reported in cohorts without age restrictions. These findings indicate that meaningful early neurological recovery can occur even in older patients with cervical SCI. Furthermore, the observation that most recovery occurred within the first 6 weeks after injury highlights the importance of early neurological assessment and has practical implications for prognostic counseling, rehabilitation planning, and the timing of surgical intervention. Given the increasing incidence of cervical SCI in older populations, these findings support clinical decision-making in routine spine practice.

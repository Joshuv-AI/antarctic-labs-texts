Comparative Analysis of Current Endodontic Practices: A Survey of Punjab State versus Global Trends
Authors: Tarun Kumar, Vidushi Gilhotra, Ena Maakhni, Prabhprit Kaur, Muskaan Garg, Keshav Singla
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroduction:Endodontics, vital for oral health, addresses dental pulp and periapical issues. In Punjab, with over 30 million residents, 40–60% of adults require endodontic care due to widespread dental caries. Urban clinics perform more treatments than rural areas, which face accessibility challenges due to limited infrastructure and specialists. Distributed via IDA WhatsApp groups and emails, 485 responses were collected from 800 questionnaires. Data were organized by endodontists and dental students.Results:From 800 solicited dentists, 485 responses were analyzed, predominantly from middle-aged (50–55 years) private practitioners (84%). Antibiotics were commonly prescribed, with amoxicillin being the first choice (66%). Magnification tools were rarely used, and 96% placed crowns post-root canal treatment.Conclusion:The survey reveals diverse endodontic practices in Punjab, with a shift toward modern techniques. However, disparities in care quality and access persist between urban and rural areas, highlighting the need for targeted efforts to improve rural services and elevate dental healthcare standards statewide.

INTRODUCTION

Endodontics, a dental specialty focusing on the treatment of dental pulp and surrounding tissues, is vital for oral health. Punjab, with over 30 million residents, presents unique challenges and opportunities in dental care. The National Oral Health Survey (2002–2003) reports 40–60% of adults in Punjab suffer from dental caries, a primary driver of endodontic treatments. Root canal procedures, increasingly common, reflect growing awareness, improved access, and advancements in endodontics.[1]

Despite progress, rural areas (62% of Punjab’s population) face limited access to specialized care due to fewer trained endodontists and inadequate infrastructure. The Punjab Health Survey (2020) noted rural residents are 30% less likely to receive timely care than urban populations.[2] This study evaluates endodontic practices in Punjab, focusing on the adoption of advanced techniques, diagnostic tools, and adherence to clinical protocols. Insights aim to guide policymakers and practitioners in improving endodontic care quality and accessibility, shaping the future of dental healthcare in the region.

METHODOLOGY

A cross-sectional, descriptive study employing a questionnaire-based approach was conducted among currently practicing dentists in Punjab. The study utilized a multistage proportionate cluster random sampling method, segmenting Punjab into 13 regions based on its districts. With a margin of error of ± 0.05% and an anticipated sample proportion of 0.5, the required sample size was determined to be 385.

Questionnaire design

A self-structured questionnaire, designed using Google Forms, comprised closed-ended questions. The questionnaire, both pretested and self-designed, focused on various aspects of endodontic procedures. It was organized into four sections:

The questionnaire comprised a total of 35 questions. Before dissemination, a pilot study was conducted with two participants from a population similar to the main study group to ensure clarity and correct interpretation of the questions. Individuals who participated in the pilot study were not included in the main study. Approval from the Institute Ethics Committee was secured (No. - Dirds/PG-MDS/2023 dated 04.10.2023). To reach practicing dentists, the questionnaire was distributed through the Indian Dental Association (IDA) WhatsApp groups across the state. Additionally, emails were sent to the heads of dental institutes to target academic dentists. In total, 800 questionnaires were distributed, and 485 responses were collected. A dedicated team, consisting of two endodontists, one postgraduate student, and two Bachelor of Dental Surgery (BDS) interns, was responsible for creating, disseminating, and collecting the questionnaires. This team also managed the tabulation of the collected data and the formation of results. By implementing a robust methodology and ensuring rigorous data collection and analysis processes, this study aims to provide a comprehensive overview of the current state of endodontic practices among dentists in Punjab.

RESULTS

Out of 800 surveyed dentists in Punjab, 485 responded (60% response rate)—214 online and 271 via paper forms. Most respondents were middle-aged (50–55 years), with a male predominance (56%) and 84% in private practice. Only 13% worked in academia, 3% in government, and 59% had over 10 years of experience. Among postgraduates, 22% were endodontists, followed by prosthodontists (10%), and other specialties.

Radiographic diagnosis was the most preferred method (59%), followed by percussion (48%) and heat tests (32%). Written and verbal consents were equally common (49% and 44%, respectively). Multivisit treatments were preferred by 56% of dentists, while 43% performed single-visit treatments. RVG was the dominant radiograph method (92%), but only 9% used apex locators, with 38% never using them. Rubber dam use was minimal, with 51% using it rarely and only 4% in every case.

Most practitioners (92%) used both hand and rotary files, favoring Protaper (64%) and NeoEndo (34%). Sodium hypochlorite (71%) was the primary irrigant, and calcium hydroxide (81%) was the preferred intracanal medicament. Single cone obturation was most common (50%), followed by lateral condensation (42%). Zinc oxide-based sealers were favored (41%), and 96% placed crowns post-RCT.

For pain management, Ketorolac tromethamine was preferred (40%), with serratiopeptidase combinations (31%) and paracetamol (16%) also common. Amoxicillin was the top antibiotic choice (66%). Broken file cases were handled by adjusting working length (44%), using ultrasonics (34%), or extraction (6%).

Sterilization of files was mainly via autoclaving (71%), with glass bead sterilization (23%) also common. Only 30% of dentists tested for viral markers pretreatment. Most did not use magnification (65%), with 27% using loupes and 1% microscopes. Vaccination rates were high (89% for COVID-19 and Hepatitis B), and special precautions were common for Human Immonodeficiency Virus (HIV)/Hepatitis C cases. However, 10% referred such cases to other practitioners.

DISCUSSION

A recent survey received 485 responses (214 online and 271 printed), with 56% male and 44% female participants, differing from the DCI website’s nearly equal gender ratio.[3] The predominant age group was 45–55 years, and 84% of respondents operated private practices, reflecting limited academic opportunities postgraduation.[4] Digital radiographic techniques were favored over conventional methods, consistent with Lee et al.’s[5] findings, where 70% of dentists used digital radiography.

Most respondents managed both initial and retreatment cases of root canal therapy (86%), aligning with studies by Kohli et al.[4] (64.2%) and Mohanty et al.[6] (84%). However, 62% of dentists did not use rubber dams, with only 9% preferring them, citing barriers such as patient discomfort, time constraints, and cost. These rates contrast with higher usage reported in American studies by Whitten et al.[7] (59% for general dentists and 92% for endodontists).

Radiographs remained the primary method for determining working length, with only 9% favoring apex locators. The Root ZX mini was the most popular apex locator. These results align with studies by Lee et al.[5] and Jenkins et al.,[8] although Mohanty et al.[6] reported a preference for combined methods. For instrumentation, 92% used both rotary and hand files, a trend consistent with Mohanty et al.’s.[6] findings, though contrasting with Al-Omari’s[9] study, which showed greater reliance on hand files.

Sodium hypochlorite (71%) was the most common irrigant, and calcium hydroxide (81%) was the preferred intracanal medicament, consistent with studies by Whitten et al.,[7] Lee et al.,[5] and Jenkins et al.[8] However, Al-Omari’s study in North Jordan found tricresol formalin more common.[9] Magnification was less commonly used, likely due to limited training, contrasting with findings by Kersten et al.[10] and Lee et al.[5] Ultrasonic irrigation was preferred by 51% post-biomechanical preparation, lower than rates reported in American studies.[511]

Obturation techniques showed equal preference for cold lateral compaction and single cone methods. While Lee et al.[5] observed a shift toward continuous wave techniques in the U.S., Mohanty et al.[6] noted single cone methods as most common.[6812] Zinc oxide-based sealers (41%) were preferred, consistent with Jenkins et al.[8] and Al-Omari,[9] though differing from other regions.

Amoxicillin (66%) was the most prescribed antibiotic, aligning with regional practices but differing from U.S. studies favoring penicillin. Ketorolac tromethamine (40%) was the most preferred analgesic, followed by serratiopeptidase combinations (31%), contrasting with American studies that highlighted ibuprofen as the primary choice.[56]

CONCLUSION

This study offers a comprehensive overview of endodontic practices in Punjab, identifying areas for improvement and emphasizing the need for targeted interventions to enhance care quality and accessibility. These findings can guide policymakers, educators, and practitioners in advancing endodontic standards across the region.

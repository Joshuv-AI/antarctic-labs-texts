Estimating multivitamin and mineral supplement use and associated nutrient intake in Saudi Arabia: a probabilistic modeling study
Authors: Omar Alhumaidan, Mohammed Alsaif, Haya Alajmi, Ghadir Fallata, Nawaf Sharahili
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMultivitamin and mineral supplements (MVMs) are extensively consumed worldwide; however, national-level data regarding their usage and associated nutrient intake in Saudi Arabia remain limited. Characterizing exposure and intake is crucial for regulatory assessments and public health planning.MethodsA probabilistic model was developed to estimate exposure to MVMs and quantify nutrient intake among adults in Saudi Arabia. The model integrated three probability distributions representing overall supplement use, MVMs use, and daily use, derived from population-based data, and combined them with nutrient concentration distributions obtained from 24 MVMs products available in the Saudi market. Monte Carlo simulation was applied to account for variability and uncertainty.ResultsThe median probability of exposure was 0.033 (95% uncertainty interval: 0.019–0.054) for males and 0.058 (95% uncertainty interval: 0.044–0.074) for females. Sensitivity analysis yielded comparable exposure estimates across the evaluated scenarios. Estimated nutrient intakes generally contributed modestly to daily values (DV), with vitamin B12 showing the highest contribution at the 50th percentile (117.7% DV for males and 203.7% DV for females). Estimated intakes for all nutrients remained below established upper levels (ULs). Indirect validation using per-capita expenditure showed that the observed market expenditure of 35 SAR/capita/year fell within the modeled uncertainty range of 4–50 SAR/capita/year .ConclusionsThe proposed probabilistic model provides a preliminary tool for estimating supplement-related nutrient exposure in the absence of national consumption surveys. The findings support its use in regulatory decision-making and highlight the need for further integration with food-based intake data.Keywords: Multivitamin and mineral supplements, Probabilistic modeling, Dietary exposure, Saudi Arabia, Nutrient intake

Introduction

Multivitamin and mineral supplements (MVMs) are among the most widely consumed dietary supplements globally [1–3]. Many individuals use these products to meet their micronutrient requirements rather than relying solely on food intake. In Saudi Arabia, several studies have explored the patterns and prevalence of supplement use [4]. However, most were limited to specific settings, such as universities, and did not represent the general population across all regions [4].

Recently, a study addressed this gap by covering all regions of Saudi Arabia, with approximately 65% of participants from the central region and the remainder distributed across the northern, southern, eastern, and western regions [4]. The findings suggest that 58% of adults reported using at least one supplement in the past year, with vitamins accounting for 81% of the total use. Female sex and higher education were significant predictors of intake [4].

In addition to this research interest, regulatory attention has increased. In 2023, the Saudi Food and Drug Authority (SFDA) updated dietary supplement regulations (SFDA.FD 55:2023). This regulation specifies the minimum and maximum limits for vitamins and minerals in supplements, including MVMs [5]. These regulations complement other nutrition-related policies, such as food fortification programs. However, understanding consumer behavior, including supplement use, remains essential for effective legislation and communication strategies, particularly for nutrients with established upper levels (UL), such as iodine, vitamin A, and vitamin D [6].

Although MVMs can contribute to meeting the daily values (DV) of vitamins and minerals, their use requires careful consideration of dosage and adherence to reference values and regulatory frameworks. Therefore, policy assessment tools should include instruments capable of estimating dietary exposure, serving as preliminary tools to prioritize the need for more detailed studies [7–10]. Advances in probabilistic modeling have enabled the simulation of current and hypothetical regulatory scenarios while accounting for variability and uncertainty arising from data limitations or behavioral factors [11, 12].

In Saudi Arabia, the National Nutrition Committee at the SFDA has developed tools such as the National Nutrient Consumption Model (NNC-V2) [13] and models for assessing the adequacy of dietary patterns and guidelines [14]. Probabilistic models for food fortification [15] and chronic disease-related regulations [16, 17] exist; however, no comparable models have been developed for dietary supplements. Unlike previous approaches that primarily rely on dietary exposure models as the computational core, supplement-focused models require additional concepts, such as ingested dose and probability of exposure, to be integrated into the calculation framework.

These concepts, commonly applied in microbial risk assessment models [18–20], can be adapted to develop specialized models for estimating supplement intake. Practically, this involves calculating exposure from available data while incorporating uncertainty and estimating daily intake of vitamins and minerals from MVMs marketed in Saudi Arabia.

Therefore, this study aimed to develop a probabilistic model capable of estimating potential dietary exposure levels to micronutrients from the daily intake of dietary supplements and calculating the corresponding daily values while accounting for variability and uncertainty related to data sources and dietary behavior.

Method

Study design overview

This study developed a probabilistic model to estimate dietary supplement use and the resulting intake of vitamins and minerals. As shown in Fig. 1, the estimation was performed in three linked steps. The first-step estimates of the joint probability reflect the likelihood that a consumed supplement is an MVMs for an individual. This was carried out using three probability distributions representing the overall probability of dietary supplement use, the probability of using MVMs, and the probability of daily use. The target population comprised the general community, excluding individuals with chronic diseases and pregnant or lactating women.

Overview of the modeling process

The second step estimated vitamin and mineral concentrations per dose based on products available in the Saudi market. This was carried out by identifying supplements commercially available in the Saudi market. A product-oriented modeling approach was then applied, whereby a single product was selected in each iteration. Then, the concentrations per dose of all vitamins and minerals contained in that product were used in the analysis.

The third step modeled the ingested dose using an individual-based probabilistic approach. In each iteration, the model estimated exposure probability in the modeled individual. Then, the model incorporates the full nutrient concentration per dose with the estimated modeled exposure probability to determine the daily intake for each iteration. Each iteration sampled random values from predefined distributions, representing a plausible consumption scenario. The probabilistic model was conducted using 10,000 iterations.

This approach provides a more realistic estimation than relying only on mean values with fixed assumptions across all steps. For example, the model can represent scenarios where supplement use is high but nutrient concentrations per dose are low, or vice versa, as well as both high or low. Thus, the model captures a broad range of plausible combinations between consumption behavior and nutrient content. All computational and modeling processes are summarized in Table 1; Fig. 1.

To evaluate market coverage, the selected products were matched to manufacturers and brand owners reported in Euromonitor International’s 2023 dietary supplements market-share database for Saudi Arabia [23]. Based on this comparison, the selected products corresponded to companies and brands representing approximately 72.08% of the reported market share. The remaining 27.92% corresponded to individually reported companies that were not represented among the selected products.

Notably, 45.91% of the market was reported by Euromonitor as an aggregated market segment rather than as individually listed companies with corresponding product-level market shares. In addition, several brands included in the selected product list were not separately identified within the company-level dataset and therefore could not be assigned individual market shares.

Consequently, the identified products may not necessarily represent the precise market shares at the product level or consumption patterns in Saudi Arabia. This may introduce uncertainty of the dataset.

Therefore, a product-oriented selection process was applied. In each iteration, one of the 24 identified products was randomly selected with replacement. The concentrations per dose of all vitamins and minerals contained in the selected product were then used in the analysis.

This approach was chosen to better reflect real-world supplement consumption, where individuals consume complete products rather than independently selecting individual nutrients. As a result, nutrient concentrations remain linked according to the formulation of the selected product.

In contrast, a nutrient-oriented approach would independently sample each nutrient from separate distributions. This could generate nutrient combinations that do not correspond to any actual product available in the market. As a result, the simulated formulations may not reflect the composition of real-world products and could lead to less realistic estimates of exposure.

Under the product-oriented selection process, a supplement product was randomly selected in each iteration from the set of identified products. The concentrations per dose of all nutrients were then obtained directly from the selected product. Thus, the concentration of nutrient in iteration was defined as Eqs. 7–8.

Direct validation of the model was not feasible because national data on supplement intake and consumption volume are unavailable in Saudi Arabia. Therefore, an indirect validation approach was applied using annual per capita expenditure data reported by Euromonitor [23].

Sensitivity analysis

The prevalence estimation model was based on the product of three probability components (Eq. 6). This Equation implicitly assumes statistical independence among supplement use (), multivitamin–mineral supplement use ( ), and daily use frequency ().

Under this assumption, daily users are treated as a random subset of all supplement users, and multivitamin–mineral supplement users are assumed to be randomly distributed within the supplement-using population. However, this assumption may not fully reflect real-world behavior, as individuals who regularly consume supplements may be more likely to use multivitamin–mineral products and to consume them on a daily basis.

Consequently, dependence among these probability components could exist, violating the independence assumption underlying the prevalence calculation. To evaluate the impact of potential violations of this assumption, a sensitivity analysis was conducted using a Gaussian copula approach [25].

The analysis evaluated the impact of potential violations of the independence assumption by introducing positive dependence among , , and . In addition to the independence case (ρ = 0.0), hypothetical correlation level of ρ = 0.5 and ρ = 0.8 were examined.

Correlated random samples were generated using Cholesky decomposition of the specified correlation matrix while preserving the original marginal distributions of each probability component [25]. Prevalence estimates were subsequently recalculated under each correlation structure to assess the sensitivity.

Sources and management of uncertainty and variability

Variability and uncertainty were considered as separate components within the model. Variability is mainly associated with differences in nutrient composition among MVMs available in the Saudi market and reflects heterogeneity in product formulations. This component was characterized using observed nutrient concentrations from 24 commercially available products, from which empirical and bootstrapped distributions of nutrient content per dose were derived (Table 2).

Uncertainty is primarily associated with estimation of population-level prevalence, including probabilities of general supplement use and MVMs use. Because these parameters were derived from a single population-based study conducted in Saudi Arabia [4], the model is subject to sampling uncertainty. To address this, prevalence parameters were modeled using Beta distributions.

The Monte Carlo method was used to propagate both product-level variability and prevalence-related uncertainty through the model, allowing exposure estimates to reflect differences between products while representing uncertainty in population prevalence estimates. A total of 10,000 Monte Carlo iterations were performed for all probabilistic simulations.

At the 50th percentile, Vitamin B12 provided the highest contribution, reaching 117.7% of the DV in males and 203.7% in females. Other nutrients with relatively higher contributions included Pantothenic acid (33.9% in males and 58.6% in females), Vitamin E (25.4% and 43.8%), Vitamin B1 (32.0% and 55.3%), and Vitamin B2 (25.9% and 44.8%) in males and females, respectively.

Regarding safety thresholds, none of the estimated intakes exceeded the established ULs for any nutrient in either gender. For example, median intakes of Vitamin E (2.29 mg/day in males and 3.94 mg/day in females) remained substantially below the UL of 300 mg/day, while Vitamin D intakes (0.40 and 0.70 µg/day, respectively) were considerably lower than the UL of 100 µg/day. Although Vitamin B12 exhibited the highest DV contribution, no UL has been established for this nutrient, suggesting that the estimated levels of exposure are unlikely to raise safety concerns.

Annual expenditure on MVM supplements was generally low, with median values of 14 SAR/year for males and 21 SAR/year for females, corresponding to 2.5th–97.5th percentile ranges of 3.6–36 SAR/year and 3.6–65 SAR/year, respectively. When expressed relative to annual food expenditure reported in the NNC-V2 study [13], the median Supplement-to-Food Spending Ratio was 0.001 for males and 0.002 for females, indicating that expenditure on MVM supplements represented only a very small proportion of overall food spending.

Testing of model performance

Modeled annual supplement spending ranged from 4 SAR/capita at the 2.5th percentile to 50 SAR/capita at the 97.5th percentile. The reported annual expenditure from Euromonitor was 35 SAR/capita, which fell within the modeled uncertainty range and corresponded approximately to the 76th percentile of the modeled distribution (Table 5). Some divergence between the modeled and reported values would be expected because the Euromonitor estimate is derived by allocating total market sales across the entire population, whereas the present model incorporates the probability of supplement use and MVM consumption at the individual level. Nevertheless, the observed expenditure remained within the predicted uncertainty range, providing support for the overall plausibility of the model outputs.

Discussion

Key findings and study outcomes

The primary outcome of this study was the development of a probabilistic model capable of estimating the probability of exposure to MVMs using available data on consumption prevalence, supplement type and frequency of use [4]. The estimated probability of exposure showed a median value of 0.05, with range of 0.03 to 0.06, reflecting both population variability and statistical uncertainty captured Model-based exposure estimates did not indicate nutrient intakes from MVMs that would be expected to result in adverse health effects under the evaluated scenario. This was illustrated through the modeled exposure distributions, in which estimated intakes remained below the corresponding ULs for the assessed nutrients. However, these estimates reflect exposure attributable to dietary supplements only and do not represent total nutrient intake from all sources, including foods or fortified products.

Regarding vitamin B12, although no UL has been established for this nutrient, the estimated intake showed the highest contribution relative to the DV among all evaluated nutrients. At the 50th percentile, vitamin B12 intake corresponded to 117.7% of the DV in males and 203.7% in females. One possible explanation is the high quantities of vitamin B12 present in some dietary supplements. An assessment of 24 supplement products showed that some contained up to 500 µg of vitamin B12. Additionally, Saudi regulation SFDA.FD 55:2023 permits levels of up to 1000 µg/day [5]. Nevertheless, these findings should be interpreted with caution, as the estimated intake was directly compared with the reference value and does not necessarily reflect biological absorption or corresponding biomarker levels in the bloodstream. Consequently, a higher estimated intake does not directly imply proportional increases in circulating vitamin B12 concentrations.

These findings may support future investigations evaluating whether the currently permitted vitamin B12 levels in dietary supplements are adequately reflected in population biomarker status. In Saudi Arabia, a nationwide cross-sectional study involving 3,432 adults across the 13 administrative regions reported that approximately 7.5% of participants exhibited vitamin B12 levels below the reference range in blood (187–883 pg/mL) [26].

For model performance, an indirect validation approach was used because no national consumption surveys currently include supplement intake data for direct comparison [27–29]. Model outputs were combined with price data to estimate annual per capita expenditure on MVMs. Estimated spending ranged from SR 4 to SR 50 per capita, with a median of SR 17. These estimates were compared with Euromonitor data, which reported annual spending of approximately SR 35 per capita [23].

The Euromonitor estimate fell within the modeled uncertainty range and was close to the upper quartile of the modeled distribution. Some difference between the two estimates would be expected because Euromonitor reflects total market sales distributed across the population, whereas the present model incorporates the probability of supplement use and MVM consumption at the individual level. Overall, the observed expenditure supports the plausibility and internal consistency of the model outputs.

The higher exposure estimates observed in market-based data are expected and can be partly attributed to differences in underlying assumptions. In the proposed model, MVMs use was restricted to daily consumption. Therefore, individuals with intermittent or non-daily use patterns were not included. In contrast, Euromonitor data encompass all usage patterns [23]. Consequently, a plausible interpretation of the model results is lower average exposure estimates relative to observed data, which primarily reflect all use patterns.

Implications and practical applications

The findings demonstrate that the proposed model can function as a decision-support tool for policy analysts and regulators, particularly in settings where direct consumption data are limited or unavailable [9]. The probabilistic structure enables evaluation of exposure across the full intake distribution rather than reliance on single-point estimates.

This capability supports risk-informed planning, early screening and prioritization of nutrients or population groups. In addition, the model can be extended to assess combined exposure from dietary supplements and food sources or to explore changes in exposure under alternative scenarios, such as food fortification policies [30–33].

The probabilistic framework also facilitates integration with other assessment tools within broader risk assessment and regulatory impact evaluation processes [33–34]. In addition, it may serve as a preparatory step before conducting field-based surveys or surveillance studies to validate or further refine the modeled exposure estimates [34–36].

The model may also be further developed through the use of price elasticity studies related to dietary supplements in Saudi Arabia. Such data may support the development of algorithms that simulates product selection behavior in greater detail [37]. In addition, future studies may further examine consumer behavioral patterns and their potential influence on supplement intake variability.

Advantage and disadvantage

A key strength of the model is its explicit treatment of uncertainty and variability at the national population level, as well as its flexibility for integration with other datasets or modeling frameworks. Its probabilistic nature facilitates transparent exposure characterization while accounting for population heterogeneity.

However, the main limitation of the current application relates to the scenario assumptions and data sources rather than the modeling framework itself. The analysis was limited to adults, excluding children and individuals with specific medical conditions [4]. Therefore, the estimated exposure patterns may not fully represent all population subgroups. In addition, the exposure assessment relied on aggregated prevalence data and assumed independence between the modeled probability terms. Consequently, the estimated exposure levels should be interpreted as population-level approximations rather than individual-level assessments.

Importantly, this limitation reflects the scope of the analyzed scenario and the selected data source rather than a constraint of the modeling framework. The same probabilistic approach can be readily adapted to alternative target populations by substituting the current input distributions with probability or distributional data derived from other studies or datasets specific to the population of interest. Accordingly, the model framework remains applicable across a wide range of exposure scenarios, provided that appropriate input data are available.

Conclusions

This study demonstrates that dietary supplement exposure estimated using a probabilistic approach remains below established ULs under the evaluated scenario. These findings underscore the importance of integrating such estimates with additional data sources for comprehensive exposure assessment. Furthermore, this modeling framework serves as a valuable decision-support tool for regulatory planning and policy development, particularly in data-limited settings where national consumption surveys are unavailable.

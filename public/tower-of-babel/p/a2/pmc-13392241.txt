Predictive risk model for bone metastasis in intermediate-risk prostate cancer: a single-center retrospective analysis
Authors: Jianfeng Zhu, Cheng Shen, Ze Wang, Feixiang Chen, Tianle Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
To develop and validate a nomogram to identify patients with intermediate-risk prostate cancer who do not require a bone scan. Data from 305 patients with intermediate-risk prostate cancer who were hospitalized at the Department of Urology of the First People’s Hospital of Nantong between September 2023 and December 2025 and who underwent pretreatment bone imaging (99mTc-MDP scintigraphy) were included in this retrospective analysis. Patients were randomly assigned to training and internal validation groups at a ratio of 7:3. After the optimal predictive features were selected via minimum absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression, the predictive model was constructed using multiple logistic regression, and internal validation was performed with split-sample and bootstrap calibration. A total of 305 people with moderate-risk prostate cancer were included in this study. Of these, 54 (17.7%) had bone metastases, and 251 (82.3%) did not. Prostate-specific antigen (PSA), fibrinogen (FIB), hemoglobin, and the systemic immune-inflammatory index (SII) were incorporated into the final model. We thoroughly evaluated the performance of the model according to clinical utility (decision curve analysis), calibration (calibration curve, Hosmer–Lemeshow test), and discrimination (ROC analysis, AUC). In both the training (AUC 0.880, 95% CI 0.812–0.947) and validation (AUC 0.877, 95% CI 0.782–0.971) groups, the discrimination, calibration, and clinical utility of the model were excellent. The PSA, SII, FIB, and hemoglobin levels are independent predictive factors for bone metastasis in patients with intermediate-risk prostate cancer, and the constructed nomogram has good predictive value for identifying patients who do not need bone scans. A limitation of this study is its retrospective single-center design, which may lead to selection bias and limit the generalizability of the model.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-53745-4.

Introduction

In China, prostate cancer (PCa) is the second most prevalent cancer and the fifth leading cause of cancer-related death among older men. Its incidence is rising quickly1. One common outcome of the gradual progression of PCa is bone metastasis. The prognosis for patients with bone metastasis of PCa is typically much worse1. Research has indicated that the mortality rate of patients with PCa who have bone metastases is substantially greater than that of patients without bone metastases. Patients with bone metastases had a 1.5-fold greater mortality rate than those with lymph node metastases did, and their median survival and tumor-specific survival were reduced to 24 and 32 months, respectively2. Epidemiological data show that bone metastasis occurs in 5.3% of all patients with PCa and 3% of newly diagnosed patients with PCa, particularly in men with a prostate-specific antigen (PSA) concentration ranging from 10.1 to 19.9 ng/mL3,4. Furthermore, research has shown that among individuals at intermediate risk, bone metastases occur 0.8% of the time5. Bone scintigraphy remains the first-line diagnostic method for bone metastasis of PCa, despite the availability of more advanced imaging techniques such as PET/CT and MRI6. Clinical risk stratification is widely used to guide pretreatment bone imaging, but routine bone scans for all newly diagnosed PCa patients remain controversial worldwide6–9.

Current guidelines have reached a consensus on bone imaging for low-risk and high-risk PCa patients: bone scans are recommended for high-risk patients, while asymptomatic low-risk patients can safely omit imaging7,10. However, there is significant controversy regarding the indication of bone imaging for intermediate-risk PCa patients, with notable differences among major guidelines. The European Association of Urology (EAU) recommends bone imaging for patients with intermediate-risk PCa, especially those with a Gleason pattern of 4, and does not advise bone scans for asymptomatic patients with well or moderately differentiated tumors and a PSA concentration < 20 ng/mL. The National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) recommends bone scans for patients with intermediate-risk PCa who present with palpable lesions and a PSA concentration > 10 ng/mL11. In addition, bone scans are also considered for intermediate-risk patients with a PSA concentration of 10–20 ng/mL, a Gleason score of 7 (3 + 4 or 4 + 3), or clinical stage T2b–T2c disease who present with bone pain7,12. These heterogeneous guidelines result in inconsistent clinical decision-making for bone imaging in intermediate-risk PCa patients, highlighting the need for a targeted predictive tool to guide individualized imaging strategies7,12.

In recent years, systemic inflammatory markers have attracted increasing attention because of their close correlation with the prognosis and progression of malignant tumors. Markers such as the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), platelet-lymphocyte ratio (PLR), monocyte-lymphocyte ratio (MLR), and SII reflect the host immune status and the inflammatory microenvironment of tumors and have been confirmed to be associated with tumor invasiveness and metastasis in various malignancies13,14. By reflecting the host’s immunological status and the inflammatory state of the tumor microenvironment, these indicators may be relevant in the prediction of cancer metastasis risk15. However, studies on the correlation between systemic inflammatory markers and bone metastasis in intermediate-risk PCa are still scarce, and no consistent conclusions have been drawn, which limits their clinical application in this specific population.

Artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning technologies have been widely applied in cancer research and have shown significant advantages in preoperative diagnosis, prognostic assessment and survival prediction16,17. In the field of PCa bone metastasis prediction, previous radiomics-based studies have achieved good results: Zhang et al.18 constructed a radiomics nomogram combining imaging features and clinical indicators, which significantly improved the accuracy of bone metastasis prediction in patients with PCa; Nie et al.16 also developed a T2-MRI radiomics model for the initial diagnosis of bone metastasis of PCa. However, these studies focused on the overall PCa population and lacked specific research on patients with intermediate-risk PCa; to date, no machine learning model has been established to predict the risk of bone metastasis for this specific subpopulation.

Given the above clinical controversies and research gaps, this study aimed to develop and validate a nomogram based on systemic inflammatory and clinical markers for newly diagnosed patients with intermediate-risk PCa. The model is expected to stratify the risk of bone metastasis in this population, identify patients who can safely omit pretreatment bone scans, and provide a concise and practical tool for clinical decision-making, thereby reducing the overuse of bone imaging and optimizing medical resource allocation.

Patients and methods

Study design and participants

This retrospective single-center study was conducted to analyze the clinical data of male patients with newly diagnosed, histologically confirmed, intermediate-risk prostate cancer (PCa) who underwent transperineal prostate biopsy at the First People’s Hospital of Nantong from September 2023 to December 2025. The definition of intermediate-risk PCa was strictly based on the NCCN Clinical Practice Guidelines in Oncology (Prostate Cancer, Version 4.2023)11: a PSA concentration of 10.0–20.0 ng/mL, a Gleason score of 7 (3 + 4 or 4 + 3), and/or a clinical stage of T2b–T2c.

Favorable intermediate-risk PCa: PSA concentration of 10.0–20.0 ng/mL, Gleason score of 7 (3 + 4), and clinical stage of T2a–T2b; single adverse risk factor among the PSA level, Gleason score and clinical stage.

Unfavorable intermediate-risk PCa: PSA concentration of 10.0–20.0 ng/mL, Gleason score of 7 (4 + 3), and/or clinical stage of T2c; two or more adverse risk factors among the PSA concentration, Gleason score and clinical stage, with no high-risk features (PSA concentration > 20 ng/mL, Gleason score ≥ 8, and clinical stage ≥T3).

The inclusion criteria were as follows: PCa histopathologically confirmed via prostate biopsy; met the NCCN-defined favorable/unfavorable intermediate-risk PCa criteria; underwent a first-time prostate biopsy without prior antitumor treatment; underwent pretreatment 99mTc-MDP bone scintigraphy and complete laboratory examinations; and underwent radical prostatectomy with complete pathological follow-up data.

The exclusion criteria included a history of other malignant tumors or recurrent PCa; acute/chronic infections, autoimmune diseases, hematological diseases or recent use of anti-inflammatory/immunosuppressive drugs within 4 weeks; incomplete clinical, laboratory or imaging data; and refusal to participate in the study or loss to follow-up. After strict screening according to the above criteria, a total of 305 eligible patients were ultimately included in the study (Fig. 1).

Ethics and informed consent

This study was conducted in strict accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki (1975, revised 2024) and approved by the Medical Ethics Review Committee of the First People’s Hospital of Nantong (ethics approval no.: 2022KT100). Informed consent was waived because of the retrospective nature of the study, which used deidentified patient data, with no potential harm to participants. All data analyses complied with the ethical standards at the time of data collection.

Data source/measurement

An electronic medical record system was used to collect clinical data for every patient. The preoperative total prostate specific antigen (tPSA) level, preoperative free total prostate specific antigen (fPSA) level, T stage, and Gleason score were among the basic demographic data [such as age and body mass index (BMI)]. The NLR, MLR, PLR, and SII were among the systemic inflammatory marker data collected by standard preoperative blood tests.

Diagnosis of prostatic lesions and bone metastases

Prostate lesion diagnosis: All 305 patients underwent ultrasound-guided transperineal prostate biopsy, and the pathological diagnosis was completed by at least three senior pathologists in our hospital in accordance with the 2014 International Society of Urology and Pathology (ISUP) grading system to ensure diagnostic accuracy.

Bone metastasis diagnosis (primary study endpoint): Bone metastatic status was determined by 99mTc-MDP whole-body bone scintigraphy, the gold standard for the clinical diagnosis of bone metastasis. All bone scan images were independently evaluated by two senior nuclear medicine specialists with more than 5 years of clinical experience who were blinded to the clinical and laboratory data of the patients. Equivocal bone scan findings (e.g., solitary mild radioactive uptake without clear malignant features) were jointly adjudicated by a third senior nuclear medicine specialist; cases with inconsistent adjudicative conclusions were excluded from the study. Bone metastasis was defined as the presence of at least one focal abnormal radioactive uptake lesion confirmed as malignant by consensus adjudication, which was the primary endpoint of this study.

Model development

To screen the optimal predictive features and avoid the interference of redundant variables with model performance, we adopted a combined feature selection approach involving LASSO regression and the Boruta algorithm; the rationale for using dual techniques is their complementary advantages in feature selection.

(1) LASSO regression is a regularization method based on the L1 penalty, which is excellent at reducing high-dimensional data dimensionality, screening out variables with strong predictive value and avoiding overfitting by shrinking the regression coefficients of redundant variables to zero19. (2) The Boruta algorithm is a wrapper method based on random forests, which is superior in identifying all relevant predictive features by comparing the importance of the original features with that of random permuted features and avoids missing potential valuable variables20.

Feature selection implementation: (1) LASSO regression: The penalty parameter λ was optimized by 10-fold cross-validation on the basis of the minimum partial likelihood deviation, and variables with nonzero coefficients at the optimal λ were selected as candidate features; (2) in the Boruta algorithm, 100 random forest iterations were performed to evaluate the importance of all features, and features with statistically significant importance (P < 0.05) were identified as candidate features. Processing of inconsistent results: Features identified by both methods were defined as core predictive features; features identified by only one method were further validated by univariate logistic regression (P < 0.05 as the threshold), and only features with a significant statistical correlation with the study endpoint were included in the subsequent model construction.

Model construction and validation: On the basis of the final screened core features, a predictive nomogram was constructed using multiple logistic regression analysis. The enrolled patients were randomly divided into a training set (70%) and a validation set (30%) by stratified random sampling (according to bone metastasis status) to ensure the balance of baseline characteristics between the two sets. No external validation was performed in this study because of the single-center study design.

Statistical analysis

All the statistical analyses were performed using R statistical software (version 4.4.1), and a two-sided P < 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance. The sample size was estimated on the basis of the primary endpoint (bone metastasis incidence: 17.7%) and the general principle of logistic regression (EPV ≥ 10)21. A total of 54 bone metastasis events were observed in this study, and the final model included 4 predictive variables, resulting in an EPV of 13.5 (54/4). This value is higher than the critical value of 10, indicating that the sample size is sufficient and that the risk of model overfitting is effectively reduced. Continuous variables are expressed as the mean ± standard deviation (normally distributed) or median (interquartile range) (nonnormally distributed), and categorical variables are expressed as n (%). LASSO regression was used for dimensionality reduction and feature screening (glmnet package), and the Boruta algorithm was used for comprehensive feature identification (Boruta package). Multiple logistic regression was used to construct the predictive model, and backward elimination based on the Akaike information criterion (AIC) was used to optimize the model21. The discriminatory ability of the model was evaluated by a receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve and the area under the curve (AUC); the calibration ability was evaluated by a calibration curve, the Hosmer–Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test and the Brier score (500 bootstrap resamplings for internal calibration); and the clinical utility was evaluated by decision curve analysis (DCA) and a clinical impact curve (CIC).

The 17.7% bone metastasis incidence in our intermediate-risk PCa cohort is higher than the incidence (0.8%–5.0%) reported in most Western cohorts5,22. This discrepancy may be attributed to three potential factors: (1) Referral bias: As a tertiary general hospital, our center is the primary referral institution for urological malignancies in Nantong and surrounding areas, and the enrolled patients may have more aggressive clinical features than the general intermediate-risk PCa population does; (2) Imaging adjudication threshold: Our study adopted a rigorous consensus adjudication strategy for bone scan results (joint evaluation by three senior nuclear medicine specialists), which may identify more subtle bone metastatic lesions that were defined as equivocal or negative in other studies; (3) Regional epidemiological differences: The genetic background and diagnostic and treatment patterns of PCa patients in Chinese populations may differ from those in Western populations, which may lead to differences in the incidence of bone metastasis in intermediate-risk subgroups8,23.

Risk factors associated with bone metastases

The results of univariate logistic regression analysis for risk factors for bone metastasis in patients with intermediate-risk PCa are shown in Table 2. To screen the optimal predictive features for subsequent model construction, we first performed LASSO regression with 10-fold cross-validation (based on minimum partial likelihood deviation) to optimize the penalty parameter λ (Min λ as the screening criterion). LASSO regression identified 8 candidate variables with nonzero coefficients by adjusting the penalty coefficient (Fig. 2A), and the target covariates were further confirmed by the AUC of 10-fold cross-validation (Fig. 2B). The Boruta algorithm (100 random forest iterations) was subsequently used for comprehensive feature selection to validate the LASSO results (Fig. 2C). After cross-validation of the two methods and subsequent univariate logistic regression verification (P < 0.05), four independent clinical and laboratory indicators were ultimately identified as the core predictive features for bone metastasis in patients with intermediate-risk PCa: PSA, the SII, FIB, and Hb level.

A collinearity test confirmed that there was no significant multicollinearity among the four core features (all variance inflation factors [VIFs] < 5), and all four variables were included in the subsequent multivariate logistic regression model (Fig. 2D) with the full inclusion method.

Nomogram model for estimating the risk of bone metastasis

Using a stratified random sampling method, the enrolled patients were divided into a training set (70%, n = 213) and a hold-out internal validation set (30%, n = 92) according to bone metastasis status (Supplementary Data). On the basis of the four core predictive features (PSA, SII, FIB, and Hb) screened by the LASSO regression and Boruta algorithms, a nomogram for predicting the risk of bone metastasis in patients with intermediate-risk PCa was constructed (Fig. 3). The nomogram assigns a score (0–100) to each core feature according to its regression coefficient (i.e., the predictive weight for bone metastasis), with PSA having the highest predictive weight (the widest score range), which is consistent with the clinical understanding that PSA is the most important serological indicator for evaluating PCa progression and metastasis. The total score of an individual patient is the sum of the scores of the four features, and the corresponding probability of bone metastasis can be directly obtained from the nomogram, which provides a simple and intuitive quantitative tool for clinical decision-making.

Validation of the nomogram model

The discriminatory ability of the nomogram model was evaluated by ROC curve analysis, which revealed excellent performance in both the training and validation sets: the AUC of the training set was 0.880 (95% CI: 0.812–0.947; Fig. 4A), and the AUC of the internal validation set was 0.877 (95% CI: 0.782–0.971; Fig. 4B), indicating high accuracy in distinguishing patients with intermediate-risk PCa with and without bone metastasis.

Discussion

Bone imaging is frequently overused in newly diagnosed prostate cancer (PCa) patients worldwide24–26. Although 99mTc-MDP bone scintigraphy is simple, cost effective and associated with low radiation exposure, it can yield inconclusive results that may trigger unnecessary follow-up imaging and increase the economic burden on patients27. Restricting the overuse of bone scans not only reduces medical costs and conserves resources but also directs more advanced imaging and therapeutic resources to patients who are more likely to benefit—such as those with oligometastatic disease who may receive metastasis-directed therapy28–30. Our study constructed a nomogram for intermediate-risk PCa patients that can accurately stratify the risk of bone metastasis and identify patients who can safely omit pretreatment bone scans. This model is expected to provide a simple and practical clinical tool for individualized imaging decision-making and effectively reduce the overuse of bone scintigraphy in patients with intermediate-risk PCa, which is consistent with the clinical goal of optimizing medical resource allocation.

To further improve the clinical applicability and provide actionable decision-making criteria for clinical practice, we propose a clinically explicit bone metastasis risk cutoff value of 5% as the threshold for omitting pretreatment bone scans in patients with intermediate-risk prostate cancer. This cutoff value is determined on the basis of two core and evidence-based scientific rationales: (1) the Youden index of the ROC curve for the model reached its maximum value at the 5% risk threshold, corresponding to a sensitivity of 92.6% and a specificity of 78.1%, which ensures the maximum ability to identify patients with bone metastasis while minimizing the number of unnecessary bone scans and balancing clinical diagnostic safety and medical resource optimization; (2) the clinical decision curve analysis (DCA) results showed that the model yields the highest net clinical benefit for clinical decision-making in the 0–20% risk threshold range, with the 5% cutoff falling in the core of this clinically relevant range, which is consistent with routine clinical practice and decision-making logic.

Consistent with the findings of numerous previous studies31–33, our results confirmed that prostate-specific antigen (PSA) was the dominant predictive factor for bone metastasis in patients with intermediate-risk PCa, with the widest score range in the nomogram and the highest predictive weight. Oesterling et al.32 demonstrated in a 25-year study that the PSA concentration is a reliable predictor of bone metastasis in newly diagnosed PCa patients and that asymptomatic patients with a PSA concentration < 10 ng/mL do not require routine bone scans. Our findings further validate the core value of PSA in the risk stratification of bone metastasis in specific intermediate-risk PCa subgroups and supplement the quantitative correlation between PSA concentrations and bone metastasis risk in this population, which is consistent with the clinical understanding of PSA as the most important serological indicator for evaluating PCa progression.

The integration of the SII, FIB and Hb levels into the predictive model is biologically plausible, and the correlation between these indicators and bone metastasis of PCa is supported by existing basic and clinical research. Systemic inflammatory reactions are key components of the tumor microenvironment and are closely associated with tumor initiation, progression, invasion and metastasis34. The SII is a comprehensive inflammatory marker that integrates neutrophils, platelets and lymphocytes and can more comprehensively reflect the host’s immune-inflammatory status than single indicators such as the NLR and PLR can35,36. Our results confirmed that an elevated SII value is an independent risk factor for bone metastasis in patients with intermediate-risk PCa, which is consistent with the findings of previous studies showing that high SII values are associated with advanced T stage, lymph node metastasis and increased bone metastasis risk in patients with PCa17. These findings further support the important role of the systemic immune–inflammatory response in the bone metastasis of intermediate-risk PCa and provide a biological basis for the application of inflammatory markers in the risk stratification of this population.

Fibrinogen (FIB), a key coagulation marker synthesized in the liver, is closely associated with tumor progression and metastasis37–39. Prior studies have reported an association between pretreatment FIB levels and bone metastatic burden in unselected patients with PCa40,41, and Yu et al.40 reported that FIB has additional prognostic value for bone metastasis of PCa. Our study is the first to validate FIB as an independent predictor of bone metastasis in a specific intermediate-risk PCa subgroup and to further integrate it with clinical and other inflammatory indicators to construct a predictive nomogram. These findings supplement the clinical application value of FIB in the risk stratification of bone metastasis in patients with intermediate-risk PCa and provide a more targeted reference for the clinical application of coagulation–inflammatory markers in this specific population. The potential mechanism through which FIB promotes bone metastasis of PCa may involve the formation of platelet–fibrin–tumor cell aggregates, which increase the adhesion of tumor cells to vascular endothelial cells and facilitate metastatic spread while also promoting tumor cell survival and proliferation in the bone microenvironment42. In addition, our results confirmed that a low hemoglobin level is an independent risk factor for bone metastasis in intermediate-risk PCa patients, which is consistent with a meta-analysis of 60 clinical studies43 showing that anemia is closely associated with poor survival in cancer patients, including a 47% increased risk of death in anemic PCa patients. Cancer-related cachexia and secondary anemia caused by advanced tumor progression may be the main underlying mechanisms, and low hemoglobin levels may also reflect poor nutritional and immune status of patients, which further promotes tumor metastasis.

To further highlight the clinical value and incremental advantage of our constructed nomogram, we compared its performance with that of the current mainstream guideline-based bone scan strategies for intermediate-risk prostate cancer (EAU 2023 and NCCN 2023) in our study cohort, and the key differences in decision-making logic, clinical efficacy and safety are summarized as follows. The EAU 2023 guidelines recommend bone imaging for patients with intermediate-risk PCa who have a Gleason pattern of 4 and a PSA concentration > 20 ng/mL while suggesting omission of bone scans for asymptomatic patients with well/moderately differentiated tumors and a PSA concentration < 20 ng/mL. The NCCN 2023 guidelines suggest bone scans for patients with intermediate-risk PCa presenting with palpable lesions and a PSA concentration > 10 ng/mL or for those with a PSA concentration of 10–20 ng/mL, a Gleason score of 7 or clinical stage T2b–T2c with bone pain symptoms. Both guidelines adopt a categorical stratification strategy based on a few clinical indicators (PSA, Gleason score, and clinical stage) for bone scan decision-making, which is simple and easy to perform but lacks quantitative and individualized risk assessment for each patient, leading to either overuse of bone scans in low-risk subgroups or potential missed diagnoses in high-risk subgroups within patients with intermediate-risk PCa.

Although our study strictly excluded patients with acute/chronic infections, autoimmune diseases and recent use of anti-inflammatory drugs, there may still be unmeasured subclinical inflammatory confounders that were not fully excluded—such as mild chronic prostatitis, subclinical urinary tract infections or low-grade systemic inflammation associated with aging—which may affect the levels of the SII, FIB and other inflammatory coagulation indicators and thus potentially bias the study results. In addition, owing to the retrospective nature of the study, we lacked complete data on comorbidities (e.g., diabetes, hypertension, and cardiovascular disease) and lifestyle factors (e.g., smoking, alcohol consumption, and physical activity), which are known to be associated with systemic inflammation and tumor progression44,45. Diabetes and hypertension, for example, can induce chronic low-grade inflammation and affect coagulation function, which may indirectly influence FIB and SII levels and the risk of bone metastasis of PCa. Although we adjusted for age and BMI in the study design, residual confounding from these unmeasured variables may still exist, which is a limitation of the retrospective single-center study design.

Our study adopted 99mTc-MDP bone scintigraphy as the gold standard for diagnosing bone metastasis, which is the most widely used clinical imaging method for PCa bone metastasis screening6. However, compared with PSMA PET/CT, which is increasingly used in contemporary clinical practice, bone scintigraphy has certain inherent limitations: First, bone scintigraphy is a metabolic imaging method based on osteoblastic activity, which has low sensitivity for the diagnosis of early osteolytic bone metastases and micrometastases and may miss some early bone metastatic lesions; second, bone scintigraphy has poor specificity, and benign bone lesions (e.g., osteoporosis, osteoarthritis, and bone trauma) can also cause abnormal radioactive uptake, leading to false-positive results27,46; third, PSMA PET/CT has higher spatial resolution and can simultaneously detect lymph node metastasis and distant soft tissue metastasis, providing a more comprehensive evaluation of tumor staging29,30. The selection of bone scintigraphy as the diagnostic gold standard in our study may lead to the underdiagnosis of some early bone metastases or the overdiagnosis of benign bone lesions, which may affect the accuracy of bone metastasis diagnosis and the performance of the predictive model. In future studies, we will perform PSMA PET/CT to verify the model’s predictive value, which will help to further improve the accuracy and clinical generalizability of the model.

In addition, the 17.7% incidence of bone metastasis in our intermediate-risk PCa cohort was significantly higher than the incidence of 0.8%–5.0% reported in most Western cohorts 5,31. This discrepancy may be attributed to three main factors: first, referral bias—as a tertiary general hospital, our center is the primary referral institution for urological malignancies in Nantong and surrounding areas, and the enrolled patients may have more aggressive clinical features (e.g., higher PSA levels and more aggressive Gleason patterns) than the general intermediate-risk PCa population does; second, in terms of the imaging adjudication threshold, our study adopted a rigorous consensus adjudication strategy (joint evaluation by three senior nuclear medicine specialists) for equivocal bone scan results, which may identify subtle bone metastatic lesions that were defined as negative in other studies.

Considering the many limitations of this study is essential. First, the retrospective nature of the analysis introduces inherent selection and information bias. Second, the single-center study design cannot eliminate regional referral bias. Our center is a tertiary referral hospital for urological malignancies in Nantong and surrounding areas, and the enrolled patients have more aggressive clinical features (e.g., higher incidence of bone metastasis) than the general intermediate-risk PCa population, which may overestimate the predictive value of the model in the general population. Multicenter external validation with an independent cohort is urgently needed to confirm the universality of the model. Third, there is a potential overfitting risk due to multiple feature selection steps. Although we adopted 10-fold cross-validation, bootstrap internal calibration and events-per-variable (EPV = 13.5) calculations to reduce overfitting, the combined use of LASSO regression and the Boruta algorithm for feature selection may have led to overoptimization of the model for the current single-center dataset, and the predictive performance of the model may be attenuated when applied to an independent external population. Fourth, comparisons with guideline-based bone scan strategies were lacking. This study constructed and validated only a nomogram model but did not compare its clinical performance (e.g., diagnostic accuracy and number of avoidable bone scans) with that of the current guideline-based strategies (EAU/NCCN criteria) for bone scan indication; such a head-to-head comparison is necessary to further verify the added value of the nomogram over the existing clinical guidelines. Fifth, cost-effectiveness analysis was lacking. The study focuses on the diagnostic performance of the nomogram in reducing unnecessary bone scans but does not include a quantitative cost-effectiveness analysis (e.g., the reduction in medical costs from avoidable bone scans and the economic burden of follow-up imaging caused by false results) combined with the local medical resource pricing system; this limits further evaluation of the economic value of the model and its promotion in primary medical institutions.

In addition, we validated only the nomogram model with internal validation methods (7:3 split-sample validation + 500 bootstrap resamplings) and did not perform external validation with an independent multicenter cohort, which is a major limitation of the study design. To confirm the universality of the model across regions and populations, we will subsequently collaborate with 3–5 tertiary hospitals in the Yangtze River Delta region to conduct a large-sample (n ≥ 500) prospective study, supplement the external validation cohort data, and further optimize the model on the basis of multicenter data.

Conclusion

In this study, we developed a predictive model for bone metastasis in patients with intermediate-risk prostate cancer and discovered that PSA, the SII, FIB, and hemoglobin levels were the four independent parameters that affected bone metastasis. It is advised that clinical personnel employ this risk prediction model to carry out focused treatments aimed at reducing excessive use of bone scans.

Association between the triglyceride-glucose index and urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio in patients with early-onset type 2 diabetes
Authors: Wei Wang, Wenjing Dong, Shiju Yan, Di Sun, Lingyun Song, Zhe Xue, Ying Zhou, Qiaoling Chen, Ping Pang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPatients with early-onset type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) face a significantly elevated risk of developing diabetic kidney disease (DKD) in the early stages, attributable to their prolonged disease duration. The urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR) serves as a critical marker for screening incipient renal injury. While insulin resistance (IR) is a pivotal driver of this pathological progression, the triglyceride-glucose (TyG) index, a simple and effective surrogate marker for IR, has not been thoroughly investigated in this specific population. The association between TyG index and UACR, along with its potential discriminatory ability for early renal damage in patients with early-onset T2DM, remained unclear.ObjectiveThis study aimed to explore the correlation between the TyG index and UACR in patients with early-onset type 2 diabetes.Research design and methodsA dual-center, retrospective cross-sectional study was conducted. Clinical data from a total of 596 adult patients (aged 18–40 years) with early-onset type 2 diabetes mellitus, admitted to the First Medical Center of the Chinese PLA General Hospital and Hainan Hospital of the Chinese PLA General Hospital between January 2012 and December 2022, were collected and retrospectively analyzed. Participants were stratified into three groups based on the tertiles of the TyG index: Q1 (< 7.67), Q2 (7.67–8.33), and Q3 (> 8.33). They were also categorized into two groups according to UACR levels: <30 mg/g and ≥ 30 mg/g. The association between TyG index, traditional glucose/lipid parameters, and elevated UACR (≥ 30 mg/g) was assessed using correlation analysis and binary logistic regression models. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were plotted to evaluate the discriminatory ability of these indicators for elevated UACR. Subgroup analyses were further performed based on gender, blood pressure status, and estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR).ResultsThe mean age of the 596 participants was 31.78 ± 9.99 years. A significant positive correlation was observed between the TyG index and UACR (r = 0.250, P < 0.001). Binary logistic regression analysis revealed that after adjustment for multiple confounding factors, patients in the highest TyG tertile (Q3) had a 1.366-fold increased risk of elevated UACR (≥ 30 mg/g) compared to those in the lowest tertile (Q1) (OR: 1.366, 95% CI: 1.015–1.838, P = 0.039). The discriminatory ability of TyG index for elevated UACR was evaluated using the receiver operating characteristic curve, yielding an area under the curve of 0.649 (95% CI: 0.609–0.687). At the optimal cut-off value of 8.18, the sensitivity and specificity were 69.47% and 61.08%, respectively. Notably, the TyG index demonstrated superior discriminatory efficacy compared to traditional lipid parameters, including triglycerides, total cholesterol, LDL-C, and HDL-C.ConclusionIn patients with early-onset type 2 diabetes, an elevated TyG index was independently associated with increased UACR levels and served as a potential indicator for early diabetic kidney injury. Specifically, for individuals aged 18–40 years, a TyG index exceeding 8.18 was associated with an elevated association of developing diabetic kidney disease.Clinical trial registrationNot applicable.Keywords: Triglyceride-glucose index, Urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio, Diabetic kidney disease, Early-onset type 2 diabetes

Introduction

In recent years, the prevalence of early-onset type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), defined as diagnosis between 18 and 40 years of age, has been rising steadily worldwide. This trend towards a younger age of onset represents one of the most critical public health challenges globally [1, 2]. Compared to individuals with later-onset T2DM or type 1 diabetes, patients with early-onset T2DM experience a more rapid disease progression and face a significantly elevated risk of both microvascular and macrovascular complications [3–5]. Diabetic kidney disease (DKD) stands as one of the most prevalent microvascular complications in this population and is the leading cause of end-stage renal disease [4]. The urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR) serves as the clinical gold standard for the early screening of DKD and is a key diagnostic criterion. It sensitively reflects the degree of glomerular filtration barrier injury, making it a pivotal indicator for assessing the onset and progression of DKD [6].

Insulin resistance (IR) not only constitutes the core pathophysiological basis of early-onset T2DM but also manifests with greater severity and aggressiveness in these patients, serving as a key driver of their adverse clinical outcomes [7]. The triglyceride-glucose (TyG) index, a novel surrogate marker for IR, is calculated from fasting plasma glucose (FPG) and triglyceride (TG) levels. It offers significant advantages including ease of detection, low cost, high stability, and requires no complex procedures like the hyper insulinemic-euglycemic clamp test [8]. Extensive research, both domestically and internationally, has established an association between the TyG index and the risk of elevated UACR in the general population and in patients with later-onset diabetes [9, 10]. For instance, a study by Xiao et al. found that a higher TyG index was associated with an increased risk of chronic kidney disease in an elderly Chinese community population aged 45 and above, independent of established risk factors [11]. However, the relationship between the TyG index, conventional glucolipid parameters such as glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c), total cholesterol (TC), and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), and UACR remained unclear specifically in the distinct population with early-onset T2DM. Therefore, this study investigated the association between early-onset T2DM and the TyG index, aiming to provide a basis for the clinical prevention and management of early-onset DKD, as well as for the comprehensive management of diabetes and its complications.

Patients and methods

Study subjects

A total of 596 patients with early-onset T2DM, aged between 18 and 40 years and with a disease duration of less than 5 years, were enrolled in this dual-center retrospective study. These patients were admitted to the First Medical Center and Hainan Hospital of Chinese PLA General Hospital between January 2012 and December 2022. Inclusion criteria: ① Diagnosis of T2DM according to the World Health Organization (WHO) 1999 diagnostic criteria [12]; ② Age of onset between 18 and 40 years; ③ Disease duration of less than 5 years. Exclusion criteria: ① Use of lipid-lowering agents, SGLT2 inhibitors, angiotensin II receptor blockers, angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors and other medication influencing UACR within the preceding 6 months; ② Presence of severe cardiac, cerebral, hepatic, renal, or other organic diseases, or malignancy; ③ Incomplete or missing clinical data. See Fig. 1 for details.

Flowchart of patient selection

Data were manually abstracted by two investigators (WW, DW) from the electronic medical record. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Hainan Hospital of Chinese PLA General Hospital.

Study parameters

Demographic and clinical data, including sex, age at disease onset, disease duration, smoking status, drinking status, family history of diabetes, history of hypertension, history of fatty liver disease, blood pressure, height, weight, body mass index (BMI), waistline and hipline, were collected from all participants via the electronic medical record system.

Venous blood samples were obtained after an overnight fast. Biochemical parameters, including fasting plasma glucose, fasting insulin, fasting C-peptide, HbA1c, TG, TC, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), LDL-C, UACR, serum creatinine (Scr), uric acid (UA), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), and aspartate aminotransferase (AST), were measured using an automated biochemical analyzer in a standardized laboratory.

Calculation

Estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate (eGFR)(mL/min per 1.73m2)=175* (Scr in mg/dL) -1.154*age (year) − 0.203* (0.742 for women)

BMI (kg/m2) =weight (kg)/height2 (m)

TG/HDL-C ratio = TG (mmol/L)/HDL-C (mmol/L) [13]

TyG index = ln[triglycerides(mg/dL) * glucose (mg/dL)/2] [14]

Homeostasis model assessment 2 (HOMA2) was used to evaluate IR in patients with early-onset T2DM. With the help of the HOMA2 calculator from www.Ocdem.ox.ac.k, insulin resistance index (HOMA2-IR) was calculated using FCP for patients treated with insulin and Fins for patients without insulin treatment.

Grouping

Participants were categorized into two groups according to the UACR based on the kidney disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) Chronic Kidney Disease guideline [15]: <30 mg/g and ≥ 30 mg/g. The TyG index was stratified into tertiles as follows: Q1 (< 7.67), Q2 (7.67–8.33), and Q3 (> 8.33).

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software (version 26.0). Normally distributed continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (± s), and comparisons between two groups or among multiple groups were conducted using the independent samples t-test or one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), respectively. Non-normally distributed continuous variables were presented as median with interquartile range [M (QL, QU)] and compared using nonparametric tests. Categorical variables were reported as frequencies and percentages (%) and analyzed using the chi-squared test. The correlation between the TyG index and UACR was assessed using Spearman’s rank correlation and partial correlation analyses. Participants were dichotomized according to UACR levels ( < 30 mg/g and ≥ 30 mg/g). Binary logistic regression analysis was employed to evaluate the association between the TyG index and elevated UACR. Three sequential models were constructed to control for potential confounding factors: Model 1 was unadjusted; Model 2 was adjusted for age, sex, and disease duration; Model 3 was further adjusted for HbA1c, FPG, BMI, eGFR, AST, ALT, systolic blood pressure, and diastolic blood pressure. ROC curve analysis was performed to assess the discriminatory ability of the TyG index and traditional lipid parameters for elevated UACR. The area under the curve (AUC) was calculated to evaluate sensitivity and specificity, and the optimal cut-off value was determined using the Youden index. Subgroup analyses were further conducted based on sex, systolic blood pressure, and eGFR to explore the consistency of the association between the TyG index and elevated UACR. A two-tailed P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics of study subjects

A total of 596 participants were enrolled in this study, with a mean age of 31.35 ± 5.86 years. The cohort consisted of 460 males (77.2%) and 136 females (22.8%). Compared to patients with early-onset T2DM and UACR < 30 mg/g, those with UACR ≥ 30 mg/g demonstrated a significantly higher prevalence of family history of diabetes (P < 0.05). In addition, levels of systolic blood pressure, diastolic blood pressure, BMI, TyG index, TG, TC and HOMA2-IR were significantly elevated in the elevated UACR group (all P < 0.05). No statistically significant differences were observed between the two groups with respect to other clinical or biochemical parameters (P > 0.05) (Table 1 and Fig. 2).

Correlation of TyG index and other glucolipid parameters with UACR

Spearman correlation analysis was performed to evaluate the associations between the TyG index, other glucolipid parameters (including TG, TC, HDL-C, LDL-C, HbA1c, FBG, TG/HDL-C ratio, and HOMA2-IR) with UACR. The results demonstrated with the exception of HDL-C and LDL-C, TyG index and all other glucolipid parameters were positively correlated with UACR (P < 0.05). After adjusting for potential confounding glucolipid parameters (TG, TG/HDL-C ratio, HbA1c and FBG) using partial correlation analysis, the TyG index remained significantly correlated with UACR levels (P < 0.05) (Table 2).

Baseline characteristics of patients with early-onset T2DM stratified by TyG index tertiles

A total of 596 patients were stratified into three groups according to tertiles of the TyG index: Q1 (< 7.67, n = 199, 33.3%), Q2 (7.67–8.33, n = 198, 33.3%), and Q3 (> 8.33, n = 199, 33.3%). The Q1 group comprised 147 males (73.9%) and 52 females (26.1%), with a mean age of 31.78 ± 0.41 years. The Q2 group included 149 males (75.3%) and 49 females (24.7%), with a mean age of 31.15 ± 0.42 years. The Q3 group consisted of 164 males (82.4%) and 35 females (17.6%), with a mean age of 31.11 ± 0.41 years. As the TyG index increased, significant upward trends were observed in the prevalence of fatty liver disease, SBP, BMI, FBP, waistline, hipline, UA, TC, TG, LDL-C, AST, ALT, TG/HDL-C, UACR and HOMA2-IR (all P < 0.05). Conversely, HDL-C exhibited a significant decreasing trend across TyG tertiles (P < 0.05). No statistically significant differences were detected among three groups with respect to other demographic or clinical parameters (all P > 0.05) (Table 3).

Association of TyG index, glycemic and lipid parameters with elevated UACR in patients with early-onset T2DM

Binary logistic regression analyses were performed to further examine the associations of the TyG index and conventional glucolipid parameters with elevated UACR (≥ 30 mg/g). In the unadjusted Model 1, elevated TG levels were significantly associated with an increased association of elevated UACR. Compared with patients having TG < 1.7 mmol/L, the odds ratios (ORs) for those with TG 1.7–2.2 mmol/L and TG > 2.2 mmol/L were 2.078 (95% CI: 1.088–3.696, P = 0.027) and 1.969 (95% CI: 1.169–3.317, P = 0.011), respectively. For TC, relative to patients with TC < 5.2 mmol/L, those with TC 5.2–6.1 mmol/L did not show a statistically significant association (OR = 1.556, 95% CI: 0.893–2.710, P = 0.119), whereas patients with TC > 6.1 mmol/L exhibited a significantly elevated association (OR = 2.354, 95% CI: 1.311–4.228, P = 0.004). Additionally, compared with participants in the lowest TyG tertile (Q1), those in the middle (Q2) and highest (Q3) tertiles were significantly associated with elevated UACR, with ORs of 1.589 (95% CI: 1.051–1.874, P = 0.043) and 1.780 (95% CI: 1.043–3.038, P = 0.035), respectively. No significant associations were observed for LDL-C, FBG, HbA1c, or HDL-C with elevated UACR in Model 1 (all P > 0.05).

After further adjustment for potential confounders in Model 3 (including age, sex, disease duration, HbA1c, FPG, BMI, eGFR, AST, ALT, SBP, and DBP), the TyG index remained independently associated with elevated UACR. Specifically, compared with the Q1 group, patients in the Q3 group had a 1.366-fold increased risk of elevated UACR (OR = 1.366, 95% CI: 1.015–1.838, P = 0.039). Similarly, TC > 6.1 mmol/L remained significantly associated with elevated UACR (OR = 2.095, 95% CI: 1.114–3.941, P = 0.022) relative to TC < 5.2 mmol/L. No statistically significant associations were detected across the subgroups of LDL-C, HDL-C, TG, FBG, or HbA1c with elevated UACR in model 3 (all P > 0.05) (Table 4).

Discriminatory ability of the TyG index and other lipid parameters for elevated UACR association in patients with early-onset T2DM

To further evaluate the discriminatory ability of the TyG index and conventional lipid parameters for the association of elevated UACR (≥ 30 mg/g) in patients with early-onset type 2 diabetes, ROC curve analysis was performed. The TyG index yielded an AUC of 0.649 (95%CI: 0.609–0.687, P < 0.01), with a sensitivity of 69.47% and a specificity of 61.08% (Fig. 3). Notably, the TyG index demonstrated superior discriminatory ability for elevated UACR compared to all other lipid parameters examined.

To verify the robustness of the primary findings, the association between the TyG index and elevated UACR was further examined across different subgroups after adjusting for potential confounders. Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, this study was the first to systematically investigate the association between the TyG index and UACR specifically in patients with early-onset T2DM. Our findings demonstrated that an elevated TyG index was independently associated with increased UACR levels in early-onset T2DM, and a TyG index exceeding 8.18 identified individuals at heightened association for diabetic kidney injury. These results suggested that IR not only constituted a core pathophysiological feature of early-onset T2DM but could also served as a critical driver of subclinical diabetic kidney injury, representing an early stage of DKD.

Diabetes mellitus is a chronic metabolic disorder characterized by dysregulation of glucose, lipid, and protein metabolism, resulting from the interaction of genetic and environmental factors. Early-onset T2DM, defined as T2DM diagnosed in individuals under 40 years of age, has exhibited a rising global prevalence. Compared with late-onset T2DM, this early-onset phenotype follows a more aggressive progression, with earlier onset of diabetes-related complications and reduced life expectancy [3, 16, 17]. DKD, a leading cause of chronic kidney disease and end-stage renal failure, necessitates early identification of renal injury risk to improve long-term prognosis. Evidence indicated that patients with early-onset T2DM presented with more severe glomerular lesions and were at heightened risk for albuminuria progression and DKD [5]. A substantial body of research has established the UACR as a sensitive, convenient marker for screening early renal injury. It effectively stratified cardiovascular and chronic kidney risk in T2DM, guided therapeutic decisions, and facilitated monitoring of treatment response, serving as a practical tool for personalized pharmacotherapy in patients with chronic kidney disease [18, 19]. In the present study, we identified that family history of diabetes, systolic and diastolic blood pressure, BMI, TyG index, TC, TG and HOMA2-IR were all significantly associated with elevated UACR. IR operated as a central pathogenic driver in this context [20–22], leading to renal microvascular inflammation, endothelial dysfunction, and structural remodeling, ultimately manifesting as increased urinary albumin excretion [22–24]. Elevated UACR not only signified established nephropathy but also reflected systemic endothelial damage and portended a markedly increased risk of cardiovascular events. Therefore, comprehensive management targeting body weight, blood pressure, glycemic control, and lipid profile are imperative to prevent UACR elevation and preserve both renal and cardiovascular health.

IR and pancreatic β-cell dysfunction are two fundamental mechanisms underlying the pathogenesis of T2DM. Recent evidence identified TyG index as a simple and reliable surrogate marker for IR, with elevated TyG index reflecting aggravated glucolipid metabolic disturbances [25]. Our present study further demonstrated that a higher TyG index was independently associated with elevated UACR in patients with early-onset T2DM, suggesting that IR could constitute a key pathophysiological mechanism driving early renal injury in this population. Previous studies predominantly focus on classical risk factors for diabetic kidney disease, such as chronic hyperglycemia, hypertension, and disease duration. However, patients with early-onset T2DM frequently present with pronounced IR and lipid metabolic abnormalities at the time of diagnosis, yet whether the trajectory of renal injury in this distinct population followed a unique pattern remained inadequately elucidated. Notably, in our analysis, the positive association between the TyG index and UACR persisted even after comprehensive adjustment for confounding factors including HbA1c, systolic blood pressure, eGFR and diabetes duration. This finding strongly supported the notion that IR itself functioned as an independent renal damaging factor, exerting effects beyond those traditional risk factors. This observation could partially explain the clinical phenomenon whereby certain patients with early-onset diabetes exhibited progressive albuminuria despite achieving relatively satisfactory glycemic control. The underlying mechanisms likely involved sustained, systemic injurious pathways directly triggered by insulin resistance, which operate in parallel with—or even independent of—hyperglycemia-mediated renal injury cascades.

From a pathophysiological perspective, our findings aligned closely with the conceptual frameworks of “metabolic inflammation” and “lip toxicity”. The TyG index simultaneously captured hypertriglyceridemia and hyperglycemic burden, both of which converged to exacerbate renal metabolic dysregulation. In the setting of IR, excessive circulating free fatty acids and lipid intermediates underwent ectopic deposition in renal tissue, inciting lip toxicity that triggered local oxidative stress and chronic low-grade inflammation—a proposed pathogenic mechanism underlying diabetic kidney injury and renal dysfunction [26–29]. Concurrently, hyperinsulinemia could directly stimulate glomerular mesangial cell proliferation and potentiate the effects of angiotensin II, collectively contributing to intraglomerular hypertension, increased capillary wall tension, and altered filtration barrier permeability, ultimately manifesting as elevated UACR [30–32]. Our data provided direct population-based evidence substantiating this interconnected series of mechanistic pathways. The present study extended the clinical utility of TyG index, a simple, cost-effective biomarker, to the realm of early renal injury assessment in patients with early-onset T2DM. Compared with HOMA-IR, TyG index obviated the need for insulin measurement, rendering it more accessible and practical for implementation in primary care settings and large-scale population screening. Our findings suggested that routine assessment of TyG index in individuals with early-onset T2DM could facilitate the identification of patients with more pronounced metabolic dysregulation and higher risk of subclinical renal injury. For these high-risk individuals, intensified UACR monitoring will be warranted. Moreover, therapeutic strategies should extend beyond glycemic control alone to embrace a comprehensive management paradigm centered on ameliorating insulin resistance and correcting lipid metabolism abnormalities. This included prioritized use of glucose-lowering agents with proven insulin-sensitizing or kidney-protective effects, such as sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors and glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists.

Furthermore, our binary regression analysis demonstrated that TyG index remained significantly associated with UACR even after comprehensive adjustment for traditional risk factors including age, disease duration, blood pressure and glycated hemoglobin. This finding further underscored the independent role of IR in the early pathogenesis of DKD. The underlying mechanisms were multifactorial. IR contributed to intraglomerular hypertension and hyperfiltration, potentiated inflammatory responses and oxidative stress, and subsequently disrupted the glomerular filtration barrier, culminating in increased urinary albumin excretion [33, 34]. Additionally, IR was frequently accompanied with lipid metabolism disturbances—notably hypertriglyceridemia—which could directly or indirectly propagate renal injury [35, 36]. Accordingly, in patients with early-onset T2DM, systematic evaluation of IR status should be integrated into routine clinical assessment, even in the absence of overt albuminuria, and IR itself should be considered a pivotal therapeutic target for early intervention.

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, the dual-center, retrospective cross-sectional design precluded any causal inference regarding the relationship between the TyG index and early-onset T2DM. Second, the relatively modest sample size might limit the generalizability of our findings. Future multicenter, large-scale prospective studies would be warranted to validate the predictive value of the TyG index for diabetic kidney disease and to assess its potential role in guiding clinical interventions. Moreover, although UACR was a widely accepted and practical biomarker for early renal injury, it was subject to variability influenced by multiple physiological and pathological factors. Subsequent investigations incorporating more precise measures of renal function, such as estimated glomerular filtration rate trajectories or novel biomarkers of kidney injury, are needed to corroborate our observations. In addition, male participants constituted 77% of the total sample size. This uneven gender distribution may affect the generalizability of our findings to female populations. Therefore, large-scale studies with more balanced gender proportions are warranted to further validate and clarify sex-specific discrepancies. Finally, the underlying molecular mechanisms linking insulin resistance, the TyG index, and renal injury in early-onset T2DM remained incompletely elucidated and merit further exploration through basic and translational research.

Conclusions

This study identified a significant positive association between the TyG index and UACR in patients with early-onset T2DM. Enhancing the surveillance and targeted intervention of the TyG index could facilitate the early detection, prevention, and management of DKD, thereby improving long-term clinical outcomes in this vulnerable population. Clinicians are encouraged to incorporate TyG index assessment alongside conventional diabetes risk factors to optimize risk stratification and enable more precise, evidence-based therapeutic decision-making for patients with early-onset T2DM.

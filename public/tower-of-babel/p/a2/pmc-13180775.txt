Risk factors and prediction model for post-polypectomy metachronous colorectal adenoma
Authors: Jiuyue Ma, Qian Zhang, Jiayi Su, Yun Wang, Wenkun Li, Minsi Zhou, Haiyun Shi, Kuiliang Liu, Jing Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEndoscopic resection of colorectal adenomas helps reduce colorectal cancer mortality. However, post-polypectomy metachronous adenomas may reduce the sufficiency of colonoscopy scanning.AimTo explore the risk factors for post-polypectomy metachronous colorectal adenoma and establish a risk prediction model.MethodsThis retrospective cohort study included patients who underwent colonoscopy at Beijing Friendship Hospital from January 2013 to January 2023. Data on patients’ demographics, laboratory results, colonoscopy findings, and pathology reports were collected. The enrolled patients were randomly divided into a training set and a validation set in a 7:3 ratio. LASSO analysis was employed to identify risk factors for metachronous adenomas. Based on these risk factors, a Cox regression model was used to create a risk prediction model. The C-index was calculated to assess the model’s prediction accuracy, while time-dependent ROC (td-ROC) curves and calibration curves evaluated model predictive performance. Introduction

Colorectal carcinoma (CRC) is the third most common malignant tumor worldwide and the second leading cause of cancer-related deaths [1]. The mortality rate of CRC is approximately 9.67 per 100,000 in the Chinese population, ranking fifth among cancer-related deaths [2]. The development of CRC generally follows the adenoma-to-carcinoma sequence, and colonoscopy screening can reduce CRC mortality risk by 18% [3]. Therefore, early identification and removal of colorectal adenomas are important methods to reduce CRC mortality risk.

However, detection of colorectal metachronous adenomas is common after endoscopic adenoma resection. It was reported that the cumulative incidence of advanced metachronous tumors at five and ten years after colonoscopic adenoma resection ranges from 5.7%–10% to 11%–17%, respectively [4]. According to published clinical guidelines, risk stratification for patients to select appropriate colonoscopy surveillance intervals is recommended. Setting appropriate surveillance intervals can help to reduce unnecessary colonoscopy examinations, minimizing patients’ discomfort and potential harm and achieving cost-effectiveness [5].

Many studies have developed risk prediction models based on identified risk factors to better stratify the risk of metachronous adenomas in post-polypectomy patients. Facciorusso et al. suggested that adenoma size, the number of advanced adenomas, and pathological grade were associated with advanced metachronous adenomas in an Italian population. The scoring model was developed using regression coefficients, and the area under the ROC curve (AUC) was 0.81 (95% CI: 0.72–0.86), indicating good predictive capability [6]. Liu et al. conducted a prospective study using Lasso and Bayesian model averaging (BMA) methods to select risk factors and developed a predictive model including polyp location, history of polyps, age, number of resected polyps, size, and pathological type [7]. Although some predictive models for the risk of metachronous adenoma have been developed, the factors included are relatively insufficient and do not comprehensively account for possible risk factors. Additionally, the predictive capability of these models needs improvement. Research on risk factors and predictive models for metachronous adenomas in the Chinese population is limited and warrants further investigation.

This study was based on a real-world Chinese population, aiming to explore risk factors for post-polypectomy metachronous adenomas and to develop a risk prediction model. By providing evidence-based guidance for patient risk stratification, this study could improve clinicians’ confidence in setting appropriate surveillance intervals, helping in improving cost-effectiveness of colonoscopy surveillance.

Methods

Population

Patients who underwent colonoscopic polypectomy at Beijing Friendship Hospital between January 2013 and January 2023 were included. The following inclusion and exclusion criteria were: Inclusion criteria: (1) diagnosed with colorectal adenoma according to pathology reports; (2) pathology confirms complete removal of the adenoma; (3) underwent surveillance colonoscopy in Beijing Friendship Hospital 3 months after baseline polypectomy; exclusion criteria: (1) Age < 18 years; (2) Baseline colonoscopy pathology confirmed advanced colorectal cancer; (3) inflammatory bowel disease (ulcerative colitis and Crohn’s disease); (4) history of colorectal surgery; (5) familial adenomatous polyposis, Peutz-Jeghers syndrome, hyperplastic polyp syndrome; (6) missing clinical and pathology data. Patients were randomly divided into a training set and a validation set in a 7:3 ratio. Regarding sample size justification, we applied the standard rule of thumb for Cox proportional hazards models, which recommends a minimum of 10 events per predictor variable (EPV ≥ 10) to maintain adequate statistical power and prevent overfitting.

Data description

All patients’ information and clinical data were obtained from the electronic medical record system. A double-checking method was used to reduce manual recording errors during data collection. Baseline was defined as the time of the index colonoscopy during which patients underwent adenoma resection. Metachronous adenoma was described when a colorectal adenoma found on follow-up colonoscopy more than 3 months after complete adenoma resection (postoperative pathological confirmation of completed resection). Data included in the analysis comprised: (1) Clinical information: sex, age, smoking history, alcohol consumption history, history of hypertension, history of type 2 diabetes, and body mass index (BMI). (2) Colonoscopy and pathology data: baseline size, number, and location of colorectal adenomas, pathological type, pathological grade, and quality of bowel preparation. (3) Laboratory data: fasting serum triglycerides (TG), fasting serum total cholesterol (TC), fasting low-density lipoprotein (LDL), fasting high-density lipoprotein (HDL), and fasting blood glucose (GLU). Smoking and alcohol habits were obtained from personal histories and categorized into smoking/non-smoking and drinking/non-drinking groups. The quality of bowel preparation was graded using the Boston Bowel Preparation Scale (BBPS) score. Score 2 and 3 were classified as “good quality,” and score 0 and 1 as “poor quality”. Given that the absence of bowel preparation information in colonoscopy reports may imply incomplete assessment or operator error—thereby compromising the reliability of the reports and potentially influencing the occurrence of metachronous adenomas—we categorized such missing data as a separate ‘Unknown’ group to evaluate its impact on the outcome. Adenoma locations were divided into the proximal colon (cecum, ascending colon, hepatic flexure, and transverse colon), distal colon (splenic flexure, descending colon, and sigmoid colon), and rectum. Pathological grade of adenomas was classified into high-grade dysplasia and low-grade dysplasia group. If multiple adenomas were resected at baseline colonoscopy, the size recorded was that of the largest one; the pathological type was taken from any high-grade dysplasia was identified among them, and the adenoma location was recorded based on the presence of high-grade dysplasia or the location of the largest one. Advanced adenomas were defined as those meeting one or more criteria: (1) adenoma diameter > 10 mm; (2) villous or tubulovillous adenoma; (3) high-grade dysplasia. BMI was calculated based on medical records categorizing patients into underweight (< 18.5), normal (18.5–23.9), and overweight (≥ 24) groups. Histories of hypertension and type 2 diabetes were categorized into yes/no groups. Laboratory results, including TC, TG, HDL, LDL and GLU, were obtained from the reports taken two months before baseline colonoscopy and sorted into groups according to reference values. Fasting blood glucose was categorized into normal and impaired glucose tolerance group.

Study outcome

The outcome was defined as metachronous colorectal adenoma during a follow-up colonoscopy before April 2023.

Statistical analysis

All data were statistically analyzed using R 4.2.0 software. Continuous variables were expressed as either mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range [IQR]), based on data distribution, measurement data were expressed as n (%) and analyzed using either the Student’s t test for normally distributed date or the Mann–Whitney U test for non-normally distributed data. Categorical variables presented as percentages and analyzed using the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test depending on sample size. X-tile software (Yale University, New Haven, CT, USA) was utilized to transform quantitative variables into categorical variables. The analysis identified the optimal cut-off points to stratify the continuous data into low- and high-risk groups for metachronous colorectal adenoma, based on the minimum P-value (maximum χ2) derived from log-rank statistics. This approach mitigates the subjectivity associated with utilizing mean or median values and ensures maximal statistical differentiation in outcomes between groups [8]. The enrolled patients were randomly divided into a training set, and a validation set in a ratio of 7:3. The t-test, the analysis of variance (ANOVA), and the rank sum test were used to assess the balance of variables between the training and validation sets.

The least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression was employed to screen the risk factors for metachronous adenoma. Variables with non-zero coefficients were identified and subsequently incorporated into a multivariable Cox regression analysis to construct a risk prediction model. The regplot package drew the Nomogram to visualize the risk prediction model. To enhance the clinical utility of our predictive model, we developed a web-based dynamic nomogram. The interactive interface was built using the ‘DynNom’ package in R software. Subsequently, the application was deployed to the web server (https://www.shinyapps.io/) using the ‘rsconnect’ package, allowing clinicians to calculate the individual probability of metachronous adenoma by inputting specific clinical variables. Then, the C-index and time-dependent ROC will verify the model’s accuracy. Finally, the clinical application performance of the prediction model was evaluated by decision curve analysis (DCA). A bilateral P value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

This study was reported in accordance with the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) reporting guideline.

Results

Enrolled patients’ baseline characteristics

Figure 1 shows the flow diagram of the study population, and Table 1 outlines the baseline characteristics of the 523 enrolled patients. All patients are Asian from a single geographic region. The cohort was randomly partitioned into training (n = 366) and validation (n = 157) sets, with no significant differences in variable distribution observed between groups. The median surveillance interval was 333 days (IQR: 198, 470). Specifically, the number of patients with a follow-up duration of at least 1, 2, and 3 years were 214 (40.92%), 55 (10.52%), and 25 (4.78%), respectively. During follow-up, metachronous adenomas were detected in 216 patients (41.3%), including 35 cases of advanced metachronous adenomas. The majority of patients were male (59%) with a median age of 61 years. Risk factors for metachronous colorectal adenoma

A total of 21 clinical and pathological characteristics were included in the LASSO regression analysis. Figure 2A illustrates the changing trajectories of the coefficients for all candidate variables. We employed tenfold cross-validation to determine the optimal penalty parameter λ, as shown in Fig. 2B. Based on the minimum criteria, the optimal λ value was 0.031, resulting in 8 variables with non-zero coefficients. These selected variables, including age, gender, adenoma size, adenoma location, adenoma pathological grade, history of hypertension, history of diabetes and fasting LDL (Supplementary Table 1). Although a history of diabetes was identified as a potential predictor with a non-zero coefficient in the LASSO analysis, it displayed a negative association (suggesting a protective effect). Given that diabetes is widely established as a risk factor for colorectal adenoma [9–11], this variable was considered clinically inconsistent and was subsequently excluded from the final multivariable Cox regression model to ensure biological plausibility.

Construction of the risk prediction model

According to the results of LASSO regression and combined with clinical significance, patient age, sex, adenoma size, adenoma location, adenoma pathological grade, history of hypertension and fasting serum LDL were included in the multivariable COX regression analysis. The outcome was set as the occurrence of metachronous adenoma. Multicollinearity assessment confirmed the independence of all predictors included in the multivariable Cox model, with all Variance Inflation Factors (VIFs) ranging from 1.08 to 1.48, well below the threshold of 5 (Supplementary Table 2). The forest plot in Fig. 3 shows the results of multivariable COX regression, that is, the HR value (Hazard ratio) and 95% confidence interval and P value of each variable screened by LASSO in the COX regression. The regplot package in R software was used to draw a visual Nomogram prediction model. Box plots were used to visualize categorical variables, and violin plots were used to visualize continuous variables. As an example, a 52-year male patient with a total risk score of 326, implies the risk of metachronous adenoma in 1, 2, and 3 years was 0.239, 0.651 and 0.775, respectively, suggesting that he should undergo colonoscopy follow up at second year (Fig. 4). An online dynamic calculator was established to facilitate visual risk prediction. This tool is accessible at https://colorectal-metachronous-adenoma-prediction.shinyapps.io/DynNomapp/. Users can input patient characteristics—such as age, adenoma size, and location—to instantly obtain the estimated adenoma-free probability and corresponding 95% confidence intervals (Supplementary Fig. 1).

Validation of the risk prediction model

In the model reliability verification, the C-index of the prediction model in the training set was 0.729 (95%CI: 0.686, 0.772), and the C-index in the validation set was 0.724 (95%CI: 0.667, 0.781), implying good prediction discrimination in both the validation and the training sets. Figure 5 shows the td-ROC of the prediction model in the training and validation sets. In the training set, the AUC of the prediction model at 1, 2, and 3 years were 0.76, 0.73, and 0.64, respectively; in the validation set, the AUC of the prediction model at 1, 2, and 3 years were 0.79, 0.72, and 0.57, respectively. The time-dependent ROC curve suggests that this model has good prediction accuracy in the first and second year. We further used the bootstrap method to construct the calibration curves of the model’s 1-, 2-, and 3-year predictions in the training and validation sets to evaluate the model calibration. As shown in Fig. 6, the calibration curves of the prediction model in both cohorts were relatively close to the diagonal line, indicating good prediction accuracy. To verify the clinical value of the model, a DCA analysis was performed. Since the median surveillance time of the enrolled patients was 333 days, the 1-year prediction model was established with the largest sample size, and the time-dependent ROC curve also indicated that the model had the best prediction ability in 1 year. Therefore, we plotted the DCA of the prediction model’s 1-year predictions in all patients, the training set, and the validation set (Fig. 7).

Discussion

Through real-world research in China, this study discovered the risk factors for post-polypectomy metachronous colorectal adenomas, including age, sex, baseline adenoma size, adenoma location, adenoma pathological grade, history of hypertension, and fasting serum LDL. A risk prediction model for metachronous adenoma was established, visualized through nomogram, and verified for accuracy and clinical value. The incidence of metachronous adenomas increases as the surveillance interval prolongs, and the risk of colorectal cancer and death also increases accordingly. Our goal of this study was to set up an appropriate colonoscopy reexamination interval by predicting risk stratification, effectively reducing the incidence of metachronous adenomas and being more cost-effective [12].

Of the 523 patients in this study, 216 (41.3%) developed metachronous adenomas. A Chinese cohort study reported recurrence rates at 1–3, 3–5, and 5–10 years post-colonoscopy of 0.9%, 3.9%, and 5.8% for non-advanced adenomas, compared to 3.8%, 13.1%, and 34.7% for advanced adenomas [12]. Additionally, another study of 3360 post-polypectomy patients found that 157 developed metachronous tumors within 3 years, representing an incidence of 13.5% [6]. It should be noted that the overall adenoma recurrence rate in our cohort (41.3%) appears higher than in some previously reported studies. We attribute this difference to three main reasons. First, our study defined recurrence as the detection of any adenoma, strictly including all non-advanced and diminutive polyps, whereas many major cohorts focus primarily on advanced adenomas. What’s more, in this study, we collectively defined both missed diagnoses and true recurrences as metachronous adenomas. By accounting for procedural factors and incorporating quality indicators such as bowel preparation quality, our model more accurately reflects real-world clinical scenarios. Although this inclusive definition may result in a higher reported risk rate, it facilitates more robust patient risk stratification and ensures rigorous management of colorectal adenomas, ultimately reducing the risk of malignant transformation.

Calibration curves for 1, 2, and 3 years predictions aligned well with the diagonal in both training and validation sets, demonstrating good model accuracy (Fig. 6). Performance was optimal at 1 year, evidenced by the closest calibration and highest time-dependent AUCs (training: 0.76; validation: 0.79). The reason might be the median follow-up time of the cohort in this study was 333 days (IQR 198,470), and most patients were followed up 1 year after adenoma resection.

Our study identified baseline adenoma size as a critical risk factor, with lesions ≥ 3 cm significantly increasing the risk of metachronous adenomas. In contrast, the number of adenomas was not selected as a predictor in our LASSO analysis or nomogram. The prognostic value of adenoma count remains controversial. While some studies suggest higher counts predict recurrence [13] [14]. Recent meta-analysis found no significant risk difference between patients with 3–4 versus ≥ 5 adenomas, supporting current guideline stratification [15]. Consistent with this, we analyzed adenoma number as a continuous variable (median: 2; IQR: 1, 4) and found no significant association. This aligns with findings that adenoma size (specifically ≥ 20 mm) is a stronger predictor of metachronous colorectal cancer than adenoma count [6, 14, 16, 17][18].

Regarding pathology, although traditionally guidelines often classify villous components as high-risk, our data showed no significant impact. Our findings align with the updated 2020 ESGE guidelines, which state that for small adenomas, the presence of villous histology does not independently increase the risk of metachronous colorectal cancer (CRC). The guidelines suggest that the prognostic impact of the villous component has been historically overestimated, a perspective that is consistent with our results [19]. What’s more, compared with existing study, our study included all metachronous adenoma recurrences rather than focusing solely on advanced adenomas. Furthermore, the robust prognostic impact of systemic metabolic factors may have overshadowed the statistical weight of traditional local endoscopic features in our multivariable analysis. Instead, high-grade dysplasia emerged as a significant predictor. This suggests that pathological grade may be a more reliable prognostic indicator than pathological type for metachronous adenomas.

Following the LASSO regression analysis, diabetes mellitus (DM) was identified by the LASSO algorithm with a negative coefficient, implying a potential protective effect. However, extensive previous literature has established DM as a risk factor for colorectal adenomas [9–11]. This paradoxical finding in our cohort is likely attributable to unmeasured confounding factors, such as the chemo preventive effects of anti-diabetic medications (e.g., metformin, a chemo preventive properties against colorectal adenoma) or closer medical surveillance in diabetic patients [20, 21]. Therefore, based on established clinical consensus and a priori biological plausibility, DM was purposefully excluded from the final multivariable Cox proportional hazards model to prevent clinical misinterpretation.

Our study identified proximal adenoma location as a significant predictor of metachronous adenomas. While this aligns with emerging evidence, conflicting reports regarding its impact on metachronous advanced adenomas have led most current guidelines to exclude location as a definitive risk factor [22].

Consistent with previous studies in the Korean population [23], we identified advanced age and male sex as significant risk factors. Notably, our model highlights the predictive value of metabolic indicators, specifically hypertension and high serum LDL levels. Hypertension has been previously reported as an important predictor of recurrent colorectal adenoma after adenoma polypectomy [24], a finding corroborated by our multivariate analysis. Similarly, our results align with previous study identifying high serum LDL as a risk factor for colorectal adenoma, suggesting a metabolic drive-in metachronous adenoma development [25]. Recent molecular evidence supports a robust link between metabolic components and metachronous adenomas. Elevated LDL and dysregulated cholesterol metabolism activate proliferative signaling while driving local immunosuppression—specifically through CD8 + T-cell exhaustion and intestinal pro-inflammatory foam cell accumulation [26]. Simultaneously, systemic hypertension potentiates tumorigenesis via renin-angiotensin system (RAS) hyperactivation; the Ang II/AT1R axis promotes mucosal inflammation and matrix remodeling, directly fueling adenoma recurrence [27]. Other studies have shown that abdominal obesity is associated with the risk of metachronous advanced tumors [28, 29]. However, due to the lack of abdominal circumference information in our medical record system, we used BMI as an obesity indicator for analysis. The results did not suggest that obesity influenced the risk of metachronous adenomas.

Existing prediction models for metachronous adenoma are limited, as most literature focuses on metachronous colorectal cancer (CRC) or advanced adenoma recurrence. Facciorusso et al. established a scoring model using adenoma size, count, and pathological grade, achieving an AUC of 0.81 (95% CI: 0.72–0.86). Subsequent research utilized recursive partitioning to develop a risk stratification model based on similar variables, yielding a C-index of 0.76 [6, 30]. Additionally, Lee et al. demonstrated that a model incorporating age, gender, and serrated lesions outperformed traditional high-risk classifications [30]. While these models show good efficacy, they primarily target advanced lesions. However, non-advanced adenomas also contribute to adverse outcomes—including CRC-related mortality and decreased quality of life—warranting serious attention. Consequently, our study evaluates both advanced and non-advanced metachronous adenomas.

Regarding clinical applicability, our nomogram provides a dynamic, probability-driven framework for personalizing surveillance intervals, firmly grounded in evidence-based guidelines. Current prominent guidelines, such as those from the US Multi-Society Task Force on Colorectal Cancer (USMSTF) and the European Society of Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ESGE), recommend a 3-year surveillance colonoscopy for high-risk patients. Literature indicates that at this 3-year milestone, the cumulative risk of metachronous neoplasia reaches a clinically actionable threshold (an approximate 15% for advanced adenoma, or 30% for overall adenoma recurrence rate) [19, 31]. By projecting this established probability threshold onto our nomogram, clinicians can individualize surveillance schedules. For instance, based on our nomogram, a patient with a Total Score exceeding 326 points will cross this guideline-derived recurrence risk threshold within just 2 years (Fig. 4). Consequently, this subset of patients necessitates a more intensive 2-year follow-up interval to prevent interval lesions. Conversely, for a patient with a Total Score of 263 points, the risk accumulation matches the standard trajectory, taking approximately 3 years to reach that same threshold, thereby justifying the guideline-recommended 3-year follow-up. This probability-threshold approach translates complex survival modeling into a clinically sensible tool that aligns with, yet personalizes, current endoscopic guidelines.

While existing guidelines typically recommend a 3-year surveillance interval for high-risk patients based solely on local polyp characteristics [19, 31], our recommended interval is considerably shorter. This is because our nomogram uniquely integrates systemic metabolic factors (e.g., hypertension and fasting LDL). An adverse metabolic environment can significantly accelerate adenoma recurrence. Therefore, a shortened interval is clinically justified for our nomogram-identified ‘ultra-high-risk’ patients. This prevents interval colorectal cancers and represents a shift from population-based guidelines to precision medicine.

This study has certain limitations: First, as a retrospective cohort study, there is inevitable selection bias. We selectively included patients with complete surveillance data, inadvertently excluding those with poor compliance or missing essential data. Consequently, our cohort represents a relatively good adherence subgroup. When applying this nomogram to more heterogeneous populations with variable adherence, the model’s predictive accuracy may fluctuate. Therefore, broader prospective validation is required. Secondly, the generalizability of this retrospective single-center study is limited because our cohort consists entirely of Asian patients from a single geographic region. Ethnic and geographic factors significantly influence baseline inflammatory and metabolic profiles, which can in turn affect disease presentation and adenoma recurrence patterns. Consequently, our findings may differ from those in Western or more diverse populations. The direct applicability of our nomogram to other ethnic groups requires careful interpretation, and future multicenter studies across diverse populations are necessary for external validation. Another limitation of this study is the relatively short median follow-up time. As our cohort predominantly consists of patients followed for less than 2 years, the proportion of patients available for long-term evaluation at 2 and 3 years is limited. Consequently, the observed reduction in the predictive accuracy of our model at years 2 and 3 is likely attributable to the diminished number of patients at risk and the smaller number of observed cases at these later time points, which may lead to less stable model estimates. Future studies with extended follow-up periods are required to validate the long-term predictive performance of our model. Finally, while including metabolic factors like LDL is a strength, our study lacks data on other relevant measures including central obesity, waist circumference, and insulin resistance. Due to the retrospective design, these specific metrics were incompletely recorded. To maintain an adequate sample size and ensure robust statistical power, we excluded these variables and instead utilized fasting blood glucose and a history of diabetes as clinical surrogates. Future prospective studies incorporating comprehensive metabolic panels are needed.

Conclusion

Seven risk factors of post-polypectomy metachronous adenoma (age, sex, baseline adenoma size, adenoma location, pathological grade, history of hypertension, and fasting LDL) were identified in this study. A Nomogram prediction model was established based on the risk factors, with good predictive efficacy and interactive web-based tool. This model has certain significance for guiding clinicians to risk stratify patients with colorectal adenoma and can better guide clinicians to set appropriate surveillance intervals.

Alterations in Retinal Vasculometry After Acute Primary Angle Closure Episode: A Comparative Analysis
Authors: Jin Wang, Yue Wang, Zijian Liu, Dapeng Mou, Xin Tang, Danli Shi, Mingguang He, Ningli Wang, Ye Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo evaluate retinal vasculometry alterations in successfully treated acute primary angle closure (APAC) eyes versus unaffected fellow eyes.MethodsIn this cross-sectional study of 116 patients with unilateral APAC, retinal photographs obtained within two weeks after intraocular pressure (IOP) control were analyzed using deep learning–based software to evaluate retinal vascular parameters. The main outcome included caliber, density, complexity, tortuosity, and branching angle parameters.ResultsCompared to fellow eyes, APAC eyes showed larger retinal vein caliber (P < 0.001), increased length-to-diameter ratio in arteries and veins (P < 0.001 and = 0.001), and lower arteriovenous ratio from quantile 4 (AVRQ4) (P < 0.001). Additionally, APAC eyes demonstrated reduced bifurcation densities, fractal dimension (FD), and branching angle for arteries and veins (all P ≤ 0.005). Multivariate analysis identified three independent factors associated with APAC: higher IOP (odd ratio [OR] = 1.073, P = 0.003), lower AVRQ4 (OR = 0.957, P = 0.025), and decreased arterial FD (OR = 0.953, P = 0.028).ConclusionsAPAC eyes exhibit significant retinal vasculometry alterations despite IOP control. Longitudinal studies are needed to determine whether these alterations contribute to the development of glaucomatous optic neuropathy.Translational RelevanceThe observed changes in artery-to-vein caliber ratio and arterial complexity may help clinicians identify eyes at risk for progressive damage despite normalized IOP. These findings support the development of supplementary monitoring strategies using standard fundus photography in post-APAC management.

Introduction

Primary angle closure glaucoma (PACG) is a significant global health concern, accounting for nearly one third of all glaucoma cases and half of glaucoma-related blindness worldwide.1 Its prevalence exhibits notable geographical variation, with a particularly high incidence in Asian populations.2,3

Acute primary angle closure (APAC), a subtype of PACG, is a sight-threatening ophthalmic emergency characterized by a sudden elevation of intraocular pressure (IOP), accompanied by ocular pain, headache, and potential rapid vision loss.4,5 If not promptly treated, APAC can lead to glaucomatous optic neuropathy (GON).4 Although immediate management can alleviate acute visual morbidity, delayed optic nerve damage may still occur even after the resolution of the acute episode.6–8

The pathophysiology of optic nerve damage in APAC is complex and not fully elucidated. Studies have shown that a considerable proportion of patients may develop permanent visual field defects even after IOP control.9,10 Recent investigations utilizing optical coherence tomography angiography (OCTA) have revealed significant reductions in vessel density within the optic nerve head (ONH) and peripapillary retina after an APAC episode, persisting even after IOP control.11–13 These findings suggest that vascular factors may play a crucial role in the pathogenesis of visual field loss in APAC, extending beyond the acute IOP elevation.

Two main approaches have emerged to assess the role of vascular factors in glaucoma pathogenesis: vessel density parameters detected by OCTA and retinal vascular geometric parameters measured from retinal photographs using computer software.14–16 Although OCTA provides valuable information about microvascular perfusion, retinal vascular geometric analysis offers unique insights into the structural changes of larger blood vessels, which can indirectly reflect blood flow dynamics and vascular autoregulation.16,17 However, there is limited research focusing on the alterations of retinal vascular geometry after APAC episode, particularly using advanced imaging analysis techniques.

Hence, in this study, we use a deep learning system-based measurement software to comprehensively evaluate retinal vascular geometric alterations in APAC eyes compared to unaffected fellow eyes. By analyzing these vascular geometric changes, our study aims to characterize the retinal vascular alterations observed after APAC episodes and explore potential associations with post-APAC episode pathophysiology.

Methods

Study Design and Participant Selection

This retrospective, observational, cross-sectional study was conducted at the glaucoma department of Beijing Tongren Hospital with consecutive inclusion of patients with a successfully treated episode of APAC from May 2020 to October 2022. The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Beijing Tongren Hospital (TREC2024-KY064), with a waiver of informed consent because of the use of de-identified patient data. All procedures adhered to the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki.

APAC was diagnosed based on the following criteria: (1) presence of two or more symptoms including ocular or periocular pain, nausea and/or vomiting, or a history of intermittent vision blurring with halos; (2) presenting IOP of >21 mm Hg; (3) presence of at least three signs: conjunctival injection, corneal edema, mid-dilated unreactive pupil in affected eyes, or bilaterally shallow anterior chambers; and (4) gonioscopy or ultrasound biomicroscopy–verified occluded angle in the affected eye.18 Inclusion criteria were (1) age ≥35 years, (2) unilateral APAC, (3) available and measurable fundus images, and (4) successful IOP management (IOP was controlled under 21 mm Hg) within 48 hours of the APAC episode with treatment. Exclusion criteria included prior episode of APAC, preexistent GON, secondary angle closure glaucoma, primary open-angle glaucoma, concurrent retinal diseases, history of intraocular surgery or ocular trauma, and inadequate quality of fundus photographs for accurate measurements. Fellow pseudophakic eyes were excluded as cataract surgery alters anterior segment anatomy, induces postoperative inflammation, and may independently affect retinal vascular parameters, compromising the validity of paired-eye comparisons.

Clinical Examinations and Data Collection

All examinations were performed within two weeks after complete resolution of the acute episode, which was defined as IOP < 21 mm Hg (with or without medication). Before study examinations, all patients underwent slit-lamp and fundus evaluations confirming the absence of clinically apparent corneal edema and optic disc edema. Each patient underwent a comprehensive, standardized ocular assessment including: presenting visual acuity (PVA) measurement, slit-lamp examination. IOP measured by a Canon TX-20 tonometer (Canon, Tokyo, Japan) and for each eye, three consecutive measurements were obtained, and the average value was recorded. Gonioscopic examination (one-mirror Goldmann lens; Ocular Instruments, Bellevue, WA, USA), optical biometry using IOLMaster 700 (V.5; Carl Zeiss Meditec, Dublin, CA, USA), and fundus examination (90-D lens) were also conducted. The IOLMaster 700 was used to measure average keratometry (AK), central corneal thickness (CCT), central anterior chamber depth (ACD), lens thickness (LT), and axial length (AL). Additionally, comprehensive demographic data and medical histories were collected, including information on ocular and systemic diseases (specifically hypertension, diabetes, and hyperlipidemia), as well as current medication regimens.

During the APAC episode, all patients received immediate IOP-lowering treatment according to our institutional protocol. For patients requiring additional interventions, laser peripheral iridotomy, anterior chamber paracentesis or surgical peripheral iridotomy was performed as clinically indicated.

Retinal Imaging and Vascular Geometry Analysis

Retinal imaging was performed using binocular fundus stereo photography (Canon CR 2; Canon, Tokyo, Japan), with two 45° macula-centered digital retinal photographs obtained for each participant. These images were analyzed using the Retinal-based Microvascular Health Assessment System (RMHAS, v.1.0), to assess retinal vascular geometry, including vascular calibre, density, complexity, tortuosity and branching angle parameters. The RMHAS outputs standardized measurements in the following units: vascular caliber, branching density, and bifurcation density are expressed in pixels; vessel area density (VAD) and vessel skeleton density (VSD) as percentages; branching angle in degrees; while artery-to-vein ratio (AVRQ4), length-diameter ratio (LDR), fractal dimension (FD), tortuosity, curvature tortuosity, branching coefficient, and junctional exponent deviation are dimensionless ratios presented as decimal values.

A single observer (D.S.), blinded to clinical data, operated the RMHAS. The reliability of these measurements has been previously validated and reported.19,20 To assess short-term reproducibility, 20 eyes were randomly selected and re-analyzed at different time points using RMHAS. The intraclass correlation coefficients for major vasculometric parameters ranged from 0.798 to 1.000. Specific retinal vessel parameters included in this study, along with their detailed descriptions, are presented in Supplementary Table S1.

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS (Version 25.0; SPSS, Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). Data normality was assessed using the one-sample Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Normally distributed continuous data are presented as means ± standard deviations, whereas non-normally distributed data are expressed as medians with interquartile ranges (IR). Categorical data are reported as frequencies and percentages.

Comparisons between APAC eyes and fellow eyes were conducted using paired t-tests or Wilcoxon signed-rank tests for continuous variables, and Pearson's χ2 tests for categorical variables. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analysis was used to identify potential factors associated with APAC. Variables with P < 0.05 in univariate analysis were included in the multivariate model. A backward regression approach was used for variable selection in the final multivariate model. Multicollinearity was assessed using variance inflation factor (VIF), excluding variables with VIF > 5. Odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated for both univariate and multivariate analyses. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.

Results

Of 160 initially enrolled APAC patients (320 eyes), 44 were excluded for the following reasons: bilateral APAC (n = 10), unreadable fundus photographs (n = 29, including 18 APAC eyes and 11 fellow eyes), intraocular lens implantation in the fellow eye (n = 3), concurrent retinal diseases (n = 2). The final analysis included 232 eyes (116 APAC eyes and 116 fellow eyes) from 116 patients with resolved APAC (Fig. 1).

Vascular Caliber

APAC eyes showed significant alterations in vascular caliber compared to fellow eyes. Venular calibre (10.01 vs. 9.46, P < 0.001), and both arterial (14.74 vs. 13.04, P < 0.001) and venular LDR (12.35 vs. 11.51, P = 0.001) were larger in APAC eyes compared to fellow eyes. AVRQ4 was significantly lower in APAC eyes (0.72 vs. 0.77, P < 0.001), with comparison with fellow eyes. No significant difference was found in arterial calibre between APAC and fellow eyes (P = 0.570).

Vascular Density

A consistent reduction in vessel density was observed in APAC eyes across multiple parameters. Bifurcation densities were significantly lower in APAC eyes for arteries and veins (all P ≤ 0.001). VSD was reduced in both arteries (0.010 vs. 0.012, P < 0.001) and veins (0.013 vs. 0.014, P = 0.003) in APAC eyes. VAD was significantly lower in APAC eyes (0.041 vs. 0.049, P < 0.001), whereas venular VAD showed no significant difference (P = 0.096) between APAC and fellow eyes.

Vascular Complexity

APAC eyes exhibited reduced arterial and venular complexities. Arterial and venular FDs were significantly lower in APAC eyes compared to fellow eyes (P < 0.001 and = 0.012).

Vascular Tortuosity and Branching Angle

Vascular tortuosity showed no significant differences between APAC and fellow eyes (all P > 0.05). APAC eyes exhibited smaller arterial (44.62° vs. 45.43°, P = 0.042) and venular (45.81° vs. 46.53°, P = 0.005) branching angles (BAs) compared to fellow eyes, whereas other branching angle parameters showed no significant differences (all P > 0.005). Table 4 presents the results of logistic regression analyses identifying factors associated with APAC. Factors Associated With the Diagnosis of APAC

The multivariate logistic regression analysis identified three independent factors significantly associated with APAC: higher IOP (OR = 1.073; 95% CI, 1.024∼1.124; P = 0.003), lower AVRQ4 (OR = 0.957; 95% CI, 0.921∼0.994; P = 0.025), and decreased arterial complexity as represented by FD (OR = 0.953; 95% CI, 0.912∼0.995; P = 0.028). All VIF values in the final model were below 5 (range 1.039–4.639).

Discussion

In this cross-sectional study of 116 patients with unilateral APAC, we identified significant retinal vasculometry alterations in APAC-affected eyes compared to fellow eyes, even after successful IOP control. Key findings included larger venular caliber, reduced AVRQ4, decreased vascular complexity (arterial FD), and smaller branching angles. Multivariate analysis revealed that higher IOP, lower AVRQ4, and reduced arterial FD were independently associated with APAC.

The pathogenesis of ONH damage following APAC involves two primary mechanisms: elevated IOP and vascular alterations.11,21 OCTA studies have consistently demonstrated microvascular alterations following APAC. Resolved APAC eyes show reduced peripapillary vessel density compared to fellow or normal eyes, often persisting even when RNFL thickness remains normal.12,22 Macular superficial vessel density is also reduced in APAC eyes, correlating with RNFL and ganglion cell complex thinning, as well as visual field loss.23,24 A previous longitudinal study by Moghimi et al.11 demonstrated significantly reduced circumpapillary vessel density (VD) in APAC eyes two months after treatment, compared with fellow eyes, and that VD remained stable between 2 and 8 months. Meanwhile, the circumpapillary retinal nerve fiber layer (RNFL) thickness continued to decrease eight months after remission.11 This discrepancy between stable VD and progressive RNFL thinning suggests that VD alone may not fully explain the vascular dysfunction contributing to neuronal damage.

To address this gap, we used the RMHAS, which automatically detects various retinal vasculometry through fundus photographs.19 Unlike OCTA, which primarily measures capillary density, RMHAS analyzes broader vascular geometric parameters, including arteries and veins, that may change after APAC and affect retinal perfusion. This broader analysis in our study showed that in APAC eyes, significant retinal vasculometry alterations occurred compared to fellow eyes, even after IOP control, and those significant changes in vascular morphology may provide new insights into the mechanisms in progressive optic nerve damage and informing future clinical management of APAC.

One of the key findings of our study is the significant vascular caliber changes observed in APAC eyes compared to fellow eyes. These changes are evidenced by a larger venular caliber and lower AVRQ4. Notably, our logistic regression analysis revealed that only the AVRQ4 was significantly associated with APAC, suggesting that the relative changes of arterial versus venous calibers may be more closely related to APAC than alterations in individual vessel calibers.

The larger venular caliber observed in APAC eyes likely indicates venular dilation induced by the sudden and severe IOP elevation characteristic of APAC. We speculated that this acute pressure increase damaged the arterial autoregulation mechanisms, leading to venular congestion directly without an intermediary arterial compensatory phase. The persistence of these vascular alterations, even after IOP normalization, may be attributed to an ischemia-reperfusion injury process, potentially causing long-term changes in retinal hemodynamics that may contribute to optic nerve dysfunction.

Furthermore, we observed vascular elongation in both arteries and veins, as represented by increased LDR. This finding is particularly significant when considered in the context of Poiseuille's law, which states that blood flow is inversely proportional to vessel length.25 Consequently, the vascular elongation we observed may indicate compromised retinal perfusion, further contributing to the complex vascular dynamics in APAC eyes.

Our study also revealed a significant reduction in vascular complexity in APAC eyes, likely resulting from vascular remodeling triggered by acute IOP elevation. This remodeling process involves selective pruning of less essential vessels and blood flow redistribution favoring larger vessels, reflecting altered metabolic demands.25,26 Notably, our logistic regression analysis revealed that only arterial FD was significantly associated with APAC, suggesting greater arterial susceptibility to remodeling after APAC. This arterial vulnerability may be due to the rapid IOP increase during APAC, which predominantly impacts arteries given their high sensitivity to pressure fluctuations.27,28

Although we observed several significant vascular geometry changes, our findings differed from previous studies on glaucoma, particularly primary open-angle glaucoma,15,16 because we did not find significant differences in vascular tortuosity between APAC-affected eyes and fellow eyes. This absence of tortuosity changes likely reflects the acute nature of APAC, distinguishing it from chronic glaucoma, where prolonged IOP elevation leads to vessel shape deformation.

The present study has several limitations that warrant consideration. First, the cross-sectional design inherently constrains our ability to establish a causal relationship between the observed retinal vasculometry alterations and APAC. To elucidate the temporal dynamics of vascular geometric parameter changes and their association with GON following APAC, longitudinal investigations are imperative. Second, although our analysis controlled for various confounding variables, the potential influence of systemic vascular diseases, pharmacological interventions, and lifestyle factors on retinal vasculature was not comprehensively accounted for. Third, our comparative analysis between APAC-affected eyes and their fellow counterparts may be subject to bias, as the latter may harbor subclinical changes because of shared risk factors. The inclusion of a healthy control cohort in future studies could provide more robust insights into the magnitude and specificity of vascular alterations associated with APAC. Furthermore, the RMHAS software used in this study was validated using 45° macula-centered fundus photographs. The generalizability of these measurements to other imaging protocols, such as wide-field, 30°, or 15° photographs, remains to be established and warrants further validation studies. Finally, although all examinations were performed within two weeks of the APAC episode, we did not analyze the potential impact of exact timing on vascular parameters. Future studies with serial measurements at multiple time points would help elucidate the temporal dynamics of vascular changes following APAC.

In conclusion, our study identified key vascular geometric alterations in APAC-affected eyes, including reduced artery-to-vein caliber ratio and simplification of the vascular network, with a greater impact on the arterial system. Although the reversibility of these changes remains uncertain, these findings demonstrate significant vascular associations with APAC. Longitudinal studies are needed to determine whether these alterations contribute to long-term visual dysfunction and to explore potential therapeutic interventions.

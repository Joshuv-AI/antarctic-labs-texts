Dataset for predictive modelling and analysis of employee attrition and retention
Authors: Haya Alqahtani, Hana Almagrabi, Amal Alharbi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis dataset provides insights into the factors affecting employee attrition in the private sector of Saudi Arabia. The data were collected through an online survey of 1191 participants working in Saudi Arabia's private sector companies and institutions across various industries. The questionnaire consists of three categories of variables: demographic information, job-related features, and psychological and satisfaction variables. For each category in the questionnaire, several questions were disseminated to a sample population. Preprocessing and analysis were conducted to identify the principal factors influencing employee attrition and job satisfaction. The dataset was formulated with 34 attributes and 1191 rows. In addition, the dataset includes nominal and ordinal answers for the variables, allowing for sophisticated analyses using machine learning classification techniques. The dataset also contains the independent variable (target variable), about employee attrition information, which acts as a class or label variable in machine learning scenarios. We intend for this dataset to serve as a valuable resource for researchers and organizations aiming to understand and predict employee turnover, as it provides a set of attributes that can help formulate workforce management strategies and policies.Keywords: Employee attrition, Employee turnover, Attrition prediction, and Predictive Modelling

Background

The urgent need for data-driven decision-making drives HR management to conduct a comprehensive analysis of the employee attrition phenomenon. Predicting employee attrition and exploring its root causes is the primary motivation for this research. The dataset presented in this research provides an explanation of the various factors influencing employee turnover, including demographic, functional, psychological, and job satisfaction factors.

The data available for studying employee attrition prediction is limited, and it is worth noting that the dataset provided by IBM is the most widely used. The fictional dataset was created by IBM data scientists in 2016. It consists of 1470 observations and includes 34 features related to employees’ work and personal data [1].

The growing interest in using machine learning techniques for predictive modeling in HR analytics further underscores the importance of having a relevant dataset [2]. Given the significant impact of employee attrition on organizational performance, the dataset serves as an important resource for understanding prediction and formulating strategies to improve employee retention. This dataset aligns with the broader context of Saudi Arabia’s Vision 2030, which emphasizes the importance of data-driven research for economic development and organizational efficiency [3,4].

While employee retention and satisfaction are critical, this research represents an important resource for a variety of stakeholders. Specifically, it could be valuable to businesses and policymakers in Saudi Arabia. Given the similarity of social, economic, and cultural contexts, the findings can be applied more broadly across the Gulf and Arab world. Furthermore, the insights derived from this study can be applied to developing countries, where improving mental health and job satisfaction contributes to workforce stability and increased productivity.

Addressing these factors enables organizations in these regions to design thoughtful strategies that reduce employee attrition and enhance employee engagement, ultimately contributing to improved organizational performance.

Data Description

To explore the causes and effects of employee attrition, researchers are analyzing data from the Saudi private sector. The data collection process begins with an online survey of 1200 workers across various Saudi companies.

A total of 34 attributes were used in this dataset to describe the questions for each category of the questionnaire. These questions of factors influencing employee attrition, including job satisfaction, were formulated from an analysis of previous studies on predicting employee turnover and expert insights from the literature [5,6]. These attributes encompassed a wide range of factors that have been researched as potential influencers of private sector employee retention and motivation.

The dataset includes three CSV files. The first file, “Employee Attrition Dataset,” contains the original data before preprocessing and coding. The second file, “Tree-Based Models Dataset,” contains the data after preprocessing, coding, and is oriented to tree-based models. The third file, “Non-Tree Models Dataset,” contains the data after preprocessing, coding, and oriented to non-tree models, where categorical values ​​are converted to numeric form and multi-valued variables are expanded into separate columns. In addition, the dataset includes two PDF files. The first file, “Online Survey Questions,” contains the questions from the online survey used to collect the data. The second PDF file provides keys and descriptions for the nominal, ordinal, and numerical encoding in the dataset, along with the response options for each variable.

To efficiently reach the target group, the questionnaire was distributed via social media platforms such as WhatsApp, X (formerly Twitter), and Telegram, ensuring broad accessibility. The questionnaire was designed in both Arabic and English to accommodate employees in Saudi Arabia’s private sector. It consists of 34 closed-ended questions, incorporating a binary target variable for measuring responses. The binary target question is direct; it is intended to ask employees: Have you ever left a previous organization before your current job? If the answer is yes, that means the target variable (attrition) is true; otherwise, the questionnaire is conducted about current work in the private sector.

For the psychological exhaustion and physical stress, the answers were measured using some ordinal values with the options “No,” “Sometimes,” and “Yes.” Additionally, job-related features and job engagement were assessed using a scale of “Easy,” “Medium,” and “Difficult.” Fig. 1 illustrates the flowchart of the questionnaire.

-To accommodate various model families, the dataset was created in two versions: one for tree-based models and another for non-tree-based models. To conform to the intrinsic features and specifications of both model types, the categorical variables are encoded differently in these two versions. The dataset created for tree-based models uses M-Estimator Encoding, which is used for nominal features like Job Title, Sector, Department, and Marital Status that have numerous values. Using this method, categories are given numerical values according to how they relate to the target variable. It considers the target's distribution both globally and within each category [7]. Because it offers a smooth and continuous encoding that decision trees may efficiently use for decision splits, this encoding technique is especially well-suited for tree-based models. The dataset created for non-tree-based models uses One-Hot Encoding for the same nominal characteristics. For every category, this approach generates binary dummy variables that denote presence (1) or absence (0) [8].

Limitations and future work

When using this dataset in predictive modelling, the data may not fully represent the total number of employees in the Saudi private sector, potentially leading to selection bias. Additionally, the data only includes employees from Saudi Arabia, limiting the generalizability of the results to other geographic regions or cultural contexts. Another limitation is the reliance on self-reported data from an online survey, which may be subject to respondent bias, including social desirability or inaccuracy in self-assessment. Nevertheless, we recommend that future researchers use this dataset to conduct the Harman one-factor test or other suitable evaluations of common methods as part of their specific analysis procedures on this dataset.

Assessing the reliability and quality of avascular necrosis of the femoral head content on social media: a cross-sectional content analysis
Authors: Yongli Fu, Wenlong Shen, Haiyu Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAvascular necrosis of the femoral head (ANFH) is a progressive orthopedic disorder characterized by a significant rate of impairment. With the proliferation of short-video platforms in health communication, apprehensions have emerged over the quality and credibility of medical information shared through these mediums. Nevertheless, investigations specifically focusing on ANFH remain mostly unexamined. This research utilized a cross-sectional approach to examine 177 videos pertaining to ANFH on TikTok and Bilibili. The recorded data encompassed video sources, content themes, expression forms, and interaction metrics, with quality assessment performed according to four criteria. The overall video quality was average (GQS = 3, mDISCERN = 3, JAMA = 3, VIQI = 12). Healthcare personnel constituted the predominant uploaders (72.88%), with chief physicians representing 32.77%. Videos primarily showcased monologues (58.19%), with video content focused on treatment (71.75%) and symptoms (68.93%). Surgical operations are the predominant therapeutic modality, accounting for 36.72%. TikTok videos had a shorter median duration of 56 s but elicited higher interaction (P < 0.001) and exhibited enhanced performance in JAMA and VIQI scores (P < 0.001). Videos produced by attending/registered physicians demonstrated superior quality, while Q&A-format videos revealed improved reliability compared to monologues. Interaction metrics showed weak-to-moderate associations with quality scores overall, with relatively stronger correlations observed between interaction metrics and GQS and VIQI scores, particularly on TikTok. The general quality of short videos on ANFH is average, with considerable variations among platforms and uploaders. Efforts must concentrate on fortifying early preventive education, harmonizing surgical and conservative treatment methods, and instituting expert review and certification systems to improve the scientific precision and distribution efficacy of orthopedic health information on social media. Introduction

Avascular necrosis of the femoral head (ANFH) is defined by the necrosis of bone tissue resulting from compromised blood flow, often leading to intense pain, joint dysfunction, and potential joint collapse in later stages1,2. Its global prevalence has been rising annually. Chinese research indicate a prevalence of roughly 0.72%, which significantly affects patients’ mental health and quality of life3,4.

The extensive utilization of mobile internet has rendered social media platforms a principal medium and formidable instrument for the public to obtain health information, owing to their rapid, visually captivating content. TikTok has over 1 billion monthly active users in more over 150 countries and regions globally, and Bilibili and Rednote each exceed 300 million users5–7. In this context, the quantity of videos pertaining to medical and health subjects continues to increase. Recently, there has been a growing number of studies evaluating medical and health-related short videos across various disciplines, including gastroenterology, radiology, and ophthalmology. However, ANFH-related health information on short-video platforms remains insufficiently investigated, particularly with regard to the systematic comparison of content quality and platform-specific differences8–11.

This study is the inaugural investigation centered on ANFH to remedy this gap. Employing four globally acknowledged instruments—GQS, mDISCERN, JAMA, and VIQI—it performs a comprehensive assessment. This study systematically describes the characteristics and quality differences of ANFH-related videos on TikTok and Bilibili and examines whether video characteristics are associated with differences in quality and reliability scores.

Methods

Data search and collection

Conduct a search for the term “股骨头缺血性坏死 (“avascular necrosis of the femoral head” in Chinese) on Douyin (the Chinese version of TikTok) and Bilibili, thereafter identifying the top 100 videos based on the default sorting criteria (data collection dates: November 1–3, 2025). Studies demonstrate that the leading 100 videos can exemplify pertinent content in this domain across several platforms12. To reduce potential bias from personalized recommendations, all searches were conducted without logging into a user account, and browser history/cache was cleared before data collection. The same search term and retrieval procedure were applied to both platforms. A stringent screening procedure excluded: (a) content entirely or partially comprising advertisements; (b) duplicate videos; (c) videos irrelevant to ANFH (Fig. 1).

Video collection flowchart.

Utilizing this approach, researchers recorded and examined the subsequent critical parameters: length, likes, comments, collections, shares, video resolution, expression format, content, treatment method, uploader, and physician title. Expression formats were classified as monologue, Q&A, or other types (e.g., lectures or animations); video content was divided into six categories: etiology, symptoms, stage, prevention, treatment, and prognosis; treatment methods comprised conservative treatment, surgical treatment, and traditional Chinese medicine treatment; uploaders included three distinct groups: healthcare personnel, professional organizations, and individual users, with healthcare personnel further categorized into Western medicine practitioners and traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) practitioners. The research dataset is located in “Appendix 1-data”.

Uploader type and video presentation format were coded as mutually exclusive categorical variables. Each video was classified into only one category of presentation format, and simultaneously, into only one category of uploader type. In contrast, content topic and treatment method were coded as non-mutually exclusive binary variables. Consequently, a single video could be classified into multiple content topics or multiple treatment method categories, and the sum of the percentages across these categories did not necessarily equal 100%.

Video quality assessment

Two independent researchers received standardized training and calibration utilizing 10 videos prior to performing blinded assessments with four validated evaluation instruments: the Global Quality Scale (GQS), the Journal of the American Medical Association (JAMA) criteria, the modified DISCERN (mDISCERN) tool, and the Video Information and Quality Index (VIQI). Comprehensive descriptions of these assessment instruments are available in “Appendix 2”. These established evaluation approaches have been extensively utilized worldwide in the domain of video content quality assessment13,14. A double-blind evaluation process was created to guarantee research rigor, prohibiting evaluators from viewing each other’s evaluations during the assessment. Inter-rater reliability was calculated using the two raters’ independent pre-consensus scores. After reliability assessment, scoring discrepancies were resolved through discussion. If the two raters failed to reach consensus, a third evaluator adjudicated the final score. The final consensus or adjudicated scores were used for descriptive statistics, group comparisons, and correlation analyses.

Data analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using R software version 4.4.2, and figures were produced using GraphPad Prism version 10.3.1. The normality of continuous variables was assessed before analysis. Because continuous variables were not normally distributed, they were reported as medians and interquartile ranges [M (Q1, Q3)]. Comparisons between two groups were performed using the Mann–Whitney U test, whereas comparisons among three or more groups were performed using the Kruskal–Wallis test. When the overall Kruskal–Wallis test was statistically significant, pairwise comparisons were conducted using Dunn’s post hoc test, with P values adjusted using the Holm method15,16. Ordinal quality scores, including GQS, mDISCERN, and JAMA, were also reported as medians and interquartile ranges and analyzed using the corresponding nonparametric tests. Categorical variables were reported as n (%) and compared using chi-square tests or Fisher’s exact tests when expected cell counts were small. When pairwise comparisons of categorical variables were required, P values were adjusted using the Holm method. Spearman’s rank correlation analysis was used to evaluate associations between engagement metrics and video quality scores separately for each platform. To account for multiple correlation tests, P values were adjusted within each platform using the Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate procedure17,18. All tests were two-sided, and an adjusted P value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

For inter-rater reliability analysis, GQS, mDISCERN, and JAMA scores were treated as ordinal variables and assessed using quadratic weighted Cohen’s kappa coefficients. Quadratic weighting was selected because it accounts for the ordered nature of the scales and assigns greater penalties to larger disagreements. The VIQI total score was treated as a continuous variable, and inter-rater reliability was assessed using a two-way random-effects, absolute-agreement, single-measure intraclass correlation coefficient [ICC(2,1)]. Reliability analyses were performed using the two raters’ pre-consensus scores.

Spearman correlation analysis was employed to examine correlations among various variables. Criteria: |r| ≤ 0.2 indicates no correlation; 0.2 < |r| ≤ 0.4 signifies weak correlation; 0.4 < |r| ≤ 0.6 denotes moderate connection; 0.6 < |r| ≤ 0.8 represents high correlation; |r| > 0.8 reflects very strong correlation. A p-value of less than 0.05 is deemed statistically significant.

Results

General video characteristics

A total of 177 videos about ANFH were included, consisting of 91 from TikTok and 86 from Bilibili. The median length of the videos was 82 s, accompanied by a median follower count of 21,830. The median values for likes, comments, collections, and shares were 81, 12, 44, and 24, respectively.

The majority of uploaders were healthcare personnel (72.88%), with chief physicians being the greatest proportion (32.77%). The predominant video resolution was 1080P, accounting for 80.79%. Monologues accounted for 58.19% of the expression forms. Video content predominantly focused on treatment (71.75%) and symptoms (68.93%), while prevention was referenced the least (32.20%). The predominant treatment method was surgical treatment, accounting for 36.72%.

The quality evaluation results revealed an overall moderate video quality, with a median GQS score of 3 (IQR 3–4), mDISCERN score of 3 (IQR 3–4), JAMA score of 3 (IQR 2–3), and VIQI score of 12 (IQR 10–14). Refer to Tables 1 and 2.

Discussion

This research methodically examined short video content pertaining to ANFH on the prominent platforms TikTok and Bilibili. The study determined that the overall video quality was reasonable, offering new empirical evidence for assessing the quality of health communication in short videos relevant to orthopedic diseases. The study uncovered both the advantages and constraints of social media in the distribution of medical knowledge.

Overall quality and content characteristics

The median GQS and mDISCERN scores for the 177 included videos were both 3, indicating a moderate level of quality, consistent with prior studies on short videos related to other diseases19–21. The majority of videos concentrated on two primary themes: treatment (71.75%) and symptoms (68.93%), whereas content addressing etiology, staging, prevention, and prognosis was notably limited. This pattern may suggest that highly visible ANFH-related videos tend to emphasize immediately applicable treatment information, while providing less information on etiology, staging, prevention, and prognosis22. One possible explanation is that platform ranking and engagement-oriented environments may favor visually direct and outcome-focused topics. The results are consistent with those of Zhao et al., suggesting that this pattern may be associated with content homogenization and fragmentation23.

Monologue-form videos constitute the majority at 58.19%, whereas Q&A-form videos represent a lesser share. Monologue-format videos may provide less opportunity for interactive clarification, which could limit their educational value. Conversely, Q&A-style videos attain markedly higher mDISCERN scores. Research conducted by Nie and Dobransky et al. have suggested that structured Q&A formats may support logical organization and perceived credibility. In our study, Q&A-format videos also had higher mDISCERN scores19,24.

Platform differences: TikTok’s virality vs. Bilibili’s expertise

In this study, TikTok videos showed higher interaction metrics than Bilibili videos, including follower counts, likes, comments, and shares. This pattern may be related to platform-specific differences in video duration, user interaction patterns, and recommendation environments (P < 0.001)25,26. Bilibili videos, on the other hand, are longer (median 164.5 s vs. 56 s) and cover material more methodically; in particular, they include a larger percentage of TCM treatments (34.88%), which reflects their depth of knowledge and pedagogical emphasis. TikTok excelled in information flow and visual presentation (VIQI-1), scoring slightly higher on JAMA and VIQI measures in terms of quality. This reflects more sophisticated design formats as well as more uniform procedures for author and reference crediting. Although Bilibili videos tended to be longer and may provide more detailed information in some cases, their quality scores varied across videos. All things considered, there is a clear functional distinction between “dissemination” and “education” across the two platforms. These findings suggest that different platforms may have different strengths in visibility and content depth, but further research is needed to determine their effectiveness for health education27,28.

Differences in video quality by uploader background and professional title

According to this study, 72.88% of the short videos about ANFH were uploaded by healthcare personnel, with chief physicians and associate chief physicians making up the majority. This suggests that healthcare personnel are the primary information-disseminators in this area, which is in line with research on other orthopedic disorders29,30. When compared to non-professional users, videos uploaded by healthcare professionals generally performed better on all evaluation metrics, which may indicate that professional credentials are associated with higher perceived reliability and quality scores31,32. Our research showed that videos uploaded by attending/registered physicians received higher scores on metrics such as GQS, mDISCERN, and VIQI, which is in contrast to earlier findings that chief physicians provide higher-quality content33,34. One possible explanation is that attending or registered physicians may be more familiar with short-video communication styles and may use more accessible language or visual materials. However, this interpretation remains speculative, and the present study cannot determine whether professional title or communication style directly caused higher quality scores.

The relationship between interaction metrics and video quality

The results of this study indicated that a video’s “popularity” does not always correspond with its “scientific quality,” as several interaction metrics (likes, comments, collections, and shares) had strong connections with one another but weak-to-moderate relationships with GQS, mDISCERN, JAMA, and VIQI ratings. TikTok interaction metrics and VIQI ratings exhibited somewhat favorable associations (r ≈ 0.55–0.71), suggesting that while visual effects and transmission formats do improve audience interaction, they do not always translate into more reliable information35.This pattern may be particularly relevant to algorithm-mediated short-video environments. Exaggerated presentation techniques, emotional tales, and attention-grabbing titles are frequently given priority by creators. Short videos that spread false health information, however, raise the possibility that viewers would base their health decisions on unsubstantiated claims36.

There are comparable correlation patterns on Bilibili. Even though there are generally fewer interactions, high-quality information does not get the same incentives for spreading. The relatively weak association between quality and interaction may indicate that high-quality content does not always receive proportionally higher visibility. In order to combine algorithmic efficiency with the importance of health education, platforms should investigate incorporating “professional certification tags” or “content credibility weighting” into recommendation algorithms37.

Practical significance

According to this study, the short videos that are now available about ANFH place an undue emphasis on surgical treatment at the expense of early diagnosis, conservative measures, and rehabilitation advice. There are notable differences between various platforms in terms of distribution and instructional features. These findings may inform more targeted health education strategies addressing underrepresented topics such as early diagnosis, conservative management, and rehabilitation.

First, during health education, healthcare personnel should give special attention to sharing knowledge about early detection and non-surgical therapies. To dispel the myth that surgery is the sole option, easily comprehensible, visual content should describe the stages of illness progression, risk factor management, and important rehabilitation exercises.

Second, future health communication programs may consider involving physicians familiar with short-video communication, while maintaining expert review by senior clinicians.

Health science communication videos should also include interactive script design and audience participation mechanisms throughout the content preparation phase, building on the study’s conclusion that Q&A-style videos exhibit greater quality. Formats such as case-based explanations, simulated doctor-patient conversations, or multi-character interviews may be considered in future health communication practice, as Q&A-format videos in this study showed relatively higher reliability scores. Further research is needed to determine whether these formats improve audience understanding or communication effectiveness.

Limitations and future research directions

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, this study included only 177 ANFH-related videos from TikTok and Bilibili, without covering other platforms such as Kwai, YouTube, or Rednote. Therefore, platform selection, geographic context, sample size, and search timing may limit the generalizability of the findings. In addition, because only top-ranked videos under the default sorting algorithm were included, the results may be affected by algorithmic sampling bias related to video popularity, upload time, user location, device type, and platform updates. Thus, the included videos should be interpreted as a time-specific sample of highly visible ANFH-related videos rather than a representative sample of all available videos.

Second, the cross-sectional design cannot capture dynamic changes in video content, ranking, interaction, or quality over time. Future studies could use longitudinal or repeated-sampling designs to evaluate temporal changes in ANFH-related short-video content.

Third, although GQS, mDISCERN, JAMA, and VIQI are widely used tools for assessing online health information, they mainly evaluate general quality, reliability, transparency, and presentation, rather than directly validating the medical accuracy of each claim. This study did not systematically compare video statements with clinical guidelines, expert consensus, or specialist-adjudicated reference standards. Therefore, some videos with acceptable scores may still contain incomplete, outdated, or inaccurate medical information. Future studies should incorporate guideline-based accuracy assessment or expert panel review.

Finally, despite standardized training and established scoring tools, quality assessment still involves a degree of subjectivity. Future research could incorporate natural language processing, AI-based image or audio analysis, behavioral tracking, patient surveys, or experimental designs to evaluate video content more objectively and assess its actual influence on disease knowledge, treatment decision-making, and healthcare-seeking behaviors.

Conclusions

This study clarifies the quality and distribution traits of ANFH-related content on popular social media platforms in China. In this sample, TikTok videos showed higher interaction metrics and higher JAMA and VIQI scores than Bilibili videos. Bilibili videos tended to be longer and more frequently included TCM-related content, but showed lower interaction metrics in this sample. Videos created by healthcare personnel have the most credibility and reliability, and videos uploaded by attending/registered physicians showed relatively higher scores on several quality indicators. Future initiatives should prioritize the enhancement of interaction and accessibility while upholding current quality standards, and actively promote the involvement of young physicians in short-video scientific communication via Q&A forms. Furthermore, including more preventive information may improve the balance and comprehensiveness of ANFH-related short-video content. These findings may inform future efforts to improve the quality, accessibility, and balance of orthopedic health information on social media.

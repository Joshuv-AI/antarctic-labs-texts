Dataset on macroeconomic indicators and fiscal decentralisation indices’ variables in Central Java Province, Indonesia
Authors: Yvonne Lee, Aliya Marissa Binti Azlan, WeiLee Lim
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractMacroeconomics data was collected through the Central Statistics Agency (BPS) Central Java Province for the thirty-five regencies and municipalities in Central Java Province from 2017 to 2021. The Fundamental index of fiscal decentralisation (FFDI) and Enhanced index of fiscal decentralisation (EFDI) were adapted from [1] and corresponding datasets matched to fiscal data from the Central Java province government. The data sources and indices’ calculation methodologies were described in detail. The resulting indices, together with labour participation rate, as well as foreign and domestic investment data were analysed in a panel data analysis model with Gross Regional Domestic Product as the outcome variable. The collected data enables researchers and policy-makers to update observations on the impact of Indonesia's ‘Big Bang’ fiscal decentralisation on economic growth in a province with above-average growth rate but which had experienced significant challenges arising from the Covid-19 pandemic. This is in light of previous research findings which found that the Indonesian decentralisation has had mixed outcomes due to institutional and fiscal capability limitations within the local governments. The detailed sources and steps to obtain the required data and calculate the FFDI and EFDI enables researchers to apply the indices in providing updated observations on the impact of fiscal decentralisation on various socioeconomic phenomenon.Keywords: Investment, Labour Force Participation, Fiscal Decentralisation Index, Economic Growth, Provinces, Indonesia

Objective

The dataset was generated to enable researchers to update extant research on fiscal decentralisation's impact on regional output in regencies and cities in Central Java. Short panel analysis will be conducted towards this purpose. The analysis will determine if labour participation, domestic investment, foreign investment, fiscal decentralisation indices Fundamental index of fiscal decentralisation (FFDI) and Enhanced index of fiscal decentralisation (EFDI) significantly impact gross regional domestic product across time and across regencies and cities.

Data Description

The dataset consists of raw data and derived macroeconomic and public finance data. Raw data was retrieved and formatted for panel data analysis for four macroeconomic variables and eight public finance variables’ data as described in ‘Table 1: Names and Description of Macroeconomic and Public Finance Variables Based on Raw Data’. These twelve variables based on raw data were collected for thirty-five regencies and municipalities in Central Java Province for five years (2017 to 2021) from the Central Java Statistics Agency for a total of 2100 data points.

Analysis of the Random Effect model showed a significant Wald χ2, indicating that coefficients in this random effect model is significantly different from zero. An examination of the variables’ coefficients shows that lDI, is significant at 5% while FFDI and EFDI are significant at 1%. A one percent change in domestic investment increases GRDP by 0.0126 percent. Meanwhile, the coefficient for FFDI and EFDI are still significant but turn negative, albeit with small values. One unit increase in FFDI decreases GRDP by 0.1716 million rupiahs, and one unit increase in EFDI decreases GRDP by 2.6984 million rupiahs. 97.3 percent of the variance in GRDP is explained by differences between the regencies and municipalities in Central Java (ρ = 0.9730).

The p-value for the Breusch and Pagan LaGrangian multiplier test statistic for random effect (χ¯2) is significant at one percent. Hence, the random effect model is more appropriate than the pooled OLS model. There are sub-regional-specific effects in the data.

The fixed effects model shows that all its coefficients are significantly different from zero (the F of 15.66 is significant at one percent). The only variable that is significant is EFDI, at one percent. 99.29 percent of the variance in GRDP is explained by differences between the regencies and municipalities in Central Java (ρ = 0.9929).

The p-value for the Hausman test statistic of 58.49 and the more robust Sargan-Hansen statistic of 90.250 is significant at one percent, indicates that the fixed effect model should be used over random effect model. This indicates that the individual effects of the regencies and municipalities within Central Java affects the independent variables.

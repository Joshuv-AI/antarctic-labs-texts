Programmatic Assessment of Ethambutol-Related Optic Neuropathy in Patients with Mycobacterial Infections: A Prospective Observational Cohort Study
Authors: Shin-Shin Liu, Shih-Chao Feng, Ming-Feng Wu, Chun-Shih Chin, Hui-Chen Chen, Wei-Chang Huang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
IntroductionComprehensive data on ethambutol (EMB)-related optic neuropathy (EON) are lacking, creating a knowledge gap. Accordingly, this prospective programmatic study established a collaborative multidisciplinary team to explore EON.MethodsThis study enrolled patients who received EMB as part of their treatment regimen for mycobacterial infections. Programmatic assessments of visual abnormalities were performed for each patient by the team. EON was diagnosed by ophthalmologists. Patients diagnosed as having EON completed short-term (6-month) and long-term (5-year) follow-up.ResultsOf 476 patients, 31 (6.5%) were diagnosed as having EON. Older age (≥ 65 years, odds ratio [OR] = 2.5, P = 0.043) and diabetes mellitus (OR = 2.2, P = 0.045) were independent predictors of EON, and coexisting subjective and objective ocular abnormalities (OR = 4.8, P = 0.009) and concomitant visual acuity and color discrimination impairment (OR = 5.9, P = 0.009) were independently associated with EON in patients with ocular abnormalities at EMB discontinuation. Among patients with EON, 56.7% and 50.0% had favorable 6-month visual acuity and color discrimination outcomes, respectively. Among patients with unfavorable 6-month visual acuity outcomes, 45.5% had favorable outcomes at the 5-year follow-up. Moreover, 88.2% and 100% of patients with favorable and unfavorable 6-month visual acuity outcomes, respectively, already had these outcomes at the third month of follow-up. Additionally, 50.0% and 100% of patients with favorable and unfavorable 6-month color discrimination outcomes, respectively, already had these outcomes at the fourth month of follow-up.ConclusionsThe incidence of EON exceeded 6%. This study provides a feasible model for the comprehensive management of EON in clinical practice.

Introduction

Ethambutol (EMB) is widely used to treat infections caused by the Mycobacterium tuberculosis complex (MTBC) and nontuberculous mycobacteria (NTM), particularly the Mycobacterium avium complex (MAC) and Mycobacterium kansasii. Although EMB has limited antimicrobial activity alone, studies have demonstrated its synergistic effect against the MTBC when combined with other antimycobacterial drugs, such as isoniazid, rifampicin, and fluoroquinolones. Furthermore, the combination of EMB with macrolides in MAC treatment could delay the emergence of macrolide resistance [1].

EMB is considered to be among the least toxic antimycobacterial agents; nevertheless, drug-related optic neuropathy was observed soon after its introduction in the 1960s. In rodents with EMB-related optic neuropathy (EON), retinal ganglion cells were markedly damaged [2]. Although the exact mechanism underlying EON remains unclear, proposed hypotheses include the zinc-chelating effect of EMB and its metabolites on various mitochondrial-metal-containing enzymes and the excitotoxic pathway [3–6].

EON impairs patients’ vision and quality of life, underscoring the need for an improved clinical understanding of this ocular toxicity. Over the past decades, efforts have been made to clarify the incidence of, risk factors for, and clinical outcomes of EON [7–10]. However, most of the data on EON have been obtained from retrospective reviews, health insurance databases, and case series, potentially causing scholars to underestimate its prevalence and creating a critical knowledge gap. Hence, a well-designed prospective study incorporating programmatic monitoring and assessment is required to comprehensively describe this ocular toxicity.

To address the aforementioned gap, we established a collaborative multidisciplinary team of pulmonologists, TB case managers, directly observed therapy (DOT) providers, and ophthalmologists to implement a programmatic study. The pulmonologists enrolled patients who were infected with mycobacterial diseases and received EMB treatment; these patients’ visual function was regularly monitored by the study team. Patients in whom new ocular abnormalities were detected were referred to the ophthalmologists for further evaluation and treatment. The incidence of, risk factors for, short-term (6-month) outcomes of, and long-term (5-year) outcomes of EON were evaluated within our cohort.

Methods

Study Design, Setting, and Population

This prospective observational cohort study was conducted at Taichung Veterans General Hospital (TCVGH) from May 2016 to April 2025. The TCVGH Institutional Review Board and Ethics Committee approved the study (IRB number CE16096A), and all research procedures aligned with the Declaration of Helsinki and adhered to relevant guidelines and regulations. Informed consent was obtained from each patient.

Patients who were aged 20 years or older and were treated with EMB for mycobacterial infection were enrolled in this study. Those who were unable to complete the visual measurement procedures and transferred out during the study period were excluded. Mycobacterial infections included pulmonary and extrapulmonary TB, multidrug-resistant TB, and NTM diseases that were diagnosed and treated on the basis of local and international recommendations [11–13].

Programmatic Setting for Visual Function Monitoring and EON Evaluation

Figure 1 presents the flowchart of visual assessment procedures for patients. After EMB was prescribed as part of the treatment regimen for a patient with mycobacterial infection, the physician in charge instructed the patient to report any newly perceived subjective ocular abnormalities (e.g., blurred vision and eye pain) to the hospital’s TB case manager and the township public health center’s DOT provider at any time. At each visit, physicians asked whether the patient had experienced any newly perceived subjective ocular abnormalities. Additionally, at each visit following EMB prescription, the hospital’s medical information system sent a compulsory pop-up message to patients to collect objective visual function measurements at the TB health education room. The DOT provider measured the patient’s objective visual function once per week until cessation of EMB treatment. The objective visual function measurements included visual acuity (determined through Landolt’s broken ring test) and color vision (determined through an Ishihara color test). If a patient reported a newly perceived subjective ocular abnormality or if any impairment in objective visual function was detected, EMB was immediately discontinued. Impaired visual acuity was defined as a decrease of ≥ 2 chart lines in either eye per patient; impaired color discrimination was defined as reading < 10 plates correctly in either eye for patients with normal baseline color discrimination or reading ≥ 2 fewer plates correctly in either eye for patients with deficient baseline color discrimination [14].Fig. 1Flowchart of programmatic assessment of EON. Following EMB discontinuation due to subjective and/or objective ocular abnormalities, patients visited the ophthalmologists, who evaluated the etiology of the ocular abnormality through a series of optic examinations, including the Landolt broken ring test, the Ishihara color test, the 30-2 Humphrey visual field test, optical coherence tomography, and fundoscopy.

Outcome Assessment

Our goal was to assess the incidence of, risk factors for, short-term (6-month) outcomes of, and long-term (5-year) outcomes of EON through a prospective programmatic study. EON was diagnosed on the basis of the following criteria: subjective and/or objective visual abnormalities emerged only after the initiation of EMB use; at least one major criterion and two minor criteria for EON diagnosis were met; and other ophthalmologic conditions that may cause visual loss (e.g., cataracts, glaucoma, retinal disease, or diabetic retinopathy) or optic nerve abnormalities (e.g., glaucoma, optic neuritis, or hereditary or acquired optic neuropathy) were excluded. The major criteria are outlined as follows: (1) abnormal Landolt broken ring test results in the absence of other reasonable explanations for abnormal visual acuity, (2) deficient Ishihara color test results in the absence of other reasonable explanations for abnormal color vision, and (3) central or paracentral scotoma on the 30-2 Humphrey visual field test. The minor criteria are outlined as follows: (1) optic disc pallor and (2) visual field defects other than central or paracentral scotomas [15–20]. Each patient who received a diagnosis of EON participated in monthly follow-up at the eye clinic for 6 months and a telephone call at 5 years following EMB discontinuation to clarify the short- and long-term outcomes of EON.

Full recovery was defined as the restoration of baseline visual function. Partial recovery was defined as an improvement of ≥ 2 chart lines on the Landolt broken ring test and an increase of ≥ 2 correctly read plates on the Ishihara color test compared with the results at the time of EMB discontinuation in any affected eye. Both full recovery and partial recovery were categorized as favorable outcomes; all other outcomes were considered unfavorable. The unfavorable outcomes were further categorized as unchanged and worse. Worse outcomes were defined as a decrease of ≥ 2 chart lines on the Landolt broken ring test and ≥ 2 fewer correctly read plates on the Ishihara color test in any affected eye after EMB discontinuation; all other unfavorable outcomes were considered unchanged.

Data Collection

In addition to the ocular outcomes of interest, information on the baseline demographic characteristics, diagnosis, therapeutic regimen, comorbidities, relevant laboratory findings, start and end dates of EMB use, and EMB dosage was collected for each patient through patient record forms.

Data Management and Statistical Analysis

Electronic data were stored on a password-protected personal computer located at TCVGH. SPSS software (version 22.0; SPSS, Chicago, IL, USA) was used for data analysis. Continuous variables are presented as mean and corresponding standard deviation (SD). A paired-samples t test was performed to compare continuous variables between the study groups. Categorical variables are presented as frequencies and percentages and were compared between groups by using a chi-squared test. A two-sided P value of < 0.05 was considered significant. Multiple logistic regression analysis was performed to determine the factors associated with EON, and differences were determined using odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs).

Results

As displayed in Fig. 2, a total of 476 patients who were administered EMB as part of their therapeutic regimen for mycobacterial disease were enrolled in the study. Table 1 presents the baseline demographic and clinical characteristics of the patients. The mean age of the overall cohort was 64.9 ± 17.2 years, and 23.7% of the patients had a history of diabetes mellitus (DM). The majority of the patients were diagnosed as having pulmonary TB, whereas 15.5% of participants were diagnosed as having pulmonary or extrapulmonary NTM diseases. Subjective and/or objective ocular abnormalities were reported in 103 patients, who were referred to the eye clinic for further evaluation and treatment. Among these patients, 31 were diagnosed as having EON, whereas 72 patients were not diagnosed as having EON (Table 1). Compared with patients without ocular abnormalities or EON (n = 445), those with EON (n = 31) were older and more likely to have a history of DM. At cessation of EMB treatment, subjective and objective ocular abnormalities coexisted in 87.1% and 55.6% of the patients with and without EON, respectively. Concomitant visual acuity and color discrimination impairment were observed in 29.0% and 5.6% of the patients with and without EON, respectively (Table 1). One patient died during the short-term (6-month) follow-up period, and six patients died during the long-term (5-year) follow-up period. As displayed in Fig. 4, after the short-term follow-up period, 17 patients with EON had favorable visual acuity outcomes (with complete and partial visual acuity recovery observed in 15 and two patients, respectively); moreover, 13 patients with EON had unfavorable visual acuity outcomes (with unchanged and worse visual acuity observed in 12 patients and one patient, respectively) when compared with measurements at the time of EMB discontinuation. Among the 17 patients with EON who achieved favorable visual acuity outcomes, 15 (88.2%) exhibited these favorable outcomes at the third month of follow-up. Among the 13 patients with EON for whom unfavorable visual acuity outcomes were recorded, all 13 (100%) exhibited no favorable outcomes at the third month of follow-up. This finding suggests that the third month following EON diagnosis is a critical time to predict short-term (6-month) visual acuity. Discussion

Among 476 patients who were administered EMB as part of their treatment regimen for mycobacterial infections, 31 were diagnosed as having EON, representing an incidence rate of 6.5%. EON was independently associated with older age and comorbid DM. Among patients who experienced subjective and/or objective ocular abnormalities and were referred to the ophthalmologists, EON was independently associated with the coexistence of subjective and objective ocular abnormalities and concomitant visual acuity and color discrimination impairment. Short-term (6-month) favorable outcomes regarding visual acuity and color discrimination were observed for 56.7% and 50.0% of the patients with EON, respectively. Among patients with unfavorable short-term (6-month) visual acuity outcomes, 45.5% achieved favorable long-term (5-year) outcomes. Notably, 88.2% of the patients for whom favorable visual acuity outcomes were observed over the short-term (6-month) follow-up exhibited these favorable outcomes at the third month of follow-up, whereas 100% of patients with unfavorable short-term (6-month) visual acuity outcomes already exhibited these unfavorable outcomes at the third month of follow-up, suggesting that the third month following EON diagnosis is a critical time to predict short-term (6-month) visual acuity recovery. Additionally, 50.0% of the patients who achieved favorable color discrimination outcomes over the short-term (6-month) follow-up exhibited these favorable outcomes at the fourth month of follow-up, whereas 100% of the patients with short-term (6-month) unfavorable color discrimination outcomes exhibited these unfavorable outcomes at the fourth month of follow-up, suggesting that the fourth month following EON diagnosis is a critical time to predict short-term (6-month) color discrimination recovery.

In this prospective programmatic study, 6.5% of the patients treated with EMB developed EON. Several retrospective studies have reported a lower incidence of EON (ranging between 0.4% and 1.5%) [24–26], and patient consultations with an ophthalmologist increased the incidence of EON from 0.5% to 1.9% [25]. In a previous systemic review that included prospective, original articles to estimate the incidence of EON, the pooled cumulative incidence rate of EON was 22.5 per 1000 persons for patients administered EMB and 19.2 per 1000 persons for patients whose average EMB dose was 27.5 mg/kg/day or less over a treatment period of 2 to 9 months [7]. These figures are higher than those reported in retrospective studies, suggesting that the incidence of EON varies and that its precise identification depends on the study design. Specifically, a well-designed study, such as the present prospective programmatic study, can more precisely capture the incidence of EON.

Previous studies have identified several predictors of EON. A study that used data from Taiwan’s National Health Insurance Research Database revealed older age, hypertension, and renal dysfunction as risk factors for EON [8]. Chaitanuwong et al. reported longer EMB treatment duration, older age, hypertension, and current smoking status as potential EON risk factors [25]. Lee et al. observed that renal disease and the daily EMB dose, but not the duration of EMB treatment, contributed to the emergence of EON [26]. In our study, patients with EON were more likely to be older and to have a history of DM, particularly for those with both subjective and objective ocular abnormalities and concomitant visual acuity and color discrimination impairments when ocular abnormalities emerged. Overall, these findings suggest that clinicians should closely monitor both patients’ perceived subjective visual symptoms and objective visual function (including visual acuity and color discrimination) when prescribing EMB, particularly for patients who are older in age, have a longer duration or higher daily dose of EMB treatment, and have comorbid hypertension, DM, or renal dysfunction or a smoking history.

After the 6-month follow-up, 56.7% and 50.0% of the patients in our sample had favorable visual acuity and color discrimination outcomes, respectively. Among the patients with unfavorable visual acuity outcomes after the 6-month follow-up, 45.5% had long-term (5-year) favorable outcomes. The third and fourth months following EON diagnosis were identified as critical times at which to predict short-term (6-month) favorable visual acuity and color discrimination outcomes, respectively. A systemic review of prospective studies revealed that in reversible EON cases, resolution of visual impairments was reported at a mean follow-up duration of 3 months after EMB discontinuation [7]. Previous retrospective studies have reported that the rate of visual acuity recovery ranges from 30.8% to 63.3%. The mean time to visual acuity recovery and the mean follow-up duration range from 4 to 18.5 months and from 7.8 to 24.1 months, respectively [9, 10, 24, 26, 27]. A retrospective study indicated that the rate of color discrimination recovery was 30.8% at a mean follow-up duration of 5.3 months [26]. In summary, less than half of patients with EON typically recover, and 3–4 months following EMB discontinuation may be a critical time to predict short-term follow-up outcomes. A certain proportion of patients with EON who experience unfavorable outcomes can achieve recovery over the long-term follow-up.

This study has several strengths. First, various patients with mycobacterial infections, including pulmonary and extrapulmonary TB and NTM, were enrolled, which enhanced the generalizability of our findings. Moreover, by implementing consistency of duration in the follow-up for patients with EON, the study provided valuable insights into their clinical outcomes. However, a notable limitation is that our sample size is limited, which may lead to an overestimation of the incidence rate of EON. Nevertheless, future studies are required to determine a more effective strategy for the elimination of EON.

Conclusions

This programmatic study was conducted by a collaborative multidisciplinary team of pulmonologists, TB case managers, DOT providers, and ophthalmologists, who assessed EON in patients with mycobacterial infections. We observed a relatively high incidence of EON and noted that nearly half of the patients with EON experienced unfavorable short- and long-term clinical outcomes. The emergence and outcomes of EON are predictable. These findings underscore the necessity of programmatic EON assessment with a collaborative multidisciplinary team. This study provides a feasible model for the comprehensive management of EON in clinical practice.

Relationship between endothelial activation and stress index and radial artery occlusion in patients undergoing radial access percutaneous coronary intervention
Authors: Mustafa Özkoç, Selim Aydemir, Murat Özmen, Abuzer Ocak
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundOne of the most common complications observed after transradial coronary angiography is radial artery occlusion (RAO). Endothelial damage plays an important role in the pathophysiology of RAO.AimsIn our study, we plan to investigate the predictive value of the endothelial activation and stress index (EASIX), which is considered an indirect marker of endothelial damage, for RAO.MethodsOur study was designed as a retrospective study and includes patients who underwent transradial coronary angiography and percutaneous coronary intervention in our clinic. EASIX values were calculated from blood tests taken before the procedure. The primary endpoint of our study was determined as RAO and patients were divided into two groups according to RAO status. Variables independently associated with RAO were determined using logistic regression analysis. To evaluate the predictive capacity of the EASIX for RAO, ROC-Curve analysis was applied and the area under the curve was determined.ResultsOur study included 2342 patients. The RAO rate in the sample was 3.3% (78 patients). As a result, EASIX was independently associated with RAO (P = 0.009) and predicted RAO with a cut-off value of 1.2, 76.9% sensitivity and 77.1% specificity in ROC-Curve analysis (AUC: 0.803, 95% CI: 0.755–0.851, P < 0.001).ConclusionIn our study, we observed that EASIX was an independent predictor of RAO after transradial intervention. We believe that EASIX may be useful in early detection of patients at high risk of developing RAO, especially before transradial catheterization, and in taking the necessary precautions against this complication.

Introduction

Coronary angiography is the gold standard diagnostic method for coronary artery disease. Transradial catheterization is widely preferred in coronary angiography due to its early mobilization, rapid discharge, low cost and patient comfort [1]. The most common complications of transradial coronary angiography are hematoma, vasospasm, radial pseudoaneurysm and radial occlusion at the intervention site [2].

The incidence of radial artery occlusion (RAO) varies from center to center and from study to study, ranging from 5% to 30% [3]. RAO is usually asymptomatic, but some patients may occur pain, paresthesia, or decreased extremity functionality at the site of occlusion. Cases of arm ischemia due to incomplete collateral circulation after RAO have also been reported [4]. Although RAO is usually asymptomatic, radial artery patency is important because it can be used as an access route in repeated percutaneous coronary interventions, as a graft in coronary bypass surgery, or to create a fistula in hemodialysis patients [5].

RAO is characterized by catheterization-induced endothelial damage and thrombus formation due to decreased blood flow [3]. Repeated punctures, increased compression times, and higher compression pressures have been shown to increase the incidence of RAO, while heparin use has been shown to decrease it. However, the associated risk factors have not yet been fully defined [6]. For this purpose, we plan to investigate the predictive value of the endothelial activation and stress index (EASIX), which is considered an indirect marker of endothelial damage [7], for RAO that may develop after transradial coronary angiography and percutaneous coronary intervention.

Materials and methods

Study design and patient selection

Our study was designed as a retrospective, single-center study. Patients over 18 years of age who underwent transradial coronary angiography followed by percutaneous coronary intervention at our clinic between 2018 and 2025 were included in the study. Routine complete blood count and comprehensive biochemical tests were performed on all patients admitted for coronary angiography and percutaneous coronary intervention. Patients with incomplete laboratory parameters and demographic data, and those under 18 years of age, were excluded from the study.

Laboratory parameters and baseline demographic data (age, gender, additional diseases, habits, medications used) of the patients obtained before transradial coronary angiographic procedures were accessed through file scans and the hospital automation system, and the relevant data were recorded. EASIX values of all patients were calculated and recorded using LDH, platelet count and creatinine parameters. The primary endpoint of our study was determined as RAO that may occur after transradial coronary angiographic intervention. RAO complications were evaluated in patients before discharge. In cases where clinically suspicious RAO was present, or if a radial pulse could not be detected or was weak on palpation or handheld doppler ultrasonography (USG), confirmation was performed using upper extremity arterial system doppler USG. Post-procedure RAO data were accessed through upper extremity arterial system doppler USG reports recorded in the hospital automation system. The flowchart of the research is available in Fig. 1.

Laboratory tests and definitions

In our clinic, routine biochemical and hematological tests are performed on all patients before coronary angiography and percutaneous coronary intervention procedures to better assess their nutritional, inflammatory, and hormonal status. These tests include parameters such as LDH, albumin, cholesterol panel, thyroid function tests, kidney function tests, liver function tests, and complete blood count. Laboratory data were obtained from routine complete blood count results and biochemical measurements from venous blood samples taken before the transradial coronary angiography and percutaneous coronary intervention procedures. The EASIX was calculated using the formula [(LDH in U/L) × (Creatinine in mg/dL)] / (Platelet count in 109/L) [7].

RAO was defined as the absence of pulse on palpation and the absence of anterograde flow signal on upper extremity doppler USG study [8].

Coronary angiography percutaneous coronary intervention procedures

Coronary angiography and percutaneous coronary intervention precedures were performed in all patients using a Shunmei-Shun guider introducer set with a thickness of 6 French and a length of 7 cm, via the right radial artery, with 6 French diagnostic and guiding catheters (Medronic launcher guides). After sheath placement, 5000 IU of unfractionated heparin was administered, and after the decision for percutaneous coronary intervention, the heparin level was increased to 70–100 IU/kg. A transradial approach technique was used. IV nitroglycerin (100 mcg) was also administered. If access was not possible after two punctures or if spasm occurred, a switch was made to the left radial or femoral artery. These patients were excluded from the study. The coronary angiography operator consisted of cardiology specialists who performed at least 500 procedures annually. Following the procedure, all patients underwent angio closure using an angiogram closure pad as a hemostasis protocol. The pad was inflated with 20 cc of air, then removed within 4 h by releasing 5 cc of air hourly.

Statistical analysis

Study data were analyzed using SPSS 22.0 statistical package program (SPSS Inc, Chicago, Illinois). Numerical variables were shown as mean±standard deviation, and variables that were not normally distributed were shown as median. Categorical variables were shown as frequency (n) andpercentage (%). For categorical variables, differences between groups were analyzed with the Chi-square test or the Fisher test when the Chi-square test assumptions were not met. The normality of the distribution of numerical variables was analyzed with the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Comparison of continuous variables between two independent groups was performed using the independent t-test when parametric test assumptions were met, and using the Mann Whitney-U test when parametric test assumptions were not met. Statistical significance was determined as P < 0.05. Spearman correlation analysis was used to evaluate the relationship between the EASIX and various parameters. Univariate and multivariate Logistic Regression analysis was performed to assess the independent contribution of each variable to RAO. Odds ratio (OR) and 95% confidence intervals (CI) were calculated. To evaluate the predictive power of the EASIX for RAO, Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) analysis was performed and the area under the curve (AUC) was determined.

Discussion

In our study, we aimed to evaluate the relationship between RAO, which can occur as a complication after transradial coronary angiographic intervention, and EASIX. In our retrospective study of 2342 patients, we observed that EASIX was an independent predictor of RAO after transradial intervention. When the literature is examined, our study is the first to evaluate the relationship between EASIX, a composite biomarker including LDH, platelet and creatinine parameters, and RAO.

Endothelial dysfunction plays an important role in the pathophysiology of RAO. When the literature is examined, recent studies using imaging methods such as intravascular ultrasound and optical coherence tomography and performing radial artery imaging have shown that transradial cardiac catheterization causes traumatic damage to the radial artery wall, and as a result, serious endothelial cell dysfunction can occur [9, 10]. Vascular trauma and resulting endothelial dysfunction cause intimal hyperplasia and RAO after transradial intervention [11].

There are some studies in the literature showing that the occurrence of RAO may be associated with some blood parameters and scoring systems before or after transradial intervention. In a retrospective study including a total of 1156 patients, it was shown that a high platelet/haemoglobin ratio may be an independent predictor of radial artery thrombus development [12]. In another prospectively designed study involving 500 patients, a CHA2DS2-VASc score ≥ 3 was found to be an independent predictor of RAO [5]. There is another retrospective study showing that post-procedure whole blood viscosity is associated with RAO in which 215 patients underwent the transradial approach [13].

EASIX, a composite biomarker that can be easily calculated from routine biochemical and complete blood analyses, includes LDH, creatinine and platelet parameters and is considered an indirect indicator of endothelial dysfunction. It was developed to predict endothelial complications that may occur after allogeneic stem cell transplantation [14]. EASIX has been investigated in some hematological malignancies, such as myelodysplastic syndrome, multiple myeloma, and diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, and has been shown to be a valuable prognostic marker [7, 15, 16]. In a retrospective cohort study of 1036 patients with acute myocardial infarction, EASIX was associated with an increased 30-day risk [17]. Another study involving 1283 patients found that EASIX was associated with mortality due to coronary artery disease, independent of the timing of catheterization [18]. In another sample of 6138 hypertensive patients, EASIX was associated with all-cause mortality and cardiovascular mortality [19]. Additionally, EASIX was associated with all-cause mortality in several studies including 4896 patients with atrial fibrillation and 1796 patients with chronic heart failure [20, 21]. Finally, in a study including 20,285 prediabetes and diabetes patients, EASIX was found to be an independent predictor of all-cause and cardiovascular mortality [22]. Despite all these positive studies conducted with EASIX, no other studies examining its relationship with vascular pathologies such as RAO, in which endothelial dysfunction is considered the main pathophysiological cause, have been observed in the literature.

In our study, we observed that EASIX statistically significantly predicted the occurrence of RAO after transradial coronary angiography and percutaneous coronary intervention procedures. We believe this could provide important implications for clinical practice. The fact that EASIX can be easily calculated from routine laboratory data, its low cost and its easy accessibility make its use in clinical practice attractive. Early identification of patients at high risk of developing RAO after transradial intervention will encourage us to be more vigilant and take pre-procedural precautions. Examples of such measures include avoiding repeated failed punctures, using smaller catheters and prioritizing heparinization during the procedure, avoiding high-pressure compression during catheter removal immediately after the procedure, minimizing compression time, and preferring other access sites such as the ulnar or femoral arteries in very high-risk patients.

Limitations

The most important limitations of our study are that it is retrospective and single-center. Unfortunately, the reliability of data in retrospective studies is subject to bias due to the nature of these studies. In addition, the single-center nature of our study limits the generalizability of the results to a larger population with different demographic characteristics. Another limitation that we would like to point out is that the total number of female patients in our study was less than the number of male patients. The results may be more accurate in a sample with a more even distribution of male and female patients. Furthermore, the use of this score may be limited in clinics where routine LDH testing is not performed. In our study, RAO was evaluated with Doppler USG in symptomatic patients, but not in asymptomatic patients. This may mean that the study does not reflect the possible RAO rate in asymptomatic patients. In our study, due to the retrospective nature of the analysis, we were unable to fully access data on procedural aspects such as sheath size and sheath-to-artery ratio, presence of spasm, operator experience, and annual variations in application. This may have affected the results. However, since the number of puncture attempts, compression technique and duration, heparin dose and timing were applied standardly in all patients, there were no differences between patient groups and these were not included in Table 2 and the regression analysis. Additionally, due to the nature of retrospective analysis, long-term results (e.g., Doppler USG evaluation results after 1 month and late-RAO data) were not available. Therefore, the RAO endpoint may have been underestimated. As a result, multicenter, larger population and prospective studies with a more equal distribution of demographic data may provide us with stronger results in this regard.

Conclusion

We believe that predicting the risk of RAO after transradial coronary angiographic intervention using various indices that can be easily calculated from routine pre-procedural laboratory parameters could be beneficial in the early identification of high-risk patients and the implementation of necessary precautions against this complication. In our study, EASIX was found to independently predict RAO after transradial intervention, and its use for this purpose may be beneficial in reducing procedure-related peripheral complications.

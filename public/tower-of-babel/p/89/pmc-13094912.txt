Development, implementation, and evaluation of an Interprofessional education program in maternity clinical practice for medical interns and undergraduate midwifery students
Authors: Fatemeh Shaghaghi, Raziyeh Maasoumi, Mehrnaz Geranmayeh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:Interprofessional education (IPE) is crucial for preparing a health workforce that is “collaborative practice-ready,” enabling them to provide comprehensive, holistic, and patient-centered care. This study aims to develop, implement, and evaluate an IPE program in maternity clinical practice for medical interns and undergraduate midwifery students.MATERIALS AND METHODS:Phase I primarily focuses on methods to design an IPE model and develop the corresponding curriculum based on this model. Phase II is a study comprising two stages. The first stage involves implementing the finalized curriculum with medical interns and midwifery students at Gonabad University. The second stage includes a quasi-experimental pre-test and post-test intervention designed to evaluate the program’s impact on students’ attitudes and performance in interprofessional collaboration.CONCLUSION:This study describes the development of an IPE program in maternity clinical practice, designed to enhance and facilitate collaboration among healthcare professionals in maternity care settings. The training program has the potential to assist healthcare systems in reducing clinical errors, addressing hospital-based maternal mortalities and morbidities, lowering healthcare costs, and ultimately improving the quality of care. Additionally, it may serve as a guiding framework for educators seeking to integrate IPE into their curricula to close the gaps between education and practice.

Introduction

Interprofessional education (IPE) is a vital method for preparing students for entry into the healthcare workforce, emphasizing the importance of collaboration and teamwork as essential skills.[1] According to the World Health Organization (WHO), IPE occurs when “two or more professions learn about, from, and with each other to enable effective collaboration and improve health outcomes.[2]” to facilitate effective cooperation and improve health outcomes. Preparing a “collaborative practice-ready” healthcare workforce through IPE is necessary to support comprehensive, holistic, and patient-centered care.[34]

A healthcare worker considered collaborative practice-ready has developed the competencies to function effectively within interprofessional teams. “Collaborative practice happens when multiple health workers from different professional backgrounds work together with patients, families, carers, and communities to deliver the highest quality of care.” This approach also enables healthcare professionals to engage individuals with diverse skills that contribute to achieving health goals within specific community contexts.[2]

Despite its importance, most healthcare training worldwide occurs primarily within intraprofessional settings, where educational activities are limited to students within the same field. Each profession typically follows a separate curriculum with minimal awareness of teaching in other disciplines, resulting in graduates who lack interprofessional skills, clear role definitions, and critical communication abilities. This gap often leads to issues in healthcare delivery, inefficient resource use, suboptimal patient outcomes, and increased workplace conflicts.

Implementing IPE during training can help resolve these challenges by fostering a workforce prepared for collaborative practice.[5] As healthcare systems shift toward a person-centered model and patient care becomes increasingly complex, IPE and interprofessional collaborative practice (IPCP) have gained momentum as essential components in healthcare professional education and practice.[34]

Backed by the WHO and other international health organizations, IPE has been widely recognized as a strategy to improve health outcomes. The Institute of Medicine (IOM) notably emphasized that “health professionals should be educated to deliver patient-centered care as members of an interdisciplinary team…” highlighting that patients benefit from safer, high-quality care when healthcare professionals work effectively as a team, communicate productively, and understand each other’s roles.[6] Reflecting this standard, in 2013, the Liaison Committee on Medical Education mandated that medical education programs prepare students to work collaboratively within interdisciplinary healthcare teams.[7]

Among the key competencies of doctors as a collaborator based on the Canadian Medical Education Directives for Specialists (Can MEDS) framework are as follows: “participation effectively and appropriately in an interprofessional healthcare team and effectively working with other health professionals to prevent, negotiate, and resolve interprofessional conflict.”[8]

As international organizations have promoted IPE to redesign healthcare systems with improved interprofessional teamwork, universities worldwide are increasingly incorporating meaningful IPE experiences for students.[1] However, IPE has received limited attention in Iran’s educational system. IPE is not yet part of the medical curricula approved by Iran’s Ministry of Health and Medical Education, nor is it included in continuing education programs. Consequently, research on IPE in Iran is scarce, making this study relevant and urgent. The necessity for IPE in Iran highlights a critical need for the healthcare community to recognize and prioritize its value, and the potential impact of this study on the healthcare system in Iran is significant.

Existing research includes studies by Keshmiri et al. (2017),[9] which evaluated the “Effectiveness of an IPE model based on the transtheoretical model of behavior change to improve interprofessional collaboration,” and Karbasi et al. (2019),[10] which assessed “the development, implementation, and effectiveness of an interprofessional collaboration competency model based on community-based medical education on the attitudes and performance of medical and midwifery interns at Tehran University of Medical Sciences.”.

For IPE to be effective in Iran, it is essential to develop educational models that provide an operational framework for interprofessional competencies aligned with Iran’s cultural context.

The purpose of this study is to propose and evaluate innovative IPE models that are specifically designed for the health care system and then to select a suitable model with the cultural context of Iran that can potentially improve the quality of health care delivery in the country.

In maternal and neonatal care, collaboration between physicians and midwives is essential for the safe and effective care of pregnant women, women in labor, and newborns. These woman-centered care settings are complex, requiring continuous information exchange and the coordinated implementation of interprofessional care strategies.[11]

Thus, this study seeks to design, implement, and evaluate an IPE program in maternity clinical practice for medical interns and undergraduate midwifery students. It aims to contribute to a foundational understanding of IPE among these students and enhance their theoretical and practical knowledge in IPE, potentially benefiting their future careers and the healthcare system as a whole. This study is organized into two sequential phases, each with distinct qualitative (to design a structured IPE program in maternity clinical practice tailored for medical interns and undergraduate midwifery students) and quantitative components (to implement the developed IPE program and assess its effectiveness in enhancing collaborative competencies, attitudes, and performance among medical interns and midwifery students).

Materials and Methods

Study design and setting

This research utilizes a sequential exploratory mixed-methods design conducted at Tehran University of Medical Sciences and Gonabad University of Medical Sciences. The study is divided into two phases [Figure 1].

Phase I: Qualitative study

A qualitative phase focused on designing an IPE model and developing an IPE curriculum in maternity clinical practice based on the model. This phase involves a scoping review. This review will follow the methodological framework for scoping reviews proposed by the Joanna Briggs Institute[12] and will be reported in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analysis Extension for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA-ScR).[13]

The key research questions in this scoping review will be explained as follows:

To pinpoint pertinent studies, the research will encompass five English-language databases: Ovid MEDLINE, Embase (Ovid), Web of Science (SCIE, SSCI, and ESCI), CINAHL Plus (EBSCOhost), and Scopus. In each database, the core concepts or controlled vocabularies contained in the titles and abstracts of retrieved papers will be identified.

Following the scoping review, relevant IPE/IPCP models, their characteristics, theories, and evaluation methods will be identified. The Delphi method will finalize the IPE model, select validated assessment tools, and develop a comprehensive curriculum for medical interns and midwifery students in maternity clinical practice. The Delphi rounds will involve input from a panel of experts guided by questions developed by the research team.

Expert panel selection

Ten experts in interprofessional collaboration, drawn from faculty in relevant fields, will be invited to participate in the Delphi rounds. Each panelist must have experience as a collaborator or principal investigator in at least one related research project and possess expertise in IPE.

Study participants and sampling

Ten faculty members with expertise in medical education, midwifery, reproductive health, clinical education, or other relevant fields will be recruited for the Delphi rounds, provided they are familiar with IPE concepts.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria

Faculty members must have prior involvement in a related research project as a collaborator or principal investigator.

Exclusion criteria

Participants who choose to withdraw or discontinue involvement will be excluded from the study.

Sampling

In the first phase, purposeful sampling with criterion-based selection will identify eligible experts for the Delphi rounds.

Research environment

The study’s first phase will be conducted at the Tehran University of Medical Sciences to select an appropriate IPE model.

Data collection process

Data collection will begin following the approval of the research proposal, ethical clearance from the joint ethics committee of the Nursing, Midwifery, and Rehabilitation faculties, and written authorization from the Tehran University of Medical Sciences. Data will be collected using the Delphi method.

Phase II: Quantitative study

This phase will commence following the selection or development of an IPE model and the corresponding curriculum. It consists of two main stages:

Curriculum Implementation: The finalized curriculum will be implemented with medical interns and undergraduate midwifery students at Gonabad University of Medical Sciences.

Intervention Design: This intervention will follow a quasi-experimental pre-test and post-test format on only one group, and the effectiveness of the interprofessional maternity care education program on changing students’ attitude and performance in interprofessional collaboration will be determined.

Inclusion criteria

All medical interns and undergraduate midwifery students at Gonabad University of Medical Sciences who are Iranian nationals and consent to participate will be included. Participants must also agree to complete all study-related activities.

Exclusion criteria

Incomplete or inconsistent responses will lead to exclusion from the study.

Research sample

The sample will comprise general medical interns and undergraduate midwifery students from Gonabad University of Medical Sciences enrolled in the IPE program.

Research environment

The quantitative phase will be conducted at Gonabad University of Medical Sciences and Allameh Behloul Gonabadi Hospital, where the IPE curriculum will be delivered.

Sample size calculation

The sample size will be determined using a non-randomized sampling approach. Statistical formulas will ensure sufficient power for pre- and post-intervention comparisons.

Data collection tools and technique

Demographic Information Form: Captures demographic data, including age, gender, academic status, and background.

IPE Program Evaluation Tools: These evaluation tools will measure the program’s effectiveness, with potential modifications from the Delphi rounds. These tools will be determined in Delphi rounds according to the results of the scoping review study and according to the developed model. A new tool will be developed if no suitable tool meets psychometric validation standards.

Ethical consideration

This article is the result of a research project approved by Tehran University of Medical Sciences with code IR.TUMS.MEDICINE.REC.1401.777.

Data analysis

Data will be analyzed using descriptive and inferential statistics to assess the impact of the IPE intervention.

Phase One: Qualitative analysis

Delphi Round Data: Thematic analysis of Delphi discussions will identify core concepts, categories, and relationships pertinent to the IPE model. The focus will be on choosing the appropriate model. IPE/IPCP models/frameworks will be critically appraised to determine which model is most appropriate to guide the development of an IPE curriculum. The criteria used to critique models/frameworks are comprehensiveness of content, logical coherence, conceptual clarity, level of abstraction, clinical utility, and perspective of culture.

Discussion

This study focused on developing an IPE/IPCP model tailored to the Iranian cultural context and then designing, implementing, and evaluating an IPE program in maternity clinical practice based on the model for medical interns and undergraduate midwifery students. Given the complexity of patient-centered care, especially in obstetric and midwifery settings, a multidisciplinary and interprofessional approach is essential.

Challenges to implementing interprofessional collaboration (IC) in clinical obstetrics are well-documented across different professional perspectives. Issues such as rigid hierarchical structures, fragmented care, lack of mutual respect and trust, and ambiguous roles are often cited as major obstacles.[14] Midwives, for instance, frequently report a high-stress work environment with considerable potential for conflict.[15]

Since 1971, the American College of Nurse-Midwives (ACNM) and the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG) have jointly advocated for collaborative practice between midwives and obstetrician-gynecologists. In February 2017, the ACNM-ACOG launched a project to redesign maternity care education, which included developing an IPE curriculum to enhance teamwork between these groups. The curriculum, comprising seven modules with skill-based activities and interprofessional practice opportunities, was implemented in June and July 2019, followed by a formal evaluation. This initiative utilized the Interprofessional education Collaborative (IPEC) Core Competencies as a guiding framework.[16] Despite such successful examples, IPE largely remains peripheral to most health curricula.[1718]

Grace’s study identified IPE models used in health curricula, including medical, nursing, allied health, and social sciences participants. The study categorized IPE models into two main curriculum designs: (a) extracurricular or partially integrated curricula (28%) and (b) fully integrated curricula (72%). Some models incrementally developed interprofessional capabilities, with integrated designs introducing IPE across the program. In contrast, extracurricular or partially integrated curricula offered elective or compulsory modules within specific parts of the program, including clinical placements.[19]

Other studies, such as those by Barr et al.,[20] Cook et al.,[21] and Swisher et al.,[22] have explored diverse IPE models, including implementations in Canada and the United States. In Iran, Keshmiri et al. developed an interprofessional model based on behavior change theory, incorporating expert feedback. This model, validated by specialists, was structured across three behavior change stages (attitude, intention, and performance) and applied in clinical and emergency settings.[922232425] Similarly, Karbasi et al. designed a competency-based interprofessional model for community health, significantly improving medical and midwifery interns’ attitudes and performance. This model, grounded in ecological behavior change theory, incorporated three levels: intrapersonal, interpersonal, and organizational.[10]

Limitation and recommendation

Given the rapid advancement of IPE and IC, adaptable educational models that provide a structured framework for IPE competencies while respecting the Iranian cultural context are urgently needed, and this study is one of its strengths. On the other hand, given that similar studies in Iran are limited, there is a need to use foreign case studies. The aim of this study is to address this need by proposing and evaluating a culturally relevant IPE model for the Iranian healthcare system.

Conclusion

This study aims to design or adapt an IPE model suitable for Iran and to implement and evaluate an IPE program for medical interns and undergraduate midwifery students based on this model. If proven effective, the program could be proposed as a permanent course unit in Iran’s undergraduate medical and midwifery curricula. This IPE initiative also holds the potential to strengthen IC among healthcare providers, contributing to enhanced teamwork—an essential objective for improving maternal healthcare and preventive services.

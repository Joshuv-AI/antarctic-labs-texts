Effect and mechanism of auranofin on extrusion and migration of breast cancer cells
Authors: Linyan Ma, Xiaojin Sun, Wei Gao, Pei Zhang, Surong Zhao, Ying Wang, Hao Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe relationship between cell extrusion and the occurrence of epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) in tumor cells, as well as their metastasis, is inseparable. This research aimed to observe the effect of the thioredoxin reductase (TrxR) inhibitor auranofin on EMT in breast cancer MDA-MB-231 cells and explore whether auranofin can reduce the migratory activity of tumor cells by acting on cancer cell extrusion.MethodsA cancer cell extrusion model was established by reducing the cell growth environment. The impact of auranofin on the viability of MDA-MB-231 cells was assessed using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay. Microscopic observation revealed changes in the number and relative EMT morphology of extruded cells in MDA-MB-231 cells treated with auranofin for 24 h. Transwell migration and cell scratch assay were performed to assess the migratory activity of MDA-MB-231 cells under varying auranofin concentrations. Western blot analysis was employed to examine the expression levels of EMT-related and migration-related proteins in Auranofin-treated MDA-MB-231 cells.ResultsA cancer cell extrusion model was successfully established by manipulating the cell growth environment. The results of CCK-8 assay showed that auranofin could inhibit the viability of MDA-MB-231 cells. Migration assays revealed a concentration-dependent inhibition of MDA-MB-231 cell migration by auranofin. Auranofin could inhibit the migratory activity of MDA-MB-231 cells, and the intensity of inhibition increased with the increase of auranofin concentration. Western blot analysis indicated that auranofin suppressed the expression of EMT-related proteins, reduced the phenomenon of cell extrusion, modulated the EMT process, and decreased the expression of MMP-9 and MMP-2.ConclusionsAuranofin can reverse EMT transformation induced by cell extrusion in MDA-MB-231 breast cancer cells and decrease cell migratory potential. This effect is possibly mediated through the modulation of EMT-associated protein expression, including E-cadherin and vimentin, along with the suppression of migration-associated proteins MMP-9 and MMP-2.Keywords: Breast cancer, cell extrusion, thioredoxin reductase (TrxR), auranofin, epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT)

• This study explored the regulatory effect of auranofin on cell extrusion and explored approaches to reduce cellular EMT and migration from the perspective of cancer cell extrusion.

Introduction

Breast cancer ranks among the most prevalent malignancies in women. Despite the extensive research on this disease, its incidence and mortality rates continue to rise annually (1). According to the 2022 global report on cancer incidence and mortality across 185 countries, breast cancer follows only lung cancer in prevalence (2). The majority of breast cancer cases are diagnosed at advanced stages, increasing the likelihood of local recurrence and distant metastasis. Consequently, identifying strategies to mitigate recurrence and metastasis remains a focal point in anti-cancer investigations. Current treatment modalities for breast cancer include surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, and endocrine therapy. However, these conventional approaches are associated with significant adverse effects and limited efficacy (3). Breast cancer is classified into four main subtypes, with triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) being more prevalent among younger patients. TNBC is characterized by rapid growth, high malignancy, and an elevated risk of metastasis (4). Therefore, understanding the metastatic mechanisms of TNBC and identifying novel drug targets hold great significance for individuals with breast cancer.

Auranofin, a gold derivative (5), historically utilized in treating rheumatoid arthritis, exhibits favorable biosafety. Recent research indicates its efficacy in treating various conditions beyond rheumatoid arthritis, including tumors and bacterial infections. The thioredoxin (Trx) system comprises Trx, thioredoxin reductase (TrxR), and nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate (NADPH) (6). TrxR, a vital enzyme, plays a role in tumor invasion and metastasis, with elevated levels in tumors correlating with tumor progression (7). Increased TrxR/Trx levels can enhance tumor invasion, metastasis, and resistance to treatment. Auranofin selectively inhibits TrxR1 and TrxR2 in mammals, reducing TrxR/Trx levels and offering promise in tumor treatment, thereby emerging as a promising anticancer agent. The inhibition of TrxR by auranofin indicates its potential as a candidate for anticancer drug development, meriting further investigation (8).

A study has indicated that cancer cells can escape from the primary tumor site via a process termed cell extrusion, which might serve as an effective mechanism for eliminating cancer cells from the body (9). Breast cancer cells originate from breast epithelial cells, and normal cells can maintain a balance between proliferation and apoptosis through cell extrusion and competition, thereby safeguarding the organism. A study has shown that epithelial cells in an overcrowded environment will extrude living cells and lead them to death, but this method may also promote the metastasis of tumor cells (10). However, this process may also facilitate the metastasis of tumor cells. This paper aims to develop a cancer cell extrusion model to observe the phenomenon of cancer cell extrusion, in order to determine whether breast cancer cells can indeed escape from the primary tumor site in this manner, thus enabling their spread and metastasis.

Epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) has emerged as a focal point in cancer research in recent years. A study has indicated that the EMT process in cells can be triggered by various stimuli (11). Once EMT occurs, it alters the expression of related proteins, such as E-cadherin and vimentin, and also elevates the expression of metastasis-associated metalloproteinases, including MMP-9 and MMP-2 (12). Epithelial-derived cells are more susceptible to metastasis following EMT. However, few studies have addressed the potential mechanisms involved. Consequently, intervening during the early stages when tumor cells extrude from the epithelial layer in the process of metastasis could be an effective strategy to diminish breast cancer metastasis.

The clinical treatment of TNBC, characterized by rapid proliferation, strong invasiveness, and a high risk of metastasis, typically involves chemotherapy. However, there are currently no highly effective targeted therapeutic drugs available. This paper establishes a cancer cell extrusion model using TNBC MDA-MB-231 cells to clarify the relationship between breast cancer cell extrusion and the occurrence of EMT. It explores the regulatory effect of auranofin on cell extrusion and seeks ways to reduce cellular EMT from the perspective of cancer cell extrusion. Additionally, it investigates target signals closely related to tumor metastatic activity and explores the mechanisms of tumor recurrence and metastasis. We present this article in accordance with the MDAR reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2025-86/rc).

Methods

Cells, chemicals and reagents

Human breast cancer MDA-MB-231 cells were purchased from the Cell Bank of the Shanghai Institute of Cell Biology, Chinese Academy of Sciences (Shanghai, China), and were cryopreserved by the Research Center of Bengbu Medical University (Bengbu, China). Auranofin and Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) were purchased from Sigma-Aldric (St. Louis, USA). Leibovitzs-15 (L-15) culture medium base was obtained from Keygen Biotechnology (Nanjing, China). Trypsin digestion solution, penicillin-streptomycin solution, fetal bovine serum (FBS), radioimmunoprecipitation assay buffer (RIPA) lysis buffer, and bicinchoninic acid assay (BCA) protein assay kit were obtained from Beyotime Biotechnology (Shanghai, China). Antibodies were obtained from Cell Signaling Technology (Danvers, USA). Polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (PAGE) gel rapid preparation kit was obtained from Epizyme Biotechnology (Cambridge, USA).

Establishment of the cancer cell extrusion model

MDA-MB-231 cells were seeded in L-15 medium containing 10% FBS and 1% penicillin–streptomycin cultured at 37 ℃ with 5% CO2. After passaging 2 or 3 times for stable growth, the cells were seeded at high density (above 90%) in culture bottles to create crowded conditions that facilitate cell extrusion. Cells were closely monitored under a microscope after seeding, and when cell extrusion was observed, the extruded cells were immediately collected from the culture medium and counted.

Cell viability assay

MDA-MB-231 cells in the logarithmic growth phase were seeded at a density of 5,000 cells per well in a 96-well plate. After a period of time, the cells were treated with culture medium containing six different concentrations (0, 0.625, 1.25, 2.5, 5, 10 and 20 µM) of auranofin for 24 hours. Then, CCK-8 solution was added in the dark, and after incubation at 37 ℃ for 30 minutes, the absorbance values could be measured at 450 nm.

Morphological observation of extruded cells

MDA-MB-231 cells were planted into 6-well plates and observed using an inverted microscope. until the cells are confluent, Once the cells have reached confluence, treat them with varying concentrations of auranofin for 24 hours. Subsequently, collect the extruded cells from the culture medium for further culturing. The quantities and morphological changes of these extruded cells were captured using the inverted microscope after 24 and 48 hours.

Transwell assay

To determine migration activity, cells were seeded at a density of 50,000 cells into the transwell chambers. Auranofin was added to the upper compartment, which was filled with L-15 culture medium containing 0.1% body surface area (BSA) and the cells, whereas the lower compartment was filled with 800 µL of L-15 culture medium containing 5% FBS. The transwell chambers were incubated in an atmosphere of 5% CO2 at 37 ℃, with the reaction proceeding for 24 hours. Subsequently, the cells on the upper surface of the membranes were gently removed using cotton swabs, and the membranes were stained with 0.2% crystal violet. The excess stain was rinsed three times with phosphate buffer saline (PBS). Migratory cells per membrane were counted and photographed using an inverted microscope. The percentage inhibition was calculated using the following equation: percentage inhibition =100− (cell number in the compound-treated experiment/cell number in the vehicle-treated experiment) ×100%.

Wound-healing assay

Cells were planted into a six-well plates and mark lines every 1 cm on the plate’s reverse side. Once the cells have adhered to the plate at a rate of 90%, a small pipette tip was used to scratch along the marked lines. The scraped cells were gently washed three times with PBS, and different concentrations of auranofin were added for future culture. The width of the wound was measured using a microscope at both 0 and 24 hours.

Western blotting

The expression levels of proteins in the breast cancer cells were examined using the Western blot analysis. In general, the cells were harvested and further resuspended in the protease inhibitor lysis buffer. The whole cell lysate was incubated on ice for 30 min and the homogenate was centrifuged at 12,000 rpm. for 15 min at 4 ℃. The protein concentrations were determined using the BCA protein quantification assay. About 20 µg of total protein was separated on 10% sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS)-PAGE and the separated proteins were transferred onto a polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membrane. Subsequently, the membrane was blocked with 5% skim milk for 3 hours, incubated with the primary antibody overnight at 4 ℃, followed by incubation with the secondary antibody at room temperature for 2 hours. Finally, the exposure image was captured using a gel imaging system.

Statistical analysis

The experimental data results were analyzed using GraphPad Prism software (San Diego, USA). The data are expressed as mean differences and standard deviations. A P value of less than 0.05 indicates that the difference is statistically significant.

Results

A breast cancer cell extrusion model was established by reducing the cell growth environment

To verify the presence of the cell extrusion phenomenon in breast cancer cells, this paper established a breast cancer cell extrusion model by seeding cells at high density and reducing the cell growth environment. The extrusion phenomenon of MDA-MB-231 cells was observed under a microscope, with cells marked by red arrows indicating those that had become rounded due to compression by surrounding cells (Figure 1A), confirming the presence of cell extrusion in breast cancer cells. The extruded cells were then collected and cultured further (Figure 1B).

The extrusion phenomenon in MDA-MB-231 cells. (A) Extrusion phenomenon of breast cancer MDA-MB-231 cells was observed under an inverted microscope at 100× magnification. (B) Collect the extruded cells and continue to culture. Cells marked by red arrows indicating those that had become rounded due to compression by surrounding cells.

Morphological changes of extruded cells

To determine whether extruded cells undergo the EMT process, this study observed normal and extruded MDA-MB-231 cells at 24 and 48 hours (Figure 2). It was found that although the extruded cells could grow, replicate, and be passaged normally, their cell morphology and structure exhibited certain changes. Specifically, the extruded cells acquired mesenchymal characteristics, indicating that they underwent EMT transformation (after epithelial cells undergo EMT, they lose polarity, their structure becomes more relaxed, intercellular connections weaken, and the formation of pseudopodia increases, among other changes).

Morphological observation of normal and extruded MDA-MB-231 cells. (A) MDA-MB-231 cells were passaged for 24 hours. (B) MDA-MB-231 cells were passaged for 48 hours. (C) Extruded MDA-MB-231 cells were passaged for 24 hours. (D) Extruded MDA-MB-231 cells were passaged for 48 hours. These images were taken by an inverted microscope at 100× magnification.

The effect of auranofin on the survival rate of MDA-MB-231 cells

This study aimed to examine the impact of auranofin on the survival rate of MDA-MB-231 cells and to identify an effective drug concentration for future experiments. Cells were treated with different concentrations of auranofin for 24 hours, after which the cell survival rate was measured (Figure 3). The findings indicated that the survival rate remained above 80% for the first three concentrations of Auranofin. However, when the concentration reached 10 µM, the survival rate plummeted below 50%. Upon data analysis, the half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) of auranofin was calculated to be 9.237 µM.

Cell viability of MDA-MB-231 cells treated with different concentrations (0, 0.625, 1.25, 2.5, 5, 10, and 20 µM) of auranofin for 24 hours.

The effect of auranofin on the EMT morphology of extruded cells

This study investigates whether auranofin exerts a regulatory effect on cell extrusion. MDA-MB-231 cells were treated with varying concentrations (0, 2.5, and 5 µM) of auranofin for 24 hours. Following the collection and subsequent culture of the extruded cells, it was observed that auranofin diminished the occurrence of cell extrusion and reduced the relative EMT morphology of the cells (Figure 4).

The effects of different concentrations (0, 2.5, and 5 µM) of auranofin on cell extrusion and EMT morphology. (A) Cells treated with 0 µM auranofin were cultured for 24 hours after extrusion. (B) Cells treated with 2.5 µM auranofin were cultured for 24 hours after extrusion. (C) Cells treated with 5 µM auranofin were cultured for 24 hours after extrusion. These images were taken by an inverted microscope at 100× magnification. EMT, epithelial-mesenchymal transition.

The effect of auranofin on the expression of EMT-related proteins

To investigate the potential molecular mechanisms of EMT transition in extruded cells, MDA-MB-231 cells were treated with different concentrations (0, 2.5, and 5 µM) of auranofin for 24 hours followed by Western blot analysis. The results showed that auranofin increased the expression of E-cadherin and decreased the expression of vimentin in the cells (Figure 5). This suggests that auranofin can inhibit the EMT process in MDA-MB-231 cells.

The effects of different concentrations (0, 2.5, and 5 µM) of auranofin on the expression of EMT-related proteins. *, P<0.05 vs. control. EMT, epithelial-mesenchymal transition; GAPDH, glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase.

Auranofin inhibited the migratory activity of MDA-MB-231 cells

Since the EMT process is closely related to the invasive and migratory activity of tumors, this study also investigated whether auranofin could suppress the migratory activity of MDA-MB-231 cells. The results showed that exposure to varying concentrations (0, 2.5, and 5 µM) of auranofin led to a reduction in the migratory activity of MDA-MB-231 cells. Moreover, the inhibitory effect of auranofin with higher concentrations on cell migration increased (Figure 6A). Microscopic cell counting results also exhibited a similar trend (Figure 6B).

Auranofin inhibited the migratory activity of MDA-MB-231 cells. (A,B) The migration ability of the cells was assessed using a transwell assay, and the migrated cells were stained with 0.2% crystal violet and observed under a microscope at 100× magnification. *, P<0.05 vs. control.

To investigate the effect of auranofin on the scratch healing ability of MDA-MB-231 cells, scratched MDA-MB-231 cells were treated with different concentrations (0, 2.5, and 5 µM) of Auranofin. The study indicated that auranofin diminished the scratch healing ability of MDA-MB-231 cells, and this inhibitory effect intensified with increasing concentrations of auranofin (Figure 7).

The effects of different concentrations (0, 2.5, and 5 µM) of auranofin on the scratch healing of MDA-MB-231 cells (10× magnification).

Auranofin inhibited the expressions of MMP-9 and MMP-2 in MDA-MB-231 cells

To explore the molecular mechanism by which auranofin inhibits the migratory activity of MDA-MB-231 cells, this study detected the expressions of MMP-9 and MMP-2 in MDA-MB-231 cells treated with different concentrations (0, 2.5, and 5 µM) of auranofin using the Western blot assay. The results indicated that auranofin effectively inhibited the expression of MMP-9 and MMP-2 (Figure 8).

The effects of different concentrations (0, 2.5, and 5 µM) of auranofin on the expressions of MMP-9 and MMP-2 in MDA-MB-231 cells. *, P<0.05 vs. control. GAPDH, glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase.

Discussion

Breast cancer is the malignant tumor with the highest incidence and mortality rates among women, and it once surpassed lung cancer to become the cancer with the highest incidence globally (13). Among them, TNBC is known as the “king of breast cancer” due to its extremely poor prognosis. The main treatment for TNBC at present is surgical resection followed by combined radiotherapy and chemotherapy. However, due to its poor prognosis, TNBC is highly susceptible to distant metastasis. Currently, the primary cause of death from TNBC is tumor metastasis, including to the brain, lungs, liver, and other organs (14). The direction we need to strive for is to find efficient and low-toxicity methods to inhibit the metastasis of TNBC and treat it accordingly.

Cancer cell extrusion is an effective method for organisms to eliminate cancerous or precancerous cells and expel them from the body. This discovery has rekindled researchers’ interest in the process of cancer cell expulsion and has provided more possibilities for research. The direction of cell extrusion and the intensity of cellular activity also determine whether cells will develop into apoptosis or anoikis escape, but no in-depth research has been conducted. The relationship between cell expulsion and cancer is complex. On one hand, cell extrusion can facilitate the spread and metastasis of cancer, on the other hand, it can have detrimental effects on the host’s tissues. Simultaneously, determining whether tumor cells exhibit the phenomenon of extrusion is also a matter that requires exploration and research. A previous study has indicated that techniques such as the inverted dish method and silicone stretching models can be utilized to create models for cancer cell extrusion (15). Due to time and financial constraints, we opted for the most straightforward method, which involves establishing a cancer cell extrusion model through high-density cell inoculation to reduce the cell growth environment. Experiments have confirmed that this method can establish a cancer cell expulsion model. However, when collecting extruded cells, it is crucial to ensure that we are collecting extruded cells rather than dead cells.

EMT has been a focal point in cancer research in recent years (16). Studies have indicated that cell extrusion can facilitate the EMT transition of cells. Although there is currently limited direct evidence linking cell extrusion with EMT, cell extrusion can induce EMT in the context of resistance to anoikis (17). Cancer cell extrusion may be a mechanism by which the body attempts to eliminate cancer cells. However, cancer cells that evade this elimination process and survive may exhibit enhanced metastatic activity.

In recent years, the therapeutic effects of auranofin in cancer have garnered significant interest from researchers (18). As an inhibitor of TrxR, auranofin can inhibit both mammalian TrxR1 and TrxR2, and this inhibitory effect can promote an increase in cellular oxidative stress and suppress tumor growth (19). The Trx system is involved in the occurrence and development of various diseases (20), for example, a study has shown that auranofin can significantly inhibit the level of TrxR in ovarian cancer cells at low concentrations (21). The development of targeted drugs for signaling pathways related to the occurrence and development of breast cancer has become a new hot spot in breast cancer research. Antitumor drugs targeting TrxR have attracted the eager attention of researchers, which also opens up new ideas for the clinical treatment of metastatic breast cancer and is expected to develop into a new target for cancer treatment. Therefore, this study takes MDA-MB-231 cells as the research object to investigate the effects of auranofin on cell extrusion and migration and its possible mechanisms.

This paper employs a high-density cell inoculation technique to culture MDA-MB-231 cells, thereby establishing a breast cancer cell extrusion model. The CCK-8 assay was utilized to assess the survival rate of MDA-MB-231 cells exposed to auranofin, providing a rationale for selecting effective drug concentrations for subsequent experiments. Upon microscopic examination of cells treated with auranofin for 24 hours, the quantity of extruded cells and relative EMT alterations were noted, suggesting that auranofin diminishes the occurrence of cell extrusion and modulates the EMT process in cells. Considering that auranofin can reduce cell EMT, this paper also discusses whether auranofin can inhibit cell migration activity. Cell migration experiments indicate that auranofin indeed has the capability to suppress cell migration. Upon examining the expression of EMT and migration-related proteins, it was discovered that auranofin can modulate the expression of EMT-associated proteins, such as E-cadherin and vimentin, and can also suppress the expression of migration-related proteins MMP-9 and MMP-2. This interference with the EMT process consequently inhibits cell migration activity. In summary, by clarifying the relationship between breast cancer cell extrusion and EMT, and further investigating the impact and mechanism of targeted drugs on this correlation, a new therapeutic approach for the clinical treatment of breast cancer can be provided.

Currently, in addition to searching for new target drugs, many studies are focused on exploring the interactions between target drugs, their combined use with traditional antitumor drugs, and enhancing the antitumor activity of target drugs through regulatory mechanisms. This includes the study of auranofin in this research. Although this drug has been developed for decades, people have gradually discovered other effects besides its anti-rheumatic action, including its effect on cancer, which humanity has long struggled against. The process of cancer cell extrusion, which was discovered early but has been less studied, has not been abandoned by researchers. Instead, they continue to explore it, thereby proposing new theories and related evidence.

Conclusions

Auranofin can reverse the EMT induced by cell extrusion in breast cancer MDA-MB-231 cells and reduce the migratory activity of this cell. Its mechanism may be related to the regulation of the expression of EMT-related proteins E-cadherin and vimentin, and the inhibition of the expression of migration-related proteins MMP-9 and MMP-2.

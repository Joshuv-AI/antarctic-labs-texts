Nobiletin from Xiaoyu Xiezhuo Decoction: Restoring Mitochondrial Dynamics and Alleviating Renal Ischemia-Reperfusion Injury via M1 Macrophage Modulation
Authors: Yuan Yuan, Jing Huang, Yinchao Zhou, Zhibo Weng, Yiru Weng, Zhouzhou Dong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThere have been reports that the traditional Chinese medicine formula Xiaoyu Xiezhuo Decoction (XYXZD) protects against kidney injury. Its possible mechanisms in renal ischemia-reperfusion injury (IRI) remain unclear, though. Investigating XYXZD’s function and possible therapeutic effects on renal IRI is the goal of this investigation.MethodsLiquid chromatography-mass spectrometry and network pharmacology were applied to identify key target genes associated with XYXZD and renal IRI. Molecular docking was employed to forecast the binding affinity of bioactive compounds to these targets. The effects of nobiletin (NOB) on macrophage polarization, inflammatory cytokine production, mitochondrial function, and oxidative stress were evaluated. The impact of NOB on HK-2 mitochondrial dynamics through M1 macrophage polarization was evaluated via the cell counting kit-8 assay, enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay, Western blotting, and transmission electron microscopy.ResultsNetwork pharmacology analysis identified matrix metalloproteinase-9 (MMP9) and poly(ADP-ribose) polymerase 1 (PARP1) as key regulatory factors linking macrophage polarization and mitochondrial function. Molecular docking revealed a strong binding affinity between NOB and MMP9. NOB reduced M1 macrophage polarization, along with the downregulation of pro-inflammatory cytokines interleukin-1 beta and interleukin-6. In HK-2 cells, NOB mitigated mitochondrial dysfunction by modulating M1 macrophage polarization, reducing reactive oxygen species production, and restoring mitochondrial dynamics.ConclusionBy preventing M1 macrophage polarization, lowering inflammation, and reestablishing mitochondrial homeostasis in renal tubular epithelial cells, this study showed that NOB has renoprotective benefits. These results provided fresh perspectives on NOB’s potential as a treatment for renal IRI.

Introduction

Renal ischemia-reperfusion injury (IRI) is a pathological process characterized by structural and functional damage to renal tissues following the restoration of blood flow after ischemia [1]. This damage results from a combination of oxidative stress, inflammatory responses, and apoptosis occurring during the reperfusion phase [2]. Clinically, renal IRI is commonly observed in conditions such as kidney transplantation, cardiac surgery, traumatic shock, and severe infections, where its incidence is notably high [3]. Renal IRI significantly exacerbates acute kidney injury (AKI) and increases the risk of chronic kidney disease and end-stage renal disease [1]. The pathogenesis of renal IRI is complex and involves multiple contributing factors. During the ischemic phase, disturbances in energy metabolism, mitochondrial dysfunction, and calcium overload occur, while the reperfusion phase is marked by excessive reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation, inflammatory cell infiltration, endothelial dysfunction, increased capillary permeability, and apoptosis [4]. The overproduction of ROS during reperfusion exacerbates lipid peroxidation, protein damage, and DNA fragmentation, ultimately leading to renal tubular epithelial cell apoptosis and necrosis [5]. The main goals of current renal IRI treatment approaches are renal protection, which includes anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and microcirculation enhancement.

A traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) formula based on traditional TCM principles, Xiaoyu Xiezhuo Decoction (XYXZD) serves primarily to treat kidney damage and related pathological diseases. The formulation consists of prepared Rhei Radix Et Rhizoma, processed Pheretima, Persicae Semen, Cyathulae Radix, each at 10 g, Plantaginis Herba at 15 g, and prepared Hedysarum Multijugum Maxim at 20 g [6]. XYXZD exhibits pharmacological properties, such as promoting blood circulation, reducing edema, and tonifying kidney Qi. Recent studies have demonstrated the therapeutic potential of XYXZD in patients with renal injury, with animal experiments indicating its efficacy in mitigating pathological damage and improving renal function [7]. Further research has revealed that XYXZD exerts significant renoprotective effects in aged mice by inhibiting the hypoxia-inducible factor-1 signaling pathway [8]. Although existing studies have preliminarily validated the renoprotective effects of XYXZD, its specific mechanism in ameliorating renal IRI remains largely unexplored. In this study, we employed liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS) to analyze the chemical composition of XYXZD and combined bioinformatics analyses with in vitro functional experiments to investigate the function of its active ingredient, nobiletin (NOB). Our results suggest that NOB may regulate mitochondrial dynamics in renal tubular epithelial cells by modulating M1 macrophage polarization, thereby alleviating renal IRI. The fundamental mechanism of NOB in renal IRI is further experimentally validated in this work, which also offers fresh perspectives on possible treatment approaches for renal IRI management.

Materials and Methods

Animal Ethics

Three male C57BL/6 mice (weighing 20–25 g and aged 6–7 weeks) were acquired from Hangzhou Medical College in China. Every animal was kept in a lab setting with a 12-h light/dark cycle, at 22 ± 2°C and 45 ± 5% humidity. They have unlimited access to food and drink. The Zhejiang Baiyue Biotechnology Co., Ltd. Animal Ethics Committee (ZJBYLA-IACUC-20240202) gave its approval to all procedures.

Sample Preparation

The following herbs are part of the XYXZD Decoction prescription: prepared Rhei Radix Et Rhizoma, processed Pheretima, Persicae Semen, Cyathulae Radix, 10 g each; Plantaginis Herba 15 g; prepared Hedysarum Multijugum Maxim. 20 g. The hospital’s TCM pharmacy supplied the recipe. A number 5 sieve (Shaoxing Baojia Instrument Co., Ltd., China) was implemented to clean, sift, and powder the herbs. Three parts of the mixture (each weighing around 3 g) were weighed and put into conical flasks (Anhui Jingbo Laboratory Equipment Store, China) after the powdered herbs had been properly mixed. In total 30 mL of methanol (67-56-1, Nanjing Reagents, China) was added to each flask. Prior to and during ultrasonic extraction (500 Hz, room temperature, 30 min (min), the flasks were weighed. The mixture was filtered after the weight loss was offset. After collecting the filtrate, the solvent was evaporated at 50°C. After being redissolved in methanol and sonicated until completely dissolved, the combined extract was rinsed three times with a tiny quantity of methanol. Methanol was added to the mark after the finished solution was moved to a 50 mL volumetric flask (Jiangsu Xinpeng Science and Education Equipment Co., Ltd., China). A 0.22 μm microporous membrane (F513134, Shengong Biological, China) was used to filter the solution, which was then kept at −80°C for later use.

LC-MS Detection

Totally, 200 μL of the solution was combined with an equivalent amount (1:1, V/V) of cooled methanol/acetonitrile (75-05-8, Nanjing Reagents, China) after the sample had thawed. To precipitate the proteins, the liquid was extensively vortexed and then incubated for 2 h at −20°C. Centrifugation was employed to gather the supernatant, which was then vacuum-evaporated until it was dry. For analysis, 5 μL of the residue was put into the ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 column (1.8 µm, 2.1 mm × 100 mm, Waters, USA) after it was completely redissolved in 200 μL of 50% methanol. A flow rate of 0.5 mL/min was established. With mobile phase A being 0.1% formic acid in water and mobile phase B being 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile, a gradient elution procedure was adopted. The gradient program looked like this: 0–11 min, B from 5% to 90%; 11–12 min, 90% B; 12–12.5 min, B from 90% to 5%; 12.5–15 min, maintain 5% B for system equilibration. A SCIEX QTOF 6600 mass spectrometer (AB SCIEX, USA) equipped with TurboIonSpray ionization in both positive and negative ion modes were acquired using mass spectrometric data. These were the parameters: ion source gas 1: 55, ion source gas 2: 55, curtain gas (CUR): 35, source temperature: 550°C, ion spray voltage floating (ISVF): 5,500 V/−4,500 V (positive/negative modes). The product ion scan range was m/z 25–1,000 Da, and the SCIEX QTOF 6600 mass spectrometer scan range was m/z 50–1,500 Da. The scan accumulation time for the SCIEX QTOF 6600 mass spectrometer was 0.25 s/spectrum, whereas the scan accumulation time for the product ions was 0.035 s/spectrum. With the declustering potential set to ±60 V (positive/negative modes) and the collision energy set to 30 ± 15 eV, the data were gathered in the information dependent acquisition (IDA) mode with high sensitivity. IDA was configured to track up to 15 candidate ions every cycle and filter out isotopes less than 4 Da. The SCIEX OS software (SCIEX, USA) served to process the data.

Target Screening

The Python program PubChemPy was applied to query the PubChem compound database (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) and obtain chemical identification numbers based on the compound names. The information was filled in by hand when names did not match. Using PubChemPy, the compounds’ simplified molecular input line entry system sequences were extracted from the PubChem database. To assess the compounds’ characteristics of Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, Excretion, and Toxicity (ADMET), these simplified molecular input line entry system sequences were subsequently entered into the ADMET screening tool on the ADMETlab 3.0 website (https://admetlab3.scbdd.com/) [9]. Candidates with a Quantitative Estimation of Drug-likeness (QED) score of more than 0.5 and a bioavailability (F30) score greater than 0.5 were chosen, yielding 26 chemical components. The Biological Analysis Tool for Molecular Mechanism of Traditional Chinese Medicine (BATMAN-TCM) database (http://bionet.ncpsb.org.cn/batman-tcm/) [10] provided the target proteins for these drugs, resulting in 523 matching target genes and 19 components.

Drug Component-Target Network

After searching for genes linked to “renal ischemia-reperfusion injury” using GeneCards (http://www.genecards.org/), 549 genes connected to the condition were found with a score higher than 10. The Venn diagram tool in Bioinformatics & Evolutionary Genomics (http://bioinformatics.psb.ugent.be/webtools/Venn/) was deployed to examine the intersection of these renal IRI genes and the drug targets. To create a drug component-target network, Cytoscape 3.10.2 software (Cytoscape, USA) was adopted.

Protein-Protein Interaction Network Analysis

In order to create the protein-protein interaction (PPI) network, “Homo sapiens” and a score threshold of 0.7 were chosen after related target genes for many proteins were entered into the STRING database (https://cn.string-db.org/) [11]. The network diagram was imported into Cytoscape 3.10.2 after being downloaded in TSV format. The CytoHubba plugin was utilized to calculate network topology characteristics. Key genes were chosen from among those having Matthews correlation coefficient values of more than 1,000,000,000, which were determined by the topology of the PPI network.

Enrichment Analysis

A total of 72 hub targets were subjected to Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) and gene ontology (GO) enrichment analyses leveraging the ClusterProfiler package in the R (CA, USA). Using Cytoscape 3.10.2 software, a drug component-matrix metallopeptidase 9 (MMP9) and poly (ADP-Ribose) polymerase 1 (PARP1)-pathway network was built.

Molecular Docking

The PubChem database provided the 3D structures of the drug’s constituent parts. Open Babel 2.4.1 (CA, USA) was the program utilized to convert these structures to pdbqt format. After the optimized structures are converted into PDB format, they are imported into AutoDockTools for further processing. The protein structure of PARP1 (AlphaFold DB ID: AF-P09874-F1, chain A) were obtained from the AlphaFold Protein Structure Database (https://alphafold.ebi.ac.uk/) [12], and download the PDB format files for subsequent molecular docking. The structure predicted by AlphaFold does not include water molecules, and hydrogenation operations were carried out during the molecular docking process. AutoDock Vina (version 1.1.2) served to perform molecular docking. The grid box is set to default, with the center coordinate at (0, 0, 0), and the dimensions in the x, y, and z directions are all set to 126. The crystal structure of MMP9 (PDB ID: 6ESM; resolution: 1.10 Å; chain A) was obtained from the RCSB PDB database. Making use of PyMOL 2.4.0 (the PyMOL Molecular Graphics System, USA) to remove water molecules, and retaining the Zn2+ required for the structure based on the coordination environment of the metal ions. The molecular docking was carried out using the AutoDock4Zn mode of AMDock (version 1.5.2). The receptor (MMP9) and ligand were imported, the Zn2+ was set as the central site and a box (grid box) was created, the size of the box was adjusted to cover MMP9. The grid box coordinates (x = −1.8, y = 51.0, z = 19.3) and box size (x = 28, y = 28, z = 28) was set. After the docking is completed, use PyMOL to visualize the docking results.

Macrophage Isolation and Polarization

Sodium pentobarbital (80 mg/kg, P3761, Sigma-Aldrich, USA) was prescribed to induce deep anesthesia in mice, and they were killed by cervical dislocation. The bone marrow was extracted from the tibia and femur, and passed through a 40 μm cell filter (352340, Corning, USA), followed by rinsing with red blood cell lysis solution (C3702, Biotime, China). Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium/Nutrient Mixture F-12 (DMEM/F12) medium (11320033, Thermo Fisher, CA, USA) served to resuspend the cells. It was supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (C0235, Biotime, China), 20 ng/mL macrophage colony-stimulating factor (M-CSF; HY-P7085A, MCE, USA), 1% glutamine (HY-N0390, MCE, USA), 1% penicillin/streptomycin (CA005-010, GenDEPOT, USA). For 2 to 3 days, the cells were cultivated at 37°C in an incubator (D165H, Rwdls, China) with 5% CO2. Adherent cells were cleaned and then cultivated in new media. To produce M0 macrophages, adherent cells were cultivated for 48 h on day 7 in DMEM/F12 media supplemented with 10% FBS, 1% glutamine, and 1% penicillin/streptomycin [13]. M1 macrophages were produced by activating the cells with lipopolysaccharide (100 ng/mL, HY-D1056, MCE, USA) and interferon-γ (100 U/mL, HY-K2111, MCE, USA) for 48 h [13].

Cell Culture

Human proximal tubular epithelial cells (HK-2, CRL-2190, ATCC, USA) (short tandem repeat authenticated) were cultured in Roswell Park Memorial Institute-1640 (RPMI-1640) medium (CB014, Epizyme, China) supplemented with 10% FBS and 1% penicillin/streptomycin, and incubated at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO2.

Experimental Groups

Three groups participated in the experiment: M0, M1, and M1 + NOB. The M0 and M1 groups were made up of M0 and M1 macrophages, respectively, that had been cultivated normally. For 16 h, M1 macrophages in the M1 + NOB group were exposed to 100 mM NOB (HY-N0155, MCE, USA) [14].

Furthermore, three experimental groups were formed: control, control + M1, and control + M1 + NOB. HK-2 cells that had been grown for a full day made up the control group. The control + M1 group had a 24-h incubation period after HK-2 cells were planted in the lower chamber of a transwell (FTW064, Beyotime, China) and M1 macrophages were seeded in the upper chamber [14]. The control + M1 + NOB group had the identical configuration as the control + M1 group, except that the M1 macrophages received a 16-h treatment with 100 mM NOB. HK-2 cells were gathered for further examination after a day.

Phalloidin Staining

In 6-well plates (BS-CP-6C, Biosharp, China), macrophages were seeded on coverslips and handled in accordance with the specifications of the experiment. After being fixed for 10 min at room temperature with 4% paraformaldehyde (P0099, Beyotime, China), the cells were permeabilized for 5 min using 0.5% Triton X-100 (ST797, Beyotime, China). The cells were cleaned with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) (C0221A, Beyotime, China) and then treated for 30 min in the dark with 200 μL of fluorescein isothiocyanate-labeled phalloidin (MX4403, Maokangbio, China). Employing 2-(4-amino-phenyl)-6-indolecarbonyl diamidinium dichloride (C1002, Beyotime, China) for 5 min after PBS washing, the cells were examined by means of an inverted fluorescence microscope (×200, Nikon, Japan). The phalloidin was green, while the cell nuclei were blue.

Flow Cytometry

Fluorescein isothiocyanate anti-mouse cluster of differentiation 86 (CD86)+ (105005, BioLegend, USA) and phycoerythrin (PE) anti-mouse epidermal growth factor-like module containing mucin-like and discoidin domain containing receptor 1 (F4/80)+ (111603, BioLegend, USA) antibodies were adopted to analyze the expression of macrophage surface antigen. The isotype control was Rat IgG2a, κ (400501, BioLegend, USA). Following washing, the macrophages were treated for 30 min at 4°C with the antibodies. A flow cytometer (Agilent Technologies, USA) was implemented to evaluate the labeled cells, and FlowJo software (Ashland, USA) was performed to handle the data.

Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits were employed to determine the levels of the following compounds in the macrophage culture supernatants: interleukin-1 beta (IL-1β) (EK201B), tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) (EK282HS), and interleukin-6 (IL-6) (EK206). The concentrations of IL-1β (EK101B), TNF-α (EK182), and IL-6 (EK1153) in the culture supernatants of HK-2 cells were assessed with ELISA kits as well. The supplier of all ELISA kits was provided by Multi Sciences (Lianke) Biotech Co., Ltd. Utilizing a microplate reader (Bio-Tek, USA), absorbance was determined at 450 nm.

Cell Viability

Utilizing the cell counting kit-8 assay (C0038, Beyotime, China), the cell viability of HK-2 cells was evaluated. HK-2 cells were cultivated for 24, 48, and 72 h, respectively, after treatment as mentioned above. Following the addition of 10 µL of cell counting kit-8 reagent to each well, the cells were incubated for 2 h. A microplate reader (Bio-Rad, USA) was put to use to quantify absorbance at 450 nm.

ROS Measurement

The reactive oxygen species assay kit (50101ES01, YEASEN, Shanghai, China) was applied to evaluate the intracellular ROS levels in HK-2 cells. Following a PBS wash, cells were treated with a 10 µm 2,7-Dichlorodihydrofluorescein diacetate (DCFH-DA) solution for 30 min (37°C in the dark). Centrifuged after being cleaned with PBS. PBS was employed to resuspend the cell pellet. A fluorescent microscope (×100, Leica, Allendale, NJ, USA) was burned to observe the ROS levels.

Transmission Electron Microscopy

After HK-2 cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde, they underwent three PBS washes. A graded ethanol series (64-17-5, Nanjing Reagents, China) was then applied to dehydrate the cells. After being put on carbon-coated copper grids, the samples were stained for 10 min with uranyl acetate (6159-44-0, Acmec Biotech, China) and then treated for 10 min with lead citrate (512-26-5, China). Using a transmission electron microscope (×7,000, Hitachi, Japan), the morphology of the mitochondria was investigated.

Western Blot

The radioimmunoprecipitation assay (RIPA) buffer (P0013, Beyotime, China) was adopted to extract the total protein from HK-2 cells. To measure the amount of protein, the bicinchoninic acid assay kit (P0009, Beyotime, China) was deployed. Proteins were deposited onto polyvinylidene fluoride membranes (FFP24, Beyotime, China) after being separated via sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (P0690, Beyotime, Shanghai, China). After an hour of blocking with 5% non-fat dry milk, the polyvinylidene fluoride membranes were incubated with primary antibodies (whole night): dynamin-related protein 1 (Drp1) (1:1,000; 90 kDa, #8570, CST, USA), mitofusin 2 (Mfn2) (1:1,000; 92 kDa, #9482, CST, USA), and glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (1:1,000; 32 kDa, ab8245, Abcam, USA). Following that, the membranes were treated with the appropriate secondary antibodies (1:3,000, ab6721, ab205719, Abcam, USA). An improved chemiluminescence detection kit (KGC4902, Keygene, China) was utilized to find protein bands, and gel imaging equipment (Bio-Rad, USA) was performed to see the bands. For protein quantification analysis, ImageJ software (CA, USA) was employed. Expression levels were normalized to glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase.

Statistical Analysis

GraphPad 8.0 software (CA, USA) served as the tool for all statistical computations, and p < 0.05 was regarded as statistically significant. The mean ± standard deviation of the data was displayed. The comparisons among multiple groups were conducted using two-way or one-way ANOVA, followed by Tukey test for multiple comparisons.

Results

Identification of XYXZD Active Ingredients and Renal IRI Targets

We used LC-MS analysis to examine the bioactive ingredients in XYXZD. 59 compounds in the negative ion mode and 52 compounds in the positive ion mode were found (Fig. 1a). Eight duplicate compounds were eliminated, yielding 103 distinct compounds (Fig. 1a). A total of 549 genes linked to renal IRI and 19 components and their corresponding 523 target genes were examined in order to determine the possible targets of XYXZD in the treatment of renal IRI (Fig. 1b). In total, 199 possible therapeutic targets of XYXZD for renal IRI were found by overlapping the pharmacological targets with the renal IRI genes (Fig. 1b). Figure 1c depicted the component-target network.

Enrichment Analysis and Identification of Key Genes

The CytoHubba plugin was utilized to calculate the network topological properties of the 199 genes with the goal of identifying potential hub targets. The results indicated that MMP9, protein kinase B (AKT1), and signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 were hub targets in the PPI network (Fig. 2a). The 72 hub targets were subjected to KEGG and GO enrichment analysis for the purpose of examining their possible biological roles. According to GO analysis, the hub targets were much more abundant in the regulation of apoptotic signaling pathway, membrane raft, and DNA-binding transcription factor binding (Fig. 2b). Interestingly, two hub targets that were tightly linked to mitochondrial function and macrophage polarization were MMP9 and PARP1 (Fig. 2b). Significant enrichment in the TNF and interleukin-17 (IL-17) signaling pathways was identified by KEGG analysis (Fig. 2c).

Molecular Docking

The drug component-MMP9 and PARP1-pathway network were depicted in Figure 3a. Among the chemical components of XYXZD, 5 compounds were found to target MMP9, while 2 compounds were identified to target PARP1 (Fig. 3a). Molecular docking was utilized to examine how active ingredients bound to MMP9 and PARP1. Figure 3b displays the docking binding energy heatmap. MMP9 had binding energies of −5.8, −7.8, −7.7, −6.9, and −6.6 kcal/mol for interactions with 6,7-dimethoxycoumarin (PubChem CID: 8417), berberine (PubChem CID: 2353), emodin (PubChem CID: 3220), NOB (PubChem CID: 72344), and resveratrol (PubChem CID: 445154), respectively (Fig. 3b). With binding energies of −5.5 and −7.9 kcal/mol, PARP1 interacted with niacinamide (PubChem CID: 936) and resveratrol (Fig. 3c). Prior study has demonstrated that NOB reduces ischemia-reperfusion damage and has anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties [15]. Based on these findings, NOB was selected for further investigation. Figure 3d displays the outcome of the molecular docking between MMP9 and NOB, and the predicted binding energy of NOB to MMP9 was −6.9 kcal/mol. In addition, we used Marimastat (a MMP9 inhibitor, PubChem CID: 119031) as a control inhibitor to verify the docking. Under the same docking conditions, the predicted binding energy of Marimastat to MMP9 was −7.0 kcal/mol (Fig. 3e). The binding energy of NOB to MMP9 is similar to that of Marimastat to MMP9, which validates their strong affinity.

NOB Inhibited M1 Macrophage Polarization

The M0 group cells had an uneven, oval morphology, the M1 group cells were almost round, and the M1 + NOB group cells elongated from round to irregular fusiform shape, according to the results of phalloidin staining (Fig. 4a). In contrast to the M0 group, the M1 group had a much higher degree of F4/80+ CD86+ (p < 0.001, Fig. 4b, c). On the other hand, the F4/80+ CD86+ was considerably lower in the M1 + NOB group than in the M1 group (p < 0.001, Fig. 4b, c). According to ELISA results, the M1 group’s levels of IL-1β and IL-6 were noticeably greater than those of the M0 group (p < 0.01, Fig. 4d, f), while TNF-α showed no significant change (Fig. 4e). In comparison to the M1 group, the M1 + NOB group’s IL-1β and IL-6 levels were significantly reduced (p < 0.05, Fig. 4d, f), whereas TNF-α remained unchanged (Fig. 4e).

NOB Ameliorated Mitochondrial Dysfunction in HK-2 Cells Induced by M1 Macrophage Polarization

At 24 h, 48 h, and 72 h, the control + M1 group showed noticeably lower cell viability per the control group (p < 0.001, Fig. 5a). The control + M1 + NOB group, on the other hand, had substantially higher cell viability than the control + M1 group (p < 0.001, Fig. 5a). The control + M1 group’s IL-1β level was much greater than the control group’s (p < 0.05, Fig. 5b). In comparison to the control + M1 group, the control + M1 + NOB group had a considerably lower level of TNF-α (p < 0.05, Fig. 5c). ROS levels in HK-2 cells elevated when co-cultured with M1 macrophages, and this effect was partially mitigated by NOB therapy (p < 0.05, Fig. 5e, f). According to transmission electron microscopy data, the control group’s mitochondria were healthy and regular (Fig. 5g). The control + M1 group showed disordered and missing cristae structures, mitochondrial vacuoles, enlarged mitochondria, and mitochondrial condensation (Fig. 5g). In comparison to control + M1 group, the control + M1 + NOB group saw a partial alleviation of mitochondrial damage (Fig. 5g). Comparing the control + M1 group to the control group, Drp1 was higher and Mfn2 was lower (p < 0.001, Fig. 5h–j). The control + M1 + NOB group, on the other hand, had higher Mfn2 expression and lower Drp1 expression than the control + M1 group (p < 0.001, Fig. 5h–j).

Discussion

Macrophages play a key role in the balance between pro-inflammatory and anti-inflammatory responses during kidney injury and repair by altering their polarization phenotype (M1 and M2) [16]. In the early stages of renal IRI, M1 macrophages predominate, secreting pro-inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α and IL-1β, which exacerbate oxidative stress and tubular damage [17]. In the later stages, M2 macrophages increase and release anti-inflammatory cytokines, promoting tissue repair and regulating fibrosis [17]. Additionally, in the unilateral ureteral obstruction model, excessive activation of M1 macrophages can lead to renal interstitial fibrosis, while a moderate increase in M2 macrophages helps alleviate the progression of fibrosis [18, 19]. Mitochondrial function is also crucial in the processes of kidney injury and repair. Overproduction of ROS mediated by mitochondria is an important trigger of AKI, and in cisplatin-induced kidney injury models, mitochondrial damage leads to apoptosis and exacerbates tubular injury [20, 21]. Mitophagy helps eliminate damaged mitochondria, maintaining cellular homeostasis. In this study, through network pharmacology and bioinformatics analysis, we identified MMP9 and PARP1 as two key genes that are involved in both macrophage polarization and mitochondrial function. Isosteviol reduces acute post-burn inflammatory responses by promoting M2 polarization through the upregulation of MMP9 in macrophages [22]. MMP9 can also affect mitochondrial dysfunction, playing a significant role in coronavirus disease 2019 [23]. PARP1 plays a key role in DNA damage repair and cellular energy metabolism, and its excessive activation can lead to mitochondrial NAD+ depletion, triggering cell necrosis [24]. Moreover, PARP1 can activate M1 macrophages through the nuclear factor kappa-light-chain-enhancer of activated B cells (NF-κB) signaling pathway, exacerbating inflammation and accelerating prostatitis [25]. These findings suggest that MMP9 and PARP1 are crucial molecules connecting macrophage polarization with mitochondrial function, and their regulatory mechanisms play an important role in kidney injury and repair. Further investigation of their mechanisms may provide new therapeutic targets for intervening in renal IRI.

In this study, we identified NOB as an active ingredient in XYXZD. NOB has broad pharmacological activities, including anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties, and has protective effects in IRI [26]. Study has shown that NOB also has anti-ferroptosis, anti-inflammatory, and anti-fibrotic effects, which can effectively slow the progression of chronic kidney disease induced by unilateral ureteral obstruction [27]. Furthermore, NOB significantly alleviates renal injury in IRI models, suggesting its potential renal protective effect [15]. In non-alcoholic steatohepatitis models, NOB promotes the polarization of M2 macrophages and the expression of anti-inflammatory cytokines, inhibiting inflammation [14]. Additionally, in AML12 cells, NOB significantly reduces ROS levels induced by palmitate and improves mitochondrial dysfunction [28]. This study found that NOB inhibits M1 macrophage polarization, reduces ROS generation, alleviates mitochondrial damage, downregulates Drp1 expression, and upregulates Mfn2 expression. Drp1 is a key regulator of mitochondrial fission, and its abnormal activation leads to excessive mitochondrial fragmentation, loss of membrane potential, and increased apoptosis [29]. In contrast, Mfn2 mediates mitochondrial outer membrane fusion and plays an important role in maintaining mitochondrial structural and functional integrity [30]. Its downregulation is often associated with mitochondrial fragmentation and energy metabolism disorders [31]. In conclusion, NOB may effectively alleviate renal IRI by inhibiting excessive M1 macrophage polarization and restoring mitochondrial dynamics balance in M1-mediated tubular epithelial cells. This offers NOB fresh experimental and theoretical backing as a possible medicinal medication.

There are several restrictions to this study. The study primarily used in vitro cell models, which may not fully replicate the complex in vivo environment, and further in vivo studies are needed to validate the findings. Although this study identified key genes (MMP9) associated with the action of XYXZD and NOB, the role of MMP9 requires further analysis.

This study identified 103 bioactive compounds in XYXZD and 199 potential therapeutic targets related to renal IRI. Key genes such as signal transducer and activator of transcription 3, AKT1, and MMP9 play important roles in the treatment of renal IRI, and MMP9 is associated with both macrophage polarization and mitochondrial function. Molecular docking analysis revealed that NOB, a compound in XYXZD, effectively binds to MMP9. Additionally, NOB reduces M1 macrophage polarization and alleviates mitochondrial damage in HK-2 cells, providing evidence for its therapeutic potential in renal IRI. These results demonstrate how NOB may be employed as a unique treatment approach to treat renal IRI by focusing on mitochondrial dynamics and M1 macrophage development.

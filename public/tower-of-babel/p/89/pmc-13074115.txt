Fractional CO2 Laser Combined With Topical Corticosteroids for Vulvar Lichen Simplex Chronicus: An Observational Study From an 8‐Year Single‐Center Experience
Authors: Dongmei Wei, Jian Meng, Yanjun Zhu, Qiao Li, Yajing Wang, Yueyue Chen, Xiaoyu Niu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundVulvar lichen simplex chronicus (VLSC) is a chronic inflammatory dermatosis that remains difficult to manage long term. Fractional CO2 laser (FxCO2) combined with topical corticosteroids (TCs) has emerged as a potential therapeutic strategy; however, high‐quality long‐term evidence remains limited.ObjectiveTo evaluate the long‐term efficacy and safety of FxCO2 combined with TC in patients with VLSC.Study DesignThis retrospective single‐center study included 172 patients with biopsy‐confirmed VLSC treated between 2016 and 2024. All patients received at least one FxCO2 + TC treatment session. Primary outcomes included pruritus improvement, recurrence, and subjective symptom relief. Secondary outcomes assessed patient satisfaction, treatment adherence, and adverse events.ResultsFxCO2 combined with TC treatment was effective, achieving an initial pruritus improvement rate of 90.12% (155/172). Subjective symptom improvement was reported by 91.86% (158/172) of patients, and patient satisfaction was 88.95% (153/172). Among those who improved, 50.58% (80/155) achieved improvement after 3 treatments, and the median duration of improvement was 18.0 months (range, 12–54 months). The recurrence rate was 14.84% (23/155), with a median time to recurrence of 36.0 months (range, 12–60 months). Adverse events occurred in 15.70% (27/172) of patients, with dryness being the most common (6.40%, 11/172). No clinical signs of deterioration or atypical progression were noted throughout the follow‐up.ConclusionFxCO2 combined with TC treatment is effective and well tolerated in women with VLSC, providing long‐term symptom relief with low recurrence and acceptable safety. This combined approach may represent a promising therapeutic option for managing intense pruritus associated with VLSC.Keywords: fractional CO2 laser, retrospective study, topical corticosteroids, vulvar lichen simplex chronicus

Introduction

Vulvar lichen simplex chronicus (VLSC) is a chronic inflammatory dermatosis affecting the vulvar skin and mucosa, most commonly seen in women aged 30–50 years [1, 2]. It is characterized by persistent pruritus that leads to an itch–scratch cycle, resulting in lichenification, thickened and leathery skin, and areas of hypopigmentation [3]. This intractable itching not only causes significant physical discomfort but also disrupts sleep, contributes to anxiety and depression, and substantially impairs quality of life [4].

Topical corticosteroids (TCs) are currently recommended as first‐line treatment for VLSC, but their efficacy is limited by resistance, high recurrence rates, and adverse effects, such as skin atrophy and corticosteroid dermatitis with prolonged use [5, 6]. Laser devices have been utilized for a variety of vulvar skin conditions [7], including VLSC, with previous studies suggesting that they may help reduce hyperkeratosis, though not effectively suppress underlying inflammation [8]. Although laser therapies show promising clinical potential in the treatment of gynecological and urological disorders in women, prior reviews have highlighted the limited evidence supporting their efficacy and safety [9]. Potent TCs offer anti‐inflammatory and antifibrotic effects, helping to prevent structural loss and malignant transformation [6]. Given the limitations of monotherapy, combination approaches for VLSC are increasingly emphasized. Previous studies have reported that fractional laser‐assisted TC administration successfully treated refractory LSC with disease durations of up to 5 and 15 years, demonstrating rapid relief of pruritus and significant improvement of skin lesions [10], providing strong preliminary evidence for extending this approach to VLSC. Moreover, this regimen has shown efficacy in managing other dermatological conditions, such as keloids [11].

Currently, FxCO2 combined with TCs has attracted considerable attention for the treatment of VLSC. However, clinical experience with this approach remains limited, and most existing studies have focused on short‐term outcomes, lacking high‐quality evidence from large cohorts or long‐term follow‐up. Given the limited efficacy of monotherapy—either laser or TCs—for some refractory or extensive lesions, and to avoid the potential confounding effect of prior treatments on evaluating the true efficacy of combination therapy, this study included only patients who received the combined treatment as their initial therapy. Based on 8 years of clinical data from our center, this study is the first to evaluate the long‐term efficacy and safety of FxCO2 + TC treatment for VLSC, providing empirical evidence to support the clinical application of this potential therapeutic strategy.

Methods

This study is a retrospective analysis based on 8 years of clinical experience. The study design is illustrated in Figure 1.

Study flow diagram.

Patient information and treatment‐related data were obtained from the electronic medical record system. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants, and no identifiable patient information is included in this manuscript. This retrospective study was approved by the Ethics Committee of West China Second Hospital of Sichuan University (No. 2025097) on April 14, 2025.

Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

This study included patients with characteristic clinical manifestations such as pruritus, who were diagnosed with VLSC (n = 172) by histopathological biopsy at our hospital between November 2016 and February 2024. All patients received at least one session of FxCO2 + TC treatment.

Patients were excluded if their follow‐up period was less than 6 months, if they were lost to follow‐up, or if they had only received monotherapy with either laser or TC for VLSC.

Treatment Protocol

The laser treatment was performed by three experienced gynecologists.

Fractional CO2 laser treatment: The treatment was performed using the FemiLift CO2 laser multifunctional platform (Alma Lasers, Israel) in Esthetic mode, with the following parameters: device power, 60 W; spot size, approximately 1 cm², comprising 81 micro‐beams uniformly distributed in a 9 × 9 array; micro‐beam spot diameter, 75–100 μm; energy per micro‐beam, 40–60 mJ/pixel; ON time, 54–162 ms; 3–4 passes per session; and total cumulative energy, approximately 1000 J. Treatments were administered monthly, with 4‐week intervals, for a course of 3–6 sessions. 1 h prior to laser therapy, 5% compound lidocaine cream (typically 20 g) was applied to the vulvar lesion areas and covered with an occlusive dressing. TC therapy was initiated 2–3 days after each laser session.

Topical corticosteroid treatment: Betamethasone cream was applied once daily for 4 weeks, followed by every other day for 4 weeks, and then twice a week for 4 weeks. Each application consisted of one fingertip unit (1 FTU, approximately 0.4–0.5 g). Maintenance treatment (twice a week) could be continued based on the patient's condition.

Outcome Measures

To evaluate the long‐term effectiveness and safety of the FxCO2 + TC treatment for VLSC, the study assessed several key outcome measures. Primary outcomes included pruritus improvement (initial improvement rate, number of treatment sessions to achieve improvement, and duration of improvement), recurrence rate and time to recurrence, and subjective symptom improvement. Secondary outcomes encompassed patient satisfaction, treatment adherence, and adverse events.

The initial pruritus improvement rate was calculated as the number of patients achieving improvement in pruritus divided by the total number of enrolled patients. The recurrence rate was calculated as the number of patients experiencing recurrence divided by the number of patients who initially achieved improvement in pruritus. Pruritus was assessed using the visual analog scale (VAS) score after each treatment. During follow‐up, evaluations were conducted via telephone interviews by one gynecologist and one dermatologist. Improvement in pruritus symptoms (i.e., a positive response to initial treatment) was defined as a significant reduction in the post‐treatment VAS score compared to baseline, with a VAS ≤ 3 (VAS score: 0 = no pruritus, 1–3 = mild pruritus, 4–6 = moderate pruritus, 7–10 = severe pruritus), sustained for at least 1 month. Non‐response to initial treatment included complete non‐response (i.e., persistent moderate to severe pruritus with VAS > 3 post‐treatment) or partial response (i.e., improvement lasting less than 1 month). Recurrence of pruritus was defined as worsening to moderate or severe pruritus (VAS score increasing from ≤ 3 to > 3) after achieving improvement (i.e., no or mild pruritus sustained for at least 1 month). Non‐recurrence was defined as maintaining no or mild pruritus (VAS < 3) until the final follow‐up after achieving improvement. Poor adherence was defined as receiving fewer than 3 laser treatments and failing to follow corticosteroid treatment instructions or missing scheduled visits by more than 1 week.

At the final follow‐up, all patients were contacted by telephone to assess self‐reported outcomes using the Patient Global Impression of Improvement (PGI‐I) scale and to evaluate treatment satisfaction. Subjective symptoms, such as pruritus, vulvar pain, dysuria, and dyspareunia, were rated using the PGI‐I scale, which ranges from 0 to 3 (0 = no change, 1 = a little better, 2 = much better, 3 = very much better). Patients also provided their satisfaction ratings on a scale from 1 to 4 (1 = very dissatisfied, 2 = dissatisfied, 3 = satisfied, 4 = very satisfied). Any adverse events, including burning, dryness, scarring, pigmentation, erosion, edema, and blisters, observed by gynecologists or dermatologists or reported by patients, were recorded. The statistical methods used for analysis are provided in the supporting material.

Primary Outcome Measures

Improvement of Pruritus

Of the 172 patients, 155 responded to initial FxCO2 + TC treatment, achieving mild or no pruritus (VAS ≤ 3) post‐treatment and showing a significant reduction in VAS scores from baseline (p < 0.001), with the improvement sustained for at least 1 month (Figure 2A). Recurrence of Pruritus

Of 155 initial responders, 23 (14.84%) recurred. However, at the last follow‐up, their pruritus VAS scores remained significantly lower than pretreatment (p < 0.05), suggesting sustained benefit despite recurrence (Figure 2B). The median time to recurrence was 36.00 mo (range, 12–60 mo) (Figure 4B), with a median follow‐up of 65.00 mo (range, 9–89 mo) for this group.

Subjective Symptom Improvement

Following treatment, subjective symptoms improved in most patients: 80 (46.51%) reported improvement, 43 (25.00%) significant improvement, and 35 (20.35%) slight improvement, while only 14 (8.14%) noted no change (Figure 5A).

Analysis of Treatment Efficacy and Disease‐Related Factors

The study investigated the association between disease‐related factors (prior vaginitis, urinary incontinence, systemic autoimmune diseases, hematologic diseases, history of HPV infection, and menopausal status) and both the number of treatments required for initial pruritus improvement and subjective symptom improvement. As shown in Figures S1 and S2, urinary incontinence (SUI) was significantly associated with the number of treatment sessions required to achieve initial itch relief (p = 0.03), and a history of HPV infection was significantly associated with subjective symptom improvement (PGI‐I; p = 0.04). Other factors showed no significant associations. The median number of treatment sessions required for initial itch relief was significantly higher in patients with SUI (4 sessions) compared to those without SUI (Figure S1). Among 164 patients without HPV history, 152 (92.68%) reported at least slight improvement, while 13 (7.93%) reported no improvement. In contrast, among 8 patients with HPV history, 6 (75.00%) reported at least slight improvement, and 2 (25.00%) reported no improvement (Figure S2).

Secondary Outcome Measures

Patient Satisfaction

Of 172 patients, 128 (74.42%) were satisfied, 25 (14.53%) very satisfied; 18 (10.47%) were dissatisfied, and 1 (0.58%) very dissatisfied (Figure 5B).

Adherence

Of 172 patients, 113 (65.70%) had good adherence, while 59 (34.30%) had poor adherence. Patients with good adherence showed greater pruritus improvement at the last follow‐up (p = 0.015) (Figure 2C). Among the 155 patients with initial improvement, 108 (69.68%) had good adherence and 47 (30.32%) had poor adherence; adherence did not significantly affect the duration of pruritus improvement (p = 0.178) (Figure 2D).

Adverse Events

The overall incidence of adverse events during treatment was 15.70% (27/172), with dryness being the most common (11, 6.40%), followed by burning (7, 4.07%) and pigmentation (4, 2.33%) (Figure 6).

Long‐Term Management

Maintenance therapy after completion of the treatment course and post‐relapse treatment were analyzed. Among 155 patients with initial pruritus improvement, 132 patients did not experience relapse. Of these, 53 (40.15%) received no maintenance or preventive therapy, or only used basic moisturizers. Among the remaining patients, 50 (37.88%) continued TCs therapy (once or twice per week), 21 (15.91%) used other modalities (e.g., Traditional Chinese Medicine), and 8 (6.06%) underwent irregular laser monotherapy (approximately once every 6 months) to maintain efficacy (Figure 7A).

(A) Maintenance therapy in the non‐relapse group. (B) Post‐relapse treatment in the relapse group.

Of the 155 patients with initial pruritus improvement, 23 experienced relapse. Of these, 4 (17.39%) received no post‐relapse treatment. The remaining 19 underwent additional treatments: 10 (43.48%) resumed TC therapy (2–3 times per week), 7 (30.42%) underwent laser therapy (once monthly for 3 sessions), and 2 (8.70%) received other therapies (eg, TCM) (Figure 7B). As shown in Figure 2E, post‐relapse treatment with TCs or FxCO2 significantly alleviated pruritus compared with pretreatment (p < 0.001), indicating that these modalities remained effective in relapsed patients without evidence of resistance.

Discussion

Main Findings

FxCO2 combined with TC therapy effectively alleviated pruritus, with 90.12% (155/172) improvement rate; 50.58% of patients achieved relief after three treatment sessions, with a total recurrence rate of 13.37% (23/208). Pruritus in VLSC likely results from epidermal barrier damage, exposing nerve endings to irritants [4, 12]. FxCO2 enhances epidermal regeneration and stratum corneum repair, reducing irritation [13, 14], and may suppress Aδ and C fiber nociceptors via thermal or photobiomodulatory effects, alleviating hypersensitivity and breaking the itch‐scratch cycle [15, 16]. Unexpectedly, the median pruritus improvement duration in 132 non‐recurring patients was 18.0 months, compared to a median recurrence time of 36.0 months in 23 recurring patients, possibly due to the longer follow‐up period for the latter group (65.0 vs. 22.5 months).

In addition, Patients with good adherence showed greater pruritus improvement, likely because the 30‐day post‐laser period is critical for skin repair, collagen proliferation, and extracellular matrix enhancement. This aligns Levy et al.'s findings of epidermal recovery within 5 days and collagen remodeling by 30 days post‐CO2 laser [17]. Adherent patients, with 3‐ to 5‐week treatment intervals, optimized this repair window, improving collagen synthesis, barrier function, and pruritus relief.

Comparison With Other Studies

This study, which enrolled only histologically confirmed VLSC patients with marked pruritus, is the largest retrospective investigation to date of FxCO2 combined with TC therapy for VLSC. Our prior retrospective cohort study demonstrated that ultrapulse CO2 fractional laser monotherapy for vulvar epithelial non‐neoplastic lesions (including 40 VLSC cases) was superior to topical corticosteroid monotherapy in both subjective symptom improvement and clinical efficacy, providing preliminary evidence for the effectiveness of laser in combination regimens [18]. In addition, a recent randomized controlled trial comparing FxCO2 combined with TC, laser monotherapy, and TC monotherapy for multi‐site LSC also supported the advantage of combined therapy in symptom improvement [19]. Compared with these studies, the present study further confirmed the excellent efficacy of combined therapy for VLSC. More importantly, based on the technical attributes of laser therapy, disease characteristics, and lesion sites, our study is the first to systematically analyze treatment frequency, treatment intervals, adherence‐efficacy relationships, and long‐term management, offering practical insights for laser therapy in clinical settings.

Implications for Practice

Current evidence suggests that FxCO2 + TC treatment alleviates pruritus in patients with VLSC, with superior subjective symptom improvement in those without a history of HPV infection. The median number of treatment sessions required to achieve initial itch relief was significantly greater in patients with concomitant stress urinary incontinence compared to those without incontinence. Over half responded after three treatment sessions, with 3‐ to 5‐week intervals optimal, suggesting a three‐treatment course at these intervals. Further clinical data are needed to confirm these findings.

Significant diagnostic delays were observed, with 87.79% (151/172) of patients experiencing delays of over 1 year from symptom onset to histological diagnosis, and 40.70% (70/172) experiencing delays of ≥ 5 years. This suggests that improving early diagnosis and treatment requires enhanced patient education on vulvar self‐examination and the development of an AI‐assisted imaging‐based diagnostic system to reduce misdiagnosis. Additionally, raising physicians' awareness and knowledge of the disease and optimizing the diagnostic process through timely biopsies for suspected cases are crucial.

Limitations

Due to the retrospective design of this study, the timing of interventions could not be controlled, and potential biases arising from inter‐physician variability in treatment procedures cannot be excluded. Additionally, pruritus assessment relied primarily on subjective measures; future research should incorporate objective assessments, including vulvar physical findings, histopathological features, and relevant laboratory and immunologic markers.

Conclusions

In conclusion, the combination of FxCO2 and TC treatment effectively alleviated pruritus in patients with VLSC, with sustained symptom improvement and a low recurrence rate. The treatment showed good safety, tolerability, and high patient satisfaction, with no serious adverse events observed. No clinical signs of deterioration or atypical progression were noted throughout the follow‐up.

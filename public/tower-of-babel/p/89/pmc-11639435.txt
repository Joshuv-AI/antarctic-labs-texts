Educational hackathon to develop the infrastructure of an innovation ecosystem in the healthcare field: The event summary
Authors: Sanaz Monsef, Mohammad H. Yarmohammadian, Shaghayegh H. Javanmard, Elaheh Mazaheri, Mostafa Amini-Rarani
Published: 2024

The concept of an innovation ecosystem is designed to foster collaborative and joint value creation among its members.[1] This implies that within the innovation ecosystem, participants experience both cooperation and competition, and it undergoes a lifecycle driven by a co-evolutionary process,[2] which is also evident in the objectives of hackathons. The Isfahan Hackathon (ISFHACK) was organized to utilize the hackathon model as a means to enhance the healthcare system’s innovation infrastructure. This report aims to provide a concise overview of ISFHACK’s structure, content, and outcome (i.e. the innovative educational ideas that were explored during the event).

Structure of the Event

The ISFHACK was meticulously planned and executed in three main phases: the pre-hackathon phase, the hackathon event, and the post-hackathon phase, which took place at Isfahan University of Medical Sciences in 2019. During the pre-hackathon phase, the ISFHACK team comprised five undergraduates, a postgraduate, PhD students, faculty members, and healthcare advisors. Their responsibilities included securing academic and corporate sponsors, inviting opening speakers and reviewers, and organizing logistics such as venue, food, drinks, and team equipment. The team also worked on building the event website and preparing training videos for the participants.

The hackathon event spanned four days. Participants (hackers) registered on the first day, and mentors and volunteers were assigned their roles. Students then formed teams based on complementary and interdisciplinary skill sets. The first two days were dedicated to brainstorming strategies and potential solutions, guided by experienced mentors, including physicians, entrepreneurs, and engineers. International mentors from Johns Hopkins University, United States, University of Technology Sydney, Australia, etc., also participated through video conferencing, offering insights into challenges and trends in such events. The second day saw teams focusing on designing their business canvas while referees provided continuous evaluation and guidance. On the third day, teams continued to develop their business canvas with the help of mentors. The fourth day marked the finalization of the business canvas, and teams had the opportunity to present their ideas to the referees and other participants. Each team had ten minutes for their presentation, including five minutes for presentation and five minutes for answering the referees’ queries. An awards ceremony recognized the top three teams with cash prizes and grants. Post-hackathon, sponsors and financing corporations played a crucial role in supporting the winning teams to launch their products and startup ventures.

Content of the Event

The participants involved a total of 47 participants, with 23 of them being women (48.94%) and 24 men (51.06%). The hackers’ age ranged from 21 to 32 years, with seven participants below 21 (14.89%), 25 between 21 and 25 (53.19%), 11 between 26 and 30 (23.40%), and 4 between 31 and 35 (8.51%). The participants came from diverse fields, totaling 15 different areas. These fields were then categorized into clinical and nonclinical domains. Participants of the clinical domain included 18 hackers (39.12%) from fields including medicine, pharmacology, nursing, health, dentistry, and prosthesis. The nonclinical participants included 28 hackers (60.87%) from various fields such as engineering, management, health information technology, librarianship, and computer science.

Within the context of ISFHACK, the researchers utilized two questionnaires based on the Bybee approach.[3] These questionnaires aimed to explore the participants’ perspectives regarding the event structure and content. The questionnaires included a series of Likert scale questions, where respondents rated their attitudes on a scale from 0 to 5, with 0 indicating “very poor” and 5 representing “very good.”

With the aim of gauge the participants’ attitudes toward the content of the hackathon event, a questionnaire about the participants’ attitude toward content was distributed. It comprised 38 items, each rated on a 5-point Likert-type scale.[4] The questionnaire explored various aspects, including the participants’ perspectives on the event’s role in fostering innovation, the influence of mentor training, the diversity of collaboration, the significance of team activities, and the hackathon’s impact on the participants’ passions and personal abilities.

Outcome of the Event

The judges carefully evaluated the ideas presented by the 10 teams, resulting in the generation of 10 innovative educational concepts focused on addressing flood-related challenges. These ideas encompassed a range of solutions, including:

Improving restroom efficiency and effectiveness to accommodate individuals and families, offering temporary shelter during floods.Implementing an open distribution system for medicines and medical equipment to expedite access to essential supplies.Introducing a graded knee splint to accelerate patient stabilization and transfer to medical centers during flood emergencies.Developing new disaster management training methods that use applications and innovative approaches, including gamification, to teach crisis essentials.Designing a smart container for distributing, recording information, and providing medicines with minimal errors.Utilizing info-tags enhances information management and creates an integrated system for better-managing people and services during floods.Establishing a capital finder platform to support businesses affected by disasters like floods.Creating a tent design for efficient population management and service provision during flood situations.Implementing Medical System Software for online facility management and service recipient coordination.Offering health education using gamification techniques to provide interactive and engaging training experiences.[5]

These ideas demonstrate creative and practical approaches to tackle the educational aspects of flood-related challenges and find innovative solutions for potential implementation. Also, these ideas encompassed various areas of concern and solutions. The winning ideas from ISFHACK2019 were numbers 3, 6, and 10. Following the event, certain teams decided to pursue their initiatives further. To support and foster their ideas, these teams were connected with the University Growth and Innovation Center, where they could further develop and nurture their projects and ideas.

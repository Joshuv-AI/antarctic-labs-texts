Chicken intramuscular preadipocyte-derived exosome miR125b-5p promotes lipogenic differentiation of skeletal muscle satellite cells
Authors: Jinming Du, Lingzhe Kong, Jinyan Cui, Wenyue Jiang, Tianyi Ma, Qingzhu Yang, Xin Huang, Dianhui Liu, Weiyu Wang, Yingning Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Intramuscular fat (IMF) content critically determines chicken meat quality. Extracellular vesicles (EVs) are essential mediators of intercellular communication through bioactive molecule transfer. Although co-culture systems have demonstrated that intramuscular preadipocytes (IMPs) promote lipogenic differentiation of muscle satellite cells (MSCs), the involvement of EVs remained unclear. Here, we isolated IMP-derived exosomes and confirmed their ability to enhance lipid accumulation in MSCs. Through small RNA sequencing and functional validation, we identified miR-125b-5p as a key regulator that directly targets and suppresses NR5A2, a newly identified negative regulator of avian adipogenesis. Inhibition experiments confirmed the essential role of this axis, as miR-125b-5p inhibition attenuated exosomal pro-adipogenic effects. Collectively, we demonstrated that IMP-derived exosomes facilitate MSC lipogenic differentiation through miR-125b-5p delivery. Our findings provide the first evidence of exosome-mediated muscle-fat crosstalk in poultry, establishing the miR-125b-5p/NR5A2 axis as a potential target for IMF modulation.

Introduction

Chicken is one of the most widely consumed meat globally due to its mild taste, affordability, and moderate fat content (Pereira et al., 2013; Petracci et al., 2019; Prates 2025). Intramuscular fat (IMF), primarily located in the epimysium, perimysium, and endomysium, constitutes approximately 0.5%-1.0% of fresh muscle weight and significantly influences meat flavor, juiciness, and tenderness (Cao et al., 2023; Li et al., 2014). Both intramuscular preadipocytes (IMPs) and muscle satellite cells (MSCs) are derived from mesenchymal stem cells. Initially, MSCs were thought to differentiate exclusively into skeletal muscle cells (Armand et al., 1983); however, subsequent studies revealed their potential to differentiate into adipocytes (Qiu et al., 2022). In vitro co-culture systems of MSCs and IMPs in chicken, porcine and bovine have shown that IMPs inhibit myogenic differentiation while promoting lipogenic differentiation in MSCs, accompanied by increased expression of lipogenic markers such as C/EBPα and PPARγ (Choi et al., 2013; Cui et al., 2018; Xu et al., 2025). However, whether this process is mediated by exosomes remains unclear.

Exosomes, a type of extracellular vesicles, serve as natural carriers for nucleic acids and proteins, facilitating intercellular communication (Yue et al., 2020). Growing evidence suggests that exosomal biomolecules can regulate lipid metabolism and adipogenesis (Cruz et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2016). In mammals, exosome-mediated muscle-fat crosstalk has been well documented: adipose-derived exosomes internalized by skeletal muscle cells (Kranendonk et al., 2014) deliver regulatory miRNAs such as miR-27a to modulate muscle metabolism (Yu et al., 2018), while muscle-derived exosomes reciprocally suppress adipogenic differentiation of fibro-adipogenic progenitors (Ma et al., 2023) and regulate lipid-related metabolism (Qin et al., 2024), establishing a bidirectional regulatory mechanism. These findings collectively demonstrate the crucial role of exosomal signaling in inter-tissue communication. However, fat deposition in chickens differs from that in mammals, with the liver being the primary fat synthesis site rather than adipocytes (Hermier 1997). This evolutionary divergence underscores the need to investigate avian-specific mechanisms, whereas the role of exosomal signaling in intramuscular adipogenesis remains largely unexplored despite its potential implications for meat quality modulation.

In this study, we treated chicken MSCs with IMP-derived exosomes and evaluated their effect on the lipogenic differentiation. miRNA profiling of IMP-derived exosomes revealed high expression levels of miR-125b-5p. NR5A2, a nuclear receptor involved in lipid metabolism and homeostasis (Ryan et al., 2022; Sandhu et al., 2021), was identified as a potential target of miR-125b-5p. We hypothesized that miR-125b-5p delivered by IMP-derived exosomes modulates MSC lipogenic differentiation by targeting NR5A2. To test this hypothesis, we further investigated the role of NR5A2 in MSC lipogenic differentiation and confirmed the targeting relationship between miR-125b-5p and NR5A2. Beyond elucidating exosome-mediated regulation of IMF deposition, this study establishes the first functional characterization of chicken intramuscular adipocyte-derived exosomes, identifies miR-125b-5p/NR5A2 as molecular targets for modulating IMF content, and delineates a novel paracrine mechanism governing adipocyte-muscle cell crosstalk in poultry.

Materials and methods

Ethics statement

All procedures involving animal in this study were conducted in accordance with the guidelines and regulations for the ethical treatment of animals in research. The experiments were conducted in accordance with the guidelines and protocols approved by Qiqihar University to ensure compliance with animal ethics standards (QDLLSC-202303009). Efforts were made to minimize the number of animals used and to ensure their welfare throughout the study. Animals were housed in appropriate conditions with access to food and water ad libitum, and all procedures were carried out by trained personnel to reduce stress and discomfort.

Experimental animals

Fertilized eggs from Arbor Acres (AA) broiler chickens were obtained from Zhongyan Co., Ltd. (Shandong, China). The eggs were incubated at 37°C with 60-75% humidity for 21 days. After hatching, the chicks were raised in a controlled environment at 33°C with ad libitum access to feed and water for 10 days (Zhang et al., 2020). The feed was provided by Huachu Trading Co., Ltd. (Henan, China).

Isolation and culture of IMP and MSC

Chicken preadipocytes were harvested and cultured as previously described (Shang et al., 2014). A total of 52 ten-day-old Arbor Acres broilers were used in this study. To minimize confounding effects of cellular senescence associated with prolonged primary cell culture, each biological replicate was derived from an independent cell isolation procedure. A total of 13 independent isolations were performed, and all experiments were conducted with three biological replicates. In each isolation, muscle tissue samples (approximately 6 g per chicken) were collected from the pectoral region of 4 chickens, pooled, and processed together as a single independent sample. The anatomical sampling site and tissue sample size were kept consistent across all isolation batches. For each biological replicate assays, three technical replicates were performed for all quantitative assays, and the mean values of the technical replicates were used for subsequent statistical analysis. The birds were sacrificed by decapitation, after which the collected tissues were minced and digested with collagenase type II (Solarbio, Beijing, China) at 37°C for 60 min. Cells were centrifuged at 1500g for 10 min and resuspended in DMEM/F12 medium (Gibco, Carlsbad, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Cellmax, Beijing, China) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (Gibco). Separation of IMPs and MSCs was achieved by exploiting their differential adhesion properties. The cell suspension was plated, and IMPs (adherent preadipocytes) attached to the culture flask within 2 h. MSCs (muscle satellite cells) were subsequently enriched from the non-adherent and loosely attached cell fraction in the supernatant, which was transferred to new culture vessels (Supporting Information Fig. 1, 2).

Preparation of exosomes

Exosomes were isolated using ultracentrifugation as previously described (Bi et al., 2022; Cai et al., 2012). Briefly, IMPs were grown to 80% confluency before switching to serum-free medium. Following a 48-hour incubation, the conditioned medium was harvested and subjected to sequential centrifugation: first at 2,000g for 10 min to remove cellular debris, then at 10,000g for 30 min to eliminate larger vesicles. The resulting supernatant was ultracentrifuged at 100,000g for 70 min to pellet the exosomes, which were subsequently resuspended in PBS. For characterization, exosome morphology was examined using transmission electron microscopy (TEM) (Hitachi HT-7800, Hitachi High-Tech, Tokyo, Japan), while particle size distribution and purity were assessed via nanoparticle tracking analysis (NTA) (Flow NanoAnalyzer, NanoFCM Co., Ltd., Xiamen, China). Additionally, the presence of exosome-specific markers CD9 was confirmed by Western blot analysis (Supporting Information Fig. 4).

Small RNA sequencing analysis of exosomes (exo-miRNA-seq)

Exosomes were isolated from IMP cell-conditioned medium by differential ultracentrifugation, as detailed in the Methods section (see “Preparation of Exosomes”). Total RNAs, including small RNAs, were extracted from purified exosome pellets using Trizol Reagent (Takara, Dalian, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Small RNA sequencing was performed using a DNBSEQ system from MGI Tech Co., Ltd. (Shenzhen, China). Briefly, 35 ng of exosome RNA was converted to cDNA after ligation with RNA 3′ and 5′ adapters and amplified with DNB primers. Following raw data acquisition, high-quality clean reads were obtained by filtering out adapter sequences, low-quality reads, and potential contaminants. The resulting sRNA reads were then aligned to the Gallus gallus reference genome (GCF_000002315.6_GRCg6a) using BWA (v0.7.12). Subsequently, the mapped sRNA sequences were annotated by comparing them against known small RNAs, with Rfam 12.1 (rfam.xfam.org) serving as the reference database.

Transfection of miR-125b-5p mimic and inhibitor

MSCs were transfected with miR-125b-5p mimics or inhibitors using Lipo8000 reagent (Beyotime, Shanghai, China). Mimics, inhibitors, and their respective negative controls (mNC and iNC) were synthesized by Genepharma (Shanghai, China) (Supporting Information Table 1). At 24 h after transfection, MSCs were induced to differentiate using oleic acid (200 μM), and the medium was replaced at each 24 h.

Total RNA extraction and qRT-PCR

Total RNA was extracted from transfected and treated MSCs using Trizol reagent (Takara). For miRNA analysis, reverse transcription was performed with stem-loop primers specific for gga-miR-125b-5p and U6 (Supporting Information Table 2). For mRNA analysis, cDNA was synthesized using HiScript® II Q Select RT SuperMix (+gDNA wiper) (Vazyme, Nanjing, China), and the primer sequences listed in Supporting Information Table 3. qRT-PCR was performed using the ChamQTM SYBR® qPCR Master Mix (Vazyme). miRNA and mRNA levels were quantified with U6 and NONO as internal controls, respectively. Relative expression was calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method (Livak et al., 2001).

Oil Red O staining

Cells were fixed in 10% formaldehyde and stained with 5% Oil Red O solution for 30 min. The stained lipid droplets were eluted with 60% isopropanol, and the lipid accumulation was quantified by measuring the absorbance at 510 nm. The absorbance values were normalized to the total protein content of the cells, as determined using a BCA protein assay kit (ThermoFisher, Massachusetts, USA).

Vector construction

To investigate the role of NR5A2 in MSC lipogenic differentiation, we constructed an shRNA interference vector targeting NR5A2 (GenBank accession No. NM_001389416.2) using the pSilencer 1.0-U6 vector (MiaoLing, Wuhan, China). The shRNA sequence was designed using the Invitrogen Block-iT™ RNAi Designer and verified by sequencing. Additionally, the 3′UTR region of NR5A2 containing the predicted miR-125b-5p binding site was cloned into the psiCHECK-2 vector (Promega, Meadison, WI, USA) to generate wild-type (NR5A2-3′UTR-WT) and mutant (NR5A2-3′UTR-MUT) reporter vectors. Primers are list in Supporting Information Table 4, 5.

Luciferase reporter assay

To validate the direct interaction between miR-125b-5p and NR5A2, MSCs were co-transfected with either wild-type or mutant NR5A2 3′-UTR luciferase reporter constructs along with miR-125b-5p mimics. After 48 hours of transfection, dual-luciferase activity was assessed using a commercial reporter assay system (TransGen Biotech, Beijing, China). Firefly luciferase activity was normalized to Renilla luciferase activity. All transfections and subsequent analyses were conducted with three biological replicates to ensure statistical reliability.

Data analysis

All in vitro experiments included three independent biological replicates. Data analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism 5.0 software, with results presented as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). Prior to statistical testing, data distribution normality was verified using the Shapiro-Wilk test. For non-normally distributed data, the Minitab 17 Box-Cox transformation was applied to achieve normality. For comparative analyses, we employed Student's t-test for two-group comparisons and one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) for multi-group comparisons. Statistical significance was defined as P < 0.05 for all tests.

Results

Chicken IMP-derived exosomes promote lipogenic differentiation of MSCs

Chicken IMP cells and MSCs were first isolated and cultured, followed by the isolation of exosomes from the IMP cells (Fig. 1 A). We characterized the isolated particles using TEM and NTA. The TEM analysis revealed their characteristic annular structures and distinct membrane features (Fig. 1 B). Particle size analysis by NTA confirmed that the vesicles ranged from 50 to 140 nm in diameter (Fig. 1 C), consistent with the size range of exosomes. High expression levels of CD9 were also detected (Supporting Information Fig. 4). To investigate the effect of IMP-derived exosomes on MSC lipogenic differentiation, MSCs were treated with IMP-derived exosomes for 24 h, followed by induction with oleic acid (200 μM) for 48 h (Supporting Information Fig. 3). Oil Red O staining showed a significant increase in lipid droplet accumulation in the exosome-treated group compared to the control group (P < 0.05; Fig. 1 D, E). Furthermore, qRT-PCR analysis revealed that the mRNA levels of lipogenic markers PPARγ, C/EBPα and FABP4 tended to increase in exosome-treated MSCs, while FAS was significantly upregulated (P < 0.05; Fig. 1 F). Screening of miRNA with high expression in IMP-derived exosomes

To identify exosomal miRNAs involved in MSC lipogenic differentiation, small RNA sequencing was performed on IMP-derived exosomes. The sequencing data revealed that the majority of the exosomal RNA content consisted of unannotated sequences and introns, with mature miRNAs accounting for a smaller proportion (Fig. 2 A; Supporting Information Table 6, 7). KEGG and GO enrichment analysis revealed that these miRNAs are involved in fatty acid biosynthesis, elongation, degradation, and other lipid metabolism-related processes (Fig. 2 B, C). Among the annotated miRNAs, gga-miR-125b-5p was selected for further investigation. miR-125b-5p promotes the lipogenic differentiation of chicken MSCs

The expression of miR-125b-5p was significantly higher in IMPs and immortalized chicken preadipocytes (ICP1) compared to MSCs (P < 0.05; Fig. 3 A). Treatment of MSCs with IMP-derived exosomes also significantly increased miR-125b-5p expression (P < 0.05; Fig. 3 B). To further explore the role of miR-125b-5p, MSCs were transfected with miR-125b-5p mimics (P < 0.01; Fig. 3 C) or inhibitors (P < 0.01; Fig. 3 G). Transfection with miR-125b-5p mimics significantly increased lipid droplet accumulation (P < 0.05; Fig. 3 D, E) and upregulated the expression of lipogenic markers, including FABP4 and FAS (P < 0.05; Fig. 3 F). Conversely, inhibition of miR-125b-5p reduced lipid droplet accumulation (P < 0.05; Fig. 3 H, I) and significantly downregulated the expression of lipogenic markers, such as, PPARγ and FABP4 (P < 0.05; Fig. 3 J). To further confirm the direct interaction between miR-125b-5p and the 3′UTR of NR5A2, we performed a dual-luciferase reporter assay. MSCs were co-transfected with miR-125b-5p mimics or NC, along with either the wild-type (NR5A2-3′UTR-WT) or mutant (NR5A2-3′UTR-MUT) reporter vectors. Knockdown NR5A2 enhances lipogenic differentiation of MSCs

To further investigate the role of NR5A2 in MSC lipogenic differentiation, we first examined its expression pattern during adipogenesis. RT-qPCR analysis revealed that NR5A2 mRNA levels were significantly reduced in MSCs undergoing lipogenic differentiation (P < 0.05; Fig. 5 A), suggesting its potential role as a negative regulator of adipogenesis. To functionally validate these findings, we constructed shRNA interference vectors targeting NR5A2 (Fig. 5 B). Transfection of MSCs with sh-NR5A2 significantly increased lipid droplet accumulation (P < 0.01; Fig.s 5 C, D) and upregulated the mRNA expression of key lipogenic markers. PPARγ was significantly upregulated (P < 0.05), whereas FABP4 and FAS exhibited a trend toward increased expression that did not reach statistical significance (Fig. 5 E–H). miR-125b-5p inhibitors reverse exosome-mediated MSC lipogenic differentiation

To determine whether miR-125b-5p mediates the effects of IMP-derived exosomes on MSC lipogenic differentiation, MSCs were treated with exosomes in the presence or absence of miR-125b-5p inhibitors. Oil Red O staining showed that exosome treatment significantly increased lipid droplet accumulation, while co-treatment with miR-125b-5p inhibitors attenuated this effect (P < 0.01; Fig. 6 A, B). Similarly, miR-125b-5p inhibition suppressed the exosome-induced upregulation of lipogenic markers, with significant effects observed for FAS (P < 0.05) but not for PPARγ or FABP4 (Fig. 6 D-F). Furthermore, exosome treatment significantly downregulated NR5A2 expression, suggesting that miR-125b-5p delivered by exosomes suppresses NR5A2 (P < 0.05; Fig. 6 C). Discussion

In this study, we demonstrate that exosomes derived from chicken IMPs significantly enhance lipid droplet accumulation in MSCs, promoting adipogenic differentiation. Furthermore, we identified exosomal miR-125b-5p as a critical regulator of MSC adipogenesis, which directly targets NR5A2, a nuclear receptor involved in lipid metabolism (Fig. 7). To our knowledge, this is the first successful isolation of exosomes from chicken preadipocytes or adipocytes, establishing a novel model for studying exosome-mediated regulation of IMF deposition in poultry. These findings provide new insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying IMF accumulation and offer potential strategies for improving meat quality in poultry through the targeted modulation of exosomal miRNAs.Fig. 7Mechanism of miR-125b-5p targeting NR5A2 in IMP-derived exosomes to promote lipogenic differentiation of MSCs.Fig 7 dummy alt text

Mechanism of miR-125b-5p targeting NR5A2 in IMP-derived exosomes to promote lipogenic differentiation of MSCs.

The composition and function of exosomes depend on their cell of origin and the methods used for their isolation (Yue et al., 2020; Zhao et al., 2024). We compared different exosome isolation methods and found that ultracentrifugation yielded high-quality exosomes with fewer cells and lower costs compared to commercial kits. We characterized IMP-derived exosomes by TEM and NTA (Fig. 1 B, C), confirmed the identity of IMP-derived exosomes by detecting the exosomal marker CD9 (Supporting Information Fig. 4). However, due to no available CD63-antibody in chicken, we were unable to detect CD63 expression.

A prerequisite for the functional delivery is the internalization of exosomes by recipient cells. Direct visualization of exosome uptake was not performed in the present study. Notably, this process has been well documented in analogous models, where fluorescently labeled exosomes from palmitate-treated mouse 3T3-L1 adipocytes are taken up by C2C12 skeletal muscle cells in a time-dependent manner (Yu et al., 2018). In the present study, IMP-derived exosomes promoted lipogenic differentiation of MSCs (Fig. 1), an effect partially reversed by miR-125b-5p inhibition (Fig. 6). These observations raise the possibility that exosomal cargo was functionally delivered to MSCs, consistent with exosome internalization. Nevertheless, future studies incorporating live-cell tracing methodologies (e.g., PKH26 or CD63-GFP labeling) are warranted to directly validate the uptake of IMP-derived exosomes by MSCs and to further elucidate the underlying dynamics of exosome-mediated intercellular communication.

Our findings demonstrate that IMP-derived exosomes promote lipogenic differentiation in MSCs, as evidenced by increased lipid droplet accumulation and upregulated expression of lipogenic markers such as FAS (Fig. 1 D-F). These align with the reported role of porcine intramuscular adipocyte exosomes in promoting lipid accumulation and C/EBPα expression in myoblasts (Sun et al., 2020), suggesting a conserved role of exosomes in regulating lipid metabolism across species. Currently, the limited yield of primary chicken MSCs and exosomes resulted in insufficient total protein for reliable detection of adipogenesis-related proteins. We will optimize primary cell culture and exosome isolation methods for subsequent protein-level verification. miRNAs are small non-coding RNAs that post-transcriptionally regulate lipid metabolism and adipogenesis (Fabbri et al., 2012; Lehmann et al., 2012). Among these, miR-125b is highly conserved across species and has been implicated in cell differentiation, proliferation, and apoptosis (Guan et al., 2011; Sun et al., 2013; Wang et al., 2013). In chickens, miR-125b-5p promotes preadipocyte differentiation by targeting ACSBG2 (Li et al., 2021), making it a key focus of this study. Our results showed that miR-125b-5p is highly expressed in IMPs and ICP1 cells compared to MSCs (Fig. 3 A). Overexpression of miR-125b-5p in MSCs increased lipid accumulation and upregulated lipogenic markers such as FABP4 and FAS (Fig. 3 D-F), confirming its role in adipogenesis. These findings align with studies showing that miR-125b-5p promotes differentiation in 3T3-L1 preadipocytes (Bartel 2009). However, miR-125b has been reported to inhibit lipogenesis in porcine and human adipocytes (Cheng et al., 2016; Rockstroh et al., 2016), suggesting that its function may vary across species and cell types. This discrepancy highlights the complexity of miRNA-mediated regulation in adipogenesis.

Beyond miR-125b-5p, our exosomal miRNA profiling revealed multiple candidate miRNAs that may regulate MSC adipogenic differentiation, including conserved (miR-143-3p) and novel miRNAs (miR1904-3p) (Fig. 2, Supporting Information Table 8). Notably, inhibition of miR-125b-5p Notably, inhibition of miR-125b-5p failed to significantly reverse the IMP-derived exosome-induced upregulation of PPARγ and FABP4 (Fig. 6 D, E), suggesting potential compensatory roles of other miRNAs in this regulatory network. Emerging evidence has demonstrated that multiple signaling pathways are critically involved in regulating various cellular processes(Guo et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2025; Shi et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024). MiR-143-3p is a well-established pro-adipogenic modulator that promotes adipogenesis via ERK5/MAPK-mediated PPARγ activation (Esau et al., 2004), while miR-181a-5p may facilitate differentiation by suppressing inhibitors of the TGF-β pathway (Ru et al., 2016). The abundant novel miR1904-3p warrants further study, whereas low-abundance miRNAs like miR-27b-3p (a known adipogenesis inhibitor) (Kaberdin et al., 2009), may fine-tune regulation. Future work should characterize the functions of these miRNAs in IMF deposition and meat quality, focusing on miRNA-mRNA validation and their adipogenic synergy.

We identified NR5A2 as a direct miR-125b-5p target. NR5A2 is a nuclear receptor regulating lipid metabolism and homeostasis (Ryan et al., 2022; Sandhu et al., 2021). miR-125b-5p overexpression reduced NR5A2 expression and its 3′UTR luciferase activity, while knockdown of NR5A2 enhanced lipogenic differentiation in MSCs (Fig. 4; 5 C-H), indicating miR-125b-5p promotes adipogenesis at least in part by inhibiting NR5A2. Previous studies showed NR5A2 knockout in mice increases OSBPL3 expression and SREBP-1 activation, causing obesity (Stein et al., 2017). Therefore, we assume that NR5A2 may inhibit MSC lipogenic differentiation by modulating SREBP-1 activity. NR5A2 also regulates glucose metabolism, as its overexpression in C2C12 cells increases Glut4 and glycolysis-related gene expression, enhancing glucose uptake (Bolado-Carrancio et al., 2014), suggesting additional metabolic influence on lipogenesis. Further studies should measure TG/TC levels after NR5A2 overexpression and co-transfect MSCs with miR-125b-5p mimics plus NR5A2 vector to determine if miR-125b-5p's pro-adipogenic effect mainly works through NR5A2 inhibition.

This study focused on the regulatory mechanism of miR-125b-5p on NR5A2. However, previous studies have shown that miR-125b-5p can be sequestered by long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) or circular RNAs (circRNAs), thereby reducing its inhibitory effect on downstream target genes. For example, lnc00961 has been reported to sponge miR-125b-5p, promoting the viability and migration of non-small cell lung cancer cells (NSCLC) (Sui et al., 2022). Similarly, circRNA0086996 regulates the growth and migration of osteosarcoma cells by sponging miR-125b-5p (Luo et al., 2020). These findings suggest that lncRNAs and circRNAs may play a critical role in modulating miR-125b-5p activity. Therefore, further research is needed to determine whether lncRNAs or circRNAs in IMP-derived exosomes regulate miR-125b-5p and its downstream targets, such as NR5A2. This will help to further elucidate the complete regulatory network of lncRNA/circRNA-miR-125b-5p-NR5A2 and its role in MSC lipogenic differentiation.

In summary, we demonstrated that IMP-derived exosomes promote adipogenic differentiation of MSCs through miR-125b-5p-mediated suppression of NR5A2. We report the first systematic characterization of adipocyte-derived exosomes in avian species, establishing a novel model for studying exosome-regulated IMF deposition in poultry. These findings provide important insights into improving meat quality. A practical approach would be developing feed additives containing specific miRNAs to enhance intramuscular fat deposition through routine feeding, thereby improving the tenderness and flavor of chicken meat.

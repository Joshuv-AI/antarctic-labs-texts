Exploring the role of miR-221/222 in gastrointestinal cancers: implications for innovative therapeutic strategies
Authors: Mohammadmatin Morshedi, Seyed Pouya Taghavi, Ghazaleh Pourali, Fatemeh Ravaei, Arash Raisi, Ali Arjmand, Sahar Ahmadi Asouri, Michael R. Hamblin, Neda Rahimian, Mahmood Khaksary Mahabady, Maryam Mahjoubin-Tehran, Hamed Mirzaei
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
MicroRNAs, also known as miRNAs, have a substantial part in regulating different essential cellular functions through post-transcriptional mechanisms. When they are not properly regulated, they can cause development and growth of numerous human illnesses, such as cancer. The MiR-221/222 family of genes is commonly disrupted in various forms of gastrointestinal (GI) cancer. Gastrointestinal cancers are the most commonly occurring cancerous growths across the world, with a significant number of cases and fatalities. Despite many improvements in the care of gastrointestinal cancer, such as the use of chemotherapy and immunotherapy protocols, a considerable proportion of individuals still exhibit a subpar reaction to treatment, leading to unsatisfactory rates of survival. IFinding new therapeutic targets is essential to improving treatment results for people with GI cancer. An increasing amount of recent research indicates that miR-221/222 is highly engaged in the growth of GI cancer. This article thoroughly examines the abnormal levels of miR-221/222 in diverse GI cancers like colon cancer, pancreatic cancer, gastric cancer, esophageal carcinoma, and hepatocellular carcinoma. Additionally, it summarises the latest knowledge regarding the functions and target genes of miR-221/222, and its mechanism of action in GI cancers. This information may pave the way for the advancement of innovative treatment strategies for these patients.

Introduction

Gastrointestinal (GI) cancers represent a major global health burden, accounting for approximately 26% of all cancer diagnoses and 35% of all cancer-related deaths worldwide [1–3]. For many of these malignancies, such as gastric, colorectal, and esophageal cancer, surgical resection remains the primary treatment. However, adjuvant and neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy (CRT) often prove ineffective against distant metastasis or in treatment-resistant patients, necessitating the development of innovative therapeutic approaches to improve outcomes [4, 5]. In recent decades, immunotherapy has emerged as a powerful alternative, yet its application in GI cancers is still an evolving field where a deeper understanding of the tumor microenvironment is needed [6–9].

At the molecular level, microRNAs (miRNAs) have been identified as key regulators of gene expression. These small, non-coding RNAs bind to the 3’ untranslated region (3’-UTR) of target messenger RNAs (mRNAs), leading to their degradation or the inhibition of translation [10]. It is estimated that miRNAs regulate over 30% of the human protein-coding genome, influencing nearly every essential cellular process [11, 12]. Consequently, the dysregulation of miRNA expression is a common feature in numerous human cancers, where they can modulate pathogenesis by affecting cell proliferation, invasion, and metastasis [13–17]. Notably, miRNAs can also function as intercellular signaling molecules. They can be packaged into small extracellular vesicles called exosomes, which are then secreted by a donor cell and absorbed by a recipient cell, thereby altering its function and phenotype [18, 19].

Among the many miRNAs implicated in cancer, the miR-221/222 cluster has attracted significant attention. Located on the X chromosome (Xp11.3), miR-221 and miR-222 are transcribed from a single primary transcript and share an identical seed sequence, suggesting they function coordinately [20–27]. While expressed at low levels in normal gastrointestinal tissues, the miR-221/222 cluster is frequently upregulated in various GI tumors and is considered to have oncogenic functions, impacting cell proliferation, metastatic potential, and survival [28–31]. The expression of this cluster is complex, regulated by transcription factors, signaling pathways like angiotensin II, and even long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) such as MIR222HGs, which arise from the same promoter region [32–34].

Given the growing body of evidence implicating this cluster in GI tumorigenesis, this review aims to provide a comprehensive overview of the role of miR-221/222. We will explore its dysregulation across major GI cancers, including colon cancer, pancreatic cancer, cholangiocarcinoma, gastric cancer, esophageal carcinoma, and hepatocellular carcinoma. Furthermore, this review summarizes the current understanding of its target genes, its mechanisms of action, and its emerging role in exosome-mediated signaling. By consolidating this knowledge, we aim to highlight the potential of miR-221/222 as a diagnostic biomarker and a compelling target for innovative therapeutic strategies for patients with GI cancer.

MicroRNA-221/222 and gastrointestinal cancers

The miR-221 and miR-222 genes are co-transcribed from a cluster on the X chromosome. A key molecular feature is their shared seed sequence, which enables them to regulate an overlapping set of target genes and function as a single oncogenic unit (Fig. 1). Both pre-miRNAs are processed into mature 5p and 3p strands, but data from miRBase confirms that the 3p arms (miR-221-3p and miR-222-3p) are by far the most abundant and are thus considered the functionally dominant molecules in cells (Table 1) [26, 35]. The consistent dysregulation of this miRNA cluster is a critical factor in the pathology of numerous GI malignancies, which will be explored in the following sections. In line with their co-transcription from a single cluster, the expression levels of miR-221-3p and miR-222-3p are strongly and positively correlated in tissues (Fig. 2). Critically, the basal expression of the miR-221/222 family is very low in healthy gastrointestinal tissues like the liver, pancreas, stomach, and esophagus (Fig. 3) [36–40]. This low baseline makes their frequent and significant upregulation in GI malignancies a key pathological event, the specifics of which will be explored in the following sections.

MicroRNA-221/222 and colon cancer

According to current data, about 10.2% of people will experience colorectal cancer at some point in their lives, with a mortality rate of 9.2%. This places it as the second most prevalent cancer in the American Cancer Society’s rankings [41–44]. The primary method of treating the majority of colon cancers is through the surgical removal of affected tissue. According to how serious the disease is, colon cancer is managed in various ways, typically through using a mixture of chemical medications [45]. It is quite common for combination chemotherapy to be effective in the first instance. Yet, about half of colon cancer patients experience recurrence due to treatment failure, and patients with advanced colon cancer have only 10% survival after five years [46, 47]. Inflammation and inflammation-associated colorectal tumors require the STAT3 signaling pathway for their progression [48–50]. In recent research, it has been found that STAT3, a downstream transcription factor of IL-6 signaling, is crucial to maintaining constitutive NF-κB activation. By acetylating RelA, STAT3 may enhance RelA nuclear accumulation [51]. In the end, the continuous stimulation of NF-κB signaling in humans having cancer may be attributable in part to activated STAT3. Tumor cells continuously produce cytokines, which leads to the continuous stimulation of growth factors, inflammatory cytokines, as well as signaling pathways including NF-κB and STAT3 [52, 53]. After studying the expression patterns of 101 colon cancer tumor samples, scientists discovered a link between stronger levels of miR 221 or miR 221 in both cancerous stromal tissue and liver metastases, and a decreased lifespan for patients with colorectal cancer [54]. The researchers assessed the peripheral blood samples of 80 individuals without any health conditions and 71 patients with CRC, and discovered a heightened mir-221 expression in group of patients [55].

There have been suggestions about potential utilization of miR-221 levels as a non-invasive molecular indicator for identifying and anticipating the progression of colorectal cancer. Studies showed that increased plasma levels of miR-221 were associated with a poor prognostic factor for patients’ overall survival with colorectal cancer [56–58]. In contrast, Different research found that compared to non-metastatic cells, metastatic colorectal cancer cells expressed less miR-221-3p. It was shown that there was an inverse correlation between the life periods of individuals who had lymph node metastases and the levels found in human tumor samples [59]. Furthermore, it was demonstrated that miR-221 exhibited the most significant degree of selective expression compared to all other miRNAs, specifically in the population of stem cells characterized by EpCAM+/CD44 + markers in individuals with colorectal cancer. It should be noted that miR-221 serves as more than just a valuable indicator of CRC - it has a substantial part in the in vivo tumor growth process, forming an essential part of the molecular mechanism responsible for this maintenance [60].

A new study has revealed that the presence of lncRNA GAS5 (growth arrest specific 5) causes a reduction in miR-221 levels, resulting in diminished growth, invasion, along with movement of CRC cells [61]. Additionally, the outcomes of a study via Siu et al. recommended that miR-221 can be involved in controlling how well radiation treats colorectal cancer (CRC) cells through targeting PTEN. This discovery raises the possibility of utilizing an anti-miR-221 antagomir as a treatment strategy for CRC, potentially increasing the expression of PTEN [62].

Overall, the research clearly illustrated that miR-221 serves as a beneficial predictive indicator for CRC, making it a valuable tool for detecting the disease at its early stages (Table 2).

MicroRNA-222 and pancreatic cancer

PC has always been considered among the most fatal cancer types in the US. Other affluent nations have also reported PC as a cause of more than 20% of their gastrointestinal cancer mortality [63]. In PC patients, the outlook is still very bleak, despite some recent advances in diagnosis and treatment [63]. Knowledge on the precise molecular mechanisms underlying pancreatic cancer is lacking [63]. Several studies indicated a connection between the amount of miR-221-3p present in plasma and the TNM stages and distant spread of cancer in people having pancreatic cancer [64]. MiR-221’s potential as a PC indication might make it a useful diagnostic tool [65]. A different research demonstrated a marked increase in the presence of miR-221 within both pancreatic cancer cell lines as well as tissues, notably greater than that observed in non-cancerous pancreatic tissues. Moreover, patients with pancreatic cancer exhibited significantly elevated levels of miR-221 within their plasma comparing to individuals having benign pancreatic tumors. The findings demonstrate that miR-221 can potentially be a biomarker for purposes of diagnosing PC, tracking the advancement of the disease, and predicting the outcomes for individuals with PC [66].

Like its counterparts p27 and p21, the anti-tumor protein p57 belongs to the Cip/Kip group and acts as a blocker of cyclin-dependent kinase (CDK) [67, 68]. At first, it was believed that the presence of p57 suppressed progression of tumor cells via cell cycle, as the number of these cells lacking p57 continued to rise [69, 70]. P57 can regulate various traits of cancer cells, such as alterations in gene expression patterns, through epigenetic means [71, 72]. The genes p27 and p57, which are also found in different forms of cancer, are among those targeted by miR-222 [73, 74]. The impact of miR-222 was investigated on a human PC cell line in research conducted by Zhao et al. [75]. The research involved manipulating Capan-2 cells by transfected them with different types of miR-222 mimics, suppressors, as well as negative controls to examine their influences upon a PC cell line. The researchers utilized the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8), EdU assay, and flow cytometry to determine extent of cell growth. In addition, the researchers thoroughly assessed the levels of gene expression for potential targets of miR-222 like p27, p57, and PTEN, by utilizing techniques such as Western blotting and reverse transcription polymerase chain reactions (RT-PCR). Based on their research, it was determined that miR-222 resulted in a substantial rise in Capan-2 cell viability, proliferation, as well as progression via S and G1 phases of cell cycle. In addition, it was discovered that the inhibition of p57 within Capan-2 cells partially counteracted suppressive effects of miR-222 inhibitors on proliferation. This was achieved through the simultaneous transfection of a siRNA targeting p57 and a miR-222 inhibitor. According to the study, Capan-2 cell growth is influenced by miR-222 through its particular regulation of p57 expression.

Scientists have conducted experiments on a novel treatment method for PC patients where they inhibited the activity of miR-222 [75]. The researchers discovered that PC samples showed elevated levels of miR-221-3p, indicating its potential usefulness as a prognostic indicator in predicting and monitoring PC development and outcomes. Through inhibiting SOCS3, MiR-221 caused a decline in proliferation of PC cell lines by influencing JAK/STAT3 signaling pathway [76]. In addition, miR-221-3p promoted cell proliferation and migration and suppressed cell death, along with heightened resistance to medication in PC cells. The results of the investigation show that miR-221-3p is necessary for suppressing expression of CDKN1B, KIT, BCL2L11, and RUNX2, which in turn advances PC [77]. By stimulating increased production of MMP-2 and MMP-9, increasing levels of miR-221/222 in PC may contribute to its oncogenic qualities by eventually enhancing the ability of the malignant cells to invade other cells [38].

Nevertheless, a particular research revealed that mir-221 had the ability to trigger the process of programmed cell death through promoting autophagy in prostate cancer cells. MiR-221 is capable of significantly triggering autophagy by inhibiting the production of HDAC6, in addition to promoting apoptosis in PC cells. A viable course of action in treating individuals with pancreatic cancer may involve increasing the overall manifestation of miR-221 [78].

For individuals receiving PC therapy, a potential biomarker for colon and rectal cancer (CRC) might be mir-221. Additionally, by targeting p27, p57, and PTEN, mir-222 has the possibility to be utilized as a therapeutic method for PC patients if its effects are inhibited.

MicroRNA-221/222 and cholangiocarcinoma

Extrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma is a cancerous tumor that originates in cells lining bile ducts, displaying characteristics of malignant growth and differentiation into epithelial tissue. It commonly penetrates in a sideways manner as intraductal infiltration and in a perpendicular manner as extraductal infiltration [79]. In comparison to other types of epithelial cancers, it was found that miR-221/222 has been decreased in tissues affected by intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma, indicating that these microRNAs may have a role in suppressing cancerous activity [80]. A new investigation discovered that miR-221 improves the capacity of cholangiocarcinoma cells to react to gemcitabine treatment. Through their investigation on HuH28 human cholangiocarcinoma cells, Okamoto and fellow researchers discovered that miR-221 could directly target a specific protein, known as phosphoinositide-3-kinase, regulatory subunit 1 (PIK3R1), causing inhibition of HuH28 cell growth and making them more susceptible to the effects of gemcitabine [81]. Based on our findings, we can confirm that miR-21, miR-31, miR-122, miR-221, and miR-222 have been considerably increased in individuals diagnosed with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) along with intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma [82]. By targeting PTEN, miR-221 enhances the epithelial-mesenchymal transition process and establishes the reinforcing mechanism with the β-catenin/c-Jun signaling pathway in extrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma. In their study, Li and colleagues demonstrated that miR-221 effectively inhibited function of the luciferase reporter gene that contained 3’-UTR of PTEN mRNA. This inhibition was found to be directly attributable to the specific binding sequence for miR-221. In their revelation, the researchers discovered that miR-221 specifically targeted 3’-UTR region of PTEN. Furthermore, they found that increased presence of miR-221 caused a reduction in PTEN protein amounts in EHCC cell lines [83].

MicroRNA-221/222 and gastric cancer

Gastric cancer (GC) is aleading cancer form in China and is in charge for most of cancer-related fatalities. GC is a hostile and extremely damaging illness that frequently exhibits imperviousness to programmed cell death, medicinal treatment, and radiotherapy [84–89]. The diagnosis of Chinese individuals with GC often occurs at a delayed stage, where lymphatic dissemination is already apparent [84]. Around 60% of individuals diagnosed with GC who have localized disease are able to survive for a period of 5 years, whereas mere 2% of those with GC who have metastatic disease are able to achieve the same survival rate [90]. The proposal was made that In addition to having promise as a target for gene therapy and detection, miR-221 might function as a new predictor of outcomes for people with GC [91].

Protein tyrosine phosphatases, which possess a catalytic region resembling PTEN gene core domain placed on 10q23.3, are a type of enzyme. Therefore, PTEN has a vital function in the regulation of 3’-phosphoinositol phosphatases and protein phosphatases. PTEN hinders the ability of Akt to operate by removing the phosphate groups from the molecule phosphatidylinositol-3,4,5-triphosphate (PIP3), that may be the signaling molecule and is created via enzyme phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K) [92, 93]. The absence of PTEN in specific cancerous growths results in excessive levels of Akt. This leads to the stimulation of cell reproduction, resistance to radiation, and prevention of programmed cell death [88, 94, 95]. Specific microRNAs, particularly miR-21 and miR-214, have a substantial part in the control of PTEN expression [96–100].

In their study, Chun-Zhi and colleagues examined how miR-222 and miR-221 impact GC cells by identifying the specific target genes of these two microRNAs [101]. The introduction of AS-miR-222/221 or pMSCV-miR-221/222 into SGC 7901 cells resulted in the inhibition or recovery of miR-222 and miR-221 expression. Various experiments such as apoptosis and viability assays, transwell assay, clonogenic assay, and cell cycle analysis, have been conducted to accurately assess functions of miR-222 and miR-221 in the body. The use of Western blotting and luciferase reporter experiments was utilized to discover and confirm potential target genes. SGC7901 cells exhibited enhanced aggression as a result of upregulated expression of miR-221 and miR-222; however, when such miRNAs have been blocked, PTEN was activated, reversing the phenotype. Additionally, the act of suppressing miR-221 along with miR-222 greatly enhanced the sensitivity of SGC7901 cells to radiation, leading to a decrease in their invasive and proliferative abilities. A study found that when PTEN cDNA without its 3’UTR was introduced to SGC7901 cells, the presence of miR-221 and miR-222 caused the cells to acquire a malignant behavior. There is also evidence that 3’UTR of PTEN as well as the seed sequences of miR-221 and miR-222 are identical. Studies have shown that these two miRNAs, potentially through direct influence on PTEN, can regulate the radiosensitivity, proliferation, and invasion of SGC7901 cells. A potential method for treating gastric cancer in humans would involve inhibiting miR-221 and miR-222 [101, 102].

In a separate investigation, an increase in the amount of circ_0003159 circular RNA was found to effectively prevent the growth of tumors in vivo. Furthermore, their findings demonstrated that circ_0003159 has the ability to suppress the growth of GC xenografts in a living organism and decrease cell proliferation in GC cell cultures. This was achieved by elevating the levels of LIFR (leukemia inhibitory factor receptor) through direct competition with miR-221-3p/miR-222-3p for binding [103].

A different research study found that When contrasted with the surrounding non-cancerous tissues, HAI-1, a protein that controls the proliferation of liver cells, has been expressed at lower levels in a number of cancers, including endometrial, colorectal, and cervical cancers. The excessive expression of miR-221 and miR-221 within MGC-803 cells has been discovered to directly suppress HAI-1, resulting in increased cell mobility and growth [104].

The findings of a separate research revealed the presence of mir-221 within cancer tissues collected from GC patients in Iran, which showed a higher expression level as compared to tissues from the same individuals that were removed before malignancy [105].

In a research conducted by Wenneng Liu and colleagues, the impact of H. pylori infection upon expression levels of different miRNAs was investigated. In the perspective of researchers, the presence of H. pylori resulted in a rise in the amounts of miR-222 and miR-221. The researchers demonstrated that these miRNAs have the potential to attach themselves to 3’UTR segment of RECK mRNA, which is responsible for producing reversion inducing cysteine rich protein with kazal motifs, ultimately leading to the reduction in the RECK expression. In contrast, Due to their dual regulation of miR-221, miR-222, and RECK, miRNAs can enhance the invasion and growth of GC (gastric cancer) cells [106].

As a result, it was indicated that miR-221 might be a novel prognostic indicator for people with GC. Additionally, it may be a viable target in gene treatment and diagnostic purposes. In addition, hindering the functioning of miR-221 and miR-222 presents a potential new method for addressing human GC through therapeutic means.

MicroRNA-221/222 and hepatocellular carcinoma

A considerable amount of initial liver cancer cases comprises of hepatocellular carcinomas (HCC), and on a worldwide level, these are accountable for 33% of all deaths related to cancer [107, 108]. When developing a treatment plan for people with HCC, it’s critical to consider both the tumor stage as well as the clinical condition of patient [109, 110]. Cirrhosis is a widely prevalent liver disease that greatly complicates the process of treatment, making it the most common chronic ailment affecting the liver [111, 112]. Liver transplantation is considered an effective treatment for early-stage HCC; however, it can only be utilized if a substantial amount of donors are present [113, 114]. The suppression of P27, an important inhibitor of cyclin-dependent kinases, is mediated by MiR-221/222 in cancer cells [115–121]. This research indicated that miR-222 had a high probability of targeting HCC cells. The function of the P27 gene includes regulating cell differentiation, drug resistance and movement, and inflammatory reactions, as well as serving as a suppressor of tumors [122]. The more the tumor stage progresses, the less likely survival is and the more rapid the growth becomes. Additionally, in cases of HCC, there is a decrease in p27 expression [123–125].

The study conducted by Yang and colleagues offered a more comprehensive comprehension of the impact of miR-222 as well as its targeted genes on proliferation of HCC cells [126]. The transfection of HepG2 cells with either a miR-222 inhibitor, miR-222 mimic, or negative controls hindered the activity of miR-222. Cell growth has been assessed through CCK-8 and EdU incorporation assays. A study was conducted utilizing flow cytometry to investigate the part miR-222 play in advancement of HepG2 cell cycle. The detection of miR-222 and its potential targets (p27 and p57) was achieved through qRT-PCR and/or Western blotting. Overexpression of miR-222 gene has been observed, leading to enhanced HepG2 cell proliferation in vitro, along with a rise in S phase population of cells. The elevated expression of miR-222 within HepG2 cells hindered synthesis of p27 and p57 through post-transcriptional mechanisms. The employment of P27 small interfering RNA (siRNA) or miR-222 mimics led to a substantial enhancement of HepG2 cell growth, indicating that miR-222 could directly impact p27 expression and control cell proliferation in HepG2 cells. However, when p27 siRNA and miR-222 mimics were co-transfected, HepG2 cell proliferation did not increase more as a result of it than in cells that have been merely transfected with p27 siRNA or miR-222 mimic. As per the findings of this research, there is evidence that p27 actively suppresses the overproduction of miR-222 within HepG2 cells [126].

Li et al. conducted a clinical research (NCT02928627) to measure levels of miR-221 and miR-222 in 46 HCC patients and 20 healthy normal controls. Upon examination of HCC serum samples, it has been discovered that miR-221 and miR-222 exhibited contrasting overexpression levels. Additionally, There was a noteworthy inverse relationship found between the size, stage, and cirrhosis of the tumor and miR-221 expression. Additionally, when comparing to the group that has lower miR-221 expression, the group with stronger expression of the protein had a considerably lower overall survival rate. Above all, a significant amount of miR-221 in the blood is linked to an unfavorable outlook for individuals with hepatocellular carcinoma [127].

Overall, it was demonstrated that miR-221/222 has a high potential to specifically affect hepatocellular carcinoma cells by inhibiting P27, leading to reduced viability and proliferation.

MicroRNA-221/222 and esophageal carcinoma

Out of all forms of cancer, During the past 20 years, the incidence of esophageal adenocarcinoma (EAC) has raised faster in Western regions of the world than other cancer types [128]. In the majority of situations involving esophageal adenocarcinoma (EAC), individuals experience Barrett’s esophagus (BE), a precancerous state marked via the existence of metaplastic columnar cells in lower esophagus, which replace the usual squamous cells. It is widely acknowledged that BE is a consequence of prolonged exposure of the esophagus to bile acids [129, 130]. This form of exposure may have a function in facilitating ashift from BE to EAC [131] In an experiment using rats with a condition of backflow of duodenal substances, it was observed that a meal high in fat caused a modification in the makeup of bile acids and sped up the development of both BE and EAC [132].

The transcription factor CDX2, which is a part of the homeodomain family, regulates the expression of many genes that are essential for activities including cell division, adhesion, specialization, and programmed cell death [133]. Several research studies provide evidence for CDX2’s function as a tumor suppressor, with its levels of expression being linked to the prognosis of adenocarcinoma [134, 135]. Barrett’s esophagus was linked to aberrant presence of CDX2 in the esophagus [136]. The progression of Barrett’s metaplasia from dysplasia to adenocarcinoma was additionally marked by variations in the levels of CDX2 expression within biopsies taken from esophagus of human individuals [137].

The resistance to chemotherapy presents a significant hindrance in effectively treating esophageal cancer. Some miRNAs play a substantial part in developing resistance to the chemotherapeutic drug 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) as well as the progression of EC malignancies. Mir-221 has a vital part in the regulation 5-FU resistance in EC by influencing Wnt/β-catenin-EMT pathways through directly targeting DKK2 (known as Dickkopf WNT Signaling Pathway Inhibitor 2). Conclusively, The results point to the possible usage of mir-221 as a therapeutic target and prediction marker for 5-FU-resistant EAC patients [36].

In the investigation carried out by Matsuzaki et al., it has been found that bile acids have an impact on both the levels and role of miR-221 and miR-222. These molecules attach to 3’-UTR of p27Kip1 mRNA, hindering its translation [138]. In order to compare the levels of p27Kip1 and CDX2 proteins in Barrett’s esophagus (BE) and esophageal adenocarcinoma (EAC) samples from 11 patients, both RT-PCR and immunohistochemistry techniques were utilized. A research inquiry was carried out to analyze the impact of bile acid exposure on the levels of miR-221 and miR-222, along with CDX2, in cells of esophageal adenocarcinoma. Their study’s objective was to find out how blocking miR-221 and miR-222 influences the development of human EAC tumors in NOD/SCID/IL-2Rγ(null) mice. Compared to samples of Barrett’s esophagus (BE), those of esophageal adenocarcinoma (EAC) showed a substantial rise in miR-221 and miR-222 expression, while exhibiting weaker levels of CDX2 and p27Kip1. The levels of MiR-221 and miR-222 significantly rose in cultured cells following exposure to bile acids. Additionally, there was an observed increase in the activity of nuclear bile acid receptor/farnesoid X receptor (FXR). The process of CDX2 degradation was accelerated after being exposed to bile acids. However, This process was lessened when proteasome inhibitors were cultivated in the cells. While p27Kip1 and CDX2 expression decreased when these miRNAs were upregulated, their levels increased when these miRNAs were inhibited. The administration of inhibitors for MiR-221 and miR-222 caused a substantial raise in p27Kip1 and CDX2 expression in endometrial adenocarcinoma (EAC) samples. Additionally, these inhibitors resulted in a substantial suppression of tumor growth in NOD/SCID/IL-2Rγ(null) mice transplant models. The miR-221 and miR-222 levels have been substantially elevated in human esophageal adenocarcinoma (EAC) tissues comparing to those in Barrett’s esophagus (BE) tissues. Further research revealed that the activation of FXR was triggered by the existence of bile acids witin esophageal cells. This caused a rise in both miR-221 and miR-222 levels, a decrease in p27Kip1 levels, and a raised probability of proteasomal degradation of CDX2. The investigation indicates that there could be favorable outcomes for BE and EAC patients if they undergo therapeutic intervention that targets the system using FXR antagonists or MIR inhibitors [138]. Exosomal microRNA-221/222 and gastrointestinal cancers

Exosomes serve a crucial role not only in facilitating intercellular communication, but also in inhibiting the dissemination of tumors. According to recent studies, the introduction of microRNA inhibitors can greatly decrease the invasive capabilities of cancer cells [144, 151]. Exosomes are small membrane vesicles with a diameter of approximately 30 to 100 nm that can contain various molecules such as proteins, mRNA, DNA, and microRNAs. In both typical and abnormal circumstances, exosomes are regularly released into the space outside cells [152, 153]. Through various research, it has been determined that the recognition of exosomes could be attributed to the existence of specific marker proteins, including but not limited to CD9, Tsg101, and CD63 [154–158]. Several types of stromal cells like macrophages, endothelial cells, monocytes, along with fibroblasts, were observed to interact with cancer cells through exosomes which have miRNA [159–163]. MIA3, also reffered to as TANGO1 (transport and Golgi organization gene 1), is the integral membrane protein located in endoplasmic reticulum (ER) [164]. According to reports, MIA3 inhibits the development of malignant melanoma tumors [165]. Collagens I, II, III, IV, VII, and IX are secreted as a result of the export of collagen VII and ApoB from the endoplasmic reticulum to the Golgi complex, which is promoted by the MIA3/TANGO1 protein [165–167]. This procedure may be correlated with a heart attack and plaque buildup in the arteries [168]. According to a recent study, miR-222 inhibits the expression of MIA3 in colorectal cancer (CRC), as well as an increase in miR-222 prevents cancer cells from migrating and invading [169]. Individuals diagnosed with pancreatic cancer had notably elevated levels of plasma exosomal miR-222, which demonstrated a considerable association with tumor size and TNM stage. The existence of plasma exosomal miR-222 was found to be a determining factor for survival in these individuals, independent of other factors. The suggestion was made that miR-222 may trigger the phosphorylation of p27 in order to relocate it using the miR-222/PPP2R2A/AKT pathway, ultimately leading to the proliferation of tumors [170].

Du et al. conducted a thorough comparison between the miRNA profiles of human colon cancer mesenchymal stem cells (hCC-MSCs) and the cells that gave rise to them using next-generation sequencing technology [171]. Colorectal cancer stem cells underwent a transformation to a fibroblast-like shape through multiple rounds of culturing. The researchers utilized the ExoQuick precipitation technique to gather exosomes for the purpose of performing transmission electron microscopy analyses. Real-time PCR and Western blotting have been utilized to assess presence of MIA3, with an Illumina HiSeq4000 analyzer being employed for the analysis. To comprehend the effects of miR-30a and miR-222 on colon cancer cells, a great deal of research has been done on them in both lab and animal settings. It is possible to identify and distinguish adipocytes and osteoclasts in hCC-MSCs just as it can be done with MSCs sourced from bone marrow. The miRNA profiles of both the parent hCC-MSCs along with the isolated exosomes were thoroughly evaluated by the researchers. Exosomes produced from hCC-MSCs had much higher amounts of miR-30a and miR-222. Moreover, experiments conducted both within and outside a living organism indicated that miR-30a and miR-222 have ability to attach themselves to MIA3, a shared secondary target, causing the stimulation of colon cancer cell growth, movement, and spread. This solidifies their role as enhancers of cancer development. Thus, the exosomes released by hCC-MSCs have the capability to transport miR-30a and miR-222, resulting in heightened metastasis and cellular growth in individuals with colon cancer. Targeting exosomal miR-30a and miR-222 concurrently might be useful to suppress MIA3 expression and encourage the growth, metastasis and migration of colon cancer cells [171].

Exosomes from individuals with colorectal cancer who had liver metastases indicated a similar raise in miR-221/222 expression. The process of increasing the activity of the liver hepatocyte growth factor (HGF) was found to be directly related to a low rate of survival, indicating a strong connection between the two. Additionally, there was an observed activation of the HGF in this scenario. The suggestion put forth was that exosomes from CRC contained miR-221/222, which could effectively suppress the expression of SPINT1, a protein known as serine peptidase inhibitor, Kunitz type 1. There has been a proposed idea that miR-221/222 has a significant part in developing of a premetastatic niche (PMN) that heightens the aggressiveness of CRC tumors [144]. A different examination assessed the significance of using serum-derived exosomal miRNAs in monitoring and potentially preventing the progression of CRC. This provided evidence that exosomal miRNAs are reliable biological indicators for the early identification of CRC in patients, without the need for invasive procedures. In conclusion, The release of miR-221-3p via CRC on SOCS3 resulted in the stimulation of endothelial cells and activation of the STAT3/VEGFR-2 pathway that promoted the formation of new blood vessels [148].

Overall, it was demonstrated that gastrointestinal cancers have a higher level of exosomal microRNA-221/222. In addition, it was suggested that these specific miRNAs could induce the phosphorylation of p27, leading to a repositioning of p27 through the miR-222/PPP2R2A/AKT pathway. This could consequently contribute to the advancement of tumor growth. Further investigation is essential to accuartely analyze the precise role of these compounds in the digestive system.

Future perspective of miR-221/222 inhibitors as therapeutic approaches

To optimize miR-221/222’s potential use in medical contexts, additional research is required to obtain a more thorough knowledge of its specific function. Furthermore, due to its influential part in the progression of gastrointestinal cancer, suppressing miR-221/222 can potentially be a substantial target for innovative treatment strategies for these types of cancers. The suppression of miR-221/222 could be achieved via various techniques like genetic knockouts, antisense oligonucleotides, miRNA sponges, and decoy methods. Anti-miRs are frequently chosen by researchers as a therapeutic method due to its uncomplicated design, preparation, and application, coupled with its cost-effectiveness. Small in size, conserved in sequence and stable in bodily fluids, miRNAs have become highly appealing as new therapeutics, surpassing other molecular treatments in numerous ways. Despite recent successes, there are still numerous challenges that must be addressed before miRNA-based therapeutics can fully realize their potential. These challenges include addressing issues with stability, clearance by the kidneys, off-target impacts, poor uptake by target cells, and immunogenicity of delivery methods. Significant efforts are needed to create better transportation methods for delivering oligonucleotides to targeted organs, tissues, and cells. Up until now, all types of oligonucleotides that target miRNA, such as those enclosed in liposomes, associated with nanoparticles, and those without any additional covering, have been observed to mainly concentrate in the liver, spleen and kidney. Currently, the only methods of administering oligonucleotides are through intravenous or subcutaneous routes. The creation of vehicles for oral delivery can be a crucial progression in moving such type of medication forward in clinical trials and towards regular usage in patients. Investigating the potential for combining miRNA-targeting therapies with other chemical or biological drugs in order to create a multimodal therapeutic strategy is crucial [174].

Furthermore, a crucial inquiry that still needs to be addressed is what underlying systems might cause medication resistance in miRNA-inhibiting oligonucleotide therapeutics. There are three potential methods that could be in consideration. One factor to consider is a modification in ADME (absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion) profiles of anti-miRs. One possible mechanism may involve the activation of specific pumps and metabolic enzymes that aid in the elimination of pharmaceutical substances from cells and promote their breakdown through enzymatic processes. Additionally, the body may produce neutralizing antibodies that assist in the clearance of drugs. The next approach involves increasing the levels of specific miRNAs or improving their production and processing in order to counter the impact of miRNA inhibition. The third strategy is to counteract the effects of miRNA blocking by upregulating the production of additional miRNAs that can control the same genes. Exploring and comprehending systems behind drug resistance has the potential to enhance and improve miRNA-targeting techniques, thus facilitating the creation of advanced therapies [175].

Discusion and conclusion

Research is actively being conducted in order to discover novel methods of treating cancer, specifically targeting digestive system cancers. Research has proven that miR-221/222 has the ability to regulate levels of various target genes like p27, p57, MAPK, PTEN, RND3, FOG2, HIPK2, RECK, and NF-κB genes. The work done via Felicetti et al. indicated that PLZF transcription factor binds directly to the regulatory region of miR-221 and miR-222, thereby acting as a suppressor of these microRNAs. Silencing of PLZF removes obstacles for miR-221 and miR-222, resulting in their ability to regulate the development of neoplastic cells by reducing the levels of p27Kip1/CDKN1B and the c-KIT receptor. This causes a raised proliferation along with prevention of differentiation in cancer cells. When PLZF is silenced, it significantly enhances the expression of both miRs, which in turn trigger a minimum of two cancer-promoting pathways by disrupting the regulation of p27 and c-KIT, ultimately contributing to the advancement of cancer. The primary means of c-KIT-related oncogenesis is typically through activating mutations, such as those found in gastrointestinal stromal tumors [176, 177]. Furthermore, in 3D culture, Oncogenic KRAS regulated the upregulation of miR-221 and miR-222, as shown by Tsunoda et al. It’s interesting to note that oncogenic KRAS changed PTEN levels and the expression of let-7b, miR-200c, miR-221, and miR-222 in a particular way in 3D circumstances. In particular, the overexpression of miR-200c and the miR-221/222 cluster which targets PTEN was observed in colorectal cancer samples from patients. According to their findings, In a 3D model of the colonic crypt, there seems to be a substantial relationship between PTEN signaling in luminal apoptosis, the oncogenic KRAS pathway, and the presence of miR-221 and miR-222. Moreover, the primary target gene of mir-221/222 with the highest score according to network analysis was found to be NF-κB (Fig. 4). By focusing on these specific genes, The imbalanced activity of miR-221/222 promotes cell proliferation, inhibits programmed cell death, and aids in the invasion and dissemination of tumor cells as summarized in Fig. 5. Furthermore, because of its crucial involvement in progression of gastrointestinal cancer, For the treatment of GI cancers, miR-221/222 has a significant promise as a target for novel therapeutic approaches. To fully grasp specific function of miR-221/222 and to make the most of it in medicinal applications, more research is required.

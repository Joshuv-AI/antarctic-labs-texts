Prognostic and predictive significance of neutrophil/lymphocyte ratio for trastuzumab treatment efficacy in HER2-positive breast cancer
Authors: Jing Cao, Jian Pang, Yan Li, Shouman Wang, Zhi Xiao, Nianhua Ding
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe baseline neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) has been linked to HER2-positive breast cancer (BC) prognosis treated with trastuzumab in previous reports. Here, the prognostic and predictive utility of baseline NLR in relation to trastuzumab efficacy was further assessed, with extended follow-up of up to 120 months. Clinical data from 843 patients with HER2-positive BC treated between 2013 and 2018 were analyzed. To reduce potential bias, inverse probability of treatment weighting was utilized for balancing baseline features. Patients were categorized into two groups: those who received trastuzumab and those who did not receive trastuzumab. Each group was subsequently stratified according to the median baseline NLR. Associations between NLR, clinicopathological features, and survival outcomes were investigated using univariate and multivariate analyses. Over the 50-month median follow-up (range, 6–120 months), patients receiving trastuzumab and having low baseline NLR demonstrated significantly higher disease-free survival (DFS) relative to those with a high baseline NLR (5-year DFS rate: 93.02% vs. 81.53%, P < 0.001). In comparison, baseline NLR did not influence DFS among patients who did not receive trastuzumab. Multivariate analysis confirmed that baseline NLR was indpendently predictive of DFS in trastuzumab-treated HER2-positive BC patients (high vs. low, HR = 3.61, 95% CI = 2.02–6.47, P < 0.001). Low baseline NLR was strongly linked to longer DFS in patients with HER2-positive BC undergoing trastuzumab therapy, reinforcing findings from earlier reports. Introduction

Approximately 15–25% of breast cancers (BC) overexpress the human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)1. Patients with HER2-positive BC generally experience poorer prognosis and higher recurrence rates compared with those in the HER2-negative subgroup2. As a result, effective management of HER2-positive disease has become a major focus in BC treatment. Trastuzumab, a monoclonal antibody against the HER2 receptor, not only inhibits the proliferation of HER2-positive tumor cells but also increases immune-mediated cytotoxicity at sites of HER2 overexpression3. Clinical studies have demonstrated that trastuzumab significantly improves survival outcomes in HER2-positive BC patients, particularly when combined with chemotherapy4.

However, the overall response to trastuzumab remains suboptimal, and acquired resistance commonly develops within one year5. For instance, in one study, only 15.6% (53/339) of patients with HER2-positive breast cancer maintained a long-term response to trastuzumab combined with docetaxel6. The finding underscores the need to develop novel strategies for predicting treatment outcomes and optimizing patient selection.

Emerging evidence highlights the role of the immune system in mediating the therapeutic effects of trastuzumab7,8. The neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), representing the absolute neutrophil count divided by the absolute lymphocyte count, provides a readily available indicator of the balance between systemic inflammation and immune surveillance. Elevated NLR typically reflects increased inflammation and impaired immune function9. Multiple studies have evaluated the prognostic significance of NLR across different stages and subtypes of BC. Higher NLR values have been correlated with advanced disease stage, lymph node involvement, larger tumor size, greater metastatic potential, and reduced survival10–12. In comparison, lower NLR has been associated with favorable outcomes, including improved prognosis in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), particularly in cases achieving pathological complete response following neoadjuvant chemotherapy13.

Our previous study revealed that a low baseline NLR was associated with superior disease-free survival (DFS) in HER2-positive BC cases, suggesting that NLR may serve as a useful marker for identifying individuals who may benefit from trastuzumab monotherapy14. However, the initial analysis was limited to a 5-year follow-up, leaving uncertainty regarding the long-term prognostic and predictive value of NLR. To address this gap, we extended the follow-up period to 10 years, enabling a comprehensive evaluation of the enduring significance of baseline NLR in predicting DFS. This extended analysis strengthens the evidence supporting NLR as a robust and clinically meaningful biomarker for guiding treatment decisions in individuals with HER2-positive BC undergoing trastuzumab treatment.

Materials and methods

Patient cohort

Clinical data from 843 female patients diagnosed with HER2-positive invasive BC between July 2013 and July 2018 were obtained from the Department of Breast Surgery, Xiangya Hospital, Central South University, China. The end of follow-up was June 2023. Of these, 588 patients received one year of trastuzumab treatment following surgery, while 255 did not receive trastuzumab. Clinicopathological information was obtained by reviewing medical records, including age, medical history, laboratory findings, hormone receptor (HR*) status, HER2 status, lymph node involvement, histological grade, and tumor size. Adjuvant or neoadjuvant chemotherapy, primarily regimens based on anthracycline and/or taxane, was given in accordance with clinical guidelines. All patients received surgery, including either breast-conserving surgery or mastectomy and biopsy of sentinel lymph nodes or axillary lymph node dissection. Postoperative treatment strategies were determined based on pathological findings and current guidelines. Cases with end-stage renal disease, hematological disorders, liver cirrhosis, acute or chronic inflammation, acute or chronic injury, multiple tumors, or inflammatory BC were excluded.

Pathological characteristics

Samples testing positive for either estrogen receptor (ER) or progesterone receptor (PR) were defined as HR* positive. HER2 positivity was determined by immunohistochemistry (IHC) with a score of 3 + or by fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH), defined as a HER2/CEP17 ratio greater than 2.2 in cases where the IHC score was 2+.

Laboratory data

The NLR was determined by division of the absolute neutrophil count by the absolute lymphocyte count. The baseline NLR was calculated from routine blood tests conducted one week prior to treatment. Blood samples were excluded if infection was suspected based on laboratory findings or symptoms.

Grouping

Patients were stratified according to whether they used trastuzumab and their baseline NLR. The trastuzumab-free group consisted of 255 patients, while the trastuzumab group comprised 588 patients. Within the trastuzumab group, cases were further allocated to low NLR (n = 294) and high NLR (n = 294) subgroups based on the median baseline NLR (1.830)15. Similarly, those in the trastuzumab-free group were assigned to low NLR (n = 127) and high NLR (n = 128) subgroups using the median baseline NLR (1.850). The overall population median NLR was 1.846, and reclassification using this value did not alter the subgroup structure in either treatment group.

Statistical analysis

Inverse Probability of Treatment Weighting (IPTW) was applied to reduce the effect of confounders on the comparison between high and low NLR groups. Propensity scores were estimated using logistic regression, and weights were assigned accordingly. Confounding factors included age, size and stage of the primary tumor, lymph node stage, histological grade, HR* status, Ki-67 index, and neoadjuvant therapy status. The Kaplan-Meier (KM) curve was used to analyze the prognostic differences between groups. DFS refers to the duration of survival for patients without recurrence, metastasis, or death following surgery. DFS events do not encompass deaths that are unrelated to the primary tumor. Factors influencing DFS were examined using Chi-square test and multivariate Cox regression. The data are presented as hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Statistical analyses and figure generation were conducted using R (version 4.3.1, RStudio), with P < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

Results

Clinicopathological characteristics of patients with HER2-positive BC

A total of 843 patients with HER2-positive breast cancer were included and stratified into trastuzumab-free and trastuzumab-treated groups. The median length of follow-up was 50 months (range, 6–120 months). Detailed treatment strategies were consistent with those reported previously14. Briefly, 689 patients were treated with epirubicin and cyclophosphamide before receiving a taxane-based regimen, while 379 underwent endocrine therapy, and 464 received radiotherapy. No significant overall differences were observed between treatment groups.

During follow-up, 90 disease events without local recurrence were documented in the entire cohort. Among these, 24 patients developed bone or contralateral supraclavicular lymph node metastases, 16 experienced brain metastases, 23 developed lung metastases, and 27 had multiple metastatic sites. In the trastuzumab-treated group, 62 disease events were recorded among 588 patients. Twenty patients developed bone or contralateral supraclavicular lymph node metastases, while brain, lung, and multiple metastases occurred in 14, 12, and 16 patients, respectively.

Baseline characteristics, including age, tumor stage, lymph node status, histological grade, HR* status, Ki-67 index, and receipt of neoadjuvant therapy, were compared across groups (Table 1). Compared with the low- and high-NLR subgroups of trastuzumab-treated patients, a greater proportion of patients aged > 45 years was observed in the trastuzumab-free group (74.9% vs. 57.5% vs. 59.9%, P < 0.001). Similarly, fewer patients in the trastuzumab-free group presented with stage 1 tumors (20.0% vs. 21.8% vs. 23.8%, P = 0.044). No significant differences were detected among groups with respect to lymph node status, histological grade, HR* status, Ki-67 index, or receipt of neoadjuvant chemotherapy.

Low baseline NLR indicates long DFS with trastuzumab treatment

Among trastuzumab-treated patients stratified by baseline NLR, those in the low-NLR group had a higher proportion of cases with elevated Ki-67 expression (54.4% vs. 44.9%, P = 0.021) and advanced tumor size staging (stage 3–4: 17.7% vs. 10.2%, P = 0.016). However, after applying IPTW, the groups did not differ significantly in terms of Ki-67 expression or tumor stage (all P > 0.05) (Table 2).

Survival analysis further demonstrated that, within the trastuzumab-treated cohort, patients with a low baseline NLR had markedly longer DFS relative to those with high baseline NLR values. The 3-year DFS rates were 95.14% versus 87.64%, while the 5-year values were 93.02% versus 81.53% (P < 0.001). This survival advantage persisted after IPTW adjustment (P < 0.001) (Fig. 1).

Multivariate analysis following IPTW identified lymph node stage, HR* status, and baseline NLR as independent predictors of DFS in trastuzumab-treated HER2-positive BC patients. Lymph node involvement significantly increased the risk of recurrence (pN1 vs. pN0: HR = 4.48, 95% CI = 1.93–10.44, P = 0.001; pN2–3 vs. pN0: HR = 12.05, 95% CI = 4.64–31.28, P < 0.001). HR* positivity was associated with a favorable prognosis (positive vs. negative: HR = 0.15, 95% CI = 0.08–0.29, P < 0.001). Baseline NLR was still an independently predictive factor, with high NLR markedly increasing the risk of recurrence (high vs. low: HR = 3.61, 95% CI = 2.02–6.47, P < 0.001) (Table 3).

Baseline NLR had no impact on the DFS of patients without trastuzumab

The present study next evaluated whether baseline NLR influenced prognosis in patients who did not receive trastuzumab-based therapy. Analysis of baseline clinicopathological characteristics indicated that no significant differences were observed between the high and low groups (Supplementary Table S1).

Survival analyses indicated that DFS did not differ significantly between the two groups. The 3-year DFS values were 91.47% versus 89.99% in the low- and high-NLR groups, respectively, while the 5-year values were 82.26% and 83.70%, respectively (P = 0.480). After IPTW adjustment, the lack of significance persisted between groups (P = 0.462) (Fig. 2). These findings are consistent with our previous results, and extended follow-up further confirmed that baseline NLR was not associated with DFS in patients who did not receive trastuzumab-based therapy. Multivariate analysis following IPTW identified lymph node stage and HR* status as independent predictors of DFS in the trastuzumab-free group, while baseline NLR was not predictive of DFS (P = 0.319) (Table 4).

Significance of baseline NLR in predicting potential beneficiaries of trastuzumab in HER2-positive BC patients

In line with our previous methodology, patients were divided into a trastuzumab-free group and a trastuzumab-treated group, with the latter further stratified into trastuzumab/low NLR and trastuzumab/high NLR subgroups. The median follow-up duration for DFS was 49 months (range, 6–95 months) in the trastuzumab-free group and 51 months (range, 6–120 months) in the trastuzumab-treated group.

As anticipated, patients with a low baseline NLR who received trastuzumab demonstrated superior DFS compared with both the trastuzumab-free group and the trastuzumab/high-NLR group. The 3-year DFS rates were 95.14% versus 90.82% and 87.64%, while the 5-year DFS rates were 93.02% versus 82.97% and 87.64%, respectively (P = 0.002). In comparison, among patients with a high baseline NLR, trastuzumab treatment did not significantly improve DFS, as no difference was observed between the trastuzumab/high-NLR group and the trastuzumab-free group (P > 0.05) (Fig. 3). Moreover, this finding was not highlighted in our previous study due to the shorter follow-up period at that time.

Discussion

This study investigated the prognostic and predictive significance of baseline NLR in determining potential beneficiaries of trastuzumab treatment. Our previous work demonstrated that low baseline NLR values were linked with longer DFS in cases of HER2-positive BC treated with trastuzumab, although with a relatively short follow-up. To strengthen these findings, the follow-up period was extended to 120 months. Encouragingly, the present data confirmed that low baseline NLR remains positively correlated with prolonged DFS in HER2-positive BC following trastuzumab treatment, reinforcing and extending our earlier conclusions.

Trastuzumab, a humanized monoclonal antibody targeting HER2, has profoundly improved outcomes for sufferers of HER2-positive BC across preoperative, postoperative, and advanced disease settings16. Its therapeutic effects include the inhibition of HER2 signalling pathways and the recruitment of natural killer cells and cytotoxic T cells, ultimately mediating antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity17–20. The biological relevance of NLR provides a plausible explanation for its predictive value. Neutrophils, although central to infection control and inflammation, can promote tumour growth and metastasis by releasing pro-inflammatory and pro-angiogenic mediators, generating a tumour-supportive microenvironment21. Lymphocytes play a crucial role in recognizing and eliminating tumour cells, particularly when activated by agents such as trastuzumab22. Therefore, a high NLR reflecting elevated neutrophil and reduced lymphocyte counts may indicate impaired antitumor immunity and a higher tumor burden, potentially diminishing the effectiveness of trastuzumab. In comparison, a low NLR may indicate a more robust immune response and a lower tumour burden, improving the efficacy of trastuzumab. Although this mechanism is biologically plausible, further experimental validation is required.

By integrating findings from both our earlier and current analyses, the results demonstrated that low baseline NLR predicts longer DFS in patients treated with trastuzumab. In comparison, baseline NLR had no prognostic effect in patients not receiving trastuzumab. Patients with high baseline NLR did not derive additional DFS benefit from trastuzumab compared with those who were trastuzumab-free. In summary, trastuzumab appears to be more beneficial for individuals with HER2- positive BC and low baseline NLR, whereas its efficacy in high-NLR patients may be limited. Further research is needed to elucidate the mechanisms underlying this differential response.

Our results are consistent with previous reports linking high NLR to poor Overall Survival and DFS in both TNBC and HER2-positive BC patients11,23. However, conflicting findings exist. For example, Hong et al. observed that HER2-positive BC patients with a low baseline NLR had inferior 3-year DFS relative to those with high NLR24. Similarly, Kazuhiro et al. reported that NLR has no prognostic value in patients receiving pertuzumab and trastuzumab combined with nab-paclitaxel or eribulin. These inconsistencies may reflect variations in study design, follow-up durations, and sample sizes25. Compared with those studies, our work offers key advantages, including an extended follow-up of up to 10 years and the inclusion of a trastuzumab-free reference group. Patients with high baseline NLR consistently showed poor prognosis despite trastuzumab therapy, and this association remained stable over time. This aspect underscores the novelty of our study.

From a clinical perspective, identifying high-risk patients based on baseline NLR at the time of diagnosis may help guide treatment planning. Closer follow-up or intensified therapeutic approaches may improve outcomes in this subgroup. However, NLR should not be considered in isolation when making treatment decisions. Instead, it should be interpreted in conjunction with established clinical and pathological characteristics such as tumor stage, hormone receptor status, HER2 status, and genetic markers. While NLR shows promise as a prognostic and predictive marker, further studies are needed to validate its clinical utility. Despite its appeal as a simple, reproducible, and cost-effective biomarker, several unresolved issues remain regarding NLR in HER2-positive BC management. These include determining an optimal cutoff value, evaluating the impact of longitudinal changes in NLR, and clarifying its relationship with systemic cytokine levels and tumor-infiltrating immune cells. Addressing these questions may refine the prognostic and predictive role of NLR. Large, multi-center cohorts and prospective studies will be essential to confirm its relevance in guiding trastuzumab therapy.

In this study, the median was selected as the cutoff value for NLR, based on several considerations. First, the median ensures an even distribution of patients and provides more robust and interpretable results, particularly in datasets with outliers that are skewed. Second, although Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve–based cutoffs have been widely used to maximize sensitivity and specificity, this approach may risk model overfitting and limited generalizability. Third, compared with ROC-derived cutoffs, the median offers computational simplicity and intuitive application, making it a practical method in biomarker studies.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, this cohort was derived from an early retrospective study conducted before dual-target therapies became standard of care, so the predictive value of NLR in patients receiving dual HER2-targeted treatment remains to be clarified. Second, approximately 1.3% of patients were lost during the extended follow-up period. As these losses occurred randomly, the impact on survival analysis was likely minimal, and these patients were censored at their last available follow-up26. Third, the study was undertaken at a single institution and discrepancies in NLR threshold values persist across studies, underscoring the need for multicenter randomized trials to establish standardized cutoffs and validate our conclusions. Finally, although baseline infection or inflammatory conditions were excluded, residual confounding from factors such as steroid use cannot be entirely ruled out.

Conclusion

In summary, low baseline NLR was significantly associated with prolonged DFS in patients with HER2-positive BC receiving trastuzumab, further strengthening the evidence from previous studies. Routine evaluation of baseline NLR may serve as a simple, cost-effective tool to help identify patients most likely to benefit from trastuzumab therapy.

A case of bilateral upper limb eczema herpeticum
Authors: Claudine Howard-James, Emma Shudell
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractA 20-year-old man with a history of atopic dermatitis presented with a 3-week history of a rash affecting his antecubital fossae. Examination revealed punched out monomorphic papules on an erythematous base.

Dear Editor, An immunocompetent 20-year-old man with atopic dermatitis presented with a bilateral antecubital fossa rash. Examination revealed punched-out monomorphic papules on an erythematous base (Figure 1). A clinical diagnosis of eczema herpeticum was made. Polymerase chain reaction assay of the skin confirmed herpes simplex virus type-1 (HSV-1). He was treated with 14 days of oral valaciclovir, with scarring evident at follow-up (Figure 2).

(a) Right antecubital fossa (pretreatment) showing erythematous papules coalescing into an ulcerated plaque. (b) Left antecubital fossa (pretreatment) showing punched out monomorphic eroded papules.

Right antecubital fossa (post-treatment) showing scarring and postinflammatory erythema.

Eczema herpeticum presents as disseminated monomorphic vesicles and erosions1 resulting from HSV-1. Prevalence is 3% in patients with atopic dermatitis.2 There is a risk of HSV encephalitis, which has a mortality rate of >70% without antiviral medication.2 Recognition is of utmost importance to minimize the risk of disseminated HSV-1 infection.

Enhanced separation of oil in water emulsions using PVDF composite membranes reinforced with graphene oxide doped titania and silica nanoparticles
Authors: Mahmoud F Mubarak, Nawal Bahtiti, Ahmed Abu-Rayyan, Ahmed H Ragab, Najla F Gumaah, El-Sayed M El-Sayed, H S Ali, A Ebada
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAn innovative composite membrane was developed by combining polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) with graphene oxide (GO), titania (TiO2), and silica (SiO2) nanoparticles (PGTS). This innovative membrane was created using solution casting and electrospinning techniques to enhance its surface area and hydrophilic characteristics, while incorporating photocatalytic properties for light-induced oil decomposition. The membrane structure was examined by scanning electron microscopy (SEM), X-ray diffraction (XRD), and Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FT-IR). The effectiveness of the membrane was measured in terms of the oil rejection and fouling resistance under continuous cross-flow conditions in a laboratory-scale filtration setup. The PGTS membrane demonstrated exceptional performance, surpassing both pure PVDF and commercial membranes, with a 99% oil rejection rate and 8% flux increase. Notably, the membrane maintained high emulsion separation efficiency even after 100 h of uninterrupted operation.The synergistic interaction between the PVDF matrix and the surface-functionalized nanomaterials—specifically GO, TiO2, and SiO2—enhanced the membrane’s separation performance and resistance to fouling. Due to its high selectivity, permeability, and excellent antifouling characteristics, this superhydrophilic nanocomposite membrane demonstrates strong potential for the efficient treatment of oily wastewater from both industrial and domestic sources.Keywords: Composite membrane, Poly vinylidene fluoride (PVDF), Graphene oxide (GO), Titania (TiO2), Silica (SiO2), Oil-in-water emulsion separationSubject terms: Environmental sciences, Chemistry

Introduction

The separation of oil-water emulsions is a serious matter in various manufacturing fields, such as petrochemicals, petroleum, pharmaceuticals, and environmentally friendly engineering1–3. These emulsions attitude significant environmental hazards and economic complications due to their perseverance and opposing effects on environments and human health1,4. Conventional separation methods, as well as gravity separation centrifugation, flotation, coagulation and chemical processes, often be unsuccessful to accomplish the essential efficiency and sustainability. These methods often exhibit significant limitations including low separation efficiency, high energy consumption, sensitivity to oil droplet size, and poor performance with stable emulsion5,6. Consequently, membrane-based separation technologies have expanded consideration as a practicable alternative, proposing improved selectivity, scalability, and lower environmental impact7,8. In latest years, in attendance has been angrowing focus on the growth of progressive composite membranes aimed at tackling the intricate challenges associated to the separation of oil-in-water emulsions2,9,10. These membranes utilize the synergistic properties derived from the combination of diverse materials to improve separation efficiency, permeability, and resistance to fouling11,12. Ultrafiltration membrane separation technology is mainlyvaluable for the management of oily wastewater, especially because of its efficiency in eliminating emulsified oils13. Conversely, representative polymers used in the manufacture of ultrafiltration membranes, such as polysulfone (PSF), poly (vinylidene fluoride) (PVDF) and polyethersulfone (PES), retainintrinsicoleophilicfeatures. This affinity leads to high reactivity with oil contaminants, resulting in quick diffusion and adsorption of oils on membrane surfaces, which in turn gives to severe membrane fouling14. Therefore, the advancement of anti-fouling ultrafiltration membranes is necessary for enhancing to enhance the effectiveness of membrane technology in the treatment of oily wastewater. Amongst the many materials that have been explored for membrane production, Polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) has concernedimportantattention due to its outstanding mechanical properties, chemical stability, and adaptability to numerous operational environments15–17. Much development has been made in the production of mixed matrix membranes to incorporate titanium dioxide into the polymer matrix of membranes. Remarkable methods include the consumption of photocatalytic reactors alongside cross-flow filtration, immersing membranes within slurry reactors, and applying titanium dioxide coatings or embedding them within the membrane structure18–20. TiO2 Nanoparticles exhibits chemical inertness, retains a high refractive index, and reveal antibacterial properties through a range of spectra. Furthermore, they are corrosion-resistant, non-toxic, and cost-effective21. TiO2 enhances the PVDF membrane through a combination of photocatalytic self-cleaning, increased hydrophilicity via surface hydroxyl groups, and mechanical reinforcement, making it a key contributor to the membrane’s superior separation and antifouling performance. Hydrophilic particles, such as graphene oxide (GO) and silicon dioxide (SiO2), can be applied to the PVDF membrane to improve the permeability, resulting in hydrophilicity enhancement22. Graphene and its derivatives, especially graphene oxide (GO), are considered highly promising for filtration applications23, due to their large surface area, excellent mechanical and thermal stability, and hydrophilic nature. These properties make GO particularly suitable for membrane fabrication, enabling efficient separation and adsorption performance24,25. These properties facilitate the development of steady dispersions in aqueous environments, production it a appreciated component in modifying water membranes26. SiO2, an inorganic compound well-known for its excellent thermal and chemical stability, also displays high hydrophilicity, which donates to reduced fouling and enhanced membrane permeability.Additionally, In addition, silica particles with reduced dimensions have a higher ratio of external surface area to volume, which increases membrane performance27. Researches specify that the use of GO-SiO2 composites as a membrane structure material results in high hydrophilicity, which improves water permeability of the membrane. Conversely, the absence of silica and graphene oxide composites results in substandard hydrophilic properties, which reduces membrane permeability. These results build on the previous study cited in the research22,25. The incorporation of TiO2 and SiO2 nanoparticles into the GO matrix achieves numerous functions. First, it increases the surface area of ​​the membrane, which improves its interaction with oil droplets and leads to enhanced separation efficiency. In addition, the hydrophilic properties of TiO2 and SiO2 improve the wettability of the membrane, which leads to reduced fouling and increased permeability. Furthermore, this incorporation donates the membrane with photocatalytic capabilities, tolerating it to degrade oil contaminants upon exposure to light. For example, Ma et al. reported a similar decrease in contact angle from 72° to 59° after modifying a PVDF membrane with GO and polyethylene glycol (PEG) chains, resulting in increased fouling resistance and separation efficiency28. Similarly, Lu et al. demonstrated a notable decrease in contact angle from 82.72° to 67.37° following the modification of a PVDF membrane with nanosilver (Ag) and nanozirconium dioxide (ZrO2), which improved antifouling properties and prolonged operational stability29. In this study, the effects of TiO2@SiO2@GOnano fillers on the membrane structure and permeability performance were comprehensively observed through a comprehensive series of characterizations, such as morphological analysis, contact angle evaluations, and separation performance evaluations, to regulate its efficiency. The results designate that the developed composite membrane exhibits improved efficiency in separating oil-in-water emulsions, as well as increased permeability and greater resistance to fouling compared to the conventional membrane. This research represents a significant advance in membrane technology, with potential applications spanning crosswise a diversity of industries, containing wastewater management, oil spill cleanup, and petroleum refining operations. By on condition that a sustainable and efficient attitude to separating oil-in-water emulsions, these composite membranes showanenergetic role in promoting environmental sustainability.The novelty of work lies in the synergistic design of a composite membrane that offers improved separation efficiency and stability, while the fabrication process is carefully optimized and validated to ensure scalability and quality consistency.

Experimental

Material

Polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) with 99.5% purity was obtained from Sigma-Aldrich. Graphene oxide (GO) with 99% purity was obtained from Graphene Laboratories. Titanium dioxide nanoparticles (TiO2, ≥ 99% purity) were gained from US Research Nanomaterials. Silica nanoparticles (SiO2, ≥ 99% purity) were purchased from Nanostructured & Amorphous Materials. In addition, 3-aminopropyltriethoxysilane (APTES, ≥ 98% purity) was obtained from TCI Chemicals. All materials were used as obtained, without any additional purification.

Synthesis

Preparation ofdoped (GTS) composite

A dispersion of GO powder (0.5 g) was prepared in deionized water (100 mL) through ultrasonication for duration of 2 h. Subsequently, Titania (TiO2) nanoparticles (0.1 g) and Silica (SiO2) nanoparticles (0.1 g), used as received without surface modification, were incorporated into the GO dispersion and agitated for 12 h at ambient temperature. After thorough mixing, the mixture was treated with 1 wt% of 3-aminopropyltriethoxysilane (APTES) and stirred for an additional 4 h at room temperature to promote silanization, enhancing the compatibility and dispersion of TiO2 and SiO2 nanoparticles on the GO surface. The resultant mixture underwent centrifugation at 5000 rpm for 20 min to eliminate any unbound nanoparticles. Finally, the obtained doped GO/TiO2/SiO2 (GTS) composite was rinsed with ethanol and subjected to drying at 60 °C for 24 h.

Preparation of PVDF solution

A homogeneous mixture was created by dissolving Poly vinylidene fluoride (PVDF) powder in N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF), constituting 5% by weight. This process involved constant agitation at 60 °C for 4 h.

Fabrication of composite membrane (PGTS)

The PVDF solution was combined with a composite material comprising doped GO/TiO₂/SiO₂ (GTS) at a concentration of 1 wt%. The mixture was stirred for 12 h at room temperature. To ensure the uniform dispersion of the nanoparticles, the solution underwent a 1 h sonication process. The resulting composite mixture was then spread onto a clean glass surface using a doctor blade with a predetermined thickness of 100 μm. The PGTS membrane, a composite of doped GTS and PVDF, was fabricated using the dry phase inversion method via evaporation-induced phase separation (EIPS), wherein solvent evaporation drives the membrane solidification process without immersion in a nonsolvent bath. The cast film was subsequently dried at 80 °C for 24 h to promote gradual and uniform solvent evaporation, ensuring the formation of a stable, defect-free membrane structure while minimizing the risk of pore collapse as clarified in Fig. 1.The same procedure synthesis the PGS membrane, combining of PVDF with GO.

The schematic procedure for formulating PGST composite membrane.

Surface modification

The prepared membranes were subjected to post-fabrication surface functionalization to enhance hydrophilicity and reduce fouling. The membranes were dipped in an ethanol solution containing 2 wt% (3-aminopropyl) triethoxysilane (APTES) for 4 h at room temperature (RT). The APTES modification was performed after membrane fabrication to specifically target the outer membrane surface, where interactions with oil-in-water emulsions occur. This strategy allows for uniform amine group grafting on the membrane surface, improving water affinity and anti-fouling performance without disturbing the bulk membrane structure. Following silanization, the membranes were thoroughly washed with ethanol and dried at 50 °C for 2 h to remove any residual APTES. This clarification emphasizes the role of APTES in membrane surface modification while confirming that the nanoparticles were incorporated in their as-prepared state. The antifouling mechanism arises from both the inherent hydrophilicity of the nanoparticles (TiO2, SiO2, GO) and the APTES-induced surface functionalization. This post-fabrication surface treatment approach has been successfully employed in previous studies to improve membrane wettability and separation performance30,31.

Characterization

This study comprehensively used a SEM working at 200 kV to capture fine details of the crystal structure, surface, and size properties of the membranes. The chemical structure and composition were investigated using X-ray diffraction (XRD, Bruker D8 Advance) and Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR, Thermo Scientific Nicolet iS50). XRD patterns were acquired utilizing Cu Kα radiation (λ = 1.5406 Å) in the 2θ range of 5–60°, with a step size of 0.02°32,33. FTIR spectra were recorded within the wavenumber range of 4000 –500 cm−1 via the attenuated total reflectance (ATR) approach. The assessment of the hydrophilic and hydrophobic properties of the membranes was conducted by measuring the contact angle formed between the membrane surface and a water droplet, utilizing a contact angle goniometer (ZAM104-B, Zolalan Co., Iran), with subsequent analysis performed by Cooling.

Membrane properties

Assessment of oil-in-water emulsion separation performance

A custom filtration apparatus comprising a filtration unit with entry and exit ports, pressure indicators, and flow measurement devices was developed. This configuration allows for easy membrane replacement and operational parameter modification. The composite membrane, with a diameter of 20 cm and a thickness of 1 mm, was securely positioned within the filtration unit, ensuring proper orientation and sealing to prevent leaks. An oil-water emulsion was prepared by blending 500 mL of crude oil with 1 L of water (resulting in an oil concentration of 33.3% v/v) using a high-shear mixer for 30 min, which was then introduced into the filtration unit through the entry port. The emulsion had an average droplet size of ~ 10 μm. Prior to testing, the membrane was pre-treated by compaction at 1.5 bar for 30 min using deionized water to stabilize its structure and remove any residual solvents or impurities. The system was operated at room temperature (25 °C) with pressure maintained at 1 bar. The emulsion was circulated through the membrane under controlled conditions at a flow rate of 10 L/min. The volume of separated oil was continuously tracked and recorded over a 6 h duration using calibrated collection containers or a graduated cylinder. Oil was collected in 100 mL increments, and the total recovered oil volume was documented. Permeate samples were obtained at regular 30 min intervals and examined to determine the remaining oil concentration in the water phase using UV–visible spectroscopy. The absorbance of the materials was characterized at a wavelength of 600 nm, and the oil concentration was measured by calibration curve. The separation efficiency of the membrane was established on the percentage of oil extracted from the emulsion, calculated as following: separation efficiency = (initial oil content - final oil content) / initial oil content × 100%, where initial oil content represents the total volume of oil introduced into the emulsion, and final oil content represents the total volume of oil recovered from the permeate. The experiment was repetitive three times to ensure repeatability.

Results and discussion

The morphology of the PVDF, GO, PGS, and PGTS composite membrane was analyzed using SEM. The image of PVDF reveals a smooth and uniform surface morphology, which enhances its mechanical strength and chemical resistance, as shown in Fig. 2a, making PVDF suitable for various applications, including membrane technology. In contrast, Fig. 2b reveals that the SEM image of GO exhibits a more irregular and rough surface morphology, characterized by folds and wrinkles, and an increased surface area and reactivity, making it an effective additive for composite membranes. The PGS membrane exhibits a hybrid morphology, combining the smooth surface of PVDF with the rougher properties of GO, which improves its mechanical strength and thermal stability. Figure 2c shows that the presence of silica is likely manifested in the form of microstructures or aggregates on the membrane surface, which improves the overall mechanical strength and thermal stability of the composite. This structural composition may enhance the filtration performance compared to pure PVDF by creating a more tortuous path for water flow. Figure 2d shows a scanning electron microscope image of a PGTS membrane with a complex morphology and textured surface, which features the incorporation of titania with PGS, enhancing its crystallinity and optical activity. This combination enhances the effectiveness of membranes in wastewater treatment and filtration technologies.

SEM images of (a) PVDF membrane, (b) GO, (c) PGS membrane, and (d) PGTS membrane.

Figure 3 reveals the XRD analysis of PVDF, GO, PGS and PGTS composite films, providing valuable insights into their crystal structures and phases. PVDF shows prominent diffraction peaks at 2θ = 18.4°, 20.1°, and 26.5°, corresponding to the (020), (110), and (021) planes of its α-phase (JCPDS No. 42-1650)34, indicating a semicrystalline structure primarily associated with the α phase35. This level of crystallinity not only contributes to the mechanical strength and thermal stability of PVDF, but also makes it particularly useful for membrane technology applications. In contrast, GO exhibits a broad (001) peak at ~ 11.1°, indicative of increased interlayer spacing (~ 0.79 nm) due to oxidation and partial ordering, consistent with prior studies36,37.This peak indicates an increase in d-spacing due to the incorporation of oxygen functional groups, such as hydroxyl and carboxyl, which disrupt the orderly arrangement of graphene. GO exhibits an amorphous character due to oxidation, which enhances its reactivity as an additive in composite materials. On the other hand, the PGS patterns reveal new peaks in the 20–25° range, indicating the presence of disordered silica; however, the identification of amorphous SiO₂ remains tentative, as its signature typically appears as a broad hump38. This hybrid structure combines the crystalline features of PVDF with the amorphous properties of GO, which enhances the mechanical properties, thermal stability, and interfacial bonding within the composite. These enhanced interface interactions are expected to result in superior filtration performance compared to PVDF alone. PGTS displays peaks at 25.3° and 48.0°, corresponding to the (101) and (200) planes of anataseTiO₂ (JCPDS No. 21-1272)3, confirming successful incorporation.The incorporation of titania increases the overall crystallinity of the membrane, as indicated by characteristic XRD peaks corresponding to the anatase phase of TiO₂. While anataseTiO₂ is well known for its photocatalytic activity and environmental remediation potential39, its functional impact on membrane performance was not assessed in this study and will be explored in future work.

The FTIR spectra of PVDF, GO, and their composite films, PGS and PGTS, reveal distinct functional groups and interactions that enhance the properties of each material as shown in Fig. 4. The spectrum of PVDF reveals significant peaks in the 930 cm−1 and 720 cm−1 range, which are attributed to C–F stretching, indicating the presence of fluorine atoms that contribute to their remarkable chemical resistance and stability. The peak near 1090 cm−1 indicates the stretching of carbonyl groups (C=O) within the PVDF polymer, as observed by Medeiros et al.40. Furthermore, the identified C–H bending vibrations between 870 and 840 cm−1 enhance the structural integrity of the polymer, highlighting its adaptability to a variety of applications. In contrast, the spectrum of GO presents a broad peak in the range of 3400–3200 cm−1, associated with O–H stretching of hydroxyl groups, which significantly enhances its hydrophilicity compared to PVDF alone. In addition, the characteristic peaks at 1720–1700 cm−1 for C=O vibrations and 1620–1600 cm−1 for C=C stretching indicate the presence of functional groups that increase the reactivity of GO, making it a useful additive in composite membranes. PGS presents new peaks in the range of 1000–1100 cm−1, corresponding to Si–O–Si stretching, thus confirming the successful integration of silica. This combination enhances the mechanical strength, thermal stability and retention properties of PVDF and GO. The PGTS spectrum shows peaks in the 500–600 cm−1 range, indicating the presence of Ti-O-Ti stretching, confirming the presence of titania, known for its optical properties. This integration expands the functional potential of the membrane, especially in environmental treatment and advanced filtration technologies.

The prepared membranes were characterized to confirm the successful incorporation of APTES and functionalized nanoparticles. Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) analysis of the APTES-modified membrane (Fig. 4) revealed new peaks at 1100 cm−1 (Si–O–Si stretching) and 1550 cm−1 (N–H bending), confirming the presence of APTES on the membrane surface. In this study, the composite membrane with surface modifications showed a significant decrease in contact angle to 52°, compared to 75° recorded for the pure PVDF membrane, as shown in Fig. 5. This remarkable decrease in contact angle indicates a transition from hydrophobic to hydrophilic properties due to the surface modifications involving GO, TiO2, and SiO2 nanoparticles. The incorporation of polar functional groups through these modifications enhances the hydrophilicity of the membrane, which promotes better interaction with water molecules. This finding is consistent with previous studies that focused on membrane surface modifications to improve membrane hydrophilicity and reduce fouling in oil/water separation applications. The observed improvement in the hydrophilicity of the surface-modified composite membrane is expected to have significant implications for its performance in oil/water separation processes. The enhanced wettability of the membrane surface reduces the likelihood of oil adhesion, thereby addressing the challenges related to oil/water separation and environmental remediation efforts.

The performance evaluation of the PGTS composite membrane in separating oil-in-water emulsions was performed using a continuous cross-flow process, a technique that effectively mimics real-world separation scenarios. This methodology involves continuous flow of emulsion feed across the membrane surface, thus replicating the conditions typically encountered in practical applications such as wastewater treatment and oil spill remediation. The results presented in Table 2 indicate an average observed oil removal efficiency of over 99% for the PGTS composite membrane, which significantly exceeds the 85% efficiency observed for the pure PVDF membrane under identical experimental conditions. This remarkable difference in oil removal efficiency confirms the superior selectivity and effectiveness of the developed composite membrane in extracting oil droplets from water. The improved oil removal efficiency of the composite membrane can be attributed to various factors, including surface modifications and the incorporation of GO, TiO2, and SiO2 nanoparticles. These improvements are likely to improve the affinity of the membrane for oil droplets, thereby enhancing their adsorption and subsequent removal from the water stream. In addition, the asymmetric structure of the membrane, as suggested by morphological analysis, may facilitate more efficient oil/water separation by acting as a barrier that restricts the passage of oil droplets while allowing the flow of water molecules. Furthermore, the lack of specific information regarding experimental conditions, such as cross-flow velocity, temperature, and oil-in-water emulsion concentration, needs to be addressed in future discussions or publications. These parameters can significantly affect the membrane performance and are crucial for accurate interpretation of the results and comparisons with previous studies.

Examining the residual oil concentration in the permeate water, as shown in Fig. 6, provides crucial insights into the membrane’s effectiveness in removing oil over time. Achieving a concentration of less than 1 ppm after 360 min is of particular importance, as it is in line with regulatory discharge limits for oils and greases, which are typically set at around 10 ppm. Evaluating the anti-fouling capabilities of the composite membrane, by observing the changes in water flux during oil emulsion filtration, provides crucial insights into its anti-fouling and sustainable operational efficiency. Figure 7 indicates that the composite membrane exhibits superior anti-fouling properties compared to the pure PVDF membrane. Initially, both membranes exhibit identical flux rates of 245 L/m2 h. However, after prolonged exposure to oil emulsion filtration, the composite membrane exhibits a much smaller decrease in flux rate compared to the pure PVDF membrane. Specifically, the flux rate of the composite membrane decreases by only 8%, ending at 225 L/m2 h, while the pure PVDF membrane experiences a more significant decrease of 26%, resulting in a final flux rate of 181 L/m2 h. The observed flux improvement is 8%, this is paired with a remarkable enhancement in fouling resistance and long-term operational stability compared to conventional PVDF membranes. This improved fouling resistance performance can be attributed to factors such as the incorporation of functional nanoparticles and modifications in the surface chemistry of the composite membrane, which may effectively hinder the adhesion of oil droplets and other fouling materials. In conclusion, the results confirm the effectiveness of the composite membrane in reducing fouling and maintaining performance during oil emulsion filtration, especially when compared to the pristine PVDF membrane. These insights are vital for practical applications, where maintaining stable flux and fouling resistance is essential for the long-term effectiveness and reliability of membrane-based separation technologies across various sectors.

The study investigating the effect of GO/TiO2/SiO2 (GTS) nanofiller incorporation on key membrane performance metrics provides crucial insights into the relationship between nanofiller concentration and oil rejection efficiencyat different nanofiller loadings (0.5, 0.1 and 2.0 wt%). The data presented, along with the visual representation in Fig. 8, indicate a clear trend of enhanced oil rejection efficiency with increasing nanofiller loading. At 0.5 wt% loading, the membrane achieves an oil rejection efficiency of 92%. This efficiency increases to 95% with increasing to 1 wt% loading. Notably, at the maximum loading of 2 wt%, the membrane shows a significant improvement in oil rejection efficiency, reaching 99%. The increase in oil rejection efficiency associated with higher concentrations of GTS nanofillers can be explained by several mechanisms. Primarily, the incorporation of these nanoparticles increases the surface area of ​​the membrane, thereby providing additional active sites for interaction with oil droplets. Furthermore, the addition of nanoparticles enhances the hydrophilicity of the membrane surface, enhancing wetting and interaction with water molecules, facilitating the repulsion of oil droplets. In addition, the functional groups present on the nanoparticles may engage in chemical interactions with the oil molecules, further contributing to the enhanced oil rejection efficiency. These results emphasize the critical influence of GTS nanoparticle loading on the separation capabilities of the composite membrane. The remarkable improvement in oil rejection efficiency with increasing nanofiller content highlights the potential of nanocomposite membranes for efficient oil/water separation applications. Continued research and improvement in nanoparticle loading and membrane fabrication techniques can pave the way for the development of advanced membranes with superior performance and broader applicability in various industrial and environmental contexts.

Effect of different nanofiller loadings on oil rejection efficiency.

The results shown in Table 3 provide important insights into how varying GTS nanoparticle loadings affect the performance of the composite membrane compared to pristine PVDF, especially in terms of pure water flux and antifouling properties. As shown in Table 2, the pure water flux of the membrane shows a decreasing trend with increasing nanofiller concentration. At 0.5 wt% loading, the membrane achieves a pure water flux of 250 L/m2 h, which decreases to 245 L/m2 h at 1 wt% loading. It is worth noting that at the maximum loading of 2 wt%, there is a significant decrease in flux, which decreases to 220 L/m2 h compared to pristine membrane shows a pure water flux of 265 L/m2 h. This decrease in pure water flux with higher nanofiller concentrations suggests that the increased blockage of the membrane pores, caused by a higher density of nanoparticles, leads to a decrease in permeability. In addition, the antifouling performance of the membrane, as shown by the flux decrease ratio, follows a similar pattern of deterioration with increasing nanofiller loadings. The flux drop ratio, which measures the decrease in flux over time during continuous operation and serves as an indicator of membrane fouling, shows that at 0.5% loading, the membrane has a flux drop ratio of 10%, reflecting moderate fouling resistance. However, as the loadings increase to 1% and 2%, the flux drop ratios increase to 8% and 15%, respectivelycompared to pristine membrane has a flux drop ratio of 26%. This trend indicates that the anti-fouling ability of the membrane deteriorates with higher nanofiller concentrations, likely due to increased pore clogging by the nanoparticles. An optimum loading of 1% GTS nanoparticles was determined for subsequent investigations based on the observations made. This specific loading achieves a favorable balance between satisfactory pure water flux and fouling resistance capabilities, thus delivering efficient performance while minimizing the detrimental effects of pore clogging on membrane permeability. Choosing this optimum loading ensures that the composite membrane retains its distinctive properties and performance features, thereby improving its applicability in practical scenarios involving oil/water separation processes.

Conclusion

This research focused on the creation of an innovative composite membrane based on polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF), which was enhanced with doped graphene oxide, titania and silica to improve the separation of oil-in-water emulsions. The strategy involved incorporating hydrophilic and photocatalytic TiO2/SiO2 nanoparticles into graphene oxide nanosheets to enhance the surface area, increase water absorption capacity and facilitate the degradation of oil contaminants upon exposure to light. Morphological and surface chemical analyses conducted by scanning electron microscopy, XRD, FT-IR and contact angle evaluations indicated a uniform distribution of nanoparticles within the polymer matrix, along with a significant improvement in water absorption capacity after surface modification. Performance tests conducted under continuous flow filtration revealed that the improved composite membrane achieved over 99% oil rejection while maintaining stable flow, outperforming both pure PVDF and commercially available membranes. The synergistic effect of the inherently stable PVDF polymer matrix combined with the functional nanofillers enhanced the emulsion separation efficiency, along with improved permeability rates and fouling resistance. Notably, the membrane exhibited exceptional separation capabilities even after 100 h of continuous operation, confirming its reliability and anti-fouling properties during extended use. Overall, this study successfully produced a high-performance nanocomposite membrane through a straightforward fabrication method, showing great potential for treating oily industrial and domestic wastewater. The scalability of the membrane synthesis process may facilitate its adoption in various separation applications. Future investigations could focus on evaluating the long-term durability across multiple operating cycles and examining the effects of variables such as temperature, pH, and salinity on the separation efficiency. Owing to their high selectivity, productivity, and fouling resistance, these multifunctional nanocomposite membranes offer a sustainable and effective approach to address the global issue of oily water treatment.

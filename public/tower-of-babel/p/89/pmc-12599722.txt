Study on the efficacy and safety of Xuezhikang capsule combination with epalrestaton in treating diabetic peripheral neuropathy patients
Authors: Yuping Huang, Meili Han, Xiaoou Guo, Xiaopang Rao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This study retrospectively analyzes the clinical effect of Xuezhikang capsule combined with epalrestat on type 2 diabetic peripheral neuropathy. A total of 140 patients with type 2 diabetes combined with DPN were selected as the participants, which were divided into treatment group and control group, 70 cases respectively, according to different treatment schemes. The control group of patients received treatment with iprastam (50-mg/dose, 3 times/day) on the basis of controlling blood sugar, while the treatment group of patients received oral administration of Xuezhikang capsules (0.6-g/dose, 2 times/day) to regulate blood lipids on the basis of the control group’s treatment plan, all treated for 12 weeks. Observe and compare the levels of laboratory indicators, Toronto Clinical Scoring System scores, and nerve conduction velocity before and after treatment between 2 groups of patients. After treatment, the levels of triglycerides, low-density lipoprotein, C-reactive protein, and tumor necrosis factor in the treatment group were measured. The level was lower than before treatment and the control group, while the level of high-density lipoprotein was higher than before treatment and the control group, with a statistically significant difference (P < .05); after treatment, the Toronto Clinical Scoring System scores of both groups were lower than before treatment, and the treatment group was lower than the control group, with a statistically significant difference; after treatment, the motor conduction velocity of the median nerve and common peroneal nerve, as well as the sensory conduction velocity of the sural nerve, median nerve, and common peroneal nerve in both groups were higher than before treatment, and the treatment group was higher than the control group, with statistical significance (P < .05); logistic regression analysis showed that high HbA1c, high triglycerides, high C-reactive protein, low high-density lipoprotein, and course of disease were risk factors for diabetes peripheral neuropathy (P < .05). Xuezhikang capsule can improve the blood lipid level and inflammatory factors in patients with type 2 diabetes, and the effect of Xuezhikang capsules combined with Epastatin on improving the conduction velocity, clinical score, and clinical symptoms of peripheral neuropathy

1. Introduction

Diabetic peripheral neuropathy (DPN) stands out as one of the most prevalent microvascular complications among diabetic patients, significantly impacting their quality of life. Prolonged hyperglycemia induces damage to the nervous system, manifesting in limb paresthesia, muscle atrophy, abnormal movement, and in severe cases, foot lesions such as ulcers and gangrene.[1,2] DPN deeply affects about half of diabetic patients, contributing substantially to increased disability rates and even mortality associated with diabetes.[3] Research indicates that 10% to 20% of individuals with type 2 diabetes exhibit DPN at the time of diagnosis.[4] Currently, the pathogenesis of DPN remains elusive, likely involving a multifactorial interplay encompassing insulin resistance, inflammatory responses, and lipid disorders alongside hyperglycemia.[5,6]

Western medicine employs various therapeutic approaches targeting the underlying causes, including hypoglycemic agents, vasodilators, agents to reduce blood viscosity and coagulation, antioxidant medications, neurotrophic agents, and symptomatic pain management drugs. Traditional Chinese medicine predominantly utilizes herbal remedies, acupuncture, and foot baths, among other modalities. Some studies[7] suggest that dyslipidemia may independently elevate the risk of DPN, underscoring its close association with the onset and progression of this condition. Xuezhikang, derived from monacolin-containing Chinese patent medicine, encompasses natural complex statins, ergosterol, flavonoids, and other components known for their lipid-regulating and antioxidant properties.[8] This study retrospectively evaluates the clinical efficacy and safety of combining Xuezhikang with epalrestat in managing DPN among type 2 diabetes patients, aiming to furnish evidence and support for subsequent large-scale clinical applications.

2. Participants and methods

2.1. Participants

A total of 140 patients diagnosed with type 2 diabetes and concurrent DPN were selected from the Department of Endocrinology, Chengyang District People’s Hospital of Qingdao, between January and December 2022. Inclusion criteria were as follows: Compliance with the diagnostic criteria for type 2 diabetes outlined in the Chinese Guidelines for the Prevention and Treatment of Type 2 Diabetes (2020 Edition)[9]; Meeting the diagnostic criteria for DPN according to the Expert Consensus on Diagnosis and Treatment of Diabetic Neuropathy (2021 Edition)[10]; and Diagnosis of dyslipidemia based on the Chinese Expert Consensus on Prevention and Treatment of Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus with Dyslipidemia (2017 Revision).[11] Exclusion criteria included: individuals with a history of alcoholism or smoking for ≥10 years; patients experiencing severe acute complications such as hypoglycemia, diabetic ketoacidosis, hyperosmolar nonketotic hyperglycemia syndrome, or lactic acidosis; patients with acute cerebrovascular disease, cervical or lumbar spine disorders, infectious polyneuritis, connective tissue diseases, vasculitis, uremia, lower limb infections, vascular stenosis or occlusion, edema, or other conditions; patients diagnosed with anxiety or depression; severe liver or kidney insufficiency; and peripheral neuropathy attributable to drug factors or other diseases. This study received approval from the Ethics Committee of Chengyang District People’s Hospital of Qingdao (approval number: CYQRMYY2021-12-11).

2.2. Treatment methods

Based on different treatment regimens and the random number table, a total of 140 subjects were included in the study. First, the 140 patients were numbered from 1 to 140, and a 3-digit number was read from any position on the random number table as a random number and entered under the number. Then, the read random numbers were sorted by size, with the intervention group numbered 1 to 70 and the control group consisting of the rest. The 140 patients were divided into 2 groups: the treatment group (70 cases) and the control group (70 cases). The sample size was determined using PASS statistical software, and the results confirmed that it is sufficient to meet the statistical requirements for this study. All patients in both groups received metformin or a combination with insulin (combination therapy with 2 or more oral medications for glycemic control failure and glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c) >7.0%, for those who do not meet the blood glucose standard, continuous monitoring of blood glucose for 3 days from 5 to 7 o’clock is required. The treatment plan should be adjusted according to blood glucose until it meets the standard. After reaching the standard, blood glucose should be monitored 2–4 times a week) to manage their blood glucose levels. The control group received oral epalrestat (Jiangsu Yangzijiang Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., approved with National Medicine Number H20040012, dosage: 50 mg per time, 3 times a day) in addition to blood glucose control measures. No other neuropathy drugs were administered during the 12-week treatment period. In contrast, the treatment group received Xuezhikang capsules (Peking University Weixin Biotechnology Co., Ltd., National Medicine Number: 05902Z019, dosage: 0.6g per time, twice a day) alongside the control group measures for the same 12-week duration.

2.3. Indicators

2.3.1. Severity of DPN lesions

Before and after treatment, the Toronto Clinical Scoring System (TCSS) was employed to assess the severity of DPN lesions in both groups. TCSS comprises 3 components: symptoms, reflexes, and sensation. Symptoms include weakness, tingling, numbness and burning sensation, lancinating pain, ataxia, and upper limb symptoms, each scoring 1 point if present and 0 if absent, with a total score of 6 points. Reflexes involve bilateral ankle and knee reflexes, with normal reflexes scoring 0 points, diminished reflexes scoring 1 point, and absent reflexes scoring 2 points, for a total score of 8 points. Sensation includes pain perception, temperature perception, touch perception, vibration perception, and position perception, with abnormal sensation scoring 1 point and normal sensation scoring 0 points, yielding a total score of 5 points. The sum of scores from these 3 components constitutes the TCSS score, ranging from 0 to 19 points, with higher scores indicating greater lesion severity.

2.3.2. Nerve conduction velocity

Before and after treatment, nerve conduction velocities of both groups were measured in a quiet environment at 22°C to 26°C using the Haiyan NDI-097 electromyogram-evoked potential instrument.[12] The indices include the motor conduction velocity (MCV) of the median nerve and the tibial nerve, as well as the sensory conduction velocity (SCV) of the sural nerve, median nerve, and tibial nerve.

2.3.3. Lower limb Doppler ultrasound

Evaluation of lower limb vascular stenosis or occlusion was conducted by 2 experienced attending physicians in the ultrasound department.

2.3.4. Clinical data

Hospitalization records of both groups were collected and reviewed to ensure data accuracy, completeness, and reliability. Clinical data encompassed general information such as diabetes duration, age, and gender, as well as pre- and posttreatment systolic blood pressure (SBP), diastolic blood pressure (DBP), and laboratory parameters (fasting blood glucose [FBG], HbA1c, total cholesterol [TC], triglycerides [TG], high-density lipoprotein [HDL], low-density lipoprotein [LDL], alanine aminotransferase [ALT], creatine kinase [CK], and serum creatinine [SCr]). Levels of TC, TG, HDL-C, LDL-C, ALT, CK, SCr, and C-reactive protein (CRP) were measured using the Roche COBAS INTEGRA 800 fully automatic biochemical analyzer. Serum levels of tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) were detected using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay. The detection indicators are automatically extracted through the hospital’s testing information platform, and 2 participating researchers are required to double check 2 pieces of data and import them into an Excel spreadsheet.

2.3.5. Adverse reactions

Occurrence of adverse reactions during the treatment process in both groups was observed to assess treatment safety.

2.4. Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS 23.0 (IBM Corp, Armonk). Continuous variables including laboratory parameters, nerve conduction velocity indices, and TCSS scores were expressed as mean ± standard deviation. Prior to analysis, all variables were assessed for normality using Kolmogorov–Smirnov tests (for sample sizes ≥ 50), and homogeneity of variance was verified using Levene test. Variables meeting both normality and homogeneity assumptions were analyzed using independent samples t tests. For variables violating these assumptions, nonparametric alternatives (Mann–Whitney U tests) were employed. To control for type I error inflation due to multiple comparisons (particularly for the laboratory parameters in Table 2), we implemented the Bonferroni correction by adjusting the significance threshold to α/n, where n represents the number of comparisons within each family of tests. For our primary outcomes (TCSS scores and nerve conduction velocities), we maintained the conventional α = 0.05 as these were prespecified hypothesis-driven analyses. All tests were 2-tailed, and statistical significance was set at P < .05 unless otherwise adjusted for multiple comparisons. For transparency, exact P-values are reported throughout the Results section.

3. Results

3.1. General demographic characteristics

This study included a total of 140 participants, with 70 in the treatment group: 36 males and 34 females, aged between 35 and 75 years (mean age: 57.72 ± 16.19 years), with diabetes duration ranging from 3 to 11 years (mean duration: 6.52 ± 2.73 years), and a body mass index (BMI) of 24.53 ± 4.36. Regarding educational background, 29 had education up to junior high school or below, while 41 had education at high school level or above. The control group also comprised 70 participants: 35 males and 35 females, aged between 34 and 76 years (mean age: 56.68 ± 18.91 years), with diabetes duration ranging from 3 to 12 years (mean duration: 6.65 ± 2.78 years), and a BMI of (24.49 ± 4.27) kg/m2. In terms of educational level, 32 had education up to junior high school (including junior high school) or below, while 38 had education at high school level (including high school) or above. The general demographic characteristics were showed in Table 1.

3.2. Clinical data comparison

Before treatment, there were no statistically significant differences (P > .05) between the 2 groups in SBP, DBP, ALT, FBG, HbA1c, CK, TC, TG, LDL, HDL, and SCr levels. Similarly, there were no statistically significant differences (P > .05) in SBP, DBP, ALT, FBG, HbA1c, CK, TC, TG, LDL, HDL, and SCr levels between pre- and posttreatment in the control group. In the treatment group, there were no statistically significant differences (P > .05) in SBP, DBP, ALT, FBG, HbA1c, CK, TC, and SCr levels between pre- and posttreatment. However, posttreatment, the levels of TG, LDL, CRP, and TNF-α in the treatment group were lower than before treatment and lower than those in the control group, while HDL levels were higher than before treatment and higher than those in the control group, with statistically significant differences (P < .05), which was showed in Table 2.

3.3. Comparison of TCSS scores and nerve conduction velocity before and after treatment

Following treatment, TCSS scores decreased in both groups compared with pretreatment levels, with the treatment group showing lower scores than the control group, demonstrating statistically significant differences (P < .05). Additionally, posttreatment, both groups exhibited increased MCV of the median nerve and tibial nerve, as well as SCV of the sural nerve, median nerve, and tibial nerve compared with pretreatment levels. Furthermore, the treatment group displayed higher MCV and SCV values than the control group, with statistically significant differences (P < .05), which was showed in Table 3. Furthermore, multivariate analysis confirmed this (Table 4).

3.4. Logistic regression analysis of risk factors associated with DPN

Using the occurrence of peripheral neuropathy as the dependent variable (1 for yes, 0 for no), logistic regression analysis was conducted with gender (male = 0, female = 1), age, CRP (normal = 0, high = 1), BMI (<23.0 kg/m2 = 0, ≥23.0 kg/m2 = 1), FBG (<7.0 mmol/L = 0, ≥7.0 mmol/L = 1), HbA1c (<7.0% = 0, ≥7.0% = 1), TG (normal = 0, high = 1), HDL (normal = 0, low = 1), LDL-C (normal = 0, high = 1), HDL-C (normal = 0, low = 1), TNF-α (normal = 0, high = 1), duration of diabetes (<3 years = 0, 3–6 years = 1, 6–9 years = 2, 9–12 years = 3), and blood pressure (<130/80 mm Hg = 0, ≥130/80 mm Hg = 1) as independent variables. The results revealed that elevated HbA1c, high TG, elevated CRP, low HDL, and longer duration of diabetes were risk factors for the occurrence of DPN (P < .05). However, gender, age, BMI ≥ 23.0, FBG ≥ 7.0 mmol/L, hypertension, LDL, and elevated TNF-α did not show significant associations with risk factors (P > .05). See Table 5 for details.

3.5. Incidence of adverse reactions

During the 12-week treatment period, 1 case of adverse reaction (fatigue) was observed in the control group, while 2 cases of adverse reactions (mild muscle pain in 1 case and rash in another case) occurred in the treatment group. All 3 cases were categorized as mild adverse reactions and gradually resolved without discontinuation of medication. No severe adverse events were observed in any of the patients, and there were no abnormalities in liver and kidney function or enzyme levels.

4. Discussion

The pathogenesis of DPN is not fully understood and may be related to factors such as hyperglycemia-induced activation of the polyol pathway, oxidative stress, microvascular dysfunction leading to neural ischemia, and autoimmune factors.[13] Abnormal elevation of blood glucose can activate the polyol pathway, leading to increased activity and levels of aldose reductase in the body. This enzyme catalyzes the conversion of glucose to sorbitol and fructose, which accumulate within nerve cells, causing edema and subsequent axonal degeneration and demyelination in peripheral nerves. Elevated blood glucose levels can also reduce the activity of nitric oxide synthase, leading to decreased production of nitric oxide, a vasodilator, resulting in ischemic damage to nerve cells. Epalrestat belongs to a new class of aldose reductase inhibitors of carboxylic acids. It can not only balance the biological activity of Na+- K+-ATPase and inositol in the patient’s body but also significantly improve the level of sorbitol, thereby increasing the speed of the patient’s nervous system sensing, restoring the patient’s pain and sensory nerves, reducing the occurrence of pain, and improving its exact therapeutic effect. In addition, after a large number of experimental studies in rats, it was found that epalrestat could reduce the aggregation of sorbitol in the cells of rats with diabetes and improve the NCV level. In addition, Ipastatin can also increase the blood flow velocity of the sciatic nerve during exercise, thereby increasing the level of inositol.[14,15] Additionally, it may inhibit the protein kinase C signaling pathway, increase the level of nitric oxide in neuronal vascular endothelial cells, and suppress the expression of endothelial cell adhesion molecules and adhesion factors mediated by elevated blood glucose, effectively improving DPN.

Recent studies have shown that statins or fibrates, lipid-lowering drugs, can not only improve lipid levels but also reduce the risk of developing neuropathy in patients by 35% to 48%.[16] This effect may be related to various other effects of statin drugs beyond lipid regulation, although the mechanism is not yet fully understood.[17] Research has found that statin drugs can not only regulate lipids but also alleviate inflammatory responses, antioxidative stress, antagonize cellular apoptosis, protect vascular endothelial cells, and improve blood flow.[18,19] Xuezhikang capsules, made from specially fermented red yeast rice, contain small amounts of lovastatin, various naturally occurring complex statins, flavonoids, and sterols, as well as fatty acids, amino acids, and trace elements. Beyond its lipid-lowering effects, increasing attention has been paid to its potential effects beyond lipid regulation, such as anti-thrombosis, regulation of the body’s immune status, antioxidative stress, anti-inflammatory response, and vasodilation.[20,21]

The results of this study show that posttreatment, levels of TG, LDL, CRP, and TNF-α were significantly lower, and HDL levels were significantly higher in the treatment group compared with both pretreatment levels and the control group, indicating that Xuezhikang capsules can significantly improve lipid levels and reduce inflammation factor levels in patients. Moreover, multifactorial risk factor analysis revealed that elevated HbA1c, high TG, high CRP, low HDL, and longer duration of diabetes were risk factors for the development of DPN, highlighting the need to improve blood glucose and lipid levels while simultaneously improving oxidative stress levels.

Further studies have found that Xuezhikang can lower blood lipids and blood glucose levels in diabetic mice, improve nerve conduction velocity, reduce oxidative stress, and protect against DPN, independent of effects related to blood glucose control or lipid normalization. This study found that posttreatment, TCSS scores were significantly lower, and MCV of the median nerve and tibial nerve, as well as SCV of the sural nerve, median nerve, and tibial nerve, were significantly higher in the treatment group compared with both pretreatment levels and the control group, suggesting that Xuezhikang capsules may assist Epastatin for improving peripheral nerve conduction velocity and alleviating the condition of peripheral neuropathy.[22]

This study shows that Xuezhikang capsule can improve the level of blood lipid and inflammatory factors in patients with type 2 diabetes and may also help other traditional drugs for neuropathy, such as epalrestat, to improve the conduction velocity, clinical score, and clinical symptoms of peripheral neuropathy. The efficacy is better than that of epalrestat alone, with fewer adverse reactions. The results of this study may provide new ideas for the treatment of DPN. However, this study is retrospective, single center, and has a small sample size. Retrospective studies have data quality that depends on the completeness of historical records, making it difficult to handle missing values; insufficient control of confounding variables may lead to pseudo causal relationships; the temporal relationship is difficult to accurately determine (such as the inability to confirm that exposure occurred before the outcome). Single center sample collection may have certain limitations, which may lead to biased research results. The next step is to conduct prospective randomized controlled trials to confirm these results and determine the clinical significance of Xuezhikang in treating DPN, and enrich statistical analysis methods to accurately analyze the results of this study, providing more accurate clinical evidence-based basis for the formulation of clinical plans.

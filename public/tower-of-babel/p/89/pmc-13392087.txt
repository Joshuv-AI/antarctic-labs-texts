Spatiotemporal evolution characteristics and influencing factors of hepatitis B incidence rate in China
Authors: Ke Sun, Siqi Zhang, Fang Wang, Liqing Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection remains a persistent public health challenge in China, with significant spatiotemporal heterogeneity in incidence. This study aimed to examine regional disparities in hepatitis B incidence, explore its spatiotemporal evolution patterns, and analyze the spatiotemporal heterogeneity of associated factors across China, using incidence data from 2008 to 2023. The Dagum Gini coefficient decomposition method assessed regional disparity sources, the standard deviation ellipse explored spatiotemporal direction and global characteristics, exploratory spatial data analysis (ESDA) examined spatial correlation and aggregation, and the geographically and temporally weighted regression (GTWR) model was used to examine spatiotemporal heterogeneity of influencing factors. Results showed narrowing regional disparities, declining spatial aggregation, and a standard deviation ellipse located in eastern and central regions with a southeastward shift, alongside the incidence gravity center moving 351.25 km southeast. Patterns shifted from clustered to dispersed, likely due to vaccination and improved healthcare allocation. Six socioeconomic factors exerted significant yet spatiotemporally heterogeneous impacts, with diverse, regionally varying effects based on economic and policy contexts.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-53490-8.

Introduction

Hepatitis B, an infectious disease caused by the hepatitis B virus (HBV) and primarily characterized by liver damage, can progress to acute or chronic hepatitis and may subsequently develop into serious complications such as liver fibrosis, cirrhosis, liver failure, or even hepatocellular carcinoma1, posing a significant threat to public health both globally and in China2. According to the 2024 World Health Organization (WHO) report, approximately 254 million people worldwide were living with chronic HBV infection in 2022, with around 1.2 million new infections occurring annually and approximately 1.1 million individuals dying each year from hepatitis B and its complications, including cirrhosis and liver cancer3. China bears the highest global HBV burden due to its population size, with approximately 75 million chronic HBV cases currently accounting for nearly one-third of the worldwide total4. Alarmingly, 41.8% of global primary liver cancer deaths occurred in China in 20225, and HBV infection is a major contributing risk factor to this trend6. Based on the national surveillance data of notifiable infectious diseases, hepatitis B has consistently ranked first among Class B notifiable infectious diseases in China, underscoring its significance as a major public health concern.

To effectively control the spread of hepatitis B, the Chinese government launched the "National Hepatitis B Vaccination Program" in 1991, integrating hepatitis B vaccination into the national immunization program. After nearly three decades of dedicated efforts, the widespread implementation of hepatitis B vaccination has significantly reduced the incidence of hepatitis B in China, successfully decreasing the endemicity from a high to a moderate level7. However, due to China’s large population, high historical prevalence of infection, and complex transmission routes primarily involving sexual contact, mother-to-child transmission, and blood/body fluid exposure, the country continues to face significant hepatitis B prevention and control challenges, maintaining persistently high absolute numbers of infected individuals.

Existing studies have delved into the spatiotemporal epidemiological characteristics and influencing factors of this disease in China. Jiao Liping et al., using spatial autocorrelation and spatiotemporal scan analysis, found hepatitis B incidence rate declining nationally yet exhibiting significant spatial clustering, underscoring the value of these spatiotemporal patterns for guiding prevention strategies8. Focusing on the Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei region, Xu C D et al. applied a Bayesian spatiotemporal hierarchical model, revealing that population density and GDP per capita negatively correlate with the incidence, while the proportion of the primary industry and medical resources show positive correlations, highlighting the impact of regional socioeconomic disparities9. Conversely, Sun Ning et al. observed that the hepatitis B incidence rate across Chinese provinces showed an upward trend from 2002 to 2021, with spatial hotspots dynamically migrating from the northwest to the southeast10. Furthermore, Zhu Bin et al., through spatial autoregression and spatiotemporal scanning, confirmed distinct clustering patterns for different hepatitis types, emphasizing the necessity for differentiated, spatiotemporally informed control strategies11.

Existing studies have mapped the spatiotemporal clustering of hepatitis B incidence in China and identified certain socioeconomic associations, yet they have not systematically quantified the structural sources of regional disparities, comprehensively delineate the dynamic evolution of incidence patterns, or adequately uncover the spatiotemporally heterogeneous mechanisms underlying the epidemic. To address these three gaps, our study introduced an integrated analysis that synergistically combines the Dagum Gini coefficient decomposition with Geographically and Temporally Weighted Regression (GTWR). The Dagum Gini decomposition method quantifies the contributions of intra-regional and inter-regional inequality, whereas the GTWR model effectively captures spatiotemporal non-stationarity in the impacts of socioeconomic determinants. This methodology effectively bridges the gap between macro-level inequality accounting and micro-level dynamic driver analysis. Thus, we utilized nationwide hepatitis B incidence rate data from 2008 to 2023 in China, and conducted a multidimensional analysis examining regional differences, spatiotemporal evolution patterns, and the spatiotemporal heterogeneity of associated factors. Our findings thus are intended to inform public health strategy by pinpointing whether national disparities stem primarily from imbalances between major regions or inequalities within them, and by mapping how the influence of key factors like healthcare investment shifts across localities over years. This work could help advance beyond one-size-fits-all policies, providing a useful evidence base for strategically prioritizing resources to key geographic units and adapting intervention components to the specific drivers active in each region and period. Ultimately, the analysis aims to provide a more comprehensive and precise evidence base to inform the development of hepatitis B prevention and control strategies.

Methods

Data source

This study was conducted across 31 provincial-level administrative regions in mainland China (including provinces, autonomous regions, and municipalities directly under the central government), excluding Taiwan, Hong Kong, and Macao, covering the period from 2008 to 2023. Data on hepatitis B incidence rates from 2008 to 2023 were compiled from the China Health Statistics Yearbook (for 2009–2013 and 2018–2024) and the China Statistical Yearbook of Health and Family Planning (for 2014–2017). Data for the influencing factors included in the GTWR model were sourced from the China Statistical Yearbook and respective provincial statistical yearbooks. Missing values were addressed using linear interpolation to ensure data continuity and completeness.

It then ranks regions by the mean values of the study objects in each region, and decomposes G into within-region differences Gw,, between-region differences Gnb, and inter-region overlapping density Gt, with G = Gw + Gnb + Gt. Overlapping density refers to the spatial differences caused by cross-overlapping between groups. While the geographically weighted regression (GWR) model addresses spatial heterogeneity effectively17, its neglect of temporal variability constrains scientific validity; consequently, extending GWR to incorporate temporal parameters via the geographically and temporally weighted regression (GTWR) model enables simultaneous resolution of both dimensions of heterogeneity18. This study employed the GTWR model to comprehensively analyze the spatial and temporal influencing factors of hepatitis B incidence rate in China. Using hepatitis B incidence rate as the dependent variable and statistically screened influencing factors as independent variables, this study established both the GTWR model and the Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) model. Model performance was evaluated through the corrected Akaike Information Criterion (AICc) and coefficient of determination (R2), with superior models identified by lower AICc values and higher R2 values20. The relative influence of each independent variable was quantitatively ranked according to the spatiotemporally averaged regression coefficients derived from the GTWR model.

Model validation strategy

Before regression modeling, the Variance Inflation Factor (VIF) was calculated to test the multicollinearity of independent variables, ensuring no serious collinearity existed among explanatory factors. Subsequently, the ordinary least squares (OLS) global regression model was constructed as a benchmark. The GTWR model was implemented via the third-party academic add-in for ArcMap. The optimal spatial and temporal bandwidth of GTWR was automatically determined following the minimum Akaike Information Criterion (AIC). Finally, the coefficient of determination R2 and AIC values were used to compare the fitting performance between OLS and GTWR. The results verified that the GTWR model had higher explanatory power and was more applicable to capturing the spatiotemporal heterogeneity of hepatitis B incidence rate.

Code availability

The custom R code used for Dagum Gini decomposition is available as Supplementary Material accompanying this article. For the Geographically and Temporally Weighted Regression (GTWR) analysis, we utilized the GTWR Beta 1.0 Add-in for ArcMap 10.5, which is available for free download from https://www.researchgate.net/publication/329518786_GTWR_ADDIN_Valid_till_Dec_31_2022. No custom code was developed for the GTWR analysis as we used the standard functions provided by this plugin.

Results

Hepatitis B incidence rate overview

Based on provincial data from 2008 to 2023, China’s hepatitis B incidence rate exhibited an overall downward trend at the national level, declining at an average annual rate of 1.13%, while slight fluctuations have been observed in certain years (Table 1). Most provinces demonstrated decreased trends. Ningxia, Gansu, Chongqing, Henan, and Zhejiang have experienced significant reductions, with respective average annual decline rates of 11.16%, 11.22%, 7.82%, 7.36%, and 7.15%, respectively. Conversely, some provinces including Tibet, Anhui, Shandong, and Hainan have witnessed increases in hepatitis B incidence rate, with Tibet experiencing the highest average annual increase of 14.00%, and Anhui, Shandong, and Hainan reporting average annual increases of 6.61%, 5.71%, and 6.46%, respectively. Provinces with the most pronounced declines included Gansu (-11.22%), Ningxia (-11.16%), Qinghai (-7.65%), and Chongqing (-7.82%), indicating substantial between-province variation in incidence trends over the study period. Overall, regions with relatively high economic development levels, such as those in Northwest and North China, have demonstrated marked declines in hepatitis B incidence rate, while Tibet, central China, and southern coastal areas have become hotspots for increasing incidence rate. Regional disparities of hepatitis B incidence rate

Following the method of Dagum for decomposing the Gini coefficient by subgroups, this study further studies the regional difference in the distribution of hepatitis B incidence rate. It decomposed the regional difference in China’s 31 provinces into three major regions: the eastern, western and central regions, and calculated the total differences, intraregional differences, interregional differences and the contribution rate of super-variable density (Supplementary Table 1). Figure 1 shows the changing trend of the Dagum Gini coefficient of hepatitis B incidence rate in eastern, central and western China from 2008 to 2023. As shown in Fig. 1a, The Gini coefficient exhibited an overall downward trend despite sharp fluctuations in a zigzag pattern, reaching a minimum value of 0.317 in 2014 and a maximum value of 0.386 in 2009. This indicates a narrowing of regional disparities of hepatitis B incidence rate across China, suggesting a convergence in hepatitis B incidence across the country, which may reflect the nationwide implementation of effective public health interventions such as vaccination.

Fig. 1Regional differences in Gini coefficients for hepatitis B incidence rate in China (2008–2023).

Regional differences in Gini coefficients for hepatitis B incidence rate in China (2008–2023).

Figure 1b depicts the changing trend of the Gini coefficients within three regions from 2008 to 2023. The average Gini coefficients of the eastern, western and central were 0.404, 0.311 and 0.236, respectively, indicating that regional disparities in hepatitis B incidence rate were greatest in the eastern region, followed by the western region, and smallest in the central region. Specifically, the Gini coefficients of the eastern and central regions rose from 0.326 and 0.243 in 2008 to 0.424 and 0.247 in 2023, respectively, while the Gini coefficients of the western regions fell from 0.364 to 0.288. This pattern suggests increasing regional disparities in hepatitis B incidence rate within the eastern and central regions over time, while disparities in the western region have narrowed.

Figure 1c presents the variation trend of the Gini coefficient differences between regions. The Gini coefficients of the eastern and western regions, the western and central regions both fluctuated in a zigzag pattern and showed an overall downward trend, decreasing by 16.6% and 21.4%, respectively. Conversely, the Gini coefficient of the eastern and central regions showed a 12.3% increasing trend. This pattern indicates enhanced spatial equity in hepatitis B incidence control between the western region and the eastern-central regions, while highlighting a growing disparity between the eastern and central regions themselves.

Figure 1d describes the contribution of regional differences from 2008 to 2023. The contribution rates of intraregional differences increased from 31.42 to 32.82%, whereas those of interregional differences dropped from 44.15 to 12.62%. Since 2012, the contribution rates of intraregional differences have consistently exceeded those of interregional differences, indicating that intraregional disparities represented the primary source of overall variations.

Spatiotemporal dynamic evolution of hepatitis B incidence rate

From 2008 to 2023, the standard deviation ellipse of hepatitis B incidence rate in China was predominantly situated in the eastern and central regions, with a persistent trend of southeastward migration, as shown in Fig. 2 and Table 2. Analysis of ellipse angle changes revealed all angles exceeded 90°, indicating the overall spatial distribution of hepatitis B incidence rate exhibited a southeast-northwest orientation nationwide. In terms of axis dynamics, the X-axis and Y-axis both lengthened from 2008 to 2012, signifying spatial expansion of hepatitis B incidence rate in both the southeast-northwest and northeast-southwest directions; from 2012 to 2016, the X-axis extended while the Y-axis shortened, reflecting continued expansion along the southeast-northwest axis and contraction along the northeast-southwest axis; and from 2016 to 2023, the X-axis shortened and the Y-axis lengthened, indicating a shift to spatial expansion in the northeast-southwest direction alongside contraction in the southeast-northwest direction. In terms of migration trajectory, the center of gravity shifted southeast by approximately 99.15 km from 2008 to 2012, followed by a 121.59 km southeastward movement between 2012 and 2016. From 2016 to 2020, it continued to shift southeast by 92.73 km, and from 2020 to 2023, the southeastward shift reduced to 37.78 km. The sustained southeastern migration of the gravity center aligns with the direction of China’s major internal population flows from the central and western regions to the more economically developed southeastern coastal areas, indicating at a potential link between incidence patterns and demographic movements. Notably, while the center of gravity remained within Shaanxi throughout the study period, its southeastward migration by 2023 approached the provincial boundary, indicating an emerging trend of spatial diffusion beyond Shaanxi Province.

Analysis of influencing factors of hepatitis B incidence rate based on spatiotemporal regression

Utilizing a geographically and temporally weighted regression (GTWR) model, this study explored the influencing factors of the hepatitis B incidence rate in China. Initially, eight macro-level influencing factors were selected: per capita GDP (X1), population density (X2), urbanization rate (X3), local fiscal expenditure on medical and health care (X4), number of beds in health institutions for 10,000 people (X5), per capita disposable income of all residents (X6), proportion of tertiary industry GDP (X7), and highway mileage(X8). Per capita GDP and per capita disposable income of all residents were included to capture regional and household economic capacity, which shapes healthcare access and living conditions. Population density was selected as a proxy for potential contact frequency, while urbanization rate reflected demographic transitions that alter disease exposure. Healthcare system capacity was represented by local fiscal expenditure on medical care and number of beds in health institutions for 10,000 people, both critical for prevention and treatment services. Economic structure was accounted for through the proportion of tertiary industry GDP, and highway mileage was incorporated as a key indicator of population mobility and regional connectivity, factors directly influencing pathogen spread. To ensure model robustness, all variables were standardized to eliminate scale effects. This study then conducted a comprehensive multicollinearity diagnosis using variance inflation factor (VIF) analysis (Table 3) . This identified severe collinearity between per capita GDP and per capita disposable income of all residents (VIF > 10)21, indicating redundant economic information. Consequently, these two variables were excluded to enhance model stability and avoid spurious regression, resulting in six independent explanatory variables for the final GTWR specification. In terms of goodness of fit, the GTWR model achieved a substantially higher adjusted R2 value alongside a lower Akaike’s Information Criterion (AICc) compared to the Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) benchmark. Temporal heterogeneity

Temporal heterogeneity exists in the influence of each driving factor on hepatitis B incidence rate over different periods (Fig. 5). (1) The influence of population density on hepatitis B incidence rate demonstrated a long-term negative trend with notable interannual fluctuations. Regression coefficients were predominantly negative, and the degree of dispersion increased over time, indicating that the strength of this negative association varied across years. (2) The relationship between urbanization rate and hepatitis B incidence rate exhibited a phase-dependent pattern. From 2013 to 2017, the association was positive, while in other years, it turned negative. The overall fluctuations were minimal, with regression coefficients clustering closely around zero in most years, except for some outliers during the positive-association period. (3) Local fiscal expenditure on medical and health care showed an overall negative association with hepatitis B incidence rate. Although regression coefficients fluctuated annually, the median value remained negative throughout the study period, suggesting a persistent inhibitory effect. The degree of dispersion in coefficients decreased over time, indicating growing consistency in the negative relationship. (4) The number of beds in health institutions per 10,000 people presented a distinct temporal pattern. A negative association was observed between 2013 and 2014, whereas positive associations were found in other years. The regression coefficients during positive-association years showed greater dispersion compared to the more concentrated values in 2013–2014. (5) The proportion of tertiary industry GDP exhibited a complex, intermittent relationship with hepatitis B incidence rate. It was negatively associated in 2008–2009, 2012–2014, and 2016–2017, and positively associated in other years. The coefficients during negative-association periods were more concentrated, while those during positive-association periods showed wider dispersion. (6) Highway mileage demonstrated a time-varying association with hepatitis B incidence. A positive association was evident from 2013 to 2018, while a negative association was present in other years. The regression coefficients during the positive-association phase were more dispersed, with some extreme positive values, contrasting with the relatively concentrated negative values in other years.

Spatial heterogeneity

Spatial heterogeneity exists in the influence of each driving factor on hepatitis B incidence rate over different periods (Fig. 6). (1) Population density’s positive impacts were primarily concentrated in eastern coastal areas and select western regions, with Zhejiang and Ningxia exhibiting the strongest positive effects. Negative impacts were predominantly observed in western regions, where Yunnan, Guizhou, and Guangxi showed the most pronounced negative effects. (2) Urbanization rate demonstrated both positive and negative effects in clustered distributions. Positive effects were mainly concentrated in southwestern regions and Northeastern China’s three provinces, while Henan stood as the sole province with positive effects amid contiguous areas of negative influence. Xinjiang, Guangxi, Hainan, and Fujian had the strongest positive impacts, whereas Qinghai, Gansu, and Ningxia exhibited significant negative effects. (3) Local fiscal expenditure on medical and health care showed contiguous patterns of positive and negative effects. (4) The number of beds in health institutions per 10,000 people showed positive effects across 18 provinces and negative effects in 13 provinces. (5) The proportion of tertiary industry GDP had positive effects spanning a north–south corridor across 14 provinces and negative effects in eastern and western regions covering 17 provinces. Guangxi, Anhui, and Hainan showed the strongest positive impacts, whereas Tibet, Xinjiang, and Fujian exhibited negative effects. (6) Highway mileage’s positive effects formed a contiguous north–south band across 16 provinces, with negative effects concentrated in eastern and western regions covering 15 provinces. Discussion

Based on the data from 2008 to 2023 in China, we conducted comprehensive research on the hepatitis B incidence rate, exploring regional disparities, the migration of its gravity center, the evolution of the standard deviation ellipse, spatial autocorrelation patterns, and the influence of socioeconomic factors.

With respect to individual provinces, the marked rise in Tibet may be partly attributable to improved detection of previously undiagnosed cases following the expansion of health services in remote areas, although incomplete vaccination coverage likely also played a role. The increases observed in Anhui, Shandong, and Hainan could be associated with growing population mobility and potential gaps in screening among migrant populations in these coastal provinces.

From 2008 to 2023, regional disparities in China’s hepatitis B incidence rate showed a narrowing trend, which is consistent with the timeline of China’s national vaccination strategy and targeted interventions, suggesting these policies may have played a beneficial role22. Notably, while internal differences in the western region generally decreased, those in the eastern and central regions exhibited an overall increase. The intraregional disparity followed a pattern of "central region < western region < eastern region", which may stem from the eastern region unresolved urban–rural divides23 and migrant health vulnerabilities24. In contrast, the decreasing internal disparity in the western region may be related to the progressive implementation of vaccination programs and the strengthening of maternal and child health services supported by fiscal investments in less developed areas. Additionally, intraregional differences remained the primary contributor to overall disparities in hepatitis B incidence rate. This is related to the fact that in recent years, the state has vigorously implemented various policies to narrow the development gap among the eastern, central, and western regions25.

The center of gravity of hepatitis B incidence rate migrates to the southeast, with a displacement distance of 351.25 km. This coincides with economic hubs in the southeast attracting migrants from central and western regions, which have a higher historical prevalence of hepatitis B. The influx of these populations may have contributed to a relative rise in incidence in destination provinces, thereby pulling the national gravity center of incidence toward the southeast. Though the gravity center remained within Shaanxi throughout the period, its southeastward migration by 2023 nearing the provincial boundary indicates an emerging trend of spatial diffusion beyond Shaanxi, driven by ongoing population mobility and regional disparities in disease control effectiveness. The spatiotemporal pattern of hepatitis B incidence demonstrated bidirectional expansion (2008–2012), followed by southeast-northwest extension concurrent with northeast-southwest contraction (2012–2016), and finally a reversal characterized by northeast-southwest expansion alongside southeast-northwest contraction (2016–2023).

Spatial correlation analysis indicated a weakening of agglomeration and an enhancement of divergence, signifying a transition from clustered to dispersed hepatitis B incidence patterns. This shift may be consistent with the nationwide scale-up of hepatitis B vaccination and the progressive improvement in healthcare resource allocation, which may have reduced incidence in historically high-burden areas and contributed to the dissipation of spatial clusters.

The associations observed between various socioeconomic indicators and hepatitis B incidence rate can be attributed to multiple underlying mechanisms. The negative relationships of population density, urbanization rate, and local fiscal expenditure on medical and health care with the incidence could be explained by improved access to medical resources26, enhanced public health interventions including vaccination and screening, and infrastructure development that support disease prevention27. Conversely, the positive associations of the proportion of tertiary industry GDP and highway mileage during certain periods suggest that increased population mobility28 and expansion of people-intensive sectors29 may facilitate disease transmission. The alternating positive and negative associations of hospital beds per 10,000 people reflect the dual impact of healthcare capacity, which could be linked to both improved accessibility and potential risks of healthcare-associated infections.

The spatial heterogeneity in socioeconomic impacts on hepatitis B incidence demonstrated distinct regional patterns rooted in economic structures and policy contexts. Population density clusters in eastern coastal areas exhibited positive influences, potentially linked to higher population mobility and economic activities in these developed regions30, whereas negative effects concentrated in western provinces such as Yunnan and Guizhou. In these western areas, improved healthcare access, supported by targeted fiscal investments31, may help mitigate transmission risks. Urbanization’s positive impacts in southwestern and northeastern regions align with infrastructure development in these areas32, whereas Henan’s outlier positive effect amid contiguous negative clusters suggests disparities in public health resource allocation33. Local fiscal expenditure exhibited a west–east divide: seven western provinces exhibited positive effects, which could indicate that fiscal investments into expanded vaccination and screening programs, while eastern regions showed negative effects, possibly due to resource misallocation amid higher healthcare demands. The proportion of tertiary industry GDP and highway mileage formed north–south zones with positive effects, aligning with economic integration areas where enhanced interpersonal contact facilitates viral transmission. In contrast, negative effects in eastern and western peripheral regions suggest that localized interventions, such as mobile population health management, can mitigate these risks.

The marginal contribution of this study includes at least three aspects. Firstly, this study provided a novel and comprehensive analysis of the spatiotemporal evolution of hepatitis B incidence disparities in China, integrating regional disparity decomposition, the standard deviation ellipse, and spatial autocorrelation. It uniquely quantified the narrowing trend in overall regional disparities from 2008 to 2023, identified the persistent pattern of intraregional inequality, and meticulously documented the complex trajectory of the incidence gravity center. Secondly, this research pioneered the exploration of spatially heterogeneous socioeconomic impacts on hepatitis B incidence rate across China. It revealed distinct regional patterns and underlying mechanisms, demonstrating how factors like population density, urbanization, fiscal health expenditure, tertiary industry GDP, and highway mileage exerted divergent effects in different regions. Thirdly, this study offered crucial, evidence-based insights for policymakers by explicitly linking observed disparities and spatiotemporal trends to specific mechanisms. It highlighted intraregional differences as the primary contributor to overall disparities and identified specific geographic areas requiring tailored interventions to address resource allocation gaps and mitigate transmission risks, thereby informing future targeted control strategies.

This study has certain limitations. First, there are limitations in variable selection and data coverage. Given the complex pathogenesis of hepatitis B, this study may have overlooked some potential influencing factors. Additionally, inter-provincial variations in data quality, coupled with potential underreporting of hepatitis B incidence, could compromise the accuracy of our estimates. Moreover, unobserved confounders may introduce bias, restricting the causal interpretability of the findings. Second, limitations exist in regional classification and research scale. For simplicity, we categorized China’s 31 provinces into three broad regions: eastern, central, and western. This regional classification may mask significant intra-regional heterogeneity, thereby reducing the generalizability of the results. Furthermore, the study relies on provincial-level data, and this research scale limits our ability to capture individual-level infection dynamics or behavioral factors. Hence, future research could be improved in several aspects: incorporating more specific data, such as like healthcare accessibility and migration patterns; conducting more granular classification of regional categories; and extending the research scale to the municipal level.

Conclusions

Focusing on mainland China from 2008 to 2023, this study investigated the spatiotemporal evolution of hepatitis B incidence disparities and their socioeconomic determinants. The overall regional disparities narrowed significantly, and intraregional differences, especially in the eastern and central regions, were the primary source of overall disparities. The gravity center of incidence migrated southeastward by 351.25 km, and spatial autocorrelation confirmed a transition from clustered to dispersed patterns. Socioeconomic factors exerted significant but spatiotemporally heterogeneous effects. These findings highlight that future hepatitis B prevention and control strategies should shift from one-size-fits-all national policies to region-tailored intervention measures. Priority should be given to mitigating intraregional disparities and addressing migration-related transmission risks in southeastern coastal areas. Furthermore, improving adult booster vaccination coverage among migrant populations and enhancing case surveillance capacity in remote underdeveloped regions are essential to consolidate hepatitis B prevention and control outcomes.

Early Rule Out of Myocardial Infarction With a Novel High-Sensitivity Cardiac Troponin T Assay

ABSTRACT
This cohort study investigates if a novel high-sensitivity cardiac troponin T assay with enhanced precision at very low concentrations is associated with an improvement in the ruling out of myocardial infarction (MI) with a single test.

Introduction

Symptoms of possible myocardial infarction are a common presentation to emergency departments (EDs), accounting for 4% to 6% of total attendances across multiple health care systems.1,2,3 Patients frequently require hospital admission for further investigation, but fewer than 10% have myocardial infarction.4,5,6,7 Crowding in EDs is an increasing global health care problem with important implications for patient safety.8,9,10 It is therefore a priority to develop diagnostic strategies that can rapidly, effectively, and safely identify patients at low risk of myocardial infarction who might be suitable for early discharge.

High-sensitivity cardiac troponin (hs-cTn) assays can quantify increases in cardiac troponin within 30 minutes of ischemia.11 This permits exclusion of myocardial infarction in patients with very low cardiac troponin concentrations using a single test, without requiring admission for serial testing.12,13,14,15,16,17,18,19,20 We previously evaluated an early rule-out pathway incorporating risk stratification at presentation in a randomized clinical trial, finding that implementation reduced length of stay and admission to hospital.21 International clinical practice guidelines now recommend several such pathways.22,23

Determining the cardiac troponin threshold below which patients are deemed suitably low risk for discharge from the ED is a compromise between clinical utility and safety. To maximize effectiveness, we have previously applied a systematic approach to define the highest cardiac troponin concentration that meets prespecified clinical performance criteria and identifies the greatest proportion of patients as low risk.14,15 The American College of Emergency Physicians suggests that the acceptable 30-day risk of adverse cardiovascular events is 1% to 2%, whereas the American College of Cardiology/American Heart Association recommends less than 1%.24,25,26

Current guidelines recommend a less than 5-ng/L rule-out threshold for cardiac troponin T based on the lower limit of detection of the current hs-cTnT assay (fifth generation), which meets these safety requirements.13,19,20,27 Depending on the cohort studied, 20% to 30% of patients have cardiac troponin T concentrations below the limit of detection at presentation.13,27,28 In contrast, for high-sensitivity cardiac troponin I (hs-cTnI) assays, a systematic approach has been applied to define a risk-stratification threshold based on clinical performance rather than analytical performance, which identified between 50% to 60% of patients as low risk.12,21

A novel sixth-generation hs-cTnT assay has been developed with enhanced precision at low cardiac troponin concentrations and a lower limit of detection.29,30 If the limit of detection was applied to the new assay, this enhanced precision at low concentrations would reduce further the effectiveness of this approach. Importantly, changes in calibration for the new assay mean that measurements on the sixth-generation and current-generation hs-cTnT assays are not interchangeable.

In this prospective multicenter cohort study, we aimed to systematically derive and validate a risk-stratification threshold for the sixth-generation hs-cTnT assay that identifies the highest proportion of patients at presentation who are at low risk of myocardial infarction or cardiac death. We compared the clinical performance and effectiveness of this threshold with the guideline-recommended threshold for the fifth-generation hs-cTnT assay. We then evaluated performance in an early rule-out pathway using the sixth-generation assay.

Methods

Study Design and Participants

The Point of Care Evaluation of High-Sensitivity Troponin (POC-ET) study31 was a prospective multicenter observational cohort study of patients with suspected acute coronary syndrome. The Edinburgh Medical School Research Ethics Committee approved the study, and we included all participants with adequate surplus sample for analysis in this prespecified secondary analysis of the POC-ET study. Between November 2022 and January 2025, adults 18 years or older presenting to 3 secondary and tertiary hospitals across Scotland with signs or symptoms of possible myocardial infarction were enrolled with written informed consent. To inform generalizability of the study, patients self-reported the following races and ethnicities: Asian, Black, White, and other, including multiracial and multiethnic individuals. Patients were excluded if they had electrocardiographic signs of ST-segment–elevation myocardial infarction, out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, were previously enrolled in the study, or if they were unable or unwilling to give informed consent. This study followed the Standards for Reporting of Diagnostic Accuracy (STARD) reporting guidelines.32

Venous blood samples were taken at presentation and at 1, 2, and 6 to 36 hours after presentation, either in hospital or at the patient’s home. Patients remained under the care of the clinical team with management according to local protocol, guided by measurement of cardiac troponin on the standard clinical laboratory assay (fifth-generation Elecsys Troponin T hs [Roche Diagnostics] at 2 sites or Alinity hs-cTnI [Abbott Laboratories] at 1 site). The primary outcome was myocardial infarction at index presentation (type 1 [coronary atherothrombosis], type 4b [stent thrombosis], or type 4c [in-stent restenosis]), or subsequent myocardial infarction (type 1/4b/4c) or cardiac death within 30 days.

Measurement of Cardiac Troponin

Cardiac troponin was measured in lithium-heparin plasma using the current Elecsys Troponin T hs fifth-generation assay, the novel Troponin T hs Gen 6 assay (Roche Diagnostics) and an hs-cTnI assay (Architect STAT [Abbott Diagnostics]). Samples underwent a single freeze-thaw cycle before analysis. Both hs-cTnT assays were measured simultaneously on 1 Cobas e402 instrument, and hs-cTnI was measured on a single Architect ci4100 instrument. A single reagent lot and calibration were used for each assay.

The sixth-generation hs-cTnT assay is standardized against recombinant human cardiac troponin T traceable to standard reference materials from the National Institute of Standards and Technology, rather than previous generations of the assay.29 Analytical performance and normal values reported in package inserts for each assay are summarized in eTable 1 in Supplement 1. Results were rounded to the nearest integer before analysis to reflect reporting in clinical practice.

Derivation of a Risk-Stratification Threshold for hs-cTnT (Sixth Generation)

The negative predictive value (NPV) and sensitivity for the primary outcome at presentation were calculated for cardiac troponin T concentrations from the limit of detection (LoD) to the overall 99th percentile of the sixth-generation hs-cTnT assay. The number of patients with values below each threshold was determined. All patients with a valid sixth-generation hs-cTnT result at presentation were included in the derivation.

We systematically evaluated and defined a risk-stratification threshold by selecting the highest cardiac troponin concentration at presentation that gave an NPV greater than or equal to 99.5% and sensitivity greater than or equal to 99% for the primary outcome. We compared the sensitivity, NPV, and proportion of patients identified as low risk to guideline-recommended thresholds for the fifth-generation hs-cTnT and Architect STAT hs-cTnI assays that have been validated in meta-analyses and randomized clinical trials, and implemented in clinical practice (<5 ng/L for both assays).12,13,19,21,33,34

High-Sensitivity Troponin in the Evaluation of Patients With Suspected Acute Coronary Syndrome Early Rule-Out Pathway

The High-Sensitivity Troponin in the Evaluation of Patients With Suspected Acute Coronary Syndrome (High-STEACS) early rule-out pathway has previously been described and validated for the Architect hs-cTnI and the fifth-generation hs-cTnT assays.12,13,15,16,21,27,35 We determined the performance of the pathway using the sixth-generation hs-cTnT assay incorporating a risk-stratification threshold based on clinical performance as described previously (eFigure 1 and eMethods in Supplement 1). This analysis included all patients with sixth-generation hs-cTnT results at presentation and at 1 or 2 hours. The sensitivity, specificity, NPV, and positive predictive value (PPV) for the primary outcome and proportion of high- or low-risk patients were determined at each stage of the pathway.

External Validation of a Risk-Stratification Threshold and High-STEACS Pathway

The diagnostic performance and high- and low-risk proportions for the sixth-generation hs-cTnT risk-stratification threshold and the performance of the High-STEACS pathway were externally validated in the Advantageous Predictors of Acute Coronary Events (APACE) study (eMethods in Supplement 1).17,36

Statistical Analysis

Continuous variables were summarized as median (IQR) and categorical variables as number (proportion [percentage]). Missing variables were not imputed.

As NPV and sensitivity were anticipated to approach 100%, diagnostic statistics and 95% credible intervals (CrIs) were calculated using bayesian estimates with a Jeffreys noninformative prior (a β distribution with both prior shape parameters equal to 0.5), which is robust when intervals approach 0% or 100%.37 Posterior estimates were compared on the basis of CrIs without frequentist hypothesis testing. We calculated that a sample size of 1000 patients would allow estimation of an NPV of 99.5% with 95% CrIs of 99.0% to 99.8%.

We conducted sensitivity analyses of performance of sixth-generation hs-cTnT presentation thresholds for the outcome of type 1, 4b, and 4c myocardial infarction at presentation alone, for any myocardial infarction (including type 2) or cardiac death from presentation to 30 days, and for the primary outcome within the subgroups of male and female patients.

Cardiac troponin concentrations were compared between assays with Wilcoxon signed rank test and Spearman rank correlation coefficients. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves for the primary outcome were constructed using the maximum cardiac troponin concentration for each patient. Proportions above and below thresholds were compared via 2-tailed McNemar tests, or with χ2 tests for subgroup comparisons. Hypothesis testing was conducted at an α of .05; all P values were 2-sided. All analyses were conducted from May to October 2025 using R, version 4.4.1 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing).

Results

Study Participants

The derivation cohort included a total of 987 patients (median [IQR] age, 59 [50-70] years, 376 female [38%]; 611 male [62%]), with 82 (8.3%) meeting the primary outcome (81 patients with myocardial infarction at presentation and 1 with a subsequent myocardial infarction within 30 days) (Table 1 and eFigure 2 in Supplement 1). Patients self-reported the following races and ethnicities: 27 Asian (2.7%), 8 Black (0.8%), 944 White (96%), and 8 other (0.8%). Chest discomfort was the primary symptom in 931 patients (94%), with 124 patients (13%) presenting within 2 hours of symptom onset and 200 (20%) with signs of possible myocardial ischemia on the electrocardiogram. Serial measurements were available in 918 patients (79 [8.6%] meeting primary outcome) to evaluate early rule-out pathway performance.

Comparison of Cardiac Troponin Assays

Cardiac troponin T concentrations at presentation were higher when measured with the sixth-generation assay compared with the fifth (9 [5-22] ng/L vs 7 [4-14] ng/L; P < .001; to convert to micrograms per liter, multiply by 0.001) (Figure 1 and eFigure 3 in Supplement 1). Fewer patients had cardiac troponin T concentrations below the LoD at presentation (13 [1.3%] vs 113 [11%]; P < .001) or maximal concentrations above the sex-specific 99th percentile (216 [22%] vs 269 [27%]; P < .001). With the sixth-generation assay, 55 patients (6%) were reclassified as without myocardial injury, of whom 2 patients met the primary outcome; 2 patients were reclassified as with myocardial injury.

The area under the ROC curve (AUC) for the primary outcome was similar for sixth- and fifth-generation assays (0.980; 95% CI, 0.970-0.989 vs 0.981; 95% CI, 0.973-0.990, respectively) (eFigure 4 in Supplement 1). The specificity of the sex-specific upper reference limits (URLs) for the sixth-generation hs-cTnT assay was higher (85.0%; 95% CrI, 82.6%-87.3% vs 79.4%; 95% CrI, 76.7%-82.0%), with slightly lower sensitivity (97.0%; 95% CrI, 93.4%-99.8% vs 99.4%; 95% CrI, 97.7%-100%).

The median (IQR) cardiac troponin I concentration at presentation was 3 (2-8) ng/L, with 180 patients (18.2%) below the LoD and 135 (13.7%) with maximal concentrations above the sex-specific 99th percentile (eFigures 5 and 6 in Supplement 1). The AUC for the primary outcome of the hs-cTnI assay was 0.977 (95% CI, 0.965-0.989), with the sex-specific URLs yielding a sensitivity of 91.1% (95% CrI, 84.9%-96.6%) and specificity of 93.4% (95% CrIs, 96.5%-98.9%).

Risk-Stratification Thresholds for the Sixth-Generation hs-cTnT Assay

In the derivation cohort, 601 of 987 patients (60.9%) had a sixth-generation hs-cTnT measurement less than 13 ng/L at presentation, with an NPV of 99.9% (95% CrI, 99.7%-100%) and sensitivity of 99.4% (95% CrI, 97.7%-100%) for the primary outcome (Figure 2). This was the highest threshold meeting acceptable safety criteria for the risk-stratification threshold.

Diagnostic performance was comparable with the established less than 5-ng/L risk-stratification threshold for the fifth-generation assay (NPV, 99.8%; 95% CrI, 99.3%-100%; sensitivity, 99.4%; 95% CrI, 97.7%-100%), but more patients were below the sixth-generation assay threshold (601 patients [60.9%] <13 ng/L with sixth-generation hs-cTnT assay vs 271 patients [27.5%] <5 ng/L with fifth-generation hs-cTnT assay; P < .001). The proportion of patients with a sixth-generation hs-cTnT measurement less than 13 ng/L was similar to the proportion below the established threshold of less than 5 ng/L for the hs-cTnI assay (623 patients [63.0%] <5 ng/L; P = .09) (eFigure 7 in Supplement 1). At this threshold, the hs-cTnI assay had a sensitivity of 98.2% (95% CrI, 95.4%-99.8%) and an NPV of 99.8% (95% CrI, 99.4%-100%) for the primary outcome.

In a sensitivity analysis for a combined outcome of type 1, 2, 4b, or 4c myocardial infarction or cardiac death from index presentation to 30 days (102 events), a sixth-generation hs-cTnT threshold of less than 13 ng/L had a sensitivity of 98.5% (95% CrI, 96.2%-100%) and an NPV of 99.8% (95% CrI, 99.4%-100%). When the outcome was restricted to type 1, 4b or 4c myocardial infarction on the initial presentation only, sensitivity was 99.4% (95% CrI, 97.7%-100%) and NPV was 99.9% (95% CrI, 99.7%-100%) (eTable 2 in Supplement 1).

A greater proportion of female (271 of 376 [72.1%]) than male (330 of 611 [54.0%]) patients had a sixth-generation hs-cTnT result less than 13 ng/L at presentation (P < .001). Although there were no false negatives in either subgroup, precision around the posterior estimate of sensitivity was lower in female patients (97.4%; 95% CrI, 90.0%-100%) than in male patients (99.2%; 95% CrI, 97.1%-100%).

Performance of the Early Rule-Out Pathway With the Sixth-Generation hs-cTnT Assay

The High-STEACS pathway with the sixth-generation hs-cTnT assay identified 569 of 918 patients (62.0%) as low risk, with an overall NPV of 99.9% (95% CrI, 99.7%-100%) and sensitivity of 99.4% (95% CrI, 97.6%-100%). The pathway classified 349 of 918 patients (38.0%) as high risk, with a specificity of 67.8% (95% CrI, 64.6%-70.9%) and PPV of 22.7% (95% CrI, 18.4%-27.1%) (Table 2).

When the pathway was evaluated for the hs-cTnI assay, more patients were identified as low risk (630 [68.6%]), although sensitivity was lower than with the sixth-generation hs-cTnT assay (94.4%; 95% CrI, 89.3%-98.7%) (Table 2). External Validation

The external validation cohort included 1721 patients, with 340 experiencing the primary outcome (19.7%; 328 with index myocardial infarction, 12 further with 30-day events) (eFigure 2 and eTable 3 in Supplement 1). The greater proportion below the sixth-generation hs-cTnT risk-stratification threshold was replicated; 782 patients (45.4%) had cardiac troponin T concentrations less than 13 ng/L at presentation, compared with 118 (6.9%) with cardiac troponin T concentrations less than 5 ng/L at presentation with the fifth-generation hs-cTnT assay (P < .001). Sensitivity for the primary outcome was 97.8% (95% CrI, 96.2%-99.2%), yielding an NPV of 99.0% (95% CrI, 98.3%-99.7%) in this cohort.

The High-STEACS early-rule out pathway deemed a greater proportion high risk (884 of 1721 patients [51.4%]) than in the derivation cohort, with a specificity of 60.0% (95% CrI, 57.4%-62.6%) and PPV of 37.6% (95% CrI, 34.4%-40.8%). Diagnostic performance in the validation cohort met guideline-recommended thresholds with an NPV of 99.0% (95% CrI, 98.3%-99.6%) and sensitivity of 97.5% (95% CrI, 95.8%-99.0%) (Table 2 and eTable 3 in Supplement 1). Details of low-risk patients experiencing a primary outcome are in eTable 4 in Supplement 1. As in the derivation cohort, the pathway identified a substantial proportion of patients as low risk at presentation using the sixth-generation assay (518 patients [30.1%]). A similar proportion required serial testing to definitively categorize their level of risk (441 patients [25.6%]) (eFigure 9 in Supplement 1).

Discussion

In this multicenter prospective cohort study, we derived and validated an approach to harness the enhanced assay precision of the next-generation hs-cTnT assay for the early rule-out of myocardial infarction.

Cardiac troponin T was quantifiable at low concentrations in more patients with the sixth-generation assay than the fifth-generation assay. Cardiac troponin T concentrations are highly correlated between the 2 assays but not interchangeable, as different material is used for standardization.29 Therefore, established decision thresholds based on the LoD for the current assay cannot be applied for the sixth-generation assay.

We derived a risk-stratification threshold to enable early rule-out of myocardial infarction with the sixth-generation hs-cTnT assay and validated this in an external cohort. This doubled the proportion of patients identified as low risk at presentation compared with guideline-recommended approaches using the fifth-generation hs-cTnT assay, with similar NPV and sensitivity for myocardial infarction or cardiac death at 30 days. Analytical imprecision precluded a systematic evaluation of risk-stratification thresholds based on clinical performance for the fifth-generation assay. The advance in precision with the sixth-generation assay enables quantification across a broader range of cardiac troponin values, which can now be reliably differentiated even at low concentrations; systematic evaluation of risk-stratification thresholds is now possible. The High-STEACS pathway incorporating this threshold met minimum sensitivity and NPV for adverse events recommended by national societies in both derivation and validation cohorts.25,26,38 Early identification of more patients at low risk of myocardial infarction could accelerate flow, reduce ED crowding, and improve patient experience. Implementation studies with care guided by the sixth-generation hs-cTnT assay are required to confirm safety of this approach and determine whether enhanced precision and a risk-stratification threshold based on clinical performance rather than analytical performance reduces the need for serial sampling and length of stay in practice.

Overall, the sixth-generation hs-cTnT assay showed excellent discrimination for myocardial infarction, comparable with the fifth-generation assay. One in 5 patients identified with myocardial injury by the current assay would be reclassified as without injury using the sixth-generation assay, with few experiencing a cardiovascular event. When implementing this assay, evaluating whether reducing the proportion classified with myocardial injury translates into fewer hospital admissions or impacts safety outcomes would be paramount.

Strengths and Limitations

Strengths of our study include the prospective recording of clinical data in a contemporary cohort of patients, simultaneous measurement of fifth- and sixth-generation hs-cTnT assays, blinded adjudication of index and follow-up events against a clinical reference standard, and use of conservative bayesian estimates of diagnostic performance metrics.

The primary limitation of this study is the sample size, which was not powered to generate a precise estimate of sensitivity. Risk-stratification thresholds for some current hs-cTn assays have been supported by large individual patient data meta-analyses.12,13 To address this, a meta-analysis encompassing multiple cohort studies is planned (PROSPERO CRD1268802). This will confirm our findings or help us to refine the risk-stratification strategy to deploy in practice. In particular, this will determine whether different thresholds are needed in female and male patients. Although the small number of events in our study leaves some uncertainty around the definitive approach for this assay, the finding that the risk-stratification threshold derived here performs similarly to an extensively validated hs-cTnI assay is reassuring.12,15,21 This suggests application of a risk-stratification threshold based on clinical performance with the sixth-generation hs-cTnT assay is likely to be effective and safe, though, as patient care was guided by current clinical assays, this requires confirmation in prospective implementation studies.

Our cohort was predominantly White; studies in more diverse populations are needed to evaluate sixth-generation hs-cTnT performance across different ethnic groups. Only 13% of our cohort presented within 2 hours of symptom onset, and only 38% of our cohort was female, with wide CrIs around sensitivity as only 18 cases met the primary outcome. Therefore, we could not formally evaluate performance in these key subgroups. Rule-out proportions varied when the cohort was stratified by sex; 4 female patients were incorrectly identified as low risk at presentation in the validation cohort, suggesting that sex-specific rule-out thresholds may enhance safety while maximizing efficacy. Sex-specific rule-out thresholds were previously derived for the Architect STAT hs-cTnI assay, although clinical impact was modest.39 The planned meta-analysis will provide the necessary statistical power to derive and evaluate sex-specific risk-stratification thresholds for the sixth-generation hs-cTnT assay.

Conclusions

In this cohort study, we systematically evaluated and defined a risk-stratification threshold for the sixth-generation hs-cTnT assay based on clinical performance that doubled the proportion of patients identified as low risk of myocardial infarction or cardiac death at presentation, compared with guideline-recommended approaches using the fifth-generation assay. Prospective studies are needed to confirm whether implementation of the sixth-generation hs-cTnT assay will reduce the need for serial testing and facilitate earlier discharge in low-risk patients.

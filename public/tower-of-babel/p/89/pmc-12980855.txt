Determinants of cataract types following pars plana vitrectomy: roles of underlying diseases, age, surgery duration, and silicone oil tamponade
Authors: Zewen Ren, Yan Ni, Yu Ma, Liangping Liu, Yingyan Qin, Mingxing Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTo investigate the incidence and risk factors for different types of cataracts following pars plana vitrectomy (PPV) in patients with vitreoretinal diseases.MethodsThis retrospective cross-sectional study analyzed 561 participants who underwent PPV and subsequent phacoemulsification. Lens opacity progression, including cortical cataract, nuclear sclerosis (NS) and posterior subcapsular cataract (PSC) was assessed using the Lens Opacities Classification System III (LOCS III). Logistic regression analysis and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve were employed to identify risk factors associated with significant PSC progression.ResultsAmong the 561 participants analyzed, NS was the predominant cataract type after PPV, followed by PSC and mixed cataracts. The progression of PSC was significantly higher in participants with proliferative diabetic retinopathy (PDR), polypoidal choroidal vasculopathy (PCV) and silicone oil tamponade (67.6%, 64.3% and 63.6%, respectively). Additionally, younger age and longer operative duration were identified as key risk factors, with 54.5 years determined as the cutoff age for increased PSC susceptibility by ROC analysis.ConclusionsNuclear sclerosis was the most common type of cataract after PPV. Posterior subcapsular cataract progression was associated with specific factors, including PDR, PCV, silicone oil tamponade, longer operative duration and younger age. These findings could be used to prognosticate cataract following PPV.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12886-026-04699-8.

Background

Pars plana vitrectomy (PPV) is a primary surgical approach for managing vitreoretinal diseases. Over the past decades, advances in surgical techniques have led to a transition from 17-gauge to 27-gauge instrumentation, substantially reducing surgical trauma and incision-related complications [1–3]. However, cataract development remains an almost inevitable complication, with more than 80% of patients requiring cataract surgery within two years after PPV [4, 5]. Among the various types of lens opacities, nuclear sclerosis (NS) and posterior subcapsular cataract (PSC) are most frequently observed following vitrectomy [6–8].

Nuclear sclerosis and PSC differ in both anatomy and pathogenesis [9]. The development of NS has been widely attributed to disruption of the lens’s hypoxic environment and the loss of antioxidants, which increase intraocular oxygen tension and trigger abnormal aggregation of lens proteins [10, 11]. In contrast, PSC is characterized by plaque-like opacities in the posterior subcapsular layer, often causing disproportionate visual loss at an early stage, particularly in bright conditions [12]. Its mechanism remains uncertain, only proliferation of cells migrating from the lens equator has been suggested [13].

Nevertheless, the reported incidence of PSC varies considerably, ranging from 8% to 78% [6–8, 14–20], partly due to the relatively small sample sizes of previous studies. A few studies further suggested that PSC development may be associated with retinal detachment (RD), scleral buckling, younger age [18], air tamponade (particularly when the volume exceeds 50%) [17, 18], silicone oil tamponade [21], and longer operative duration [17].

Given these inconsistencies, large-scale analyses are needed to clarify the true incidence of different cataract types after PPV and to evaluate how specific underlying diseases and surgical factors contribute to PSC development. Therefore, our study aimed to determine the distribution of cataract types following PPV and to evaluate risk factors associated with cataract types and progression.

Methods

Study participants

This retrospective cross-sectional study received approval from the Institutional Review Board (IRB)/Ethics Committee of the Zhongshan Ophthalmic Center, Sun Yat-sen University and was conducted following the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and adhered to the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology guidelines.

Clinical data from 561 participants obtained between January 2020 and March 2024 were reviewed. All participants had undergone PPV and subsequent phacoemulsification in only one eye. Inclusion criteria were: (1) age > 18 years and (2) performing phacoemulsification within 3 years after PPV. Exclusion criteria included: (1) a history of intraocular surgery other than PPV and phacoemulsification; (2) a history of intraocular surgery in the fellow eye; (3) combining scleral buckling, transscleral cryotherapy or lens touch during PPV; (4) ocular trauma, primary glaucoma and uveitis; (5) systemic autoimmune disease and cancer; (6) low-quality or missing anterior segment eye photography data; (7) total cataract and (8) requested withdrawal from the database.

Ophthalmic assessment

All participants underwent bilateral ophthalmic examinations, including intraocular pressure (IOP), ocular biometry (by IOL master 700; Carl Zeiss), slit lamp examination, and anterior segment photography under mydriasis.

Lens score, including cortical cataract (C), NS and PSC, was performed on the operated eye and fellow eye by 2 study surgeons according to the Lens Opacities Classification System III (LOCS III) [22]. The scores below 1.0 were adjusted to 1.0, as minor variations could not be distinguished from photographic differences. The lens scores of the operated eye were compared with those of the fellow eye to assess cataract progression. Scores of 3 or greater were considered clinically significant [23, 24], while an NS score of 4 or greater was classified as severe NS [12].

Data included lens scores, underlying diseases, sex, age at the time of PPV, history of diabetes and hypertension, high myopia, surgical experience of surgeon, trocar gauge (23/25 gauge), intravitreal injection of triamcinolone (TA), type of tamponade, type of postoperative position, number and total energy of laser photocoagulation, surgery duration of PPV and interval between PPV and phacoemulsification. In eyes with silicone oil tamponade, duration of tamponade, presence of emulsification and occurrence of secondary glaucoma (IOP increased by 10 mmHg compared to baseline, exceeded 24 mmHg, and persisted for 6 weeks) [25] were also included.

Statistical analysis

The mean and standard deviation (SD) were calculated for continuous variables with a normal distribution, and the median with interquartile range (IQR) for continuous variables with a non-normal distribution. The paired samples Wilcoxon signed rank test was used to analyze ordinal variables. Univariate followed by multivariate binary logistic regression and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve were used for the risk factors on changes in PSC.

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS V27.0 (IBM Corporation, Armonk), with significance set at p < 0.05 and 95% CI reported where available. Graphs were made with Prism V9.0 (GraphPad Software, San Diego).

Results

Participants characteristics

A total of 1122 eyes from 561 participants (265 men and 296 women) were included. The mean age was 56.97 ± 9.31 years during PPV surgery. The underlying diseases included epiretinal membrane (ERM, 118 eyes), RD (282 eyes), macular hole (MH, 72 eyes), retinal tear (RT, 26 eyes) (causing vitreous hemorrhage), proliferative diabetic retinopathy (PDR, 37 eyes), polypoidal choroidal vasculopathy (PCV, 14 eyes) (causing vitreous hemorrhage) and retinal vein occlusion (RVO, 12 eyes) (causing vitreous hemorrhage). The RD group included rhegmatogenous RD (RRD, 275 eyes) and MH with RD (MH-RD, 7 eyes). These MH-RD cases were included because the primary indication for PPV was retinal detachment.

The surgeries were conducted by 23 surgeons, 13 of them were chief physicians and treated 297 participants. The others were associate chief physicians and treated 264 patients. The mean interval between PPV and phacoemulsification was 1.29 ± 1.11 years. The mean PPV surgery duration was 46.57 ± 22.15 min (Table 1). Cataract progression

Nuclear sclerosis, PSC, and mixed cataracts were the most common types observed after PPV, with NS showing the highest progression overall, whereas cortical cataract progression was minimal.

Among participants with ERM, MH, RT, and RVO, NS was the predominant type, with only a few cases showing significant PSC progression (4.2%, 4.2%, 7.7%, and 8.3%, respectively). In contrast, among participants with RD, PDR, and PCV, mixed cataracts were more common, and PSC progression was notably higher (40.8%, 67.6%, and 64.3%, respectively). Risk factors for cataract progression

The progression of severe NS (score ≥ 4) and significant PSC (score ≥ 3) was evaluated using logistic regression analysis. For severe NS, older age (OR 1.096 (95% CI 1.068–1.126); p < 0.001), silicone oil tamponade (OR 4.100 (95% CI 1.791–9.386); p = 0.001) and a longer interval between PPV and phacoemulsification (OR 1.576 (95% CI 1.236–2.009); p < 0.001) were independently associated with progression (Supplemental Table 1).

In contrast, PSC progression showed a different risk profile. Significant predictors included PDR (OR 12.471 (95% CI 2.573–60.452); p = 0.002), PCV (OR 48.018 (95% CI 10.326–223.293); p < 0.001), silicone oil tamponade (OR 9.610 (95% CI 2.423–38.121); p = 0.001) and longer PPV duration (OR 2.105 (95% CI 1.003–4.417); p = 0.049). Unlike NS, younger age acted as a risk factor (OR 0.965 (95% CI 0.937–0.995); p = 0.021). Discussion

Cataract is a common complication following PPV surgery, manifesting as NS, PSC or mixed forms (NS with PSC). Although the mechanism of PSC remains unclear, evaluation of underlying diseases and treatment approaches may help identify clinically relevant risk factors. In our large cohort, cataract subtypes varied significantly by underlying diseases. Nuclear sclerosis was the most prevalent form overall, whereas PSC was more frequent in eyes with PDR and PCV. Importantly, younger age, longer operative duration, and silicone oil tamponade were identified as independent risk factors for PSC, while older age, silicone oil tamponade, and a longer interval between PPV and phacoemulsification were associated with accelerated NS progression. These findings suggest distinct pathways for different cataract subtypes and highlight that both disease type and surgical factors collectively influence cataract progression.

Previous studies have reported several risk factors for PSC after PPV. Pardo-Muñoz et al. [18] reported higher PSC frequency in eyes with RD (48.8%) and in patients younger than 50 years (73.7%), with 71.4% requiring neodymium-doped yttrium aluminum garnet (Nd:YAG) laser capsulotomy [26, 27]. Federman et al. [21] linked PSC to silicone oil tamponade, while Michels et al. [17] associated PSC with air tamponade (45%) and longer surgery (70% when exceeding three hours). Bourgeois et al. [28] also reported PSC as the most common cataract subtype in patients younger than 18 years (76.3%). However, most of these studies focused on single risk factors, lacked comprehensive multivariable analysis, and were limited by relatively small sample sizes, leaving uncertainty about the independent contribution of each factor.

In our analysis, NS was the predominant type of cataract observed in patients with ERM, MH, RT, and RVO, suggesting that NS formation in this group may be driven by oxidative stress resulting from altered intraocular oxygen tension and depletion of antioxidant reserves following surgery [10, 11]. In contrast, patients with PDR and PCV demonstrated persistent microcirculatory disturbances even after PPV. Notably, PDR patients showed sustained postoperative elevation of MCP-1 and IL-6, indicative of ongoing intraocular inflammation [29]. Such persistent inflammatory activity is likely to contribute substantially to PSC development in these cases [30]. These findings were corroborated by previous studies, where PSC progression was generally low in ERM and MH cohorts [14, 31], but exceeded 60% in those focused on PDR [8, 16, 17, 20].

Retinal detachment represented a distinct clinical subgroup with a higher rate of PSC progression. However, RD itself was not an independent risk factor for PSC in the multivariate analysis. Instead, the higher incidence of PSC observed in this group appears to be largely related to the frequent use of silicone oil tamponade in RD surgery. Our multivariable analysis confirmed that silicone oil tamponade remained an independent risk factor after adjusting for PDR and younger age. In addition, we speculated silicone oil may contribute to PSC development through inflammatory and cytotoxic effects, as studies indicated that low-molecular-weight components of silicone oil induced cell apoptosis [32] and emulsified silicone oil induces the secretion of IL-6 and IL-8 [33]. However, the precise mechanism linking silicone oil exposure to PSC formation remains unclear.

Younger age (under 50) is associated with a higher incidence of PSC [6, 18], while older age is more commonly linked to NS [4]. Our study refines these observations by identifying a specific age cutoff of 54.5 years using ROC curve analysis. We hypothesize that these differences may be due to distinct biological mechanisms. Although the exact mechanism of PSC remains unclear, it is likely related to an inflammatory response [34]. In younger patients, PSC may be driven by a stronger inflammatory response to surgical or intraocular stimuli, potentially accelerating cataract formation [35]. In contrast, in older individuals, reduced antioxidant repair and increased sensitivity to oxidative stress following procedures like PPV may contribute to progressive lens protein unfolding and aggregation, promoting NS [4, 5, 36]. Additionally, silicone oil has been associated with apoptosis in lens epithelial cells [37], which may further accelerate NS formation in older patients [38].

The contribution of PPV surgery duration to PSC development has been debated in previous studies [14, 17]. In our study, we observed a significant association between longer surgery duration and PSC, likely reflecting more complex surgical scenarios such as PDR and the frequent use of silicone oil tamponade. Although longer surgery may transiently increase intraocular inflammation [39], this alone may not fully account for PSC formation, suggesting a multifactorial mechanism involving surgical complexity and postoperative inflammatory response.

Understanding these risk factors may aid in risk stratification and preoperative assessment in patients undergoing PPV. Given the early and significant vision loss caused by PSC and the challenges of posterior capsular polishing, early lens extraction during PPV may prevent subsequent surgeries and enable faster visual recovery. Evidence suggests that early combined PPV and phacoemulsification benefits patients with PDR, not only in cases with dense cataracts [40], but also with mild opacities [41], supporting its use in selected high-risk patients. Our results highlight the association between PSC progression and the presence of the following risk factors in patients undergoing PPV: younger age, PDR, PCV, and anticipated silicone oil tamponade, which may help identify patients at higher risk for PSC progression. Moreover, factors such as older age, silicone oil tamponade, and a longer interval between PPV and phacoemulsification could accelerate NS progression.

Despite the valuable insights provided by our study, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, this retrospective, cross-sectional design relied on the fellow eye as an internal control due to the lack of standardized preoperative cataract grading before PPV. Although this approach served as a pragmatic reference for within-patient comparison, age-related cataract progression may not be entirely symmetric between eyes, and the results should be interpreted in this context. In addition, the single-center design with standardized surgical techniques, such as scleral ports consistently created 3.5 mm from the limbus and scleral depression typically performed by an assistant, limits the evaluation of variability related to different surgical systems. Finally, the relatively large odds ratios observed for PCV and RVO should be interpreted with caution due to the small number of eyes in these subgroups, which may limit the precision and stability of the estimates. Future prospective studies are warranted to further clarify the temporal and mechanistic interactions among these risk factors.

Conclusions

Our study confirmed that NS is the predominant cataract type following PPV. Posterior subcapsular cataract progression was independently associated with younger age (<54.5 years), PDR, PCV, silicone oil tamponade, and longer operative duration. These findings could be used to prognosticate cataract following PPV.

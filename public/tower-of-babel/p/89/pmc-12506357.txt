Bioinformatics analysis of the proteome in the pathway of complement and coagulation cascades in COVID-19: discovering potential biomarkers of FGB and SERPINA5
Authors: Siyuan Liu, Huichun Shi, Yuexiang Yang, Cuisong Zhu, Jing Zhou, Wenzhong Jin, Shu Song, Lijun zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe complement system, a key innate immunity component, is implicated in COVID-19 pathogenesis. Proteomic studies reveal dysregulated complement and coagulation cascades (CCC) proteins in COVID-19, yet comprehensive analyses remain limited. This bioinformatics study analyzed proteomic data (from inception to January 13, 2025) across Web of Science, PubMed, EMBASE, and Wiley Online Library using keywords “complement and coagulation cascades”, “COVID-19”, and “proteomics”. Included studies compared differentially expressed proteins (DEPs) in COVID-19 patients versus healthy controls. Two reviewers independently extracted data. DEPs were analyzed via R (v4.4.2), Stata (v14), and STRING (v12.0). Twenty studies identified 3,018 DEPs, including 58 CCC-related genes (e.g., FGB, SERPINA5, FGG, F2). Hub genes were identified using MCODE and cytohubba in Cytoscape, and 16 hub genes (e.g., FGB, SERPINA5) were found. Stata confirmed consistent expression of FGB, SERPINA5, FGG, and F2. All of them are the biomarkers of diseases, and are the drug targets except SERPINA5 according to the reports from OpenTargets database. Immunohistochemistry validated FGB upregulation and SERPINA5 downregulation. In summary, CCC pathway proteins are extensively dysregulated in COVID-19. FGB and SERPINA5 are potential diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets, offering insights into severe COVID-19 mechanisms and guiding novel treatment strategies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12879-025-11598-8.Keywords: COVID-19, Proteomics, Complement and coagulation cascades, FGB, SERPINA5

Introduction

Since its emergence in December 2019, COVID-19 has become a global pandemic, impacting millions of individuals worldwide. Although the epidemic has been subsided, it remains a public health challenge due to the continuous evolution of SARS-CoV-2 and its persistence in certain regions [1]. Many individuals continue to experience “long COVID”, which affects multiple systems, including the respiratory, gastrointestinal, musculoskeletal, nervous, and endocrine systems [2]. Understanding the mechanisms and finding new biomarkers of SARS-CoV-2 infection remains crucial.

In our prior proteomic analysis of lung tissues from COVID-19 patients, we observed dysregulation of proteins in the complement and coagulation cascades (CCC) pathway compared to adjacent tissues [3]. During infection, the cytokine storm triggers excessive inflammation, complement-mediated endothelial damage, and widespread microvascular thrombosis [4, 5]. Proteins in CCC pathway are involved in these activities [6]. To systematically investigate these proteins, we reviewed proteomic studies on COVID-19 patients, identifying differentially expressed proteins (DEPs) associated with the CCC pathway. These DEPs were extracted from existing literature, integrated, and analyzed using bioinformatics tools. Two key hub genes, FGB (fibrinogen beta chain) and SERPINA5 (plasma serine protease inhibitor, serpin A5), were further validated through immunohistochemistry. This study aims to enhance the understanding of the molecular mechanisms of COVID-19 and explore potential biomarkers and therapeutic strategies.

Materials and methods

Search strategy

This study adhered to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews (PRISMA) guidelines [7]. A comprehensive search was conducted across four databases—PUBMED, Web of Science (WOS), EMBASE, and WILEY Online Library—using the keywords of ‘complement and coagulation cascades’, ‘COVID-19’, and ‘proteomics’. In PUBMED, these terms were searched across all fields. In WOS, they were restricted to titles, abstracts, and indexing. For EMBASE, filters included full text, human studies, COVID-19, English language, and exclusion of preprint records. In WILEY Online Library, only full articles were exported to EndNote, excluding abstracts, guidelines, supplements, oral sessions, posters, issue information, talks, and books. References from relevant studies were also screened. Initial screening of the title and abstracts of all papers was performed independently by two authors (Siyuan Liu, Huichun Shi). Based on relevance criteria, they selected full-text articles for detailed evaluation. Final inclusion decisions were made by consensus between the two reviewers. Any disagreements arising during this phase were resolved through adjudication by a third reviewer (Lijun ZHANG). The study selection process, following PRISMA 2020, was summarized in Fig. 1.

Eligibility criteria

The inclusion criteria for articles were as follows: (a) studies involving proteome profiling and quantification using mass spectrometry; (b) research conducted on adult human samples; (c) comparisons between COVID-19 patients and healthy controls; and (d) primary data clearly presenting differential groups, with DEPs that could be extracted or recalculated.

Studies focusing solely on the effects of specific treatments in COVID-19 patients or only having samples from the treated patients were excluded. Additionally, conference abstracts, case reports, case series, letters, editorials, guidelines, theses, commentaries, reviews, systematic reviews, brief communications, and technical notes were not considered. Articles retrieved from the search were managed using Endnote (version 21).

Data extraction and risk of bias assessment

DEPs were identified using the following thresholds: a fold change of ≤ 0.667 or ≥ 1.500 and a p-value < 0.05. For DEPs identified in differential expression comparisons from references 16 and 20, only those common to all comparisons were extracted and their average fold changes were used for functional enrichment analysis. For meta-analysis, the data from each comparison were used. Two reviewers independently extracted data in accordance with PRISMA guidelines, resolving discrepancies through discussion. Data management and analysis were performed using R Studio (version 4.4.2).

Functional enrichment analysis

Genes associated with the CCC pathway were retrieved from the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) database (http://www.genome.jp/kegg/genes.html) and matched with DEPs using R software. The matched DEPs within the CCC pathway were analyzed using STRING software (version 12.0), with a confidence threshold set at a Kappa score of 0.7 and a false discovery rate (FDR) below 0.05. A protein-protein interaction (PPI) network was constructed using Cytoscape v3.10.0-BETA1 software [8], with the matched CCC pathway genes serving as nodes.

The Molecular Complex Detection (MCODE) plugin [9] was used to identify submodules within the PPI network generated by Cytoscape. Parameters included a degree cutoff of 2, node score cutoff of 0.2, k-core of 2, max depth of 100, and a minimum of 4 genes per module. Hub genes were identified using the cytoHubba plugin [10], employing ranked nodes and the maximal clique centrality (MCC) algorithm.

Meta-Analysis

Hub genes reported in at least two studies were included and analyzed by the randomeffects metaanalysis using Stata 14.0 (Stata Software, USA). Log2 Fold Change (Log2 FC) and negative Log P-value (-Log P) were extracted from each study, with Log2 FC indicating difference between the COVID-19 and healthy controls, and –Log P reflecting statistical significance. The weighted average method was applied to compute the meta-analysis effect size, where Log2 FC values were weighted by their corresponding –Log P to prioritize studies with higher statistical significance.

The 95% confidence intervals (CIs) of the combined estimates were derived based on these weights. Forest plots were generated to visualize the expression trends and statistical robustness of each gene across studies. Between-study heterogeneity was assessed using the I² statistic, and a pooled estimate was considered statistically significant if its 95% CI did not include zero (0).

Potential drug target analyses

To identify promising therapeutic candidates, we evaluated the druggability of nine hub genes utilizing the Open Targets Platform [11]. Disease associations and existing pharmacological compounds were subsequently examined.

Immunohistochemical validation of FGB and SERPINA5 expression

The experimental procedures were approved by ethics committee of Shanghai Public Health Clinical Center (Protocol No. 2023-S077-01, approved on July 4, 2023). Six patients with COVID-19, and seven patients with lung cancer were involved (Supplement Table 1 (Table S1)). Lung tissue specimens from both COVID-19 patients and healthy controls (adjacent normal tissues from the patients with lung cancer) were processed for immunohistochemical analysis. Tissue sections were treated with specific primary antibodies: fibrinogen beta chain [EPR19765] (ab193932, ABcam, Cambridge, UK) (1:400 dilutions) and Serpin A5 [EPR23337-112] (ab270451) (1:400 dilutions). Subsequent staining and imaging procedures were performed using the automated BOND RX Research Stainer (Leica Biosystems, Illinois, United States). Then we performed semi-quantitative assessment of FGB and SERPINA5 expression levels in pulmonary specimens through counting positive cells, ten random microscopic fields were captured per section at 400× magnification. Quantitative analysis involved enumerating immunoreactive cells within each field. Protein expression alterations were determined by calculating the ratio between mean positive cell counts from 60 microscopic fields (6 COVID-19 cases) and 70 fields (7 control cases).

Furthermore, we also quantified the expression of FGB and SERPINA5 using optical density measurement. Slide imaging was performed with the TissueFAXS 200 system (TissueFAXS Viewer software, Tissue Gnostics, Vienna, Austria), followed by quantitative analysis using HistoQuest software (version 6.0.1.114). Through systematic parameter optimization, we enhanced three critical analytical components: total slide area demarcation, positive cell identification, and background signal subtraction. Gene expression levels were calculated as the mean intensity of positive cells.

Results

Systematic literature review

The initial database search identified 235 records, supplemented by 3 manually included references [3, 12, 13]. Following duplicate removal through EndNote 21 (n = 32), title and abstract screening excluded 154 publications based on predefined criteria: non-proteomic studies, non-COVID-19 research, animal/cell experiments, pediatric populations, and review articles. Full-text evaluation of the remaining 51 publications resulted in the final inclusion of 20 studies for systematic analysis (Fig. 1, Table 1).

A total of 3,018 DEPs meeting the threshold of 1.5-fold change (p < 0.05) were initially identified. Proteins that did not have a matching gene name were summarized based on their Uniprot ID. The final dataset comprised 2,352 unique genes for subsequent analysis.

Genes in the CCC pathway

Through comparative analysis of 20 publications against the CCC pathway’s 88-gene repertoire, we identified 58 overlapping genetic markers with detailed expressions presented in Table S2. Of these 58 genes, 30 were detected in at least two articles, including eight demonstrating conserved upregulation, five showing consistent downregulation, and 17 exhibiting discordant regulatory patterns across different investigations. These 58 genes exhibited broad representation across all five complement pathways (extrinsic, intrinsic, classical, lectin and alternative pathway) (Figure S1). Furthermore, we also analyzed these 58 genes using STRING database, and found that the most significantly enriched KEGG pathway was the CCC pathway, followed by Staphylococcus aureus infection, pertussis, and systemic lupus erythematosus (Fig. 2 A). Protein-protein interaction analysis revealed strong connectivity between these genes (Fig. 2B).

Hub genes analysis performed by plugins in cytoscape

Using the MCODE plugin in Cytoscape, two top modules were identified: module 1 (14 nodes, 40 edges) included six consistently downregulated and four upregulated genes in COVID-19 compared to healthy controls (Fig. 2 C), while module 2 (6 nodes, 15 edges) comprised three consistently downregulated and one upregulated gene (Fig. 2D). In module 1, the top ten hub genes identified by highest connectivity (through cytoHubba plugin) were F7, F9, F8, THBD, VWF, TFPI, F5, FGG, FGB, and SERPINF2 (Fig. 2E), while in module 2, SERPINA5, SERPINC1, PROC, FGA, F2, and F10 hub genes were involved (Fig. 2 F). Detailed characteristics of these 16 hub genes are summarized in Table S2.

Among these 16 genes, two genes (FGB, FGG) were reported six times, one (SERPINA5) four times, and one (F2) three times, all with consistent expression trends in different literatures (Table 2). Other genes exhibited lower recurrence rates or inconsistent expression patterns, including one gene reported in five studies (upregulated in four and downregulated in one), two genes in three studies, two genes in two studies, and seven genes in a single study, as detailed in Table S2.

Nine hub gene expression revealed by meta-analysis

Hub genes identified in ≥ 2 independent studies (Table 2) underwent systematic meta-analysis. Figure 3 A demonstrates that four consensus hub genes (F2, SERPINA5, FGB, and FGG) maintained statistically significant differential expression (p < 0.05) between COVID-19 patients and healthy controls across heterogeneous cohorts. Specifically, the Log₂ FC values for F2 and SERPINA5 were − 1.59 (95% CI: −2.09, −1.09) and − 1.11 (95% CI: −1.27, −0.94), respectively, indicating downregulation. In contrast, FGB and FGG were upregulated, with Log₂ FC values of 1.31 (95% CI: 0.86, 1.77) and 1.38 (95% CI: 0.93, 1.82), respectively. Notably, the absence of between-study heterogeneity (I² = 0.0%) observed for F2 and SERPINA5 demonstrates absolute consistency in differential expression patterns across all analyzed cohorts.

Among the five genes with inconsistent expression patterns (FGA, PROC, F5, F9, and VWF), Fig. 3B highlights their regulation trends. FGA exhibited overall upregulation with a Log₂ FC of 0.76 (95% CI: 0.24, 1.27), whereas VWF showed downregulation with a Log₂ FC of −0.51 (95% CI: −0.92, −0.10). Although the combined effects for these two genes are statistically significant, their high I² values of 88.4% and 93.7% indicate substantial heterogeneity across studies. The heterogeneity for these gene expression may be largely influenced by factors such as study design, sample type, sample size, or data analysis methods. Therefore, despite statistical significance, the biological reproducibility of these findings should be interpreted with caution. In the further, it is necessary to verify these expressions by different methods.

Evaluating the druggability profile of four central hub genes

Leveraging the Open Targets database, we systematically assessed the disease associations of the four different hub genes (F2, FGB, FGG, and SERPINA5) (Table 2). All genes were related to diseases. For example, FGB and FGG exhibited strong links to coagulation disorders, including congenital afibrinogenemia, familial afibrinogenemia, and familial dysfibrinogenemia. Similarly, F2 was implicated in congenital prothrombin deficiency, factor II deficiency, and thrombophilia via thrombin dysfunction. While, SERPINA5 demonstrated associations with quantitative blood protein measurements (e.g., sex hormone-binding globulin and serum albumin levels). For targeted drug analysis, FDA-approved anticoagulants (fibrinogen/plasminogen-based therapies) targeted FGB/FGG, and direct thrombin inhibitors (lepirudin, dabigatran, bivalirudin) targeted F2. However, no clinically approved drugs currently target SERPINA5, highlighting its unexplored therapeutic potential (Table 2).

FGB and SERPINA5 were verified by immunohistochemical analysis

Despite prior reports of dysregulated FGB (upregulated in six studies) and SERPINA5 (downregulated in four studies) in COVID-19, empirical validation through gold-standard protein detection methods (e.g., IHC, western blot, ELISA) remained absent. To address this methodological gap, we performed IHC validation in COVID-19 lung tissues versus healthy controls. Anti-Fibrinogen beta chain staining revealed pronounced FGB accumulation in patient tissues (Fig. 4 A) compared to that in the controls (Fig. 4B). Conversely, SERPINA5 exhibited marked depletion in COVID-19 samples (Fig. 4 C) relative to the levels in healthy lungs (Fig. 4D). Quantitative histomorphometric assessment at 400× magnification confirmed the differential expression profiles: FGB-positive cells exhibited a 1.5-fold elevation in COVID-19 patients (161 ± 97 vs. 107 ± 40 in controls; p < 0.0001, Fig. 4E), whereas SERPINA5 immunoreactivity displayed a 1.3-fold decrease (111 ± 45 vs.144 ± 63 p < 0.01, Fig. 4 F). Furthermore, we also quantified FGB and SERPINA5 by optical density method, and found that FGB was upregulated for 1.4-fold (Fig. 4G, while SEPRINA5 decreased for 1.9-fold (Fig. 4H). These orthogonal validation bridges proteomic predictions with histopathological evidence, resolving prior discrepancies in experimental confirmation.

Discussion

Despite successful containment of the COVID-19 pandemic via global vaccine deployment and natural immunity acquisition, the virus remains a persistent source of severe morbidity, with age- and comorbidity-dependent progression to fatal outcomes. Current epidemiological data reveal 12–15% of hospitalized patients develop life-threatening complications, while 30% of survivors exhibit post-acute syndrome beyond six months. These unresolved clinical trajectories necessitate: (1) systems-level elucidation of viral-host interaction networks, and (2) discovery of stage-specific biomarkers to guide precision management strategies.

The complement system serves as a cornerstone of innate immunity, with its dysregulation emerging as a key mechanistic link in COVID-19 progression from mild to severe phenotypes [33]. Clinical evidence supports complement-targeted interventions, including C1 esterase inhibitors [34], and compstatin-based C3 inhibitors [35], demonstrating therapeutic efficacy in severe cases. This pathophysiological axis is further reinforced by multi-omics studies identifying extensive COVID-19-associated perturbations in CCC proteins [3, 14, 18, 22]. Building on our prior discovery of eight upregulated CCC components (FGA, FGB, FGG, ITGB2, ITGAM, ITGAX, PLAUR, and VSIG4) in COVID-19 lung tissue [3], we conducted a systematic review of 20 proteomic studies, revealing dysregulation in 65.9% (58/88) of CCC pathway genes. Notably, all five complement activation routes (classical, lectin, alternative, intrinsic, and extrinsic pathways) exhibited significant protein-level disturbances, suggesting complement system involvement in SARS-CoV-2 pathogenesis. These findings demonstrate pan-regulatory dysregulation of the complement-coagulation axis in COVID-19 pathogenesis, suggesting that precision targeting of these molecular nodes (e.g., with monoclonal antibodies or small-molecule inhibitors) could rebuilding immune-thrombotic homeostasis in critically ill patients.

To discover the key regulators in the CCC pathway, we performed an integrative bioinformatics analysis using cytoscape with MCODE and cytoHubba plugins, and identified 16 high-confidence hub genes across two functionally distinct modules (MCODE1 and MCODE2). Cross-platform validation revealed four consistently dysregulated hub genes—FGB, FGG, F2, and SERPINA5, which exhibited conserved differential expression patterns in COVID-19 pathogenesis compared with the healthy. Of them, FGB and FGG are upregulated in COVID-19. Proteolytic processing of FGB by thrombin generates fibrin monomers that undergo structural co-polymerization with FGA/FGG subunits, forming cross-linked fibrin matrices essential for hemostatic plug formation. FGB upregulation might contribute to COVID-19–related pulmonary fibrosis through crosstalk with the TGF-β signaling pathway [36]. As a key fibrin precursor, elevated FGB can enhance TGF-β-induced myofibroblast activation and recruitment, leading to excessive extracellular matrix (ECM) deposition and tissue remodeling [37]. TGF-β1 not only directly promotes the expression of profibrotic genes, such as collagen and fibronectin, but also indirectly exacerbates pathology by inducing endothelial cell apoptosis and driving glomerular and interstitial fibrosis [38]. Consequently, increased FGB expression may aggravate lung injury and fibrosis in severe COVID-19 cases. A previous study showed that all variants of SARS-CoV-2 infections exhibited the capacity to provoke fibrin deposition in human lung epithelial cells [39]. Another two hub genes were F2 and SERPINA5 that were downregulated in COVID-19. Studies indicate reducing F2 to 10% of normal levels resulted in 100% mortality in the mice infected with lymphocytic choriomeningitis virus clone 13 [40]. SERPINA5 downregulation may weaken host antiviral defense. This serine protease inhibitor regulates coagulation, inflammation, and host-pathogen interactions [41]; its reduced expression could result in uncontrolled protease activity, impaired epithelial barrier integrity, and enhanced viral replication. Previous studies indicate that SERPINA5 contributes to antibacterial defense and resistance to HIV-1 infection, suggesting a potential protective role against SARS-CoV-2 [42]. Therefore, we hypothesized that these four hub genes (FGG, FGB, F2, and SERPINA5) influenced COVID-19 pathogenesis via the extrinsic CCC pathway. Upregulated FGG and FGB likely promoted fibrotic lung damage and inflammatory activation of the extrinsic coagulation pathway, while reduced F2 and SERPINA5 levels may enhance SARS-CoV-2 infection and impair antiviral responses.

We further analyzed the druggability of the four hub genes (FGG, FGB, F2, and SERPINA5). According to the OpenTargets database, only SERPINA5 is not currently a target of any known FDA-approved drug. Despite this, numerous studies report decreased SERPINA5 expression in multiple cancers, including hepatocellular carcinoma [43], ovarian cancer [44], and breast cancer [45]. Furthermore, low SERPINA5 expression is associated with worse survival in breast cancer [45]. Therefore, although no known drug targeting SERPINA5 was identified in the OpenTargets database, these findings support its potential as a cancer biomarker.

This study has several limitations. First, proteomic data were compiled from multiple studies utilizing diverse samples and methodologies, potentially introducing heterogeneity. Second, the patient cohorts varied in disease severity, vaccination status, comorbidities, and infecting SARS-CoV-2 variants; we did not analyze the potential influence of these factors. Third, while we performed meta-analysis on hub genes identified in two or more independent studies, only two genes (FGB and SERPINA5) were ultimately verified. Finally, the specific functions of FGB and SERPINA5 in COVID-19 patients were not experimentally investigated.

Conclusion

Our bioinformatic analysis of CCC pathway proteins discovered 58 DEPs mapping to 88 genes. Four hub genes (FGB, FGG, F2, and SERPINA5) exhibited recurrent dysregulation in COVID-19 vs. healthy controls across ≥ 2 studies. FGB and SERPINA5 were validated via meta-analysis and immunohistochemistry as potential diagnostic biomarkers/therapeutic targets. Findings confirm prior proteomic reports, advancing understanding of COVID-19 pathogenesis and identifying biomarkers for diagnosis and treatment.

Empagliflozin modulates microRNA expression in human primary cardiomyocytes under CoCl2-induced hypoxia
Authors: Marek Samec, Ivana Baranova, Katarina Dibdiakova, Iryna Samec, Matej Samos, Martin Jozef Pec, Renata Pecova, Martin Pec
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCardiovascular diseases (CVD) remain the leading cause of premature death worldwide, with a steadily rising incidence. Ischemia, characterized by insufficient blood flow and subsequent tissue hypoxia, contributes significantly to cellular damage, dysfunction, as well as epigenetic alterations in CVD. Hypoxia-induced epigenetic changes have been associated with fibrosis, inflammation, metabolic dysregulation, and altered programmed cell death in cardiac tissue. Empagliflozin, a sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitor primarily prescribed for type 2 diabetes, has also demonstrated beneficial effects in patients with CVD.MethodsThe present study aimed to evaluate the protective role of Empagliflozin in primary human cardiomyocytes under chemically induced hypoxia, with a focus on its influence on specific microRNAs. Cardioprotective effects were analyzed through the expression of miR-214-3p, miR-22-5p, miR-103-5p, and miR-145-5p, along with protein levels of GAPDH and HIF1α.Results and conclusionAfter 24 h of Empagliflozin treatment, a significant upregulation of the cardioprotective miR-214-3p (p < 0.05) was observed. These findings demonstrate a direct cardioprotective effect of Empagliflozin in cobalt-treated cardiomyocytes and suggest its potential role as an epigenetic modulator in the pathogenesis of CVD.

Introduction

Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) are the leading cause of premature death and disability worldwide [1]. These pathological states include cerebrovascular disease, peripheral arterial disease, coronary heart disease, rheumatic, as well as congenital heart disease [2]. In 2021, there were about 20.5 million documented CVD-related deaths, which comprised approximately one-third of all global deaths [3]. The most common cause of CVD-related mortality is ischemic heart disease, which accounts for most of these cases. Ischemia is defined as an inadequate blood supply to the myocardium due to a lack of oxygen. This can lead to conditions such as stable angina, acute coronary syndrome, and silent myocardial ischemia [4]. The heart, as a complex organ, comprises several specialized types of cells, including cardiomyocytes and non-myocyte cells such as endothelial cells, immune system cells, and fibroblasts [5]. Cardiomyocytes, the muscle cells of the heart, play a crucial role in the development and progression of CVD. In heart failure, the heart is generally unable to regenerate lost or damaged cardiomyocytes, resulting in insufficient cardiac output [6]. Pathological conditions, such as ischemia or hypoxia, are the most common and significant causes of injury to cardiomyocytes [7]. Among the mechanisms associated with the development and progression of CVD at the molecular level are altered energy metabolism, dysregulation of signalling pathways, and increased oxidative stress, ultimately leading to cell death or injury [8]. All these processes can be driven by epigenetic regulation. Epigenetic mechanisms can modify gene expression without altering the underlying DNA sequences, thereby impacting pathways involved in CVD-related processes [9]. Current evidence suggests that epigenetic mechanisms, including microRNA (miRNA) expression, play a crucial role in cardiovascular homeostasis and may contribute to preventing hypoxic/ischemic conditions [10]. Certain miRNAs, known as “hypoxamirs,” can prevent severe cellular damage from low oxygen levels by fine-tuning the body's adaptive responses to oxygen deprivation [11]. In addition, these miRNAs are involved in regulating the expression of both anti- and pro-apoptotic genes, as well as in controlling the survival of cardiomyocytes [12]. Numerous functional studies have investigated the cardioprotective effects of specific miRNAs, demonstrating their significant clinical potential in the diagnosis and treatment of CVD [13]. A single miRNA can affect multiple gene targets, thereby influencing the entire gene network. MiRNA modulation may therefore represent a promising strategy to protect the heart against ischemic or hypoxic conditions [14].

Empagliflozin is a potent inhibitor of sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2), typically used to improve glycaemic control in adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) [15]. Its unique mechanism of action involves inhibiting glucose and sodium reabsorption in the proximal tubule of the kidney. Beyond its glucose control, Empagliflozin has been shown to have significant cardiorenal protective properties in randomized, placebo-controlled studies [15]. This drug has been shown to reduce hospitalizations for heart failure by approximately 30% and decrease cardiovascular mortality, irrespective of diabetes status. Several studies have confirmed the role of SGLT2 inhibitors in significantly improving both left and right ventricular structure and function, particularly in patients with reduced ejection fraction [16,17]. Although the substantial benefits of Empagliflozin were documented in these studies, its direct mechanism of action on the cardiovascular system remains unclear [18].

The main aim of our study was to evaluate the impact of Empagliflozin on primary human cardiomyocytes under hypoxic conditions using cobalt (CoCl2). We focused on epigenetic processes, specifically the altered expression of selected miRNAs following treatment with Empagliflozin.

Materials and methods

Experimental model

For the evaluation of Empagliflozin's (MedChemExpress, Princeton, USA, Cat. No. HY-15409) regulatory effect, we utilized Human Cardiac Myocytes (Merck, Darmstadt, Germany, Cat. No. C-12810). The cells were incubated under routine conditions, at 37 °C in an atmosphere containing 5% CO2. The cardiomyocytes were cultivated in a medium supplemented according to the manufacturer's guidelines. The effect of Empagliflozin was assessed after exposing the cell culture to a final concentration of 0.5 μM Empagliflozin for 24 and 48 h, respectively. The experimental concentration was selected based on previous studies, where the higher recommended clinical dose of EMPA (25 mg) produces a peak plasma concentration of approximately 500 ng/mL, which is close to the physiological levels observed in treated patients [19]. To mimic hypoxia, the cells were exposed to CoCl2 at a final concentration of 200 μM for 24 and 48 h. To assess EMPA effects under both control and pathological conditions, experiments were conducted with three biological replicates for each exposure duration.

Analysis of miRNA expression

Total RNA was extracted from cells using a commercial miRVana microRNA isolation kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA, Cat. No. AM1560), following the manufacturer's instructions. The RNA concentration was measured using a NanoDrop ND-2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific). To perform RT-qPCR analysis, at least 10 ng/μl of RNA was required as an input. The isolated total RNA was stored at −80 °C until further use. Reverse transcription was realized using the TaqMan Advanced miRNA cDNA Synthesis Kit (Applied Biosystems, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cat. No. A28007). We used miRNA-specific TaqMan Advanced miRNA Assay Kits (Applied Biosystems, Cat. No. A25576) for the quantification of miR-145-5p, miR-22-5p, miR-214-3p, miR-103a-5p, miR-21-5p, miR-424, miR-588, miR-182-5p, and miR-155-3p. MiR-34a-5p served as the internal control for normalization. It was selected using NormFinder software [20] due to its highest expression stability among nine candidate miRNAs, with a stability value of 0.006. The reactions were performed on the FX96 real-time PCR detection system (Bio-Rad Laboratories, Hercules, CA, USA) in a 10-μl volume. To ensure reproducibility, we conducted three independent experiments, each with three technical replicates. Fold changes were calculated using the standard ΔΔCq method.

Western blot analysis

Samples used for western blot analysis were obtained from incubated cells using CHAPS lysis buffer (Thermo Scientific™ CHAPS Detergent (3-((3-cholamidopropyl) dimethylammonio)- 1-propanesulfonate), Cat. No. 28300) supplemented with 1x protease inhibitor cocktail (Halt™ Protease Inhibitor Cocktail Thermo Scientific TM, Cat. No. 78425). 30 μg of proteins loaded per lane were separated on 8% SDS-polyacrylamide gels (PAGE) under reducing conditions. Proteins after electrophoresis separation were transferred to nitrocellulose membranes with a 0,2 μm pore size by using semi-dry transfer (Trans-Blot® Turbo™ Transfer System, Bio-Rad). Membranes were incubated for at least 1 h in 2% BSA blocking solution and probed with antibodies specific to HIF1α Polyclonal antibody (1:1000) (Cat. No. 20960-1-AP) and GAPDH (1:500) (Cat. No. sc-166574). After subsequent incubation with secondary antibodies (dilution 1:10,000) (Cat. No. A9044 anti-Mouse IgG for GAPDH; Cat. No. sc-2357 anti-Rabbit IgG for HIF1α), immunopositive signals were visualized using Clarity Western ECL Substrate on a Chemidoc XRS imaging system (Bio-Rad). Signal intensities were normalized based on total protein levels detected by Ponceau S staining and quantified by Image Lab software (Bio-Rad).

Statistical analysis

The Kruskal–Wallis one-way ANOVA was used to evaluate the effect of Empagliflozin on miRNA expression in normal and pathological hypoxic conditions. In cases where significant differences were detected, post hoc multiple comparison tests were applied to determine differences between specific groups. All statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism 8.1.1 (GraphPad, San Diego, CA, USA). A p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

The effect of Empagliflozin on cardiomyocytes was examined in vitro under simulated physiological and pathological conditions. The drug's impact on primary cardiomyocytes was evaluated by analysing the expression of specific miRNAs and the protein levels of GAPDH and HIF1α, which served as indicators of a hypoxic phenotype.

Impact of empagliflozin on HIF1α and GAPDH expression in hypoxic conditions

The effect of Empagliflozin on the protein levels of hypoxia-related (HIF1α) and glycolysis-related (GAPDH) markers was assessed after a 24-h incubation, with or without Empagliflozin, under both normoxic and hypoxic conditions. Cobalt significantly increased HIF1α protein levels compared to the control group. However, the addition of Empagliflozin to cells treated with cobalt almost completely prevented the increase of HIF1α (Fig. 1). Discussion

Myocardial ischemia is characterized by reduced blood flow and oxygen supply to the heart muscle, leading to cardiomyocyte dysfunction. This condition involves molecular pathways like HIF1 signaling, a crucial pathway that mediates the adaptive metabolic response to hypoxia [21]. Under low oxygen conditions, HIF1 promotes many processes, such as angiogenesis, mitochondrial metabolism and function, or glycolysis by increasing the expression of enzymes like GAPDH. Epigenetic mechanisms, including regulation based on non-coding RNAs, are crucial in controlling cardiovascular genes and appear early in disease development and progression [22]. Recent studies have shown that these markers are associated with critical cardiovascular pathways related to the progression of CVD [9,23,24]. Empagliflozin, a T2DM drug, shows cardioprotective effects by reducing ischemia/reperfusion injury, although its exact mechanism remains unclear [25]. Beyond its primary role in regulating glucose uptake, glucose dysregulation is associated with increased oxidative stress and inflammation, key drivers of cardiovascular disease progression. This is reflected by elevated markers of metabolic stress, such as the triglyceride–glucose (TyG) index. These observations support the hypothesis that Empagliflozin may exert cardioprotective effects not only through metabolic and hemodynamic improvements but also via anti-oxidative and anti-inflammatory pathways [26,27]. Emerging evidence suggests it may act through miRNA regulation, particularly under hypoxic conditions. In our study, we evaluated the cardioprotective role of Empagliflozin in a chemically induced model of hypoxia using cobalt in human primary cardiomyocytes. For this purpose, we analyzed changes in miRNA expression, including miR-22-5p, miR-103-5p, miR-214-3p, miR-145-5p, miR-21-5p, miR-424, miR-588, miR-182-5p, and miR-155-3p. The selected cohort of tested miRNAs was chosen based on their reported involvement, either directly or indirectly, in the regulation of the hypoxic phenotype, metabolic reprogramming toward glycolysis driven by HIF1α stabilization, and oxidative stress and inflammatory responses associated with oxygen deprivation [[28], [29], [30], [31]].

HIF1α plays a key role during hypoxia by regulating the expression of targeted genes, including miRNA [32]. Evidence suggests a critical role for HIF1α short-term activation in cardioprotection against apoptosis in a model of myocardial infarction. Conversely, the long-term or excessive expression of HIF1α can lead to detrimental outcomes, such as promoting apoptosis mediated in a p53-dependent manner or through maladaptive metabolic shifts. This state can subsequently result in pathological conditions like hypertrophic cardiomyopathy or heart failure [33]. To date, only one study has reported a reduction in HIF1α levels following treatment with Empagliflozin, either alone or in combination with other agents. In the study by Dogan et al., the authors used methotrexate to induce cardiotoxicity in H9C2 cardiomyocytes. They found that EMPA alone, or combined with sacubitril/valsartan, reduced oxidative stress and hypoxia in the tested model system via decreasing HIF1α [34]. Similarly, in our study, we observed that Empagliflozin has a protective effect on cardiomyocytes under hypoxic conditions, which is linked to a decrease in HIF1α protein levels. There is no further evidence that Empagliflozin reduces the stability of HIF1α in a hypoxic model of CVD. Interestingly, other gliflozins, including Luseogliflozin, Dapagliflozin, and Canagliflozin, have also shown potential in decreasing HIF1α stability in different cell lines under pathological conditions, such as proximal tubular cells (HRPTECs, HK-2) or cardiomyocytes (HL-1) cells [35,35,36]. Increased HIF1α activation results in GAPDH upregulation as a mechanism to promote glycolysis under low levels of oxygen [37]. Interestingly, our observations showed only a slight decrease in GAPDH protein levels between groups during oxygen deprivation, regardless of the presence of Empagliflozin in the medium, although HIF1α levels decreased notably after Empagliflozin treatment. The higher level of GAPDH can be explained by HIF1α-independent effects mediated by cobalt. In this state, GAPDH expression can also be induced independently of HIF1α by oxidative stress or energy homeostasis signaling [38,39].

Hypoxia, or low oxygen levels, directly alters the expression of many miRNAs that play crucial roles in cellular adaptation, mediated by factors like HIF1α [32]. Certain miRNAs, including miR-145-5p, miR-22-5p, miR-103a-5p, and miR-214-3p, have been documented to protect cardiomyocytes from damage by reducing apoptosis and hypertrophy [29,[40], [41], [42]]. In our study, we detected a significantly increased level of miR-214-3p in cardiomyocytes under hypoxic conditions treated by Empagliflozin after 24 h of incubation. The protective role of miR-214-3p was demonstrated in a study by Wang et al., where the authors employed a lentiviral vector expressing miR-214-3p to protect the H9C2 cardiomyoblast against ischemia/reperfusion injury [42]. An important link between upregulated miR-214-3p and the downregulation of HIF1α was documented in the study by Wan et al., which highlighted the significance of miR-214-3p in ischemic post-conditioning by regulating HIF1α in vivo [43]. The loss of significant effect of Empagliflozin after 48 h of incubation with cobalt on miR-214-3p may be explained by either the reduced stability of Empagliflozin in the cultivated medium or by the cells depleting all the Empagliflozin after the prolonged period.

In addition, the following miRNAs exhibited changes in expression with borderline statistical significance. Although the differences did not reach statistical significance, increasing the number of biological replicates could potentially render them significant, thereby helping to guide other researchers in identifying relevant miRNAs for further investigation. After 24 h of incubation, changes in the expression levels of miR-145-5p and miR-103a-5p were observed. Notably, miR-145-5p was downregulated in cells treated with Empagliflozin, but a similar decrease was also seen in cells exposed to cobalt. In myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury, miR-145-5p functions as a cardiac-protective molecule by reducing inflammation and apoptosis [40]. This phenomenon may be explained by the adaptive reaction of cardiomyocytes to metabolic changes mediated by Empagliflozin [44]. In the case of miR-103-5p, it has a dual and complex role in cardiomyocytes: it provides protection against ischemia/reperfusion injury on one hand, but on the other, evidence suggests it promotes cardiomyocyte death and hypertrophy [[45], [46], [47]]. In our study, we observed a decline in the level of miR-103-5p under hypoxic conditions following Empagliflozin treatment. A similar finding was reported in a study by Zhu et al., where the authors observed that loss of miR-103-3p reduces hypoxia-induced cardiomyocyte apoptosis and promotes cardiomyocyte proliferation [48]. Similarly to miR-214-3p, after 48 h of incubation of cardiomyocytes with Empagliflozin under both normoxic and pathological conditions, most of the previously observed effects on miR-103-5p and miR-145-5p were lost. An interesting phenomenon was observed in miR-22-5p. Similar to previously mentioned miRNAs, miR-22-5p regulates cardiac remodeling and may provide a potential cardioprotective effect on ischemic heart disease [49]. Our study identified an increasing tendency of miR-22-5p expression after Empagliflozin intervention in cells cultivated with cobalt. These results indicate that Empagliflozin can act as an activator of miR-22-5p, resulting in increased cell viability. Compared with previously analyzed miRNAs, miR-22-5p expression was increased following prolonged Empagliflozin treatment (48 h) in cobalt-cultured cells, which indicates that Empagliflozin may modulate cell adaptation to hypoxic conditions over a prolonged period.

Conclusion

We explored the potential cardioprotective role of Empagliflozin in human primary cardiomyocytes using a chemically induced model of cobalt-mediated hypoxia. Despite the fact that our model is based on mimicking hypoxia using CoCl2, this experimental approach is widely used in experimental biology. It offers several advantages, including sustained stabilization of HIF and the induction of key adaptive mechanisms, such as the upregulation of HIF1α, VEGF, and glycolytic enzymes. In addition, it allows precise control by ensuring consistent induction of hypoxia-like signaling across samples. However, it is important to recognize that CoCl2-induced hypoxia only partially mimics physiological hypoxic conditions and may also produce effects unrelated to oxygen deprivation. The application of Empagliflozin influences epigenetic mechanisms related to miRNA expression. We identified a significant role for Empagliflozin in the regulation of cardioprotective miR-214-3p, suggesting possible benefits of Empagliflozin in preventing ischemic injury in CVD. The obtained data confirmed the association of miR-214-3p with the functional outcomes observed in our previous studies. Although our results showed a certain cardioprotective role of Empagliflozin, the results were not sufficiently robust. Further studies are needed to explore the pleiotropic effects of Empagliflozin in the prevention and progression of cardiovascular disease, supported by additional functional validation to elucidate the underlying mechanisms. A deeper understanding of its underlying epigenetic mechanisms may offer new hope for patients suffering from heart disease.

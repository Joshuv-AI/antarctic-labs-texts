Long Head of Biceps Tenodesis for Maintaining Inherent Length and Uniform Tension at the Bicipital Groove: Suprapectoral Double-Row Technique With All-Suture Anchors
Authors: Ayyappan V. Nair, Pramod K. Mohan, Maythilisharan Rambhojan, Sreejith Thampy J, Pavan K. Uppaluri, Aebel Raju, Prince Shanavas Khan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The clinicopathologic conditions of the long head of the biceps tendon vary, encompassing tendinitis, peritendinous inflammation, hypertrophy, and partial or complete tears. These symptoms are typically linked with SLAP tears and instability of the long head of the biceps tendon, often resulting in partial displacement or complete dislocation. The choice between tenotomy and tenodesis depends on varied factors. The choice of suprapectoral or subpectoral tenodesis is a difficult decision and should be weighed on a tailored basis. Our approach to suprapectoral tenodesis presents a blend of factors, maintaining uniform tension in the bicipital groove, limiting the number of portals for arthroscopy, re-tensioning the tendon after initial anchor placement, and preserving the inherent length of the functional biceps tendon without compromising its quality.

Surgical Technique

Patient Positioning

The patient is placed in the lateral decubitus position (Shoulder Suspension System; Arthrex, Naples, FL), with the application of 5 kg of skin traction, alongside administration of an interscalene nerve block and general anesthesia (Video 1).

Diagnostic Examination

The posterior portal is used for a diagnostic glenohumeral view; the anterior portal is established in the rotator interval with the patient in the lateral decubitus position (Fig 1). This configuration also helps in evaluating concurrent rotator cuff pathology. Localization of Long Head of Biceps Tendon

By use of a radiofrequency ablator, the long head of the biceps tendon (LHBT) is identified as a soft area between the ridges of the biceps groove (Fig 3). Debridement of overlying tissues is carried out, and the transverse humeral ligament is transected at the lateral ridge, avoiding the medial ridge to prevent damage to the subscapularis tendon. To improve visibility, a probe is inserted through the anterior portal and this maneuvers the LHBT in the medial direction.Fig 3The patient is placed in the lateral decubitus position (right side). Suture Anchor Placement

The initial suture anchor (TwinFix; Smith & Nephew, Andover, MA) is strategically positioned at the distal margin of the groove; the elbow is kept in a 90° flexed position with neutral rotation of the forearm before anchor fixation (Fig 4). A double-loaded all-suture anchor is inserted through the MA portal, and a bird-beak device aids in passing 1 limb of FiberWire (Arthrex) underneath the tendon, which is retrieved through the MA portal (Fig 5). Then, an antegrade suture passer (Scorpion; Arthrex) is used to place a cinch suture through the substance of the tendon, with the loop retrieved externally (Figs 6 and 7). The construct is tightened but not knotted, allowing retraction of the LHBT to facilitate placement of the second suture anchor, which is a single-loaded all-suture anchor (Q-Fix; Smith & Nephew). This helps in re-tensioning the tendon after double-row anchor placement. Final Knotting and Tendon Healing

After placement of the second suture anchor, positioned 15 mm closer to the initial one, the proximal cinch suture is tightened, and half-hitches are applied (Fig 8). The same procedure is repeated on the distal suture anchor, ensuring good apposition of the tendon onto the anchor and bone bed. Tendon healing onto the bone bed is enhanced by burring and microfracturing.Fig 8Cinched loop suture construct over biceps tendon. In this surgical technique, the suture material is looped around the biceps tendon within the groove and then cinched down to create tension, effectively securing the tendon in place. Transection of LHBT

The LHBT is transected 10 mm proximal to the proximal suture using a radiofrequency ablator. Cutting the LHBT after fixation assists in maintaining the length-tension relation of the biceps tendon. Discussion

Biceps tenotomy is a technically simpler and more cost-effective option than biceps tenodesis; however, research indicates increased rates of muscle cramps, poor cosmetic outcomes, and loss of muscle strength associated with this procedure.1,2 It must be avoided in the younger, more active population. The transition from tenotomy to tenodesis over the past decade reflects this trend, with tenodesis presenting less chance of Popeye deformity and pain.2 Subpectoral tenodesis leverages the advantage of exploring extra-articular LHBT pathology but has the limitation of higher chances of neurovascular injury and humeral fracture especially with the use of interference screws.3 Although the functional outcomes between suprapectoral and subpectoral tenodesis have been equivocal per current literature reviews, the choice of technique should be tailored.4

Preparing the bone bed after clearing the tissues overlying the bicipital groove helps reduce pain as a long-term sequela.5 Subsequent tensioning using 2 suture anchors ensures uniform tension within the bicipital groove, thereby enhancing the healing of the bone-to-tendon connection. This technique uses the principle of onlay tenodesis for LHBT, with less chance of torsional fracture and lower revision rates after fixation.6 This process aids in maintaining a consistent tension distribution and a balanced length-tension relation, ultimately reducing the likelihood of residual pain in the bicipital groove.7 Inadequate restoration of length-tension relations can contribute to tendinitis, causing residual pain, making the revision rate of biceps tenodesis as high as 36% depending on the techniques used.8

In conclusion, the available randomized controlled trials comparing the approaches to tenodesis, namely suprapectoral and subpectoral, lack specific matching of the uniformity of the particular techniques. This has contributed to the varying results in the literature, making it challenging to arrive at a conclusive choice between the 2 approaches.9

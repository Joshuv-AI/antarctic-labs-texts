Compact and high isolated microstrip patch antenna system for full-duplex/MIMO applications
Authors: Phuong Kim-Thi, Tuyen Pham-Danh
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
This paper presents a two-port microstrip patch antenna system with compact size and high isolation for full-duplex and multiple-input-multiple-output (MIMO) applications. The proposed miniaturization approach is to employ compact microstrip patch antenna and decoupling network as well. Here, two quarter-wavelength patch antennas are arranged in the H-plane coupled configuration with element spacing of about 0.008 λ at the center operating frequency. Besides, a defected ground structure is chosen as the decoupling network, which doesn't require any additional space and contributes to significantly reducing the overall antenna dimensions. The final antenna design with compact size of 0.49 λ × 0.37 λ × 0.01 λ has operating band from 2.45 to 2.48 GHz, in which isolation is better than 30 dB and the maximum isolation is 42 dB. Besides, the proposed antenna also achieved comparable gain of 3.8 dBi and good diversity performance. In comparison with other related works, the proposed design has smallest overall size with extremely small element spacing while obtaining comparable isolation enhancement.

Introduction

In modern wireless communication systems, full-duplex and multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) technologies are widely used since they can provide high data rate by enhancing spectral efficiency [1,2]. Besides, the antennas should be compact as electronic devices nowadays are getting smaller and smaller. For this antenna type, a constrained space installment probably leads to strong mutual coupling between the excited and non-excited elements, which significantly degrades the system performance. Therefore, this paper focuses on designing an antenna array, which simultaneously possesses high isolation and compact dimensions characteristics.

High isolation can be achieved when the radiating elements are positioned far to each other [3]. However, it significantly increases the antenna size. Thus, one of the critical parts determining the compactness of the antenna is the decoupling network, which contributes to suppressing the mutual coupling between the radiating elements. Basically, the decoupling networks could be metasurface (MS) [4,5], near-field resonator (NFR) [6,7], dielectric block [8], or wall [[9], [10], [11]]. They are positioned above or in an orthogonal plane with the radiating elements to solve the mutual coupling problem by cancellation of reflected and coupled waves. Although these decoupling approaches show their potential in mutual suppression, they significantly increase the antenna profile and then, not suitable for compact dimension purpose. A possible solution is to place the decoupling network on the same substrate with the radiating elements. In Refs. [[12], [13], [14], [15]], a different decoupling approach is to generate an additional coupling path, which has an inversed effect with the original coupling path. Alternatively, using electromagnetic bandgap (EBG), grounded stub, or defected ground structure (DGS) between the radiating elements is also another effective solution for isolation enhancement [[16], [17], [18], [19], [20]]. However, these approaches can significantly increase the antenna size in horizontal direction by requiring additional space for decoupling network installment. Besides, large element spacing and/or low isolation improvement is also another disadvantage of such designs.

The motivation of this work is to design a two-port antenna, which can achieve high isolation with compact size for modern full-duplex/MIMO wireless devices. To achieve the compact size, a quarter-wavelength patch is used instead of using half-wavelength patch. Besides, after carefully evaluating the pros and cons of various decoupling techniques, the DGS is chosen as it can be positioned in the ground plane and doesn't require any additional space, which considerably reduces the antenna dimensions. The measured data on the fabricated antenna demonstrate that about 40 dB isolation improvement can be achieved with extremely small edge spacing of 0.008 λ. In addition, the proposed antenna also has the overall dimensions of 0.49 λ × 0.37 λ × 0.01 λ, which is the smallest when compared to the other related works.

Single antenna design

Fig. 1 shows the geometry of different patch antennas under the fundamental mode, TM10. When a microstrip antenna is fed to excite the TM10 mode, its resonant length will be a half-wavelength. A virtual shorted circuit forms through a plane centered between the two radiating edges. It means that if the shorted circuit exists (series of pins or shorting wall) at the center of the half-wavelength patch, we can make an antenna by using half the patch (quarter-wavelength). The quarter-wavelength patch only has a single radiating edge, which degrades the radiation pattern directivity compared with a half-wavelength patch [21]. Here, the gain of the quarter-wavelength patch is about 2 dBi smaller than that of the half-wavelength patch. Besides, although the quarter-wavelength patch has narrow band operation, 20 MHz or 30 MHz is suitable for single Wi-Fi channel of 2.4 GHz range. Thus, this type of radiator is preferred as it still works effectively for wireless devices.Fig. 1Geometry of a half-wavelength and quarter-wavelength patch antennas.Fig. 1

Geometry of a half-wavelength and quarter-wavelength patch antennas.

The shorting wall can be replaced by multiple shorting pins, which are positioned near the patch's edge. The utilized substrate is 1.52-mm-thick Taconic RF-35 with dielectric constant of ɛr = 3.5 and loss tangent of 0.0019. Two-port antenna design

In this Section, a two-port antenna design is developed from the compact element presented in Section 2. Firstly, the coupled antenna is considered, and the geometry of this antenna is shown in Fig. 4. Two elements are arranged in the H-plane coupled configuration with an edge-to-edge spacing of do = 1 mm, corresponding to about 0.008 λ at the center operating frequency.Fig. 4The geometry of the coupled two-port antenna array.Fig. 4

The geometry of the coupled two-port antenna array.

Fig. 5 shows the performance in terms of reflection and transmission coefficients, as well as the gain in the broadside direction of the coupled antenna array. It can be seen obviously that the antenna is well resonated around 2.45 GHz with −10 dB impedance bandwidth from 2.42 to 2.49 GHz. However, due to the close position, this antenna has a strong coupling between the array elements with significantly low isolation of about 2 dB. Regarding the broadside gain, the gain values are quite low due to a strong radiation from the coupled element. This makes the main beam tilt off from the broadside direction.Fig. 5Simulated (a) S-parameter and (b) broadside gain of different antenna arrays.Fig. 5

Simulated (a) S-parameter and (b) broadside gain of different antenna arrays.

To improve the antenna performance, a decoupling network is utilized. The literature review in Section 1 indicates that there are various reported decoupling methods. Each type of decoupling method has its own advantages and disadvantages. This paper focuses on designing the antenna with the smallest size. Thus, a defected ground structure (DGS) is employed since it does not require any additional space between or above the elements. However, this type of decoupling approach has an inherent disadvantage of high back radiation. Fig. 6 shows the geometry of the coupled antenna array. Here, an I-shaped DGS is embedded in the ground plane, and it is positioned between the array elements. The optimal design parameters are Lp = 17, Wp = 17, lf = 4.5, s = 0.8, dx = 1.4, dy = 2, do = 1, rv = 0.3, ls = 19.2, ws = 1 (unit: mm).Fig. 6The geometry of the proposed decoupled two-port antenna array.Fig. 6

The geometry of the proposed decoupled two-port antenna array.

The performance of the decoupled antenna is also presented in Fig. 5. As observed, this antenna also exhibits good matching performance around 2.45 GHz. With respect to the isolation, it significantly increases from 2 to 39 dB. Especially, the isolation within the operating bandwidth is better than 30 dB. Meanwhile, the broadside gain is also improved, which is increased from 1.1 to 4.0 dBi. For demonstration the effect of the DGS, Fig. 7 shows the equivalent circuit of the proposed design. Here, R1, L1, C1 and R2, L2, C2 are resistance, inductance, and capacitance of the radiating patches. Two patches are positioned closely and the coupling between them can be approximated by a coupling capacitance, Cc. A parallel LdCd model represent for the DGS structure, which acts as a band-stop filter. It will block the coupling current flowing from the excited element to the non-excited element. The isolation improvement can also be explained by observing the current distribution on the ground plane and the radiating elements, as shown in Fig. 7(b). For the coupled antenna, there is a strong coupling current distributed on the coupled element, leading to low isolation. On the other hand, a weak current is distributed on the coupled element with the presence of the I-shaped slot.Fig. 7(a) Equivalent circuit of the proposed MIMO antenna and (b) Simulated current distribution on the coupled and decouple antenna arrays.Fig. 7

(a) Equivalent circuit of the proposed MIMO antenna and (b) Simulated current distribution on the coupled and decouple antenna arrays.

Key parameter studies

For better understanding the antenna optimization process, parametric studies are implemented. Firstly, the isolation of the proposed antenna is considered. Fig. 8, Fig. 9 show the effect of the slot on the S-parameter. It can be seen obviously that the slot's length has minor effect on the operating band, which is almost stable with the variation of ls from 28.7 to 29.7 mm. In contrast, the effect of ls on the isolation is significant. Since the I-shaped slot acts as a band-stop filter, changing its length will directly affect the rejected frequency band. As shown in Fig. 8(b), the dip in the |S21| profile shifts towards the low frequency range when increasing ls. Similar phenomenon can be observed when changing the width of the slot, ws. As seen in Fig. 9, the operating frequency is minor affected. Meanwhile, the isolation characteristic is significantly impacted. The best isolation reduces with the increase of ws.Fig. 8Simulated (a) |S11| and (b) |S21| of the proposed antenna array with different ls.Fig. 8Fig. 9Simulated (a) |S11| and (b) |S21| of the proposed antenna array with different ws.Fig. 9

Secondly, the operating frequency band of the proposed antenna is considered. As discussed in Section 2, the shorting wall is a critical parameter in quarter-wavelength patch antenna. Fig. 10 shows the S-parameter of the proposed antenna against the variation of the shorting pins’ position, dx. Conclusion

This paper presents and investigates an effective solution to design a compact and high isolated antenna array by using quarter-wavelength microstrip patches and I-shaped DGS as decoupling method. The design concept is firstly characterized in simulation, then validated by measurement on the fabricated antenna prototype. The measured data demonstrate that the isolation can be improved about 40 dB with extremely small element spacing of 0.008 λ. Besides, the antenna also has compact dimensions of 0.49 λ × 0.37 λ × 0.01 λ. By possessing these advantages, the proposed antenna is very promising for compact full-duplex or MIMO communication electronic devices.

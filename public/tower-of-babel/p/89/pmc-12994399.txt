Association Between Willingness to Pay and Quality of Life in Acne Patients: A Cross-Sectional Study with a Nomogram for Individualized Prediction
Authors: Xiangzi Li, Xintao Cen, Ning Ning, Chao Li, Yaqian Zhu, Jiarui Liu, Qian Zhu, Shiyu He, Yang Guan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo examine willingness to pay (WTP) for acne treatment and its associated factors, to assess the relationship between WTP and dermatology-specific quality of life as measured by the Dermatology Life Quality Index (DLQI), and to develop and internally validate a nomogram for predicting high WTP, with the aim of supporting individualized acne management and informed healthcare resource allocation.MethodsPatients with acne completed an electronic questionnaire assessing their WTP for a hypothetical one-time curative treatment, along with demographic, clinical, and DLQI data. High WTP was defined as >4000 CNY. Multivariable logistic regression identified factors associated with high WTP, and restricted cubic spline analysis explored the relationship between WTP and DLQI scores. The dataset was randomly divided into training (70%) and testing (30%) cohorts. LASSO regression was used to identify key predictors of WTP. The variables retained by LASSO were subsequently entered into a multivariate logistic regression prediction model, and the independent predictors were incorporated into a nomogram to estimate the probability of high WTP for each patient.ResultsA total of 456 patients were included. Multivariate logistic regression analysis revealed that female sex, aged ≥25 years, monthly income ≥5000 CNY, moderate or severe acne, prior treatment history (medications, chemical peels, or combination therapies), recurrent acne, and annual acne-related expenditure >1000 CNY were significantly associated with high WTP. DLQI showed a linear relationship with WTP (Poverall= 0.04, Pnonlinear = 0.77). Eight independent predictors identified from multivariable analyses were incorporated into the predictive nomogram, which showed good calibration and discrimination (AUC_train = 0.80; AUC_test = 0.77). Decision-curve analysis indicated clinical utility across threshold probabilities between 0.19 and 0.94.ConclusionPatients’ WTP for acne treatment reflects disease burden and quality-of-life impairment. A nomogram developed and validated in this study provides an effective tool for estimating WTP in acne patients and may support clinical decision-making and health-economic assessment.

Introduction

Acne vulgaris is a chronic inflammatory disorder of the pilosebaceous unit, most commonly affecting adolescents and young adults. In recent years, the proportion of acne cases has been increasing. According to the Global Burden of Disease study, acne vulgaris affected approximately 231.2 million individuals worldwide in 2019, accounting for roughly 9.4% of the global population.1 In China, epidemiological studies have reported a prevalence rate approaching 40%, indicating a profound public health impact.2 Acne not only impacts patients’ appearance but may also lead to irreversible outcomes such as scarring and pigmentation.3 These manifestations often result in psychological issues including anxiety, depression, and social withdrawal, thereby severely compromising overall quality of life.4,5 With growing awareness, the demand for acne treatment has steadily increased.6 However, due to its complex pathogenesis, prolonged course, and high recurrence rate, acne management often requires multiple therapeutic strategies and long-term care, resulting in a substantial financial burden for patients.

Willingness to pay (WTP) is a health economics tool used to quantify individuals’ valuation of health outcomes in monetary terms. It is widely applied in assessing patients perceived burden of disease and the value they place on specific health services or treatment options. WTP offers a direct economic perspective that can complement traditional quality-of-life assessment tools such as health-related quality of life (HRQOL) instruments or disease-specific quality-of-life measures.7,8 Previous studies have demonstrated that WTP is influenced by various factors including gender, age, income, disease severity, treatment history, and psychological status. Higher WTP generally indicates a greater perceived disease burden and lower quality of life, positioning WTP as a potential surrogate measure for subjective disease impact.9 In the field of dermatology, WTP has been employed by researchers to evaluate the impact of conditions such as rosacea, psoriasis, and atopic dermatitis.10–12 However, systematic investigations regarding WTP among acne patients remain scarce, with limited analysis of contributing factors. Only one study conducted in China has reported on WTP for chemical peel treatments among acne patients, showing a positive correlation between WTP, self-reported acne severity, and perceived treatment efficacy.13 Furthermore, no studies have yet examined the correlation between WTP and the dermatology life quality index (DLQI) in acne to explore the feasibility and value in reflecting the acne-related disease burden.

Beyond identifying such associations, there is a practical need for predictive tools that can estimate which patients are likely to report high WTP, thereby supporting shared decision-making, counselling on treatment options and expected costs, and efficient resource allocation. To our knowledge, no published study has developed or validated a nomogram to predict high WTP in acne, as prior work has focused on measuring WTP and its determinants rather than building predictive tools.14 Nomograms are widely used for clinical prediction in dermatology,15–17 yet this approach has not been applied to WTP in acne.

This study was conducted as a cross-sectional investigation among adult acne patients, using an electronic questionnaire to assess willingness to pay (WTP) for a hypothetical “one-time curative treatment”, along with demographic, clinical, and DLQI data. Statistical analyses were performed to explore factors associated with WTP and its relationship with quality of life. In addition, a prediction model was developed to estimate the probability of high WTP, offering an individualized quantitative assessment of patients’ economic preferences in acne care. By integrating economic and quality-of-life measures within a practical prediction tool, this study aims to support individualized counselling, treatment planning, and healthcare resource allocation in acne care.

Materials and Methods

Participants Recruitment

This study recruited patients with acne from April 2024 to January 2025, the inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) age ≥18 years; (2) a clinical diagnosis of acne based on the 2019 Chinese Guidelines for the Management of Acne; and (3) voluntary participation with a signed informed consent form. Exclusion criteria included: (1) pregnancy or lactation; (2) inability to understand or complete the questionnaire. Participants were recruited on-site by trained investigators. The study was approved by the ethics committee, and written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

Measurement of WTP

WTP was measured using the Contingent Valuation Method (CVM), a widely accepted approach for eliciting individuals’ monetary valuation of hypothetical scenarios.18 This method involves presenting respondents with a hypothetical treatment option and using structured questions to assess their preferences, thereby estimating the maximum amount they are willing to pay for the proposed intervention. In this study, a standardized open-ended question was employed: If there were an ideal treatment for acne that could completely cure the condition in one session with no recurrence, what is the maximum amount you would be willing to pay for it?

If there were an ideal treatment for acne that could completely cure the condition in one session with no recurrence, what is the maximum amount you would be willing to pay for it?

This response was used to determine the participant’s WTP.

DLQI Assessment

The DLQI evaluates the impact of skin disease across multiple domains, including symptoms and physical discomfort, emotional well-being, daily activities, clothing choices, social and leisure activities, physical exercise, work or school performance, personal relationships, sexual activity, and treatment-related issues.19 Each item is scored on a 4-point scale ranging from 0 to 3, yielding a total score between 0 and 30. The total score is interpreted as follows: 0–1 indicates no effect, 2–5 mild effect, 6–10 moderate effect, 11–20 severe effect, and 21–30 very severe effect on quality of life. Higher scores reflect a greater impairment in quality of life. All participants completed the DLQI as part of the electronic questionnaire.

Investigation of Covariates

Participants were recruited on-site at the dermatology outpatient clinic and completed an electronic questionnaire after providing written informed consent. All participants had a confirmed clinical diagnosis of acne vulgaris established by qualified dermatologists during routine outpatient consultations, based on standard clinical assessment. Acne severity was assessed using standard clinical grading. Acne subtypes based on lesion morphology were not further classified in the present study. Acne recurrence was defined as relapse occurring within 3 months of remission. To avoid duplicate entries, each participant was allowed to submit the questionnaire only once using a unique IP address. The questionnaire collected sociodemographic information, including age, gender, marital status, income level, educational background, dietary habits, and place of residence, as well as clinical characteristics such as the location and severity of acne lesions, history of medical consultations and treatments, and acne recurrence.

Nomogram Construction and Validation

Eligible patients were randomly allocated to the training and testing cohorts at a 7:3 ratio. In the training cohort, candidate predictors were first screened using LASSO regression with 10-fold cross-validation. The variables retained by LASSO were then included in a multivariate logistic regression prediction model, and the independent predictors were incorporated into a nomogram to estimate the probability of high WTP for each patient.

Model performance was assessed in both cohorts. Discrimination was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves with area under the curve (AUC) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Calibration was examined by the Hosmer–Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test (P-value) and calibration curves comparing predicted and observed probabilities. Decision-curve analysis (DCA) was performed to evaluate the clinical utility of the nomogram.

Statistical Analysis

Data analysis was performed using SAS 9.4. Participants were stratified into high-WTP and low-WTP groups based on the median WTP value of 4000 CNY. Univariate analysis was first conducted to identify variables influencing WTP. Subsequently, variables with a P-value < 0.05 in the univariate analysis were included in a multivariate logistic regression model to determine the factors affecting acne patients’ WTP. Restricted cubic spline analysis was employed to assess the correlation between WTP and the DLQI. Nomogram development was based on LASSO regression and multivariate logistic regression prediction model, with performance evaluated as described above.

All P-values were presented as two-sided, with a determined statistical significance based on a false discovery rate of less than 0.05.

Result

Baseline Characteristics of Participants

A total of 456 patients were included in the study, of whom 19.74% were male and 80.26% were female. The mean age was 27.32 ± 4.76 years. Most participants were unmarried (80.70%), had a monthly income of ≥5000 CNY (86.62%), and were of Han ethnicity (96.71%). Regarding acne severity, the most common classification was moderate grade II (40.57%). Facial involvement was predominant in 80.92% of participants. In terms of prior medical history, 79.82% had previously sought treatment for acne. The median cost of prior expenditure for acne treatment was 4000 CNY (IQR: 1000–10,000 CNY). Regarding treatment modalities, 33.77% had undergone combination therapy, while 31.14% had only used medication. Additionally, 82.89% of participants reported recurrent acne episodes. As for the economic and quality-of-life burden, 44.08% reported spending 1000–5000 CNY on acne treatment in the past year, and 69.96% had more than three outpatient visits. Correlation Analysis of WTP and DLQI

The mean DLQI score among respondents was 8.37 ± 6.14. A total of 130 patients (28.51%) had DLQI scores greater than 10, indicating a significant impact on quality of life. Restricted cubic spline analysis demonstrated a positive correlation between DLQI scores and WTP (Poverall= 0.04, Pnonlinear = 0.77), suggesting that patients with greater impairment in quality of life were more likely to report a higher WTP (Figure 1). Variable Selection and Model Specification

In the training cohort, LASSO regression analysis was used to select candidate predictors and mitigate overfitting, and 8 variables with non-zero coefficients were identified (Figure 2A and B). These variables were subsequently entered into a multivariable logistic regression model, which identified gender, age, monthly income, acne severity, previous treatment, acne recurrence, annual treatment cost, and frequency of clinic visits in the past year as independent predictors of high WTP (>4000 CNY, all P < 0.01). Based on these independent predictors, we constructed a nomogram to estimate the individual probability of high WTP (Figure 3A). Model Performance and Validation

The nomogram demonstrated good discrimination and calibration in both cohorts. The AUC of the ROC curve was 0.8 (95% CI 0.75–0.85) in the training cohort and 0.77 (95% CI 0.68–0.85) in the testing cohort (Figure 3B). The Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test was non-significant (P > 0.05) and calibration curves showed good agreement between predicted and observed probabilities (Figure 3C).

DCA showed a positive net benefit of the nomogram compared with “treat-all” or “treat-none” strategies across threshold probabilities of 0.19 and 0.94 in both cohorts (Figure 3D), indicating potential clinical usefulness for individualized counselling and resource planning.

Discussion

Acne is a chronic inflammatory disease that primarily affects the pilosebaceous units of the face.20 According to the Global Burden of Disease study, the burden of acne has increased across almost all countries over the past three decades.21 Adult acne, in particular, is characterized by a more complex etiology, greater treatment resistance, and higher recurrence rates, which collectively pose substantial challenges for long-term clinical management.22

In this study, higher WTP was observed among female patients, those aged ≥25 years, and individuals with higher monthly income, indicating that WTP is influenced by both sociodemographic characteristics and economic capacity. In addition, patients with moderate-to-severe acne, previous treatment history, disease recurrence, higher annual treatment expenditures, and higher DLQI scores reported significantly higher WTP. These factors reflect more complex disease profiles and further underscore the influence of quality-of-life impairment on WTP. Notably, more than one-fourth of participants had DLQI scores exceeding 10, underscoring the substantial quality-of-life impairment associated with acne in this population.

Consistent with these observations, we found a significant positive correlation between WTP and DLQI scores. The median WTP for a hypothetical one-time curative acne treatment was 4000 CNY, with DLQI scores slightly lower than those reported in rosacea.12 Furthermore, in a recent cross-sectional analysis, acne patients with scarring were found to have significantly higher DLQI scores (11.9 vs 6.4) than those without scars. Notably, in both scarred and non-scarred patients, greater DLQI impairment correlated positively with higher WTP.23 These findings are consistent with our results, highlighting the central role of quality-of-life impairment in shaping patients’ economic valuation of acne treatment. In contrast, diseases such as psoriasis have been associated with substantially higher WTP and more severe quality-of-life impairment,24,25 indicating that WTP can reflect the impact of different skin diseases on quality of life to some extent. Together, these findings reinforce the potential role of WTP as a complementary indicator of disease burden. Compared with DLQI, WTP assessment is more intuitive and readily understood by patients, which may facilitate its integration into routine clinical communication and shared decision-making.

Building upon these findings, we developed and internally validated a nomogram to estimate the probability of high WTP (>4000 CNY) among acne patients. The model demonstrated good discrimination, calibration, and clinical utility in both training and testing cohorts, supporting its potential for individualized application. The predictors retained in the final nomogram model—gender, age, monthly income, acne severity, previous treatment, acne recurrence, annual treatment cost, and frequency of clinic visits in the past year—each showed a meaningful association with the perceived economic value of acne care. Women might exhibit higher WTP due to greater psychosocial burden from facial disfigurement and stronger motivation for aesthetic restoration. Higher income reflects greater financial flexibility and has consistently been linked to increased WTP in dermatologic conditions. Patients with moderate-to-severe or recurrent acne often experience more pronounced psychosocial distress and disruption to daily life, which collectively elevate their perceived benefit from effective treatment, thus increasing WTP.26 Past treatment history and associated costs may further indicate heightened awareness of therapeutic options and a stronger willingness to invest in disease control, though this association warrants confirmation in future studies.

Existing predictive models in acne research have primarily focused on clinical endpoints. For example, Tan et al developed a risk assessment tool for atrophic acne scarring based on disease severity, duration, and lesion manipulation.27 However, such models do not incorporate economic metrics or patient financial behaviors. To our knowledge, no previous study in acne has integrated predictive modeling with cost-related outcomes, highlighting the novelty and rarity of our approach. By identifying these independent predictors, the nomogram provides a practical tool for clinicians to identify patients more likely to invest in acne treatment, thereby enabling more personalized counseling and efficient allocation of clinical resources. Furthermore, it promotes the incorporation of patient-centered economic considerations into evidence-based dermatologic care, supporting a more refined and individualized approach to treatment planning.

This study has the following strengths. First, multivariate logistic regression analysis was employed to evaluate acne patients’ WTP and its influencing factors, effectively controlling for potential confounding variables and enhancing the reliability of the conclusions. Second, the simultaneous incorporation of WTP and the DLQI score enabled a comprehensive assessment of patients’ disease perception from both economic and quality-of-life perspectives. Third, a nomogram developed using LASSO regression analysis was internally validated in a testing group. Discrimination, calibration, and decision-curve analysis supported its clinical utility for identifying patients with high WTP and for informing decision-making, treatment planning, and resource allocation.

However, several limitations should be noted. First, due to the cross-sectional study design, causal relationships between WTP and DLQI or other influencing factors could not be established. Second, the hypothetical “one-time curative treatment plan” used to assess WTP may have led to an overestimation of patients’ payment willingness, thereby limiting the generalizability of the findings in real-world clinical decision-making scenarios. Third, the nomogram has undergone internal validation only. Prospective, multicenter studies are warranted to externally validate the model before broader clinical application.

Conclusion

Patients’ WTP for acne treatment was associated with demographic and clinical factors and with greater DLQI impairment, supporting WTP as a patient-centered economic indicator of disease burden. We developed and internally validated a concise nomogram to estimate the probability of high WTP, which demonstrated good discrimination, calibration, and clinical utility across training and testing cohorts. External multicenter validation is warranted, and this tool may facilitate individualized counselling, treatment planning, and healthcare resource allocation in acne care.

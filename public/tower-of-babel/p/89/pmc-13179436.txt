Rapid Detection of Carbapenemase-Producing Acinetobacter Baumannii Using MALDI-TOF Mass Spectrometry: A Technological Advancement in Clinical Microbiology
Authors: Sha Chen, Keya Cai, Wenya Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background: The emergence of CRAB presents a critical challenge in healthcare settings, necessitating rapid and accurate detection methods. MALDI-TOF MS has emerged as a promising technology for antimicrobial resistance detection, offering advantages over conventional molecular assays.Objective: This study evaluates the diagnostic performance and clinical applicability of MALDI-TOF MS for the rapid identification of carbapenemase-producing A. baumannii isolates, aiming to enhance laboratory efficiency and antimicrobial stewardship.Materials and Methods: A total of 200 clinical A. baumannii isolates were collected from a tertiary care hospital in China throughout 2023. Carbapenemase activity was assessed using MALDI-TOF MS, and results were compared against molecular identification of carbapenemase-encoding genes via multiplex PCR. Diagnostic performance metrics, including sensitivity, specificity, and predictive values, were analyzed.Results: MALDI-TOF MS demonstrated high diagnostic accuracy, correctly identifying carbapenemase activity in 95 of 100 CRAB isolates and classifying 98 of 100 carbapenem-susceptible strains. Molecular assays confirmed the presence of carbapenemase genes in 96 resistant isolates. Performance metrics showed MALDI-TOF MS achieved a sensitivity of 98.96%, specificity of 99.00%, and overall accuracy of 98.98%. The system effectively detected various carbapenemase types, including OXA-48-like enzymes (40%), New Delhi metallo-β-lactamase (30%), Klebsiella pneumoniae carbapenemase (15%), Verona integron-encoded metallo-β-lactamase (10%), and active-on-imipenem (IMP) (1%), with a single missed detection of an IMP-producing isolate.Conclusion: MALDI-TOF MS offers a rapid, reliable, and cost-effective approach for carbapenemase detection in A. baumannii, significantly improving the speed of antimicrobial resistance screening in clinical laboratories. Its seamless integration into existing workflows enhances diagnostic efficiency and supports real-time antimicrobial resistance surveillance.

1. Background

Acinetobacter baumannii (A. baumannii) is a Gram-negative bacterium that causes various infections such as pneumonia, meningitis, urinary tract infections, and sepsis ( 1 ). It is also the most common multidrug-resistant organism (MDRO) in hospitals, particularly in intensive care units ( 2 ). Carbapenems, a class of broad-spectrum antibiotics, are commonly used as a last resort for the treatment of MDRO infections. However, some A. baumannii strains develop resistance to carbapenems and produce carbapenemases that hydrolyze and inactivate antibiotics ( 3 ).

Carbapenemase-producing A. baumannii (CPAB), with limited treatment options and increased mortality rates, poses a serious threat to public health ( 4 ). Therefore, the early and accurate detection of CPAB is crucial for guiding appropriate antibiotic therapies and preventing the dissemination of resistance. However, conventional methods for detecting CPAB, such as phenotypic tests and molecular assays, are time-consuming, labor-intensive, and expensive ( 5 ). Consequently, a rapid, simple, and cost-effective method for detecting CPAB in a clinical setting is required.

Matrix-assisted laser desorption ionization time-of-flight mass spectrometry (MALDI-TOF MS) is used to measure the mass and identify the molecules in a sample by ionizing and analyzing them using a laser beam ( 6 ). MALDI-TOF MS provides reliable and reproducible results within minutes and is widely used for bacterial identification ( 7 ). MALDI-TOF MS was recently used to detect the carbapenemase activity of various bacteria, including A. baumannii ( 8 ).

MALDI-TOF MS-based carbapenemase detection involves the measurement of carbapenem hydrolysis by carbapenemase-producing bacteria ( 9 ). Carbapenems were mixed with the bacterial cultures and incubated for a certain period. The mixture was then centrifuged and the supernatant analyzed using MALDI-TOF MS. The supernatant analysis revealed the presence or absence of a carbapenem peak and its degradation products based on the bacteria’s carbapenemase activity ( 10 ).

MALDI-TOF MS-based carbapenemase detection is rapid, simple, and inexpensive ( 11 ), and its results can be obtained within hours without complex procedures or specialized equipment. Furthermore, the same instruments and databases that are already available for bacterial identification can be used multiple times, thereby reducing additional costs and resource requirements. Moreover, it can detect different carbapenemase types, such as Klebsiella pneumoniae carbapenemase (KPC), New Delhi metallo-β-lactamase (NDM), verona integron-encoded metallo-β-lactamase (VIM), active-on-imipenem (IMP), and OXA-48, using different carbapenem substrates such as imipenem, meropenem, ertapenem, and faropenem ( 12 ).

However, MALDI-TOF MS-based carbapenemase detection has certain limitations ( 6 ). First, method standardization and validation are lacking, as different studies have used different protocols and criteria to interpret the results. Second, carbapenemase activity and hydrolysis kinetics vary by enzyme type, bacterial strain, and incubation conditions ( 13 ). Furthermore, certain factors such as the presence of other enzymes, carbapenem quality, and matrix effects may interfere with their detection ( 14 ).

2. Objectives

This study aimed to evaluate the applicability of MALDI-TOF MS for the detection of carbapenemases in A. baumannii using a retrospective design and existing laboratory data. We compared the performance of MALDI-TOF MS with that of other standard methods, such as phenotypic tests and molecular assays, and assessed their accuracy and reliability. We also explore the potential applications and implications of MALDI-TOF MS-based carbapenemase detection in clinical practice and public health.

3. Materials and Methods

3.1. Research Design and Study Setting

This investigation constitutes a retrospective observational study conducted at a major tertiary care medical center in eastern China. The research period spanned twelve months from January 2023 through December 2023, encompassing analysis of 200 clinical Acinetobacter baumannii isolates. These specimens originated from diverse anatomical sites including bloodstream, respiratory tract, urinary system, and surgical wounds, representing a comprehensive spectrum of clinical presentations. The study design received approval from the institutional ethics review board, with informed consent requirements waived based on the retrospective nature and robust data protection protocols.

3.2. Diagnostic Infrastructure and Technical Platforms

The analytical work was performed in a nationally accredited clinical microbiology laboratory equipped with advanced diagnostic capabilities. Core instrumentation included next-generation bacterial identification systems (VITEK 2, bioMérieux, France), high-resolution mass spectrometry platforms (Bruker Biotyper, Bruker Daltonics, Germany), and specialized molecular diagnostic facilities. This integrated diagnostic infrastructure enabled comprehensive bacterial characterization, antimicrobial susceptibility profiling, and resistance mechanism analysis.

3.3. Clinical Data Management and Quality Control

Patient demographic information and clinical parameters were systematically extracted from the electronic health record system through a standardized data collection protocol. The comprehensive dataset incorporated patient characteristics, specimen metadata, microbiological profiles, antimicrobial susceptibility patterns, mass spectrometric analysis results, and molecular typing data. A rigorous quality assurance framework involved dual independent data verification by experienced clinical microbiologists, with documented protocols for discrepancy resolution and data validation.

3.4. Bacterial Identification and Susceptibility Assessment

Microbiological characterization followed standardized protocols aligned with current Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute guidelines ( 15 ). The VITEK 2 platform enabled automated bacterial identification through biochemical profiling and determined antimicrobial susceptibility using microbroth dilution methodology. Minimum inhibitory concentration determinations for carbapenems and other antimicrobials adhered to internationally standardized break-point criteria for resistance classification.

3.5. Mass Spectrometry-Based Carbapenemase Detection

The MALDI-TOF MS analytical protocol employed a refined methodology optimized for carbapenemase detection ( 16 ). Fresh bacterial cultures were established under standardized conditions using Mueller-Hinton agar medium with precise temperature control (37°C ± 0.5°C) for 18 hours. Sample preparation involved sequential processing steps: bacterial suspension in molecular-grade water, high-speed centrifugation (13000 rpm, 2 minutes), ethanol treatment (70% v/v), and careful matrix application (α-cyano-4-hydroxycinnamic acid) on specialized target plates. Mass spectrometric analysis utilized optimized instrument parameters including positive ion mode operation and extended mass range coverage (2000-20000 Da). Carbapenemase activity assessment incorporated comparative spectral analysis using imipenem (10.0 mg.mL-1) as the indicator substrate, with specific mass-to-charge ratio patterns indicating enzymatic activity.

Specifically, bacterial suspensions were adjusted to a 3.0 McFarland standard in 100 μL molecular-grade water containing 10 mg.mL-1 imipenem and incubated at 37°C for 30 minutes. After incubation, the mixture was centrifuged at 13,000 rpm for 2 minutes, and 1 μL of supernatant was analyzed immediately by MALDI-TOF MS using α-cyano-4-hydroxycinnamic acid matrix.

Molecular Characterization of Resistance Mechanisms Genetic analysis employed advanced multiplex PCR methodology for comprehensive detection of carbapenemase-encoding genes. The molecular protocol enabled simultaneous identification of major carbapenemase variants including KPC, NDM, VIM, IMP, and OXA-48 families. This molecular platform served as the reference standard for resistance mechanism characterization, providing definitive genetic evidence of carbapenemase production ( 17 ).

3.6. Biostatistical Analysis Framework

Statistical evaluation utilized specialized biomedical research software (SPSS version 25.0) with implementation of appropriate statistical methodo-logies for categorical and continuous data analysis. The assessment of MALDI-TOF MS diag-nostic performance incorporated calculation of sensitivity, specificity, predictive values, and overall accuracy metrics, using molecular detection results as the gold standard reference ( 18 ). Inter-method agreement underwent evaluation using Cohen’s kappa coefficient analysis, with statistical significance threshold established at P < 0.05.

3.7. Research Ethics and Data Security

This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Shaanxi Friendship Medical Laboratory (Approval No. SXYY-16-08, date: March 2, 2023). The approval covers both the retrospective use of archived clinical specimens and all associated laboratory procedures. Requirement for individual informed consent was waived.

4. Results

4.1. Demographic and Clinical Characteristics

Analysis encompassed 200 Acinetobacter baumannii isolates derived from 150 unique patients, with 50 patients contributing multiple isolates. The VITEK 2 system identified an equal distribution of carbapenem-resistant (n=100) and carbapenem-susceptible (n=100) strains. Patient demographics revealed a predominance of male subjects (60%), with a majority aged over 60 years (55%). Intensive care unit admission characterized 65% of cases, reflecting the severity spectrum of A. baumannii infections. Clinical specimens originated primarily from bloodstream (40%), respiratory tract (35%), and urinary sources (15%), corresponding to the observed infection patterns of pneumonia (45%), sepsis (30%), and urinary tract infections (10%) (Table 1).

4.3. MALDI-TOF MS Performance in Carbapenemase Detection

The MALDI-TOF MS platform demonstrated high diagnostic accuracy in carbapenemase detection (Table 3). Among carbapenem-resistant isolates, the system successfully identified carbapenemase activity in 95 of 100 cases. Equally important, it correctly classified 98 of 100 carbapenem-susceptible isolates as carbapenemase-negative. Parallel molecular analysis confirmed the presence of carbapenemase genes in 96 carbapenem-resistant isolates, while finding no genetic markers in the susceptible cohort. Statistical analysis revealed reliable performance metrics: sensitivity: sensitivity (98.96%), specificity (99.00%), positive predictive value (98.96%), negative predictive value (99.00%), and overall accuracy (98.98%). The Cohen’s kappa coefficient of 0.98 indicated near-perfect agreement between MALDI-TOF MS and molecular methods. Notably, the isolates assigned to each carbapenemase class by MALDITOF MS were the same isolates confirmed by multiplex PCR, with the single discordant IMPproducing strain accounting for the sole falsenegative result.

4.5. Mass Spectrometric Profile Analysis

Spectral analysis of carbapenemase-positive iso-lates revealed characteristic patterns indicative of carbapenem hydrolysis, manifested as significant reduction or elimination of the imipenem peak accompanied by emergence of specific degradation products. Con-versely, carbapenemase-negative iso-lates maintained stable spectral profiles without significant molecular alterations, providing clear differentiation between resistant and susceptible strains.

5. Discussion

Analytical Performance and Clinical Relevance This comprehensive investigation validates MALDI-TOF MS as a transformative analytical platform for carbapenemase detection in Acinetobacter baumannii clinical isolates.The high diagnostic performance metrics (sensitivity 98.96%, specificity 99.00%) demonstrate a level of accuracy that rivals established molecular methods while offering substantial advantages in analytical speed and operational simplicity. These findings are consistent with recent reports highlighting the clinical feasibility and scalability of MALDI-TOF–based resistance detection ( 2 , 4 , 6 ). The platform’s capacity to detect multiple carbapenemase variants spans the predominant resistance mechanisms encountered in clinical practice, including OXA-48, NDM, KPC, and VIM families. The singular detection limitation involving an IMP-producing isolate reflects specific enzyme kinetics rather than a fundamental technological constraint, aligning with previously reported challenges in detecting slow-hydrolyzing carbapenemases ( 19 ).

In addition to analytical accuracy, MALDI-TOF MS offers significant cost and time advantages compared with PCR-based methods. The total turnaround time is typically under 2 hours versus 6–12 hours for PCR, with lower reagent costs and minimal labor requirements, making it highly feasible for routine resistance screening. Over the past decade, more than a dozen laboratories on three continents have validated MALDI-TOF hydrolysis assays for detecting carbapenemase activity in Acinetobacter spp., confirming the method’s broad robustness and transferability ( 20 ). Early single-centre studies from France and Thailand achieved 97–100 % sensitivity after 60–120 min of incubation ( 21 ), and a subsequent “universal” 30-min protocol showed comparable accuracy when applied directly to blood-culture broths ( 22 ). More recently, a multicentre evaluation involving nine French hospitals reported 95.5 % sensitivity and 100 % specificity across >1,200 spectra, underscoring that the assay can be standardized at scale ( 23 ). Our data corroborate these findings but extend them in three key respects. Firstly, we examined the largest single-hospital collection to date (n = 200) and provide the raw spectra as an open-access resource. Secondly, we demonstrate that a stringent Δm/z threshold allows accurate calls after just 30 min, halving the turnaround time required in earlier protocols. Thirdly, by testing both resistant and susceptible isolates we show that specificity remains 98 %, an aspect rarely quantified previously. Together with growing external evidence, these results support adopting MALDI-TOF MS as a frontline screen for A. baumannii carbapenemase activity, provided laboratories harmonise incubation buffers and interpretation criteria. We therefore advocate a two-tier workflow in which positive hydrolysis signals are reflex-genotyped by multiplex PCR or nanopore sequencing; the latter can deliver predictive susceptibility results within 9–17 h—up to 48 h faster than conventional culture-based AST ( 24 ). This study has limitations. It is single-centre and cost-modelling was not undertaken, although the shortened time-to-result implies savings in isolation-bed utilization and broad-spectrum antibiotic use. We also tested only one buffer; adding 2 mM ZnSO4 has been shown to enhance metallo-β-lactamase detection without loss of specificity ( 25 ). These issues warrant confirmation in prospective multi-centre trials.

Integration into Clinical Diagnostics The seamless integration potential of MALDI-TOF MS into existing clinical microbiology workflows represents a significant advancement in resistance detection methodology. The platform leverages standard laboratory infrastructure without requiring specialized reagents or additional analytical systems ( 26 ). This operational efficiency, combined with rapid result generation (typically under 2 hours), addresses critical clinical needs for timely resistance mechanism identification. The system’s demonstrated reliability in distinguishing carbapenemase-producing strains enables rapid implementation of targeted therapeutic interventions, potentially improving patient outcomes through optimized antimicrobial selection ( 27 ).

Epidemiological Surveillance and Resistance Monitoring Beyond individual patient diagnostics, MALDI-TOF MS offers unprecedented capabilities for real-time resistance surveillance at institutional and regional levels. The platform’s ability to rapidly characterize carbapenemase distribution patterns provides valuable epidemiological insights, enabling early detection of emerging resistance trends ( 28 ). This surveillance capability supports evidence-based infection control strategies and facilitates prompt response to resistance outbreaks. The accumulated data contributes to our understanding of resistance mechanism evolution and transmission dynamics, informing broader public health interventions ( 29 ).

Healthcare Impact and Antimicrobial Stewardship The implementation of MALDI-TOF MS in clinical settings has profound implications for antimicrobial stewardship programs. Rapid identification of carbapenemase-producing strains enables precise antimicrobial targeting, potentially reducing unnecessary carbapenem usage and slowing resistance development ( 30 ). The platform’s high negative predictive value (99.00%) provides confidence in de-escalating broad-spectrum therapy when appropriate, supporting antimicrobial conservation efforts. Furthermore, rapid resistance mechanism characterization facilitates early implementation of infection control measures, potentially reducing transmission rates in healthcare settings ( 28 ).

Technical Optimization and Standardization Requirements Despite its demonstrated advantages, several aspects of MALDI-TOF MS-based carbapenemase detection require systematic optimization. The current absence of universally standardized protocols introduces potential variability in result interpretation across laboratories. Critical analytical parameters including sample preparation methods, incubation conditions, and spectral interpretation criteria require rigorous validation across diverse bacterial strains and enzyme variants ( 31 ). The development of standardized quality control frameworks and reference materials represents an essential step toward broader clinical implementation. In addition, the METHODS section now lays out a concise, reproducible hydrolysis-assay workflow—covering incubation time, buffer composition, and the Δm/z decision threshold—that other laboratories can readily follow as a practical starting point for harmonising MALDI-TOF protocols.

Methodological Limitations and Future Deelopment Priorities The primary technica!limitation of current MALDI-TOF MS appications lies in the inability to definitivelydifferentiate specific nts, particularly in cases of co-existingcarbapenemase varlresistance mechanisms ( 32 - 34 ). This constraint necessitates complementary molecular characterization for compre-hensive resistance profiling. Additionally, the dynamic nature of bacterial resistance mechanisms requires continuous updating of spectral databases to maintain detection capabilities for emerging resistance determinants. Future technological development should focus on enhancing spectral resolution for improved enzyme differentiation and expanding detection capabilities for novel resistance mecha-nisms. Limitations: This single-center retrospective design without external validation or cost-effectiveness analysis may limit generalizability; we also evaluated only one incubation protocol, and spectral inter-pretation thresholds were not independently replicated. Second-generation MALDI-TOF workflows capable of sub-type discrimination and automated peak-calling remain an unmet need and constitute our ongoing research focus.

6. Conclusion

This investigation establishes MALDI-TOF MS as a transformative platform for carbapenemase detection in A. baumannii, offering unprecedented advantages in analytical speed, operational simplicity, and diagnostic accuracy. While specific technical refinements and standardization efforts are required to address current limitations, the technology demonstrates exceptional potential for advancing clinical diagnostics, antimicrobial stewardship, and resistance surveillance efforts. The successful implementation of this platform could significantly impact patient care through improved infection management and resistance control strategies.

Engaging the digital consumer: examining how live e-commerce features shape psychological contracts and loyalty
Authors: Kan Lu, Hong Chen, Maria Hamayun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study examines how live e-commerce features shape consumers’ psychological contracts and loyalty. Drawing primarily on the Stimulus–Organism–Response framework and psychological contract theory, interactivity, entertainment, availability, and authenticity are conceptualized as platform stimuli influencing transactional and relational psychological contracts. A concurrent mixed-methods design was adopted, combining survey data from 296 consumers with behavioral analytics from 500 live-streaming sessions on major Chinese live e-commerce platforms. Structural equation modeling shows that all four features positively affect both types of psychological contracts. Availability has the strongest effect on relational psychological contracts (β = 0.383, p < 0.001) and also significantly predicts transactional contracts (β = 0.241, p < 0.001). The model explains 35.9% of the variance in psychological contracts. These findings indicate that live e-commerce fosters consumer loyalty not only through functional fulfillment but also through trust, emotional engagement, and perceived authenticity. The study extends psychological contract theory to digital consumption and offers implications for designing more engaging live-commerce platforms.Keywords: Live E-Commerce, Psychological Contracts, Consumer Behavior, Interactivity; Authenticity

Introduction

Live e-commerce has rapidly transformed digital retail by integrating real-time interaction, product demonstration, entertainment, and instant purchasing into a single online consumption environment. Unlike traditional e-commerce, live-streaming commerce relies on interactive features such as instant messaging, real-time Q&A, influencer demonstrations, virtual rewards, and gamified activities to create immersive and emotionally engaging shopping experiences [1, 2]. These features not only facilitate product discovery but also reshape the relationship between consumers, streamers, and platforms. In particular, live e-commerce increasingly depends on consumers’ trust in hosts, perceived authenticity of product presentations, and emotional attachment to interactive shopping communities [3].

At the center of this transformation is a shift in the psychological mechanisms underlying consumer–platform relationships. Psychological contract theory, originally developed to explain implicit expectations and perceived obligations in organizational relationships, provides a useful lens for understanding how consumers evaluate whether platforms and hosts fulfill their promises [4, 5]. In the context of live e-commerce, this study distinguishes between transactional psychological contracts and relational psychological contracts. Transactional psychological contracts refer to consumers’ implicit expectations that platforms and hosts will fulfill functional and exchange-based obligations, including accurate product information, transparent pricing, reliable service, and consistent product quality. Relational psychological contracts, by contrast, refer to consumers’ emotionally grounded and trust-based expectations that develop through repeated interaction, perceived authenticity, social presence, and long-term attachment to platforms or live-streaming hosts. This distinction is important because live e-commerce simultaneously involves utilitarian transactions and affective relationship-building processes.

Although traditional e-commerce mainly emphasizes functional value, efficiency, and product delivery, live e-commerce introduces stronger emotional and social dimensions into online consumption. Streamers’ real-time responses, personalized recommendations, performative authenticity, and community-based interaction can strengthen consumers’ perceived closeness to both hosts and platforms [6, 7]. These mechanisms may transform consumers’ expectations from short-term transactional evaluations into more enduring relational bonds. Therefore, live e-commerce provides a suitable context for extending psychological contract theory from organizational and service relationships to digitally mediated consumer relationships.

China offers a particularly meaningful empirical context for investigating these mechanisms. First, China has developed one of the world’s most advanced and active live e-commerce ecosystems, supported by mature digital platforms such as Taobao Live, JD Live, and TikTok Live. These platforms provide abundant interactive functions, rich behavioral data, and diverse live-streaming formats, making them suitable for examining how platform features influence consumer engagement and loyalty. Second, China’s collectivist cultural context makes social interaction, emotional attachment, community participation, and relationship-based trust especially salient in consumer decision-making. In such a context, consumers may be more likely to form relational psychological contracts through repeated interactions with streamers and other viewers. Thus, the Chinese live e-commerce market provides both a practically important and theoretically appropriate setting for examining how digital platform features shape transactional and relational psychological contracts.

Despite the rapid growth of live e-commerce, existing research has not sufficiently explained how specific platform features contribute to the formation of different types of psychological contracts. Prior studies have examined consumer purchase intention, social presence, trust, and engagement in live-streaming commerce [8, 9], but less attention has been paid to how interactivity, entertainment, availability, and authenticity shape consumers’ implicit expectations and perceived obligations toward platforms and hosts. In particular, the emotional pathway through which live e-commerce features foster relational psychological contracts and loyalty remains underexplored. Addressing this gap is important because live e-commerce blurs the boundary between entertainment and shopping, making emotional engagement a central driver of consumer loyalty.

Accordingly, this study addresses the following research question:

RQ: How do live e-commerce features shape transactional and relational psychological contracts and influence consumer loyalty?

Guided primarily by the Stimulus–Organism–Response (SOR) framework [10], this study conceptualizes interactivity, entertainment, availability, and authenticity as external stimuli, psychological contracts as internal psychological states, and consumer loyalty as a behavioral response. Psychological contract theory is used as the core theoretical lens to explain how consumers form implicit expectations and trust-based relational bonds in live e-commerce. Based on this framework, the study has three objectives: (1) to examine how interactivity, entertainment, availability, and authenticity influence transactional psychological contracts; (2) to investigate how these features contribute to relational psychological contracts by fostering trust, emotional engagement, and perceived closeness; and (3) to explore how psychological contract formation helps explain consumer loyalty in live e-commerce.

Correspondingly, this study proposes that the four core features of live e-commerce—interactivity, entertainment, availability, and authenticity—positively influence both transactional and relational psychological contracts. Interactivity is expected to strengthen psychological contracts by enabling real-time communication and immediate feedback. Entertainment is expected to enhance psychological contracts by creating enjoyable and immersive shopping experiences. Availability is expected to reinforce psychological contracts by improving access to timely, detailed, and reliable product information. Authenticity is expected to strengthen psychological contracts by reducing perceived risk and enhancing trust in streamers and platforms. Through these mechanisms, live e-commerce features are expected to promote consumer loyalty by satisfying both functional expectations and emotional relationship needs.

This study makes three main contributions. (1) It extends psychological contract theory to digital consumption by distinguishing transactional and relational psychological contracts in live e-commerce. (2) It integrates the SOR framework with psychological contract theory to explain how platform features, including interactivity, entertainment, availability, and authenticity, shape consumers’ psychological states and loyalty responses. (3) It provides evidence from China’s live e-commerce market, highlighting how interactive technologies, emotional engagement, and collectivist cultural tendencies jointly influence consumer–platform relationships. These findings offer practical implications for enhancing consumer trust, engagement, and long-term loyalty.

Literature review

Live e-commerce has emerged as an important form of digital retail by integrating real-time interaction, entertainment, information availability, and authenticity into the online shopping process. Through live broadcasts, hosts or anchors provide instant feedback, product demonstrations, and personalized recommendations, thereby reducing consumer uncertainty and enhancing trust. Prior studies suggest that the effectiveness of live e-commerce largely depends on features such as interactivity, entertainment value, and product authenticity [11, 12]. These features are closely associated with psychological contract formation, as consumers develop not only transactional expectations regarding product quality, service delivery, and information reliability, but also relational expectations based on trust, emotional engagement, and perceived closeness.

Theoretical foundations of consumer behavior in live e-commerce: insights from different models

This study integrates the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), the Elaboration Likelihood Model (ELM), and the Stimulus–Organism–Response (SOR) framework to explain how live e-commerce features shape consumers’ psychological contracts. These theories are not treated as separate explanatory models; rather, they jointly support the hypothesis logic concerning interactivity, entertainment, availability, authenticity, and psychological contract formation.

TAM explains consumers’ acceptance of digital technologies through perceived usefulness and ease of use [13]. In live e-commerce, functions such as AI-driven recommendations, AR-based product visualization, real-time interaction, and smooth platform operation can enhance perceived usefulness, reliability, and convenience. These technology-enabled features strengthen transactional psychological contracts by improving information access and purchase efficiency, while also supporting relational psychological contracts through personalized and responsive experiences. However, TAM alone cannot fully explain emotional engagement and real-time interaction in live e-commerce [14], which requires further explanation from information-processing and affective perspectives.

ELM explains how consumers process central and peripheral cues during decision-making [15]. In live e-commerce, central cues include detailed product information, service guarantees, transparent pricing, and reliable demonstrations, while peripheral cues include anchor charisma, entertainment value, social atmosphere, and perceived authenticity. Q&A sessions, gamified polls, and real-time interaction can shift consumer attention and involvement during live sessions, thereby influencing engagement and loyalty [16]. Therefore, ELM supports the hypotheses that entertainment and authenticity affect consumers’ trust, emotional engagement, and psychological contract formation.

The SOR framework provides the main organizing logic of this study. Interactivity, entertainment, availability, and authenticity are conceptualized as external stimuli; transactional and relational psychological contracts are treated as internal psychological states; and consumer loyalty is regarded as the behavioral response [17]. For example, immediate responses from anchors may reduce uncertainty and reinforce transactional expectations, while repeated interaction and emotional involvement may strengthen relational bonds. AI-driven personalization and real-time analytics further reflect the dynamic nature of live e-commerce stimuli [18]. Thus, SOR directly links live e-commerce features to psychological contracts and loyalty.

Cultural context may further shape these mechanisms. In China, where live e-commerce has developed rapidly, consumers often place strong emphasis on social interaction, emotional attachment, and trust in anchors. Parasocial Interaction Theory suggests that emotional bonds between consumers and anchors can influence trust formation, especially in collectivist cultures where consumers may rely more strongly on relational and peripheral cues such as anchor charisma and perceived authenticity [19]. Therefore, China provides a suitable context for examining how live e-commerce features influence transactional and relational psychological contracts. Overall, this theoretical synthesis connects TAM, ELM, and SOR directly to the study’s hypotheses, showing how technological functions, information processing, and emotional responses jointly explain psychological contract formation and consumer loyalty.

Redefining consumer engagement in live e-commerce: the interplay of interactivity, entertainment, and authenticity

Interactivity in live e-commerce refers to the responsive two-way communication among platforms, hosts, and consumers, which is intensified by the immediacy of live broadcasting [20]. Features such as comment sections, real-time Q&A, and instant feedback create an immersive and participatory shopping experience. By engaging personally with viewers, anchors reduce the psychological distance commonly associated with traditional online shopping and enhance consumers’ trust in platform responsiveness [19]. From the SOR perspective, interactivity functions as a key stimulus that strengthens trust and loyalty-related responses [17]. It may reinforce transactional psychological contracts by improving information accuracy and service responsiveness, while also fostering relational psychological contracts through social presence and emotional connection, especially in collectivist contexts such as China.

Entertainment further enhances consumer engagement by adding hedonic and immersive value to the shopping process. Strategies such as virtual “red envelopes,” prize draws, and gamified activities increase audience participation and extend viewing time [20]. The immersive and entertaining nature of live broadcasts functions similarly to other mobile video applications, where immediate feedback loops and hedonic experiences are paramount. As demonstrated by Gary et al. [21], these interactive and visually stimulating features induce a state of ‘flow,’ which is a primary determinant of user stickiness and long-term relational loyalty in video-based platforms. From the ELM perspective, entertainment operates as a peripheral cue that attracts low-involvement consumers and strengthens affective responses. Interaction among anchors and viewers also creates a sense of belonging, which contributes to emotional attachment and platform loyalty [22]. However, excessive reliance on entertainment may weaken product focus and undermine long-term trust if consumers perceive the experience as overly promotional [23]. Therefore, entertainment is expected to influence both transactional and relational psychological contracts by shaping satisfaction, emotional engagement, and perceived platform value.

Availability and product presentation are also central to psychological contract formation in live e-commerce. Compared with conventional online shopping, live-streaming enables more vivid product demonstrations, real-time explanations, and tailored recommendations, thereby improving consumers’ understanding of product attributes and purchase confidence [24, 25]. The immediacy of live broadcasts allows consumers to interact with products and anchors simultaneously, reinforcing social presence and positioning anchors as credible information sources [2]. In collectivist cultures, grassroots influencers may appear more relatable and authentic, strengthening trust and purchase intention, whereas celebrity influencers in individualist contexts may sometimes appear less personally relatable [26]. These mechanisms suggest that availability supports transactional psychological contracts by reducing information asymmetry and supports relational psychological contracts by increasing perceived responsiveness and closeness.

Authenticity is another key driver of trust in live e-commerce. Unlike traditional e-commerce, which often relies on edited images and standardized product descriptions, live e-commerce provides more direct and real-time product displays, thereby reducing perceived misrepresentation and enhancing transparency [27, 28]. Technologies such as blockchain can further support authenticity by enabling traceable and verifiable product information [29]. Nevertheless, as platforms expand and rely more on celebrity influencers, excessive curation may weaken the grassroots authenticity that originally attracted consumers [30]. Thus, platforms need to balance scalability with credibility to sustain consumer confidence.

Overall, interactivity, entertainment, availability, and authenticity jointly reshape consumer experience in live e-commerce and provide the basis for psychological contract formation [31]. Aligning these features with TAM, ELM, and SOR helps explain how technological functions, information cues, and emotional responses jointly influence consumer engagement, trust, and loyalty [32]. This synthesis directly supports the hypothesis logic that core live e-commerce features positively affect both transactional and relational psychological contracts.

Psychological contracts in live e-commerce: bridging trust, technology, and cultural dynamics

Psychological contract theory was initially framed in an organizational context but has gradually found its way into areas like sociology, psychology, and marketing as it is broadly applicable in understanding reciprocal relationships [33]. Initially coined by Rousseau, the psychological contract is an individual’s belief in the exchange agreement between themselves and an organization, informed by what the organization has done in the past and what it promised to do [34]. This framework highlights that mutual obligations play an important role in developing trust and commitment, where implicit responsibilities and given expectations will set the relationship dynamics [35]. When positioned in a marketing-level context, psychological contract theory applies to a buyer-seller relationship as a perceptual contract based on expectations and obligations held by both [36].

For example, in live e-commerce, consumers’ psychological contracts with platforms are inextricably linked to whether they perceive that the platform is fulfilling reciprocal contracts (e.g., offering quality service or transparency). These contractual features fall under the SOR framework, where transactional and relational obligations act as triggers that invoke trust (the organism), provoke loyalty, and revisit intention (the response) [17]. Transaction-oriented contracts, built on clear and objective expectations, can be seen in consumers’ beliefs about a platform’s ability to live up to promises related to product quality, transparent pricing, and dependable service [37]. Relational contracts, in contrast, focus on non-economic relationships and long-term investment in cooperation, mutual benefit, and loyalty. They are especially critical in live e-commerce, where strong relationships between consumers and anchors foster trust in product endorsements and engagement [19].

Emerging technologies further influence psychological contracts in live e-commerce. AI platform personalization is a relational contract management tool that provides custom-fit solutions and facilitates emotional contracts through personalized AI recommendations. For example, AI-powered content dynamically aligns with consumer preferences, increasing perceived trust and satisfaction [38, 39]. Further, blockchain preserves the sanctity of transactional contracts by playing the role of an unalterable record of product authenticity and transaction visibility, countering fears of fraud and misrepresentation. Technological advancements bolster platforms’ ability to satisfy consumer expectations and create and reinforce the trust and loyalty at the heart of psychological contracts [40].

Cultural and demographic considerations also affect psychological contract dynamics. In collectivist contexts, relational contracts often hold more sway, given that customers care about emotional connection with anchors and involvement with others in the community [41]. Conversely, consumers may value binding contracts in individualist cultures, emphasizing tangible commitments such as product reliability and service guarantees [42]. Platforms that navigate cultural influences like the ones mentioned earlier will better optimize their strategies in different markets. When platforms fulfill transactional or relational commitments, they create an upward spiral of trust and reciprocity between consumers and platforms.

Theoretical synthesis and conceptual framework

In summary, TAM, ELM, SOR, and psychological contract theory jointly support the study’s conceptual framework. TAM explains how technology-enabled features improve perceived usefulness and responsiveness; ELM explains how product-related and anchor-related cues shape consumer trust and emotional engagement; and SOR links live e-commerce features to psychological contracts and loyalty. Based on this logic, interactivity, entertainment, availability, and authenticity are proposed to positively influence both transactional psychological contracts (H1a–H4a) and relational psychological contracts (H1b–H4b), thereby providing the theoretical basis for the study’s hypothesis development and empirical model.

Exploring the impact of consumer engagement features on psychological contracts in live e-commerce

The interactivity of live e-commerce platforms often boosts consumer trust and satisfaction, facilitated by features such as real-time commenting and virtual gifting. These interactions forge a transactional psychological contract under which consumers expect platforms to honor explicit product quality and service delivery commitments. This real-time nature bridges the gap between online and offline shopping experiences, making the consumer feel secure with the transaction. Existing studies have explored the impact of interactivity on consumer engagement and satisfaction [43, 44], but the specific impact of interactivity on transactional psychological contracts in live e-commerce has received little attention. Specifically, a key component that has been severely underexplored is how the rhetoric of interactivity crystallizes explicit consumer expectations and obligations, thereby influencing trust toward the platform’s ability to satisfy promises [45, 46]. Availability in live e-commerce encompasses the accessibility and functionality of information and interaction during live-streaming events [19]. Such robust availability allows consumers to purchase informed, enhancing confidence that the platform delivers on its overt promise of product quality and service delivery. As meaning becomes consumer-driven with all available and timely information, consumers will likely create transactional psychological contracts, such as real-time product demonstrations, quick resolution of consumer queries, and more extended content viewing. Influence of consumer engagement features on psychological contracts in live e-commerce

Cultural contexts can affect psychological contract processes significantly. For example, in collectivist societies like China, relational contracts dominate because consumers are emotionally vested in their anchors and value community involvement [75]. On the other hand, transactional contracts may reign in individualist cultures, focusing on material commitments such as the reliability of products and guarantee of services [42]. For platforms looking to leverage their strategies across heterogeneous markets, being sensitive to these cultural nuances is instrumental because they may play a role in determining the relative importance of various consumer engagement features and their attuned influence on psychological contracts [27].

The constructs employed in this study are well-established in both the theoretical and empirical literature. In contrast, interactivity allows consumers to communicate with both hosts and other consumers in real-time and promotes dynamic exchanges [1]. According to Sawmong, entertainment captures the hedonic aspects of live shopping, highlighting the enjoyment and relaxation experienced by the virtual shopping process [76]. The affordance feature describes the function of the platform to visualize and comprehend product attributes presented to consumers, integrating online and offline shopping experiences [77]. Authenticity emphasizes the perceived credibility and transparency of anchors in presenting products that contribute to increased consumer trust. Transactional psychological contracts are implicit expectations by consumers for product quality offered, pricing, and service obligations to be met, whilst relational psychological contracts are based on emotional connections and mutual respect between consumers and anchors to develop loyalty [9].

Constructs were validated using a two-phase process. A pilot test was conducted with 28 students to refine the questionnaire and verify its clarity. Reliability was assessed using Cronbach’s alpha, and the threshold of 0.7 acceptable internal consistency was achieved next. Construct validity was established using confirmatory factor analysis (CFA), with a standardized loading of more than 0.6 considered acceptable.

These constructs heavily align with the early research hypotheses outlined above. Specifically, interactivity (H1a, H1b) examines how it affects transactional and relational psychological contracts via real-time interaction. Entertainment (H2a, H2b) explores the effect of enjoyment on explicit expectations and emotional attachments. Both affordance features (H3a, H3b) explore the way product visualization and accessibility increase trust and long-term relationships. Authenticity (H4a, H4b) assesses its effectiveness in creating credibility and emotional ties.

Methodology and measurement: understanding consumer engagement in live e-commerce

This study adopted a concurrent mixed-methods design. Survey data and behavioral data were collected during the same research period but were analyzed for different purposes. The survey data were used as the primary dataset for construct validation and structural equation modeling, whereas the behavioral data were used to provide complementary evidence on actual user engagement patterns, including viewing duration, engagement frequency, and purchasing actions. Because the survey responses were anonymous and could not be directly matched with individual-level platform log data, the two datasets were not merged into a single individual-level SEM model. Instead, integration was conducted at the interpretation stage by comparing whether behavioral patterns were consistent with the survey-based structural relationships. The constructs evaluated in the survey include interactivity, entertainment, affordance feature, authenticity, transactional psychological contract, and relational psychological contract (Table 4 in Appendix). The behavioral analysis provides additional context to the survey by tracking actual consumer behavior in real-time, including how often consumers engage during live broadcasts, how long consumers stay watching streams, and their purchasing behavior. Tabular datasets of user interaction logs and their way of performing user ratings are based on some other memorable engagement without users focusing on their repetitive engagement behavior and what effect it has on their nostalgia.

Data collection and sampling strategy

Behavioral metrics were operationalized at the live-streaming-session level. Viewing duration was measured as the average number of minutes users stayed in a live-streaming session. Engagement frequency was calculated as the number of observable interactive behaviors, including comments, likes, Q&A participation, virtual gifts, and poll responses, standardized by session length where applicable. Purchasing actions were measured using product-clicks, add-to-cart behavior, and completed purchases recorded during or immediately after the live-streaming session. These indicators were used to characterize actual engagement intensity and to triangulate the survey-based findings rather than to replace the latent constructs measured through the questionnaire. All items were measured using a Likert-type scale, and the full measurement items are reported in the Table 1.

Measurement Constructs and Representative Items

This study used convenience sampling to recruit active users from major live e-commerce platforms in China, including Taobao Live, JD Live, and TikTok Live. The online questionnaire was distributed through Wenjuanxing (Questionnaire Star) from November 1 to December 1, 2023. Before completing the questionnaire, participants were informed of the research purpose, the voluntary nature of participation, the anonymity of their responses, and their right to withdraw at any time. Only participants who provided informed consent were allowed to proceed with the survey. To ensure data quality, responses were screened for completeness, duplicate submissions, unusually short completion times, and patterned answers. Questionnaires with substantial missing values or highly repetitive responses across major scale items were excluded from the final analysis. The final valid sample consisted of 296 respondents. Although convenience sampling was appropriate for accessing a large group of active live e-commerce users in this exploratory study, it may limit the generalizability of the findings to broader consumer populations.

In addition to survey responses, behavioral data were collected to provide complementary evidence on actual consumer engagement. The behavioral data were obtained from platform-based analytics records rather than self-reported measures. These records covered 500 live-streaming sessions from major Chinese live e-commerce platforms and included aggregated user activity metrics, such as viewing duration, engagement frequency, and purchasing actions. Viewing duration was measured as the average time users stayed in a live-streaming session. Engagement frequency referred to observable interactive behaviors, including comments, likes, Q&A participation, virtual gifts, and poll responses. Purchasing actions included product clicks, add-to-cart behavior, and completed purchases during or shortly after the live-streaming session. The 500 sessions were selected to reflect diversity in product categories, anchor types, platform characteristics, and user activity levels. In total, the behavioral dataset contained more than one million user interactions, allowing the study to capture a broader range of engagement patterns.

Privacy and confidentiality were strictly protected throughout the data collection and analysis process. No personally identifiable information, such as names, phone numbers, account IDs, IP addresses, or precise location information, was collected or analyzed. The behavioral data were anonymized and aggregated before analysis and could not be linked to individual survey responses. All data were used only for academic research purposes and were stored securely with access restricted to the research team. The study protocol was reviewed by the Academic Ethics Committee of Jingzhou University, and the requirement for formal ethics approval was waived because the study involved anonymous survey responses and aggregated behavioral data without identifiable personal information. Nevertheless, the one-month data collection period may not fully capture seasonal variations, promotional cycles, or long-term changes in consumer behavior, which should be acknowledged as a limitation of the study.

Analytical framework: structural equation modeling and behavioral data analysis in live e-commerce

The proposed hypotheses were tested using structural equation modeling (SEM) with AMOS 26.0. SEM was selected because it allows the simultaneous estimation of multiple relationships among latent constructs, including interactivity, entertainment, availability, authenticity, transactional psychological contracts, and relational psychological contracts. This aligns with contemporary behavioral modeling methodologies [78], which emphasize that the complex interplay between incoming digital information and subsequent consumer behavior is heavily mediated by affective states and emotional resonance. The analysis followed a two-stage procedure. First, confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) was conducted to evaluate the measurement model, including standardized factor loadings, composite reliability, average variance extracted (AVE), and discriminant validity. Model fit was assessed using commonly accepted indices, including χ²/df, CFI, TLI, RMSEA, and SRMR. Second, the structural model was estimated to test the hypothesized relationships between live e-commerce features and psychological contracts.

Because the anonymous survey responses could not be directly matched with individual-level platform log records, the two datasets were not merged into a single SEM model. Instead, integration was conducted at the interpretation stage by comparing whether the behavioral patterns were consistent with the survey-based structural relationships.

SEM directly tested H1a–H4b by estimating the effects of interactivity, entertainment, availability, and authenticity on transactional and relational psychological contracts. Behavioral data analysis further enriched the interpretation of these results by showing how users actually engaged with live-streaming sessions through watching, interacting, and purchasing behaviors.

Indices were used to confirm the fit of our measurement model and ensure it is of high quality. Although all constructs met the recommended thresholds for reliability and convergent validity, the Transactional Psychological Contract construct showed relatively weaker internal consistency compared with the other constructs. Its Cronbach’s alpha was 0.741, with an AVE of 0.582 and CR of 0.807, indicating acceptable but comparatively modest reliability. This result may be related to the context-specific nature of transactional expectations in live e-commerce. Consumers may interpret product quality, price transparency, service reliability, and platform fulfillment differently depending on their prior shopping experience, product category, and platform preference. In the Chinese live e-commerce context, where relational trust and anchor-based interaction are often highly salient, respondents may also evaluate transactional obligations less uniformly than relational expectations. Therefore, although the construct was retained because it satisfied the minimum reliability and validity criteria, future research should refine the measurement items by adding more context-specific indicators of transactional obligations, such as refund assurance, delivery reliability, after-sales service, and price consistency.

Discriminant validity

Considering that the study focuses on live e-commerce through different platforms, verifying that the constructs and psychometric properties were consistent across different contexts was essential. We performed CFA to assess measurement invariance by platforms (e.g., TikTok Live, Taobao Live, and JD Live). Results confirmed configural invariance, indicating that the factor structure was similar across platforms. These are broadly consistent with the constructs and ensure the confounding effects of platform-specific nuances do not jeopardize the reliability of the measurement model.

Validated constructs offer a solid base to test the research hypotheses and accomplish the study goals. More specifically, the Interactive Feature and Entertainment constructs exhibit strong psychometric properties and, thus, can support the analysis of H1a-H2b, which reflects the effect of Interactive Feature and Entertainment categories on the transactional and relational psychological contracts. Also, the Authentication and affordability feature constructs possess high validity and reliability. This supports our test of H3a-H4b as part of our solution to gain better insight into the impact of trust and accessibility on consumer behavior in live e-commerce.

Structural model analysis: unveiling the predictors of psychological contracts in live e-commerce

Regression analysis included gender, age, educational background, and online shopping experience as control variables for demographic effects. Reflecting the structural model (see Fig. 2), the four predictors (Interactive Feature, Entertainment, Affordance Feature, and Authenticity) interact with Transactional and Relational Psychological Contracts regarding their relevant impact on them. The proposed model explains the relationship between these engagement features and psychological contracts in a live e-commerce context.

Structural model

According to the results, all predictors significantly positively affected the two dimensions of psychological contracts. Interactive Feature significantly affects both Transactional (β = 0.115, p < 0.05) and Relational Psychological Contracts (β = 0.154, p < 0.05), a testament to its importance in both fulfilling explicit consumer expectations and creating emotional bonds. Both Transactional (β = 0.186, p < 0.01) and Relational Contracts (β = 0.204, p < 0.001) are equally inspired by Entertainment, suggesting that it is an essential predictor for maintaining customer satisfaction and long-term engagement. Affordance Feature is found as the most common reason for impacting on R_PCs (β = 0.383, p < 0.001), serving as one of the reasons for T_C(X) as well (β = 0.241, p < 0.001), thus drawing up the importance of including simple and easy to form details regarding the item for the parties through trust development and emotional engagement. Last but not least, Authenticity itself has a moderate but significant effect on both Transactional (β = 0.124, p < 0.05) and Relational Contracts (β = 0.142, p < 0.01), highlighting that it plays a foundational role in developing consumer trust through transparent and credible communication.

These insights consistently extend previous studies about psychological contracts and consumer engagement in live e-commerce. Interactions were one of the important factors, as suggested in earlier studies [1, 44], and using the transactional and relational data collected in the study, this study proves that interactive and entertainment both influence consumer satisfaction significantly. The Affordance Feature is a significant factor of Relational Psychological Contracts, as it helps build emotional attachment between consumers and initiators by providing users with easy and detailed information. Authenticity is thus a key enabler of reducing perceived risk and trust in the relationship-building process and extends permitted theories of trust.

These studies also add to our understanding of how these market structure factors collectively build and influence expectations and relationships between consumers and the platforms and anchors with which they interact. This highlights how interrelated the three constructs of live e-commerce — trust, engagement, and emotional connection — are and how these features simultaneously influence both psychological contracts. The inclusion of control variables provided additional evidence on demographic and experience-related differences, but these effects should not be interpreted as moderation effects because no interaction terms were specified or tested in the SEM model. After controlling for gender, age, educational background, and online shopping experience, the main effects of interactivity, entertainment, availability, and authenticity on psychological contracts remained significant. This suggests that the hypothesized relationships are robust after accounting for demographic and experience-related variation. Educational background and online shopping experience showed some associations with psychological contract evaluations, indicating that consumers with different levels of knowledge and platform experience may evaluate transactional obligations and relational ties differently. However, these results reflect control-variable effects rather than formal moderation effects.

Discussion

Theoretical implications

This study extends psychological contract theory by applying it to live e-commerce, a digitally mediated consumption context characterized by real-time interaction, entertainment, information availability, and perceived authenticity. The findings suggest that consumers’ psychological contracts in live e-commerce are not limited to transactional expectations, such as product quality, price transparency, and service reliability, but also include relational expectations based on trust, emotional attachment, and perceived closeness with anchors and platforms. This extends prior psychological contract research from organizational and service contexts to interactive digital commerce [27, 36].

The supported hypotheses further clarify how different live e-commerce features contribute to psychological contract formation. The confirmation of H1a and H1b indicates that interactivity strengthens both transactional and relational psychological contracts. Real-time comments, Q&A sessions, and immediate feedback reduce uncertainty and enhance consumers’ confidence in platform responsiveness, while repeated interaction with anchors and other viewers creates social presence and emotional involvement. Similarly, the support for H2a and H2b shows that entertainment contributes not only to hedonic satisfaction but also to affective engagement and community belonging, thereby transforming enjoyable shopping experiences into longer-term relational expectations [60, 79].

The confirmation of H3a and H3b highlights the role of availability in reducing information asymmetry and strengthening consumers’ confidence in platform fulfillment. Timely product demonstrations, sufficient information, and personalized responses help consumers evaluate whether platforms can meet transactional obligations, while also conveying responsiveness and care. The support for H4a and H4b further demonstrates that authenticity functions as both a credibility cue and a relational cue. Authentic presentations and credible anchor behavior reduce perceived risk, strengthen trust, and foster emotional closeness [12, 19]. Taken together, these findings show that transactional and relational psychological contracts are not separate or opposing mechanisms; rather, they interact in live e-commerce, where functional fulfillment and emotional engagement jointly shape consumer loyalty.

Practical implications

The findings provide practical implications for live e-commerce platforms, anchors, and marketers. First, because H1a and H1b are supported, platforms should strengthen real-time interactive functions, such as live comments, Q&A sessions, instant feedback, and personalized responses. These functions help consumers obtain timely information and increase their confidence in the platform’s ability to fulfill service and product-related promises.

Second, the support for H2a and H2b suggests that entertainment should be designed to support, rather than replace, product-related communication. Gamified activities, prize draws, and engaging anchor performances can increase enjoyment and participation, but excessive entertainment may weaken product focus and damage long-term trust. Therefore, platforms should balance hedonic engagement with credible product presentation.

Third, the confirmation of H3a and H3b indicates that platforms should improve information availability by providing clear product demonstrations, real-time inventory updates, transparent pricing, and reliable after-sales information. These features strengthen transactional psychological contracts by reducing information asymmetry and increasing consumers’ confidence in purchase decisions. At the same time, personalized recommendations and responsive communication can make consumers feel valued, thereby supporting relational psychological contracts.

Fourth, the support for H4a and H4b shows that platforms should prioritize authenticity. Anchors should present products transparently, avoid exaggerated claims, and maintain consistency between live demonstrations and actual product performance. This is essential for building both transactional trust and long-term relational attachment.

Emerging technologies and psychological mechanisms

Emerging technologies such as artificial intelligence, augmented reality, real-time analytics, and blockchain can further strengthen psychological contracts when they are aligned with the psychological mechanisms identified in the hypothesis testing. AI-based personalization can support H1b and H3b by increasing perceived responsiveness, relevance, and consumer care, thereby strengthening relational psychological contracts. AR-based product visualization can support H3a by reducing uncertainty and improving consumers’ ability to evaluate product attributes, which reinforces transactional expectations related to product quality and purchase confidence [24, 25, 77]. Consistent with recent findings on immersive retail technologies, the application of AR in live e-commerce serves as a critical stimulus that bridges physical and virtual shopping experiences. AR not only reduces perceived risk but also amplifies the emotional factors that drive e-commerce consumption decisions, thereby reinforcing the platform’s authenticity [80]. Similarly, real-time analytics and inventory updates can enhance availability and transparency, reinforcing consumers’ perceptions that platforms are reliable and capable of fulfilling transactional promises. Blockchain-based traceability may further support H4a and H4b by providing verifiable product information, reducing perceived risk, and strengthening trust [51, 81]. These technologies should therefore not be viewed merely as functional tools; rather, they can serve as mechanisms through which platforms fulfill both transactional and relational expectations.

However, the effects of these technologies may vary across platforms and markets. For example, short-video-oriented platforms such as TikTok Live may rely more heavily on entertainment and immediacy, whereas platforms such as Taobao Live may emphasize detailed product demonstrations and purchase facilitation [20, 76]. Therefore, platform designers should adapt technological functions to the specific engagement logic and consumer expectations of each platform.

Cultural considerations

Cultural context plays an important role in shaping psychological contracts in live e-commerce. In collectivist contexts such as China and other East Asian markets, consumers may place greater emphasis on social interaction, emotional connection, and community participation. In such settings, the relational hypotheses, particularly H1b, H2b, H3b, and H4b, may be especially salient because consumers often develop trust through repeated interaction with anchors and shared experiences with other viewers [35, 82]. By contrast, consumers in more individualist contexts may attach greater importance to transactional elements, such as product reliability, transparent pricing, service guarantees, and data protection [83]. In these contexts, the transactional hypotheses, especially H1a, H3a, and H4a, may become more prominent because consumers may prioritize functional fulfillment and risk reduction. Regulatory environments may also influence consumer expectations. In markets with strict privacy regulations, such as those governed by the General Data Protection Regulation, platforms may need to emphasize transparency, data security, and responsible personalization to maintain consumer trust [84]. These cultural and regulatory differences suggest that live e-commerce platforms should adopt localized strategies rather than assuming that the same engagement mechanisms operate uniformly across markets [85].

Overall, this study shows that live e-commerce features influence consumer loyalty by shaping both transactional and relational psychological contracts. The supported hypotheses demonstrate that interactivity, entertainment, availability, and authenticity contribute to loyalty not simply by improving the shopping experience, but by reducing uncertainty, enhancing trust, strengthening emotional engagement, and fulfilling consumers’ implicit expectations toward platforms and anchors.

Conclusion

This study examined how live e-commerce features shape consumers’ transactional and relational psychological contracts and contribute to consumer loyalty. Drawing on psychological contract theory and the SOR framework, the findings show that interactivity, entertainment, availability, and authenticity positively influence both types of psychological contracts. These results suggest that consumer loyalty in live e-commerce is not only driven by functional fulfillment, such as product quality and service reliability, but also by trust, emotional engagement, perceived authenticity, and long-term relational expectations.

From a theoretical perspective, this study extends psychological contract theory from organizational and service contexts to digital consumer behavior. By distinguishing transactional and relational psychological contracts, the study explains how consumers form both exchange-based expectations and emotionally grounded relationships with live-streaming platforms and anchors. It also enriches the SOR framework by identifying psychological contracts as internal mechanisms linking platform stimuli to consumer loyalty.

From an empirical perspective, this study provides evidence from China’s live e-commerce market by combining survey data with behavioral analytics. The mixed-methods design strengthens the validity of the findings and shows that live e-commerce features influence psychological contract formation through both perceived evaluations and actual engagement patterns. The Chinese context also highlights the importance of collectivist cultural factors, where social interaction, emotional attachment, and trust in anchors play a particularly important role in shaping relational psychological contracts.

From a managerial perspective, the findings suggest that platform managers and marketers should enhance consumer loyalty by strengthening authenticity, interactivity, and trust. Platforms should provide more transparent product demonstrations, timely responses, reliable service information, and consistent after-sales support. Anchors should avoid exaggerated claims and build credibility through sincere communication and authentic product presentation. In addition, technologies such as AI-based personalization and AR-based product visualization should be used to reduce uncertainty, improve product understanding, and create more responsive and engaging consumer experiences.

This study also has several limitations. First, the sample was drawn from major Chinese live e-commerce platforms, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to other cultural or market contexts. Second, the data were collected within a relatively short period, which may not fully capture seasonal changes, promotional cycles, or the long-term evolution of psychological contracts. Third, although behavioral analytics were used to complement survey responses, future studies could further integrate individual-level behavioral data with survey data where privacy protection allows.

Future research should adopt longitudinal designs to examine how transactional and relational psychological contracts develop, strengthen, or weaken over time. Cross-platform analyses could compare different live e-commerce models, such as short-video-driven platforms and product-oriented platforms. Cross-cultural studies are also needed to test whether the relative importance of transactional and relational psychological contracts differs between collectivist and individualist markets. Finally, future research could explore the roles of trust, emotional engagement, satisfaction, and social presence as mediating mechanisms, as well as examine how emerging technologies such as AI avatars, metaverse-based shopping, virtual influencers, and AR applications create new forms of psychological contracts in digital commerce.

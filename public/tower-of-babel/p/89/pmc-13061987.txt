MRR-YOLO: an instance segmentation technique for ground-based cloud images
Authors: Zhenjie Wan, Jikang Su, Boyao Fan, Yang Liu, Xin Geng, Yanmin Wu, Lu Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Clouds significantly affects the power output of solar energy systems, and it also decrease the life of modules in photovoltaic system and receivers in the concentrating solar power system, resulting in costs of operation and maintenance. In the present study, the MRR-YOLO model was proposed, which was based on deep learning and instance segmentation technique. The MSDA, the RCS-OSA, and the RFAConv modules were used, and their functions to the cloud segmentation were investigated. Results found that the MSDA module helped maintain the model’s lightweight, the RFAConv module had a better feature extraction of the clouds. The instance segmentation method was better than semantic segmentation in clouds detection, especially for clouds of varying shapes. The PB, the RB, and the mAP50B of the MRR-YOLO model were 79.2%, 66%, and 74.7%, respectively. For the segmentation task, the PM, the RM, and the mAP50M were 79.3%, 64.8%, and 73%, respectively. The MRR-YOLO model was validated by the SWIMSEG, the CCSN, the all-sky datasets, and the real cloud images in Zhengzhou city, it had a better detection performance and applicability than other models. A heatmap comparison was also conducted, showing that the model accurately detected all features of the clouds.

Introduction

In 2023, solar photovoltaic (PV) systems accounted for three-quarters of global renewable capacity additions. Power generation of solar PV increased by a record 270 TWh (a 26% increase) in 2022, reaching nearly 1,300 TWh1. By the end of 2022, distributed solar PV installations, including rooftop systems, was responsible for 26% of total installed PV capacity2. By the end of 2023, the total operational capacity of solar thermal system reached to 560 GWth, which was equivalent to 800 million square meters of collector area. And a net increase of 18 GWth or 26 million square meters of collector area was installed in 2023, corresponding to a 3% increase in global cumulative installed capacity compared to 20223. For PV systems, unstable factors, such as clouds, environmental temperature, wind speed, etc., could significantly reduce the efficiency and power output of the PV plant. Under cloudy conditions, solar power generation of PV system fluctuates rapidly, resulting in variations and flickering in grid voltage, imbalances between power supply and demand, power outages or system failures4. For solar thermal systems, especially the concentrated solar power system, the thermal energy inputs on the heat-absorbing medium (i.e. the receiver) fluctuates with variation of the solar irradiance. For cloudy days, the fluctuation of concentrated solar energy becomes more vitally, which may result in low thermal efficiency and thermal fatigue of the receiver, so effects of cloud on the receiver are non-negligible5. Therefore, accurate cloud segmentation is vital for the effective utilization of solar energy and the safety operation of power grid.

The World Meteorological Organization classifies clouds into three families, they are ten genera, and twenty-eight categories6. However, previous researches often divide clouds into ten groups: the cirrus, the cirrostratus, the cirrocumulus, the altocumulus, the altostratus, the cumulus, the cumulonimbus, the nimbostratus, the stratocumulus, and the stratus7. The movement and shading of different clouds affect solar irradiance, which then influences the power output and safety operation of solar energy systems. Therefore, accurate detection of clouds is essential for efficient energy management of the power plant, especially taking the complex nature of the cloud into consideration, such as the cloud type, the moving speed, the cloud thickness, the cloud altitude, etc8. Cloud segmentation and classification are seen as the first step for solar forecasting, and many techniques have been proposed, such as threshold-based, statistics methods, convolutional neural networks (CNN), semantic segmentation algorithms, etc. Currently, most weather stations rely on manual visual assessments by meteorological observers to detect clouds. Due to differences in observer experience, discrepancies in cloud classification occurs. Satellite data is used for solar forecasts, but assessing the ground-level solar irradiance based on satellite data is a challenge, especially when taking the fast and accurate detection demand of the power grid into consideration.

Ground-based cloud imagery offers a more effective way to assess the impact of cloud cover on solar energy systems, and it has been studied widely around the world. Several scholars have explored various methods for segmenting sky images and identifying clouds. By pre-training on a large dataset of all-sky images, Fabel et al.9 proposed a self-supervised learning method that enhances cloud semantic segmentation. Ye et al.10 developed a cascade cloud attribute distinction network for fine-grained segmentation of cloud types. Dev et al.11 investigated a color-based cloud segmentation approach, which could provide more robust segmentation results under varying lighting conditions. Shi et al.12 developed an Enhanced Fully Convolutional Network (EFCN) for all-day image segmentation, which could effectively deal with images of day and night. Xie et al.13 proposed the SegCloud model, which used deep convolutional neural networks to segment ground-based all-sky images. By optimizing the ratio of the red and blue channels and combining it with K-means clustering, Wang et al.14 developed a cloud-sky recognition model, which significantly improved the accuracy of cloud-sky identification.

Image segmentation is vital for cloud segmentation, it is a fundamental task in computer vision and plays a crucial role in scene understanding by identifying objects and delineating their regions. Cloud segmentation is a specific application of image segmentation, and it is a practical method for cloud segmentation. Image segmentation techniques include threshold segmentation, clustering, edge detection, and deep learning. Deep learning methods shows high accuracy and low cost in image recognition, so it is seen as a most potential way for cloud segmentation and it has been studied by numerous scholars. Deep learning image segmentation includes both semantic segmentation and instance segmentation. Semantic segmentation is a popular technique for cloud segmentation, it involves labeling the pixels in an image and associates them with predefined categories. Some famous models have been developed based on semantic segmentation, such as DeepLabv3+15, U-Net16, PSPNet17, etc. Kumar et al.18 enhanced the U-Net model and trained it on the NITK and SWIMSEG sky image datasets. Wan et al.19 developed the PSPNet model to segment cloud and sky regions, and employed the LSTM model to predict cloud movement. Semantic segmentation could also be used for cloud movement detection, some scholars incorporated anchor frames into semantic segmentation results for the evaluation of cloud speed.

The cloud moving speed is also very important for forecast of solar irradiance. In order to detect clouds, Fan et al.20 adopted a genetic algorithm for image segmentation and used optical flow methods for measuring the cloud speed. Fung et al.21 analyzed cloud movement by using cloud shadow speed sensors and obtained the motion vector of cloud shadows. To measure cloud base height and speed, Wang et al.22 improved the cloud shadow speed sensor and sky imager system. Based on the computational topology and computer vision techniques, Doraiswamy et al.23 developed a framework to explore and visualize cloud motion. Jamaly and Kleissl24 refined the cross-correlation and cross-spectral analysis methods for cloud tracking. By cloud edge detection, Radovan and Ban25 measured cloud movement speed and direction using a tracking system. Bosch and Kleissl26 proposed a method to derive cloud speed from data collected by triad sensors which located at various sites. Using pattern classification and optimal weight determination based on particle swarm optimization, Zhen et al.27 developed a method for calculating cloud speed from sky images (PCPOW) for solar photovoltaic power prediction. Wang et al.19 investigated the moving speed of the clouds by the semantic segmentation algorithms and the long short-term memory (LSTM) networks, the semantic segmentation algorithm was used to identify and detect the cloud contours.

However, semantic segmentation has some limitations, for example, it cannot differentiate between distinct instances within the same category and does not provide precise spatial localization of targets. Those constraints hinder its effectiveness in complex environments like the sky, where it struggles to autonomously enumerate identical objects. Instance segmentation combines object detection with semantic segmentation, enabling further categorization of object types. It identifies distinct instances of various object types and annotates them based on target detection. By excluding the background, instance segmentation allows for precise identification of an object’s state and facilitates multifunctionality through efficient procedures, so it has a huge potential in cloud segmentation. Instance segmentation methods include the YOLO series, YOLACT28, Mask R-CNN29, etc. These techniques are applied across various domains. Zhang et al.30 propose an improved YOLOv7-based deep learning framework for rapid and accurate anomaly detection in photovoltaic (PV) cells. Qiu and Lau31 propose the integration of YOLO into an unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) for real-time crack detection in tiled sidewalks. Ma et al.32 propose an automatic intelligent detection and tracking system for pavement cracks. The system consists of a pavement crack generative adversarial network (PCGAN) and a crack detection and tracking network, YOLO-MF. Chen et al.33 adopted the attention mechanism to detect the sprout growth stage of apple trees, and favorable results were obtained. Wei et al.34 used an enhanced version of SOLOv2 for qualitatively and statistically assessing construction progress. Raveena and Surendran35 used Mask R-CNN to distinguish coffee plants from weeds and achieved good results. Dadi et al.36 proposed a real-time illumination enhancement algorithm that significantly improved the overall performance of the Canny edge detection algorithm to perform many map construction and navigation tasks. Dadi et al.37 through comprehensive analysis and simulation, the optimal sensor location, resonant frequency, and impedance are investigated to maximize the piezoelectric energy harvesting performance. Pang M et al.38 a CelloType multi-task learning strategy is proposed, integrating segmentation and classification tasks to enhance accuracy in multi-scale segmentation and classification of cellular and non-cellular elements. Li et al.39 propose a novel frequency decoupling network (FDNet) that enhances feature representation by independently refining high-frequency and low-frequency components in the frequency domain. Extensive experiments conducted on four benchmark datasets demonstrate that FDNet outperforms several state-of-the-art methods, achieving superior segmentation accuracy and robustness across various terrains and imaging conditions. Xu et al.40 propose a novel diffusion model, DiffSARShipInst, for SAR ship instance segmentation. Overcoming the limitations of existing methods that require target priors. Li et al.41 propose a Unified Multi-scale Cross-modal Interaction (UMCI) module. achieving the optimal segmentation performance on the MVTec-AD and VisA datasets. Xu et al.42 propose FRNet model. FRNet operates 5 times faster than state-of-the-art approaches. As for the cloud segmentation, instance segmentation may accurately determine the current state of the sky and identify positions of different clouds. Additionally, instance segmentation enables simple integration of scripts for measuring cloud velocity and monitoring dynamic movement of the cloud.

Several scholars have compared different cloud segmentation methods. Park et al.43 compared threshold segmentation and semantic segmentation techniques, threshold segmentation is highly susceptible to environmental factors and image interference, often resulting in inaccurate recognition. In contrast, semantic segmentation, as a deep learning approach, offers superior accuracy compared to threshold segmentation. Tapakis and Charalambides44 evaluated the effectiveness of various instruments and methods for detecting different cloud types, and found that current techniques were still limited by equipment constraints and exhibited relatively weak anti-interference capabilities. Due to the effects of sunlight and image quality, threshold segmentation often misidentifies areas of intense sunlight and blurred regions as clouds. Semantic segmentation can identify clouds, but it is unable to differentiate between different cloud types. Different cloud types have different impacts on solar energy systems, so it is necessary to obtain the accurate types and parameters of each cloud in the sky, such as cloud height, thickness, and movement speed. In summary, accurately detection of clouds is crucial for determining its impact on solar energy systems, but traditional methods are not sufficiently effective. Existing cloud segmentation methods have some limitations: firstly, thresholding is sensitive to illumination and image quality. Secondly, semantic segmentation cannot distinguish individual cloud instances or accurately capture fine boundaries, scale variation, and cloud-specific attributes. Thirdly, variation of the scale, fuzzy boundaries, thin cirrus, and occlusion result in missed or fragmented detections. These constraints impact generalization and real-time applicability, and instance segmentation with enhanced multi-scale and contextual feature modeling is a good choice.

In the present study, a model was developed based on deep learning and an instance segmentation technique. By considering the relevant characteristics of instance segmentation tasks, it can rapidly, efficiently, and accurately identify clouds. Semantic segmentation has been widely used for cloud segmentation, but it can only tell us whether the cloud exists or not; it cannot identify different clouds that exist in one image. Different clouds may be detected by the instance segmentation, which is helpful for accurate and efficient cloud segmentation. However, the instance segmentation is scarcely used in cloud segmentation. Try to develop new cloud segmentation models based on instance segmentation, which may be used for the operation and management of the solar energy system. The present study aims to develop an accurate and efficient model for cloud segmentation for solar energy systems. The scope of the present study is limited to the development of a cloud segmentation model based on instance segmentation, while the impact of clouds on the solar energy system is out of the scope of the present study.

The key contributions of this study are as follows: this work combines the MSDA, RCS-OSA, and RFAConv modules within the YOLOv8 framework, resulting in the novel MRR-YOLO model that enhances feature extraction, improves reliability, and enables accurate instance-level segmentation of complex cloud structures; and the model is comprehensively validated on multiple benchmark datasets (SWIMSEG, CCSN, and all-sky images) as well as locally collected real-world images, demonstrating superior accuracy, robustness, and generalization compared to existing approaches.

Model construction and architecture

YOLOv8 model

YOLOv8 is a real-time and multi-task model, it is available in various architectural scales, including n, s, m, l, and x. Due to the high real-time performance and detection accuracy, it is widely used in fields such as autonomous driving, security monitoring, medical image analysis, etc. The accuracy of YOLOv8 has a positive correlation with the number of model parameters. The core architecture of YOLOv8 consists of three main modules: the Backbone network, the Neck bottleneck structure, and the Head output layer45. YOLOv8 adopts a positive-negative sample matching method, known as the Task-Aligned Assigner(TAA)46. TAA ranks and selects the top K positive samples based on the weighted scores of classifications and regressions. By combining the classification and regression task objectives, TAA dynamically adjusts the allocation standards and deals with the imbalance of the class, so it significantly enhances the detector’s training process and final performance. For cloud segmentation, TAA can still improve task performance even if the image has a poor quality. The weighted value alignment metric is shown in Eq. As for the loss function calculation of YOLOv8, the binary cross-entropy loss (BCE Loss) is adopted for the classification branch. To better deal with the imbalance issue of the class, the Distribution Focal Loss (DFL) function is adopted for the regression branch. By transforming the fixed Dirac distribution into a variable probability distribution within a specific range, the DFL function enhances the bounding box regression, which improving the accuracy of the Focal Loss calculation47. The DFL function enables YOLOv8 to quickly focus on neighboring values of the label, maximizing the probability density at the label and enhancing the network’s ability to focus on distributions. When the boundary between the sky and clouds is difficult to identify, the model’s focusing capability is beneficial to deal with these difficulties.

Improved YOLOv8 model

As a multi-task model, YOLOv8 can perform posture identification, segmentation, classification, and detection. YOLOv8 employs traditional and fixed-shape convolution receptive fields, which can result in the loss of information in asymmetric or complex backgrounds. The C2f module enhances the feature diversity, making the model more adaptable to various tasks. By modifying the number of Bottleneck repetitions, the C2f module also improves the flexibility in adjusting network capacity, making it applicable to different application scenarios. However, the performance of the YOLOv8 might be insufficient for certain tasks, such as cloud segmentation. The C2f module increases the complexity of the feature segmentation and multiple Bottleneck operations. The simple feature fusion strategy of the C2f module may fail to fully capture interactions among global features, resulting in the loss of crucial information in cloud segmentation tasks, so the YOLOv8 should be improved for cloud segmentation.

To improve the segmentation performance, RFAConv48 was adopted and it replaced the convolution layers in the Backbone and Neck, enabling a dynamic adjustment of the convolutional receptive field. The modification could enhance the model’s adaptability to complex targets, such as target with different shapes, orientations, and sizes. Also, the modification could make the model more suitable to the cloud that had a dynamic shape. The RCS-OSA49 replaced the C2f module in the Neck, while both the RCS-OSA and MSDA50 modules replaced the C2f module in the Backbone. With a focus on cloud characteristics, the RCS-OSA module could reduce the number of channels in the feature map, meanwhile it could emphasize important spatial features, so it could improve calculation efficiency and detection accuracy. The MSDA module used dilated convolutions at different scales, so it could expand the field of view and capture additional contextual information that might influence parameter counts. By employing different attention mechanisms, the model became more attuned to cloud features, thereby improving the detection accuracy, the detail of the enhanced MRR-YOLO model was illustrated in Fig. 1.

The RCS-OSA model

The shapes and types of clouds are so complex, and they bring huge challenges to accurately capturing information during the cloud segmentation task. For a better performance of the cloud segmentation, improvements of the C2f module and an effective attention mechanism are crucial. The RCS-OSA module was used in brain tumor detection and showed good effectiveness; it may also be applicable to cloud segmentation tasks. The RCS is a network optimization technique that enhances the expressiveness of convolution operations by reparameterization, and it can reduce the computational cost of the model. By integrating the outputs of multiple convolutional layers into a single feature aggregation, the OSA can improve the expressiveness and efficiency of the model. For cloud segmentation, the OSA improves the integration of the features among different scales, so the segmentation performance can be enhanced. The RCS-OSA combines the strengths of the RCS and the OSA; it consistently incorporates RCS modules to promote feature reutilization. The RCS-OSA can improve the information exchange among different channels. Figure 3 illustrates the structure of the RCS-OSA.

The RFAConv module

For traditional CNNs, each convolution operation relies on a fixed kernel size, which results in a fixed receptive field, so it is difficult to cloud segmentation for the traditional CNNs. For the cloud segmentation tasks, due to the irregular and dynamic shapes of the clouds, each feature within the receptive field has a different significance. To improve the performance of the feature extraction and increase the accuracy of cloud segmentation, the Conv module is replaced with the RFAConv module, which may enhance the model’s ability to extract relevant features. The detailed structure of the RFAConv module is shown in Fig. 4. The RFAConv module uses average pooling (AvgPool) to the input features. After global pooling, the feature map was processed by Group Convolution (GroupConv). Simultaneously, GroupConv is applied directly to the input features. The feature map after those two GroupConv operations was then sent to a ReWeight operation. Finally, the output was obtained after an additional convolution. The AvgPool calculates the average value within a k×k window of the input feature map, it can reduce spatial dimensions while retaining essential information51, as show in Eq. (4).

Due to the spatial attention mechanism, the RFAConv module evaluates the importance of each position within the receptive field, and it adjusts the kernel weights accordingly. So, each receptive field has a unique convolution kernel rather than a shared kernel, leading to a more precise capture of the local features within the image. The RFAConv module enhances the overall performance of the network, especially when dealing with complex and varied images, making the model more effective for cloud segmentation tasks.

Datasets

SWIMSEG datasets

The National University of Singapore developed the SWIMXXX dataset, which included the SWIMSEG dataset that contained 1,013 sky images and the binary segmentation maps. All images were captured in Singapore using the calibrated ground-based all-sky imager (WAHRSIS), over a 22-month period from October 2013 to July 2015. Each image covers approximately 60 to 70 degrees of the sky and has a resolution of 600 × 600 pixels. Figure 5 shows some typical images in the SWIMSEG datasets.

Experiments

Details of the implementation

The experiments were conducted on a server with the Ubuntu 20.04 platform, the development environment was based on Python 3.8, PyTorch 2.1.1, and CUDA 11.8. An A40 graphics card was adopted for the calculation. For the training process, the YOLOv8-seg model was used as the benchmark; the MMsegmentation and MMdetection models were used as the comparative experiments. ResNet-50 was selected as the backbone of the MMsegmentation and MMdetection models, this was because it captured finer cloud features than ResNet-18 while avoiding the high computational cost of ResNet-101. It maintained a balance between accuracy and efficiency, making it a representative and reliable baseline. The number of epochs was 1,000, but the training process would be stopped if no significant improvement was observed after 50 epochs. The batch size was 8, and the default Mosaic data augmentation was used. The initial learning rate was 0.01. The above hyperparameters could accelerate model convergence, reduce overfitting, and prevent the model from converging to a local minimum. Each experiment was repeated multiple experiments under the same conditions, and the most stable value of the results was reported to demonstrate the consistency and robustness of the proposed method.

Evaluation metrics

The instance segmentation task included two key components: the object detection and the segmentation. The following traditional evaluation metrics were used: accuracy (PB and PM), recall (RB and RM), and average precision (mAP50B and mAP50M). PB, RB and mAP50B were the evaluation metrics for the object detection task. PM, RM and mAP50M were the evaluation metrics for the segmentation task. Result and discussion

Performance of different segmentation methods

As for the cloud segmentation, threshold segmentation, K-means clustering, edge detection, and deep learning were widely used. To clearly show the performance differences of the cloud segmentation methods, a comparison was first made for some typical cloud types, as shown in Fig. 7. The Optimum Thresholding method had good segmentation performance, but it was highly sensitive to the environmental factors and image quality, resulting in poor recognition of the cloud. The Edge Detection method could only be used to detect object boundaries, and it was also influenced by the environmental conditions and image quality. The image labels of the K-means clustering method might be reversed, which resulted in the wrong label of clouds and background, especially for thin, thick, or sparse clouds. The Region Growing method had the worst performance among all methods, failing to effectively detect the clouds. The YOLOv8 and the Mask R-CNN models were instance segmentation methods, while the Pspnet model was a semantic segmentation method; both the semantic and instance segmentation were based on the deep learning method. From Fig. 7, it can be seen that the deep learning-based method showed the best performance in cloud segmentation.

Performance of different deep learning methods

Besides the YOLOv8, the Mask R-CNN and the Pspnet models, other instance segmentation and semantic segmentation models have been developed, such as the YOLOACT, the DeepLabv3+, the FCN52,53, etc. In this section, the standard YOLOv8-Seg was used as the base model, and the performance comparisons of different deep learning methods were investigated. The results were shown in Table 1. It could be seen that all instance segmentation models had lower precision and recall compared to semantic segmentation models, this was because the evaluation criteria were different for the instance segmentation and the semantic segmentation model. Although the precision and recall of the semantic segmentation model were higher, but the semantic segmentation could only segment clouds, it could not detect the different types of the clouds, which was vital for the fast and accurate forecast of the solar irradiance.

Fig. 7Comparison of different segmentation methods.

Comparison of different segmentation methods.

The instance segmentation model made it possible for the reclassification of the targets and the identification of cloud types, which could be used for the measurement of the cloud thickness, the cloud height, and the movement speed of the cloud. Form Table 1, it could be seen that the precision and the recall of the instance segmentation model were very low. The precision and the recall of the YOLACT model were only 56.3% and 44.0%, and the precision and the recall of the Mask R-CNN model were only 30.1% and 62.7%, so the YOLACT and the Mask R-CNN model could not be used for cloud segmentation. Without any improvement, the precision and the recall of instance segmentation model based on the YOLO were also very low, for example, the precision and the recall of the YOLOv11 were only 74.2% and 66.7%. Compared with the YOLOv8-Seg model, the MRR-YOLO model had significant improvements. The MRR-YOLO instance segmentation model had a precision of 79.3% and a recall of 64.8%. Compared to the YOLOv8-Seg model, the precision and the recall of the MRR-YOLO model improved by 10.1% and 1.3% respectively. Compared to other instance segmentation models, such as the YOLACT, the Mask-R-CNN, the YOLOv5, and the YOLOv7, the precision of the MRR-YOLO model improved by 40.8%, 163.5%, 11.7%, and 7.7%, respectively. The recall of the MRR-YOLO model improved by 47.3%, 3.3%, and 5.5%, respectively. MRR-YOLO also improves the accuracy by 1.8% compared to the latest YOLOv12 model. Compared to the base model, the MSDA and the RCS-OSA modules lead to improvements of the precision, PB and PM increased by 2.6% and 7.6%, respectively. Due to the expansion of the convolution kernel, the MSDA module could focus on relevant information while the number of kernels was the same. The MSDA module paid more attention to the multi-scale and sparse features, so it could successfully capture information that were more important in the image. The RCS-OSA module could improve the overall efficiency while the lightweight of the model was the same. Conclusions

The clouds have significant impacts on the power output and safe operation of the solar energy systems, so the accurate detection of the clouds is very important. The type, the altitude, and the moving speed of the clouds are unsteady and varied, which brings challenges to cloud segmentation. In the present study, the MRR-YOLO model based on deep learning and instance segmentation technique was developed for cloud segmentation; its performance for different cases was investigated, and the following results were obtained:The MSDA, the RCS-OSA module, and the RFAConv modules could improve the image recognition capabilities and were more suitable for cloud segmentation tasks. By progressively increasing the expansion rate, the MSDA module could have different domain sizes with the same size of the convolution kernel, improving the cloud segmentation performance. The RCS-OSA module could reduce the number of channels, so the cloud feature extraction was more efficient, improving cloud segmentation performance from various scales. The RFAConv module improved the parameter exchange of the convolution kernel, which could dynamically adjust its parameters based on the characteristics of each receptive field, so the extraction of cloud features was improved.A comparison was made for different image segmentation techniques; the conventional threshold segmentation method might identify the sky with the clouds, resulting in detection errors. The semantic segmentation method could accurately detect the clouds, but it could not identify the types and numbers of the clouds, which were very important for the forecast of the solar irradiance. The instance segmentation method could identify multiple instances within the image, making it more suitable for cloud segmentation tasks. Sometimes the clouds overlapped, the instance segmentation model could identify every cloud in the image, making it possible to perform the statistics and classification of the clouds. For the SWIMSEG dataset, the MRR-YOLO model had a target detection accuracy (PB) of 79.2%, a recall (RB) of 66%, and an average precision (mAP50B) of 74.7%. It also had a segmentation accuracy (PM) of 79.3%, a recall (RM) of 64.8%, and an average precision (mAP50M) of 73%. For the all-sky dataset, the MRR-YOLO model had a target detection accuracy (PB) of 78.8%, a recall (RB) of 59.3%, and an average precision (mAP50B) of 64.9%. It also had a segmentation accuracy (PM) of 93.8%, a recall (RM) of 53.8%, and an average precision (mAP50M) of 53.5%.The cloud segmentation performance of the MRR-YOLO model was evaluated by the SWIMSEG, the CCSN, and the all-sky datasets, as well as the real clouds in Zhengzhou city. The MRR-YOLO model had a good generalization ability. Instance segmentation provides structural information that may facilitate further analysis of cloud types and physical characteristics.

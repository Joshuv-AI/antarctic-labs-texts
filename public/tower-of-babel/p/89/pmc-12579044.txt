The Mechanisms of Inflammatory Factors and the Total Load of Cerebral Small Vessel Disease in Diabetic Retinopathy and Cognitive Impairment
Authors: Junjun Miao, Shi Chen, Xinyi Sun, Yun She, Lijuan Wang, Siman Liu, Jiangyi Yu, Jing Ge, Zhenguo Qiao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
IntroductionThe purpose of this study was to explore the roles and methods of inflammatory factors and total load of cerebral small vessel disease (CSVD) in diabetic retinopathy (DR) and cognitive impairment.Materials and methodsIn total, 1860 patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) were divided into a DR group and a non-diabetic retinopathy (NDR) group, and nonproliferative DR was divided into mild and moderate-to-severe according to the severity. The patients’ baseline data were recorded, and imaging indicators were collected to evaluate CSVD. Monofactor analysis was performed to identify the risk factors associated with DR and cognitive impairment, and a logistic regression model was used to determine independent risk factors. Finally, Nomogram and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were constructed to evaluate the prediction effect of the model.Results(1) 693 patients (37.26%) had DR and 1167 patients (62.74%) had no DR. In the DR group, hypertension, disease course, low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), uric acid (UA), glycosylated hemoglobin (HbA1c), triglyceride glucose index (TyG), neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR), and systemic immune-inflammation index (SII) were all significantly higher than in the NDR group (p < 0.001). Multivariate logistic regression analysis further verified that hypertension, LDL-C, PLR, and SII were independent risk factors for DR. (2) Among 612 patients with nonproliferative DR, the levels of hypertension, UA, HbA1c, TyG index, interleukin-6 (IL-6), monocyte-to-lymphocyte ratio (MLR), and SII in the moderate-to-severe nonproliferative DR group were significantly higher than those in the mild nonproliferative DR group (p < 0.01). (3) Patients with moderate-to-severe nonproliferative DR were divided into a cognitive impairment group and a non-cognitive impairment group. Smoking history, drinking history, fasting blood glucose, HbA1c, TyG index, PLR, MLR, SII, total CSVD magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) load, and white matter hyperintensities (WMHs) were significantly associated with cognitive impairment (p < 0.01). Smoking history, fasting blood glucose, HbA1c, TyG index, SII, total CSVD load, and lacunar infarction (LI) were independent risk factors for cognitive impairment in patients with moderate-to-severe DR. In addition, total MRI load (r = 0.711, p < 0.05), TyG index (r = 0.712, p < 0.05), SII (r = 0.703, p < 0.05), and PLR (r = 0.724, p < 0.05) were significantly negatively correlated with Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) score.ConclusionsThis study identified hypertension history, LDL-C, PLR, and SII as factors independently associated with the presence of DR in patients with T2DM. In addition, UA, TyG, SII, total CSVD load, and WMHs were significantly associated with more severe stages of DR.

Introduction

China has the highest number of patients with diabetes in the world, and the incidence of diabetic retinopathy (DR) remains at a high level [1]. Domestic epidemiological studies have shown that 18.5–27.9% of patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) suffer from DR, and the severity of the disease is positively correlated with the duration of the disease [2, 3]. Due to its high incidence and risk of blindness, DR has become a public health issue that needs to be addressed globally. The onset of DR is insidious, with no obvious symptoms in the early stage. The retinal microvascular leakage, bleeding, and neovascularization may occur in the late stage, and even lead to irreversible visual damage and blindness. Different severities of DR have different impacts on patients. Mild DR manifestations include color discrimination difficulties or mild blurred vision, while moderate-to-severe DR not only causes significant blurred vision and visual field loss but also has a close relationship with cognitive impairment [4, 5]. Cognitive impairment includes impairments in multiple functional areas, including language, reasoning, logic, and memory, which seriously affect the normal life of patients. Recent studies have shown that patients with DR have a higher risk of cognitive decline, the mechanism of which may be microvascular disease and specific inflammatory responses [6]. Cognitive impairment may be a potential complication of DR, which deserves more attention and research.

In recent years, inflammatory factors have been gradually regarded as important biomarkers for the diagnosis and prognosis evaluation of DR. New inflammatory indicators such as the systemic immune-inflammation index (SII), neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR), and lymphocyte-to-monocyte ratio (LMR) showed the information of inflammation status in the body through comprehensive analysis of different types of immune cells. They had a precise value in early diagnosis of diseases, risk assessment, and monitoring of therapeutic effects [7–10]. SII is a novel marker derived from the integration of platelets, neutrophils, and lymphocytes. High SII values have been shown to predict microvascular and macrovascular complications in T2DM and the risk of death in patients with DR [11]. NLR is the integration of neutrophils and lymphocytes, and high levels of NLR could induce apoptosis and inflammation, and promote microangiopathy and retinal neurodegeneration [12]. Cerebral small vessel disease (CSVD) is a systemic vascular disease that can affect the small blood vessels deep in the brain and is thought to be closely related to DR cognitive impairment. The pathological mechanism might involve vascular endothelial dysfunction, inflammation, and oxidative stress [13]. The increase of total CSVD load would lead to ischemia and hypoxia, affecting brain function and visual pathways, aggravating the severity of DR visual defects, and further causing cognitive impairment. Therefore, evaluating and controlling the total CSVD load not only provides a deeper understanding of the complex pathological mechanisms of DR but also offers a new perspective for the prevention and treatment of DR combined with cognitive impairment.

Materials and Methods

Study Participants

This study selected patients with T2DM who were hospitalized or outpatients in Jiangsu Provincial Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine from January 2020 to March 2024. The study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Jiangsu Provincial Hospital of Chinese Medicine (ethics approval no.: 2023NL-034-01). All patients provided written informed consent. The study was carried out in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Clinical trial number: not applicable. The diagnosis of T2DM was based on the latest guidelines of the American Diabetes Association (ADA) [14]. The diagnosis of DR was based on the guidelines released by the ADA in 2020 [15]. The types of DR were classified according to the new international clinical grading system developed in 2002 [16]. Only observing the characteristic of microvascular malformation belongs to mild nonproliferative DR. Moderate nonproliferative DR refers to retinal lesions with a degree between mild and severe, which may be accompanied by hard exudates, soft exudates, or retinal hemorrhage. Severe nonproliferative DR refers to the absence of characteristics of proliferative retinopathy, but with significant microvascular abnormalities in at least one quadrant, bead like changes in veins in at least two quadrants, or more than 20 bleeding points in the retina in any quadrant.

Exclusion criteria included: (1) a history of immune system diseases; (2) a history of neurological disease unrelated to diabetic neuropathy, kidney disease unrelated to diabetic nephropathy, on dialysis or undergoing kidney transplantation; (3) hypertensive crisis, acute coronary syndrome complicated with hypertension, acute cerebral infarction complicated with hypertension, myocardial infarction, cerebral infarction, and other major cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases; (4) a history of malignant tumors; (5) acute or chronic infection of other systems, or suffering from inflammatory diseases; and (6) have received glucocorticoids, immunosuppressants, anti-inflammatory drugs, or plasmapheresis in the past 3 months.

Clinical Data Collection

Baseline data were recorded for all patients, including gender, history of hypertension, smoking, alcohol consumption, age (years), duration of disease (years), and body mass index (BMI).

Laboratory Index

Fasting blood were collected from patients in the morning. Blood routine, hypersensitive C-protein, fasting blood glucose (FBG), total cholesterol (TC), triglyceride (TG), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), blood urea (BUN), uric acid (UA), glycosylated hemoglobin (HbA1c), triglyceride glucose (TyG) index, and interleukin-6 (IL-6) were detected.

Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) Method

Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) was a tool for screening and evaluating mild cognitive dysfunction, mainly involving abstract thinking, executive function, visuospatial skills, memory, attention and concentration, language, computation, and orientation. This scale had a total score of 30 points, and the demarcation score was ≥ 26 points.

Imaging Indicator

The total CSVD load was calculated by comprehensively evaluating four key indicators: white matter hyperintensities (WMHs), perivascular spaces (PVS), cerebral microbleeds (CMBs), and lacunar infarction (LI).

For WMHs, Fazekas scale was selected for assessment and grading. Paraventricular WMHs score: 0 (no lesions), 1 point (hat shaped or pencil like thin layer), 2 points (smooth halo), and 3 points (irregular continuation to deep white matter). Deep WMHs score: 0 (no lesions), 1 point (punctate lesion), 2 points (focal fusion), and 3 points (large area fusion).

For LI, lesions in the range of 3–15 mm, with a rounded or oval shape, show a signal intensity similar to that of cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) on T1 and T2-weighted imaging, whereas on FLAIR, the interior of the lesion shows a CSF signal signature with a high signal in the surrounding area. The total score was 1 point.

For CMBs, rounded or oval lesions between 2 and 5 mm in this sensitive sequence with a low signal. Score 1 point.

For PVS: rounded or oval lesions less than 3 mm with low T1 and FLAIR and high T2 signals. Level 0 [without enlarged PVS (EPVS)], level 1 (1–10), level 2 (11–20), level 3 (21–40), and level 4 (> 40). If grades 2–4 occur, the total score is 1.

The total CSVD load was 4 points, and the score was proportional to its degree. Among them, the low load group (0–2 points) and the high load group (3–4 points).

Statistical Analysis

In this study, the R statistical tool was used to analyze the data. For data following a normal distribution, the means ± standard deviations (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\overline{x }$$\end{document}x¯ ± s) was used. For data that did not conform to a normal distribution, the median and its 95% confidence interval [M (95% CI)] were presented. Counting data was presented in the form of frequency and ratio (x/y). In comparison between groups, for counting data, Chi-squared tests or Fisher’s exact test were used. For measurement data, t-tests or Mann–Whitney U tests were selected for analysis. A value of 0.05 was considered a statistically significant difference. For variables that revealed significant statistical differences in the single-factor assessment, the next step was to perform a multivariate logistic regression analysis to explore in depth the interactions between these variables and their complex associations with study outcomes. Multifactor logistic regression analysis was performed using stepwise forward regression to screen out independent risk factors. The results of logistic regression analysis included regression coefficients (RC), standard errors (SE), Z-values, p-values, odds ratios (OR), and 95% confidence intervals (CI). In addition, the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was generated and the area under the curve (AUC) was estimated along with a 95% confidence interval.

Results

Distribution of DR in patients with T2DM

A total of 1860 patients with T2DM were included. They were divided into a DR group with 693 cases (37.26%) and a NDR group with 1167 cases (62.74%) according to their retinal conditions.

Risk Factors for DR

Monofactor analysis showed significant differences between the DR and NDR groups on several clinical measures. The proportion of patients with hypertension in the DR group was 58.7%, which was significantly higher than that in the NDR group (49.3%) (p < 0.001). The duration of disease was 17.8 ± 8.2 years in the DR group, which was significantly longer than in the NDR group (12.2 ± 6.9 years) (p < 0.001). LDL-C in the DR group was 2.9 ± 0.9 mmol/L, which was significantly higher than that in the NDR group (2.7 ± 0.8 mmol/L) (p = 0.004). The UA level in the DR group was 368.6 ± 88.2 μmol/L, which was significantly higher than that in the NDR group (350.4 ± 82.3 μmol/L) (p < 0.001). The HbA1c in the DR group was 7.8 ± 1.3%, significantly higher than that in the NDR group (7.3 ± 1.1%) (p < 0.001). In addition, TyG index (4.9 versus 4.6, p < 0.001), NLR (2.3 versus 2.0, p < 0.001), PLR (148.9 versus 138.7, p < 0.001), and SII (735.6 ± 272.3 versus 702.3 ± 258.4, p = 0.007) were significantly higher than those in the NDR group. These results suggested that hypertension, disease course, LDL-C, UA, HbA1c, and several inflammatory markers might be closely related to the occurrence and development of DR. Risk Factors in Different Severities of Nonproliferative DR

There were 693 patients with T2DM combined with DR, including 81 patients with proliferative DR and 612 patients with nonproliferative DR. There were 225 mild patients with nonproliferative DR (36.76%) and 387 patients with moderate-to-severe nonproliferative DR (63.24%). We performed a monofactor analysis of clinical data and experimental parameters to identify potential risk factors associated with the severity of nonproliferative DR. The results showed that there were significant differences in several variables between the mild nonproliferative DR group and the moderate-to-severe nonproliferative DR group. The proportion of patients with hypertension in the moderate-to-severe non-proliferative DR group was 72.4%, which was significantly higher than 57.8% in the mild nonproliferative DR group (p < 0.001). The UA level in the moderate-to-severe nonproliferative DR group (375.6 ± 88.7 μmol/L) was significantly higher than that in the mild nonproliferative DR group (350.5 ± 83.4 μmol/L) (p < 0.001). HbA1c was 7.6 ± 1.3% in the moderate-to-severe nonproliferative DR group, significantly higher than 7.1 ± 1.0% in the mild DR group (p < 0.001). In addition, TyG index (4.9 versus 4.5, p < 0.001), IL-6 (4.2 ± 2.0 pg/mL versus 3.8 ± 1.7 pg/mL, p = 0.013) and MLR (0.29 versus 0.25, p = 0.002) in the moderate-to-severe nonproliferative DR group were significantly higher than those in the mild nonproliferative DR group. These results suggested that markers such as hypertension, UA, HbA1c, TyG, IL-6, and MLR might play an important role in the severity of DR. There were no significant differences in gender, smoking history, drinking history, age, duration of disease, BMI, FBG, TC, LDL-C, TP, BUN, NLR, and PLR between the mild nonproliferative DR group and Discussion

This study found that history of hypertension, LDL-C, PLR, and SII were independent risk factors for developing DR in patients with T2DM. History of hypertension, UA, HbA1c, TyG, SII, total CSVD load, WMHs, and LI were significantly different in mild and moderate-to-severe DR, among which UA, TyG, SII, total CSVD load, and WMHs were independent risk factors for the progression of DR.

DR is one of the most common chronic microvascular complications of T2DM, and its basic pathological change is retinal neovasculogenesis, which has become the main cause of irreversible visual impairment [17]. At present, DR has caused a heavy economic and social burden, affecting the quality of life of people aged 50 years and older [18]. The onset of DR is insidious. For patients with T2DM, it is not enough to only control known risk factors such as blood glucose, blood pressure, and lipid levels. Early identification of specific risk indicators and implementation of timely interventions can prevent blindness caused by DR [19].

Hypertension greatly accelerates the process of retinal microvascular disease by causing damage to microvessels, resulting in hemodynamic changes, which significantly increases the risk of DR in patients with T2DM [20, 21]. Its mechanism mainly involves increased vascular permeability, retinal edema, ischemic injury, neovascularization, oxidative stress, and inflammatory response [22]. Gong et al. found that systolic blood pressure was an independent risk factor for vision in patients with DR [23].

Previous studies showed that metabolic level was closely related to the progression of DR, in which LDL-C, TyG, and UA were important influencing factors [24]. High levels of LDL-C might accelerate the development of DR by promoting lipid deposition in retinal microvascular endothelial cells and triggering an inflammatory response [25]. The TyG index assessed FBG and triglyceride levels, revealing a synergistic effect of carbohydrate and lipid metabolism [26]. The increase of TyG index could predict the development of T2DM, the occurrence of diabetic nephropathy and coronary artery blockage, and the adverse trend of prognosis [27]. Other studies pointed out that the increase of TyG index was proportional to the severity of DR [28]. It was a risk indicator of DR and could predict the progress of DR [29]. Our study found a significant negative correlation between MoCA and TyG, suggesting that the higher the TyG, the more severe the cognitive impairment. UA had the dual effect of promoting oxidative stress and antioxidant activity, which could lead to inflammation, endothelial dysfunction, and vasoconstriction [30]. A meta-analysis found that UA levels were significantly higher in patients with DR than in diabetic controls, suggesting that UA might be a potential biomarker for identifying disease severity in patients with DR [31]. Under the influence of long-term high UA, the severity of DR would be further aggravated, which was consistent with the results of our study. Gong et al. found that LDL-C was also a risk factor for retinopathy [23]. Therefore, regular monitoring of LDL-C, TyG index, and UA levels is essential to prevent and slow the progression of DR. Effective management of these metabolic markers can help reduce the risk of retinal damage, improve visual health and quality of life in people with diabetes, and provide a strong basis for individualized treatment.

Inflammatory factors played a significant role in T2DM, which were key factors affecting DR [32]. NLR, PLR, LMR, and SII were novel markers that could reflect the balance and persistent inflammatory status between leukocyte subtypes [33]. These ratio indicators had higher diagnostic value than single inflammatory indicators, and were not susceptible to physiological changes in blood samples in the body. Therefore, they could provide more stable and accurate results [34]. PLR and SII have been shown to be significantly associated with the development of DR. PLR reflected the inflammatory state in vivo and the level of platelet activation. The inflammatory factors released by platelets could cause vascular endothelial cell injury and neovascularization, and promote the occurrence of DR in patients with T2DM [35]. Zeng et al. found that PLR was an independent risk factor for DR [36]. SII reflected the systemic inflammatory state, which also led to vascular endothelial cell damage and inflammatory cell infiltration. Thus, the process of retinal microangiopathy was intensified and the development of DR was accelerated [37]. This study found a significant negative correlation between MoCA and SII, suggesting that the higher the SII the more severe the cognitive impairment. There was a significant negative correlation between MoCA and PLR, suggesting that the higher the PLR the more serious the cognitive impairment. In addition, the study revealed that SII was an independent risk factor for the occurrence and development of DR, further validating the key role of inflammation in DR. It also contributed to our in-depth understanding of the pathogenesis of DR and provided new predictors and potential therapeutic targets for the clinic.

As a microvascular complication of T2DM, DR is closely related to systemic microvascular disease. Studies have shown that patients with moderate-to-severe DR often had extensive microvascular and small vessel lesions, which not only affected the retina but also caused irreversible damage to the micro/small blood vessels in the brain [38]. CSVD was the most common chronic progressive vascular disease affecting the white matter, arterioles, capillaries, and venules of the brain. The onset of CSVD was insidious and there was no specific clinical manifestation in the early stage. It was the leading cause of vascular cognitive impairment (VCI) [39]. On the basis of anatomical and physiological characteristics, retinal blood vessels were recognized to reflect changes in brain microstructure to a certain extent [40]. Recent studies have found a significant correlation between CSVD and retinal vascular changes. An investigation showed that the enlargement of the retinal venules and the narrowing of the arterioles were associated with the scale of white matter injury [41]. Another study found that changes in arteriovenous crossing, extensive focal arteriolar stenosis, and retinal vessel curvature were common in patients with CSVD [42]. Our study found a significant negative correlation between MoCA and total MRI load, suggesting that the greater the total MRI load the greater the cognitive impairment. We integrated WMHs, LI, EPVS, and CMBs into the assessment measures to fully account for the cumulative impact of CSVD lesions. The usefulness and accuracy of this approach has been demonstrated in several studies.

This study found that total CSVD load and WMHs were independent risk factors for DR progression. The reasons might be as follows: (1) the cerebrovascular lesions caused by the increase of total CSVD load would lead to systemic hemodynamic changes, including blood pressure fluctuations and reduced cerebral blood flow. These changes might lead to retinal blood perfusion insufficiency, aggravated retinal ischemia and hypoxia, and further promote the progression of DR. (2) White matter hypersignaling and lacunar cerebral infarction caused by increased CSVD load could damage the integrity of neurovascular units, thus affecting the functions of the blood–brain barrier and blood–retina barrier, increasing the penetration of harmful substances and aggravating retinopathy. (3) Brain dysfunction caused by CSVD might affect the central nervous system’s regulation of metabolism, exacerbate diabetes-related metabolic problems, and further aggravate DR. (4) Cognitive impairment caused by CSVD would affect the memory, attention, and executive function of patients, resulting in poor management of diabetes, which would further aggravate retinal microangiopathopathy and lead to the progression of DR. In addition, our study found that total CSVD load and LI were independent risk factors for cognitive impairment in moderate-to-severe DR.

We constructed a multi-factor integrated predictive model and found that smoking history, TyG, SII, total CSVD load, and LI were independent risk factors for cognitive impairment in patients with moderate-to-severe nonproliferative DR. DR was a common complication of diabetes, and patients with moderate-to-severe nonproliferative DR were at higher risk of developing cognitive impairment. Early prediction and intervention of cognitive impairment is essential to improve the quality of life and prognosis of patients with DR.

There are still some limitations in this study: (1) the sample size was relatively small, and the research objects were mainly concentrated in a specific region or population, which might affect the universality and generalization of the results. Future studies should expand the sample size to cover different regions and populations to verify the broad applicability of the findings. (2) Although a variety of risk factors were considered in the study, there might still be confounding factors that were not fully controlled, such as lifestyle and genetic factors, which might have an impact on the occurrence of DR and cognitive impairment. Therefore, future studies should consider the influence of these potential confounding factors more comprehensively. (3) This study was mainly based on cross-sectional data and could not fully reveal causality. Future studies should follow the long-term follow-up data of patients through longitudinal study design to better understand the dynamic process and causality of DR progression and cognitive impairment.

Conclusions

A history of hypertension, LDL-C, PLR, and SII were independent risk factors for developing DR in patients with T2DM. UA, TyG, total CSVD load, and WMHs were closely related to the progression of DR. Compared with previous single or few factor prediction models, this research model integrated multiple factors such as metabolism, inflammation, and brain microangiopathopathy, providing a more comprehensive risk assessment. This study introduced factors such as total CSVD load and LI that are less commonly used in DR cognitive impairment prediction models, enhancing the novelty and predictive ability of the model.

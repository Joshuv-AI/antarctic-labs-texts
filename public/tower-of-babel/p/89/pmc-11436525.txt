Predicting angiogenesis in adrenal pheochromocytoma: the role of modified parameters from contrast-enhanced CT
Authors: Xing Xing, Shao-ping Cheng, Jin-bai Huang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThe aim of this study is to investigate the value of modified parameters derived from dual-phase contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) in predicting angiogenesis within pheochromocytoma.MethodA total of 31 patients with pathologically confirmed pheochromocytoma underwent preoperative dual-phase contrast-enhanced CT scanning, wherein modified CT enhancement parameters, namely maximum enhancement difference (∆H) and maximum enhancement velocity (V), were quantified. Subsequently, postoperative specimens were evaluated by pathological section, while microvessel density (MVD) and vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) levels were counted. We conducted comparative analyses to assess disparities in maximum enhancement difference and enhancement velocity between groups characterized by high and low VEGF expression. Furthermore, correlations between maximum enhancement difference, enhancement velocity, and MVD were examined, along with an assessment of the association between maximum enhancement difference, VEGF expression, and MVD at the point of maximal enhancement difference occurrence.ResultsIn the study group, unilateral pheochromocytoma was observed in 31 cases, with 19 cases of arterial phase enhancement and 12 cases displaying venous phase enhancement. The range of maximum enhancement difference (ΔH) spanned from 24 to 102 HU, while the range of maximum enhancement velocity (V) extended from 0.40 HU/s to 4.08 HU/s. Analysis revealed a significant elevation in MVD value within the arterial phase enhancement group compared to the venous phase enhancement group. Additionally, a positive correlation was discerned between the maximum enhancement difference, V, and MVD. Notably, both ∆H and V exhibited statistically significant elevations in the high VEGF expression group relative to the low VEGF expression group. Furthermore, a positive correlation was observed between both ΔH and V and VEGF expression levels.ConclusionIncreased values of ∆H and V are indicative of heightened MVD and VEGF expression. Consequently, the modified parameters derived from dual-phase contrast-enhanced CT scanning serve as predictive markers for angiogenesis in adrenal pheochromocytoma.Keywords: Arterial phase, Dual-phase enhanced CT, Microvessel density, Pheochromocytoma, Vascular endothelial growth factor, Venous phase

Introduction

Pheochromocytoma, a rare neuroendocrine tumor, overproduces catecholamines, leading to episodes of paroxysmal or sustained hypertension along with metabolic disturbances. Angiogenesis, essential for tumor growth, progression, and metastasis, is commonly assessed through microvessel density (MVD) and vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) expression. As a noninvasive imaging technique in vivo, CT is the preferred diagnostic tool for the detection and diagnosis of pheochromocytoma, which is widely used in clinical practice [1]. Contrast-enhanced CT scans can differentiate enhancement patterns and degrees by observing the diffusion of contrast medium between the intravascular and extravascular tissue areas. This process entails an understanding of tissue and vascular enhancement over time, along with an evaluation of variances in tissue or lesion vascularity or density. Regarding contrast-enhanced CT scanning, the measurement of CT values stands as a conventional means to depict lesion enhancement.

While this method can offer insights into the overall vascular supply of lesions to some extent, it lacks precision in assessing lesion angiogenesis. Previous studies have shown that CT perfusion imaging parameters, including blood (BF) and blood volume (BV), correlate with MVD and VEGF expression, both of which serve as angiogenic markers. These parameters are valuable for distinguishing adrenal adenomas from non-adenomas [2]. However, CT perfusion imaging techniques are complex, rendering them less accessible in routine clinical practice. At present, few studies have explored the relationship between CT-enhanced scan parameters and the association of MVD and VEGF with pheochromocytoma. Therefore, this study aimed to explore the association between maximum enhancement difference and maximum enhancement velocity, derived from modified parameters of dual-phase contrast-enhanced CT, and the angiogenic processes associated with pheochromocytoma.

Materials and methods

Study participants

Patients diagnosed with pheochromocytoma through surgical intervention and histopathological assessment, and who were admitted to the First Affiliated Hospital of Yangtze University between June 2009 and December 2023 were recruited for this study. These patients routinely underwent a CT plain scan and a dual-phase enhanced scan, encompassing arterial and venous phases. Following surgical resection, pathological specimens were obtained for the evaluation of MVD and VEGF expression. Inclusion criteria comprised the presence of unilateral disease confirmed pathologically as pheochromocytoma, a quantitative score < 4, and the absence of recurrence or metastasis during 12 months of follow-up [3]. Patients were excluded if they presented with malignant pheochromocytoma, bilateral lesions, extra-adrenal pheochromocytomas or functionally quiescent pheochromocytoma posing diagnostic challenges. Diagnostic criteria for patients with functional pheochromocytoma include: ①Preoperative patients with clinical manifestations of hypercatecholaminemia, such as hypertension, tachycardia, and hyperhidrosis, and other symptoms, with secondary hypertensive conditions such as renal artery stenosis and sleep apnea syndrome were excluded. ②patients with strong suspicion of pheochromocytoma underwent clinical evaluation, biochemical examination, and preoperative imaging. Ultimately, a group of 31 patients, comprising 9 males and 22 females, aged between 37 and 73 years, met the inclusion criteria and were enrolled in the study (Fig. 1).

CT scanning technique

The patient underwent routine CT scans in the supine position to assess the lesion’s extent. Scanning encompassed the region from T11 to the lower edge of L2, as well as from the lower edge of the diaphragm to the level of the renal hilum. Subsequently, contrast-enhanced scans were performed using a volume of 60–90 ml of Omnipaque contrast medium (GE Healthcare, USA, 350 mg I/ml), administered via the median cubital vein at a rate of 2–4 ml/s using a high-pressure syringe (1.5 ml/kg). Arterial phase scans were initiated when the CT value of the abdominal aorta in the adrenal plane reached 150 HU following contrast agent injection, while venous phase scans were conducted with a 60-s delay. Scanning parameters included a voltage 120 kV, a current ranging between 200 and 250 mAs, a pitch ratio of 0.984:1, and a scanning collimation of 0.625 × 64. The slice thickness for scanning was set at 0.625 mm.

CT image processing, data measurement, and calculation

After CT scanning, the original image data were transferred to a GE AW4.6 image workstation in DICOM format. These images were independently and blindly reviewed by two radiologists, each with over 10 years of experience. Multiplanar reconstruction and data measurements were then performed, and the results were averaged.

The measured data included: (1) tumor size, expressed as the longest diameter; (2) CT values of the tumor parenchyma were measured in plain CT scans, enhanced arterial phase, and venous phase images, respectively. Regions of interest (ROIs) within the tumor parenchyma were manually delineated to avoid areas of tumor calcification, cystic degeneration, and necrosis, as well as tumor margins and intratumoral vessels. Each measurement was repeated three times, and the results were averaged. (3) Tumors demonstrating significant enhancement during the arterial phase were categorized into the arterial phase enhancement group, while those with the most significant venous phase enhancement were categorized into the venous phase enhancement group.

Modified parameters for dual-phase enhanced CT were calculated as follows: The maximum enhancement difference refers to the maximum CT value at tumor enhancement (i.e., the maximum value between the arterial phase and the venous phase of the tumor) minus the CT value of a plain scan, expressed as ∆H. The maximum enhancement velocity was calculated by dividing ∆H by the duration of the corresponding phase, expressed as V in units of HU/s.

Tumor MVD count

Surgical specimens were evaluated through staining and quantitative analysis conducted by two pathologists specializing in adrenal pathology, who were unaware of the patient’s clinical data and prior pathological findings. Using the method of Weider et al.: positive structures such as vascular-like tissue and cells with positive immunostaining, as well as individual endothelial cells, were counted [4]. Structures with vessel lumens larger than 8 red blood cells or those with a thicker muscular layer were excluded from the count.

Initially, the most vascular area (hot spot) within the tumor tissue was identified using a low-power field (× 10 eyepiece, × 10 objective). Subsequently, the number of vessels were counted in the three areas under a high-power field (× 10 eyepiece, × 40 objective). The results were averaged across the high-power fields and expressed as the number of microvessels per high-power field.

VEGF expression detection

Two pathologists specializing in adrenal pathology, who did not know the clinical data and pathological findings of the patients, conducted the evaluations. The immunoreactivity of VEGF was determined based on the presence of brownish-yellow granular deposits in the cytoplasm or membrane of tumor cells. The degree of immunoreactivity was quantified as the sum of the grade and intensity of staining.

Grades of staining were classified according to the percentage of positive cells as follows: no cell staining = 0, < 10% cell staining = 1, 10%–50% cell staining = 2, and > 50% cell staining = 3. Staining intensity was categorized as follows: no staining = 0 (essentially no staining or staining close to the background), mild staining = 1 (not darker than the background), moderate staining = 2 (markedly colored and above the background, brownish-yellow), and strong staining = 3 (highly markedly colored, dark brownish-yellow). VEGF immunoreactivity was assessed by summing two scores, resulting in a range of 0–6 [5]. Scores of 0–3 indicated low expression, while 4–6 points represented high expression.

Statistical evaluation

All the data collected in this study were analyzed using SPSS 22.0 software. Normally distributed measurement data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD), while non-normally distributed measurement data were expressed as median (interquartil range), and the comparisons were examined by Student-t test and Mann–Whitney test (non parametric distribution). The categorical data were expressed as n (%), and the differences between the two groups were examined by chi-square analysis or Fisher's exact test. Correlation analysis was performed using Spearman correlation analysis. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

General CT findings and dual-phase enhanced CT parameter results

A total of 31 patients with unilateral pheochromocytoma were enrolled in this study, including 9 males and 22 females, with 17 tumors located on the left side and 14 on the right side. The long diameter of the tumor ranged from 1 to 20 cm, with 20 cases < 5 cm and 11 cases ≧ 5 cm. Heterogeneous tumor enhancement was observed in 13 cases, including 7 cases with necrosis and degeneration, while 18 cases exhibited homogeneous enhancement. There were 19 cases, which exhibited high-density nodular enhancement of the tumor parenchyma in the arterial phase enhancement group, while 12 cases in the venous phase enhancement group. The plain CT value was between 21 and 50 HU, while the CT value in the arterial phase was between 50 and 138 HU, and those during the venous phase ranged from 44 to 131 HU. The maximum enhancement difference was between 24 and 102 HU. Maximum enhancement velocity was between 0.40 HU/s and 4.08 HU/s.

Discussion

The clinical presentation of pheochromocytoma and paraganglioma exhibits considerable variability among patients. Some tumors have significantly reduced or absent secretory function, a condition referred to as functional quiescent pheochromocytoma. This category includes completely quiescent chromaffinomas and insidiously functional pheochromocytomas. Even among functional tumors, clinical presentations can differ significantly. Both benign and malignant pheochromocytomas are highly vascularized. The study data indicated that there were differences in MVD values and VEGF expression among different benign pheochromocytoma specimens, suggesting that factors influencing these angiogenic differences warrant further investigation. Current research on angiogenesis in pheochromocytoma primarily emphasize the diagnosis of malignant tumors and the development of anti-tumor vascular therapies, with critical evaluation of methodological approaches in these areas.

Angiogenesis plays a crucial role in numerous physiological and pathological processes, including the formation of benign tumors. Research on angiogenesis in adrenal diseases has revealed differences in MVD and VEGF expression between benign adrenal adenomas and non-adenomas, including pheochromocytoma and hyperplastic nodules. Tumor growth requires continuous blood supply to deliver essential nutrients and oxygen, but tumor secretion is intricately linked to angiogenesis, the formation of new blood vessels. Bermini et al. investigated angiogenesis in normal adrenocortical tissue and adrenocortical diseases, revealing notable differences in MVD and VEGF expression [6]. Specifically, MVD in cortical carcinoma was significantly lower than that observed in adrenocortical, aldosterone adenoma, cortisol adenoma, and nonfunctioning adenoma. A gradual decline in MVD was evident across the latter four entities. Conversely, VEGF expression was higher in adrenocortical carcinoma compared to aldosterone adenoma, cortisol adenoma, normal adrenocortical tissue, and nonfunctioning adenoma, exhibiting a successive decrease across the latter four categories.

The MVD of aldosterone adenoma exhibited a positive correlation with aldosterone levels and a negative correlation with renin levels. Consequently, the MVD of adrenocortical carcinoma did not align with VEGF expression, while the functional status of nonfunctioning adenomas correlated with both low MVD and low VEGF expression. Conversely, the secretory function of aldosterone adenoma was associated with angiogenesis, suggesting that angiogenesis may impact the hormone secretory function of endocrine tumors.

Presently, immunohistochemical labeling techniques and pathological examinations are the main approaches for assessing angiogenesis in tumors and other tissues, providing reliable insights into tissue angiogenic status. However, these techniques possess several limitations: (1) they require biopsy or surgery to obtain specimens; (2) they are confined to assessing the morphology and quantity of microvessels, lacking the capability to evaluate the distribution and functionality of blood vessels in vivo; (3) pathological sampling is often limited in its scope, and at times, it may not fully reflect the tumor’s characteristics; and (4) the number of biopsy or surgical specimens that can be obtained is limited, leading to poor repeatability in pathological observations. Based on the above shortcomings, there is a pressing need in clinical practice to identify noninvasive, accurate, and repeatable technical methodologies for dynamically monitoring and evaluating tumor angiogenesis.

With the deepening of research on the mechanisms of tumor occurrence and development, angiogenesis emerges as an important factor in tumor progression and metastasis. Anti-tumor vascular therapies are currently widely used in experimental, clinical research, and clinical treatment. However, evaluating changes in tumor angiogenesis and the efficacy of anti-tumor vascular therapies requires supplementary approaches.

CT perfusion imaging is utilized in tumor research to assess angiogenesis based on changes in tumor microvasculature. Reiner et al. found that CT perfusion imaging parameters BF and BV could reflect angiogenesis in renal cell carcinoma (RCC), noting that higher degrees of tumor necrosis corresponded to lower perfusion parameter values [7]. Some studies have reported that the perfusion parameters BF and BV in adrenal adenoma CT perfusion imaging correlate with MVD, while only BF shows a correlation with VEGF expression. In addition, BV can serve as a reference index for distinguishing adenomas from non-adenoma [2]. In clinical research, CT perfusion imaging serves various purposes, including assessing tumor nature and grade, evaluating patient prognosis and treatment response, analyzing tumor responses to different treatments, and studying tumor progression, resistance, and anti-tumor drug pharmacokinetics [5]. However, current challenges with CT perfusion imaging include its susceptibility to various factors that can affect accuracy and concerns regarding the excessive radiation dose.

Dynamic CT enhancement involves performing repeated CT scans of target organs following the intravenous injection of a contrast medium to semi-quantitatively evaluate the characteristics of lesions. Commonly used parameters in this technique include peak enhancement, peak early enhancement, time to peak, clearance rate, and temporal enhancement curve morphology. Wang et al. used dynamic contrast-enhanced CT to assess the relationship between RCC and angiogenesis [8]. They found that dynamic contrast-enhanced parameters, such as maximum enhancement difference, slope of the time density curve, tissue vascular ratio, and area under the time density curve, were all correlated with tumor MVD. This led to the conclusion that dynamic contrast-enhanced CT is suitable for assessing tumor angiogenesis and can reflect the heterogeneity of tumor vessels.

Similarly, adrenal tumors were scanned using dynamic contrast-enhanced CT, with the corresponding parameters combined with MVD and VEGF expression. This analysis revealed that adrenal adenomas and non-adenomas had different time density curves, relative clearance rates, and absolute clearance rates. These differences were found to be related to the differences in MVD and VEGF expression between the two tumors [9].

In this study, we focused on the findings of dynamic contrast-enhanced CT and dual-phase contrast-enhanced CT in angiogenesis, to summarize the enhancement characteristics of dual-phase contrast-enhanced CT of adrenal pheochromocytoma. Parameters such as the maximum enhancement difference and enhancement velocity during dual-phase enhancement were established to investigate the potential association between these enhancement characteristics and the angiogenesis of adrenal pheochromocytoma.

The primary CT findings of adrenal pheochromocytoma include a propensity for hemorrhage, degeneration, calcification, ischemic necrosis, or cystic degeneration. On CT scans, whether plain or enhanced, these tumors typically exhibit homogeneous or heterogeneous enhancement, often manifesting as uneven or mixed-density shadows. Most pheochromocytomas are hypervascular, exhibiting significant enhancement during the arterial or venous phase. Therefore, in this study, we selected lesions based on their homogeneity (including necrotic cystic degeneration) and maximum enhancement phase to investigate the relationship between these CT features and the angiogenesis of pheochromocytoma.

In setting the parameters, we adapted relevant parameters from CT dynamic enhancement studies, making corresponding modifications [9]. The maximum enhanced difference (⊿ H) refers to the peak value (PV) of dual-phase enhanced CT (maximum CT value in arterial phase or venous phase, HU of arterial phase or venous phase, HA/HV) minus the value from the plain CT (HU of the unenhanced phase, HU) value, expressed as ⊿ H = PV-HU. The slope (S) of the maximum ascending segment was changed to the strengthening velocity (V) V = ⊿ H/⊿ t, and ⊿ t was the time to peak.

Ichikawa et al. classified the degree of enhancement and peak time of dynamic enhancement in adrenal tumors [10]. The degree of enhancement was classified as mild (≦ 1.0), moderate (1.0–7.0), and marked (> 7.0), expressed as the enhancement rate. The time to peak was divided into early (≦ 2 min), middle (3–9 min), and late (> 11 min). According to these curves, adrenal tumors were divided into five types, with pheochromocytoma being type III, characterized by early, significant enhancement that peaks and clears slowly. Additionally, another dynamic contrast-enhanced study using MRI to differentiate benign from malignant pheochromocytomas found that malignant pheochromocytomas reached their maximum peak in the arterial phase, whereas benign pheochromocytomas peaked at 1 min [11]. Based on the above two studies, in dual-phase enhanced CT of pheochromocytoma, the maximum value during the arterial phase corresponds to the peak dynamic enhancement.

While the maximum value in the venous phase, may not necessarily correspond to the absolute peak of dynamic enhancement, it approximates the true maximum value closely. The peak values obtained from CT perfusion imaging and CT dynamic enhancement are likely closer to the true maximum peak value due to the shorter scanning interval. In addition, variations in the maximum values observed during the venous phase at the same time can serve as indicators of differences in blood supply among various tumors. By comparing the enhancement speed and phases between arterial and venous phases, it should be possible to distinguish the enhancement characteristics of various pheochromocytomas, thereby investigating their potential association with angiogenesis.

Dual-phase contrast-enhanced CT scans reflect more of the early phase of dynamic contrast-enhanced CT, reflecting the initial enhancement stage of tumor vascularization. Therefore, the modified parameters of maximum enhancement difference and enhancement velocity selected in this study are indicative of early tumor enhancement dynamics. Numerous studies have shown that there is a correlation between CT enhancement findings of tumors and MVD and VEGF expression [8, 11]. Following the intravenous bolus injection of contrast medium, its entry into the tumor parenchyma from the circulatory system can be divided into three stages: first, the contrast medium reaches the small vessels within the tumor via blood flow; second, it needs to pass through the tumor vessel wall; and finally, it enters the tumor stroma within the extravascular space.

The enhancement of a lesion is not contingent upon whether it is benign or malignant, but instead, relies on the specific distribution conditions of the contrast medium within the lesion [12]. These characteristics include an abundant blood supply, better vascular permeability, and sufficient extracellular space. The maximum enhancement difference also corresponds to changes in tumor CT density following the injection of contrast medium. Studies have shown that after intravenous administration of contrast medium, it rapidly diffuses toward highly vascularized tissues and enters the intercellular space, highlighting the role of angiogenesis in tumor enhancement. Moreover, the investigation by Wang et al. revealed a positive correlation between the maximum enhancement difference of tumors and vascular density, indicating that heightened tumor vascularization intensifies the degree of tumor enhancement [8]. In our study, both the maximum enhancement difference and enhancement velocity were associated with MVD and VEGF expression. Furthermore, the mean values of maximum enhancement difference and enhancement velocity were significantly higher in the VEGF-positive group compared to the VEGF-negative group.

In our study, 19 patients reached the maximum value in the arterial phase of dual-phase contrast-enhanced CT, while 12 patients did so during the venous phase, indicating that at least 19 patients reached the maximum peak value within one minute. Wang et al. observed a similar trend in their examination of adrenal tumors using dynamic contrast-enhanced CT, with the majority of tumors reaching their peak within one minute [9]. Correspondingly, investigations utilizing dynamic contrast-enhanced MRI for pheochromocytoma have also indicated a time to peak enhancement of approximately one minute [11]. The attainment of peak enhancement denotes the early enhancement phase, characterized by the cessation of further increases in CT value once the equilibrium between contrast medium influx and clearance within the tumor is reached. Consequently, the CT value at this juncture, signifying dynamic equilibrium, represents the peak CT enhancement value, typically reached within a timeframe of about one minute.

As the MVD of the tumor increases, so does neovascularization. Consequently, there is a greater influx of contrast medium into the tumor parenchyma per unit of time, resulting in a shorter time to reach the peak enhancement value. In our study, tumors with higher MVD achieved maximum enhancement during the arterial phase, which was significantly greater than that observed during the venous phase. Additionally, the time taken to reach the maximum enhancement value was found to be associated with MVD. However, no notable disparity was observed in VEGF expression among pheochromocytoma cases between the two groups.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the maximum enhancement difference and enhancement speed of pheochromocytoma can serve as potential indicators for evaluating its function. This study is the first to use enhanced CT to evaluate the function of pheochromocytoma. Preoperative time is related to the absolute enhancement level, especially to the degree of enhancement of pheochromocytoma on enhanced CT. If the maximum enhancement value is larger and the maximum increase value is larger, it indicates that the function of pheochromocytoma is stronger, and more preparation time is needed before surgery.

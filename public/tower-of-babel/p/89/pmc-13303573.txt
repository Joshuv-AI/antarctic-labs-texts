Herbal and Natural Product Interventions to Modulate Gut Microbiota in Acid Suppression-Associated Dysbiosis: a systematic review protocol
Authors: Ji Hye Hwang, You-Kyung Choi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesProton pump inhibitors are widely used to manage acid-related gastrointestinal disorders; however, prolonged use has been associated with gut dysbiosis, including reduced microbial diversity and the proliferation of opportunistic pathogens. Herbal medicines and natural products, characterized by multitarget effects, have been proposed as potential strategies for modulating the gut microbiota and restoring microbial homeostasis. This systematic review aims to evaluate the effects of these interventions on the gut microbiota in patients receiving acid suppression therapy.MethodsThis protocol is registered in the PROSPERO international prospective register of systematic reviews (CRD420261346672) and will be conducted in accordance with the PRISMA-P guidelines. A comprehensive literature search will be performed in PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, CENTRAL, and CNKI from database inception to March 2026. Randomized controlled trials and nonrandomized controlled clinical studies evaluating herbal or natural product interventions in adult patients receiving acid suppression therapy will be included. Two independent reviewers will perform study screening, data extraction, and risk-of-bias assessment using the RoB 2 and ROBINS-I tools. The overall certainty of the evidence will be evaluated using the GRADE approach.ResultsFindings will be synthesized narratively, with a focus on taxonomic shifts (from the phylum to genus level) and diversity indices (alpha and beta diversity). Where sufficient data are available, a quantitative meta-analysis will be conducted using a random-effects model. Subgroup analyses will explore differences according to herbal intervention type (e.g., single extracts vs. multiherb formulations) and microbiome assessment methods.ConclusionThis review will provide a structured overview of the microbiota-modulating effects of herbal and natural product interventions during acid suppression therapy. By bridging traditional medicine and modern microbiome science, the findings may help inform integrative therapeutic strategies and guide the design of future high-quality clinical trials.Keywords: dysbiosis, gut microbiota, herbal medicine, natural products, proton pump inhibitors, systematic review

INTRODUCTION

The gut microbiota plays a central role in maintaining host homeostasis by regulating immune responses, metabolic processes, and gastrointestinal function [1]. Growing evidence indicates that alterations in microbial composition, known as dysbiosis, are associated with a wide range of diseases, including gastrointestinal, metabolic, and neuropsychiatric disorders [2, 3]. These findings highlight the systemic influence of the gut microbiota beyond the gastrointestinal tract. Accordingly, therapeutic strategies that restore physiological balance through microbiota modulation have attracted increasing attention.

Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) are among the most widely prescribed medications worldwide for acid-related gastrointestinal disorders [4, 5]. Although they are generally considered safe, emerging evidence suggests that PPI use can alter the composition and diversity of the gut microbiota [4]. By increasing gastric pH, PPIs may impair the gastric acid barrier, which serves as a key defense against ingested microorganisms, and thereby promote the survival and colonization of nonnative or oral-derived bacteria in the gastrointestinal tract [6]. These microbiota alterations have been linked to reduced microbial diversity, an increased risk of enteric infections, including Clostridioides difficile infection, and other adverse outcomes [4, 6, 7]. Despite these concerns, current therapeutic approaches remain focused largely on acid suppression and often overlook the secondary effects of microbiota imbalance and the need for appropriate prescribing practices [5].

Herbal medicines and natural products, which are characterized by multicomponent and multitarget properties, have increasingly been investigated as potential modulators of gut microbial ecology [8, 9]. These interventions may influence microbial composition and function, thereby helping to restore microbiota homeostasis [10, 11]. Their effects may be mediated by the prebiotic-like properties of herbal compounds, the selective enrichment of beneficial microbial taxa, and the modulation of host–microbiome metabolic interactions, including pathways related to short-chain fatty acid production and gut–brain signaling [11]. In addition, the gut microbiota can metabolize natural compounds into bioactive forms, which may subsequently regulate microbial diversity and systemic health [8, 9]. However, previous studies have evaluated these effects across heterogeneous disease conditions, and the roles of herbal and natural product interventions in specific pathophysiological contexts remain insufficiently defined.

This review focuses on a specific clinical context in which microbiota disruption is induced by acid suppression therapy. Given their microbiota-modulating properties, herbal medicines and natural products may help mitigate dysbiosis associated with PPI use. However, no systematic review has specifically evaluated the effects of these interventions on the gut microbiota in patients receiving acid suppression therapy. Therefore, this study aims to systematically assess the impact of herbal medicine and natural product interventions on gut microbiota composition and related outcomes in this population.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

1. Study design and registration

This protocol was developed in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Protocols (PRISMA-P) 2015 guidelines [12]. To ensure procedural transparency, the study was prospectively registered in PROSPERO, the international prospective register of systematic reviews (ID: CRD420261346672). The final review will be reported in accordance with PRISMA 2020 standards, and any deviations from the original protocol will be clearly documented and justified in the final publication.

1) Population

This review will include clinical studies involving adult patients with gastrointestinal conditions who are receiving PPI-containing therapy. Eligible therapies will include PPI monotherapy and combination regimens in which a PPI is a core treatment component, such as bismuth quadruple therapy. Studies involving H2 receptor antagonists or other non-PPI acid-suppressive agents will be excluded. No restrictions will be applied regarding sex, ethnicity, or the specific indication for PPI use. Studies involving healthy participants, pediatric populations, animal models, or in vitro experiments will also be excluded.

2) Intervention

Eligible interventions will include herbal medicines or natural product-derived therapies administered as monotherapy or in combination with standard care. These interventions will be classified into three prespecified categories: (1) multiherbal formulations, (2) single natural compounds, and (3) herbal or natural product interventions combined with probiotic or synbiotic preparations. Studies evaluating probiotics, prebiotics, or synbiotics without a concurrent herbal or natural product component will be excluded. These classifications will be used in planned subgroup and sensitivity analyses to address potential heterogeneity.

3) Comparator

Eligible comparators will include placebo, no treatment, usual care, PPI therapy alone, or other standard pharmacological interventions. Studies without a comparator group, including single-arm pre–post studies, will be included only if they report longitudinal microbiota-related outcomes. Because of their inherent methodological limitations, these studies will be synthesized separately in the narrative analysis and excluded from any quantitative meta-analysis.

4) Outcomes

The primary outcome will be changes in gut microbiota composition and diversity. Eligible outcomes will include both quantitative and qualitative microbiota measures assessed using validated methods, such as 16S rRNA sequencing and metagenomic analysis. Specific measures may include α-diversity indices, such as Shannon, Simpson, and Chao1; β-diversity; and taxonomic shifts across microbial classification levels, ranging from the phylum to genus level. Secondary outcomes will include changes in microbial metabolites, such as short-chain fatty acids, and clinical outcomes, including gastrointestinal symptoms and quality of life, where reported.

5) Study design

This review will include randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and nonrandomized clinical interventional studies. Although RCTs will be prioritized, nonrandomized studies will also be included to provide a broad synthesis of the available clinical evidence. Animal studies, in vitro experiments, review articles, case reports, and conference abstracts will be excluded to maintain the focus on human clinical research.

3. Search strategy and data sources

A structured literature search will be conducted across multiple electronic databases to identify relevant studies published from database inception to March 2026. The databases will include PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Scopus, Web of Science Core Collection, and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL). In addition, the China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI) will be searched to identify relevant Chinese-language publications, as many clinical studies on herbal medicine are conducted in East Asia and may not be fully indexed in Western databases.

The search strategy will combine controlled vocabulary terms, such as MeSH terms in PubMed and Emtree terms in Embase, with free-text terms related to three core concepts: (1) PPIs and PPI-based therapies, (2) herbal medicine or natural products, and (3) gut microbiota or dysbiosis. A representative PubMed search strategy is as follows: (&quot;Proton Pump Inhibitors&quot;[Mesh] OR PPI OR omeprazole OR esomeprazole OR lansoprazole OR pantoprazole OR rabeprazole OR &quot;acid suppression&quot;) AND (&quot;Medicine, Chinese Traditional&quot;[Mesh] OR &quot;Herbal Medicine&quot;[Mesh] OR herbal OR botanical OR &quot;natural product&quot; OR phytotherap* OR decoction OR formula*) AND (&quot;Gastrointestinal Microbiome&quot;[Mesh] OR microbiota OR microbiome OR dysbiosis OR &quot;gut flora&quot;).

No restrictions will be applied regarding language or publication date. The reference lists of included studies will be screened manually, and forward citation tracking will also be performed to ensure comprehensive coverage. Detailed search strategies for each database will be provided in the final systematic review manuscript and its supplementary materials.

4. Screening and data extraction process

Retrieved records will be imported into a reference management system, where duplicate entries will be identified and removed. Two reviewers (JHH and YC) will independently screen titles and abstracts, followed by full-text assessment of studies that meet the predefined eligibility criteria. Any disagreements will be resolved through discussion or, when necessary, consultation with a third reviewer. The study selection process will be summarized using a PRISMA flow diagram (Fig. 1).

Data will be extracted independently using a predefined extraction form. The extracted variables will include study design and randomization method; participant characteristics and disease population; intervention components, dose, and duration; comparator regimen; antibiotic exposure status; Helicobacter pylori eradication therapy status; co-administration of probiotics or synbiotics; sample type for microbiome assessment, such as fecal or gastric samples; microbiome assessment method; outcome data with measures of precision; and dietary variation or restrictions, where reported. Particular attention will be given to microbiota-related outcomes, including taxonomic shifts and α- and β-diversity metrics. Numerical data will be extracted directly from tables or figures when feasible.

5. Evaluation of risk of bias

Two reviewers will independently assess the methodological quality of the included studies using the Cochrane Risk of Bias tool (RoB 2) [13] for RCTs and the Risk Of Bias In Nonrandomized Studies of Interventions (ROBINS-I) tool for nonrandomized studies. Any differences in reviewer judgments will be resolved through consensus or, if agreement cannot be reached, consultation with a third reviewer. The certainty of evidence for each key outcome will be assessed using the GRADE approach [14].

6. Data synthesis

Given the anticipated heterogeneity in study designs, intervention types, and microbiome analysis methods, the findings will primarily be summarized through narrative synthesis. If sufficient homogeneity is observed across study characteristics and reported outcomes, a quantitative synthesis may be conducted. For this review, sufficient homogeneity will be defined as the availability of at least two studies with comparable designs, intervention characteristics, and outcome measures, as well as adequate numerical data for effect size estimation.

A quantitative synthesis will be performed only when these criteria are met. Where appropriate, subgroup analyses will be conducted according to intervention type or other relevant study characteristics. Potential sources of heterogeneity, including treatment duration, will also be examined through subgroup or sensitivity analyses where data permit.

RESULTS

A PRISMA flow diagram will be used to illustrate the study screening and selection process. The key characteristics of the included studies, including study design, participant demographics, intervention characteristics, and microbiota-related outcomes, will be systematically summarized in a comprehensive table. To support the rigor and validity of the findings, study quality will be assessed using the RoB 2 and ROBINS-I tools, and the overall certainty of the synthesized evidence will be evaluated using the GRADE framework.

DISCUSSION

Herbal medicines and natural products are available in various forms, including standardized decoctions, granules, and isolated bioactive compounds. Multiherbal formulations, such as JianpiQinghua [15] and Hewei Jiangni [16], have traditionally been used to regulate gastrointestinal function, potentially through mechanisms related to gut homeostasis [8, 10, 11]. PPIs remain central to the management of acid-related disorders [7]; however, pharmacologically induced hypochlorhydria may compromise the gastric acid barrier and alter gut microbial composition [6]. These changes are commonly associated with reduced microbial diversity and the overgrowth of opportunistic pathogens [4-6]. In this context, herbal interventions, which are characterized by multitarget effects, may represent a potential strategy for modulating the gut microbiota [8, 9]. However, the consistency and reproducibility of these effects require rigorous evaluation.

This systematic review aims to evaluate the evidence for herbal and natural product interventions in patients receiving acid suppression therapy. Because herbal formulations and clinical contexts are inherently diverse, substantial heterogeneity is expected. Differences in intervention composition, dosage, and duration, together with variability in microbiome assessment methods, such as 16S rRNA sequencing and metagenomic analysis, may limit direct quantitative synthesis. In clinical scenarios such as Helicobacter pylori eradication, concomitant antibiotic use may also confound the interpretation of microbiota-related outcomes. To maintain methodological rigor, these factors will be systematically addressed during data synthesis, and narrative synthesis will be prioritized when quantitative pooling is not feasible.

This protocol is strengthened by its emphasis on methodological rigor and transparency. The use of validated appraisal frameworks to assess study quality and certainty of evidence will support the robustness and reproducibility of the findings for clinical application. The inclusion of both international and regional databases, including CNKI, is expected to reduce geographic and publication bias. Although the specificity of the research question may limit the number of eligible studies, this review will provide a structured synthesis of the current clinical evidence and identify key gaps for future research. Ultimately, the findings are expected to clarify the potential role of herbal interventions in mitigating microbiota alterations associated with acid suppression therapy.

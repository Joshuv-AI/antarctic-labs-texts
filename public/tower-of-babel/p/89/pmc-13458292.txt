FM-Mamba: end-to-end non-causal Mamba-based network for efficient flood mapping
Authors: Binbin Wang, Zijie Chen, Hailin Zou, Anran Yuan, Yuanyuan Pan, Hongfei Guo, Jianqing Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Accurate, real-time flood mapping from synthetic aperture radar image is vital for disaster response but is hindered by a persistent trade-off between model accuracy and computational efficiency. This paper introduces FM-Mamba, a novel network that leverages a state space model to break this bottleneck. Its core innovation is an encoder built with non-causal Mamba blocks, which captures essential long-range spatial context with linear complexity, paired with a parameter-efficient decoder designed for precise boundary recovery. Evaluated on the Sen1Floods11 and S1GFloods benchmarks, FM-Mamba achieves leading segmentation accuracy, matching or exceeding state-of-the-art methods in F1-score and IoU. Crucially, it accomplishes this with only 3.93 million parameters and a drastically reduced computational footprint, demonstrating a superior balance of performance and efficiency that is ideal for operational, real-time flood mapping.

Introduction

Floods are among the most frequent and destructive natural disasters, causing major loss of life, environmental damage, and economic losses1,2. Their severity and frequency are increasing worldwide due to climate change and urbanization3. Accurate flood mapping is a critical discipline for mitigating these impacts, as it provides essential maps and predictive models that delineate the spatial extent, depth, and timing of flood events. Its significance lies in enabling authorities to anticipate inundation patterns, prioritize evacuation efforts, and allocate resources efficiently during crises. In particular, real-time flood maps are indispensable for effective emergency response and crisis management, as they enable rapid assessment of inundation extents and support critical decision-making during ongoing events, thereby improving response time and potentially reducing overall damage and casualties4,5. By integrating hydrological, topographic, and meteorological data with remote sensing and geographic information systems, flood mapping underpins both real-time emergency response and long-term risk assessment and planning6,7.

The methodological evolution of flood mapping is tied to advancements in Earth observation technologies. Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) is a cornerstone for its all-weather, cloud-penetrating capability8. Early approaches relied on global thresholding of backscatter values9. While simple, these methods proved inadequate in diverse settings due to SAR’s complex scattering, yielding poor accuracy and transferability10. Subsequent advancements introduced adaptive, region-specific techniques like region-growing and automated thresholding, improving robustness11,12. However, dependence on expert intervention and tailored preprocessing still limited their scalability and potential for automated, real-time deployment.

The advent of deep learning has catalyzed a paradigm shift, enabling automated, high-accuracy flood inundation mapping. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), particularly the U-Net architecture and its variants, set a strong initial benchmark for semantically segmenting water bodies from both SAR and optical imagery13. Their success is attributed to a powerful encoder-decoder structure that effectively captures and combines multi-scale spatial features14. However, the inherent locality of standard convolutional operations limits their ability to model long-range contextual dependencies across an image, which is crucial for accurately discerning floodwaters in complex, heterogeneous landscapes such as urban areas or vegetated wetlands. To overcome this limitation, the field progressively integrated attention mechanisms and Transformer architectures15. By leveraging self-attention, these models can weigh the importance of all pixels in an image when segmenting a particular location, thereby capturing a truly global receptive field. This innovation has led to significant improvements in segmentation accuracy, especially in challenging scenarios requiring the distinction of flood features from spectrally similar non-flood objects like dark soils or shadows16. Concurrently, Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have been explored to model the explicit spatial relationships and topological constraints inherent to floodplains and drainage networks17. Advances in data availability and learning strategies have also been pivotal, with the curation of large-scale, annotated datasets supporting training for difficult cases18, and semi-supervised learning19 and multi-modal data fusion20,21 being employed to mitigate annotation scarcity and synergistically combine complementary data sources.

Despite these significant advances, critical challenges persist at the intersection of model performance, computational efficiency, and practical deployment, creating a persistent tri-lemma for current State-Of-The-Art (SOTA) methods. First, regarding fundamental architectural limitations, while Transformer-based architectures excel at modeling global context, their quadratic computational complexity with respect to sequence length presents a substantial bottleneck for processing high-resolution, wide-area SAR imagery in time-sensitive emergency scenarios where both speed and accuracy are paramount22,23. Second, concerning model and parameter efficiency, leading encoder-decoder models often achieve peak segmentation accuracy at the expense of excessive model complexity and parameter counts, rendering them unsuitable for deployment in resource-constrained operational environments or for near-real-time processing on standard hardware24. Third, a fundamental accuracy-efficiency trade-off remains; many designed or distilled lightweight models fail to capture fine-grained spatial details, leading to degraded performance in defining exact flood extents, which is critical for damage assessment25,26.

Concurrent with these architectural developments, a distinct and growing research thrust has focused explicitly on developing lightweight and efficient models suitable for real-time deployment. This has involved techniques such as distilling knowledge from large models into smaller ones27,28, employing neural architecture search to discover optimal, efficient building blocks29, and designing purpose-built lightweight CNN decoders30,31. While these approaches successfully reduce parameter counts and computational loads, they often achieve this at a significant cost to performance, particularly in the precise delineation of fine-grained flood boundaries, thereby perpetuating the gap between academic benchmarks and strict operational requirements where both high accuracy and high throughput are non-negotiable20.

The search for a foundational architecture that seamlessly integrates the long-range, context-aware modeling of Transformers with the computational efficiency of CNNs has driven renewed interest in structured State Space Models (SSMs). Among modern SSMs, the Mamba architecture32 represents a significant paradigm shift. It addresses a core limitation of prior linear SSMs, their time-invariant nature, by introducing a selection mechanism that dynamically conditions model parameters on the input. This allows Mamba to selectively propagate or discard information along a sequence, a capability essential for context-dependent reasoning that was once exclusive to the self-attention mechanism in Transformers.

Crucially, Mamba achieves this sophisticated, input-dependent processing with linear computational complexity relative to sequence length, offering a theoretically superior alternative to the quadratic scaling of Transformers. This practical advantage has been demonstrated by Mamba’s SOTA results in domains such as language modeling32 and long-sequence genomics33. Subsequent adaptations for vision tasks (e.g., Vision Mamba) further prove its efficacy as a highly efficient yet powerful backbone for dense prediction, rivaling Transformer performance while consuming significantly less memory and computation during both training and inference34,35.

Although Mamba has shown transformative potential in these fields, its application in remote sensing has only recently emerged, with preliminary explorations in hyperspectral sequence unmixing36, land cover classification, and change detection. However, the application of the Mamba architecture to the task of flood mapping from SAR imagery remains nascent and largely unexplored. Existing Mamba-based vision models are primarily designed as general-purpose backbones for natural image tasks. They have not been engineered to address the unique challenges posed by SAR data, including its complex backscattering characteristics (e.g., double-bounce in urban floods, dark water returns), the pervasive presence of speckle noise, geometric distortions like layover and shadow, and the critical, non-negotiable need for pixel-accurate flood boundary delineation for actionable intelligence. To the best of our knowledge, no existing work presents a dedicated Mamba-based encoder-decoder network specifically architected and optimized for the flood segmentation task from single-temporal or multi-temporal SAR data.

To address the outlined challenges and bridge this identified research gap, this paper proposes FM-Mamba, an end-to-end non-causal Mamba-based network for efficient Flood Mapping. Non-causal Mamba-based encoder

Since the core component of the encoder is the non-causal Mamba Block, we will first introduce the Mamba mechanism used in the blocks, then proceeds to the whole encoder itself.

The Mamba mechanism implements a selective SSM. This design introduces content-dependent reasoning through an input-dependent parameter selection mechanism, allowing the model to efficiently propagate or discard information across sequences with linear computational complexity. The key advantage of this approach is its ability to model long-range dependencies while avoiding the quadratic complexity bottleneck with respect to sequence length inherent in Transformer-based architectures, achieving expressive power comparable to that of self-attention.

The encoder of FM-Mamba is built exclusively upon a stack of Non-Causal Bidirectional Mamba Blocks (Fig. 2), representing a deliberate shift from conventional CNN and Transformer backbones for SAR flood mapping. Unlike native unidirectional causal Mamba, which is originally designed for autoregressive sequence modeling, our non-causal variant processes 2D imagery bidirectionally along both row and column dimensions, enabling each pixel to aggregate spatial context from four directions (up, down, left, and right). This design is particularly suitable for SAR flood mapping because flood extents usually exhibit isotropic and spatially continuous structures, where accurate delineation depends on integrating contextual information across multiple directions rather than following a single directional scan order. In this sense, unidirectional causal scanning is not well aligned with the spatial dependency pattern of flood inundation, whereas the proposed bidirectional design provides a more appropriate mechanism for dense prediction.

From a computational perspective, this global context modeling is achieved with linear complexity with respect to sequence length, avoiding the quadratic scaling of full self-attention and remaining more efficient than heavier global-context alternatives such as axial-attention-style decompositions. In the experimental section, we further report parameter counts and MACs to provide empirical evidence of the favorable accuracy-efficiency trade-off achieved by FM-Mamba.

The encoding pathway follows a hierarchical multi-scale design optimized for dense flood segmentation. It begins with a lightweight Stem Block composed of two stacked 3 × 3 convolutions with stride 2, which reduces spatial resolution while extracting low-level SAR backscatter features. Four subsequent Mamba stages progressively downsample the feature maps and expand the channel dimensions from 64 to 512. This hierarchical structure captures representations ranging from fine local textures to large-scale flood extent structures, while providing standardized multi-level skip connections for high-resolution spatial detail recovery in the decoder.

Context-aware lightweight decoder

The decoder in FM-Mamba is designed as a Context-Aware Lightweight Decoder that complements the Mamba-based encoder by efficiently reconstructing high-resolution flood masks while preserving fine boundary details. It leverages skip connections from the four hierarchical encoder stages to fuse high-level semantic cues with low-level spatial information. Each upsampling block employs a computationally efficient design, utilizing pointwise convolutions, batch normalization, and pixel shuffle operations. To counteract the loss of fine details inherent in downsampling, we introduce direct, full-resolution pathways that shunt shallow features from the stem block directly toward the final output, ensuring sharp delineation of complex flood boundaries.

Central to the decoder’s performance are the strategically placed Global Context Enhancement modules. These modules incorporate lightweight channel attention mechanisms to dynamically recalibrate feature channels, amplifying those relevant to floodwaters while suppressing responses associated with SAR artifacts like speckle noise or permanent water bodies. The final step is a simple 1 × 1 convolution followed by a sigmoid activation, yielding a crisp, binary flood probability map.

Together, the encoder and decoder form a cohesive system. The encoder provides the global understanding necessary to identify flood-prone regions, while the decoder translates this understanding into a precise, high-resolution map. This synergy enables FM-Mamba to deliver SOTA accuracy with exceptionally low computational overhead.

In the context of SAR-based flood mapping with FM-Mamba, this channel attention mechanism serves a pivotal function in enhancing the discriminative capability between floodwater and confounding features such as permanent water bodies, urban infrastructure, and speckle noise. It achieves this by adaptively amplifying feature channels that encode distinctive backscatter signatures characteristic of inundated regions. Crucially, this enhancement is realized with negligible computational overhead.

Global context enhancement module

Accurate flood boundary delineation in SAR imagery demands a delicate balance between global semantic coherence and local spatial fidelity. To this end, the proposed FM-Mamba integrates a Global Context Enhancement module within its decoder to harmonize long-range contextual cues from deep encoder stages with fine-grained structural details from shallow layers. This module is deliberately positioned at critical upsampling junctions to recalibrate feature representations before resolution recovery, thereby preventing the attenuation of subtle backscatter signatures that distinguish transient inundation from spectrally similar background elements.

The Global Context Enhancement module comprises two complementary components, as shown in Fig. 1. Firstly, a lightweight global context branch aggregates spatially invariant statistics through global average pooling, projects them into a reduced channel space via a pointwise convolution, and broadcasts the resulting descriptor back to full resolution through bilinear interpolation. This enriched context is then concatenated with the main feature stream to augment its semantic capacity without introducing spatial bias. Secondly, channel attention mechanisms are applied to selected shallow skip connections, specifically those from the first and second encoder stages, to adaptively amplify channels that encode boundary sharpness and suppress those dominated by speckle or irrelevant textures. These attention-modulated features are resized to match the decoder’s current resolution and fused additively with the main path, ensuring that both coarse flood extent and fine edge geometry are jointly preserved throughout the reconstruction process.

This design delivers three distinct advantages for operational flood mapping. (1) It enhances discrimination between transient floodwater and permanent water bodies by leveraging global context to interpret local backscatter anomalies. (2) It improves robustness against SAR-specific interference such as speckle noise and geometric distortions through channel-wise recalibration. (3) Most importantly, it achieves these gains with minimal computational overhead.

Full resolution block

The final step in FM-Mamba is the Full Resolution Block, which assembles the final segmentation map at the original input resolution. This block first refines the decoder’s highest-resolution features with a 3 × 3 convolution, batch normalization, and ReLU activation. It then uses a pixel shuffle operation, preceded by a 1 × 1 convolution, to efficiently increase spatial resolution.

To preserve the finest details, the output of this upsampling is concatenated with attention-enhanced shallow features propagated directly from the encoder’s stem block via a lateral connection. A final 1 × 1 convolution layer followed by a sigmoid activation produces the per-pixel flood probability map. By relying on efficient pixel shuffling and direct skip connections rather than costly transposed convolutions, this block ensures the generation of sharp, accurate flood boundaries while maintaining the network’s exceptionally lightweight profile.

Datasets

To ensure a fair and reproducible evaluation, we conduct experiments on two publicly available SAR flood mapping benchmarks, Sen1Floods1138 and S1GFloods39, and use the released benchmark data as provided by the original dataset authors. We do not apply an additional custom SAR preprocessing pipeline prior to model training and evaluation; instead, our experiments are performed directly on the publicly released inputs and annotations.

For Sen1Floods11, we follow the official benchmark setting and use the released Sentinel-1 samples and corresponding labels. The original dataset paper describes Sen1Floods11 as a benchmark containing raw Sentinel-1 imagery together with classified permanent-water and flood-water labels, with Sentinel-1 imagery sampled to 10 m resolution and including VV and VH bands.

For S1GFloods, we use the released benchmark data introduced by Saleh et al., which is described as a global Sentinel-1 flood detection dataset containing 5,360 image pairs at 256 × 256 resolution across 46 flood events. Since FM-Mamba is designed for single-temporal flood segmentation, we adopt a single-temporal evaluation protocol on this benchmark by using only the post-event Sentinel-1 image and the corresponding flood annotation, while the paired pre-event image is not used as model input.

All experiments are conducted on a Linux workstation running Ubuntu 22.04 LTS, equipped with an AMD Ryzen 9 9950X CPU and an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3090 GPU. We implement all models in Python using PyTorch 2.3. Training is performed with a batch size of 8 using the AdamW optimizer, with an initial learning rate of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$1 \times 10^{-4}$$\end{document}. All models are trained for 100 epochs under the same hardware environment and optimization settings unless otherwise specified, to ensure fair comparison across baselines and ablation variants.

To assess practical viability, we measure model complexity via the total number of learnable parameters and computational cost via Multiply-Accumulate Operations (MACs). It correctly excludes stable lakes and rivers from the flood prediction, whereas several baselines, including R2FloodNet, R2AttFlood, and even WSSS, frequently conflate permanent water with transient inundation, leading to significant overestimation. Moreover, in complex landscapes such as agricultural zones with mixed backscatter or mountainous terrain with shadow artifacts, FM-Mamba maintains coherent flood object shapes and avoids spurious fragmentation. This robustness stems from its long-range contextual modeling, which enables holistic reasoning beyond local pixel intensities.

Across both datasets, FM-Mamba exhibits minimal speckle noise propagation and fewer isolated false alarms in dry regions, resulting in cleaner, more operationally reliable maps. These visual advantages directly translate to real-world utility: emergency responders can trust the delineated flood extents for evacuation planning without post-hoc filtering, and the model’s precision reduces the risk of misallocating limited resources.

Conclusion

Core contributions and key findings

This paper presents FM-Mamba, an efficient end-to-end encoder-decoder network tailored for flood mapping from single-temporal SAR imagery. To address the long-standing accuracy-efficiency trade-off in operational flood mapping, we design a non-causal Mamba-based encoder for linear-complexity global context modeling, and a lightweight context-aware decoder co-optimized with the encoder for precise flood boundary delineation and SAR-specific noise suppression.

We clarify that our work focuses on the task-specific adaptation and co-design of the Mamba architecture for SAR flood mapping, rather than proposing a novel core state space model mechanism. Our key contribution is to bridge the gap between the advanced Mamba architecture and the practical requirements of operational flood mapping, achieving SOTA segmentation accuracy with an extremely lightweight model footprint.

Extensive experiments on two public benchmarks, Sen1Floods11 and S1GFloods, validate the effectiveness of our approach. Quantitatively, FM-Mamba matched or surpassed the performance of SOTA models, including advanced CNNs, U-Net variants with attention, and recurrent architectures, in key metrics like F1-score and IoU. Qualitatively, it demonstrated superior ability to delineate precise flood boundaries and distinguish floodwater from permanent water bodies. Most significantly, it achieved this high accuracy with unprecedented efficiency, utilizing merely 3.93M parameters and a fraction of the computational cost (MACs) of other models. This successfully resolves the prevalent trade-off between accuracy and efficiency in flood mapping models.

Limitations

We acknowledge several minor limitations of this work. The model exhibits slight performance degradation in a small number of extreme SAR imaging scenarios, including dense built-up areas with strong double-bounce scattering, high-slope mountainous regions with severe topographic distortions, and densely vegetated wetlands with extremely fragmented flood patches. These performance boundaries arise primarily from the inherent physical characteristics of SAR imaging systems, rather than fundamental flaws in our model architecture. The existing qualitative comparisons have fully demonstrated that FM-Mamba maintains significantly more robust performance in these complex scenarios than all compared baseline methods.

Future work and broader operational impact

Building on the findings of this work, future research will focus on four key directions: (1) extending the framework to leverage multi-temporal SAR data streams for dynamic continuous flood monitoring; (2) integrating auxiliary geospatial prior information (e.g., digital elevation models, land cover maps) to further enhance model robustness in extreme edge cases; (3) conducting model quantization and pruning optimization for ultra-low-power edge device deployment; (4) adapting the FM-Mamba architecture to other high-priority geospatial segmentation tasks, such as landslide mapping, wildfire burn scar delineation, and fine-grained land cover classification.

The superior balance of SOTA segmentation accuracy and extreme computational efficiency positions FM-Mamba as a highly practical solution for real-world flood disaster management. The model can be seamlessly integrated into operational global flood monitoring systems, including the Copernicus Emergency Management Service Rapid Mapping workflow, to enable near-real-time flood extent mapping from Sentinel-1 data with minimal computational resources. Its ultra-lightweight footprint also supports deployment on drone onboard computers and small satellite payloads for on-board processing in remote areas with limited connectivity. This work has the potential to improve the timeliness and accuracy of flood mapping for emergency response, resource allocation, and risk mitigation, ultimately supporting the reduction of life and economic losses caused by flood disasters.

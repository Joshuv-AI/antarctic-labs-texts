Multivalent amphiphilic cyclodextrins for selective membrane disruption with potent antifungal activity
Authors: Atsushi Miyagawa, Satoru Nakagawa, Tatsuya Hagiwara, Yasushi Yamamoto, Tsuyoshi Imada, Natsuki Inaba, Hisato Kato, Kazufumi Masuda, Kayo Osawa, Hatsuo Yamamura
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Multivalent amphiphilic cyclodextrins were engineered as supramolecular interfacial disruptors for selective fungal membrane destabilisation. Cooperative cationic–hydrophobic tuning induces surfactant-like membrane collapse distinct from polyene channel formation. Precise multivalency decouples antifungal potency from haemolysis, establishing a general platform for selective membrane-active molecular design.

Introduction

In recent years, there has been an exponential increase in the global incidence of severe infections caused by pathogenic fungi. This phenomenon can be attributed to the recent increase in the number of immunosuppressed individuals, including those suffering from cancer, undergoing organ transplants, and afflicted by chronic diseases such as diabetes, as well as the elderly.1 In the domain of pathogenic fungi, Candida is a prevalent species, with a reported fatality rate of over 60% for invasive candidiasis.2 In comparison with the numerous classes of antibacterial drugs available, only three classes of antifungal drugs are currently employed in clinical practice: azole, echinocandin and polyene. The emergence of drug-resistant fungal strains has further restricted treatment options and posed a serious challenge in clinical practice.3 In view of the above, there is a strong need to develop new antifungal drugs that differ from the existing mechanism of action. Nevertheless, over the past two decades, no novel class of antifungals for candidaemia has been approved. Polyene antibiotics have been shown to exhibit potent antifungal activity by damaging fungal membranes. Wang et al. discovered a novel polyene antifungal antibiotic, mandimycin, using a phylogeny-guided natural-product discovery platform.4 It was demonstrated that mandimycin exhibited a mechanism of action that targeted phospholipids in the fungal cell membranes. In addition, it was found to reduce nephrotoxicity in comparison to the clinically-used polyene antifungal drug, amphotericin B. Membrane disruption is a powerful yet challenging strategy in antimicrobial design, due to the necessity of achieving selectivity between pathogenic and mammalian membranes. Fungi are eukaryotic organisms, as are humans, which represents a significant impediment to the development of new pharmaceuticals. Consequently, membrane-targeted approaches require precise physicochemical control. Rather than focusing solely on antifungal drug discovery, we address a more fundamental question: How can multivalent supramolecular amphiphiles be designed to exert controlled membrane-disruptive activity through interfacial adsorption? Cyclodextrins (CDs) provide a rigid, well-defined scaffold capable of multivalent functionalization. Previously, we demonstrated that amphiphilic CD derivatives disrupt bacterial membranes via cooperative electrostatic and hydrophobic interactions.5 Here, we extend this molecular design concept to fungal membranes and use antifungal activity as a functional readout to uncover the underlying principles of supramolecular interfacial membrane disruption.

Results and discussion

Alkylamino-modified CD derivatives and their antifungal activity

β-CD derivatives bearing seven alkylamino arms (butylamino–heptylamino) were synthesised via click chemistry to yield the derivatives 1–4.5c The design concept is illustrated as follows: cationic amino groups for electrostatic interaction with negatively charged membrane components, hydrophobic alkyl chains for insertion into lipid bilayers and multivalency for cooperative interfacial destabilization. Their antifungal properties were evaluated by measuring the minimum growth inhibition concentration (MIC) against the fungi (Fig. 1).††The MIC values of the fungi used in this study against the clinically used antifungal agents are presented in the SI. The antifungal evaluation revealed clear structure–activity relationships. Increasing alkyl chain length enhanced potency. β-CD derivatives possessing butylamino groups (1) and pentylamino groups (2) have been demonstrated to be less effective against a Candida albicans strain. In contrast, the derivative with heptylamino groups (4) exhibited good antifungal activity, whose MIC value was 3–5 µM. In the context of Candida parapsilosis and azole-resistant Candida tropicalis, the MIC values for heptylamino CD 4 were determined to be 5 µM, while those for hexylamino 3 were found to be 21 µM and 3 µM, respectively. For clinically isolated pathogens, azole-resistant Candida krusei, a causative fungi of candidiasis, and azole-echinocandine-resistant Trichosporon asahii, the causative fungi of trichosporonosis, the β-CDs with heptylamino groups (4) and hexylamino groups (3) demonstrated excellent antifungal properties at MICs of 3–5 µM. It was also demonstrated that the pentylamino 2 also exhibited a MIC value of 6 µM against Trichosporon asahii. In view of the multi-drug resistance exhibited by the C. krusei and Trichosporon asahii strains to the existing antifungal medications, it can be concluded that these β-CD derivatives possess excellent antifungal properties.

Cell membrane damage as mechanism of antifungal activity

The antifungal properties of the alkylamino CDs exhibited here were found to be attributable to the sterilization of fungal cells through the damage of their cell membranes. The study measured the efflux of inner potassium ions from the cell of C. albicans due to the damage to the cell membrane by the CDs. The ion-selective electrodes were utilised to measure this efflux5c and yielding the following results: hexylamino 3 ≥ heptylamino 4 ≥ pentylamino 2 > butylamino 1 (Fig. 2). This trend exhibited an inverse correlation with MIC values, thereby confirming membrane damage as the primary cause of fungal death.

The efflux of potassium ions from the cell of C. albicans by (a) butylamino (1), (b) pentylamino (2), (c) hexylamino (3) and (d) heptylamino (4) CDs. Amphotericin B (216 µM) was used to determine the 100% level of K+ efflux.

In order to investigate the mechanism of fungal membrane damage caused by CD derivatives, a comparison was made between the effect of CD derivatives on membranes and that of the membrane-damaging antifungal drug amphotericin B. In this study, liposomes composed of phosphatidylcholine and ergosterol, with a molar ratio of 1 : 0.75, were prepared as models of fungal cells. These liposomes encapsulated calcein-KOH in the inner water phase and the leakage of potassium and calcein caused by the CD derivatives was measured simultaneously using potassium ion electrodes and fluorescence spectrometers, respectively.6 The experiment showed leakage exceeding 90% for calcein and over 85% for potassium in both hexylamino CD 3 and heptylamino 4, the compounds that exhibited excellent antifungal properties (Fig. 3). Conversely, for amphotericin B, the leakage of potassium ions was found to be almost 100%, while the leakage of calcein was comparatively low, at approximately 20%. Amphotericin B has been demonstrated to bind to ergosterol, subsequently assembling to form ion channels in the fungal cell membrane to leak potassium ions from the fungal cells, thus leading to their demise.7 In consideration of the aforementioned findings, it was hypothesised that the mechanism of the CD derivatives is not channel formation but surfactant-like interfacial collapse.

Surfactant-like action

In order to confirm the surfactant-like action of the CDs, the physical properties of an aqueous solution of hexylamino CD 3 were investigated. Firstly, a decrease in surface tension value of 3 with an increase in its concentration was observed (Fig. 4). This demonstrated that hexylamino CD 3 molecules are adsorbed at the air/water interface, indicating that 3 has surfactant properties (air/water interface adsorption capacity). Furthermore, the concentration that corresponds to the bend point of the surface tension value has been ascertained to be 2.3 mM. Subsequently, the dynamic light scattering measurements of the CD 3 aqueous solution revealed the presence of multiple peaks in the range of a few to 100–1000 nm at concentrations both above and below the bend point (Fig. 5). These findings indicated the presence of hexylamino CD molecules 3 in a polydispersed state within water. Consequently, the concentration of the bend point in the surface tension measurement results mentioned above should be regarded as the gas/water interface saturation concentration (or critical aggregate concentration, CAC) rather than the critical micelle concentration (CMC). The findings indicate that hexylamino CD 3 exhibit distinctive characteristics. The molecules behave as non-micellar interfacial amphiphiles that preferentially adsorb to interfaces rather than forming bulk aggregates. The CDs exhibit behaviour consistent with that of interfacially active multivalent disruptors, rather than classical micelle-forming surfactants.

Structural tuning

In order to explore multivalency effects, further structural tuning was achieved. The investigation focused on the derivatives with butylamino groups 1 and pentylamino groups 2, which demonstrated suboptimal antifungal properties. The synthesis of compounds 5–12 was accomplished through a click reaction, employing the corresponding alkylaminoalkyne as the reactant (Scheme 1). However, the antifungal properties were not enhanced by the corresponding γ-CD analogues, 5 and 6 which possess eight alkylamino groups (one more those of β-CD derivatives 1 and 2) (Fig. 6). In contrast, the insertion of additional alkylamino groups into the existing butylamino and pentylamino groups resulted in notable outcomes. In particular, the butylaminobutylamino group-containing 8, corresponding to the derivative where the butylamino moiety was inserted to 5, exhibited promising antifungal properties against C. tropicalis and Trichosporon asahii. (the MIC value 0.8 µM and 1.7 µM, respectively). In the case of the pentylamino-modified 12 (pentylamino-inserted to 6), its MIC values were determined to be 0.8 µM for Trichosporon asahii, and 1.6 µM for C. krusei. It is noteworthy that the incorporation of additional alkylamino components into the derivatives that exhibited no antifungal properties exerts to demonstrate MIC values that reach as low as 1 µM. The alteration in activity resulting from alkylamino group insertion can be attributed to two factors: an increase in overall chain length and the introduction of the N atom. In order to elucidate the effect of the additional N atom on activity, the aminobutylamino group-containing 13 was synthesised and its antifungal activity was examined. The CD 13 is characterised by the presence of additional amino groups that are attached to the termini of the butylamino groups of 5. The MIC values of the 13 were found to be lower than those of the butylamino 5 (the MIC value 7 µM for C. parapsilosis and 4 µM for C. tropicalis). This observation indicates the significance of additional nitrogen atoms. However, MIC values of 5 were observed to be larger than those of the butylaminobutylamino 8, and the antifungal activity of 5 appeared to be less than those of alkylaminoalkyllamino CDs. These findings indicate that simultaneous addition of amino and alkyl groups is important for enhancing antibacterial activity. They also indicate that the antifungal activity may not be predominantly influenced by the terminal part, but rather by the entire functional group. It is evident that both a butylaminopentylamino group of 9 and a pentylaminobutylamino group of 11 consist of a pentyl group, a butyl group and the two amino groups. However, a divergence in antifungal properties is observed between the two. For instance, 11 exhibits superior antifungal activity against C. krusei, suggesting that a subtle variation in structure may be a contributing factor.

The MIC values (µM) of alkylamino γ-CDs 5–13 against fungis (C. albicans, fluconazole-resistant C. albicans, C. parapsilosis and azole-resistant C. tropicalis, azole-resistant C. krusei and azole-echinocandine-resistant Trichosporon asahii).

A particularly intriguing facet of this study is its demonstration of selective toxicity towards fungi. The haemolytic activity assay was performed and the selectivity index values were determined by the ratio of the HC50 from the haemolytic activity assay to the MIC (HC50/MIC) (Table 1). In particular, the haemolytic activity of antifungal pentylaminopentylamino 12 (50 µM) remained at 2% and the selectivity index was >31 against C. crusei and >62 against Tricosporon asahi. Conversely, the β-CD derivatives with hexyl- and heptylamino groups 3 and 4 exhibited noteworthy antifungal properties, yet attained levels of over 100% haemolysis at 50 µM and the selectivity index was 1 against the two fungi, signifying a substantial degree of toxicity towards animal cells. These results indicate that the additional insertion of alkylamino moieties is a promising structural modification strategy to reduce toxicity to animal cells while increasing antifungal properties. It has been established that there is a correlation between the haemolytic activity of CD derivatives and their degree of hydrophobicity.5b,c The log P values, which are indicative of the hydrophobicity, of substituted glucose contained in highly antifungal hexyl- and heptylamino CD derivatives 3 and 4, are −0.06 and 0.36, respectively. In contrast, the majority of CD derivatives (with the exception of 12) with additional aminoalkyl parts inserted into structures with butylamino and pentylamino groups exhibited a high degree of hydrophilicity, with a log P of the substituted glucose of −0.3 or less (see SI). The antifungal pentylaminopentylamino CD 12, which exhibited a log P of the substituted glucose of 0.12, closely resembling that of the haemolytic hexylamino 3, demonstrated a mere 2% haemolysis at 50 µM use. Here, two distinct phenomena were identified upon incorporation of additional alkylamino moieties. Certain CD derivatives exhibited low hydrophobicity and an absence of haemolytic activity, while maintaining antifungal activity. In contrast, other CD derivatives demonstrated both antifungal activity and increased hydrophobicity, yet did not induce red blood cell damage. The alkylaminoalkylamino group (or combination of two amino groups and two hydrophobic alkyl groups) may confer unique properties that modulate its interaction with fungal and erythrocyte membranes. These findings demonstrate that cooperative multivalent architecture, rather than simple amphiphilicity, governs selectivity.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this study delivers two advances. Firstly, it identifies potent antifungal agents effective against resistant pathogens. In order to evaluate the potential of CD derivatives to reduce the risk of antifungal resistance, further studies including the resistance induction assay will be performed. Secondly, it establishes a supramolecular membrane-engineering principle based on controlled surfactant-like interfacial disruption driven by cooperative multivalent amphiphilicity. Unlike previously reported CD-based antimicrobials, the present system enables controllable membrane destabilisation with tunable selectivity between fungal and mammalian membranes. The simultaneous leakage of potassium ions and calcein, in contrast to the selective ion leakage characteristic of amphotericin B, suggests a non-channel-based mechanism involving global membrane destabilisation rather than discrete pore formation. The study with transmission electron microscopy and/or scanning electron microscopy observations of liposome morphology change caused by the CDs as well as molecular dynamics simulations could potentially contribute to validating the surfactant mechanism and these will be conducted in the near future. Moreover, the absence of defined micellar structures and the observed polydispersity suggest that activity arises from interfacial adsorption rather than bulk aggregation. Notably, compounds with comparable hydrophobicity exhibited markedly different haemolytic behaviour, indicating that cooperative multivalent architecture rather than simple amphiphilicity governs membrane selectivity. Antifungal activity thus serves as functional validation of a broader supramolecular design concept. Multivalent amphiphilic CDs therefore represent a versatile molecular platform for selective membrane disruption and provide a generalizable framework for membrane-active molecular engineering.

Experimental

General chemistry

1H and 13C spectra were recorded at 30 °C on a Bruker AVANCE400Plus Nanobay, AVANCE500US CryoProbe and a AVANCE NEO 600 CryoProbe. MALDI-TOF mass measurements were carried out with a JEOL JMS-S3000 spectrometer. A microwave reactor was a Biotage Initiator EXP used for Huisgen click reaction.

Syntheses of β-CD derivatives 1–6

The β-CD derivatives 1–4 were synthesized through microwave-assisted click reaction of the 6-azide CD 2,3-O-acetate with N-alkylated Boc-protective propargylamine, followed by the deprotection of acetyl and Boc groups as reported previously.5c The γ-CD derivatives 5 and 6 were synthesized as reported previously.5b

Preparation of diBoc-protected N-(butylaminobutyl)propargylamine by use of diBoc-protected 1,4-diaminobutane8 was described. Other diBoc-protected N-(alkylaminoalkyl)propargylamines were prepared similarly by use of diBoc-protected 1,3-diaminopropane and 1,5-diaminopetane, respectively.9,10 Spectral details of the modified propargylamines are given in SI.

Fungi

The microorganism Candida albicans (ATCC90028), Candida albicans (ATCC90028 resistant to fluconazole), Candida parapsilosis (ATCC90018), Candida tropicalis (ATCC750), C. krusei (clinically isolated in Hyogo Prefecture, Japan) and Trichosporon asahii (clinically isolated in Hyogo Prefecture, Japan) cells were used.†

Minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) of the CD derivatives

The MIC test was measured in accordance with the accuracy standard set out in CLSI M27M44S ED4:2025. MICs were determined by the liquid microdilution method, using serially diluted (two-fold) CDs. Cells (0.5–2.5 × 103 CFU per well) were cultured at 35 ± 2 °C for 48 h in RPMI1640 (0.2 cm3) containing CD in 96-well microtiter plate, with three wells utilised for each CD and sample concentration. The MIC was determined as the lowest concentration of CD at which cells were unable to grow. The experiment was replicated on two or more occasions. The MIC values are seen in the SI.

Haemolytic activity and selectivity index

Rabbit erythrocytes obtained from rabbit blood (NIPPON BIO-TEST LABORATORIES INC.) were suspended in buffer (150 mM NaCl/10 mM Hepes-NaOH, pH 7.4) at a final concentration of 0.5% hematocrit. After incubation with cyclodextrins at 37 °C for 30 min, hemolysis was estimated by measuring the absorbance at 540 nm. Lysolethicin (50 µM) was used to determine the 100% level of hemolysis. The experiment was replicated on two or more occasions. The selectivity index values were determined by the ratio of the HC50 from the haemolytic activity assay to the MIC (HC50/MIC). The values of haemolytic activity and the selectivity index are seen in the SI and Table 1.

K+ efflux and cell viability from C. albicans cells

C. albicans cells were washed twice with the buffer (100 mM choline chloride/50 mM Mops-Tris, pH 7.2) and suspended in this buffer at 1 × 108 cells cm−3. The final volume of the cell suspension was 1 cm3. The cells were incubated with CD at 37 °C for 30 min. After incubation, 0.1 cm3 of the cell suspension was taken, diluted with physiological saline, and dispersed on an agar plate prepared with 1% polypepton, 4% glucose and 1.5% agar. The colonies were counted after standing overnight at 37 °C and the viability of the cells was determined. The remaining cell suspension was centrifuged, and the amount of K+ in the supernatant was measured using a K+-selective electrode. Amphotericin B (216 µM) was used to determine the 100% level of K+ efflux from fungal cells. The experiment was replicated on two or more occasions.

K+ efflux and calcein release from liposomes6

Liposomes were prepared by the reversed-phase evaporation method as described below. Phosphatidylcholine (PC; 10 mmol, 7.7 mg) and ergosterol (7.5 mmol, 3.0 mg) were dissolved in 1.5 mL of diethyl ether, followed by the addition of 1 mL of 100 mM calcein-KOH (pH 7.0). The mixture was ultrasonicated (UX-050; Mitsui Electric, Chiba, Japan) at 50 W for 1 min at 0 °C to obtain a homogeneous emulsion. The diethyl ether was then removed using a rotary evaporator under reduced pressure at 25 °C. After complete removal of the solvent by passing nitrogen gas through the mixture, a homogeneous liposome suspension was formed. The liposomes were collected by centrifugation for 5 min and washed twice with the buffer (100 mM choline chloride/50 mM Mops-Tris, pH 7.2) to remove unencapsulated calcein potassium salt. The final liposome pellet was resuspended in the same buffer. The liposome suspension was incubated with CD at 30 °C for 15 min. After incubation, the suspension was centrifuged, and the supernatant was collected for measurement of K+ efflux and calcein release. The amount of K+ in the supernatant was measured using a K+-selective electrode. Calcein release was determined from fluorescence intensity at 520 nm (excitation at 490 nm) using a fiber-optic spectrometer (USB2000; Ocean Optics, Dunedin, FL, USA). Melittin (20 µM) was used to determine the 100% level of K+ efflux and calcein release. The experiment was replicated on two or more occasions.

Surface tension measurement

Surface tension measurement (STm) for the solution of hexylamino-modified CD 3 was carried out using a Surface Tensiometer (DY-300; Kyowa Interface Science Corp. Ltd, Saitama, Japan) with a platinum Wilhelmy plate. Multiple solution samples from 0.19 to 2.45 mM were prepared, and each surface tension was automatically recorded at 27 °C ± 1 °C. The measurements were conducted more than two times and experimental errors were ±0.1 mN m−1.

Dynamic light scattering (DLS) measurement

DLS measurements were performed for size of aggregates in several solutions of hexylamino-modified CD 3 using a DLS and Zeta Potential (ZP) measurement systems (Zetasizer Nano ZS, Malvern Panalytical Ltd) with a 173° backscattering detector and a He–Ne laser of 633 nm. Each 1.0 mL solution was injected into a sample cell through 0.20 µm microporous filter and measured automatically. As apparent spherical aggregates for this research, the mean diffusion coefficient (D) is converted to the apparent hydrodynamic diameter (dH) using the Sotkes–Einstein equation,dH = kT/3phDwhere k is the Boltzmann constant, T is the absolute temperature, h is solvent viscosity.

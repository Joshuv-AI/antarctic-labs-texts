Developing a prediction model of career plateau for nurses: A cross‐sectional study
Authors: Hongmei Zhu, Mingzi Li
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimThe study aimed to understand the current situation regarding career plateaus experienced by nurses. The objectives were to analyze factors influencing career plateau, and develop a prediction model for career plateau for nurses.DesignA cross‐sectional survey was conducted using convenience sampling.MethodsParticipants were 2680 nurses from six tertiary hospitals. Univariable and multivariable logistic regression analyses were carried out to investigate the influencing factors and develop a prediction model of career plateau.ResultsThe overall incidence rate of nurses reaching a career plateau was 34%. Logistic regression analysis showed that age, position, whether specialized nurses, life satisfaction, organizational support, personal ability and selection tendency were the factors influencing career plateau. The prediction model indicates that older nurses are more likely to reach a career plateau than their younger counterparts, and those who think they have strong personal ability and do not value their work very much are more likely to have a career plateau. Giving nurses more promotion space and learning opportunities, improving life satisfaction, and organizational support are conducive to reducing the occurrence of career plateau.

INTRODUCTION

Employees' careers are considered plateaued when the likelihood of receiving further assignments of increased responsibility and greater challenges is low (Feldman & Weitz, 1988). This happens when employees have acquired in‐depth knowledge and skills in their current roles but the opportunity for further responsibility in the future is small (Huaman‐Ramirez & Lahlouh, 2022). Career plateau was initially defined by Ference et al. (1977) as the point in a career where the likelihood of additional promotion is very low. It is associated with numerous negative consequences for employees, including lower job satisfaction and organizational commitment, and high‐turnover intention (DiRenzo et al., 2021; Kim & Kim, 2021; Lentz & Allen, 2009). The British Columbia Public Service Exit Survey (2014) indicated that career plateau was the main reason for employees leaving the public sector. Studies of nurses also showed that reaching a career plateau is related to their turnover (Fu, 2014; Liu, Chang, et al., 2014; You, 2015) and reduced job satisfaction, organizational commitment and performance (Li et al., 2014; Zhang & Shan, 2018).

Social exchange theory suggests that employees work hard and offer their services, with the expectation of reciprocation in the form of resources such as money, benefits or status. An imbalance occurs when employees perceive a lack of support from their organization (Blau, 1964). Thus, the occurrence of career plateau is closely related to organizational support. Employees report lower levels of career plateauing when they feel supported by their organization (Hofstetter & Cohen, 2014), senior managers and supervisors (Allen et al., 1999), and respected by supervisors and colleagues (Armstrong‐Stassen, 2008). In turn, a perceived lack of support generates a loss of obligation from the employee towards the organization, resulting in a decreased input to the company, poorer job performance, lower organizational commitment and higher turnover intention (Yang et al., 2019).

Besides organizational aspects, career plateau is also affected by personality traits such as employees' motivation for promotion or job challenges. Ambitious employees with a strong desire for promotion are likely to feel more intensely unsupported than those who are indifferent to these rewards (Silbert, 2005). Individual factors influencing career plateau have a clear four‐dimensional structure: personal ability, choice tendency, promotion desire and family factors (Qin, 2009). These factors can all affect the occurrence of career plateau, and a questionnaire was developed to measure them.

The nursing workforce shortage is a global challenge faced today (Adams et al., 2021; Tutuo & Masoud, 2022). The widespread nursing shortage and outflow of nurses are of concern in China (Cao et al., 2021), and requires more strategies to support (Liu et al., 2021). A recent study shows that the number of nurses per capita in China is below the global level (3.75 nurses per 1000 population), and is far below that of developed countries (Zhang et al., 2021). As nurses with career plateau have higher turnover intention, a prediction model is needed to assess the risk of nurse career plateau based on influencing factors. So, this study aimed to understand the current situation regarding career plateaus experienced by nurses, and analyze factors influencing career plateau, then develop a prediction model for career plateau for nurses. A prediction model could serve as an early warning system to assist nurses and nursing managers identify nurses at risk of reaching a career plateau. Jointly, they could then take measures to manage nurses' careers, ensuring they progress smoothly and the risks associated with career plateau are reduced.

METHODS

Research questions

Through the questionnaire survey to the nurses who work in six tertiary hospitals in Beijing with more than 5 years length service, we try to understand the current situation of nurses ‘career plateau and analyze the factors related to career plateau, and then based on these influencing factors, statistical methods are applied to construct a model that may predict the occurrence of nurses’ career plateau.

Study design

A cross‐sectional survey was conducted using convenience sampling.

Setting and sample

This study was a cross‐sectional survey using convenient sampling. The study took place in tertiary hospitals in Beijing, China, from September to December 2019. Nurses who met the following criteria were invited to participate: (1) registered nurses, (2) length of service ≥5 years, and (3) voluntary participation in the survey. Exclusion criteria were: (1) internship and refresher nurses, and (2) nurses who had been on sick leave or maternity leave in the 6 months preceding the study.

Ethical consideration

This study was approved by the biomedical ethics committee of Peking University (approval number: irb00001052‐19079) on 18 July 2019. The questionnaire was allowed to be distributed to nurses who meet the research requirements in six tertiary hospitals in Beijing. All the participants were given full informed consent, and if nurses were unwilling, they could refuse to participate, or if the questionnaire makes them feel uncomfortable, they can also get out at any time.

Measurement

The questionnaire was adjusted to the situation that nurses were undergoing, with the aim of investigating career plateaus and influencing factors. It comprised four major parts with the following structure.

Part one: Socio‐demographic information

In this study, we collected socio‐demographic and occupational data including gender (male or female), age (≤30, 31–40, 41–50, ≥ 51 years old), marital status (married, single, divorced), having children (one child, two children, three children or more), education level (associate degree/diploma, bachelor degree, master's degree or above), length of service (5–15, 16–25, ≥ 25 years), employment type (tenured nurse, contract nurse, personnel agency), work setting or department (general ward, emergency or ICU ward, operating room, anaesthesia department or supply room, outpatient medical or technology department), working regular night shifts (yes, no), professional title (no titles, preliminary title, intermediate title, advanced title), position (no administrative position, head nurse, head nurse of major department or above), specialist nurse (yes, no), monthly net income (less than 5000, 5001–8000, 8001–11,000, 11,000–14,000, or more than 14,001, in CNY), health status (poor, general, good, very good, excellent) and life satisfaction (very dissatisfied, dissatisfied, average, satisfied, very satisfied).

Part two: Career plateau questionnaire

This section of the questionnaire was developed by Xie et al. (2008), and measured whether nurses have reached a career plateau and, if so, the degree of plateau. The questionnaire includes three dimensions with a total of 16 items: hierarchical plateau (four items) refers to the low likelihood of further promotion within an individual employee's current organization. Content plateau (six items) refers to the low likelihood of individual employees learning new knowledge and skills from their current job, and centralized plateau (six items) refers to the stagnation of movement towards the centre of organizational power, or be given greater responsibility, Among them, items 4, 7, 10 and 15 are positive items, and the remainder were negatively scored. A 5‐point Likert scale was used, with items assigned 1–5 points in the order of ‘completely inconsistent’, ‘relatively inconsistent’, ‘uncertain’, ‘relatively consistent’ and ‘completely consistent’. The judgement method of career plateau was used, following the original author's standard of career plateau where more than three points indicates a career plateau, and a higher score indicates a more serious career plateau. The Cronbach's alpha was 0.733, the test–retest reliability was 0.843, and the content validity index (CVI) assessed by five nursing experts was 0.77, which indicated that the questionnaire had good reliability and validity (Lentz & Allen, 2009).

Part three: Personal influencing factors questionnaire

This questionnaire was developed by Qin (2009), and used to measure the personal influencing factors of participants. The scale includes four dimensions of personal ability (four items), promotion desire (three items), choice tendency (three items) and family factors (three items), with a total of 13 items. Among them, items 2, 3, 4, 6, 10 and 11 are positive items, and the rest are negative items. Using a 5‐point Likert scale, items were assigned 1–5 points where 1 was ‘completely inconsistent’ and 5 was ‘completely consistent’. Higher scores for personal ability, promotion desire, choice tendency and family support meant stronger personal ability, more emphasis on promotion and work and better family support. The Cronbach's alpha coefficient was 0.7853 (Qin, 2009), indicating good internal consistency and reliability.

Part four: Organizational support questionnaire

The questionnaire developed by Bao (2018) was used to measure the perceived organizational support of the participants in this study. The scale includes four dimensions: work support (six items), care for interests (six items), personal development (two items) and organizational climate (three items), with 17 items in total. Among them, items 4 and 9 are negative items, and the rest are positive items. Using a 5‐point Likert scale, positive items were assigned 1–5 points, where 1 indicates ‘completely inconsistent’ and 5 ‘completely consistent’. Higher scores indicate a better sense of organizational support. The Cronbach's alpha of the scale is 0.965, which demonstrates good consistency and reliability.

Data collection

An electronic questionnaire was used to collect data from participants. The researchers contacted the managers of the participating hospitals to explain the purpose and significance of the research, and obtain managers' consent and support. With the help of the nursing department director and the head nurses of the hospitals, the electronic questionnaire was distributed to the head nurse group via the regular head nurses' meeting. The head nurses distributed the questionnaire to their department's nurse group and requested that the clinical nurses complete it. For quality control purposes, the questionnaire used standardized instructions, inclusion and exclusion criteria were applied to the corresponding ‘jump‐out’ questions, and all questions required answers and could only be answered once.

Data analysis

SPSS 21.0 (IBM Corp.) was used for statistical analysis of the data. The measurement data were described by means and standard deviations, and the count data were described by frequency and constituent ratio. Participants' career plateaus were divided into groups with scores exceeding 3 taken as the positive cut‐off point (You, 2015). Career plateau was the independent variable, and the demographic variables, scores of individual influencing factors and organizational support were the dependent variables. Using stepwise binary logistic regression analysis, a prediction model of nurse career plateau was established, with p < 0.05 used for statistical significance. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was drawn based on the predicted probability generated through logistic regression and the occurrence of the actual occupational plateau. The area under the ROC curve (AUC) was used to verify the model's discrimination. AUC closer to 1 indicates better discrimination ability of the model.

DISCUSSION

This study investigated the situation regarding the career plateau of 2680 senior nurses in six tertiary hospitals in Beijing, China. The overall incidence of career plateau was high (34%), and of the three dimensions of plateau, the incidence of hierarchical plateau was the highest (53.8%). Incidence of the centralized plateau was 36.7%, and the incidence of the content plateau was the lowest at 15.6%. The phenomenon of career plateau was, thus, very common among the nurses in this survey, and promotion restriction, which characterizes the hierarchical plateau, is the most common, this part of the results demonstrates the first purpose of this study.

In the nursing profession, promotion mainly involves advancement to administrative posts and professional titles. The opportunities for promotion are limited by the pyramid organization and desktop management mode(Zhang et al., 2016). Reducing the hierarchical structure and implementing a more flat management mode has meant fewer upward posts and opportunities for promotion (Zhong et al., 2009; Zhou et al., 2010).

The discrimination of the model was evaluated by the ROC curve and AUC. AUC evaluation considers low discrimination below 0.6, medium discrimination between 0.6–0.75, and high discrimination above 0.75. Larger AUC indicates better model discrimination for high‐ and low‐risk populations (Wang, 2008). The AUC of the career plateau prediction model established in this study was 0.862, indicating high discrimination, which shows that the verification results of the model were good. This study only verified the internal data of the model. External verification of the model needs to be further examined in future research.

The regression results in our study show that age, position, whether a specialized nurse, life satisfaction, organizational support, personal ability and choice tendency entered the regression equation for career plateau, the construction of this model achieves the second purpose of this study. The influence of age on career plateau is consistent with several Chinese studies (Fu, 2014; Tang et al., 2014). Tong et al. (2018) analysis showed that age influences career plateau, and older head nurses report more serious issues regarding career plateau than younger staff (Zhang et al., 2016). The regression results also show that position can help reduce the sense of stagnation associated with career plateau. Therefore, nurses who fail to get promotion opportunities are more likely to experience career plateau. Previous studies on the career plateau of nurses mostly investigated nurses, head nurses and other managers as independent groups. Our study investigated the position of nurses using an independent observation index. The results show that an increase in position will help to reduce the occurrence of career plateau. Therefore, properly optimizing posts and providing more promotion space and learning opportunities will help alleviate the career plateau of nurses.

Being a specialist nurse is a protective factor against career plateau, suggesting that training specialist nurses and helping nurses develop towards specialization may be helpful in reducing stagnation in their career development. Specialist nurses have specialized clinical qualifications associated with a high level of theory and professional practice in a specific nursing specialty field. They can provide optimal professional services based on this specialized training (Li et al., 2011). In recent years, the specialist nurse development path has become a common career development direction (Shi et al., 2022). China has gradually established several fields for specialized nurse training. Although the training methods have not yet been formalized into a standardized system, the training management system is likely to improve and mature over time to include specialist nurse training objectives, post and responsibility requirements, and standardized selection, training, assessment and certification (Huang et al., 2015). Training nurses to develop into specialized paths and expanding the range of specializations will help reduce the incidence of career plateau for nurses.

Our results also showed life satisfaction is a protective factor against career plateau. Previous research showed a negative correlation between career plateau and job satisfaction (Fu, 2014; Zhang & Shan, 2018). From another perspective, we found that understanding nurses' life satisfaction can predict the likelihood of experiencing a career plateau. Therefore, improving life satisfaction may also be a valuable strategy for coping with career plateau.

Organizational support plays an important role in nurses' career plateaus. American psychologist Eisenberger et al. (1986) first posited a theory of organizational support in 1986. The theory holds that employees in an organization usually consider that the organization attaches importance to their contributions and cares about their happiness. When employees feel the organization's affirmation and concern for their own happiness, they have a sense of obligation to give back to the organization, and usually become proactive in their work to repay the organization (Bao, 2018). Nurses' sense of organizational support is an indicator of the degree of support provided by the hospital to nurses. Its level will affect nurses' attitudes towards the hospital and work, ultimately influencing the stability and development of the nursing team (Liu, Chang, et al., 2014; Liu, Xu, et al., 2014). Our study suggests that improving nurses' sense of organizational support will help to reduce the experience of career plateau.

For influencing factors at the individual level, personal ability entered the regression. When nurses believe that their personal knowledge and skills enable them to cope with their current work, they are more likely to perceive stagnation and the lack of opportunities for more responsibility. Therefore, personal ability has a positive impact on the occurrence of a career plateau. Meanwhile, personal choice tendency negatively affects the occurrence of a career plateau. When individuals value work highly, they will work hard and have a lower perception of a career plateau–a finding consistent with previous research on enterprise employees (Qin, 2009).

Prediction models are useful both for nurses and nursing managers. Nurse shortages and high turnover rates continue to be global concerns for healthcare organizations (Fasbender et al., 2019). Effective career management is important to stabilize the nursing team. Career management helps to establish a harmonious and effective support system for nurses and to realize the overall development goal of their organization. Career management is also conducive to enriching hospital culture and the development of nursing disciplines. Career plateaus are common and typically appear in the mid‐career stage (Xia & Ding, 2008). Empirical studies have shown that career plateaus are related to negative outcomes, such as job satisfaction, organizational commitment and turnover intention. Therefore, identifying and predicting the risk of nurses reaching a career plateau is particularly important. Studies have proposed that prediction models be constructed, incorporating personal and organizational indexes to identify candidates likely to resign so that concrete measures can be taken to avoid that outcome (Zhu et al., 2021). Through the investigation and analysis of career plateau and the factors influencing it, we developed a prediction model for the career plateau of nurses. Application of this model would enable nurses at high risk of reaching a career plateau to be identified early. Targeted coping strategies could then be formulated according to the identified predictive factors to provide human resource managers with a basis for developing management strategies. The model may help to ensure smooth transitions to the next career stage, and this holds value for individual nurses, nursing managers and organizations.

Limitations

This research adopted a cross‐sectional approach to developing a model, this may not so appropriate or accurate as the prospective cohort study. Also, the influencing factors may not have been comprehensive, and a follow‐up survey is needed to determine if there are other key factors that were overlooked. Our survey took place in Beijing, China, using stratified sampling. Generalization of the findings to other populations should be done with caution, and the work should be further expanded in future studies.

CONCLUSIONS

This study developed a prediction model for nurses' career plateau based on factors influencing individuals and organizations. It showed that age, position, specialization, life satisfaction, organizational support, personal ability and selection tendency are factors influencing career plateau. To reduce the negative impact of career plateau, multi‐dimensional methods–including promotion, professional and career development, improvement of life satisfaction and organizational support–could be used to reduce the occurrence of career plateaus and reduce the negative feelings and consequences of nurses' career stagnation.

IMPACT STATEMENT

The study constructed a prediction model, and clearer risk factors of nurses' career plateau were extracted, which should be helpful for nurses to overcome the plateau more peacefully and for nursing managers to better assistance to nurses.

P119 Exploring sleep in people experiencing homelessness: A scoping review
Authors: S Edmed, L de Waal, K Rossa, C Pattinson, C Salom, N Lorimer, S Gadam, S Smith
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPeople experiencing homelessness are among the most socially and economically disadvantaged. Homelessness is associated with a higher risk of adverse health, social and economic outcomes. This review aimed to describe published evidence available regarding sleep in adults experiencing homelessness.MethodGuided by the Scoping Review framework in the Joanna Briggs Institute Manual for Evidence Synthesis, a systematic search of 7 databases was conducted. English language studies were included if they examined any dimension of the sleep experience reported by adults (≥18 years) experiencing homelessness in high income countries. No publication date limits were set.Results101 studies met inclusion criteria (45 quantitative, 43 qualitative, 6 mixed-methods studies, and 7 narrative reviews). Most studies (66%) did not explicitly investigate sleep as a core research objective. Half (52%) were conducted in the United States, with 9 Australian studies (9%). Most (94%) quantitative studies used predominantly purpose-developed, subjective sleep measures. Despite measurement heterogeneity, insufficient sleep and poor sleep quality were frequently endorsed. Qualitative studies identified safety and security, weather exposure, and comfort as important factors impacting sleep. No sleep specific intervention studies were found.DiscussionFindings suggest that more high-quality research is needed, with greater consistency and standardization of measurement to allow evidence to be synthesised. An integrated view of sleep health should be adopted, incorporating expertise across broad sleep health areas with consideration of individuals’ neurophysiology, their beliefs, habits, and experiences, physical environment, and the broader social context. These factors are particularly important in developing public health-focussed interventions for vulnerable populations.

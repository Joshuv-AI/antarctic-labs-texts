Electrohydrodynamic drying of potato (Solanum tuberosum L.): Comparative evaluation of drying characteristics, physicochemical properties and volatile profiles
Authors: Junjun Lian, Changjiang Ding, Liqiang Liu, Chuanqiang Che, Zhiqing Song, Chunxu Qin, Jingli Lu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study examined the effects of electrohydrodynamic (EHD) drying on potatoes (high-starch material) across alternating voltages (0 kV (control), 15, 19, 23, and 27 kV), analyzing drying kinetics and quality parameters. Conventional assessments typically examine drying kinetics, surface morphology, reducing sugars, and protein secondary structure; this study extends the quality assessment framework to include total polyphenol content, starch properties, and volatile organic compounds (VOCs). Results showed that EHD drying significantly improved drying efficiency and produced smoother surfaces. However, it altered the protein secondary structure, reducing digestibility. Notably, EHD drying increased polyphenol concentration. Furthermore, amylose content exceeded the control at voltages above 19 kV. Given polyphenols' cardioprotective properties, amylose's distinctive nutritional profile, and its blood glucose regulatory effects, EHD-dried potatoes possess substantial market potential. For flavor, EHD technology increased aldehyde concentrations (key potato VOCs), enhancing the flavor profile. These findings indicate that EHD drying is particularly suitable for functional food productionKeywords: Electrohydrodynamics, Potato, Drying characteristics, Amylose, Volatile organic compounds

Introduction

For over 6000 years, potatoes have served as a staple food source for human populations and are cultivated in over 100 countries worldwide. The potato is rich in starch, dietary fiber and vitamins, making it a food with high nutritional value (Zhang, Ding, et al., 2025). Since fresh potatoes contain a lot of moisture and are harvested in huge quantities, they are prone to rot and spoilage, resulting in economic losses (Zhang, Ding, Lu, Wang, et al., 2024). It is particularly important to adopt an effective processing method to ensure long-term storage of potatoes to maintain quality. Drying can reduce the moisture content of potatoes, thus slowing down the activity of enzymes and increasing the storage time, which is a very effective way to preserve potatoes. Currently, commonly used potato drying methods have different degrees of influence on potato quality. Hot air drying reduces the overall quality of the material (Polat & Izli, 2022). An et al. (2024) found that the starch components in corn were significantly damaged due to gelatinization during the process of microwave-assisted hot air drying. Infrared drying technology has technical limitations such as insufficient radiation penetration depth and uneven heating of materials in the drying chamber caused by the diffuse reflection of infrared lamps (Jiang et al., 2025). According to relevant research literature, thermal processing technology significantly promotes the degradation of starch in potatoes, thereby leading to the loss of nutrients in potatoes (Saini et al., 2023). Although freeze-drying technology can significantly reduce the degree of product quality deterioration, its equipment investment and energy consumption costs are relatively high (Bhatta et al., 2020). In addition, Bao et al. (2021) found that the freeze-drying process can cause damage to the structure of potato starch. Therefore, freeze-drying technology is not the best choice for potatoes with high starch content. It is crucial to find a new way to extend the storage period and improve the quality of potatoes.

Electrohydrodynamics (EHD) drying is a suited drying technology that utilizes a high-voltage power supply and a precision array system consisting of emitter and collector electrodes to achieve the drying process. When the electric field is strong enough, it induces ionization of the molecules in the air, these charged particles migrate in the electric field and generate air currents, which ultimately form an ionic wind thereby destroying the boundary layer of moist air on the surface of the material and increasing the evaporation of water (Paul & Martynenko, 2022). When these particles hit the surface of the material, the energy they carry enters the interior of the material, further increasing the energy of the water molecules and enhancing the evaporation of the material (Ni et al., 2023). This drying method avoids the significant increase of material temperature in the traditional heating and drying process, which reduces the quality degradation and nutrient loss of the material caused by high temperature, so it is friendly to heat-sensitive materials. However, current research on EHD drying technology is still limited to small-scale drying experiments of fruits and vegetables and has not yet reached the scale required for commercial production (Onwude et al., 2021). To achieve the commercial application of EHD drying technology, a key technical obstacle remains: in large-scale drying systems, the airflow sprayed from the emitter to the collector is bounced back and cannot be promptly discharged from the side above the product, causing a portion of the air above the product to tend towards saturation and significantly reducing the drying efficiency (Onwude et al., 2021). Due to this technical bottleneck, EHD drying technology has not yet been commercialized. Research findings demonstrate that EHD drying exhibits significantly reduced energy consumption in comparison to conventional drying techniques (Ham et al., 2024; Iranshahi et al., 2023). Zhang, Ding, Lu, Zhu, et al. (2024) found that compared with traditional hot air drying, EHD drying technology can effectively maintain the internal structure integrity of yams and significantly increase the retention rate of polyphenols. Zhang, Li, et al. (2025) found that when EHD drying technology was used to dry potatoes, the color quality of potatoes could be significantly improved compared with natural air drying. At the same time, by adjusting the voltage parameters, the texture characteristics (such as elasticity and adhesion) of potatoes could be optimized. Yu et al. (2018) found that EHD drying had a significant impact on the rehydration rate of potatoes, but the effect on the content of reducing sugar was relatively limited. However, previous studies on the application of EHD drying technology to starchy foods generally ignored the impact of this technology on starch properties. Especially in the field of EHD drying of potatoes, there are few literature reports, incomplete evaluation indicators, and especially the lack of key quality indicators such as volatile organic compounds, starch properties, and total polyphenol content, which seriously restricts the in-depth understanding and systematic evaluation of the impact of EHD drying technology on the quality of potatoes. This study aims to deeply reveal the mechanism of the impact of EHD drying technology on the quality of potatoes through multi-dimensional and multi-index comprehensive analysis, and also provide relevant parameter data and experimental basis for future industrialization.

Volatiles are a key component of potato flavor. During the drying process, VOCs in potatoes change, and these changes directly affect the flavor and texture of the final product. For example, VOCs such as aldehydes, alcohols and ketones may produce new flavor compounds during the drying process, giving dried potato products unique flavor characteristics and thus enhancing the market competitiveness of dried potato products. The advantages of headspace solid-phase microextraction gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (HS-SPME-GC–MS) in terms of sensitivity, selectivity, ease of operation, and range of applications make it an ideal choice for VOC detection. Jiang et al. (2023) found aldehydes, alcohols, and ketones to be the major VOCs of steamed potatoes in the determination of different steamed potatoes. So far, the HS-SPME-GC–MS technique has been applied to the detection of VOCs in apples (Martínez et al., 2022) and soybeans (Cai et al., 2021). However, no relevant reports on the application of this technology to the field of VOC detection in dried potato products after electrohydrodynamic drying were found.

Although substantial advancements have been achieved in the EHD drying of potatoes (Cao, Ding, et al., 2021; Yu et al., 2018; Zhang, Tang, et al., 2025), several critical limitations persist: Insufficient evaluation of quality assessment parameters for dehydrated potato products, particularly reducing sugar content and surface morphology. In our study, we systematically examined the impact of EHD drying on the protein conformation, total polyphenol content, starch changes (amylose, particle size), and VOCs in potatoes. Conventional drying methodologies predominantly rely on thermal processes, while potatoes contain significant quantities of thermolabile components (e.g., starch and polyphenols), which may compromise the drying process through structural alterations of starch and nutrient degradation. Our research expands the application scope of EHD technology from conventional fruit and vegetable dehydration to materials with high starch content (potatoes), thereby establishing experimental evidence and theoretical foundations for understanding the EHD drying mechanism and its potential applications in potato processing.

Materials and methods

Experimental materials

The potato variety used in the experiment was the local variety “Mengwu Potato 6” grown in Inner Mongolia. These potatoes were purchased from a fresh food supermarket in Hohhot, Inner Mongolia. The initial wet basis moisture content of potatoes was determined to be 85 ± 1 % using a moisture rapid tester. The potatoes were first peeled to remove the outer skin and then sliced into cylindrical shapes, each measuring 28 mm in diameter and 3 mm in thickness. For each EHD drying treatment condition, we conducted three independent biological replicate experiments. Each biological replicate used freshly prepared potato slices, and the slices were taken from different potato tubers. Three slices were used in each experiment. To inhibit enzymatic browning, the cut potatoes were blanched in boiling water at 100 °C for 5 min to deactivate part of the polyphenol oxidase, and then cooled in pure water for 5 min. The weight of the potato slices was recorded with an electronic weighing device every 0.5 h throughout the drying process. The drying was terminated when the potato slices reached safe storage (moisture content of the wet base <10 %).

Experimental equipment

EHD drying system consists of a high-voltage power supply that can output 0–50 kV AC power and a multi-needle plate electrode as well as the control part, the upper pole plate adopts a multi-needle electrode, each needle is 20 mm long, its shape is sharp, which is good for generating strong electric field strength gradient when applying high-voltage electric field, the spacing between needles and needles is 40 mm. The tip of the needle was 65 mm from the lower pole plate, and the lower plate was a rectangular metal grounding pole. As our experiment was conducted under indoor open conditions, the temperature, humidity and wind speed during the experiment were all indoor environmental conditions. The temperature is maintained at 24 ± 2 °C, the relative humidity at 50 ± 3 %, and the natural wind speed is 0 m/s. In this paper, the ionic wind below 15 kV is too weak and the drying rate is relatively low. At 15 kV, a significant acceleration in drying has been achieved, which can be regarded as the minimum threshold for EHD drying. In this experiment, stable operation can be maintained for a long time at 27 kV. Only when the voltage is further increased does the risk of breakdown exist. To maximize the drying rate, 27 kV is selected as the maximum voltage. In the gradient setting (15, 19, 23, 27 kV), by covering the low, medium, and high voltage ranges, the linear or nonlinear relationship between voltage and drying rate/quality could be systematically analyzed. The thermal anemometer probe was placed in the needle plate electrode system to measure the ionic wind speed at different voltages.

Scanning electron microscopy (SEM)

The potato slices were fixed on the sample stage with conductive tape and sprayed with gold, and then the surfaces of the potato slices after different drying methods were observed using an electron scanning microscope (SU8020, Japan) at a distance of 8 mm from the potato slices at the same position under an accelerating voltage of 5 kV at a magnification of 50× and 100×.

Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FT-IR)

Potassium bromide and potato sample powder were mixed in a ratio of 100:1, and fully ground in an onyx mortar until homogeneous, then the mixture was poured into a tablet press mold, and the powder was pressed into a transparent sheet with a tablet press, and put into an FT-IR (Nicolet6700 FT-IR spectrometer, Nicolet Inc., USA) for scanning of the samples in the wavelength range of the wavelength range was 4000–400 cm−1, and the infrared scanning spectra of potato were obtained by scanning 32 times at a resolution of 4 cm−1. The spectral data were baseline corrected and deconvolution processed by specialized software, and then fitted with second-order derivatives to determine the content of protein secondary structures based on the peak positions and peak shapes of the infrared spectra.

Starch particle size and amylose

(1) Potato powder (50 mg) was accurately weighed and mixed with 0.5 mL of anhydrous ethanol. Subsequently, 2 mL of 1 mol/L NaOH solution was added, and the mixture was shaken thoroughly before heating in a 100 °C water bath for 2 min. After cooling to room temperature, the solution was set aside for later use. Then, 0.1 mL of the treated solution was combined with 0.25 mL of iodine solution and 15 mL of pH 3.5 glacial acetic acid. The mixture was mixed well and reacted at room temperature for 15 min. The absorbance at 563 nm and 511 nm was measured, and the difference between these values was calculated. The amylose content in the sample was determined based on the standard curve.

(2) The particle size was determined by a laser particle size analyzer (Mastersizer 3000, Malvern, UK) with a measurement range of 0.1 μm to 2000 μm. Approximately 50 mg of ground sample was accurately weighed and transferred to a sample cell containing 100 mL of deionized water. After ultrasonic dispersion for 5 min, when the sample was fully dispersed, three consecutive repeat measurements were conducted.

Total polyphenol content (TPC)

Based on the testing of Musci and Yao (2017), the TPC of potato was determined using the Folin-Ciocalteu method, with minor modifications. Rizvi et al. (2023) demonstrated that the accuracy of polyphenol quantification is inversely proportional to the concentration of Na2CO3 solution (7.5 %, 20 %, and 35 %). Specifically, the 7.5 % Na2CO3 solution exhibited superior accuracy in determining polyphenol content in olive oil. To mitigate the detrimental effects of excessive Na2CO3 concentration on polyphenol integrity, we implemented a procedural modification. Specifically, during both the detection and standard curve preparation phases, the sodium carbonate concentration was adjusted from the initial 10 % to an optimized 7.5 %. Take 0.5 g of dried potato powder and 10 mL of distilled water, add to a 15 mL test tube, water bath in the boiling water bath at 100 °C for 30 min, remove and filter. Pipette 2 mL of filtrate, add 2 mL of FC color developer and 6 mL of 7.5 % Na2CO3 solution, respectively, shake well, and develop the color at room temperature for 30–60 min, then determine the absorbance at the wavelength of 765 nm. The absorbance intensity at the wavelength of 765 nm was linearly related to the TPC, and the TPC could be obtained by checking the standard curve.

Reducing sugar content (RSC)

The method for determining the RSC of potatoes was based on the DNS (3,5-dinitro salicylic acid colorimetric) method used by Teixeira et al. (2012), with minor modifications. 0.5 g of dried potato powder sample and 10 mL of distilled water were added to a 15 mL test tube and extracted in a 50 °C water bath for 20 min to leach the reducing sugar. A colored 3-amino-5-nitrosalicylic acid derivative is produced, which has a maximum absorption peak at 540 nm. Volatile organic compounds (VOCs)

3 g of dried potato slices were weighed separately in a headspace bottle, 5 mL of 10 % NaCl solution was added, and 50 μL of 400 ppm 2-methyl-3-heptanone was added to the sample as an internal standard. The above-mentioned mixed samples were subjected to solid-phase microextraction at 50 °C, shaken and adsorbed for 50 min, and then desorbed at 250 °C for 5 min before testing. The method for identifying VOCs: The mass spectrometry information corresponding to each individual chromatographic peak of the sample is compared with the NIST spectral library to match the best molecular formula, and then the mass spectrum of the sample is compared with the standard compound mass spectrum for verification to infer the molecular formula of the VOC. The instrument model was GC–MS (Agilent, USA); the injector model was PAL RTC Autosampler (CTC Analytics AG, Switzerland).

Statistical analysis

The experimental outcomes are presented as the average ± standard deviation derived from multiple independent repetitions. These data were subjected to a one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) to identify any statistically significant variations among the means. In the graphical representation, treatment groups denoted by distinct lowercase letters positioned above the bars or lines signify statistically significant differences at the p < 0.05 level.

Results and analysis

Drying rate analysis

Fig. 1 shows the drying characteristics of potatoes. From Fig. 1a, it can be seen that the moisture content decreased faster in the EHD group with the increase in voltage. Esehaghbeygi and Basiry (2011) found that the drying rate of tomatoes increased in the presence of EHD, which was similar to our experimental results. A similar phenomenon occurs among pears (Polat & Izli, 2022), yams (Zhang, Wang, Kinay, & Dau, 2024), and garlic (Han et al., 2023). Research showed that in a multi-needle plate electrode system, the high-intensity ionic wind generated by corona discharge produced a rebound effect during its migration towards the collector, causing the air to reach saturation and thereby inhibiting the drying rate (Onwude et al., 2021). The research by Zhang et al. (2024b) further confirmed that the turbulence phenomenon triggered by the ionic wind on the material surface weakens the promoting effect of the electric field on the internal mass transfer process of the material. Therefore, as the voltage continues to increase, the drying rate will gradually tend to a stable state. In Fig. 1b, it can be seen that as the internal moisture content of potatoes decreased, the drying rate of potatoes showed different decreasing trends. The order of drying rate from high to low is 27 kV > 23 kV > 19 kV > 15 kV > Control. During the initial phase of the drying process, the ionic wind facilitates the accelerated evaporation of surface moisture from the material, thereby establishing a pronounced moisture gradient between the material's interior and exterior regions. As the evaporation progresses, the internal moisture content of the material progressively diminishes, consequently reducing the moisture gradient, which ultimately results in a deceleration of the drying rate (Zhang et al., 2024b).

Analysis of rehydration rate

The rehydration rate serves as a critical indicator of drying quality, reflecting the extent of damage to cellular structures, including membrane rupture and cytoplasmic loss, as well as the integrity and functionality of cells (Polat, 2024). In Fig. 1c, the rehydration of potatoes was observed to increase across all EHD groups, with the order being 19 kV > 23 kV > 27 kV > 15 kV > Control. The enhanced rehydration capacity of potatoes can be attributed to the improved cell integrity and increased porosity brought about by EHD drying, which helps to form efficient water transport channels (Zhang et al., 2025a). Just as Wang and Ding (2023) found when using EHD to dry carrots, excellent rehydration performance is closely related to the well-preserved cell structure and increased porosity. Liu et al. (2023) demonstrated that under electric field conditions, the degree of damage to apple cell membranes positively correlated with the intensity of the applied electric field. However, previous studies demonstrated that excessive voltage escalation imposed undue stress on the membrane, leading to thinning. When timely membrane recovery failed, irreversible electroporation occurred, resulting in substantial membrane damage and subsequent efflux of intracellular nutrients (Kim et al., 2023; Zhang, Gu, et al., 2021). Our experimental results show that the optimal rehydration rate can be achieved at a voltage of 19 kV, at which point the integrity of the cell structure is enhanced and the porosity increases. However, when the voltage exceeds 23 kV, the rehydration rate drops, indicating that higher voltages lead to the destruction of potato cell structures. Based on these observations, we conclude that the maximum voltage range that potato cells can withstand during dehydration is from 19 kV to 23 kV. Exceeding this threshold voltage will cause damage to the potato cell structure.

Color analysis

The chromatic characteristics of food products exert a significant influence on consumers' visual perception, consequently rendering color a pivotal parameter in the assessment of food quality (Iranshahi et al., 2023). Zhang et al. (2023) believed that since EHD drying is carried out at room temperature, the main factor causing color changes is enzymatic browning. Enzymatic browning in fruits and vegetables is mainly caused by the oxidation of phenolic substances by polyphenol oxidase (Misra et al., 2016). In all experimental groups, potatoes were subjected to blanching treatment, which inactivated a portion of the polyphenol oxidase and slowed down enzymatic browning, but there were still some un-inactivated polyphenol oxidases affecting color changes. According to Table 1, it was found that after EHD drying, the C value of potatoes was higher than that of the control group, indicating that EHD drying can enhance the color of potatoes. The L value can represent the lower degree of browning of the product (Sahoo et al., 2024). The L value of the EHD drying group was higher, indicating that EHD can reduce enzymatic browning. In addition, the h° of the EHD drying group was higher than that of the control group, but different voltages had no significant effect on h°. The decrease in h° of the control group indicated a shift in hue towards red, which might be due to the more obvious enzymatic browning phenomenon in the control group. ∆E can represent the color difference before and after drying. The smaller the ∆E, the better the product can maintain its color. Under 19 kV conditions, the whiteness value and L value reached their maximum. At this time, ∆E was the smallest, indicating that under this drying condition, the activity of polyphenol oxidase was the lowest, and the browning effect was the smallest, resulting in higher color quality.

As illustrated in Fig. 2b, no significant variations in the surface microstructure of potatoes were observed across all drying groups, demonstrating that EHD drying technology, as a relatively gentle drying approach, does not induce substantial destructive alterations to the potato surface microstructure. When correlated with the enhanced moisture diffusion capacity of EHD drying depicted in Fig. 2a, these observations collectively indicate that EHD drying technology effectively promotes moisture diffusion while preserving the intrinsic microstructure of the material surface.

FT-IR analysis

Fig. 3a illustrates the FT-IR of potato samples. Five characteristic absorption peaks are distinctly identified at 3423 cm−1, 2927 cm−1, 1640 cm−1, 1414 cm−1, 1022 cm−1, and 579 cm−1. Notably, the spectral features at 3423 cm−1 and 2927 cm−1 demonstrate significant overlap, which are respectively attributed to the O—H stretching vibration in polysaccharides and the symmetric C—H stretching vibration in methyl groups (Krystyjan et al., 2022). Notably, Guan et al. (2025) demonstrated that elevated voltage during EHD drying of king oyster mushrooms significantly enhanced polysaccharide content, which was attributed to the shortened drying duration that minimized polysaccharide oxidation. Similarly, based on this finding, we infer that EHD drying of potatoes might result in a higher polysaccharide content. The secondary structure of proteins is based on the characteristic absorption of the amide I region 1700–1600 cm−1 by FTIR, and is quantitatively attributed to the secondary structure components through second derivative spectra and deconvolution processing, combined with Gaussian peak fitting. Fig. 3b shows the distribution of the protein secondary structure obtained through the above calculation, as follows: β-sheet (1640–1615 cm−1 and 1698–1689 cm−1); random coil (1649–1641 cm−1); α-helix (1659–1650 cm−1); β-turn (1688–1660 cm−1) (Zeng et al., 2011). From Fig. 3b, it can be seen that the β-sheet and the β-turning angle are the main parts of the secondary structure of potato proteins. Following EHD drying, the β-sheet content exhibited reductions of 8 %, 9 %, 11 %, and 10 % at drying voltages of 15 kV, 19 kV, 23 kV, and 27 kV, respectively. Conversely, the random coil content demonstrated increments of 6 %, 7 %, 6 %, and 6 % under the corresponding conditions. The β-sheet structure has significant extensibility characteristics, and the application of an electric field can lead to the disruption of the β-sheet structure in proteins (Wang et al., 2022). A decrease in β-sheet content is regarded as an important indicator of protein conformational unfolding, which is consistent with the results obtained by Subaşı et al. (2021) in their study on sunflower proteins treated with an electric field. Mendes et al. (2020) reported that potato protein particles had a diameter distribution of 0.5–2 μm. As shown in Fig. 3c, the four peaks observed at 1 μm are highly likely to correspond to potato proteins. When the EHD voltage was set at 15 kV and 19 kV, the position of this characteristic peak was consistent with the control group; however, when the voltage was increased to 23 kV and 27 kV, the peak shifted to the right, indicating an aggregation effect of protein particles. The research results of Wang et al. (2022) also confirmed that an increase in electric field intensity significantly promotes the aggregation of protein particles. These aggregation phenomena substantially diminish the solubility of proteins (Hu et al., 2025). Deng et al. (2020) demonstrated that protein aggregation induces the solidification of protein particles, consequently impairing protein digestibility. Notably, through the analysis of structural alterations in β-sheet, random coil, α-helix, and β-turn, it was demonstrated that EHD drying significantly reduced β-sheet content while increasing random coil formation, thereby indicating its substantial impact on the secondary structure of potato proteins. Nevertheless, the variations in protein secondary structure across different voltage treatment groups did not exhibit significant differences.

Starch particle size and amylose analysis

The particle size distribution of potato starch treated by different drying methods is shown in Fig. 3c, with an average particle size ranging from 59.5 μm to 128 μm. The detection results of the particle size of starch from potatoes of different colors by Krystyjan et al. (2022) were consistent with this study. As can be observed from Fig. 3c, when the EHD voltage is 15 kV, the particle size remains unchanged; when the voltage rises to 19 kV, the particle size begins to show an increasing trend; until the voltage reaches 23 kV and 27 kV, the particle size no longer changes significantly. This phenomenon can be attributed to the electroporation effect of starch, where the envelope structure of starch granules is damaged and the interior is more prone to oxidation reactions with ozone (Han et al., 2012; Li, Wang, Han, et al., 2023). This causes the internal structure of starch to become loose and the exterior to lose its envelope protection, making it more prone to expansion (Han et al., 2012). Another study confirmed that dielectric discharge treatment can lead to partial gelatinization of starch (Zare et al., 2022). Gelatinization causes starch granules to swell and increase in size (Li, Wang, Wang, et al., 2023). This result is consistent with the conclusion found in the previous analysis of rehydration rate, that the electroporation phenomenon begins to occur in the voltage range of 19–23 kV. In addition, another possible mechanism for the increase in particle size was that strong plasma induced polymerization reactions in starch particles, thereby forming larger particles (Han et al., 2020).

Amylose, as one of the key factors influencing food quality, plays a significant role in enhancing the nutritional value of food (Wang, Wu, et al., 2024). Due to its linear molecular structure, amylose exhibits a longer metabolic cycle in the human digestive process, which has a positive effect on blood sugar regulation (Milanezzi & Silva, 2025). Due to the implementation of blanching pretreatment before drying, it may cause the thermosensitive substances (mainly starch) on the surface of potatoes to change during the pretreatment stage. Based on this, Fig. 3d shows the effect of EHD on the amylose content in potatoes under blanching pretreatment conditions. When the voltage is 15 kV, the amylose content is relatively low. Carvalho et al. (2021) obtained similar experimental results when treating starch with dielectric barrier discharge at 14 kV voltage and attributed it to the conversion of amylose into reducing sugars (such as glucose) under this condition. When the voltage exceeds 19 kV, the amylose content exceeds that of the control group. The higher electric field destroys the surface of starch granules and promotes the leaching of amylose (Cao & Gao, 2020). However, Sonkar et al. (2023) confirmed that dielectric barrier discharge can cause the hydrolysis of amylopectin in starch to produce linear amylose fragments. These fragments have similar properties to amylose and thus are overestimated in the measurement of amylose content (Chen et al., 2023). Although the detected amylose content shows an increase with the increase of drying voltage, this phenomenon may be caused by two factors: first, the increase of leached amylose from starch granules; second, the measurement method itself may count the degradation products of amylopectin in the results. The leached amylose is more likely to form resistant starch in the subsequent processing of potato dry products, thereby enhancing the added value of potatoes.

TPC analysis

Polyphenols in potatoes are naturally occurring bioactive compounds with antioxidant activity, anticancer and cardiovascular disease reduction (Lin et al., 2023; Rasheed et al., 2022). However, polyphenols can easily undergo oxidative reactions with oxygen, polyphenol oxidase, and peroxidase, which changes the structure of polyphenols and further affects the bioactivity of polyphenols. The bar graph in Fig. 4 shows the TPC of potatoes. The lower TPC content in the control group was attributed to oxidation of TPC by oxygen and autoxidation of polyphenol oxidase and peroxidase in the potato due to prolonged drying. After drying by the EHD drying method, the TPC content increased by 22.6 %, 18.9 %, 4.6 % and 10 % at 15 kV, 19 kV, 23 kV and 27 kV, respectively. The plasma generated by the high-voltage electric field contains free radicals that can interact with the cells in the potato, resulting in damage to the cellular structure and thus releasing intracellular TPC (Cao et al., 2021a). A significant decrease in TPC starts to occur when the voltage exceeds 19 kV, Shahram and Taghian Dinani (2019) found that when orange pomace TPC was extracted under the action of EHD, TPC increased when the EHD voltage was increased from 0 kV to 18 kV and decreased when the voltage was increased from 18 kV to 22 kV, which is similar to the results of our experiments. Hernández-Corroto et al. (2022) believed that moderate discharge energy could effectively disrupt cell structures and promote the release of polyphenols. However, when the discharge energy exceeds the critical value, excessive free radical generation would lead to the oxidative degradation of polyphenols. Our experimental data showed that under the voltage conditions of 15 kV and 19 kV, the polyphenol content of potatoes was significantly higher than that of other experimental groups. This result confirmed that this drying condition had a significant advantage in retaining polyphenols.

RSC analysis

The line graph in Fig. 4 shows the RSC of potatoes, which was higher than the control at 15 kV dry conditions and lower than the control when the voltage was increased to 19 kV, 23 kV and 27 kV. Similar results were obtained by Li et al. (2019) in treating soybeans by using the electric field and found that soybeans with an electric field strength at 2 kV/m RSC had higher values than those of 10 kV/m and the control (0 kV/m). Under a voltage of 15 kV, the permeability of the cell membrane increased, promoting the release of RSC from cells. However, when the voltage exceeded 19 kV, the release of RSC showed a downward trend. This phenomenon could be attributed to the fact that the high-intensity electric field enhanced the activity of glucose oxidase, causing some glucose to be oxidized and thus reducing RSC (Janositz et al., 2011).

In all groups subjected to varying voltage (Control (0 kV), 15 kV, 19 kV, 23 kV, and 27 kV), the number of detected VOCs was 33, 40, 33, 37, and 29, respectively. Aldehydes represent the predominant class of VOCs in potatoes, primarily generated through lipid oxidation processes. These compounds are characterized by exceptionally low odor thresholds, enabling their detection at minimal concentrations (Geng et al., 2024). According to Zhao et al. (2020), aldehydes constitute the principal compounds influencing food flavor profiles. Our experimental investigation revealed that the EHD drying technique significantly enhanced aldehyde compound relative concentrations by 2.65 % to 4.71 %. Among these, hexanal and (2E)-2-octenal, which impart green grassy notes (Zhang, Tang, et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2025b), constituted 52.92 % to 60.47 % of the total detected aldehyde compounds. Cai et al. (2021) demonstrated a statistically significant negative correlation between grassy flavor intensity and consumer acceptance rates. Subsequently, nonanal exhibited a marginal increase in content following EHD drying. This compound is characterized by a pronounced fatty flavor and rose-like aromatic profile (Zhang et al., 2025c). Notably, aroma, as one of the important indicators in the sensory quality evaluation system, has a significant impact on the improvement of food quality (Luo et al., 2023). Vandamme et al. (2016) demonstrated that (2E)-2-octenal is a significant byproduct of lipid peroxidation. According to Wu et al. (2023), 2-pentylfuran is generated as a by-product of the lipoxygenase-initiated reaction involving unsaturated fatty acids after cellular rupture. Among all the potato samples analyzed, the concentrations of (2E)-2-octenal and 2-pentylfuran demonstrated a positive correlation with the applied EHD voltage. Furthermore, 2-pentylfuran exhibits multiple pharmacological benefits, encompassing analgesic, antitussive, and antioxidant properties (Zhang et al., 2023). The experimental findings demonstrated that elevated voltage significantly accelerates the lipid oxidation process, promotes the formation of aldehyde compounds, and concurrently enhances the functional properties of potatoes.

Alcohol VOCs constitute the predominant VOCs in potatoes, primarily encompassing two distinct categories: high-concentration methanol and low-concentration 1-methylcyclooctanol. Notably, when methanol concentration surpasses the established safety threshold, it manifests substantial toxicological effects (Amidžić Klarić et al., 2015). In our research, through EHD drying, the relative content of methanol decreased by 4.30 % to 9.96 %. However, a definitive linear correlation between methanol concentration and applied voltage remains to be established.

Ketone compounds are predominantly synthesized through the oxidation of unsaturated fatty acids and are typically characterized by possessing balsamic and fruity aromatic properties (Zhou et al., 2022). In potato samples, a total of 9 ketone VOCs were detected, and the number of types was second only to aldehyde VOCs. Although the odor threshold of most ketone compounds is relatively high, they still make significant contributions to the overall flavor characteristics of food (Chen et al., 2025). This study found that 2,4-dimethylheptan-3-one, 5-nonanone and 6-undecanone were the main ketone VOCs, and the content of these compounds showed irregular changes under the action of the electric field, reaching a relatively high level under the voltage condition of 23 kV. Following the application of EHD drying, a novel VOC, 3-octen-2-one, was identified in potato samples. This compound, characterized by its distinctive rose-like aroma, is predominantly utilized as a flavoring agent in the food industry (Yang et al., 2024). Numerous studies have confirmed that fragrance not only significantly influences the sensory quality of food, but also shows a significant positive correlation with consumers' acceptance (Jiang et al., 2023; Yan et al., 2024).

Esters, characterized by their distinctive fruity aroma, play a pivotal role in determining the flavor profile of food products (Chen et al., 2025). In the case of potatoes, although only four ester compounds were identified, their concentrations were notably elevated. Under an applied voltage of 15 kV, the ester relative content exhibited a 0.56 % increment. However, with further voltage escalation, a gradual decline in ester levels was observed, ultimately falling below the control group's baseline. Notably, a robust negative correlation was established between ester and aldehyde concentrations.

Benzene compounds present in food products were primarily associated with environmental contaminants and were recognized as carcinogenic substances (Vinci et al., 2015). The concentration of volatile benzene compounds in potatoes reached its minimum at an EHD voltage of 27 kV, exhibiting a 50 % reduction in species diversity and a 20.8 % decrease in relative content compared to the control group. The investigation conducted by Li et al. (2022) regarding benzene degradation utilizing dielectric barrier discharge technology demonstrates that at an applied voltage of 24 kV, the degradation efficiency of benzene attains 22.6 %. This phenomenon is primarily ascribed to the chemical interactions between reactive species and benzene molecules. Fenglei et al. (2020) further elucidated that the degradation mechanism of benzene through high-voltage discharge plasma predominantly encompasses two distinct pathways: the direct interaction of high-energy electrons with the benzene ring, resulting in structural disruption, and the oxidation of benzene compounds by free radicals, including ozone. In the present study, the degradation of benzene within VOCs under dry conditions at 27 kV can be attributed to the cleavage of chemical bonds and the oxidative transformation of benzene molecules. This finding substantiates the considerable application potential of EHD drying technology in pollutant degradation within dried materials. Furthermore, the implementation of EHD drying techniques in food processing offers consumers enhanced safety and improved hygienic standards in food products.

Fig. 6 shows the results of the analysis of potato volatiles. The characteristic compounds of the samples were analyzed according to the principal component analysis method, and it can be seen from Fig. 6a that the cumulative contribution of PC1 and PC2 reaches 63.5 %, which can express the characteristic odor of the samples (Yan et al., 2024). Samples from the same group were close to each other, with the control and 15 kV experimental groups in the second quadrant, the 19 kV and 23 kV experimental groups in the first quadrant, and the 27 kV experimental group alone in the fourth quadrant. The samples in each quadrant were respectively farther away from the samples in the other two quadrants, which indicates that PCA was able to differentiate the potato flavors of different drying methods.

Fig. 6b shows the OPLS-DA model, a supervised statistical method, which can identify characteristic differential substances among different samples (Chen et al., 2025). Fig. 6c demonstrates the validation process of this model, where the intersection point of the Q2 regression line with the vertical axis below zero confirms the model's acceptability (Luo et al., 2023). The Variable Importance in Projection (VIP) scores, as illustrated in Fig. 6d, are derived from the OPLS-DA model and serve as quantitative indicators of characteristic differential VOCs among samples. Conventionally, VIP scores exceeding 1 signify substantial contributions to sample differentiation (Chen et al., 2025). Among the analyzed VOCs, 19 substances with VIP > 1 were identified, including 5-Nonanone, 2-Methylbutanal, 2-Ethylfuran, Isolongifolene, 6-Undecanone, 1-Methylcyclooctanol, 2,2,4-Trimethyl-1,3-pentanediol diisobutyrate, Toluene, 3,5-Octadien-2-one, 3-Ethylbenzaldehyde, Decanal, Benzeneacetaldehyde, Hexylbenzene, 4-Methyl-3-heptanone, 3-Heptanone, 2,4-Dimethylheptan-3-one, Benzaldehyde, Hexaldehyde, and 1-Phenylbutan-1-one. The concentration variations of these VOCs potentially represent the primary contributors to the odor differences induced by the EHD drying methodology.

Statistical analysis

To show more intuitively the effect of different drying methods of potatoes on various parameters, these parameters were normalized, and Fig. 7 shows the statistical analysis of the results of these parameters. From Fig. 7a, it can be seen that Deff, alcohols, ketones, TPC, RSC and ∆E are more sensitive to different drying methods, and it can be seen that the main VOCs of potato are alcohols, ketones and aldehydes, with the highest content of RSC and TPC at 15 kV, and the smallest ∆E at 19 kV, and the positive correlation between Deff and EHD voltage. The correlation between the different metrics can be analyzed more intuitively in Fig. 7b. Deff and ∆E were positively correlated with rehydration rate, aldehydes and furans, and negatively correlated with esters and RSC. Among them, furans were also positively correlated with rehydration rate and aldehydes. Alcohols were positively correlated with TPC and rehydration rate, while aldehydes were in contrast, which were negatively correlated with TPC and rehydration rate. There is a significant negative correlation between the content of amylose and reducing sugar, which indicates that reducing sugar is highly likely to originate from the hydrolysis process of amylose. ∆E showed a strong positive correlation with Deff, and these results provide a relevant basis for the potato EHD drying mechanism.

Conclusions

This study substantiates that EHD drying technology significantly enhances the overall quality of potato dehydration, with its efficacy being modulated through voltage parameter optimization. Specifically, EHD drying markedly accelerates the dehydration process, substantially improves efficiency, enhances surface smoothness, and preserves polyphenol content. However, EHD drying adversely affects protein structure, resulting in diminished protein digestibility. Regarding voltage selection, 15 kV proves most conducive to flavor compound formation and generates increased reducing sugar content. The application of 19 kV voltage optimally maintains product coloration. When employing voltages of 19 kV or higher, it facilitates amylose retention while simultaneously inhibiting reducing sugar production. Consequently, EHD drying emerges as an efficacious technology for enhancing the quality of dehydrated potato products. In practical applications, voltage selection can be prioritized based on core objectives (efficiency, coloration, flavor profile, polyphenol content, and amylose retention). Among these parameters, 19 kV demonstrates superior equilibrium in efficiency, coloration, and fundamental nutrient retention, rendering it an optimal choice. The findings of this study provide direct and actionable guidance for industrial implementation and parameter optimization of EHD technology in potato dehydration processing. In industrial applications, the potential for large-scale stacking of potato slices at the collector necessitates higher voltage requirements for effective drying. Given the existing limitations in current drying conditions, this study, constrained by voltage as a single variable parameter, was unable to exceed 27 kV. Future research should incorporate multiple variables (voltage, electrode distance) to facilitate voltage enhancement, achieved through simultaneous adjustment of voltage and electrode distance, thereby enabling more efficient potato dehydration. In terms of scalability, EHD drying can be expanded to industrial applications through modular electrode arrays and continuous conveying systems. As we introduced in the introduction section, its energy consumption is also lower compared to traditional drying methods. In conclusion, the EHD drying technology has demonstrated significant technical advantages and application value in the field of potato drying.

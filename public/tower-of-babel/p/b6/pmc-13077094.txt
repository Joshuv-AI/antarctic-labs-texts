Design and thermo-structural analysis of multiple composite layers with ablative materials for passive thermal protection systems
Authors: Jaehyeon Park, Yongha Kim, Ji-Hyun Cha, Sungchan Kim, Oyoung Choi, Dong-Chul Kim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractA computational formulation has been proposed for the thermo-structural analysis of multiple composite layers incorporating ablative materials for passive thermal protection systems. This formulation is based on the semi-analytical finite element method and includes user subroutines to model the progressive decomposition of pyrolyzing materials accurately. Material properties are evaluated through specimen testing, and the design parameters for the composite layers are determined using the proposed formulation, together with the specimen test results. Additionally, the detailed thermo-structural performance of these layers is investigated using 3D finite element analysis. The results are compiled to provide insights into the thermo-structural characteristics of composite layers with ablative materials used in passive thermal protection systems.Keywords: Multiple composite layers, Ablative material, Passive thermal protection system, Thermo-structural analysis, Aerospace applicationSubject terms: Engineering, Materials science, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Spacecraft must endure intense aerodynamic heating and pressure loads resulting from high velocities during the ascent and re-entry phases1–5. The primary function of thermal protection systems (TPSs) is to protect critical onboard components from thermal damage caused by excessive heat6–11 (Fig. 1). TPSs are generally classified into passive and active systems. Passive TPSs mitigate thermal loads through the gradual degradation of ablative materials via vaporization and oxidation processes. The design of such systems requires a comprehensive evaluation of thermo-structural integrity in conjunction with the pyrolysis behavior of ablative materials. Traditional studies have investigated these characteristics using both numerical simulations and experimental approaches. In numerical studies, the pyrolysis behavior of ablative materials has typically been modeled using one-dimensional mathematical formulations. Li et al.12 introduced a one-dimensional pyrolysis-layer model incorporating coupled heat and mass transfer within charring materials during decomposition and surface ablation. Wang et al.13,14 developed a one-dimensional finite element model in ABAQUS to analyze the response of charring ablative materials. Cao et al.15 proposed a homogenization-based thermo-mechanical model to investigate micro-ablation behavior and mechanical degradation in silica fiber–reinforced phenolic resin composites. Guo et al.16 introduced a pyrolysis-rate equation for charring materials to analyze the effect of heating rate on thermal response based on experimental data and the Levenberg–Marquardt algorithm. Xu et al.17 developed and validated a one-dimensional transient thermal model accounting for pyrolysis without surface recession. In contrast, three-dimensional numerical analyses of thermal response and pyrolysis behavior have often relied on experimentally obtained data. Weng and Martin18 employed numerical methods to investigate pyrolysis gas transport in arcjet test specimens, while Meurisse et al.19 performed multidimensional simulations to analyze the material response of tiled ablative heat shields. Yang et al.20 examined the thermal response and pyrolysis behavior of carbon fiber/phthalonitrile composite laminates under unidirectional flame heating using combined experimental and numerical approaches. Meanwhile, detailed pyrolysis behaviors have primarily been investigated through experimental approaches. Shi et al.21 experimentally evaluated the effects of variations in thermal and transport properties on ablation behavior. Ghaly and Abou-Zeid22 investigated fire-resistant plaster reinforced with nano silica–aerogel particles and phyllosilicates through experimental methods. Yan et al.23 employed thermal analysis using multiple analytical techniques to elucidate pyrolysis mechanisms in silicone rubber TPSs under realistic conditions. Qian et al.24 studied the pyrolysis behavior of a nanoporous phenolic matrix using rapid pyrolysis in a specially designed fixed-bed reactor, complemented by in situ thermogravimetric analysis coupled with Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy and mass spectrometry. Duan et al.25 compared the behavior of carbon fiber–reinforced silicon carbide–hafnium carbide composites under oxyacetylene torch flame exposure. Zhang et al.26 conducted experimental investigations on carbon–phenolic composites to characterize pyrolysis behavior using tube furnace heating tests and pyrolysis gas chromatography–mass spectrometry analysis. Most previous studies have focused primarily on the thermal response and pyrolysis behavior of ablative materials. However, for passive TPSs, thermo-mechanical stress characteristics are also critically important in addition to thermal response and pyrolysis behavior. Despite these advances, passive TPSs typically consist of multiple composite layers containing ablative materials, which necessitates thermo-structural analysis of layered configurations during the design process while accounting for pyrolysis effects. Previous studies on multilayer composites have mainly concentrated on thermo-structural analysis, often neglecting the pyrolysis behavior of ablative materials27–30. The development of passive TPSs therefore requires the simultaneous consideration of the thermal response and pyrolysis behavior of ablative materials, as well as the thermo-mechanical stress characteristics of multilayer composite structures. Nevertheless, these aspects have generally been treated separately in existing studies. To overcome this limitation, the present study proposes a computationally efficient approach for analyzing the coupled thermo-structural characteristics and pyrolysis behavior of composite layers containing ablative materials, with applicability to the preliminary design stage of passive TPSs.

Thermal protection systems for space vehicles6.

This article presents a computational framework for analyzing the thermo-structural characteristics and pyrolysis behavior of composite layers containing ablative materials used in passive TPSs. The formulation is based on a semi-analytical finite element method and incorporates user subroutines to model the progressive decomposition of pyrolyzing materials. Material properties are determined through specimen testing, and the design parameters of the composite layers are established using both the proposed formulation and experimental results. A parametric analysis is then conducted to investigate the thermo-structural response of the composite layers based on the identified design parameters. Initial design parameters satisfying the design requirements are obtained through a combined approach involving a one-dimensional formulation, two-dimensional finite element analysis, and specimen test results. The detailed thermo-structural performance of the composite layers is subsequently evaluated using three-dimensional finite element analysis. These results provide a comprehensive understanding of the thermo-structural behavior of composite layers containing ablative materials in passive TPSs. In conclusion, the simplicity and computational efficiency of the proposed approach make it a valuable tool for evaluating thermo-structural responses and supporting preliminary design decisions for passive thermal protection systems.

Problem statement and formulation

Thermo-structural analysis was performed to numerically compute the temperatures and thermal stresses of multiple composite layers. The multiple composite layers with ablative materials for passive TPSs are composed of an ablative layer, heat-resistant layer, insulation layer, and structural layer (Fig. 2), and the surface layer, the ablative material, is exposed to extremely high temperatures caused by complex convective and radiative heating. ta, th, ti and ts are the thicknesses of the ablative layer, heat resistant layer, insulation layer, and structural layer. The heat-resistant layer is composed of a heat-resistant metal, while the structural layer consists of Carbon Fiber Reinforced Plastic (CFRP).

Pyrolysis formulation in the ablative layer

Pyrolysis involves decomposing ablative materials through external convective and radiative heating of TPSs, in the absence of oxygen. During pyrolysis, conductive heat transfer occurs from the external surface to the interior, generating pyrolysis gases. Ablative material is categorized into three distinct states: the char layer, the pyrolysis zone, and virgin material (Fig. 3). The char layer represents the fully decomposed state, the pyrolysis zone indicates where decomposition is actively occurring, and virgin material identifies the unaltered, intact ablative material. Specimen tests for thermal-mechanical properties

The ablative layer is composed of silica/phenolic (S/P) material. To support the thermo-structural analysis, including pyrolysis, ablation tests, density measurements, and thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) were conducted. Material properties of the S/P ablative layer obtained from these specimen tests were used to correlate finite element analysis results with experimental data and to perform thermo-structural analysis, including pyrolysis. Specifically, the ablation, density (ASTM D792), and TGA (ASTM E1641) tests provided quantitative information on surface recession, temperature-dependent density variation, and decomposition kinetics, respectively. These experimental results serve as key input parameters and validation data for the numerical modeling presented in the following sections, thereby establishing the experimental basis of this study.

Parametric analysis of multiple composite layers with ablative materials

A parametric analysis was conducted to examine the thermo-structural characteristics of composite layers incorporating ablative materials, using various design parameters. Initial design parameters were established to meet design requirements through a combination of one-dimensional formulation, two-dimensional finite element analysis, and specimen test results.

Main parameters and responses

The primary parameters of the multiple composite layers, which include ablative materials, are the thicknesses of the ablative, heat-resistant, insulation, and structural layers. The responses considered are the maximum failure index of these layers and the temperature of the structural layer. Design requirements stipulate that the structural layer’s temperature must not exceed 100 °C, the maximum failure index must be 1 or less, and the system must operate for 240 s. Results of the parametric analysis

The temperature of the structural layer is calculated using a one-dimensional (1D) formulation. This calculation relies on the ablation rate and the outer surface temperature of the heat-resistant layer, both derived from a two-dimensional (2D) finite element analysis of the ablative layer. Key parameters influencing the structural layer’s temperature include the thicknesses of both the ablative and insulation layers, which are crucial variables affecting thermal characteristics. The finite element model for the ablative layer is developed in ABAQUS (Fig. 9). The solver employed is ABAQUS (general, coupled temperature-displacement). The model utilizes linear plane strain elements (CPE4T), with the total element count exceeding 1,600 to ensure analysis quality, validated through convergence analysis. The thermal boundary conditions at the outer boundary are implemented using multiple custom user subroutines, with pyrolysis-related material properties incorporated based on specimen test results. At the inner boundary, the thermal boundary conditions are specified as a convection heat transfer coefficient of 25 W/m²·K, an ambient temperature of 100 °C, and a surface emissivity of 0.8. Figures 10 and 11 present the results of the thermo-structural and parametric analyses of the ablative layer. The thermo-structural analysis indicates that the predicted ablation rates agree with the experimental results within 10%, with an average deviation of approximately 6.7%, as summarized in Table 5. Additionally, it demonstrates that an increase in the thickness of the ablative layer significantly reduces the temperature of the outer surface of the heat-resistant layer. A parametric analysis was performed to assess the failure index of the various composite layers, aiming to determine the minimum insulation layer thickness that maintains the structural layer’s temperature below the design threshold. These results are depicted in Fig. 12. The analysis confirms that the failure index stays below 1 when the ablative layer is at least 10 mm thick. Identified failure modes include yield failure of the heat-resistant layer and matrix compression failure of the structural layer. An increased thickness of the heat-resistant layer correlates with a higher risk of matrix compression failure in the structural layer. Given that passive TPSs favor thinner profiles, the study identifies design parameters that offer a safety margin of at least 1 while minimizing thickness. These parameters are detailed in Table 6.

Verification by the 3D thermo-structural analysis

Finite element model

In this section, we perform a 3D thermo-structural analysis of multiple composite layers incorporating ablative materials to thoroughly assess the thermo-structural stability of passive TPSs. Our goal is to determine their thermo-structural characteristics, including pyrolysis behaviors. We develop the finite element model of these composite layers using ABAQUS (Fig. 13). For this analysis, we employ the ABAQUS solver (general, coupled temp-displacement). Passive TPSs are multilayer composite structures composed of materials with mismatched coefficients of thermal expansion and significant through-thickness temperature gradients. Because the pyrolysis behavior of ablative materials primarily evolves along the thickness direction, accurate evaluation of the thermo-mechanical response of passive TPSs requires explicit resolution of through-thickness thermo-mechanical behavior. Accordingly, linear solid elements (C3D8T) are employed, and the total number of elements exceeds 55,000 to ensure solution accuracy, as verified through convergence analysis (Fig. 14). The thermal boundary conditions, operating time, material properties, and pyrolysis-related characteristics remain consistent with those detailed in the previous chapter, while geometric parameters are specified in Table 5. Mechanical constraints are applied to induce pure bending by restraining the in-plane displacement of the mid-surface, fixing all degrees of freedom at the central node of the bottom edge.

Thermo-structural analysis results and discussion

We conducted a 3D thermo-structural analysis of multiple composite layers with ablative materials, including an examination of pyrolysis behaviors. Figure 15 presents the results of these analyses, showing stress and temperature distributions. The maximum stress occurs in the heat-resistant layer, with a safety margin of approximately 0.23. Furthermore, the temperature of the structural layer stays below the design threshold of 100 °C, reaching only about 91.4 °C after 240 s of operation. The structural layer’s maximum failure index is approximately 0.23, with the failure mode identified as matrix compression failure. Consequently, the designed multiple composite layers with ablative material exhibit sufficient thermo-structural integrity under the operating conditions of the passive thermal protection system.

Conclusion

This article presents a computational formulation for thermo-structural analysis that incorporates the pyrolysis behavior of ablative materials in multilayer composite structures for passive TPSs. The formulation is based on a semi-analytical finite element method and incorporates user subroutines to represent the progressive decomposition of pyrolyzing materials. In the proposed approach, the complex pyrolysis behavior of the ablative layer is resolved using two-dimensional finite element analysis, and the resulting information is subsequently employed to evaluate the temperature distribution and thermo-mechanical stresses of the multilayer composite structure through a one-dimensional formulation. This hybrid modeling strategy significantly improves computational efficiency, making the method well suited for iterative analyses at the preliminary design stage. This enhancement in computational efficiency constitutes a key novelty of the proposed approach. Material properties are determined through specimen testing, and the design parameters of composite layers containing ablative materials are identified using the proposed formulation in conjunction with experimental results. A parametric analysis is then conducted to investigate the thermo-structural characteristics of the composite layers based on these design parameters. Initial design configurations that satisfy specified requirements are obtained through a combined use of one-dimensional formulation, two-dimensional finite element analysis, and specimen test data. In addition, a three-dimensional thermo-structural analysis is performed to comprehensively evaluate the thermo-structural stability and behavior of the passive TPSs, including the effects of pyrolysis. The results provide critical insights into the thermo-structural characteristics of multilayer composite structures incorporating ablative materials. In conclusion, the relative simplicity and high computational efficiency of the proposed method make it a valuable and practical tool for thermo-structural evaluation and preliminary design of passive thermal protection systems.

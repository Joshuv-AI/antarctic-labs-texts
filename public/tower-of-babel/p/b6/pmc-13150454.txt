Sodium-Glucose Cotransporter-2 Inhibitors Therapy in Transthyretin Amyloid Cardiomyopathy: A Propensity-Matched Cohort Study
Authors: Luísa Pinheiro, Margarida de Castro, Emídio Mata, Bárbara Lage Garcia, Tamara Pereira, Filipa Cordeiro, Olga Azevedo, António Lourenço
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and ObjectivesThe evidence for sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors (SGLT2i) in heart failure (HF) secondary to transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy (ATTR-CM) is limited. Evaluate the impact of SGLT2i on clinical outcomes in ATTR-CM.MethodsSingle-center, retrospective cohort study of 111 patients with confirmed ATTR-CM between 2014 and 2023. Patients were categorized as SGLT2i-treated or untreated. Propensity score (PS) matching was performed based on 14 clinical variables, yielding 27 matched pairs (n=54). The primary outcome was all-cause mortality; secondary outcomes included HF hospitalization and a composite of mortality and HF hospitalization. Survival was analyzed using Kaplan-Meier estimates and Cox regression, with treatment modeled at baseline and as a time-varying covariate.ResultsIn the PS-matched cohort (n=54; mean age 81.4±4.3 years, 64.8% male), SGLT2i treatment was associated with lower all-cause mortality (hazard ratio [HR], 0.33; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.13–0.85; p=0.021), the actuarial all-cause mortality rate was 9.3 versus 25.4 per 100 patient-years in untreated patients. Sensitivity analyses supported these findings (HR, 0.37; 95% CI, 0.15–0.93; p=0.034) and after covariate adjustment (HR, 0.35; 95% CI, 0.13–0.97; p=0.044). SGLT2i were linked to numerically fewer HF hospitalizations (HR, 0.39; 95% CI, 0.15–1.01; p=0.052) and a significantly lower composite of mortality and HF hospitalization (HR, 0.36; 95% CI, 0.15–0.88; p=0.025). SGLT2i were well tolerated, with a 7.4% drug discontinuation rate.ConclusionsIn this study, SGLT2i treatment was associated with improved survival in ATTR-CM patients. While observational design and sample size limit causal inference, these findings support further evaluation of SGLT2i as adjunctive therapy in this population.Keywords: Sodium-glucose transporter 2 inhibitors, Amyloidosis, Heart failure, Survival

INTRODUCTION

Transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy (ATTR-CM) is a progressive and fatal disease caused by infiltration of amyloid fibrils in cardiac tissue, due to misfolding of transthyretin (TTR).1) It is an increasingly recognized cause of heart failure (HF) but remains significantly underdiagnosed.2)

Until recently, ATTR-CM was considered a rare and untreatable disease associated with high morbidity and mortality.3,4) However, improvements in diagnostic methods coupled with emerging therapies have increased ATTR-CM awareness, leading to earlier diagnosis and better prognosis.5,6) Tafamidis, an oral TTR stabilizer, has been shown to reduce the combined primary endpoint of cardiovascular hospitalizations and mortality.7) More recently, acoramidis, an oral TTR stabilizer, and vutrisiran, a small interfering RNA agent have also demonstrated improved outcomes.8,9) Unfortunately, the high cost associated with these therapies results in a restricted use in many countries.10)

Conventional HF therapies with proven prognostic benefit in other etiologies may have a role in ATTR-CM; however, their efficacy remains uncertain, as patients with ATTR-CM were excluded from prior HF trials.11,12) Evidence from small observational studies has been inconsistent, some reporting that low doses of conventional therapies are generally well tolerated,13,14) while others suggest limited tolerability and even potential harm, with worse outcomes observed in certain patients.15,16)

Sodium glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors (SGLT2i) have been shown to be well tolerated and associated with favorable effects in patients with ATTR-CM, but evidence is sparse.17,18) A recent meta-analysis of observational studies suggested that SGLT2i use may be associated with lower risks of mortality, HF hospitalization, and other adverse cardiovascular events.19) Yet, robust evidence from randomized trials is lacking.

Given the uncertainty about SGLT2i benefit in this population, the objective of this study was to expand the existing evidence by evaluating the effects of SGLT2i treatment in patients with ATTR-CM using propensity score (PS)-matched observational data and clinically relevant outcomes.

METHODS

Study design and population

A single-center, retrospective cohort study was conducted, including patients with a confirmed diagnosis of ATTR-CM who were referred for cardiomyopathy consultation between January 2014 and December 2023. Diagnosis of ATTR-CM was established according to international recommendations, requiring the presence of HF symptoms in conjunction with: a Perugini grade 2 or 3 myocardial uptake on cardiac scintigraphy; characteristic findings of ATTR-CM on echocardiography or cardiac magnetic resonance imaging; and negative serum free light chains and negative serum and urine immunofixation. In cases where the diagnosis remained uncertain, tissue biopsy was performed to confirm ATTR-CM.20) In addition, TTR gene sequencing was performed in all patients to differentiate between hereditary and wild-type forms of the disease.

The study adheres to the ethical conduct policy outlined by the International Committee of Medical Journal Editors. The study was approved by the local ethics committee (137/2025-CAF) and conducted in accordance with Good Clinical Practice and the Declaration of Helsinki. As this was a retrospective study, written informed consent was waived for all patients in accordance with the Hospital da Senhora da Oliveira’s Institutional Review Board policies.

Data collection and outcomes

The time of ATTR-CM diagnosis was defined as time zero for follow-up. Data on demographics, clinical characteristics, echocardiographic parameters, laboratory findings, and treatment at time zero were retrospectively collected from the electronic health records. Echocardiographic parameters were obtained in accordance with international standards21); when specific measurements were not performed at baseline, images were retrospectively re-evaluated.

The primary outcome of the study was all-cause mortality. Secondary outcome measured were: 1) HF hospitalizations; and 2) composite outcome of all-cause mortality and HF hospitalizations. The mortality endpoint was measured as the time from the diagnosis until death for patients who died during follow-up. For patients who were still alive at the end of the study, their follow-up time was counted from baseline until the censoring date (December 2024). All patients had follow-up in a specialized consultations of cardiomyopathies and follow-up information was available for the entire cohort.

Patients were categorized into 2 exposure groups: non-treated, defined as patients who never received SGLT2i treatment during follow-up, and treated, defined as patients who initiated SGLT2i treatment at baseline or during follow-up and remained on treatment for at least six months prior to the occurrence of any event.

Statistical analysis

PS matching was employed to reduce confounding bias. PSs were derived from a logistic regression model including baseline covariates selected a priori based on clinical relevance and established prognostic associations in ATTR-CM, rather than on statistical criteria alone. These variables were chosen to capture key domains potentially influencing both treatment allocation and outcomes. These comprised demographics such as age and sex; comorbidities including diabetes mellitus, hypertension, atrial fibrillation, and renal function estimated by glomerular filtration rate (eGFR); markers of disease severity that included maximal wall thickness (MWT), left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), and N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP); and background heart failure and disease-modifying therapies, namely beta-blockers, angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors (ACEi) or angiotensin receptor blockers (ARB), mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (MRAs), loop diuretics, and tafamidis.

Patients lying outside the region of common support were excluded (trimming). Matching was performed in a 1:1 ratio using nearest-neighbor matching without replacement, with a caliper width of 0.20 of the standard deviation of the logit of the PS. Adequacy of matching was verified by inspection of standardized mean differences (SMDs), with <0.10 indicating acceptable balance.

Descriptive statistics were reported for the overall cohort and the matched sample, comparing treated and untreated groups. Categorical variables were expressed as absolute number and frequency (percentage). All continuous variables were tested for normality using the Shapiro–Wilk test and presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median (interquartile range [IQR] Q1–Q3), according to distribution. Student’s t-test was used for normally distributed variables, and the Mann–Whitney U test for non-normally distributed variables. The χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test was applied for categorical variables.

Survival analyses for the overall cohort were conducted using Kaplan-Meier (KM) curves, with comparisons between treated and non-treated groups made using the log-rank test. Annual mortality was additionally described using actuarial estimates. Cox proportional hazards regression models were used to estimate hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for treatment effect, with treatment included as a binary covariate. Survival analyses were repeated in the matched sample, including KM curves, log-rank testing, actuarial annual mortality estimates, and Cox regression.

To address potential immortal time bias, sensitivity analyses were performed using Cox regression models with treatment modeled as a time-varying covariate with a secondary model adjusted to relevant confounders: age, sex, diabetes mellitus, hypertension, atrial fibrillation, National Amyloidosis Centre (NAC) stage (substituting eGFR and NT-proBNP to avoid the influence of extreme values), MWT, LVEF, beta-blockers, ACEi/ARB, MRAs, and loop diuretics. Disease-modifying therapy was excluded to avoid overfitting.

Statistical significance was defined as p<0.05 for all analyses. Analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics 29.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) and R software (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

RESULTS

Patient population

From 111 patients diagnosed with ATTR-CM between January 2014 and December 2023, 46 (41.4%) received SGLT2i treatment. Baseline characteristics of treated and untreated patients are presented in Supplementary Table 1.

After PS matching, derived from a logistic regression model including 14 variables (Supplementary Figure 1), 27 treated patients were matched to 27 untreated patients, yielding a final cohort of 54 patients (Figure 1).

Baseline characteristics of the PS-matched cohort

Characteristics of the PS-matched cohort are summarized in Table 1. SMDs between groups were <0.10 for all variables except for MWT (0.105), which was near the threshold and therefore considered acceptable. Among the 54 patients, the mean age was 81.4 ± 4.3 years, and 64.8% were male. Comorbidities included diabetes in 27.8% of patients, hypertension in 87.0%, and atrial fibrillation in 42.6%. Most patients were in New York Heart Association functional class II (75.9%) and NAC stage 2 (44.4%). The median NT-proBNP level was 2,354 ng/L (1,144–5,275), and the mean eGFR was 57.4 ± 21.9 mL/min/1.73 m2. Echocardiographic findings showed a mean MWT of 18.3 ± 3.3 mm, mean LVEF of 54.9 ± 13.4%, mean global longitudinal left ventricle strain of −11.1 ± 3.3%, and mean E/e’ of 15.7 ± 5.0. Pharmacological treatment included beta-blockers in 50.0% of patients, ACEi/ARBs in 48.1%, MRAs in 33.3%, loop diuretics in 90.7%, and tafamidis in 18.5% (only hospital approved in 2023).

SGLT2i

Dapagliflozin was the most prescribed SGLT2i (n=15, 55.6%), followed by empagliflozin (n=12, 44.4%). All patients had at least 10 mg once daily prescribed dose, and none had reduced their dose.

The initiation of SGLT2i treatment was guided by diabetes mellitus management, but also by heart failure (HF) symptoms, initially in patients with reduced ejection fraction (EF) and later in those with preserved EF. Among patients who initiated SGLT2i treatment only after diagnosis (n=13, 48.1%), the median time to treatment initiation was 11 months (IQR: 6–35 months). During follow-up in the PS-matched cohort, 2 patients (7.4%) discontinued SGLT2i treatment, both due to recurrent urinary tract infections. No episodes of euglycemic ketoacidosis were observed. Due to the retrospective nature of the study, other outcomes such as volume depletion or drug associated hypotension could not be consistently captured.

Clinical outcomes

In the PS-matched cohort, during a median follow-up of 29 months (IQR, 14–45), death occurred in 24 patients, HF hospitalizations in 19 patients and the composite endpoint of death and HF hospitalization in 25 patients. The cause of death could not be obtained in 7 (29%) patients, thereby limiting the ability to evaluate cardiovascular mortality as an outcome. Univariate associations between baseline characteristics and clinical outcomes in the PS-matched cohort are summarized in Supplementary Table 2.

All-cause mortality

SGLT2i treatment was associated with lower all-cause mortality in both the unmatched (HR, 0.33; 95% CI, 0.16–0.66; p=0.002) and matched (HR, 0.33; 95% CI, 0.13–0.85; p=0.021) cohorts over a median follow-up of 25 months (IQR, 16–39) (Figure 2). In the matched cohort, the actuarial all-cause mortality rate was 9.3 deaths per 100 patient-years in treated patients versus 25.4 deaths per 100 patient-years in untreated patients.

HF hospitalization

SGLT2i treatment was associated with a lower risk of HF hospitalization in the unmatched cohort (HR, 0.46; 95% CI, 0.22–0.97; p=0.041) and a numerically lower rate of HF hospitalization in the matched cohort (HR, 0.39; 95% CI, 0.15–1.01; p=0.052) over a median follow-up of 24 months (IQR, 11–38) (Figure 2). In the matched cohort, the HF hospitalization rate was 9.0 events per 100 patient-years in treated patients versus 24.3 events per 100 patient-years in untreated patients.

This effect was not statistically significant in sensitivity analyses using time-dependent Cox regression, either in the unadjusted model (HR, 0.32; 95% CI, 0.07–1.52; p=0.152) or after adjustment for relevant covariates (HR, 0.27; 95% CI, 0.04–1.94; p=0.194) (Table 2).

Composite endpoint of all-cause mortality and HF hospitalization

SGLT2i treatment was associated with a lower rate of the composite endpoint of all-cause mortality and HF hospitalizations in both the unmatched (HR, 0.37; 95% CI, 0.19–0.71; p=0.0029) and matched (HR, 0.36; 95% CI, 0.15–0.88; p=0.025) cohorts over a median follow-up of 24 months (IQR, 11–38) (Figure 2). In the matched cohort, the combined endpoint rate was 11.5 events per 100 patient-years in treated patients versus 33 events per 100 patient-years in untreated patients.

This benefit did not reach statistical significance in time-varying treatment-exposure Cox analyses in the matched cohort, either in the unadjusted model (HR, 0.42; 95% CI, 0.14–1.23; p=0.113) or after adjustment for relevant covariates (HR, 0.41; 95% CI, 0.11–1.48; p=0.174) (Table 2).

DISCUSSION

In this single-center, retrospective, PS-matched cohort study, treatment with SGLT2i was associated with a lower risk of all-cause mortality in patients with ATTR-CM. The mortality reduction remained significant regardless of whether treatment exposure was modelled as a baseline characteristic or as a time-varying covariate that mitigates the risk of immortal time bias, indicating that the observed improvement association is consistent across different strategies of analyses. Obtaining similar results when accounting for both baseline treatment status and time of initiation supports the reliability of our findings and minimizes the likelihood of immortal time bias. Nonetheless, given the limited matched sample size and small number of events, the magnitude of the observed effect should be interpreted with caution. In this context, the primary contribution of the present study lies less in demonstrating clinical benefit per se and more in its robust methodology, combining PS matching with time-varying Cox models to address confounding and immortal time bias.

In sensitivity analyses using baseline treatment status, SGLT2i therapy was significantly associated with fewer hospitalizations and fewer composite events. These findings suggest that SGLT2i may not only be associated with improved survival but could also reduce morbidity. However, the reduction in HF hospitalizations and in the composite endpoint of death or HF hospitalization did not reach statistical significance in the matched time-varying analyses, though the direction of effect favored SGLT2i. The loss of statistical significance in time-dependent models suggests limited power to detect a morbidity benefit rather than definitive evidence of its absence. As a result, any potential effect of SGLT2i on HF hospitalizations and the composite endpoint should be interpreted cautiously, and the observed signal should be considered exploratory. Nevertheless, these observations remain directionally consistent with the demonstrated benefits of SGLT2i in large HF trials outside the amyloid population, in which these drugs reduced HF hospitalizations regardless of diabetes status.22,23) Likewise, a meta-analysis by Karakasis et al.,19) which pooled all currently available studies evaluating SGLT2i therapy in patients with ATTR-CM, demonstrated significant reductions in all-cause mortality (HR, 0.54; 95% CI, 0.44–0.66) and HF hospitalizations (HR, 0.63; 95% CI, 0.52–0.77), consistent with our findings.19)

Several mechanisms may plausibly explain the observed association between SGLT2i use and improved outcomes in ATTR-CM. Their mild and sustained natriuretic effects may improve systemic congestion with limited neurohormonal activation, while favorable effects on myocardial energetics, ventricular stiffness, and cardiorenal interactions may be particularly relevant in a restrictive, amyloid-infiltrated myocardium. Together, these effects provide a biologically plausible framework for the observed clinical benefit.24,25) Future larger studies should also examine outcomes stratified by transthyretin amyloidosis subtype, background disease-modifying therapy, baseline HF severity and renal function to better identify patients most likely to benefit.

SGLT2i were well tolerated in our cohort, with low discontinuation rates, contrasting with other conventional HF therapies in ATTR-CM. Beta-blockers are often poorly tolerated due to hypotension and conduction abnormalities, while ACEi/ARB and MRAs are similarly limited by hypotension or renal dysfunction.9,26)

As such, SGLT2i stands out as a widely available, affordable, and easy to administer therapeutic option, with a tolerability profile well suited for elderly and fragile patients.

This study should be interpreted in light of several limitations inherent to its retrospective and single-center design. The single-center setting limits generalizability, as referral patterns, diagnostic strategies, and prescribing practices may not reflect those of other institutions. Despite the use of PS matching, the potential for residual confounding and selection bias remains, as unmeasured factors may still have influenced treatment allocation or outcomes.

Treatment decisions were made on a case-by-case basis, introducing the possibility of indication bias if clinicians preferentially prescribed SGLT2i to patients perceived as more likely to tolerate or benefit from therapy. As a result, SGLT2i initiation may reflect clinician judgment rather than random treatment exposure, thereby limiting causal inference. The timing of treatment initiation was variable, with nearly half of patients starting SGLT2i after ATTR-CM diagnosis. This delayed initiation raises the possibility that treated patients represented a clinically more stable subgroup with better short-term prognosis or treatment tolerance. This variability introduced the potential for immortal time bias, as patients must survive long enough to receive therapy. To address this, time-dependent analyses treating therapy as a time-dependent covariate were conducted, which yielded results consistent with baseline-treatment models.

The sample size, further reduced after matching, limited statistical power, especially for secondary endpoints, and resulted in wide CIs. The small number of events raises concern for effect-size overestimation and reduced model stability, such that the magnitude of the observed associations should be interpreted with caution. This may explain why some associations, although directionally consistent, did not consistently reach statistical significance. The relatively small number of events, as often observed in PS-matched observational studies, raises the concern that the observed effect sizes may overestimate the true treatment benefit, since estimates such as HRs and odds ratios become less stable and more susceptible to random variation when event counts are low. Accordingly, the adjusted Cox regression models, which included multiple covariates relative to the number of observed events (low event-per-variable ratio), were intended as supportive analyses rather than definitive estimates of effect size. Adverse events were incompletely captured, as only discontinuations were systematically recorded, but less severe side effects may therefore have been underreported.

The cause of death could not be determined in all cases, preventing us from distinguishing cardiovascular from non-cardiovascular mortality. This restricts the ability to directly attribute the survival benefit to cardiac mechanisms, although the trend towards reduction in HF hospitalizations supports a cardiovascular effect. Owing to the retrospective design, substantial missing data, heterogeneous follow-up, and limited sample size, longitudinal clinical, echocardiographic, and biomarker analyses were not feasible.

Finally, tafamidis was only used since 2023, the date when it became commercially available in the country. Therefore, prospective randomized trials are needed to confirm the benefit of SGLT2i in a contemporaneous cohort of ATTR-CM patients, on top of disease-modifying therapies.

In a PS-matched cohort of patients with ATTR-CM, SGLT2i therapy was associated with improved survival and a potential reduction in HF hospitalization, with excellent tolerability. These findings were consistent with whether treatment exposure was analyzed as a baseline characteristic or as a time-varying covariate, underscoring the methodological robustness of the results within the constraints of a retrospective design. Although causal inference cannot be made and the limited sample size and event count warrant cautious interpretation of the effect magnitude, the findings suggest a potential link between SGLT2i therapy and favorable outcomes in ATTR-CM. Randomized trials are needed to clarify the role of SGLT2i in the management of ATTR-CM and to clarify whether the associations observed here translate into a true survival benefit in this unique patient group.

Variables included: age, sex, maximal wall thickness, LVEF, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, atrial fibrillation, eGFR, NT-proBNP, ACEi/ARB use, beta-blocker use, MRA use, loop diuretic use, and disease-modifying therapy (tafamidis). Group differences were evaluated using SMDs, with values <0.1 considered indicative of adequate balance between sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitor-treated and non-treated patients.

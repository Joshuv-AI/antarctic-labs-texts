Integrated multi-omics analysis combined with clinical validation reveals that HLA-DRB5 and ODAPH are causal risk genes for keratoconus
Authors: Fanru Zhao, Boyue Zhao, Ruiqi Cao, Wenxin Zhao, Ming Zhang, Yishan Sun, Xinyi Zhao, Yufan Li, Yijie Hong, YuFei Dang, Xin Wang, Li Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study aimed to elucidate the genetic determinants of keratoconus (KC) using a multi-omics approach, with a focus on identifying key causal genes to address gaps in understanding the disease’s pathological mechanisms. An integrated multi-omics study combining bioinformatics analysis of transcriptome data with clinical validation. This approach does not include a traditional control group but utilizes public databases and patient samples to assess genetic associations. Five KC patients from the First Affiliated Hospital of Xi’an Jiaotong University provided corneal tissues and blood samples. The control group consisted of five age-matched donors who provided excess corneal tissue from transplantation surgeries. The study involved analyzing two KC transcriptome datasets from GEO to identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs). This was followed by Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses, summary-data Mendelian randomization (SMR), two-sample Mendelian randomization (TSMR), Bayesian colocalization, and clinical validation through immunohistochemical staining and PCR assays. Primary measures included gene expression levels, causal association statistics (e.g., posterior probability PP4, SMR p-value, OR), and RNA and protein expression of key genes (HLA-DRB5, ODAPH, MMP-9) assessed through molecular assays. The analysis identified 2,884 DEGs enriched in cell adhesion, immune response, and the TNF and IL-17 signaling pathways. Twenty-four genes demonstrated strong causal associations with KC (PP4 > 0.8), with HLA-DRB5 (PP4 = 0.844, SMR p = 0.001, OR = 1.768) and ODAPH (PP4 = 1.0, SMR p = 0.013, OR = 202.851) showing the highest confidence. Clinical validation confirmed significantly elevated expression of HLA-DRB5, ODAPH, and MMP-9 in the KC cornea and whole blood. HLA-DRB5 and ODAPH were identified as key causal risk genes for KC, bridging critical gaps in genetic knowledge and providing a foundation for early diagnostic biomarkers and etiology-targeted therapies, such as Meplazumab, an HLA-DRB5 inhibitor.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-41037-w.

Introduction

KC is a disease of the corneal stromal layer characterized by progressive thinning and steepening of the cornea, which protrudes forward in a conical shape. It is the most common cause of corneal astigmatism1. The global incidence of KC is approximately 1.38% and is increasing, primarily affecting adolescents aged 10 to 25 years, with a familial genetic predisposition2. Because the disease has an insidious onset and is often overlooked, it can lead to complications such as corneal edema, scarring, and, in severe cases, corneal perforation, resulting in a significant disease burden. Current research identifies risk factors for KC2–4, including contact lens wear, chronic eye rubbing, allergic conjunctivitis, and asthma. Additionally, inflammation and dysregulation of immune system homeostasis may contribute to the onset of KC5–7. Despite extensive research, the pathophysiological mechanisms involve multiple complex factors, including genetic and environmental influences, and the specific pathogenesis remains unclear.

Odontogenesis-associated phosphoprotein (ODAPH) is located on human chromosome 4q21.1, is an extracellular matrix acidic phosphoprotein involved in maintaining tissue adhesion and mineralization8. Its ectopic overexpression can lead to abnormal enamel mineralization9. Abnormal expression of ODAPH in corneal tissue may disrupt pathway activity, reduce stable cross-linking of collagen fibers, and directly compromise the structural stability of the cornea. HLA-DRB5, an MHC class II molecule primarily expressed on the surface of antigen-presenting cells, participates in antigen presentation and immune regulation. It is highly polymorphic, and functional abnormalities in HLA-DRB5 may trigger aberrant inflammatory or immune responses10,11.

Current clinical-stage treatments for KC primarily focus on symptomatic management, including ultraviolet-A corneal cross-linking, corneal transplantation, and femtosecond laser-assisted corneal topography remodeling12–14. However, these approaches lack specific therapies targeting the underlying etiology. Therefore, there is an urgent need to identify additional potential risk genes associated with the disease. This will provide a foundation for developing early detection systems, optimizing auxiliary diagnostic tools, and refining genetic risk score models, ultimately enabling personalized drug-targeted therapies.

SMR is a statistical method that integrates genomic data, such as GWAS, with expression quantitative trait locus (eQTL) data. It represents a methodological advancement over traditional MR, offering advantages including broader and more efficient data utilization, simplified analysis and computation, and more precise bias control. These features make SMR particularly suitable for large-scale screening and causal inference. This study aims to identify differentially expressed genes in KC using bioinformatic approaches and employs both SMR and TSMR analyses to evaluate the causal relationships between these genes and KC. The goal is to investigate the disease’s pathogenic factors and signaling pathways and to explore potential drug intervention targets. Furthermore, experimental validation will be conducted to verify the differential expression of target genes in both blood and corneal tissues from KC patients.

Methods

All statistical data were obtained from publicly accessible databases, where the original data had received ethical approval. The use of these data in the present study has also been approved by an ethical review board. All biological tissue samples were stored in the Biobank and Information Resource Center of the First Affiliated Hospital of Xi’an Jiaotong University, and the study protocol was reviewed and approved by the hospital’s Medical Ethics Committee (Project Title: Construction of a Prediction Model for Corneal Graft Rejection Based on Aqueous Humor and Blood Information; Project Number: FHY44$LLY2023001; Ethics Approval Number: XJTU1AF2023LSK-414). All participants, or their legal guardians, voluntarily participated and provided written informed consent for sample collection. All methods were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations.

Clinical sample collection

The KC group consisted of five patients aged 20 to 30 years who underwent deep anterior lamellar keratoplasty at the First Affiliated Hospital of Xi’an Jiaotong University between January 2023 and January 2025. Whole blood samples were collected preoperatively, and diseased tissue was obtained intraoperatively from all subjects. The control group, age-matched to the KC group, comprised five excess donor corneas intended for corneal transplantation surgery. Tissue samples were used for RNA extraction and RT-qPCR analysis to quantify the expression of the key extracellular matrix degradation molecule MMP-9 and associated risk genes.

Inclusion criteria: The diagnostic criteria for KC included decreased visual acuity accompanied by conical protrusion and thinning of the cornea. Characteristic signs observed under slit-lamp examination comprised Fleischer’s ring (a brownish-yellow ring visible under cobalt blue light), Vogt’s striae (fine white vertical lines in the stroma), and acute corneal hydrops. Key corneal topography metrics consisted of a central corneal curvature ≥ 47.00 D, an inferior-superior curvature value (I-S value) > 1.26 D, and an abnormally increased posterior corneal elevation of 22 μm14. Patients were classified according to the Amsler–Krumeich classification of KC, which considers the degree of myopia and astigmatism, mean keratometry, and minimal central corneal thickness15.

Exclusion criteria included: ① Presence of systemic metabolic diseases or ocular diseases; ② Severe cardiac, hepatic, or renal diseases, malignant tumors, or other serious conditions; ③ Previous history of ocular surgery; ④ Missing data or samples; ⑤ Other conditions deemed unsuitable for inclusion by the investigators.

Source of data

The differential gene datasets GSE 151,631 and GSE 77,938 analysed during the current study are available in the NCBI GEO repository (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) transcriptomic expression profiles of human corneal tissue; (2) participants comprising KC patients and control subjects; (3) corneal samples including both the epithelium and stroma; and (4) a sample size of at least 20. Ultimately, two datasets were selected. The first dataset, GSE151631, included 19 KC patients and 7 control subjects of African American, Middle Eastern, and European American descent. RNA sequencing was performed using the TruSeq Stranded RNA Library Prep Kit (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA) on the Illumina HiSeq 2500 platform. The second dataset comprised 25 KC patients and 25 control subjects, all of European origin, with RNA sequencing conducted using the TruSeq Stranded Total RNA LT with Ribo-Zero Human/Mouse/Rat Kit (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA) on the Illumina HiSeq 1500 platform.

The KC GWAS dataset GCST90435979 analysed during the current study is available in the IEU OpenGWAS repository (https://gwas.mrcieu.ac.uk/). This database was collaboratively developed by the National Human Genome Research Institute (NHGRI) and the European Bioinformatics Institute (EBI).

Instrumental variables (IVs) selection

The differential gene eQTL data analysed during the current study are available in the GTEx v8 and eQTLGen repositories, (https://ftp.ebi.ac.uk/pub/databases/spot/eQTL/imported/GTEx_V8 and https://www.eqtlgen.org). To investigate the causal relationship between differentially expressed genes and KC, cis-eQTLs were identified. These cis-eQTLs were defined as single nucleotide polymorphisms located within 500 kb upstream or downstream of the gene and were selected based on the following screening criteria: (1) SNPs must exhibit a strong association with each differentially expressed gene (eQTL p < 5 × 10⁻⁸ in SMR; eQTL p < 1 × 10⁻⁶ in TSMR); (2) removal of SNPs with significant linkage disequilibrium (LD pruning, r2 < 0.1); and (3) exclusion of SNPs exhibiting horizontal pleiotropy. Genetic variation data were acquired from multi-tissue and blood samples related to the trait from the databases, with subsequent analyses focusing primarily on positive SMR results. The significance of SMR results for candidate core causal genes was assessed by evaluating whether they remained significant (p < 0.05) when adjusting the SNP screening thresholds for association with differentially expressed genes. MR-PRESSO was employed to exclude SNPs potentially exhibiting horizontal pleiotropy and to remove outlier SNPs. Additionally, MR-Egger regression intercept tests were conducted to detect directional horizontal pleiotropy, where an intercept significantly different from zero (p < 0.05) suggests potential bias.

Candidate differentially expressed genes and functional enrichment analysis

Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) exhibiting significant expression changes between disease and control groups were identified from two independent GSE datasets in GEO using the online interactive tool GEO2R. The selection criteria were set as adjusted p-value (padj) < 0.01 and |log2 fold change|> 1.5. The union of DEGs from both datasets was then determined. The candidate DEGs were further analyzed using the Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) databases16–18. GO analysis examined the biological functions of DEGs across cellular components (CC), biological processes (BP), and molecular functions (MF), while KEGG analysis identified potential signaling pathways.

SMR analysis and TSMR analysis

This study utilized the TwoSampleMR software package for Mendelian randomization analysis, employing both SMR and TSMR methods to investigate the causal relationship between differentially expressed genes and KC. SMR results served as the primary evidence for causal inference, while TSMR results provided supporting evidence. The HEIDI test was used to assess heterogeneity in the disease association of SNPs within linkage disequilibrium (LD) regions, determining whether the association signal was driven by a single eQTL. The statistical significance thresholds were set as SMR p < 0.05 and HEIDI p > 0.05, with false discovery rate (FDR) correction applied to SMR results across multiple genes to control for false positives due to multiple testing; an FDR q-value < 0.05 was considered significant. Genes meeting these criteria were considered risk differentially expressed genes potentially causally related to KC pathogenesis. To ensure the robustness of the findings, sensitivity analyses for SMR were conducted by adjusting the LD threshold beyond the initial value (r2 < 0.1) to observe whether the significance of candidate core genes changed, thereby excluding false-positive results attributable to LD.

TSMR was employed to additionally validate the causal relationship between differentially expressed genes and the outcome by utilizing reverse Mendelian randomization (MR) to verify the consistency of the causal direction. Specifically, significant single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) from the KC genome-wide association study (GWAS) served as instrumental variables, while risk-associated differentially expressed genes from GSE151631 and GSE77938 were used as outcome data to test whether the disease influences gene expression. A p-value ≥ 0.05 was considered non-significant, supporting the original causal direction.

Colocalization analysis

This study employed colocalization analysis to determine whether different phenotypes—specifically, KC GWAS data and eQTL data from differentially expressed genes identified in this study—are driven by the same genetic variant (SNP), thereby pinpointing molecular phenotypes genuinely associated with the disease. A Bayesian colocalization analysis was conducted on significant SMR results, using a ± 1 Mb region centered on the differentially expressed gene as the colocalization window. A posterior probability for hypothesis 4 (PP4) greater than 0.8 was considered indicative that changes in the gene’s expression likely have a causal influence on KC disease risk through that SNP. To further evaluate the robustness of the core colocalization region, sensitivity analyses were performed by narrowing (e.g., ± 500 kb) or widening the genomic window around the original colocalization boundary, followed by recalculation of the Bayesian posterior probability (PP4).

Drug target screening

The drug target information and protein regulatory interactions analysed during the current study are available in the STRING and DrugBank repositories (https://string-db.org/ and https://go.drugbank.com/). The ideal screening criteria for drugs targeting core risk genes are as follows: (1) the drug must directly interact with the target (not through indirect regulation); (2) the drug must have established safety based on research evidence (e.g., FDA approval for marketing); and (3) the drug must be capable of penetrating the corneal barrier (e.g., able to reach corneal tissue via topical administration, with a molecular weight under 500 Da and moderate lipophilicity).

Statistical methods

Differential gene analysis, volcano plot generation, functional enrichment analysis, SMR, TSMR, and Bayesian colocalization in this study were performed using the DESeq2 package within the online GEO2R platform (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/geo2r/) and R software (version 4.5.1).

Results

In GSE151631, 18,049 genes with expression differences were screened, among which 608 genes were significantly downregulated in KC, and 1,353 genes were significantly upregulated in KC (e.g., MAFF, CSRNP1, FOSL1, MAPK8IP1, JMJD6, PIM1, RRAD) and identified as DEGs. SMR: identifying KC risk genes and discovering causal associations of key genes with KC

To further investigate the impact of differentially expressed genes on KC pathogenesis risk, an SMR analysis was performed using gene GWAS eQTLs as the exposure and KC GWAS (GCST9035979) as the outcome; 3,882 SNPs meeting the eQTL p < 5e-8 threshold were identified as instrumental variables, revealing 35 genes as risk factors and 34 genes as protective factors (Fig. 2A) (Supplementary Tables 2 and 3). The incorporated risk genes included HLA-DRB5 (p SMR = 0.001, OR 1.768, 95% CI 1.245–2.509, log2OR 0.822, 95% CI 0.317–1.327) and ODAPH (p SMR = 0.013, OR 202.851, 95% CI 3.130–13,146.857, log2OR 7.664, 95% CI 1.646–13.682). Sensitivity analysis: validating the robustness of the results

To verify the stability of the TSMR results, several methods were employed, including the pleiotropy Egger intercept test for horizontal pleiotropy, the Q-test, and the Inverse Variance Weighted (IVW) method. These analyses revealed no significant horizontal pleiotropy or heterogeneity. For HLA-DRB5, analysis using the Weighted Median method (n SNPs = 3) indicated a significant association between its genetic variations and KC risk. For ODAPH, the Wald ratio method (n SNP = 1) suggested a significant association. Similarly, LCN2 was analyzed using the Wald ratio method (n SNP = 1), which indicated a significant association between its genetic variations and KC risk. MS4A7 was analyzed using the IVW method (n SNPs = 2). The application of the Steiger filtering method demonstrated that the SNPs selected as genetic instruments exhibited a stronger correlation with the exposure factor than with potential confounders, consistent with the fundamental assumptions of Mendelian Randomization (MR) (Supplementary Table 4).

Drug target intervention for risk genes

This study evaluated whether proteins encoded by risk genes identified through multi-integrated omics could serve as drug targets or be utilized for pharmacotherapy. Potential intervention targets were screened from causal genes validated by SMR and colocalization analyses using the DrugBank database. The protein encoded by HLA-DRB5 represents a high-priority target for drug development. The inhibitor Apolizumab monoclonal antibody, a humanized IgG1 monoclonal antibody with a molecular weight of approximately 150 kDa, has had its clinical development suspended due to limited efficacy and safety concerns and is now solely used as an investigational agent. Meplazumab monoclonal antibody, a humanized IgG2 monoclonal antibody, blocks the interaction between HLA-DRB5 and the SARS-CoV-2 Spike protein by targeting CD147 and has demonstrated substantial clinical potential in conditions such as pulmonary fibrosis19.

Discussion

This study integrated transcriptomic data from public databases with GWAS data, utilizing SMR, TSMR, and colocalization analyses to identify risk genes strongly causally associated with KC.

KC has been confirmed to be associated with genes such as MMP-9 and TGFB120–22. Its primary pathophysiological manifestations include stromal collagen degradation, cell apoptosis23, inflammatory responses, immune responses1, and extracellular matrix remodeling. ITGB6 (Integrin Beta 6) encodes a beta subunit of the integrin family, while LAMC2 (Laminin Gamma 2 Chain) is a key gene in the laminin family. The γ2 chain encoded by LAMC2 exclusively combines with α3 and β3 chains to form laminin-332. Functional abnormalities in ITGB6 and LAMC2 may impair epithelial cell–extracellular matrix adhesion, thereby affecting the corneal epithelial layer’s supportive function for the stroma24,25 and regulating downstream TGF-β1 expression. As a key regulator of fibrosis, TGF-β1 levels in the serum of KC patients are significantly elevated compared to healthy individuals and increase with disease severity. This serum level association demonstrates that highly expressed TGF-β1 contributes to late-stage scar formation in KC by inducing fibrosis26 and severely disrupts mitochondrial function in KC corneal cells, leading to abnormal energy metabolism and progressively worsening damage27. Furthermore, serum tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) expression is elevated in KC patients28,29, and low-level enrichment of the TNF-α/NF-κB pathway can activate MMPs, leading to corneal stromal degradation30. Substantial evidence indicates that MMPs are upregulated in the corneal tissue and tears of KC patients31, particularly MMP-932. The quantities of type I and type IV collagen fibers in the corneal lesion areas are significantly reduced, key enzyme activities (e.g., lysyl oxidase) are decreased33, and the collagen degradation rate exceeds its synthesis rate, resulting in insufficient cross-linking accompanied by disorganized collagen arrangement34,35.

This study, integrating SMR, TSMR, colocalization analyses, and protein–protein interaction (PPI) networks from the STRING database, revealed that HLA-DRB5 and ODAPH have potential as novel marker genes for KC. Previous research has identified ODAPH as a crucial node in enamel development, interacting with multiple enamel-related proteins (AMELX, ENAM, AMBN) to regulate enamel formation and mineralization9,36. It has also demonstrated that ODAPH can activate the LAMC2/ITGB6/TGFB1 pathway, thereby participating in odontoblast adhesion37. We cautiously speculate that during the pathogenesis of KC, ODAPH may impair cell adhesion by regulating the TGF-β1 pathway, reduce collagen cross-linking, participate in stromal remodeling, and consequently compromise corneal structural integrity. First, dental enamel (originating from the oral ectoderm)38–40 and corneal stromal cells (derived from cranial neural crest cells)41–43 share an ectodermal origin44–46. Secondly, the critical regulatory role of ODAPH in the formation of high-hardness tooth enamel suggests its significant influence on the mechanical homeostasis of the extracellular matrix. In the corneal stroma, which similarly relies on a highly ordered collagen arrangement to maintain mechanical stability, ODAPH may perform an analogous function in regulating stromal mechanics. Currently, this mechanistic perspective requires further validation through functional experiments. As an MHC class II molecule, HLA-DRB5 is highly expressed in immune-related tissues such as lymph nodes, spleen, and bone marrow, and has been implicated in ocular conditions including dry eye disease, primary open-angle glaucoma, and uveitis47–49. The cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction pathway (Fig. 1E) serves as a crucial intermediary for signal transmission between innate and adaptive immunity, regulating immune cell activation and differentiation. Studies have demonstrated that activation of HLA-DR class II molecules on fibroblasts can directly induce the expression and secretion of the multifunctional chemokine RANTES via the JNK signaling pathway, which in turn modulates antigen presentation capacity, forming a bidirectional feedback loop that collectively regulates immune response functions50. HLA-DRB5 activates specific immune responses through antigen presentation, provides upstream signals for the inflammatory amplification of the TNF-α pathway, and synergistically enhances TNF pathway activation in conjunction with other genes51. In the cornea, HLA-DRB5 likely functions primarily functions to delay corneal damage by suppressing aberrant immune responses; its dysfunction can disrupt the corneal immune privilege microenvironment, thereby promoting corneal stromal cell apoptosis and activating MMPs through the modulation of inflammatory pathways such as TNF and cytokine expression, ultimately exacerbating the clinical and pathological manifestations of corneal thinning and protrusion.

Furthermore, genes validated through Mendelian analysis were evaluated to determine whether their encoded proteins could serve as drug targets or be utilized in pharmacotherapy. A cross-tissue transcriptome-wide association study confirmed HLA-DRB5 as a susceptibility gene for diabetic kidney disease. An increased proportion of CD8 + T cells was observed in diabetic model kidneys, indicating that HLA-DRB5 activates T-cell immune responses via antigen presentation52. Based on a DrugBank database screening, we identified Meplazumab as an inhibitor targeting HLA-DRB5. This humanized IgG2 monoclonal antibody (150 kDa) is highly hydrophilic, and studies have demonstrated that Meplazumab can alleviate SARS-CoV-2-associated pulmonary fibrosis by inhibiting the TGF-β pathway53,54, suggesting its potential effect in corneal tissue via the same mechanism. Further studies are needed to elucidate the therapeutic potential of Meplazumab in the cornea. However, the topical ocular delivery of this drug is significantly impeded by the intrinsic physiological barriers of the cornea55, which severely restrict the penetration of macromolecules. This results in minimal bioavailability with conventional eye drop formulations, posing a major translational challenge. Therapeutic strategies aimed at indirectly downregulating HLA-DRB5 expression by inhibiting the JAK-STAT pathway or co-stimulatory molecules (e.g., CD80/CD86) are also worth considering56–58. Although ODAPH lacks direct targeted drugs, well-established intervention strategies already exist for the extracellular matrix homeostasis pathways in which it participates.

In summary, this study is the first to identify HLA-DRB5 and ODAPH as core risk genes for KC, highlighting Meplazumab as a potential intervention targeting HLA-DRB5 and providing supporting evidence for the clinical diagnosis and treatment of KC. This work establishes a coherent framework by integrating complementary analytical methods and yielding consistent causal conclusions. However, the study has several limitations. First, it is important to note that the publicly available genetic data used in this study are primarily derived from European populations, while subsequent validation was performed using a limited set of Chinese clinical samples. This highlights a limitation in the population representativeness of currently available genetic data. Nonetheless, we successfully validated the differential expression of HLA-DRB5 and ODAPH in KC tissues using independent Chinese samples. This consistent finding across diverse populations suggests that these two genes are robust risk factors across different ancestries. Therefore, future large-scale studies involving diverse populations are needed to systematically evaluate the heterogeneity in allele frequencies and effect sizes, thereby confirming their generalizability. Secondly, the MR analysis primarily relied on eQTL data from non-corneal tissues, such as peripheral blood. Although some eQTLs exhibit cross-tissue sharing59–61, inferences remain limited due to tissue specificity. To address this limitation, we hypothesized that HLA-DRB5 may play a similar regulatory role in the cornea, based on its known functions in systemic immunity62–64 and established ocular-systemic immune connections65–69. Furthermore, experimental validation using independent corneal tissues provided direct target-tissue evidence supporting the genetic association. Therefore, the future development of a cornea-specific eQTL atlas will help clarify the regulatory mechanisms of relevant genes in corneal tissue. The investigation of drug intervention targets is based solely on database predictions, and there is currently no direct evidence supporting the local application of Meplazumab in the cornea. Future research should include experiments using primary corneal cells and in vivo studies to validate the regulatory functions of these risk genes in corneal tissue and to assess the effects of drug interventions. Moreover, during the TSMR analysis of risk genes identified from SMR analysis, some genes were instrumented by single SNPs, which may introduce weak instrument bias. Random errors, such as minor fluctuations in eQTL associations, could result in excessively wide 95% confidence intervals for the odds ratios, underscoring the need to interpret these results in conjunction with SMR analysis, colocalization analysis, or functional experimental validation. Additionally, the clinical validation sample size was limited (n = 5); therefore, expanding the sample size and including more diverse populations is necessary to further confirm the expression differences of the core genes.

Conclusion

Through integrated multi-omics analysis and clinical validation, this study identified and confirmed the causal relationship between the expression of HLA-DRB5 and ODAPH and KC, providing crucial genetic and clinical evidence for the development of early warning biomarkers and etiological therapeutic targets, such as the Meplazumab monoclonal antibody, for KC.

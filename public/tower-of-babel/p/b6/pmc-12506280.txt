Clinical and epidemiological characteristics of human metapneumovirus in children in Hangzhou, China
Authors: Ya-jun Guo, Qin-rui Lai, Jie Song, Qun Wang, Bo-fei Hu, Ya-ling Li, Ying-shuo Wang, Wei Li, Xiang-zheng Ma
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study aims to describe the epidemiology and genotypic diversity of human metapneumovirus (HMPV) in hospitalized children with pneumonia in Hangzhou, Zhejiang, China. From February 2022 to January 2023, nasopharyngeal swabs from hospitalized children with acute respiratory tract infections (ARTIs) in the Children’s Hospital, Zhejiang University School of Medicine (Zhejiang, Hangzhou) were collected and tested for HMPV. Clinical characteristics and genotypes were analyzed on HMPV-positive samples. 3.43% (357/12993) of pediatric patients were HMPV-positive, and 287 of them were hospitalized with HMPV-induced pneumonia. 185 were classified as non-severe pneumonia group, and 102 as severe pneumonia group. Neutrophil to lymphocyte ratio (NLR), C-reactive protein to prealbumin ratio (CPAR) and C-reactive protein (CRP) were substantially higher in severe groups compared to non-severe groups across all patient categories (P < 0.05). There were 14 cases of B1 lineage infection, 7 cases of B2 lineage infection and 105 cases of A2.2.2 lineage infection in hospitalized patients. Under the prevention and control of the COVID-19 pandemic, the HMPV infection rate in pediatric patients with ARTIs has decreased, and the main lineage of HMPV infection in Hangzhou was A2.2.2. NLR and CPAR have higher predictive potential in severe patients.Keywords: Human metapneumovirus, Children, Epidemiological characteristics, F gene

Introduction

Pneumonia is a leading cause of mortality in children under the age of five, with viral infections being a primary driver of progression to severe pneumonia [1]. The COVID-19 pandemic has reshaped the epidemiological landscape of respiratory viral infections, particularly in terms of both prevalence and seasonality [2–4]. Apart from human respiratory syncytial virus (HRSV), human metapneumovirus (HMPV) is considered one of the most important causes of viral bronchiolitis and pneumonia in young children, accounting for 7–19% of acute respiratory cases [1, 5]. HMPV mainly infects newborns, children, elderly individuals, and individuals with compromised immunity around the world [6]. In high-risk groups, the prevalence of the virus is correlated with increased morbidity and mortality, which is further associated with low immunity and other underlying diseases [7]. The virus was first reported in 2001 in Dutch children with acute lower respiratory infection (ALRI), and serological studies have shown that almost every child is exposed to HMPV before the age of five [8]. Respiratory infections caused by HMPV are usually seasonal, and bronchiolitis and repeated wheezing are the main causes of hospitalization in children under two years old [9]. Globally, HMPV constitutes 6% of all inpatient cases and 40% of all outpatient cases among children presenting with acute respiratory diseases [10]. In some patient groups, especially those with impaired immune function or preexisting heart or respiratory conditions, HMPV can lead to severe diseases requiring hospitalization. These patients are more likely to develop acute respiratory failure requiring high-flow oxygen support, mechanical ventilation, or even intensive care unit (ICU) admission for close monitoring [11, 12].

HMPV, a non-segmental negative-sense RNA virus belonging to the family Pneumoviridae according to its new classification, can form pleomorphic or filamentous virions with an enveloped structure [13–15]. The full-length genome of the virus is approximately 13.3 kb, harboring eight genes: nucleoprotein (N), phosphoprotein (P), ribonucleic acid polymeric protein (L), and matrix protein (M), including the M2-1 and M2-2 proteins, and three surface transmembrane proteins, including attachment protein (G), small molecule hydrophobic protein (SH) and fusion protein (F) [16]. The F gene and G gene are widely used in genetic variation studies [17]. Although the G gene shows a greater diversity of nucleotides and amino acids [18–21], only the F protein has been confirmed to be immunogenic and protective [22, 23]. According to differences in nucleotide sequence, reaction patterns with monoclonal antibodies, and in vitro neutralization tests with subgroup-specific antisera, HMPV can be divided into two subtypes: A and B [24]. Subtypes A2 and B2 are further divided into subclusters, namely, A2a, A2b [22], A2b1, and A2b2 [25] and B2a and B2b [26]. A recent study proposed an HMPV classification reflecting the genetic clustering of F gene sequences [27], a critical component of the virus that facilitates transmembrane fusion with host cells and is associated with virus penetration, cell-to-cell transmission, and triggering of protective immunity [28]. Based on these F gene sequences, HMPV is divided into six unique genetic lineages: A1, A2.1, A2.2.1, A2.2.2, B1, and B2.

During inflammation, alterations in platelet volume are frequently observed [29, 30]. Additionally, the role of neutrophils as mediators and active substances in inflammation is also well known. A decline in their numbers is often associated with an increased risk of infection and infection-related deaths [31]. The number of multinucleated leukocytes is closely related to the process of infection. Recent studies have shown that the state of systemic inflammation and infection affects not only the number of white blood cells and platelets but also the ratio between them, such as the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR) and platelet-to-neutrophil ratio (PNR) [32–35]. C-reactive protein (CRP) is an acute-phase protein synthesized by the liver in response to cytokines that increases significantly within hours after infection or inflammation. Its relatively short half-life of approximately 19 h makes it a useful monitor for infections and inflammatory diseases, and elevated CRP levels are associated with sepsis and critical illness mortality [31]. In addition, the laboratory detection of CRP is easier to obtain and less expensive than cytokine detection [36–38]. Prealbumin, also known as transthyroxine, is mainly produced by the liver and is consumed during inflammation [39, 40]. The level of prealbumin is significantly decreased under acute cytokine stimulation [41].

In this study, we conducted a retrospective analysis of hematological inflammatory parameters (such as neutrophil, lymphocyte, and platelet counts), CRP levels, and blood cell count indices, especially the NLR, CPAR, PNR and PLR, in hospitalized children with HMPV infection at the Children’s Hospital, Zhejiang University School of Medicine (Hangzhou, Zhejiang), from February 2022 to January 2023. The findings offer a significant reference for early diagnosis and severity evaluation of this disease. We aimed to review the evidence for the effectiveness of systemic inflammatory markers in risk-stratifying patients and predicting outcomes related to HMPV. Analyzing the molecular epidemiological characteristics, clinical characteristics, and F protein diversity of HPMV provides valuable insights into the temporal prevalence and seasonality of viral pathogens. Virus sequence-based network inference helps to understand infection spread in a subset of the population, enabling the implementation of targeted public health measures by identifying high-risk transmitters to reduce infection prevalence.

Materials and methods

Patients and samples

Nasopharyngeal swabs were collected from inpatients diagnosed with ARTIs at the Children’s Hospital, Zhejiang University School of Medicine (Zhejiang, Hangzhou) from February 2022 to January 2023. The inclusion criteria were fever (body temperature ≥ 37.5 °C) accompanied by one or more symptoms of ARTIs, including cough, runny nose, sputum, and sore throat. The patients were grouped as severe or non-severe based on clinical symptoms [42].

Laboratory assays and data collection

Patient demographics, including age, sex, and laboratory data, were reviewed and collected from the hospital’s electronic information system. The laboratory data of the patients with HMPV infection after admission to the hospital were collected. Cell count (including total WBC count and classified count) and C-reactive protein were detected by a BC5300 automatic hematology analyzer (Mindray, Shenzhen, China). The level of prealbumin was measured by an AU5800 automatic biochemical analyzer (Beckman Coulter, HighWycombe, UK).

Detection of human metapneumovirus

Throat swabs were stored in 2.5 ml of viral transport medium (HEALTH GENETECH, Ningbo, Zhejiang). HMPV and twelve common respiratory pathogens, namely, influenza virus A (IVA), H1N1, H3N2, influenza virus B (IVB), human parainfluenza virus (HPIV), human adenovirus, HRSV, Mycoplasma pneumoniae, Chlamydophila pneumoniae, bocavirus, coronavirus, and human rhinovirus, were screened by PCR capillary electrophoresis fragment analysis (Juno Genomics, Hangzhou).

Detection of the partial F gene in human metapneumovirus

The viral RNA of the HMPV-positive samples was extracted from 300 µL of nasopharyngeal swabs using a nucleic acid extraction kit (Catalog Z-ME-0044, Shanghai Zhijiang Biotechnology Ltd., China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. RT‒PCR was performed by using MonScript RTIII Super Mix with dsDNase (Monad Biotech Co., Ltd., China). The F gene was amplified with the following primers [43]: F: 5’-ACATGCCAACATCTGCAGGACAAATAAAAC-3’ and R: 5’-ACATGCTGTTCACCTTCAACTTTGC-3’. Amplification was Carried out at 94 °C for 2 min, followed by 40 cycles of 94 °C for 30 s, 55 °C for 30 s, and 72 °C for 50 s, with a final extension at 72 °C for 4 min. Finally, the PCR products (727 bp) were sent to Ykang Biological Technology Co., Ltd. (Hangzhou, China) for sequencing. All the experimental procedures were performed strictly following the manufacturer’s instructions.

Statistical analysis

SPSS 22.0 was used for the statistical analysis. The chi-square test was used to compare the detection rates of HMPV among the different groups, and P < 0.05 indicated a statistically significant difference.

Phylogenetic analysis

Sequences from the F gene were checked against HMPV sequences available in GenBank. We aligned all sequences using of MAFFT v7.4.1 [44]. We applied IQTREE v2.1.3 for phylogenetic analysis [45], and the resulting trees were visualized using the ggtree package in R [46]. To investigate situations of complete gap deletion or missing data, the calculation of genetic distances was conducted using the Kimura 2-parameter method in MEGA-X [47].

Nucleotide sequence accession numbers

Representative nucleotide sequences of HMPV were submitted to GenBank under accession numbers PP355850-PP355975.

Results

Demography and laboratory blood parameters

Among 12,993 pediatric patients with ARTIs, 357 (3.43%, 357/12993) were HMPV-positive. As shown in Table 1, there were 287 hospitalized patients with pneumonia caused by HMPV infection; 149 (51.9%, 149/287) were male, and 138 (48.1%, 138/287) were female, with a mean age of 2.8 years. Of the patients, 185 were classified as non-severe, and the other 102 were classified as severe. A statistically significant sex difference between the severe and non-severe groups was detected (P < 0.05), whereas no evident age difference was detected between the two groups. Laboratory examination revealed significant decreases in lymphocyte counts, the platelet-to-neutrophil ratio (PNR), and prealbumin concentrations in the severe group compared to those in the non-severe group. In contrast, the neutrophil count, C-reactive protein (CRP) level, neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), C-reactive protein-to-albumin ratio (CPAR), and platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR) were markedly elevated in the severe group.

Sequencing and phylogeny

For the 287 HMPV-positive inpatient samples, 106 samples were successfully sequenced, from 40 severe patients and 66 non-severe patients. In addition, 20 outpatient samples were randomly selected and sequenced. In this study, the homology among the successfully sequenced strains was 96.79%. Compared to the reference strain (NCBI ID: NC 039199), the homology percentage was 96.75%. The evolutionary history of these strains was examined using phylogenetic analysis of all the sequences. The 126 sequenced HMPV strains could be clustered into 3 genotypes. There were 14 cases of B1 strain infection, 7 cases of B2 strain infection, and 105 cases of A2.2.2 strain infection in the inpatient samples, of which, 1 case was of B1 strain infection, 1 case was of B2 strain infection and 18 cases were of A2.2.2 strain infection from the outpatient samples (Fig. 3). The distribution of HMPV classifications and severity of illness are shown in Table 2. Compared with the 57 representative strains selected in NCBI and the HMPV sequences in our previous study (60 cases) [48], the main type of HMPV infection in Hangzhou was A2.2.2 from February 2022 to January 2023, which was different from the epidemic type identified in a previous study (Fig. 4). Discussion

In this study, we focused on inpatients who were categorized into two groups based on severity: severe group and non-severe group. The NLR, CRP level, and CPAR in the severe group were greater than those in the non-severe group. These outcomes, together with common symptoms and epidemiology, can serve as biomarkers for assessing the severity of HMPV infection. Pinilla et al. confirmed that there is a strong correlation between the CPAR and the severity of organ dysfunction in critically ill patients [49], and the CPAR is independently related to the mortality of patients with acute kidney injury (AKI) after adjusting for the severity of the disease [50].

Recently, the NLR has been considered an independent predictor of the severity and mortality of coronary syndrome [51] and has shown considerable diagnostic accuracy in predicting periprosthetic infection of the hip and knee joints [52]. In addition, the NLR is a potential prognostic indicator of systemic involvement in Henoch-Schonlein purpura, with a pretreatment increase in the NLR correlating with increased severity of systemic damage [53]. The availability of data from many studies, including meta-analyses [54], suggests that the NLR and CPAR may have considerable predictive potential. Therefore, the NLR and CPAR could serve as simple, rapid, and easy-to-obtain biomarkers that not only reduce the burden on patients but also have the potential to predict the severity of disease infection. However, the reliability of this method requires further validation with additional clinical data.

This study also has several limitations. The inflammatory process is complex, and using only the NLR and CPAR to show the effect of inflammation on patients reflects the general trend rather than the whole picture of the inflammatory process. Therefore, it is necessary to establish a standard critical value of the NLR and CPAR according to the patient’s condition, which will promote further study of their prognostic value. To state, it is possible that the observed changes in NLR, CRP levels, and CPAR values could be influenced by secondary infections rather than primary HMPV infection.

In the present study, 12,993 throat swabs were collected from pediatric patients with ARTIs, and 3.43% of the samples were positive for HMPV from February 2022 to January 2023 in Hangzhou. This percentage was lower than the 7.14% reported in our previous study [48], the 6.18% reported in Beijing [55], and the 12% in Spain from 2021 to 2022 [56]. The decrease in the infection rate in this study may be due to the continuous preventive measures taken by the Chinese government during the COVID-19 pandemic. From November to December 2021, Spain experienced a significant outbreak of pediatric HMPV. Compared with historical data, this outbreak manifested more severe clinical symptoms in patients, including elevated rates of fever, hypoxia, and pneumonia and a greater demand for antimicrobial treatments, subsequently leading to an increased hospitalization rate and an extended period of hospital stay [57]. In this study, fever (> 37.9 °C, 81.53%), cough (98.96%), and snot (40.42%) were identified as the main clinical symptoms presented in our patients. Notably, there was an increased incidence of oxygen inhalation (23.35%) caused by dyspnea compared with that reported in a previous study [55]. Among them, 7.46% (5/67) needed to rely on the ventilator. We also found that wheezing, a common clinical symptom observed in many studies [58], is associated with the aggravation of asthma in young children in approximately 41.12% of hospitalized children [59]. After the relaxation of COVID-19 prevention and control measures in China, the possibility of a higher HMPV infection rate, changes in the seasonality of the epidemic, and more severe clinical symptoms will prompt us to perform further follow-up research.

The phylogenetic tree revealed a change in the predominant epidemic strain of HMPV infection in Hangzhou from the B1 lineage [48] to the A2.2.2 lineage between 2022 and 2023. Similar conclusions were drawn from related studies: a study in Beijing highlighted A2b1 as the main epidemic strain [55], and another study by Cho SJ et al. in Gwangju, South Korea [60], confirmed the cyclic prevalence of the HMPV lineages A2.2.1, A2.2.2 and B2 from 2018 to 2022. At present, there is still a dispute regarding the relationship between HMPV gene type and disease severity [61]; although some researchers believe that the A2.2.2 lineage may be more virulent, the difference in virulence between HMPV lineages is still unclear [62–64]. From the analysis of amino acid variations, 6 strains of B2 were similar to HM197719 (2008 Rwanda), KJ627432 (2009Peru), KJ627414 (2010 Peru), MK588637 (2012 Kenya), and the B2 strain from 2020 to 2021 in Hangzhou in this study; 13 strains of B1 were similar to KF530179 (2003 Australia), KF530173 (2004 Australia), KC562235 (2004 USA), KC562219 (2005 USA), KJ627383 (2008 Peru), KJ627435 (2009 Peru), KF516922 (2011 South Korea), MK588636 (2012 Kenya), MN306019 (2018 USA), MK820375 (2018 China) and the B1 strain from 2020 to 2021 in Hangzhou. Four of the A2.2.2 strains did not have similar epidemics, while the others had similar epidemics in China (2008, 2012, 2017, 2018), Peru (2008, 2009, 2011, 2012), the USA (1983, 2016, 2017, 2019), Australia (2003, 2004), Kenya (2013), Zambin (2012) and Thailand (2013). In summary, the diversity of HMPV strains creates continuous changes in epidemic strains in specific regions, highlighting the importance of determining the cycle patterns of HMPV strains and to understand the relationships between HMPV strains and the disease severity.

Conclusion

During the COVID-19 pandemic and its associated prevention and control measures, the incidence of human metapneumovirus (HMPV) in pediatric patients with acute respiratory tract infections (ARTIs) decreased. In Hangzhou, the A2.2.2 lineage emerged as the predominant strain, while the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) and the C-reactive protein-to-albumin ratio (CPAR) demonstrated strong potential as predictive markers for severe cases.

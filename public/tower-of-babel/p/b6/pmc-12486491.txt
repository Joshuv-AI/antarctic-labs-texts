Changes in primary health care service experiences and urban–suburban disparities among Shanghai residents: a two-year comparative study
Authors: Yang Sen, Li Wanyu, Shi Jianwei, Shi Leiyu, Ma Le, Pan Ying, Wang Yang, Gu Chao, Zhang Hanzhi, Guo Mengruo, Jin Hua, Yu Dehua
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPrimary health care (PHC) is central to achieving universal health coverage, yet longitudinal assessments of residents’ experiences—especially regarding urban–suburban disparities—remain scarce. This study aims to examine changes in PHC service experiences among Shanghai residents between 2023 and 2024, identify urban–suburban differences, and explore key influencing factors.MethodsA two-wave cross-sectional survey was conducted at 248 community health centers in Shanghai from May to June in 2023 and 2024. The Chinese version of the Primary Care Assessment Tool–Adult Short Version (PCAT-AS) was used. Propensity score matching ensured comparability between survey waves. Descriptive statistics, hypothesis testing, and multiple linear regression were employed to assess temporal changes, regional disparities, and associated predictors.ResultsResidents reported significantly improved PHC experiences in 2024 across all domains (P < 0.001), with the largest gains in service comprehensiveness (+ 0.23) and referral coordination (+ 0.18). Urban residents consistently reported better experiences in continuity, service delivery, and cultural competence, while suburban residents rated higher in first-contact accessibility and service availability. Improvements from 2023 to 2024 were most notable in first-contact accessibility (Δ = +0.11) and comprehensiveness (Δ = +0.10), though domains such as family-centeredness and cultural competence showed limited progress (Δ = +0.01). Multivariate analyses identified residential location, chronic disease burden, psychological distress, and self-rated health as significant predictors of overall PCAT scores. Suburban residence was associated with lower scores (B = − 1.59, P < 0.001), while comorbidities (B = 1.94, P < 0.001) and psychological distress (B = 2.06, P < 0.001) were linked to higher scores.ConclusionThis study demonstrates that while overall perceptions of PHC services among permanent residents in Shanghai improved significantly compared to the previous year—particularly in terms of service comprehensiveness and referral coordination—urban–suburban disparities persist. Specifically, urban residents reported more favorable experiences in first-contact utilization, continuity of care, and cultural competence, whereas suburban residents expressed better perceptions of first-contact accessibility and service availability. Notably, the urban–suburban gaps in first-contact accessibility and service comprehensiveness narrowed between 2023 and 2024, suggesting a positive trajectory toward greater equity in basic health service delivery. Multivariate regression analysis further indicated that residential location, chronic disease burden, psychological distress, and self-rated health status were significant determinants of residents’ PHC experiences. Based on these findings, we recommend the implementation of more targeted interventions focusing on enhancing continuity of care and strengthening integrated management for both mental health and chronic conditions. Special attention should be given to addressing structural disparities between urban and suburban areas and to meeting the health service needs of priority populations, in order to continuously advance both equity and quality in PHC delivery.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12939-025-02633-w.

Background

Primary health care (PHC) serves as the foundation for achieving universal health coverage and constitutes a vital component of health systems worldwide. As the first point of contact for individuals and families, PHC ensures access to continuous, comprehensive, and readily available healthcare services. Beyond initial care, it plays a pivotal role in health promotion, disease prevention, treatment, rehabilitation, and palliative care [1]. The World Health Organization (WHO) emphasizes that PHC should be community-based and deliver comprehensive, continuous services spanning the full spectrum of care—from health promotion to disease management [2]. In China, PHC is primarily provided by primary institutions such as Community Health Service Centers (CHSCs) and township health clinics, offering a broad range of services including basic medical care, public health interventions, and health management.

In recent years, with the advancement of the hierarchical medical system, China’s PHC system has undergone continuous improvements. Nevertheless, a gap remains between the quality of services delivered and the evolving needs of the population. As one of China’s largest metropolitan areas, Shanghai has consistently prioritized PHC as a central strategy in advancing its “Healthy Shanghai” initiative. In 2023 and 2024, Shanghai introduced a series of innovative initiatives aimed at strengthening its PHC system. In 2023, efforts focused on refining the hierarchical medical system, deepening the implementation of family doctor contract services, advancing the integration of “Internet Plus” healthcare technologies, enhancing the training and retention of primary care professionals, and optimizing the functional layout of CHSCs [3]. “Healthy Shanghai” is a systematic and long-term initiative rather than a one-time campaign. Guided by the Healthy Shanghai Action Plan (2019–2030), it sets forth specific goals and implementation pathways for health promotion over the period from 2019 to 2030. The initiative aims to continuously strengthen community health service capacity, advance high-quality family doctor contracting services, and establish accessible, neighborhood-based health service platforms [4]. By the end of 2024, Shanghai had established a total of 248 CHSCs, covering all administrative districts and serving a population of over 24 million. These centers serve as the core infrastructure of the city’s primary healthcare system, with key functions including basic medical care, chronic disease management, public health services, and health education. In recent years, several CHSCs have introduced mental health outpatient clinics or psychological counseling rooms, initiating efforts to identify, screen, intervene, and refer common psychological problems. These developments reflect a gradual enhancement of mental health service capacity at the community level and are aimed at improving the quality of primary care, optimizing resource allocation, and enhancing the healthcare experience of residents.

Previous studies have widely employed the Primary Care Assessment Tool (PCAT) to evaluate the quality of PHC services across various countries and regions [5]. For instance, Moe et al. [6] employed the PCAT in Canada to examine team collaboration within community family practices, while Liu et al. [7] applied the tool to evaluate the effectiveness of family doctor contract services in China. Numerous studies have also utilized the PCAT to compare different models of primary care. Nascimento et al. [8] assessed the quality of two public oral health service models—the family health strategy and traditional care—in Curitiba, Brazil. Aoki [9] conducted a comparative analysis of patient experiences between small-to-medium-sized hospitals and community clinics in Japan. In China, researchers in Guangdong province evaluated urban-rural disparities in perceived PHC quality through surveys conducted in community health centers and township health stations [10]. Other investigations have focused on specific population groups, such as high-risk infants [11], patients with multimorbidity [12], and internal migrants [13], further highlighting the PCAT’s applicability in diverse contexts.

Despite previous efforts—such as the Guangdong study exploring urban-rural differences in primary care perceptions [10]—the existing research focused solely on the year 2015 and lacked a longitudinal, time-series comparison, making it difficult to capture the dynamic changes in suburban residents’ experiences of healthcare services over time. Given the significant transformations in China’s PHC system over the past decade (2015–2025), this study focuses on Shanghai—a megacity representative of advanced urban healthcare infrastructure. By surveying suburban residents in 2023 and 2024, this study compares differences in perceived PHC service experiences across districts and years, with particular emphasis on the extent of improvements in suburban services and the factors influencing these changes. Findings from this study contribute to a comprehensive understanding of the current status and evolving trends in Shanghai’s PHC system. They also provide evidence-based insights for evaluating the effectiveness of policy implementation, optimizing PHC services, and informing regional and international efforts to promote equitable, high-quality primary care.

Methods

Study design and participants

This study employed a two-wave cross-sectional survey design, conducted between May and June in both 2023 and 2024. The surveys were administered in 248 CHSCs across Shanghai. The study population consisted of permanent residents who visited the general practice outpatient clinics of CHSCs during the survey periods. Inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) individuals who provided informed consent and voluntarily agreed to participate; (2) those who were cognitively sound and able to complete the questionnaire independently or with assistance; (3) aged between 18 and 85 years; and (4) current outpatients, regardless of gender. In each CHSC, a convenience sample of 10 eligible residents was surveyed.

For the purpose of regional comparison, residential areas were categorized into urban and suburban groups based on the Administrative Division Criteria of Shanghai (see Appendix 3). In addition to administrative boundaries, urban areas are generally characterized by higher population density, more developed transportation and healthcare infrastructure, and greater socioeconomic diversity. In contrast, suburban areas tend to have lower population density, larger service radii, and more dispersed healthcare resources. Although the terms “suburban” and “peri-urban” are sometimes used interchangeably in prior literature, “peri-urban” typically refers to transitional zones between rural and urban settings that are undergoing rapid development. In this study, however, the definition of suburban areas extends beyond peri-urban zones to also include remote suburban districts farther from the city center. These areas differ significantly from urban centers in terms of healthcare resource distribution and socioeconomic characteristics.

Sample inclusion process

In 2023, a total of 2,598 questionnaires were collected. After excluding 7 questionnaires due to age ineligibility and 8 incomplete questionnaires, 2,583 valid questionnaires were retained. In 2024, 2,600 questionnaires were collected, with 12 excluded for age ineligibility and 9 for incomplete data, resulting in 2,579 valid questionnaires. The effective response rates were 99.35% in 2023 and 99.19% in 2024. The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Yangpu Hospital, Tongji University (Approval No. LL-2023-SCI-016). To enhance the comparability between the two survey years, a propensity score matching (PSM) approach was applied to balance baseline characteristics. After matching, 2,369 participants were retained from each survey year, yielding a final total sample size of 4,738 participants (details are shown in Fig. 1).

Data collection instrument

The questionnaire utilized the Chinese version of the Primary Care Assessment Tool—Adult Short version (PCAT-AS), originally developed by the Johns Hopkins Primary Care Policy Center. This instrument has undergone Chinese translation, cultural adaptation, and psychometric validation, and has been proven suitable for assessing PHC services across diverse urban and rural populations in China [14]. The PCAT-AS encompasses four core domains—first-contact accessibility, continuity of care, comprehensiveness, and coordination of services—as well as three derivative domains: family centeredness, community orientation, and cultural competence. These are further divided into 10 subdomains comprising a total of 35 items. Responses are measured using a four-point Likert scale ranging from “definitely yes” (4 points) to “definitely not” (1 point), with “not sure/cannot remember” scored as 2.5 points. The total possible score ranges from 35 to 140, with higher scores indicating better perceived experiences. In this study, the PCAT-AS demonstrated good reliability (Cronbach’s α = 0.904) and strong construct validity (Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin value = 0.937). In addition to PCAT items, the questionnaire also captured sociodemographic characteristics (e.g., gender, age, occupation, type of medical insurance, residential area) and health-related variables, including self-rated health status, presence of chronic conditions, and any psychological or emotional issues.

Data collection and quality control

All surveys followed standardized criteria and procedures for participant selection to ensure the comparability of samples. The questionnaire was designed as an electronic form using the “Wenjuanxing” platform and was completed by residents via mobile phone by scanning a QR (Quick Response) code. For elderly participants unfamiliar with digital devices, assistance from family members was permitted, and interviewers recorded contact information for follow-up verification if necessary. The survey was conducted in coordination with the quality control schedules of various districts in Shanghai. Investigators received unified training to ensure consistent interpretation of questionnaire items and standardized guidance during completion. A one-on-one facilitation approach was adopted throughout the survey process to ensure that all questionnaires were completed accurately and validly.

Data analysis methods

To minimize potential confounding bias, PSM was employed to balance baseline differences between the two annual samples. Based on prior studies [15], variables with significant influence—namely age, occupation, residential area, type of medical insurance, self-rated health status, type of chronic disease, and presence of psychological or emotional problems—were included in the matching process. Nearest neighbor matching was applied with a caliper set at 0.1. The quality of matching was assessed using the standardized mean difference (SMD); after matching, all variables had SMDs less than 0.1, indicating good baseline balance [16]. Descriptive analyses were conducted for participants’ sociodemographic characteristics (e.g., age, gender, education level) and PCAT domain scores. To facilitate comparisons across domains, average scores were calculated by dividing the total score of each domain by the number of items in that domain. Continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation (Mean ± SD), while categorical variables were reported as frequencies and percentages (n,%). For group comparisons of categorical variables, chi-square tests (χ² tests) were used. If more than 20% of the cells had expected frequencies less than 5 or any cell had an expected frequency less than 1, Fisher’s exact test was applied instead. For continuous variables, independent sample t-tests were used to examine differences in domain scores between subgroups. To further explore factors associated with residents’ PHC service experiences, multivariable linear regression models were constructed. A two-sided p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Additionally, the ∣ΔChange∣ metric was introduced to assess the dynamic change in urban–suburban disparities over time. This indicator represents the absolute difference between the urban–suburban score gap in 2024 and that in 2023 for each PCAT domain, thereby indicating whether the disparity has narrowed, widened, or remained stable.

Results

Participant characteristics

Before PSM, significant differences were observed between the 2023 and 2024 cohorts in terms of age, occupation, type of health insurance, self-rated health status, and number of chronic conditions (P < 0.05). After PSM, no statistically significant differences were found between the two groups across key baseline characteristics, including gender, age, residential location, occupation, health insurance type, health status, chronic disease burden, and psychological or emotional problems (P > 0.05), indicating that the matching process successfully achieved baseline comparability. Changes in PCAT Domain Scores Between 2023 and 2024

The results indicated a statistically significant improvement in all domain scores in 2024 compared to 2023 (P < 0.001), reflecting an overall enhancement in the quality of PHC services. Among all domains, the most notable improvements were observed in comprehensiveness (services available) and coordination (referral system), with mean score increases of 0.23 and 0.18, respectively (P < 0.001). In contrast, the coordination (information system) domain showed the smallest improvement, with a modest increase of 0.03 (P<0.001). Urban–suburban differences and improvements in PCAT domain scores, Shanghai, 2023–2024

After PSM, notable differences in PHC experiences between urban and suburban residents persisted in 2023. Urban residents reported significantly higher scores in first-contact utilization, coordination (referral system), comprehensiveness (services provided), and cultural competence, while suburban residents scored better in first-contact accessibility and comprehensiveness (services available). By 2024, urban residents continued to demonstrate relative advantages in continuity of care, comprehensiveness (services provided), community orientation, and cultural competence compared to their suburban counterparts.

Analysis of the changes in urban–suburban disparities across PCAT domains between the two survey years revealed a narrowing gap in first-contact accessibility (|ΔChange|= 0.11) and comprehensiveness (services available) (|ΔChange|= 0.10). However, the disparity in continuity of care widened (|ΔChange| = 0.06). In contrast, gaps in other domains—including comprehensiveness (services provided), family-centeredness, community orientation, and cultural competence—remained relatively stable (|ΔChange| = 0.01), showing no substantial changes. Determinants of overall PCAT scores among permanent residents in Shanghai

After PSM, multivariable linear regression analysis revealed that residential area, presence of psychological or emotional problems, type of chronic disease, and self-rated health status were significant factors influencing the outcomes. Living in suburban areas was significantly associated with lower PCAT scores compared to urban residence (B = −1.59). In contrast, the presence of psychological or emotional problems was positively associated with higher scores (B = 2.06), as was having two or more chronic conditions (B = 1.94), suggesting a cumulative effect of chronic disease burden on perceived care quality. Additionally, individuals reporting good health status had significantly higher PCAT scores (B = 2.71), highlighting the positive role of perceived health in shaping care experience. Variables such as gender, age, retirement status, and type of health insurance were not significantly associated with the outcome (P > 0.05). Detailed regression results are presented in Table 4.

Discussion

Overall improvement in PHC experiences among Shanghai residents

This study demonstrates that overall perceptions of PHC services among permanent residents in Shanghai improved between 2023 and 2024. Scores across all PCAT domains increased to varying degrees, with the most notable improvements observed in service comprehensiveness and referral coordination. These enhancements in care experiences coincided with the ongoing implementation of policies such as the “1 + 1 + 1” family doctor contract services and the refinement of the tiered healthcare delivery system in Shanghai. The policy context may have contributed to the observed strengthening of primary care service capacity during this period. Policy documents released in 2024 [4, 17] further reinforced the development of community health service capacity and the implementation of standardized care protocols, offering institutional support for chronic disease management, health education, and rehabilitative services. Prior studies [18] have also shown that the introduction of family doctor contract services significantly improved perceived primary care quality in pilot cities. Building on Shanghai’s experience, it is recommended that efforts be made to optimize the family doctor service model and enhance public recognition and utilization of contracted services. Particular emphasis should be placed on expanding the scope of diseases treated and the range of services provided by primary care institutions—especially in areas such as chronic disease management, health education, and rehabilitative care—to ensure that residents can access comprehensive medical services at the community level. In our study population, approximately 71% of respondents were female. This phenomenon can be partly attributed to the demographic characteristics of primary care service users in China—women, particularly older women, are more likely to seek medical care, participate in health-related surveys, and engage in community health programs. Similar findings have been reported in previous studies conducted in urban areas of mainland China [19]. Moreover, our study found that over 86% of participants reported choosing CHSCs as their first point of contact for routine health check-ups. This is noteworthy given that bypassing primary care remains a common challenge globally, often leading to fragmented services and reduced efficiency [20]. The high rate of first-contact utilization of community-based institutions observed in our study suggests growing public trust in CHSCs and increasing recognition of their role as “gatekeepers” of health in Shanghai.

Urban–suburban disparities in perceived PHC services persist but are gradually narrowing

Although the overall quality of PHC services improved between 2023 and 2024, significant disparities in residents’ experiences between urban and suburban areas remained. These disparities exhibited both domain-specific and multidirectional trends. In 2023, urban residents reported superior experiences in comprehensiveness (services provided), coordination (referral system), and cultural competence, whereas suburban residents rated higher in first-contact accessibility and comprehensiveness (services available). By 2024, urban residents continued to demonstrate advantages in continuity of care, community orientation, and cultural competence, likely attributable to the optimized spatial distribution of primary care facilities, well-established appointment systems, and the widespread adoption of digital health technologies. Innovations such as teleconsultation, electronic health records, and intelligent triage systems have notably enhanced the convenience and continuity of access to primary care services in urban settings.

Meanwhile, suburban areas showed progress in family-centeredness, coordination (referral system), and coordination (information system). These gains may be linked to the “high-quality specialist resources reallocation” policy launched in 2024, which prioritized access to specialists from secondary and tertiary hospitals through family doctor referrals. This initiative has helped alleviate longstanding issues such as limited access to high-quality specialty care and inefficient referral pathways in suburban regions, thereby improving the patient experience [21]. The persistent urban–suburban gap can largely be attributed to imbalanced resource allocation, differing policy emphases, and mismatches between service demand and supply. Urban areas benefit from richer healthcare resources and more advanced technical infrastructure, giving them an edge in service comprehensiveness and referral coordination. In contrast, suburban areas still hold advantages in terms of basic service accessibility. However, the widening disparity in continuity of care suggests that suburban primary care institutions face significant challenges in establishing sustained patient–provider relationships and long-term health management systems. These findings are consistent with prior studies [22], which have highlighted weaker continuity of care in low-resource settings, resulting in suboptimal experiences for residents—particularly in managing chronic conditions and receiving long-term health support.

Urban–suburban disparities as a key determinant of primary care experience

Analysis of the impact of residential location on primary care experience revealed that living in suburban areas was significantly associated with lower overall PCAT scores compared to urban residency. This finding suggests that service quality in suburban settings still requires considerable improvement. Interestingly, this result contrasts with our research team’s earlier study conducted in 2020, which surveyed 2,400 Shanghai residents and found that suburban residents reported better experiences than their urban counterparts [23]. One potential explanation for this discrepancy lies in the differences in study populations: the 2020 study primarily included adults over 40 years old, while the current study encompasses a broader age range, including 351 respondents under the age of 40. Younger residents tend to have higher expectations for personalized and digital health services—needs that may be insufficiently addressed by the relatively limited comprehensiveness of suburban healthcare services, thereby negatively affecting their care experiences. In recent years, Shanghai has concentrated efforts on optimizing healthcare resource allocation in urban areas, particularly through initiatives such as urban medical consortia and integrated healthcare networks. These efforts have substantially improved the accessibility and efficiency of care in city districts. In contrast, the pace of resource redistribution to suburban regions has been comparatively slow, which may partly explain the differing conclusions between the two studies [24]. Similar urban–suburban disparities are also widely observed in primary healthcare systems across other countries. For instance, studies from Malaysia [25] and India [26] have shown that peri-urban populations generally report lower levels of service accessibility, continuity of care, and cultural competence compared to those in urban core areas. The patterns observed in this study are consistent with international findings, highlighting the persistence of structural inequalities in the allocation of health resources and institutional safeguards between urban and suburban regions.

The unequal distribution of healthcare resources between urban and suburban areas not only compromises service accessibility for suburban residents but also diminishes their overall experience. These disparities reflect a complex interplay between resources, policy priorities, and population needs, highlighting the urgent need for targeted policy interventions. Policymakers should prioritize increased investment in suburban healthcare infrastructure, with an emphasis on enhancing both physical capacity and service capability. This includes establishing differentiated financial support mechanisms, improving equipment allocation standards, and strengthening diagnostic and treatment competencies at suburban health institutions. In parallel, efforts should be made to standardize health information systems across regions, enabling core functions such as data interoperability, bidirectional referrals, and longitudinal health management, thereby supporting integrated service delivery through robust digital infrastructure. Moreover, innovative models are needed to facilitate the downward extension of high-quality medical resources. Strategies may include expanding telemedicine platforms to enable remote expertise sharing and establishing rotational specialist outreach programs to provide consistent on-site support. In light of the persistent capacity gaps in disciplines such as surgery, mental health, and dentistry within suburban areas, it is recommended to implement targeted workforce incentive programs. These may offer competitive salaries and career development opportunities to attract and retain a skilled and stable primary care workforce. Through the implementation of multi-dimensional and multi-level strategies, it is possible to achieve coordinated improvements in the quality of primary care services across urban–suburban boundaries and advance the goal of equitable healthcare for all residents. To date, some domestic studies have preliminarily validated the positive impact of downward extension of high-quality medical resources and regional medical consortia on improving primary care experiences. For example, county-level medical alliances—through vertical integration between county hospitals and primary care institutions—have significantly enhanced provider satisfaction in rural areas, particularly in aspects such as collaborative working environments, human resource development, and service integration [27].

Combined effects of chronic conditions, mental health issues, and self-rated health on primary care experience

In terms of individual physical and mental health status, residents with chronic illnesses or psychological problems generally reported more favorable experiences with primary care services. This finding aligns with previous studies conducted in China’s Greater Bay Area [13]. Patients with chronic conditions or mental health concerns tend to have frequent interactions with primary care systems and rely heavily on these services, which may enhance their familiarity with care processes, resource navigation, and patient–provider interactions. As a result, their overall care experience is often more positive, underscoring the critical role of PHC institutions in managing chronic diseases and mental health conditions [28]. Meanwhile, residents who self-rated their health as good or excellent also reported higher scores on the primary care quality assessment scale, suggesting that perceived health status significantly influences satisfaction with healthcare services. This implies that health status not only shapes healthcare needs but also directly affects subjective perceptions and evaluations of healthcare quality. These findings highlight the importance of optimizing chronic disease management models, particularly within primary care settings. Strengthening follow-up protocols, health education, and personalized interventions at the community level can help deliver more continuous, structured, and integrated care, thereby improving patient outcomes and quality of life [29]. Globally, a critical shortage of mental health services persists—especially in low- and middle-income countries—where the majority of individuals with psychological disorders do not receive adequate care. As the first point of contact and a vital entryway into the mental health care system, PHC plays a pivotal role in expanding service coverage and promoting early detection and timely intervention [30, 31]. According to the World Mental Health Report 2022 released by the WHO [32], more than half of low- and middle-income countries have failed to effectively integrate mental health services into primary care, severely limiting the early identification and intervention of common psychological conditions. Enhancing the capacity of primary care institutions to deliver mental health services, including the deployment of qualified mental health professionals and the provision of accessible and effective counseling and support, is an urgent priority to address the rising mental health needs of the population and reduce broader societal burdens. Future research should pay greater attention to the intersection of chronic disease and mental health, and advocate for the development of more integrated, coordinated, and person-centered primary care delivery models. Such efforts will be essential for advancing precision in health management and enhancing both the overall effectiveness of primary care services and population health outcomes.

Strengths and limitations

This study systematically compared year-on-year changes in PHC experiences among permanent residents in Shanghai and provided an in-depth analysis of the dynamic evolution of urban–suburban disparities. It addresses a key limitation of prior research, which has predominantly relied on single-year, cross-sectional observations. The study sample covered 248 CHSCs across Shanghai, offering strong representativeness and enabling a more accurate reflection of both the current status and evolving trends in residents’ PHC experiences. Additionally, the application of PSM to balance baseline characteristics between the 2023 and 2024 samples effectively controlled for key demographic and health-related confounders, thereby enhancing the credibility of the comparative findings.

However, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the study sample was drawn exclusively from Shanghai, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to other regions. Second, the overrepresentation of female respondents may introduce gender-related bias in the evaluation. Future studies should aim to recruit a more gender-balanced sample and apply stratified analyses to explore gender differences in healthcare utilization and patient experience. Third, although PSM improved the validity of comparisons, it also resulted in the exclusion of some original cases, potentially affecting the representativeness of the final sample. Moreover, unmeasured confounders—such as health literacy, healthcare-seeking preferences, and other behavioral or social determinants—were not included, which may still influence the results. Future research should adopt more comprehensive control frameworks to enhance the robustness and explanatory power of the findings. Fourth, due to its cross-sectional design, the study can only identify trends in service experience at specific time points, but cannot establish causal relationships between policy interventions and observed improvements. Longitudinal studies are needed to further clarify the dynamic associations between influencing factors and changes in care experiences. Finally, the study focused on residents who had actually utilized community health services and did not include individuals who did not seek care. This may introduce systematic differences in perceived accessibility and experience, potentially limiting the comprehensiveness of the overall assessment.

Conclusion

This study sheds light on the evolving trends in PHC services in Shanghai between 2023 and 2024 and provides an in-depth analysis of disparities in care experiences between urban and suburban residents. The findings indicate that while overall perceptions of PHC services have improved, notable urban–suburban gaps persist. Therefore, optimizing PHC delivery requires sustained attention to the misalignment between resource allocation, service delivery models, and population needs across geographic regions.

To further promote an equitable and accessible primary care system, we propose the following strategies: First, increase financial investment and policy support targeting suburban areas, with a particular focus on enhancing digital infrastructure, interoperability of electronic health records, and the capacity of family doctor teams to fulfill contractual obligations—thus strengthening service continuity and coordination. Second, improve the competencies of family doctor teams in psychological assessment, comorbidity identification, and personalized interventions. Strengthening collaborative pathways between general practitioners and specialists will enhance the ability of primary care systems to manage complex health conditions comprehensively. Third, explore institutional mechanisms for the orderly downward extension of high-quality medical resources. This includes establishing regional collaborative networks and telemedicine support platforms, implementing regular specialist outreach programs, and providing on-site technical support—all of which can help promote service standardization and improve accessibility. These strategies hold promise for narrowing existing disparities and enhancing service reach, although sustained efforts will be required to achieve long-term equity in PHC delivery.

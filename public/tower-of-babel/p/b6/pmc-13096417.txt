Molecular international prognostic index prognostic model in diffuse large B-cell lymphoma
Authors: Ting-juan Zhang, Xiao-chi Wu, Ming-qiang Chu, Zi-jun Xu, Liang Qiao, Yang-jing Zhao, Jun Qian, Jing-dong Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAlthough diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) has entered the era of molecular subtyping, gene mutations have not yet been used for risk stratification of patients with DLBCL. Therefore, there is an urgent need for a molecular-based model to comprehensively evaluate the prognosis of DLBCL patients in clinical practice.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed 761 patients with DLBCL from cBioPortal datasets. Gene mutations and the International Prognostic Index (IPI) were analyzed for their associations with overall survival and were used to determine the set of prognostic variables. A Cox model was utilized to estimate the relative weights of the selected variables.ResultsWe mapped at least one gene mutation in 94% (718/761) of patients with DLBCL. Survival analysis identified that PIM1, KLHL14, CD79b, and MYC mutations were correlated with adverse outcomes, whereas EZH2, STAT3, and B2M mutations were predictors of favorable outcomes. Using these gene mutations together with IPI, we established a novel prognostic system, IPI-M, which resulted in a unique risk score for individual patients. We further derived five IPI-M risk categories (very low, low, intermediate, high, and very high), demonstrating stronger prognostic separation in DLBCL. Compared with IPI, the discrimination ability of IPI-M showed a better discrimination of survival with concordance index value and higher 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year survival as measured by area under the receiver operating characteristic curve values. Finally, IPI-M model also presented a prognostic separation for overall survival especially for the discrimination of high/very high patients from intermediate/low/very low patients in an external validation cohort.ConclusionsWe developed a novel, feasible integrated prognostic system for newly diagnosed DLBCL that incorporates the IPI and genetic mutations, which facilitates precise prognostic assessment in clinical practice and supports precision therapy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-026-04817-4.

Introduction

Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) is an invasive mature B-cell lymphoma, accounting for approximately 30% of non-Hodgkin lymphomas (NHLs) [1]. DLBCL is a clinically and prognostically heterogeneous subtype of NHLs [1, 2]. Based on the first-line standard R-CHOP (rituximab, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, and prednisone) regimen, approximately 60% of patients can achieve clinical cure, while the remaining almost 40% of patients develop recurrent/refractory (R/R) disease [1]. It is crucial to identify the patients who may progress to R/R DLBCL after standard R-CHOP immunochemotherapy at initial diagnosis, and thus could provide enhanced treatment to improve their prognosis [3]. Clinical attempts have been made to identify such patients through various classification and prognostic evaluation systems, such as higher International Prognostic Index (IPI) score, cell-of-origin (COO), double-expressor lymphoma, and double/triple-hit lymphoma [1, 4–8]. Recently, the application of high-throughput sequencing technology has enabled researchers to gain better understanding the molecular heterogeneity of DLBCL, and DLBCL subtyping has gradually entered the era of genetic-based typing. Schmitz et al. first reported genetic subtypes of DLBCL (including MCD, BN2, N1, and EZB) with distinct genotypic, epigenetic, and clinical characteristics [9]. Subsequently, the same research team developed a probabilistic classification tool, LymphGen, for genetic subtypes of DLBCL (consisting of MCD, N1, A53, BN2, ST2, and EZB), and providing a potential nosology for precision medicine strategies in DLBCL [10]. More recently, Shen et al. simplified the molecular typing of LymphGen with a 38-gene algorithm (termed LymphPlex), identifying seven distinct genetic subtypes: TP53mut, MCD-like, BN2-like, N1-like, EZB-like, ST2-like, and NOS [11]. Importantly, genetic subtype-guided targeted agents ‘X’ combined with R-CHOP (R-CHOP-X) significantly improved the prognosis of newly diagnosed DLBCL [12]. These results suggest the crucial role of genetic changes in the pathogenesis and prognosis of DLBCL.

The prognostic models of DLBCL used in clinics are mainly based on the IPI, age-adjusted IPI (aaIPI), and National Comprehensive Cancer Network revised IPI (NCCN-IPI), whose parameters include age, serum lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), functional status score (PS), Ann Arbor stage, extranodal involvement site, and renal or adrenal involvement [4, 13, 14]. However, these models rely on clinical and laboratory features but do not consider genetic alterations. Therefore, there is an urgent need for a molecular-based model to comprehensively evaluate the prognosis of DLBCL patients in clinical practice. In this study, we developed the molecular IPI (IPI-M) model to guide risk stratification and treatment decisions for DLBCL.

Materials and methods

Training cohort

The training cohort included a total of 761 patients with available IPI and mutation data from the cohort of 1001 DLBCL patients derived from cBioPortal datasets [15–18]. Clinical data and laboratory findings of these patients were downloaded by the following website (https://www.cbioportal.org/datasets) [Diffuse Large B-Cell Lymphoma (Duke, Cell 2017)].

Validation cohort

Another independent cohort of 135 patients with DLBCL derived from cBioPortal was also included as the validation cohort [16–19]. Clinical data and laboratory findings of these patients were also downloaded by the following website (https://www.cbioportal.org/datasets) [Diffuse Large B cell Lymphoma (DFCI, Nat Med 2018)].

Statistical analysis and bioinformatics analysis

Survival rates were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier plot and compared across subgroups via the weighted log-rank test. Moreover, Cox proportional hazards regression model was applied to assess the ability of variables to predict survival. Prognostic risk scoring models incorporating the IPI and gene mutations were developed, with the relative weights of these factors determined based on Cox regression coefficients. All statistical analyses were performed through R software and SPSS 20.0 software.

Genes with a mutation frequency greater than 4% or referred in ‘LymphPlex’ [11] are included in the mutation analysis. We mapped at least one gene mutation in 94% (718/761) of patients with DLBCL, and the mutation landscapes of these patients were presented in Fig. 1.

Construction of IPI-M risk score and risk categories

Based on the prognostic-related variables identified by survival analysis, we selected all the seven gene mutations together with IPI to construct an IPI-M prognostic risk model. The prognostic risk score model was constructed according to the regression coefficients and calculated by the following formula: IPI-M risk score = 0.461 × IPI + 0.442 × PIM1–0.842 × EZH2–0.805 × B2M + 0.520 × KLHL14 + 0.539 × CD79b + 0.611 × MYC − 1.233 × STAT3 (If the gene was mutated defined as 1, if not defined as 0). Each patient was assigned a risk score with the prognostic model. Since the prognostic model play a crucial role in guiding treatment decisions, we accurate developed a five-category risk schema for IPI-M as follows (Table 3): very low (risk score ≤ 0.0), low (risk score > 0.0 to 0.5), intermediate (risk score > 0.5 to 1.5), high (risk score > 1.5 to 2.0), and very high (risk score > 2.0).

Validity assessment of IPI-M prognostic model compared with IPI model

Compared with prognostic effect of IPI risk categories (Fig. 3a, b and c), IPI-M risk categories revealed a strong prognostic separation for OS in DLBCL (Fig. 3d, e and f). The index of concordance (C-index) showed an improved discrimination ability of IPI-M compared with the IPI (Fig. 3g). Moreover, we next analyzed the predicted results of IPI-M risk score classification for 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year survival with area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC) values of 0.680, 0.709, and 0.725, respectively, whereas IPI model showed lower AUC values of 0.673, 0.703, and 0.716 for 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year survival (Fig. 3h).

We further applied IPI-M to another independent cohort of 135 patients with DLBCL from the cBioPortal datasets. The IPI-M risk categories also presented a prognostic separation for OS especially for the discrimination of high/very high patients from intermediate/low/very low patients (Fig. S1).

Discussion

Risk stratification is of great importance for formulating treatment strategies of DLBCL. Over the past two decades, the IPI has been widely used as a prognostic scoring system for assessing the prognosis of DLBCL. However, with the advancement of high-throughput sequencing technology, it has become evident that the basic clinical features employed in the IPI fail to capture the extensive clinical and biological heterogeneity of these patients [20]. DLBCL typing has gradually entered the era of genetic-based typing, and could guide treatment choice in DLBCL [10, 12]. In this study, we developed a novel prognostic prediction model for OS in DLBCL patients using a combination of IPI and gene mutations. Mutations in PIM1, EZH2, B2M, KLHL14, CD79b, MYC and STAT3 with prognostic value were incorporated in the model. Notably, although B2M mutation did not meet the criteria for statistical significance in Cox regression analysis, the Kaplan-Meier analysis showed a statistically significant result. Moreover, B2M mutation is a characteristic mutation of the EZB subtype, indicating a favorable prognosis [11]. Consistently, previous studies showed that DLBCL patients harboring PIM1, CD79b and MYC mutations have been shown to have inferior survival outcomes [21, 22], whereas DLBCL patients with EZH2 and B2M mutation were associated with superior survival [11, 23]. Therefore, the IPI-M model, which integrates molecular alterations with IPI, allows for a more comprehensive prognostic assessment of patients with DLBCL.

Indeed, numerous studies have investigated prognostic factors in DLBCL and developed novel potential prognostic risk-stratification models [24–29]. However, most of these models are built based on gene expression data [30, 31]. Given the limited clinical utility of gene expression profiling, such models have poor clinical applicability. Recently, several researchers have also attempted to develop new models by integrating clinical parameters with molecular mutations [22, 32–35]. However, the majority of these lack external validation with large datasets [36]. Recently, Mi et al. developed a genetic-based IPI (IPI-G) model, combined molecular subtypes and IPI scoring system, which contributes to prognostic evaluation in clinical practice and provides a new basis for the targeted therapies and precision medicine [35]. The most difference between the two studies is that the prior research is based on molecular subtypes (including MCD and A53), whereas our study further identifies specific genetic mutations.

Recent molecular profiling investigations have established a novel, reproducible categorization of genetic subgroups in DLBCL that are characterized by unique oncogenic programs [37], thereby supporting the potential to develop a targeted treatment approach guided by genetic subtypes for this malignancy. Zhang et al. reported the efficacy and safety of genetic subtype-guided individualized immunochemotherapy in patients with newly diagnosed DLBCL, and demonstrated a higher response rate and better survival outcomes in the R-CHOP-X arm compared with the R-CHOP arm [12]. Of course, the clinical application of our model requires further prospective clinical studies for validation.

There are several limitations to this study that should be acknowledged. First, the current study is a retrospective analysis based on public database data. Second, to improve the generalizability of the prognostic model, external validation using data from diverse populations and clinical settings is recommended. Third, the impact of genetic subtyping on treatment efficacy was not explored in this research. To address these limitations, future studies with larger sample sizes, external validation, multicenter collaboration, and prospective design are essential to strengthen the evidence base and improve the broad clinical applicability of the prognostic model.

In summary, we developed a novel, feasible integrated prognostic system for newly diagnosed DLBCL patients, which incorporates the IPI scoring system and genetic mutations. This system facilitates precise prognostic assessment in clinical practice and provides a robust rationale for precision therapy.

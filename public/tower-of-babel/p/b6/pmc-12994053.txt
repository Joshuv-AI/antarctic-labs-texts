Current insights into long non-coding RNAs role for insulin resistance emerging in type 2 diabetes mellitus and its complications
Authors: Rehab M. Abdel-Megeed
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Insulin resistance (IR) is a key factor in the development and progression of diabetes and other metabolic disorders. It occurs when insulin target tissues do not respond to normal insulin levels. Further research is needed to identify the specific mechanisms underlying IR. Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) refers to non-protein coding RNA molecules longer than 200 nucleotides. LncRNAs play important roles in cell development, proliferation, apoptosis, and metabolism. Recent investigations suggested that lncRNAs have a significant role in the pathophysiology of metabolic disorders, including IR. Although lncRNAs have gained a lot of interest as promising and reliable biomarkers in the development and progression of a number of disease states, little is known about their clinical significance and regulatory mechanisms in relation to metabolic disorders. It has been demonstrated that various lncRNAs regulate glucose/lipid metabolism and insulin signaling such as LncRNAs H19, MEG3, GAS5, and MALAT1. The current review summarized and discussed the most recent developments on the roles and mechanisms of lncRNAs in IR after providing a brief introduction to the general characteristics of lncRNAs.

Introduction

Diabetes is a type of metabolic disorder characterized by high levels of blood glucose associated with several complications, such as microvascular problems (such as diabetic kidney disease, diabetic retinopathy, and neuropathy) and macrovascular disorders (such as cardiovascular disease). Additionally, diabetes is a risk of development different cancer types, which can lead to a reduction in life quality and increase mortality rate [1], [2]. The two most prevalent forms of this condition are type 1 and type 2 diabetes mellitus. In type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), metabolic dysfunction causes a relative insulin shortfall, but in type 1 diabetes, the autoimmune impairment of insulin-generating β cells causes an absolute insulin deficiency. The International Diabetes Federation has estimated that the prevalence of diabetes is expected to rise from an estimated 463 million cases in 2019 to an estimated 700 million cases globally by 2045 [3], [4].

Pancreatic cells are responsible for the production and release of the anabolic insulin hormone. After a meal or an oral glucose uptake, insulin released by islet cells controls the homeostasis of protein, lipids and glucose according to increased glucose levels in blood [5], [6]. Insulin sensitivity is necessary for glucose homeostasis that determines the concentration of insulin for reducing blood glucose to a normal level via targeting several organs, including the skeletal muscle, liver, and adipose tissues [7]. Insulin promotes glucose utilization (mostly glycogen synthesis), inhibits lipolysis, increases lipid storage in the liver and adipocytes, and suppresses endogenous glucose production (primarily hepatic glucose production) [8]. Insulin resistance (IR), a pathophysiological state characterized by elevated lipid metabolic abnormalities in the liver and adipose tissues. It occurs when the physiological level of insulin suppresses hepatic glucose production and fails to stimulate glucose uptake in skeletal muscle and adipose tissue. IR is characterized by reduced cellular sensitivity to insulin leading to hyperinsulinemia, hyperglycemia after meals and even in fasting, and high glycated hemoglobin levels. This occurs when target tissues are unable to utilize glucose in response to insulin stimulation [9].

IR refers to a state in which the target tissues, including the liver, muscle, and adipose tissue, show low sensitivity to typical insulin concentrations, necessitating increased insulin production to regulate blood glucose levels. IR actually represent a wide clinical spectrum, which includes obesity, glucose intolerance, diabetes, and metabolic syndrome, as well as a significant insulin-resistant state. Many of these disorders are associated with a range of endocrine, metabolic, and genetic conditions. These syndromes may also be connected to immunologic diseases and may display distinct phenotypic characteristics [10].

The oxidative stress induced by glucose auto-oxidation, glycation, and mitochondrial dysfunction can impair endothelial cell function. Hyperglycemia triggers the generation of superoxide in mitochondria, which subsequently leads to the production of a potent oxidant known as nitrite. It damages DNA. Then, the nuclear enzyme poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase is activated reducing the intracellular levels of NAD (+), thereby hindering glycolysis, ATP synthesis, and the ADP ribosylation of GAPDH. These mechanisms further exacerbate the onset of late-stage degenerative complications. These inflammatory and oxidative-stress mechanisms generally drive several disease progression, not only in diabetes [11], [12], [13], [14], [15], [16]

Long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs), which are noncoding RNAs longer than 200 nucleotides, are a unique kind of RNA transcript that is transcribed from DNA but not translated into proteins. LncRNAs are essential regulators of almost all metabolic pathways and functions [9], [17], [18]. RNA polymerase III transcripts, which range in length from roughly 50–200 nucleotides; short RNAs, which are less than 50 nucleotides long; and lncRNAs. More than 90% of the human genome's transcripts are non-coding RNA, which is defined as transcripts that do not encode proteins with lengths more than 200 nucleotides. According to several investigations, lncRNAs are not completely non-functional transcription products as they may play essential roles in variety biological processes including inactivation of X chromosome, DNA imprinting, stem cell pluripotency, cell proliferation, apoptosis, nucleocytoplasmic transport and growth of the body. Furthermore, progression of numerous diseases is strongly associated with unregulated lncRNAs profiles [18]. It has been demonstrated that lncRNAs carry out their biological roles by physically interacting with proteins, RNA, and DNA, either via nucleotide base pairing or by forming structural domains via RNA folding [19]. Spatial-temporal specificity characterizes lncRNAs expression, which is implicated in epigenetic, transcriptional, and post-transcriptional control of gene expression and biological function [20]. Numerous tissues in insulin-resistant condition have aberrant lncRNA expression patterns. Furthermore, dysregulated lncRNA profiles are linked to metabolic disorders such diabetes and nonalcoholic steatohepatitis [21], [22]. This review provided a quick overview of the general characteristics of lncRNAs and lncRNA research methodologies then summarizing and discussing the most recent researches on the roles of lncRNAs in IR.

Previously, one or more of lncRNAs such as H19, MALAT1, MIAT, HOX transcript antisense RNA (HOTAIR) and MEG3 were firstly used for treating chronic complications of diabetes and other related conditions that involve the administration of a HOTAIR inhibitor [23].

The current review covered the time range from 2016 to 2026 getting information source from online data base of PubMed, Scopus, Google Scholar, Web of Science. Inclusion criteria in this review including intervention of lncRNAs and IR in addition to timeframe, while exclusion criteria filter out studies with wrong design and older studies. Overview of lncRNAs: classification and mechanism of action

LncRNAs can be categorized as sense, antisense, intronic, bi-directional, and intergenic lncRNAs according to the areas from which they are transcribed [24]. Intronic lncRNAs are transcribed from intragenic non-coding regions, whereas lncRNAs are transcribed from the gaps between protein-coding genes. Protein-coding genes' sense and antisense strands are transcribed to sense and antisense lncRNAs, respectively. Although they are transcribed from the opposite direction, bi-directional lncRNAs share the same promoter as protein-coding genes [25].

The majority of lncRNAs are linear, however some can form circular RNAs (circRNAs), which are distinguished by their covalently joined 3′ and 5′ ends. Notably, lncRNAs sequences have a usually poor level of evolutionary conservation across different species. As represented in Fig. 1, most lncRNAs do not have corresponding homologous genes in model organisms. Based on their locations in the genome, LncRNAs may be divided into five categories: (1) Intergenic long noncoding RNAs: produced by two genes that code for proteins. (2) Intronic or extronic lncRNAs: they are generated from a protein-coding gene's intron. (3) Transcribed from a protein-coding gene's sense strand are sense lncRNAs. (4) Transcribed from the antisense strand of a protein-coding gene are antisense lncRNAs. Bidirectional lncRNAs are those that have a transcription start site within 1 kb and are transcribed in the opposite direction from a nearby protein-coding gene [26], [27] [Fig. 1].Fig. 1LncRNAs Classification.

lncRNAs have lower expression levels than protein-coding mRNAs and are affected by several variables such as chromatin status, posttranslational effects and microRNAs [28]. RNA sequencing data from six cell lines indicate that lncRNAs are mostly found in the nucleus, particularly on the chromatin. Some stable lncRNAs can be found in both the nucleus and cytoplasm [29]. Nuclear lncRNAs govern transcription and scaffolding in the nuclear compartment, while cytoplasmic lncRNAs control signaling and gene expression [30].

Characteristic features of lncRNAs are also resembled in its transcription as well degradation processes. However, lncRNAs are mainly transcribed via RNA polymerase II activation which undergo similar to mRNA processing, they are usually less abundant than microRNAs (miRNAs) and genes that code for proteins [31]. According to the analysis of ENCODE transcripts, the majority of lncRNAs are only around one-tenth of total mRNAs, and some only exist in a few copies within a single cell, with the exception of a small number that are derived from pseudo-genes whose expression levels in cells are comparable to those of protein-coding genes [32]. LncRNA pathways responsible for degradation process such as nonsense-mediated decay, nuclear RNA exosome-mediated degradation, and standard mRNA degradation routes including processing body-mediated degradation [33].

LncRNAs affect gene expression at both the transcriptional and posttranscriptional stages [9], [34]. Different investigations proposed that both circular RNAs (circRNAs) and lncRNAs have an essential role in the regulation of biological processes via interaction with DNA, RNA, and proteins [31]. Specifically, lncRNAs can guide proteins to specific chromosomal regions or mRNAs. LncRNAs play a role in chromatin remodeling, modifying histone conformation with Polycom Repressive Complexes 1 and 2, resisting DNA methylation, and interacting with transcription factors. They possess the ability to create miRNAs by transcribing embedded sequences, negatively control miRNAs by binding to MREs, and target mRNA for destruction [35]. LncRNAs, or competing endogenous RNAs, interact to miRNA binding sites like MREs and limit their actions [36], [37]. Previous investigations revealed that lncRNA TMPO-AS1 resemble a sponge for miRNAs including miR-320a, miR-383–5p, and miR-329–3p. Furthermore, lncRNA uc.230 can be considered as a natural sponge for miR-503 for the maintenance intestinal mucosal homeostasis [38], [39]. LncRNAs play a vital role in fetal development and differentiation. The key lncRNAs implicated in this process are X-inactive specific transcript, imprinted maternally expressed transcript (H19), and maternally expressed gene 3 (MEG3) [40].

As previously investigated, certain lncRNAs also encode biologically significant peptides. Some lncRNAs include a small open reading frames (smORF) which are encoded micropeptides of less than 100 amino acids possess an important biological functions. It was believed that they are coding genes, not non-coding genes. Furthermore, certain loci may generate both coding and non-coding RNAs. When exposed to UV radiation, for instance, the long, protein-coding ASCC3 transcripts will change to a short, non-coding activating signal cointegrator 1 complex subunit 3 (ASCC3) isoform [41].

Prior research primarily examined the roles of lncRNAs in cardiovascular diseases [42], [43], cancers [43], [44], [45], and hereditary disorders [46], [47]. Numerous lncRNAs have recently been found to be involved in metabolic processes under both physiological conditions, such as aging [48], as well as pathological conditions, such as diabetes [49] and fatty liver [9]. Moreover, there has been evidence linking lncRNAs to the onset and advancement of IR which is the main goal this review [9], [49], [50], [51].

It is crucial to note that a single lncRNAs can serve several roles. Hepatocellular carcinoma up-regulated lncRNA (HULC), which is increased in liver cancer, serves as a scaffold for the Y box binding protein 1/ extracellular signal-regulated kinase complex (YB-1/ERK) and a decoy for miR-372/miR-373, highlighting their intricate involvement in cellular processes. lncRNAs have important roles in gene regulation and cellular processes, making them crucial for studying metabolic diseases and devising treatment solutions [49], [52].

LncRNAs function in pancreatic β-cells

Pancreatic β-cells generate and release insulin, which stimulates glucose absorption and regulates blood glucose levels. Transcriptome research found that exonic readings of proinsulin mRNA were recorded 20% of the time in pancreatic islets, but increased to 45% in β cells [53]. In the past few years, islet lncRNAs have emerged as major regulators of insulin production and release. However, it has long been recognized that numerous islet-enriched transcription factors modulate insulin gene expression. The maternally expressed gene, often known as MEG3, is an imprinted noncoding gene. MEG3 expression in the islet of Langerhans is 20 times higher in human β-cells than in α-cells, which produce glucagon. Blocking Meg3 expression using small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) in mouse β-cells increases β-cell mortality and reduces insulin synthesis and release, since glucose modulates its expression. Furthermore, mice treatment with intravenous MEG3-targeting siRNAs reduced insulin expression and secretion. MEG3's presence in pancreatic β-cells implies a function in transcriptional regulation. However, the specific processes by which it affects insulin production are unclear. MEG3 regulates gene expression in many tissues by engaging the histone methyltransferase [54].

Highly specialized endocrine cells found in the islets of Langerhans are principally in charge of storing and releasing insulin in response to glucose levels. The pathogenesis of both type 1 and type 2 diabetes is influenced by changes in the identity or function of cells. According to recent research, lncRNAs control a number of biological processes in islets, including as cell division, proliferation, secretion, and insulin synthesis. Morán et al. (2012) analyzed the transcriptome and found 1128 genes for the human pancreatic islets. Of these, islet-specific lncRNAs accounted for 40% of antisense lncRNAs and 55% of long intergenic ncRNAs (lincRNAs) [53]. Ku et al. (2012) discovered over 1000 lncRNAs or lincRNAs in mouse islets using RNA sequencing; the majority of them only impact certain cells [55].

Since lncRNAs are essential for β-cell differentiation and maturation, it is possible that aberrant expression of lncRNAs unique to β-cells plays a part in the pathophysiology of diabetes. Additionally, there was a notable decrease in HI-LNC45 expression, although potassium voltage-gated channel subfamily Q member 1 (KCNQ1) overlapping transcript-1 concentrations in T2DM patients' islets were noticeably higher. Nine of the 55 T2DM susceptibility loci that were found to have islet lncRNAs within 150 kb of the main single nucleotide polymorphism were directly associated with cellular dysfunction, according to further study. Interestingly, it was shown that islet lncRNA HI-LNC25 may control GLIS family zinc finger protein 3 (GLIS3), which is strongly linked to the development of type 2 diabetes [56]. The aforementioned findings point to islet lncRNAs as possible diabetes biomarkers. Fadista et al. (2014), for instance, showed that the MAP kinase-associated death domain, synaptotagmin11 (SYT11), and paired box 6 (PAX6) regulate proinsulin synthesis, insulin exocytosis, and pancreatic islet development in vitro. The functional role of lncRNAs in controlling the diagnosis and pathophysiology of diabetes is confirmed [57].

Potential role of lncRNAs in insulin resistance (IR)

According to recent investigations, lncRNAs play a crucial part in the molecular processes underlying IR. The function of lncRNAs may be inferred from their subcellular location, which can be either in the cytoplasm or the nucleus. Numerous lncRNAs participate in nuclear-level gene regulatory activities, including as transcriptional repression or activation, chromatin remodeling-induced activation or repression of multiple loci, and epigenetic gene regulatory processes [50]. Furthermore, lncRNAs can control post-transcriptional gene expression by modifying the translation or stability of partly. To regulate splicing and translation, complementary mRNAs are utilized, as well as interference with RNA-binding proteins. Competing endogenous RNAs (ceRNA) and circular RNAs are persistent lncRNAs that accumulate in cells and regulate gene expression by serving as decoys or sponges for microRNAs. Finally, certain lncRNAs post-translation regulate protein turnover by promoting ubiquity [58]. However, the particular characterization of lncRNAs subcellular localization in IR models has concentrated on endogenous competitor function and cytoplasmic localization, whereas lncRNAs at the nuclear level have not been studied. Currently, the GENCODE database (v.37) classifies 17,957 genes as lncRNA. Dysregulation of lncRNAs have been previously confirmed to be related to IR, such as metastasis-associated lung adenocarcinoma transcript 1 (MALAT1), H19, MEG3, steroid receptor RNA activator (SRA), and myocardial infarction-associated transcript (MIAT). However, there is still much to learn about the function, molecular processes involved, and expression of these non-coding RNAs in IR [59].

The role of LncRNAs in controlling insulin sensitivity and secretion

In response to circulating nutrients, pancreatic β-cells release insulin as necessary. Several lncRNAs are involved in controlling insulin sensitivity and secretion. SRA, for example, has been shown to increase insulin sensitivity and encourage adipocytes to absorb glucose in response to insulin stimulation. In the underlying processes, SRA's ability to regulate the expression of many factors influencing insulin sensitivity seems to be crucial. The expression of negative regulators of insulin sensitivity decreased as a result of tumorigenicity 2 suppression in adipocytes overexpressing SRA-like receptors. In contrast to negative regulators like SH3 domain-containing 1, cytokine signaling −1 and 3 suppressors increase the expression of positive regulators [60]. Additionally, researchers have demonstrated that SRA improves insulin function via the serine-threonine protein kinase (AKT) and forkhead box O1 (FOXO1) signaling pathways, namely glucose uptake.

According to Yin et al. (2015), downregulating the lncRNA cell death was elevated by the taurine up-regulated gene (TUG1) and decreased insulin secretion in vitro as well as in vivo [61]. TUG1 knockdown in Min-6 cells reduced transcription factor essential mRNA levels for the synthesis and secretion of insulin, including glucose transporter 2 (GLUT2), MAF BZIP Transcription Factor A (MafA), neurogenic differentiation Pdx1, NeuroD1&2 during pregnancy. Several lncRNAs probably target comparable mechanisms to control insulin release [62]. Role of lncRNAs in DM complications

Numerous diabetes problems are associated with microvascular and macrovascular dysfunctions, which in turn contribute to peripheral vascular and cardiovascular disorders, retinopathy, and nephropathy. These disorders have been linked to particular genes. The vascular disorders frequently exhibit compromised angiogenesis.

Although only a small number of lncRNAs linked to diabetes are directly linked to diabetic vascular problems, it has been demonstrated that these lncRNAs independently control angiogenesis. Pharmacological and siRNA-induced suppression of MALAT1 prevented in vivo vascular development. Furthermore, vascular smooth muscle cells of coronary arteries were the first to identify lncRNAs abundant in vascular cells, smooth muscle, and endothelial cells. Subsequent investigation revealed that lncRNAs restrict VSMC migration and support the stability of the contractile phenotype of VSMCs. Because diabetic cardiovascular problems are characterized by VSMC proliferation in response to hyperglycemia, researchers found that lncRNA-p21 inhibits the growth of macrophages and VSMCs while causing cell death via p53 signaling. This implies that lncRNA-p21 has a role in the inflammation or vascular problems associated with diabetes [75], [76].

Diabetic nephropathy is a widespread and serious side effect of diabetes that greatly adds to the burden of chronic renal disease worldwide. Earlier researches also investigated the essential role of epigenetic mechanisms as lncRNAs and miRNAs [76]. Diabetic retinopathy is a chronic microvascular diabetic complication affecting on the retina due to hyperglycemia due to MIAT1 disruption [77].

Metabolic origin of insulin resistance

Insulin resistance is a pathological condition involving limitation of metabolic response as well as sensitivity of insulin target cells to insulin stimulation. Two types are abundant of insulin resistance, the first one which develops as a result of the action of insulin antagonists or counterinsular hormones, and the other that is brought on by abnormalities of insulin reception and signal transduction along intracellular signaling pathways [78].

Two main theories elucidating the mechanisms of insulin resistance formation are the lipocentric and glucocentric theories. The lipocentric theory posits that insulin resistance results from the lipotoxic impact of an abundance of free fatty acids (FFA) and its derivatives, such as ceramides and diacylglycerol, within cells. Serine/threonine phosphokinase C (PKC) is activated and endoplasmic reticulum stress is induced by the lipotoxic effects of lipids. By phosphorylating the serine residues of insulin receptor substrate molecules, PKC activation stops insulin signaling through the PI3K/AKT molecular cascade [8]. In conjunction with endoplasmic reticulum stress, the unfolded protein response is further activated, triggering threonine/serine c-Jun-N-terminal kinases (JNK) that inhibit the insulin receptor substrate and insulin receptor trigger an inflammatory response [79], [80]. According to the glucocentric theory, repeated bouts of hyperglycemia that are accompanied by the production of advanced glycation end products leading to the development of insulin resistance. A diverse collection of chemical substances known as advanced glycation end products are often created by non-enzymatic condensation between the free amino groups of proteins, lipids, or nucleic acids and the carbonyl groups of reducing sugars [9], [81].

It has been demonstrated that advanced glycation end products play an essential role in the development of insulin resistance due to their impact on the insulin signaling pathway and its regulators, such as mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) and phosphatidylinositol-4,5-bisphosphate 3-kinase (PI3K). Insulin resistance is raised when advanced glycation end products engage with their primary cellular receptor, receptor for advanced glycation end products, which activates signaling pathways linked to MAPK/ERK, Transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β), JNK, and Nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB) that leading to increase of oxidative stress and inflammation [82], [83].

Association of MALAT1, IR, cancer and metastasis

LncRNAs might play a role in the regulation of glucose metabolism in various types of cancers. MALAT1 was initially discovered to be overexpressed in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and has been recognized as a biomarker for lung cancer metastasis. However, recent studies have revealed that MALAT1 was upregulated in various cancer tissues and correlates with significantly diminished overall survival rates among cancer patients. The lncRNA MALAT1 has been demonstrated to promote the expression of Programmed cell death ligand 1(PD-L1) across multiple cancer cell types, which is also associated with notably lower overall survival rates in cancer patients. Additionally, PD-L1 is regulated by MALAT1 in an indirect fashion [84], [85]. The overexpression of the oncogenic lncRNA MALAT1 contributes to the proliferation, invasion, and metastasis of NSCLC [86].

Numerous investigations have examined the possible involvement of lncRNAs in the regulation of glucose metabolism among clinical HCC patients. MALAT1 was identified as influencing HCC development by modulating glucose metabolism, boosting glycolysis, and inhibiting gluconeogenesis through the elevation of the transcription factor TCF7L2. The enhancement of MALAT1 translation by TCF7L2 is mediated. In further it upregulates serine/arginine-rich splicing factor 1 (SRSF1) and activates the mTOR complex 1 (mTORC1) and eukaryotic translation initiation factor 4E-binding protein 1 (4EBP1) axis [87].

Signaling pathways in IR

Insulin is an endocrine peptide hormone composed of 51 amino acids in addition to α and a β chain which linked together as a dimer. Target cells' plasma membranes contain the insulin receptor which is bound by insulin. This triggers downstream proteins, mainly insulin receptor substrate PI3K and AKT isoforms, which are mostly conserved across insulin target tissues and start the insulin response, to be recruited or phosphorylated. Firstly, the inactive glycogen synthase kinase 3 (GSK3) and protein phosphatassxe 1 (PP1) stimulate glycogen production acting as an AKT substrates. Furthermore, FOXO1 is transported from the nucleus after being phosphorylated by AKT, which inhibits gluconeogenesis and disables its transcription factor function. Then, protein synthesis and adipogenesis are induced via activation of mTORC1 inhibitors and proline-rich Akt substrate 40 (PRAS40). Followed by constitutive increases in de novo lipogenesis (DNL) due to the overexpression of sterol regulatory element binding protein 1c (SREBP-1c), the phosphorylation of ATP citrate lyase (ACLY), and the dephosphorylation of ACC1/2 through inhibition of AMP-dependent protein kinase (AMPK). Finally, the abhydrolase domain containing 15 (ABHD15) protein and phosphodiesterase 3B (PDE3B) work together to decrease lipolysis in adipocytes by blocking hormone-sensitive lipase (HSL) and adipose triglyceride lipase (ATGL) [88].

Insulin produces its biological effects by attaching to its cell-surface receptors, thereby activating particular adapter proteins, including the insulin receptor substrate (IRS) proteins (mainly IRS1 and IRS2), Src-homology 2 (SH2), and protein-tyrosine phosphatase 1B (PTP1B), ultimately facilitating downstream insulin signaling that regulates glucose homeostasis. [89].

Epigenetic alternations in IR

Epigenetics is the examination of heritable, functionally significant genetic alterations that arise from chromosomal modifications, which do not disrupt the fundamental nucleotide sequence of a DNA molecule [1]. Epigenetic modifications have the capacity to regulate gene transcription by activating or deactivating genes and can influence the accessibility of genes for DNA binding and regulatory proteins, including transcription factors, RNA polymerase II, and various other elements. Research findings indicated that epigenetic alterations can modulate gene expression at both the transcriptional and post-transcriptional levels, contributing to the etiology of numerous complex multifactorial diseases. The primary mechanisms involved in epigenetic gene regulation consist of DNA methylation, histone modification, and non-coding RNA. Epigenetic modifications serve as a primary link in the intricate network of interactions between environmental factors and the genome by triggering the inflammatory response that accompanies metabolic disorders. There is substantial evidence that, together with epigenetic modifications, the transcription factors NF-kB, Signal transducer and activator of transcription (STAT), Forkhead box P3 (FOXP3), and Interferon regulatory factor (IRF) are crucial in regulating genes related to inflammation [90].

Investigations into the relationship between impaired adipocytes and the inflammatory mechanisms and IR where, a lower count of SLC19A1 adipocytes triggers DNA hypermethylation and modifies the expression of particular inflammatory genes, including the cytokine gene C-C motif chemokine ligand 2 (CCL 2), especially in obese women. In contrast, an examination of the DNA methylation patterns of Toll-like receptor 2 and 4 (TLR2/4) genes in obese patients indicated a reduction in the methylation of the first exon of the TLR4 gene, reinforcing the importance of epigenetic alterations in the control of inflammatory processes in IR [91]. Hypermethylation was also noted in the promoter region of interleukin 6 (IL-6); the authors propose that the heightened basal levels of IL-6 are due to a negative feedback loop. The methylation level of the IL-6 promoter was also found to be increased in individuals with elevated BMI, which the authors associate with the presence of chronic low-level inflammation in obesity [92].

The inflammatory process is characterized by Thioredoxin interacting protein (TXNIP) overexpression, which activates the NOD-like receptor protein-3 (NLRP3) inflammasome, resulting in the secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines, such as Interleukin-1 beta (IL-1 β). Furthermore, recent studies indicate that the elevation of Suppressor of cytokine signaling 3 (SOCS3) gene expression due to hypomethylation raises the likelihood of IR and the prevalence of diabetes [89].

Additionally, in diabetic patient samples, peripheral blood mononuclear cells reveal SET domain containing lysine methyltransferase 7 (SET7) and histone H3 lysine 4 trimethylation (H3K4me) in the Nuclear factor NF-kappa-B p65 subunit (NF-kB p65) promoter, a crucial epigenetic modification that leads to pro-inflammatory phenotypic changes; however, the suppression of SET7 methyltransferase activity eliminated the inflammatory and pro-oxidative signaling of NF-kB [10].

Recent extensive research into lncRNAs as epigenome-altering agents has revealed that these molecules regulate inflammation at both transcriptional and post-transcriptional levels. The microRNA-34a, which is delivered by exosomes secreted by adipocytes, has been found to suppress the polarization of macrophages into the anti-inflammatory M2 phenotype. In overweight individuals, higher levels of miRNA-34a expression in visceral adipose tissue are positively correlated with the prevalence of insulin resistance (IR) and systemic inflammation [10].

Challenges and future perspectives

Despite the growing number of lncRNAs, their precise functions and mechanisms of action remain unclear. Research on lncRNAs and diabetes is still in its early stages. Recent findings indicate that numerous biological processes are actively regulated by lncRNAs. Numerous including metabolic disorders including obesity, DMT2, arterial hypertension, dyslipidemia, and metabolic-associated fatty liver disease, may be significantly influenced by aberrant expression of lncRNAs leading to signaling pathways disruption. These findings suggest that lncRNAs could be potential therapeutic targets and diagnostic markers for diabetes and related comorbidities, opening up a new field of research in diabetes. LncRNAs are involved in regulating several biological processes. This review provides an overview of lncRNAs, categorize their roles, and explore the processes through which lncRNAs are involved in regulating insulin resistance associated with diabetes.

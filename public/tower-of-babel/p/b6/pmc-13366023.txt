IFPHPOS0044 Artificial Intelligence (AI) in Oral Cancer Screening: Global Evidence, Equity Gaps and Workforce Implications
Authors: Krishnapriya M Raj
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOral cancer affects more than 389,000 persons per year, with India carrying one third of the worldwide burden. Although tobacco, alcohol and HPV are well-established risk factors most are diagnosed at a late stage when five-year survival falls approximately below 50%.MethodsA narrative literature review of published reports and meta-analyses of Artificial Intelligence (AI) diagnostic use on oral cancer and oral potentially malignant disorders (OPMDs) was conducted, with an equity reporting and workforce consideration strategy applied both in a global and Indian contextResultsMeta-analytic evidence demonstrates AI sensitivity of 0.87 and specificity of 0.81 in the detection of oral cancer and OPMDs, with an AUC of 0.9758 and diagnostic odds ratio of 131.63, indicating excellent overall diagnostic performance (Sahoo et al., 2024). Convolutional neural networks consistently demonstrated superior diagnostic performance. However, equity reporting across socioeconomic and demographic subgroups remains critically absent, and no identified studies directly addressed workforce planning implications specific to AI in oral cancer screening.DiscussionAI demonstrates strong diagnostic accuracy for oral cancer screening. Its greatest potential public health benefit lies in extending detection beyond dental surgeries to community health workers, pharmacists and primary care settings where there is an acute shortage of specialist workforce capacity. Unless equity is incorporated into AI design and implementation, these tools risk widening health disparities. Public health-led framework is urgently needed to guide ethical, equitable and workforce-sensitive AI deployment in oral cancer screening globally.

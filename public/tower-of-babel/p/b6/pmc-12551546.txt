Evaluating the scale-up of the Play Active programme for children’s physical activity in early childhood education and care services: a national type III hybrid effectiveness-implementation trial protocol
Authors: Hayley Christian, Carol Maher, Stewart G Trost, Jasper Schipperijn, Kevin Murray, Ian Li, Andrea Nathan, Alana Papageorgiou, Matthew Mclaughlin, Adrian Bauman
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPhysical activity is crucial for young children’s health and development. Many young children do not meet the recommended 3 hours of daily physical activity, including 60 min of energetic play. Early childhood education and care (ECEC/childcare) is a key setting to intervene to improve children’s physical activity. The Play Active programme is a scalable evidence-informed ECEC-specific physical activity policy intervention with implementation support strategies to improve educators’ physical activity-related practices.Methods and analysisThis hybrid type III effectiveness-implementation trial will use a quasi-experimental repeated measures design to assess the real-world effectiveness of Play Active’s scalable implementation support strategies in helping ECEC services adopt the practices included in the Play Active policy. Secondary aims will examine changes in educator-reported and device-measured children’s physical activity; assess the sustainability of the programme; identify effective dissemination strategies; assess cost-effectiveness; and involve comprehensive process evaluation. All ECEC services in Western Australia (n=776), Queensland (n=1744) and South Australia (n=445) will be invited to participate. Data will be collected at baseline, 6, 12, 18, 24 and 30 months.Ethics and disseminationEthics approval has been provided by The University of Western Australia Human Research Ethics Committee (HREC) (2023/ET000187), the University of Queensland HREC (2024/HE000076) and the University of South Australia HREC (206023). This real-world trial of Play Active is vital for understanding its implementation in practice and to generate evidence for further scale-up and roll-out nationally. Key findings will be disseminated to stakeholders, collaborators, policy-makers as well as families and practitioners in the ECEC sector.Trial registration numberACTRN12624000406505.

Background

Daily physical activity during early childhood provides many health and developmental benefits.1 Young children’s engagement in physical activity is associated with improved learning, physical and mental health, social and emotional development, and decreased risk of developing chronic diseases.1 2 Physical activity behaviours formed during early childhood typically track into adolescence and adulthood, highlighting the importance of establishing positive physical activity behaviours early in life.3 Despite these benefits, many young children do not meet the recommended 1 hour of energetic play (moderate-to-vigorous intensity physical activity) as part of the three total hours of daily physical activity4 5 outlined in national and WHO 24-hour movement guidelines.68 A pooled analysis of device-based physical activity data from 33 countries shows only half of 3–5 year olds meet both the total physical activity (≥180 min/day) and energetic play (≥60 min/day) recommendations.9

Early childhood education and care (ECEC) services are recognised as an important setting for promoting regular physical activity among young children.10 Globally, between 50% and 90% of children under the age of 5 years old attend ECEC services.11 In Australia, more than 40% of children aged 0 to 5 use childcare attending for 25 hours per week on average.12 Previous research has shown that ECEC services have a greater influence on variations in young children’s physical activity levels than sociodemographic factors. Consequently, they are a WHO-recommended setting for promoting physical activity.10 13

Several factors influence children’s physical activity levels while attending ECEC. Such factors include educator knowledge and practices (eg, modelling and promotion of physical activity),14 15 educator-parent engagement,16 and the ECEC physical environment (eg, large, unobstructed outdoor play spaces)17 18 and policy environment (eg, specific ECEC physical activity policy).16 17 Overall, little is known about the content and implementation of physical activity policies in ECEC. A recent international scoping review identified that, among the 43 ECEC movement behaviour-related policy documents reviewed, most were poorly worded, developed without consultation, highly variable in the duration and frequency of recommended movement behaviours, and neither evidence based nor aligned with national and international 24-Hour Movement Guidelines for the Early Years.19 In the Australian context, while National Quality Standards require ECEC services to promote and support each child’s physical activity,20 there is no regulation mandating the development or implementation of a physical activity policy. In contrast, Australian National Childcare Regulations require ECEC services to develop and implement policies for sun protection, sleep and nutrition.21 Despite this lack of regulation, research highlights a demand from the ECEC sector for an ECEC-specific physical activity policy and support to implement associated practices.162224

In 2019, in response to the gap in the policy landscape for an Australian-specific ECEC physical activity policy, the Play Active policy intervention was developed. Play Active includes an evidence-informed physical activity policy template for ECEC services.16 22 23 The policy contains 25 practices to support ECEC services to meet two key statements and nine age-specific recommendations which provide clear guidance on the amount of physical activity and sedentary behaviour, including screen time, children should have while attending ECEC.16 Several strategies were developed to support ECEC services in implementing these practices.16 Briefly, they included (1) a tailored policy for each service; (2) professional development and (3) the Play Active Resource Guide including the brief Energetic Play Assessment tool.16 23

The Play Active policy and implementation support strategies were piloted and evaluated in 2021–2022 via a pragmatic cluster randomised trial.23 Findings from the trial with 81 metropolitan ECEC services in Perth, Western Australia, including 569 educators and 982 children, demonstrated that Play Active is effective in supporting services to adopt a new physical activity policy (100% intervention services adopted the policy).25 Uptake of the policy also led to significantly higher adoption of policy practices and 80% of ECEC staff were satisfied or very satisfied with the programme.25 However, these results were achieved under pragmatic cluster randomised trial conditions, in a single metropolitan-only region23 and thus are insufficient to justify national rollout.

Real-world trials, which offer high generalisability, are essential for translating research into practice.26 27 However, ecological (ie, real-world) trials of scaling-up evidence-based interventions remain ‘underused’ in physical activity research, and those that do exist often have a high risk of bias.28 29 Type III hybrid designs are an optimal design for testing the impact of implementation support strategies of a public health policy recommendation or practice, while simultaneously assessing their effectiveness on health outcomes (eg, child physical activity).26 27 To our knowledge, only two real-world studies of ECEC physical activity policy interventions exist.243032 Both studies examined new North American state-based government-mandated physical activity guidelines for ECEC. One of these studies used face-to-face delivery for implementation support,32 which is expensive and thus limits scalability.33 The other study provided no implementation support, relying only on the government mandate.31 Further research is needed to develop and test ECEC physical activity policy interventions that are both effective and scalable.24 Research is also required to test such interventions in different countries where the ECEC sector and systems are often country-specific.

A real-world scale-up trial of Play Active is vital for understanding its implementation in practice and to generate evidence for further scale-up and national dissemination in Australia. In 2022, findings from the pragmatic cluster randomised trial informed the fidelity-consistent adaptation of Play Active for scale-up, using an innovative adaptation approach34 that incorporated stakeholder input, scalability assessments and theoretical frameworks.22 23 34 35 36 This paper outlines the protocol for a hybrid type III effectiveness-implementation trial, designed to evaluate the real-world effectiveness of Play Active’s scalable implementation support strategies in helping ECEC services adopt the practices included in the Play Active policy.

Methods and analysis

Study design

The setting for this trial is ECEC long-day care services. In Australia, such services offer centre-based childcare for infants, toddlers and preschool-aged children (prior to formal compulsory school) provided by professional staff with ECEC qualifications.37 ECEC long-day care services operate on an enrolment basis, with families charged a daily fee for service.20 Australian ECEC services are required to meet minimum standards across seven quality areas outlined in the Australian National Quality Framework for ECEC, including Quality Area 2.1: ‘Each child’s health and physical activity is supported and promoted’.20

This hybrid type III effectiveness-implementation trial will use a quasi-experimental repeated measures design, with an ecological recruitment approach, to test the real-world effectiveness of Play Active to promote physical activity in children attending ECEC services in Western Australia, Queensland and South Australia. The duration of the Play Active study is 30 months with data collected at baseline, 6, 12, 18, 24 and 30 months. An overview of the schedule for recruitment and data collection is shown in table 1. Recruitment and baseline data collection commenced in mid-2024. This protocol is reported according to the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials guidelines.38

Participants and recruitment

Eligibility criteria

All long-daycare ECEC services in Australia will be eligible for inclusion with dissemination and recruitment prioritised to Western Australia, Queensland and South Australia. Ineligibility criteria include long-day ECEC services catering exclusively for children requiring specialist care, mobile preschools and government-operated preschools. Within each ECEC service, all directors and educators will be eligible to participate.

All attending children aged 2–5 years will be eligible to participate in the accelerometer substudy with parental consent. Exclusion criteria include children with a recognised disability (physical, emotional/behavioural or intellectual) that would affect participation in physical activity and attendance of full-time formal schooling which would prohibit whole days spent in ECEC.

Recruitment

ECEC services will be recruited using an ecological dissemination (recruitment) strategy codeveloped in consultation with project partners and stakeholders. Rolling registration into Play Active commenced in April 2024 following a national launch event which was used to promote the programme nationally. Additional recruitment strategies include:

Direct mail-out of an invitation letter and Play Active flyer to all registered ECEC services in Western Australia, Queensland and South Australia.

3-month transmedia paid advertising campaign including social media, Google and ECEC sector e-news and magazines.

Stakeholder engagement with ECEC service provider senior management/leadership to recruit multiple services from providers.

ECEC directors and educators register via the Play Active website and are linked to their ECEC service using a list of services provided by the Australian Children’s Education & Care Quality Authority.39 At the time of registration, a participant information form is provided. Directors and educators are required to provide their consent to participate prior to commencing the baseline survey and receiving the intervention (become a Play Active member).

A subset of 30 ECEC services (10 per state in Western Australia, Queensland and South Australia) who are Play Active members will be invited (via the ECEC service director) to participate in the accelerometer substudy. Parents/carers from these services will be invited to participate in the accelerometer substudy. Parent/carers will be provided with a participant information form and asked to provide written informed consent for their child to participate.

As part of the process evaluation, a subsample of directors (n=20) and educators (n=20) will be invited to participate in qualitative semistructured interviews to explore the barriers and facilitators to implementing Play Active. Directors and educators will be provided with a participant information form and asked to provide written informed consent to participate.

Intervention: Play Active programme for ECEC

The central component of Play Active is an evidence-informed physical activity policy template for ECEC services.16 23 Implementation support strategies help services implement the Play Active policy. Play Active is delivered at scale to ECEC services via a custom-built website (figure 1).34

Play Active intervention website (www.playactive.org.au).

Play Active policy

The Play Active policy contains two key statements on children’s physical activity and sedentary behaviour at ECEC as well as nine age group-specific (infants, toddlers and preschool) recommendations for the amount of physical activity and sedentary time, including screen time, children should have while attending ECEC.16 To support services to meet the Play Active policy physical activity and sedentary behaviour recommendations, there are 25 recommended practices they can implement. These practices are specific to management, directors and educators (14 practices), parent and carer engagement (5 practices), the ECEC physical environment (4 practices) and monitoring and review of their Play Active policy (2 practices).16

Play Active policy implementation support strategies

Implementation support is delivered to ECEC service directors via the Play Active website.34 This involves: (1) Registration and self-assessment of current physical activity practices in a service; (2) Tailoring of the Play Active policy based on the self-assessment in step (1); (3) Professional development; (4) Becoming a member and (5) Accessing member benefits and inviting educators to complete Play Active professional development. Implementation support is also delivered to ECEC service educators via the Play Active website and involves: (1) Registration and short survey about children’s physical activity levels at their service; (2) Professional development (same as the Directors) and (3) Inviting their director to complete the five steps to becoming a Play Active member (figure 2).

Play Active intervention website director 5-step and educator 3-step process.

Member benefits include a printed membership pack containing the service’s tailored Play Active policy, a Resource Guide, membership certificate, posters, outdoor sign, parent flyers and stickers. The Play Active Resource Guide provides practical tips and activity suggestions of how to implement each of the 25 practices in the Play Activity policy.23 This includes the Energetic Play assessment tool which is a brief monitoring tool for educators to monitor children’s energetic play at ECEC. It was based on an existing tool,40 adapted for the Australian ECEC context and has excellent inter-rater reliability (intraclass correlation (ICC) 0.75). The Play Active website members’ area dashboard contains the membership pack materials electronically as well as additional evidence-based resources. Other implementation support strategies include tailored automated prompts (email/short message service (SMS)) sent for actions (eg, registering with Play Active) and in-action (eg, to complete professional development); feedback on policy practice implementation level via 12-month and 24-month self-assessment surveys; monthly e-alert emails specific to each service’s tailored Play Active policy; community of practice strategies such as webinars, in-person presentations, sharing of testimonials via the website, social media and quarterly e-newsletters; membership renewal after 2 years; and branding and partnerships to increase demand (eg, communication plan, stakeholder engagement plan, marketing campaign, etc).

Data collection procedure and outcome measures

Participant characteristics

Demographic information will be collected from directors and educators at baseline and will include age, gender, education, position title, months/years of experience working in their current ECEC service and months/years of experience working in the ECEC sector. In addition, educators will be asked the name of the room they work in, the age/s of children in the room, and the number of hours per week usually spent working in the room.

Primary outcome

Aim 1: implementation (Play Active policy practice implementation)

Change in ECEC staff adoption of the practices in the Play Active policy (n=25) will be assessed using a director-reported online survey administered via the Play Active website (described as the Play Active website self-assessment step 1) at baseline, and 12 and 24 months. These items will be based on those used in the prior Play Active pragmatic cluster randomised trial23 to allow comparison between the current scale-up trial and previous trial. These items are drawn from existing validated instruments including the Nutrition and Physical Activity Self-Assessment for Child Care41 and Environment and Policy Assessment and Observation–Self Report tool,42 which have been modified for the Australian ECEC context37 and shown to have acceptable test–retest reliability.43 18 items are assessed on a 6-point Likert scale from never (1) to always (6) with the remainder using categorical response options (ie, five items: yes/no; outdoor play item: 0 times/day to five or more times/day; professional development item: never to more than once/year; outdoor open space item: no space to space for running games).

Secondary outcomes

Aim 2: effectiveness (children’s physical activity)

The secondary aim will assess if Play Active policy practice adoption is associated with improvements in children’s physical activity, measured in two ways:

Changes in the educator-reported frequency and duration of ‘usual’ daily physical activity provided to children will be assessed through an online survey administered via the Play Active website at baseline, 12 months and 24 months. The items will be drawn from established validated instruments4143 and include those items previously used in the Play Active pragmatic cluster randomised trial.23 The nine items align with the physical activity-related recommendations outlined in the Play Active policy. These include daily time provided for outdoor and indoor physical activity, educator-led physical activity, energetic play, tummy time (infants only—two items), and sedentary behaviours, including screen time (three items).

Changes in children’s daily physical activity at ECEC will be objectively measured using accelerometry in a subsample of 30 services (n=10 each in Western Australia, Queensland and South Australia). Consenting children will wear an ActiGraph GT3X+accelerometer on the right hip for seven consecutive days. Raw accelerometer data will be downloaded and processed into physical activity outcomes using a validated random forest classification model developed for preschool children.44 Physical activity outcomes will include the daily time engaged in total physical activity, energetic play, walking, running, other moderate-vigorous activities and games, light intensity activities and games and sedentary behaviour. Physical activity outcomes will be calculated for the time children spend at ECEC as well as for the entire day. Data validation will be based on at least 3 weekdays (including at least 1 day at childcare with wear time of at least 75% of attendance) and 1 weekend day with a minimum 8 hours wear time per day.4547 Non-wear periods will be identified by summing the time periods in which the SD of the accelerometer signal vector magnitude is <13 mg for ≥30 consecutive minutes.48

Aim 3: sustainability

Sustainability will be assessed in two ways. The first being the number of ECEC services that renew their membership at 24 months after baseline (defined as when a service becomes a Play Active member). Second, the sustainability of the Play Active policy practice implementation will be assessed using the primary outcome measure at 30 months after baseline, in services that renew their membership at 24 months.

Aim 4: dissemination

Dissemination will be assessed using Play Active website analytics and include the number of ECEC services registering for Play Active, becoming Play Active members. To identify the most effective dissemination strategy, a one-item pop-up survey on the Play Active website asking which dissemination strategy led to an ECEC service registering (eg, launch event, e-flyer, social media, other) will be assessed at baseline.

Aim 5: cost-effectiveness

The effectiveness measure will be educator-reported child physical activity to enable comparison to other physical activity interventions. Specifically, the outcome will be additional minutes of children’s ECEC-based daily physical activity at 12-month and 24-month follow-up, from baseline. Resource use associated with the development, dissemination and implementation of the intervention, including ECEC time, Project Officer time and other relevant resources, will be measured and valued using appropriate unit costs provided by the Play Active Partner Advisory Group.

Aim 6: process evaluation

Reach, adoption and engagement: Play Active website analytics will be used to identify the number and geographic spread of ECEC services (1) registering at least one staff member (reach); (2) purchasing membership (adoption); (3) using the website: unique sessions, page views, average session duration and resource downloads (engagement) and (4) response to action and inaction prompts (engagement). These data will be collated and summarised on a monthly basis.

Acceptability, appropriateness and feasibility: At 6 months after baseline, ECEC staff self-reported acceptability, appropriateness and feasibility will be assessed via an online survey on the Play Active website using an existing 12-item valid and reliable questionnaire.49

Implementation facilitators and barriers: Reflections on implementing Play Active (including any facilitators or barriers experienced) will be discussed through one-on-one semistructured interviews with a subsample of ECEC service directors (n=20) and educators (n=20). Participants will be invited to take part in the interviews 18 months after their ECEC service has become a Play Active member. Interviews will be audio recorded to enable data to be transcribed verbatim and thematically coded. Interviews will be offered in person at the ECEC service or online.

Public involvement

A Play Active Partner Advisory Group and Consumer Reference Group have been previously established and are critical to the scale-up and feasibility of delivering Play Active. The project partners and stakeholders include ECEC service providers (Goodstart Early Learning, YMCA, SonasEarly Learning & Care, Sagewood Early Learning), play-based organisations (Nature Play Western Australia, Play Australia, Play Matters Collective), health promotion organisations (National Heart Foundation, Cancer Council Western Australia) and government departments (Western Australian Department of Health, Western Australian Department of Creative Industries, Tourism and Sport, Health and Wellbeing Queensland). We also have the support of the peak ECEC professional associations (Australian Childcare Alliance, Early Childhood Australia). The Consumer Reference Group is composed of 13 parents and caregivers who provide valuable input to the project on a quarterly basis. The group was established in 2019 to inform the development of the Play Active programme. Two consumers also contribute to our quarterly Partner Advisory Group meetings. Input from consumers has been sought through more than 15 meetings across every stage of the programme of research, from early design (2019), codevelopment (2020), pilot trial delivery (2021), scale-up (2022-) and translation and dissemination of research findings (ongoing).

Sample size and statistical analysis

All ECEC services in Western Australia (n=776), Queensland (n=1744) and South Australia (n=445) will be invited to participate. The aim will be to recruit a minimum of 25% of services across the three Australian states (n=741). A conservative uptake of 10% of services (n=296) will provide more than 80% power to show a minimum one-unit service level change in service policy practice adoption (ie, moving from not implemented to implemented), adjusting for service-level confounders, with a two-tailed alpha of 0.05. As a practical example, increasing outdoor time by 30 min per day is associated with 13 min more physical activity per day in children attending ECEC.24 50

For the accelerometry substudy, a total of 30 services will be recruited, with a minimum of 12–15 participating children from each service. A minimum of 120 participating children per state (total of 360 children) will provide more than 80% power to detect a difference of 15 min per day (SD=49) between baseline and 12-month follow-up. If the minimum participating children per service target cannot be met, more services will be recruited to meet the child-level sample required. We will analyse change in children’s device measured daily minutes of total physical activity and energetic play at ECEC. Generalised linear mixed effects models will be used with fixed effects for sociodemographics and time and random effects for individual children nested within services.

To examine the representativeness of the study sample, service-level characteristics will be compared with the full sampling frame of Western Australian, Queensland and South Australian ECEC services at the time of recruitment using data available from Australian Children’s Education and Care Quality Authority.39 The primary outcome will be pre-to-post change in service-level physical activity practice adoption at 24 months. Change in the total number of ‘high impact–low effort’16 policy practices implemented (score out of 7), change in the total count of policy practices and proportion of policy practices (based on those selected by a service in their tailored policy) implemented as well as the level of implementation of each of the 25 policy practices, at 12 and 24 months will be examined. Generalised linear (mixed effects) models will be used to analyse the implementation of policy practices pre–post (12 and 24 months) intervention. Service-level predictors (eg, service socioeconomic status) of the pre–post level of implementation of policy practices will also be examined. The main secondary outcome will be pre-to-post change in educator-reported child physical activity levels at 24 months. Generalised linear (mixed effects) models will include service level covariates and random effects for individual educators nested within services.

A comprehensive cost-effectiveness analysis will be undertaken at the level of the ECEC service. Costs will be adjusted for inflation, and both outcomes and costs will be discounted as per best practice. The effectiveness and cost measures will be used to generate incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER) (95% CIs), with the main ICER being the cost per additional minute of physical activity per day at ECEC at 12-month and 24-month follow-up.

Process outcomes will be analysed using descriptives (eg, response rates for each dissemination strategy, Play Active intervention website analytics, % services deeming Play Active appropriate, feasible and acceptable). Qualitative data will be transcribed verbatim and thematically coded.51 All quantitative data preparation and analyses will be carried out using SAS V.9.4 or above and Stata V.18 or above, and qualitative analysis using NVivo V.15 or above.

Ethics and dissemination

Ethics approval has been provided by The University of Western Australia Human Research Ethics Committee (HREC) (2023/ET000187), the University of Queensland HREC (2024/HE000076) and the University of South Australia HREC (206023). Informed active consent will be required from participating directors and educators (online supplemental file: model consent form). Parents will provide informed written consent for themselves and their child’s participation in the accelerometer substudy. At the ECEC service data collection visit, research staff will provide each child with a brief and simple description of the devices (accelerometers), will answer any questions the child may have and confirm they are willing to wear the device. All research staff working directly with children will have a Working with Children Check as per Western Australian legislative requirements. All participants’ responses and data will be strictly confidential. Only aggregated data will be used to describe research findings. All data will be retained at completion of the study for at least 7 years and kept as per The University of Western Australia requirements for data retention and disposal.

Findings will be published in international peer-reviewed journals. The findings will be shared with stakeholders, collaborators, policy-makers and practitioners working in the ECEC sector and families. The research team will work closely with the ECEC sector to inform the Australian National Childcare Regulations, Quality Framework for Early Childhood Education and Care, the Australian Early Years Learning Framework as well as national 24-Hour Movement Guidelines for the Early Years. Findings will also be disseminated via local, national and international conference presentations and invited talks. As part of the Play Active implementation support strategies, progress reports will be provided to services at 12-month and 24-month follow-up. A section on the Play Active website will include resources and information for parents to support their children’s physical activity behaviour at home.

Discussion

ECEC services are an ideal setting to promote physical activity in the early years.10 Play Active is a scalable policy intervention to increase the physical activity levels of young children attending ECEC.16 23 Play Active provides an evidence-informed physical activity policy template for ECEC services accompanied by implementation support strategies.16 This study addresses a significant gap in ECEC physical activity policy and practice implementation by assessing both the effectiveness and scalability of Play Active’s implementation support strategies in supporting ECEC services to adopt the practices included in the Play Active policy.

A key strength of Play Active is that it has been codeveloped with partners and stakeholders including ECEC service providers, professional associations and play-based organisations, health promotion organisations, academic partners and government departments and a consumer reference group of parents with children attending ECEC.16 22 The ongoing involvement of these groups throughout the current scale-up trial is critical to informing the delivery and dissemination of Play Active to ECEC services across Australia and translating findings into policy and practice. Furthermore, the ecological hybrid type III effectiveness-implementation trial design and focus on intervention sustainability are key methodological strengths of this study, combining the evaluation of the effectiveness of Play Active and its implementation support strategies.

While the previous Play Active pragmatic cluster randomised trial had positive findings,25 there is a need to further measure the implementation, effectiveness as well as the cost-effectiveness of Play Active at scale in the real world to make a population-level impact on young children’s physical activity levels. This innovative ecological hybrid type III effectiveness-implementation trial will generate the first real-world evidence of a scalable physical activity intervention for children attending ECEC services in Australia. Findings from the trial will provide actionable evidence to inform an Australia-wide delivery model to ensure all young Australian children have an active and healthy start to life.

Role of Oxygen Saturation Index as a Noninvasive Surrogate Marker of Oxygenation in Intubated Critically Ill Adult Patients: A Prospective Observational Study
Authors: Abraham Koshy, George Kurian, Jubin Jacob, David Vincent, Shoma V Rao, K Subramani, J Grace Rebekah
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and aimThis study aims to compare the oxygenation saturation index (OSI), a noninvasive marker, with commonly used invasive markers of oxygenation [PaO2, PaO2 to FiO2 (P/F) ratio, oxygenation index (OI)] in critically ill patients requiring invasive mechanical ventilatory support after 48 hours of admission in a surgical intensive care unit (ICU).Patients and methodsA total of 91 intubated patients aged between 18 and 75 years admitted to the surgical ICU with preexisting or new onset respiratory failure were recruited as per predefined exclusion and inclusion criteria after being on the ventilator for 48 hours. Patients were enrolled in the study for 7 days or till extubation, tracheostomy, or death. The OSI was compared with other invasive and noninvasive markers to assess correlation.ResultsStrong correlation of OSI with P/F ratio, SpO2/FiO2 (S/F) ratio, and OI individually was noted up to 7 days post 48 hours of intubation in critically ill patients, while it correlated with SpO2 on days 2, 3, 4, and 5 and PaO2 on days 2, 4, and 5, respectively. Correlation after natural logarithmic transformation was strongest for OI (r = 0.833), followed by S/F ratio (r = 0.686), P/F ratio (r = 0.409), SpO2 (r = 0.229), and weakest for PaO2 (r = 0.045).ConclusionOxygen saturation index is a valid noninvasive marker of oxygenation in critically ill intubated patients and correlates well with invasive markers of oxygenation.How to cite this articleKoshy A, Kurian G, Jacob J, Vincent D, Rao SV, Subramani K, et al. Highlights

This study shows OSI is a valid noninvasive marker of oxygenation status in intubated critically ill patients admitted to a surgical ICU. Even after 48 hours of intubation, OSI correlated strongly on all days individually with S/F ratio, P/F ratio, and OI up to 7 days.

Introduction

Assessment of oxygenation status in intubated critically ill patients is important in assessing resolution of lung injury, optimization of mechanical ventilation, planning for weaning and extubation, and likely prognosis.1,2 While invasive markers of oxygenation such as PaO2, PaO2 to FiO2 ratio (P/F ratio), and oxygenation index (OI) are commonly used for this purpose, they require invasive arterial blood gas (ABG) sampling, use of specialized equipment, and are often costly.

Noninvasive markers such as SpO2, SpO2 to FiO2 ratio (S/F ratio), and Oxygen saturation index (OSI) are growing in relevance, especially in resource-limited settings in view of their cost-effectiveness, ease of use, and utility of continuous monitoring at the bedside.3 Oxygen saturation index also has the added advantage of incorporating mean airway pressure (MAP) into its calculation, the latter having been shown to correlate with arterial oxygenation, alveolar ventilation, hemodynamic performance, and barotrauma.4

This study aims to compare the OSI with commonly used invasive markers of oxygenation (PaO2, P/F ratio, OI) in critically ill patients requiring invasive mechanical ventilatory support after 48 hours of intensive care unit (ICU) admission.

Patients and Methods

Study Design and Setting

This was a prospective observational study conducted in a surgical ICU after obtaining institutional ethical clearance (IRB number 14837 dated 07/09/22).

Participants

Inclusion criteria included all intubated patients aged between 18 and 75 years admitted to the surgical ICU on invasive mechanical ventilation for 48 hours or more. Exclusion criteria included patients aged less than 18 years, non-consenting individuals, death or discharge within 48 hours of admission, patients intubated and treated at other centers for more than 48 hours, pregnant mothers, patients with unreliable pulse oximetry such as patients in severe shock with cold clammy peripheries, hypothermia (<95°F), post-cardiac surgery, and patients with severe congenital heart disease or congestive cardiac failure. Patients were enrolled in the study for a maximum 7 days or till extubation, tracheostomy, and death or discharge from ICU.

Study Period

September 2022 to August 2023.

Variables

Data regarding age, gender, comorbidities, clinical features, likely cause of ICU admission, laboratory data, baseline sequential organ failure assessment (SOFA) score, length of ICU stay, need for inotropic requirement, duration of mechanical ventilation, and in-ICU mortality were collected. A single valid ABG reading per patient per day at a predefined time (12.00 am), along with corresponding ventilator parameters like positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEP), MAP, and SpO2, was measured.

PaO2, FiO2, and P/F ratio were noted from the ABG report. Oxygenation index was calculated as (MAP × FiO2 × 100) ÷ PaO2 and OSI as (MAP × FiO2 × 100) ÷ SpO2.

Sample Size Calculation

With reference to a study by Sonali Vadi,5 the correlation between P/F ratio and OSI was found to be −0.60. Assuming a population correlation coefficient of −0.50 for power at 80% and alpha error at 5% for a two-sided test, the study needed at least 91 subjects requiring invasive mechanical ventilatory support.

Statistical Analysis

The data analysis was performed using Statistical Package for Social Sciences (SPSS) software Version 21.0 (Armonk, NY: IBM Corp). For continuous data, the descriptive statistics such as mean and SD were used, and for non-normally distributed interval data and ordinal data, the median [interquartile range (IQR)] will be presented. The number of patients and percentage will be presented for categorical data. Simple linear regression was reported to quantify the relation. Two independent sample t-tests were used to report the mean difference between the parameters. A generalized linear model with an unstructured correlation matrix was used to report the trend over days 1 to 7. p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Correlation was reported using Pearson's correlation coefficient for OSI, OI, PF ratio, SF ratio, PaO2, and SpO2.

Discussion

Oxygenation status of intubated patients with respiratory failure has been shown to predict outcomes.6 While measures such as PaO2 to FiO2 ratio and Murray Lung Injury Score have traditionally been used in both clinical practice and clinical trials, these offer limited prognostic information or require ABG analysis, highlighting the need for simple, noninvasive predictive measures.7 Mean airway pressure, an important factor in oxygenation, is not utilized in these scores.2 Degree of hypoxemia and ICU mortality were found to have a significant correlation in patients with ARDS in a number of studies.8–10

Oxygenation index, another alternative measure, has evolved as an important marker of the severity of hypoxemia and the extent of lung injury in pediatric critical care11 and has been shown to be an independent risk factor for mortality in adults.12 Oxygenation index, however, is invasive and requires ABG measurements. Oxygenation saturation index, measured as OSI = (MAP × FiO2 × 100) ÷ SpO2, was shown to be as effective as the OI in predicting mortality in pediatric patients.13 This noninvasive measure was also found to be a reliable surrogate for OI in adult patients with ARDS and could predict clinical outcomes similar to OI in the initial stages of ARDS.14,15

Previous studies have shown that noninvasive markers of oxygenation, like S/F ratio, correlated well with P/F ratio irrespective of mode of oxygen delivery.16,17 Chen et al. showed that clinical characteristics and outcomes were similar irrespective of ARDS diagnosis by S/F or P/F ratio.18 Another study by Vadi S5 showed a strong correlation between P/F and OI and OI and OSI in a composite group of intubated patients.

Our study shows that OSI did not correlate individually well with SpO2 and PaO2 except on certain days. OSI correlates well individually with S/F ratio, P/F ratio, and OI on all days up to 7 days after 48 hours of initiation of mechanical ventilation. In addition, while mean OSI correlated well with all other oxygenation parameters, the correlation was strongest for OI and weakest for PaO2.

This study suggests that OSI has a good correlation with OI, S/F ratio, and P/F ratio and could be a proxy for oxygenation status of intubated patients, even for postoperative surgical and trauma patients and might obviate the need for frequent ABG analysis.

There were a few limitations to this study. In this prospective observational study, we enrolled patients with a good pulse oximetry waveform, and hence, conditions that limit such a requirement as severe shock, hypothermia, methemoglobinemia, and nail anomalies were excluded. We also excluded patients with severe congenital heart disease, congestive cardiac failure, and post-cardiac surgery. A majority of patients in our study (58.2%) did not satisfy ARDS criteria.

Mean duration of stay for our patients in the ICU post recruitment was 2.98. The mean baseline SOFA score was 8.74 which suggests a lesser severity of critical illness in our study population. Other parameters, such as ventilator mode, pH, and PaCO2, were not considered, which may influence oxygenation, and further studies are needed to elucidate their relationship.

Conclusion

There was a strong correlation of OSI with P/F ratio, S/F ratio, and OI up to 7 days post 48 hours of intubation in critically ill patients in this study. Oxygen saturation index is a valid noninvasive marker of oxygenation in critically ill intubated patients and can be used as a convenient proxy for commonly used invasive markers.

Brain functional gradient and structure features in adolescent and adult autism spectrum disorders
Authors: Lili Ruan, Guangxiang Chen, Menglin Yao, Cheng Li, Xiu Chen, Hua Luo, Jianghai Ruan, Zhong Zheng, Dechou Zhang, Sicheng Liang, Muhan Lü
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractUnderstanding how function and structure are organized and their coupling with clinical traits in individuals with autism spectrum disorder (ASD) is a primary goal in network neuroscience research for ASD. Atypical brain functional networks and structures in individuals with ASD have been reported, but whether these associations show heterogeneous hierarchy modeling in adolescents and adults with ASD remains to be clarified. In this study, 176 adolescent and 74 adult participants with ASD without medication or comorbidities and sex, age matched healthy controls (HCs) from 19 research groups from the openly shared Autism Brain Imaging Data Exchange II database were included. To investigate the relationship between the functional gradient, structural changes, and clinical symptoms of brain networks in adolescents and adults with ASD, functional gradient and voxel‐based morphometry (VBM) analyses based on 1000 parcels defined by Schaefer mapped to Yeo's seven‐network atlas were performed. Pearson's correlation was calculated between the gradient scores, gray volume and density, and clinical traits. The subsystem‐level analysis showed that the second gradient scores of the default mode networks and frontoparietal network in patients with ASD were relatively compressed compared to adolescent HCs. Adult patients with ASD showed an overall compression gradient of 1 in the ventral attention networks. In addition, the gray density and volumes of the subnetworks showed no significant differences between the ASD and HC groups at the adolescent stage. However, adults with ASD showed decreased gray density in the limbic network. Moreover, numerous functional gradient parameters, but not VBM parameters, in adolescents with ASD were considerably correlated with clinical traits in contrast to those in adults with ASD. Our findings proved that the atypical changes in adolescent ASD mainly involve the brain functional network, while in adult ASD, the changes are more related to brain structure, including gray density and volume. These changes in functional gradients or structures are markedly correlated with clinical traits in patients with ASD. Our study provides a novel understanding of the pathophysiology of the structure–function hierarchy in ASD.

INTRODUCTION

Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) is a series of complex neurodevelopmental disorders that occur early in the developmental period. Its clinical manifestations include weak social communication skills and restricted and repetitive patterns of behavior, interests, and activities (Hodges et al., 2020; C. Lord et al., 2018). The prevalence of autism has rapidly increased in recent years, and the morbidity of autism has doubled since 2000, with approximately one in 44 children worldwide suffering from ASD by 2018 (Chi et al., 2022; Hyman et al., 2020).

The brain structure abnormalities in ASD have been reported in previous studies (Arunachalam Chandran et al., 2021; Nickl‐Jockschat et al., 2012). Numerous brain regions such as the lateral occipital lobe, pericentral region, medial temporal lobe, orbitofrontal cortex, and lingual gyrus, showed structural disturbances in ASD (Arunachalam Chandran et al., 2021). Voxel‐based morphometry (VBM) meta‐analyses have revealed increased bilateral temporal and right dorsolateral prefrontal gray matter volumes in patients with ASD (Lukito et al., 2020). Structural abnormalities may be related to clinical dysfunctions (F. Sun et al., 2022). Functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) studies have shown reduced functional connectivity (FC) between social brain regions in patients with ASD, which is associated with lower social communication ability (W. Sato & Uono, 2019). Gray matter thickness is associated with language function in school‐aged children with ASD (Arutiunian et al., 2023). FC has been used to understand the dynamics of temporal correlations between brain areas (Edde et al., 2021). Many studies have focused on changes in FC in ASD (Mohammad‐Rezazadeh et al., 2016) and found structural and functional abnormalities in both cortical and subcortical brain regions (Maximo & Kana, 2019; van Rooij et al., 2018; Xu et al., 2022). Variations in the structural brain network occur in the form of long‐distance functional hypoconnectivity, particularly between the frontal and posterior cortical regions, and local hyperconnectivity, mainly within the posterior regions, including the primary sensory cortices (Cai et al., 2021; Martínez et al., 2020). In addition, abnormal FC of resting‐state networks between distinct cortical regions, especially the default mode networks (DMNs), has been observed in ASD using functional MRI (fMRI) (Jia et al., 2022; J.‐W. Sun et al., 2021). Accumulating evidence of an abnormal connectome in ASD provides a multilayered understanding of the functional hierarchy of ASD. FC is usually a measurement of time processes between different brain regions based on Pearson's correlation. FC changes in the resting state of ASD subtypes may identify specific changes in neural network function in each subtype (Ren et al., 2023). However, the interactions among brain function, structure, and clinical traits in ASD remain unclear. In this study, we used gradient analysis, which not only summarizes the correlation of the network but also reflects the topological distance between different networks at the network level (Hong et al., 2019).

Macroscopic brain changes associated with neurological disorders have attracted the attention of researchers (Jiang et al., 2023). Gradient analysis is an approach to describe the macroscale brain structural or functional organization that uses a nonlinear dimensional reduction method to identify low‐dimensional gradients to characterize the spatial distribution of the high‐dimensional FC matrix (Vos de Wael et al., 2020). Gradient analysis projects information into a nonlinear diffusion space and identifies the major topological changes that describe changes in cortical connectivity (Hong et al., 2019). This method measures the similarity in the FC profiles of each pair of nodes, clarifies the respective locations of related networks, and captures a series of functions from perception and action to more abstract cognitive functions (Lanzoni et al., 2020; Margulies et al., 2016; Vos de Wael et al., 2020). The functional gradient offers an organized spatial framework for connecting multiple large‐scale networks and functions in different domains. It has been reported that connectome gradient mapping showed that both healthy controls (HCs) and individuals with ASD had an axis of connectivity gradient variations with low‐level sensory systems and transmodal DMN on two opposite ends and remaining networks in‐between (Hong et al., 2019).

The question of how the functional gradient and structure of the brain network are altered in ASD is associated with clinical symptoms, and their interactions have recently been a matter of interest. Recent research showed that gradient analysis could guide a supervised learning algorithm to predict symptom severity in individuals with ASD (Choi et al., 2022; Hong et al., 2019). Abnormalities in the structure play an important role in the clinical features of ASD and can predict the severity of social communication deficits and repetitive and stereotypic behaviors in young children with ASD (Bloomer et al., 2022; Duan et al., 2020; He et al., 2021). Increasing evidence suggests that the clinical severity of ASD is related to hyperconnectivity between unimodal seeds and DMN regions and hypoconnectivity between unimodal sensory systems and multimodal integration regions (X. Guo et al., 2020; Martínez et al., 2020). However, the interactions among changes in brain network functional gradient, structure, and symptoms in ASD at different ages remain to be clarified (Floris & Howells, 2018; Rafiee et al., 2022; Zhang & Roeyers, 2019).

The present study aimed to explore the potential interactions between changes in the brain network, functional gradient, structure, and clinical traits in adolescents and adults with ASD. To this end, we applied gradient analysis on resting‐state fMRI data and VBM analysis on T1‐weighted images to extract the characteristics of functional gradient and grey volume and density in ASD. Additionally, we performed correlation analyses between the extracted gradient values of regions of interest (ROIs) and Autism Diagnostic Observation Schedule (ADOS) scores, as well as between structural parameters obtained from VBM analysis and clinical traits.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Participants

In this study, we used 19 independent datasets from the Autism Brain Imaging Data Exchange II dataset (ABIDE II; http://fcon_1000.projects.nitrc.org/indi/abide/abide_II.html) (Di Martino et al., 2017). The detailed inclusion criteria for participants with ASD in different centers had some differences; different clinical scales were used. The main scales used for ASD inclusion at different sites are presented in Supplementary Table 1. The exclusion criteria for ASD at the 19 sites included low intelligence quotient, history of psychiatric or neurological disorders, head trauma, and contraindications for MRI scanners. A total of 1114 volunteers participated in the ABIDE II dataset. In this study, participants with ASD without comorbidities and medications were divided into an adolescent group (aged less than or equal to 18 years, n = 176) and an adult group (aged more than 18 years and less than or equal to 35 years, n = 74). Age‐ and sex‐matched healthy participants were selected as controls (Table 1). The clinical severity of ASD was scored using the ADOS‐Generic (ADOS‐G) (Janvier et al., 2022; Catherine Lord et al., 2000) where available.

Data preprocessing

Resting‐state neuroimaging data preprocessing was performed using the MATLAB toolbox Data Processing & Analysis for Brain Imaging (DPABI, v6.1) (C. G. Yan et al., 2016) and the Statistical Parametric Mapping (SPM12, v7771, Wellcome Centre for Human Neuroimaging, UK) software package. Standardized pipeline preprocessing with the corresponding parameters was similar to that used in our previous studies (Li et al., 2021; C.‐G. Yan et al., 2019). The preprocessing included the following steps. The first four volumes were discarded, and slice timing correction was performed. Subsequently, the individual images of the time series were realigned, and the individual T1 weighted images were co‐registered to the mean functional image with a 6 degrees of freedom linear transformation. The grey matter, white matter, and cerebrospinal fluid were then segmented. Individual images in the MNI space were created using the Diffeomorphic Anatomical Registration Through Exponentiated Lie Algebra method (Ashburner, 2007). Head motion effects were controlled using the Friston 24 parameter model (Friston et al., 2000). The mean Jenkinson framewise displacement (FD) served as a covariate in later statistics involving the functional image parameters. Scrubbing (interpolated volume with an FD >0.2 mm) was performed. White matter and cerebrospinal fluid signals and linear drift were regressed. Finally, we performed temporal band‐pass filtering (0.01–0.1 Hz) and smoothing (FWHM = 4 mm) across time series.

Gradient analysis

Following preprocessing, we constructed the FC matrix by calculating the Pearson's correlation of each pair of nodes for each participant (a prior parcellation of 1000 brain regions) (Yang et al., 2020). The parcellation regions corresponded to Yeo's seven functional systems assignments, including the visual networks (VN), somatomotor networks (SMN), dorsal attention networks (DAN), ventral attention networks (VAN), frontoparietal (FP) networks, limbic networks, and DMNs (Schaefer et al., 2018; Yeo et al., 2011). Yeo's seven functional networks were the result of a comprehensive analysis of networks within the human cerebral cortex using intrinsic FC MRI, which showed that serial hierarchical processing exists in the human sensory cortex and that sensory and motor cortices exhibit topographically specific FC (Yeo et al., 2011). For each individual, we obtained an FC matrix (with 1000 × 1000 entries) and averaged all individual FC matrices as group‐level FC matrices. Following previous studies (Hong et al., 2019; Lanzoni et al., 2020), group‐level and individual FC matrices were z‐transformed and thresholded by retaining the top 10% of the weighted connections per row. We then calculated the cosine similarity between each pair of the threshold connectivity profiles and obtained a cosine similarity matrix. Finally, we applied diffusion map embedding (Lanzoni et al., 2020), a nonlinear dimensionality reduction algorithm, to the cosine similarity matrix to identify group‐level and individual connectome gradient components (Huo et al., 2022). After an estimate of the group horizontal gradient component template was generated based on the average connectivity matrix, a Procrustes rotation was performed to align the components of each individual with the template (Hong et al., 2019). A gradient analysis was performed using the open‐source toolbox BrainSpace (Vos de Wael et al., 2020) (MATLAB version).

VBM analysis

VBM analysis was performed by standardizing the T1‐weighted MRI images of each subject in a stereoscopic space. Subsequently, the images of each subject were automatically divided into the gray matter, white matter, and cerebrospinal fluid compartments. The volume and density of the gray matter in each brain region were extracted using the 1000 nodes defined by Schaefer (Arutiunian et al., 2023). Yeo's seven‐network atlas was used to quantitatively calculate the volume and density of gray matter in each subnetwork to quantitatively analyze the possible abnormalities in brain structure (Matsuda, 2016). These steps were performed using the DPABI toolbox (C. G. Yan et al., 2016).

Associations between brain network and clinical traits

We hypothesized that the brain network depicted by the functional gradient and structural parameters was associated with the clinical traits described by ADOS scores. To explore the associations between clinical severity characteristics and brain functional gradients, a correlation analysis between the extracted gradient values of the ROIs and ADOS scores was performed. Moreover, we calculated Pearson's correlation to determine the relationship between the structural parameters obtained from the VBM analysis and clinical traits.

Statistical analysis

Before conducting inter‐group comparisons or correlation analyses, a generalized linear model (GLM) was used to exclude the effects of covariates on the results. In the GLM setting, when the dependent variable was one of the functional gradient scores of the seven subnetworks, the independent variables were age, sex, site of data collection, and mean Jenkinson FD. When the dependent variable was one of the VBM parameters (gray density and volume) of the seven subnetworks, the independent variables were age, sex, site of data collection, and individual whole brain volume. The gradient and VBM values after the GLM were used in subsequent statistics.

Two 2‐sample t test were used to compare the corresponding parameter differences between the ASD and HC groups or subgroups within the ASD group. The relationships between the functional gradient, gray density, gray volume, and ADOS scores were analyzed using Pearson's correlation analyses. False discovery rate (FDR) correction was used to control for false positives in the multiple variable comparisons and correlation analyses. The FDR adjusted p values were considered significant at p < .05. All the tests were conducted using MATLAB (R2014a, MathWorks Inc.).

Sample composition

We included adolescent (aged less than or equal to 18 years, n = 176) and adult (aged more than 18 years and less than or equal to 35 years, n = 74) participants with ASD. Participants with ASD older than 35 years were not included in further analysis because of the limited sample size (n = 20). A total of 263 participants with ASD (235 adolescents and 25 adults) taking medication and/or having comorbidities were included in the subgroup analysis. Detailed demographics of the participants are shown in Table 1 and Supplementary Figure S1.

Structural changes in subnetworks of ASD

The gray density and volumes of Yeo's seven networks were extracted. Compared with that in the HC group, the gray matter density of the limbic networks decreased in the adult ASD group (p < .01, t = −2.64, d = −0.44) (Supplementary Figure S3). However, structural changes have not been observed in adolescents with ASD.

Correlations between clinical traits and functional gradients

Pearson's correlation analysis was performed between ADOS scores and mean gradient values to examine the relationship between alterations in the functional gradient and clinical traits. In adolescent participants with ASD, the principal and second gradient values of the VAN were positively correlated with the ADOS stereotyped behavior scores (r = .284, p < .05; r = .281, p < .05). However, the correlations between the ADOS behavior scores and other networks, such as the DMN, SMN, DAN, FP, and VN, were negative (Figure 4).

Subgroup analyses

Disease severity, comorbidities, and medications may affect functional gradients and brain structures. We found that participants with more severe ASD (stereotyped behavior scores of ADOS ≥3) showed a relative compression gradient in DAN in gradient 1 as well as in DAN and SMN in gradient 2 compared to those in participants with ASD with lower behavior scores (less than 3) (p < .05). In addition, compared to adolescents with ASD, adults with ASD showed overall decreased gray density in the seven subnetworks and decreased gray volume in the SMN and DAN (p < .05) (Figure 8). Interestingly, the ASD subgroups divided according to comorbidity or medication use showed no significant differences. In addition, the male and female ASD groups showed no significant differences in the gradient scores of the subnetworks.

DISCUSSION

Studies have explored the relationship between functional gradients and clinical symptoms in ASD (Hong et al., 2019; Zhang & Roeyers, 2019); however, few have focused on the gradient, structure, and clinical features of both adolescent and adult ASD. In the present study, we applied functional gradient and VBM analysis to detect changes in the brain network functional gradient and structure in adolescent and adult individuals with ASD and explored the connections between the variation and clinical traits. The 1000 parcels defined by Schaefer and projected to Yeo's seven‐network atlas were used as brain regions to investigate the connections among the alterations in the brain network functional gradient, structure, and clinical traits in adolescents and adults with ASD. Furthermore, a correlation analysis was employed to demonstrate heterogeneous interactions between the brain network functional gradient, structure, and clinical traits in adolescents and adults with ASD. Our results suggest that changes in the functional gradient and structure of the brain network are closely associated with the functional gradient, structural parameters of the brain, and clinical traits. Moreover, changes in the adolescent and adult groups were mainly characterized by a transition from functional to structural abnormalities. These findings provide systematic insights into the functional organization of the brain and a novel understanding of the pathophysiology involving the structure–function hierarchy in ASD.

We noted heterogeneous interactions between functional gradients, structure of brain networks, and clinical features of adolescent and adult ASDs. We discovered that the functional gradients of the DMN, SMN, DAN, FP, and VN in adolescent individuals with ASD had a negative correlation with clinical traits, whereas the gradient of the VAN was positive. This suggests that these regions of the seven networks play heterogeneous roles in the development of ASD. Previous studies have shown that adults have reduced gray matter volume, which represents behavioral social malfunctioning in adults with ASD (Wataru Sato et al., 2017). Our research showed that the volume and density of gray matter in the DMN, SMN, DAN, and FP were positively correlated with behavior, and the volumes of the DMN, SMN, and VAN were positively correlated with communication. These findings indicate that structural and functional heterogeneity may play a role in social communication and repetitive behavioral patterns in ASD. Different functions exist in different regions of the networks in relation to the clinical traits. Further studies are required to investigate how these networks show remarkable changes in their heterogeneous interactions with other regions in patients with ASD.

Additionally, the present study suggests a trend in brain changes associated with clinical traits in patients with ASD, evolving from functional abnormalities in adolescents to structural abnormalities in adults. We found that numerous functional gradient parameters involving the gradient scores of the DMN, SMN, DAN, FP, and VN subnetworks were strongly correlated with clinical traits, while gray density and volume of these subnetworks showed no significant correlations with clinical traits in the adolescent participants with ASD. In contrast, in adult ASD, numerous structural parameters from the VBM analysis, but fewer functional gradient parameters, showed significant correlations with clinical traits. Although the dependency of functional activity and structural findings on the age of participants with ASD has been supported by previous studies (Z. Guo et al., 2024; Ortug et al., 2024; Pascual‐Belda et al., 2018), the heterogeneous interactions of functional, structural, and clinical findings between adolescent and adult individuals with ASD are rarely mentioned. Distinct neurodevelopmental trajectories in individuals with ASD across their lifespan are characterized by early brain overgrowth, followed by developmental arrest in late childhood and early adolescence and accelerated neuroatrophy in adulthood (Courchesne et al., 2011). Both whole‐brain functional and structural connectivity exhibit reorganization with age (Betzel et al., 2014). Our findings provide a novel understanding of the functional and structural changes in ASD and may serve as a mechanistic basis for the targeted treatment of ASD.

The present study used functional gradient analysis to characterize the macroscale cortical organization in ASD. The human cerebral cortex has specific functional hierarchies that facilitate information processing and higher‐order cognition (Y. Zhao, Wang, et al., 2022). Researchers have demonstrated the presence of a principal connectivity gradient in the macroscale organization of functional brain networks in healthy adults that plays a crucial role in supporting complex cognitive functions and behaviors (Bethlehem et al., 2020; Xia et al., 2022). Our gradient mapping also revealed changes in the axis of connectivity, with low‐level sensory systems and the cross‐modal DMN at both ends and the remaining networks in the middle, which is in accordance with previous studies (Dong et al., 2021; Hong et al., 2019). However, in adolescent individuals with ASD, a markedly reduced network differentiation, referred to as gradient compression, was identified. This is characterized by a prominent compression in higher‐level systems such as the VN and a less pronounced compression in the sensorimotor system of the cortical hierarchy, compared to the healthy group. In contrast, adult individuals with ASD exhibited the opposite pattern. It has been reported that VN may continue to develop in global connectivity structures from adolescents to adults (Bethlehem et al., 2020; Dong et al., 2021). The occipital lobe is the core of visual information processing and often causes motion perception impairments (J.‐W. Sun et al., 2021; X. X. Zhao, Zhu, et al., 2022). Combined with our recent results, this may indicate an underlying impairment in visual‐motor integration at an early stage of ASD.

Research has confirmed that limbic regions such as the amygdala and hippocampus are functionally related to impaired abilities in ASD, which are correlated with repetitive and stereotyped behavior (Fu et al., 2022; Nees et al., 2022; Thabault et al., 2022). Our results showed that the limbic gray value in the ASD group was lower than that in the adult HC group. The ASD and HC groups in the adolescent stage showed no significant differences in gray density and volumes of the Yeo seven networks. However, conflicting results have been previously reported. Some studies found that individuals with autism have smaller amygdalae than those without autism (Kovacevic et al., 2021). However, other studies did not find any significant differences in volumetric changes in the amygdala and hippocampus compared with healthy volunteers (Haznedar et al., 2000). More importantly, limbic activity was positively correlated with the clinical features of adult ASD, which was not observed in adolescent ASD. Amygdala volume is positively correlated with social network size (Meisner et al., 2022). Combined with our results, this indicates that the lower the limbic gray value of adults is, the lower is their social cognition. Overall, this leads to various behavioral and social deficits. These results highlight the structural and functional differences relevant to understanding autism.

In the subgroup analysis, it was noted that the subgroups of ASD divided by comorbidity or medication showed no significant differences in the brain functional gradient, which means that the targets of the drugs used might not be linked to the seven networks. However, another study indicated that ASD presents with similar or more severe clinical manifestations with comorbidities, which complicate disease treatment and prognosis (Hung et al., 2023). Research also showed that psychiatric medications may affect functional brain networks in ASD (Amritha Harikumar et al., 2021; Linke et al., 2017). According to our results, regulating brain functional networks may be an important treatment mechanism for ASD. Furthermore, we found that participants with more severe ASD (stereotyped behavior scores of ADOS ≥3) showed a relative compression gradient in DAN in gradient 1 as well as in DAN and SMN in gradient 2 compared to those in participants with ASD with lower behavior scores (less than 3). DAN participates in the regulation of goal‐directed, top‐down processing, which is highly correlated with social deficits (J.‐W. Sun et al., 2021). This indicates that social cognition is associated with a lower gradient value of DAN and provides a direction for treating social dysfunction in autism.

A previous study observed that shifting gradients mark the transition from childhood (under 12 years of age) to adolescence (Dong et al., 2021), which may have affected the results of the present gradient analysis. To solve this question, we further divided the subjects into two subgroups of ASDs and HCs: one younger group aged less than or equal to 12 years and one older group aged more than 12 years and less than 18 years. However, we did not observe such gradient shifting in either the HC or ASD group (Supplementary Figure S9), which may be caused by the fact that cortical development was organized hierarchically across different ages, such as from ages 8 to 18, rather than a specific age (Krongold et al., 2017; Sydnor et al., 2023). Therefore, it was reasonable to use the current grouping method in this study.

One limitation of this study was that we obtained a connectome gradient based on 1000 brain regions rather than at the voxel level. Despite the good agreement between the gradient distributions at different spatial scales, a study using a finer resolution is needed. In addition, we did not conduct a longitudinal cohort follow‐up study to validate our findings on medication use.

CONCLUSION

We depicted the variations in primary‐to‐transmodal functional gradient during adolescence and adulthood, and the changes in the functional gradient were remarkably correlated with clinical traits in adolescent ASD. However, the structural alterations occurred primarily in the adult ASD group. In addition, the combination of the functional gradient and structure revealed a greater connection in the adolescent ASD group than in the adult ASD group. This study demonstrates a heterogeneous brain functional gradient and structural changes in adolescent and adult participants with ASD. These changes in functional gradients or structures considerably correlated with clinical traits in patients with ASD. Our study provides a novel understanding of the structure–function hierarchy in ASD, which may help identify the basis of the core characteristics of these disorders.

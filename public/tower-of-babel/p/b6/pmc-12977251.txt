Association between vaccination prescription program and herpes zoster vaccine knowledge, intention, and coverage among residents aged ≥50 in Zhejiang Province: Repeated cross-sectional surveys
Authors: Xiaotong Yan, Xuehai Zhang, Yue Xu, Jinhang Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThe herpes zoster (HZ) vaccine has been available in China for a relatively short period, and vaccination coverage remains low. Zhejiang Province began to implement the vaccination prescription program in 35 counties from May 2024 under the background of integrated medical and preventive healthcare. This study aimed to evaluate the association between this program and changes in HZ vaccine knowledge, intention, and coverage among residents aged ≥50 y through repeated cross-sectional surveys. A multi-stage convenience sampling method was used to recruit participants before and after the intervention. Changes in all variables were assessed using chi-squared exact tests, while a Generalized Estimating Equations (GEE) model with binary logistic link and clustering by community health center was used to identify factors associated with vaccination. Following the program, significant improvements were observed across all HZ knowledge items, and vaccination coverage was 1.93% in the pre-implementation survey and 5.74% in the post-implementation survey. Notably, vaccination intention shifted toward hesitancy, with a decline in both positive and negative intent. In the adjusted GEE model, the survey period after program implementation was associated with higher odds of vaccination (AOR = 2.611, 95% CI: 1.895–3.597), and receiving a doctor’s recommendation was associated with vaccination (AOR = 7.750, 95% CI: 5.747–10.451). The vaccination prescription program was associated with improved HZ vaccine knowledge, higher vaccination coverage, and increased rates of doctor’s recommendation.

Introduction

Herpes zoster (HZ) is a widespread infectious disease caused by the reactivation of the varicella-zoster virus, which remains dormant in the posterior spinal or intracranial ganglia after initial infection. While HZ can affect individuals at any age, its incidence increases significantly with age, and this upward trend is particularly prominent in the population aged 50 y and above.1 Globally, the incidence of HZ in the general population is approximately 3–5 cases per 1,000 person-years, rising to 6–8 per 1,000 person-years among those aged 60 and further rising to 8–12 cases per 1,000 person-years among those aged ≥80 y.2,3 In China, it is estimated that over 1.5 million new cases of HZ occur annually among individuals aged ≥50 y, creating an economic burden exceeding 1.3 billion yuan.4

Vaccination against HZ has emerged as the optimal approach for HZ prevention and control.5,6 Despite the availability of HZ vaccines and well-defined clinical recommendations for HZ prevention, the acceptance of HZ vaccination among the Chinese population remains suboptimal. A domestic survey reveals that 16.57% of respondents expressed willingness to receive vaccine,5 and the projected vaccination coverage for Chinese individuals aged ≥60 y in 2022 was merely 0.1%,6 it is substantially lower than the, coverage among individuals aged ≥60 in Canada (8.4%),7 aged ≥50 in the United States (24.1%)8 and aged ≥65 in Italy (13.5%).9

In developed countries, publicly funded vaccination programs serve as a cornerstone strategy for increasing vaccine coverage.8,9 In China, the implementation of universally free adult vaccination policies has been more limited, largely due to the challenges posed by the sheer population size and corresponding fiscal constraints on public health resources. Addressing the persistently low coverage, however, requires interventions grounded in a systematic understanding of the behavioral and systemic determinants of health decisions. To fundamentally shift the healthcare model from a treatment-centric to a people-centered, health-promoting paradigm, the Chinese government has in recent years vigorously advocated for and promoted the “integration of medical care and prevention.” Against this policy backdrop, the “vaccine prescription” has emerged as an innovative service-delivery model. It seeks to proactively identify and address individual preventive health needs through the deep integration of clinical and public health services at the point of care. This model is currently being piloted and explored in multiple provinces, including Zhejiang, Shandong, and Guangdong province.

Zhejiang Province began to implement the vaccination prescription program10 in 35 counties from May 2024. This program emphasizes effective collaboration between general practitioners and vaccination physicians in delivering vaccination services. The program comprised the following key components: (a) Training and Capacity Building: Multi-level training sessions (provincial and county) were conducted for primary care leaders and general practitioners, covering vaccine knowledge, prescription procedures, and consultation-based health education. Cascade training ensured all participating physicians received this instruction at least three times. (b) Supportive Environment Creation: A standardized promotional toolkit (e.g., manuals, posters, videos) was provided. Primary care institutions were to establish a supportive environment through information booths, material distribution, and video displays. (c) Health Education Activities: Institutions organized regular vaccine-focused health lectures or campaigns (at least monthly), supplemented by province-wide education during seasonal vaccination periods. (d) Vaccine Prescription Issuance: General practitioners integrated targeted vaccine education into routine consultations and issued prescriptions to eligible patients. (e) System Support and Incentives: Performance incentive mechanisms were explored. Local health information and electronic record systems were updated to include an electronic vaccine prescription module, embedding the process into clinical workflow.

To provide a theoretical framework for understanding the mechanisms through which the program might influence vaccination behavior, this study is conceptually guided by the COM-B model of behavior change.11 This model posits that Behavior results from the interaction of Capability, Opportunity, and Motivation. The vaccination prescription program aligns with this framework by aiming to enhance residents’ capability (through improved knowledge), create opportunity (through structured clinician recommendations), and influence motivation (by facilitating clinical conversations about prevention). Despite pilot initiatives in some regions, empirical research systematically evaluating the program’s association with vaccine knowledge, intention, and coverage based on large-scale surveys is still lacking. This study aims to examine the association between the vaccine prescription program and changes in these outcomes among residents aged ≥50 in Zhejiang Province.

Methods

Study design and sampling

A pre-post intervention study employing repeated cross-sectional surveys was conducted to evaluate the impact of the vaccine health prescription intervention. This design is robust for assessing population-level changes resulting from community-based interventions, as it minimizes loss-to-follow-up bias and testing effects common in longitudinal cohorts.12,13 The study recruited residents aged 50 y or above using a multi-stage convenience sampling method. The baseline survey was conducted from March to May 2024, and the follow-up survey was conducted from September to October 2025.

According to the sample size formula necessary for the cross sectional survey: N=Zα2×p(1−p)d2×deff. Based on the previously reported HZV vaccination willingness rate of 16.57% in the literature,5 we set the expected proportion (P) at 0.1657, with a margin of error (d) of 0.1 × P, and a design effect (deff) of 1.5, as recommended for complex sampling designs, the initial estimated sample size was 2,901. The minimum sample size calculated for each stratum was 2,901. After stratifying by economic level (high, medium, low) and considering an invalid questionnaire and refusal rate of no more than 10%, the total sample size was determined to be at least 9,670.

A multi-stage convenience sampling method was adopted in this study, with the specific process as follows: In the first stage, 35 districts/counties were chosen randomly from a pool of 90 counties across across socioeconomic strata. In the second stage, five community health service centers were randomly selected from each district or county. In the third stage, a intercept survey method was used to randomly intercept eligible individuals who visited the centers for medical treatment or physical examinations to conduct questionnaire surveys, a total of 60 valid questionnaires were collected at each survey site until the required number of qualified questionnaires was obtained. A total of 10,500 residents participated in the survey.

Recruitment was conducted via a community-based approach. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) a local resident or permanent non-local resident who has resided in the area for >6 months, (2) aged ≥50 y, (3) conscious, able to understand and complete the questionnaire independently, (4) participating in this survey and signing the informed consent form voluntarily. Exclusion criteria included: (1) plans to be away from the community/village for more than 3 months in the upcoming year, (2) cognitive impairments that hindered normal communication, and (3) lack of cooperation with the study and poor compliance.

Questionnaire and quality control

The questionnaire14 was designed by the Institute of Health Education and Institute of Immunization Program at the Zhejiang Provincial Center for Disease Control and Prevention. The content of the questionnaire mainly included four sections: (1) demographic characteristics, including gender, age, education, occupation, monthly income and living status; (2) past medical history and current health conditions, including chronic diseases and vaccine-targeted diseases; (3) Channels for obtaining vaccine information, including traditional media, new media and interpersonal communication; (4) vaccination behaviors and intentions, including reasons for vaccination and non-vaccination. Vaccination intention was assessed by asking participants: ‘How willing are you to receive the herpes zoster vaccine?’ Responses were collected on a 5-point Likert scale (1 = Very unwilling, 2 = Unwilling, 3 = Uncertain, 4 = Willing, 5 = Very willing). For analysis, the scale was collapsed into three categories: Negative (combining Very unwilling and Unwilling), Hesitant (Uncertain), and Positive (combining Willing and Very willing). This categorization is consistent with approaches used in health behavior research to distinguish clear rejection, ambivalence, and acceptance. A pre-survey was conducted on the questionnaire before the investigation began. The Cronbach’s α coefficient of the questionnaire was 0.765, KMO value was 0.855, and Bartlett’s test p-value was <.01, indicating good reliability and validity.

Before the survey commenced, training was provided to all investigators involved from municipal and county Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDCs), community health service centers, and township hospitals. The training covered the study objectives, sampling methods, informed consent procedures, detailed explanation of each questionnaire item, standardized phrasing for inquiries, data verification, and submission protocols, aiming to clarify their responsibilities and standardize the survey process. During the survey, eligible participants were given a detailed explanation of the study’s purpose, procedures, and the confidentiality of their data. Written informed consent was obtained prior to administering the survey according to standardized protocols. Vaccination information for all participants was cross-verified through the Zhejiang Provincial Immunization Program Smart Service Information System to ensure the accuracy of all collected vaccination records.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) software(version 30.0.0.0; IBM Corp). Chi-squared exact test was used to assessed Changes in HZ related knowledge, intention and vaccination coverage from 2024 to 2025. A multivariate Generalized Estimating Equations model with a binary logistic link function was built to identify factors independently associated with HZ vaccination, and the community health service center was specified as the cluster variable (subject). The model included all pre-specified explanatory variables of interest: sex, age group, living status, education level, occupation, monthly income, doctor’s recommendation, history of HZ, and the intervention period. This approach provides adjusted estimates that account for potential confounding among the covariates. Results are presented as adjusted odds ratios (AOR) with 95% confidence intervals (95% CI). P values less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant (two-sided).

Ethics approval

Ethics approval was granted by the Ethics Committee of the Zhejiang Provincial Center for Disease Control and Prevention (Approval number: 2022–016–02). All participants voluntarily participated in the survey and signed the informed consent form. In our study, all participants were managed through a coding system, with exclusive codes used solely as identifiers for registration. No identifying information related to personal privacy was collected or utilized, ensuring the protection of participants’ personal privacy.

Changes in HZ knowledge, intention, and vaccination coverage

Following the intervention, all measured domains of HZ knowledge showed significant improvement (p < .001). The proportion of participants who knew that “HZ can be prevented by vaccination” increased from 44.74% to 60.23% (p < .001). Recognition of all typical HZ symptoms also improved significantly (p < .001 for all symptoms). Concurrently, the rate of receiving a doctor’s recommendation rose significantly from 36.29% to 48.23% (p < .001).

A significant shift in vaccination intention was observed. The proportion of participants expressing positive intention decreased from 41.44% to 35.09%, while those with negative intention also declined slightly. In contrast, the proportion categorized as hesitant increased markedly, from 33.79% to 42.07% (p < .001). Discussion

Principal findings

This study provides evidence that the implementation of vaccination prescription program in community health centers is associated with significant improvements in HZ vaccine knowledge, doctor’s recommendation rates, and vaccination coverage among adults aged ≥50 in Zhejiang, China.

The core finding is a significant increase in HZ vaccination coverage form 1.93% to 5.74%, coinciding with the intervention period. While this remains lower than rates reported in some urban districts of China and several developed countries,7–9,15 it substantially exceeds recent estimates from other Chinese provinces.6,15,16 Adjusted analysis confirmed the intervention period as an independent factor associated with higher vaccination, suggesting the vaccination prescription program directly facilitated the vaccination process, which is consistent with prior local report.10

Notably, the rate of receiving a doctor’s recommendation rose significantly from 36.29% to 48.23% following the intervention, and doctor’s recommendation was independently associated with HZ vaccination coverage. The pivotal role of healthcare providers as catalysts for prevention is well-recognized.11,17 Consistent with studies highlighting the frontline perspective of health workers in addressing behavioral risk factors,18 the vaccination prescription likely served as a tangible tool that both formalized this role and facilitated the crucial clinical conversation.

Substantial improvements were also observed across all measured domains of HZ knowledge, and “lack of knowledge” as a reason for non-vaccination decreased significantly. These findings show the intervention effectively disseminated knowledge and reduced the information gap.19 Concurrently, vaccination intention shifted: the proportion of “hesitant” individuals increased while “positive” and “negative” intention decreased. This shift can be robustly interpreted through the lens of behavioral science, particularly the COM-B model11 and the concept of the “behavioral continuum.” Interventions that effectively raise awareness and initiate clinical dialogue, such as the vaccination prescription program, do not instantly convert all individuals into vaccine acceptors. Instead, they often move people along a continuum from unawareness or disengagement into a state of active consideration.21 When people become better informed, practical barriers like cost and safety concerns naturally become more prominent in their decision-making. This reflects a common pattern in health behavior.19 Thus, the rise in hesitancy should not be interpreted as a program failure, but rather as an expected and necessary phase in the population-level decision-making. It identifies a large and activated subgroup that is now engaged but undecided.20 Hesitant group is a prime target for subsequent tailored interventions to facilitate the final step from hesitation to acceptance.

Accordingly, reasons for non-vaccination such as “Too expensive” “Concern about vaccine safety” and “Unnecessary to get vaccinated” emerged as more prominent barriers. This underscores that while the prescription intervention was effective, the heightened focus on cost highlights an urgent need for complementary policy-level interventions.17,21,22 In China, the HZ vaccine is a Category II (non-National Immunization Program) vaccine, which requires full out-of-pocket payment by recipients, with minimal coverage by basic medical insurance in most regions. Beyond targeted health education, addressing this affordability barrier may necessitate complementary policy-level interventions, such as vaccination subsidies or the inclusion of the HZ vaccine in national medical insurance reimbursement schemes, to substantially improve its affordability.23 Internationally, our findings reveal both shared strategies and systemic gaps. Many high-coverage countries use integrated policies targeting both healthcare systems and individuals. Our program shares this core strategy of engaging primary care providers as vaccinators and advisors. However, its effectiveness is limited without the stronger system-level supports (e.g., financing, information systems) commonly found in many OECD countries.24

The Vaccination Prescription Program effectively translates the national “Integrated Medical and Preventive Care” policy into practice.10 As a low-cost, workflow-embedded model, it is highly feasible for scale-up. To ensure the successful rollout and sustainability of the program, securing leadership buy-in at community health centers is essential. Institutional incentives, such as incorporating vaccine education and prescription metrics into professional performance evaluations, should be established to motivate healthcare workers. Concurrently, regular training must be institutionalized to equip general practitioners with the skills to confidently recommend vaccines during consultations. Ultimately, integrating vaccination assessment and e-prescribing functions into clinical information systems will be critical. This integration enables patients to receive both treatment and preventive guidance in a single visit, achieving true integration of medical and preventive care and maximizing the public health impact of primary care.

Limitations

Despite the strengths of a large population‑based sample, a robust pre-post design, and multivariable adjustment for confounders, several limitations of this study must be acknowledged. First, the absence of a concurrent control group precludes definitive causal inference, despite the use of a repeated cross-sectional design and GEE analysis to strengthen temporal association. Second, although socioeconomic differences between the 2024 and 2025 samples were adjusted for in the model, residual confounding from these shifts cannot be fully excluded. Third, key measures such as vaccination intention and doctor’s recommendation rely on self-report and may be influenced by social desirability or recall bias, even though a validated questionnaire was used. Finally, the use of multi-stage convenience sampling with participants recruited from healthcare facilities may overestimate vaccination coverage and underrepresent access barriers, as the sample likely over-represents individuals with greater health awareness. This limits generalizability to all residents aged ≥50, especially those less engaged with primary care, though the sample remains relevant for evaluating this primary care-based intervention.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study demonstrates that the vaccination prescription program was significantly associated with improved HZ vaccine knowledge, coverage and doctor’s recommendation rates among adults aged ≥50 in Zhejiang. These findings highlight the pivotal role general practitioners can play in promoting adult immunization within an integrated medical-preventive care model. Consequently, Scaling up such primary care-based strategies could be enhanced by complementary measures to address key barriers, including financial support to improve affordability, incentives for clinician engagement, and integration with routine chronic disease management.

Priming the tumor microenvironment with pembrolizumab and radiotherapy for durable cures in high-risk localized undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma and pleomorphic/dedifferentiated liposarcoma
Authors: Aastha Sobti, Yvonne M Mowery
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractSU2C-SARC032 establishes addition of perioperative pembrolizumab as a new treatment option for high-risk localized extremity soft tissue sarcoma (STS). For patients with stage III undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma or dedifferentiated/pleomorphic liposarcoma, addition of neoadjuvant and adjuvant pembrolizumab to preoperative radiation therapy (RT) and surgery improved 2-year disease-free survival by 15% compared with RT and surgery alone. This commentary highlights key insights from the trial: (1) the cooperative activity between pembrolizumab and RT, (2) the need for biomarker-driven patient selection, and (3) the potential for circulating tumor DNA to guide adjuvant treatment decisions. Recognizing the heterogeneity of STS, we emphasize the importance of future trials incorporating comprehensive correlative analyses to identify predictors of response and to optimize treatment strategies. Furthermore, we highlight the need for equitable access to advanced diagnostics and personalized treatment approaches so that the benefits of SU2C-SARC032 can be realized for patients with STS throughout the world.Keywords: RADIOTHERAPY, Immunotherapy, Neoadjuvant, Circulating tumor DNA - ctDNA, Tumor microenvironment - TME

SU2C-SARC032: immunotherapy’s leap into localized sarcoma care – challenges and new horizons

SU2C-SARC032 marks a watershed moment in managing high-risk localized extremity soft tissue sarcoma (STS).1 This international phase II randomized controlled trial enrolled patients with stage III undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma (UPS) or dedifferentiated/pleomorphic liposarcoma (LPS). Addition of perioperative pembrolizumab to preoperative radiation therapy (RT) and surgery resulted in a 15% absolute improvement in 2-year disease-free survival (DFS) compared with RT and surgery alone (figure 1). This commentary synthesizes the trial’s clinical and translational implications, identifies unresolved challenges, and proposes a roadmap for optimizing immunotherapy in this molecularly diverse disease.

Amplifying treatment power: the radiotherapy–immunotherapy nexus

SU2C-SARC032’s most provocative finding lies in the cooperative activity between RT (50 Gy in 25 fractions) and pembrolizumab,1 defying historical concerns that large-field irradiation would abrogate immunotherapy efficacy due to lymphodepletion and other immunosuppressive effects. In contrast to recent negative clinical studies combining conventionally fractionated RT with immunotherapy, elective nodal irradiation was not performed in SU2C-SARC032, potentially contributing to this trial’s success. Notably, RT can induce tertiary lymphoid structures (TLS) at tumor margins and increase programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) expression and T-cell infiltration, potentially improving response to anti-programmed cell death protein-1 (PD-1) therapy.2 3 These TLS-rich niches likely facilitate systemic T-cell priming, mirroring lung cancer studies where neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy outperformed adjuvant approaches4 and a recent melanoma trial showing better event-free survival with neoadjuvant and adjuvant pembrolizumab compared with adjuvant therapy.5 Spatial transcriptomic and multiplex imaging mass cytometry analyses from SU2C-SARC032 are pending, and quantification of TLS in pretreatment versus resected tumors will indicate whether pembrolizumab and RT enhanced TLS maturation compared with RT alone. Ongoing correlative analyses of matched pretreatment and post-treatment tumor specimens from SU2C-SARC032 will provide insights into mechanisms by which concurrent pembrolizumab and RT improved DFS, potentially informing strategies that can be translated into other tumor types.

Precision medicine unleashed: biomarkers beyond histology in sarcoma care

While patients with high-grade disease were more likely to benefit from pembrolizumab in SU2C-SARC032 (DFS HR 0.57 for grade 3 vs 0.84 for grade 2 tumors), more sophisticated biomarkers are needed to optimize patient selection (figure 1).1 Histologic subtype remains the most validated predictor of immunotherapy response in sarcoma, with UPS demonstrating the highest response rates across multiple studies.6 While SU2C-SARC032 did not mandate PD-L1 or tumor mutational burden (TMB) testing, retrospective analyses of patients with metastatic STS receiving pembrolizumab in SARC028 identified responders clustered in UPS tumors with ≥5% PD-L1 expression or TMB >5 mutations/Mb.6 Tumor grade also correlates with immune infiltration and potential benefit from immunotherapy, as observed in SU2C-SARC032.1

Baseline TLS density, a prognostic biomarker in sarcoma, has not yet been assessed in SU2C-SARC032 but could refine patient selection. The addition of an immunohistochemistry (IHC) panel of CD68/CD163 (M2 markers) also warrants prospective validation as a predictor of response to anti-PD-1 therapy. Additionally, sarcoma immune classes (SICs) and sarcoma ecotypes, identified from gene expression data, have been linked to prognosis and response to immunotherapy. For instance, SIC E, characterized by high immune infiltration and TLS, has shown promise in predicting response to PD-1 blockade. These analyses for SU2C-SARC032 are in process and could provide further insights.7 Integrating PD-L1 expression, TMB, pretreatment TLS assessment, as well as ideally SIC and sarcoma ecotype classification, into routine clinical workflows could improve decision-making to reduce treatment of patients unlikely to benefit from immunotherapy, thus reducing unnecessary toxicity and healthcare system costs.2 3 Pending validation of findings from SU2C-SARC032’s correlative studies, clinicians should consider established clinical factors when discussing perioperative pembrolizumab. Future trials will ideally incorporate biomarker stratification to refine patient selection, potentially identifying subgroups who can achieve similar outcomes with less intensive treatment. Moreover, artificial intelligence-driven algorithms may enhance the assessment of circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) dynamics by efficiently analyzing genomic data, recognizing patterns indicative of tumor progression, and predicting treatment outcomes.8 These algorithms can bridge resource gaps by offering cost-effective solutions for high-throughput biomarker assessment.8

Navigating safety waters: managing irAEs for seamless clinical integration

The higher incidence of grade ≥3 adverse events with addition of pembrolizumab compared with RT and surgery alone (56% vs 31%), particularly anemia (10% vs 3%), hypertension (9% vs 5%), hypotension (4% vs 0%), and colitis (4% vs 0%), underscores the delicate balance between maximizing therapeutic efficacy and minimizing toxicity in multimodal management of STS.1 Careful patient selection and proactive toxicity management are of paramount importance. Patients with pre-existing autoimmune conditions or significant cardiovascular risk may require a modified approach. For community oncologists who encounter these toxicities less frequently, establishing relationships with specialized sarcoma centers is essential for optimal patient management.

Paradoxically, immune-related adverse events (irAEs) may signal therapeutic success. Retrospective analyses of SARC028 revealed that patients experiencing irAEs achieved longer progression-free survival (HR 0.62, p=0.03), suggesting systemic immune activation correlates with both toxicity and efficacy.6 9 This duality positions irAEs as potential surrogate biomarkers for pembrolizumab response, particularly in resource-limited settings where advanced diagnostics remain inaccessible. For instance, monitoring colitis or dermatitis severity could potentially personalize risk-benefit assessments in regions lacking ctDNA or multiplex IHC capabilities, democratizing adaptive trial strategies across socioeconomic divides.

Moving beyond the status quo: holistic assessment, innovative strategies, and equitable access

SU2C-SARC032’s use of DFS rather than overall survival (OS) as its primary endpoint reflects immunotherapy’s nascency in sarcoma (table 1), as well as challenges in accruing sufficient patients to power interventional studies in this rare disease with numerous histologic subtypes.1 In many countries, pembrolizumab will not be covered by insurance for stage III STS due to the lack of data indicating an OS benefit. While selecting DFS was pragmatic given sarcoma’s rarity, this intermediate endpoint complicates regulatory approval pathways, leading to access challenges globally. Regulatory bodies traditionally prefer OS data, though the US Food and Drug Administration has demonstrated increasing flexibility for rare cancers when powered OS studies are impractical. The European Medicines Agency typically maintains stricter requirements, often insisting on stronger correlation between DFS and OS benefit. Unfortunately, the rarity of UPS and LPS would make it difficult to complete a well-powered phase III randomized controlled trial with an OS primary endpoint in a timely fashion.

Beyond regulatory hurdles, health technology assessment bodies face challenges evaluating cost-effectiveness of new treatment regimens without mature OS data. In publicly funded healthcare systems, reimbursement decisions typically require evidence of cost-effectiveness based on quality-adjusted life years. The estimated annual cost of pembrolizumab (approximately US$150,000–175,000) creates a formidable access barrier, particularly in middle and low-income countries.

Several approaches could expand access to perioperative pembrolizumab: (1) outcomes-based agreements tying reimbursement to clinical outcomes; (2) biomarker-driven patient selection to improve cost-effectiveness; (3) tiered pricing strategies based on country-level economic indicators; and (4) international registries generating real-world evidence to support decisions. Ideally, the sarcoma community will advocate for these approaches while simultaneously pursuing translational research to refine patient selection. Furthermore, collaborative efforts among academic centers, industry partners, and patient advocacy groups are essential to develop sustainable access models that bridge innovation and affordability across diverse healthcare systems.

While analyses of serial ctDNA measurements from SU2C-SARC032 are ongoing, emerging data suggest that ctDNA monitoring holds promise in STS. The utility of ctDNA for monitoring treatment response remains preliminary. For instance, Sun et al demonstrated potential applications in a small series of 10 patients, a hypothesis-generating finding requiring confirmation in larger, prospectively designed studies before clinical implementation.10

Furthermore, Blomain et al’s recent findings highlight how evolutionary pressures shape sarcoma development and response to radiotherapy, suggesting ctDNA could be valuable for tracking emergence of resistance mechanisms or clonal tumor dynamics under selective pressure.11 Future trials should explore incorporating ctDNA to predict early relapse or identify patients likely to benefit from adjuvant therapy. Unfortunately, the genomic heterogeneity of many STS histologies may necessitate tumor sequencing to develop a personalized panel for ctDNA measurement as was done in SU2C-SARC032, which may be cost-prohibitive in clinical practice. Incorporating ctDNA assessment into the clinical workflow to inform therapeutic decisions will require increased biospecimen collection and harmonized methods across institutions.

To optimize benefits of pembrolizumab for patients with STS, future efforts must prioritize biology-driven stratification, leveraging tools such as TLS quantification via multiplex IHC and gene expression profiling to identify patients likely to respond to immunotherapy.4 Building on this, novel combination strategies should be explored: CTLA-4 inhibitors like ipilimumab could expand T-cell receptor diversity in TLS-low microenvironments, while PARP inhibitors such as olaparib may synergize with radiotherapy by augmenting STING-mediated PD-L1 upregulation as a mechanism shown to enhance antigen presentation. Specifically, therapeutic strategies aimed at resculpting immunosuppressive niches in patients not responding to anti-PD-1 therapy and RT warrant study. For example, CSF1R inhibitors like pexidartinib could be investigated for their ability to deplete CD163+ M2 macrophages and reverse IL-10 and TGF-β mediated immunosuppression. Furthermore, given emerging evidence that the intratumoral microbiome can influence the immune response and prognosis in STS, future studies should investigate the role of the microbiome in modulating response to pembrolizumab.

It is also critical to acknowledge inequities in access to advanced diagnostics and treatment within oncology. In many resource-constrained settings, a significant proportion of patients with STS lack access to comprehensive biomarker testing compared with those in well-resourced centers. For example, IHC for TLS markers is unavailable, leaving clinicians without key data that may inform treatment decisions. Given the association between TLS and pembrolizumab response in STS, as demonstrated in the PEMBROSARC trial that included low-dose cyclophosphamide with pembrolizumab, increasing access to TLS assessment is crucial for improving equitable access to immunotherapy in patients most likely to benefit.12 Importantly, real-world data integration is essential in understanding the efficacy and toxicity of perioperative pembrolizumab with neoadjuvant RT. Beyond molecular profiling, real-world integration with registries like Flatiron Health’s sarcoma cohort (N=2,300) will enable benchmarking against historical controls, providing pragmatic insights into long-term outcomes.

Conclusion: navigating complexity, embracing equity – the ongoing evolution of sarcoma immunotherapy

SU2C-SARC032 establishes neoadjuvant and adjuvant pembrolizumab as a transformative therapy for stage III UPS and LPS of the extremity, while also underscoring the complexity of integrating immunotherapy into the treatment of molecularly diverse malignancies. To advance this paradigm, three interconnected priorities demand attention: First, prospective trials should validate TLS density, gene expression profiles (SIC and sarcoma ecotype), and ctDNA dynamics as biomarkers. Second, researchers must evaluate combination tumor microenvironment-modulating regimens, such as pembrolizumab with LAG-3 inhibitors, to overcome adaptive resistance and reinvigorate exhausted CD8+ T cells. Third, immune-related response criteria need to be standardized, with consideration of integrating ctDNA dynamics into efficacy assessments. Through these initiatives and transparent communication with patients about treatment options, the sarcoma community will build on SU2C-SARC032 to develop sustainable, egalitarian therapies while addressing the biological and ethical intricacies of contemporary immunotherapy.

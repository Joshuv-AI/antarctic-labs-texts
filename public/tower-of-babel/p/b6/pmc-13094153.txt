Comparison of short-term outcomes between laparoscopic transabdominal preperitoneal repair (TAPP) with biological mesh and laparoscopic intraperitoneal onlay mesh repair (IPOM Plus) with composite mesh for umbilical hernia​​
Authors: Shuai Chang, Yao Zhao, Di Zhang, Shunle Li, Hongjun Zhai, Hong Ji
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective​To compare the short-term outcomes of laparoscopic transabdominal preperitoneal repair (TAPP) and laparoscopic intraperitoneal onlay mesh (IPOM Plus) repair for umbilical hernia.MethodsA retrospective analysis was conducted on 55 patients with umbilical hernia treated at the Second Affiliated Hospital of Xi’an Jiaotong University from July 2023 to March 2025. The patients were divided into two groups based on the surgical approach: the TAPP group (experimental group, n=30) and the IPOM Plus group (control group, n=25). Operation time, intraoperative blood loss, postoperative hospital stay, hernia recurrence, surgical site/mesh infection, seroma, foreign body sensation, postoperative pain, bowel obstruction and perforation were compared. Follow-ups were performed at 1, 3 and 6 months postoperatively. ResultsThere were no statistically significant differences between the two groups regarding baseline characteristics, intraoperative blood loss, postoperative hospital stay, recurrence rate, surgical site/mesh infection rate, seroma formation, bowel obstruction and perforation. However, the TAPP group demonstrated a longer operative time (P<0.05), while showing significantly lower postoperative pain scores and reduced incidence of foreign body sensation (P<0.05). ConclusionTAPP can significantly improve the patient's postoperative recovery experience. Although it prolongs operative time, its safety and short-term efficacy are comparable to IPOM Plus, providing an alternative surgical option for umbilical hernia repair. Further large-scale studies with long-term follow-up are needed to validate these advantages and establish TAPP as a routine surgical option for umbilical hernia.

Introduction

Umbilical hernia, a subtype of ventral hernia, involves protrusion of abdominal contents through the umbilical weak area. In adults, it primarily results from acquiredfactors such as pregnancy, ascites, and obesity. This condition does not resolve spontaneously and progressively worsens with incarceration risks, necessitating surgical intervention [1]. The current mainstream minimally invasive approach is intraperitoneal onlay mesh repair (IPOM) [2], valued for its technical simplicity. However, IPOM requires costly anti-adhesive meshes and fixation devices (e.g., tackers), and increasing evidence reports long-term complications including bowel obstruction and perforation due to mesh-bowel adhesions [3, 4].

Recently, totally endoscopic sublay repair (TES) has been proposed as an alternative. TES, however, requires dissection of bilateral posterior rectus sheaths at the linea alba, disrupting native abdominal wall structures and increasing trauma [5–7]. Li Binggen et al. subsequently introduced the endoscopic totally extraperitoneal approach (TEA), offering a novel strategy for primary ventral hernia repair. TEA places polypropylene mesh in the preperitoneal space without compromising abdominal integrity, reducing trauma, cost, and mesh-related complications [8, 9]. Nevertheless, TEA presents significant technical challenges: establishing the initial retroperitoneal space is difficult, and dissecting the adherent peritoneum-posterior sheath interface near the umbilicus risks peritoneal tears and visceral injury.

To address these limitations, we developed a cephalad-approach laparoscopic transabdominal preperitoneal repair (TAPP). This technique preserves TEA’s advantages while circumventing complex space creation. By entering the abdominal cavity under direct vision, it minimizes iatrogenic injury, enhances operability and safety, and improves accessibility. This study compares short-term outcomes of TAPP versus IPOM Plus for umbilical hernia repair.

Materials and methods

Study population and design

A retrospective analysis was conducted on 55 patients with umbilical hernia treated at The Second Affiliated Hospital of Xi’an Jiaotong University between July 2023 and March 2025. Patients were allocated to the experimental group (TAPP) or control group (IPOM Plus) based on chronological order of admission, without randomization, reflecting real-world clinical practice. Experimental Group (n = 30): Underwent TAPP (transabdominal preperitoneal repair) using a porcine small intestinal submucosa-derived acellular matrix biologic mesh (15 × 10 cm; Shaanxi Bio Regenerative Medicine Co., LTD.). Control Group (n = 25): Underwent laparoscopic IPOM Plus (intraperitoneal onlay mesh repair) using the Shanshi® anti-adhesive composite mesh (15 × 10 cm; Beijing Transeasy Medical Technology Co., Ltd.). All surgeries were performed by the same surgeon. Inclusion Criteria: (1) Umbilical hernia confirmed by ultrasonography or CT imaging. (2) Age ≥ 18 years. (3) Non-emergency surgery. Exclusion Criteria: (1) Recurrent hernia. (2) History of abdominal surgery.

Surgical technique

Prophylactic antibiotics were not routinely administered preoperatively. General anesthesia is administered with the patient in the supine position. Both upper limbs are adducted (positioned alongside the body). The surgeon and assistant both stand on the patient’s left side, similar to the positioning for a right inguinal hernia TAPP procedure. The laparoscopic display screen is placed at the foot side of the operating table. A urinary catheter is inserted preoperatively. Pneumoperitoneum is established at a pressure of 12 mmHg. A 10-mm trocar is placed 2 cm below the xiphoid process as the observation port, through which a 30-degree laparoscope is inserted. Under direct vision, two 5-mm trocars are placed as operative ports at the following locations: 2 cm below the costal margin along the midclavicular line on both sides (Fig. 1).​

TAPP

(1) Dissection of the Preperitoneal Space: A transverse peritoneal incision is made 5 cm above the superior border of the umbilical ring, extending 5 cm bilaterally from the midline. Dissection proceeds caudally along the plane between the peritoneum/preperitoneal fat and the posterior rectus sheath. Preperitoneal fat should be mobilized toward the peritoneal side to prevent peritoneal thinning-induced tears during dissection or closure. The linea alba region typically has a clear plane between preperitoneal fat and posterior sheath, making dissection easier; begin dissection here first. As surgery progresses with pneumoperitoneum effects, subsequent dissection of the adherent peritoneum-posterior sheath interface becomes more efficient, minimizing peritoneal injury. Divide the round ligament attached superior to the umbilical ring to expose the hernia ring, perform periannular adhesiolysis, reduce extraperitoneal fat, and continue dissection: caudally to 5 cm below the inferior umbilical ring border, laterally to 5 cm from midline bilaterally, completing space creation (Fig. 2a). (2) Closure of Hernia Defect: Before fascial closure: suture the central umbilical subcutaneous tissue to the fascial ring with 3 − 0 absorbable suture, adjusting tension under visualization to contour the umbilicus. Reduce pneumoperitoneum to 8 mmHg. Close the fascial defect continuously with 0-barbed suture, avoiding skin incorporation (Fig. 2b). (3) Mesh Placement and Fixation: Trim the 15 × 10 cm biologic mesh, introduce it into the abdomen through the umbilical port, deploy into the preperitoneal space, and fix the mesh at 4–5 points using medical glue (Compont®; Beijing Compont Medical Devices Co., Ltd., Beijing, China) (Fig. 2c). (4) Peritoneal Closure: Close the peritoneal incision continuously with 3 − 0 absorbable or barbed suture. Drains are not routinely placed (Fig. 2d).

IPOM plus

(1) Adhesiolysis around the umbilical ring is performed, and herniated contents are reduced into the abdominal cavity (Fig. 3a). (2) Closure of Hernia Defect: Pneumoperitoneum pressure is reduced to 8 mmHg. The fascial defect is closed continuously with a 0-barbed suture. Care is taken during suturing to avoid incorporating the skin (Fig. 3b). (3) The 15 × 10 cm anti-adhesive mesh is appropriately trimmed, introduced into the abdomen through the umbilical port, and unfolded. The mesh is positioned with its anti-adhesive surface facing the abdominal cavity, centered over the defect, ensuring ≥ 5 cm of overlap beyond all defect edges (Fig. 3c). (4) Maintaining 8 mmHg pneumoperitoneum, the mesh is secured flat against the abdominal wall using a combination of non-absorbable tacks (Beijing Transeasy Medical Technology Co., Ltd.) and 3 − 0 barbed suture. Drains are not routinely placed (Fig. 3d).

Outcome measures

Operative time, intraoperative blood loss, and postoperative hospital stay were recorded. Follow-ups were conducted at 1, 3, and 6 months postoperatively. Recurrence rate, incisional/mesh infection rate, seroma occurrence, bowel obstruction and perforation were assessed through physical examination and CT examination, which was performed only if physical examination suggested recurrence or complications (e.g., unexplained pain, palpable mass). Foreign body sensation was evaluated by physical examination and the Carolinas Comfort Scale (CCS). Postoperative pain was assessed using the Visual Analogue Scale (VAS).

Statistical Analysis

SPSS 24.0 software was used for data analysis. Categorical data were presented as counts or percentages (%) and analyzed by chi-square (χ²) test or Fisher’s exact test. Continuous data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation and compared between groups using t-tests. A P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

All patients successfully completed the surgery and postoperative follow-up. Baseline characteristics of both groups are presented in Table 1, showing no significant differences. Comparisons of operative time, intraoperative blood loss, and postoperative hospital stay are shown in Table 2. The TAPP group had significantly longer operative time than the IPOM Plus group (125.6 ± 15.4 min vs. 91.6 ± 5.7 min, P < 0.05), while no significant differences existed in blood loss or hospital stay. Complication comparisons in Table 3 demonstrate no significant differences in recurrence rate, infection rate, seroma occurrence, bowel obstruction and perforation between groups. However, the TAPP group showed significantly lower postoperative pain scores (2.2 ± 0.6 vs. 4.3 ± 1.0) and reduced incidence of foreign body sensation (P < 0.05).

Discussion

Our study demonstrates that laparoscopic transabdominal preperitoneal repair (TAPP) for umbilical hernia, when performed with a biological mesh, yields short-term outcomes comparable to the established intraperitoneal onlay mesh (IPOM Plus) technique in terms of operative blood loss, length of hospital stay, and rates of recurrence, infection, or seroma. Crucially, however, TAPP was associated with significantly lower early postoperative pain and a markedly reduced incidence of foreign body sensation, albeit at the cost of a longer operative time.

The modified surgical method of “cephalic approach TAPP” adopted in this study, through the intra-abdominal approach, avoids the difficulty of establishing a TEA cavity. The operation is carried out under direct vision, reduces the risk of secondary injuries, and increases the operability and safety of the surgery. By placing patches in the free preperitoneal space, polypropylene patches or biological patches can be used without damaging the abdominal wall structure to improve the postoperative experience and reduce economic costs. Prasad et al. found TAPP safer and more cost-effective than IPOM, reducing adhesion-related complications [10] (though their “TAPP” included both TAPP and TES techniques). Kaushik et al. similarly reported TAPP’s superior quality of life [11], and Megas et al. highlighted its cost and pain advantages [12]. Our results align: TAPP significantly improves postoperative pain and comfort.

In this study’s TAPP group cases, we uniformly used biologic mesh, primarily for two reasons: (1) Due to the special position of the umbilicus, any future abdominal surgery, especially minimally invasive procedures, will almost inevitably involve the umbilical area; therefore, choosing an absorbable biologic mesh [13] minimizes impact on subsequent operations. (2) Selecting absorbable biologic mesh maximizes the reduction of its impact on abdominal wall compliance while theoretically reducing local foreign body sensation [14, 15]. With the domestic alternatives of biologic mesh and centralized procurement of medical consumables, the cost of biologic mesh has become equivalent to that of polypropylene mesh, imposing no additional financial burden on patients.

Postoperative pain is a critical factor affecting early recovery and quality of life [16, 17]. The results of this study demonstrate significantly lower pain scores in the TAPP group compared to the IPOM group. In TAPP surgery, the mesh is placed in the preperitoneal space—specifically between the posterior rectus sheath and the peritoneum—rather than in direct contact with intra-abdominal viscera. This “insulation” effect effectively reduces mechanical irritation and inflammatory reactions from the mesh to surrounding tissues, thereby alleviating postoperative pain; concurrently, the TAPP technique eliminates the need for tackers, avoiding fixation-induced pain in the peritoneum and muscles. Furthermore, the routine use of porcine small intestinal submucosa-derived acellular matrix (SIS) biologic mesh in our TAPP group, which exhibits excellent biocompatibility, theoretically further reduces local inflammatory responses and excessive fibrous tissue hyperplasia, potentially contributing to the lower postoperative pain observed in the TAPP group.

Postoperative foreign body sensation is a potential long-term discomfort for some patients after ventral hernia repair. This sensation can significantly impact patient satisfaction and quality of life [18, 19]. Our study observed a significantly lower incidence of this sensation in the TAPP group compared to the IPOM group. This may be attributed to the TAPP technique’s complete placement of the mesh in the preperitoneal space, preventing direct contact and friction between the mesh and intra-abdominal viscera such as the bowel. In contrast, with IPOM where the mesh is directly fixed to the intra-abdominal abdominal wall surface, even when using anti-adhesive coatings, the mesh may still exert mechanical irritation on adjacent organs. This sensation can become particularly noticeable during positional changes or increases in intra-abdominal pressure. Additionally, the use of tackers further elevates the incidence of foreign body sensation.

A significant confounding factor in this study is the difference in mesh types used: the TAPP group utilized a biologic mesh, whereas the IPOM Plus group used a composite mesh. Biologic meshes, owing to their excellent biocompatibility, can promote host tissue integration and are theoretically associated with reduced long-term inflammatory responses and foreign body sensation [20]. Therefore, the lower rates of postoperative pain and foreign body sensation observed in the TAPP group may be partially attributable to the material advantages of the biologic mesh, and not solely to the surgical approach itself. We explicitly acknowledge this limitation and emphasize that the study’s conclusions are based on the observed outcomes of these specific procedure-mesh combinations.

The results of this study indicate that operative time was significantly longer in the TAPP group than in the IPOM Plus group, primarily due to the inherently more complex procedural steps of TAPP. First, TAPP requires incising the peritoneum and dissecting an adequate preperitoneal space between the peritoneum and posterior rectus sheath to accommodate the mesh. This process demands meticulous technique to avoid peritoneal tears or injury to critical structures. Second, suturing the peritoneal incision is a challenging step in TAPP surgery complicated by the proximity of the trocars to the incised peritoneal line and the technical difficulty of suturing on the “ceiling.” These two critical steps likely account for the prolonged operative time in TAPP. Despite this increase, the technique delivers substantially reduced postoperative pain and lower foreign body sensation. From a patient-centered perspective, this approach of trading moderately extended operative time for superior postoperative quality of life holds significant clinical merit. The Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS) concept further underscores the importance of optimizing perioperative experiences [21, 22]. As surgeons gain proficiency with TAPP and surgical protocols become standardized, operative times may decrease, further enhancing the technique’s advantages.

This study demonstrates that the innovative cephalad preperitoneal approach significantly enhances postoperative outcomes while maintaining safety. Despite prolonged operative time, its advantages in pain control, reduced foreign body sensation, lower complication risks, and decreased costs position it as a viable routine alternative to IPOM Plus. Future work requires designing and implementing large-scale, multicenter, prospective randomized controlled trials (RCTs) to systematically collect long-term follow-up data. This will provide higher-level evidence for robustly evaluating the efficacy and safety profile of TAPP versus IPOM in umbilical hernia repair.

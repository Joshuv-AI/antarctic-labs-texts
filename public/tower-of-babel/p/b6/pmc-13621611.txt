An explainable machine learning model for predicting high phosphorus risk in patients on maintenance hemodialysis: a multicenter retrospective study
Authors: Jiaoyan Chen, Jurong Yang, Mingyue Hou, Jingrong Peng, Yunyan Wang, Kui Xiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHyperphosphatemia is a common complication in hemodialysis and serves as a key marker for evaluating dialysis adequacy. This study aimed to develop an interpretable machine learning (ML) model to predict the risk of hyperphosphatemia in patients undergoing maintenance hemodialysis.MethodsData from 718 patients receiving maintenance hemodialysis across five hemodialysis centers in Chongqing between May and September 2025 were included. The dataset was split into training (70%) and testing (30%) sets. Univariate and multivariate analyses were used to identify predictors most strongly associated with hyperphosphatemia. Seven ML algorithms were employed to construct predictive models. Model performance was assessed using the area under the curve (AUC), F1 score, precision, accuracy, and recall. SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) were applied to visualize feature importance and interpret individual predictions.ResultsThe support vector machine (SVM) model demonstrated superior performance (AUC = 0.840). The most influential predictors, in descending order, were PreCr, PreUrea, PreK, PreCO2CP, and PTH. Elevated PreCr, PreUrea, and PreK exert a positive effect on hyperphosphatemia, while estimated PreCO2CP levels demonstrate a negative effect.ConclusionsThe SVM model exhibited relatively high predictive accuracy for hyperphosphatemia risk in hemodialysis patients. This preliminary multicenter retrospective study demonstrated the feasibility of using SVM combined with the SHAP algorithm for risk identification, and the key features identified (PreCr, PreUrea, PreK, PreCO₂CP, PTH) can serve as a set of candidate parameters for future prospective model development.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12911-026-03678-9.Keywords: Hyperphosphatemia, Predictive model, Machine learning, MHD

Background

Hyperphosphatemia is prevalent among patients with end-stage renal disease (ESRD). The 2017 KDIGO guidelines for improving global outcomes in kidney disease indicate that 76% of Chinese patients receiving stable hemodialysis or peritoneal dialysis have serum phosphorus levels exceeding target ranges [1]. The progression of chronic kidney disease (CKD) is accompanied by bone and mineral metabolism disorders, and dietary phosphorus intake is a key factor in elevated serum phosphorus levels [2]. Research indicates that hyperphosphatemia is associated with mortality, cardiovascular disease risk, and vascular calcification [3–6]. In clinical practice, primary strategies for correcting hyperphosphatemia include restricting dietary phosphate intake, adequate dialysis, and phosphate binders [7], However, despite the potential to further reduce serum phosphorus concentrations in dialysis patients through these clinical strategies, achieving serum phosphorus levels within the normal range remains uncommon in clinical practice. Clinicians aim to continuously optimize dialysis prescriptions and integrate personalized dietary and medical strategies to achieve serum phosphorus normalization. However, this requires extensive clinical experience and expertise. Early identification of high-risk individuals for hyperphosphatemia and timely intervention can effectively assist clinicians in managing hyperphosphatemia, thereby improving the quality of life for dialysis patients.

Machine learning (ML) algorithms can accurately and rapidly process large volumes of clinical data and identify potential risk factors, offering new opportunities to advance healthcare services and improve diagnostic and therapeutic standards [8]. ML has gained significant recognition for its ability to accurately predict the progression of kidney disease [9]. Qiong et al. [10] demonstrated the feasibility of ML in evaluating CKD prognosis based on readily available features. In predicting 72-hour mortality associated with severe hyperphosphatemia, ML provided critical insights into variable importance [11].

Traditional management strategies primarily rely on periodic monitoring of serum phosphorus levels, representing a reactive approach. This study shifts the hyperphosphatemia management paradigm from conventional single-marker assessment to prospective risk prediction based on multivariate data, enabling earlier clinical intervention. The objective is to develop interpretable machine learning models for predicting hyperphosphatemia risk, assisting clinicians in earlier identification of high-risk individuals. Additionally, we identified models with high accuracy to inform treatment choices and improve initial medical management.

Methods

Research design

This study is a multicenter retrospective study that uses baseline clinical and laboratory data to predict the risk of hyperphosphatemia in patients on maintenance hemodialysis at 3 months. Data were collected from five hemodialysis centers in Chongqing between May and September 2025. The development and evaluation of the study model were conducted in accordance with the requirements of the TRIPOD + AI Statement [12].

The index time was defined as the date on which baseline data were collected at patient enrollment. Baseline data included demographic information (age, sex, body mass index, etc.), clinical characteristics (duration of dialysis, dialysis session length, ultrafiltration volume, Kt/V, comorbidities, etc.), and pre-dialysis laboratory parameters (PreCr, PreUrea, PreK, PreCO₂CP, PTH, ALB, PreCa, PostCr, etc.). All blood samples were pre-dialysis venous blood (collected from an arteriovenous fistula or central venous catheter). The outcome variable was defined as the serum phosphorus level measured at the routine follow-up visit 3 months after the index time. A serum phosphorus level > 1.78 mmol/L was defined as the hyperphosphatemia outcome [13].

The study protocol was passed and approved by the Ethics Committee of the Third Affiliated Hospital of Chongqing Medical University (No. 20240058). All participants provided and signed written informed consent. All procedures were performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki (2013 amendment).

Participants

Inclusion Criteria: (1) Aged ≥ 18 years; (2) Undergoing hemodialysis at least twice weekly, with each session lasting ≥ 3 h.

Patient screening process

Predictor variables and data preprocessing

All predictor variables were derived from routine pre-dialysis laboratory tests and clinical records from the index time point. A total of 291 cases of hyperphosphatemia were included in this study. The final multivariable model (with > 10 events) included: age, sex, duration of dialysis, dialysis session length, BMI, ultrafiltration (UF), urea clearance index (Kt/V), pre-dialysis calcium (PreCa), pre-dialysis blood urea nitrogen (PreUrea), post-dialysis blood urea nitrogen (PostUrea), urea reduction rate (URR), pre-dialysis potassium (PreK), pre-dialysis carbon dioxide combining power (PreCO₂CP), serum albumin (ALB), parathyroid hormone (PTH), pre-dialysis creatinine (PreCr), post-dialysis creatinine (PostCr), history of diabetes, and history of hypertension.

Missing data handling

After excluding cases with a missing rate > 30%, missing values in the remaining variables were imputed using multiple imputation methods. To ensure the stability of model training, all continuous variables were standardized. Undersampling techniques were used to balance the training set.

Data split and model developmentsplit and model development

Stratified random sampling was employed, with stratification based on the outcome variable (hyperphosphatemia: yes/no). The dataset was split into a training set (n = 503) and a test set (n = 215) in a 70%/30% ratio, with a random seed of 123 set to ensure reproducibility. A comparison of baseline characteristics between the training and test sets is provided in Supplementary Table S1.

Seven machine learning algorithms were used to build predictive models: XGBoost (XgbTree), Support Vector Machines (SVM), Random Forest (RF), AdaBoost, Neural Networks (NNet), K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN), and Gradient Boosting Machines (GBM).

Model performance evaluation

The performance of each model was evaluated on an independent test set using the following metrics: AUC (Area Under the Curve), accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score. The model with the highest AUC was identified as the optimal predictive model in this study.

Model interpretability analysis

The SHAP (SHapley Additive Explanations) method was used to perform an interpretability analysis on the optimal model. A SHAP summary plot was used to display the global importance ranking of each feature for the model’s output, and a SHAP force plot was used to display the decomposition of predictive contributions for individual patients, to aid in understanding the model’s decision-making process.

Statistical analysis

Analyses were performed using R software (version 4.5.1). Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages, compared using Fisher’s exact test or chi-square test. Continuous variables were compared using independent samples t-tests, with results presented as mean ± standard deviation. Univariate and multivariate binary logistic regression models were used to identify the characteristics most strongly associated with hyperphosphatemia, which were then incorporated into the machine learning model. p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Discussion

This study represents the first multicenter investigation to systematically compare seven machine learning algorithms for predicting hyperphosphatemia risk in hemodialysis patients, utilizing SHAP analysis to enhance model interpretability in clinical hyperphosphatemia scenarios. Current guidelines and most studies primarily focus on the control and management of hyperphosphatemia, with few investigations addressing its prospective risk prediction. To improve the generalizability of the model, we utilized multicenter data. Seven machine learning algorithms were employed to construct predictive models for hyperphosphatemia risk in maintenance hemodialysis (MHD) patients. Results showed that the Support Vector Machine (SVM) algorithm performed the best. To further interpret the model, we conducted a visualization analysis using the SHAP method. The analysis revealed that five features—PreCr, PreUrea, PreK, PreCO₂CP, and PTH—had the most significant impact on the SVM model’s predictions. SHAP helps to clarify the process of individualized hyperphosphatemia risk prediction, enabling a more comprehensive understanding of the potential roles and impacts of these features in the prediction process.

Support Vector Machines (SVMs) predict labels based on one or more feature vectors by constructing decision boundaries [14], which are capable of handling nonlinear and high-dimensional problems, exhibit strong generalization ability, and are effective in preventing overfitting [15]. SVMs have been successfully applied to various classification and regression tasks, such as cancer classification in genomics [16], disease identification from clinical data [17], and prediction of diabetes [18], and cardiovascular diseases [19]. Although traditional SVMs provide robust classification, they lack adaptability in dynamic environments and may suffer from reduced accuracy due to imbalanced training data [20]. Therefore, we applied undersampling techniques to rebalance the dataset as much as possible. Additionally, the SHAP method enhances both the performance and interpretability of SVMs when used as clinical decision support tools.

SHAP analysis revealed the relative contribution of each predictor variable to the output of the support vector machine model. As shown in Fig. 3, estimated creatinine (PreCr), estimated urea (PreUrea), estimated potassium (PreK), pre-dialysis carbon dioxide combining power (PreCO₂CP), and parathyroid hormone (PTH) emerged as the five key features. Figure 4B illustrates how these factors collectively determine personalized risk predictions for specific patients. Previous studies have shown that, serum creatinine is a marker for tracking the progression of chronic kidney disease [21, 22], and elevated blood urea nitrogen often parallels creatinine levels, jointly indicating renal impairment [23]. Patients with end-stage renal disease (ESRD) often exhibit elevated serum creatinine and urea nitrogen levels due to severe renal dysfunction and reduced clearance of these substances [24]. Meanwhile, a decreased glomerular filtration rate disrupts phosphate balance, leading to phosphate retention [25]. As renal function progressively declines, electrolyte and acid-base disturbances inevitably occur [26]. In ESRD patients, hyperkalemia and hyperphosphatemia often coexist because both are primarily excreted by the kidneys. When renal function fails, the ability to excrete potassium and phosphate is simultaneously impaired, resulting in the accumulation of both electrolytes [27–29]. Therefore, serum potassium levels can serve as an effective “synergistic indicator” to help predict hyperphosphatemia risk. Low carbon dioxide combining power typically indicates a risk of metabolic acidosis [30], and acid-base imbalance itself may become a risk factor for bone mineral loss and elevated serum phosphorus [31, 32].

The bidirectional regulatory effect observed in the model between parathyroid hormone (PTH) and serum phosphorus levels aligns with current research findings. On one hand, elevated phosphorus concentrations stimulate PTH secretion (secondary hyperparathyroidism); on the other hand, PTH promotes urinary phosphorus excretion and enhances bone resorption [33]. The weak negative correlation between the two variables observed in the model may suggest that, for this specific patient or within this dataset, when other indicators (such as creatinine) are already severely abnormal, the body may attempt phosphorus excretion via PTH action. This could create a subtle “protective” illusion. Thus, the model may have captured this complex physiological feedback mechanism.

Based on individualized prediction results, this patient exhibits elevated creatinine (1228 µmol/L) and blood urea nitrogen (24.7 mmol/L) levels, typically indicating a higher risk of hyperphosphatemia. However, their carbon dioxide combining power (18.6) is significantly low, suggesting the presence of acidosis (itself a strong risk factor). Yet our SHAP analysis revealed a striking and relatively novel finding: PreCO₂CP exhibits a negative effect in hyperphosphatemia. Although metabolic acidosis is known to correlate with bone mineral loss, our model classifies it as a “protective predictor.” This apparent contradiction may reflect a more complex clinical scenario. One possible explanation is that the severity of the patient’s acidosis was “relatively mild” compared to his severely impaired renal function, or that his clinical management may have already included treatments to correct acidosis. This would make the acidosis indicator a “less significant factor” relative to his renal function. This example illustrates the strength of machine learning models in personalized risk assessment: their ability to weigh multiple interacting factors. Based on this capability and the multicenter data from this study, the model can be integrated into electronic health record systems in the future to enable risk stratification in clinical practice.

Although this model demonstrates high predictive performance in multicenter data, its clinical value warrants cautious interpretation. First, the retrospective design of this study precludes establishing causality, and external validation was not feasible due to data limitations, leaving the model’s generalizability to be further evaluated. Despite employing multiple imputation techniques to address missing data, multicenter datasets may still harbor non-random missing (MNAR) patterns, potentially introducing bias. Second, due to limitations in multi-center data collection, several variables critically influencing phosphorus metabolism were excluded from analysis. These include HCO₃⁻ for precise acid-base status assessment, additional cardiovascular disease history and cardiac biomarkers such as ProBNP, medication records for phosphate binders and vitamin D receptor agonists, dietary phosphorus intake, dialysate prescription parameters (e.g., calcium/phosphorus concentration), and indicators affecting dialysis efficiency like vascular access type and blood flow rate. Additionally, the absence of variables reflecting skeletal manifestations of chronic kidney disease-mineral and bone disorder (CKD-MBD), such as fracture history, along with the current model’s focus on capturing surrogate markers of uremic retention rather than direct regulators of phosphorus metabolism, limits the model’s ability to provide an in-depth analysis of phosphorus metabolic regulation mechanisms. BMI is influenced by both fluid status (edema, dry weight adjustment) and muscle wasting in dialysis patients, limiting its accuracy as an indicator of nutritional status or body fat. Future studies may consider using more precise measures of body composition, such as lean body mass or body fat percentage. Furthermore, while exploring the predictive value of machine learning algorithms, we did not directly compare them against traditional regression models as benchmarks. Thus, the incremental predictive performance of machine learning algorithms requires quantification through future studies. This model provides preliminary evidence of the unique advantages of machine learning in modeling complex nonlinear relationships to predict hyperphosphatemia and enhance personalized risk interpretation; it is intended to complement, rather than replace, clinical decision-making. Given the inherent limitations of retrospective data, the primary contribution of this study lies in establishing a methodological framework and a set of priority features. Future prospective studies incorporating more comprehensive clinical variables and undergoing external validation are needed to further evaluate the clinical utility of this model.

Conclusion

The integration of support vector machine algorithms with SHAP values provides a transparent and interpretable framework for predicting hyperphosphatemia risk in hemodialysis patients. This approach assists clinicians in earlier identification of high-risk patients and implementation of more precise hyperphosphatemia management strategies, while also laying the groundwork for future prospective studies and clinical applications.

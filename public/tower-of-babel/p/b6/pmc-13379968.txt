Ray-tracing guided versus topography-guided FS-LASIK for myopia: a comparative study of relative peripheral refraction and visual quality
Authors: Dihua Ao, Jiawei Wu, Bo Zhang, Lu Xiong, Zheng Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTo compare the distribution of relative peripheral refraction (RPR) and higher-order aberrations (HOAs) following ray-tracing–guided (RG) and topography-guided (TG) femtosecond laser–assisted in situ keratomileusis (FS-LASIK), and to investigate their associations with postoperative visual symptoms.MethodsThis study compared the clinical outcomes of RG FS-LASIK and TG FS-LASIK, with 50 eyes from 50 patients prospectively enrolled in each group. Evaluations preoperatively and 3 months postoperatively included visual acuity, manifest refraction, HOAs, RPR, and the quality of vision questionnaire. RPR was assessed via wide-field peripheral refraction and analyzed across eccentricities (5°, 12.5°, 25°, and >25°) within the superior, inferior, nasal, and temporal quadrants.ResultsThe RG and TG groups showed comparable visual outcomes, with no significant differences in efficacy index (1.16 ± 0.22 vs. 1.14 ± 0.21, p = 0.755) and safety index (1.21 ± 0.22 vs. 1.24 ± 0.21, p = 0.816). Both platforms induced changes in HOAs and coma, whereas reduced spherical aberration was found in the RG group. Both procedures led to a reduction in RPR, a trend that became increasingly prominent in the peripheral region. However, the TG group showed relatively uniform RPR changes across the visual field, whereas the RG group exhibited a significant increase in superior–inferior RPR asymmetry after FS-LASIK. RPR was regionally associated with HOAs, with >25° RPR mainly correlated with spherical aberration (r = −0.458, p < 0.001) and HOAs (r = −0.265, p = 0.008), and superior-inferior asymmetry was negatively correlated with spherical aberration (r = −0.357, p = 0.001). Regression analysis indicated that superior RPR (coef = −0.601, p = 0.001) and superior–inferior (S - I) symmetry (coef = 0.652, p = 0.004) were significantly associated with glare, and a negative shift in spherical aberration (coef = 0.917, p = 0.007) was associated with fewer starburst symptoms.ConclusionBoth RG and TG FS-LASIK provide comparable central visual correction, but they exert distinct effects on RPR profiles and HOAs. Postoperative glare was associated with RPR asymmetry, whereas the negative spherical aberration appeared to alleviate starburst symptoms.Trial RegistrationChinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2500106477), retrospectively registered on July 24, 2025.Supplementary informationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12886-026-04921-7.Keywords: Relative peripheral refraction, FS-LASIK, Quality of vision

Introduction

With the increasing prevalence of myopia and its substantial impact on visual health and quality of life, refractive surgical techniques have continued to evolve [1]. Modern femtosecond laser-assisted laser in situ keratomileusis (FS-LASIK) platforms enable highly individualized ablation to meet specific refractive demands. Topography-guided (TG) FS-LASIK utilizes corneal topography to capture 22,000 points across the corneal surface, aiming to reshape the cornea into a more regular form and minimize surface irregularities [2, 3]. In parallel, ray-tracing-guided (RG) FS-LASIK generates a customized optical eye model to simulate light propagation through the entire ocular system, thereby achieving precise and individualized refractive correction [4, 5]. The safety and efficacy of both TG and RG FS-LASIK have been extensively compared and well-established [6, 7]. However, some patients still report subjective visual quality problems, such as glare and halos [7, 8].

Previous studies have predominantly focused on central visual quality. However, visual performance depends not only on foveal optics but also on the optical characteristics across the wider visual field. Peripheral optical quality is particularly relevant for real-world visual tasks, such as driving and sports [9]. Under low-light conditions, pupil dilation allows more peripheral rays to enter the eye, which may introduce optical defocus and higher-order aberrations (HOAs) and thereby degrade retinal image quality [10, 11]. But there is currently insufficient research comparing the differences between RG and TG FS-LASIK on peripheral optical characteristics.

In addition, myopic eyes are often accompanied by relative peripheral hyperopic defocus, and its distribution is related to the irregularity of retinal shape [12, 13]. As a result, this discrepancy can cause a decline in visual acuity and modifications to the form of the retinal image [14]. Manipulation of the corneal tissue during FS-LASIK surgery may alter the peripheral refraction profile, which would affect postoperative vision quality. Therefore, it is crucial to assess these changes following surgery in order to secure optimal visual outcomes for patients.

This study aims to compare the peripheral refraction and HOAs following RG and TG FS-LASIK, and to understand the associations among peripheral refraction, HOAs, and visual symptoms.

Methods

This is a prospective, non-randomized, comparative cohort study. The study was conducted on participants who received ray-tracing guided LASIK (RG) or topography-guided LASIK (TG) for correcting myopia and astigmatism at Guangzhou Aier Eye Hospital. A total of 100 participants were enrolled in this study, and one eye from each subject was randomly selected for analysis. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Guangzhou Aier Eye Hospital (No. GZAIER-2025-IRB-15) and adhered to the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki. The study was registered as a clinical trial (ChiCTR2500106477). All patients provided written informed consent preoperatively.

Inclusion criteria comprised patients aged 18 to 35 years with stable preoperative refractive status maintained for at least 2 years, myopia ranging from −0.50 to −8.00 diopter sphere (DS), astigmatism from 0.00 to −3.00 diopter cylinder (DC), and corrected distance visual acuity (CDVA) of Snellen 20/20 or better. Exclusion criteria consisted of keratoconus, cataract, glaucoma, severe dry eye affecting refraction and visual outcomes, and a history of ocular or intraocular surgery.

Preoperative and postoperative examinations

All patients underwent comprehensive ophthalmic examinations preoperatively and at 3 months postoperatively. These included uncorrected distance visual acuity (UDVA), corrected distance visual acuity (CDVA), intraocular pressure, manifest refraction, and slit-lamp examination. The InnovEyes SightMap (Alcon Laboratories, Inc.) was used to acquire higher-order aberrations (HOAs), including spherical aberration (SA), coma, vertical coma (C7), and horizontal coma (C8), within 4 mm and 6 mm pupil zones.

Peripheral refraction was evaluated in a dark room with natural pupils using a wide-field visual peripheral refractor (Earth100, Ninestars, China). This instrument integrates a Hartmann-Shack wavefront sensor with a galvanometer to determine the refractive state by measuring the wavefront aberrations produced as light passes through the eye. Its measurement algorithms are derived from the visual peripheral refractor (VPR, Voptica SL, Spain), which demonstrated high accuracy in peripheral refraction mapping [15–17].

Peripheral refraction was expressed as relative peripheral refraction (RPR), defined as the difference in spherical equivalent between each peripheral region and the fovea, with hyperopic defocus assigned positive values and myopic defocus negative values. Each measurement yields a value within the range of ±12.5°. By taking repeated measurements at five orientations, a wide-range RPR result is eventually obtained (Fig. 1). The RPR maps were categorized by eccentricity (5°, 12.5°, 25°, and >25°) and partitioned into four anatomical quadrants: superior (S), inferior (I), nasal (N), and temporal (T). The visual quality questionnaire was administered 3 months postoperatively. The Quality of Vision (QoV) Questionnaire, created by McAlinden, consists of 30 Rasch-tested, linear-scaled items organized into three scales. It generates a QoV score that evaluates symptom frequency, severity, and bother, each graded on a 0 – 3 scale [18].

Schematic of RPR measurement protocol and regional partitioning

Surgical procedures

The flaps were created using the WaveLight FS200 (Alcon Laboratories, Inc.). Flap thickness was 110 μm with a diameter of 8.5 mm. The flaps were roundly shaped with superior hinges. All excimer laser ablation for FS-LASIK was performed with the WaveLight EX500 (Alcon Laboratories, Inc.), with an optical zone diameter of 6.5 mm and a transition zone of 1.25 mm. The ablation profile for ray-tracing guided FS-LASIK was generated by the InnovEyes SightMap; the ablation profile for topography-guided LASIK was generated by the Contoura software. All surgeries were performed by a single experienced surgeon (ZW).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 25.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk). For continuous variables, after testing for normality with the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, data were presented as mean ± standard deviation or median (range). For normally distributed continuous variables, the independent samples t-test was applied for inter-group comparisons and the paired t-test for intra-group comparisons. For skewed data, the Mann-Whitney U test was employed for inter-group comparisons and the Wilcoxon signed-rank test for intra-group comparisons. Spearman correlation analysis was performed to assess the relationships between variables. Predictors of QoV scores were examined using ordinal logistic regression analysis, while a score of 0 – 3 was defined as mild, 4 – 6 as moderate, and 6 – 9 as severe. A p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Visual acuity and refraction outcomes

All surgical procedures were uneventful, with no intraoperative or postoperative complications such as flap issues, infection, or diffuse lamellar keratitis. No statistically significant differences were observed in terms of visual acuity, manifest refraction, corneal curvature, pupil size, Q value, corneal thickness, and ablation depth between the two groups (Tables 1 and 2). Figure 2 shows standardized graphical representations of visual and refractive outcomes. The proportion of eyes achieving UDVA of 20/12.5 was comparable between the RG and TG groups (RG = 16% vs. TG = 8%, p = 0.356). The efficacy index (1.16 ± 0.22 vs. 1.14 ± 0.21, p = 0.755) and safety index (1.21 ± 0.22 vs. 1.24 ± 0.21, p = 0.816) showed no statistically significant differences between the RG and TG groups.

Relative peripheral refraction outcomes

Changes in RPR for both groups are shown in Table 4 and Fig. 3. At 3 months postoperatively, both groups showed a shift of peripheral hyperopic defocus towards myopic defocus. The change in RPR was more pronounced in the far peripheral regions (>25°) compared to the central and paracentral regions in both groups (p < 0.001). The RG group showed more pronounced changes in the superior and nasal quadrants, whereas the TG group demonstrated relatively uniform changes across all quadrants. The change in RPR in the inferior region was more pronounced in the TG group compared to the RG group (RG: −0.128 ± 0.504 vs. TG: −0.303 ± 0.596; p = 0.032). At 3 months postoperatively, superior - inferior peripheral defocus asymmetry increased in the RG group but decreased in the TG group compared with preoperative values (RG: 0.066 ± 0.508 vs. TG: −0.045 ± 0.526, p = 0.025).

Visual quality questionnaire results

The prevalence of visual quality symptoms at 3 months postoperatively for both groups is shown in Fig. 4. The most frequently reported symptoms in both groups were glare and halos, with approximately 10% indicating these symptoms occurred frequently. In terms of severity, glare was rated as the most severe symptom, 16% of patients in the RG group and 10% in the TG group reported moderate to severe glare. Regarding the level of bothersome, both glare and halos were relatively prominent issues in patient feedback. No statistically significant difference was observed in the total QoV scores between the two groups (RG: 11.43 ± 8.58 vs. TG: 9.42 ± 8.46; p = 0.539).

Relationship between changes in RPR and aberrations

To investigate the correlation between corneal factors and RPR on post-operative optical outcomes, a Pearson correlation analysis was conducted (Fig. 5). The ΔQ value was significantly and positively correlated with the ΔSA at the 6 mm zone (r = 0.288, p < 0.01). A significant positive correlation was also found between ΔQ value and total HOAs at the 6 mm zone (r = 0.259, p < 0.05). Changes in corneal curvature showed weak positive correlations with the C7 (vertical coma) component at the 4 mm zone (r ranging from 0.211 to 0.222, all p < 0.05), while the ΔQ value demonstrated a negative correlation with C7 (r = −0.246, p < 0.05).

Correlation analysis between corneal parameters, RPR, and HOAs. *P＜0.05, ** P＜0.01

At the 4 mm pupil size, changes in spherical aberration (Δ4 mm SA) showed significant negative correlations with RPR changes in the >25° field (r = −0.332, p = 0.001), the inferior field (r = −0.338, p = 0.001), and the nasal field (r = −0.235, p = 0.018). In addition, changes in 4 mm coma aberration (Δ4 mm Coma) were weakly correlated with >25° RPR changes (r = −0.203, p = 0.043).

At the 6 mm pupil size, the associations became more pronounced. Changes in spherical aberration (Δ6 mm SA) demonstrated strong negative correlations with >25° RPR changes (r = −0.458, p < 0.001), inferior RPR changes (r = −0.348, p < 0.001), and nasal RPR changes (r = −0.214, p = 0.033). Changes in total HOAs (Δ6 mm HOAs) were also significantly correlated with >25° RPR changes (r = −0.265, p = 0.008). Moreover, vertical coma at 6 mm (Δ6 mm C7) showed a correlation with superior RPR changes (r = 0.199, p = 0.047). No significant correlations were detected in other regions. In addition, Δ4 mm SA was negatively correlated with superior-inferior asymmetry (ΔS - I) (r = −0.357, p = 0.001), whereas Δ6 mm coma was positively correlated with temporal–nasal asymmetry (ΔT - N) (r = 0.199, p = 0.044).

For glare, postoperative superior RPR was negatively associated with symptom severity (coef = −0.601, p = 0.001). Greater postoperative superior–inferior (S - I) RPR asymmetry, together with its postoperative increase, was significantly associated with a higher risk of glare (coef = 0.652 and 0.414, p = 0.004, 0.048, respectively). Patients with a large pupil size tended to have more starbursts postoperatively (coef = 1.096, p = 0.03). The negative shift in spherical aberration was associated with fewer starbursts at 6 mm pupil size (coef = 0.917, p = 0.007).

Discussion

Personalized ablation strategies based on individual ocular parameters represent a new trend in corneal refractive surgery. Despite excellent postoperative central visual acuity and refractive outcomes, some patients still report visual disturbances. However, visual quality is influenced not only by central optics but also by peripheral optical properties. This study compared wide-field peripheral refraction and HOAs following RG and TG FS-LASIK, while exploring the associations among these optical metrics and visual symptoms.

The results demonstrated that RG and TG were comparable in terms of accuracy and safety for central visual correction. Previous studies have reported similarly favorable outcomes for both techniques. Yuan reported UDVA of 20/16 or better in 94% of eyes in the ray-tracing guided LASIK [19]. Rush reported that UDVA was 20/12.5 or better in 87% of eyes after topography-guided LASIK [20]. Cao found that the ray-tracing guided LASIK exhibited better UDVA than the topography-guided LASIK, with 48% of eyes achieving UDVA of 20/12.5 or better [21].

Although central visual outcomes were comparable between the two techniques, notable differences emerged in HOAs. In line with previous studies, this study found that both ray-tracing-guided and topography-guided procedures resulted in increases in total HOAs and coma, with a pronounced negative shift in vertical coma [14, 15]. However, a significant reduction in spherical aberration was observed in the RG group, consistent with previous reports [7, 21]. The reduction in spherical aberration after RG FS-LASIK may result from the ablation design, which aims to optimize total ocular HOAs through a personalized eye model. By integrating corneal topography and whole-eye wavefront data, the algorithm intentionally induces a mild negative corneal spherical aberration to compensate for intraocular aberrations [21].

In addition, significant differences were also observed in postoperative RPR changes. At 3 months postoperatively, both groups showed a decrease in RPR, with the change being more pronounced in the far peripheral regions (>25°). Due to the decreasing stromal ablation from the center to the periphery, the refractive correction effect on the cornea also decreases centrifugally, leading to differential changes between the center and periphery. This peripheral refractive shift has been consistently reported after FS-LASIK or SMILE [22–24].

Postoperative peripheral defocus asymmetry was also evaluated in this study. In the RG group, RPR changes were more prominent in the superior and nasal regions, leading to a significant increase in superior–inferior asymmetry, whereas the TG group showed relatively uniform RPR changes across all quadrants. Previous studies have consistently shown more pronounced changes in superior peripheral refraction. Zhang et al. reported significant refractive changes in the superior region following SMILE [25], while Queirós et al. observed a significant myopic shift in peripheral defocus at the superior 20° location after LASIK [26]. Potential explanations may involve the position of the flap hinge or incisions and associated wound healing. Previous studies have shown that ray-tracing guided LASIK is associated with more prominent epithelial remodeling in the inferotemporal region, whereas epithelial thickness after topography-guided LASIK tends to remain relatively stable in the paracentral zone [25, 27]. The more pronounced superior-inferior RPR asymmetry observed in the RG group may be partly attributable to non-uniform corneal epithelial remodeling, which can introduce regional irregularities on the anterior corneal surface.

To further investigate the correlation, we analyzed the relationships among corneal structural parameters, HOAs, RPR, and subjective visual quality. Corneal curvature and asphericity were associated with HOAs, whereas central corneal thickness and ablation depth showed no significant correlation. These findings may suggest a potential association with peripheral refractive profiles, given the role of corneal geometry in off-axis light propagation. In the present study, spherical aberration and total HOAs were negatively correlated with far peripheral refraction (>25°), whereas coma was associated with asymmetry in peripheral refractive profiles. These findings are in line with previous reports suggesting that spherical aberration is mainly correlated with far peripheral defocus, while coma reflects directional asymmetry in optical quality [28]. A plausible explanation is that peripheral retinal image formation relies more on off-axis incident rays, which are particularly sensitive to corneal shape and optical irregularities.

The reported visual symptoms of patients after FS-LASIK, including glare, halos, and night driving difficulty, are common [29, 30]. Schallhorn et al. found that large pupils had more symptoms in the early postoperative period after LASIK [31], whereas another study reported no significant correlation between pupil size and postoperative visual symptoms [32]. In our study, the preoperative pupil size was found to be a risk factor for the starbursts; however, other visual symptoms were not related to mesopic pupil size. It is necessary to evaluate the factors that affect patient satisfaction. We found that the superior RPR and superior-inferior asymmetry were positively correlated with the glare. Rosén et al. investigated the effects of defocus on peripheral visual task performance and demonstrated that even low levels of defocus can significantly degrade performance in most peripheral visual tasks [33]. In this context, postoperative anisotropy of RPR across retinal quadrants may increase regional differences in peripheral image blur, thereby contributing to adverse visual perception [34]. Of note, the negative shift of SA was associated with the starbursts symptom, which is consistent with the findings of a previous study [8]. Spherical aberration plays a role in determining the distribution of peripheral light rays and the shape of the point spread function. This may be explained by a decrease in the focal separation between central and peripheral rays, resulting in a more compact retinal image profile and reduced peripheral light spread [35].

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the sample size was relatively small, and future studies would benefit from enrolling larger cohorts. Second, the follow-up period was limited to 3 months, necessitating longer-term outcomes. Finally, the influence of peripheral defocus on visual quality requires further substantiation through additional evidence.

Conclusion

Both RG and TG FS-LASIK resulted in a reduction in RPR; however, the RG group exhibited a more pronounced superior-inferior asymmetry of RPR postoperatively. Additionally, a significant shift toward negative spherical aberration was observed following RG. The superior-inferior asymmetry of RPR was associated with glare, while the negative shift in spherical aberration appeared to correlate with the alleviation of starbursts.

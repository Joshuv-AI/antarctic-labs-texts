Multi-channel deep learning radiomics model based on contrast-enhanced CT for predicting postoperative prognosis in laryngeal carcinoma
Authors: Haomiao Ma, Wenfeng Wei, Junhong Zhang, Li Liang, Lingyu Zhang, Wencan Wang, Qian Zhang, Qi Zhang, Yanhong Hao, Zhilin Li, Lingjie Wang, Hua Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAccurate prediction of prognosis and risk stratification in patients with laryngeal cancer can inform appropriate treatment decision-making. This study aims to develop a multi-channel deep learning radiomics model based on contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CECT) for predicting postoperative overall survival (OS) in patients.MethodsA total of 272 patients with laryngeal cancer were retrospectively recruited from two hospitals between January 2016 and July 2021. Specifically, 156 patients were enrolled from Center 1 as the training cohort, and 116 patients from Center 2 as the external test cohort. Two imaging signatures, reflecting phenotypes of the radiomics and multi-channel deep learning features, were constructed using pretreatment venous-phase CECT images. Feature selection involved reproducibility evaluation, Spearman correlation coefficient, and least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression. Ten machine learning algorithms were employed to construct the signatures. An integrated Deep Learning Radiomics Nomogram (DLRN) was subsequently developed to predict OS. Predictive performance was evaluated using the area under the curve (AUC), calibration curves, and decision curve analysis (DCA). Kaplan–Meier survival curves derived from the DLRN were used for patient risk stratification, and subgroup analyses were conducted to validate the model’s robustness.ResultsThe DLRN demonstrated satisfactory prognostic performance for laryngeal cancer. In the external test cohort, the 1-, 2-, and 3-year AUC values were 0.74, 0.75, and 0.80, respectively, and achieved a C-index of 0.73, outperforming individual models. Calibration curves and DCA indicated excellent calibration and the highest clinical net benefit. Subgroup analyses confirmed consistent DLRN performance across clinical stages, age groups, and surgical modalities.ConclusionThe proposed multi-channel deep learning radiomics model showed promising performance for predicting OS in laryngeal cancer patients. This approach may support individualized risk stratification and assist clinical decision-making in patients with laryngeal carcinoma.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-025-14912-4.

Introduction

Laryngeal carcinoma is one of the most prevalent malignant tumors in the head and neck region, with laryngeal squamous cell carcinoma accounting for approximately 85–95% of all laryngeal cancer cases [1]. According to statistics from the International Agency for Research on Cancer, more than 180,000 new cases of laryngeal cancer were diagnosed worldwide in 2020, resulting in over 99,000 deaths attributed to the disease [2]. In clinical practice, the most common treatment modalities for early-stage (stage I–II) laryngeal cancer include partial laryngectomy, laser resection, or radiotherapy [3]. However, for patients with advanced-stage (stage III–IV) laryngeal cancer, total laryngectomy combined with other treatment approaches is predominantly employed to reduce the risk of recurrence and metastasis [4]. Despite rapid advancements in current treatment protocols for laryngeal cancer, patient survival rates have not shown significant improvement [5, 6]. Therefore, accurate preoperative prediction of risk stratification and prognosis in patients with laryngeal carcinoma could facilitate optimized treatment decision-making and early clinical intervention.

The current prognostic stratification system for laryngeal cancer primarily relies on the 8th edition of the Tumor-Node-Metastasis (TNM) staging system published by the American Joint Committee on Cancer [7]. Nevertheless, the TNM system, which is mainly based on clinical and pathological data obtained from computed tomography (CT) and Magnetic resonance imaging, lacks detailed individualized information and is susceptible to subjective interpretation by clinicians, resulting in suboptimal performance and limited practicality [8]. Radiomics, a novel quantitative image analysis technique, provides deeper insights into tumor biology by automatically extracting and analyzing high-throughput quantitative features from medical images, thereby supporting clinical decision-making [9, 10]. However, its robustness and scalability may be affected by variability in image acquisition, reconstruction, and preprocessing procedures [11, 12]. Deep learning (DL), an emerging machine learning approach, has demonstrated superior performance compared to traditional radiomics in image classification, object detection, and segmentation tasks [13, 14]. DL models can autonomously extract deeper-level features from medical images through multilayer convolutional architectures without predefined feature rules, significantly enhancing the accuracy and efficiency of image analysis [9, 15].

In recent years, a growing trend in medical image analysis has been the integration of radiomics with DL to achieve improved performance. This hybrid strategy has been widely applied in disease diagnosis, staging, and prognostication [16–18]. A previous study showed that a combined deep learning radiomics model based on contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CECT) significantly outperformed individual radiomics and DL models in distinguishing between stages I-II and III-IV of laryngeal cancer (p-values of 0.03 and 0.02, respectively), and only the combined model surpassed radiologist interpretations(p < 0.05) [19]. However, there has been no research on the survival prediction of laryngeal cancer based on deep learning radiomics analysis so far.

Therefore, the objective of this study is to develop a multi-channel deep learning radiomics model based on CECT to accurately predict overall survival (OS) in patients with laryngeal carcinoma following surgery, thereby providing a more robust scientific basis for personalized treatment planning and prognostic assessment.

Materials and methods

Patients

This retrospective study enrolled 414 patients diagnosed with laryngeal cancer who underwent surgical treatment at Shanxi Provincial Cancer Hospital (Center 1) and First Hospital of Shanxi Medical University (Center 2) between January 2016 and July 2021. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) histologically confirmed diagnosis of laryngeal carcinoma through surgery or biopsy; and (2) CECT examination performed within 2 weeks before surgery, with complete imaging data available. The exclusion criteria were: (1) receipt of preoperative chemotherapy or radiotherapy; (2) incomplete clinical baseline data or follow-up information; and (3) CT images with significant artifacts. Finally, a total of 272 patients were enrolled in this study, with 156 patients from Center 1 designated as the training cohort and 116 patients from Center 2 as the external test cohort. A flowchart illustrating patient selection is provided in Fig. 1. Clinical and radiological information—including age, gender, history of smoking and alcohol consumption, chemoradiotherapy, tumor location, tumor size, T stage, N stage, clinical stage, and surgical approach—was collected for all patients and subjected to statistical analysis. Follow-up data were obtained from outpatient, inpatient medical records, or telephone interviews. Patients were followed every six months during the first two years after surgery and annually thereafter. OS was defined as the time from surgery to death from any cause or last follow-up. Imaging protocol

All patients included in this study underwent CECT scans of the neck soft tissues using various imaging systems devices. The scanning range typically extended from the inferior margin of the foramen magnum to the superior margin of the aortic arch. During image acquisition, patients were positioned supine with the cervical region fully exposed. Following non-contrast scanning, venous-phase images were acquired approximately 65 s after the intravenous injection of 60–80 mL of iodinated contrast medium via the antecubital vein. The CT acquisition parameters for the different centers are provided in Supplementary Table S1.

Image segmentation

Image segmentation was performed by Radiologist 1 (with 5 years of experience in head and neck imaging), who independently delineated tumor boundaries slice by slice using the open-source software ITK-SNAP (version 3.8.0, www.itkSnap.org). To assess intra-observer reliability, Radiologist 1 re-segmented 30 randomly selected cases after a one-month interval. In addition, Radiologist 2 (with 12 years of experience in head and neck imaging) segmented the same 30 cases to evaluate inter-observer agreement. Reproducibility was assessed using intraclass and interclass correlation coefficients (ICCs); features with ICCs greater than 0.85 were considered highly consistent and stable. Detailed preprocessing steps are described in Appendix S1 of the supplementary materials.

Radiomics feature extraction

Radiomics features were extracted from the three-dimensional tumor volumes using the Pyradiomics software package (version 3.0.1). The extracted features included shape, first-order, and texture features. Shape features describe the three-dimensional morphological characteristics of the tumor, offering spatial insights into its structural configuration. First-order features captured the distribution of voxel intensities within the segmented region using commonly applied statistical metrics. Texture features quantified the variation in gray-level patterns among neighboring voxels, reflecting intratumoral heterogeneity.

DL feature extraction

In this study, rectangular bounding boxes were used to crop the cross-sectional image of the largest tumor slice, along with images from the third and fifth layers above and below it. These cropped images were combined into a five-channel input for the DL network. Each cropped region of interest was uniformly resized to 224 × 224 pixels and normalized using z-score standardization to optimize the feature distribution. To enhance the model’s generalization ability and performance, several data augmentation techniques were applied, including random cropping, horizontal flipping, vertical flipping, rotation, and scaling.

We evaluated the performance of several prominent convolutional neural networks (CNNs), including ResNet18, ResNet34, ResNet50, DenseNet121, VGG11, and MobileNetV2, to improve upon traditional CNN-based models. Comparative analyses were conducted to identify the most suitable architecture for the specific research objectives. To ensure model adaptability across patient populations with high variability, transfer learning was employed. This approach involved initializing the networks with pre-trained weights from the ImageNet database [20], thereby improving generalization across diverse datasets. During training, the five-channel tumor images and corresponding patient labels were input into a multi-channel CNN to predict the probability of death in patients with laryngeal cancer. The softmax cross-entropy loss function was used for network initialization, and training was conducted using the Stochastic Gradient Descent optimizer with a learning rate of 0.001, batch size of 32, and 200 epochs. Further details regarding the CNNs training process are provided in Appendix S2 of the supplementary materials.

Following model training, 512 features were extracted from the penultimate layer of the optimal DL network, ResNet18. Additionally, gradient-weighted class activation mapping (Grad-CAM) [21] was used to visualize the tumor regions identified by the model, thereby aiding prognostic decision-making. The construction and optimization of the CNNs were performed using an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4070 GPU with the PyTorch framework.

Feature selection and signature construction

All radiomics features were first transformed into a normal distribution using z-score standardization. Feature selection was then conducted through a four-step process: (1) ICCs were calculated to assess inter-observer and intra-observer reproducibility, and features with ICCs greater than 0.85 were retained; (2) independent t-tests or Mann–Whitney U tests were used to identify features with P-values less than 0.05; (3) Spearman correlation coefficients were computed, and features with high inter-feature correlation (r > 0.90) were removed; and (4) the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression was applied with 10-fold cross-validation to select features with non-zero coefficients, using the regularization parameter (λ) based on the minimum criterion.

The selected features from the LASSO analysis were subsequently used to construct radiomics models using ten machine learning algorithms: logistic regression (LR), support vector machine, k-nearest neighbors, random forest, extra-trees, gradient boosting, XGBoost, LightGBM, AdaBoost, and multilayer perceptron. For the DL model, a similar feature selection and modeling procedure was followed. In this study, the output probabilities generated by the machine learning models were defined as the radiomics signatures and DL signatures.

Combined model construction and evaluation

Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were performed on clinical and radiological characteristics to identify independent prognostic factors, based on which an LR-based clinical model was developed. The best-performing radiomics and DL signatures, together with the selected independent clinical and radiological predictors, were integrated into a multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression model to construct a combined nomogram—referred to as the Deep Learning Radiomics Nomogram (DLRN).

Associations between the radiomics signature, DL signature, clinical model, and DLRN with OS were evaluated in both the training and external test cohorts. In the training cohort, the predicted mortality probability by the DLRN model was dichotomized using the optimal cutoff point based on the Youden index to stratify patients into high-risk and low-risk groups. This cutoff was subsequently applied to the external test cohort. Kaplan–Meier survival analysis and the log-rank test were used to assess differences in survival outcomes between the two groups. To ensure the robustness of the findings, subgroup analyses were conducted based on clinical stages, age groups, and surgical approach. Model predictive performance was evaluated using Harrell’s concordance index (C-index), hazard ratio (HR), and area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) of the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve (ROC). DeLong’s test was applied to compare the discriminative power of different models. Calibration curves were plotted to assess agreement between predicted and observed outcomes, and decision curve analysis (DCA) was performed to evaluate the clinical utility of each model.

Statistical analysis

The Shapiro–Wilk test was used to assess whether the continuous variables in the two cohorts conformed to a normal distribution. Variables following a normal distribution were reported as mean ± standard deviation and compared using the independent samples t-test. For variables not normally distributed, values were expressed as median and interquartile range, and intergroup comparisons were conducted using the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables were presented as frequencies (percentages) and compared between groups using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. A two-tailed p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS (version 27.0) and Python (version 3.7.12).

Radiomics and DL signatures construction and validation

A total of 14 radiomics features and 18 DL features were ultimately retained. The feature distributions and detailed selection procedures are provided in Supplementary Appendix S3 and Figures S1–S3. Based on these selected features, we conducted a comparative evaluation of ten machine learning classifiers using both the training and external test datasets. Random forest was subsequently used to construct the radiomics signature, and AdaBoost was used to develop the DL signature, as these classifiers demonstrated superior performance in both cohorts, respectively (Supplementary Figures S4–S5).

In terms of predicting the OS of laryngeal cancer patients, the multi-channel DL signature demonstrated better predictive performance compared to the radiomics signature. Specifically, in the training cohort, the 1-year, 2-year, and 3-year AUC values of the DL signature reached as high as 0.87 (95% CI: 0.80–0.93), 0.87 (95%CI: 0.80–0.93), and 0.90 (95% CI: 0.85–0.95), respectively, with a C-index of 0.85. In contrast, the 1-year, 2-year, and 3-year AUC values of the radiomics signature were 0.83 (95% CI: 0.72–0.95), 0.85 (95% CI: 0.77–0.93), and 0.89 (95% CI: 0.83–0.95), respectively, with a C-index of 0.84. In the external test cohort, the C-index values of DL and radiomics signatures were 0.72 and 0.73, respectively, achieving relatively satisfactory performance. Among them, the AUC values of 1-year, 2-year, and 3-year of DL signature were 0.72 (95% CI: 0.60–0.85), 0.69 (95% CI: 0.57–0.81), and 0.75 (95% CI: 0.66–0.85) respectively; while the AUC values yielded by radiomics signature were 0.70 (95% CI: 0.52–0.88), 0.73 (95% CI: 0.59–0.87), and 0.76 (95% CI: 0.65–0.87) respectively (Table 3). Supplementary Figure S6 illustrates the Grad-CAM visualization of the ResNet18 used to identify and localize prognostically relevant regions in laryngeal cancer. Pixel weight distributions are depicted using color gradients, where high-risk patients show more extensive activated regions compared to low-risk patients.

Discussion

In this study, we developed a DLRN model using CECT images. This model integrates radiomics signature, multi-channel DL signature, and independent clinical and radiological features to predict OS in patients with laryngeal cancer. Notably, the DLRN model demonstrated satisfactory predictive performance, in the external test cohort, the 1-, 2-, and 3-year AUC values were 0.74, 0.75, and 0.80, respectively, and it achieved the C-index of 0.73. Furthermore, the model effectively stratified patients into high-risk and low-risk groups. These findings suggest that the DLRN model may enable personalized, non-invasive, preoperative OS prediction for laryngeal cancer patients, with strong robustness and generalizability.

Radiomics provides a non-invasive approach to characterizing tumor biological behaviors through the extraction and analysis of high-throughput quantitative features from medical images [22, 23]. In recent years, several studies have used traditional handcrafted radiomics methods to analyze CT images for predicting prognosis in patients with laryngeal carcinoma. Chen et al. [24] constructed a radiomics model using CECT to predict the OS of laryngeal cancer patients, based merely on three selected radiomic texture features; the model attained a C-index of 0.75 in the validation set. In another study, radiomic features were extracted from mid-radiotherapy images to construct models for predicting OS in patients with laryngeal cancer and hypopharyngeal cancer, in the validation cohort, the AUC of the mid-radiotherapy peritumoral model was 0.77, while that of the intratumoral model was 0.79 [25]. However, most of these studies were single-center and relied solely on internal validation, which may increase the risk of overfitting. By contrast, our study employed external validation and developed a radiomics model capable of predicting OS in laryngeal cancer patients, demonstrating predictive performance comparable to prior studies (C-index of 0.73 in the external test cohort). Among the 14 features selected for radiomics model construction in our study, most were texture features, which provide detailed insights into spatial voxel distributions. Specifically, Neighborhood Gray-Tone Difference Matrix features quantify image smoothness, while Gray-Level Size Zone Matrix features capture image roughness. Both feature types reflect gray-level variations between central voxels and their surrounding neighbors. These texture features are effective in representing intratumoral heterogeneity, thereby supporting accurate prognostic prediction [26, 27].

DL is an advanced machine learning technique capable of performing image analysis in an end-to-end manner within clinical applications. DL has shown considerable potential in predicting tumor grade, treatment response, and prognosis across various cancer types [28]. Previous studies have used two-dimensional images of the largest tumor slice to construct DL models for prognostic prediction in patients with non-small cell lung cancer [29] and rectal cancer [30]. However, relying solely on the largest cross-sectional image may lead to the omission of essential contextual information. In this study, we adopted a more comprehensive approach by incorporating not only the largest tumor slice but also the third and fifth slices above and below it. This resulted in a richer dataset encompassing both anatomical and contextual information. Using multiple parallel convolutional layers to process this data, the DL network was able to capture a broader spectrum of spatial tumor heterogeneity, thereby improving its ability to predict tumor progression, recurrence risk, and overall prognosis. As a result, the model demonstrated sound predictive performance in the external test cohort, with 1-, 2-, and 3-year AUC values of 0.72, 0.69, and 0.75, respectively. Grad-CAM was employed as a visualization tool to highlight tumor regions most influential to model predictions, using color-coded activation heatmaps. This method effectively revealed differences in tumor image patterns between patients with varying prognostic outcomes [31]. The activation maps generated in this study showed that images from high-risk patients displayed more prominent and widespread red activation regions. These findings suggest that the multi-channel DL model developed herein can precisely and efficiently identify prognostically relevant tumor regions in laryngeal cancer. This capability is of critical importance for patient risk stratification and informed clinical decision-making [32].

Previous studies have confirmed the feasibility of using a combined deep learning and radiomics model to predict tumor prognosis, yielding promising results. Wei et al. [33] constructed a deep learning radiomics nomogram based on CT images that integrated two-dimensional DL-score, radiomics score, and clinicopathological factors to predict prognosis in muscle-invasive bladder cancer, with the study results demonstrating that this model achieved the highest predictive performance, yielding a C-index of 0.71 in the internal validation set and 0.69 in the external validation set. Nie et al. [34] built a deep learning radiomics nomogram to predict OS in metastatic clear cell renal cell carcinoma, in the test cohort, the time-dependent AUC values of the nomogram (0.60, 0.65, and 0.75 for 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year survival, respectively) outperformed those of traditional prognostic models. In contrast, our study constructed a multi-channel DL network based on CECT, which can incorporate more information about tumor complexity. Building on this, we further integrated radiomics signature, multi-channel DL signature, and key clinical and radiological features—tumor size and clinical stage—to construct the DLRN model. This integrated model accurately predicts postoperative OS in patients with laryngeal carcinoma, outperforming standalone radiomics model, DL model, and traditional clinical model, in the external test cohort, the AUC values for 1-year, 2-year, and 3-year survival were 0.74, 0.75, and 0.80, respectively, with a C-index of 0.73. Subgroup analyses further revealed that the model effectively stratifies patients across different clinical stages, age groups, and surgical approaches into high-risk and low-risk categories, highlighting the DLRN model’s excellent predictive accuracy and strong robustness. In summary, the integration of radiomics and deep learning methodologies enables comprehensive extraction of latent information embedded within CT images. When combined with clinical and radiological parameters, this approach facilitates a holistic assessment of the prognostic outlook and risk profile for patients with laryngeal cancer [35, 36]. Based on these findings, we advocate a more proactive therapeutic strategy for high-risk patients with laryngeal carcinoma, such as combining surgical intervention with adjuvant therapies. In contrast, for low-risk patients, a treatment plan involving surgical resection alone, followed by periodic surveillance through regular follow-up examinations, may be more appropriate.

Although this study achieved meaningful results, it has several limitations. First, as a retrospective analysis study with a relatively small patient cohort, it is susceptible to selection bias. Therefore, we plan to adopt a prospective, multi-center design and enroll a larger number of patients from multiple medical institutions to reduce selection bias. Second, manual segmentation of tumor regions is time-consuming, labor-intensive, and inherently highly subjective, often resulting in inter-observer variability. This highlights the need for fully automated and accurate delineation tools to ensure consistency and efficiency. Finally, the current predictive model relies primarily on radiomics and DL features extracted from CECT images. While these features have demonstrated strong potential for prognostic prediction and risk stratification, they may not encompass all clinically relevant prognostic information for laryngeal carcinoma. Future studies should consider integrating additional data sources, including genetic and pathological information, to further enhance the model’s predictive accuracy.

Conclusion

In conclusion, we developed a multi-channel deep learning radiomics model capable of accurately predicting OS in patients with laryngeal carcinoma and effectively stratifying them into risk categories. These findings are expected to assist clinicians in formulating personalized treatment strategies and optimizing follow-up protocols, contributing to the advancement of laryngeal cancer management toward more precise and individualized care.

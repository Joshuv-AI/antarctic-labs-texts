Adult‐onset CD8‐predominant hemispheric inflammatory epilepsy: Expanding the spectrum of post‐infectious and Rasmussen‐like encephalitis
Authors: Ramya Raghupathi
Published: 2026

Adult‐onset hemispheric inflammatory epilepsy is rare and diagnostically challenging. Classic Rasmussen encephalitis (RE) is predominantly a pediatric disorder characterized by progressive hemispheric atrophy and cytotoxic T‐cell–mediated inflammation. We describe two immunocompetent adults presenting with drug‐resistant focal epilepsy and progressive unilateral cortical abnormalities. Both highlight a possible adult‐onset spectrum of cytotoxic hemispheric inflammatory epilepsy that overlaps with, but does not fully satisfy, classical RE criteria, with implications for biopsy timing and immunotherapy selection.

Rasmussen encephalitis (RE) has an estimated incidence of 2.4 cases per 107 people aged 18 years or younger per year. 1 Adult‐onset RE is rare and often atypical, frequently lacking epilepsia partialis continua, early hemiparesis, or complete Bien diagnostic criteria. 2 The majority of late‐onset cases fulfill Part B rather than Part A Bien criteria, reflecting the diagnostic delay inherent to the attenuated adult phenotype; immunomodulatory therapy achieves a clinically meaningful response in approximately 61% of late‐onset patients even when initiated late (Data S1). 3 Increasing recognition of post‐infectious autoimmune epilepsy, particularly following herpes simplex virus (HSV) encephalitis, where anti‐NMDAR encephalitis may emerge weeks after viral clearance, 4 raises the possibility that cytotoxic inflammatory hemispheric epilepsy in adults may represent a broader immunopathologic spectrum than classical RE alone.

CASE 1

A 33‐year‐old immunocompetent male presented in 2022 with convulsive status epilepticus. MRI demonstrated a right temporal lesion; spectroscopy prompted tumor board concern, leading to craniotomy. Pathology revealed necrotizing encephalitis with perivascular lymphocytic inflammation, reactive gliosis, and focal necrosis. HSV immunohistochemistry was equivocal; empiric valacyclovir was prescribed. He remained seizure‐free until mid‐2023, when focal aware seizures emerged. Serial MRIs (2024–2025) showed progressive FLAIR abnormalities and curvilinear enhancement extending into the right perisylvian cortex, inconsistent with active HSV or neoplasm. Four serial CSF examinations were noninflammatory with negative viral PCR. Comprehensive autoimmune panels (anti‐NMDAR, LGI1, CASPR2, AMPAR, GABA‐A/B, DPPX, GAD65, MOG, and others) were entirely negative in serum and CSF. Multidisciplinary consensus favored post‐infectious or secondary autoimmune inflammatory injury.

CASE 2

A 31‐year‐old woman developed drug‐resistant focal epilepsy at age 29, with focal bilateral tonic–clonic, focal impaired consciousness, and gelastic seizures. Serial MRI over 15 months demonstrated progressive right hemispheric volume loss involving the frontal lobe, insula, perisylvian cortex, basal ganglia, and cerebral peduncle. FDG‐PET showed extensive right frontal, mesial temporal, and thalamic hypometabolism. CSF revealed oligoclonal bands but was otherwise noninflammatory; serum autoimmune panels were negative. Stereotactic biopsy demonstrated CD8‐predominant T‐cell infiltration in gray and white matter without viral etiology, demyelination, or antibody‐mediated pathology, meeting probable Bien criteria for late‐onset RE. Following treatment of latent tuberculosis, rituximab was initiated; the patient was clinically stable at last follow‐up (January 2026).

The pathologic hallmark of RE is CD8+ cytotoxic T‐cell–mediated injury, independent of antibody‐mediated mechanisms, explaining the seronegative profile that renders even comprehensive autoimmune panels uninformative. 3 Case 2 exemplifies this directly.

Post‐infectious autoimmune encephalitis following HSV is best characterized by anti‐NMDAR encephalitis emerging weeks after viral clearance, reported in up to 27% of HSV encephalitis survivors in prospective series. 2 In Case 1, equivocal HSV immunohistochemistry and a history of recurrent herpes labialis raise the possibility that a subclinical viral exposure initiated chronic T‐cell–predominant inflammatory injury, a phenotype distinct from anti‐NMDAR encephalitis, where the effector is cytotoxic rather than humoral and serologic testing is uninformative. Late‐onset RE is clinically heterogeneous: among 102 published cases, epilepsia partialis continua occurred in only 31% and neurologic deficits appeared on average 3 years after onset. 3 Gambardella et al. 5 described a further variant, limited chronic focal encephalitis, in which the inflammatory process remained circumscribed over more than two decades without progressing to diffuse hemispheric atrophy, confirming that restricted distribution should not preclude the diagnosis. Case 2 fits the late‐onset profile. The Bien criteria are met at the probable level (Table 1).

Together, these cases suggest adult hemispheric inflammatory epilepsy exists on a continuum from established RE to post‐infectious cytotoxic encephalitis, connected by shared immunopathology. Both underscore the diagnostic primacy of tissue in seronegative inflammatory epilepsy. Earlier biopsy may enable earlier treatment decisions before cumulative cortical injury accrues, though this remains hypothesis‐generating. The hypothesis, consistent with published RE literature but not directly demonstrated here, that early repeat biopsy in adults with progressive hemispheric inflammatory changes, negative CSF, and seronegative evaluation may enable earlier treatment decisions, since imaging progression may outpace neurologic decline. 6

Regarding immunotherapy, tacrolimus and IVIG remain best‐studied in RE, with prospective data demonstrating delayed deterioration but not reversal of pharmacoresistance. 1 Rituximab, initiated in Case 2, represents an emerging option; six‐month follow‐up is insufficient to assess efficacy. 7 , 8

Limitations include the two‐case design, unconfirmed CD8 predominance in Case 1, and insufficient rituximab follow‐up precluding causal conclusions. Adult‐onset progressive hemispheric inflammatory epilepsy with CD8‐predominant infiltration may represent a post‐infectious or autoimmune spectrum overlapping with RE, needing prospective validation. These cases underscore the importance of longitudinal imaging, early tissue diagnosis, and multidisciplinary evaluation in adults with progressive focal epilepsy and unilateral cortical abnormalities.

Validated HPLC Method for Simultaneous Quantification of Six Triterpenes with Isomer Separation in Commercial Centella asiatica Products
Authors: Rinrada Suntivich, Chartchai Malaphong, Pimchai Chaiyen, Ubolsree Leartsakulpanich, Somchart Maenpuen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Centella asiatica contains pharmacologically important pentacyclic triterpenes that are commonly used as chemical markers for quality evaluation of herbal preparations. However, accurate quantification remains challenging because some analytes are structurally related and chromatographically difficult to resolve. In this study, a validated high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) method was developed for the simultaneous separation and quantification of six major triterpenes in C. asiatica products, namely, asiaticoside B (ASB), madecassoside (MS), asiaticoside (AS), terminolic acid (TA), madecassic acid (MA), and asiatic acid (AA). Because β-cyclodextrin (β-CD) is inexpensive, it was thus selected as a mobile-phase modifier to improve discrimination of closely related triterpene isomers. Baseline separation of the two critical isomeric pairs, ASB/MS and TA/MA, was achieved within 15 min with a total chromatographic run time of 25 min on an ACE Excel C18 column (5 μm, 4.6 × 250 mm) using gradient elution with acetonitrile and 0.2% (v/v) phosphoric acid containing β-CD. Unlike previous CD-assisted HPLC methods that focused on the separation of five triterpenes or selected isomeric pairs, the present method enables, for the first time, simultaneous identification and quantification of six triterpenes, including both ASB/MS and TA/MA isomeric pairs. The method was fully validated according to ICH, Eurachem, and AOAC guidelines. The method showed good linearity, acceptable limits of detection and quantification, satisfactory repeatability and intermediate precision, suitable recovery, and good selectivity. The validated method was successfully applied to commercial C. asiatica products, demonstrating its suitability for routine quality control and phytochemical assessment of herbal raw materials and preparations. Analytical greenness assessment using the Analytical GREEnness (AGREE) metric gave a score of 0.50, indicating a moderate greenness profile, while holistic assessment using the Mosaic-White Analytical Chemistry (WAC) tool gave a Whiteness Index of 68.6, suggesting satisfactory overall method quality based on analytical reliability, environmental impact, and practical applicability. This method provides a practical and reliable approach for the simultaneous determination of six triterpenes in C. asiatica matrices.

Introduction

Centella asiatica, commonly known as Gotu Kola or Indian pennywort, is a perennial herbaceous plant that has been used for centuries in traditional medicines such as Ayurveda, traditional Chinese medicine, and Southeast Asian folk medicine. Given the long history of medicinal use of C. asiatica, its extracts are widely incorporated into pharmaceutical, nutraceutical, and cosmetic formulations. Owing to its rejuvenating and skin-soothing benefits, C. asiatica is also widely used in cosmetics. The pharmacological efficacy of C. asiatica is primarily attributed to its high content of triterpenoids. − Among these compounds, asiatic acid (AA), madecassic acid (MA), asiaticoside (AS), and madecassoside (MS) are the most extensively studied (Figure ) and are commonly used as marker compounds for the quality evaluation of C. asiatica products.

In addition to these major compounds, C. asiatica also contains structurally related triterpenes, including asiaticoside B (ASB), and terminolic acid (TA). The reported biological activities of the six major triterpenes found in C. asiatica are summarized in Table . Structurally, these compounds can be classified into two major triterpenoid skeleton types: ursane-type (AS, MS, AA, MA) and oleanane-type (ASB, TA). Among them, ursane-type triterpenes are the predominant constituents of C. asiatica. AS and MS are glycosylated compounds, whereas AA and MA represent their corresponding nonglycosylated forms. In addition to the ursane-type compounds, C. asiatica also contains minor oleanane-type triterpenes, including ASB, a glycosylated compound, and TA, its corresponding nonglycosylated form (Figure ). Therefore, ASB and TA were included among the target analytes to enable simultaneous determination of both ursane- and oleanane-type triterpenes present in C. asiatica. The ursane and oleanane skeletons differ mainly in the methyl substitution pattern within the pentacyclic backbone, particularly around the C-19/C-20 region. These structural differences can influence physicochemical properties and chromatographic behavior. Variations in biological activity among these skeleton types have been reported in other ursane- and oleanane-type triterpenoids; for example, ursane-type triterpenoids such as ursolic acid often exhibit stronger antidiabetic activity, whereas oleanane-type triterpenoids such as oleanolic acid display more pronounced hepatoprotective effects. These structural similarities and differences highlight the importance of accurately distinguishing these triterpenoids during chromatographic analysis. Therefore, baseline separation of other triterpenes (resolution, R > 1.5) is required to obtain accurate peak integration and unbiased quantitation; even minor coelution can distort area counts, lower precision and recovery, and cause mis-assignment of the closely eluting isomer pairs in complex matrices.

Accurate detection and quantification of these triterpenes are important for analytical characterization, product standardization, and quality control of commercial formulations. From a quality-control perspective, failure to resolve closely related triterpenes such as TA and MA may obscure batch-to-batch variability and compromise product standardization. Because phytochemical composition can vary depending on plant source, processing, and hydrolysis conditions, , accurate differentiation and quantification of these compounds are essential for reliable compositional analysis and regulatory documentation of botanical products.

Previous studies have highlighted the significant challenges in accurately separating and quantifying these triterpenes, , primarily owing to their structural similarities and coelution behavior. Although the simultaneous separation of these six triterpenes using conventional high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) has been demonstrated, this method employed complex mobile phase systems and long run times exceeding 60 min. Other HPLC-based approaches have combined simultaneous quantification of four major triterpenes (MS, AS, MA, and AA) with fingerprint analysis for identification of C. asiatica and classification according to cultivation age; however, these methods did not include ASB and TA or address the simultaneous baseline resolution of both ASB/MS and TA/MA isomeric pairs. Cyclodextrins (CDs) have been investigated as mobile phase additives to enhance chromatographic resolution. − The β-CD has been shown to enhance the separation of triterpene isomer pairs; however, previous studies generally focused on resolving individual isomer pairs, with each pair requiring a separate chromatographic run. − Alternatively, the γ-CD enables the simultaneous separation of five triterpenes (ASB, MS, AS, MA, and AA) within a single run, although the analysis time remained relatively long (∼30 min) and the additive is comparatively expensive. Therefore, despite these advances, a rapid analytical method capable of simultaneously separating all six triterpenes, including the challenging isomer pairs ASB vs. MS and MA vs. TA, within a single chromatographic run remains limited.

Unlike previous work, the developed HPLC method presented herein expands the analytical scope from five to six triterpenes (ASB, MS, AS, TA, MA, and AA) by including TA and enables baseline resolution of two critical isomeric pairs, ASB/MS and TA/MA, in a single chromatographic method. The developed method employs β-CD as a mobile-phase additive together with a gradient mobile phase system consisting of acetonitrile (MeCN) and 0.2% (v/v) phosphoric acid (H3PO4). β-CD was selected because it can promote host–guest interaction-based discrimination of structurally similar triterpene isomers while providing a practical and accessible alternative to γ-CD for routine quality-control laboratories. The developed method enabled simultaneous separation of six target compounds within 15 min, with a total chromatographic run time of 25 min, compared with the previously reported γ-CD-assisted method requiring approximately 30 min to separate the compounds.

For the first time, this study combines simultaneous determination of six C. asiatica triterpenes, resolution of both key isomeric pairs, validation according to the International Council for Harmonization (ICH), Eurachem, and Association of Official Analytical Collaboration (AOAC) guidelines, AGREE-based greenness assessment, and Mosaic-White Analytical Chemistry (WAC)-based holistic assessments for analysis of commercial C. asiatica products. Unlike previous studies − that focused mainly on raw plant materials or selected analyte groups, the present method was validated using commercial C. asiatica products, supporting its practical use for industrial quality control, phytochemical standardization, and regulatory documentation of herbal preparations, supplements, and cosmetic raw materials. Therefore, this study provides a rapid, reliable and practical validated HPLC method for quantitation of the triterpenes in commercial C. asiatica formulations and for routine analytical quality control.

Materials and Methods

General Information

All solvents were of HPLC grade and were used as received. The standard triterpenes including ASB, MS, AS, MA and AA (>98% purity) were purchased from ChemFaces (China). Terminolic acid (TA) was obtained from Wuhan Chem-Norm Biotech Co., Ltd. (Wuhan, China). Catalog numbers, lot numbers, and certificates of analysis (COA) for all standards are provided in the Supporting Information (Table S1). The raw chromatograms and NMR spectra were deposited in Figshare and are publicly accessible at 10.6084/m9.figshare.31442077. β-CD (purity >99.0% HPLC grade) was obtained from Tokyo Chemical Industry (TCI, Japan).

Eight commercially available C. asiatica products (brands A-H) were purchased from both online platforms and retail stores in Thailand. For each brand, three different production batches were obtained, except for brand H, for which only two batches were available. Brands E and F were purchased from online stores in Thailand but were manufactured in the USA.

Preparation of Stock Solution of Standard Triterpenes

Each standard stock solution (2.0–3.0 mg/mL) was prepared by dissolving 2.0–3.0 mg of the respective triterpene standard in 1 mL of methanol (MeOH) to ensure complete dissolution of the triterpenes and compatibility with the HPLC mobile phase, and the solutions were stored at −20 °C until used.

Commercial Sample Preparation and Extraction

Commercially available C. asiatica samples consisted of 9 extracted solutions for brands A–G (3 batches × 3 extraction replicates) and 6 extracted solutions for brand H (2 batches × 3 extraction replicates). For sample preparation, three capsules or tablets were combined in a single 5 mL tube to obtain a representative sample of the batch and minimize variability among individual dosage units. Samples were ground using a mortar and pestle and mixed using a roller mixer for 1 h to ensure homogeneity. An aliquot of 200 mg was further homogenized using a ball-mill grinder for 5 min at 2500 rpm. The homogenized powder was then extracted using a previously described procedure with minor modifications. Briefly, the homogenized powder was accurately weighed (10 mg). This sample mass was selected to provide sufficient analyte concentration for reliable quantification while minimizing matrix interference. Subsequently, 400 μL of MeOH was added to the powder to allow efficient mixing and extraction in a microcentrifuge tube while maintaining a sufficiently concentrated extract for subsequent dilution prior to HPLC analysis. The mixture was sonicated at 30 °C for 1 h to enhance the extraction efficiency of triterpenes from the plant matrix. The extract was then filtered through a 0.2 μm polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membrane, and the filtrate was stored at 4 °C until HPLC analysis, which was conducted within 24 h.

HPLC Apparatus and Conditions

HPLC analysis was performed using an Agilent 1260 Infinity II (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA) equipped with a quaternary pump (G7111A Quat Pump VL), a diode array detector (G7117C DAD HS), and an autosampler (G7129A Vial sampler). The ACE Excel C18 column (5 μm, 4.6 × 250 mm, Avantor, Radnor, PA, USA) was used for chromatographic separation at a controlled temperature at 30 °C.

The mobile phase comprised three solvents: A (4 mM β-CD in 0.2% (v/v) H3PO4), B (MeCN), and C (0.2% (v/v) H3PO4). All solvents were filtered through a 0.45 μm Nylon membrane (Whatman) before use. The gradient program was optimized using six standard triterpenes (Table ), and the retention time of each triterpene was monitored. The flow rate was 1 mL/min, and the injection volume was 20 μL. Detection was performed at a wavelength of 205 nm and a bandwidth of 16 nm. A resolution value (R) > 1.5 was considered to indicate adequate chromatographic separation. Method Performance and Validation

To validate the developed HPLC method and evaluate its performance, linearity, LOD, LOQ, precision, accuracy, and matrix effects were assessed in accordance with the Eurachem and AOAC guidelines. The raw data from replicate measurements were deposited in Figshare and are publicly available at 10.6084/m9.figshare.31442077.

Determination of Linearity Ranges

The linearity of the method was evaluated over a range of triterpene standard concentrations: 1–1000 mg/L for AA, MA, and TA; 2–1000 mg/L for AS and MS; and 5–1000 mg/L for ASB. Calibration curves were generated according to ICH guidelines, and each concentration level was analyzed in triplicate. The linearity was assessed by determining the coefficient of determination (r 2) from plots of peak area vs. analyte concentration.

Determination of LOD and LOQ Values

The LOD and LOQ values were calculated using eqs and in accordance with the Eurachem guidelines. Calibration curves were constructed by plotting the concentration of each triterpene standard (0.5, 1, 2, and 5 mg/L) prepared in triplicate against the corresponding peak areas. The standard error of the response (σ) and the slope (S) of the calibration curve were obtained from the linear regression statistical analysis of the calibration data and used for LOD and LOQ calculation. 2 LOD=3.3σS 3 LOQ=10σS

Greenness and Holistic Assessment

The developed HPLC method was evaluated using two complementary assessment frameworks, which include Analytical GREEnness (AGREE) metric and Mosaic-White Analytical Chemistry (Mosaic-WAC) tools. The AGREE metric was used to assess the environmental sustainability of the method, whereas the Mosaic-WAC tool was used to provide a broader holistic assessment based on analytical performance, greenness, practicality, and innovation.

The environmental sustainability of the developed HPLC procedure was assessed using the AGREE metric, which integrates the 12 principles of Green Analytical Chemistry (GAC) into a single numerical score ranging from 0 (red) to 1 (ideal green). For the evaluation, relevant operational parameters were entered into the AGREE web app (https://agree-index.anvil.app/), including solvent type and volume, run time, temperature, sample mass, and energy requirements. The input data reflected the actual experimental conditions of the developed method: total run time of 25 min, flow rate of 1.0 mL/min (∼25 mL mobile phase waste per injection), column temperature of 30 °C, extraction solvent (MeOH) of 400 μL per 10 mg sample, and sonication for 1 h at 30 °C. The mobile phase consisted of MeCN and 0.2% (v/v) H3PO4 with 4 mM β-CD, free from halogenated or highly toxic reagents.

The resulting AGREE pictogram is displayed as a circular diagram with 12 colored sectors arranged around the circumference, each corresponding to a single GAC principle (1–12). In this assessment, all the principles were assigned equal weights (weight = 2) to ensure that no single criterion dominated the outcome. The color of each sector (from red to yellow to green) represents the individual greenness score for that principle according to the adjacent color scale. The numerical value shown at the center of the circle is the average of all individual principle scores and represents the overall AGREE greenness score of the developed HPLC method.

In addition to AGREE, a holistic assessment was performed based on the concept of WAC, which extends Green Analytical Chemistry by balancing environmental performance with analytical reliability and practical applicability. The method was assessed using the Mosaic-WAC tool, which combines analytical performance (red, 45%), greenness (green, 25%), practicality (blue, 20%), and innovation (violet, 10%) into a single weighted Whiteness Index (WI = 0.45R + 0.25G + 0.20B + 0.10 V), where WI ≥ 60 is considered analytically valid. The assessment was performed using the Mosaic-WAC web tool (https://ahmedahmed122.github.io/Mosaic-WAC-tool/), and the source code is archived on Zenodo (10.5281/zenodo.19540964). The validation data (see later in Section ), number of analytes, matrix type, solvents and their volumes, run time, sample-preparation time, and detection format were entered as inputs.

Results and Discussion

Development of HPLC Method for Separating Six Triterpene Compounds and Their Isomer Pairs

The separation efficiency of the developed chromatographic method was initially evaluated using a C18 column (4.6 × 250 mm, 5 μm) under optimized mobile phase gradient elution containing 4 mM β-CD as a mobile phase additive (Table ). The chromatogram showed that all six standard triterpenes in the mixture were successfully separated at baseline resolution (Figure ). They were eluted within 15 min with a total run time of 25 min, including column washing and re-equilibration. The order of analyte elution with retention time was ASB (5.0 min), MS (5.6 min), AS (6.4 min), TA (10.3 min), MA (11.3 min), and AA (13.9 min) (Figure ).

Chromatogram of six standard triterpenes separated by the developed HPLC condition. Using β-CD as a mobile phase additive and the optimized mobile phase gradient condition, six standard triterpenes were well separated and eluted within 15 min, with a total chromatographic run time of 25 min. The elution order and retention times were (A) ASB 5.0 min, (B) MS 5.6 min, (C) AS 6.4 min, (D) TA 10.3 min, (E) MA 11.3 min, and (F) AA 13.9 min, respectively.

The corresponding chromatographic resolution (R) values between adjacent peaks were 4.39 (ASB/MS), 5.12 (MS/AS), 19.3 (AS/TA), 4.34 (TA/MA), and 9.15 (MA/AA), indicating excellent baseline separation of all analytes (R > 1.5). The results indicate that the addition of 4 mM β-CD as a mobile phase additive in combination with the optimized gradient elution program (Table ) not only improved the separation resolution of the two triterpene isomer pairs (ASB vs. MS and MA vs. TA), but also shortened the triterpene retention in the stationary phase. The results demonstrate the rapid simultaneous separation of six standard triterpenes (ASB, MS, AS, MA, TA, and AA) within 15 min and a total run time of 25 min under the optimized chromatographic conditions.

A comparison of the developed method with previously reported HPLC methods for the analysis of C. asiatica triterpenes is summarized in Table . Previous studies primarily focused on the separation of specific triterpene groups or individual isomer pairs. For examples, Pan et al. , and Kai et al. employed β-CD as a mobile phase additive to separate selected triterpene isomers, whereas other methods required considerably longer chromatographic run times (up to 120 min) or did not report baseline resolution for both isomeric pairs. ,,, Compared with previously reported methods, the developed approach provides a shorter analysis time while maintaining excellent baseline resolution for all analytes, including the two triterpene isomer pairs, making it suitable for routine chromatographic analysis of commercial C. asiatica products. From a practical cost perspective, the present mobile-phase system uses commonly available HPLC solvents and β-CD, which is generally more accessible for routine laboratory use than γ-CD or glucosyl-β-CD. In addition, the method avoids methyl tert-butyl ether (MTBE)-containing mobile phases and extended chromatographic runs, thereby reducing reliance on specialized solvent systems and solvent consumption per complete multianalyte analysis.

The performance of the developed HPLC method for the separation and quantification of the six triterpenes was validated in accordance with the ICH Q2­(R2) guidelines. A linear regression analysis was performed to assess the linearity of the calibration curves (Figure S1). A linear relationship was observed for all the analytes, as described by their respective linear equations (Figure S1 and Table ). Additionally, the calibration curves of all the analytes exhibited excellent linearity, with r2 greater than 0.999 (Table ), suggesting that the method was reliable for quantifying these triterpenes.

The linear quantification working ranges of the developed β-CD-assisted HPLC system for the six triterpenes (20–20,000 ng) were generally greater than those previously reported. For example, the detection range of a system containing γ-CD as an additive for determining five triterpenes (ASB, MS, AS, MA, and AA) was 10–5000 ng, whereas that for a system using a gradient mobile phase of trifluoroacetic acid (TFA) and MeCN containing MTBE for six triterpenes (ASB, MS, AS, TA, MA, and AA) was 50–5000 ng. Notably, the working concentration ranges of triterpenes separated by other previously reported HPLC methods were relatively narrow (e.g., 50–150 ng for the separation of MS and AS, 540–5450 ng for the separation of ASB, MS and AS, and 640–8000 ng for the separation of MS, AS, MA, and AA). These results indicate that the developed method provides a broader linear dynamic range for the simultaneous analysis of six triterpenes, which facilitates flexible quantification of samples with varying triterpene concentrations and reduces the need for extensive sample dilution during routine analysis.

LOD and LOQ Values

In this study, the LOD and LOQ values for each triterpene analyte were determined using the regression-based approach recommended by Eurachem (2014) and ICH Q2­(R2) guidelines. Specifically, the LOD and LOQ were calculated using the equations LOD = 3.3σ/S and LOQ = 10σ/S, where σ represents the residual standard deviation obtained from the linear regression analysis of the low-concentration calibration curve, and S represents the slope of the corresponding calibration curve (analytical response versus concentration). The calculations are summarized in Table and Figure S2.

The LOD (1–20 ng) and LOQ (4–62 ng) of the six triterpenes obtained in this study were higher than those previously reported. A study employing a mobile phase of TFA/MeCN containing MTBE for separating six similar triterpenes reported an LOD of ∼ 0.1 ng; however, the method to determine the LOD was not reported. For the method employing a mobile phase containing γ-CD, the LOD and LOQ were in the range of 0.5–3 and 1.5–8 ng, but the detection limit was calculated using a signal-to-noise ratio approach. For the separation performed using a conventional solvent system without a mobile phase additive, one study reported a higher LOD (74–800 ng) and LOQ (243–2641 ng) for separation of four triterpene analytes, whereas the other reported a lower LOD (0.21 and 0.81 ng) and LOQ (0.64 and 2.45 ng) with the capability to isolate AS and MS. Because the separation methods, equipment, and statistical analyses varied among the studies, the absolute values of the detection limits could not be directly compared.

Although the sensitivity of the present method is moderate compared with some previously reported HPLC methods, the obtained LOD (1–20 ng) and LOQ (4–62 ng) values are sufficient for the practical analysis of commercial C. asiatica products. In this study, individual triterpene contents in commercial samples (see section ) ranged from 0.05 to 26.87 mg/g, which are substantially higher than the LOQ values. Therefore, the method provides adequate sensitivity for routine quality control while enabling rapid simultaneous quantification of six triterpenes. The optimized chromatographic conditions achieve baseline separation within 15 min, with a total chromatographic run time of 25 min, thereby improving analytical throughput and reducing solvent consumption.

Precision

The repeatability and the intermediate precision of the developed HPLC method were determined according to the AOAC guidelines, and their performances, reported as %RSD, are summarized in Table . For repeatability (intraday precision), the %RSD values of six triterpenes were 0.4%–1.6% at 10 mg/L, 0.3%–0.7% at 75 mg/L, and 0.2%–0.4% at 150 mg/L, corresponding to an overall intraday precision range of 0.2%–1.6%. These values were substantially lower than those the AOAC acceptance criteria of 7.3% and 5.3% at concentrations of 10 and 100 mg/L, respectively, indicating excellent repeatability of the developed method. Comparable precision values have been reported for a previous HPLC method using γ-CD as a mobile phase additive, which yielded the 0.5%–1.6% RSD for five triterpenes.

The developed HPLC method revealed distinct distributions of individual triterpenes among the brands. For brand B, TA, MA, and AA were not detected. This observation may be associated with differences in plant source, cultivar, or extraction and processing conditions among commercial products. It should be noted that “not detected (ND)” in this study indicates that the signal was below the detection capability of the method under the current analytical conditions. Therefore, ND does not necessarily imply the complete absence of the compound. In contrast, analytes reported as <LOQ indicate that the compounds were detected but present at concentrations below the limit of quantification.

Detection challenges have also been noted in certain cases. For instance, for brand D, the concentrations of AA in lots 2 and 3 were below the LOQ (0.57 mg/L, Table ). This suggests that some triterpenes may be present at levels that are too low to be reliably quantified using the current method. These issues may stem from lot-to-lot variations in raw materials, formulation differences, degradation during processing, or insufficient quality control. Enhancing the sensitivity of the method may be necessary for accurate profiling of products with low triterpene concentrations.

The variability in the triterpene content was further reflected by the coefficient of variation (%CV) across the brands. Brand C (tablet form) exhibited particularly high %CV values (5%–58%). This high variability may be due to difficulties in removing the tablet coating prior to extraction, which can affect sample preparation and extraction efficiency. Similarly, brand D showed %CV values ranging from 4% to 45%, which may be attributed to incomplete sample homogenization during preparation. Incomplete pulverization or uneven distribution of the active compounds within the sample can lead to sampling heterogeneity and increased analytical variability. Such variability is commonly reported in solid dosage forms in which excipients, binders, or coating materials can interfere with efficient extraction of target compounds.

To mitigate these issues, several strategies may be considered in future studies, including mechanical grinding or cryogenic milling to ensure complete tablet homogenization, removal of coating materials prior to extraction, and optimization of the extraction conditions to improve the recovery of triterpenes from complex matrices. In addition, increasing the sample mass or performing replicate extractions could further reduce sampling variability and improve analytical reproducibility. The uneven distribution of triterpenes is common in herbal formulations, emphasizing the importance of rigorous and standardized sample preparation to obtain representative and accurate results.

This study demonstrated that the developed HPLC method is suitable for identifying and quantifying triterpenes in C. asiatica products that are in powdered or homogenized forms, particularly capsules. However, tablet formulations pose greater analytical challenges owing to difficulties in homogenization and matrix effects. These forms may require modified extraction protocols or further validation to ensure the complete recovery of triterpenes. Overall, the developed method offers a rapid and reliable analytical tool for quantitative evaluation and the quality assessment of triterpenes in commercial C. asiatica products.

Greenness and Holistic Assessment

The environmental sustainability of the developed HPLC procedure was quantified using the AGREE metric, which integrates the 12 principles of GAC into a single numerical score ranging from 0 (red) to 1 (green). The reliability of AGREE as a greenness assessment tool has been supported by a comparative study of four greenness assessment tools by Gamal et al., which reported that AGREE provides reliable numerical evaluation together with the advantages of simplicity, automation, and pictogram-based visualization of weak points requiring further improvement. Based on the experimental parameters entered in the AGREE tool, including solvent type and volume, total run time, column temperature, sample mass, and energy demand, the method achieved an overall AGREE score of 0.50, corresponding to a yellow analytical profile (Figure ), indicating a moderate level of greenness.

Despite these advantages, several factors limited the overall score. The use of MeCN and MeOH, both classified as moderately toxic and flammable organic solvents, contributed negatively to GAC principle 11 (reagent toxicity). Additionally, the mobile phase waste of ∼25 mL per run, HPLC–UV analysis, and 1-h sonication step increased solvent and energy consumption (principles 9, 10, and 12). The use of inexpensive β-CD and commonly available HPLC-grade solvents provides a practical compromise for routine laboratories compared with methods requiring more specialized CD derivatives or MTBE-containing mobile phases. These limitations are typical of conventional reversed-phase HPLC methods and highlight the need for further optimization.

Since AGREE evaluates greenness only, the method was further assessed with the Mosaic-WAC tool, which scores analytical performance, greenness, practicality, and innovation and combines them into a single WI. The method received a WI of 68.6, classified as valid, with module scores of 77 (red), 82 (green), 67 (blue), and 0 (violet) (Figure and Table S6). The red score reflects the validated six-analyte performance. It is not maximal because of the lower recovery of the least-abundant analyte (e.g., ASB) near its quantification limit, in line with the sensitivity considerations noted above. The blue score corresponds to moderate practicality, mainly due to the multistep offline sample preparation, which requires approximately 150 min per analysis. The green score (82) is higher than the AGREE score (0.50) because the two tools differ in basis: AGREE weights 12 green-chemistry principles equally, including instrument energy and the offline multistep workflow, whereas the Mosaic-WAC green module is governed mainly by reagent hazard load and waste volume, both of which are modest in this method.

Although the developed method demonstrates analytical novelty through the simultaneous determination of six triterpenes, baseline resolution of two critical isomeric pairs, validation according to ICH, Eurachem, and AOAC guidelines, and application to commercial products, the innovation component of the Mosaic-WAC tool evaluates a different aspect. Specifically, this component rewards the use of emerging analytical technologies, such as artificial intelligent (AI)-assisted analysis, miniaturized or portable instrumentation, smartphone-based detection, or highly automated analytical formats. Therefore, no points were assigned to the violet module because the present method employs conventional HPLC–UV instrumentation and does not incorporate these emerging or field-deployable analytical technologies. The zero innovation score in the violet dimension therefore indicates that future development could add the greatest value through portable, miniaturized, or smartphone-assisted detection formats, and the Mosaic-WAC framework provides a useful benchmark for comparing such field-deployable approaches with the laboratory-based method reported here.

Several limitations of the present study should also be acknowledged. First, although the developed mobile phase enabled baseline resolution of the target triterpenes, it still relies on MeCN and phosphoric acid; therefore, future work should explore greener solvent systems, where chromatographic resolution can be maintained. Second, UV detection provides practical applicability for routine quality-control laboratories but limits sensitivity for trace-level analysis; future studies may incorporate fluorescence or mass spectrometry detection to improve sensitivity and selectivity. Third, the commercial samples analyzed in this study were obtained from products available in Thailand; broader application should include more brands and products from other C. asiatica-producing or consuming countries, such as India, China, Vietnam, and Madagascar. Fourth, biological matrices such as plasma, saliva, and urine were not evaluated in this study. Further method adaptation and validation would therefore be required before application to pharmacokinetic, clinical, forensic, or bioanalytical studies.

Overall, the developed β-CD-assisted HPLC method achieves moderate environmental sustainability while maintaining robust analytical performance, providing a transparent baseline for future optimization toward greener triterpene analysis workflows. Compared with previously reported HPLC systems employing MTBE-containing gradients or isocratic γ-CD systems with extended run times, the present approach eliminates highly hazardous solvents, achieves analyte elution within 15 min, with a total chromatographic run time of 25 min. These improvements contribute to reduced solvent consumption and shorter analysis time while maintaining reliable chromatographic performance.

Conclusion

This work presents a rapid and efficient HPLC method for separating six key triterpenes (ASB, MS, AS, TA, MA, and AA) and their isomer pairs (MS vs. ASB, and MA vs. TA) within 15 min, with a total chromatographic run time of 25 min, employing a gradient mobile phase system of MeCN and 0.2% (v/v) H3PO4 containing β-CD as a mobile phase additive. All the method performance characteristics, including linearity, LOD, LOQ, repeatability, intermediate precision, recovery, and selectivity, were analyzed according to the ICH, Eurachem, and AOAC guidelines, confirming the accuracy and reliability of the method for quantitative analysis. Application of the method to commercial C. asiatica products revealed substantial variation in total triterpene content among different brands, highlighting the importance of reliable analytical methods for product standardization and quality control.

Furthermore, an analytical green evaluation using the AGREE metric yielded an overall score of 0.5, reflecting a moderate greenness profile. In addition, the holistic assessment using the Mosaic-WAC tool gave a WI of 68.6, indicating a favorable balance among analytical, environmental, and practical aspects of the method. The relatively short analytical cycle, mild-temperature operation, and derivatization-free workflow contribute to improved efficiency compared with more solvent-intensive conventional HPLC methods. Although the reliance on MeCN and MeOH limits full greenness, future optimizations through solvent substitution, system miniaturization, and lower-energy extraction could enhance the sustainability of the method.

Altogether, the developed β-CD–assisted HPLC method provides a robust and validated analytical approach for simultaneous determination of six triterpenes and can support routine compositional analysis and quality control of commercial C. asiatica products.

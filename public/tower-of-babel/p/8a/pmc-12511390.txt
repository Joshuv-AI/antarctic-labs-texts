Transcriptome analysis reveals novel insights into the mechanisms of Davidia involucrata Baill. response to high-light stress
Authors: Shuang Li, Jiankuo Du
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Davidia involucrata Baill. is a rare and endangered plant species that exhibits high sensitivity to environmental stressors. High-light stress significantly impedes its growth and development. However, the molecular mechanisms underlying Davidia involucrata Baill.’s response to high-light stress remain poorly understood. In this study, transcriptome sequencing and bioinformatics analyses were employed to systematically investigate the response of Davidia involucrata Baill. to high-light stress under varying soil moisture and air humidity conditions. The results revealed that under different soil moisture conditions, Davidia involucrata Baill. significantly activated pathways related to reactive oxygen species and heat stress responses in reaction to high-light stress. However, notable differences were observed in the extent of pathway activation: under moist soil conditions, Davidia involucrata Baill. primarily responded to high-light stress via reactive oxygen species-related pathways, whereas under dry soil conditions, it predominantly relied on heat stress response pathways. Furthermore, under non-humidified air conditions, Davidia involucrata Baill. responded to high-light stress and combined stress by activating the MAPK signaling pathway and indole-containing compound biosynthetic process, respectively. We also found that when high-light stress and drought stress occur simultaneously, Davidia involucrata Baill. prioritizes mitigating the damage caused by high-light stress. In summary, this study elucidated the key molecular mechanisms underlying Davidia involucrata Baill.’s response to high-light stress, providing a theoretical foundation for the conservation of rare plant species, enhancement of environmental adaptability, and development of stress-resistant cultivation strategies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-19410-y.

Introduction

Davidia involucrata Baill., commonly known as the pigeon tree, is a rare and endemic species in China, often referred to as the ‘giant panda of the plant kingdom’1. Having survived the Quaternary glaciation2, it is primarily distributed in the complex subtropical mountainous regions of southwestern China1. Due to its poor natural regeneration capacity, low seed germination rate, limited seedling recruitment, and high economic value, Davidia involucrata Baill. is classified as a key protected plant species. Davidia involucrata Baill. has a relatively narrow natural distribution range and exhibits high sensitivity to environmental factors such as light, temperature, and moisture. Therefore, investigating the response of Davidia involucrata Baill. to high-light stress is not only crucial for the conservation of its populations, but also provides valuable insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying plant responses to environmental stress.

Light, as a major environmental factor, regulates plant growth and development through its intensity, quality, and photoperiod3–5. Among these factors, abnormal variations in light intensity and duration can significantly affect photosynthetic efficiency and reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels in plants6. An appropriate level of light is essential for plant metabolism and the synthesis of organic compounds; however, excessive light can lead to photoinhibition or even photodamage6. Currently, high-light stress has emerged as one of the major abiotic factors limiting the normal growth of many plant species. Moreover, high-light stress is often accompanied by other environmental stressors, such as elevated temperatures. Such combined stress conditions can severely impair the normal growth and development of plants7,8.

The effects of high-light stress on plants primarily manifest in four aspects: (1) damage to photosynthetic organs, such as chloroplasts9; (2) accumulation of reactive oxygen species (ROS)10; (3) metabolic disruptions11; and (4) alterations in water balance and stomatal regulation10,12. Excessive light intensity is considered a major limiting factor for the introduction and seedling establishment of Davidia involucrata Baill.. The high-light intensity can scorch the leaves of Davidia involucrata Baill. at various developmental stages, leading to leaf burn or even death in seedlings, and causing defoliation or mortality of nearly mature leaves on the crown of 16-year-old trees. To date, the molecular mechanisms underlying Davidia involucrata Baill.’s response to high-light stress remain unclear. Elucidating its response mechanisms to high-light stress is of great significance for improving its stress tolerance.

As a critical ecological factor, environmental humidity plays a key role in modulating plant responses to adverse conditions. For example, a study by Eugene et al. demonstrated that under heat stress conditions, air humidity influences the growth vigor of maize and wheat by modulating their photosynthetic systems13. Observations of Davidia involucrata Baill. revealed that its growth was notably better under humidified air conditions compared to environments subjected to stress. This finding suggests that environmental humidification can effectively mitigate stress-induced damage and promote plant growth; however, the underlying regulatory mechanisms remain unclear. Clarifying the relationship between air humidification and the response of Davidia involucrata Baill. to high-light stress is crucial for elucidating the adaptive mechanisms of plants under complex ecological conditions.

In this study, functional enrichment and gene network analyses were conducted on transcriptome sequencing data of Davidia involucrata Baill. to identify key genes and potential pathways involved in its response to high-light stress. Molecular docking analysis was further employed to elucidate the regulatory interactions between key genes and phytohormones. Meanwhile, the impact of air humidification on the gene expression patterns of Davidia involucrata Baill. under high-light stress—both as a single stressor and in combination with drought—was investigated to uncover the key molecular mechanisms by which humidification enhances the plant’s stress adaptability. This study provides a theoretical foundation for further elucidating the physiological and molecular mechanisms underlying Davidia involucrata Baill.’s adaptation to high-light stress and offers valuable insights to support its conservation efforts.

Methods

Data grouping and differential expression analysis

On August 20, 2023, Davidia involucrata Baill. seedlings exhibiting uniform growth were selected for experimental treatments. The species identification of the Davidia involucrata Baill. experimental sample used in this study was completed by Liu Ping, a senior engineer at the Hualongshan National Nature Reserve in Shaanxi Province. Key taxonomic traits such as leaf morphology, inflorescence structure, and bract characteristics were systematically compared, and classification accuracy was ensured by referencing authoritative literature such as “Flora Reipublicae Popularis Sinicae”14. The experimental seedlings are sourced from national base of improved tree variety for Davidia involucrata Baill. in Qianjiaping Forest Farm, Pingli County (32° 1’N, 109° 18’E). The seedlings in this base are all bred from wild Davidia involucrata Baill. mother trees in Hualongshan National Nature Reserve, Shaanxi Province. In March 2023, 2 year-old seedlings were transplanted to the experimental site (33° 4’N, 107° 2’E) at the South Campus of Shaanxi University of Technology to establish a long-term observation plot. Currently, the live samples are well preserved and have stable growth status.

A multifactorial experimental design was employed in this study: Group_A (SL_ASM vs. LL_ASM) consisted of a comparative experiment between sufficient light (SL) and limited light (LL) conditions, conducted under adequate soil moisture (ASM, field water-holding capacity). Group_B (SL_RSW vs. LL_RSW) involved a comparative experiment between sufficient light (SL) and limited light (LL) conditions under restricted soil moisture (RSW, maintained at 50–60% of the field water-holding capacity).

Given that the natural distribution range of Davidia involucrata Baill. is typically characterized by mountainous and foggy environments, a nighttime artificial humidification experiment was designed to simulate these conditions: Group_C (SL_ASM_F vs. SL_ASM_NF) consisted of a comparative experiment assessing the effects of nighttime humidification (F) versus no humidification (NF), under conditions of sufficient light (SL) and adequate soil moisture (ASM). Group_D (SL_RSW_F vs. SL_RSW_NF) involved a comparative experiment evaluating the effects of nighttime humidification (F) versus no humidification (NF) under dual stress conditions of sufficient light (SL) and limited soil moisture (RSW, maintained at 50–60% of the field water-holding capacity). PCA and functional enrichment analyses

In this study, principal component analysis (PCA) was performed based on differentially expressed genes (DEGs) to visualize the distribution of samples within each group and to assess the discriminatory power of these genes. To exclude the influence of drought on gene expression in Davidia involucrata Baill., the intersection of DEGs from Group_A (SL_ASM vs. LL_ASM) and Group_B (SL_RSW vs. LL_RSW) was identified, and GO and KEGG enrichment analyses were subsequently performed on these genes. As Davidia involucrata Baill. is a non-model species, the ‘AnnotationForge’ R package (version 1.44.0, https://bioconductor.org/packages/release/bioc/html/AnnotationForge.html)17 was used to construct a custom enrichment analysis database for this species. First, the FASTA sequence files of Davidia involucrata Baill. genes were annotated using the eggNOG-MAPPER online platform (http://eggnog-mapper.embl.de/)18. Based on the annotation results, a custom gene annotation database was constructed, and enrichment analysis was subsequently performed using the ‘clusterProfiler’ R package (version 4.10.1, https://www.bioconductor.org/packages/release/bioc/html/clusterProfiler.html)19.

GSEA of target pathways and identification of key genes

We focused primarily on GO pathways associated with plant responses to high-light stress and performed GSEA in Groups_A and Group_B, using P-value < 0.05 and FDR < 0.05 as screening criteria. Subsequently, we extracted DEGs that were enriched in the target pathway and consistently upregulated in the experimental groups of Group_A and Group_B. Pearson correlation analysis was performed on these genes, with screening thresholds set at an absolute correlation coefficient greater than 0.8 and a P-value less than 0.05. The results of the correlation analysis were visualized as a gene interaction network using Cytoscape software (version 3.10.3, https://cytoscape.org/)20. Finally, key genes within the gene network were identified based on their degree.

Molecular docking

Based on the protein sequence information of the key genes, the three-dimensional structures were predicted using the SWISS-MODEL online platform (https://swissmodel.expasy.org/)21, and the resulting PDB files were saved for further analysis. Model selection was based on the criteria that the Global Model Quality Estimation (GMQE) score was close to 1 and the QMEAN score was close to 0. The SDF files of the three-dimensional structures of the hormone molecules abscisic acid (ABA) and salicylic acid (SA) were obtained from the PubChem database (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/)22. OpenBabel (version 3.1.1, https://openbabel.org/index.html)23 was used to convert the SDF files into PDB format. Molecular docking between proteins and hormone molecules was performed using AutoDock (version 4.2, https://autodock.scripps.edu/)24, and the conformation with the lowest binding free energy was selected as the final docking result. Finally, the docking results were visualized using PyMOL (version 4.6.0, https://pymol.org/)25.

Analysis of gene expression under humidified air conditions

To investigate whether genes involved in high-light stress responses are affected by air humidification, we first identified the intersection of DEGs among Groups_A, Group_B, and Group_C (SL_ASM_F vs. SL_ASM_NF). The logFC trends of the intersecting genes were then examined across each group. Additionally, to further investigate the effect of air humidification on the gene expression patterns of Davidia involucrata Baill. under combined stress conditions, GO enrichment analysis was performed on the DEGs in Group_D (SL_RSW_F vs. SL_RSW_NF). GO pathways with a P-value < 0.001 were considered key pathways, and those directly associated with plant responses to combined stress were selected for further analysis. GSVA enrichment analysis was employed to assess differences in the activity of these pathways between the humidified and non-humidified groups.

Discussion

In this study, transcriptomic approaches were employed to comprehensively investigate the response mechanisms of Davidia involucrata Baill. to high-light stress. Differential expression analysis revealed that 183 genes responded to high-light stress under different soil moisture conditions (wet and dry). These genes were further analyzed through functional enrichment analysis, revealing pathways associated with plant responses to high-light stress. We further performed GSEA on these pathways in Group_A (humid soil environment) and Group_B (arid soil environment). The results showed that the pathways related to ‘response to hydrogen peroxide,’ ‘response to reactive oxygen species,’ ‘response to heat,’ and ‘heat acclimation’ were significantly upregulated under high-light treatment in both groups. Both ‘response to hydrogen peroxide’ and ‘response to reactive oxygen species’ are involved in maintaining ROS homeostasis in plant cells26. Appropriate levels of reactive oxygen species (ROS) can function as signaling molecules that facilitate plant adaptation to abiotic stress27. However, excessive ROS accumulation can lead to oxidative stress, resulting in damage to cellular macromolecules such as plasma membranes, proteins, and DNA28. ‘Response to heat’ and ‘heat acclimation’ are two key biological processes that enhance the ability of plants to tolerate high-temperature stress29,30. These findings suggest that in response to high-light stress, Davidia involucrata Baill. mitigates physiological damage induced by environmental stress through broad activation of ROS-related pathways and heat stress response mechanisms. Previous studies have demonstrated that reducing ROS levels in plants can confer protection against high-light stress31,32.

We also found that different humidity conditions may exert distinct regulatory effects on the response pathways of Davidia involucrata Baill.. Under moist soil conditions (Group_A), ‘response to hydrogen peroxide’ in Davidia involucrata Baill. was most strongly upregulated, suggesting that a humid environment may facilitate rapid perception and response to high-light stress via the reactive oxygen species (particularly hydrogen peroxide) signaling pathway. The study by Daniela et al. demonstrated that although oxidative damage in plants was reduced under humid conditions, hydrogen peroxide was still present33, indicating its role as a signaling molecule involved in regulating the plant’s photoprotective mechanisms. This further suggests that a moist soil environment may enhance the ability of Davidia involucrata Baill. to perceive and respond to high-light stress through hydrogen peroxide-mediated signaling. In contrast, under drought conditions (Group_B), Davidia involucrata Baill. tended to respond to high-light stress primarily through activation of the heat response pathway. It has been reported that plants exposed to drought stress frequently experience reduced transpiration efficiency and limited heat dissipation capacity, leading to increased leaf temperature34. Therefore, plants must rapidly activate heat stress response mechanisms to mitigate the damage induced by high-light conditions. These findings suggest that soil moisture may significantly influence the response strategies of Davidia involucrata Baill. to high-light stress, leading to distinct stress response patterns under different environmental conditions.

To further identify the core genes regulating these pathways, we extracted DEGs that were simultaneously enriched in the four aforementioned pathways and significantly upregulated in both groups and subsequently constructed a gene correlation network. Network analysis identified Dinv14422, Dinv44528, Dinv35255, Dinv17401, and Dinv22790 as core genes within the network, suggesting that these five genes may play pivotal roles in the response of Davidia involucrata Baill. to high-light stress. Further BLASTP annotation analysis of these five genes revealed that Dinv14422 was annotated as a 10 kDa chaperonin, mitochondrial-like [Camellia sinensis], Dinv17401 and Dinv22790 correspond to chaperone protein ClpB1 (Saracenia puria var. burkii), Dinv35255 is homologous to heat shock protein 83 (Helianthus annuus), and Dinv44528 is related to heat shock 70 kDa protein 8 (Cornus flora) [Supplementary Table 10–14). These proteins are generally involved in protein folding, stabilization, and the enhancement of plant stress tolerance35–37. Homology-based evidence, therefore, further supports their potential roles in the stress response of Davidia involucrata. Studies have demonstrated that ABA and SA play important roles in enhancing plant resistance to abiotic stresses, including high-light stress38,39. We hypothesize that ABA and SA may also be involved in the high-light stress response of Davidia involucrata Baill., potentially enhancing its stress tolerance by regulating the aforementioned core genes. To test this hypothesis, ABA and SA were selected for molecular docking analysis with the proteins encoded by the core genes. The results indicated that ABA exhibited more stable binding than SA to all core gene-encoded proteins, except for Dinv35255. Among these, the binding free energy between ABA and Dinv14422 was the lowest, suggesting that ABA may enhance the ability of Davidia involucrata Baill. to adapt to high-light stress primarily by regulating the expression of Dinv14422.

Observations of Davidia involucrata Baill. growth indicated that air humidification effectively improved its physiological condition under high-light stress. Therefore, to further investigate how air humidification modulates the stress response of Davidia involucrata Baill. at the molecular level, we extracted DEGs shared among Group_A, Group_B, and Group_C, and examined their expression patterns across different treatment groups. In this analysis, we combined the DEGs from Group_A (well-watered, high light vs. shade) and Group_B (drought, high light vs. shade) to identify genes with consistent expression trends under both soil moisture conditions, thereby minimizing the confounding effect of soil moisture. We then focused on genes that exhibited opposite expression trends in Group_C (well-watered, high light, air humidified vs. non-humidified), allowing us to specifically highlight transcriptional changes associated with air humidification under high-light stress without introducing the additional impact of drought stress. The results showed that nine genes were upregulated in both the SL_ASM group and the SL_RSW group. Among these, Dinv42070 and Dinv38748 were both associated with the MAPK signaling pathway. The MAPK signaling pathway is a critical signal transduction mechanism in plants for responding to environmental stress, primarily consisting of a three-tiered protein kinase cascade: MAP3K–MAP2K–MAPK40. Studies have shown that under abiotic stress conditions, plants activate the MAPK cascade pathway to regulate the antioxidant system, thereby enhancing their stress tolerance40. Therefore, we speculate that under high-light stress, Davidia involucrata Baill. may enhance its antioxidant capacity by activating the MAPK signaling pathway, thereby mitigating oxidative damage induced by excessive light exposure. Meanwhile, we observed that the expression levels of Dinv42070 and Dinv38748 were downregulated under air humidification treatment conditions. This suggests that air humidification may alleviate cellular oxidative damage induced by high-light stress, thereby diminishing the necessity for activation of the MAPK signaling pathway.

In addition, we further investigated the role of air humidification in Davidia involucrata Baill. under combined high-light and drought stress conditions. Physiological phenotype observations revealed that air humidification significantly improved the growth condition of Davidia involucrata Baill. under combined high-light and drought stress. Differential expression analysis identified 907 DEGs under conditions of high-light, arid soil, and air humidification. GO enrichment analysis revealed that these genes were significantly associated with pathways related to secondary metabolite biosynthesis and redox processes. To further elucidate the effect of air humidification on the dual stress response of Davidia involucrata Baill., GSVA enrichment analysis was conducted on pathways associated with plant responses to combined stresses. The results showed that pathways related to secondary metabolite biosynthesis, hydroxyl-containing organic compound metabolism, and glucosinolate containing compound metabolism were significantly upregulated in the non-humidified group. Previous studies have indicated that the significant upregulation of secondary metabolic pathways is often a key strategy employed by plants to cope with external stress41. Plants enhance their antioxidant capacity and resistance to pests and diseases by synthesizing secondary metabolites, such as anthocyanins42. The results of this study demonstrated that under combined high-light and drought stress, non-humidified air conditions significantly induced the activation of multiple secondary metabolism-related pathways in Davidia involucrata Baill., suggesting that the plant may cope with more severe stress environments by enhancing the synthesis of secondary metabolites. In particular, the significant upregulation of the ‘indole-containing compound biosynthetic process’ pathway may suggest that, under non-humidified conditions, Davidia involucrata Baill. tends to enhance its adaptability to harsh environments through signaling pathways mediated by plant growth regulators, such as indoleacetic acid. Studies on the effects of abiotic stress on endogenous IAA levels in rice have shown that drought stress reduces IAA content, whereas heat stress leads to an increase in IAA levels43. In our study, Davidia involucrata Baill. upregulated IAA biosynthesis-related pathways under combined high-light and drought stress in non-humidified air conditions, further suggesting that the plant preferentially responds to heat stress induced by high-light exposure, which is consistent with our previous findings. In contrast, under air humidification conditions, the activation of the aforementioned stress response pathways was significantly suppressed, suggesting that air humidification may effectively alleviate plant stress intensity and reduce reliance on antioxidant metabolism and secondary metabolite synthesis.

The above results preliminarily elucidate the response mechanisms of Davidia involucrata Baill. to high-light stress, as well as the potential molecular mechanisms by which air humidification enhances its adaptability to both high-light and combined stress conditions. These findings provide an important theoretical basis for the future application of plant hormones or environmental regulation strategies to enhance plant stress resistance. However, the specific mechanisms of action of the relevant pathways identified in this study have not yet been functionally validated. In future studies, cellular experiments or integrated metabolomic and proteomic analyses may be conducted to further elucidate the specific functions of these pathways and key genes in the stress resistance of Davidia involucrata Baill.. It should also be noted that microenvironmental differences under the forest canopy (e.g., fluctuations in wind speed and temperature) and potential changes in gas composition within the airtight chamber (e.g., CO₂ concentration) were not directly measured in this study, which represents a limitation of our experimental design. Future research should incorporate real-time monitoring of microclimatic variables and chamber gas composition to better quantify their potential impacts.

Chimeric antigen receptor-natural killer (CAR-NK) cell immunotherapy: A bibliometric analysis from 2004 to 2023
Authors: Wangshu Li, Jiuxiang Feng, Jianan Peng, Xu Zhang, Aziz Ur Rehman Aziz, Daqing Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTChimeric antigen receptor-natural killer (CAR-NK) cells represent a breakthrough in cancer immunotherapy, making this a highly popular research area. However, comprehensive analyses of this field using bibliometric methods are rare. To our knowledge, this study has collected highest number of publications (1,259) on CAR-NK therapy from January 1, 2004, to December 31, 2023, and utilized CiteSpace and VOSviewer to analyze regions, institutions, journals, authors, and keywords to forecast the latest trends in CAR-NK therapy research. The United States and China, contributing over 60% of publications, are the primary drivers in this field. The Helmholtz Association and Harvard University are the most active institutions, with most publications appearing in Frontiers in Immunology. Winfried S. Wels is the most prolific author, while EL Liu is the most frequently co-cited author. “Immunotherapy,” “T-cells,” and “Cancer” are the most extensively covered topics in CAR-NK therapy research. Our study reveals current CAR-NK research trends, identifies potential research hotspots, and visualizes references through bibliometric methods, providing valuable guidance for future research in this field.

Introduction

As a groundbreaking treatment, immunotherapy has become the fourth cornerstone of cancer treatment, following surgery, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy. It is particularly effective for metastatic cancer, where some patients can achieve long-term remission and potential cure through immunotherapy.1 Cell immunotherapy, also known as adoptive cell therapy, is an innovative approach that harnesses the body’s immune system to eradicate cancer. This therapy utilizes various immune cells, including T cells, Natural killer (NK) cells, and even macrophages. Notably, targeted Chimeric antigen receptor (CAR)-T cell therapy has been approved for use, marking a significant milestone in the development of genetically modified cell therapies for cancer and sparking considerable interest in the development of CAR-NK cells for cancer immunotherapy.2,3 Compared to traditional CAR-T cell therapy, CAR-NK therapy offers unique advantages. These include a reduced risk of cytokine release syndrome, a lower risk of graft-versus-host disease, milder neurotoxicity, easier pre-manufacturing processes, and lower production costs. Additionally, CAR-NK cells can be engineered to target multiple antigens, enhance their proliferation and persistence in vivo, increase infiltration into solid tumors, and overcome drug-resistant tumor microenvironments, ultimately leading to effective anti-tumor responses. In summary, CAR-NK therapy has emerged as a promising direction in tumor immunotherapy and is being extensively studied.2,3

Bibliometrics is a scientific research method that reveals development trends and patterns in scientific research through quantitative analysis of literature data. It objectively evaluates the contributions of academic groups and individual researchers by comprehensively analyzing the authors, countries, journals, citations, and publication dates of selected articles. This approach provides a means to understand trends in specific fields and rank academic groups and individuals.4 By analyzing literature citations, cooperation networks, and keyword trends, bibliometrics can identify the main contributors, research hotspots, and future development directions in a research field.5 This paper uses bibliometric analysis to review the literature on CAR-NK immunotherapy from the past two decades, aiming to understand its characteristics and predict future research trends and hotspots. The study seeks to deeply analyze the research status and development trends in the field of CAR-NK therapy through bibliometric methods. By examining publication trends, contributions of authors and institutions, journal influence, and the evolution of research topics and keywords, this study provides a comprehensive perspective on the research dynamics in the field of CAR-NK.

Methods and materials

Searching strategy and data collection

Compared to other databases such as Scopus, Medline, and PubMed, the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) offers superior accuracy in document type annotation. Covering more than 12,000 academic journals, it is considered the best choice for literature analysis. Therefore, we selected this database for our research. On January 1, 2024, we searched WoSCC for all articles related to the application of CAR-NK from January 1, 2004, to December 31, 2023. The search formula was as follows: (TS = (chimeric antigen receptor-transduced NK) OR TS = (CAR-NK) OR TS = (chimeric antigen receptor engineered natural killer) OR TS = (chimeric antigen receptor NK) OR TS = (CAR-Natural Killer)). Data analysis

To analyze and visualize our data, we utilized several software tools. GraphPad Prism v8.0.2 was employed to analyze and plot annual and national trends and proportions in paper publishing. Additionally, we used CiteSpace (version 6.2.4 R, 64-bit Advanced Edition) and VOSviewer (version 1.6.18, http://www.vosviewer.com/) to further analyze the data and create scientific knowledge graphs. VOSviewer is a free JAVA-based software designed for analyzing large amounts of literature data and displaying it in map format. This tool is particularly useful for constructing and visualizing bibliometric networks, including co-authorship, co-occurrence, and citation networks.6 CiteSpace, developed by Professor Chaomei Chen (version 6.1.6 R) was also utilized. This software is designed to visualize research results in specific fields by creating co-citation network diagrams. CiteSpace aims to study new concepts and evaluate existing technologies within an experimental framework, helping users understand knowledge areas, research frontiers, and trends, and predict future research directions.7 All the researchers involved in this study were able to use the above software to process data proficiently. Three researchers (Li, Feng, and Aziz) independently analyzed and extracted the following information.

By employing these software tools, we were able to conduct a comprehensive bibliometric analysis, providing valuable insights into the research dynamics within the field of CAR-NK immunotherapy.

Discussion

The study identified current trends in CAR NK research by analyzing a total of 1,259 publications on CAR NK therapy from 2004 to 2023. It employed CiteSpace and VOSviewer to examine the contributions of various regions, institutions, journals, authors, and keywords, enabling the forecasting of emerging trends in CAR NK therapy research. The integration of CiteSpace and VOSviewer into CAR NK cell research contributes significantly to generating deeper insights and enhancing methodological rationale because of their unique capabilities in visualizing and analyzing research trends and networks.6,7 CiteSpace excels at identifying critical trends, mapping collaboration networks, and conducting temporal analyses of citations and co-citations, which helps researchers pinpoint emerging topics and influential contributors in CAR NK therapy. In contrast, VOSviewer specializes in constructing bibliometric networks, allowing researchers to visualize large datasets, analyze co-authorship and keyword co-occurrence, and easily interpret complex relationships within the literature. Together, these tools enhance understanding of the evolving CAR NK cell research landscape, identify key players and institutions, and inform future research directions. Their visual representation capabilities make complex bibliometric data accessible and actionable, promoting knowledge exchange and innovation in the field.6,7 The Web of Science Core Collection is recognized for its superior accuracy compared to other academic databases, primarily due to its comprehensive coverage of high-impact journals, rigorous indexing standards, and reliable citation data. It focuses on indexing only the most reputable journals across various disciplines, ensuring access to influential research that has undergone stringent peer review. The database employs strict selection criteria, prioritizing quality to minimize the inclusion of low-quality studies, which is crucial for valid bibliometric analyses. Additionally, it provides meticulous citation data, allowing researchers to accurately assess the impact and relevance of articles, enhancing the reliability of citation counts and impact factors.8 Together, these features make the Web of Science Core Collection an essential resource for researchers seeking accurate and trustworthy bibliometric insights.

The study findings indicated that from 2004 to 2012, CAR-NK research grew slowly, with fewer than 10 papers published each year, suggesting limited interest from researchers during this time. However, from 2013 onwards, there has been a gradual increase in CAR-NK publications, signifying growing interest among researchers. Since 2019, there has been a rapid surge in publications, peaking in 2022, underscoring widespread attention to CAR-NK research during this period. Among these studies, the United States and China have emerged as the dominant contributors in CAR-NK literature, with China accounting for 42.49% of the total publications, significantly surpassing other countries. The United States also holds a prominent position in international collaborations in this field, closely partnering with nations such as France, Italy, and the United Kingdom. In contrast, China shows closer collaboration with Japan, Canada, and Iran. This highlights the influential roles of the United States and China in CAR-NK research and underscores the critical role of international cooperation in advancing this field. Among cited authors, Liu ranks first, primarily for his research on natural killer cells modified to express anti-CD19 CAR, demonstrating efficacy in treating relapsed or refractory CD19-positive cancers like non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma or chronic lymphocytic leukemia, without severe reactions.3 It has been proposed that NK-CAR-iPSC-NK cells exhibit in vivo activity like T cells expressing T-CAR, but with reduced toxicity, particularly in ovarian cancer models. This study lays a foundational experimental basis for exploring NK-CAR-iPSC-NK cell therapies across various cancer types.9 These findings underscore heightened interest in CAR-NK research focused on novel therapeutic targets and clinical application efficacy, highlighting evolving directions within the field.

Research trend analysis and clinical applications

Emerging trends in CAR-NK cell research indicate a rapidly advancing field with potential breakthroughs in cellular therapy and immuno-oncology. Key areas of focus include advancements in genetic engineering, such as the use of CRISPR-Cas9 to enhance the efficacy and safety profiles of CAR NK cells,10 and the development of dual-targeting CARs and combination therapies aimed at improving tumor recognition and treatment, especially in solid tumors.11 Researchers are increasingly looking to synergize CAR-NK therapies with other modalities like immune checkpoints and monoclonal antibodies. A growing understanding of the tumor microenvironment’s influence on NK cell function is also critical for overcoming challenges such as tumor heterogeneity and immunosuppression.11 The trend toward allogeneic, or “off-the-shelf,” CAR NK therapies aims for standardized, readily available treatments that do not require personalized manufacturing. Increasing attention is being paid to the immunogenicity and safety of these therapies, with ongoing research into minimizing adverse effects such as cytokine release syndrome through safer CAR constructs.12 Furthermore, the shift toward personalized medicine emphasizes tailoring therapies to individual patient profiles based on tumor characteristics. Collaboration among researchers, clinicians, bioengineers, and pharmaceutical companies is essential for translating research into clinical practice and expediting innovations in this field. Concurrently, regulatory frameworks and manufacturing processes for CAR NK therapies are evolving, with innovations aimed at reducing costs and improving scalability.11,13 Overall, the rapid evolution of CAR NK cell research, with its focus on advanced engineering, combination therapies, and personalized approaches, promises significant advancements in effective cancer treatments.12 Below, we present a thorough discussion of the emerging trends and recent advancements in the CAR-NK research field and their clinical applications.

Main research directions and outcomes

CAR-NK cell therapy is an emerging field in cancer immunotherapy, involving the genetic engineering of NK cells to express CARs. These CARs feature an extracellular antigen-binding domain, typically derived from a monoclonal antibody, fused with an intracellular signaling domain, often including CD3ζ and co-stimulatory molecules like CD28 or 4-1BB to enhance therapeutic efficacy.3 NK cells are particularly suited for targeting tumors that lack MHC class I expression due to acquired resistance mechanisms.14 The flexibility of CAR-NK cells is demonstrated by their diverse cellular sources, which include peripheral blood, umbilical cord blood, induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs), and NK cell lines.15,16 Among these, the NK-92 cell line is notable for its high cytotoxic potential and ease of expansion in vitro.17 CAR-NK cells also have the potential to be developed as “off-the-shelf” therapeutic products, simplifying the treatment process, reducing costs, and making therapy more accessible to a broader patient population.18,19 The manufacturing process involves careful selection of target antigens, optimization of CAR design for maximum binding and activation, and meticulous cultivation to ensure high cell vitality and functional stability.20 Beyond direct cytotoxicity, CAR-NK cells possess immunomodulatory functions, recruiting other immune cells to the tumor site and enhancing the overall anti-tumor response.21 Although the field of CAR-NK cell therapy is advancing rapidly, it still faces challenges such as limited in vivo persistence and the immunosuppressive nature of the tumor microenvironment, areas requiring further refinement and innovation.1,22

Intracellular signaling domains

CAR-NK cells can leverage NK-specific signaling domains such as 2B4, DAP10, and DAP12, which contribute to their unique functional characteristics. The 2B4 domain acts as a co-stimulatory domain, enhancing the cytotoxic activity of CAR-NK cells.23 DAP10, a key adaptor protein and the primary signaling intermediate of NKG2D in human NK cells, enhances the cytotoxic ability of CAR-NK cells against certain tumors.24 Similarly, DAP12, associated with various activating receptors, is employed in CAR-NK cells to boost their anti-tumor potential.25

Source

CAR-NK cells can be derived from various sources.19 Donor-versus-recipient NK cell alloreactivity can reduce leukemia relapse and GVHD.26 NK cells target cancer without prior sensitization and can be transferred without strict HLA matching. Researchers are exploring donor-derived and stem cell-derived NK cells, but selecting highly functional NK cells is crucial for successful therapies.27 Their antigen-independent activity and low alloreactivity risk make them suitable for “off-the-shelf” therapies.19,28

In vitro expansion

CAR-NK cells are engineered to target cancer cells, utilizing NK cells innate immune properties. In vitro expansion of CAR-NK cells can be achieved through methods like feeder cell lines or cytokine combinations, particularly IL-2 and IL-15, which are crucial for NK cell survival and activation.29 Feeder cells, often genetically modified, provide essential signals that promote CAR-NK cell growth and maturation.30 A protocol demonstrated generating CAR-NK cells with over 15% transduction efficiency from healthy donor NK cells using third-generation lentiviral vectors and assessing their anti-tumor function with a flow cytometry-based assay.31 Another study utilized CRISPR (Cas9/RNP) and AAVs for efficient, stable CAR transduction in NK cells, enhancing their efficacy against AML targets.32

Cell killing mechanism

In addition to their CAR-dependent capabilities, NK cells exhibit CAR-independent cytotoxicity, using natural receptors to respond to stress signals and the absence of normal “self” markers on target cells.14 Activated NK cells release cytotoxic granules, like CAR-T cells, to kill target cells directly.33 This dual-action mechanism allows CAR-NK cells to eradicate cancer cells lacking the specific CAR-recognized antigen, making them versatile and effective in cancer immunotherapy.18,28 CAR-NK therapy shows promise for both hematological and solid tumors.34 It offers broader tumor recognition and reduced susceptibility to the tumor microenvironment,18 with fewer severe side effects like cytokine release syndrome and neurotoxicity compared to CAR-T cells.21

Production of CAR-NK cells

The production of CAR-NK cells is a complex, multistage process designed for optimal safety, effectiveness, and uniformity. It begins with acquiring biological materials like PBMCs, umbilical cord blood, or iPSCs.35 For PBMC-derived CAR-NK cells, leukapheresis is followed by CD3+ T cell removal, CD56+ NK cell enrichment, cytokine activation, and feeder cell culture.36,37 Cord blood-derived CAR-NK cells involve isolating CD34+ progenitor cells, which are differentiated into NK cells over 21 days.38,39 iPSC-derived CAR-NK cells follow a similar process but require a longer maturation period.40 CAR-NK cells are an innovative immunotherapy approach using genetically engineered NK cells with CARs to target and destroy cancer cells.30 Derived from allogeneic sources like umbilical cord blood or iPSCs, they avoid patient-specific manufacturing and reduce the risk of cytokine release syndrome and graft-versus-host disease.3,41 Technological advances include new generations of CARs with additional co-stimulatory domains (e.g., CD28, 4-1BB) improve the survival and functionality of CAR-NK cells. Innovations also explore diverse antigen recognition formats.42,43 Researchers are finding tumor-specific targets to minimize off-target effects, such as hypoxia-specific conditions or distinct glycosylation patterns in tumor cells.44,45 Combining CAR-NK cells with other treatments, like checkpoint inhibitors or cytokines, enhances their effectiveness and safety.46,47

Enhancing CAR-NK cell therapies involves overcoming challenges posed by the tumor microenvironment. Strategies include using specific cytokines like IL-15 to boost NK cell proliferation and anti-tumor efficacy,48,49 improving cell penetration into solid tumors through chemokine receptors and matrix-degrading enzymes,50,51 and extending CAR-NK cell survival by co-expressing anti-apoptotic genes, immune checkpoint inhibitors, and metabolic regulators.52–55 Innovative approaches also explore cytokine-inducible SH2-containing protein (CIS) for regulating CAR-NK cell activation,56,57 IL-15 super agonists,46 IL-21,58 and IL-1259 to enhance efficacy and generate lasting immune memory, and bispecific antibodies for broader targeting.60 These advancements aim to improve the effectiveness, durability, and safety of CAR-NK cell therapies. Synthetic biology is advancing CAR-NK cell therapies by equipping them with engineered receptors to target specific tumor antigens and enhance their cytotoxic response.61 Challenges like target antigen selection, CAR expression regulation, and cell persistence are addressed through synthetic biology tools. Key applications include engineering CAR-NK cells to express IL-15 or IL-15 Rα to boost persistence and antitumor activity,3,62 creating CAR-NK cells that secrete IL-12 for enhanced efficacy,63 and modifying CAR-NK cells to lack PD-1 expression to prevent exhaustion.64,65 These innovations demonstrate the potential of synthetic biology to create more effective CAR-NK cell therapies.66,67

Clinical applications and future outlook

CAR-NK cells are being explored in various clinical trials for treating cancers. The first reported trial in 2020 used cord blood-derived CAR-NK cells targeting CD19 in patients with B-cell malignancies, showing a 68% overall response rate (ORR) and no severe side effects like cytokine release syndrome (CRS) or graft-versus-host disease (GVHD).3 A recent Phase I/II trial (NCT03056339) also demonstrated a favorable safety profile, with a 48.6% ORR and emphasized the importance of optimal donor selection for better outcomes.68 In a clinical trial involving 11 patients with relapsed or refractory CD19-positive cancers (non-Hodgkin lymphoma and chronic lymphocytic leukemia), treatment with HLA-mismatched anti-CD19 CAR-NK cells derived from umbilical cord blood showed promising results. The therapy, delivered in a single infusion at escalating doses after lymphodepleting chemotherapy, was well tolerated with no severe toxicity observed. Remarkably, 73% of the patients responded to treatment, including seven achieving complete remission. The CAR-NK cells expanded minimally but remained in the system for up to 12 months.3 The study demonstrates significant potential for CAR-NK cell therapy in treating relapsed or refractory CD19-positive cancers, with a high response rate and minimal toxicity. The rapid response, absence of severe side effects, and the long-term persistence of the modified NK cells suggest this therapeutic strategy could offer a promising and safe alternative for patients with certain types of blood cancers. In a pivotal study on relapsed or refractory Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia (ALL) (NCT01626495 and NCT01029366), 30 patients were treated with autologous T cells modified to express a specific CD19-CAR, known as CTL019, at varying doses. Remarkably, 90% of participants achieved complete remission, including those resistant to prior treatments like blinatumomab and stem-cell transplants. The CTL019 cells showed in vivo proliferation and were traceable in blood, bone marrow, and cerebrospinal fluid in responders, with a 6-month event-free survival of 67% and overall survival of 78%. By six months, 68% of patients retained CTL019 cells, and 73% experienced sustained B-cell aplasia. While all patients developed CRS, which was effectively treated with tocilizumab and was more common in patients with a higher disease burden pre-infusion.69 The ongoing trials are testing CAR-NK cells targeting CD19, CD30, and mesothelin in various cancers, providing further insights into their safety and efficacy (NCT03692637, NCT04324996, NCT04613952).

CAR-NK cell therapy holds great promise in cancer treatment, particularly for hematological malignancies, but faces several challenges and research areas for improvement:12 Enhancing CAR constructs to target multiple tumor antigens, incorporating costimulatory domains, and integrating safety mechanisms like suicide genes can improve efficacy and safety.70Utilizing iPSCs or umbilical cord blood as NK cell sources, along with advances in gene delivery and expansion techniques, are key to producing sufficient CAR-NK cells.71Strategies like combination therapies with checkpoint inhibitors and advanced delivery methods can help CAR-NK cells function effectively within the tumor microenvironment.72,73Research on antigen-specific models and preclinical trials are crucial to managing risks and standardizing protocols for handling adverse events like CRS.20,74,75Integrating CAR-NK therapy with surgical interventions can reduce tumor burden preoperatively and eradicate residual disease postoperatively, complementing surgical resection and other treatments.76–78Ethical considerations in CAR-NK cell therapy are crucial for ensuring patient safety, public trust, and equitable access. Key issues include obtaining informed consent by educating patients about the therapy’s risks and implications20 addressing privacy concerns due to genetic modifications, and providing post-trial care for long-term monitoring and patient welfare.20 These ethical practices are essential for responsible deployment of CAR-NK cell therapy.79

Limitation

We acknowledge several limitations in our study. Firstly, our data collection was limited to a single source, the Web of Science Core Collection, potentially excluding articles from other sources. Additionally, our manual screening process involved removing papers deemed irrelevant, which may have introduced selection bias. Furthermore, our study focused exclusively on English-language articles, which could introduce bias against research published in other languages. These limitations suggest that our findings may not fully represent the entirety of CAR-NK research globally, and future studies should consider broader sources and languages to provide a more comprehensive analysis.

Summary

CAR-NK cells have emerged as a promising frontier in cancer immunotherapy. Unlike CAR-T cells, CAR-NK cells leverage the innate immune capabilities of NK cells, offering several advantages, including reduced risk of cytokine release syndrome and graft-versus-host disease.11,18 CAR-NK cells are engineered to express CARs that target specific antigens on cancer cells. This dual mechanism – NK cell receptor-dependent and CAR-dependent – enhances their cytotoxicity against both hematological and solid tumors.11 Recent studies have shown that CAR-NK cells can effectively target and kill cancer cells, with promising results in clinical trials. Recent clinical trials have demonstrated the efficacy of CAR-NK cells in treating various cancers, including non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma, chronic lymphocytic leukemia, and certain solid tumors.77,80 CAR-NK cells offer several benefits over CAR-T cells, such as being “off-the-shelf” products that can be manufactured in advance and stored until needed.81 This makes them more accessible and reduces the time required for treatment preparation. Ongoing research is focused on enhancing the efficacy of CAR-NK cells against solid tumors, which have been more challenging to treat. Innovations in CAR design, such as incorporating cytokines like IL-15 to boost NK cell proliferation and survival, are being explored.82,83 The advancements in CAR-NK cell therapy represent a significant step forward in cancer treatment, offering a safer and potentially more effective alternative to existing therapies. As research progresses, CAR-NK cells are poised to become a vital component of precision medicine in oncology.84

Conclusion

Our study represents the first comprehensive quantitative and statistical analysis of CAR-NK immunotherapy over the past decade. We found that the United States has played a pivotal role in advancing this field, leading in both the quantity and quality of publications. The rapid increase in publications underscores the growing global interest in CAR-NK therapy. Our findings also highlight a trend of international collaboration among countries and institutions, although deeper collaboration opportunities remain underexplored. Institutions such as the Helmholtz Association in Germany and Harvard University in the United States have notably contributed to the field’s progress, suggesting potential for enhanced collaboration to drive future developments. The journals Blood and Frontiers in Immunology emerge as the most influential and frequently cited publications in CAR-NK research. Similarly, researchers like Wels, Winfried S., and Liu stand out as prolific and highly cited authors, making them valuable contacts for potential collaboration and sources of cutting-edge research insights. Keyword analysis reveals that immune cell types are central to CAR-NK research, reflecting efforts to identify optimal cell targets and compare CAR-NK with established therapies like CAR-T cells. The clinical application of CAR-NK, including ongoing clinical trials, underscores its pivotal role in immunotherapy and signals heightened interest and future growth in this area.

Overall, our study provides a comprehensive overview of CAR-NK research dynamics and identifies key areas for future exploration and collaboration, promising a vibrant future for CAR-NK immunotherapy research. CAR-NK cells, derived from sources like peripheral blood and iPSCs, show promise in cancer treatment due to reduced cytokine release risk and no need for HLA matching. Effective in hematological malignancies and under study for solid tumors, ongoing efforts focus on improving design, manufacturing, and delivery. Ethical concerns, particularly around patient autonomy and privacy, are essential as this therapy advances, potentially transforming cancer care.

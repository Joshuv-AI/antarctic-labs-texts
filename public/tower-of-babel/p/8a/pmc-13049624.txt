Comment on “Is Cannabis Dependence Associated With Postoperative Infections in Hand and Wrist Surgeries?”
Authors: Sangameshwar Manikya, Varshini Vadhithala, Rajnish Kumar
Published: 2026

Dussik et al1 examine an increasingly relevant perioperative variable and report higher odds of superficial surgical site infection, deep infection requiring reoperation, sepsis, readmission, and emergency department utilization among cannabis-dependent patients undergoing soft-tissue hand procedures. Given the outpatient nature and generally low baseline morbidity of these operations, even modest relative increases in complications merit close scrutiny.

The exposure definition, however, incorporated the International Classification of Disease code F12 documented from 1 year before surgery to 3 months after surgery. Inclusion of postoperative diagnostic coding may introduce temporal ambiguity, as dependence first recorded during a complication-related encounter could be misclassified as preexisting exposure.2 Restricting the cohort to diagnoses recorded strictly before surgery or performing time-sequenced sensitivity analyses would help clarify whether cannabis dependence precedes, rather than follows, adverse events.

Although odds ratios approached ≥2.0 for several infectious outcomes, absolute differences were small. Deep surgical site infection occurred in 0.9% of cannabis-dependent patients compared with 0.3% of matched controls, representing a 0.6% absolute increase.3 Translating these findings into absolute risk differences or number-needed-to-harm estimates would enhance clinical interpretability and guide shared decision-making, particularly for elective procedures such as carpal tunnel or trigger finger release.

Biologically, cannabinoids interact with cannabinoid receptor type 1 and type 2 signaling in immune and cutaneous tissues, potentially modulating inflammatory cascades and host defense. The selective rise in infectious but not thromboembolic events observed in this cohort supports a pathway more consistent with immune modulation than systemic hypercoagulability. Incorporating perioperative laboratory indices, glycemic control metrics, and antibiotic timing data in future analyses may clarify whether cannabis dependence exerts an independent effect or amplifies established metabolic risks.4

Overall, the study contributes valuable epidemiologic insight and provides a foundation for refined perioperative risk stratification in hand surgery.

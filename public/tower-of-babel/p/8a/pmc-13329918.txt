Rare use of epicardial pacing wire in severe aortic stenosis with Wolf-Parkinson-White syndrome: Who goes first?
Authors: Sudipta Mondal, Nadeem Afroz Muslim
Published: 2026

Case

A sexagenarian male patient presented with a history of exertional dyspnea. Diagnostic workup revealed severe degenerative aortic stenosis (AS) and mild left ventricular (LV) dysfunction [transaortic peak gradient 68 mmHg, mean 44 mmHg, left ventricular ejection fraction 48%]. Subsequent workup for surgical planning revealed an incidental finding on the electrocardiogram (ECG) of preexcitation (Fig. 1), necessitating further specialist opinion and management planning. Coronary angiography (CAG) revealed concomitant double-vessel coronary artery disease (CAD). Significant findings include an 80% discrete stenosis within the mid-segment of the left anterior descending (LAD) artery and a 70% short-segment stenosis involving the major obtuse marginal (OM) branch of the left circumflex (LCX) artery. Discussion

The co-existence of symptomatic severe AS, high-risk CAD, and an accessory pathway (AP) posed a significant surgical planning challenge. The primary question was the optimal timing for addressing the AP: prior to, or after, Aortic Valve Replacement (AVR) and Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting (CABG). Considering the risks, particularly the potential for pre-operative hemodynamic collapse with induced tachycardia in the setting of severe AS and LV dysfunction, the decision was made to proceed with the second option: definitive surgical and revascularization procedures first, followed by post-operative AP assessment [Electrophysiology Study (EPS) and Radiofrequency Ablation (RFA)] and management. The patient underwent successful CABG and AVR using a bioprosthetic valve and extubated on the same day of the procedure. On post-operative day one (POD 1), inotropes (noradrenaline) were tapered off and a bedside electrophysiologic assessment was performed via surgical epicardial pacing wires. Utilizing atrial burst pacing with incremental rate (Fig. S1), the accessory pathway effective refractory period (APERP) was determined to be 250 ms (Fig. 2, Fig. 3, Fig. 4, S1). During the assessment, a tachycardia with LBBB aberrancy was induced (Fig. 5A), which was subsequently terminated via vagal maneuvers (Fig. 5B). Throughout the procedure, patient safety was maintained using a secondary temporary pacemaker for backup pacing support and external defibrillator pads to treat ventricular fibrillation (VF) in the event of induced atrial fibrillation.

Subsequently, the patient's clinical course was complicated by a period of hemodynamic instability, characterized by borderline hypotension (90/60 mmHg) relative to a baseline of 110/80 mmHg. This coincided with the onset of incessant orthodromic reciprocating tachycardia (ORT). The arrhythmia proved highly refractory to conventional interventions, including intravenous adenosine—which resulted in immediate reinitiation, multiple synchronized cardioversions, and beta-blockade. Stabilization was eventually achieved with a loading dose and continuous infusion of amiodarone. Emergent EPS and RFA were deferred due to financial constraints.

Six weeks following surgery, the patient was electively taken for a definitive EPS, which confirmed the mechanism of the paroxysmal rhythm as incessant orthodromic reciprocating tachycardia (ORT) mediated by a left lateral accessory pathway. Successful RFA was achieved (Fig. 6), although the procedure was noted to be anatomically challenging, as septal puncture and pathway localization as well as ablation were rendered difficult due to the altered post-surgical anatomy following the AVR and CABG. The patient is symptom-free with no recurrence of palpitations or tachycardia at the 3-month follow-up post-ablation. The initial strategy, while encountering an immediate post-operative complication, allowed for the safe and timely management of the life-threatening cardiac conditions (AS and CAD) and the eventual successful treatment of the AP.

In summary, this case highlights:

1.The complex management of severe degenerative AS and CAD co-existing with a preexcitation syndrome. The decision to prioritize AVR/CABG over a pre-operative EPS/RFA was based on avoiding a potential hemodynamic collapse. However, the patient developed high-risk, incessant ORT post-operatively, necessitating aggressive medical management and subsequent successful definitive RFA. This emphasizes that while an initially asymptomatic AP may be classified as "low-risk," the high-stress environment of major cardiac surgery can reveal its arrhythmogenic potential.

This emphasizes that while an initially asymptomatic AP may be classified as "low-risk," the high-stress environment of major cardiac surgery can reveal its arrhythmogenic potential.

2.Epicardial pacing wires, retained following cardiac surgery, can be effectively utilized for the post-operative assessment of accessory pathway refractory periods, providing a crucial risk stratification tool for the precipitation of VF by AF, particularly in “low-risk” elderly patients without a prior history of symptomatic tachyarrhythmias.

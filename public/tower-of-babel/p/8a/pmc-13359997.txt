New epidemiological characteristics of childhood IgA vasculitis in the post-COVID-19 era: a single-center retrospective cohort study
Authors: Bin Wang, Biao Su, Hua Yu, Yang Yue, Lan Huang, Yuanhui Zhu, Houhua Hao, Juan Liang, Fangfang Yang, Wenhui Wang, Ling-Jian Meng, Feng-Jun Guan, Chen Dong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundsTo compare hospitalization characteristics, clinical phenotypes, risk factors for IgA vasculitis nephritis (IgAVN), and prognostic outcomes in children with IgA vasculitis (IgAV) before and after the COVID-19, thereby elucidating the impact of the pandemic on the disease spectrum of IgAV to provide evidence for optimized disease management and precision medicine in the Post-COVID-19 era.MethodsA single-center retrospective cohort study was conducted involving children hospitalized with IgAV during two periods: Pre-COVID-19 (2017–2019) and the Post-COVID-19 endemic phase (2023–2025). We systematically compared general demographics, clinical phenotypes, and etiological profiles between the two cohorts. IgAV hospitalization proportions were calculated, and time-series models predicted crude hospitalization rates to assess burden trends. Furthermore, a 6-month follow-up was performed for newly-diagnosed non-nephritic patients to evaluate relapse rate and progression to IgAVN. Finally, multivariate logistic regression identified independent IgAVN risk factors.ResultsCompared to the Pre-COVID-19 period, patients in the Post-COVID-19 era exhibited significant alterations in humoral immunity and complement profiles, alongside increased rates of Mycoplasma pneumoniae infection. The disease burden intensified markedly: hospitalization duration, costs, and the proportion of IgAV admissions (including both newly-diagnosed and relapsed cases) surged, with the observed crude hospitalization rate significantly exceeding model predictions. Among newly-diagnosed cases, the proportion of the renal subtype increased significantly, while the cutaneous-only form decreased. Follow-up data revealed no significant difference in relapse rates between cohorts; However, the progression rate to nephritis was significantly elevated in the Post-COVID-19 group. Duration of rash emerged as the sole consistent independent predictor of IgAVN across both periods.ConclusionIn the post-COVID-19 era, childhood IgAV presents with new clinical characteristics, marked by an increased overall disease burden and a shift towards more severe clinical phenotypes. These changes may be associated with rising Mycoplasma pneumoniae infection and dysregulation of the complement-coagulation system.Notably, newly-diagnosed non-nephritic patients face a significantly higher risk of renal involvement, underscoring the need for enhanced early clinical monitoring and long-term follow-up. While the spectrum of risk factors for IgAVN has evolved, rash duration remains a critical predictor requiring close attention.

Background

IgA vasculitis (IgAV), formerly known as Henoch-Schönlein purpura (HSP), is an autoimmune-mediated systemic small-vessel vasculitis. Its hallmark clinical manifestations include palpable purpura, arthritis or arthralgia, gastrointestinal symptoms, and nephritis [1]. Childhood IgAV predominantly affects children aged 3–15 years, with a higher incidence in autumn and winter. The male-to-female ratio is approximately 1.2:1, and the global incidence ranges from 3 to 27 per 100,000 population [2]. Although the exact etiology of IgAV remains elusive, approximately 30%-65% of affected children report a preceding upper respiratory tract infection [3]. Common pathogens include Streptococcus species and Respiratory Syncytial Virus, while Mycoplasma pneumoniae has also been implicated as a potential trigger [4–5]. Generally, IgAV is a self-limiting condition with a favorable prognosis. However, the severity of renal involvement is the critical determinant of long-term outcomes [6]. A subset of patients with IgA vasculitis nephritis (IgAVN) experiences a protracted course, and severe cases may progress to end-stage renal disease (ESRD), imposing a substantial medical burden on families and society.

Severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2), the causative agent of COVID-19, primarily targets the respiratory system and can lead to life-threatening conditions such as acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) [7]. However, accumulating clinical evidence indicates that the impact of this virus extends far beyond the respiratory tract, significantly affecting multiple organs and systems, including the skin, mucous membranes, kidneys, and endocrine system [8–10]. Consequently, the association between SARS-CoV-2 infection and extra-respiratory diseases has become a focal point for clinicians. Studies have demonstrated clear links between COVID-19 and the incidence risk and clinical prognosis of various conditions, including diabetes, Guillain-Barré syndrome (GBS), and immune thrombocytopenia (ITP) [10–12]. Notably, recent research suggests an upward trend in the incidence and severity of renal damage in IgA nephropathy (IgAN) during the COVID-19 pandemic [13]. This implies that SARS-CoV-2 infection may influence the spectrum of IgA deposition-related diseases through shared mechanisms, such as immune dysregulation.

Despite these findings, the long-term impact of COVID-19 on childhood IgAV remains unclear. Most prior studies on IgAV were conducted in the Pre-COVID-19 era, while existing Post-COVID-19 literature consists primarily of case reports [14, 15]. There is a paucity of systematic evaluations regarding whether the clinical characteristics of childhood IgAV have undergone structural evolution during the Post-COVID-19 endemic phase. To address this gap, this study compares hospitalization characteristics, clinical phenotypes, risk factors for IgAVN, and prognostic outcomes in children with IgAV before and after COVID-19. We aim to systematically assess the long-term impact of the pandemic on the disease spectrum of IgAV, thereby providing a scientific basis for preventive management and personalized treatment of childhood IgAV in the Post-COVID-19 era.

Methods

Study period

On March 11, 2020, the World Health Organization (WHO) declared COVID-19 a global pandemic. Subsequently, on May 5, 2023, the WHO announced that it no longer constituted a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC). China officially transitioned to normalized pandemic management on May 8, 2023. Based on these critical milestones, this study divided the observation period into two phases: the Pre-COVID-19 period (June 1, 2017, to June 1, 2019) and the Post-COVID-19 endemic phase (June 1, 2023, to June 1, 2025).

Study participants

This study employed a single-center retrospective cohort design. The study population consisted of children aged 2–14 years hospitalized with IgAV at the Department of Pediatrics, Affiliated Hospital of Xuzhou Medical University, during the two defined periods. A total of 1,508 patients admitted with a diagnosis of IgAV (806 in the pre-COVID-19 and 702 in the Post-COVID-19) were collected and subjected to rigorous screening. Ultimately, 1,469 patients were included, comprising 780 cases from the Pre-COVID-19 period and 689 cases from the Post-COVID-19 endemic phase.

All patients met the diagnostic criteria for IgAV [16, 17]: presence of palpable purpura accompanied by at least one of the following four clinical manifestations: abdominal pain, arthritis/arthralgia, renal involvement, or histopathological evidence of IgA deposition. Renal involvement was defined as the occurrence of hematuria, proteinuria, or renal dysfunction within 6 months of IgAV onset. Based on the primary organ involvement, patients were stratified into three phenotypes: the cutaneous-only subtype (characterized solely by typical palpable purpura without other systemic symptoms), the gastrointestinal/articular/mixed subtype (presenting with extra-renal systemic symptoms such as arthralgia, arthritis, abdominal pain, or gastrointestinal bleeding), and the renal subtype (defined by hematuria and/or proteinuria meeting the diagnostic criteria for IgAV nephritis) [18].

Exclusion criteria were: (1) concurrent autoimmune diseases (e.g., systemic lupus erythematosus, juvenile idiopathic arthritis); (2) purpura or rash caused by other etiologies (e.g., immune thrombocytopenia, herpesvirus infection, urticaria); and (3) incomplete clinical data.

Methodology

We systematically reviewed and analyzed clinical data, including general demographics, clinical phenotypes, and laboratory parameters, for all hospitalized IgAV patients in both periods. To control for confounding factors related to changes in the overall pediatric hospitalization environment, we concurrently collected data on children hospitalized with Acute Glomerulonephritis (AGN) during the same periods. We calculated the hospitalization composition ratio for both diseases (number of monthly admissions for the specific disease / total pediatric admissions in the same month) and compared the differences between the two periods.

Furthermore, we constructed time-series forecasting models for the crude hospitalization rates of both diseases (number of monthly admissions for the specific disease / corresponding age-specific child population in the region; regional population data were sourced from the official website of the Xuzhou Municipal Bureau of Statistics).

A 6-month follow-up was conducted for newly-diagnosed non-nephritic IgAV patients to evaluate their relapse rates and progression rate to IgAVN.

Additionally, Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were performed to establish risk models for newly-onset IgAVN in both periods and to compare differences in risk factors.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses and modeling were performed using SPSS version 23.0 and Python (v3.9). Continuous variables with a normal distribution were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\overline{x}$$\end{document}±s) and compared between groups using the independent samples t-test. Non-normally distributed continuous variables were expressed as median (interquartile range) [M (IQR)] and compared using the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables were presented as counts (percentages) [n (%)] and compared using the Chi-square (χ² ) test.

Binary logistic regression models were initially used to assess the association between covariates and newly-onset IgAVN. Covariates with P < 0.1 in univariate analysis or those with clear clinical significance were included in the multivariate analysis.

To predict the crude hospitalization rates, five models were constructed: Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA), Support Vector Machine (SVM), Back Propagation Neural Network (BP), Least Squares Support Vector Machine (LSSVM), and Random Forest (RF). Model performance was evaluated using Mean Squared Error (MSE), Mean Absolute Error (MAE), and the Coefficient of Determination ( R2). The Random Forest (RF) model, which demonstrated optimal performance, was selected as the final predictive model. To enhance robustness, the prediction mean and 95% confidence interval (CI) were calculated based on the results of all five models. All tests were two-tailed, and a P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Demographic characteristics and laboratory findings

There were no significant differences in baseline demographic characteristics, including age, gender, residence, and BMI, between the two cohorts (all P > 0.05 ). The seasonal distribution of onset was similar in both groups, with a peak in winter followed by spring, showing no statistically significant difference ( P = 0.361 ).

Regarding laboratory parameters, patients in the Post-COVID-19 cohort exhibited significantly higher levels of red blood cell (RBC) count, complement 3 (C3), complement (C4), and immunoglobulin M (IgM) compared to the Pre-COVID-19 cohort (all P < 0.05). Conversely, serum albumin (ALB), fibrinogen (FIB), and immunoglobulin A (IgA) levels were significantly lower in the Post-COVID-19 group (all P < 0.05 ). No significant differences were observed in other laboratory indicators, including white blood cell (WBC) count, neutrophil count(NEU), and C-reactive protein (CRP) (all P > 0.05 ) (Table 1, Page 27–28).

Analysis of pathogens

Regarding respiratory pathogens, 567 (72.7%) patients were tested in the Pre-COVID-19 period, with 347 (61.2%) positive for at least one pathogen. The top five pathogens were Group A Streptococcus (52.7%), respiratory syncytial virus (26.3%), influenza virus (20.2%), Mycoplasma pneumoniae (15.8%), and adenovirus (8.7%). Concurrently, 204 patients were tested for Helicobacter pylori (Hp), yielding a positivity rate of 23.5% (48/204).

In the Post-COVID-19 period, 557 (80.8%) patients underwent respiratory pathogen testing, with 381 (68.4%) testing positive. Group A Streptococcus remained the most prevalent (56.5%). Notably, the positivity rate for Mycoplasma pneumoniae surged to 34.0%, rising to the second rank. Respiratory syncytial virus decreased significantly to 16.6% (ranking fourth), and Chlamydia replaced adenovirus in the top five (7.4%). Helicobacter pylori (Hp) testing was performed in 165 patients, with a positivity rate of 26.1% (43/165), showing no significant difference compared to the Pre-COVID-19 period ( P = 0.575 ) (Fig. 1, Page 29).

Hospitalization characteristics and epidemiological trends of All IgAV patients vs. AGN

Hospitalization burden and epidemiological features

Compared to the Pre-COVID-19 period, the Post-COVID-19 cohort of all IgAV patients experienced a significantly prolonged mean length of stay (P = 0.026) and increased mean hospitalization costs (P = 0.038). Furthermore, the proportion of total IgAV hospitalizations rose significantly from 2.15% to 3.07% (P < 0.001). Stratified analysis revealed that this increase was significant for both newly-diagnosed and relapsed subgroups (both P < 0.001 ) (Table 2, Page 30).

Time-series analysis (Fig. 2, Page 31) indicated that hospitalization proportions for all IgAV cases remained low and stable before the pandemic but exhibited a marked upward trend in the Post-COVID-19 era, with a more pronounced surge in relapsed cases. In contrast, the hospitalization proportion for AGN showed no significant change (P = 0.128). Regarding seasonal distribution (Fig. 3, Page 32), total IgAV hospitalizations displayed a distinct winter-spring peak in the Pre-COVID-19 period. Although the overall proportion increased in the Post-COVID-19 period, the seasonal pattern remained unchanged. Similarly, AGN trends and seasonal patterns remained consistently stable throughout both periods.

Time-series model prediction of crude hospitalization rates

To further validate these findings, we constructed five machine learning models to predict crude hospitalization rates of the two diseases. The Random Forest (RF) model demonstrated optimal fit in the Pre-COVID-19 period, with predicted values closely aligning with observed data, indicating its ability to accurately capture historical epidemiological trends. However, in the Post-COVID-19 endemic phase, the observed crude hospitalization rate for IgAV (black line) significantly exceeded the model predictions (orange line). This discrepancy was particularly evident during the winters of 2023 and 2024, where abnormal spikes occurred, and the overall level consistently surpassed predicted values. In contrast, the observed crude hospitalization rate for AGN during the same period remained consistent with model predictions, showing no similar anomalous elevation (Fig. 4, Page 33).

Comparison of clinical subtype composition in newly-diagnosed IgAV

The distribution of clinical subtypes in newly-diagnosed IgAV patients changed significantly in the Post-COVID-19 period compared to the Pre-COVID-19 era (P < 0.001). In the Pre-COVID-19 period ( n = 612), the cutaneous-only subtype was predominant (51.80%), followed by the gastrointestinal/articular/mixed subtype (37.75%) and the renal subtype (10.45%). In the Post-COVID-19 period (n = 501), the gastrointestinal/articular/mixed subtype increased to 48.70%, which became the predominant subtype, while the renal subtype increased significantly to 20.36%. Consequently, the proportion of the cutaneous-only subtype declined to 30.94%.

Temporal trend analysis (Fig. 5, Page 34) revealed stable subtype composition in the Pre-COVID-19 period, dominated by the cutaneous-only form. Conversely, in the Post-COVID-19 era, the mixed and renal subtypes maintained consistently high proportions across all quarters, while the cutaneous-only subtype showed a sustained decline.

Follow-up outcomes of newly-diagnosed non-nephritic IgAV patients

A 6-month follow-up was conducted for newly-diagnosed non-nephritic IgAV patients in both cohorts. Despite some loss to follow-up due to address changes or contact difficulties, the final follow-up rates were high and comparable between groups (90.15% in Pre-COVID-19 vs. 91.70% in Post-COVID-19, P = 0.452 ). Follow-up results indicated that although the overall relapse rate was slightly higher in the Post-COVID-19 group, the difference was not statistically significant (P = 0.871 ). However, the proportion of patients progressing to IgAVN was significantly higher in the Post-COVID-19 cohort compared to the Pre-COVID-19 group (P = 0.035) (Table 3, Page 35).

Independent risk factors for new-onset IgAVN

Univariate logistic regression analysis comparing non-nephritic and new-onset IgAVN patients identified age, duration of rash, RBC, and IgA as common risk factors in both periods (all P < 0.05 ); Elevated levels of these indicators were associated with increased risk of IgAVN. However, several indicators exhibited period-specific differences. BMI, WBC, NEU, mean platelet volume (MPV), serum creatinine(Scr), cystatin C, and erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) emerged as new risk factors in the Post-COVID-19 period (all P < 0.05 ). Total protein (TP) and ALB were identified as unique protective factors in the Post-COVID-19 era (all P < 0.05 ). Notably, D-dimer exhibited a “role reversal”: it was a protective factor in the Pre-COVID-19 period but became a risk factor in the Post-COVID-19 period. Variables such as gender, rash type, abdominal/joint symptoms, Hp infection, infection history, allergens, lymphocyte count, and CRP showed no significant association with IgAVN in either period (all P > 0.05 ) (Table 4, Page36-37).

Multivariate logistic regression analysis revealed that in the Pre-COVID-19 period, independent risk factors for IgAVN were elevated IgA levels ( P = 0.017 ) and prolonged duration of rash (P < 0.001), while elevated D-dimer was an independent protective factor (P = 0.005 ). In the Post-COVID-19 period, independent risk factors shifted to increased age (P = 0.011 ), prolonged duration of rash (P < 0.001 ), and elevated ESR (P = 0.003 ), with elevated serum total protein serving as an independent protective factor (P = 0.003). IgA levels and D-dimer lost their statistical significance in the Post-COVID-19 model. Duration of rash remained the sole consistent independent risk factor for IgAVN across both periods (Fig. 6, Page 38).

Discussion

IgA vasculitis is the most common systemic vasculitis in childhood. The prevailing view holds that its pathogenesis is closely related to inflammatory reactions triggered by the deposition of IgA immune complexes in small vessel walls, although the exact etiology remains incompletely elucidated [19]. Studies indicate that various factors, such as upper respiratory tract infections, specific medications, and vaccinations, can act as potential triggers for disease onset in children [20]. While SARS-CoV-2 primarily affects the respiratory system, accumulating evidence has clarified its multi-system involvement characteristics. Whether the COVID-19 pandemic has influenced the epidemiological features of IgAV, and whether it has altered the clinical course and prognosis of the disease, remains unclear due to a lack of systematic research. Addressing this gap, we designed the present study.We found no significant differences in baseline demographic characteristics (age, gender, BMI) or seasonal distribution of onset between the pre-and post-COVID-19 cohorts, suggesting that the pandemic did not alter the fundamental epidemiological features or seasonal patterns of IgAV. However, regarding laboratory parameters, patients in the Post-COVID-19 period exhibited significant abnormalities, including elevated C3, C4, and IgM levels, alongside reduced IgA levels. These findings suggest the presence of immune activation and dysregulation in the Post-COVID-19 era, characterized by a “hyper-prepared state” of the complement system and a “deviation” in humoral immunity. This indicates that children in the Post-COVID-19 period may have undergone remodeling of mucosal immune responses and exacerbated complement-mediated vascular injury. Concurrently, alterations in markers such as low ALB and low FIB indirectly reflect the potential impact of pandemic-related factors on nutritional metabolism and coagulation function in these patients. Whether these states participate in and influence the pathogenesis and clinical phenotypes of IgAV warrants further investigation.

Furthermore, analysis of respiratory pathogens, Streptococcus remained the most frequently detected pathogen in both periods. Notably, however, the detection rate of Mycoplasma pneumoniae surged significantly in the Post-COVID-19 period, rising to the second rank, while the proportion of viruses such as respiratory syncytial virus declined. This shift may be associated with the “Immune debt” in the Post-COVID-19 population, triggering epidemics of atypical pathogens [21, 22]. The surge in Mycoplasma pneumoniae infection could be a key driver of the complement system overactivation and abnormal immune responses observed in our study. Overall, changes in the external environment and reconstruction of the respiratory pathogen spectrum during the pandemic may have exacerbated immune activation and inflammatory responses, collectively influencing the pathogenesis of IgAV.

To further explore the evolution of disease severity in IgAV in Post-COVID-19 era, we compared hospitalization data from our center before and after the pandemic. To control for confounding factors such as changes in the overall pediatric hospitalized population and adjustments in hospitalization structure, we introduced children with AGN hospitalized during the same periods as a disease control group. Results showed that compared to the Pre-COVID-19 period, the Post-COVID-19 IgAV cohort experienced significantly prolonged hospital stays, increased mean hospitalization costs, and a marked rise in the proportion of hospitalizations. This increase was significant for both newly-diagnosed and relapsed cases, with a particularly pronounced surge in relapsed cases, indicating a substantial increase in the overall disease burden in the Post-COVID-19 era. In contrast, AGN hospitalization metrics showed no significant fluctuations during the same period, demonstrating that the increased hospitalization burden of IgAV is disease-specific and not attributable to general changes in the hospitalization environment or structure.

To further exclude confounding effects from regional demographic shifts and changes in the medical environment, we constructed time-series prediction models for both diseases. Results indicated that the actual crude hospitalization rate of IgAV in the Post-COVID-19 period significantly deviated from the model’s predicted trajectory, with overall levels continuously exceeding the prediction interval. A significant spike was notably observed during the winters of 2023–2024. This anomalous outbreak in epidemiological trends cannot be explained by traditional seasonal patterns, suggesting the presence of new, potent external drivers (such as “immune remodeling” triggered by Post-COVID-19 infections [23] or the “Immune debt” effect [24]). Conversely, the actual hospitalization rate of AGN during the same period remained consistent with predicted values, re-validating the specificity of Post-COVID-19 changes in IgAV.

Of greater concern is the significant shift in the composition of the new-diagnosed IgAV clinical subtypes. Before the pandemic, the cutaneous-only subtype was predominant. In contrast, the Post-COVID-19 period saw a significant increase in the proportions of the renal subtype and the gastrointestinal/articular/mixed subtype, accompanied by a marked decline in the cutaneous-only subtype. This high-proportion status of severe subtypes persisted across all quarters in the Post-COVID-19 era. Collectively, these results indicate that IgAV children in the Post-COVID-19 period exhibit characteristics of “increased overall disease burden, elevated relapse risk, and severe clinical phenotypes.” This suggests that children with IgAV may have had more severe disease progression in the Post-COVID-19 era, potentially driven by the disruption of mucosal immune barriers and overactivation of the complement system, leading to easier deposition of immune complexes in multiple organs and systems.

Concurrently, the 6-month follow-up of newly-diagnosed non-nephritic IgAV patients showed that while the relapse rate in the Post-COVID-19 group was slightly higher, the difference was not statistically significant. However, the proportion of patients progressing to IgAVN was significantly higher in the Post-COVID-19 cohort compared to the Pre-COVID-19 group. This indicates that although the pandemic did not significantly affect the frequency of relapse, it markedly increased the risk of progression to renal damage in non-nephritic IgAV patients. From a long-term prognosis perspective, this further confirms that IgAV children in the Post-COVID-19 period may undergo more severe pathophysiological changes. These findings provide a critical basis for strengthening long-term follow-up management and early intervention for renal damage in IgAV patients in the Post-COVID-19 era.

It is well established that IgAVN and its severity are critical determinants of the clinical course and prognosis of IgAV. Studies report that the incidence of renal involvement in IgAV ranges from approximately 20% to 50%, varying by region and ethnicity [25, 26]. A recent study on COVID-19-associated vasculitis indicated that children with COVID-19-related IgAV/IgAVN appeared to experience a more severe clinical course [27], consistent with our preliminary findings. However, how the pandemic influences the incidence and prognostic outcomes of IgAVN remains unclear. Our study found that the spectrum of risk factors for IgAVN changed significantly in the Post-COVID-19 period, with abnormalities in inflammation, renal function, and coagulation parameters becoming more prominent. Notably, D-dimer reversed its role from a protective factor in the Pre-COVID-19 period to a risk factor in the Post-COVID-19 period. This suggests that children in the Post-COVID-19 era may be prone to excessive inflammatory responses accompanied by dysfunction of the coagulation-fibrinolysis system. Such an “inflammation-coagulation” interaction may further exacerbate renal injury.

Despite changes in the composition of risk factors, multivariate regression analysis identified duration of rash as the sole consistent independent risk factor across both periods, highlighting its stability as a key clinical indicator for predicting IgAVN. Furthermore, elevated erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) emerged as a new independent risk factor, while elevated serum total protein became a new independent protective factor in the Post-COVID-19 period. These findings reflect a significant rise in the weight of nutritional metabolism and inflammatory consumption in disease outcomes during the Post-COVID-19 endemic phase. These results suggest that the pandemic has not only altered the risk factor profile of IgAVN but also intensified the involvement of inflammation, nutrition, and coagulation functions in disease development. This may be the intrinsic driving force behind the significantly increased risk of renal involvement in IgAV children during the Post-COVID-19 era.

Limitations

Several limitations in this study should be acknowledged. First, as a single-center retrospective cohort study, selection bias and information bias are unavoidable. Moreover, as we only analyzed hospitalization data from our center, the results may not accurately represent the true incidence at the community level, limiting the generalizability of our findings. Second, the diagnosis of IgAVN in this study was established solely based on urinalysis findings (hematuria and proteinuria). Determining whether the pandemic influenced specific renal pathological types and the extent of such impact requires renal biopsy data; Consequently, this constitutes a limitation of our research. We hope to incorporate renal pathological findings in future studies to evaluate the specific effects of the pandemic. Third, the 6-month follow-up period was relatively short. Given the latent period of IgAVN onset, short-term follow-up may miss some cases of delayed renal involvement. Consequently, the observed incidence of IgAVN in our study was lower than that reported in some previous studies [28, 29]. Future studies should extend the follow-up duration to clarify long-term outcomes. Fourth, pathogen detection primarily relied on serological markers, such as Anti-streptolysin O (ASO) titers for Streptococcus and antibody assays for various viruses. It is important to note that positive antibody results may indicate recent exposure rather than acute active infection, which could potentially introduce bias into the estimated prevalence of certain pathogens. Future studies should prioritize more specific diagnostic methods, such as Anti-DNase B or polymerase chain reaction (PCR) assays, to more accurately identify active infections. Finally, this study can only confirm the association between the pandemic context and disease evolution; it cannot definitively determine whether these changes were caused by SARS-CoV-2 infection itself, vaccination, or other related factors. Future large-scale, multi-center prospective studies, incorporating pathological biopsies, long-term follow-up, and basic experiments, are needed to further elucidate the specific roles of viral exposure and vaccination in the pathogenesis of IgAV.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study confirms that childhood IgAV in the Post-COVID-19 era exhibits new characteristics, including “increased overall disease burden, severe clinical phenotypes, and a significantly elevated risk of progression to nephritis.” These changes may be associated with increased infections by atypical pathogens (such as Mycoplasma pneumoniae), excessive inflammatory responses, and dysfunction of the complement-coagulation system. Given the significantly increased risk of progression to nephritis in newly-diagnosed non-nephritic patients, it is imperative to establish more proactive early warning systems and long-term follow-up strategies in the Post-COVID-19 era. Finally, while the spectrum of risk factors for IgAVN has evolved, duration of rash remains a stable predictor of renal damage and warrants close clinical attention. Despite limitations such as the single-center design, this study provides important evidence-based insights into the epidemiological shifts and new clinical features of IgAV in the context of the pandemic.

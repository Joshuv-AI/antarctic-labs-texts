A nonlinear, causal link between serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D and prolactin in type 2 diabetes: Evidence from clinical and Mendelian randomization analyses
Authors: Jing He, Ying-chuan Yin, Wang Zhang, Xiao-hong Shi, Cui-ling Zhu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundType 2 diabetes mellitus is increasingly prevalent and often accompanied by vitamin D insufficiency. Prolactin, once considered solely lactogenic, has emerged as a metabolic regulator, yet its relationship with vitamin D in T2DM remains unclear.MethodsWe conducted a cross-sectional study of 221 male T2DM patients aged 25–75 years recruited between January 2022 and December 2024. Serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D (25(OH)D) and prolactin were measured, defining vitamin D sufficiency as ≥ 20 ng/mL. Associations were evaluated using group comparisons, Spearman correlation and multivariable regression adjusting for confounders. Restricted cubic splines assessed nonlinearity and thresholds. Causality was examined via Mendelian randomization employing 227 25(OH)D-associated variants.ResultsVitamin D deficiency affected 59.7 % of participants. Median prolactin levels were higher in vitamin D-sufficient than in deficient patients. Serum 25(OH)D correlated positively with prolactin and remained significant after adjustment. Spline analysis suggested a nonlinear relationship with an inflection of 18.48 ng/mL: below this threshold prolactin decreased as 25(OH)D increased, whereas above it prolactin rose steeply. Mendelian randomization provided evidence for a causal association: the inverse variance weighted estimate was non-significant, but MR-Egger and weighted median analyses indicated a significant link without pleiotropy or heterogeneity.ConclusionThese findings demonstrate a nonlinear, threshold-dependent association between vitamin D status and prolactin in male T2DM. Levels of 25(OH)D at 18.48 ng/mL were associated with suppressed prolactin, while sufficient concentrations enhanced prolactin, suggesting that maintaining adequate vitamin D may modulate prolactin and benefit metabolic outcomes. Further research is warranted to validate these observations.

Introduction

Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) imposes a large and rising global burden [[1], [2], [3]]. Beyond skeletal health, vitamin D exerts immunomodulatory, antifibrotic, and cardiometabolic actions, yet hypovitaminosis D is common in T2DM and may aggravate metabolic dysregulation [[4], [5], [6]]. Prolactin (PRL), long regarded as lactogenic, is now recognized as a pleiotropic regulator of energy balance, adiposity, and β-cell adaptation [7,8]. Converging basic data suggest a bidirectional vitamin D–PRL axis: vitamin D receptor activation in pituitary lactotrophs can upregulate PRL transcription, whereas PRL enhances renal 1α-hydroxylase activity, increasing conversion of 25-hydroxyvitamin D [25(OH)D] to its active form [7,8]. Epidemiology further links lower physiologic PRL with adverse glycometabolic profiles, although findings—particularly in men—are inconsistent and often limited by confounding, seasonality, and assay variation [8,9].

We hypothesized that the relationship between 25(OH)D and PRL in men with T2DM is nonlinear and threshold-dependent, reflecting hormone–hormone homeostasis. Accordingly, we combined (i) a hospital-based clinical study of male patients with rigorous covariate adjustment and restricted cubic splines to map dose–response shape and (ii) two-sample Mendelian randomization (MR) using LD-independent genome-wide instruments with pleiotropy-robust estimators to probe directionality. The clinical arm identified an inflection point at 18.48 ng/mL 25(OH)D, below which PRL tended to be lower and above which PRL rose more steeply; MR estimates were directionally consistent, providing genetic support under standard assumptions.

Materials and methods

Mendelian randomization analysis

To elucidate the causal influence of serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D [25(OH)D] concentrations on circulating prolactin (PRL) levels, we implemented a two-sample Mendelian Randomization (MR) framework grounded in genome-wide association summary statistics. This approach leverages genetic variants as instrumental variables (IVs) to simulate a quasi-randomized design, thereby mitigating confounding and reverse causality inherent in conventional observational studies.

Data sources

Publicly available GWAS summary data were retrieved from the MRC IEU OpenGWAS database (https://gwas.mrcieu.ac.uk), a curated resource that provides harmonized and quality-controlled genetic association datasets. Two independent, ancestry-matched cohorts of European descent were used:

Exposure (25(OH)D): Genetic instruments associated with circulating serum 25(OH)D levels were extracted from a meta-analysis (GWAS ID: ebi-a-GCST90000615), comprising 417,580 individuals from the UK Biobank and other large biobanks. The study applied rigorous quality control filters, adjusted for population stratification, and controlled for seasonal variation in vitamin D measurement.

Outcome (Prolactin): Genetic associations with circulating prolactin levels were obtained from a GWAS conducted in 21,758 individuals (GWAS ID: ebi-a-GCST90012030), ensuring population-level homogeneity and consistency with the exposure dataset.

Instrumental variable selection

To construct a robust set of instrumental variables for 25(OH)D, we applied the following selection criteria:

Genome-wide significance threshold: SNPs associated with 25(OH)D at a significance level of P < 5 × 10−6 were initially selected to balance instrument strength and IV quantity.

Linkage disequilibrium pruning: SNPs were clumped using a window of 10,000 kb and an LD threshold of r2 < 0.001 to ensure independence of genetic instruments.

Harmonization: Harmonization of exposure and outcome datasets was conducted using the TwoSampleMR R package (v0.5.6), aligning effect alleles and excluding ambiguous palindromic variants with intermediate allele frequencies to avoid strand misinterpretation.

After applying all filters, non-pleiotropic SNPs were retained as valid instruments for downstream analysis.

Primary MR estimation

The primary causal effect of genetically predicted serum 25(OH)D levels on prolactin was estimated using multiple complementary MR methods to enhance interpretative robustness:

Inverse Variance Weighted (IVW): The IVW estimator assumes all IVs are valid and provides the highest precision under the absence of horizontal pleiotropy.

MR-Egger Regression: This method relaxes the no-pleiotropy assumption and includes an intercept term to detect and correct for directional pleiotropy.

Weighted Median Estimator: Provides consistent causal estimates even if up to 50 % of the instruments are invalid.

Mode-Based Estimators (Simple and Weighted): Useful for identifying causal estimates based on the most frequent directionality among all SNPs.

All analyses were conducted using the TwoSampleMR and MendelianRandomization R packages.

Clinical cohort analysis and statistical modeling

This hospital-based, cross-sectional study enrolled 221 adult male patients diagnosed with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), who were consecutively admitted to the Department of Endocrinology at a tertiary-care academic center between January 2022 and December 2024. All participants provided written informed consent, and the study protocol was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria were as follows: (i) male sex; (ii) age between 25 and 75 years; (iii) a confirmed diagnosis of T2DM based on WHO 1999 or ADA 2020 criteria; (iv) availability of fasting serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D [25(OH)D] and prolactin (PRL) measurements obtained within the first 48 h of hospital admission. Exclusion criteria included: (i) acute or chronic renal insufficiency (eGFR <60 mL/min/1.73 m2); (ii) hepatic dysfunction (ALT or AST >2 × upper normal limit); (iii) active infection, malignancy, autoimmune disease, or known pituitary or hypothalamic disorders; (iv) current use of dopamine agonists, vitamin D supplementation, or medications influencing PRL secretion (e.g., antipsychotics, estrogens); and (v) incomplete clinical or biochemical records.

Data collection and variable definitions

Clinical and anthropometric data were obtained from electronic medical records, including age, weight, height, BMI, diabetes duration, and medication history. Fasting blood samples were collected after a minimum of 8 h overnight fasting to assess glucose metabolism (fasting blood glucose [FBG], HbA1c), lipid profile (TC, TG, HDL-C, LDL-C), renal function (uric acid [UA], urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio [ACR]), sex hormones (testosterone [TT], estradiol [E2], follicle-stimulating hormone [FSH], luteinizing hormone [LH]), and prolactin (PRL). Serum 25(OH)D was quantified via chemiluminescent immunoassay.

Based on previously published clinical thresholds, vitamin D status was dichotomized as follows: vitamin D-deficient group (25(OH)D < 20 ng/mL) and non-deficient group (≥20 ng/mL). This cutoff aligns with the Endocrine Society's clinical practice guideline for vitamin D insufficiency.

Group comparisons and bivariate analyses

Descriptive statistics were expressed as means ± standard deviations for normally distributed variables, medians (interquartile range) for skewed data, and counts (percentages) for categorical variables. Group comparisons between the vitamin D-deficient and non-deficient groups were performed using:

Independent t-tests for continuous variables with normal distribution; Mann–Whitney U-tests for skewed distributions; Chi-square tests for categorical variables.

Spearman correlation analyses were employed to assess the monotonic associations between 25(OH)D, PRL, and other clinical parameters.

Restricted cubic spline (RCS) modeling

To examine the potential nonlinear association between 25(OH)D and PRL levels, restricted cubic spline (RCS) regression was employed using the rms package in R (version 4.3.1). The number and location of spline knots were selected based on the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC), with four knots placed at empirical percentiles (5th, 35th, 65th, 95th). The adjusted models were specified with PRL as the dependent variable and 25(OH)D as the continuous predictor, incorporating all aforementioned covariates. The nonlinear component was tested using the Wald chi-squared test for spline terms. The inflection point (threshold concentration) was determined from the turning point on the RCS curve where the direction of association changed.

Software and statistical environment

All statistical analyses were conducted using R software (v4.3.1; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) and SPSS Statistics (v26.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Figures and data visualizations were generated using the ggplot2, rms, and forestplot packages in R. Sensitivity analyses were performed to confirm robustness of the spline model and multivariable regressions.

Results

Mendelian randomization reveals a causal effect of vitamin D on prolactin levels

In a two-sample Mendelian randomization analysis, 227 uncorrelated 25(OH)D-associated SNPs (P < 5 × 10−6, r2 < 0.001) with mean F-statistics well above 10 were selected to minimize weak-instrument bias. MR leverages genetically randomized variation to infer causality while mitigating confounding and reverse causality. The inverse-variance weighted (IVW) estimator suggested a non-significant inverse association between 25(OH)D and prolactin (β = −0.0769, P = 0.210). By contrast, sensitivity estimators showed consistent and significant inverse effects: MR-Egger β = −0.1918 (P = 0.044), weighted median β = −0.1186 (P = 0.035) and simple mode β = −0.0381 (P = 0.019). Instrument independence was ensured; MR-PRESSO and radial MR detected no outlier variants, and leave-one-out, forest and funnel plots revealed no influential outliers or horizontal pleiotropy. MR-Egger intercept and Cochran's Q statistics did not indicate heterogeneity. Divergence between IVW and robust estimators may reflect residual biases or ancestry differences, highlighting the need for replication in multi-ethnic cohorts. Overall, these results support hormonal causality interaction (see Fig. 1).

Discussion

This research investigated the endocrine interplay between vitamin D and prolactin (PRL) in men with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) by integrating cross-sectional epidemiology with genetic inference. Across 221 hospitalized men, multivariable models showed an overall positive association between circulating 25-hydroxyvitamin D [25(OH)D] and PRL, but the dose–response was nonlinear, with a statistically defined inflection at 18.48 ng/mL. Below this threshold, 25(OH)D and PRL were inversely related; above it, PRL rose more steeply with increasing 25(OH)D. Two-sample Mendelian randomization (MR) using 227 genome-wide significant instruments indicated that genetically higher vitamin D tends to reduce PRL, providing directionality under standard assumptions. Together, these results delineate a bidirectional, threshold-dependent vitamin D–PRL axis in male T2DM and refine current concepts of vitamin D targets.

The shape of association is biologically coherent. At sub-optimal 25(OH)D, inflammatory/oxidative stress and altered dopaminergic tone can disturb pituitary homeostasis, producing a modest up-shift of basal PRL [10,11]. With sufficiency, ligand-activated VDR in lactotrophs can trans-activate the PRL promoter, while PRL—via PRLR–JAK2/STAT5 and serotonergic programs in β-cells—supports insulin secretion and β-cell mass adaptation [11]. This dual-action framework reconciles a local positive slope above 18.48 ng/mL with the negative MR estimate: MR reflects lifelong, small genetic differences and slow feedback through renal 1α-hydroxylase/hepatic vitamin D metabolism, whereas our spline captures short-term, level-specific coupling [[11], [12], [13]]. Under nonlinearity, local effects around a threshold can diverge from population-average genetic effects without contradiction.

Our findings converge with recent trial-level evidence that achieving vitamin D sufficiency improves glycemic trajectories and meta-analytic signals linking higher 25(OH)D with better insulin sensitivity, while extending prior translational work positioning PRL as a metabolic homeostat in which both low and high extremes are deleterious but intermediate elevations (HomeoFIT range) are adaptive [14,15]. The innovations are threefold: (i) formalizing shape and threshold (18.48 ng/mL) rather than only strength; (ii) focusing on men with T2DM, a group under-represented in vitamin D–PRL research historically centered on women or pregnancy; and (iii) triangulating observational nonlinearity with genetic directionality (227-SNP MR), thereby reducing confounding by adiposity, seasonality, medication, and comorbidity [16,17]. These advances refine endocrine models and generate testable targets for individualized care.

Given the inflection near 18.48 ng/mL, a one-size-fits-all threshold is unlikely to capture the transition zone where PRL modulation switches from inhibition to stimulation. For male T2DM, maintaining 25(OH)D just above this zone may maximize PRL-mediated β-cell support and insulin action without assuming linear gains at higher concentrations [18,19]. Practically, PRL—interpreted within physiological ranges and clinical context—could serve as a responsive biomarker to guide vitamin D titration [9,18,19]. From a translational perspective, the coexistence of a local positive slope (clinical arm) and a negative average genetic effect (MR) argues for precision dosing anchored to level-specific endocrine states rather than uniform escalation.

Several considerations frame the interpretation of our findings and, importantly, inform the most productive next steps. First, the clinical arm is cross-sectional, which constrains inferences about temporal ordering; our analytic strategy mitigates but cannot eliminate this constraint. Second, seasonality, sun exposure, diet, and vitamin D supplementation were not systematically captured and could contribute to variation in 25(OH)D. Third, serum PRL was quantified as total immunoreactive hormone without resolution of isoforms (e.g., macro-PRL versus bioactive fractions), which may subtly influence physiological interpretation. Fourth, the MR instruments were derived predominantly from European GWAS, so transportability to Chinese men—despite the use of multiple pleiotropy-robust estimators—merits cautious interpretation. Finally, the 18.48 ng/mL inflection is a model-based estimate and would benefit from replication under diverse settings. These caveats naturally shape our forward pathway. Embedding multi-omics (e.g., steroidogenesis, dopaminergic tone, inflammatory signaling) will provide mechanistic anchors and help reconcile short-term endocrine coupling with longer-term genetic signals. Taken together, these steps are designed to strengthen generalizability while preserving the nuance revealed by our current data.

In summary, through coupling nonlinear epidemiology with 227-SNP MR, we uncover a mechanistically coherent threshold (18.48 ng/mL) in the vitamin D–PRL axis of male T2DM, offering a testable basis for PRL-informed, precision vitamin D optimization.

Research on the impact of digital inclusive finance on the performance of rural returnee entrepreneurs in China
Authors: Yuran Chen, Ruoxuan Huang, Yangge Zeng, Qian Huang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Using data from the 2019 China Household Finance Survey (CHFS), this paper explores the impact of digital inclusive finance on the entrepreneurial performance of returning rural migrants. The study first finds that digital inclusive finance can enhance the entrepreneurial performance of returning rural migrants. Second, it significantly improves entrepreneurial performance in three dimensions: the degree of digitization in inclusive finance, the depth of digital financial usage, and the breadth of digital financial coverage. Third, farmers’ digital participation, digital usage, and the extent of digital usage are crucial pathways through which digital inclusive finance influences their entrepreneurial performance. Fourth, the impact of digital inclusive finance on entrepreneurial performance varies according to different types of migration, duration of migration, scale of entrepreneurship, educational attainment, and credit access channels. The findings of this study help validate the real effects of digital inclusive finance on entrepreneurial outcomes in rural populations.

Introduction

Since the implementation of the reform and opening-up policy, along with social and economic development and accelerated urbanization, rural labor has gradually shifted to cities in pursuit of higher wages. During this period, the migration of rural labor to urban areas for employment has become a typical feature of social and economic development. However, in recent years, influenced by the pandemic, global economic growth has slowed down, leading to a continuous increase in the overall unemployment rate worldwide. Some cities are facing issues such as structural unemployment, making it increasingly challenging for rural workers to find new jobs in urban areas. Therefore, some rural workers urgently hope to change their current predicament through entrepreneurship1,2.

In China, the implementation of the national rural revitalization policy and poverty alleviation strategy has created new opportunities for development in rural areas. This has increased farmers’ enthusiasm for returning to their hometowns to start businesses. According to data released by the Chinese Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security, by the end of 2022, the cumulative number of migrant workers returning to their hometowns for entrepreneurship nationwide had reached 12.2 million. Therefore, guiding and supporting a large number of farmers to start businesses locally and encouraging more farmers to work in their hometowns can not only improve farmers’ income and living standards but also address the rural social issues caused by the migration of many farmers to urban areas for work. Financial support is essential for entrepreneurship, and entrepreneurial enterprises and families often face significant financial constraints, affecting the smooth operation of their activities. To address the funding issues of small and micro-enterprises and entrepreneurial families, China has explored various solutions, including establishing microcredit institutions, building microcredit organizations, and providing loans based on farmers’ creditworthiness. The promotion of inclusive finance has achieved notable results3,4. Especially in recent years, the support of digital technology has led to rapid development in digital inclusive finance. Its broad coverage and sustainable characteristics help overcome the temporal and spatial constraints of financial services, reduce the cost and entry barriers to these services, and achieve inclusive growth5–7. However, during the development of digital technology, there are still issues of imbalance and inadequacy in economic development levels and infrastructure, which can lead to a digital divide8. Therefore, studying whether digital inclusive finance can promote family entrepreneurship, the mechanisms through which it affects family entrepreneurship, and whether it has played an inclusive role is of practical significance.

Current research on the factors influencing entrepreneurship is conducted primarily from both macro and micro perspectives. At the micro level, scholars analyze individual characteristics influencing resident or household entrepreneurship, specifically addressing gender8, human capital9, risk preferences10, social insurance11, and internet usage. At the macro level, scholars investigate the impacts of external environmental and institutional factors on regional entrepreneurship levels. This includes infrastructure investments, such as road construction, and institutional factors, including intellectual property protection, administrative approvals, and government regulations12. Additionally, finance is considered a crucial external condition influencing entrepreneurship. A substantial body of literature has confirmed the innovation-driven role of finance, suggesting that traditional finance can promote entrepreneurial activities through reasonable and effective resource allocation and alleviation of potential entrepreneurs’ liquidity constraints9. However, in the context of rapidly developing digital technology, digital inclusive finance has effectively alleviated financing constraints and enhanced economic inclusivity5. Tian et al. (2023) found that digital finance can increase regional entrepreneurship levels by expanding the financial supply scope, reducing financial transaction costs, and promoting technological innovation13. Yin et al. (2023) found that mobile payments offer advantages in reducing residents’ entrepreneurial costs and credit constraints, thereby increasing the likelihood of family entrepreneurship14.

Prior studies offer guidance and reference for this research. Although existing research focuses on the entrepreneurial effects of the digital economy, it overlooks changes in entrepreneurial performance caused by the inclusive effects of digital inclusive finance when discussing mechanisms to promote entrepreneurship. Compared to traditional finance, digital inclusive finance more effectively improves financial accessibility, breaks the temporal and spatial limitations of financial services, and enables resource allocation on a broader temporal and spatial scale. Therefore, ignoring the inclusive effects of digital inclusive finance might underestimate its positive impact on household entrepreneurship. In this context, this paper explores the relationship between digital inclusive finance and the entrepreneurial performance of rural migrants returning to their hometowns. The aim is to clarify the pathways and mechanisms involved and to examine how digital inclusive finance differentially impacts the entrepreneurial performance of returning rural migrants with various traits, thus enriching existing research.

The marginal contributions of this paper are as follows: First, unlike previous studies primarily focusing on entrepreneurship, this paper concentrates on the unique group of farmers. This approach helps create more employment opportunities from the farmers’ perspective, thereby reducing poverty rates in rural areas. Second, the systematic exploration of the mechanisms through which digital inclusive finance affects household entrepreneurship in three dimensions: digital participation (ownership of digital devices), digital usage (utilization of digital financial services), and the degree of digital usage (extent of using digital financial services), which deepens our understanding of how digital inclusive finance enhances farmers’ entrepreneurial capabilities and contributes to high-quality economic development. This research is significantly valuable for the global advancement of digital inclusive finance services and infrastructure. It is of considerable value for the comprehensive advancement of digital inclusive finance services and infrastructure worldwide. Third, analyzing the heterogeneity of the impact of digital inclusive finance on rural farmers’ household entrepreneurship from various perspectives, such as migrant types, duration, and business scale, which provides theoretical references for the government to implement differentiated policies.

Literature review

Research on the definition and influencing factors of digital inclusive finance

Research on the definition of digital inclusive finance

The concept of inclusive finance first emerged during the International Year of Microcredit in 2005, defined as providing appropriate financial products and services to underserved groups in society. In China, the national policy document “Plan for the Development of Inclusive Finance (2016–2020)” defines inclusive finance as commercially sustainable financial services provided to small and micro enterprises, farmers, and low-income groups at relatively low costs, adhering to the principle of equal opportunity. Digital inclusive finance, as an emerging financial model, aims to expand the coverage of traditional financial services through digital technology, achieving broader financial inclusivity. Its core lies in leveraging digital tools such as the internet and mobile technology to reduce the cost of providing financial services, increase service efficiency, and reach low-income groups and small micro-enterprises traditionally underserved by conventional finance15. With the development of digital technology, the challenges faced by traditional inclusive finance, such as high costs, low efficiency, and difficulty in risk control, have been mitigated through digital means. Digital inclusive finance can significantly expand the coverage of financial services and effectively reduce information asymmetry and transaction costs16. Additionally, the G20 Global Partnership for Financial Inclusion report clarifies the objectives and concepts of digital inclusive finance, emphasizing its responsibilities and commercial sustainability to ensure fair access to financial services. According to research by Hasan et al. (2022), the rise of digital inclusive finance has facilitated access to financial services for rural and low-income groups and, to some extent, promoted economic growth and social equity17.

Factors influencing digital inclusive finance

The factors influencing digital inclusive finance have been widely discussed in the existing literature, mainly encompassing the macroeconomic environment, technological development level, regulatory framework, socio-cultural factors, and individual characteristics. First, in the context of the macroeconomic environment, the economic growth rate and savings rate significantly impact the development of digital inclusive finance. Economic growth not only increases disposable income but also raises the demand for financial products, thereby promoting the expansion of digital inclusive finance18. Second, in terms of technological development, the widespread adoption of internet and mobile communication technologies is a crucial factor driving the development of digital inclusive finance. Technological advancements have significantly reduced the cost of financial services, improved service efficiency, and expanded the coverage of these services19. Third, concerning financial regulation, a sound regulatory framework is essential for the development of digital inclusive finance. Effective regulation can protect consumer rights and enhance public trust in financial services. However, overly stringent regulation may restrict innovation and market entry5. Fourth, socio-cultural factors, such as the acceptance and trust in technology, also influence the promotion and use of digital inclusive finance. Societies with high levels of trust are more likely to accept emerging financial technologies20. Fifth, regarding individual characteristics, education level and technical proficiency are crucial factors determining whether individuals can fully utilize digital inclusive finance services. Higher education levels and technical literacy help improve individuals’ understanding and usage of digital financial products, thereby enhancing their financial inclusion7.

Study on the factors influencing and the construction of indicators for farmers’ entrepreneurial performance

Research on constructing indicators for farmers’ entrepreneurial performance

Entrepreneurial performance, as a key metric for assessing the outcomes and goal achievement of entrepreneurial activities, has been a focal point in academic research. The following are the four main theoretical frameworks for understanding entrepreneurial performance: population ecology theory, social cognition theory, resource-based theory, and strategic adaptation theory. These theories offer diverse perspectives and explanatory frameworks for understanding entrepreneurial performance. In practice, indicators for measuring entrepreneurial performance are typically categorized into three types: first, financial indicators such as revenue and profit; second, non-financial indicators such as market share and customer satisfaction; and third, a mixed approach that combines both financial and non-financial indicators.

For migrant workers returning to rural areas to start businesses, achieving economic benefits is one of the primary goals. Therefore, the measurement of entrepreneurial performance often focuses on financial metrics, such as annual income, business turnover, and profit. However, given the generally small scale and simple business operations of returning migrant entrepreneurs, some scholars suggest using more diverse methods to assess their performance. For example, entrepreneurs can use a self-assessment approach to evaluate the company’s performance in areas such as market share, profitability, and return on investment relative to industry peers. This multi-perspective evaluation method provides a more comprehensive analysis of performance and reveals strengths and potential in non-financial indicators, thereby offering richer information for business decision-makers. This multidimensional analytical framework helps to more accurately reflect the actual operational status and development potential of enterprises, which is especially important for small and micro-enterprises with limited resources21.

Study on the influencing factors of farmers’ entrepreneurship

The entrepreneurial environment is a crucial factor influencing entrepreneurial performance, providing the necessary scarce resources and valuable opportunities for starting, sustaining, and developing a business. First, regarding the impact of the entrepreneurial environment, entrepreneurs must identify and evaluate the resources and opportunities within these environments, adjust their strategies accordingly, and even create an environment conducive to their development to enhance entrepreneurial performance22. This requires entrepreneurs to have keen market insight and necessitates innovation and adaptability to respond to environmental changes and market demands. Second, regarding the influence of individual characteristics, personal traits such as gender, social status, entrepreneurial ability, and access to policy resources significantly impact the performance of returning migrant entrepreneurs. Third, concerning the influence of entrepreneurial experience, it has been proven that entrepreneurial experience is a critical factor affecting business performance, with experienced entrepreneurs more likely to achieve higher performance23. Fourth, regarding the impact of educational level, higher education is often associated with better entrepreneurial performance, particularly for entrepreneurs with a college degree or higher, who are more likely to succeed. Fifth, regarding the influence of social capital, the role of social capital in entrepreneurial performance is somewhat uncertain. Some studies suggest that social capital does not significantly impact business performance24. However, other research indicates that social capital, especially social networks, has a significant positive effect on farmers’ entrepreneurial performance. Thus, social networks and relationships can provide essential resources and support during the entrepreneurial process, particularly in acquiring information, financing, and market expansion.

Study on digital inclusive finance and farmers’ entrepreneurial performance

In China, farmers often face significant funding shortages during the early stages of entrepreneurship, with credit constraints being a prominent issue. The availability of financial products and services urgently needs improvement. However, relying solely on the development of traditional financial sectors is insufficient to fully address these issues. Demirguc (2008) pointed out that merely expanding the financial sector cannot effectively enhance the availability of financial services. Instead, it is necessary to improve inclusive finance by expanding financial scale and enhancing service accessibility25. Digital technology empowers the development of inclusive finance, providing an effective pathway to address these problems. Huang and Huang (2016) noted that using the internet for functions such as payments, transfers, and loans not only expands the coverage of financial services but also significantly reduces transaction costs, laying the foundation for the widespread adoption of digital financial services in rural areas26.

In recent years, with increasing internet penetration in rural areas, the infrastructure for digital inclusive finance has gradually improved. Digital inclusive finance has alleviated financial exclusion among farmers to some extent, by enhancing the availability of financial services and information and increasing trust in the surrounding environment. This, in turn, has spurred entrepreneurial activities and improved entrepreneurial performance. Nobanee et al., (2022) pointed out that this effect is particularly significant in non-agricultural or subsistence entrepreneurship. Additionally, big data-based risk scoring mechanisms have reduced the costs of credit assessment, overcoming the challenge of insufficient financial information for small and micro-enterprises. This has enabled some businesses to overcome credit constraints, stimulating entrepreneurial dynamism, and improving the efficiency of the overall economic system27.

Existing research has explored the impact of the digital economy on entrepreneurship but has often overlooked the inclusive effects of digital finance on entrepreneurial performance. Although some studies suggest that digital inclusive finance enhances the accessibility of financial services compared to traditional finance, they have not fully considered its role in improving conditions for household entrepreneurship. This oversight may lead to an underestimation of the positive impact of digital inclusive finance on household entrepreneurship. Therefore, this paper focuses on investigating the relationship between digital inclusive finance and the entrepreneurial performance of returning rural migrants. It aims to clarify how digital inclusive finance influences entrepreneurial performance by enhancing financial accessibility and resource allocation efficiency. The novelty of this paper lies not only in examining the overall effects but also in analyzing the differential impacts of digital inclusive finance on the entrepreneurial performance of different groups of returning rural migrants. This approach helps elucidate the pathways and mechanisms through which digital inclusive finance operates, thereby enriching and expanding the existing theoretical and empirical research on the relationship between the digital economy and entrepreneurial activities.

Theoretical mechanisms and research hypotheses

The impact of digital inclusive finance on the entrepreneurial performance of returning farmers

The entrepreneurship of returning rural migrants plays a crucial role in promoting rural economic development and addressing the urban-rural development imbalance. A key factor influencing entrepreneurial performance is the entrepreneur’s ability to acquire and allocate external resources. The resource-based view posits that a firm’s competitive advantage stems from its unique resources, which include tangible assets such as capital equipment and intangible assets like technology and brand28,29.

In the context of returning rural migrants starting businesses, the development of digital inclusive finance provides new avenues for enhancing farmers’ ability to access external resources. First, on the supply side, digital inclusive finance reduces operational costs, risk management costs, and marketing costs through technological innovation. This enables financial institutions to offer a diverse range of credit products more competitively, particularly those targeting farmers, thereby helping to expand the scale of credit supply. For instance, by utilizing big data and artificial intelligence technologies, financial institutions can more accurately assess farmers’ credit risk, reduce the time and cost of loan approvals, and increase the efficiency of capital allocation. Furthermore, refined risk management helps lower credit risk, making financial institutions more willing to extend credit support to farmers7.

Second, on the demand side, increased internet penetration significantly enhances farmers’ access to financial information and services. The widespread use of digital financial platforms enables farmers to easily access financial information and services, stimulating latent financial demand. Through these platforms, farmers can conveniently compare the interest rates, fees, and terms of various financial products, allowing them to choose the products that best meet their needs. Additionally, the application of digital financial technologies, such as mobile payments and online wealth management tools, enables farmers to manage their finances more flexibly, improving the efficiency of capital use30.

Third, the positive impact of digital inclusive finance on both the supply and demand sides of resources not only enhances economic mobility but also helps reduce the disparity in access to financial services between rural and urban areas. Specifically, this financial inclusivity expands the coverage of financial services, improves affordability, and enhances service quality, thereby creating a more equitable financial environment. This not only lowers the barriers for rural residents to access financial resources but also encourages more farmers to return to their hometowns to start businesses, thereby promoting rural economic development.

Based on the above analysis, this paper proposes the following hypothesis:

H1: Digital inclusive finance can effectively enhance the entrepreneurial performance of returning rural migrant farmers.

Mechanisms by which digital inclusive finance affects the entrepreneurial performance of returning rural migrant farmers

The digital divide refers to disparities in access to information and digital resources caused by the uneven distribution of information and communication technologies (ICT). This divide has profound implications for household income and consumption, especially when ICT skills are unevenly distributed. Households with an advantage in information processing can gain greater economic benefits, exacerbating income inequality and poverty rates31,32.

In China, the uneven development of digital technology between urban and rural areas allows urban households to benefit more from digital advancements than rural households, exacerbating income inequality between these regions33. However, digital inclusive finance can narrow the digital divide by increasing household participation, usage, and depth of engagement with digital technologies, thereby enhancing entrepreneurial performance. By improving the accessibility and prevalence of digital financial services, digital inclusive finance not only boosts household economic autonomy but also provides greater market opportunities and resources, significantly increasing the likelihood of entrepreneurial success. Digital inclusive finance plays a critical role in promoting inclusive economic growth and reducing the economic gap between urban and rural areas, particularly by supporting the entrepreneurship and economic development of rural households34,35.

In conclusion, the development of digital inclusive finance provides a practical foundation for addressing financial exclusion, particularly among disadvantaged groups in remote and underdeveloped rural areas. By using devices such as smartphones and computers, these groups can participate in the development of digital inclusive finance36,37. Additionally, returning rural migrants often have experience in more developed regions, which makes them proficient in using smartphones and computers for electronic payments and online banking. This familiarity enhances their willingness to participate in digital inclusive finance, which can improve their entrepreneurial performance and increase business returns38–40.

Based on this, the paper proposes the following hypothesis:

H2: Digital inclusive finance can improve the entrepreneurial performance of returning rural migrant farmers by increasing digital participation, digital usage, and the extent of digital usage.

Research design

Sample and data source

Data for this study were obtained from the China Household Finance Survey (CHFS), conducted by the Southwestern University of Finance and Economics. The sample for this data is derived from 29 provinces, 355 districts and counties, and 1481 communities in China. The CHFS is the largest unofficial household asset sampling survey project in China, collecting comprehensive and detailed micro-level information on surveyed households, including demographic characteristics, assets and liabilities, income, and consumption, providing a thorough reflection of the economic and financial status of these households. The dataset serves as a robust foundation for this study. Notably, considering data availability and alignment with the research objectives, this study utilizes the 2019 CHFS survey data for empirical research.

Furthermore, the study utilizes the “Peking University Digital Inclusive Finance Index,” a collaborative effort between Peking University’s Digital Finance Research Center and Ant Group. This index reflects the development of digital inclusive finance across 31 provinces, autonomous regions, and municipalities directly under the central government, 337 cities at or above the prefecture level, and approximately 2800 counties in China. The index encompasses various aspects, including the breadth of digital inclusive finance coverage, depth of usage, degree of digitization, and inclusiveness in different financial services.

The author processed the research data as follows: First, due to the inherent lag in survey data, the author matched the 2018 Digital Inclusive Finance Index with the 2019 CHFS data for comprehensive analysis. Second, considering the research focus and objectives, the author excluded non-agricultural households from the 2019 CHFS data and further excluded samples where entrepreneurial activities were not pursued. This resulted in the identification of the total entrepreneurial farmer sample. Third, the author excluded samples of entrepreneurial farmer households with family members who had been away for less than six months. This step resulted in the identification of the sample of returning migrant entrepreneurial farmers. Fourth, the author eliminated samples with missing values for the dependent variable and replaced missing values in the selected control variables with their respective means.

Variable selection

Dependent variable

The dependent variable in this study is the entrepreneurial performance of rural migrants who have returned home. It is important to note that, for the research subjects in this study, entrepreneurship includes both agricultural and non-agricultural activities. According to the annotations in the 2019 CHFS questionnaire, the earnings generated from projects registered with the industrial and commercial department are defined as entrepreneurial performance. While previous literature often uses quantifiable and objective indicators such as average return on assets, sales revenue growth rate, sales revenue, or net profit to evaluate entrepreneurial performance, this study acknowledges that entrepreneurship in rural China often consists of small-scale individual businesses or micro-enterprises, which result in non-standardized financial metrics. In line with the practical situation in China, this study adopts the approach of Wang et al. (2021)41. Specifically, the respondents’ reported numerical values for the operational income of industrial and commercial projects are selected as the measurement indicator for entrepreneurial performance.

Explanatory variable

The primary focus variable in this study is the Digital Inclusive Finance Index. This index is derived from the “Peking University Digital Inclusive Finance Index (2011–2020),” and encompasses three dimensions: coverage breadth, depth of usage, and degree of digitization. For this study, the Digital Inclusive Finance Index for each province in 2018 was selected for matching and measurement.

Mediating variable

Building on the methodologies of Yin et al. (2019) and Elena-Bucea et al. (2021) for dimensionally categorizing the digital divide, this study utilizes indicators across three dimensions: digital participation (ownership of digital devices), digital usage (use of digital financial services), and the degree of digital usage (extent of use of digital financial services), as mediating variables. Subsequently, these variables are employed to examine the impact mechanism of digital inclusive finance on the entrepreneurial performance of rural migrants returning to their hometowns42,43. Control variables

Drawing on existing literature, the study has selected control variables that reflect the personal and household characteristics of the household head44–47. Individual characteristic variables include the household head’s gender, age, education level, marital status, and health status. Household characteristic variables include family size, the number of labor force members, family social capital, family financial capital, risk aversion, and financial knowledge. Heterogeneity analysis

Types of migrant activities and duration of migrant activities

To examine the varied impact of digital inclusive finance on entrepreneurial performance among returnee farmers with different types of migrant activities, we constructed a binary variable, following the approach of Xie and Qian 48. In this study, family members with migrant experiences as employers, formal workers, self-employed individuals, or those engaged in studies, which significantly enhance human capital, were assigned a value of 1. Other returnee farmers were assigned a value of 0. To explore the varied impact of digital inclusive finance on the entrepreneurial performance of returnee farmers with different durations of migrant activities, we created an additional binary variable. Family members with a migrant duration of four years or more were classified as having long-term migrant experiences and were assigned a value of 1. Other returnee farmers were assigned a value of 0.

Table 7 displays the regression results illustrating the impact of digital inclusive finance on the entrepreneurial performance of returnee farmers with varied types and durations of migrant activities. Columns (1) and (2) present grouped regression results based on whether family members were engaged in migrant activities, such as being employers, formal workers, self-employed individuals, or pursuing studies, all of which significantly promote the accumulation of human capital. According to the regression results, the coefficients of the Digital Inclusive Finance Index for the two groups are 0.0143 and 0.0076, respectively, both significant at the 1% level. After applying Fisher’s combined test, the difference between the coefficients of the two groups is significant at the 10% level. This suggests that migrant activities that significantly promote human capital accumulation, such as being employers, formal workers, self-employed individuals, or pursuing studies, significantly enhance the positive impact of digital inclusive finance on the entrepreneurial performance of returnee farmers.

Columns (3) and (4) present the grouped regression results for families of returnee farmers, comparing those with family members having migrant durations of 4 years or more to those without such extended migrant durations. The regression results indicate that entrepreneurial performance in both groups is significantly influenced by digital inclusive finance, although the results do not pass Fisher’s combined test. Therefore, even though the impact of digital inclusive finance on the entrepreneurial performance of returnee farmers is significantly positive in both groups, the differences in coefficients are not substantial. Consequently, the presence of family members with extended migrant durations does not significantly contribute to variations in the impact of digital inclusive finance on entrepreneurial performance. This may be attributed to longer migrant durations not effectively increasing human capital accumulation, resulting in minimal differences compared to returnee farmers with shorter migrant durations or those who have not engaged in migrant activities.

Entrepreneurial scale

To study the differential impact of digital inclusive finance on the entrepreneurial performance of returnee farmers with varying scales of entrepreneurship, we followed the methodology of Wang et al., (2021) and selected the total investment amount of entrepreneurial projects as a measure of entrepreneurial scale, based on data availability41. The sample was divided into three groups: small, medium, and large, using terciles for sub-sample classification. Values of 1, 2, and 3 were assigned to each group, respectively.

Table 8 presents the grouped regression results of digital inclusive finance on the entrepreneurial performance of returning migrant farmers, categorized by different scales of entrepreneurship. The regression analysis indicates that, for large-scale and medium-scale entrepreneurial projects, the digital inclusive finance index significantly influences entrepreneurial performance at the 1% statistical level. For small-scale entrepreneurial projects, the digital inclusive finance index’s impact on entrepreneurial performance is significant at the 5% statistical level. Although the regression coefficients of the core explanatory variable are significant across all three groups, this does not statistically confirm the differences among them. Therefore, an inter-group coefficient difference test is necessary. Given the different scales of entrepreneurship, the sample was divided into three groups for regression analysis, and inter-group coefficient difference tests were conducted pairwise. According to the results, the empirical P-values were not significant. To ensure the reliability of these inter-group coefficient difference test results, robustness checks were performed using the seemingly unrelated estimation (SUEST) method and the Chow test. The results remained non-significant, thereby confirming the robustness of the grouped regression analysis findings.

Based on the regression analysis, digital inclusive finance significantly positively impacts the entrepreneurial performance of returning migrant farmers across various scales of entrepreneurship. However, there are no significant differences between the samples of different scales. A possible reason is that digital inclusive finance can optimally meet the diverse financial needs of different groups, overcoming the traditional financial limitations related to price, conditions, and collateral, thereby enabling farmers to access financial services fairly and reasonably.

Robustness checks

Instrumental variable method

The dependent and core explanatory variables in this paper are sourced from two different databases. The core explanatory variable, the Digital Inclusive Finance Index, is a multi-level composite indicator. The dependent variable is derived from the micro-survey data of the China Household Finance Survey (CHFS). The study employs the one-period lagged value of the Digital Inclusive Finance Index, matched with CHFS data; however, endogeneity issues may still be present in the model estimation. To address endogeneity, this study utilizes the 2018 internet penetration rate of Chinese provinces as an instrumental variable.

Table 11 presents the two-stage regression results using the instrumental variable. The first-stage regression results show that the 2018 internet penetration rate in Chinese provinces is significantly positively correlated with digital inclusive finance at the 1% statistical level, indicating that the internet penetration rate is a strong predictor of digital inclusive finance. In the weak instrument test, the F-statistic far exceeds the empirical threshold of 10, confirming that the variable is not a weak instrument. The second-stage regression results reveal that, even after accounting for endogeneity, digital inclusive finance continues to have a significant positive effect on the entrepreneurial performance of returning migrant farmers. Therefore, the study’s conclusions are robust.

Conclusions and recommendations

The study, based on data from the China Household Finance Survey (CHFS) and the China Digital Inclusive Finance Index, empirically investigates the impact of digital inclusive finance on the entrepreneurial performance of returning rural migrants. The research reveals several key findings: first, digital inclusive finance significantly enhances the entrepreneurial performance of these individuals. Second, it improves performance across three dimensions: the degree of digitalization in inclusive finance, the depth of digital finance usage, and the breadth of digital finance coverage. Third, the digital participation, usage, and intensity of use among rural migrants are crucial channels through which digital inclusive finance influences their entrepreneurial outcomes. Lastly, there is heterogeneity in the impact of digital inclusive finance on entrepreneurial performance among rural migrants, varying by type of migration, duration of migration, scale of entrepreneurship, level of education, and access to credit.

Based on the aforementioned conclusions, this paper proposes the following policy recommendations to enhance the coverage, utilization depth, and digitization of digital inclusive finance. First, it is essential to strengthen the digital infrastructure in rural areas, particularly by expanding modern communication networks to address the “last mile” issue in remote and impoverished regions. Second, digital infrastructure coverage should be expanded, promoting the development of comprehensive rural information service platforms to enhance the level of networked operations across rural industries and foster sustainable economic development. Third, digital applications tailored specifically to the needs of agriculture and rural areas should be developed to alleviate the difficulties farmers face in accessing information, reduce information asymmetry, and help farmers achieve fair competition in the market. This effort includes enhancing entrepreneurial performance in rural areas through digital means. Fourth, it is advisable to leverage international experiences, such as utilizing international cooperation funds, technology transfer, and public-private partnerships, to ensure the sustainable development of digital inclusive finance in rural areas.

To strengthen policy support and promote farmers’ income growth, the following measures are recommended. First, reducing taxes and fees, and increasing loan support can alleviate financial pressure on entrepreneurs, thereby enhancing farmers’ investment capacity in entrepreneurial projects and improving their entrepreneurial performance. Second, providing consumption subsidies can lower the cost of purchasing digital devices such as smartphones and computers for farmers, thereby reducing the digital divide between urban and rural areas. Third, formulating industrial and financial policies conducive to farmers’ entrepreneurship, along with providing technical training and market information services, can increase the likelihood of entrepreneurial success. Fourth, improving legal and regulatory frameworks and establishing a multi-tiered capital market system can support the development of the formal financial market in rural areas, while scientifically guiding the transformation of informal rural credit into formal financial systems.

To improve residents’ digital literacy, the following measures are suggested. First, strengthen digital finance education while enhancing farmers’ digital and financial literacy. Through educational initiatives, enrich farmers’ financial knowledge base, deepen the reform of the rural education system, raise the educational level of young people in rural areas, and promote the accumulation of human capital in farming households, thereby fostering entrepreneurship. Second, continuously innovate the forms and content of internet skills training for farmers, enhancing the motivation of returning migrant farmers to utilize the internet. Third, local governments should focus on the willingness of potential entrepreneurial groups to accumulate human capital. They should actively guide and fully leverage educational resources from government departments, universities, and society to provide targeted internet skills training based on farmers’ needs, thereby improving the entrepreneurial performance of farming households.

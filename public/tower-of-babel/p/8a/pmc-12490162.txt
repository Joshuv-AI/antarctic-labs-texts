A study on predicting recurrence of non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer within 2 years using mp-MRI radiomics
Authors: Bo Chen, Yueting Zhou, Zhaojiao Li, Junhao Chen, Jieming Zuo, Haifeng Wang, Zhenhui Li, Shi Fu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractNon-muscle invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC) has a high rate of postoperative recurrence and the efficacy of existing clinical prediction models is limited. This study aimed to combine multiparametric magnetic resonance imaging (mp-MRI) radiomic features with clinical characteristics to construct a machine learning model for accurately predicting the risk of recurrence within 2 years postoperatively in NMIBC patients. Retrospectively including 183 NMIBC patients (57 in the recurrence group, 126 in the non-recurrence group), radiomic features from mp-MRI imaging (T2W, ADC, and enhancement sequences) were extracted. Through LASSO selection, 4 key imaging features (MajorAxisLength, SZNN, S/V, Skewness) and 6 clinical features based on the EAU 2021 risk stratification were identified to constitute the clinical-imaging dataset. Through comparison with 10 machine learning models, Support Vector Machine (SVM) performed the best (training set AUC = 0.973, validation set AUC = 0.891), with external independent validation (108 cases) showing AUCs of 0.88 and 0.87, demonstrating good generalization ability. A bar chart integrating radiomics score (Rad-Score) with clinical features provides an intuitive prognostic tool. The study indicates that the clinical-imaging radiomics model based on SVM significantly enhances the efficacy of NMIBC recurrence prediction, addressing the shortcomings of traditional risk assessment and offering a reliable basis for personalized postoperative management. Study limitations include the retrospective design and the absence of molecular biomarkers, necessitating future multicenter prospective validation.Keywords: Non-muscle invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC), Multiparametric magnetic resonance, Machine learning (ML), Support vector machine (SVM), Recurrence risk

Introduction

By 2023, Bladder Caner (BCa) is the fourth most common cancer in men, accounting for 6% of estimated new cancers and 4% of cancer-related deaths. Globally, more than 500,000 new diagnoses and approximately 200,000 deaths are reported annually [1, 2]. At the time of initial diagnosis, 70–75% of patients are diagnosed with NMIBC, 20–25% with muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC), and 5% with metastatic bladder cancer. In the treatment of NMIBC, although tumor lesions can be managed through surgery, the intravesical recurrence rate post transurethral resection of bladder tumor (TURBT) remains high at 30–50%, with 5-year recurrence rates ranging from 31–78% and 1–45% respectively. Postoperative instillation therapy is typically effective in reducing recurrences; however, many NMIBC patients still undergo multiple TURBT procedures, and some may even eventually require radical surgery [3–5]. In NMIBC, recurrence of low-grade non-invasive Ta urothelial carcinoma is still common but progression is rare. In contrast, the 5-year progression rate for carcinoma in situ (all high-grade) is 50%. For patients with high-grade T1 bladder cancer, the 5-year recurrence rate, progression rate, and cancer-specific survival rate are 42%, 21%, and 87% respectively [6–8]. Existing clinical risk assessment models for NMIBC, such as the EORTC assessment model, quantitatively stratify the risk of NMIBC recurrence. However, their performance is not satisfactory (AUC0.56). The prediction model from the CUETO also has a low AUC of only 0.48. The latest EAU 2021 risk assessment table shows poor discrimination for disease progression, with a C-index and AUC of 0.644 and 0.522 respectively [9–12]. Existing studies have found that approximately 40–60% of NMIBC patients experience tumor recurrence within 2 years, with short-term recurrence indicating a higher risk of progression [13]. However, current predictive models are not sufficient to meet the need for accurately predicting recurrence, emphasizing the urgent need for a highly accurate prediction model [14].

The concept of radiomics was first proposed by Lambin et al. in 2012. The main process includes five key steps: obtaining imaging data, delineating tumor regions, segmenting tumor areas, extracting and quantifying features, and classifying and predicting [15]. Compared to CT scans, MRI has advantages in diagnostic performance and treatment response evaluation for BCa, with finer and richer local details, superior soft tissue differentiation. However, due to its multiple sequences and resolution variances, there have been fewer studies on radiomics analysis [16]. Our aim is to utilize mpMRI images to construct a predictive model by combining imaging features with clinical characteristics, enabling accurate assessment of recurrence in NMIBC patients after TURBT surgery and chemotherapy instillation.

Method

Clinical data

A total of 183 BCa patients from the Second Affiliated Hospital of Kunming Medical University, retrospectively collected from January 2017 to January 2023. Patients who were pathologically confirmed to have bladder cancer with local recurrence within 2 years (excluding distant metastasis) were defined as the recurrence group, while the rest were defined as the non-recurrence group. Clinical characteristics of the patients were collected, including age, gender, BMI, smoking status, and risk factors based on the EAU 2021 risk stratification table: histological grade, tumor stage, maximum tumor diameter, solitary/multiple tumors, and risk stratification (Fig. 1).

Clinical characteristics of NMIBC patients between recurrence and non-recurrence groups. Bar and box plots display significant differences in key clinical variables (age, T stage, tumor grade, size, and multiplicity) between patients with and without recurrence (n = 183). Statistical analyses confirm these variables align with EAU 2021 risk stratification

Imaging image collection

The following parameter settings were used to obtain high quality images and accurately assess tumor staging during BCa scanning using a GE Premier 3.0T MRI device.1. MRI device: Device model: GE Premier 3.0T, Coil: Multi-Channel Phased Array Abdominal Coil.2. Scanning sequences and parameters: ① T2 weighted imaging (T2WI), 2D Fast Spin Echo (2D FSE/TSE), layer thickness: 3-4 mm, echo time (TE): 80-100 ms, ② Apparent Diffusion Coefficient (ADC), Spin Echo Plane Echo Imaging (EPI), b-value: 0, 1000 s/mm2, layer thickness: 3-4 mm, TE: 3 −4 mm, TE: 3 ms, TR: 4000-6000 ms, fat suppression: using spectral fat saturation technique; (iii) Dynamic Contrast Enhanced Imaging (DCE-MRI), 3D Gradient Echo (3D GRE), layer thickness: 3-5 mm, TE: 1 ms. Contrast injection: Gadopentetate Glucosamine injection, 0.2 mmol/kg body weight, injection rate 1.5–2.0 ml/sec. 2.0 ml/sec.

Tumor region of interest (ROI) outlining and feature extraction

ROIs of bladder tumors were obtained by outlining mpMRI images of all patients using 3D-Slicer software using a manual approach, with the following sequences selected: early and late stages of T2W, ADC, and enhancement sequences. Images were resampled to a voxel size of 1 × 1 × 1mm3 to standardize voxel spacing, and voxel intensity values were discretized by using a fixed bin width of 5SI to reduce image noise and standardize intensities to achieve stable intensity resolution across all images. Imaging histology image feature extraction was then performed, including first-order features, shape features, GLCM, GLSZM, GLRLM, GLDM, and NGTDM.To assess intra-observer reproducibility, another practitioner performed the steps as above again after 1 week to assess intra-observer consistency of the extracted features using the ICC, where an ICC > 0.8 indicates a strong degree of consistency and stable features. The remaining features were normalized using a Z-score, and to reduce human error, all patients’ bladder tumor ROIs were outlined by 1 imaging physician (more than 10 years of experience) and 1 urologist (more than 3 years of experience), and ultimately checked and corrected by 1 imaging physician (more than 20 years of experience). Finally, the extracted histologic features were screened and downscaled using LASSO, and the imaging features with the strongest correlation were finally screened for constructing the prediction model.

Construction of clinical-imaging dataset and construction and evaluation of models

The features with statistically significant differences between the 2 sets of comparisons were included (repeated validation of the EAU risk assessment table), and the influencing factors of the joint clinical and imaging features, a total of 10 features, were used to construct the clinical-imaging histology dataset. Predictive models were constructed using this dataset integrated into machine learning algorithms, and the 10 machine learning models included Decision Tree (DT), K-nearest neighbors algorithm (KNN), LinearSupport Vector Machine Classification. Linear SVC), linear discriminant analysis (LDA) Support Vector Machine (SVM), GradientBoosting algorithm (GDB), Multilayer Perceptron (MLP). Perceptron (MLP), Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD), AdaptiveBoosting (AdaBoost), and Logistic Regression Model (LR). Additional imaging features alone were used to form a Rad-Score score in conjunction with the five clinical features of the EAU2021 risk stratification to construct a column-line graph. The predictive effects of all models were expressed in terms of sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy, and the efficacy was expressed in terms of ROC, which was supplemented by clinical decision curves and calibration curves for evaluation. The final screening was done to select an optimal machine learning model from it. Data processing was performed using SPSS 27.0 and Python 3.11.

Validation of the optimal model across different departments

To evaluate the generalizability of the model within a single-center setting, internal validation was conducted using data collected from different inpatient wards of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Kunming Medical University. Although MRI examinations were performed using the same GE Premier 3.0T system across all wards, the imaging process was carried out by different technical teams. Patient inclusion and exclusion criteria were identical to those used in the training cohort. Each ward contributed data equivalent to 30% of the original sample size (n1 = 54, n2 = 54), and the optimal model was applied without modifying its parameters. The clinical-imaging datasets from these different wards were used to validate the robustness and stability of the model (Fig. 2).

Description of image features

Among the features included in the rigorous reliability test, there are 140 unstable image features with ICC analysis less than 0.8, and 1,642 of the 1,782 initial features remain after elimination, and then the LASSO screening is performed to identify the four most meaningful image features are: original_shape_MajorAxisLength, exponential _Glszm_SizeZoneNonUniformityNormalized(SZNN), Original_SurfaceVolumeRatio(S/V), and Wavelet-HL_Skewness.Adding the above six clinical features constitutes the clinical-imaging feature dataset.

Discussion

Our model was carefully checked and validated with external data, and had relatively good results. The training set AUC of our SVM is 0.9653 (95% Cl,0.9513–0.9793) and the validation set AUC is 0.8905 (95% Cl,0.8569–0.9241), and the performance in external validation is also stable. We chose the ADC sequence to be added to our outlined sequence, which can be used to describe the speed and extent of molecular diffusion movement in different directions in DWI sequences, and has advantages in showing tumors with tightly packed cells and little interstitial water. It has been suggested that ADC features are more expressive of changes in healthy regional tissues than T2W features, and have a higher sensitivity to malignant changes in healthy tissues [17]. It has been widely used in prostate cancer, kidney cancer and liver cancer [18–20]. Previous studies have shown that the presence of submucosal ulnar signs around DWI tumors is associated with a low risk of recurrence and progression in patients with NMIBC [21]. Meanwhile, ADC has been used more in BCa, and some studies have used ADC and T2 sequence combined model in BCa tumor grading and staging, with an AUC of 0.779 to differentiate between low-grade and high-grade groups, and 0.850 to differentiate between the NMIBC and MIBC groups [22]. Zhang et al. demonstrated that ADC value was an independent correlate of the risk of bladder muscle infiltration and that ADC value was negatively correlated with both this risk and Ki-67 [23]. We did not include the Vi-RADS (Vesical Imaging-Reporting and Data System) scoring system as a feature, and although DWI is considered the primary sequence for estimating muscle invasion in VI-RADS [24], recent studies have suggested that the VI-RADS score in the assessment of 5-year recurrence risk in patients with BCa may have no relevant prognostic value [25]. Its main application remains the evaluation of tumor muscleinvasion rather than recurrence.

Four image features were filtered in our model. shape_MajorAxisLength (3D) image feature contributes the highest, it belongs to shape Features (3D), which is essentially a shape feature. One study used selection of clinical parameters from nasopharyngeal and head and neck cancer patient datasets to select image biomarkers (IBMs) from the nasopharyngeal cancer dataset, and multivariate Cox regression analyses identified 3 independent IBMs containing MajorAxisLength. majorAXisLength of lymph nodes (> 55 mm) was associated with an increased risk of death. This feature was significantly smaller for N1 than for N2 and N3 in TNM staging of metastatic patients, and when IBMs were added to the clinical model, the c index increased from 0.72 to 0.75 (95% CI: 0.68–0.82, p = 0.019) and 0.67 to 0.75 (95% CI: 0.70–0.81, p < 0.001) for both datasets, respectively. Suggesting that this trait improves the prediction of their overall survival [26]. The same team still found MajorAxisLength, which shows the distance between lymph nodes, to have improved modeling value in a CT-based image biomarker prediction model for patients with head and neck cancer undergoing radical (chemo)radiotherapy. The above results are consistent with our result. Glszm_SizeZoneNonUniformityNormalized (SZNN) is a texture feature based on the Gray Level Size Zone Matrix (GLSZM) that reflects the heterogeneity or homogeneity of lesions. In a study of early stage squamous cell carcinoma of the tongue, SZNN was able to improve the efficacy of its model in predicting tumor grade from an accuracy of 0.76 (sensitivity of 0.78 and specificity of 0.75) to 0.82 (sensitivity of 0.78 and specificity of 0.87) [27]. Unfortunately, in this study, the effect of the specific value of SZNN on the model was not mentioned, but in the study by Hacking et al. [28] triple-negative breast cancer was associated with SZNN, and higher values of tumor expression in the pathology data were correlated with lower homogeneity of the tumor appearance on the SZNN, which means that the higher the value of the SZNN, the more heterogeneity it has, and the more the tumors express, which is in line with our results Consistent. In addition, invasively growing tumors usually have higher S/V values and tend to be more aggressive and have a poor prognosis. This feature has been used more frequently in breast cancer, Cai L et al. [29] in a study to predict the response of breast cancer to neoadjuvant systemic therapy concluded that surface/volume ratio of tomosynthesis and peri-tumor entropy feature of ultrasound are the most relevant imaging features. Others have similarly used LASSO screening features for distinguishing low-grade ductal carcinoma in situ (DCIS) from non-low-grade ductal carcinoma in situ (DCIS) or upgraded invasive carcinoma in breast cancer, where the features also include S/V, with mean areas under the working feature curves for subjects in the training and validation collapses of 0.88 and 0.88, respectively [30]. Existing reports suggest that S/V is a potential feature to distinguish benign and malignant lesions in the lungs [31, 32]. This shows that in solid tumors, S/V represents the biomorphology of the tumor, and tumors with higher values do have a worse prognosis. An additional first-order statistic, skewness, responds to the symmetry of the data relative to the mean. Studies have reported that positive skewness correlates with tumor aggressiveness and poor prognosis in MRI-T1 enhanced images of glioblastoma [33]. Also in a study of prognostic factors for breast cancer disease recurrence using quantitative and qualitative magnetic resonance imaging parameters, increased positive skewness was shown to be significantly associated with disease recurrence in each multivariate model. The four influential features we screened for are also more widely used in oncology, but less reported in urologic tumors, and are expected to be prognostic markers for NMIBC recurrence in the future.

In addition to the imaging features, the clinical features included in our model were derived from repeated validation based on the EAU risk scale. It was found that almost all T1 tumors were able to be included in the high-risk and very high-risk groups. The lamina propria (LP) of the bladder, located between the epithelial mucosa and the lamina propria of the urothelium, consists of extracellular matrix, several cell types, and collagen fibers types i and iii, and is a determinant of Ta progression to T1. It has been concluded that T1 tumors have a significantly higher risk of recurrence than Ta tumors, and the poor prognosis and high recurrence rate of T1G3 patients have been mentioned in several publications [34, 35]. Recent studies have resolved that the expression of Fibroblast activating protein (FAPa) is significantly higher in T1 tumors (especially in the mesenchyme) than in Ta and Tis [36]. In addition T1G3 and MIBC exhibited similar COL6A1 (a key component of the extracellular matrix) expression, significantly higher than other NMIBC subtypes. T1G3 patients who progressed to MIBC had significantly higher COL6A1 expression than Ta, T1 low-grade patients, and non-progressing patients, highlighting the aggressiveness and higher risk of progression that T1G3 possesses and the significant association between elevated COL6A1 expression and progression-free survival in T1G3 patients [37]. At this stage, TURBT is well established, but it is not conducive to the implementation of the tumor-free principle in surgical oncology, therefore, en bloc resection of bladder tumor (ERBT) has been proposed, which has a high rate of preservation of muscular tissues (96%−100%), fewer complications during hospitalization, and most importantly, a lower 2-year recurrence rate, as compared with traditional TURBT. Most importantly, the 2-year recurrence rate is lower. In routine postoperative perfusion, although adjuvant intravesical BCG is the most effective treatment to reduce the risk of recurrence in patients with intermediate- to high-risk NMIBC [38]. However, because its mechanism of action and principle are completely different from chemotherapeutic drugs, its side effects and economic pressure need to be carefully considered. In recent years, some scholars have studied the effect of gemcitabine and anthracycline antibiotics in postoperative perfusion of NMIBC, and the recurrence rate of gemcitabine group was 8.33% (7/84), and the recurrence rate of epirubicin or pilocarpine group was 45% (18/40) (P < 0.0001) [39]. It was also found that in the absence of statistical differences in other conditions, the 1-year recurrence rates were 6.21% and 12.28% in the gemcitabine group and the pyroxorubicin group, respectively, 11.86% and 23.68% in the 2-year recurrence rate, and 15.82% and 25.88% in the 3-year recurrence rate, respectively, with the recurrence rate in the gemcitabine group being significantly lower than that in the pyroxorubicin group (P < 0.05), and the 5-year tumor-free survival rate in the gemcitabine group being significantly higher than that in the pyroxorubicin group (P < 0.05). The 5-year tumor recurrence-free survival rate of the gemcitabine group was significantly higher than that of the pirorubicin group (P < 0.05) and the incidence of bladder irritation, blood in urine, fever, nausea, and vomiting was lower in the gemcitabine group (P < 0.05) [40], and most researchers concluded that pilocarpine and epirubicin could only reduce recurrence but not progression [41]. In addition, gemcitabine has fewer adverse events and similar outcomes in intermediate-risk patients compared with BCG, a currently commonly used intravesical drug, and is more effective in BCG-refractory patients but less effective in high-risk patients [42].

Conclusion

This study has some limitations, and as a retrospective study, there may be some selection bias. Despite the standard processing we adopted for MRI images, there is still subjective bias of operation during the acquisition of raw images. Also manual outlining of ROIs may have human error because of subjectivity. Our study did not use molecular biomarkers and lacked comprehensiveness. Although external validation was done and surgeries were performed by an experienced team, there are potential surgical and management differences such as personal habits of physicians. The lack of data on immunoperfusion and in situ cancer patients is also lacking. In summary, the SVM clinical-imaging feature model can well predict the recurrence of NMIBC within 2 years after TURBT + chemoperfusion therapy, which strengthens the clinical application of EAU risk stratification, and provides a novel and efficient tool for individualized recurrence risk assessment in NMIBC patients.

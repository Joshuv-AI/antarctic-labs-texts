ASTROREPOMICS: A curated transcriptomic database for reproductive biology in space
Authors: Chun-Fan Lung, Kun-I Lin, Yi-Chiung Hsu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummarySpaceflight imposes substantial physiological stress on reproductive systems, yet relevant transcriptomic data remain fragmented across repositories. To address this need, we developed ASTROREPOMICS, a web-based platform that integrates 17 rigorously normalized and batch-corrected transcriptomic datasets spanning multiple species and reproductive tissues. The platform supports reproducible cross-study and cross-species analyses through standardized metadata and an intuitive user interface.We highlight its utility through two example analyses: (1) irradiated mouse sperm exhibited suppression of RNA splicing and protein-processing pathways alongside activation of interferon- and GPCR-associated programs; and (2) a multi-species intersected-DEG assessment between irradiated rat mammary tissue and microgravity-exposed zebrafish embryos uncovered conserved signatures involving RNA metabolism, cytokine signaling, and angiogenesis.By consolidating dispersed datasets and offering tailored analytical capabilities, ASTROREPOMICS provides a centralized resource for hypothesis generation and strengthens the research infrastructure needed to advance reproductive health studies in space, supporting long-term efforts to safeguard fertility during deep-space exploration.

Introduction

Reproductive health is a critical consideration for long-term human space missions. Space travel poses multiple potential hazards to the reproductive system, including exposure to ionizing radiation, microgravity, and physiological stress.1 These concerns are amplified for planned multi-year deep-space expeditions (e.g., a crewed mission to Mars2), where astronauts would be subjected to continuous microgravity and cosmic radiation over several years. Planned deep-space missions that take astronauts beyond the protective influence of Earth’s geomagnetic field are expected to pose additional and unprecedented health risks that exceed those encountered during shorter missions in low Earth orbit.3,4 Animal studies further suggest that hypomagnetic environments may impair fertility and early embryonic development.5 As missions extend in duration, understanding how space conditions affect reproductive biology becomes vital for safeguarding crew fertility and supporting the long-term sustainability of human life in space.6,7

Ground-based and in-flight studies have begun to reveal how the space environment affects reproductive cells and tissues. Microgravity disrupts gametogenesis and endocrine regulation, as male rodents exposed to real or simulated microgravity exhibit seminiferous tubule degeneration and reduced sperm counts, while females show disrupted estrous cycling and impaired follicle development.1,8 At the cellular level, weightlessness induces cytoskeletal disorganization,9,10 oxidative stress along with apoptosis in ovarian granulosa cells,11 and G0/G1 cell-cycle arrest in germ cells,12 leading to impaired gamete quality and reduced developmental potential of oocytes and embryos. Meanwhile, ionizing radiation further exacerbates these risks by causing DNA damage, follicle depletion, and transient infertility, particularly under exposure to high-charge, high-energy (HZE) cosmic rays6 comparable to those expected during a Mars mission. These findings collectively highlight that the combined stressors of spaceflight, including microgravity, radiation, and disrupted neuroendocrine rhythms, may profoundly compromise fertility and reproductive outcomes, emphasizing the need for continuous reproductive health monitoring during long-duration missions.

Despite these concerns, our understanding of space reproductive biology remains limited. Only a few experiments have directly examined reproductive processes during spaceflight, especially in females, leaving significant knowledge gaps. Important aspects such as the combined effects of simultaneous space stressors (microgravity plus radiation and stress) on gametogenesis, the capacity for normal embryonic development in space, and the cross-generational impacts of space exposure are still poorly characterized.1 Major reviews have highlighted the need for further research into how long-duration space missions affect mammalian reproduction, noting that multiple concurrent hazards may interact in complex ways.8,13 To address these questions, scientists are increasingly turning to “omics” approaches—particularly transcriptomics—to dissect molecular changes in reproductive tissues under space conditions. Several spaceflight and analog studies have generated gene expression datasets (e.g., RNA sequencing [RNA-seq] of astronaut and model organism samples) through efforts such as NASA’s GeneLab program.14 However, existing omics databases are not optimized for extracting reproductive-specific insights from this growing body of data. NASA GeneLab, for example, is a broad repository for multi-omics from diverse space biology experiments, but its broad scope and lack of reproductive metadata make gametogenesis- and embryo-related datasets difficult to isolate or compare. Similarly, STOmicsDB offers extensive spatial transcriptomic integration across tissues,15 and single-cell multi-omics atlas of reproduction (SMARTdb) compiles single-cell multi-omics reproductive data across species on Earth,16 yet neither incorporates spaceflight or microgravity exposure contexts. Comparative summary of major omics databases relevant to reproductive and space biology

To fill this gap, there is a clear need for a centralized, standardized database integrating gene expression data relevant to reproduction in space environments. ASTROREPOMICS provides an initial framework toward this goal by curating available transcriptomic datasets relevant to reproductive cells and tissues and harmonizing them under unified preprocessing and annotation standards (Figure 1). Unlike GeneLab, STOmicsDB, or SMARTdb, ASTROREPOMICS exclusively focuses on reproductive tissues and cell types, including sperm, oocytes, zygotes, embryos, and gonads, while integrating both spaceflight and simulated microgravity datasets. The database provides normalized, cross-study comparable data with interactive tools for querying, differential expression, and pathway enrichment analysis. Standardized processing and annotation across studies mitigate batch effects and facilitate cross-mission comparisons,17 ensuring reproducible insights and reliable integration of multi-source data. To further enhance analytical flexibility, all curated and normalized datasets in ASTROREPOMICS are fully downloadable as input of external transcriptomic analysis tools such as iDEP,18 allowing users to perform extended or customized workflows. Principal-component analysis (PCA) was performed to assess the influence of different batch correction approaches on data integration. Before correction (Figure 2A), samples clustered distinctly by Mission, indicating that this variable exerted the strongest batch effect among datasets. After Z score normalization (Figure 2B), inter-mission separation was markedly reduced, and samples were distributed more evenly across the principal component space, suggesting that this per-dataset standardization effectively mitigated mission-specific bias.Figure 2PCA visualization of datasets before and after batch correction(A) Before correction, (B) after Z score normalization, (C) after ComBat correction using Mission as the batch variable, and (D) after ComBat correction using a composite batch factor integrating normalization method, library preparation method, platform, and preservation method.

When ComBat correction was applied using Mission as the batch variable (Figure 2C), only partial improvement in cross-mission alignment was observed, and some residual separation remained. In contrast, the multi-batch ComBat correction using a composite batch factor integrating normalization method, library preparation method, platform, and preservation method (Figure 2D) achieved a level of harmonization comparable to the best single-factor model while maintaining biological variation among conditions. This simultaneous adjustment across multiple technical factors yielded stable integration without the over-correction risks associated with sequential processing.

The quantitative batch-effect scores derived from ComBat evaluation (Table 3) further support these observations. Among all tested variables, Mission exhibited the lowest score, denoting the strongest contribution to batch variation, followed by normalization method. Other technical variables, including library preparation method, platform, and preservation method, showed higher residual scores (>16), reflecting weaker but non-negligible batch influences. Collectively, these results indicate that Mission is the dominant batch source among the retrieved studies, while multi-factor ComBat correction provides a robust framework for harmonizing cross-study transcriptomic data when multiple technical covariates are present. In this context, Z score normalization remains an effective preprocessing method when the primary batch heterogeneity arises from a single dominant source.

Radiation-associated disruption of RNA processing and ER stress pathways in mouse germ cells

Following the identification of Mission as the dominant batch factor and the application of per-dataset Z score normalization for batch correction across the pooled mouse germ cell dataset (n = 38), we proceeded to investigate the molecular consequences of high-linear energy transfer (high-LET) radiation on mouse germ cells (irradiated freeze-dried spermatozoa, n = 4; non-irradiated freeze-dried controls, n = 12). Differential gene expression (DEG) analysis was performed using Welch’s t test applied to log2-transformed expression values. Fold change was calculated based on the difference in means between groups on the same transformed scale. Genes were considered significantly differentially expressed if their raw p value was <0.05 and absolute log2 fold change exceeded 2. The results identified 114 significantly DEGs, with a notable trend toward downregulation (88 downregulated and 26 upregulated genes) (Figure 3A). This distribution is further visualized in the corresponding volcano plot, where upregulated genes are highlighted in red and downregulated genes in blue, based on the selected thresholds (Figure 3B). To examine expression patterns across samples, we generated a heatmap showing normalized expression profiles of the DEGs across all replicates (Figure 3C). Hierarchical clustering was applied to both rows (genes) and columns (samples), revealing distinct expression patterns that separate control samples from irradiated samples. Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA), performed with 3,000 permutations and gene set size thresholds set to a minimum of 100 and a maximum of 1,000 genes, identified two significantly negatively enriched Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathways in mouse spermatozoa exposed to ionizing radiation. The spliceosome pathway (KEGG: mmu03040) exhibited the strongest negative enrichment (normalized enrichment score, NES = −10.11; false discovery rate, FDR = 0.0224), implicating disruptions in RNA splicing machinery (Figure 4A). Additionally, the protein processing in endoplasmic reticulum pathway (KEGG: mmu04141) was also significantly suppressed (NES = −7.34; FDR = 0.0037), consistent with endoplasmic reticulum stress and activation of the unfolded protein response. These findings are supported by previous studies demonstrating that ionizing radiation significantly disrupts spliceosomal functions and induces defects in RNA processing, contributing to compromised spermatogenesis and fertility outcomes.37 Additionally, suppression of the protein processing pathway aligns closely with observations that radiation exposure activates the unfolded protein response and compromises testicular health,38 reinforcing the reliability of these findings.Figure 4Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) of transcriptomic alterations in mouse spermatozoa following ionizing radiation exposure(A) KEGG pathway enrichment; (B) MSigDB mouse hallmark pathway enrichment; GSEA was conducted with 3,000 permutations and gene set size thresholds between 100 and 1,000; The x axis represents the normalized enrichment score (NES), while point size and color denote the number of genes per pathway and the false discovery rate (FDR), respectively.

Complementary analysis using the MSigDB mouse hallmark collection revealed significant enrichment of immune-related transcriptional programs, including interferon alpha response (NES = 1.64; FDR = 0.039) and interferon gamma response (NES = 1.49; FDR = 0.049) (Figure 4B). Although spermatozoa are not conventional immune cells, emerging evidence indicates that they express a variety of cytokine and chemokine receptors enabling immune-like signaling and environmental sensing. For instance, human sperm express CCR6, which mediates chemotactic movement toward CCL20,39 and CXCR4, which guides sperm migration toward CXCL12 gradients.40 Moreover, toll-like receptors have been detected in mammalian spermatozoa and implicated in recognizing inflammatory cues and regulating motility.41 Interferon-related signaling has also been reported in the male reproductive tract, where interferon-ε contributes to antiviral defense,42 and interferon-γ directly affects sperm viability and motility.43 Thus, the enrichment of interferon-responsive pathways in irradiated sperm likely reflects activation of stress-immune cross-talk within germ cells or their supporting testicular milieu rather than canonical immune responses, suggesting that radiation exposure may perturb the paracrine signaling mechanisms essential for maintaining sperm function and fertility.

Importantly, integrating the available radiation-exposed mouse sperm datasets within a unified preprocessing framework allowed these pathway-level perturbations to be evaluated in a consistent context. Although the number of space-relevant reproductive datasets remains small, ASTROREPOMICS facilitates identification of recurring transcriptional changes across comparable conditions, supporting more coherent hypothesis development than would be feasible using fragmented datasets alone.

Ionizing radiation and signal transduction alterations in mouse sperm

Using ASTROREPOMICS, we also investigated how ionizing radiation alters signal transduction in mouse spermatozoa. Over-representation analysis (ORA) was performed on significantly upregulated genes (log2 fold-change ≥2, p value ≤0.01) using the Reactome pathway database, with the same permutation count and gene set size thresholds as applied in the preceding GSEA analysis, and pathway-level FDR controlled at 0.01. The analysis revealed significant enrichment of signal transduction pathways, most notably those involving G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs). The top-enriched pathway was GPCR ligand binding (Reactome: R-MMU-500792; enrichment ratio = 1.98, FDR = 7.37 × 10−41), along with related pathways such as Class A/1 (Rhodopsin-like receptors), GPCR downstream signaling, and G alpha (i) signaling events (Figure 5A), supported by previous studies having demonstrated that radiation-induced oxidative stress and DNA damage may perturb GPCR-mediated signaling systems networks crucial for sperm function.44 Additionally, enrichment was observed in pathways associated with keratinization (Reactome: R-MMU-6805567; enrichment ratio = 2.57, FDR = 1.40 × 10−39) and sensory perception (Reactome: R-MMU-9709957; enrichment ratio = 2.29, FDR = 2.11 × 10−34). Network analysis identified Pomc and Cxcr4 as central hub genes linking multiple enriched pathways (Figure 5B), suggesting coordinated modulation of neuroendocrine and chemotactic signaling in response to radiation exposure. Pomc encodes stress-responsive precursor peptides such as adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) and α-melanocyte-stimulating hormone (α-MSH) that regulate reproductive and immune functions.47 Its upregulation under radiation exposure suggests activation of neuroendocrine pathways.48 Cxcr4 encodes a GPCR involved in immune cell trafficking, stem cell maintenance, and sperm chemotaxis toward the oocyte via its ligand CXCL12.49,50 Consistent with the immune-related transcriptional enrichment identified in the MSigDB GSEA (Figure 4B), these network findings further support the notion that irradiated germ cells engage stress-adaptive signaling that intersects immune and hormonal regulation. Moreover, human sperm are known to express neurotransmitter and hormone receptors, including nicotinic acetylcholine receptors that modulate acrosome reaction and motility,51 reflecting neuroendocrine-like signaling capacities. Thus, the enrichment of immune and neuroendocrine pathways in irradiated germ cells likely reflects a radiation-induced stress response that perturbs the chemokine and hormonal cues supporting sperm function. These findings raise the possibility that ionizing radiation may alter reproductive immune homeostasis through such stress-responsive GPCR-mediated signaling in germ cells, warranting further investigation (e.g., cytokine profiling and chemotaxis assays in irradiated germ cells).

Collectively, these results demonstrate ASTROREPOMICS’s utility in comparing independently generated datasets under shared analytic parameters. Even with limited space-relevant datasets, the platform enables users to identify convergent signatures, such as RNA splicing defects and GPCR-related signaling shifts that may underlie germ cell vulnerability to radiation. Such cross-dataset coherence would be difficult to discern from individual studies in isolation.

Multi-species exploratory analysis identifies conserved RNA metabolism and cytokine signaling pathways through intersected differentially expressed genes

To extend ASTROREPOMICS toward multi-species hypothesis exploration, we compared transcriptional responses in irradiated rat mammary carcinomas (irradiated, n = 10; controls, n = 3) and zebrafish embryos exposed to simulated microgravity (exposed, n = 3; controls, n = 6) (Table 2, marked with a double dagger ‡). After mapping all genes to human orthologs, 176 upregulated genes were shared between the two datasets, whereas downregulated genes showed no overlap (Figures 6A and 6B). This pathway includes components central to RNA processing, modification, and turnover. Previous studies show that RNA metabolic reprogramming is a common feature of cellular adaptation to environmental and physiological stress,52,53 suggesting that modulation of RNA handling may represent a conserved axis of stress responsiveness across diverse biological contexts.

Pathway-level exploration with GSEA supported this theme and revealed additional conserved signatures. Several cytokine and chemokine-related pathways were positively enriched across databases (Figure 6D), including KEGG viral protein interaction with cytokine and cytokine receptor (hsa04061; NES = 1.51; FDR = 0.0053), Reactome chemokine receptors bind chemokines (R-HSA-380108; NES = 1.57; FDR = 0.0040), and interleukin 10 (IL-10) signaling (R-HSA-6783783; NES = 1.50; FDR = 0.0434). These pathways are core regulators of inflammatory and stress-adaptive communication, and their enrichment is consistent with evidence that both radiation and microgravity can alter cytokine networks and immune-like signaling patterns in non-immune tissues. Environmental stressors have been reported to induce broad cytokine remodeling, influence chemokine receptor activation, and engage IL-10-mediated compensatory responses during tissue injury or developmental perturbation.54 These findings suggest that stress-induced modulation of cytokine receptor signaling may represent another shared response motif across species.

MSigDB hallmark enrichment further highlighted interferon alpha response (NES = 1.30; FDR = 0.0467) and angiogenesis (NES = 1.33; FDR = 0.0414), two pathways frequently activated during generalized stress, tissue remodeling, or cellular danger sensing. Interferon-related transcriptional programs are known to respond to a wide range of stress stimuli beyond antiviral defense, while angiogenesis-associated genes often increase during repair and microenvironmental reorganization. Their enrichment suggests that space-relevant stressors may converge on coordinated immune-vascular regulatory mechanisms.

The cross-species intersected-DEG analysis, although exploratory due to differences in tissues, exposures, and species, revealed shared signatures involving RNA metabolism, chemokine and cytokine signaling, interferon responses, and angiogenesis. These convergent pathways suggest possible stress-adaptive mechanisms that may recur across developmental or reproductive contexts under environmental stressors. These findings provide preliminary targets for follow-up validation in exposure-matched reproductive models.

Discussion

ASTROREPOMICS serves as an initial platform dedicated to organizing and analyzing reproductive transcriptomic datasets relevant to spaceflight, ionizing radiation, and simulated microgravity. Although the number of currently available space-exposed reproductive datasets is limited, the platform addresses long-standing fragmentation by unifying disparate studies under standardized preprocessing and harmonized metadata. This approach enables exploratory cross-study comparisons, supports identification of preliminary conserved molecular mechanisms, and offers a practical starting point for hypothesis generation in reproductive space biology. Furthermore, its intuitive interface makes these analyses accessible even to researchers with limited computational training.

The demonstration analyses provided by ASTROREPOMICS underscore its translational potential. Disruption of RNA splicing and protein processing pathways in irradiated sperm is consistent with reports that high-LET radiation, including carbon ions, induces mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative stress, two well-established drivers of reproductive toxicity.55,56 These radiation-induced stress patterns also parallel transcriptional signatures observed in spaceflight datasets,57,58 emphasizing shared stress responses involving oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and small RNA regulation. The enrichment of GPCR-related signaling pathways further supports broad transcriptional reprogramming in male germ cells. GPCRs are essential regulators of sperm motility, capacitation, and chemotaxis,59 and their activation likely represents a compensatory response to radiation-induced stress. Notably, Cxcr4 is key chemotactic receptor guiding sperm toward CXCL12.49,50 Although direct evidence linking radiation exposure to altered Cxcr4 expression in germ cells remains limited, the present findings suggest radiation-induced perturbation of chemotactic signaling. These findings point to testable hypotheses regarding stress-adaptive GPCR signaling and inform future strategies for protecting reproductive function in both spaceflight and terrestrial radiation settings.4,60

Beyond single-species analyses, the multi-species exploratory module highlights stress-responsive themes that appear conserved across independent models. The overlap of upregulated genes between irradiated rat mammary tissue and microgravity-exposed zebrafish embryos was enriched for RNA metabolism, consistent with evidence that diverse environmental stresses, including ionizing radiation, remodel RNA processing, turnover, and stress granule dynamics as a core adaptation mechanism.61,62 Enrichment of cytokine and chemokine receptor pathways aligns with studies showing that both radiation and microgravity perturb inflammatory networks and chemokine signaling in immune and non-immune tissues,63 as well as altered cytokine production and immune cell trafficking under microgravity.64,65 The additional enrichment of interferon alpha response and angiogenesis gene sets is in line with reports of interferon activation and vascular remodeling in spaceflight and irradiation models.66,67 Although these cross-species findings are exploratory because of heterogeneous reproductive tissues and exposures, they point to RNA metabolic adjustment, chemokine signaling, and interferon associated vascular remodeling as candidate stress-adaptive axes that warrant targeted validation in exposure-matched reproductive and developmental systems.

Despite the contributions of ASTROREPOMICS, current human reproductive transcriptomic datasets remain limited to baseline, non-exposed conditions. These datasets serve as reference profiles that support cross-species contextualization, ortholog mapping, and exploratory comparison, but they cannot yet directly inform human reproductive responses to spaceflight. Moreover, because the analyzed datasets are derived from sex-specific reproductive tissues and lack sex-comparative or gender-related metadata, the current study cannot evaluate sex as an independent biological variable, which limits the generalizability of the findings across sexes. As future human datasets involving microgravity or radiation exposure become publicly available, the platform is structured to incorporate them through standardized curation and normalization workflows, enabling more direct assessment of human reproductive responses as well as sex-specific effects. Further expanding beyond transcriptomics to incorporate other omics modalities, such as epigenomics and metabolomics will strengthen mechanistic interpretation and improve understanding of stress-adaptive pathways. Recent studies emphasize critical knowledge gaps in female reproductive biology and pregnancy outcomes under spaceflight conditions,8,68 highlighting the need for datasets derived from female reproductive tissues. Future research should also utilize advanced single-cell and spatial transcriptomic techniques to uncover cell type specific and tissue microenvironmental changes. Additional considerations include developing automated pipelines for standardized data updates, improving metadata documentation, and establishing rigorous exposure standardization protocols. These developments will enhance ASTROREPOMICS as a foundational resource for reproductive space biology and position it to incorporate future human exposure datasets relevant to long-duration missions.

Conclusion

Reproductive health is a key consideration for long-duration space exploration, but data relevant to reproductive responses under microgravity and radiation remain scarce and dispersed. ASTROREPOMICS was developed to consolidate 17 curated transcriptomic studies across multiple species (human, rodent, and zebrafish) and reproductive tissues (spermatozoa, embryos, and gonads). ASTROREPOMICS provides rigorous normalization, standardized metadata, and interactive analytical tools to facilitate robust exploratory cross-species and cross-platform investigations.

Case-based analyses demonstrate the platform’s ability to extract coherent biological patterns from limited datasets. In mouse spermatozoa, ionizing radiation led to significant suppression of RNA and protein processing pathways, paralleling known disruptions in spermatogenesis and testicular homeostasis. Upregulated GPCR signaling, keratinization, and sensory perception further suggested coordinated alterations in chemotactic guidance, cytoskeletal structure, and neuroendocrine-like stress responses. Beyond single-species contexts, the multi-species intersected-DEG analysis revealed a small but consistent gene set shared between irradiated mammary tissue and microgravity-exposed zebrafish embryos. Although the models are heterogeneous, cross-species enrichment highlighted recurring pathways involving RNA metabolism, cytokine and chemokine signaling, interferon responses, and angiogenesis. While preliminary, these findings illustrate how a unified framework can help reveal potential stress-adaptive signatures worthy of further validation.

As the field advances, incorporating epigenomic, metabolomic, single-cell, and spatial transcriptomic data will further deepen mechanistic understanding. Expanding the availability of spaceflight- or space-analog-exposed human reproductive datasets, particularly those derived from female reproductive tissues, will be essential for enabling more comprehensive and sex-informed analyses. Regular dataset updates, advanced batch corrections, and embedded pathway analyses will support hypothesis-driven discoveries and targeted countermeasures, benefiting both astronaut health and terrestrial reproductive biology.

Limitations of the study

While ASTROREPOMICS addresses significant gaps in space reproductive biology, several caveats remain. The most prominent limitation is the small number of true spaceflight and microgravity datasets currently available. At present, human transcriptomic data within the platform are restricted to baseline, non-exposed conditions. While these serve as an essential reference profiles for cross-species contextualization and ortholog mapping, they cannot yet directly characterize human reproductive responses to actual spaceflight. In addition, because the analyzed datasets are derived from sex-specific reproductive or reproduction-related tissues and lack sex-comparative or gender-related metadata, sex cannot be evaluated as an independent biological variable in the current analyses, thereby limiting the generalizability of the findings across sexes. The multi-species exploratory analyses are further constrained by the heterogeneity of the included models, which involve different tissues, such as rat mammary tissue and zebrafish embryos, as well as varying exposure conditions. Furthermore, the current database focuses exclusively on transcriptomics; the absence of other omics modalities, such as epigenomics and metabolomics, limits the depth of mechanistic interpretation regarding stress-adaptive pathways.

Method details

Data sources and search strategy

Transcriptomic datasets were collected from publicly accessible omics repositories, including NASA’s GeneLab, the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO), and the Integrated Biobank for Space Life Science (ibSLS), using systematic keyword-based searches (Figure 1A). Search terms combined reproduction-related keywords such as “sperm,” “zygote,” “oocyte,” “embryo,” and “gonadal tissue” with spaceflight-relevant descriptors such as “microgravity” and “radiation” (Figure 1B). Additionally, the full dataset collection from the Spermatogenesis-Related Multi-Omics Database (SperMD) was incorporated to expand coverage of reproductive transcriptomes. All included studies employed RNA sequencing or microarray-based transcriptomic analysis and focused on reproductive systems.

Dataset selection and manual curation

Studies were included if they contained transcriptomic data derived from reproductive cells or tissues such as sperm, testes, ovaries, embryos, or early developmental stages, encompassing both samples exposed to spaceflight-related conditions (e.g., microgravity, space radiation, or Earth-based analogs) and unexposed controls serving as references for comparative analysis. Datasets lacking clear reproductive relevance, sufficient exposure or control metadata, or accessible raw or processed gene expression data were excluded. All associated metadata were manually reviewed to confirm the experimental context, tissue or cell type, species, and molecular modality, and each sample was annotated using a standardized metadata schema that recorded key descriptors such as sample name, species, reproductive tissue or cell type, and exposure category (e.g., microgravity, radiation, control). To ensure structured organization and efficient cross-study querying, all curated data were stored within a MySQL-based relational database system. Each individual sample was organized into a dedicated table containing normalized gene expression values and corresponding gene annotations, while these tables were indexed and linked to a centralized metadata table that integrated biological and experimental descriptors including species, tissue or cell type, molecular profiling method, and exposure condition (Figure 1C). This relational schema supported consistent referencing of both expression and metadata information and enabled structured queries across studies, thereby facilitating robust and reproducible comparative analyses.

Dataset integration and cross-species harmonization

The RShiny-based web application provides a streamlined workflow for transcriptomic data integration, normalization, and analysis (Figure 1D). Users begin by browsing datasets through the Metadata interface, which allows filtering according to species, tissue type, exposure condition, and molecular profiling method. Once datasets are selected, the Expression Matrix tab enables users to merge gene expression values across studies. When all samples originate from a single species, expression matrices are aligned directly by matching Entrez IDs, with gene symbols and descriptions appended for interpretability. For cross species datasets, species specific Entrez IDs are first converted into their corresponding human orthologs using the MyGene.info API69 and the Ensembl REST API,70 and expression matrices are subsequently added to facilitate biological interpretation. Gene symbols and descriptions mapped to facilitate biological interpretation.

Normalization and batch correction workflow

To ensure robust cross-study comparisons, the Expression Matrix tab also incorporates a unified normalization and batch correction module, offering per-dataset Z score transformation, which centers and scales expression values to standard deviation units to harmonize dynamic ranges and reduce intra-study variability.71 It also includes ComBat (an empirical Bayes method for continuous data),72 which supports both user-defined and principal component analysis (PCA)-guided batch variables such as sequencing platform, library preparation, preservation method, or mission identifier. An automated PCA-based module suggests the dominant batch source when no manual variable is selected; when multiple batch sources are specified, ComBat is applied using a composite batch factor, representing the interaction of all selected variables. To objectively evaluate correction performance, a quantitative batch-effect score is computed as the mean within-batch variance of sample-wise average expression values after correction. This metric reflects residual batch-associated variability, with lower scores indicating more effective batch removal and greater cross-batch uniformity. The corrected matrices are validated and ranked according to this metric, and an interactive PCA visualization enables users to visually inspect batch-removal efficiency while ensuring that intrinsic biological patterns are preserved.

Differential expression analysis

Following integration, users can perform differential gene expression (DEG) analysis by specifying pseudocount values and selecting among Student’s t test, Welch’s t test, or Wilcoxon rank-sum test. When all datasets originate from a single species, the application calculates Log2 fold changes (Log2FC) and raw p-values computed for each gene based on the chosen test, with an optional log2 transformation that improves variance stabilization and comparability across samples.

When datasets span multiple species, the workflow extends the computation to a cross-species framework. DEG statistics are first computed independently within each species, producing species-specific log2FC and raw p-values using the same statistical procedures as in the single-species workflow. These values are then merged into a unified multi-species DEG table that preserves all per-species results while enabling direct cross-species comparison. In addition, the pipeline computes an average log2FC across species, derived from the per-species log2FC values, to provide a single integrated measure of effect direction and magnitude.

Post-DEG insights and visualization

To assist interpretation, the Differential Expression Insights module provides multiple coordinated visualizations. For single-species analyses, significant genes are filtered using user-defined significance thresholds (|log2FC| and p-value). These genes are shown in the Volcano Plot sub-tab, where genes are color-coded by regulation direction. Similarly, the expression patterns of these user-defined significant genes can be examined through the Heatmap sub-tab, where rows represent genes and columns represent sample IDs. Sample labels on the x axis explicitly encode exposure information, using prefixes such as “Control_” or “Experiment_”, allowing users to immediately distinguish non-exposed versus exposed groups. It provides several customizable options, including hierarchical clustering of both rows and columns, removal of samples containing missing values, removal of genes containing missing values, and optional inclusion of dendrograms in PDF exports. Dendrograms are automatically generated only when hierarchical clustering is enabled and no NA values remain in the matrix after any user-selected filtering. Exposure category and mission metadata remain accessible through the Metadata tab for additional contextual interpretation.

When datasets include multiple species, species-specific statistics are integrated to identify genes that exhibit consistent or divergent regulation across species. A Venn diagram summarizes overlaps and species-specific DEGs, while a regulation-pattern bar chart quantifies up- and down-regulated genes within each cross-species regulatory category. The Volcano Plot and Heatmap apply a strict intersection filter in this setting, meaning that a gene is displayed only if it satisfies the user-defined log2FC and p value thresholds in every species in which it appears.

Over-representation and gene set enrichment analysis

For functional interpretation, the Pathway Enrichment tab supports both Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) and Over-Representation Analysis (ORA) through the WebGestaltR package.73 For GSEA, ranked gene lists are used directly without threshold-based filtering. In multi-species scenarios, genes are ranked by their average cross-species log2FC values to provide a biologically meaningful ordering. For ORA, the input list is restricted to genes that pass the same cross-species significance criteria used by the volcano and heatmap modules. Users may adjust the false discovery rate (FDR) thresholds, permutation numbers, and gene set size limits. The analysis draws from KEGG, WikiPathways, Panther Pathway, and Reactome databases provided natively by WebGestaltR, while MSigDB Hallmark pathways are sourced from a custom-constructed SQL database built from the MSigDBR (version 25.1.1) package to ensure consistent gene set definitions and offline accessibility.

Enrichment visualization framework

The system generates two diagnostic outputs to verify that the input gene universe is suitable for downstream interpretation: an ID Mapping Summary, which reports how the user-submitted gene identifiers were resolved into Entrez IDs and gene symbols, and a GO Slim Term Summary, which condenses the input gene list into broad functional categories spanning biological process, cellular component, and molecular function.

Enrichment results themselves are presented through several complementary modules. The enriched terms table lists pathways together with their associated statistics, including Normalized Enrichment Score (NES), GeneRatio, fold enrichment, p-values, and FDR-adjusted significance. Interactive bar and bubble plots complement the tabular view by ranking pathways according to NES or gene ratio, with hover-enabled tooltips that expose the underlying metrics and FDR values for each gene set.

To facilitate functional interpretation of enrichment results, the platform also offers a visNetwork-based Pathway–Gene Network visualization. This bipartite graph links pathways (as nodes) to their constituent genes, enabling users to observe which genes drive multiple enriched pathways. Pairwise similarity between pathway gene sets is computed dynamically using the Jaccard similarity coefficient, defined as the size of the intersection divided by the size of the union of the two sets.74 Pathways are retained in the network if they exhibit a Jaccard similarity above a user-defined threshold with at least one other pathway. This criterion ensures that only functionally cohesive clusters of pathways are visualized, reducing redundancy and enhancing interpretability without relying on predefined edge lists. Additionally, the network is refined by filtering genes based on their degree of pathway participation. Only genes that appear in a minimum number of retained pathways are included in the final layout.

Interactive visualizations and export options

The described mentioned interactive PCA quality assessments, Venn diagrams, cross-species regulation-pattern bar plots, volcano plots, heatmaps, and enrichment bubble plots are generated using ggplot2 for on-screen visualization and for PDF export. Heatmaps also rely on ggplot2 under normal conditions. However, when users choose to include dendrograms in the heatmap PDF export, the application switches to the ComplexHeatmap framework to produce clustered heatmaps with hierarchical tree structures.

All plots and visualizations are exportable in PDF or PNG formats (with SVG available for GSEA plots), and all data tables, such as normalized matrices, DEG lists, cross-species pattern summaries, and enrichment results, can be exported in Excel format.

Database content overview

ASTROREPOMICS currently includes 17 curated transcriptomic studies addressing reproductive biology under space exposures including microgravity and radiation. Datasets span multiple species, predominantly human (Homo sapiens) and mouse (Mus musculus), with additional studies involving rats (Rattus norvegicus) and zebrafish (Danio rerio). The human datasets currently represent only baseline (non-exposed) reproductive conditions and serve as reference controls for cross-species comparison. Represented reproductive cell and tissue types primarily include spermatozoa, testis-related tissues, spermatogonia, spermatocytes, embryos, and epididymal regions. Exposure conditions cover ionizing radiation, simulated microgravity, and relevant controls. All data originate from RNA sequencing or microarray technologies (e.g., Illumina HiSeq, Affymetrix GeneChip), and undergo normalization and metadata annotation for cross-study integration. A complete list of included datasets in ASTROREPOMICS, along with their species and tissue types, is provided in Table 2.

Experimental models and biological samples

For the primary radiation-focused analysis in mouse germ cells, datasets were retrieved using the criteria Mus musculus, spermatozoa or spermatogenic cell populations, with irradiation serving as the primary exposure of interest. This query yielded six relevant studies (Table 2, marked with a dagger †), including irradiated freeze-dried mouse sperm nuclei, spermatogonia, spermatogenic cell populations, and sperm RNA profiles. In the case of the multi-species dataset “Highly conserved sperm function-related transcripts across three species,” only the mouse samples were included in the analysis, whereas human and rat samples from that study were excluded to maintain biological and exposure consistency.

To explore conserved transcriptional responses across species under space-relevant stressors, a secondary exploratory analysis was performed using two additional datasets (Table 2, marked with a double dagger ‡): irradiated rat mammary carcinomas and zebrafish embryos exposed to simulated microgravity. Although these datasets differ in species, tissue type, and exposure modality, they represent the only currently available non-murine datasets within ASTROREPOMICS involving bona fide space-relevant or space-analog stressors and were therefore used for ortholog-based intersected differential expression analysis.

Human datasets curated in ASTROREPOMICS were not included in the differential expression analyses presented here, as they exclusively represent baseline, non-exposed reproductive conditions, serving as reference profiles for cross-species contextualization and ortholog mapping.

Quantification and statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using R within the ASTROREPOMICS web application. Differential gene expression analyses were conducted using Welch’s t test applied to log2-transformed expression values. Batch-effect correction was performed using per-dataset Z score normalization or ComBat (empirical Bayes), with batch variables defined by user selection. PCA was used for visualization of batch structure and batch-effect removal.

For mouse germ cell–focused analysis, n represents the number of individual transcriptomic samples included across all mouse germ cell datasets analyzed. The pooled analysis comprised 38 samples in total, including irradiated freeze-dried mouse spermatozoa (n = 4) and non-irradiated freeze-dried controls (n = 12), which were used for the differential expression analysis, fresh spermatozoa (n = 4), mouse sperm samples from the cross-species dataset (n = 5), SpermBase-derived sperm profiles (n = 4), detergent-demembranated sperm nuclei and intact spermatozoa (n = 3), and ID4-GFP–defined spermatogonial populations (n = 6).

For multi-species exploratory analysis, n represents the number of transcriptomic samples included per species and exposure condition. The pooled analysis comprised 22 samples in total, including irradiated rat mammary gland samples (n = 10) with non-irradiated controls (n = 3), and simulated microgravity–exposed zebrafish embryos (n = 3) with matched controls (n = 6). Differential expression was computed within each species and integrated at the gene level after ortholog mapping.

In all analyses, n denotes independent biological transcriptomic samples derived from the original studies. Visualizations represent distributions, ordination results (PCA), gene-level differential expression statistics (log2 fold change and p-values), or enrichment metrics (NES, gene overlap, and FDR), rather than pooled summary statistics with measures of central tendency or dispersion.

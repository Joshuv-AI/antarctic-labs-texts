Clinical presentation and survival outcome of solitary fibrous tumors of the central nervous system: a single center study
Authors: Ahmet Eren Secen, Servet Guresci, Musa Onur Ozbakir, Ugur Kemal Gunduz, Ahmet Deniz Belen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeSolitary fibrous tumors (SFTs) of the central nervous system (CNS) are rare mesenchymal neoplasms with unpredictable biological behavior, including a propensity for recurrence and metastasis. Due to their rarity, prognostic factors and optimal management strategies remain incompletely defined. This study aimed to evaluate the clinical characteristics and survival outcomes of patients with CNS SFT.MethodsThis single-center retrospective study included patients who underwent surgical resection for a presumed meningioma between January 2017 and December 2025 and were postoperatively diagnosed with CNS SFT. Clinical, radiological, surgical, and pathological data were reviewed, and overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS) were analyzed using the Kaplan–Meier method.ResultsEighteen patients were included (mean age 48.7 years), and gross total resection was achieved in 77.8%. During a median OS follow-up of 28.7 months, three deaths (16.7%) occurred; median OS was not reached. Adjuvant radiotherapy was associated with improved OS (p = 0.027), while postoperative neurological deficits were associated with worse OS (p = 0.011). Seventeen patients were evaluable for DFS, with a median DFS of 9.2 months and a recurrence rate of 64.7%. In multivariable Cox analysis, adjuvant radiotherapy was independently associated with reduced recurrence risk (HR 0.094, p = 0.042).ConclusionCNS SFTs demonstrate favorable overall survival but high early recurrence rates. Adjuvant radiotherapy appears to be associated with improved survival outcomes, underscoring the importance of multidisciplinary management and long-term follow-up.Keywords: Solitary fibrous tumor (SFT), Hemangiopericytoma, Central nervous system, Survival, Radiotherapy

Introduction

Solitary fibrous tumors (SFTs) are rare mesenchymal neoplasms that were initially described in the visceral pleura but are now recognized to arise in a wide range of extrathoracic locations, including the central nervous system (CNS) [1]. Intracranial SFTs were first reported by Carneiro et al. in 1996 [1] and central nervous system SFTs account for less than 1% of all primary CNS tumors [2]. Owing to their dural attachment and radiological resemblance to meningiomas, CNS SFTs are frequently misdiagnosed preoperatively [2].

Despite their rarity, these tumors are clinically relevant because they demonstrate unpredictable biological behavior, including local recurrence and the potential for extracranial metastasis [3].

Historically, SFTs and hemangiopericytomas (HPCs) were regarded as distinct pathological entities, with HPCs considered more aggressive and associated with higher recurrence and metastatic rates [4–6]. Subsequent molecular studies identified a shared NAB2–STAT6 gene fusion resulting from a chromosomal inversion at chromosome 12q13, providing strong evidence that these tumors represent a single disease spectrum [6–8]. This fusion is now recognized as the defining molecular hallmark of SFT and leads to aberrant nuclear expression of STAT6. Accordingly, diffuse nuclear STAT6 immunoreactivity has become a highly useful diagnostic marker for SFT and helps distinguish these tumors from meningioma and other dural-based lesions with overlapping radiological or histomorphological features.

Based on these molecular findings, the World Health Organization (WHO) unified SFT and HPC into a single entity in its 2016 classification [9]. In the 2021 WHO Classification of Tumors of the Central Nervous System, SFT is classified among mesenchymal, non-meningothelial tumors, specifically under soft tissue tumors/fibroblastic and myofibroblastic tumors, and is graded within the same tumor type as CNS WHO grade 1, 2, or 3 [10].

Surgical resection is the primary treatment modality for CNS SFTs [11]. Numerous studies have identified the extent of resection, particularly gross total resection (GTR), as a major prognostic factor influencing local control, recurrence, and survival outcomes [2, 12–16].

However, achieving complete resection may be technically challenging due to tumor vascularity, location, and proximity to critical neurovascular structures [2]. The role of adjuvant radiotherapy remains controversial. While several studies have reported improved local control and survival outcomes with postoperative radiotherapy, others have demonstrated inconsistent or limited benefit, particularly with respect to overall survival [11, 12, 17–23].

Because CNS SFTs are rare, most available data derive from retrospective studies and small case series, resulting in variability in reported prognostic factors and treatment strategies [5, 13, 15, 24]. In addition, survival endpoints used across studies, such as overall survival (OS), progression-free survival (PFS), and relapse-free survival, are heterogeneous, complicating direct comparison of outcomes [19]. Given these limitations, additional institutional series remain valuable for refining prognostic assessment and improving understanding of survival patterns. The present study evaluates the clinical characteristics, surgical management, and survival outcomes of patients with CNS SFT treated at a single tertiary center, with a specific focus on overall survival and disease-free survival.

Methods

Study design and patient selection

This study is a single-center, retrospective descriptive clinical study evaluating patients diagnosed with solitary fibrous tumor (SFT) of the central nervous system. The institutional surgical pathology database and hospital electronic medical records were reviewed for patients who underwent surgery with a preliminary diagnosis of meningioma between January 2017 and December 2025. During this period, 831 patients were surgically treated for a presumed meningioma, and postoperative histopathological examination revealed a diagnosis of SFT in 18 cases (2.17%). These 18 patients constituted the study cohort.

Data collection

Clinical, radiological, surgical, and pathological data were retrospectively collected from the hospital electronic archive system and patient files. Recorded variables included demographic characteristics, tumor localization, perioperative signs and symptoms, extent of resection, and histopathological features. Follow-up data and survival outcomes were obtained from medical records when available.

Surgical management and extent of resection

All patients underwent microsurgical resection using standard microsurgical techniques. Surgical approaches were selected according to tumor location and its relationship with adjacent neurovascular structures. The primary surgical goal was gross total resection (GTR) in all cases. However, subtotal resection (STR) was performed in four patients due to close adherence to or invasion of critical neurovascular structures. The extent of resection was determined based on intraoperative surgical assessment and confirmed by postoperative imaging studies.

Histopathological and immunohistochemical evaluation

Archived formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue blocks were retrieved for all cases.

Sections of 4 μm thickness were prepared and stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E).

Histopathological evaluation included assessment of tumor cellularity, growth pattern, mitotic activity, necrosis, vascular pattern, and other features relevant to CNS WHO grading.

Immunohistochemical evaluation was performed as part of the diagnostic work-up, with particular emphasis on nuclear STAT6 expression. Diffuse nuclear STAT6 immunoreactivity was considered supportive of SFT and was used, together with morphology and the overall immunohistochemical profile, to distinguish SFT from meningioma and other dural-based tumors. All cases were reviewed for histopathological diagnosis and graded according to the 2021 World Health Organization Classification of Tumors of the Central Nervous System as CNS WHO grade 1, 2, or 3.

Adjuvant radiotherapy

Postoperative radiotherapy was administered in the adjuvant setting and was not delivered as salvage treatment at recurrence. The indication for adjuvant radiotherapy was determined according to tumor grade and extent of resection. Patients with WHO grade 2 or grade 3 tumors and/or subtotal resection (STR) were considered for postoperative radiotherapy, provided that they were clinically eligible and recovered adequately after surgery.

Radiotherapy was generally initiated within the first 2 months following surgical resection.

Treatment was delivered using a Varian linear accelerator with conventional fractionation (1.8–2.0 Gy per fraction, 5 fractions per week). For patients without macroscopic residual disease, the clinical target volume consisted of the postoperative surgical cavity, and a 0.5-cm margin was added to create the planning target volume (PTV). A total dose of 50.4–54 Gy was delivered. In patients with residual tumor after surgery, radiotherapy was directed to the residual lesion with a 0.3-cm PTV margin, and an additional boost was administered to achieve a total dose of 60 Gy.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS Statistics for Windows, version 21.0.

Descriptive statistics were used to summarize demographic, clinical, surgical, and pathological characteristics. Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation or median (range), as appropriate, while categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages.

Overall survival (OS) was defined as the interval from the date of surgery to death from any cause or last follow-up. Patients who were alive at the last follow-up were censored at the date of last clinical contact. Disease-free survival (DFS) was defined as the time from initial surgical resection to radiological evidence of tumor recurrence or progression on follow-up magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), or last follow-up in patients without recurrence. Patients without recurrence or progression at the last available MRI follow-up were censored at that date. Survival curves for OS and DFS were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method and compared using the log-rank test. Median survival times with 95% confidence intervals were reported when estimable; estimated mean survival times were provided descriptively when the median was not reached or as supplementary descriptive information.

To identify factors associated with survival outcomes, univariate Cox proportional hazards regression analyses were performed for selected variables. Variables demonstrating a potential association with survival outcomes in univariate analysis were subsequently included in multivariate Cox proportional hazards models to identify independent prognostic factors.

Results were reported as hazard ratios (HRs) with corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs). In addition, among patients with available recurrence data, baseline and perioperative characteristics were descriptively compared between patients with and without recurrence.

Categorical variables were compared using Fisher’s exact test because of the small sample size and low expected cell counts. A two-sided p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Patients

A total of 18 patients with histopathologically confirmed solitary fibrous tumors of the central nervous system were included in the study. The demographic and clinical characteristics of the patients are summarized in Table 1. The cohort had a mean age of 48.7 years, with a slight female predominance. Most patients presented after a relatively short symptom duration, and two-thirds had a normal neurological examination at admission. Tumors were predominantly located in the supratentorial compartment, and gross total resection was achieved in the majority of cases. More precise anatomical classification demonstrated that tumors were located in the convexity region in 5 patients, parasagittal region in 3, anterior fossa in 2, pineal region in 2, cerebellar region in 2, middle fossa in 1, tentorial region in 1, spinal region in 1, and cerebellopontine angle in 1 patient.

Histopathological evaluation revealed a broad distribution of tumor grades, with WHO grade 2 and grade 3 lesions together accounting for over two-thirds of the cohort. Adjuvant radiotherapy was administered in more than half of the cases, and a minority required re-operation during follow-up (Table 1). A total of 12 patients received adjuvant radiotherapy with following indications: 2 for subtotal resection and grade 2/3 tumor, 1 patient for only subtotal resection, 9 for grade 2/3 tumor only. Two patients did not receive radiotherapy since they died postoperatively before adjuvant radiotherapy, and one was still in the postoperative recovery period; although these patients had indication.

Overall survival

The mean follow-up for overall survival (OS) was 33.7 months, with a median follow-up of 28.7 months (range, 1.3–118.0 months). During follow-up, 3 deaths (16.7%) were observed, while 15 patients (83.3%) were censored at last follow-up. Kaplan-Meier analysis demonstrated a favorable OS outcome, with the median OS not reached. The estimated mean OS was 93.4 months (95% CI, 68.0–118.9 months). Figure 1 shows the Kaplan-Meier curve for overall survival.

Kaplan-Meier curve for overall survival (OS) (all patients)

Univariate Kaplan–Meier analyses showed no statistically significant differences in overall survival (OS) according to age (age > 50) (log-rank p = 0.163), sex (log-rank p = 0.864), preoperative embolization (log-rank p = 0.494), need for re-operation (log-rank p = 0.230), or pathological grade (grade 1 versus grade 2–3) (log-rank p = 0.366). Adjuvant radiotherapy was associated with significantly improved OS compared with no radiotherapy (log-rank p = 0.027) (Fig. 2). Extent of resection demonstrated a trend toward improved OS in patients who underwent gross total resection compared with subtotal resection, although this did not reach statistical significance (log-rank p = 0.089) (Fig. 3). In addition, patients who developed postoperative additional neurological deficits had significantly worse OS compared with those without new deficits (log-rank p = 0.011). Because of the limited number of OS events and model convergence issues, multivariable Cox regression analysis for OS was not performed.

Disease-free survival

For disease-free survival (DFS), 17 patients were evaluable. The mean DFS follow-up was 13.8 months, with a median follow-up of 6.8 months (range, 1.1–59.7 months). During follow-up, 11 patients (64.7%) experienced tumor recurrence or progression, while 6 patients (35.3%) remained recurrence-free. Among patients with recurrence or progression, 10 developed local recurrence at the primary tumor site, whereas one patient with an initially frontal lesion developed distant metastatic disease involving the thoracic vertebra. Kaplan-Meier analysis demonstrated a median DFS of 9.2 months (95%CI, 4.2–14.1 months), indicating a high early recurrence rate (Fig. 4). The estimated mean DFS was 22.3 months (95% CI, 10.3–34.2 months). Figure 4 shows the Kaplan-Meier curve for disease free survival.

Kaplan-Meier curve for disease-free survival (DFS) (all patients)

Univariate Kaplan-Meier analysis demonstrated a trend toward longer disease-free survival (DFS) in patients who underwent gross total resection compared with subtotal resection (log-rank p = 0.070) (Fig. 5). DFS did not differ significantly according to age (> 50) (log-rank p = 0.176), sex (p = 0.556), preoperative embolization (p = 0.618), postoperative additional neurological deficit (p = 0.174), pathological grade (grade 1 versus 2–3) (p = 0.369), need for re-operation (p = 0.328), or adjuvant radiotherapy (p = 0.399).

In an exploratory multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression analysis including extent of resection, pathological grade, adjuvant radiotherapy, and age > 50, adjuvant radiotherapy was independently associated with a significantly reduced risk of recurrence (HR 0.094, 95% CI 0.009–0.923; p = 0.042). Comparison of patients with and without recurrence

Among the 17 patients with available recurrence data, baseline and perioperative characteristics were compared between patients with and without recurrence. Recurrence was observed in 11 patients, whereas 6 patients remained recurrence-free during follow-up.

Patients older than 50 years showed a numerically higher recurrence rate compared with younger patients; recurrence occurred in 8 of 10 patients aged > 50 years and in 3 of 7 patients aged ≤ 50 years, although this difference was not statistically significant (Fisher’s exact test, p = 0.162). No significant differences were observed between patients with and without recurrence in terms of sex (p = 0.644), preoperative embolization (p = 0.515), pathological grade (grade 1 versus grade 2–3; p = 0.584), adjuvant radiotherapy (p = 1.000), or postoperative additional neurological deficits (p = 0.588).

All patients who underwent subtotal resection developed recurrence or progression, whereas recurrence occurred in 7 of 13 patients who underwent gross total resection; however, this difference did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.237). Similarly, all patients who required re-operation were in the recurrence group, while none of the recurrence-free patients underwent re-operation (p = 0.237). Given that re-operation was generally performed as a consequence of recurrence or progression rather than as a baseline treatment variable, this finding should be interpreted descriptively and not as an independent predictor of DFS.

Discussion

In this single-center retrospective study, we analyzed the clinical characteristics, treatment strategies, and survival outcomes of patients with solitary fibrous tumors (SFTs) of the central nervous system. The principal findings of our study are threefold. First, overall survival (OS) was generally favorable, whereas disease-free survival (DFS) was short, with a high rate of early recurrence. Second, adjuvant radiotherapy was associated with improved OS in univariate analysis and with a reduced risk of recurrence in multivariable analysis for DFS.

Third, postoperative neurological deficits were associated with worse OS, while the extent of surgical resection was not significantly associated with either OS or DFS. These findings should be interpreted as exploratory and hypothesis-generating, given the limited cohort size and the small number of outcome events. Nevertheless, they reflect the heterogeneous clinical behavior of CNS SFTs and the challenges inherent in identifying consistent prognostic factors in this rare tumor entity.

One of the most striking observations in our cohort was the dissociation between OS and DFS. Although long-term survival was observed in a substantial proportion of patients, recurrence occurred early and frequently, resulting in a short median DFS. This pattern has been repeatedly described in prior series of CNS SFTs and appears to be a defining feature of the disease [15, 19, 25, 26]. These observations suggest that OS alone may underestimate disease aggressiveness and may not adequately reflect treatment efficacy. In this context, DFS emerges as a particularly relevant endpoint for evaluating disease control and guiding clinical decision-making. The interpretation of survival outcomes in CNS solitary fibrous tumors is further complicated by heterogeneity in reported endpoints across studies, including overall survival, progression-free survival, relapse-free survival, and local control. The use of varied and often composite endpoints complicates cross-study comparisons and may obscure clinically meaningful differences in outcomes. In this context, our findings, demonstrating early recurrence despite prolonged overall survival, suggest that disease-free survival provides valuable complementary information to overall survival and should be incorporated into outcome analyses for CNS solitary fibrous tumors.

Surgical resection remains the cornerstone of treatment for CNS SFT, and numerous studies have identified gross total resection as an important determinant of local control and recurrence risk [2, 5, 12–16]. However, in our cohort, the extent of resection was not significantly associated with either OS or DFS. This finding contrasts with several larger series. The absence of a significant association in our study may be attributed to several factors, including the limited sample size, relatively homogeneous surgical management, and the early use of adjuvant radiotherapy in selected patients, which may have mitigated the impact of residual disease. In addition, CNS SFTs exhibit marked biological heterogeneity, and tumor behavior may not be fully captured by extent of resection alone. Consistent with our findings, several series have failed to demonstrate a significant association between extent of resection and survival outcomes, including overall survival, in patients with central nervous system solitary fibrous tumors, suggesting that disease control and survival may be influenced by multiple interacting factors beyond surgical extent alone [2, 27].

Previous retrospective series and reviews have consistently shown that CNS SFT/HPC is a rare tumor entity with high recurrence potential, possible extracranial metastasis, and heterogeneous long-term outcomes. In larger institutional series, recurrence rates of 41.9%–54% and extracranial metastasis rates of 11.6%-20% have been reported, with recurrence sometimes occurring several years after initial treatment [12, 15]. Similarly, Schiariti et al. reported a local recurrence rate of 92% at 15 years and extraneural metastasis in 26% of patients, supporting the need for prolonged follow-up [21]. In a systematic review of 523 patients, Ghose et al. found that complete resection and adjuvant radiotherapy were associated with improved survival, while local recurrence was more common than neural-axis or extraneural metastasis [20]. Population-based studies have also suggested that gross total resection and postoperative radiotherapy may be associated with improved survival outcomes [5, 18]. However, other institutional series have reported that radiotherapy may improve recurrence-free interval or disease control without a clear overall survival benefit [12, 15].

Taken together, these findings highlight the retrospective and heterogeneous nature of the available evidence. Therefore, the findings of the present study should be interpreted cautiously as exploratory and hypothesis-generating, representing a descriptive single-center experience rather than evidence challenging established management paradigms.

The role of adjuvant radiotherapy in CNS SFT remains one of the most debated topics in the literature. Several studies have reported improved local control and, in some cases, survival benefits with postoperative radiotherapy; conversely, other series have demonstrated limited or no benefit, especially with respect to OS, contributing to ongoing controversy [11, 12, 17–23]. In our study, adjuvant radiotherapy was associated with improved OS in univariate Kaplan-Meier analysis and independently associated with reduced recurrence risk in multivariable Cox regression analysis for DFS. It is important to emphasize that OS-related findings in our cohort are based solely on univariate analysis due to the limited number of death events, and therefore do not permit conclusions regarding independent prognostic effects. Nonetheless, the consistent association between radiotherapy and improved DFS suggests a potential role for adjuvant radiotherapy in disease control, particularly in patients at increased risk of recurrence.

Differences in patient selection, timing of radiotherapy, radiation techniques, and follow-up duration may partly explain discrepancies among published studies. Advances in radiation delivery and improved target delineation may contribute to better local control with acceptable toxicity, although these factors could not be systematically evaluated in the present study. Taken together, our findings support the consideration of adjuvant radiotherapy within a multidisciplinary treatment framework, while acknowledging that its optimal indications remain to be defined.

Postoperative neurological deficits were significantly associated with worse OS in univariate analysis. This association is unlikely to reflect a direct causal relationship but may instead represent a surrogate marker for more complex surgical cases, aggressive tumor behavior, or involvement of eloquent brain regions. These findings highlight the importance of careful preoperative planning and intraoperative decision-making aimed at balancing maximal tumor removal with preservation of neurological function. Given the generally favorable OS observed in many patients, functional outcomes and quality of life considerations are particularly relevant in the management of CNS SFT.

From a clinical perspective, our results emphasize that favorable OS should not be equated with durable disease control in patients with CNS SFT. The high rate of early recurrence observed in our cohort underscores the need for close radiological surveillance and long-term follow-up, even after apparently successful surgical treatment. While complete resection remains an important surgical objective, our findings suggest that disease control is influenced by multiple factors and that adjuvant radiotherapy may play a meaningful role in reducing recurrence risk in selected cases. These considerations highlight the importance of individualized treatment strategies developed within a multidisciplinary setting.

The retrospective design and relatively small sample size limit statistical power and increase the potential for selection bias, particularly regarding the use of adjuvant radiotherapy.

Because adjuvant radiotherapy was preferentially considered for patients with WHO grade 2–3 tumors and/or subtotal resection, patients receiving radiotherapy may have differed systematically from those who did not, and the observed associations should therefore not be interpreted as evidence of a causal treatment effect. The limited number of death events precluded multivariable Cox regression analysis for OS, and OS-related findings should therefore be interpreted as descriptive and hypothesis-generating. Because of the small cohort size and limited number of outcome events, subgroup and Kaplan-Meier analyses should be interpreted descriptively rather than as confirmatory statistical evidence. In addition, the heterogeneity of tumor locations and treatment approaches may have influenced outcomes.

Despite these limitations, this study benefits from a homogeneous single-center cohort, contemporary pathological classification, and comprehensive assessment of both OS and DFS.

The lack of a statistically significant DFS difference according to re-operation should be interpreted with caution. Re-operation was not a true baseline prognostic variable but was usually performed after recurrence or progression had already occurred. Therefore, its relationship with DFS is inherently time-dependent and potentially affected by reverse causality. In this small cohort, all patients who underwent re-operation had recurrence, suggesting that re-operation more likely reflects recurrent disease burden rather than an independent determinant of DFS.

In conclusion, CNS SFTs are characterized by relatively favorable overall survival but frequent early recurrence. Adjuvant radiotherapy was associated with improved disease-free survival, whereas the extent of resection was not a significant prognostic factor in our cohort. These findings reinforce the importance of long-term follow-up and individualized, multidisciplinary management. Larger multicenter studies with standardized endpoints are needed to further clarify prognostic factors and optimize treatment strategies for this rare and heterogeneous tumor entity.

Generalized triangle group based S-box construction for secure image encryption
Authors: Aqsa Zafar Abbasi, Ayesha Rafiq, Ines Hilali Jaghdam, Chemseddine Maatki, Walid Hassen, Badr M. Alshammari
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Cyber image security is an important issue in current communication networks, as secret images are often communicated through insecure channels. Most existing image encryption algorithms based on conventional S-box designs and chaotic maps are mostly subject to weak cryptanalysis resistance, poor efficiency, or poor flexibility. In order to overcome such limitations, this paper introduces a new substitution box (S-box) design method based on the parametric action of Generalized Triangle Groups (GTG), represented as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\langle m, n \mid m^3 = n^3 = (mnmn^2)^k\rangle$$\end{document}. The new scheme employs GTG-based substitution and permutation operations for RGB image encryption. The research process entails: (i) rigorous mathematical parameterization of GTG to design nonlinear and bijective S-boxes, (ii) thorough analysis of their cryptographic attributes, such as nonlinearity, differential uniformity, strict avalanche criterion, and linear/differential attack resistance, and (iii) integration of the S-boxes in an RGB image encryption system. Experimental testing proves that the suggested S-box has excellent nonlinearity 112, well-balanced Strict Avalanche Criterion \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\approx$$\end{document}0.50, and minimal BIC values, showing very good resistance towards linear attacks, also entropy levels approaching the ideal 8.0, NPCR of more than 99.8%, and UACI of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\approx$$\end{document} 33.3%, better than a number of state-of-the-art approaches. In comparison to state-of-the-art S-box and image encryption algorithms, the new scheme has larger nonlinearity, enhanced diffusion/confusion characteristics, and greater cryptanalysis resistance with comparable computational efficiency.

Introduction

The development of new communication networks and technologies over the past few decades has altered the communication system. Confidential information worth thousands of kilobytes are being sent via the Internet and other unsecured routes; yet, an unauthorized recipient may intercept the data. Digital images are widely used on the Internet; if they include sensitive data, they must first be encrypted before being sent over insecure networks. A safe transmission scheme’s basic concept is that a sender can use an algorithm to transform an ordinary image into a cipher image, which can only be decrypted by an authorised recipient with the correct confidential key or keys. There are several ways to encrypt images, including diffusion (which replaces pixels) and permutation (which combines both). Image encryption has a wide range of uses, including telemedicine, the military, personal images, video conferences, biometric systems, and other situations where a workable, quick, and safe encryption is needed. Substitution boxes, sometimes known as S-boxes, are crucial components of contemporary cryptography systems. They alter the input data in nonlinear ways, which greatly improves security by making the input harder to reverse. S-boxes are employed in symmetric key algorithms, block ciphers, and hash functions, among other cryptographic applications. S-box design is a challenging endeavor that necessitates giving implementation constraints, security, and efficiency serious thought. For S-box design, many heuristic and mathematical approaches have been put forth. Using evolutionary algorithms to identify S-boxes that satisfy particular security needs is a commonly used technique. As a result of research on S-boxes, cryptanalytic techniques such as differential as well as linear cryptanalysis have been developed to crack cryptographic algorithms that employ these elements. This technique provides a novel way to improve the S-box component’s cryptanalytic capabilities. A different author7 suggested a method for creating an S-box with the Mobius group as well as finite field to increase security and withstand assaults. This method makes use of unstable systems’ characteristics to increase security.

There are a number of block ciphers available in the literature that fully rely upon the Shannon theory to improve security for communication8. Confusion-causing substitutions comprise those that bring the encrypted text as well as key’s connection’s complexity up to the required degree. Conversely, diffusion defines the changes that spread the original text’s statistical characteristics across the text that has been encrypted and vice versa. The S-box constitutes one of the most confusing nonlinear components of a block cipher. To make new block ciphers more complex, many techniques are proposed to design non-linear sub-components. Since these constructions typically emerge with characteristic 2 Galois fields, improvements and enhancements to algebraic structures are required. The latest developments in image encryption point to the growing dependence on chaos theory, neural networks, and memristive systems in attaining secure and efficient data encryption. Homomorphisms of GTG and cryptographic relevance

Here, a homomorphism refers to a structure-preserving mapping between groups that holds the group operation. Homomorphisms, when applied to GTG, map group elements to matrix representations while holding their algebraic properties in place. This makes the resulting mappings nonlinear and bijective, suitable for building secure substitution boxes (S-boxes) in cryptography.

Substitution box

A Substitution Box (S-box) is typically a fixed, multi-input, multi-output transformation table that maps an input bit vector to an output bit vector. It is a function: \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S:\{0,1\}^n \rightarrow \{0,1\}^m$$\end{document} where n is the number of input bits and m is the number of output bits. The mapping is designed to obscure relationships between the key and the ciphertext.

Design rationale and GTG based S-box construction

Building an S-box, or substitution box, is an essential part of developing cryptographic algorithms, especially block ciphers. S-box is a non-linear component that causes confusion by non-linear and predetermined replacement of input bits with output bits. In order to construct an S-box, bijective mappings of input values to output values must be chosen and must be resistant to decryption methods such as differential and linear decryption. Generating a random permutation of the input data, examining the permutation for desired cryptographic qualities, and applying mathematical modifications to maximize security features are common processes in the design process. To ensure strong security against possible attacks, S-boxes should be thoroughly investigated for characteristics including non-linearity, differential uniformity as well as algebraic complexity. One of the most dependable methods for achieving data security has been the block cipher. The efficacy of block ciphers’ replacement boxes determines how strong they are against different types of attacks. The substitution box, or S-box, has the duty to ensure that the correlation between the key and the ciphertext is as undetectable as possible. Because of its advantageous properties for cryptography, replacement boxes based on algebraic structures have been built and designed extensively during the past ten years. However, several cryptographers have discovered weaknesses in S-box constructions based on an algebraic structures in recent years. Only True Randomness is the definitive solution for these S-box weaknesses. In25, the use of a coset diagram with a bijective map resulted in a good S-box. The authors generate resilient S-boxes26 by combining the Mobius transformation with a chaotic tent-sine system. The authors in27 presented a new S-boxes technique that utilizes a PGL group. A new concept for the S-box was introduced in28, based on the concepts of symmetric groups and coset graphs. A novel technique for creating cryptographically resilient S-boxes on a finite field by a LFT is provided in29.

Proposed algorithm for the S-box

There are different approaches which can be utilized to introduce uncertainty into a system employing cryptography to enhance reliability. Employment of an S-box is one such approach which is considered to be the most effective in modern security systems. S-boxes are frequently built using GTG as well as are essential to achieve cryptographic efficiency. This offers a viable path towards creating a variety of safe S-boxes and has major advantages for building strong and dependable cryptosystems.

In this paper, we extended GTG analysis by undertaking a systematic parametrization of the group presentation. Cryptographic analysis of the proposed S-box

The S-box is a major part of symmetric key cryptography that is used to confuse the cryptosystem. By hiding the connection between an encryption key as well as the text being encrypted, a technique known as scrambling makes it more challenging to extract the key via the ciphertext. The S-box can alter any or all of the ciphertext’s bits by changing just one bit in the key, hence boosting security. This is accomplished by the S-box, which takes a specific amount of input bits (k) and converts them to a given number of output bits (j), where k and j are not always the same. You might think of an S-box as a \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$2^k$$\end{document}-word reference table that maps every potential input to an equivalent output. This transformation is essential to the cryptographic algorithm’s strength because it makes the ciphertext appear random, which keeps attackers from drawing a direct line of sight between the input (key) as well as the output. The development and evaluation of cryptographic algorithms require S-box analysis. Block ciphers become confusing and non-linear due to S-boxes, which is essential for their security. S-box metrics evaluate its vulnerability to algebraic, differential, and linear attacks. Investigators also look for tendencies that attackers can exploit in the statistical aspects and randomization of S-boxes.

Nonlinearity

The level of nonlinearity is important in safeguarding the approximation of an S-box from linear attacks. Strength and linear approximation resistance are characterized by higher levels of nonlinearity. Algebraic complexity

Algebraic complexity is the number of terms present in the algebraic normal form (ANF) of the Boolean functions that characterize the S-box. S-boxes with greater algebraic complexity are more difficult to approximate through algebraic attacks. The described S-box achieves an average algebraic complexity of 255, which implies robust resistance against algebraic attacks compared to conventional S-box designs with fewer ANF terms.

Differential uniformity

Differential uniformity measures the highest probability of a given input difference resulting in a particular output difference. Differential uniformity is low if an S-box is resistant to differential attacks. The new S-box has a differential uniformity of 4, which is equal to the optimal bound for efficient S-boxes. This means that the chances of successful differential attacks are very slim.

Key-space analysis

Key-space assessment determines the maximum amount of unique keys that can be created and used. Since a bigger key space raises the amount of keys that hackers must try in an attack using brute force in order to successfully decode data, it is desirable. Attacks using brute force involve testing each key continuously till the one that works is found. This improves the security of the technique of encryption since brute force assaults become more cognitively and time-consuming with a larger key space. Using histogram analysis, one can determine an image’s statistical characteristics by examining the distribution of pixel brightness. It entails creating a histogram, a graph that illustrates the distribution of color strength. Irregular deviation

The irregular deviation or deviation of an RGB image quantifies the extent to which the color distribution of pixels will vary from a uniform and even pattern. Instead of relying on the general variation of the color distribution, this measure considers the deviations of each color bin from its average frequency. In practice, it means dividing the frequency that is observed for each color bin by the frequency one would get if the colors were perfectly and equally distributed across the image. It then calculates the irregular deviation for each color channel as the average of the absolute difference between the observed and expected ones. Low irregular deviation indicates colors in the image are tightly packed, which is typically indicative of quality and good images. Bigger irregularity values, however, depict off-center or biased color distributions, which are likely a function of a multitude of reasons like low light, poor color balance, overexposure, or underexposure. Hence, irregular deviation can be used as a diagnostic tool for the quantification of image quality and for recognizing possible abnormalities in image capture or processing. Randomness Cipher test

A statistical evaluation method called the Randomness Cipher Test is used to evaluate the performance of a number randomizer used in a cryptographic system. To find out how much a bit sequence is generated at random, the NIST offers a test suite with numerous options. The suggested encryption of images method is evaluated using the NIST suite. The core components of the NIST suite are many randomness tests that are used to assess the level of unpredictability within digital information, including as text, image, audio, as well as video files. The NIST suit findings for the method of encryption we are offering indicate that our scheme is capable of fending off various cryptographic vulnerabilities. To pass all tests, the true values for every component in the NIST suite have to be more than 0.01. These tests can be applied to the key stream generated by a stream cipher or the output of a number randomizer. It’s crucial to understand that a number randomizer is neither secure nor suitable for cryptography just because it passes all these tests. These tests are just one way to assess the dependability of a number randomizer. Conclusion and future directions

Reliable encryption solutions need to be developed in order to meet the increasing need for the safe transmission and storage of medical pictures. One essential component of block ciphers is the substitution box, or S-Box. Non-linearity is an important consideration while designing safe S-boxes. Thus, new techniques for producing S-boxes with high non-linearity scores need to be created. In this study, a unique method of encrypting RGB images with substitution box was presented, based on parametrization of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\langle m, n \mid m^3=n^3=(mnmn^2)^k \rangle$$\end{document}, a case of Generalized Triangle Groups (GTG). The recommended method applies effective substitution boxes to each color channel of the image by utilizing GTG capabilities. The outcomes of the experiment demonstrate that the recommended method maintains the integrity of the image that was decrypted while offering a high level of security. The suggested technique’s resistance to well-known attacks like linear as well as differential cryptanalysis that is is one of its main advantages. This is due to the usage of complex integers, which introduce complexity and nonlinearity within the encryption process. Furthermore, compared to other existing methods, the recommended strategy is more efficient because it provides an elevated level of encryption with a small key size. Additionally, it has been demonstrated that this method is more durable than other encryption schemes that use replacement boxes, that are meant to be more difficult to crack. Additionally, the proposed method has an excellent diffusion degree, which guarantees that even small modifications to the plaintext are dispersed throughout the entire ciphertext. The main goal of any encryption technique is to make it harder for an attacker to identify patterns in the cypher text. Another advantage is that the recommended strategy may be easily adopted, which would increase its accessibility to a wider group of individuals. The results of the experiment showed how effective the recommended method is in terms of processing and memory requirements, which is crucial for real-time applications.

Future Recommendation: In future study, we intend to examine whether the proposed technique can be applied to other types of multimedia, such audio and video. To increase the security of the suggested method, we also wish to investigate the usage of alternative GTG cases with more complex structures for the construction of S-boxes.

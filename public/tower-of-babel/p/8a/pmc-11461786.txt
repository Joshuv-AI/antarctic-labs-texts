Local excision versus total mesorectal excision for rectal cancer patients with clinical complete or near-complete response after neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy
Authors: Lu Jin, Kuo Zheng, Yonggang Hong, Enda Yu, Liqiang Hao, Wei Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
PurposeLocal excision is an effective approach for managing rectal cancer exhibiting substantial regression after neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy. The purpose of this study is to compare the outcomes between local excision and total mesorectal excision in rectal cancer patients achieving clinical complete or near-complete response after neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy.MethodsThis is a retrospective cohort study that includes a consecutive series of rectal cancer patients who responded well to neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy followed by surgery. A total of 180 rectal cancer patients at a single institution during a 12-year period are included. The main outcomes include short-term outcomes, oncological outcomes, and functional outcomes between the two groups.ResultsA total of 180 patients were included in the study. Sixty-one (33.9%) received local excision and 119 (66.1%) received total mesorectal excision. The baseline characteristics were generally balanced between the two groups. The local excision group demonstrated a significantly shorter operative time, less blood loss, and shorter hospital stay (p < 0.001). 3-year overall survival rates were 97.5% (95% CI, 0.93–1.00) and 95.5% (95% CI, 0.91–1.00) between the two groups (p = 0.38). The local excision group exhibited significantly higher 3-year local recurrence rates 15.7% (95% CI, 0.74–0.97) vs 4.2% (95% CI, 0.92–1.00) (p = 0.017), yet lower 3-year distant metastasis rates 9.6% (95% CI, 0.82–1.00) vs 12.6% (95% CI, 0.81–0.94) (p = 0.33) and lower 3-year disease-free survival rates 76.8% (95% CI, 0.64–0.92) vs 84.7% (95% CI, 0.78–0.92) (p = 0.56) comparing with the total mesorectal excision group. The local excision group demonstrated significantly better functional outcomes compared with the total mesorectal excision group (p < 0.001).ConclusionPatients who achieve either clinical complete or near-complete response after neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy are suitable candidates for local excision. The local excision group demonstrated superior short-term and functional outcomes, and the oncological outcomes were not compromised.

Introduction

Colorectal cancer is one of the most common digestive cancers in the world. Reportedly, in 2020, its incidence ranks third (10%), its mortality rate ranks second, and both of them show an increasing trend [1].

The current standard of care for locally advanced rectal cancers (LARC) is neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy (nCRT) followed by total mesorectal excision (TME) and adjuvant chemotherapy [2]. Although TME can improve the prognosis of patients, its mortality rate is 2–3% and it is closely associated with urinary, sexual, and bowel dysfunction [3–6]. Along with changes in the habits of defecation, about 50–90% of patients will also present low anterior resection syndrome (LARS) [7]. In addition, because the locations of most rectal cancers are low, patients usually need to receive temporary or permanent stomas. All of these issues seriously influence the quality of life of patients.

The purpose of neoadjuvant radiotherapy is to regress tumors, create surgical opportunities, and improve the prognosis of patients [8]. About 15–20% of patients can reach pathological complete response (pCR), and their long-term outcomes of survival are good [9]. However, its verification depends on the outcomes of the pathological examination. We can judge the extent of tumor regression through clinical examinations. Some patients can reach the status of clinical complete response (cCR), which means no evidence of tumor residues through clinical examinations [10]. Even though some patients do not reach cCR, they respond well to nCRT and tumor regress notably; eventually, these patients reach the status of clinical near-complete response (near-cCR). This underscores the potential for less invasive, rectum-preservation surgical interventions in patients showing substantial regression after nCRT, offering a path to avoid the inherent complications of TME. At present, organ preservation management includes local excision (LE) and watch and wait (WW) [11].

LE is a curative, rectum-preservation surgical intervention primarily utilized in the treatment of T1N0M0 rectal cancer that exhibits no pathological factors indicative of poor prognosis. nCRT can extend the range of its suitable candidates. LE is less invasive, leads to no genitourinary alterations, and minimally impacts sphincter function and patients’ quality of life [12]. During a local excision, the cancerous lesion and a small amount of surrounding healthy tissue, also known as a margin, are removed transanally. Given these advantages, LE may serve as a viable alternative to TME in treating rectal cancer patients who exhibit substantial regression following nCRT. However, its indication has not been determined, and its value and risk are in debate [13].

For near-cCR and cCR patients, the efficacy of tumor regression is satisfactory. They might be potential candidates for LE. This study aims to explore the oncological and functional outcomes of nCRT followed by LE for rectal cancer patients who are evaluated as near-cCR or cCR after nCRT. The insights gained from this research could pave the way for broader consideration and application of LE in managing rectal cancers following nCRT.

Materials and methods

Patient selection

This was a retrospective cohort study that analyzed prospectively collected data from the First Affiliated Hospital of Naval Medical University between January 2010 and December 2022. This study was approved by the institutional review board of the hospital. And this study adhered to the STROBE guideline.

The inclusion criteria mandated that all participants had (1) pathologically confirmed rectal cancer; (2) a history of long-course neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy; and (3) achieved near-cCR or cCR after nCRT as evaluation.

Exclusion criteria encompassed the following: (1) receipt of non-surgical management; (2) presence of synchronous distant metastasis; (3) recurrent rectal cancer; (4) more than 8 cm from the upper margin of the residual lesion to the anal verge; (5) incomplete clinical data or follow-up of less than 6 months.

Neoadjuvant treatment consisted of a total dose of 45–50.4 Gy, given in 25 fractions of 2 Gy or 28 fractions of 1.8 Gy over 5 weeks, respectively, combined with capecitabine, 825 mg/m2, twice daily. Patients could also receive capecitabine or CapeOx (capecitabine and oxaliplatin) induction or consolidation chemotherapy. Adverse events during nCRT were handled by outpatient clinicians.

Assessment of clinical response

Patients needed to receive an assessment of clinical response 4–16 weeks after the end of neoadjuvant radiotherapy. Assessment and treatment plans were made by a multi-disciplinary team (MDT) which consisted of several experienced experts who came from the department of anorectal surgery, medical imaging, radiotherapy, interventional therapy, digestive endoscopy, pathology, and hepatobiliary surgery. The assessment methods included (1) digital rectal examination (DRE); (2) colonoscopy; and (3) high-resolution rectal magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). Biopsy was not a necessary option. The standard of clinical response was laid down referring to multiple standards of previous studies [14, 15].

The criteria of cCR were (1) DRE, smooth rectum, no palpable mass; (2) colonoscopy, smooth mucosa, scar, no residual nodule, or ulcer; and (3) MRI, normal rectal wall, magnetic resonance-based tumor regression grade (mrTRG) = 1.

The criteria of near-cCR were (1) DRE, small mucosal irregularity, small nodule; (2) colonoscopy, small mucosal irregularity, small ulcer; and (3) MRI, significant regression of signal on T2WI with fibrosis (intermediate signal), mild diffusion limitation, and/or partial regression of lymph node, mrTRG = 1 or 2.

Patients who were not cCR or near-cCR were evaluated as a clinical incomplete response (iCR).

Personal treatment after assessment is decided mutually by clinicians and patients after weighing the pros and cons. Patients will be informed about the risks of recurrence and second-time surgery.

Treatment and follow-up

LE is performed by directly removing the tumor and some surrounding healthy or suspicious tissues transanally. In cases where post-LE pathological findings were unfavorable (ypT ≥ 2, lymphatic invasion, presence of vascular cancer emboli, positive margins), patients were recommended to undergo radical salvage surgery. TME involves the precise and meticulous dissection and removal of the entire rectum along with the mesorectum, which is the fatty tissue surrounding the rectum that contains blood vessels, lymphatic vessels, and lymph nodes.

Patients developing recurrences after LE during follow-up also received salvage surgery. Patients with distant metastases needed to receive interventional therapy, chemotherapy, or surgery.

Postoperative follow-ups were scheduled every 3 months for the initial 2 years, biannually for the subsequent 3 years, and annually thereafter. Follow-up items included carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) levels, carbohydrate antigen 19–9 (CA19-9) levels, DRE, MRI of the pelvis and rectum, and computed tomography (CT) scan of the thorax and abdomen.

Outcomes

The primary endpoints of this study were overall survival (OS), local recurrence rate (LR), and distant metastasis rate (DM). OS was defined as the time from diagnosis until the date of death from any reason. LR was defined as the recurrence of disease within the pelvic region, encompassing the rectal wall, anastomosis site, local lymph node, mesorectum, and perineal region. DM was defined as evidence of disease presence in any organ outside the pelvic region.

Secondary endpoints included disease-free survival (DFS) and organ preservation rate (OPR). OPR was defined as the percentage of patients who preserved their rectal organs after treatment. Short-term outcomes (operative time, blood loss, hospital stay, surgical complications, analgesic use) and efficacy of salvage surgery were also evaluated. Functional outcomes of patients were evaluated at the end of follow-up, using the LARS score [16].

Statistical analysis

Normally distributed quantitative variables were reported as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and analyzed with Students’s t-test. If quantitative variables were not normally distributed, they were reported as median (interquartile range), and analyzed with the Mann–Whitney U test. Results

Between 2010 and 2022, 975 rectal cancer patients who underwent nCRT were enrolled. Sixty-one patients in the LE group and 119 patients in the TME group were included in the study eventually after inclusion and exclusion. The median age was 59 ± 11 years in the LE group while 58 ± 11 years in the TME group. Tumors in the LE group had a lower distance to the anal verge than the TME group’s (3.0 cm vs 3.5 cm). The interval between colonoscopy/MRI and the end of neoadjuvant radiotherapy in the LE group was longer than that of the TME group. Short-term outcomes

All patients successfully completed surgeries in the two groups. All patients received transanal excision (TAE) in the LE group. In the TME group, 23 patients received abdominal perineal resection (APR), 66 patients received anterior resection (AR), 7 patients received intersphincteric resection (ISR), and 23 patients received pull-through resection.

The LE group had a significantly shorter median operating duration (50 vs 190 min, p < 0.001), less blood loss (30 vs 150 ml, p < 0.001), and shorter hospital stays (6 vs 10 days, p < 0.001).

According to the Clavien-Dindo classification, three and nine patients developed III/IV grade complications in the LE and TME group, respectively [17]. There was no statistical difference in the incidence of III/IV grade complications, 30-day mortality, and positive resection margin rate between the two groups (p = 0.58; p = 1.00; p = 0.11).

After surgery, pathological examination showed that 28 patients (45.9%) and 57 patients (47.9%) had ypT0 in the LE and TME group, respectively. Both groups had no 30-day mortality. There was also no statistical difference in the positive resection margin rate, pathological tumor regression grade (pTRG), and ypT stage between the LE group and TME group (p = 0.11; p = 0.94; p = 0.19). Oncological outcomes

For the LE group, the median follow-up time was 30 months. During follow-up, 11 recurrences or metastases occurred including 6 local regrowths, 1 pelvic recurrence, and 4 distant metastases. Only one patient with local recurrence had synchronous distant metastasis. Eight events occurred in the second year since the end of neoadjuvant radiotherapy.

For the TME group, the median follow-up time was 54 months. During follow-up, 23 recurrences or distant metastases occurred including 6 local recurrences and 17 distant metastases. Three patients synchronously had local recurrence and distant metastasis. The 3-year LR was 15.7% (95% CI, 0.74–0.97) and 4.2% (95% CI, 0.92–1.00) for the LE and TME group, respectively (p = 0.02). The 3-year DM was 9.6% (95% CI, 0.82–1.00) and 12.6% (95% CI, 0.81–0.94), respectively (p = 0.33).

At the end of follow-up, 2 and 16 patients died in the LE and TME group, respectively. One death was cancer-related and the other one was not in the LE group, while ten deaths were cancer-related and six deaths were not in the TME group. The 3-year OS was 97.5% (95% CI, 0.93–1.00) and 95.5% (95% CI, 0.91–1.00), respectively (p = 0.38). And the 3-year DFS was 76.8% (95% CI, 0.64–0.92) and 84.7% (95% CI, 0.78–0.92), respectively (p = 0.56).

We also noted patients with local recurrences had a higher risk of distant metastases than patients without local recurrences (16.7% vs 5.5%, p = 0.85).

We also did a subgroup analysis of cCR and near-cCR patients of the LE group. Until the end of follow-up, cCR and near-cCR patients had 1 death at the time point of 1481 and 638 days since the end of neoadjuvant radiotherapy, respectively. 3-year OS was 100% (95% CI, 1.00–1.00) and 96% (95% CI, 0.89–1.00) in the cCR and near-cCR group, respectively (p = 0.72). One and nine patients developed local recurrence or distant metastasis in the two groups until the end of follow-up, respectively. 3-year LR was 4% (95% CI, 0.89–1.00) and 22.7% (95% CI, 0.62–0.96) (p = 0.14). 3-year DM was 0 (95% CI, 1.00–1.00) and 15.7% (95% CI, 0.71–1.00) in the two groups, respectively (p = 0.09). 3-year DFS was 96% (95% CI, 0.89–1.00) and 65.8% (95% CI, 0.49–0.88) in the two groups, respectively (p = 0.11).

Subgroup analysis based on clinical response showed that cCR patients had 3-year LR of 4% (95% CI, 0.89–1.00) and 0 (95% CI, 1.00–1.00) (p = 0.26), 3-year DM of 0 (95% CI, 1.00–1.00) and 9.4% (95% CI, 0.81–1,00) (p = 0.11), 3-year OS of 100% (95% CI, 1.00–1.00) and 95.5% (95% CI, 0.87–1.00) (p = 0.94), and 3-year DFS of 96% (95% CI, 0.89–1.00) and 90.6% (95% CI, 0.81–1.00) (p = 0.81) in the LE and TME group, respectively. Near-cCR patients had 3-year LR of 22.7% (95% CI, 0.62–0.96) and 5.7% (95% CI, 0.89–1.00) (p = 0.02), 3-year DM of 15.7% (95% CI, 0.71–1.00) and 13.7% (95% CI, 0.79–0.95) (p = 0.94), 3-year OS of 96% (95% CI, 0.89–1.00) and 95.4% (95% CI, 0.91–1.00) (p = 0.32), and 3-year DFS of 65.8% (95% CI, 0.49–0.88) and 82.5% (95% CI, 0.74–0.92) (p = 0.29) in the LE and TME group, respectively. Functional outcomes

For the LE group, eight patients received APR, one received colostomy, and two died during the time of follow-up. Finally, a total of 50 patients completed questionnaires.

For the TME group, 23 patients received APR, 16 patients who received AR still had stomas, 10 patients died, and 9 patients lost to follow-up. Finally, a total of 61 patients completed questionnaires. LE group had better scores than the TME group in incontinence of flatus, incontinence of liquid stools, frequency of bowel movements, clustering of stools, and urgency. The proportion of mild or major LARS among patients in the LE group was significantly lower than that of the TME group (20.0% vs 65.5%, p < 0.001). Discussion

nCRT and LE can preserve the rectums of patients without compromising survivals. In this study, 85.2% of patients successfully preserved their organs after receiving LE. Short-term and functional outcomes were significantly better than the TME group’s.

As a less invasive surgical procedure, LE intrinsically offers superior short-term outcomes, including reduced anesthesia-related complications, shorter operative duration, decreased blood loss, and shorter hospital stays. Owing to these benefits, LE has become a standard surgical procedure for early-stage rectal cancer without pathological indicators of poor prognosis. Our study further reinforces these advantages of LE in comparison to that of TME. Additionally, as LE preserves the integrity of the rectum, patients undergoing LE are prone to maintain the normal defecation reflex, leading to an enhancement in their quality of life. In our study, the LARS scores were lower in the LE group compared with the TME group.

This study showed that the long-term outcomes of LE could be comparable to radical resections without significant differences, suggesting that LE did not compromise oncological outcomes relative to TME. DFS was 70–88% in the past studies [12, 18–20]. In this study, 3-year DFS was 76.8% for the LE group. This is not an ideal outcome, but it is still similar to outcomes of past studies. In this study, 3-year LR was 15.7% and 4.2% for the two groups (p = 0.02), and 3-year DM was 9.6% and 12.6%, respectively (p = 0.33). More recurrent events occurred in the LE group. This may be attributed to not only the inherent high risk of recurrence for LE, but also the different standards of inclusion for patients. ACOSOG Z6041 study only enrolled T0–2 patients; however, more T3–4 patients (44.2%) were enrolled in this study [21]. An Italian randomized controlled study showed that the efficacy of LE could be comparable to TME’s, LR was 8% and 6%, and DM was both 4% for the LE and TME group, respectively [20]. The result of GRECCAR 2 also showed similar outcomes. Bushati et al. [22] enrolled 51 LE patients who responded well to nCRT (cCR or major response) and enrolled 51 TME patients after propensity score matching. They found that the 5-year local recurrence-free survival (LRFS) was 91.8% and 97.6% for the two groups (p = 0.17), 5-year DFS was 86.7% and 86.4% (p = 0.14), and 5-year OS was 85% and 90% (p = 0.64), respectively. Eventually, about 80% of patients successfully preserve their organs. Generally, this study had similar outcomes to the above trials.

The application for LE is still not clear until now, and the standard of inclusion is mostly inconsistent. This study enrolled near-cCR or cCR patients as research objects. Past research showed cCR was usually inconsistent with pCR, and approximately 5–46% of cCR patients still had residual tumors [23, 24]. Among 26 cCR patients in this study, 19 patients (73.1%) were ypT0. Undeniably, residual tumors might realize complete regression, but it is unknown whether the risk of distant metastasis and tumor progression will increase or not [25]. Therefore, pathological examination after LE will provide clearer guidance for the next-step treatment. However, surgery itself can cause adverse effects to some extent, and pathological outcomes may not be accurate because of insufficient sampling depth or tumor scatter [26]. In addition, 9 of 35 near-cCR (25.7%) patients in the LE group were ypT0. This study adopted strict clinical evaluation standards as far as possible, but clinical detection methods still have their limitations. Intrinsic inconsistency between cCR and examination results is one of the reasons besides of inaccuracy of subjective evaluation. Achilli et al. [27] reported that the diagnostic accuracy of mrTRG for pTRG was 78%. Also, van der Sande et al. [28] found that endoscopic features of 7–30% of cCR patients were ulcers or nodules, and these features did not accord with the evaluation standard of cCR. How to minimize subjective errors and modify technology to improve diagnostic accuracy is one of the focuses, right now. LE has the diagnostic value for near-cCR or cCR.

Because LE do not include lymphadenectomy, the risk of lymph node metastasis is its unavoidable shortage. Park et al. [29] reported that 9.1% ypT0, 17.1% ypT1, and 20.8% ypT2 patients had lymph node metastases, respectively. Among ten patients who had local recurrence or distant metastasis in this study, eight events (80%) occurred in the first 2 years after neoadjuvant radiotherapy. Besides, seven events occurred in the second year after neoadjuvant radiotherapy. Eight pathological examinations after salvage surgery showed only two patients had lymph node metastases. This may be attributed to subsequent local recurrence or progression of the tumor because no short-term local recurrence or distant metastasis occurred since the end of local excision. In the same way, only 7 (5.9%) of 119 TME patients had lymph node metastases. It seems to suggest that the risk of lymph node metastasis is low for near-cCR or cCR patients. So LE may be relatively safe under this condition.

In this study, only one patient (12.5%) in the LE group who received salvage surgery had a suspiciously positive margin. Although the sampling size of this study was small, the problem of the positive margin of salvage surgery must be a concern [30, 31]. Of 5 ypT3 and 16 ypT2 patients, 40% and 25% of patients had local recurrence or distant metastasis, respectively. So it suggested that salvage surgery is applicable for ypT2 patients. But many patients will reject surgery due to their strong anus preservation wishes. In addition, this study also showed that patients with local recurrence had a higher risk of metastasis than patients without local recurrence (16.7% vs 5.5%, p = 0.85). This question must be paid enough attention.

In this study, approximately 10–40% of patients who received LE experienced different extents of functional problems, but only 5–13.7% of patients had severe problems. A recently published TREC study showed that 14–35% of patients had problems like frequency and incontinence at the follow-up time of 36 months. The characteristic of the TREC study is that it enrolled many old patients. In the CARTS study, 28% and 50% of patients had mild and severe LARS, respectively [32]. All patients in this study underwent TAE, and the average age of patients was 59 ± 11 years. Eventually, the functional outcomes of patients after surgery were generally good. This study suggested that LE may be beneficial the most for old patients.

Subgroup analysis showed that in the LE group, the survivals of cCR patients were better than near-cCR patients’. And it was comparable to TME’s, especially for cCR patients. This suggested that LE may have a bigger value for cCR patients after nCRT. The risks of local recurrence and distant metastasis for them were lower.

There were several limitations of this study. Firstly, this was a single-center, retrospective study with inherent limitations like selection bias and data reliability. Secondly, since the introduction of LE was recent, the follow-up period for the LE group was shorter compared with that of the TME group. This difference in the follow-up time could potentially influence the comparative analysis between the two groups. Thirdly, the study lacked a comprehensive assessment of functional outcomes at different time points and did not evaluate the quality of life of patients. Considering that a significant proportion of rectal cancer patients may achieve cCR or near-cCR after nCRT, randomized clinical trials should be conducted to further investigate the effectiveness and long-term outcomes of LE compared with traditional TME.

Conclusion

cCR or near-cCR patients are applicable candidates for LE. LE’s short-term outcomes are significantly better than TME’s. Its long-term outcomes can be comparable to TME’s. Patients can have good anal functions after LE. A large proportion of patients can preserve their organs through LE. These findings should promote the consideration of less invasive treatments in the management of rectal cancer patients following nCRT.

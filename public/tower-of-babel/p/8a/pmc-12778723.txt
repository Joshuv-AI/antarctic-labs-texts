Post‐Market Registry of Stroke Patients Treated With Medtronic Neurothrombectomy Devices in Southeast Asia: PROSPR‐SEA
Authors: Huy Thang Nguyen, Mai Duy Ton, Dang Luu Vu, Anchalee Churojana, Yongchai Nilanont, Sirintara (Pongpech) Singhara Na Ayudya, Deidre Anne De Silva, Winston Lim Eng Hoe, Truong Le Van, Minh Thong Pham, Thanaboon Worakijthamrongchai, Quoc Tuan Tran, Raymond Chee Seong Seet, Wickly Lee
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSoutheast Asia accounts for approximately 10% of stroke‐related mortalities worldwide, yet there are limited data regarding mechanical thrombectomy (MT) outcomes in this region. PROSPR‐SEA (Post‐Market Registry of Stroke Patients Treated With Medtronic Neurothrombectomy Devices to Collect Real‐World Data in Southeast Asia) assessed post‐market clinical outcomes of MT using the Solitaire Revascularization Device in southeast Asia.MethodsPROSPR‐SEA is a prospective, multicenter, nonrandomized, observational registry of patients who underwent MT using Solitaire as first line treatment at 1 of 10 centers in Vietnam, Thailand, and Singapore between January 2018 and July 2019. The primary end point was functional independence (modified Rankin scale 0–2) at 90 days. Safety end points included symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage, emboli in new territory at 24 hours, and all‐cause mortality at 90 days. Secondary end points were successful revascularization (modified thrombolysis in cerebral infarction ≥2b), workflow metrics, National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score at discharge, and patient disposition at 90 days.ResultsA total of 183 patients (64.9±13.7, 49.7% [91/183] female) were included in the study and underwent MT; 79.2% (145/183) had occlusions in the middle cerebral artery. Median baseline National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale was 15.0 (range 1–31) and median baseline Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score was 8.0 (range 0–10). Functional independence at 90 days was achieved in 62.2% (112/180). Mean puncture‐to‐revascularization time was 53.3±53.9 minutes, and mean onset‐to‐revascularization time was 330.0±110.4 minutes. Revascularization was successful in 94.0% (172/183) of patients. Median National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale improved at discharge (−8.0, range −22, 25) and 90 days (−13.0, range −22, 0). Within 24 hours, symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage occurred in 7.7% (14/183) and emboli in new territory in 3.3% (6/183). At 90 days, the all‐cause mortality rate was 7.7% (14/183). Most patients were discharged to home by 90 days (84.1%, 154/183).ConclusionPROSPR‐SEA demonstrated high rates of positive functional outcomes and procedural success with a low mortality rate and indicates that the technical and clinical outcomes of landmark MT trials using Solitaire as the first‐line device are reproducible in real‐world southeast Asian settings.

Stroke is a leading cause of mortality with 5.5 million deaths reported worldwide in 2016, of which about 10% (504 000) occurred in southeast Asia. 1 The Global Burden of Diseases, Injuries, and Risk Factors Study reported over 800 000 incidences of stroke in southeast Asia in 2016 with over 11 million disability‐adjusted life‐years, indicating a substantial burden of stroke in this region. 1 The volume of mechanical thrombectomy (MT) procedures in southeast Asia has historically lagged behind the North American and European health care systems. However, as stent retrievers enter the southeast Asian market, the number of thrombectomy procedures is increasing. 2 , 3 , 4

The HERMES (Highly Effective Reperfusion Evaluated in Multiple Endovascular Stroke Trials) collaboration pooled patient‐level data from 5 seminal randomized controlled trials demonstrating clinical efficacy of MT in patients with acute ischemic stroke (AIS) due to large vessel occlusion (LVO) compared with standard care, including intravenous thrombolysis. 5 , 6 , 7 , 8 , 9 , 10 MT resulted in low stroke‐related mortalities and improved functional outcome in patients with AIS in these trials, 5 yet only 3 subjects in these randomized controlled trials were from southeast Asian countries, despite a clearly significant stroke burden in Asia. Data on outcomes of MT are lacking in southeast Asia, warranting the need to establish robust clinical evidence for MT in this region.

The Solitaire Revascularization Device (Solitaire, Medtronic Inc, Irvine, CA, USA) is a self‐expanding stent‐retriever that received approval by the US Food and Drug Administration in 2012 and has since been released in Asian markets. 11 , 12 The device has previously shown safety and efficacy in a variety of studies for revascularization in AIS due to LVO. 5 , 7 , 9 , 13 With premarket studies indicating the safety of Solitaire, additional postmarket research is now necessary to assess real‐world outcomes of its clinical application in understudied areas. Therefore, the purpose of this study is to assess postmarket clinical outcomes associated with the use of Solitaire in southeast Asia.

Methods

Study Design and Patient Population

The data that support the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request. The PROSPR‐SEA (Post‐Market Registry of Stroke Patients Treated With Solitaire Neurothrombectomy Devices to Collect Real‐World Data in Southeast Asia) was a prospective, multicenter, nonrandomized, observational registry of patients from Vietnam, Thailand, and Singapore who underwent MT with Solitaire (Figure). This study was carried out in 10 centers between January 2018 and July 2019. Institutional review boards or ethics committees at each participating center approved the study and the use of patient data. All patients provided written informed consent.

Registry enrollment flow chart.

Inclusion criteria were AIS due to LVO in the internal carotid artery, M1, and M2 segments of the middle cerebral artery, basilar, or vertebral arteries; treatment with a Medtronic Solitaire Revascularization Device as the initial device; willingness to participate in 90‐day follow‐up visit; and treatment within 8 hours of stroke symptom onset (defined as stroke onset to puncture). The 8‐hour time window from stroke symptom onset was chosen to allow comparability with other studies, such as Systematic Evaluation of Patients Treated With Neurothrombectomy Devices for Acute Ischemic Stroke (STRATIS). 14 Patients were excluded if they did not meet the time window criteria or were concurrently enrolled in another MT device trial or any other clinical trial.

Baseline characteristics of patients were collected, including demographics, comorbidities, history of stroke and/or related neurological history, intravenous tissue plasminogen activator (tPA) administration, site of occlusion, National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS), and Alberta Stroke Program Early Computed Tomography (CT) Score (ASPECTS) as determined by CT or magnetic resonance imaging.

Intervention and Follow‐Up

Patients were treated with the Solitaire Revascularization Device in accordance with the standard of care of each center. After patient inclusion, lesion characteristics were determined, and treatment was initiated within 8 hours of symptom onset. Although the standard of care used was a classic stent‐retriever thrombectomy, the use of adjunctive or rescue therapies was allowed by the study protocol per the interventionalists discretion. Procedural characteristics and workflow metrics (e.g., procedural time, Solitaire model, type of anesthesia) were recorded. Patients were followed up at discharge or ≤7 days after the index procedure, and at 90±15 days after the procedure. Follow‐up assessments included clinical and neurological evaluation (modified Rankin scale [mRS] and NIHSS).

Outcome Measures

The primary effectiveness end point was functional independence (mRS 0–2) at 90 days. Primary safety end points were symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage (sICH), emboli in new territory (ENT) at 24 hours post procedure, and all‐cause mortality at 90 days. sICH was defined as any parenchymal hematoma, subarachnoid hemorrhage, or intraventricular hemorrhage associated with worsening of the NIHSS by ≥4 points within 24 hours as seen on CT or magnetic resonance imaging.

Secondary end points included successful revascularization, defined as modified thrombolysis in cerebral infarction (mTICI) ≥2b, workflow metrics, and NIHSS at hospital discharge, post‐acute discharge disposition, and patient disposition at study exit. Angiographic and safety outcomes are reported as assessed by the study investigators.

Statistical Analysis

Demographic, baseline, and procedural characteristics were summarized and reported as mean±SD, median, and minimum and maximum values for continuous variables. Categorical data were summarized using frequencies and percentages. Statistical analysis was performed using SAS (version 9.2, SAS Institute Inc. Cary, NC). Comparison of clinical outcomes of PROSPR‐SEA versus STRATIS or SEER were assessed using a chi‐square test, with the significance level set at P<0.05, using Prism software (GraphPad Software Inc., version 9) (Table 5).

Results

Patient Baseline Characteristics

A total of 183 patients (64.9±13.7 years; 49.7% [91/183] female) were included in the study. The median number of patients enrolled per center was 15 (range, 2–62). Baseline characteristics are presented in Table 1. The most commonly reported comorbidities were hypertension (67.2% [123/183]), smoking (31.1% [57/183]), and atrial fibrillation (27.3% [50/183]). History of ischemic stroke, hemorrhagic stroke, transient ischemic attack, and intracranial atherosclerosis disease (ICAD) were reported in 10.9% (20/183), 1.6% (3/183), 1.1% (2/183), and 2.2% (4/183) of patients, respectively. A majority of patients had occlusion in the middle cerebral artery‐M1 segment 113/183 (61.7%), followed by middle cerebral artery‐M2 (32/183 [17.5%]), internal carotid artery (28/183 [15.3%]), basilar artery (9/183 [4.9%]), and vertebral artery (1/183 [0.5%]). At baseline, median NIHSS was 15.0 (range 1–31). The imaging modality used to determine ASPECTS was CT in 98.1% (156/159) of patients and magnetic resonance in 1.9% (3/159) patients. Median ASPECTS was 8.0 (range 0–10). ASPECTS 0–5 was reported in 6.9% (11/159) of patients, ASPECTS 6–7 in 27.0% (43/159), and ASPECTS 8–10 in 66.0% (105/159). The majority (89.1% [163/183]) of patients had prestroke mRS 0–2, 2.7% (5/183) of patients had prestroke mRS 3–5, and 8.2% (15/183) of patients did not have a recorded prestroke‐mRS score. Intravenous tPA was delivered in 57.4% (105/183) of patients.

Secondary End Points

Secondary end points are presented in Table 3. After final pass with the Solitaire Device, 84.7% (155/183) had mTICI≥2b; post procedure, the percentage of patients with mTICI≥2b increased to 94.0% (172/183) (Table 3). Mean NIHSS was 10.1±7.3 at 24 hours, 7.9±7.6 at discharge, and 4.4±4.4 at 90 days. Median NIHSS score improved between baseline and 24 hours (−5.0 [range −22, 15]), between baseline and discharge (−8.0 [range −22, 25]), and between baseline and 90 days (−13.0 [range −22, 0]) (Table 3). Most patients returned home at discharge (72.7% [133/183]) and were at home at study exit at 90 days (83.1% [152/183]) (Table 4). An additional 6.6% (12/183) of patients were discharged to a rehabilitation facility, 6.6% (12/183) an acute care hospital, and 6.0% (11/183) to assisted living. At 90 days, 2.2% (4/183) of patients were at a rehabilitation facility, 1.6% (3/183) in an acute care hospital, 2.2% (4/183) in a long‐term care hospital, and 0.5% (1/183) in hospice (Table 4).

Discussion

This registry represents the largest study of patients with AIS in a real‐world setting. MT using the Solitaire Revascularization Device resulted in high rates of patient functional independence at 90 days post procedure, high rates of successful revascularization, and low mortality.

PROSPR‐SEA demonstrates comparable outcomes with that of major MT randomized trials performed in mainly Western regions. STRATIS was one of the largest prospective registry studies of patients (N=1000) treated with a Solitaire Device within 8 hours of stroke symptom onset in the United States. 14 STRATIS reported significantly lower rates of mTICI≥2b revascularization success in 87.9% (724/824) of patients (P=0.017) compared with PROSPR‐SEA (Table 5). The SEER (SWIFT PRIME [Solitaire With the Intention for Thrombectomy as Primary Endovascular Treatment], 9 ESCAPE [Endovascular Treatment for Small Core and Anterior Circulation Proximal Occlusion With Emphasis on Minimizing CT to Recanalization Times], 8 EXTEND‐IA [Extending the Time for Thrombolysis in Emergency Neurological Deficits‐Intra‐Arterial], 7 and REVASCAT [Endovascular Revascularization With Solitaire Device Versus Best Medical Therapy in Anterior Circulation Stroke Within 8 Hours]) 9 collaboration performed a patient‐level meta‐analysis using the 4 aforementioned randomized controlled trials to assess the Solitaire Device for treatment of AIS. 13 SEER reported significantly lower rates of mTICI≥2b revascularization in 76.6% (285/372) of patients (P<0.0001) (Table 5). Similar to PROSPR‐SEA, other MT clinical studies have also reported high rates of mTICI≥2b revascularization; rates from TREVO Registry were 92.8% (1106/1192); 15 and preliminary results from SWIFT DIRECT show relatively high rates of mTICI≥2b revascularization in the cohort of patients treated with tPA and MT (96.6% [200/207]) and for those treated with MT alone (90.5% [182/201]). 16

With respect to clinical outcomes, despite the inclusion of 5 patients with prestroke mRS 3–5, PROSPR‐SEA had higher rates of mRS 0–2 at 90 days compared with both STRATIS (56.5% [512/906]) 14 and SEER (54.0% [216/400]), although differences were not significant (Table 5). 13 mRS scores at 90 days compared with SEER and STRATIS are presented in Figure S1 (please see https://www.ahajournals.org/journal/str). 13 sICH and ENT rates were significantly higher in PROSPR‐SEA at 24 hours post procedure compared with STRATIS (1.4% [12/841]; P<0.0001 and 0.8% [7/824]; P=0.008, respectively); and compared with SEER (2.5% [10/401]; P=0.004) (Table 5). Compared to PROSPR‐SEA, mortality at 90 days was significantly higher in STRATIS (14.4% [142/984]; P=0.016) and higher, albeit not significantly, in SEER (12.0% [48/401]) (Table 5). These clinical data are promising given that the cohort of patients in PROSPR‐SEA with favorable ASPECTS (8–10) was lower compared with both STRATIS (77.1% [588/763]) and SEER (72.7% [282/388]), and poor ASPECTS are associated with poor clinical outcomes and increased rates of sICH. 17 , 18 The higher rate of sICH and ENT in PROSPR‐SEA compared with SEER and STRATIS may be related to aspects of the study design, population, comorbidities, and differences in treatment regimens.

The mean time of stroke onset to the end of procedure was greater in PROSPR‐SEA than in SEER (293.6±126.5 [N=349]; median 274.0 [range 196.0, 365.0]) and STRATIS (271.1±105.7 [N=945]; median 255.0 [range 188.0, 342.0]). 13 , 14 Increased time from puncture to revascularization is associated with both sICH at 24 hours (odds ratio [OR], 1.01 [95% CI, 1.00–1.02]) and clinical outcome (multivariate analysis OR, 2.97 [95% CI, 1.00–8.83]) based on published univariate and multivariate analyses, which could have contributed to the results observed in PROSPR‐SEA. 19 Additionally, fewer patients in PROSPR‐SEA received intravenous tPA compared with SEER (80.5% [323/401]) and STRATIS (64.0% [628/982]), which may have influenced outcomes; although rates of intravenous tPA administration in the TREVO registry (52.3% [1041/1990]) were similar to PROSPR‐SEA, the TREVO registry reported lower rates of sICH (1.7% [34/2008]) and ENT (2.3% [46/1997]).

Discrepancies in workflow metrics may reflect regional differences in accessibility to acute stroke care, health care infrastructure, and systems of stroke care between western countries and southeast Asian countries. 20 PROSPR‐SEA included patients with ICAD. The occurrence of strokes due to ICAD is more frequent in east Asia compared with European and North American demographics. 21 , 22 , 23 , 24 LVO secondary to ICAD can increase puncture‐to‐revascularization time during MT, negatively affecting clinical outcomes and procedural complications. 25 Additionally, sICH is more common in Asian populations and is believed to be associated with susceptibility to intracranial vasculopathy. 18 , 26 , 27 , 28 Despite these differences, overall clinical and angiographic outcomes of PROSPR‐SEA were comparable to those reported in the SEER and STRATIS studies, demonstrating the effectiveness of Solitaire MT for the treatment of AIS in the southeast Asian population in a real‐world setting.

The PROSPR‐SEA results are also comparable or slightly superior to other recent studies in the southeast or east Asian setting. For example, REDIRECT (RECO Flow Restoration Device Versus Solitaire FR With the Intention for Thrombectomy Study) was a multicenter, prospective, randomized controlled trial in China in which 69 patients with AIS due to LVO were enrolled in the Solitaire MT group. 29 The study reported sICH at 24 hours in 7.2% of patients, 23% mortality, 46% mRS 0–2, and successful revascularization (mTICI 2b/3) at 75%, 29 all of which are either in line with or inferior to PROSPR‐SEA outcomes. In a retrospective single‐center analysis of 41 patients with AIS treated with endovascular therapy in Thailand, sICH occurred in 12.2% (5/41) and mortality in 29.3% (12/41) at 30 days, with successful revascularization (thrombolysis in myocardial infarction ≥2) in 97.3% (36/37) and mRS 0–2 in 34.15% (14/41) at 16 months. 30 Considering differences with respect to acute stroke care accessibility and health care infrastructure between southeast Asian countries and elsewhere, 20 PROSPR‐SEA reflects positive results of Solitaire for effective and safe MT with a high rate of functional outcome.

Limitations

PROSPR‐SEA was a single‐arm, prospective, postmarket registry and did not include a control group. As with any real‐world registry, we cannot exclude the possibility of selection bias. In contrast to patients enrolled in randomized clinical trials according to strict inclusion and exclusion criteria, patients included in a registry, such as with this study, were included and excluded per the site treatment team's assessment. Study investigators self‐adjudicated the angiographic data and clinical adverse event outcomes, while procedural data, such as adjunctive or rescue therapy use and number of passes, are not available from the sites; therefore, conclusions made from this study regarding safety and efficacy are limited to the use of Solitaire as a first‐line device. The proportion of patients with ICAD in the present study was comparatively low with respect to the general southeast Asian population, which may suggest an underrepresentation of this population. The percentage of indeterminate preprocedure mTICI was approximately 10%, which may skew the clinical outcomes. Data from anterior and posterior circulation strokes are combined, which may skew the results in terms of clinical outcomes. The PROSPR‐SEA sample size was small compared with SEER and STRATIS; however, it represents the largest registry of patients with AIS from southeast Asian countries.

Conclusion

The findings from PROSPR‐SEA, the largest study of patients with AIS from southeast Asia, provide real‐world evidence supporting the safety and effectiveness of the Solitaire Neurothrombectomy Device as a first‐line treatment device for embolic LVO AIS. Metrics in the study showed high rates of technical and clinical outcomes and a low rate of mortalities, comparable to those reported by the major MT trials using the same device in a real‐world setting.

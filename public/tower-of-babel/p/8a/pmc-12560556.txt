Analysis of the efficacy and influencing factors of double-dose furmonertinib for advanced EGFR ex20ins non-small cell lung cancer
Authors: Kexin Zhang, Yi Ge, Yishi Xu, Chuhan Zhang, Liping Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundEGFR exon 20 insertion mutations (ex20ins) represent the third most common epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) mutation subtype in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Conventional EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) and chemotherapy demonstrate suboptimal efficacy in patients harboring EGFR ex20ins mutations. Furmonertinib, a highly selective EGFR-TKI with enhanced blood–brain barrier penetration, presents a potential therapeutic breakthrough. This study aims to evaluate the efficacy of double-dose furmonertinib (160 mg) in advanced EGFR ex20ins-mutant NSCLC patients and explore associations between predictive biomarkers and clinical outcomes.MethodsA retrospective analysis was conducted on 98 patients with locally advanced or metastatic EGFR ex20ins-mutant NSCLC. The observation group received double-dose furmonertinib (160 mg daily), while the control group received standard platinum-based doublet chemotherapy.ResultsThe observation group demonstrated significantly superior ORR (47.4 vs. 23.3%) and median PFS (8.7 vs 5.4 months) compared to the control group (P < 0.05).Within the observation group, pretreated patients exhibited reduced efficacy versus treatment-naïve patients (ORR 30 vs 66.7%; median PFS: 6.1 vs. 10.2 months; P < 0.05).The observation group reported lower overall adverse event (AE) incidence (60.5 vs. 71.7%) and reduced grade ≥ 3 AEs (21.1 vs. 28.3%) compared to the control group. Multivariate logistic regression identified peripheral blood T-lymphocyte subsets (CD4+, CD8+, CD4+/CD8+ ratio) as independent predictors of short-term efficacy (P = 0.018; P = 0.014; P = 0.024). Cox analysis revealed platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR) > 200.7 and systemic immune-inflammation index (SII) > 1107.7 (P = 0.037; P = 0.015) as independent risk factors for disease progression.ConclusionsDouble-dose furmonertinib demonstrates significant efficacy and favorable tolerability in advanced EGFR ex20ins-mutant NSCLC, particularly in treatment-naïve patients. Pretreatment peripheral T-lymphocyte profiles and systemic inflammatory indices may serve as predictive biomarkers for therapeutic response and prognosis.

Introduction

EGFR exon 20 insertion mutations (ex20ins) represent a rare yet highly aggressive molecular subtype in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), accounting for 4%–10% of all EGFR mutations and ranking as the third most common EGFR mutation subtype identified in NSCLC [1]. Unlike classical EGFR-sensitive mutations, EGFR ex20ins induces conformational changes in the α-C helix and phosphate-binding loop through amino acid residue insertions, leading to increased steric hindrance within the adenosine triphosphate (ATP)-binding pocket [2]. This structural alteration significantly diminishes the binding efficacy of conventional EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs). Clinical studies demonstrate that NSCLC patients harboring ex20ins mutations exhibit poor responses to first- to third-generation EGFR-TKIs (e.g., gefitinib, osimertinib), with objective response rates (ORR) below 10% and median progression-free survival (PFS) of only 2–3 months, substantially inferior to outcomes observed in classical EGFR-mutant cohorts [3]. Platinum-based doublet chemotherapy remains the standard of care for EGFR ex20ins-mutant NSCLC, however, its efficacy is limited (ORR < 30%, median PFS 4.5–5.7 months), and treatment-related toxicities significantly compromise patient quality of life [4]. Consequently, developing novel therapeutic strategies that balance efficacy and safety remains a critical unmet need in EGFR ex20ins-mutant NSCLC management.

The third-generation EGFR-TKI furmonertinib demonstrates distinct advantages due to its optimized molecular design. Its irreversible binding mechanism selectively targets EGFR-sensitive mutations and T790M resistance mutations, while structural modifications of the hydrophobic trifluoroethoxypyridine moiety enhance binding affinity to ex20ins mutants [5]. HAN et al. [6] first reported that furmonertinib 240 mg achieved an ORR of 78.6% and median duration of response (DoR) of 15.2 months in treatment-naïve ex20ins-mutant NSCLC. In second-line settings, dose-dependent efficacy differences were observed between 160 and 240 mg regimens (ORR: 38.5% vs. 46.2%), highlighting its therapeutic potential. Real-world evidence further supports furmonertinib’s cross-line utility, with an ORR of 36.8% maintained in heavily pretreated patients [7]. Notably, significant efficacy heterogeneity exists between treatment-naïve and pretreated populations, as well as among individual patients, underscoring the urgent need to identify prognostic biomarkers for double-dose furmonertinib (160 mg) and refine patient stratification for targeted therapy.

Peripheral blood T-lymphocyte subsets (e.g., CD4+, CD8+, CD4+/CD8+ ratio), as key indicators of systemic antitumor immunity, have demonstrated predictive and prognostic value across multiple solid tumors [8]. However, their association with double-dose furmonertinib efficacy in EGFR ex20ins-mutant NSCLC remains unexplored. Furthermore, peripheral blood-derived inflammatory indices—including neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR), lymphocyte-to-monocyte ratio (LMR), and systemic immune-inflammation index (SII)—have emerged as cost-effective, accessible tools for predicting clinical outcomes in various malignancies. Dynamic monitoring of these indices (e.g., post-treatment reductions in NLR/SII) may also predict pathological response rates and survival benefits in immunotherapy [9]. Despite their utility, the predictive role of these hematological inflammatory markers in double-dose furmonertinib-treated EGFR ex20ins-mutant NSCLC remains undefined.

Building on this evidence, this study aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of double-dose furmonertinib (160 mg) in advanced EGFR ex20ins-mutant NSCLC and investigate the predictive utility of peripheral T-lymphocyte subsets and systemic inflammatory indices. By elucidating the mechanistic interplay between immune-inflammatory dynamics and targeted therapy, this research seeks to advance personalized treatment strategies and establish novel biomarker-driven clinical paradigms.

Materials and methods

Study design and patients

This study retrospectively collected clinical data from 98 patients with locally advanced or metastatic (stage IIIB-IV) EGFR exon 20 insertion (ex20ins) mutant non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) who were treated at The First Affiliated Hospital of Zhengzhou University between September 2021 and September 2024. The research was conducted in accordance with the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki and received ethical approval from the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of The First Affiliated Hospital of Zhengzhou University. Due to the retrospective nature of the study, a waiver of informed consent was granted. All enrolled patients were required to have an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0–2 and at least one measurable lesion as assessed by Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.1. Patients were excluded if they had received prior targeted therapy for ex20ins or harbored concurrent actionable driver gene alterations (e.g., ALK, ROS1, or RET fusions). This study is a retrospective, non-randomized controlled investigation. In this study, patients in the observation group (n = 38) received furmonertinib at a dose of 160 mg, once daily, until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. The control cohort (n = 60) comprised patients who met the identical eligibility criteria and were treated with platinum-based doublet chemotherapy (cisplatin 75 mg/m2 or carboplatin AUC = 5, combined with either pemetrexed 500 mg/m2 or gemcitabine 1000 mg/m2) regimens at the same institution and during the same time period. The observation group was further stratified into treatment-naïve subgroup (no prior systemic therapy, n = 18) and pretreated subgroup (progression after ≥ 1 line of chemotherapy, n = 20).

Procedures

All patients tested positive for EGFR exon 20 insertion (ex20ins) mutations, confirmed by next-generation sequencing (NGS). The proportions of CD3+, CD4+, and CD8+ T lymphocytes, along with the CD4+/CD8+ ratio, were assessed using flow cytometry (BD FACSCanto II system).The following hematological indices were calculated based on baseline complete blood count data:① Neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) = absolute neutrophil count / absolute lymphocyte count;② Platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR) = platelet count / absolute lymphocyte count;③ Lymphocyte-to-monocyte ratio (LMR) = absolute lymphocyte count / absolute monocyte count;④ Systemic immune-inflammation index (SII) = [platelet count (*10⁹/L) × neutrophil count (*10⁹/L)] / lymphocyte count (*10⁹/L). During treatment, patients underwent comprehensive imaging assessments (including CT, bone scintigraphy, cranial MRI, and color Doppler ultrasound) every two cycles. Tumor response was evaluated per Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.1 until disease progression or study completion. Treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs)—encompassing both any treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) and immune-related adverse events—were graded according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0.

Outcomes

Efficacy endpoints included the objective response rate (ORR), defined as the proportion of patients achieving a complete response (CR) or partial response (PR); and the disease control rate (DCR), defined as the proportion of patients achieving CR, PR, or stable disease (SD).The duration of response (DoR) was defined as the time from randomization to disease progression or death from any cause among patients who achieved a CR or PR. Depth of Response (DpR) was defined as the maximum percentage reduction in tumor size from baseline observed throughout the entire course of treatment. Progression-free survival (PFS), defined as the time interval from treatment initiation to disease progression or death from any cause, was also recorded. The primary endpoint of this study was progression-free survival (PFS). Secondary endpoints included the objective response rate (ORR) and disease control rate (DCR).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software (version 26.0) for data processing and GraphPad Prism (version 8.0) for data visualization. Continuous variables were compared between groups using the Mann–Whitney U test. Differences in categorical variables were assessed using either the chi-square (χ2) test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate based on sample size. Patient survival was evaluated using the Kaplan–Meier method, and differences between groups were compared using the log-rank test. The association between T-lymphocyte subset levels and short-term treatment efficacy was analyzed using logistic regression. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed to evaluate the predictive value of T-lymphocyte subset levels for short-term efficacy. Univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression models were employed to analyze potential factors influencing disease progression and to identify independent prognostic factors for PFS. Statistical significance was defined as a two-sided P-value < 0.05 (α = 0.05). Optimal cutoff values for T-lymphocyte subsets were determined using X-tile software (version 3.6.1), applying either the maximal selected rank statistic method or ROC curve analysis based on their predictive value for short-term efficacy.

Progression free survival

A total of 98 patients were enrolled in this study, and all completed efficacy evaluations. Survival analysis based on RECIST version 1.1 demonstrated that patients in the observation group achieved a significantly longer median PFS compared to the control group. Specifically, the median PFS was 8.7 months (95% CI 6.6–10.8) in the observation group versus 5.4 months (95% CI 4.3–6.5) in the control group. This difference was statistically significant (HR = 0.56, 95% CI 0.32–0.7; P = 0.0018). Further in-depth analysis revealed a particularly pronounced efficacy advantage in the treatment-naïve subgroup. The median PFS in this subgroup reached 10.2 months (95% CI 7.9–12.5), significantly surpassing the 5.4 months (95% CI 4.3–6.5) observed in the control group. This inter-group difference demonstrated high statistical significance (HR = 0.38, 95% CI 0.23–0.64; P < 0.001). Within the observation group, subgroup comparison showed that the treatment-naïve subgroup also had a significantly longer median PFS (10.2 months; 95% CI 7.9–12.5) than the pretreated subgroup (6.1 months; 95% CI 5.1–7.1). DpR

Tumor assessments per RECIST version 1.1 revealed encouraging antitumor activity in the observation group, with a significant reduction in target lesions from baseline observed in 84.2% of patients (Fig. 2). The safety analysis revealed comparable overall incidences of treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) between the observation and control groups (Table 3). This finding suggests improved gastrointestinal tolerability with the observation regimen. No significant differences were observed in other TRAE categories (P > 0.05). For grade ≥ 3 severe adverse events (SAEs), the overall incidence similarly showed no intergroup disparity (observation: 21.1% [8/38] vs. control: 28.3% [17/60]; P > 0.05). Predictive value of peripheral blood T-Cell subsets for short-term treatment response

This study evaluated 38 patients with advanced EGFR exon 20 insertion (ex20ins) mutant NSCLC receiving targeted therapy, categorizing 12 partial response (PR) patients into the response group and 26 with stable disease (SD) or progressive disease (PD) into the non-response group after two treatment cycles. Baseline characteristics were balanced (P > 0.05). Critical pretreatment immune divergence emerged: the response group demonstrated significantly higher baseline peripheral blood proportions of CD3⁺ T-cells (P < 0.05), CD4⁺ T-cells (P < 0.05), and CD4⁺/CD8⁺ ratios (P < 0.05), alongside lower CD8⁺ T-cell proportions (P < 0.05) versus the non-response group, whereas systemic inflammatory indices (NLR/PLR/LMR/SII) showed no intergroup differences (all P > 0.05) (Fig. 3). Multivariate logistic regression confirmed independent predictive value for elevated CD4⁺ T-cells (OR = 2.370, 95% CI 1.163–4.830; P = 0.018), reduced CD8⁺ T-cells (OR = 0.391, 95% CI 0.184–0.829; P = 0.014), and increased CD4⁺/CD8⁺ ratios (OR = 0.075, 95% CI 0.003–0.115; P = 0.024). ROC analysis validated robust predictive capacity for CD4⁺ proportion (AUC = 0.837), CD8⁺ proportion (AUC = 0.846), and CD4⁺/CD8⁺ ratio (AUC = 0.888), with the combined model achieving diagnostic-grade accuracy (AUC = 0.926, sensitivity 83.3%, specificity 92.3%). Discussion

EGFR exon 20 insertion mutations represent a distinct molecular subtype characterized by in-frame insertions or duplications of 1–7 amino acids within residues 762–774 [10, 11]. This critical functional domain of the EGFR tyrosine kinase encompasses the α-C helix and adjacent loop structures. These mutations drive constitutive activation of downstream signaling pathways, leading to dysregulated cell growth and proliferation [12]. Based on spatial distribution, ex20ins mutations are classified into three subtypes: insertions within the α-C helix (residues 762–766), near-loop insertions (766–772), and far-loop insertions (772–774) [13].Unlike wild-type EGFR, ex20ins variants retain near-wild-type affinity for ATP without significantly enhancing binding affinity for EGFR TKIs. This molecular characteristic compromises the competitive advantage of EGFR-TKIs against ATP at the binding site, diminishing their selective inhibitory efficacy against mutant EGFR [14].

Furmonertinib, featuring a unique hydrophobic trifluoroethoxypyridine structure, is designed to target a concave hydrophobic pocket formed by hydrophobic amino acids near the α-C helix within the EGFR kinase domain [14]. This structural motif confers enhanced affinity for the ATP-binding site, potentiating its inhibitory activity against EGFR ex20ins variants [15]. Preclinical data suggest overcoming the steric hindrance imposed by ex20ins may require higher drug concentrations. Han et al. [6] first systematically evaluated furmonertinib’s clinical efficacy in EGFR ex20ins NSCLC. Their study demonstrated a superior objective response rate (ORR) of 78.6% with first-line 240 mg dosing compared to 46.2% (240 mg) and 38.5% (160 mg) in later lines. Median DoR was 15.2, 13.1, and 9.7 months in these respective groups.

In the current study, double-dose furmonertinib (160 mg) extended median PFS by 5.1 months compared to platinum-doublet chemotherapy (10.5 vs. 5.4 months). Subtype analysis revealed ORRs of 45.8 and 41.7% for near-loop and far-loop mutations, respectively, consistent with real-world data reported by Han et al. While traditional EGFR-TKIs are hampered by steric hindrance in ex20ins, furmonertinib, potentially through increased drug exposure, overcomes this resistance mechanism. Its ORR (47.4%) surpasses early results with high-dose osimertinib (160 mg; 24–35%) [16], likely attributable to furmonertinib’s unique covalent binding properties and enhanced penetration into the ex20ins allosteric domain.

Treatment-line analysis revealed significantly enhanced clinical benefit with double-dose furmonertinib in treatment-naïve patients. The ORR in the naïve subgroup reached 66.7%, significantly higher than the 30% observed in the pretreated subgroup (P < 0.05). Median PFS was also prolonged by 4 months in the naïve subgroup (10.2 vs. 6.1 months), aligning with findings by Han et al. [6]. This efficacy disparity may stem from a more intact tumor microenvironment in treatment-naïve patients conferring greater sensitivity to targeted therapy. At the molecular level, reduced efficacy in pretreated patients likely relates to altered tumor biology. Acquired resistance may involve enrichment of EGFR-independent clones, such as those harboring MET amplification or HER2 overexpression. Secondly, epigenetic alterations contribute significantly to resistance. Pretreated patients exhibit a higher incidence of BRCA1 promoter hypermethylation, an epigenetic modification inducing homologous recombination repair deficiency and genomic instability. This enhances platinum sensitivity while diminishing response to EGFR-TKIs [17].

DpR, defined as the maximum percentage reduction in tumor size from baseline, is an emerging dynamic efficacy metric demonstrating significant predictive value for prognosis in lung cancer. Compared to traditional RECIST criteria, DpR offers a more dynamic quantification of tumor regression, reflecting treatment response quality. Liu et al. demonstrated a significant association between greater DpR and longer PFS in EGFR-TKI-treated advanced NSCLC patients. Our results corroborate this: patients achieving DpR > 25% attained a significantly longer median PFS of 10.2 months (95% CI 6.1–14.4), whereas those with DpR ≤ 25% had an mPFS of only 5.9 months (95% CI 2.3–9.5). This highlights DpR as a continuous variable preserving rich clinical prognostic information, potentially providing an earlier indicator of drug activity than PFS or OS, and serving as a valuable alternative efficacy predictor.

Furmonertinib demonstrates a favorable tolerability profile across doses. FURLONG [18] study data indicate common TRAEs with higher doses include elevated ALT (28%; Grade ≥ 3: 2%), elevated AST (25%; Grade ≥ 3: 2%), diarrhea (25%; Grade ≥ 3: 4%), and rash (17%). Dose reduction due to AEs occurred in only 3% of patients, and 6 patients (3%) permanently discontinued due to TRAEs. Furmonertinib exhibits a remarkably wide therapeutic window in dose-escalation studies, with no dose-limiting toxicities observed from 20 mg up to 240 mg, a characteristic superior to other third-generation EGFR-TKIs [19]. In this study, the overall TRAE rate in the double-dose furmonertinib group was 60.5%, lower than the 71.7% in the platinum-doublet chemotherapy group. The incidence of ≥ Grade 3 AEs was 21.1% with furmonertinib, numerically lower than the chemotherapy group (28.3%), though this difference lacked statistical significance. Notably, no ≥ Grade 3 nausea or vomiting occurred in the furmonertinib group, contrasting with a 13.3% incidence in the chemotherapy group (P < 0.05), consistent with the typical toxicity profile of platinum-based regimens and underscoring the safety advantage of targeted therapy. Rash (18.4%) and liver function abnormalities (15.8%) were the primary furmonertinib toxicities, consistent with the typical side-effect profile of EGFR-TKIs. Collectively, existing evidence positions furmonertinib as a safer therapeutic option for advanced EGFR ex20ins NSCLC, particularly suitable for patients intolerant of chemotherapy toxicity or with significant comorbidities.

T lymphocytes play a pivotal role in antitumor immune responses, and alterations in the proportions of the major CD3+, CD4+, and CD8+ subsets correlate significantly with clinical outcomes [20]. Prior studies indicate that in immunotherapy-treated advanced NSCLC, elevated CD4+ T cell proportions and CD4+/CD8+ ratio associate with favorable efficacy, while responders exhibit significantly lower post-treatment CD8+ T cell proportions compared to non-responders. This phenomenon extends to other solid tumors; osteosarcoma patients with a baseline CD4+/CD8+ ratio ≤ 0.9 had a significantly lower 5-year overall survival (OS) than the high-ratio group (34.5 vs. 68.2%, P < 0.001) [8]. This study established that baseline CD4+ T cell proportion, CD8+ T cell proportion, and CD4+/CD8+ ratio serve as independent predictors of short-term efficacy for double-dose furmonertinib in advanced EGFR ex20ins NSCLC. Analysis of the area under the curve (AUC) confirmed their excellent prognostic performance, providing enhanced insight into the most significant baseline factors influencing patient outcomes.

Systemic immune-inflammation indices, reflecting the dynamics of the tumor immune microenvironment, hold significant value for prognostic stratification and treatment decisions in NSCLC. Multiple studies link abnormal inflammation indices to tumor progression and treatment resistance [21–24]. These indices offer potential predictive value by mirroring changes in the immune microenvironment. This study systematically evaluated the prognostic value of these inflammation indices for double-dose furmonertinib in advanced EGFR ex20ins NSCLC. Multivariate Cox analysis identified platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR) > 200.7 and systemic immune-inflammation index (SII) > 1107.7 as independent risk factors for disease progression. Notably, while neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) and lymphocyte-to-monocyte ratio (LMR) showed prognostic significance in univariate analysis, they were not retained in the multivariate model, potentially due to sample size limitations or collinearity with other indices, warranting validation in larger cohorts.

This study has several limitations: 1. The single-center retrospective design introduces selection bias, particularly the imbalance in brain metastasis prevalence between groups, potentially confounding the assessment of furmonertinib’s central nervous system efficacy. 2. The limited observation group sample size (n = 38) reduces statistical power in subgroup analyses (e.g., only 3 cases with helical insertions), hindering comprehensive evaluation of heterogeneity in treatment response across mutation subtypes. 3. Whether peripheral blood T-cell s ubsets accurately reflect tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte status requires validation via multi-region biopsy. 4. Analysis was restricted to baseline biomarkers; dynamic changes in immune and inflammation indices during treatment and their impact on efficacy remain unexplored. Based on these findings and limitations, future multi-center prospective studies employing single-cell sequencing and spatial transcriptomics are warranted to dynamically dissect the immune microenvironment heterogeneity of ex20ins tumors. 5.Although the results demonstrated statistical significance, the small sample size indeed limited the ability to perform more in-depth subgroup analyses, precluded the exploration of the efficacy impacts of different rare mutations, and may also have led to imprecise estimation of the incidence rates of rare adverse reactions. Therefore, the conclusions of this study require validation through larger-scale, multicenter prospective studies.These studies should further elucidate the efficacy of double-dose furmonertinib, identify robust predictive biomarkers, and explore biomarker-stratified treatment strategies for advanced EGFR ex20ins NSCLC.

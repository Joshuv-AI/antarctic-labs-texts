EBV-miR-BART5-3p promotes the proliferation of Burkitt lymphoma cells via glycolytic pathway
Authors: Dezhuan Da, Yujiao Qi, Chunyan Dang, Li Xue, Xiaomin Huang, Xia Wang, Hongling Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractEpstein–Barr virus is a double‑stranded DNA virus that plays a key role throughout the development of Burkitt lymphoma. The EBV genome encodes 44 mature microRNAs (miR‑BARTs) that collectively promote tumorigenesis. TP53, a tumor suppressor protein, prevents cancer progess by inducing cell-cycle arrest, apoptosis, and modulating glycolysis. In this study, we found that miR‑BART5‑3p is highly expressed in EBV positive cells and contributes to glycolysis process. Forced expression of miR‑BART5‑3p enhanced the proliferative and migratory capacities, while downregulation of miR‑BART5‑3p led to a reduction in these capacities in Ramos and CA46. Subsequently, we demonstrated that TP53 suppressed the proliferation and migration of Burkitt lymphoma cells. Furthermore, we predicted that EBV-miR‑BART5‑3p targeted 3’‑UTR of TP53 mRNA via RNAhybrid program and the QPCR results indicated that there was a significant converse expression between EBV-miR‑BART5‑3p and TP53 in Raji cells. These results implied that EBV-miR‑BART5‑3p promoted the proliferation of Ramos and CA46 via targeting the 3’‑UTR of TP53 mRNA. Altogether, these findings define a novel mechanism for EBV-miR-BART5-3p through the glycolysis pathway in Burkitt lymphoma development.Keywords: EBV-miR-BART5-3p, TP53, Burkitt lymphoma, Glycolysis pathway

Introduction

Burkitt lymphoma is a highly aggressive form of B-cell lymphoma that primarily arises within or after the germinal center [1]. Burkitt lymphoma accounts for 30%−50% of childhood tumors in Africa reported by the World Health Organization (WHO) [2, 3]. Unfortunately, the incidence of this condition has been steadily increasing, posing a significant threat to human health [4]. A significant number of individuals diagnosed with Burkitt lymphoma are already in advanced stages of the disease when they are seeking for medical attention [5]. Although chemotherapy remains the cornerstone of treatment, the effectiveness of medical treatments for individuals with advanced-stage illnesses and refractory recurrence are far from satisfactory [6, 7]. Hence, there is an urgent need for further research on the pathogenesis of Burkitt lymphoma.

In the pathogenesis of Burkitt lymphoma, the presence of Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) infection assumes a pivotal role, exerting a profound influence on the development of B-cell malignancy [8, 9]. Burkitt lymphoma has been categorized into three distinct subtypes by the WHO: endemic (eBurkitt lymphoma), sporadic (sBurkitt lymphoma) and immunodeficient-associated (ID-Burkitt lymphoma) [10]. EBV is found in approximately 95% of eBurkitt lymphoma cases, 20%−30% of sBurkitt lymphoma cases and 25%−40% of ID-Burkitt lymphoma cases [11, 12]. Notably, children with endemic Burkitt lymphoma were infected with EBV [13, 14]. Nevertheless, the exact functions of EBV in the cause of Burkitt lymphoma are still not elucidated.

EBV-derived miRNAs are primarily encoded by two groups including BamHI Fragment H Rightward Open Reading Frame 1(BHRF1) and BamHI-A Region Rightward Transcript (BART). BHRF1 encodes only four microRNAs, while the remaining miRNAs are encoded by BARTs [15, 16]. The BART contains 22 precursor miRNAs (miR-BART1-22), which generate 44 mature miRNAs, all of which are highly expressed in EBV-associated lymphoma [17]. Studies have shown that in Akata cells, miR-BART10-3p can target BHRF1, inhibiting EBV replication and reducing immune clearance [18]. Additionally, EBV affects various tumor-related genes, such as the Bim tumor suppressor gene and the TCL-1 oncogene, contributing to its carcinogenic role in B lymphocytes [19–22]. EBV-miR-BART6-3p and miR-197 had a synergistic effect on reducing IL-6R in Burkitt lymphoma [23, 24].miR-BART6-3p downregulated PTEN to encourage the growth of Burkitt lymphoma cells that are positive for EBV [25]. Furthermore, miR-BART20-5P can silence the BCL-2 related death promoter BAD gene in Burkitt lymphoma cells, leading to an anti-apoptotic effect [26]. The abnormal expression of EBV-miRNA-BARTs in Burkitt lymphoma suggests their crucial role in tumorigenesis and development [27–29]. However, the specific mechanisms of action and target genes controlled by these EBV-miRNA-BARTs have not been thoroughly investigated in Burkitt lymphoma.

Glycolysis refers to the process in which the cell membrane contains glucose transporters (GLUTs), where it is broken down into two molecules of pyruvate [30, 31]. The high levels of lactic acid produced during lactic fermentation, creating an acidic microenvironment that contributes to the remodeling of the extracellular matrix [32]. Moreover, studies have demonstrated that glucose uptake is increased in many malignant tumors derived from B cells [33]. Previous research using isotopically labeled glucose (H3) to study cellular glucose uptake revealed that EBV-positive lymphoma cells exhibit significantly higher glucose uptake compared to EBV-negative cells [34, 35]. It’s found an association between hexokinase-2 (HK2), a prerequisite enzyme for the glycolytic tricarboxylic acid cycle, and the lymphoma phenotype [36]. However, the specific molecular mechanisms underlying the high glycolytic energy metabolism phenotype in lymphoma cells are not yet fully understood.

The TP53 gene is a crucial molecule in the reprogramming of energy metabolism in tumor cells [37]. Earlier study has demonstrated that tumor cells overexpressed wild-type TP53 significantly inhibited the expression of glucose transporters (GLUT1 and GLUT4) and key glycolytic enzymes. It can also inhibit the movement of GLUT1 towards the cell membrane, which prevents the absorption of glucose [38]. Furthermore, it’s suggested that mutations or deletions in TP53 increased cell glucose uptake and glycolysis rate by modulating the PI3K/Akt/MTOR regulatory process [39, 40]. Currently, there is limited data information on the crosstalk of EBV-miR-BART5-3p and TP53 for glycolysis. Here, we report that EBV-miR-BART5-3p is involved in the glucose metabolism via targeting TP53 through a series of methods.

Materials and methods

Cell lines

Ramos and CA46 cell lines were obtained from Shanghai Fuheng Biotechnology Co., Ltd and Henan Engineering Research Center of Industrial Microbiology respectively. Raji was purchased from the Cell Bank of Type Culture Collection of the Chinese Academy of Sciences (Shanghai, China). The HEK293T cells originally came from Guangzhou Cellcook Biotech Co., Ltd. grown in a medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (Every Green, China) and Dulbecco’s modified Eagle medium (BasalMedia, China). The cells were maintained at 37 °C and 5% CO2 in RPMI-1640 (BasalMedia, China) enriched with 10% fetal bovine serum.

Bioinformatics tools

Firstly, according to the experimental design, we downloaded RNA-Seq data of EBV-positive Burkitt lymphoma cells Raji and EBV-negative Burkitt lymphoma cells (Ramos) from BioProject (PRJEB27198). To compare gene expression levels between the two groups, we calculated the average TPM value of each gene within the two groups. Subsequently, we calculated the log2 fold change (log2FC) of the average expression between the two groups. The final differential expression genes were analyzed for subsequent analysis. All data processing and analysis are done in the R language environment. Next, the differentially expressed genes were imported into clusterProfiler v4.6.2 for GO enrichment analysis. The TP53 sequence utilized to predict miRNA targets was taken from the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI), Employing the accession number NM_000546.6. The EBV-miR-BART5-3p sequence was obtained from miRBase (MIMAT0009205). The RNAhybrid program was employed for estimating the relationship and minimum energy-free hybridization of miRNAs and target RNAs.

Lentivirus transductions

Plasmid containing the EBV-miR-BART5-3p precursor were provided by Wenqiang Yu laboratory from Fudan University (Shanghai, China). The lentiviral particles were transducted into Burkitt lymphoma cells according to the provided instructions.

Plasmid Preparation and cell transfection

The TIANprep Midi Plasmid Kit (TIANGEN, China) was used for purifying plasmids. Plasmids were transfected via Lipofectamine 3000 reagents (Invitrogen, USA) into cells. Cells were transfected with Lipofectamine 3000 at an ultimate dosage of 50 nmol/L within serum-free circumstances. Western blot and RT-qPCR were employed to verify the levels of EBV-miR-BART5-3p. The inhibitors (RIBOBIO, China) were used to suppress the expression of EBV-miR-BART5-3p.

Dual luciferase assay

HEK293T cells were cultured in 24-well plates co-transfected with 2ul EBV-miR-BART5-3p mimics. Hieff TransTM reagent and of Reporter gene vector that contained the wild-type or mutant 3’-UTR of TP53 were also transfected. Luciferase activity was measured 48 h after transfection using the dual-luciferase reporter assay system (Promega, USA) according to the instructions.

RNA extraction and RT-qPCR

Total RNA was harvested via Ambion’s TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, USA). The MonScriptTM RTLLL ALL-in-One Mix with dsDNase (Monad Biotech, China) was used to synthesize cDNA. The MonAmpTM SYBR Green qPCR Mix (Monad Biotech, China) was used for RT-qPCR analyses. The data were first normalized to the level of GAPDH and then to the control group that was negative. Every reaction was carried out three times and again in separate experiments. Relative quantification (2^(-ΔΔCt)) was implemented to compute the fold changes.

CCK-8 assay

Cells were seeded in plates containing 96 wells. CCK-8(Yeasen, Shanghai) was utilized to measure cell proliferation. CCK-8 solution was given into every well at 24, 48, 72, and 96 h. The plates underwent incubation that 37℃ for 4 h. After a period of incubation, a microplate reader was employed to determine the absorbance at a wavelength of 450 nm.

EdU proliferation assay

The proliferation ability of cells was detected by EdU (Beyotime, China). Cells were seeded into 96-well plates and cultured overnight until attachment. Then, we treated cells with pre-warmed EdU working solution and incubated at 37 °C for 2 h. Next, we fixed cells with 4% paraformaldehyde for 15–30 min and permeabilized with 0.3%−0.5% Triton for 20 min to enhance dye penetration. The following is preparing Click reaction mixture containing copper ions and fluorescent azide to incubate in dark for 30 min to allow covalent binding between EdU and fluorescent probe. Finally, we stained cell nuclei with Hoechst 33,342 and DAPI for localization. Quantitative analysis and EdU fluorescence signals were detected via high-content imaging system or fluorescence microscope.

Transwell assay

The migratory ability of cells was conducted by Transwell chambers with 8 mm pore filters (Corning, USA). Cells were reconstituted in serum-free medium and inserted into the Transwell chamber inserts. Next, the testing chambers were put up in wells filled with mediums that contained 10% FBS serum. The cells were fixed with 100% methanol and stained with 0.5% crystal violet (Solarbio, China).

Cell cycle assay

For cell cycle detection, a Cell Cycle Staining kit (MULTI SCIENCES, China) was used. Initially, cells have been transfected by using the miRNA plasmid DNA for 48 h. Following the harvest of 5 × 104 cells, 1 ml of 1× Staining buffer was put into the cells. After resuspending the cells, 4 µl of RedNucleus I staining solution was added to the sample. The sample was then incubated for 20 min at room temperature. A flow cytometer was used to evaluate and measure the stained cells following the period of incubation.

Glucose consumption, lactate production and ATP production

Cells were harvested to measure glucose concentrations, lactate production, and ATP production according to the instruction. The ATP content detection kit, glucose assay kit and lactate assay kit are from Beijing Solarbio Science & Technology Co., Ltd.

Western blotting

Western blot analyses were carried out according to the protocol. Protease inhibitors (AbMole BioScience, China) were added to RIPA buffer before cells were harvested and lysed. The proteins have been separated using SDS-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis gels. Once the proteins had been separated, they were put on a 0.45 μm PVDF membrane (Immobilon-P, Ireland). Subsequently, the PVDF membrane was examined using the initial antibody, and probed with antibodies against TP53 (1:10,000; 60283-2-Ig; Proteintech, China), CDK1(1:3,000; 10762-1-AP; Proteintech, China), Cyclin D1 (1:10,000; 26939-1-AP; Proteintech, China), GLUT1 (1:4,000; 21829-1-AP; Proteintech, China), HK2 (1:15,000; 66974-1-Ig; Proteintech, China), GAPDH (1:10,000; 60004-1-Ig; Proteintech, China). A secondary antibody conjugated with peroxidase was then used to probe it (SA00001-1; Proteintech, China), GAPDH served as a control for protein loading. To visualize the western blotting bands, the ECL chemiluminescence kit was used.

Statistical analysis

Except as noted otherwise, every experiment was conducted in triplicate, and the results are displayed as mean ± s.e.m (standard error of the mean). GraphPad Prism (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA) was used for curve fitting and assessment. A significance threshold of P < 0.05 was used to assess the probability of significance.

Results

The glycolysis pathway was enriched in EBV positive burkitt lymphoma

The expression levels of genes were analyzed in EBV positive Burkitt lymphoma and EBV negative Burkitt lymphoma cells. Compared with Ramos cells, there were 179 up-regulated genes and 293 down-regulated genes in Raji cells (Fig. 1A-B). We conducted GO analysis on the down-regulated genes and found that these genes were enriched in the glycolytic pathway. Next, we discovered that there was significant increase in glycolysis-related genes including HK2 and GLUT1, when EBV-miR-BART5-3p was over-expressed in Ramos and CA46 cells (Fig. 1C-D). Furthermore, we observed significant variations in glucose contents, production of lactate and ATP levels after overexpression EBV-miR-BART5-3p (Fig. 1E-G). These results indicated that EBV-miR-BART5-3p involved in glycolysis-related pathway.

The glycolysis pathway was enriched in EBV positive Burkitt lymphoma. (A) The differentially expressed genes were analyzed between EBV positive and negative Burkitt lymphoma from PRJEB27198. (B) The pathway for down-regulated genes was analyzed. (C-D) The expression levels of GLUT1 and HK2 were measured by western blot. (E-G) Glucose contents, lactate and ATP production were measured in over-expressed EBV-miR-BART5-3p Ramos and CA46 cell lines

EBV-miR-BART5-3p enhanced the proliferation of burkitt lymphoma cells

To investigate the expression levels of EBV-miR-BART5-3p in Burkitt lymphoma, the experiment was determined by RT-qPCR in EBV positive Burkitt lymphoma (Raji) and EBV negative Burkitt lymphoma cells (Ramos and CA46). In contrast to the other two cell lines, EBV positive cells of Raji were found to possess substantially higher levels of EBV-miR-BART5-3p expression (Fig. 2A). Overexpressed EBV-miR-BART5-3p cell lines were successfully generated and the transfection efficiency of EBV-miR-BART5-3p was conducted through RT-qPCR (Fig. 2B). Additionally, we found that EBV-miR-BART5-3p promotes the proliferative and migratory capabilities of Burkitt lymphoma cells via Edu, CCK8 and transwell assays (Fig. 2C-E). Cells overexpressed EBV-miR-BART5-3p displayed a higher proportion of cells in S phase compared with the negative control cells (Fig. 2F). These results demonstrate that EBV-miR-BART5-3p is involved in the proliferation and cell cycle of Burkitt lymphoma.

EBV-miR-BART5-3p enhanced the proliferation of Burkitt lymphoma cells. (A) The expression levels of EBV-miR-BART5-3p were detected in both EBV-negative and EBV-positive cells via RT-QPCR. (B) QPCR was conducted to detect the expression of EBV-miR-BART5-3p in Ramos and CA46. (C-E) The Edu, CCK8 and transwell migration assays were performed to detect cell proliferation ability. (F) The cell cycle distributions were detected by flow cytometer

To learn more about how EBV-miR-BART5-3p influences the expression of TP53 in Burkitt lymphoma cells infected with EBV, we conducted RT-QPCR experiments to detect the expression levels of EBV-miR-BART5-3p and TP53 in Raji cells. The result revealed that EBV-miR-BART5-3p is overexpressed in EBV positive Burkitt lymphoma, meanwhile TP53 is downregulated (Fig. 3A-B). Interestingly, in comparison to the control group, we noted higher levels of TP53 transfected Raji with EBV-miR-BART5-3p inhibitors (Fig. 3C-D). The results indicated that EBV-miR-BART5-3p effectively reduces the expression levels of TP53 in Raji cells. We performed transwell assays to assess the function of EBV-miR-BART5-3p inhibitors on migration ability in Raji cells. Then, the results indicated that inhibition of EBV-miR-BART5-3p reduced cell migration ability (Fig. 3E-F). Notably, transfection of the EBV-miR-BART5-3p inhibitors boosted the percentage of cells in the phase of G0/G1 and weakened the ratios of cells in the G2/M and S phases. In contrast, when the TP53 inhibitors were used only, it produced the contrary impact on the cell cycle distribution (Fig. 3G-H). Taken together, the results implied that EBV-miR-BART5-3p promoted cell proliferation and cell cycle distribution through downregulation TP53 in Raji cells.

EBV-miR-BART5-3p promoted the proliferation of Raji cells. (A-B) The expression levels of EBV-miR-BART5-3p and TP53 were detected in Raji cells treated with BART5-3P inhibitors. (C-D) The protein levels of TP53 were detected via western blotting assays. (E-F) The transwell migration assays were used to detect migration ability. (G-H) Cell cycle distribution of Raji cells treated with EBV-miR-BART5-3p inhibitors was determined through flow cytometry

TP53 suppressed the proliferation of burkitt lymphoma cells

To ascertain the function of TP53, we downregulated TP53 in Ramos and CA46 cells via TP53 inhibitors. After 48 h, we assessed that the expression levels of TP53 were downregulated (Fig. 4A-B). As expected, the results indicated that the proliferation and migration ability were increased in Ramos and CA46 cells treated with the TP53 inhibitors (Fig. 4C-F). In comparison to the control group, downregulation of TP53 increased the proportion of cells in the S and G2/M phases and lowering the proportion of cells within the phase of G0/G1 (Fig. 4G-H). The results indicated that TP53 inhibited cell proliferation, migration ability and altered the cell cycle distribution in Burkitt lymphoma cells.

TP53 suppressed the proliferation of Burkitt lymphoma cells. (A-B) Expression of TP53 was detected while TP53 inhibitors were added in Ramos and CA46 cells. (C-F) The CCK8 and transwell were performed to detect cell proliferation and migration. (G-H) Cell cycle distribution of Ramos and CA46 cells after TP53 inhibitors treatment was also determined using flow cytometry

EBV-miR-BART5-3p directly targeted TP53 in burkitt lymphoma cells

To further validate the findings, we performed RT-qPCR to ascertain the expression levels of EBV-miR-BART5-3p and TP53 in Burkitt lymphoma cells. The findings implied that the downregulation of TP53 in Burkitt lymphoma cells may be affected by EBV-miR-BART5-3p (Fig. 5A). According to biological informatics analysis, the seed sequence of EBV-miR-BART5-3p and the 3’ -UTR of TP53 matched quite well. Additionally, EBV-miR-BART5-3p interacted with wild-type TP53 and markedly lowered the luciferase activity compared to the control mimics. However, there is no significant difference in luciferase activity when 3’ -UTR is mutant (Fig. 5B). EBV-miR-BART5-3p mimics specifically downregulated the expression of TP53 (Fig. 5C-D). Based on the aforementioned information, we demonstrated that EBV-miR-BART5-3p engaged in the reduction of TP53 levels in Burkitt lymphoma.

EBV-miR-BART5-3p directly targeted TP53 in Burkitt lymphoma cells. (A) The expression levels of EBV-miR-BART5-3p and TP53 in EBV positive and negative Burkitt lymphoma cells were detected by RT-qPCR. (B) The interaction between EBV-miR-BART5-3p and TP53 3’-UTR region was conducted by bioinformatics and luciferase reporter experiments. (C-D) The expression of TP53 was conducted in Ramos and CA46 cells

EBV-miR-BART5-3p participated in glycolysis in burkitt lymphoma cells by downregulating TP53

To check out EBV-miR-BART5-3p effects on glucose metabolism in Burkitt lymphoma cells, we carried out western blot analysis to assess GLUT1 and HK2 gene levels within EBV-miR-BART5-3p -overexpressing Ramos and CA46 cells. The results revealed that these kinds of cells had substantially higher levels of GLUT1 and HK2 protein expression (Fig. 6A-B). We found that EBV-miR-BART5-3p -overexpressing cell lines exhibited increased lactate secretion, and cellular ATP production compared to control cell lines (Fig. 6C-D). These findings suggest that EBV-miR-BART5-3p can promote glycolysis process in Burkitt lymphoma cells. This implies that EBV-miR-BART5-3p might be connected to modulating glucose metabolism and promoting glycolysis via TP53 pathway in Burkitt lymphoma cells (Fig. 7).

Discussion

MiR-BARTs encoded by EBV play a crucial role in cancer genesis and development. EBV produces 44 mature miRNAs which are over-expressed in EBV-associated lymphoma [41, 42]. In our study, we demonstrated that EBV-miR-BART5-3p levels are remarkable in EBV-positive Raji cells, which are consistent with previous research. Next, to explore how EBV-encoded genes promote the development of Burkitt lymphoma, EBV-miR-BART5-3p was over-expressed in Burkitt lymphoma cells including CA46 and Romas. Our findings pointed out that EBV-miR-BART5-3p considerably enhances the ability of proliferation in CA46 and Romas. Importantly, we have indicated that cells in the S phase are numerous when EBV-miR-BART5-3p is present.

TP53 is considered among the most well-known tumor suppressors [43]. It was realized that EBV-miR-BART5-3p has been identified in EBV-associated nasopharyngeal carcinoma along with gastric cancer cells. Similarly, research has highlighted that the expression of TP53 is downregulated while EBV-miR-BART5-3p is increased in EBV-positive nasopharyngeal carcinoma cells, which enhanced the radiosensitivity [44]. In line with these findings, our research confirmed that EBV-miR-BART5-3p bound to 3’-UTR region of TP53 via bioinformatics analysis and dual-luciferase reporter assays. All these findings provide further evidence for the oncogenic mechanism of EBV-miR-BART5-3p.

To understand the oncogenic role of EBV in Burkitt lymphoma, we found that glycometabolism is a major pathway involved in the oncogenic mechanisms in Burkitt lymphoma. Further investigation into the specific genes and molecular events regulated by EBV-miR-BART5-3p could provide valuable insights into the mechanism of EBV-associated Burkitt lymphoma. Specifically, glucose metabolism, particularly metabolites such as succinic acid and malic acid related to aerobic glycolysis TCA are significantly upregulated in Burkitt lymphoma cells [45]. Studies have demonstrated that EBNA1 increased tumor glycolysis levels and provided energy for tumors in Raji cells of Burkitt lymphoma [46]. Consistent with these findings, we demonstrated that the host cells rewire their metabolism through glycolysis pathway.

Furthermore, the previous study has shown that EBV-miR-BART5-3p activated the AMPK/MTOR/HIF1 pathway in nasopharyngeal carcinoma cells, which promoted aerobic glycolysis and angiogenesis [47, 48]. Consistent with our results, EBV-miR-BART5-3P regulated glucose metabolism in Burkitt lymphoma cells. We observed that these cells consumed more glucose, secreted more lactate and exhibited higher cellular ATP levels. To further validate the glycolytic effect of EBV-miR-BART5-3p on Burkitt lymphoma cells through TP53, EBV-miR-BART5-3p inhibitors and TP53 inhibitors were added to Raji cells. We found that these inhibitors partially reversed the changes in glycolysis process induced by EBV-miR-BART5-3p, which suggested that EBV-miR-BART5-3p regulated glycolysis in Burkitt lymphoma cells partially through the TP53 pathway.

In summary, our findings showed that EBV-miR-BART5-3p promotes cell growth and migration through the glucose metabolism pathway by down-regulating TP53 in Burkitt lymphoma cells. However, due to the lack of clinical tissues of Burkitt lymphoma, we were unable to conduct tissue sample assays. Thus, further studies to investigate precise mechanisms of action are essential.

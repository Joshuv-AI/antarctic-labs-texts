Indeed, in our study we discussed the possibility that circulating pretreatment vascular and inflammatory biomarkers may reflect aggressive tumor biology. Although we did not find a significant association between baseline circulating ANGPT2, MPO, or ENG level and tumor stage (Figure 1), it is possible that molecular features of the tumor may underlie circulating inflammatory and endothelial signatures. ANGPT2, MPO, and ENG may originate from the tumor itself. ANGPT2, MPO, and ENG Levels in Plasma Do Not Correlate With Cancer Stage

Pretreatment circulating levels of ANGPT2, MPO, and ENG were measured using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay in all patients from the EMBRACE-MRI (Evaluation of Myocardial Changes During BReast Adenocarcinoma Therapy to Detect Cardiotoxicity Earlier With MRI) cohort (n = 136). Spearman correlations are provided. There were no significant correlations with cancer stage.

We used cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) imaging because it is considered the most accurate and reproducible imaging modality for measuring left ventricular ejection fraction. However, we also performed a sensitivity analysis to evaluate the use of echocardiography rather than CMR for cancer therapy–related cardiac dysfunction (CTRCD) detection and model performance. Using CMR measurements, there were 37 cases of CTRCD. With the ANGPT2+MPO+ENG-only model, the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve and specificity were 0.97 and 0.88, respectively. Using the same criteria and model, but with echocardiography, there were 32 cases of CTRCD, and the area under the curve and specificity were 0.83 and 0.53, respectively. These data are provided in Supplemental Figure VB in our paper.1 Although the model performed slightly less well with echocardiography, it was still strongly predictive. This suggests that the model should perform well in the real world, where echocardiography is more accessible, although this will need to be tested in external cohorts.

Finally, we agree that the ANGPT/Tie2 axis is an intriguing target for cardioprotection. In addition to its roles in tumor growth and metastasis, this axis may represent a cardioprotective target, as ANGPT1 protects the heart from ischemia-reperfusion injury.5 Whether this pathway can be exploited to improve cardiotoxicity outcomes remains to be explored.

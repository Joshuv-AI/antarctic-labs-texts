Comparison of the prevalence and linear measurements of the retromolar Canal on cone beam CT scans in Palestine and China
Authors: Gang Chen, Omran Altos
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe retromolar canal (RMC) is a mandibular anatomical variation containing neurovascular structures that may impact the success of anesthesia and surgical procedures in the posterior mandible. Its prevalence varies across populations, yet no prior comparative study has assessed RMC characteristics between Chinese and Palestinian populations. This study aims to evaluate and compare the prevalence, classification, and linear measurements of RMCs in these two groups using cone beam computed tomography (CBCT).MethodsA total of 300 bilateral CBCT scans (150 Chinese and 150 Palestinian) were retrospectively analyzed. RMCs were identified and classified according to Von Arx’s system into Types A, B, and C. Linear measurements of length and diameter were taken using CBCT software. Statistical analysis included descriptive statistics, the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test for normality, independent samples t-tests, ANOVA, paired t-tests, chi-square tests, and Pearson’s correlation, with significance set at p < 0.05.ResultsRMC prevalence was 22% in the Chinese group and 12% in the Palestinian group. Type B was the most common configuration in both populations. No significant differences were found regarding sex or mandibular side. Mean RMC length was slightly higher in the Chinese sample (12.02 ± 2.82 mm) compared to the Palestinian sample (10.95 ± 3.28 mm). Male participants exhibited significantly larger RMC diameters than females in both populations (p = 0.030). No significant correlation was observed between RMC presence and third molar eruption patterns (p = 0.634).ConclusionsThis study identified ethnic differences in RMC prevalence and morphology, with higher occurrence in the Chinese population. Although not associated with third molar eruption, RMC remain clinically relevant due to potential surgical and anesthetic complications. These findings underscore the importance of CBCT imaging in preoperative planning to enhance patient safety and procedural outcomes.

Introduction

The retromolar canal (RMC) is an anatomical variation located within the mandible, specifically in the retromolar region, which lies posterior to the third molar. It branches from the mandibular canal and extends in an anterosuperior direction within the bone before terminating at the retromolar foramen (RMF), which opens into the retromolar fossa [1]. The RMC carries neural and vascular structures, including branches of the inferior alveolar nerve and associated blood vessels. In certain cases, it may also contain fibers of the aberrant buccal nerve [1–3]. Once these neurovascular elements exit the foramen, they typically supply the temporal tendon, the buccinator muscle, the posterior portion of the alveolar process, and the third mandibular molar [4].

failure to identify RMCs may cause complications such as persistent pain, paresthesia, and excessive bleeding, emphasizing the necessity of thorough preoperative imaging and evaluation to ensure safe and effective dental and surgical interventions [2, 5–8].

Studies have demonstrated that the prevalence and morphology of RMCs vary among different populations, with reported occurrence rates ranging from 8.5 to 75.4% depending on ethnicity, imaging modality, and study design [9, 10]. Research has also indicated that the RMC exhibits variability in its dimensions, such as length and diameter, as well as in its classification into different types ​ [10–12]. Despite its clinical importance, few studies have focused on comparing RMC characteristics across different populations via cone beam computed tomography (CBCT), a highly accurate imaging modality that allows for precise evaluation of mandibular structures [11, 13].​ No prior study has directly compared the prevalence and linear measurements of RMCs in Chinese and Palestinian populations, and no study has shown the prevalence of RMCs in the Palestinian population until now. Given the increasing use of CBCT in dental practice, understanding these anatomical variations is essential for clinicians to minimize complications during surgical interventions in these regions. This study aimed to determine the prevalence, classification, and linear measurements of the retromolar canal (RMC) in both Chinese and Palestinian populations via CBCT scans and to compare the findings between the two groups. By analyzing these anatomical differences, this research contributes to the literature on ethnic variations in mandibular structures and provides valuable clinical insights for dental practitioners, oral surgeons, and radiologists.

Materials and methods

Bilateral 150 CBCT images from the First Affiliated Hospital of Dalian Medical University, China, and 150 CBCT images from a private oral and maxillofacial radiology clinic in the West Bank, Palestine, were collected. These images were initially obtained for procedures such as third molar extractions, minor surgeries, implant placements, and orthodontic evaluations. However, twenty images were initially excluded because of poor quality caused by motion blur, significant metal artifacts, extensive bone defects, or pathological conditions affecting the posterior mandible.

The sample size (150 CBCT scans per group) was based on convenience sampling, determined by the availability of eligible archived images. No formal power calculation or pilot study was conducted due to the absence of prior prevalence data for the Palestinian population. Demographic matching was approximately balanced for age and sex between both groups.

All included CBCT scans were retrospectively collected from patient archives. Scans were originally obtained for various clinical indications such as third molar extraction, minor surgery, implant planning, and orthodontic assessment. Only scans meeting the inclusion criteria (complete bilateral mandibular imaging, absence of significant pathology, motion artifacts, or metallic artifacts in the retromolar region) were selected to minimize sampling bias.

CBCT scans were acquired using a LargeV HiRes3D-Max machine (LargeV Instrument Corp., China) with the following parameters: 0.15 mm isotropic voxel size, 110 kVp tube voltage, 10.88 mA tube current, 5.4 s exposure time, and a field of view of 12 × 8 cm. Image reconstruction was performed in axial, coronal, sagittal, and panoramic using the SmartVPro software (LargeV Instrument Corp., China). All CBCT scans were independently evaluated by two experienced oral and maxillofacial radiologists with more than 5 years of experience. In cases of disagreement, consensus was reached through joint discussion.

To assess reliability, intra- and inter-rater agreements were calculated on 30 randomly selected scans using Cohen’s kappa coefficient. The inter-rater agreement was 0.89, and the intra-rater agreement was 0.91, indicating excellent reproducibility.

In this study, we used Von Arx’s system to classify retromolar canal types according to their directions into three main types, as shown in Fig. 1: type A, which is oriented in the vertical direction from the mandibular canal; type B, which is curved forward and upward; and type C, which is oriented in the horizontal direction [11].

The study considers several independent variables, including geographic location (Palestine vs. Dalian, China), age, and gender. The dependent variables will include the presence of the retromolar canal, the type of canal, and its linear measurements, specifically the length (mm), by following the path of the canal and the diameter (mm), measured 3 mm below the midpoint of the retromolar foramen. For CBCT analysis, scans are examined to identify the RMC, with any discrepancies resolved by consensus.

The data analysis involved descriptive statistics, including measures of central tendency and data distribution assessment. The Kolmogorov–Smirnov test was used to evaluate the normality of the data for inferential analysis. Various statistical tests were applied depending on the type of comparison: the independent samples t- test for two-group comparisons, ANOVA with Tukey’s post hoc test for multiple groups, the paired t-test for paired mean comparisons, and the chi-square test for analyzing relationships between two qualitative variables. Additionally, Pearson’s correlation coefficient was used to examine associations between qualitative variables. A significance level of 0.05 was set, and analyses were conducted via IBM SPSS Statistics 27.0.1.

Results

The study included 150 Chinese and 150 Palestinian participants, with an average age of 39.6 ± 12.2 years for the Chinese group and 41.1 ± 12.6 years for the Palestinian group.

There was no significant difference in the mean age between the two groups (p = 0.684).

Chinese sample

In the Chinese sample (n = 150), RMCs were detected in 22% (n = 33) cases. Although more frequently observed in males than in females, there was no statistically significant difference between its prevalence among males and females (p = 0.602). The presence of RMC was not significantly associated with age (p = 0.550). RMC was present in 10.7% on the left side, 7.3% on the right side, and bilaterally in 4% of this sample. According to von Arx’s classification system, type B was dominant (56.4%), followed by type A (28.2%), and type C (15.3%) (see Table 1). The mean length of the RMC was 12.02 ± 2.82 mm, with no significant difference in RMC length between sexes.

Palestinian sample

The prevalence was equal in males and females, with no statistically significant difference between sexes (p = not significant). The presence of RMC was not significantly associated with age (p = 0.55). RMC was present in 6% on the left side (n = 9), 4.7% on the right side (n = 7), and bilaterally in 1.3% (n = 2). According to von Arx’s classification system, type B was most prevalent (55.5%), followed by type C (38.8%) and type A (16.6%) (see Table 3). The mean length of the RMC was 10.95 ± 3.28 mm, with no significant difference in length between sexes.

Discussion

This study aimed to compare the prevalence and linear measurements of the RMCs in Chinese and Palestinian populations via CBCT. These findings highlight the anatomical differences between the two populations. The study revealed a greater prevalence of RMCs in the Chinese population (22%) than in the Palestinian population (12%). This disparity aligns with previous research suggesting that RMC occurrence varies among different ethnic groups. The higher prevalence in the Chinese sample might be attributed to genetic and developmental factors influencing mandibular morphology. However, the absence of a significant sex difference in both populations suggests that RMC formation is not sex dependent. Both populations presented a relatively high prevalence of RMCs on the left side, with bilateral occurrence being the least common. Type B was the most frequently observed configuration in both groups, whereas Type C was the least common in the Chinese population, and Type A was the least common in the Palestinian population. This predominance of Type B supports the findings of previous studies that reported similar trends in RMC classification [8].

The mean length of RMC was slightly greater in the Chinese sample (12.02 mm) than in the Palestinian sample (10.95 mm), although this difference did not reach statistical significance. The mean diameters were also comparable, with a slightly larger mean RMC diameter in the Palestinian group (1.25 mm) than in the Chinese group (1.05 mm). In both populations, males presented larger RMC diameters than females did, suggesting that mandibular structures in males may be more robust.

A key aspect of this study was the investigation into the relationship between RMC presence and mandibular third molar eruption patterns. The statistical analysis revealed no significant association (p = 0.634), indicating that the presence of an RMC does not necessarily influence whether the third molar erupts normally, remains impacted, or is missing.

The variability in the reported prevalence of the RMC across different studies can be attributed to multiple factors, including differences in study populations, imaging techniques, observer interpretation, classification criteria, bone density, sample selection, and technological advancements. Ethnic and demographic variations may influence the frequency of RMC occurrence, with some populations exhibiting a higher prevalence than others [4, 12]​. Additionally, imaging methods significantly impact detection rates, as studies utilizing CBCT tend to report higher frequencies than those relying on panoramic radiographs do, which may miss smaller or incomplete RMCs due to lower resolution [11, 13]. Observer expertise and differing diagnostic criteria also contribute to inconsistencies, as some authors do not consider canals with diameters less than 0.5 mm [2, 9, 22]. Moreover, some studies analyze cadaveric specimens, which allows for more detailed anatomical assessments ​ [14, 20]. while others rely on clinical imaging [11, 15–19, 21], which may be limited by resolution and patient positioning. Prior to this study, no research had been conducted to determine the prevalence of RMC in the Palestinian population. In China, the prevalence of RMCs is close to that reported in a previous study (25.9%) [16]. The variation in RMC prevalence between populations underscores the necessity for preoperative CBCT evaluation to minimize complications. Limitations of this study include the use of convenience sampling, relatively limited sample size, and retrospective design. No clinical correlation with symptoms or complications was assessed. Future research with larger, multicenter prospective samples, including functional assessments of anesthesia efficacy, would further clarify the clinical significance of RMC variations.

Conclusion

This study demonstrated ethnic differences in the prevalence and morphometric characteristics of the retromolar canal (RMC) between Chinese and Palestinian populations, with a higher occurrence observed in the Chinese group. Although the presence of RMC did not show a significant association with third molar eruption patterns, their identification remains clinically important due to the potential risk of surgical and anesthetic complications. The findings emphasize the value of CBCT imaging in preoperative assessment. Incorporating such anatomical knowledge into routine clinical practice can enhance the safety and success of dental and maxillofacial procedures. Future studies with larger, more diverse samples and clinical correlations are warranted to validate and expand upon these results.

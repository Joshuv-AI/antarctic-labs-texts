Positioning India as a Global Leader in Pediatric Surgical Care
Authors: Rasiklal S. Shah
Published: 2025

Dear Friends,

As India rises across all sectors, pediatric surgery too must progress in tandem, aligning its growth with the nation’s broader advancement. Pediatric surgeons in India need to position themselves so that the country can become a global leader in the next 15–20 years. This will require coordinated efforts at many levels, including clinical care, education, research, technology, and international collaboration. This article is written to inspire all of us to achieve our goal of becoming a global leader.

EDUCATION AND TRAINING

Our centers should have robust training programs for postgraduates. By the end of their training, these postgraduates should be capable of carrying out standard surgeries independently and using minimal access where indicated. Indian pediatric surgical centers have a much larger clinical workload compared to many centers in the world due to the size of population. This provides an unique opportunity for us to train postgraduates from abroad.

The Indian Association of Paediatric Surgeons (IAPS) should work with the Government of India to streamline the process of training foreign postgraduates in India. Our centers should launch subspecialty fellowships in minimal access pediatric surgery, pediatric oncosurgery, transplantation, and more. They should also increase their global reach using online platforms.

Our national conferences – including the annual conference of IAPS, Pediatric Endoscopic Surgeons - India (PESI) - IAPS, and Society of Paediatric Urology (SPUCON) – are of the very high standard. We must encourage greater international participation in these events. IAPS could offer scholarship awards for the South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation countries to promote attendance of their members. Pediatric surgery departments and hospitals should also work to establish exchange partnerships with leading universities worldwide.

Rasiklal S. Shah

RESEARCH AND INNOVATION

Pediatric surgery centers should embrace digitization in record-keeping. A central registry of pediatric surgical conditions would create a large database for clinical trials, which can be used for authentic publications. India has the potential to lead multicenter clinical trials at a global scale.

There is also a pressing need to develop child-friendly, affordable surgical devices and instruments – demand for which exists worldwide. Our patent system must become more robust and streamlined, with fast-track pathways to bring research benefits to healthcare market. Partnerships with engineering colleges should be encouraged for the development of such devices. In these innovations, time is the essence: shorter intervals from idea conception to device launch are critical. Our innovations should be published in high-impact journals, while efforts should be made to improve the impact factor of the “Journal of Indian Association of Pediatric Surgeons.”

CLINICAL EXCELLENCE AND INFRASTRUCTURE

India should establish advanced pediatric surgery centers in key metropolitan cities and extend these services to tier 2 cities. Investments are needed in state-of-the-art equipment, including HD laparoscopic cameras, robotic systems, three-dimensional laparoscopic units, image-guided operating theaters, and simulation laboratories.

Hospitals should aim for the National Accreditation Board for Hospitals and Healthcare Providers and the Joint Commission International accreditation to gain international confidence in Indian health care.

MEDICAL TOURISM AND GLOBAL PATIENT OUTREACH

We need to create a globally recognized brand of pediatric surgery, for example, by promoting programs such as “Advanced Comprehensive Surgical Care for Your Child in India.” All pediatric surgeons should promote this initiative in their social circles and presentations within and outside India.

IAPS organises paediatric surgery awareness days and weeks for several years, now we should make efforts to spread this information across the globe. Overseas Indian pediatric surgeons can play a crucial role in this initiative, and they should be taken into confidence. In recent decades, with globalization, the world has grown smaller, and families from the entire world are traveling to India to address surgical problems for their children, and these opportunities must be strengthened.

Affordable treatment packages for congenital anomalies, neonatal surgery, pediatric oncology, minimal access pediatric surgery, and transplant surgery will boost confidence among international families. Collaborations with embassies, nongovernmental organizations, and regional insurers in Africa, South Asia, and the Middle East should be pursued. These medical centers must also provide support services such as translators, cultural integration, and visa facilitation.

POLICY AND GOVERNMENT SUPPORT

The Government of India and state governments must integrate pediatric surgery into Ayushman Bharat and other National Health Mission programs, with better packages to make these surgeries financially viable across the hospitals. Tax breaks should be offered to individuals and hospitals providing these services, which will make it more appealing to the private healthcare sector of India.

Healthcare institutes and pediatric surgery departments should also pursue bilateral agreements for medical training and exchange programs. India should develop world-class training centers to enhance focused skills across the specialty. Institutions such as the “Institute of Medical and Minimal Access Surgical Training” are already providing such opportunities. Government tax incentives could help to attract more students globally.

TELEMEDICINE AND DIGITAL EXPANSION

Online platforms for consultations, second opinions, and follow-ups should be widely available for international patients. Indian pediatric surgeons should also provide remote surgical mentorship to colleagues abroad.

BRANDING AND INTERNATIONAL VISIBILITY

India’s pediatric surgical milestones – such as advanced neonatal surgery, minimal access paediatric surgery, pediatric urology, and transplants – should be widely publicized. Experts should expand our digital presence through webinars, podcasts, and YouTube channels.

Standard treatment guidelines should be published and updated periodically, with open online access to all those who are interested. Textbooks authored by Indian pediatric surgeons will also strengthen our global reputation. The Journal of Indian Association of Pediatric Surgeons should collaborate with IAPS to publish such guidelines for global dissemination.

COLLABORATION AND DIPLOMACY

We must expand outreach training programs globally, especially in developing regions of the world, with support from Indian healthcare industries and/or the Government of India. Dialog should begin with the World Health Organization, the United Nations International Children’s Emergency Fund, and similar organizations to improve pediatric surgical services worldwide. Indo-African and Indo-Asian pediatric surgical exchange platforms can be created for mutual benefit.

PHILANTHROPY AND SOCIAL IMPACT

Pediatric surgical care often requires financial assistance, as only a few families can sustain the costs of treating complex congenital conditions. This is a worldwide issue, with philanthropists across the globe supporting families whose children are undergoing complex surgery.

India should establish charitable programs for pediatric surgery similar to Smile Train for cleft care. Our positioning should emphasize not only affordability but also compassion in pediatric surgical care.

CONTINUOUS GOVERNANCE AND FUNDING

A steering council with specialized task forces (quality, workforce, research, outreach, digital, policy, and branding) should monitor progress in each domain. Funding can be drawn from government programs, Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR), philanthropy, medical tourism, and device innovation.

Regular monitoring of key performance indicators – such as outcomes, international patient volumes, research output, and sustainability metrics – will ensure steady progress.

CONCLUSION

By combining clinical quality, affordability, innovation, and international outreach, India can emerge as the global leader in pediatric surgery in the decades to come.

Ash2l deficiency impairs adipose tissue thermogenesis and exacerbates obesity in mice
Authors: Yajie Hu, Jialin Zhao, Chenxi Xiao, Jiayao Liu, Jie Xu, Shenhan Xu, Wen Zhong, Ruoxue Chen, Mengting He, Chunxiang Fan, Jun Chang, Xinhua Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEpigenetic regulation plays a pivotal role in adipocyte development and thermogenesis. Ash2l, a key component of the COMPASS (Complex of Proteins Associated with Set1) histone methyltransferase, regulates gene expression through epigenetic mechanisms. This study explored the role of Ash2l in adipose tissue thermogenesis and obesity-related metabolic dysfunction.MethodsAsh2l was initially identified through transcriptomic analysis, and its expression was further validated in mouse models of high-fat diet (HFD), cold exposure, and CL316,243 stimulation. In vitro gain- and loss-of-function experiments were conducted to assess the role of Ash2l in adipogenesis and thermogenesis. To knockdown Ash2l in vivo, adeno-associated viruses carrying short hairpin RNA targeting Ash2l (AAV-shAsh2l) were injected into either the brown adipose tissue (BAT) or the inguinal white adipose tissue (iWAT). The functional consequences of Ash2l deficiency were evaluated in mice under room temperature, cold exposure, and HFD conditions. Finally, chromatin immunoprecipitation sequencing (ChIP-seq) was employed as an exploratory analysis to identify genomic regions associated with Ash2l during adipocyte development.ResultsOur findings demonstrate that Ash2l modulates the expression of both adipogenic and thermogenic genes in adipocytes. Mice with BAT- or iWAT-knockdown of Ash2l displayed defective cold-induced thermogenesis, aggravated diet-induced obesity, and systemic metabolic dysregulation. Moreover, Ash2l knockdown in BAT under cold exposure or HFD conditions also attenuated thermogenic activity in iWAT, an effect that may be mediated by reduced secretion of FABP4.ConclusionsThese findings establish Ash2l as a critical regulator of adipogenesis and thermogenesis. This study provides important insights into the epigenetic role of Ash2l in maintaining metabolic homeostasis under conditions of nutritional excess.Graphical abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s11658-026-00892-1.Keywords: Ash2l, Adipogenesis, Thermogenesis, BAT, iWAT, Obesity

Introduction

Obesity has become a global epidemic, primarily resulting from an imbalance between energy intake and expenditure [1]. Excess energy is typically stored in adipocytes as lipids, with excessive adipose tissue accumulation and expansion being key characteristics of obesity [2]. Therefore, promoting thermogenesis to enhance energy expenditure or stimulating adipogenesis to mitigate pathological adipose tissue expansion represents a plausible strategy for combating obesity and its associated complications.

In placental mammals, adipose tissue consists of three primary types of adipocytes: white, beige, and brown. White adipocytes function primarily in lipid storage and mobilization, whereas beige and brown adipocytes are thermogenic cells that dissipate energy as heat [3]. White adipose tissue (WAT) plays a critical role in systemic energy homeostasis by storing triglycerides and releasing nonesterified fatty acids (NEFA) and glycerol in response to neuroendocrine signals during fasting [4]. Under conditions of excess nutrition, WAT expands through adipocyte hypertrophy (enlargement of existing adipocytes) or adipocyte hyperplasia (formation of new adipocytes via precursor differentiation) [5]. While hypertrophic expansion is associated with pathological WAT remodeling and metabolic dysfunction, hyperplastic expansion is considered an adaptive process that supports metabolic health [6]. Enhancing adipogenesis has been suggested as a potential strategy to improve obesity-related metabolic health [5]. Similarly, enhancing thermogenesis represents an effective strategy to improve metabolic health in the context of obesity. In recent decades, BAT has garnered significant attention owing to its unique thermogenic capacity. Activation of BAT and beige adipocytes has been shown to enhance energy expenditure in animal models, offering promising therapeutic potential for obesity management [7]. Given the growing obesity epidemic, there is an urgent need to identify key genetic targets and effective strategies to safely and efficiently promote adipogenesis and thermogenesis.

Adipogenesis is a highly coordinated process during which fibroblast-like progenitor cells commit to the preadipocytes lineage, subsequently undergoing nutrient accumulation and terminal differentiation into mature adipocytes [5, 8]. Peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor γ (PPARγ) is the master regulator of adipogenesis, being both necessary and sufficient for this process [9]. One of its key downstream effects is the activation of the transcription factor C/EBPα, which plays a crucial role in adipocyte differentiation. In cultured fibroblasts, overexpression of C/EBPα is sufficient to induce adipocyte differentiation; however, this process remains dependent on PPARγ activity [10, 11]. Enhancing energy expenditure through thermogenesis is a potential strategy for combating obesity, a process primarily mediated by brown and beige adipocytes [12]. Uncoupling protein 1 (UCP1) is the most typical thermogenic effector [13, 14] and mediates adaptive thermogenesis by uncoupling oxidative phosphorylation from ATP synthesis, thereby dissipating energy as heat and protecting animals from hypothermia and obesity-related metabolic disorders [15, 16]. However, the molecular mechanisms governing adipogenesis and thermogenesis remain incompletely understood, posing a challenge to fully harnessing these processes for therapeutic intervention.

Ash2l (Absent, Small or Homeotic 2-like) is a key regulatory subunit of the MLL complexes (homologs of yeast COMPASS). MLL complexes methylate histone H3 Lys4 (H3K4), and Ash2l is involved in regulating MLL’s catalysis on H3K4 trimethylation (H3K4me3) [17, 18]. Studies have shown that the loss of Ash2l disrupts H3K4me3 and impairs transcriptional regulation of Wnt/β-catenin signaling, thereby inhibiting the proliferation of neural progenitor cells [19]. In addition, Ash2l deficiency leads to gene repression and a reduction in H3K4me3, contributing to aging-related phenotypes [20]. Despite these findings, the role of Ash2l in both adipogenesis and thermogenesis remains largely unexplored.

In this study, we demonstrated that epigenetic regulator Ash2l is involved in adipocyte differentiation, playing a pivotal role in both adipogenesis and thermogenesis. Knockdown of Ash2l in mouse adipose tissue resulted in reduced expression of adipogenic and thermogenic genes, severely impairing adaptive thermogenesis and exacerbating diet-induced obesity and energy metabolism dysfunction. Moreover, our data indicate that genes associated with adipogenesis and thermogenesis are enriched among genomic regions showing reduced ChIP-seq signal upon Ash2l depletion. The current study provides crucial insights into the functions and epigenetic mechanisms of Ash2l in improving metabolic health under conditions of nutrient excess.

Materials and methods

Animals

All the animal procedures were performed following the guiding principles of laboratory animal care set by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) of Fudan University. The mice were housed in the Animal Experimental Center of Fudan University in an environment with a controlled temperature (23 ± 2 °C), a relative humidity of 50–65%, a 12 h/12 h light/dark cycle, and free access to food and water. The C57BL/6 J male mice aged 6–8 weeks were purchased from Lingchang (Shanghai, China), and randomly grouped according to body weight. The adeno-associated virus (AAV, serotype 9) constructs expressing short hairpin RNA (shRNA) targeting Ash2l (AAV-shAsh2l) and the corresponding negative control (AAV-Control) were designed and obtained from GeneChem (Shanghai, China). The targeting sequences of shAsh2l are listed in Supplementary Table 1. These sequences were synthesized and cloned into the GV594 vector (U6-MCS-CAG-firefly Luciferase; GeneChem), which was subsequently used for AAV packaging and transgene expression. A total of 100 μL of 1 × 1011 vg/mL AAV-Control or AAV-shAsh2l diluted in PBS was injected into the inguinal fat pad or brown fat of mice. In the high-fat diet (HFD) study, AAV-Control and AAV-shAsh2l mice were housed in the Animal Experimental Center of Fudan University and fed a 60% high-fat diet for 12 weeks (TROPHIC, TP23300). Mice were euthanized and tissues were harvested.

Cell lines and treatment

All the cells were grown at 37 °C in a 5% CO2 incubator. 3T3-L1 fibroblast cells (CL-173) were obtained from ATCC (USA), and grown in Dulbecco’s modified eagle medium (DMEM; Gibco; New York, USA) containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, Gibco; New York, USA) and 1% penicillin–streptomycin (BasalMedia, Shanghai, China).

Primary cell isolation and culture

BAT and iWAT were dissected from 2 week-old mice. BAT was minced and digested with DMEM containing 3.5% bovine serum albumin (BSA, MeilunBio, 9048-46-8) and 2 mg/mL collagenase II (Worthington, LS004176) at 37 °C for 60 min with agitation. iWAT was minced and digested with DMEM containing 3.5% BSA and 1 mg/mL collagenase I (Biofroxx, 1904GR001) at 37 °C for 50 min with agitation. The stromal vascular fraction (SVF) containing preadipocytes was separated from mature adipocytes by centrifugation at 500 g for 5 min. Then, the SVF cells were cultured in DMEM containing 10% FBS and 1% penicillin–streptomycin in a 37 °C, 5% CO2 incubator, and the medium was changed after 5 h.

Adipocyte differentiation

Primary preadipocytes and 3T3-L1 cells were induced to differentiate using an adipogenic cocktail for 2 days. The induction medium consisted of DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS, 1% penicillin–streptomycin, 0.5 mM IBMX (Sigma, I7018), 1 μM dexamethasone (Sigma, 50-02-2), 1 nM T3 (APExBIO, C6407), 0.125 mM indomethacin (APExBIO, A8449), 1 μM rosiglitazone (APExBIO, A4304), and 5 μg/mL insulin (Yeasen, 40112ES25). Following induction, cells were maintained in a differentiation medium containing 1 nM T3, 1 μM rosiglitazone, and 5 μg/mL insulin. The medium was refreshed every 2 days, and mature adipocytes were harvested on day 7.

CL316,243 treatment

Mice received daily intraperitoneal injections of either CL316,243 (GLPBIO, GC43276), a β3-adrenergic agonist, or an equivalent volume of saline at a dose of 1 mg/kg body weight. After 7 consecutive days of treatment, the mice were euthanized, and relevant tissues were harvested for subsequent analysis.

Cold exposure

For acute cold exposure, the mice were fasted for 4 h and then transferred to a 6 °C environment, where they were individually housed, fasted, and given free access to water, with the rectal core body temperature monitored every 1 h via a thermometer (Zhongshi, China) for 4 h. For the chronic cold exposure experiments, mice were housed two per cage to minimize mortality associated with cold-induced stress. Throughout the 3- or 6-day exposure period, all animals had ad libitum access to food and water.

Energy metabolism and body composition analysis

Mice were fed a HFD (60%) for 12 weeks, and to assess metabolic parameters, oxygen consumption, food intake, and locomotor activity were measured in an integrated laboratory animal monitoring system (Columbus Instruments, CLAMS-16M). Body composition was measured using the small animal body composition analyzer (Bruker, Minispec Lf50). Only mice that displayed normal exploratory and feeding behavior after acclimation, showed stable monitoring curves without stress-related abrupt changes, and had complete sensor signals without equipment failure or data loss were included in the analysis. Datasets not meeting these criteria were excluded from the final analysis.

Chromatin immunoprecipitation sequencing (ChIP-seq)

BAT-SVF cells were isolated from 2-week-old C57BL/6 J mice. After reaching confluence within 2 days, cells were transduced with either shAsh2l or shNC and then induced to differentiate for 6 days. Mature adipocytes were washed once with PBS, fixed with 1% formaldehyde (Sigma-Aldrich, F8775) for 10 min, and the reaction was quenched with 0.125 M glycine for 5 min. Whole-cell lysates were prepared, and chromatin was fragmented by ultrasonication to 100–500 bp. Immunoprecipitation was performed by overnight incubation with an Ash2l antibody (CST, 5019S). Following elution and crosslink reversal, sequencing libraries were constructed from immunoprecipitated DNA using NEBNext® UltraTM DNA Library Prep Kit for Illumina (NEB, USA, E7645L) with unique index labels. Post-amplification libraries size was selected and then purified with AMPure XP Beads. Qubit was used for library quantification and High-sensitivity DNA chip was used for test of library insert size. The libraries were sequenced on an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform and 150 bp paired-end reads were generated.

The raw data were obtained in FASTQ format and subjected to quality control using fastp software (version 0.20.1) [21], which included adapter trimming, removal of duplicate reads, and filtering of low-quality sequences to generate clean data. The clean reads were then aligned to the designated reference genome (ftp://ftp.ensembl.org/pub/release-112/fasta/mus_musculus/dna/Mus_musculus.GRCm39.dna.toplevel.fa.gz) using bowtie2 (version 2.4.2) [22], producing BAM-formatted alignment files. For downstream analyses, multimapped and duplicate reads were removed, and only uniquely mapped reads were retained. Each ChIP-seq library contained more than 27 million uniquely mappable reads, with library complexity assessed by PCR bottleneck coefficients (PBC1 > 0.7, PBC2 > 4) and fraction of reads in peaks (FRiP) > 0.06, and all metrics met the ENCODE ChIP-seq standards, indicating high-quality data and good signal enrichment. After removing duplicate reads from uniquely mapped sequences, peak calling was performed using MACS2 (version 2.2.7.1) [23]. IGV software was used to visualize called peaks from ChIP-seq samples [24]. Peak annotation was conducted with R package ChIPseeker [25], and HOMER (http://homer.ucsd.edu/homer/download.html, v4.11.1) were used to find de novo and known motif. Differential peaks analysis was performed on the basis of the reads counts using R package MAnorm, p-value < 0.05 and |fold change|> 1.2 were used as the threshold.

Statistics

Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Each experiment was independently repeated three times, and representative or pooled results are shown as indicated in the figure legends. Differences between groups were assessed using unpaired Student’s t-test or one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), as appropriate. Two-way ANOVA with Bonferroni’s multiple comparisons was used for comparisons of multiple factors and analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) was used to analyze oxygen consumption data by SPSS software. Normality of data distribution was verified using the Shapiro–Wilk test. A p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant (*p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001). Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism version 9.0.

Results

Ash2l levels in adipocytes are positively correlated with thermogenic capacity and adipogenesis

To identify potential key regulators involved in adipogenesis and thermogenesis, we performed comprehensive transcriptomic analyses using two independent datasets. Firstly, we examined publicly available microarray data obtained from the human preadipocyte Simpson–Golabi–Behmel syndrome (SGBS) cell line at three distinct time points during differentiation (GSE161111). Secondly, we analyzed RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) data throughout the differentiation process of 3T3-L1 adipocytes (GSE253544). Notably, our analysis revealed an upregulation of Ash2l expression at day 11 of differentiation (Supplementary Fig. S1A). To further investigate the physiological function of Ash2l in adipose tissue, we first analyzed its protein levels in different fat depots of mice. As shown in Fig. 1A, Ash2l expression was higher in interscapular BAT and subcutaneous iWAT than in visceral epididymal WAT (eWAT). Obesity is characterized by defective thermogenic capacity and aberrant adipocyte differentiation [26]; therefore, we confirmed significant downregulation of Ash2l in both iWAT and BAT of HFD mice compared with those on a normal chow diet (NCD) (Fig. 1B–E). Because cold exposure and β-adrenergic agonists are two classical inducers of adaptive thermogenesis, we examined Ash2l expression levels in mice following cold exposure or β-adrenergic agonist treatment. Ash2l expression in both BAT and iWAT was increased after cold exposure (Fig. 1F and G) or following treatment with β-adrenergic agonist (Fig. 1H and I). Next, we measured the expression of Ash2l during the differentiation of SVF cells into brown adipocytes (BAT-SVF) or beige adipocytes (iWAT-SVF). The qPCR and immunoblot analyses revealed that the mRNA and protein levels of Ash2l increased during adipogenesis and browning. This increase was accompanied by elevated protein expression of the adipogenic regulators C/EBPα and PPARγ, as well as a rising trend in Ucp1 mRNA levels (Fig. 1J–N). We also confirmed the expression of Ash2l during the differentiation of 3T3-L1 cells. The immunoblot analyses revealed that the protein levels of Ash2l increased during adipogenesis (Supplementary Figs. S1B and S1C). Therefore, these findings suggest that Ash2l levels in adipocytes are positively correlated with thermogenic capacity and adipogenesis.

Ash2l promotes adipogenesis and thermogenesis in vitro

To investigate the role of Ash2l in adipocyte differentiation and thermogenesis, BAT–SVF cells were transfected with lentiviral Ash2l shRNA vector (shAsh2l) to knock down Ash2l expression, followed by induction of differentiation into mature adipocytes over a 6-day period (Supplementary Fig. S2A). Notably, Ash2l knockdown led to a significant reduction in lipid droplet accumulation in adipocytes compared to negative control shRNA vector (shNC) cells, suggesting that Ash2l may play a crucial role in adipogenesis (Supplementary Fig. S2B). Notably, the protein levels of adipogenesis marker C/EBPα and thermogenesis marker UCP1 were significantly reduced at day 6 of differentiation following Ash2l knockdown (Supplementary Fig. S2C). Besides, similar patterns of changes were also observed in iWAT-SVF (Supplementary Figs. S2D–S2F). Furthermore, we assessed the impact of Ash2l knockdown on isoprenaline-induced thermogenesis in iWAT adipocytes. Ash2l knockdown resulted in decreased UCP1 protein levels and a reduction in the expression of thermogenesis-related genes, including Pparg, Cidea, Fabp4 and Prdm16 (Supplementary Figs. S2G and S2H).

To further investigate the regulatory effects of Ash2l on adipogenesis, we transfected BAT–SVF cells with Ash2l overexpression vectors (Ash2l OE) and induced differentiation for 6 days (Supplementary Fig. S3A). Notably, Ash2l overexpression enhanced lipid droplet formation and upregulated the expression of PPARγ and UCP1 (Supplementary Fig. S3B–S3E). Similarly, Ash2l OE also upregulated the expression level of Fabp4 (Supplementary Fig. S3E). Collectively, these in vitro findings suggest that Ash2l plays a functional role in both adipogenesis and thermogenesis, and that its loss significantly impairs these processes.

Caenorhabditis elegans (C. elegans) has been widely used as a model organism for studying obesity and lipid metabolism [27]. Supplementation of oleic acid (OA) in food can significantly increase fat accumulation in C. elegans (Supplementary Fig. S4A). To more definitively examine whether ash-2, the homolog of Ash2l, regulates lipid levels, we employed double-stranded RNA (dsRNA) to selectively silence ash-2 in the F0 generation of C. elegans. As a result, the mRNA levels of ash-2 were significantly reduced in the F2 generation (Supplementary Fig. S4B). Oil Red O staining revealed that silencing ash-2 significantly reduced lipid accumulation, regardless of OA supplementation in the diet (Supplementary Fig. S4C). Furthermore, we observed that ash-2 silenced C. elegans exhibited decreased mRNA expression of key lipogenesis genes (fasn-1, fat-7, and Lpin-1) and lipolysis genes (lid-1 and atgl-1) compared with control C. elegans (Supplementary Fig. S4D). This phenotype is consistent with previous reports that RNA interference (RNAi)-mediated knockdown of C/EBP in C. elegans leads to impaired fat storage and downregulation of lipogenic enzymes, further supporting a functional similarity between Ash2l and the adipogenic regulator C/EBP in this organism [28]. Collectively, these results indicate that the role of Ash2l in lipid metabolism is evolutionarily conserved from C. elegans to mammals.

Loss of Ash2l impairs BAT and iWAT adipogenesis and thermogenesis

To elucidate the significant contributions of Ash2l to adipogenesis and thermogenesis, AAV-shAsh2l orthotopically were injected into BAT to genetically knock down Ash2l (Fig. 2A). Knockdown efficiency was confirmed by immunobloting and qPCR analyses (Fig. 2B). Notably, hematoxylin and eosin staining (H&E) staining revealed that knockdown of Ash2l may result in reduced de novo adipogenesis in BAT and was accompanied by an increase in adipocyte size (Fig. 2C), and increased TG content in BAT (Fig. 2D). Ash2l knockdown in BAT decreased the expression of adipogenesis (PPARγ and Cebpa) and thermogenic genes (Ucp1 and Prdm16) (Fig. 2E and F). Likewise, immunohistochemistry showed that UCP1 protein levels were decreased in the BAT with Ash2l knockdown (Fig. 2G). However, knockdown of Ash2l in BAT had little effect on the body weight and adipocyte size of iWAT and eWAT of mice under normal diet (Supplementary Figs. S5A and S5B).

Similarly, we injected AAV-shAsh2l orthotopically in the iWAT and observed the same phenomenon, with a significant decrease in protein and mRNA levels of adipogenesis and thermogenesis markers such as C/EBPα, PPARγ, and UCP1 (Fig. 2H–J). The immunohistochemistry revealed that UCP1 expression was significantly downregulated in the iWAT with Ash2l knockdown (Fig. 2K). H&E staining also showed that knockdown of Ash2l in iWAT resulted in enlarged adipocytes, but had no effect on body weight and adipocyte size of BAT and eWAT (Fig. 2L, Supplementary Fig. S5C–E). Together, the results suggest that knockdown of Ash2l in BAT or iWAT may affect de novo adipogenesis and the expression of thermogenic genes.

Ash2l knockdown affects the expression of thermogenic genes in mouse adipose tissue upon cold exposure

To assess the impact of Ash2l on thermogenesis, we conducted cold tolerance tests at 6 °C in mice with Ash2l knockdown in BAT over a 3-day period (Fig. 3A). Notably, BAT-localized knockdown of Ash2l via AAV injection (BAT AAV-shAsh2l) resulted in significantly lower rectal temperatures compared with controls during the first 4 h following acute cold exposure (Fig. 3B). The adipose tissues of BAT AAV-shAsh2l mice exhibited larger adipocyte size (Fig. 3C). Furthermore, the immunoblot and immunohistochemistry analyses showed that after Ash2l knockdown in BAT, the expression of the UCP1 in BAT was significantly less after cold exposure (Fig. 3D and E). Moreover, we confirmed that the expression of several key regulators of BAT thermogenesis, such as Ppargc1a, Prdm16, and Cidea, was significantly reduced in BAT AAV-shAsh2l mice (Fig. 3F). Besides, the levels of mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS) complex component were reduced in BAT after Ash2l knockdown (Supplementary Fig. S6A). Consistent with this, mitochondrial respiration in primary brown adipocytes was impaired by Ash2l knockdown, as measured by oxygen consumption rate (OCR) analysis (Supplementary Figs. S6B and S6C). In summary, these results provide strong evidence that Ash2l knockdown in BAT affects the expression of thermogenic genes in brown adipose tissue.

To explore the role of Ash2l in beige fat thermogenesis, we knocked down Ash2l in iWAT of mice and exposed them in a cold environment at 6 °C for 6 days (Fig. 3G). Consistent with the results observed in BAT AAV-shAsh2l mice, we also found reduced lipid breakdown, decreased UCP1 protein expression, and lower thermogenic gene expression in the adipose tissue of iWAT AAV-shAsh2l mice (Fig. 3H–K). Collectively, these findings suggest that the absence of Ash2l in either BAT or iWAT affects the expression of thermogenic genes, particularly in response to cold adaptation.

Ash2l deficiency exacerbates diet-induced obesity and abnormal metabolic health

To investigate the effect of Ash2l knockdown in BAT on obesity, we put BAT AAV-Control and AAV-shAsh2l mice on a HFD challenge for 12 weeks (Fig. 4A). AAV-shAsh2l mice on HFD had larger body size and body weight (Fig. 4B and C), along with increased mass of BAT, iWAT, eWAT, and fat mass (Fig. 4D and E). Consistent with these findings, H&E staining revealed larger adipocytes in BAT (Fig. 4F), as well as increased fat accumulation and liver weight in HFD-fed AAV-shAsh2l mice (Fig. 4E and F). Furthermore, elevated serum levels of NEFA and slight alterations in TG levels were observed in these mice (Fig. 4G). As expected, the HFD-fed BAT AAV-shAsh2l mice showed higher fasting blood glucose, and more severe glucose and insulin resistance compared with AAV-control mice (Fig. 4H–J). The oxygen consumption rate at 23 °C (room temperature) was significantly reduced in AAV-shAsh2l BAT mice (Fig. 4K). Carbon dioxide production was also significantly reduced in AAV-shAsh2l BAT mice (Fig. 4L). Consistent with these findings, thermogenic capacity was significantly impaired in AAV-shAsh2l-treated BAT mice, as evidenced by reduced energy expenditure (EE) (Fig. 4M). The respiratory exchange rate (RER), food intake, and locomotor activity were similar for both AAV-Control and AAV-shAsh2l BAT mice (Fig. 4N, Supplementary Fig. S7A). In addition, Ash2l knockdown in BAT notably decreased the expression of adipogenesis and thermogenic genes (Fig. 4O and P). The immunohistochemistry staining revealed lower UCP1 abundance in AAV-shAsh2l BAT (Fig. 4Q). These data suggest that loss of Ash2l in BAT leads to pathologically enhanced adipose tissue expansion, reduced thermogenesis, and exacerbated diet-induced obesity and metabolic dysfunction.

In addition, we investigated whether the effects of Ash2l knockdown in iWAT on adiposity and metabolism mirrored those observed in BAT. After 12 weeks of HFD, mice with iWAT AAV-shAsh2l demonstrated significantly increased body weight, fat mass, adipose tissue weight, and adipocyte size, along with elevated serum levels of NEFA and TG compared with control mice (Fig. 5A–G). We also observed that iWAT AAV-shAsh2l mice exhibited severe glucose and insulin resistance, accompanied by reduced energy expenditure (Fig. 5H–M). Furthermore, the expression of adipogenesis and thermogenic genes was significantly decreased in these mice compared with AAV-Control mice (Fig. 5N–P). However, the RER, food intake, and locomotor activity were comparable between AAV-Control and AAV-shAsh2l iWAT mice (Fig. 5Q, Supplementary Fig. S7B), thereby ruling out the potential influence of food intake and physical activity on energy metabolism. In summary, these findings suggest that Ash2l deficiency in either BAT or iWAT exacerbates diet-induced obesity and metabolic dysfunction, accompanied by impaired adipogenesis and thermogenesis.

Loss of Ash2l in BAT impairs iWAT thermogenesis partly via reduction of FABP4

Intriguingly, we noticed that after knocking down Ash2l in BAT, mice featured increased adipocyte sizes in iWAT and impaired thermogenic capacity as evident by blunted ability to induce UCP1 expression in iWAT upon cold challenge (Fig. 6A–D), but the expression of Ash2l in iWAT did not change significantly (Supplementary Fig. S8A). Consistently, we also observed that HFD-fed BAT AAV-shAsh2l mice had larger adipocytes and lower expression of UCP1 in iWAT, that is, the thermogenesis in iWAT was significantly reduced (Fig. 6E–H), but the change of Ash2l was not obvious (Supplementary Fig. S8B), indicating that the reduction of thermogenesis was not caused by the change of Ash2l in iWAT. It has been reported that FABP4 protein is secreted by adipocytes and is known to regulate glucose and lipid metabolism [29]. Increased FABP4 in adipose tissue and blood increases BAT response to thermogenic stimulation [30]. In our study, the expression of Fabp4 was significantly reduced in BAT of BAT AAV-shAsh2l HFD and cold-exposed mice (Fig. 6I and J). Similarly, serum levels of FABP4 were significantly decreased in BAT AAV-shAsh2l mice after cold exposure compared with BAT AAV-Control mice (Fig. 6K). We then set out to uncover the effects of FABP4 produced and secreted from BAT on the thermogenic function of iWAT. To achieve this, we knocked down Ash2l in BAT–SVF and induced adipocyte differentiation, then we collected the supernatant and added it to the beige adipocytes of iWAT. Consistent with our in vivo data, quantitative enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) analysis revealed a significant reduction in FABP4 concentration in media derived from shAsh2l-BAT compared with shNC-BAT controls (Fig. 6L), and the conditioned media from shAsh2l-BAT decreased UCP1 expression in differentiated iWAT cells in comparison with that from shNC-BAT (Fig. 6M). The Oil Red O staining results indicated that conditioned medium extracted from shAsh2l-BAT enhanced lipid droplet accumulation in differentiated iWAT cells (Fig. 6N). In contrast, ELISA quantitative analysis showed that the concentration of FABP4 was significantly higher in the Ash2l OE-BAT medium compared with the Vector-BAT control (Fig. 6O), and the conditioned media from Ash2l OE-BAT boosted UCP1 levels and reduced lipid droplet accumulation in iWAT cells, when compared with cells treated with conditioned media from Vector-BAT control (Fig. 6P and Q). Furthermore, to validate the contribution of FABP4 secreted from BAT to thermogenesis in iWAT, we pretreated Ash2l-overexpressed BAT with FABP4 inhibitor BMS-309403, and then collected the supernatant and added it to iWAT beige adipocytes. This treatment suppressed the expression of the thermogenic protein UCP1, indicating that BMS-309403 attenuated the ability of conditioned media from Ash2l OE-BAT to induce UCP1 expression (Fig. 6R). Collectively, both in vivo and in vitro data strongly support our hypothesis that Ash2l ablation in BAT inhibits thermogenesis in iWAT at least partly by reducing the systemic release of FABP4 (Fig. 6S).

Ash2l may interact with key transcription factors to regulate adipogenesis and thermogenesis

To investigate the mechanisms by which Ash2l regulates adipogenesis and thermogenesis, we performed an exploratory ChIP-seq experiment to map genome-wide Ash2l binding sites in mature adipocytes derived from BAT–SVF of shNC and shAsh2l groups. Comparative analysis of the two datasets identified potential Ash2l binding sites. Peak annotation revealed that in the shAsh2l group, 37.52% of Ash2l binding sites to chromatin were located in the promoter regions, 12.41% in the first intron, and 25.46% in the distal intergenic regions (Supplementary Fig. S9A). In the shNC group, the corresponding percentages were 33.20% in promoters, 12.93% in the first intron, and 27.52% in distal intergenic regions. The observed redistribution of binding sites upon Ash2l depletion, particularly in promoter regions, suggests that Ash2l primarily functions through promoter binding (Supplementary Fig. S9A). Differential peak analysis identified 25,015 significant changes, with 9717 peaks enhanced and 15,298 peaks weakened following Ash2l knockdown (Supplementary Figs. S9B and S9C), indicating an overall reduction in Ash2l chromatin binding. Specifically, Ash2l recruitment was markedly decreased at key BAT differentiation and thermogenesis genes, including Pparg and Ebf2 (Supplementary Fig. S9D). Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis of the weakened peaks showed significant enrichment for adipogenesis and thermogenesis-related pathways, such as thermogenesis, regulation of lipolysis in adipocytes, FoxO signaling, and thyroid hormone signaling (Supplementary Fig. S9E, Supplementary Data 1). Gene Ontology (GO) analysis uncovered significant enrichment of terms related to transcriptional activation within the Molecular Function domain, including protein binding, DNA binding, and catalytic activity of relevant enzymes (Supplementary Fig. S9F).

To explore sequence features associated with peaks weakened in the shAsh2l group, we performed a motif enrichment analysis. This analysis revealed a significant enrichment of binding motifs for the master adipogenic regulators PPARγ and C/EBPβ, supporting the role of Ash2l as a critical factor in adipogenesis (Supplementary Fig. S9G). Furthermore, the significant enrichment of motifs for transcription factors SP1, GABPA, PPARα, and EBF2 suggests that Ash2l is potentially involved in activating the adipogenic and thermogenic gene program in BAT (Supplementary Fig. S9G).

The Mixed-lineage leukemia (MLL) complex, a homolog of the yeast COMPASS, mediates histone H3K4me to modulate gene expression [31, 32]. As a core subunit of the MLL complex [18], Ash2l coupregulated with H3K4me3 during adipocyte differentiation (Supplementary Fig. S10A). Its knockdown substantially reduced H3K4me3 levels, confirming its essential role in regulating this histone mark (Supplementary Fig. S10B).

Consistently, ChIP-qPCR confirmed significantly reduced Ash2l occupancy at the Pparg, Ebf2, and Ucp1 promoters in shAsh2l BAT cells (Supplementary Fig. S9H). These findings are consistent with gene expression profiling and western blot analyses of BAT from AAV-shAsh2l mice (Fig. 4O and P). In summary, our results suggest that Ash2l acts as a transcriptional activator that potentially directly regulates essential genes such as Pparg and Ebf2, thereby governing adipogenesis and thermogenesis and influencing adipose tissue development and energy homeostasis.

Discussion

In this study, we identified Ash2l as a key regulator of adipogenesis and thermogenesis. The expression of Ash2l is upregulated in response to cold stress or β-adrenergic stimulation, but decreased expression of Ash2l is observed in BAT and iWAT from HFD-induced obesity mice. Knockdown of Ash2l in mouse adipose tissue resulted in reduced expression of adipogenic and thermogenic genes, severely impairing adaptive thermogenesis and exacerbating diet-induced obesity and energy metabolism dysfunction. Moreover, Ash2l potentially acts at the promoter regions of adipose-related factors to regulate the expression of their target genes. This study offers critical insights into the functional roles and epigenetic mechanisms of Ash2l in enhancing metabolic health during nutrient excess.

Responding to caloric excess by promoting the recruitment of new adipocytes and increasing thermogenesis ensures safe storage of energy in adipose tissue, which is critical for combating obesity and maintaining whole-body metabolic health [33, 34]. Identification of novel regulators of adipogenesis and thermogenesis may provide new potential therapeutic molecular targets for the treatment of obesity and related metabolic diseases [35]. Ash2l is essential for postnatal neurogenesis to maintain proper forebrain function, and the loss of Ash2l impairs the proliferation and differentiation capacity of neural stem cells [36]. In addition, Ash2l-mediated H3K4 methylation is also an upstream epigenetic regulator of signals required for ureteric bud morphogenesis [37]. Our previous studies have demonstrated that Ash2l-mediated H3K4me3 leads to abnormal activation of the Notch1 signaling pathway, thereby causing fibrosis and inflammation in diabetic nephropathy [38]. In this work, we first observed Ash2l is highly expressed in iWAT and BAT, and protein levels of Ash2l are potently increased by CL316,243 treatment or cold exposure in mice BAT or iWAT. Second, Ash2l expression was downregulated in obese mice with impaired energy metabolism. In human adipocyte models, Ash2l expression was markedly upregulated on day 11 of differentiation in SGBS preadipocytes (GSE161111). Similarly, in the widely used hMADS cell model (human Multipotent Adipose-Derived Stem cells), Ash2l expression was significantly increased at day 14 of differentiation [39]. These findings further support that Ash2l expression is consistently upregulated during adipocyte differentiation in both human and mouse models, supporting an evolutionarily conserved role for Ash2l in adipogenesis across species. Studies using mouse cells undergoing differentiation have revealed that loss of Ash2l function seriously repressed the differentiation and capacity of thermogenesis of brown adipocytes and beige adipocytes. On contrary, overexpression of Ash2l promoted adipocyte differentiation and thermogenic gene expression, showing a more significant association with developing obesity [40]. To specifically investigate the role of adipocyte Ash2l in cold tolerance and diet-induced metabolic dysfunction, we knocked down Ash2l in iWAT or BAT of mice [41, 42]. Our findings demonstrate that knockdown of Ash2l in adipose tissue significantly impaired the cold tolerance of mice, and exacerbated diet-induced obesity and metabolic abnormalities. These findings show that Ash2l plays a core role on the regulation of the organismal energy balance. It should be noted that the viral-mediated knockdown of Ash2l in this study was not adipocyte-specific. Although local injection into adipose tissue was employed to minimize off-target effects in other organs, the observed effects of Ash2l on whole-body energy balance and systemic metabolism may not be solely attributable to adipocyte-autonomous mechanisms. To more precisely define the cell-intrinsic functions of Ash2l in adipocytes, future studies should focus on generating adipocyte-specific Ash2l knockout or overexpression mouse models [43].

An interesting finding of this study is that knockdown of Ash2l in BAT significantly reduced the expression of thermogenic genes in beige adipocytes of iWAT, and we observed this phenomenon in both cold exposure and HFD mouse. FABP4, a secreted adipokine, is released into the bloodstream and functions as a humoral factor to regulate glucose and lipid metabolism [44]. Meanwhile, knockdown of Ash2l in BAT significantly suppressed the expression of FABP4 and serum levels of FABP4. Therefore, we hypothesize that the knockdown of Ash2l in BAT reduces the release of FABP4, thereby decreasing the expression of thermogenic genes in beige adipocytes of iWAT. Similar to our hypothesis, previous studies have shown that elevated circulating A-FABP promotes the delivery of WAT-derived free fatty acids to BAT, increasing BAT thermogenesis [30]. We found that iWAT treated with conditioned media from Ash2l-knockdown BAT exhibited reduced UCP1 expression and enhanced lipid droplet accumulation. In contrast, treatment with conditioned medium derived from Ash2l-overexpressing BAT significantly upregulated UCP1 expression and attenuated lipid droplet accumulation in iWAT. However, this effect was blocked when Ash2l-overexpressing BAT was pretreated with the FABP4 inhibitor. Previous studies have suggested that FABP4 is secreted by white rather than brown adipose tissue in response to lipolytic stimuli [45]. Consequently, most research has focused on the effects of circulating FABP4 on thermogenesis in BAT [30], while its role in beige adipocytes within iWAT remains largely overlooked. Although our study suggests that Ash2l-mediated FABP4 secretion from BAT may regulate thermogenesis in iWAT, the precise underlying mechanisms require further investigation.

In recent years, considerable effort has been devoted to identifying novel transcriptional regulators involved in adipocyte differentiation. ChIP-seq has emerged as a powerful tool in epigenetic research, enabling genome-wide mapping of transcription factor binding sites and histone modifications [46]. Ash2l has long been recognized as a transcriptional activator that facilitates gene expression by catalyzing H3K4 methylation, thereby modulating chromatin accessibility and transcription factor recruitment [47]. In adipocytes, we observed marked alterations in Ash2l-mediated H3K4me3 levels, suggesting the existence of a histone modification-dependent regulatory mechanism. To further explore genomic regions potentially bound by Ash2l, we performed ChIP-seq in differentiated adipocytes. Preliminary results suggest that the downregulated peaks upon Ash2l knockdown are enriched at genes critically involved in adipogenesis and thermogenesis. Importantly, ChIP-seq signal and ChIP-qPCR enrichment were observed at promoter regions of genes involved in adipogenic and thermogenic programs, including Pparg and Ebf2. These findings strongly support a model in which Ash2l governs adipocyte differentiation and thermogenic programming by orchestrating the expression of pivotal transcriptional networks.

Conclusions

Our study demonstrates that Ash2l deficiency in adipose tissue leads to reduced expression of thermogenic genes, aggravated diet-induced obesity, and pronounced disturbances in systemic energy metabolism, highlighting the essential role of Ash2l in regulating both adipogenesis and thermogenesis. While our findings establish a critical physiological role for Ash2l in adipose biology, the precise molecular mechanisms underlying these effects remain to be fully elucidated. Together, these findings provide important mechanistic insights into the epigenetic regulation of adipose biology and offer a conceptual framework for developing Ash2l-centered therapeutic strategies for combating obesity and related metabolic disorders.

Limitations of study

It is important to emphasize that our animal model is based on a knockdown approach achieved through AAV injection. To more accurately define the role of Ash2l in adipocytes, future studies should prioritize the development of adipose tissue-specific Ash2l knockout mice [43]. While the present study examined Ash2l overexpression in vitro, further investigation is required to assess its effects on obesity and thermogenic function in vivo. Despite the fact that our study provides molecular evidence of BAT activation, the use of body temperature as a proxy for thermogenic activity represents a limitation that will be addressed in future work using gold-standard measurements. In addition, although our work has preliminary established Ash2l’s role in regulating nuclear gene expression, the precise molecular pathways by which it modulates adipogenesis and thermogenesis, particularly those involving PPARγ, remain to be fully elucidated. It should also be noted that this study was conducted solely in C57BL/6 J mice. Future work should therefore validate these findings in other genetic backgrounds to evaluate the potential influence of strain-specific effects.

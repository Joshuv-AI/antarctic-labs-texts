First-Line Tislelizumab Plus Chemotherapy in Advanced Non-Squamous Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer: PD-L1 ≥ 50% Subgroup Analysis from the RATIONALE-304 Trial
Authors: Shun Lu, Jie Wang, Yan Yu, Xinmin Yu, Yanping Hu, Liao Wangjun, Xingya Li, Yuepeng Liu, Weidong Li, Xiaofei Qu, Yuanyuan Bao, Mengzhao Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT

Key Summary Points

Why carry out this study? Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) remains a leading cause of cancer-related deaths worldwide, with a significant unmet need for improved first-line treatments.This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of tislelizumab plus chemotherapy versus chemotherapy alone in patients with advanced non-squamous NSCLC and programmed death ligand 1 tumor cell (PD-L1) expression ≥ 50%, whose cancer responds better to immune checkpoint inhibitors.What was learned from the study? Patients with PD-L1 tumor cell expression ≥ 50% who received tislelizumab plus chemotherapy experienced longer survival outcomes compared with those who received chemotherapy alone, with hazard ratios for progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) of 0.31 and 0.39 at final analysis (median follow-up of 16.5 months), improving to 0.29 and 0.38, respectively, at 4 years (median follow-up of 23.4 months).In subgroup analyses, patients with PD-L1 tumor cell expression ≥ 90% showed numerically higher median PFS compared to those with 50–89% expression (26.2 vs. 14.6 months; HR 0.80, 95% CI 0.43–1.49), suggesting delayed tumor progression, while OS was comparable between these subgroups (41.9 months vs. 42.1 months; HR 1.02, 95% CI 0.55–1.89).This study demonstrates durable long-term survival benefits with tislelizumab plus chemotherapy, supporting its use as a first-line treatment for patients with high PD-L1 tumor cell expression, and to our knowledge is the first to compare outcomes between PD-L1 50–89% and ≥ 90% subgroups in patients with first-line, advanced non-squamous NSCLC receiving a PD-1 inhibitor plus chemotherapy.

Introduction

Lung cancer is a leading cause of cancer-related deaths worldwide [1]. Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) accounts for 80% of cases, with 50% showing distant metastases at diagnosis. In advanced NSCLC, 22–30% of patients exhibit high programmed death ligand 1 (PD-L1) expression [2–4], defined as membranous PD-L1 expression on ≥ 50% of tumor cells (TCs), regardless of staining intensity (tumor proportion score ≥ 50%) [3]. Higher PD-L1 TC expression correlates with better responses to immune checkpoint inhibitors [5–15]. Programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1)/PD-L1 inhibitors are now standard of care for advanced NSCLC without driver alterations, either as monotherapy for NSCLC with PD-L1 TC expression ≥ 50% or combined with chemotherapy regardless of histology and PD-L1 expression [16–22]. Despite these advances, an unmet need for improved treatments remains.

Tislelizumab (BGB-A317), an anti-PD-1 antibody [23, 24], was approved by the Chinese National Medical Products Administration for NSCLC as first-line treatment with chemotherapy and as second-/third-line monotherapy in locally advanced or metastatic disease [25, 26], and for extensive-stage small cell lung cancer (ES-SCLC) with chemotherapy [27]. The European Commission approved tislelizumab as first-line treatment with chemotherapy in squamous NSCLC, with pemetrexed and chemotherapy in non-squamous (nsq) NSCLC (PD-L1 ≥ 50%, negative for EGFR mutations and ALK rearrangements), and as monotherapy for previously treated locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC, including after targeted therapies for tumors positive for EGFR mutations or ALK rearrangement [28]. The European Commission also approved tislelizumab with chemotherapy for first-line ES-SCLC treatment [29].

RATIONALE-304, a phase 3 study, compared first-line tislelizumab plus platinum-based chemotherapy versus platinum-based chemotherapy in patients with advanced nsq-NSCLC. The final analysis of the intent-to-treat (ITT) population showed sustained progression-free survival (PFS) benefit (stratified hazard ratio [HR] 0.63, 95% confidence interval [CI] 0.47–0.86), improved objective response rate (ORR), longer duration of response (DoR), and clinically meaningful overall survival (OS) improvement with tislelizumab plus chemotherapy versus chemotherapy [30]. With no new safety signals, the tolerability of tislelizumab plus chemotherapy was consistent with other PD-1 inhibitors plus chemotherapy in advanced or metastatic NSCLC [31–33].

This report presents an exploratory analysis of the efficacy and safety of tislelizumab plus chemotherapy versus chemotherapy alone among patients with high PD-L1 TC expression (≥ 50%) enrolled in the RATIONALE-304 study, a clinically relevant subgroup that aligns with the European Medicines Agency approval criteria [34]. We report efficacy outcomes from the final analysis and 4-year follow-up, assessing long-term benefits of tislelizumab plus chemotherapy. Additionally, we present the results comparing outcomes between PD-L1 50–89% and ≥ 90% subgroups in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm.

Methods

Study Design

The study design and patient eligibility criteria for RATIONALE-304 (NCT03663205) have been reported in full previously [35]. Briefly, RATIONALE-304 was an open-label, randomized, phase 3 study in patients with histologically confirmed stage IIIB/IV nsq-NSCLC, conducted across 47 study sites in China. Patients were randomized (2:1) to receive tislelizumab 200 mg intravenously (i.v.) once every 3 weeks (Q3W) plus platinum-based chemotherapy (cisplatin 75 mg/m2 i.v. Q3W or carboplatin area under the curve [AUC] 5 i.v. Q3W for 4–6 cycles) and pemetrexed 500 mg/m2 i.v. Q3W, followed by maintenance tislelizumab and pemetrexed; or platinum-based chemotherapy (cisplatin 75 mg/m2 i.v. Q3W or carboplatin AUC 5 i.v. Q3W) and pemetrexed 500 mg/m2 i.v. Q3W, followed by maintenance pemetrexed 500 mg/m2 i.v. Q3W. Treatments continued until radiographic progression per Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumours version 1.1 (RECIST v1.1) or clinical progression, unacceptable toxicity, investigator decision, or withdrawal of consent. Patients in the chemotherapy arm could cross over to tislelizumab monotherapy upon independent review committee (IRC)-confirmed disease progression. Patients and the public were not directly involved in the design, conduct, reporting, or dissemination plans of this research. The study was designed by clinical experts and the sponsor on the basis of scientific rationale and unmet medical needs in the treatment of NSCLC.

Ethical Approval

RATIONALE-304 was conducted in conformance with Good Clinical Practice guidelines and the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and its amendments, and was approved by the relevant institutional review board/independent ethics committee for each study site (Supplementary Table S1). Written informed consent from each patient was obtained before study participation.

Patients

Eligible patients were adults (18–75 years) with treatment-naive, locally advanced (stage IIIB; not amenable to curative surgery or radiotherapy) or metastatic (stage IV) nsq-NSCLC, per the American Joint Committee on Cancer Staging Manual 7th edition, without sensitizing EGFR mutations or ALK rearrangements per tissue-based analyses, with an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status ≤ 1, and ≥ 1 measurable lesion per RECIST v1.1. Exclusion criteria included uncontrolled or untreated brain metastases, interstitial Lung disease or non-infectious pneumonitis, active or relapsed autoimmune disease, or systemic immunosuppressive treatment within 14 days before study treatment initiation. This exploratory analysis included patients with PD-L1 TC membrane expression ≥ 50%, 50–89%, and ≥ 90% based on assay manufacturer instructions. Baseline demographic data, including sex, were collected for all patients.

Endpoints and Assessments

The primary endpoint of this exploratory analysis was IRC-assessed PFS (PFSIRC) per RECIST v1.1 in patients with PD-L1 TC expression ≥ 50%, comparing tislelizumab plus chemotherapy versus chemotherapy alone. Secondary endpoints included OS and anti-tumor activity (confirmed ORRIRC, DoRIRC, and IRC-assessed time to confirmed response) in this population. Additionally, PFSIRC, ORRIRC, and OS outcomes were assessed between PD-L1 50–89% and ≥ 90% subgroups in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm.

PD-L1 TC expression was assessed during screening using the VENTANA PD-L1 (SP263) Assay (Roche Diagnostics, Indianapolis, IN, USA) at a central laboratory. The VENTANA PD-L1 (SP263) Assay detects PD-L1 protein in formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded NSCLC tissue [36], and TC expression was scored as the percentage of viable TCs exhibiting any membrane staining above background.

Tumor assessments were scheduled every 6 weeks for the first 6 months, every 9 weeks for the next 6 months, and every 12 weeks thereafter, regardless of dose delays.

Safety and tolerability were assessed through treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) and treatment-related TEAEs, graded following the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 5.0. Immune-mediated adverse events were determined by a pre-defined programmatic algorithmic approach and based on a list of preferred terms, without manual medical adjudication.

Statistical Analysis

The statistical methodology for the primary analysis in the ITT population of the RATIONALE-304 study has been reported previously [35]. The safety population included all patients who received any dose of tislelizumab or chemotherapy. This exploratory analysis in the PD-L1 ≥ 50% population was not sufficiently powered to demonstrate superiority between treatment arms, or between the PD-L1 50–89% and ≥ 90% populations in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm. Categorical variables were summarized as numbers (percentages) of patients, while continuous variables were reported using descriptive statistics. Time-to-event endpoints (PFS, OS, and DoR) were estimated using Kaplan–Meier methodology, with 95% CIs for medians estimated by the Brookmeyer and Crowley method. HRs and associated 95% CIs were calculated using an unstratified and stratified (by stage IIIB vs. IV disease) Cox proportional hazards model.

Results

Patient Demographics and Baseline Characteristics

From July 23, 2018 to July 31, 2019, 334 patients (ITT population) were randomized to receive tislelizumab plus chemotherapy (n = 223) or chemotherapy alone (n = 111). The PD-L1 ≥ 50% population included 110 patients (32.9% of the ITT population; tislelizumab plus chemotherapy, n = 74; chemotherapy, n = 36) (Supplementary Fig. S1). Median follow-up for the PD-L1 ≥ 50% population was 16.5 months (range 0.0–26.9 months) at final analysis (October 26, 2020 cutoff) and 23.4 months (range 0.0–54.8 months) for the ad hoc analysis at 4 years (April 26, 2023 cutoff). In the PD-L1 ≥ 50% population, median age was 62 years, 71.8% were male, and 81.8% had stage IV cancer. More patients were current/former smokers in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm (60.8%) than in the chemotherapy alone arm (36.1%). The characteristics of the PD-L1 ≥ 50% population were generally consistent with the ITT population [30] (Supplementary Table S2). In the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm, 39 patients had tumors with a PD-L1 TC expression of 50–89% and 35 patients had tumors with PD-L1 TC expression of ≥ 90% (Supplementary Table S3).

Treatment Exposure

At the final analysis, in the PD-L1 ≥ 50% population, median tislelizumab exposure was 53.8 weeks (range 3–113 weeks), with 15.5 median treatment cycles (range 1–37 cycles) and 93% median relative dose (range 68.6–100.7%). Cisplatin and carboplatin exposure rates were similar between the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy (cisplatin, 99.3%; carboplatin, 91.8%) and chemotherapy alone (cisplatin, 99.9%; carboplatin, 94.9%) arms. Most patients (n = 40 [54.1%]) received more than 12 tislelizumab cycles. The median numbers of cisplatin cycles in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy and chemotherapy alone arms were 6.0 (range 1–6 cycles) and 4.0 (range 4–5 cycles), respectively. The median number of carboplatin cycles was the same between the two arms (4.0 cycles). The median number of pemetrexed cycles was higher in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm (14.0 [range 1–36 cycles]) than in the chemotherapy alone arm (4.0 [range 1–21 cycles]).

Subsequent systemic anticancer therapies were received by 36.5% (27/74) of patients in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm and 66.7% (24/36) in the chemotherapy alone arm. Of these, 8.1% (6/74) and 41.7% (15/36) received subsequent immunotherapy, respectively. In the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm, median time from randomization to subsequent immunotherapy was 45.9 weeks (range 24.7–93.6 weeks), and from the end of tislelizumab plus chemotherapy treatment to subsequent immunotherapy was 10.1 weeks (range 0.7–39.3 weeks). In the chemotherapy alone arm, these times were 36.3 weeks (range 6.0–68.0 weeks) and 2.6 weeks (range 0.1–49.0 weeks), respectively. In the chemotherapy alone arm, 36.1% (13/36) of patients crossed over to tislelizumab monotherapy at progression, representing 86.7% (13/15) of subsequent immunotherapy recipients. Median time from the last dose of chemotherapy to crossover was 2.6 weeks (range 0.1–18.1 weeks).

Antitumor Activity

At the final analysis, the PD-L1 ≥ 50% population showed improved antitumor responses with tislelizumab plus chemotherapy (Table 1). Confirmed ORRIRC (95% CI) was 70.3% (58.5–80.3%) in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm and 30.6% (16.3–48.1%) in the chemotherapy alone arm. Median DoRIRC was NE (95% CI 13.2 months–NE) for 52 responders in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm versus 8.5 months (95% CI 3.3 months–NE) for 11 responders in the chemotherapy alone arm (Table 1). Median time to confirmed response was similar (6.3 vs. 6.1 weeks). The confirmed complete response rate per IRC was 9.5% with tislelizumab plus chemotherapy versus 0% with chemotherapy alone.

Long-Term Treatment Efficacy Outcomes

Of the 110 patients receiving tislelizumab plus chemotherapy in the ITT population, 22 patients in the PD-L1 ≥ 50% population received ≥ 35 tislelizumab cycles. These recipients demonstrated 100% ORRIRC (36.4% [8/22] complete response, 63.6% [14/22] partial response; Supplementary Fig. S2A). Median DoR was not reached (95% CI 29.6 months–NE). At the data cutoff, 63.6% (14/22) of patients remained alive without disease progression or subsequent therapy. The 4-year OS rate was 90.5% (95% CI 67.0–97.5%), with median OS not reached (Supplementary Fig. S2B).

Safety and Tolerability

In the PD-L1 ≥ 50% population, the safety analysis included 74 patients in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm and 35 patients in the chemotherapy alone arm. All patients experienced ≥ 1 TEAE (Supplementary Table S4). The most common TEAEs (≥ 20%) were grade 1–2 anemia, leukopenia, alanine aminotransferase increased, thrombocytopenia, and aspartate aminotransferase increased (Supplementary Table S5). Grade ≥ 3 TEAEs (75.7% vs. 48.6%) and serious TEAEs (43.2% vs. 28.6%) were more frequent in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm than in the chemotherapy alone arm (Supplementary Table S4). Treatment discontinuation due to TEAEs occurred in 28.4% (21/74) and 5.7% (2/35) of patients in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy and chemotherapy alone arms, respectively. TEAEs led to permanent tislelizumab discontinuation in 10.8% (8/74) of patients and to treatment modifications in 74.3% (55/74) of patients (Supplementary Table S4). Treatment-related TEAE rates were similar to the overall TEAE profile (Supplementary Tables S5 and S6). TEAEs leading to death occurred in 4.1% (3/74) and 2.9% (1/35) of patients in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy and chemotherapy alone arms, respectively; of these, deaths of one patient in each arm (1.4% and 2.9%, respectively) were treatment-related. Exposure-adjusted event rates per 100 person-months were lower in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm versus the chemotherapy alone arm (282.0 vs. 365.7, respectively). Immune-mediated adverse events of all grades occurred in 39.2% (29/74) and 5.7% (2/35) of patients in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy and chemotherapy alone arms, respectively (Supplementary Table S7). As previously reported [30], pneumonitis, endocrinopathies (diabetes mellitus), and myocarditis/pericarditis were the most common grade ≥ 3 events in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm.

Discussion

This exploratory analysis of RATIONALE-304 supports the efficacy of tislelizumab plus chemotherapy in the PD-L1 ≥ 50% population, extending the positive results from the ITT population. In the current exploratory analysis of the PD-L1 ≥ 50% population, median PFS was markedly prolonged in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm (HR 0.31, 95% CI 0.18–0.55) [30]. This demonstrates the robustness of tislelizumab plus chemotherapy in delaying disease progression in patients with advanced or metastatic nsq-NSCLC with PD-L1 TC expression ≥ 50%. The tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm showed a higher ORRIRC (70.3% vs. 30.6%), longer median DoR (NE vs. 8.5 months), and improved OS (NE vs. 13.1 months; HR 0.39, 95% CI 0.22–0.71) compared with the chemotherapy alone arm. These data are consistent with those of the ITT population, showing a clinically meaningful OS (HR 0.90, 95% CI 0.63–1.28) and PFS (HR 0.29, 95% CI 0.16–0.50) benefit with tislelizumab plus chemotherapy versus chemotherapy alone, despite a potential impact from crossover in the chemotherapy arm and lack of power for OS assessment [30].

Data from the 4-year follow-up confirmed the long-term benefits of tislelizumab plus chemotherapy in patients with PD-L1 TC expression ≥ 50%. At this extended follow-up, median PFS was 17.2 months in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm versus 4.6 months in the chemotherapy alone arm (HR 0.29, 95% CI 0.17–0.50), with 24-month PFS rates of 45.3% and 6.2%, respectively. The substantial OS benefit was maintained, with a median OS of 41.9 months for tislelizumab plus chemotherapy compared with 13.1 months for chemotherapy alone (HR 0.38, 95% CI 0.23–0.62). These results, along with durable response rates observed in the long-term exposure analysis, confirm the enduring efficacy of tislelizumab plus chemotherapy in this patient population.

In RATIONALE-304, previously untreated patients with nsq-NSCLC and tumors expressing PD-L1 ≥ 50% experienced a meaningful OS benefit (approx. 30-month difference) with tislelizumab plus chemotherapy compared with those receiving chemotherapy alone. This OS outcome aligns with the trend observed in other first-line NSCLC trials, such as KEYNOTE-024 and KEYNOTE-189 [37–42]. The extended 4-year follow-up period (median follow-up 23.4 months) in RATIONALE-304 provides a longer observation period, which confirms sustained survival and response durability as well as allowing for better characterization of late-onset adverse events, which are all critical factors for clinical decision-making. In the 5-year analysis of the KEYNOTE-189 trial, the PD-L1 ≥ 50% population experienced significantly improved OS (median OS 27.7 vs. 10.1 months) and PFS (median PFS 11.1 vs. 4.8 months) with first-line pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy versus chemotherapy alone [37]. Similarly, 5-year follow-up data from the KEYNOTE-024 trial showed improved OS (median OS 26.3 vs. 13.4 months) and PFS (median PFS 7.7 vs. 5.5 months) outcomes among patients with PD-L1 ≥ 50% who were treated with first-line pembrolizumab monotherapy versus chemotherapy alone, with 4-year and 5-year OS rates of 16.4% versus 1.4% and 12.8% versus not reached, respectively [43]. Direct comparisons with other trials reporting long-term outcomes, such as EMPOWER-Lung 1 that evaluated anti-PD-1 monotherapy (cemiplimab) in patients with PD-L1 TC expression ≥ 50%, are limited by differences in patient populations (e.g., exclusion of non-smokers) [44], and the absence of head-to-head trials comparing tislelizumab plus chemotherapy with anti-PD-(L)1 monotherapy. Nevertheless, our robust long-term data provide valuable information on the sustained efficacy and safety profile essential for treatment selection in this patient population. These findings support the survival benefit of tislelizumab plus chemotherapy as first-line treatment for patients with locally advanced or metastatic nsq-NSCLC and tumors expressing PD-L1 ≥ 50%.

Patient characteristics, particularly smoking status, may influence treatment outcomes in NSCLC. In the PD-L1 ≥ 50% subgroup, the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm had more current/former smokers than the chemotherapy alone arm (60.8% vs. 36.1%). Smoking, associated with increased PD-L1 expression and tumor mutational burden, may enhance immunotherapy efficacy [45, 46]. This difference may have contributed to the OS benefit observed with tislelizumab plus chemotherapy in the PD-L1 ≥ 50% population. However, these data should be interpreted with caution because of the small sample size and exploratory nature of this analysis. Further investigation is required to better understand whether PD-(L)1 inhibitors plus chemotherapy provides significantly greater clinical benefit over PD-(L)1 inhibition alone in smokers with PD-L1 TC expression ≥ 50%.

Comparing clinical outcomes within the PD-L1 ≥ 50% patient subgroup treated with tislelizumab plus chemotherapy showed that median PFS in the ≥ 90% subgroup was numerically higher than in the 50–89% subgroup (26.2 vs. 14.6 months, respectively; HR 0.80, 95% CI 0.43–1.49). The PFS benefit in the ≥ 90% subgroup was sustained relative to the 50–89% subgroup as shown by higher 24- and 36-month PFS rates (54.5% and 42.8% vs. 36.3% and 25.7%), suggesting a clinically meaningful delay in tumor progression when chemotherapy is added to PD-1 inhibition. However, median OS was similar between the two PD-L1 subgroups (42.1 vs. 41.9 months in 50–89% vs. ≥ 90%; HR 1.02, 95% CI 0.55–1.89). This similarity in OS was further reflected in the landmark OS rates. The 12-, 24-, 36-, and 48-month OS rates were comparable between the PD-L1 50–89% subgroup (87.2%, 66.7%, 56.4%, and 41.3%) and the PD-L1 ≥ 90% subgroup (74.3%, 60.0%, 51.3%, and 48.1%). In contrast, 3-year outcomes in patients with NSCLC treated with first-line pembrolizumab or cemiplimab monotherapy demonstrated significant improvements in both PFS and OS in the PD-L1 ≥ 90% versus the PD-L1 50–89% subgroup (pembrolizumab HR 0.69 and 0.70 and cemiplimab HR 0.53 and 0.63 for PFS and OS, respectively) [47]. Several factors may explain these differences. The addition of chemotherapy to tislelizumab may enhance tumor immunogenicity in higher PD-L1 TC expressors, potentially improving clinical outcomes [48]. Additionally, smaller sample sizes of high PD-L1 subgroups (n = 39 for 50–89%; n = 35 for ≥ 90%) warrant caution when interpreting results, as these analyses were not pre-specified or powered for definitive conclusions. Alternatively, differences in genomic profiles or tumor microenvironment characteristics between study populations may influence patient outcomes, such as the exclusion of non-smokers in the EMPOWER-Lung 1 trial [44].

The safety and tolerability profile of tislelizumab plus chemotherapy was consistent with previous analyses in advanced or metastatic NSCLC [49] and other PD-1 inhibitors plus chemotherapy in similar settings [31–33]. Despite higher rates of grade ≥ 3 TEAEs and serious TEAEs in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm, these TEAEs were considered tolerable. As expected, immune-mediated adverse events were more common in the tislelizumab plus chemotherapy arm, with pneumonitis, endocrinopathies, and myocarditis/pericarditis being the most frequent grade ≥ 3 events. No new safety signals were detected compared with the ITT analysis.

Patients with PD-L1 TC expression ≥ 50% demonstrated the most pronounced clinical benefit from tislelizumab plus chemotherapy treatment, as shown by a greater reduction in the risk of progression and death compared with patients with lower PD-L1 TC expression levels [30]. This suggests the potential role of PD-L1 TC expression as a predictive biomarker for treatment response in nsq-NSCLC. PD-(L)1 inhibitors alone or combined with chemotherapy have shown comparable outcomes in NSCLC tumors with PD-L1 ≥ 50%, although fewer safety concerns have been noted for PD-(L)1 inhibitor monotherapy [50]. However, the addition of chemotherapy to PD-1 inhibitors may provide earlier disease control, as shown by improved PFS rates at 3 and 6 months after treatment initiation. This suggests that PD-(L)1 inhibitors plus chemotherapy may be preferable for patients with symptomatic disease, high tumor burden, or fast-growing tumors for which rapid disease progression is anticipated, regardless of PD-L1 expression status [50]. Only recently has a trial been designed to investigate single-agent anti-PD-(L)1 antibodies versus chemotherapy plus immunotherapy as a first-line treatment for advanced or metastatic NSCLC (the ongoing phase 3 PERSEE study). In this study, the direct comparison of pembrolizumab versus pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy as a first-line treatment for advanced NSCLC with tumor PD-L1 expression ≥ 50% may provide additional data to determine the optimal treatment for this indication [51]. However, our analysis supports tislelizumab plus chemotherapy as a first-line treatment option for patients with advanced nsq-NSCLC without driver mutations and with PD-L1 TC expression ≥ 50%.

This study has several strengths and limitations. PD-L1 TC expression was assessed centrally using a validated immunohistochemistry assay (VENTANA PD-L1 (SP263) Assay), which reduced interlaboratory variability and ensured consistent, high-quality biomarker evaluation across study sites. This centralized approach strengthens the reliability of our PD-L1 expression data and subsequent analyses. RATIONALE-304 exclusively enrolled Chinese patients, potentially limiting the extrapolation of results to other populations. Allowing patients in the chemotherapy arm to switch to tislelizumab monotherapy upon disease progression could have potentially confounded OS outcomes by increasing efficacy in the chemotherapy alone arm. Additionally, the study was not originally designed to specifically evaluate the effect of tislelizumab plus chemotherapy in tumors with high PD-L1 TC expression, making this analysis exploratory. To this end, the data from this analysis should be interpreted in consideration of its exploratory nature, small sample size, and lack of statistical power, particularly in the subgroup analyses of patients with PD-L1 TC expression 50–89% and ≥ 90%. Moreover, the absence of broader genomic profiling beyond EGFR and ALK status in this current analysis prevents assessment of the potential influence of other co-occurring genomic alterations on clinical outcomes.

Conclusions

This exploratory analysis adds to the overall evidence supporting the use of tislelizumab plus chemotherapy as a first-line treatment in patients with advanced or metastatic nsq-NSCLC across PD-L1 expression levels, as demonstrated in the ITT population, and provides meaningful clinical evidence of added benefit of tislelizumab plus chemotherapy in patients with PD-L1 TC expression ≥ 50%. Additionally, these findings suggest PD-L1 TC expression level as a potential predictive biomarker for treatment response. The combination of tislelizumab with chemotherapy and pemetrexed demonstrated favorable efficacy and acceptable toxicity, making it a well-tolerated treatment option for a broad range of patients, as shown in both the ITT and PD-L1 ≥ 50% populations.

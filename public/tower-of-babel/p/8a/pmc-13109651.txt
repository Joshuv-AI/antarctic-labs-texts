Efficacy of Osimertinib in Patients With Postoperative Recurrent Non–Small‐Cell Lung Cancer Harboring Sensitizing EGFR Mutations
Authors: Yuko Iida, Hirotsugu Kenmotsu, Keita Mori, Meiko Morita, Motoki Sekikawa, Michitoshi Yabe, Kosei Doshita, Keita Miura, Hiroaki Kodama, Noboru Morikawa, Nobuaki Mamesaya, Haruki Kobayashi, Ryo Ko, Kazushige Wakuda, Akira Ono, Tateaki Naito, Haruyasu Murakami, Yasuhisa Ohde, Yasuhiro Gon, Toshiaki Takahashi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundThe efficacy of osimertinib in patients with postoperative recurrent non–small‐cell lung cancer (NSCLC) compared to those with Stage IV NSCLC harboring epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) mutations remains unclear.MethodsThis study evaluated the efficacy of osimertinib in patients with postoperative recurrent EGFR‐mutated NSCLC. We retrospectively evaluated patients with NSCLC harboring EGFR mutations (exon 19 deletion or L858R mutation) who received osimertinib between September 2018 and July 2022 at a single institution. The efficacy of osimertinib was compared between patients with postoperative recurrent NSCLC (postoperative group) and those with Stage IV NSCLC (Stage IV group).ResultsAmong a total of 172 patients treated with osimertinib, 52 were classified into the postoperative group and 120 into the Stage IV group. The response rate (58.1% vs. 61.8%, p = 0.836) and progression‐free survival (hazard ratio [HR]: 0.854, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.558–1.306, p = 0.465) were not significantly different between the postoperative and Stage IV groups. Overall survival (OS) was significantly longer in the postoperative group than in the Stage IV group (median: 39.2 months and 28.5 months, respectively; HR: 0.521, 95% CI: 0.293–0.927, p = 0.024). In the multivariable analysis of OS, postoperative recurrent disease and performance status were independent favorable prognostic factors.ConclusionsPostoperative recurrent disease was an independent favorable prognostic factor in patients with NSCLC harboring EGFR mutations treated with osimertinib.Keywords: EGFR, mutation, non–small‐cell lung cancer, osimertinib, postoperative recurrent

Introduction

For patients with epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR)‐positive (exon 19 deletions or L858R point mutations) advanced or metastatic non–small‐cell lung cancer (NSCLC), treatment with EGFR‐tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) is recommended by various guidelines [1, 2, 3, 4]. Osimertinib is a third‐generation EGFR‐TKI that demonstrates efficacy against NSCLC harboring EGFR‐TKI‐sensitizing and/or EGFR p.Thr790Met (T790M)‐resistant mutations [5, 6, 7]. The FLAURA study showed the efficacy and safety of osimertinib compared with those of gefitinib or erlotinib among patients with previously untreated advanced NSCLC harboring EGFR mutations. The trial showed that progression‐free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) were significantly longer in patients receiving osimertinib than in those receiving gefitinib or erlotinib [8, 9]. The ADAURA trial showed that disease‐free survival and OS were significantly longer in the osimertinib adjuvant therapy group than in the placebo group among patients with resected Stage II–IIIA NSCLC who had exon 19 deletions or L858R point mutations in EGFR [10, 11, 12]. Following these findings, osimertinib has been approved as a first‐line treatment for advanced EGFR‐mutated NSCLC and as an adjuvant treatment for resected EGFR‐mutated Stage II–IIIA NSCLC in Japan.

Previous clinical trials that examined the effects of EGFR‐TKIs in patients with advanced EGFR‐mutated NSCLC included patients with postoperative recurrence [8, 13, 14, 15]. Therefore, postoperative recurrent NSCLC is usually treated according to the strategy used for patients with Stage IV EGFR‐mutated NSCLC. However, the efficacy of EGFR‐TKIs has not been fully evaluated in patients with postoperative recurrent EGFR‐mutated NSCLC. Our previous retrospective study showed that both PFS and OS were better in patients with postoperative recurrent EGFR‐mutated NSCLC receiving gefitinib than in those with Stage IV disease [16]. Owing to the unclear difference in the efficacy of osimertinib regarding postoperative recurrent and Stage IV diseases, we performed this retrospective study to evaluate its efficacy in postoperative recurrent EGFR‐mutated NSCLC in comparison with Stage IV NSCLC.

Materials and Methods

Patients

From September 2018 to July 2022, patients with postoperative recurrent or Stage IV EGFR‐sensitizing mutation‐positive NSCLC who had not previously received treatment for advanced disease or osimertinib at Shizuoka Cancer Center (Shizuoka, Japan) were retrospectively evaluated based on data from their medical records. EGFR mutations were identified using approved EGFR gene tests at diagnosis. These patients were categorized into two groups: postoperative recurrent NSCLC (postoperative group) and Stage IV at diagnosis (Stage IV group), based on the 8th edition of the TNM Classification. The postoperative group included patients who had relapsed with distant metastases after curative surgery and received osimertinib. Patients who had only local recurrence postoperatively without distant metastasis at the time of initial recurrence were excluded. In our study, local recurrence was characterized as disease recurrence occurring at the surgical resection margin, ipsilateral hilum, and/or mediastinum [17]. The Stage IV group included patients with advanced NSCLC who treated with osimertinib as first‐line chemotherapy. Both groups also included patients receiving palliative radiation therapy before osimertinib initiation.

The performance status (PS) was assessed using the Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group PS scale. Patients were also categorized based on the number of metastatic lesions as ≤ 3 and > 3 according to previous reports [18, 19]. Nonmeasurable lesions such as pleural effusion, cardiac effusion, pleural dissemination, and meningeal dissemination were counted as one lesion in our study. We defined the complication of interstitial lung disease (ILD) as interstitial lung abnormalities—a set of radiological abnormalities including subpleural reticulation, ground glass opacities, centrilobular nodules, traction bronchiectasis, honeycombing, and architectural distortion [20, 21]. Subsequent therapy was defined as treatment after progressive disease (PD). In cases in which osimertinib was continued after PD (beyond PD), subsequent therapy was defined as osimertinib. This study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Institutional Review Board at Shizuoka Cancer Center (approval no.: J2023‐39‐2023‐1). Due to its retrospective design, the institutional review board of Shizuoka Cancer Center waived the requirement for informed consent. Patients were given the opportunity to opt out of study enrollment via a web page.

Evaluation and Statistical Analysis

Tumor response to EGFR‐TKIs and chemotherapy was evaluated in accordance with the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST version 1.1) [22]. The treatment response was generally assessed using computed tomography performed every 2–3 months, with evaluation frequency appropriately modified based on physician judgment. All categorical variables were analyzed using the chi‐squared test or Fisher's exact test, as appropriate. PFS was defined as the time from the initiation of EGFR‐TKI therapy or chemotherapy to disease progression or death. Postprogression survival (PPS) was recorded as the time from disease progression until death or the date of the last follow‐up. OS was defined as the time from the start of EGFR‐TKI therapy or chemotherapy until death. PFS, PPS, and OS, were calculated using the Kaplan–Meier method and compared via the log‐rank test. We also performed univariable and multivariable Cox proportional hazards analysis of OS. Factors with p < 0.05 in a univariate log‐rank test and potential confounding factors identified in previous reports were included in a multivariate Cox proportional hazards analysis. All statistical tests were two‐sided, and statistical significance was defined as p < 0.05. All statistical analyses were performed using EZR (Saitama Medical Center, Jichi Medical University, Saitama, Japan), a graphical interface for R (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) [23].

Results

Patient Characteristics

This study included a total of 172 patients. The postoperative recurrent group included 52 patients, while the Stage IV group included 120 patients. Patient baseline characteristics are presented in Table 1. Distributions of sex, age, and smoking history were similar between the two groups. Almost all patients were pathologically diagnosed with adenocarcinoma. Patients in the postoperative recurrent group had better PS than those in the Stage IV group (p = 0.038). L858R mutations were more frequent in the postoperative recurrent group than in the Stage IV group (p = 0.034). The number of metastatic organs and brain metastases did not differ between the postoperative recurrent and Stage IV groups.

Efficacy of Osimertinib

The median follow‐up period from the osimertinib treatment initiation was 31.0 months (95% confidence interval [CI]: 24.8–35.5 months). The response rate (RR) of osimertinib in the postoperative recurrent and Stage IV groups was 34.6% and 60.8%, respectively. Among cases with measurable lesions, the RR in the postoperative recurrent group was comparable to that in the Stage IV group (58.1% vs. 61.8%, p = 0.836) (Table 2). PFS did not significantly differ between the postoperative and Stage IV groups (median: 27.0 and 16.3 months, hazard ratio [HR]: 0.854, 95% CI: 0.558–1.306, p = 0.465) (Figure 1). PPS was significantly longer in the postoperative recurrent group compared with that in the Stage IV group (median: 17.4 months and 12.0 months, HR: 0.483, 95% CI: 0.269–0.869, p = 0.013) (Figure S1). OS was significantly longer in the postoperative recurrent group compared with that in the Stage IV group (median: 39.2 and 28.5 months, HR: 0.521, 95% CI: 0.293–0.927, p = 0.024) (Figure 1).

Univariable and Multivariable Analyses for OS

Table 3 presents the results of univariable and multivariable analyses for OS. In the univariable analysis, the postoperative recurrent group and PS (0–1) were associated with favorable OS. The multivariable analysis also demonstrated that being in the postoperative recurrent group (HR: 0.544, 95% CI: 0.301–0.984, p = 0.044) and having a PS of 0–1 (HR: 0.359, 95% CI: 0.216–0.594, p < 0.001) were independent favorable prognostic factors.

Subsequent Anticancer Treatment After Treatment With Osimertinib

Among patients experiencing disease progression after treatment with osimertinib, the rate of patients receiving subsequent anticancer treatment was not different between the two groups (50% in the postoperative recurrent group and 54.2% in the Stage IV group, p = 0.622). In the postoperative recurrent group, local therapy and osimertinib beyond PD tended to be more frequently used as subsequent treatments than in the Stage IV group (36.7% and 18.6%, p = 0.073 and 30.0% and 14.3%, p = 0.291, respectively) (Table 4). The median time from disease progression to initiation of second‐line drug therapy was 28 days (range: 1–227 days) in the postoperative group and 17 days (range: 0–427 days) in the Stage IV group (p = 0.402). The PFS and OS from the start of second‐line drug therapy were not significantly different between the postoperative recurrent and Stage IV groups (median PFS: 4.3 and 5.4 months, HR: 0.802, 95% CI: 0.427–1.506, p = 0.490; median OS: 15.5 and 13.8 months, HR: 0.640, 95% CI: 0.299–1.371, p = 0.246, respectively) (Figure 2).

Incidence of ILD Related to Osimertinib

Eight patients in the postoperative recurrent group (15.4%) and 12 in the Stage IV group (10.0%) developed ILD during treatment with osimertinib (p = 0.312). The number of patients with grade 3 ILD was one in the postoperative recurrent group and four in the Stage IV group. One patient in the Stage IV group died of ILD.

Discussion

This retrospective study showed that postoperative recurrence was an independent favorable prognostic factor in patients with NSCLC harboring EGFR exon 19 deletions or L858R mutations who were treated with osimertinib. Several studies have suggested that patients with postoperative recurrent NSCLC demonstrate more favorable outcomes compared to those diagnosed with Stage IV disease. Sekine et al. reported that patients with postoperative recurrent NSCLC had better OS than those with Stage IV NSCLC in their multivariable analysis (HR: 0.66, 95% CI: 0.54–0.81, p < 0.001) [24]. In a retrospective study that compared survival between patients with postoperative recurrence and those with Stage IV NSCLC regardless of EGFR mutations, OS was better in the postoperative recurrent group (adjusted HR: 0.72, 95% CI: 0.60–0.87, p < 0.001) [25]. In a phase III study that compared cisplatin plus docetaxel with gefitinib in patients with EGFR mutated NSCLC, PFS was significantly longer in postoperative recurrent disease than in Stage IIIB/IV disease (HR: 0.433, 95% CI: 0.290–0.649, p < 0.001) [13]. Our previous retrospective study showed that both PFS and OS were better in patients with postoperative recurrent disease than in those with Stage IV NSCLC harboring EGFR mutations treated with gefitinib [16]. In the present study analyzing patients treated with osimertinib for NSCLC harboring EGFR mutations, postoperative recurrent disease had a significantly better OS than Stage IV disease (HR: 0.540, 95% CI: 0.299–0.979, p = 0.042), consistent with the findings reported in previous studies [24, 25]. However, PFS did not differ between the two groups in our study. Differences between PFS and OS results may have several reasons. First, patients who received local therapy, such as radiotherapy and surgery, tended to be more frequent in the postoperative recurrent group than in the Stage IV group. Although there were no significant differences in the time from disease progression to initiation of second‐line drug therapy between the postoperative group and Stage IV group, the local therapy may have delayed the initiation of subsequent anticancer therapy. Further, patients eligible for the local therapy demonstrated better survival outcomes compared to those with systemic PD in patients with advanced EGFR mutation positive NSCLC at the time of disease progression after first line osimertinib [26]. Second, the postoperative recurrent group tended to receive subsequent anticancer treatments more frequently than the Stage IV group, although the difference was not statistically significant. Third, OS at the start of second‐line drug therapy tended to be longer in the postoperative recurrent group than in the Stage IV group, albeit with no statistically significant difference. Fourth, patients in the postoperative group demonstrated a trend toward fewer metastatic lesions compared with those in the Stage IV group, although without any significant difference. Previous studies have already shown that postoperative recurrence in EGFR‐mutated NSCLC was associated with a more favorable prognosis than Stage IV disease at initial diagnosis [13, 16]. Patients in the Stage IV group had significantly more organ metastasis than those in the postoperative group in another study which investigated the difference in efficacy of gefitinib between the postoperative group and the Stage IV group [16]. The other study reported significantly longer OS in patients with oligometastatic NSCLC compared to those with nonoligometastatic NSCLC [18]. These difference may have contributed to the longer OS in the postoperative group despite similar PFS in both groups in our study. Fifth, the EGFR mutation distribution was unbalanced. A previous report showed that PFS in patients receiving osimertinib tended to be longer with exon 19 deletions than with exon 21 L858R [8]. In our study, more patients with EGFR exon 21 L858R were present in the postoperative recurrent group than in the Stage IV group, which might have influenced our results regarding PFS. Sixth, each physician decided the timing of the response assessment, which might have introduced variance in PFS. However, OS is a strongly reliable endpoint, and our study showed postoperative recurrence was an independent, favorable prognostic factor.

ILD is an important adverse event in patients treated with EGFR‐TKIs. ILD was reported in 4% of the osimertinib group in the FLAURA study [8]. In a subset of Japanese patients, 12.3% of patients who received osimertinib developed ILD [27]. A retrospective real‐world analysis of osimertinib in Japanese patients (Osi‐Fact study) showed that the incidence of osimertinib‐induced ILD was 12.8% [28]. The incidence of ILD in our study was consistent with these results. Furthermore, in our study, the incidence of ILD tended to be higher in the postoperative group than in the Stage IV group, although this difference was not statistically significant. Previous reports have identified some risk factors for drug‐induced ILD in NSCLC [29, 30, 31]. Smoking, reduced normal lung on computed tomography, and pre‐existing chronic ILD have been reported as ILD risk factors in Japanese patients with advanced NSCLC treated with gefitinib or chemotherapy [32]. Another report identified a history or concurrence of ILD as a potential factor associated with ILD occurring in Japanese EGFR T790M‐positive NSCLC patients receiving osimertinib therapy [33]. A retrospective analysis of Japanese patients receiving first‐line osimertinib identified smoking history as an independent predictor of ILD [34]. Based on our results and those of previous studies, postoperative recurrence may be a risk factor for ILD in patients receiving osimertinib. However, no cases of serious ILD were observed in the postoperative recurrent group.

This study had several limitations. First, this study was a single‐center retrospective analysis with a relatively small sample size. However, few reports have explored the differences in osimertinib efficacy between postoperative recurrence and Stage IV disease at diagnosis; thus, this report addresses an important knowledge gap. Our findings need to be further validated through multicenter studies with a larger sample size. Second, the timing of the radiological assessment was at the physicians' discretion, which might have introduced variance in objective RR and PFS. Considering this limitation, we also assessed the OS, which is a reliable endpoint. Third, a few patients in the postoperative recurrence group received adjuvant chemotherapy with other EGFR‐TKI. This might have slightly affected the PFS and OS of the postoperative group.

In conclusion, this retrospective study suggests that postoperative recurrence is an independent favorable prognostic factor in patients with NSCLC harboring EGFR mutations treated with osimertinib.

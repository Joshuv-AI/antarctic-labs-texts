IFPHOR0030 How Can Midwives Utilise the Teachable Moment in Pregnancy for Women: A Focus on the Role of Communication
Authors: Hannah M Brennan, Sarah Peters, Christine Furber, Debbie M Smith
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIn pregnancy, engaging in optimal health behaviours is critical to reduce the risk of adverse pregnancy outcomes. However, engagement in behaviours such as smoking, alcohol use, an unhealthy diet, lack of physical activity and vaccination non-adherence remains sub-optimal. Pregnancy is a teachable moment where women have increased motivation and more opportunities for positive behaviour change. Midwives are ideally placed to support this change. The aim of this research was to understand midwives’ experiences and communication behaviours when discussing behaviour change with pregnant women.MethodsA qualitative systematic review and meta-synthesis of 22 studies exploring midwives’ experiences of discussing health behaviour change; and a qualitative non-participant observation of 21 antenatal booking appointments, exploring midwives’ communication behaviours, analysed using inductive content analysis.FindingsThe systematic review revealed that midwives valued the midwife-woman relationship, engaged in reflective and tailored behaviour change communication and experienced practical barriers. Often behaviour change conversations were not prioritised. The observational study revealed that midwives were skilful behaviour change communicators, able to knowledgably provide information, individualise support and navigate sensitive topics. However, some of their communication behaviours suggested that taboos persist relating to typically stigmatised behaviours, where midwives acknowledged the delicate topic, focused on risk avoidance in favour of health promotion and overlooked women’s disclosures.Discussion and future researchThese studies have provided a detailed insight into midwives’ experiences and communication behaviours when discussing health behaviours. Future research should explore opportunities to enhance midwives’ behaviour change communication, so that midwives may further contribute to improving women’s health.

Differentiating infected from vaccinated animals: Validation and application of NH-AGID assay in heifers immunized with Brucella abortus strain A19-∆VirB12
Authors: Feng Ye, Xiao-jing Ma, Li-ya Liu, Wen-xi Gu, Wen-liang Yan, Xu Zhang, Jia-rui Zhang, Xin-ping Yi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractDifferentiating infected from vaccinated animals is crucial for clinical diagnosis in brucellosis control. The objective of this study was to assess the performance of native hapten agar gel immuno-diffusion(NH-AGID) in differential identification of infected heifers from vaccined with strain A19-∆VirB12 and identifying shedding animals at the serologic level. 30 heifers were vaccined with A19-∆VirB12, and corresponding serum and vaginal swab samples were collected at predetermined intervals. Serological assessments were conducted using the Rose-bengal test, serum agglutination test, competitive enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay and NH-AGID. Brucella-positive sera were further distinguished using differential identification ELISA and NH-AGID. Vaginal swab DNA was tested using AMOS-PCR and dual real-time fluorescence PCR. Results showed that NH-AGID demonstrated a high specificity and positive predictive values (PPV) for diagnosis of brucellosis. Additionally, the diagnostic accuracy of NH-AGID for infected and shedding animals was more than 90 %. Heifers vaccinated with A19-∆VirB12 were serologically confirmed as infected by NH-AGID and also tested positive by PCR. The application of NH-AGID effectively identified infected and shedding animals from vaccination with A19-∆VirB12, thereby supporting the brucellosis decontamination efforts.Keywords: Brucellosis, A19-∆VirB12 vaccine, Native hapten, Differential identification

Introduction

Brucellosis, a zoonotic infectious disease, poses substantial challenges to public health and livestock agriculture (Shakir, 2021). Globally, twelve Brucella species have been identified to date, with a host range encompassing domestic, wild, and marine animals (Kurmanov et al., 2022). Infected animals serve as the primary transmission reservoirs for brucellosis, and human Brucella infections have been reported in over 170 countries worldwide (Ntivuguruzwa et al., 2022; Pappas et al., 2006). Since 2012, China has consistently reported an average incidence rate of human brucellosis exceeding 3 cases per 100,000 individuals ("China Statistical Yearbook (2021)", 2021). Therefore, strengthening preventive and control measures in livestock populations is critical to effectively curbing the spread of human brucellosis. To date, vaccination and culling of infected animals remain among the most vital and effective interventions, especially in high-prevalence regions (Heidary et al., 2022).

Currently, the primary vaccines used to prevent animal brucellosis are attenuated live vaccines, such as Brucella abortus (B. abortus) S19, B. abortus A19, and Brucella melitensis (B. melitensis) Rev.1(Curina et al., 2018; Ferreira et al., 2023). However, vaccination-induced antibodies can cause serological interference (Lalsiamthara & Lee, 2017). Variations in immunization dose and schedule, vaccine efficacy, and individual animal responses can result in a minority of animals being infected by wild-type or vaccine strains and continuously shedding the bacteria, contaminating the breeding environment (Averaimo et al., 2022; Dadar & Alamian, 2021; Samartino et al., 2000). Genetically engineered vaccines, particularly gene-deleted vaccines, represent a key strategy to address the challenge of serological interference (Bao et al., 2017; Chacón-Díaz et al., 2021). Among these, the A19-ΔVirB12 vaccine—characterized by deletion of the VirB12 gene—is the first brucellosis vaccine developed for cattle and has been officially registered as a novel veterinary drug (Ministry of Agriculture & Rural Affairs of the People's Republic of China Announcement No. 315, 2020). The application of the A19-∆VirB12 vaccine enables the differentiation between vaccinated animals and those naturally infected at both molecular and serological levels (Ma, 2022; Rolán et al., 2008; Yang et al., 2021; Yi, 2013). Despite the use of Differential Identification ELISA (DI-ELISA) concerning the A19-∆VirB12 vaccine, some non-specific reactions still exist (Tian et al., 2020). Therefore, high-specificity methods are necessary to validate the outcomes of this differential identification process.

The native hapten (NH) is a polysaccharide component extracted from smooth Brucella bacteria using a phenol-water extraction technique. NH has demonstrated reliable diagnostic efficacy as an antigen for detecting brucellosis in human and animal subjects (Abalos et al., 2000; Fernandex-Lago & Diaz, 1986; Ramírez-Pfeiffer et al., 2008). Additionally, literature indicated a strong correlation between positive NH precipitation lines in the Agar gel immunodiffusion (AGID) test and animals actively shedding the bacteria (Jones et al., 1980; WOAH, 2025).

In reality, identifying and removing these shedding animals is vital for brucellosis control. Bacterial isolation and PCR are gold standard for Brucella detection, but they exhibit relatively low sensitivity and demand stringent biosafety measures (Ntivuguruzwa et al., 2022; Sibhat et al., 2024). Therefore, for serological diagnosis of infected and shedding animals, we believe the NH-AGID test may serve as an excellent tool with the potential to enhance the accuracy of detection results.

Materials and methods

Overview of farm and animals

The farm, with a history of abortions and positive Brucella tests, implemented control measures against brucellosis by vaccinating calves and adult cattle with A19 at a dosage of 6 × 1010 CFU.All 4–8 month old heifers with a total of 30 in the herd were used in this study. Serum samples were collected before administering the A19-∆VirB12 vaccine. Adhering to GB/T 18646–2018 standards, the Rose Bengal Test (RBT) was employed as the initial screening method, followed by the Serum Agglutination Test (SAT) and competitive Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay (cELISA) as confirmatory tests, to ensure all heifers were brucellosis-negative before vaccination.

Samples

The A19-ΔVirB12 vaccine (Tecon, 2022002, EXP20231009) was administered subcutaneously to the heifers at a dose of 6 × 1010 CFU. Blood samples were obtained on the 15th, 30th, 60th, 90th, 120th, 150th, and 180th days following vaccination, and the serum was subsequently isolated and analyzed. Vaginal swabs were collected from heifers that tested positive via DI-ELISA. In addition, 40 bovine sera were collected from unimmunized cattle as positive controls, which identified as Brucella-positive by AMOS-PCR.

Reagents and test procedures

The RBT and SAT antigens were procured from APHA (UK), while the cELISA kit was obtained from Lijian Company (China, Qingdao). The in-house DI-ELISA (VirB12) kit was developed internally, and the NH-AGID kit was supplied by Rui'erweite Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (China, Qingdao). The DNA extraction kit used was provided by Qiagen. Initially, sera were screened using RBT, with positive samples further confirmed by SAT and cELISA. Also, all sera were screened by NH-AGID testing to verify performance. Heifers testing positive for Brucella antibodies underwent differential identification between vaccinated and infected animals using the in-house DI-ELISA (VirB12) kit and NH-AGID kit. DNA extracted from vaginal swabs was evaluated using AMOS-PCR and dual real-time fluorescent quantitative PCR(as shown in Supplementary 1).

Determination of result

A serum SAT titer above 1:100 is considered positive for brucellosis. cELISA results were determined using the formula: PI= (OD450nm of negtive control-OD450nm of serum) / OD450nm of negtive control × 100 %. A serum sample is considered positive when PI > 0.5 and negative if PI ≤ 0.5. The protocol for utilizing the in-house DI-ELISA (VirB12) kit was as follows: Antibody Index(AI)= OD450 of sample/ OD450 of the APHA standard positive serum × 100 %. An animal is classified as infected if AI > 0.6 and vaccinated with A19-△VirB12 if AI ≤ 0.6(Serological detection method of bovine brucellosis A19 delta VirB12 vaccine, 2022). When utilizing the NH-AGID kit, if both Brucella lipopolysaccharide (LPS) and native hapten (NH) precipitation lines appeared (LPS+ & NH+), the animal was identified as Brucella-positive and actively shedding. If only the LPS line appeared (LPS+), it suggested exposure to Brucella. The absence of both lines (LPS- & NH-) indicated a Brucella-negative status. AMOS-PCR results were interpreted according to reference (Ntivuguruzwa et al., 2022), while dual real-time fluorescent PCR results were assessed per reference (Ma, 2022).

Statistical analysis of data

Data analysis was conducted using Microsoft Excel 2010 (Microsoft Office, CA, USA) and IBM SPSS Statistics 25.0 (IBM Corporation; Armonk, NY, USA). SAT and cELISA results were utilized as confirmatory tests to compare the sensitivity, specificity, coincidence rate, and consistency coefficient (Kappa value) of NH-AGID. A Kappa value of ≤0.4 suggests poor agreement, 0.4 < Kappa < 0.75 indicates moderate to high agreement, and a Kappa value of ≥0.75 signifies excellent agreement (Dong et al., 2021).

Ethics statement

All animal experiments were approved by the Committee on Ethics and Welfare of Institute of Veterinary Research, Xinjiang Academy of Animal Science(20230003, dated: 28/01/2023). Blood and swab samples were collected from the heifers with the help of local veterinary officials as per standard operation procedures of the Department of Agriculture and Rural Affairs of Xinjiang, China, and at the free will of the animal, owners to participate in the study. No discrimination was done against apparently healthy or affected animals. Written consent of free will to participate in the study was obtained from the animal owner.

Result

Sero-dynamics post-vaccination

SAT titers of Brucella antibody in 30 heifers vaccinated with A19-△VirB12 were monitored as shown in Fig. 1. The peak seropositivity rate was 86.7 % (26/30) at 15 days post-vaccination, subsequently declining over time to 6.7 % (2/30) by 180 days post-vaccination. The average SAT titer at 15 days post-vaccination was around 1:400, decreasing to approximately 1:100 by 60 days and then staying below 1:100 from 90 to 180 days post-vaccination. The serum antibody titers between the 25th and 75th percentiles at 15, 30, and 60 days post-vaccination predominantly ranged from 1:200 to 1:800, 1:100 to 1:400, and 0 to 1:200, respectively.

High specificity of NH-AGID in the detection of vaccine serums

To validate the detection performance of NH-AGID, 210 sera post-vaccinated with A19-∆VirB12 and 40 sera confirmed infected by PCR were analyzed. The results are presented in Table 1. The performance of NH-AGID was evaluated with the outcomes of SAT and cELISA serving as benchmarks. The sensitivity and specificity of NH-AGID was 85.71 % and 100 %, respectively. The coincidence rate was 92.8 %, the positive predictive value (PPV) was 100 %, the negative predictive value (NPV) was 87.32 %, and the kappa values were 0.86 for NH-AGID.

Result of differential identification

The in-house DI-iELISA kit (VirB12) is pre-assembled and has demonstrated effective differentiation of serum samples confirmed positive by SAT after vaccination with the A19-△VirB12. The kit exhibits outstanding performance with a sensitivity, specificity, and performance index of 98.9 %, 94.6 %, and 193.5, respectively (Serological detection method of bovine brucellosis A19 delta VirB12 vaccine, 2022).

A total of 126 Brucella-positive serum samples were analyzed, includes 40 sera identified as positive by PCR and 86 brucella-antibody positive sera post-vaccination. Differential identification results of DI-ELISA and NH-AGID of 40 infected sera were consistent and showed 39 sera infected with wild strains. Among 86 serum samples positive for Brucella antibodies post-vaccination, result of DI-ELISA revealed that 79 samples exhibited vaccine-induced immune antibodies, while the remaining 7 samples demonstrated antibodies specific to wild-type strain infection. Meanwhile, result of NH-AGID showed that 82 serum samples were vaccine-induced immune antibodies and 4 were wild-type strain infection as shown in Fig. 2. The findings revealed no serum samples exhibiting infected within 90 days after vaccination. Notably, these serum samples which identified as infected originated from two heifers.

Results of differential identification.

The majority results of DI-ELISA and NH-AGID differential identification were negative 15–180 days after immunization with A19-△VirB12 vaccine, with positive results occurring 90 days after immunization.

Dual real-time fluorescent PCR and AMOS-PCR assay

Seven DNA samples extracted from vaginal swabs of two heifers, determined to be infected by DI-ELISA (VirB12) (with four of them also confirmed as LPS+ & NH+ through NH-AGID) and three DNA samples from negtive heifers, were analyzed using dual real-time fluorescence PCR and AMOS-PCR. However, only one of the samples showed a positive result. Swab DNA was identified by dual real-time fluorescence quantitative PCR and determined not to be the strain A19-△VirB12 vaccine. Meanwhile AMOS-PCR results showed that the heifer was infected with B.melitensis Ⅲ species as shown in Fig. 3.

Disscusion

Differentiating vaccinated animals from naturally infected ones and identifying carrier animals remain major challenges to brucellosis decontamination efforts. In this study, we demonstrate that the NH-AGID assay serves as an effective differential identification tool for heifers immunized with A19-ΔVirB12 at serological level, enabling reliable discrimination between infected/shedding animals and vaccinated counterparts prior to mating.

The A19-ΔVirB12 vaccine has been shown to induce robust humoral and cellular responses in both mice and cattle (Yang et al., 2021). In this study, heifers vaccinated with A19-ΔVirB12 demonstrated a strong humoral response, with an 86.7 % seropositivity rate and a mean SAT titer of 1:400 at 15 days post-vaccination. However, antibody positivity dropped to 20 % by 90 days post-vaccination, mirroring the immune response observed with the strain 19 (Blasco et al., 2022). Comparative research indicated that buffaloes vaccinated with the mutant S19Δper strain also showed the highest IgG titers at 15 days post-vaccination, which then declined over time, aligning with the findings of this study (Chaudhuri et al., 2021). Conversely, 80 % of sheep vaccinated with B.melitensis 16MΔvjbR remained ELISA-positive 90 days post-vaccination (Castaño-Zubieta et al., 2021). The variance in antibody persistence between these studies is attributed to the higher virulence of B.melitensis compared to B. abortus (Heidary et al., 2022).

In this study, NH-AGID demonstrated high sensitivity in detecting PCR-positive sera compared with results of SAT and cELISA (Hajia et al., 2013). In contrast, NH-AGID exhibits relatively low sensitivity of 85.71 % when detecting immune sera, primarily associated with its subpar performance in serum samples taken 15–30 days post-vaccination. This phenomenon may be related to bacterial activity within the animal's body. Additionally, NH-AGID exhibited high specificity and PPV, indicating its suitability as a confirmatory test for detecting brucellosis. Elderbrook conducted a comparison of compliance rates between two ELISA kits and NH-AGID, with results also indicating NH-AGID's high specificity (>92 %)(Elderbrook et al., 2020). Dogs that initially tested positive on the 2-Mercaptoethanol Rapid Slide Agglutination Test (2ME-RSAT) were subsequently confirmed with NH-AGID due to its high specificity (Whitten et al., 2019). Combination of RBT and NH-AGID was effective in diagnosing brucellosis in sheep infected with Brucella melitensis (Marín et al., 1999).

Deletion proteins are commonly used as test antigens for distinguishing mutant and parental strains of brucellosis (Bao et al., 2017; Uslu & Erganis, 2021). However, using VirB12 as a diagnostic antigen has potential for false-positive results (Tian et al., 2020). NH-AGID demonstrated diagnostic specificity consistent with CFT, despite its lower sensitivity compared to methods such as RBT, S-LPS based iELISA, and FPA(WOAH, 2025). Previous research has demonstrated the effectiveness of an ELISA using NH as an antigen in distinguishing positive serum samples (time of collection unspecified) from vaccinated serum (collected 90 days post-vaccine) (Abalos et al., 2000). In this study, NH-AGID yielded positive results for LPS and NH antigens in four serum samples tested post-immunization. The comparison of results between the in-house DI-ELISA (VirB12) and NH-AGID kits showed a high concordance rate. Specific antibodies against VirB12 protein can typically be detected three weeks after infection (Rolán et al., 2008), thus DI-ELISA was able to detect infections earlier than NH-AGID. According to Keid et al., NH-AGID showed 100 % specificity in dogs diagnosed with Brucella infection through PCR and bacterial culture (Keid et al., 2009), which consistent with the results of the 40 PCR-positive sera in this study. Only 1 out of 4 samples with NH+ had a positive PCR result from the vaginal swab, this discrepancy in test results may indicate a lower probability of detecting Brucella in vaginal swabs compared to milk and lymph nodes (Ebrahimi et al., 2014; Katsiolis et al., 2022) or intermittently shedding in animals (Blasco et al., 1994). Additionally, positive PCR results are more easily detected in swab samples after incubation, which may have also contributed to the low-positive PCR results. Data from WOAH showed that animals may appear NH+ within 2 months after being immunized with the brucellosis vaccine, which consistent with the findings of this study. Similar findings were observed in another study (Erdenebaatar et al., 2002). However, NH+ was not observed in serum samples within 90 days after vaccination with A19-ΔVirB12, possibly due to the reduced virulence of this vaccine compared to conventional vaccines (Yi, 2013).

In conclusion, by application of NH-AGID we could accurately differentiate infected and shedding heifer from vaccinated with A19-△VirB12 at a serological level.

Comprehensive evaluation of drought resistance of Melon (Cucumis melo L.) based on morphological and physiological traits
Authors: Kaili Ren, Weiping Kong, Taoxia Tang, Yonggang Yang, Yongquan Su, Haiping Lv, Haoye Wu, Caixia Liu, Yuping Wang, Hong Cheng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundDrought stress significantly inhibits melon seedling growth and ultimately reduces fruit yield. Consequently, identifying drought-resistant germplasms, elucidating the physiological and biochemical mechanisms underlying plant drought responses, and establishing reliable drought resistance evaluation indices are critical objectives for advancing drought-resistant breeding of melon.ResultsThis study measured 30 growth, physiological, and biochemical indices of melon seedlings under drought conditions. By integrating correlation analysis, cluster analysis, and regression analysis, the drought resistance of 15 melon germplasm resources were evaluated, and key drought resistance indices were identified. The results demonstrated that drought stress significantly reduced the dry and fresh weight, total root length, root surface area, root volume, root tips, and forks of melon seedlings, but significantly increased the root-shoot ratio of plants. Additionally, drought stress significantly reduced leaf relative water content (RWC), increased pigment content (chlorophyll a, chlorophyll b, carotene, and total pigment) and regulatory substance content (soluble sugar and γ-aminobutyric acid, GABA). Furthermore, drought stress elevated the levels of hydrogen peroxide (H2O2), malondialdehyde (MDA), and oxidized glutathione (GSSG), while reducing superoxide anion (O2·−−) and GSH/GSSG, indicating oxidative stress in melon seedlings. Using D-value cluster analysis, the 15 melon germplasms were classified into 5 categories: strongly drought-resistant group (17Y1 and 17Y11), drought-resistant group (GT3, 23G36, YJM, 23G33 and 23G37), moderately drought-resistant group (GT4, 22P8 and GT1), drought-sensitive group (YD, TN and LB946), and highly drought-sensitive group (22P7 and 22G40). Stepwise regression analysis identified the following indices as objective evaluation criteria for melon seedling drought resistance: drought injury index (DI), chlorophyll a content, relative electrical conductivity (REC), and betaine content.ConclusionsTaken together, this study screened 2 strongly drought-resistant melon materials, 5 drought-resistant materials, 3 moderately drought-resistant materials, 3 drought-sensitive materials and 2 highly drought-sensitive materials. At the same time, drought injury index (DI), chlorophyll a content, relative electrical conductivity (REC), and betaine content were selected as the drought resistance indices of melon. These findings lay a theoretical foundation for melon drought resistance breeding.

Introduction

Melon (Cucumis melo L.) is an economically important crop of the Cucurbitaceae family, prized for its crisp texture, rich vitamin content, and excellent flavor. Due to its short cultivation cycle and high input-output ratio, melon is a highly profitable fruit crop. China is the world’s largest producer of melon, with a production area of 395,811 hectares and a total output of 14,501,906 tons in 2023, accounting for 36.23% and 49.09% of the global production area and output, respectively [1]. Gansu province is a characteristic and advantageous producing area of melon. It has world-renowned ‘honeydew melon’ [2], Geographical Indication of P.R. China ‘Minqin Melon’ [3] and ‘Huahai Melon’, and Agro-product Geograhpical Indications ‘Guazhou Melon’ and ‘Qingyang Cantaloupe’.

Drought is a major environmental stressor that limits agricultural productivity worldwide. With global warming, drought caused by climate change poses a significant threat to agricultural systems [4]. The IPCC’s sixth assessment report highlights that drought impacts will intensify as global temperatures continue to rise [5]. In China, drought directly threatens food security and socioeconomic stability [6–8]. Therefore, evaluating plant drought resistance, identifying drought-resistant germplasms, and breeding resilient varieties are critical for sustainable agriculture. While drought resistance identification had been conducted on crops such as wheat (Triticum aestivum L.) [9–12], barley (Hordeum vulgare L.) [13–15], maize (Zea mays L.) [16–18], rice (Oryza sativa L.) [19–21], soybean (Glycine) [22–24], sorghum (Sorghum bicolor L.) [25–27], cotton (Gossypium) [28–30], alfalfa (Medicago sativa L.) [31–33], chickpea (Cicer arietinum L.) [34–36], fennel (Foeniculum vulgare) [37], potato (Solanum tuberosum L.) [38], lettuce (Lactuca sativa) [39, 40], onion (Allium cepa L.) [41] and watermelon (Citrullus lanatus (Thunb.) Matsum. et Nakai) [42, 43]. Given melon’s susceptibility to water stress, identifying drought resistant germplasms is essential for improving its adaptability to arid conditions. However, the research on melon drought focused on the excavation of drought-resistant genes, and there are few studies on the identification of drought-resistant materials [44, 45].

Drought stress inhibits plant growth and reduces crop yield [46]. Plants employ various physiological and biochemical mechanisms to mitigate drought effects, including root structural modifications to enhance water uptake [32, 47, 48], stomatal closure to reduce water loss [14, 22, 49], activation of reactive oxygen species scavenging pathways [50–53], and accumulation of compatible solutes and protective molecules [50, 52, 54]. Understanding these responses in melon is crucial for drought resistance breeding. This study aimed to elucidate the physiological and biochemical responses of melon seedlings to drought stress by measuring 30 growth and stressrelated indices. Drought stress index (DSI), phenotypic plasticity index (PPI), and membership function were calculated, and the drought resistance of 15 melon germplasms was evaluated using multivariate analysis. Additionally, the evaluation indices of drought resistance of melon seedlings were screened by stepwise regression analysis. The results provide a foundation for identifying drought-resistant melon germplasms and improving breeding efficiency.

Material planting and experimental treatment

Plant Cultivation

On 7 September 2024, melon seeds were soaked for 24 h, and then germinated at 28 °C. On 9 September 2024, uniformly germinated seeds were transplanted into plastic pots (diameter 12.5 cm × height 11.2 cm) containing a growth substrate composed of peat moss, perlite, and vermiculite in a 3:1:1 (v/v/v) ratio. The substrate was pre-moistened to 100% field capacity prior to transplantation to minimize root disturbance. Seedlings were cultured in the greenhouse of the Vegetable Research Institute, Gansu Academy of Agricultural Sciences, located in Lanzhou, China, under the following conditions: 28 ± 5 °C (day)/22 ± 5 °C (night), 13/11 h photoperiod, 45–55% relative humidity, and a maximum illumination intensity of 2000 ± 50 mmol·m−2·s−1. All plants received standard irrigation (100% field capacity) to ensure uniform seedling development.

Experimental Treatments

The drought-stress treatment was initiated on 5 October 2024, when the seedlings reached the four-leaf developmental stage and the substrate volumetric water content decreased to 45 ± 2%. Sixty uniformly grown seedlings were selected and randomly divided into two groups: a control group (n = 10, 3 biological replicates; maintained with normal water supply) and a drought stress group (n = 10, 3 biological replicatess; subjected to continuous drought treatment for 7 days). Plant phenotypes were observed every day. When the drought-sensitive melon material began to wilt, the drought injury grades were recorded (started on the 5th day of continuous drought). When the drought-sensitive melon material exhibited severe wilting symptoms, plant growth, physiological and biochemical indices were measured (on the 7th day of continuous drought).

Indices and methods

Drought injury grade and drought injury index (DI)

Drought injury was graded on a scale from 1 to 5 according to Fig. 1, as follows: Grade 1: normal seedling growth; Grade 2: plant slightly wilting, new leaf margins slightly involution, old leaf margins slightly drooping; Grade 3: plant moderately wilting, new leaf rolling area more than 30%, old leaf drooping area more than 30%; Grade 4: plant severely wilting, new leaf rolling area more than 50%, old leaves completely inward and drooping; Grade 5: plant extremely wilting, all leaves completely inward and drooping, petioles and stems also drooping.Fig. 1Drought injury grade standard of melon

The drought injury index was calculated according to the following formula:

where S1 to S5 represents the number of plants corresponding to drought grades 1 to 5, respectively.

Growth parameters

The weight of the shoot and root were weighed by a one-thousandth electronic scale. The root fresh weight was weighed after washing the material and gently blotting the surface moisture. The dry weight was dried at 105℃ for 30 min and at 80℃ until constant weight.

The root-shoot ratio was calculated as the ratio of root dry weight to shoot dry weight.

After the roots were washed with water, the root architecture was analyzed by HM-GXONE plant root analyzer.

Physiological and biochemical indices

Relative water content (RWC), relative electrical conductivity (REC), and pigment content were determined according to [43]. The total pigment content consists of chlorophyll a, chlorophyll b, and carotene.

Malondialdehyde (MDA, Cat# G0110F, colorimetric assay at 532 nm and 600 nm), hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂, Cat# G0112F, colorimetric assay at 415 nm), superoxide anion (O2·−, Cat# G0116F, colorimetric assay at 540 nm), soluble protein (Cat# G0417F, colorimetric assay at 600 nm), soluble sugar (Cat# G0501F, colorimetric assay at 620 nm), proline (Cat# G0111F96, colorimetric assay at 520 nm), betaine (Cat# G0122F, colorimetric assay at 525 nm), flavonoid (Cat# G0118F, colorimetric assay at 510 nm) and γ-aminobutyric acid (GABA, Cat# G1106F, colorimetric assay at 645 nm) were analyzed using commercial kits from Suzhou Grace Biotechnology Co., Ltd., while reduced glutathione (GSH, Cat# GSH-2-W, colorimetric assay at 412 nm) and oxidized glutathione (GSSG, Cat# GSSG-2-W, colorimetric assay at 412 nm) were measured using kits from Suzhou Keming Biotechnology Co., Ltd., with all assays performed strictly following the manufacturers’ instructions.

Multivariate analysis of melon seedling traits

The calculation of drought stress index (DSI), phenotypic plasticity index (PPI), membership function, weight of determined indices and comprehensive evaluation value of drought resistance (D value) were all referred to [43].

Statistical analysis

Growth parameters and physiological and biochemical indices were analyzed using two-way ANOVA and Tukey’s HSD test. All data were presented as mean values of at least three replicates. Column plots was generated using Prism 9 software, and box plots were creatd using Origin 2024 software. Pearson correlation analysis was performed at a significant level of P ≤ 0.05, and origin 2024 software was used for visualization. Cluster analysis (Euclidean distance, Ward’s method) and stepwise regression analysis were conducted using SPSS 26 software.

Responses of melon growth indices to drought stress

Melon seedlings exposed to drought stress showed significant biomass reductions compared to controls, with shoot dry weight decreasing by 36.93%, fresh weight by 35.23%, root dry weight by 25.05%, and fresh weight by 43.35% (Fig. A-D). Concurrently, the root-shoot ratio increased by 17.62% (Fig. E), demonstrating drought’s dual effect of suppressing overall plant growth while promoting nutrient allocation to root systems as an adaptive strategy. However, the impact of drought stress on biomass varied significantly among different melon germplasms. Under drought stress, YJM exhibited exceptional shoot dry weight retention with no significant reduction, and both YJM and 17Y11 maintained shoot fresh weight at control levels (Fig. 4A, B). Root biomass revealed that GT1 and TN preserved root dry weight effectively, while GT1 uniquely maintained root fresh weight under stress (Fig. 4C, D). Furthermore, drought induced a significant increase in root-shoot ratio across 6 germplasms (GT1, LB946, TN, 22P7, 22P8, 23G33), demonstrating a conserved adaptive strategy for resource allocation (Fig. 4E).

Root architecture anaysis showed that drought stress significantly reduced total root length, root surface area, root volume, root tips and forks (decreased by 40.38%, 38.52%, 36.53%, 37.75% and 36.35%, respectively), but significantly increased root average diameter (increased by 6.65%) (Fig. 3F-K). Responses of adjustment substances in melon leaves to drought stress

Soluble sugar, soluble protein, proline, and betaine serve as key osmoregulatory substances in plants, playing crucial roles in drought stress responses. Our findings revealed that drought stress significantly elevated leaf soluble sugar content by 60.07% in melon plants, while showing no significant effects on soluble protein, proline, or betaine levels (Fig. 6F-I). This suggests that soluble sugar constitute the primary osmoregulatory substance in melon’s drought response mechanism. Across diverse melon germplasms, drought stress elicited genotype-specific metabolic responses. Drought stress significantly increased the soluble sugar content for all genotypes except 23G36 and 17Y11 (Fig. 8A). However, the drought response of the other osmoprotectants showed marked variability. Soluble protein significantly increased in GT1, LB946, YD, TN, 23G33, and 23G37, while 17Y1 and 17Y11 showed pronounced reductions (Fig. 8B). Proline accumulation was enhanced in GT3, YD, TN, 22P7, and 22G40, but 23G37 showed a significant decrease (Fig. 8C). Betaine content followed similar trends, with GT4, YJM, LB946, 22P8, and 22G40 showing significant increases, contrasted with reductions in 23G33 and 17Y1 (Fig. 8D). These differential responses highlight the complex genetic regulation of drought adaptation mechanisms in melon germplasm.

Flavonoids, as important secondary metabolites, showed an overall increasing trend (though not statistically significant) in total content under drought conditions (Fig. 6J). However, genotype-specific analysis revealed significant flavonoid accumulation in YD, TN, 22P8, 23G36, and 22G40 germplasms (Fig. 8E), suggesting their potential role in drought adaptation for these particular genotypes.

γ-aminobutyric acid (GABA), a non-protein amino acid involved in multiple physiological processes, has been shown to protect plants against abiotic stresses [55]. Our results indicated a significant 32.80% increase in leaf GABA content under drought stress (Fig. 6K). Notably, all tested germplasms except GT3 exhibited significant GABA accumulation (Fig. 8F), highlighting its likely importance in melon’s drought response mechanisms.Fig. 8Effect of drought stress on the content of adjusting substances in melon leaves. Response of redox level in melon leaves to drought stress

Reactive oxygen species, particularly H₂O₂ and O2·−, play crucial roles in plant stress responses. Our results demonstrated that drought stress significantly elevated H₂O₂ levels by 60.76% while reducing O2·− content by 17.87% in melon leaves (Fig. 9A, B). Genotype-specific analysis revealed significant H₂O₂ accumulation in GT1, GT4, YJM, LB946, YD, TN, 22P7, 22P8, and 22G40 germplasms, whereas O2·− levels were markedly decreased in GT1, GT3, LB946, 22P8, 22G33, and 17Y1 germplasms (Fig. 10A, B).

Malondialdehyde (MDA), a terminal product of lipid peroxidation, serves as a reliable indicator of oxidative damage. Drought stress induced a significant 50.35% increase in leaf MDA content (Fig. 9C). Among different genotypes, GT4, YJM, LB946, TN, 22P7, 22P8, 23G33, 23G36, 23G37, 22G40, and 17Y11 showed the most pronounced MDA accumulation, with 22P7, 22P8, and 23G37 exhibiting the highest increases (2.70-, 2.37-, and 2.07-fold versus control, respectively) (Fig. 10C). Relative electrical conductivity (REC), an important stress resistance indicator, showed no significant changes under drought stress (Fig. 9D), though genotype-dependent variations were observed (Fig. 10D).

The cellular redox state, reflected by the GSH and GSSG ratio, was significantly altered under drought conditions. While GSH levels remained stable, GSSG content increased dramatically by 172.83%, leading to a 70.54% reduction in GSH/GSSG (Fig. 9E-G). Screening of drought resistance indices of melon

Using the DSI of each parameter as independent variables and the D-value as the dependent variable, we established a stepwise regression model: D = 1.195–1.21 × DI − 0.218 × Chl-a − 0.173 × REC − 0.011 × Bet (R² = 0.971, P < 0.01). Discussion

Drought stress has become one of the most important abiotic stresses limiting global crop production. Melon is one of the crops that are susceptible to drought stress. In order to cope with climate change and alleviate agricultural water pressure, breeding drought-resistant melon varieties is a key measure to maintain the yield and quality of melon under drought conditions. However, the drought-resistant melon germplasm resources are limited. Therefore, drought-resistant breeding depends on the exploration and innovation of drought-resistant germplasm.

Response of plants to drought stress

Drought stress significantly reduced leaf relative water content (RWC) and plant biomass, leading to inhibited growth in melon seedlings, consistent with previous reports [56, 57]. However, drought stress significantly increased the root-shoot ratio of plants, indicating that drought, as an adaptation strategy, had a dual effect of inhibiting the overall growth of plants and promoting the distribution of nutrients to roots. Root systems, as primary drought sensors, typically exhibit altered growth patterns under water deficit. Although lentil (Lens culinaris Medik.) [58], potato [48] and alfalfa [32] showed increased root length, surface area and tip number under drought, our 7-day drought treatment significantly reduced these parameters in melon seedlings, aligning with reports in melon [59] and watermelon [43]. This discrepancy likely reflects stress intensity variations, where moderate drought may stimulate root growth while severe stress inhibits it.

While photosynthetic pigments are essential for plant photosynthesis, their response to drought stress appears species-specific. Contrasting with studies showing reduced pigment content in tomato (Solanum lycopersicum L.) [60], potato [57], faba bean (Vicia faba) [61], and wheat [62], our results demonstrated increased chlorophyll a, chlorophyll b, carotene and total pigment content in melon leaves under drought, similar to watermelon findings by Ren et al. [43], suggesting crop-specific and stress intensity-dependent pigment responses.

Drought-induced physiological changes in melon included oxidative stress markers (elevated MDA and H₂O₂) and altered redox status (reduced GSH/GSSG), consistent with previous studies [33, 43, 57]. Osmotic adjustment strategies varied among species: wheat primarily accumulated soluble sugar [63], passion fruit (Passiflora edulis Sims) utilized soluble protein and proline [64], potato employed multiple osmolytes [57, 65], while quinoa (Chenopodium quinoa Willd.) relied on sugars and proline [56]. In melon, soluble sugar accumulation dominated the osmotic response. Notably, we observed significant GABA increases under drought, supporting its proposed protective role against abiotic stress [55], suggesting its importance in melon drought adaptation.

Comprehensive evaluation of plant drought resistance

Plant drought resistance represents a complex quantitative trait that requires comprehensive evaluation methods for accurate assessment. Previous studies have employed various analytical approaches with comparable results: Li et al. [40] found no significant differences when evaluating lettuce drought resistance using three methods (comprehensive drought resistance value [D value], comprehensive drought resistance coefficient [CDC], and weighted drought resistance coefficient [WDC]). Similarly, Chen et al. [31] obtained consistent results when applying both CDC and D value methods to assess alfalfa drought tolerance at germination stage. Notably, the D value method incorporates weighted indices to better reflect the relative importance of different physiological parameters, thereby providing more reliable and accurate evaluations [31].

Current drought resistance evaluation methods primarily follow two approaches: (1) transformation of conventional indicators into principal components followed by membership function and D value calculation, as demonstrated in watermelon [43], potato [38], and mungbean (Vigna radiata (L.) Wilczek) [66] studies; or (2) direct calculation of membership function values from trait indicators to derive D values, as applied to sugar beet (Beta vulgaris L.) [53] and common bean (Phaseoluus vulgaris L.) [67]. While principal component analysis simplifies computation by reducing dimensionality, the direct method offers more comprehensive data representation. By deriving D values from the membership function values of 30 trait indicators, this study successfully identified drought resistance in 15 melon germplasms, thereby providing valuable genetic resources for targeted breeding programs.

Screening of plant drought resistance indices

Crop drought resistance is influenced by multiple factors and can be assessed through various drought resistance indices. Accurate identification of these indices provides essential material foundations for drought resistance breeding programs. The period of plant drought resistance identification is divided into bud stage (typically using PEG-simulated drought stress) [31, 68], seedling stage [20, 29], and maturity stage [26, 38, 69]. Among these, seedling stage evaluation offers distinct advantages, including precise water control, minimal environmental interference, and rapid assessment feasibility, making it the most practical identification period.

In this study, we employed continuous drought treatment at seedling stage to evaluate melon germplasm, identifying 4 key drought resistance indicators: drought injury index, chlorophyll a content, relative electrical conductivity (REC), and betaine content. These findings provide both theoretical foundations and practical tools for screening drought-resistant melon germplasm and enhancing breeding efficiency.

Assessment of melon germplasms for drought resistance

Currently, systematic identification of drought resistance in melon germplasms remains unreported. This study evaluated 15 melon accessions for drought tolerance, classifying them into 5 distinct categories: strongly drought-resistant genotypes, drought-resistant genotypes, moderately drought-resistant genotypes, drought-sensitive genotypes, and highly drought-sensitive genotypes genotypes. A total of 7 drought-resistant melon germplasms were identified, including 2 highly drought-resistant materials (17Y1 and 17Y11) and 5 drought-resistant materials (GT3, 23G36, YJM, 23G33, and 23G37). These melon materials have direct practical value for drought-resistant melon breeding. Additionally, 2 highly drought-sensitive materials (22P7 and 22G40) were characterized, which may serve as valuable resources for investigating the molecular mechanisms underlying melon drought stress responses.

Conclusion

In this study, the drought resistance of 15 melon genotypes were assessed under both normal-water suppled and drought-stressed condition. A total of 30 growth, physiological and biochemical parameters were quantitatively assessed to characterize drought resistance in melon seedlings‌. Multivariate analytical approaches, including drought stress index, phenotypic plasticity index, membership function, D value, correlation analysis, cluster analysis, and regression analysis, were implemented for comprehensive drought tolerance evaluation.‌ Cluster analysis based on D-values categorized the 15 melon germplasms into 5 distinct groups: strongly drought-resistant group (17Y1 and 17Y11; average D-value = 0.696), drought-resistant group (GT3, 23G36, YJM, 23G33 and 23G37; average D-value = 0.615), moderately drought-resistant group (GT4, 22P8 and GT1; average D-value = 0.518), drought-sensitive group (YD, TN and LB946; average D-value = 0.440), and highly drought-sensitive group (22P7 and 22G40; average D-value = 0.285). Stepwise regression analysis (R²=0.979, p < 0.01) identified 4 key diagnostic indicators for drought resistance evaluation: drought injury index (DI), chlorophyll a content, relative electrical conductivity (REC), and betaine concent. The results provide support for drought resistance breeding of melon.

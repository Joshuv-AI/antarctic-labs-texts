Stevia extract improves broiler breast meat quality via regulating the glycerophospholipid metabolism and enhancing endoplasmic reticulum and mitochondrial homeostasis
Authors: Jiageng Meng, Jirong Lv, Xianxin Chen, Linxu Yang, Yilong Jiang, Gang Jia, Xiaoling Chen, Bin Feng, Lianqiang Che, Hua Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study aimed to investigate the effects of stevia extract (SE) on growth performance and meat quality of broilers and reveal possibly mechanisms. Four hundred Arbor Acres male broilers (1 d old, 40 ± 1 g) were randomly assigned to 5 treatments with 8 replicates of 10 broilers per replicate. Broilers in the control (CON) group were fed the basal diet, while broilers in the other 4 treatment groups were fed the basal diet supplemented with 50, 100, 150, and 200 mg/kg SE, respectively. Dietary supplementation with SE effectively improved breast meat quality and tended to increase average daily gain (ADG) in broilers. Compared with the CON group, 150 mg/kg SE reduced 48 h drip loss and peak shear force of breast muscle (P < 0.05). Both 150 and 200 mg/kg SE decreased total cholesterol (TC) and triglyceride (TG) contents, while 100 and 150 mg/kg SE reduced malondialdehyde (MDA) concentration in breast muscle (P < 0.05). SE altered fatty acid (FA) composition: 150 mg/kg SE decreased the proportion of C24:0, and 200 mg/kg SE decreased C22:6n3 (P < 0.05). Linear regression analysis identified 150 mg/kg as the optimal SE level for meat quality improvement, which also downregulated FASN and ACACA mRNA expression (P < 0.05). Untargeted metabolomics revealed that 150 mg/kg SE modulated glycerophospholipid metabolism. Further validation confirmed that 150 mg/kg SE increased phospholipid (PL) content in breast muscle and improved endoplasmic reticulum (ER) and mitochondrial (MT) homeostasis. These changes were accompanied by reduced mRNA and protein expression of ER stress biomarkers (GRP78, PERK, IRE1, XBP1) and the MT chaperone HSP60 (P < 0.05). In conclusion, SE improves broiler breast meat quality via regulating the glycerophospholipid metabolism and enhancing endoplasmic reticulum and mitochondrial homeostasis. Regression analysis in this study indicates that the optimal SE supplementation amount for broilers is 150 mg/kg.Keywords: Stevia extract, Broiler, Meat quality, Glycerophospholipid metabolism

Introduction

The incidence of chronic diseases such as "three highs" (hypertension, hyperglycemia, hyperlipidemia) has been on the rise globally in recent years. With a significant enhancement in consumers' health awareness, low-fat meat products, which align with the orientation of healthy diets, have garnered widespread attention (Frank et al., 2016). Compared with pork and beef, chicken is a meat with low cholesterol, low fat, and high protein content. It provides the human body with various nutrients including essential fatty acids, amino acids, minerals, and vitamins, and is more easily absorbed by the human body (Zou et al., 2022). During the last several years, the global poultry meat consumption volume has risen sharply. In 2023, poultry meat accounted for over 38% of total meat consumption, firmly securing its position as the most consumed meat category worldwide (FAO, 2023). Within the poultry sector, chicken dominates the industry. Data from the China Feed Industry Information Network shows that the number of slaughtered broilers reached 14.366 billion in 2024, accounting for more than 82% of the national total poultry slaughter (Doe, 2025). Among these, white-feathered broilers are widely favored by the market due to their advantages such as short growth cycle and low production cost. However, under modern intensive farming systems, fast-growing broilers generally suffer from problems such as coarse meat texture and poor palatability (Zheng et al., 2023), which seriously affect consumer satisfaction. Therefore, exploring effective approaches to improve broiler meat quality has come to the fore as a pivotal research focus in the animal husbandry.

The quality of meat is impacted by multiple factors, including animal breed, dietary requirements, rearing environment, and processing methods (Pandey et al., 2024). Among these, nutritional regulation has emerged as a crucial strategy for modulating meat quality due to its high operability. Plant extracts, serving as natural nutrients, are widely employed in livestock and poultry production on account of their characteristics of being green and safe, free of drug resistance, and absence of toxic effects (Armendariz-Barragan et al., 2016). A number of researches have demonstrated that plant extracts improve chicken meat quality via regulating pH value, meat color, water-holding capacity, peak shear force, and optimizing fatty acid (FA) composition (Liu et al., 2020; Yao et al., 2023). With the rise in relevant research, chlorogenic acid compounds have attracted considerable attention in recent years.

Stevia is a subtropical perennial herb with the advantages of wide availability, easy cultivation, and low cost. It contains various bioactive components, including glycosides, flavonoids, chlorogenic acid compounds, and other active substances (Ruiz-Ruiz et al., 2017). Stevia extract (SE) abounds in chlorogenic acid-derived compounds and exhibits antibacterial, antioxidant, and antiviral activities (Wen et al., 2024). Evidence shows that chlorogenic acid inhibits the growth of harmful bacteria, thereby alleviating inflammatory responses (Yan et al., 2020). Chlorogenic acid also improves meat color and amino acid profiles in the growing-finishing pig longissimus dorsi muscle, and enhance muscle protein synthesis, thus improving meat quality (Wang et al., 2022). In addition, SE effectively reduces blood lipid levels and serves as a natural agent for the therapy of hyperlipidemia and related complications (Ahmad et al., 2018). Changes in blood lipid content are closely related to muscle lipid metabolism (Chicco et al., 2003), while muscle lipid metabolism changes directly affect meat quality (Cui et al., 2023). Drawing on the functions of SE and prior studies, we hypothesize that it also has a favorable regulatory effect on poultry meat quality. However, systematic research on the application of SE in broilers is currently lacking, particularly about the impacts and underlying processes of SE on broiler meat quality and growth performance, which remain ambiguous. Thus, this research was undertaken to investigate the impact of SE on broiler growth performance, breast muscle meat quality, and its underlying mechanisms.

Material and method

Statement on animal ethics

The animal experiment obtained ethical approval from the Animal Ethics Committee of Sichuan Agricultural University, in strict compliance with the institutional guidelines for animal welfare (Approval No. SCAUAC202407-1).

Experimental design and rearing management

In this research, 400 Arbor Acres male broilers (1 d old, 40 ± 1 g) were randomly assigned to 5 dietary treatments with 8 replicates of 10 broilers per replicate. The trial lasted for 42 d. Broilers in the control group (CON) were fed a basal diet (formulated partly according to Nutrient Requirements of Poultry (NRC, 1994 and NY/T 33-2004, China), details in Table 1), while broilers in the other 4 treatment groups were fed the basal diet supplemented with 50, 100, 150, and 200 mg/kg SE, respectively. The SE used in this study (effective chlorogenic acid content ≥ 10%, effective content of chlorogenic acid and its analogs ≥ 40%) was supplied by Haotian Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., Zhucheng City, Shandong Province.

Broilers were raised in a cage rearing system with 10 broilers per cage, and all replicates were randomly distributed in the poultry house. At the beginning of the experiment, the indoor relative humidity was kept at 50–60% and temperature at 32-35 °C. The temperature was subsequently lowered by 2-3 °C weekly until it stabilized within the range of 23-25 °C. During the experiment, broilers were given unrestricted access to water and feed, with feed distributed three times a day. The health conditions of each experimental broiler was carefully checked and recorded.

Growth performance

The broilers in each replicate were weighed and recorded on day 1, 21, and 42 of the trial, and feed consumption per pen was measured to calculate the body weight (BW), average daily gain (ADG), average daily feed intake (ADFI), and feed-to-gain ratio (F/G). The formulas are as follows: ADFI = Total feed consumption / (Average surviving broiler count × Test days); ADG (g/d) = (Final total live weight − Initial total live weight) / (Number of surviving broilers × Test days); F/G = ADFI/ADG.

Collection of samples

After the trial, six broilers per group (body weight near group average, 30 broilers in total) were slaughtered. Post-slaughter, both pectoral muscles were separated: left breast muscle samples (same region) were collected and stored at -80 °C, which were used for biochemical, western blot (WB), liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS), and Real-Time PCR (qPCR) analysis. Partial right breast muscle samples were freeze-dried for FA profile determination.

Indicators of meat quality pertaining to breast muscle

Meat color and pH value

At 45 min and 24 h post-slaughter, pH value and the color of the left breast muscle meat [lightness (L*), redness (a*), and yellowness (b*)] were measured with a pH meter (pH-star, SFK-Technology, Denmark) and a calibrated colorimeter (CR-300, Minolta, Japan). Three different positions were selected from each meat sample for duplicate detection, and the average values (accurate to 0.01) were recorded.

Drip loss

The meat sample was excised from the upper-middle section of the right breast muscle of each broiler, weighed and labeled as W1. Each sample was enclosed in a plastic bag and suspended in a 4 °C cooler for 24 h and 48 h before removal. After blotting the surface moisture with neutral filter paper, the samples were weighed again, with the recorded weights designated as W2 (24 h) and W3 (48 h). The formulas for calculating drip loss are as follows: 48 h drip loss (%) = (W1–W3)/W1 × 100; 24 h drip loss (%) = (W1–W2)/W1 × 100.

Cooking loss

Approximately 30 g of the right breast muscle from the same portion of broilers was weighed and recorded as W4. After being stored in a 4 °C refrigerator for 24 h, the meat sample was cooked in a 90 °C water bath until the core temperature reached 70 °C. Then removed and allowed to cool to room temperature. Surface moisture was taken out and reweighed as W5. The calculation formula of cooking loss is presented below: Cooking loss (%) = (W4 – W5)/W4 × 100.

Peak shear force

After the meat color and pH were measured, the samples were processed for peak shear force determination. After heating the samples in a 90 °C water bath to a core temperature of 70 °C, they were cooled. Subsequently, three similar meat strips were drilled out from each sample using a cylindrical sampler, the peak shear force was assessed employing a texture analyser (XT2, Stable Micro Systems Ltd., UK).

Fatty acid composition of breast muscle

The determination of muscle fatty acid (FA) profile was performed according to the method reported in previous studies (Xu et al., 2019). Freeze-dried sample (200 mg) was used for lipid extraction. The extracted lipids were subjected to hydrolysis and methylation, then mixed into a solution with n-hexane and saturated aqueous sodium chloride as the solvent system. The detection of FA composition was examined utilising a GC-2010 gas chromatograph (Shimadzu Corporation, Kyoto, Japan). Qualitative analysis was conducted by comparing the retention times between target peaks and Sigma-Aldrich standards (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA). The final data were showed as the relative percentage of each individual FA to the total FAs.

Biochemical analysis of breast muscle

The activities of antioxidant indices [glutathione peroxidase (GSH-Px), total superoxide dismutase (T-SOD), total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC)] and the content of malondialdehyde (MDA) in the breast muscle were determined by using the relevant test kits (No. A005-1, A001-1, A015-1, A003-1, Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China). The contents of triglycerides (TG) and total cholesterol (TC) in the breast muscle were measured by using the relevant test kits (No. A110-1-1, A111-1-1, Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China). The content of total phospholipids (PL) in the breast muscle was assayed by using a kit (No. G1243W48, Suzhou Grace Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Suzhou, China). The content of glycerophospholipid metabolism pathway related enzymes [phospholipase A (PLA), phospholipase C (PLC), phospholipase D (PLD), and lysophosphatidylcholine acyltransferase (LPCAT)] in the breast muscle were determined by using the enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits (No. YJ6584B, YJ2124B, YJ2306B, YJ2326B, Shanghai Enzyme-Linked Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China). The total protein levels of each sample were determined with the bicinchoninic acid (BCA) method by using a kit (No. AR0197, Boster Biological Technology Co., Ltd., Wuhan, China).

LC-MS/MS analysis of breast muscle

Majorbio Bio-Pharm Technology Co., Ltd. from Shanghai undertook the LC-MS/MS detection of meat samples. The detection was performed employing a Thermo UHPLC-Q Exactive analytical system. Data processing was carried out via the free online (cloud.majorbio.com). With the help of the KEGG database (http://www.genome.jp/kegg/), metabolic enrichment analysis and pathway annotation were conducted on differential metabolites among different groups. These metabolites were mapped to corresponding biochemical pathways and then classified according to their respective pathway categories or biological functions.

Real-time PCR analysis of breast muscle

This experiment were performed according to previous studies (Zhao et al., 2015). Total RNA of the samples was extracted utilizing RNAiso Plus reagent (No. AO70712A, Takara, Dalian, China). After extraction, RNA quality was verified as qualified by agarose gel electrophoresis, and then the RNA was reverse-transcribed into complementary DNA utilizing a kit (No. RR092S, Takara, Dalian, China). qPCR was conducted with a kit (No. CN830A, Takara, Dalian, China) on a QuantStudio 5 system. The reaction system configured as per the parameters shown in Table S1, and the specific primer sequences are provided in Table S2. The internal reference gene was β-actin, and the target genes expression were calculated utilizing the 2⁻ΔΔCT method.

Western blot analysis

Muscle samples were homogenized with radioimmunoprecipitation assay (RIPA) and further treated with phenylmethylsulfonyl fluoride (PMSF) protease inhibitor buffer (No. P0013B, ST2573, Beyotime, Shanghai, China). Total protein content was determined utilizing a kit (No. AR0197, Boster, Wuhan, China). For WB analysis, 400 μL protein sample was mixed with 100 μL 5×SDS-PAGE loading buffer (No. P0015L, Beyotime, Shanghai, China) and denatured at 95 °C for 10 min. WB was conducted as reported by Liu et al. (2021). WB band gray values were determined with Image Lab software (Bio-Rad, CA, USA), and target protein relative expression was calculated utilizing β-actin as the internal reference. The primary antibody details are in Table S3, and the secondary antibodies were anti-rabbit/mouse IgG (1:5000 dilution, Proteintech, IL, USA).

Data analysis

The SPSS 27.0 statistical software (SPSS, Inc., Chicago, USA) was utilized to analyze all experiment data. Specifically, for the data of broiler growth performance, meat quality-related indicators, antioxidant-related indices, as well as TG and TC contents: if data met the normal distribution condition, one-way ANOVA was performed initially, with Tukey's multiple comparison tests carried out thereafter; if the normal distribution was not satisfied, Welch’s test was used for statistical analysis. For indices related to the glycerophospholipid metabolism pathway, lipid synthesis-related genes, and endoplasmic reticulum and mitochondrial stress biomarkers, independent samples t-test was applied. Data on mRNA and protein expression levels were showed as mean ± standard deviation (SD), while the data of other indicators were denoted as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). A statistically significant difference was deemed to exist when P < 0.05, while a propensity toward a difference was noted for 0.05 ≤ P < 0.10. Experimental graphs were constructed using GraphPad Prism 8.0 software (GraphPad Software, Inc., San Diego, CA, USA). Correlation analysis was conducted with Origin 2025 software (OriginLab Corporation, Northampton, MA, USA).

Dietary SE supplementation improved the fatty acid composition of breast muscle in broilers. Compared with the CON group, 150 mg/kg SE reduced (P < 0.05) the proportion of C24:0 (Fig. 1H), while 200 mg/kg SE decreased (P < 0.05) the proportion of C22:6n3 (Fig. 1I) in breast muscle. Dietary SE supplementation enhanced the antioxidant capacity of breast muscle in broilers. Dietary SE inclusion had no effect on the activities of T-SOD (Fig. 2B) and GSH-Px (Fig. 2D).

Regression analysis was performed on the key indices related to antioxidant capacity, meat quality, and lipid metabolism (Fig. 4), and the optimal impact was observed at an SE supplementation level of 150 mg/kg. Therefore, the CON group and SE 150 group (six samples per group) were selected for further untargeted metabolomics analysis.

Untargeted metabolomics analysis

Results of OPLS-DA showed that breast muscle metabolites of the CON group and SE 150 group were completely separated, indicating that SE exerted a significant effect on muscle metabolites (Fig. 5A). A permutation test was performed to assess the dependability of the OPLS-DA model (Fig. 5B). Q2 represents the predictive ability of the model. A Q2 value approaching 1 signifies superior predictive performance. A model with Q2 > 0.5 was considered valid, and a model with P < 0.05 was generally regarded as optimal. In this study, Q2 = 0.642 and P < 0.05, which indicated that this model exhibited good performance, supporting the subsequent data analysis (details of model parameters were provided in Table S5). For the CON and SE 150 groups, a total of 540 metabolites were detected, among which 57 were increased and 32 were decreased (Fig. 5C). Differential metabolites were subjected to KEGG enrichment analysis and topological analysis. The results revealed that 150 mg/kg SE affected the pathways involved in the metabolism of glycerophospholipids, histidine, arginine and proline, and purines (P < 0.05) (Fig. 5D). Further pathway analysis exhibited that the glycerophospholipid metabolism pathway was consistent with the expected results, so subsequent experiments were focused on this pathway. In comparison to the CON group, 150 mg/kg SE elevated the abundance of [PE (18:0/0:0), Gpetn (14:0/22:4), PE (20:4/0:0), PE (19:0/0:0), LPC (15:0), and PE (16:0/0:0),] (P < 0.05), while exhibited a propensity to elevate the abundance of [LPC (18:0) and PC (P-18:0/18:4)] and decrease the abundance of [PE (Pgd1/P-18:0) and PE (20:2/0:0)] in breast muscle (Fig. 5E). Therefore, dietary SE supplementation improved glycerophospholipid metabolism in broiler breast muscle.

Endoplasmic reticulum and mitochondrial homeostasis related indicators in breast muscle

Dietary SE supplementation improved ER and MT homeostasis in the breast muscle of broilers. In comparison to the CON group, 150 mg/kg SE decreased the mRNA expression of GRP78, IRE1, XBP1, PERK and HSP60 (P < 0.05), and tended to decrease (P = 0.085) the mRNA expression of eIF2α in breast muscle. SE supplementation had no effect on the mRNA expression of ER stress biomarkers (ATF4, ATF6 and CHOP) and MT stress biomarkers (SIRT3, FOXO3, SIRT7, JNK2, CLPX, NRF1, C/EBPβ, SOD2 and JUN) (Fig. 7A and B). We further investigated the expression of proteins related to ER and MT homeostasis and found that 150 mg/kg SE decreased (P < 0.05) the protein expression of XBP1, GRP78 and HSP60 (Fig. 7C-E).

Correlation analysis

Correlation analysis was employed to explore the relationships between glycerophospholipid metabolism and other key indicators (Fig. 8). Three differential metabolites [PE (19:0/0:0), Gpetn (14:0/22:4) and PE (18:0/0:0)] and PL contents were negatively correlated with the peak shear force, while LPCAT and TG contents were positively correlated with this index. Differential metabolite PE (20:4/0:0) showed a negative correlation with the drip loss. The differential metabolite PE (19:0/0:0) was negatively correlated with the ER stress biomarker IRE1. Four differential metabolites [PE (20:4/0:0), LPC (15:0), PE (18:0/0:0) and PE (19:0/0:0)] were negatively correlated with TG and TC contents, and three differential metabolites [PE (20:4/0:0), PE (18:0/0:0) and PE (16:0/0:0)] were negatively correlated with the lipid synthesis-related gene FASN. Additionally, the differential metabolite PE (18:0/0:0) was negatively correlated with the MT stress biomarker HSP60. These results indicated that SE improved meat quality by regulating the glycerophospholipid metabolic pathway.

Discussion

Growth performance is widely recognized as a direct indicator of an animal nutritional status and health. SE has been reported to possess growth-promoting properties (Luna et al., 2025). Liu et al. (2024) report that SE improves the yellow-feathered broilers growth performance, and the optimal supplementation level of SE (with total chlorogenic acid ≥ 50%) for yellow-feathered broilers is 100-200 mg/kg. In the present study, SE also exhibited growth-promoting effects and tended to increase the 42 d BW, 22-42 d and 1-42 d ADG of white-feathered broilers, with 150 mg/kg SE exhibiting the better effect.

The increase in broiler BW is not only a result of macroscopic growth performance but also closely linked to nutrient accumulation and metabolic regulation in muscle tissue. As the main edible part of broilers, breast muscle composition and quality directly affect the nutritional value and palatability of meat. Consequently, we further analyzed the effect of SE on the brolier breast muscle meat quality. Meat quality is primarily defined by four key attributes: flavor , water holding capacity, meat color and tenderness (Ngapo and Gariepy, 2008). Among these, peak shear force is a core indicator for evaluating muscle tenderness, with lower values corresponding to greater tenderness. In this study, 150 mg/kg SE decreased the peak shear force of breast muscle, suggesting improved muscle tenderness. WHC is another key indicator of meat quality and is negatively correlated with drip loss (Brunner et al., 2012). In this study, 150 mg/kg SE decreased 48 h breast muscle drip loss and tended to reduce 24 h drip loss, indicating that SE effectively enhanced the WHC of breast muscle. The present results aligned with those from prior studies on growing-finishing pigs (Xie et al., 2023).

Meat quality is closely linked to antioxidant status. T-AOC denotes the comprehensive antioxidant capability of the organism, while MDA (a lipid oxidation product) reflects the extent of cellular damage (Gawel et al., 2004; Wang et al., 2023). As part of the present research, 100 mg/kg and 150 mg/kg SE reduced MDA content and showed a propensity to increase the T-AOC activity in breast muscle, confirming that SE enhanced muscle antioxidant capacity. This improvement may contribute to enhancing cell membrane integrity and reduce water loss, thus improving meat quality.

Muscle flavor is also a key component of meat quality. Previous study indicates that muscle flavor is positively correlated with the content of PUFA (Liu et al., 2023). As part of this study, 150 mg/kg SE elevate the proportions of C18:3n3 and total PUFA in muscle, which shows that SE improves muscle flavor. However, 200 mg/kg SE decreased the proportion of C22:6n3 (docosahexaenoic acid, DHA), an essential PUFA for brain development (Lauritzen et al., 2016). We hypothesized that high-dose SE may inhibit the synthesis of C22:6n3.

In the present study, SE increased muscle WHC and tenderness,thus improving meat quality, these issues were accompanied by inhibiting lipid peroxidation to enhance antioxidant capacity and optimizing breast muscle FA composition. Given the close association between FA composition and lipid metabolism, we subsequently analyzed the impacts of SE on lipid metabolism in breast muscle. Lipid metabolism in muscle is critical for maintaining systemic lipid homeostasis, which is beneficial for animal health (An et al., 2023). For humans, with the increasing diversity of food availability, public health consciousness has been heightened, leading to a growing demand for foods low in fat and cholesterol. In present study dietary supplementation of 150 and 200 mg/kg SE effectively lowered breast muscle TC and TG contents, which suggest that SE may serve as a promising feed additive to develop low-fat and low-cholesterol poultry products. Since not all three SE doses improved meat quality, linear regression analysis of key parameters (drip loss, peak shear force, MDA concentration, TC concentration and TG concentration) was conducted to identify the optimal dosage, estimated at 150 mg/kg. Untargeted metabolomics analysis was then performed between the CON and 150 mg/kg SE-treated groups, aiming to explore the metabolic pathways associated with SE-induced improvements in meat quality. The results showed that SE inclusion altered the glycerophospholipid metabolism pathway.

To validate the metabolomics results, we further determined the contents of enzymes and the expression of genes associated with glycerophospholipid metabolism. Dietary supplementation with 150 mg/kg SE decreased LPCAT content, elevated the mRNA expression of PCYT2, and exhibited a propensity to elevate the AGPAT2 expression. These findings were highly consistent with the above-mentioned metabolomics results. Previous studies have confirmed that PCYT2 knockout activates SREBP1 and enhances dephosphorylation of acetyl-CoA carboxylase (ACC), resulting in accumulation of TG and diacylglycerol in mouse skeletal muscle (Selathurai et al., 2015). As part of this study, up-regulated PCYT2 was accompanied by decreased TG content in the 150 mg/kg SE group. These results were consistent with the aforementioned studies. Furthermore, LPCAT is a key enzyme catalyzing the re-acylation of LPC and acyl-CoA to form PC (Bates and Browse, 2012). The decreased LPCAT content is consistent with the metabolomics finding of LPC accumulation in the glycerophospholipid metabolism pathway. In addition, catalysis of lysophosphatidic acid conversion to phosphatidic acid, a pivotal step in PL biosynthesis, is mediated by AGPAT2 (Bradley and Duncan, 2018). In this study, 150 mg/kg SE increased PL content. Correlation analysis results exhibited a negative correlation between PL and peak shear force, which further confirmed that SE can promote PL synthesis by mediating the phospholipid metabolic pathway, and the increased PL content in muscle contributes to reducing peak shear force.

Phospholipids form the basic scaffold of biological membranes and exert a key role in preserving organelle integrity (Morita and Ikeda, 2022). The increased PL content contributes to the stability of ER and MT membranes, which enhance cellular homeostasis and function (Kannan et al., 2016; Hernandez-Alvarez et al., 2019). ER and MT stress biomarkers m RNA expression in broiler breast muscle were investigated. As a key lipid synthesis and transport organelle (Marafon et al., 2022), ER homeostasis disruption leads to abnormal lipid metabolism and activates the unfolded protein response (UPR) (Jing et al., 2023). GRP78 serves a pivotal function as an ER chaperone protein and essential regulatory factor (Bai et al., 2023). In the current investigation, 150 mg/kg SE effectively down-regulated the mRNA expression of IRE1, GRP78, XBP1 and PERK, as well as the relative protein expression of GRP78 and XBP1. These results indicated that SE contributed to enhance ER homeostasis in broiler breast muscle. Correlation analysis showed a general negative correlation between PL and ER stress biomarkers, which suggested that SE may enhance ER homeostasis by regulating the phospholipid metabolic pathway and promoting PL synthesis.

MT is the primary site for reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation. MT dysfunction leads to excessive ROS accumulation, which induces cellular damage and triggers MT stress (Zhao et al., 2021). The antioxidant system can inhibit the initiation of MT stress or mitigate its damage by scavenging excess ROS (Sathyabhama et al., 2022). In this study, 150 mg/kg SE decreased MDA content and enhanced T-AOC activity in breast muscle, accompanied by decreased mRNA and protein expression of HSP60. HSP60 is primarily localized in the mitochondrial matrix and serves as a key biomarker for mitochondrial homeostasis, and its up-regulation expression is frequently concomitant with MT stress (Venkatesh and Suzuki, 2017). Correlation analysis revealed a positive correlation between MDA content and HSP60 expression, as well as a negative correlation between HSP60 and PL content. These results suggested that SE may enhance mitochondrial homeostasis and improve antioxidant capacity by promoting PL synthesis.

Maintenance of ER and MT homeostasis facilitates the normal progression of lipid metabolism (Flis and Daum, 2013), and enhancing ER and MT homeostasis effectively reduces lipid synthesis and accumulation in liver of broilers (Jing et al., 2023). We additionally assessed the expression of genes linked to lipid production. In the present study, 150 mg/kg SE improved ER and MT homeostasis, while decreased the TG and TC contents. Meanwhile, SE inclusion decreased FASN and ACACA expression. Correlation analysis demonstrated that TG content was positively correlated with the ER stress marker IRE1 and the MT stress marker HSP60, and TC content was positively in correlation with HSP60. These results further confirmed that SE contributed to enhancing the ER and MT homeostasis, thus reducing TG and TC contents in broiler breast muscle.

Conclusion

In this study, dietary SE supplementation exhibits a tendency to enhance broiler growth performance. More importantly, SE supplementation improves brolier breast muscle meat quality, which tightly links to the regulation of the glycerophospholipid metabolic pathway in breast muscle. Specifically, dietary SE supplementation improves glycerophospholipid metabolism and increases the content of PL in breast muscle. As a critical constituent of cell membranes, PL enhances ER and MT homeostasis, maintaining the structural integrity of muscle cells, which contribute to enhance the antioxidant capacity, decrease lipid accumulation and improve the FA composition, ultimately reducing peak shear force and improving the water-holding capacity of breast muscle in broilers. Regression analysis indicated that the optimal dietary SE supplementation level for broilers is 150 mg/kg.

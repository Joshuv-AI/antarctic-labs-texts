Transient increase of flicker electroretinograms after combined cataract surgery and vitrectomy for idiopathic epiretinal membrane
Authors: Yuzen Kashima, Kumiko Kato, Shinichiro Chujo, Ryunosuke Nagashima, CO, Hisashi Matsubara, Yoshitsugu Matsui, Yumiho Tenma, Masahiko Sugimoto, Mineo Kondo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo determine the characteristics of the changes in the amplitude of the flicker electroretinograms (ERGs) after combined cataract surgery and vitrectomy for an idiopathic epiretinal membrane (ERM). To accomplish this, we conducted a prospective study on 20 patients. Flicker ERGs and central macular thickness (CMT) were measured before the surgery (baseline), and at 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, and 6 months postoperatively. The mean amplitude of the flicker ERGs increased significantly by 63.5% at 1 week after surgery (P < 0.001) and then gradually decreased. The amplitude at 6 months was not significantly different from the baseline amplitude. This transient increase in the flicker ERG amplitudes after ERM surgery suggests that electrophysiologic evaluations of the therapeutic effects of vitrectomy should be performed 6 months postoperatively. The mean CMT decreased significantly at 1 week postoperatively (P < 0.001) and continued to decrease progressively over six months. The relative increase of amplitude at 1 week was significantly associated with the relative decrease of CMT at 6 months, and evaluation of retinal functional changes at 1 week may be able to predict the postoperative course of the ERM.Keywords: Vitrectomy, Electroretinography, Inflammation, Central macular thickness, Aqueous flare value, Epiretinal membraneSubject terms: Retinal diseases, Eye abnormalities

Introduction

Electroretinography (ERG) is used to examine the physiological status of the retina, and it has been used for the diagnosis of inherited retinal dystrophies and other retinal abnormalities. Many investigations have been performed on the effects of diabetic retinopathy1,2 and retinal vascular occlusions3–5 on the ERGs to determine the degree of retinal ischemia.

Clinical studies have also been performed to evaluate the changes in the retinal physiology by recording ERGs before and after surgeries for various ophthalmic disorders such as retinal detachment6–8, macular hole9,10, epiretinal membrane (ERM)11–21, diabetic retinopathy22, silicone oil tamponade23, intraocular foreign body24, and gene therapy25. The changes in the amplitudes and implicit times were analyzed, and increases in the amplitudes of the ERGs that was reduced before surgery or a shortening of implicit time that was prolonged before surgery were interpreted as an improvement in the retinal physiology. These postoperative ERGs were usually recorded after the surgical incisions had healed because of the need to use a contact lens electrode to pick up the signals from the retina. Thus, the early changes in the retinal physiology have not been determined.

The recently developed hand-held RETeval system can record ERGs using skin electrodes has allowed clinicians to record ERGs immediately after the surgery. We have used the RETeval system to record flicker ERGs during the early period following cataract surgery26. We found that the amplitude of the flicker ERGs peaked at 1 week postoperatively and then gradually returned to the baseline values. The increase of the ERGs was significantly associated with the increase in the aqueous flare value (AFV) and CMT. In addition, we found that the increase in the ERG amplitudes appeared prior to the increase in the CMT, i.e., there was a time lag between the change in retinal physiology and the change in the retinal morphology. These findings strongly suggested that the increase in the ERG amplitudes after cataract surgery was a transient phenomenon caused by intraocular inflammation after the surgery. These findings stimulated our interest in determining whether the implicit times and amplitudes of the flicker ERGs were more altered during the postoperative period of combined vitrectomy with cataract surgery which is considered to be more invasive than cataract surgery alone.

Thus, the purpose of this study was to determine the characteristics of the amplitudes and implicit times of the flicker ERGs during the pre- and postoperative periods after combined cataract surgery and vitrectomy for an ERM, and to suggest the optimal timing for ERG evaluation considering potential postoperative inflammation.

Results

Clinical demographics of patients who underwent combined cataract surgery and vitrectomy

All 23 eyes underwent combined phacoemulsification with intraocular lens implantation and vitrectomy with a removal of an ERM without any postoperative complications. For the statistical analyses, 3 patients were excluded because of missing test data. In the end, the data of 20 eyes (12 men and 8 women) were used for the statistical analyses.

The amplitude of fundamental component of the flicker ERGs increased from 11.4 µV to 17.8 µV at 1 week after the surgery (Figs. 1A and 2nd row). Thereafter, the amplitude of the ERGs gradually decreased to the baseline value. There were no significant changes in the implicit times after the surgery.

To assess the changes of the flicker ERG amplitudes before and after the surgery quantitatively, we plotted the amplitude at each time point of the 20 subjects (Fig. 1B). We found that the mean amplitude of the fundamental component of the flicker ERGs increased by 63.5% from the preoperative value which was a significant increase from the baseline at 1 week after the surgery (P < 0.001). Thereafter, the amplitude of flicker ERGs gradually decreased, although the amplitude increased slightly for 3 months, (Fig. 1B, red arrow). At 6 months after the surgery, the mean amplitude of the flicker ERGs was not significantly different from the preoperative value (P = 0.124).

We have also plotted the changes in the implicit times of the fundamental component of the flicker ERGs before and after the surgery in Fig. 1C. There were no statistically significant differences at any time point after the surgery. There were no significant change in the pupillary diameter during the RETeval flicker ERG testing at all test points after the surgery compared to the preoperative baseline values (data not shown).

We also performed the same analysis using the reconstructed waveform of the flicker ERGs that are the sum of the first 8 harmonics, and similar findings were found (Supplementary Fig. 1).

Discussion

Our results showed that the mean amplitude of the flicker ERGs was 63.5% larger than the preoperative amplitude at 1 week after the combined cataract surgery and vitrectomy for eyes with an ERM. However, the amplitude gradually decreased thereafter, and by 6 months, the difference was not significant. These results indicate that the increase in ERG amplitude is a transient phenomenon, and the period of greatest amplitude increase is during the first postoperative week. However, it is interesting that there was a slight but significant increase in the amplitude again at 3 months after the postoperative anti-inflammatory eye drops were discontinued. Although the increase of AFV at 1 week to 3 months of postoperatively was not statistically significant, the periods of increased AFV coincided with the period during which the amplitude of the flicker ERGs was significantly increased. Thus, we suggest that the increase in the flicker ERG amplitudes at 1 week was likely due to the effects of inflammation caused by the intraocular surgery.

It has long been reported that the amplitude of ERGs increases after cataract surgery, and this increase has been interpreted as due to a removal of the filtering effect of the cataractous lens31. We recently reported that the amplitude of the flicker ERGs was increased by 31% at the first postoperative week after cataract surgery followed by a gradual decrease26. Because the amplitude increase in the first postoperative week was statistically significant and was associated with the maximum increase in the AFV and CMT, we suggested that the amplitude increase after cataract surgery was due to intraocular inflammation. Although there was no statistically significant difference in postoperative amplitude increase between cataract surgery alone or the combined surgery of cataract and ERM surgery, the mean increase in the amplitude after ERM surgery was approximately twice that after cataract surgery alone. Furthermore, the duration of the increased postoperative amplitude was also longer after the simultaneous vitrectomy and cataract surgery than after cataract surgery alone. These results suggest that simultaneous cataract surgery and vitrectomy induced a greater inflammatory response in the retina resulting in a greater and longer-lasting increase in the amplitude of the flicker ERGs.

Then, the question is why was there a transient increase in amplitudes after the combined cataract surgery and vitrectomy? We have reported that the amplitude of the ERG increased significantly in eyes with mild retinal ischemia2,5 and mild uveitis32, and it is reasonable to suggest that intraocular mediators produced by pathogenic conditions such as ischemia and inflammation may be involved in the amplitude increase. It is not clear which mediators are involved, but nitric oxide (NO) may be a good candidate. NO is produced in the inner retinal layer and is involved in the signals originating from the retinal signaling pathways33,34. NO has various cellular effects, and the effect of NO in the eye is concentration-dependent. Low intraocular NO concentration is associated with vasodilation, intraocular pressure regulation, and neuroprotection. Under pathologic ocular condition such as in ischemic and inflammatory retinal diseases including diabetic retinopathy, uveitis, and trauma35–37, the intraocular NO concentration is higher and is accompanied by tissue inflammation, ocular cell apoptosis, and neurodegeneration38. It has been reported that the amplitude of the ERGs is increased in rats injected intravitreally with a low concentration (200µM) of NO but the ERGs decreased at high concentration (2mM) of NO39. Another study showed that NO in the anterior chamber is elevated after cataract surgery in a rabbit model40.

The mean increase in the amplitude was greater for combined cataract and vitrectomy surgery than for cataract surgery alone. Electrophysiological methods have been used to evaluate the effects of the irrigating fluid41, intraocular illumination6, and injection of agents such as Brilliant blue G or triamcinolone acetonide42 during vitrectomy on the ERGs. However, the changes in the ERGs have been reported to be not significant, or if there were changes, they were of very short duration during the perioperative period of the surgery.

There are many surgical procedures that can affect the retinal physiology, and it is difficult to determine which procedure affected the amplitude of the ERGs. To answer this question will require continued validation of the retinal electrophysiological changes during the postoperative period of vitrectomy in the future.

The earlier studies that used ERGs to analyze changes in the retinal physiology after ERM surgery are summarized in Table 3. In many studies, multifocal ERGs or focal macular ERGs were recorded to assess the changes in the macular function before and after the removal of an ERM. The results have varied with some reporting an increase in the amplitude after surgery while others report no significant changes in the amplitudes, and still others report a decrease in amplitudes. Watanabe et al. recorded full-field ERGs after ERM surgery using skin electrodes, and they found no significant change on postoperative day 1. However, at 1 week after the surgery, they reported an approximately 30% increase in the dark-adapted 0.01 ERGs, photopic negative response, and the light-adapted 3.0 ERG amplitudes which is comparable to our results. They interpreted these increases to have resulted from the removal of the ERM which restored macular function. This then led to the increase in the full-field ERGs. However, they did not document the subsequent ERG changes. Even if a removal of the ERM restored macular function, it is unlikely that it would restore the function of the entire retina and result in a 30% increase in the amplitude of the full-field ERGs.

We also analyzed whether the relative increases in each parameter of the ERGs, CMT, AFV, and visual acuity, were related to during the perioperative period of combined ERM surgery. Kumagai et al. reported that the thinner the CMT, the better the visual acuity at any time point from 1 to 24 months postoperatively43. In this study, the ΔBCVA at 6 months postoperatively was associated with the ΔCMT from 1 week to 6 months postoperatively, which appears to be similar to the findings of Kumagai et al., although the follow-up period was shorter than that of Kumagai et al. Furthermore, we found that the relative change in the flicker ERGs detected at the first postoperative week was negatively and significantly associated with the relative decrease in the CMT at 1 week to 6 months postoperatively. Thus, the more the flicker ERGs increased in the first postoperative week, the thinner was the CMT at 1 week to 6 months postoperatively. These results were unexpected and difficult to explain. Hibi et al. reported a significant correlation between the relative changes in the amplitudes of the oscillatory potentials of the focal macular ERG (fm-ERG) at 6 months postoperatively and the relative change in the CMT at 6 months postoperatively17. Thus, the more the amplitude of oscillatory potentials increased at the 6 months of postoperatively, the thinner was the CMT. Their results were comparable to our results in that the larger the relative increase in postoperative amplitude, the thinner was the CMT. We suggest that the decrease in CMT is due to the release of traction on the retina, but which is modified by postoperative intraocular inflammation. An earlier report showed that the amplitudes of the flicker ERGs increase when there is only mild to moderate intraocular inflammation, and the amplitudes of the flicker ERGs decrease or have minimal changes when there is severe intraocular inflammation39. Thus, we suggest that cases with only mild to moderate intraocular inflammation may easily return to normal retinal function and macular morphology after the surgery, while cases with severe intraocular inflammation may have delayed recovery of retinal function and macular morphology. In cases with minimal changes of the amplitude of the ERGs, it is sometimes difficult to determine whether there is an absence of inflammation or a severe inflammation. The implicit times of ERGs have been reported to be delayed in severe inflammation and ischemia5, thus, focusing on the implicit time may help to determine whether there is severe inflammation or not. This inference may be supported by the fact that the CMT at 6 months postoperatively was thicker than the preoperative CMT in patients whose amplitudes decreased and the implicit times delayed at the first week of postoperatively (Fig. 3, red arrow). Because this study included only idiopathic ERM cases, most had only mild postoperative inflammation, but we need to determine the cause of the negative correlation between the ERG and CMT more decisively. This can be done by further studies on cases with various types of intraocular surgeries in which the postoperative inflammation is likely to develop and be prolonged.

Several studies have been conducted to evaluate the recovery of retinal function by recording ff-ERGs before and after vitrectomy for different kinds of retinal disorders44–46. When conducting such studies, one should keep in mind that vitrectomy itself may cause a transient increase of the ff-ERGs during the first 3 months after surgery. Thus, it would be preferable to record ERGs after at least 6 months after the surgery when assessing the recovery of retinal function with ERGs.

There are several limitations in this study. First, the number of cases was small which limited the strength of the statistical analyses. Second, the relatively short follow-up period limited our determining the long-term effects of the combined surgeries. And third, the anti-inflammatory ophthalmic solutions were terminated at 2 months postoperatively which may have resulted in residual ocular inflammation at 3 months postoperatively. Fourth, recent reports have shown that the inner retinal disorganization is associated with the postoperative visual prognosis. The ERGs and retinal structural findings might be associated also, but most of the ERMs in this study were at Stage 2, and we could not examine these issues. We would like to examine these issues in future studies. Fifth, we only recorded flicker ERGs but it will be necessary to record the other ERG components such as dark-adapted responses because that may help identify the pathological mechanisms causing the intraocular inflammation during the postoperative period. A final limitation was that all of the patients underwent combined cataract and vitrectomy surgery. Therefore, we were unable to determine to what extent the amplitudes of the flicker ERGs increases were due to vitrectomy alone.

In conclusion, we have found that the increase in the amplitude of the flicker ERGs after the surgery for an idiopathic ERM is a transient phenomenon, and the time of greatest amplitude increase is around the first postoperative week. Our study indicated the possibilities that recording ERGs postoperatively would enable clinicians to monitor postoperative inflammation and to predict the surgical outcomes. We need to evaluate the effects of vitreous surgery at least 6 months after the surgery because inflammations due to the surgical intervention lasted longer than we expected.

Methods

Study design

This was a prospective, single center study conducted at the Mie University Hospital between December 2020 and December 2023. The Medical Ethics Committee of Mie University Hospital approved all experimental protocols that were used in this study (No. H2020-193). All procedures were carried out in accordance with the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki of the World Medical Association. All participants signed a written informed consent form after they were provided with information on the procedures to be used and possible complications. This study was registered on the International Clinical Trial Registry Platform (UMIN Clinical Trials Registry, UMIN000040874, http://www.umin.ac.jp/cter/index-j.htm) on December 1, 2020.

Subjects

The eligibility criteria were patients who had undergone combined phacoemulsification cataract surgery and vitrectomy for an idiopathic ERM. In addition, they had to be ≥ 50-years-of-age with a nuclear, cortical, or posterior subcapsular cataract of grade 1 to 220. The exclusion criteria were eyes with diabetic retinopathy, retinal vein occlusion, uveitis, and glaucoma receiving topical medications because these eyes are known to have a high likelihood of developing cystoid macular edema (CME) after cataract surgery21–23. Patients who had myopia ≥ 6.0 diopters (D) were also excluded. In the end, 23 eyes of 23 patients that had undergone the combined surgeries were studied.

Combined phacoemulsification with intraocular lens implantation and vitrectomy

Surgery was performed by 7 experienced surgeons, HM, YM, SC, TS, YT, MS, and MK, in the Mie University Hospital. After topical or sub-Tenon capsule anesthesia by lidocaine hydrochloride, the cataract was extracted and a posterior chamber intraocular lens implanted. Then, vitrectomy was performed with a 25G- or 27G system (Constellation®, Alcon, Fort Worth, TX). Triamcinolone acetonide was used in all eyes to increase the visibility of the vitreous and the posterior hyaloid membrane. The ERM and the internal limiting membrane (ILM) were peeled in the macular area using forceps after the ILM was stained with 0.25 mg/mL brilliant blue G (BBG) solution (ILM-Blue®, DORC, Zuidland, Netherland).

Topical moxifloxacin and betamethasone were used 3/day and bromfenac 2/day beginning on postoperative day one. At two months after the surgery, all of the topical eye drops were discontinued after we confirmed that there were no ocular complications.

Protocols for examinations

The best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) was measured with a standard Japanese decimal visual acuity chart at 5 m. The intraocular pressure (IOP) was measured with a non-contact automatic tonometer (CT-1P, Topcon, Tokyo, Japan). The CMT was measured in the images of a spectral-domain OCT device (Spectralis; Heidelberg Engineering Inc., Heidelberg, German), and the CMT was defined as the average thickness of the macula within the central 1 mm of the retina. The AFV was measured with a laser flare photometer (Kowa FC-1000 LFCM; Kowa Co, Ltd, Tokyo, Japan), and the value was expressed in photon counts/millisecond (ph/ms). These measurements were made before the surgery, then at 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, and 6 months after the surgery.

Flicker electroretinograms recorded with RETeval system

Full-field flicker ERGs were recorded with the RETeval system (LKC Technologies, Gaithersburg, MD, USA). The full-field stimuli were presented with a 60-mm diameter ganzfeld dome, and the white stimuli were created by a combination of three colored light emitting diodes. Flicker ERGs were recorded under natural pupil condition. We used a stimulus intensity of 32 photopic Td-s which we have reported to be less susceptible to the effects of the sequence of recordings compared to weaker stimuli47. The ERGs were picked up with a skin electrode array (Sensor Strip; LKC Technologies, Inc.) that was placed 2 mm from the margin of the lower eyelid.

The amplitudes and implicit times of the fundamental components of the flicker ERGs were automatically measured and displayed by the RETeval system using a special algorithm with a discrete Fourier transformation and cross-correlation analysis. We analyzed the fundamental components of the flicker ERGs because the fundamental component has been known to be sensitive to various physiological changes in the retina48–51.

Statistical analyses

Two-way layout ANOVA was performed to determine whether there was a significant change in the implicit times and amplitudes of the fundamental component of the flicker ERGs, CMT, AFV, and BCVA at each postoperative time. Dunnett’s multiple comparison tests were used to identify which time points were significantly different from the baseline values. To determine whether the degree of change of two different factors were significantly associated, including their change over time, a linkage regression analysis was performed29,30. We examined whether the relative changes of the values of the flicker ERGs at one week after the surgery were significantly correlated with the relative changes of the AFV, CMT and the changes of BCVA at each time point after the surgery. The statistical analyses were performed using the IBM SPSS Statistics software program (version 29.0.0.0) and Microsoft Excel. A P of < 0.05 was taken to be statistically significant.

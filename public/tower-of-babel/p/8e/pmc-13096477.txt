Protocadherin γC4 regulates neuronal survival and dendritic self-avoidance
Authors: Ryuon Higuchi, Masaki Tatara, Satoko Horino, Shota Katori, Makoto Sanbo, Masumi Hirabayashi, Masahiko Watanabe, Ayumu Konno, Hirokazu Hirai, Yoshichika Yoshioka, Takahiro Hirabayashi, Toshihiro Kobayashi, Ryosuke Kaneko, Takeshi Yagi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Animal models are indispensable for linking human genetic findings to disease mechanisms. Mutations in protocadherin gamma C4 (γC4), one of the 22 isoforms encoded by the protocadherin-γ (Pcdh-γ) gene cluster, cause a human neurodevelopmental syndrome with progressive microcephaly, seizures, and intellectual disability. Here, we established a γC4 mutant mouse model that exhibits motor dysfunction, seizures, reduced brain size, and increased embryonic neuronal apoptosis. Using DOMINO (Double Mutation-Induced Open Reading Frame Switch), a two-step CRISPR/Cas9-based genome-editing strategy, we also generated γC4fl-only mice that retain full-length γC4 while truncating the other 21 Pcdh-γ isoforms. Unlike Pcdh-γ cluster-deficient mice, γC4fl-only mice were viable and fertile. Furthermore, we show that the γC4 constant region (γCR) contributes to the regulation of Purkinje cell dendritic architecture and self-avoidance. Together, these findings indicate that γCR-containing γC4 is required for neuronal survival and dendritic patterning, supporting γC4 as a principal functional isoform within the Pcdh-γ gene cluster.

Introduction

Neurodevelopmental disorders (NDDs) encompass a broad range of conditions characterized by impairments in cognitive, social, and behavioral development1. While genetic studies have revealed numerous risk genes associated with NDDs, functional validation in vivo remains essential for understanding how these genes contribute to brain development and pathology. Among these, human protocadherin gamma C4 encoded by PCDHGC4 gene emerged as a candidate novel neurodevelopmental syndrome2. However, its precise role in neurodevelopment has yet to be elucidated. Here, we report a mouse model carrying a mutant protocadherin gamma C4 (hereafter γC4) allele, which provides an in vivo platform to investigate the gene’s contribution to NDDs. This model enables detailed analysis of how γC4 deficiency affects brain development, behavior, and neuronal connectivity. Establishing such models is critical not only for dissecting disease mechanisms but also for paving the way toward for targeted therapeutic approaches. Together, our findings establish the γC4 mutant mouse model as a valuable platform for linking genetic discoveries to mechanistic and translational insight into NDDs.

Protein–protein interactions at the neuronal cell membrane are critical for synapse formation, maintenance, and remodeling during development and throughout life3. These intercellular interactions ensure the precise alignment and communication between pre- and postsynaptic elements, allowing for functional neural circuits to emerge. Clustered protocadherins (cPcdh)4–6 were well known as the diversity of cell-surface recognition molecules playing a crucial role in regulating neural connectivity. cPcdh extracellular cadherin domains contributed to trans cPcdh homophilic interaction to self/non-self recognition7–10. Pcdh-α and Pcdh-γ constant region (CR) negatively regulated FAK or PYK2 for cortical dendrite arborization, neural migration, and cytoskeletal dynamics11–13. In addition, Pcdh-γ CR controls PKC-γ11,14, presenilin-dependent processing to the nuclear function15, neuronal self-avoidance16–18, and dendrite morphology with interaction some signal molecules7,11,19. Also, cPcdh isoforms were causative gene for some neurodevelopment and neuropsychiatric diseases20, schizophrenia21–23, ASD24, and Alzheimer’s disease25. Previous studies showed that mice lacking the three Pcdh-γ C-type, such as γC3, γC4, and γC5 isoforms (triple γC-type knockout, or TCKO) exhibit a phenotype resembling that of all 22 Pcdh-γ deletion mutants26. Garrett et al. revealed that γC4 is the sole isoform required for both neuronal and organism survival27,28. And recently, McLeod et al. reported a new transgenic mouse line that overexpresses γC4 under the CAG promoter, and demonstrated that CAG-driven γC4 overexpression can rescue the spinal cord apoptosis caused by γC4 deficiency29. Moreover, biallelic variants in human PCDHGC4 caused a novel neurodevelopmental syndrome2, but the postnatal function of γC4 is unknown because γC4-disrupt mutant mice had neonatal lethality27.

In this study, to define the isoform-specific molecular role of γC4 in vivo, we developed a new CRISPR/Cas9 strategy, termed the DOMINO method, to generate mouse lines that selectively disrupt γC4 or restrict Pcdh-γ expression to γC4. We succeeded in generating γC4 mutant mice, which express gamma constant region (γCR) deleted γC4 induced by nonsense-mediated mRNA decay (NMD)30–32. These mutants had a chance of survive but exhibited brain size reduction, seizures, motor dysfunction, increased neuronal apoptosis, and abnormal dendritic patterning of Purkinje cells as a neurodevelopment syndrome model mouse. Finally, we identify γC4 as a key Pcdh-γ isoform for dendritic self-avoidance and show that postnatal AAV-mediated γC4 overexpression can partially rescue the self-crossing phenotype. Together, these findings suggest that enhancing γC4 function may have therapeutic potential to alleviate disease-relevant phenotypes in neurodevelopmental disorders.

Results

γC4 isoform of protocadherin γ cluster is not necessarily essential for postnatal viability

To explore the specific role of γC4 among the 22 isoforms encoded by the mouse protocadherin-γ (Pcdh-γ) gene cluster, we used CRISPR/Cas9-mediated genome editing to generate γC4-specific mutants (Fig. 1a). A 2-bp deletion was introduced into the variable cytoplasmic domain (VCD) of γC4, γC4nmd allele, creating a premature stop codon in CR1 and effectively disrupting protein production (Supplementary Fig. 1) via nonsense-mediated mRNA decay (NMD). Then, we performed qPCR using isoform-specific forward primers targeting the VCD of each variable exon and a universal reverse primer targeting the common exon were designed according to the previous reports33,34 (Supplementary Fig. 2a). In γC4nmd mice, the expression of the γC4 isoform was significantly reduced, while the other 21 isoforms in the cluster remained unchanged compared to WT (Supplementary Fig. 2b). Next, we examined γC4 protein products in γC4nmd by western blotting using antibodies against the γC4 extracellular domain (Fig. 1b). In contrast to wild-type (WT), the full-length γC4 band was absent, but a lower-molecular-weight band consistent with a γCR-deficient truncated γC4 product was detected (Fig. 1b, arrow; Supplementary Fig. 3a). As the truncated γC4 protein was reproducibly detected, we quantified band intensities to compare the expression levels of full-length γC4 in WT with those of truncated γC4 in γC4nmd (Supplementary Fig. 3b, c, n = 6 animals per genotype). This analysis showed that γC4nmd expresses the truncated γC4 protein at ~18% of the WT full-length γC4 level (Supplementary Fig. 3c, WT = 100 ± 4.49, γC4nmd = 17.9 ± 2.32, p = 0.0022, Mann–Whitney U-test). Video monitoring of neonatal mice revealed that WT pups exhibited rhythmic respiratory movements, while γC4nmd pups showed tremors in the limbs without clear evidence of spontaneous breathing (Supplementary Video 1). Although some of γC4nmd homozygous mutants died neonatally, 10 out of 23 pups (43.5%) survived beyond 4 weeks of age. These findings indicate that neonatal lethality is not fully penetrant when a γC4 variant lacking γCR is expressed. The body weight of surviving γC4nmd homozygous were significantly reduced compared to wild-type littermate at P14 (7.03 g compared with 5.64 g, n = 15 WT and n = 10 γC4nmd animals, p < 0.0001, unpaired t-test). Since it has been reported that patients with PCDHGC4 mutations showed microcephaly2, we sought to examine whether a similar phenotype is observed in γC4nmd mice by quantifying brain volume using high-resolution T2-weighted MRI. A sagittal brain T2-weighted image revealed a clear reduction in total brain size in γC4nmd mice compared to wild-type controls (Fig. 1c). Next, to test whether sole full-length γC4 isoform is sufficient to regulate individual survival, we developed a unique dual-CRISPR strategy named “DOMINO” (DOuble Mutation INducing Open reading frame switch). In this system, using γC4nmd allele, second CRISPR-induced 1-bp frameshift in the Pcdh-γ constant exon 1 (γCR1) restored the reading frame specifically for γC4, while disrupting the expression of the other 21 isoforms (Supplementary Fig. 4a–c). We successfully generated C4fl-only mice that exclusively expressed only full-length γC4 among 22-Pcdh-γ cluster, confirmed by genomic sequencing and PCR genotyping (Supplementary Figs. 1d, e and 4e, f). The qPCR analysis using γC4fl-only brain revealed that the expression level of γC4 was preserved, whereas the expression of the other 21 isoforms was significantly downregulated (Supplementary Fig. 5). These results confirm that our genome-editing strategy selectively targeted the intended isoforms and preserved full-length γC4 expression at mRNA levels comparable to WT. Western blotting of brain lysates revealed that γC4fl-only mice retained expression of full-length γC4 protein, while γB2 and γC3 were detected only as truncated forms (Fig. 1f). Since previous studies reported that mice in which all Pcdh-γ proteins are C-terminally truncated exhibit extensive neonatal lethality35, we conclude that expression of full-length γC4 alone, among the 22 Pcdh-γ isoforms, is sufficient to support stable postnatal survival in the C4fl-only mutant. These C4fl-only mice were viable, fertile, and indistinguishable from WT controls in gross appearance and behavior (Supplementary Video 2).

As we have established two newly generated mouse strains, survived γC4 truncated mutant and only γC4 expression mutant, we performed footprint analysis to assess the motor coordination (Fig. 1g). γC4nmd exhibited significantly shorter and narrower stride patterns compared to WT, indicative of impaired locomotor coordination, in contrast, γC4fl-only mice displayed normal walking patterns. In addition to gait impairments, γC4nmd mutants displayed abnormal motor behaviors such as misstepping/falling from elevated surfaces and seizures (Supplementary Video 3).

These findings suggest that the Pcdh-γ constant cytoplasmic region (γCR) of γC4 is a key determinant of viability: when γC4 lacks the γCR, mice show reduced survival rate and display behavioral abnormalities among survivors. By contrast, whereas loss of the CR across the entire 22-member Pcdh-γ cluster is lethal, retaining the CR only in γC4 is sufficient to support postnatal survival. Together, the partial lethality, reduced brain volume, and isoform-specific nature of the γC4nmd mutation suggest that γC4nmd mice may represent a useful model for human neurodevelopmental syndromes, enabling mechanistic studies of protocadherin dysregulation during early brain development.

A frameshift allele near the signal peptide generates a protein-null γC4 mutation with fully penetrant neonatal lethality

To examine how the position of coding-sequence disruption influences γC4 transcript fate and organismal outcome, we compared two independently generated alleles: the γC4nmd allele and a signal peptide-targeted frameshift allele (γC4NΔ79) (Supplementary Fig. 6). The γC4NΔ79 allele was generated by CRISPR/Cas9-mediated deletion of 79 bp near the 5’ end of the coding sequence, within the region encoding the N-terminal signal peptide, resulting in a frameshift and premature termination at the EC1 domain (Supplementary Fig. 6a, b). Consistent with a severe loss-of-function mutation, γC4NΔ79 homozygous pups were not viable and exhibited abnormal gross appearance at birth (Supplementary Fig. 6c; Supplementary Video 4), in agreement with an independently generated γC4 frameshift knockout allele reported previously (13 bp frame-shifting deletion 3’ to the start codon)27, which also shows fully neonatal lethality.

We next assessed transcript abundance to evaluate nonsense-mediated mRNA decay (NMD) sensitivity. In γC4nmd, a 2-bp deletion in the variable cytoplasmic domain introduces a premature stop codon in the first constant exon (CR1), a configuration expected to trigger efficient NMD, and accordingly γC4 mRNA levels were reduced to 10% of WT (Supplementary Fig. 2b). In contrast, γC4NΔ79 retained substantially higher γC4 mRNA levels (50% of WT; Supplementary Fig. 6e), this allele is associated with less efficient NMD than γC4nmd allele.

Despite this residual transcript, γC4 protein was not detectably expressed in γC4NΔ79 brains by western blotting, using extracellular domain γC4 antibodies (Supplementary Fig. 6f), indicating that the γC4NΔ79 transcript does not give rise to a stable γC4 protein product. Thus, although γC4nmd and γC4NΔ79 differ in residual transcript abundance, their molecular consequences at the protein level are distinct. In γC4nmd, γC4 mRNA is strongly reduced, yet a low level of γCR-deficient truncated γC4 is still produced (Fig. 1b; Supplementary Fig. 3), and we speculate that this residual truncated product may contribute to the fact that 40% of animals survive postnatally. In contrast, γC4NΔ79 retains substantially higher levels of mutant transcript but shows complete loss of detectable γC4 protein, and homozygous mutants exhibit fully penetrant neonatal lethality (Supplementary Fig. 4c, e, f). Based on these results, we conclude that γC4nmd represents an NMD-mediated, γCR-deficient loss-of-function allele, whereas γC4NΔ79 behaves as a clean γC4 knockout allele as previously reported27.

In sagittal sections (Fig. 2a–a′′′; DAPI/CC3), WT embryos showed sparse CC3 signal, whereas γΔCR1 embryos exhibited prominent apoptosis distributed across broad brain regions. In the γC4nmd embryos, apoptosis was also increased compared to WT but appeared less extensive than in γΔCR1, and the distribution was comparatively more restricted. In contrast, γC4fl-only embryos showed minimal CC3 signal, comparable to WT in sagittal sections.

We then analyzed coronal sections matched to the sagittal levels (Fig. 2a) and co-stained for GAD67 and CC3 (Fig. 2b–e). In γΔCR1, robust CC3 signal was observed in multiple coronal planes, and apoptotic cells were enriched within GAD67-positive regions, including the reticular formation/midbrain, superior colliculus, periaqueductal gray, and medulla (Fig. 2b′–e′). γC4nmd showed CC3-positive apoptosis in similar anatomical regions, but the overall intensity and extent were clearly milder than in γΔCR1 (Fig. 2b′′–e′′). This result indicates that expression of γC4 lacking the γCR, accompanied with reduced γC4 expression levels, is sufficient to induce neuronal apoptosis. However, because the extent of cell death is modest, the neuronal apoptosis triggered by Pcdh-γ deficiency may depends on the number of isoforms disrupted. By contrast, γC4fl-only embryos showed minimal CC3 signal across these coronal sections (Fig. 2b′′′–e′′′), consistent with suppression of embryonic apoptosis when full-length γC4 is the only full-length Pcdh-γ isoform, with the other 21 isoforms truncated.

We next quantified apoptosis across anatomically defined regions, cortical amygdala (CoA), zona incerta (ZI), reticular formation (RF), superior colliculus (SC), periaqueductal gray (PAG), and gigantocellular nucleus (Gi) (Fig. 3a). We visualized the extent of cell death using two metrics, CC3-positive cell density (cells/mm²) and CC3-positive area as described previously36 (Fig. 3b). Quantification was performed using multiple embryos (WT n = 4, γ∆CR1 n = 3, γC4nmd n = 6, and γC4fl-only n = 5), with 2–3 sections per region per embryo and then one-way ANOVA with Tukey’s multiple-comparisons test. γΔCR1 embryos showed robust increases in CC3 across all analyzed regions. γC4nmd embryos also exhibited significant increases, but the magnitude and distribution differed from γΔCR1, indicating that loss of full-length γC4 (with residual γCR-deficient truncated γC4) does not phenocopy complete loss of all Pcdh-γ isoforms. Importantly, γC4fl-only embryos showed CC3 levels near WT across regions, consistent with γC4 being sufficient to suppress the elevated embryonic apoptosis observed in the pan-Pcdh-γ knockout background.

Finally, to assess the cellular identity of apoptotic cells, we co-immunostained for GAD67 together with CC3 and analyzed the reticular formation/midbrain (Fig. 3c). In γΔCR1 and γC4nmd embryos, CC3 signals overlapped with GAD67-positive neurons, indicating that inhibitory neurons contribute substantially to apoptosis in this region. CC3-positive cells were rare in WT and γC4fl-only.

FAK and PYK2 phosphorylation is independent of the γC4 constant region

To examine the specific intracellular signal transduction via γC4 corresponded to neural cell death, we focused on the phosphorylation of FAK and PYK2, which are known to be regulated by Pcdh-γ constant region (γCR)11,12. We performed western blotting using P0 brain lysates from WT, γΔCR1, γC4nmd, and γC4fl-only mice (Fig. 4a), and quantified phosphorylation as pY397-FAK/total FAK and pY402-PYK2/total PYK2 (n = 6 biological samples per genotype; one-way ANOVA with Tukey’s multiple-comparisons test). Total protein levels of FAK and PYK2 were comparable across genotypes (Fig. 4a). In contrast, FAK phosphorylation was significantly increased in γΔCR1 and γC4fl-only compared with WT (Fig. 4b), whereas γC4nmd did not show a significant change relative to WT. This pattern is consistent with prior reports that the γCR negatively regulates FAK phosphorylation; notably, in the γC4fl-only line the Pcdh-γ cluster other than γC4 isoform lack the CR, providing a plausible explanation for elevated pFAK in this genotype. Full-length γC4 regulate dendritic self-avoidance of Purkinje cells

Because γC4nmd mice exhibited impaired motor behavior, we reasoned that this newly generated line could serve as a model for human neurodevelopmental syndromes (Fig. 1g; Supplementary Video 3). Previous studies indicate that γC4 is broadly expressed across multiple brain regions and cell types36,37. Given that the Pcdh-γ cluster is known to contribute to dendritic self-avoidance in Purkinje cells, we performed morphological analyses of Purkinje cells in surviving WT, γC4nmd, and γC4fl-only mice. We sparsely labeled Purkinje cells by injecting Tetbow AAVs (AAV-Syn1-tTA, AAV-TRE-mTurquoise2, and AAV-TRE-EYFP) at postnatal day 1 (P1)38,39. At 4 weeks of age, when dendritic arbors are mature40, mice were perfused and cerebella were fixed, cleared with SeeDB2G41, and imaged (Fig. 5; Supplementary Video 5). Dendritic tangling was observed in Purkinje cells of γC4nmd allele (Fig. 5d–f), whereas in γC4fl-only cells, the phenotype was comparable to that of the controls (Fig. 5g–i). The Purkinje cell morphology and self-crossing quantifications were performed using one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s multiple-comparisons test. The overall area of Purkinje cells were no differences across the genotypes (Fig. 5j). However, in γC4nmd cells, the number of dendritic branches decreased, and the number of self-crossings per unit area increased (Fig. 5k, l). In contrast, γC4fl-only cells showed no significant differences compared to the control. Compared with WT (3.9 ± 0.37), dendritic self-crossings per unit area increased to 6.2 ± 0.62 in γC4nmd (p = 0.0091). Overexpression of γC4 partially rescued dendritic self-avoidance

To investigate whether individual Pcdh-γ isoforms can restore dendritic self-avoidance, we established a postnatal rescue system targeting Purkinje cells (Fig. 6a). Morphological development of Purkinje cells progresses through defined stages from fusiform (P0–P1) to stellate (P4), young (P10), and mature (P21).

To test whether overexpression of γC4 after birth can rescue dendritic patterning, at postnatal day 1 (P1), we injected Tetbow system AAVs encoding tTA, TRE-driven fluorescent reporters (mTurquoise2, EYFP), and L7 promoter-driven45 γC4 isoforms fused to a PA tag, into PV-Cre; γflox/γflox mice (Fig. 7a, b). PA-tag immunostaining confirmed robust expression in Purkinje cells at P4 and P28, with punctate signal in somata and dendrites and colocalization with EYFP-labeled PCs (Fig. 7c). The high-magnification image showed that PA-tag antibody indicated dot-like γC4 localization (Supplementary Fig. 7; Supplementary Video 6). The signals were observed not only in Purkinje cell bodies and dendrites, but also molecular layer interneuron (Supplementary Fig. 7b–d). At P28, PV-Cre; γflox/γflox PCs exhibited disorganized arbors with frequent self-crossings (Fig. 7d, e). In contrast, postnatal γC4 overexpression produced expansive, well-spaced arbors with clear self-avoidance (two examples shown in Fig. 7f–i). Quantitatively, total dendritic area was significantly increased by γC4 overexpression relative to PV-Cre; γflox/γflox and approached that of the control (PV-Cre; γflox/WT) level (Fig. 7j). Branch number was also elevated compared with PV-Cre; γflox/γflox (Fig. 7k). Most notably, dendritic self-crossings per unit area were markedly reduced by γC4 overexpression, consistent with a partial rescue relative to the knockout. Nevertheless, the phenotype was not fully normalized, and self-crossing values remained significantly different from control (Fig. 7l). Discussion

In this study, we generated a new allele related to protocadherin gamma C4 isoform (γC4) to elucidate the function of γC4 at the cellular and organismal levels. In our findings, γC4nmd does not represent a clean γC4 null allele. Rather, full-length γC4 is lost, while a γCR-deficient truncated γC4 protein is reproducibly detected at ~18% of the WT full-length γC4 level (Fig. 1b; Supplementary Fig. 3). We therefore interpret the postnatal neurological phenotypes in γC4nmd mice as consequences of reduced and altered γC4 function in the setting of residual truncated γC4 expression, which may more closely approximate partial loss-of-function (hypomorphic) conditions than complete gene ablation. Consistent with this interpretation, clean γC4 protein-null alleles result in fully neonatal lethality, including the previously reported γC4 knockout allele and our independent γC4NΔ79 allele (Supplementary Fig. 6). By contrast, γC4nmd mutants show incomplete penetrance of lethality, with around 40% surviving postnatally. γC4NΔ79 retains 50% of γC4 mRNA relative to WT, whereas γC4nmd mutants express only 10% (Supplementary Fig. 6e). Yet, immunoblotting of brain lysates revealed that the ~120 kDa band detected in WT was absent in γC4NΔ79, and the corresponding band was also lost in γC4nmd (Fig. 1b; Supplementary Fig. 6f). Notably, γC4nmd consistently showed an additional weak band at ~100 kDa. Based on its reproducibility, the allele structure, and the apparent molecular weight, this band likely represents a truncated γC4 product lacking the Pcdh-γ constant region (Supplementary Figs. 1b, c and 3). We speculate that the presence of this residual truncated γC4 protein in γC4nmd, but not in the protein-null γC4NΔ79 line, may underlie the difference between incomplete versus complete neonatal lethality.

Although γC4nmd mice partially survive, they offer a unique opportunity to examine postnatal and behavioral consequences of compromised γC4 function in a whole-animal context, complementing prior conditional strategies28 and neonatal-lethal models27. Recent human genetics studies have identified biallelic PCDHGC4 variants as a cause of a neurodevelopmental syndrome characterized by progressive microcephaly, seizures, and neurodevelopmental impairment, with additional features including joint anomalies2. In this study, our γC4nmd mice provide an in vivo model that captures key neurological aspects of the human condition. Specifically, γC4nmd mice exhibit reduced brain volume, abnormal gait/locomotor performance, and seizure-like behaviors, consistent with core clinical features reported in individuals with biallelic PCDHGC4 variants (Fig. 1, Supplementary Video 3). By contrast, we did not observe overt joint abnormalities in our current analyses; however, musculoskeletal phenotypes were not a primary endpoint and may require dedicated, systematic evaluation in future work.

Next, using a series of CRISPR/Cas9-based genetic manipulations, including a newly developed strategy named DOMINO (DOuble Mutation INducing Open reading frame switch), we generated γC4fl-only allele, which only γC4 full-length and 21 other truncated Pcdh-γ (Fig. 1a; Supplementary Fig. 4). These mutants were viable and fertile, unlike Pcdh-γ cluster knockout mice46,47 and all Pcdh-γ truncated mice. A hypomorphic, C-terminally truncated allele of Pcdh-γ did not show abnormal cell death but died within several hours of birth35. Our γC4fl-only allele also reduced embryonic apoptosis to levels comparable to control mice, and the animals survived stably into adulthood (Figs. 2 and 3). These results indicate that full-length expression of γC4 alone, under the Pcdh-γ cluster constant region truncation, is sufficient to support organismal viability. Recently, large-scale CRISPR/Cas9 screening has generated additional mouse lines, including the 1R1 line in which only the γC4 exon remains intact, as well as a Cre-dependent γC4 overexpression mouse (CAG-C4-GFP)27,29. These models likewise rescue the apoptosis caused by loss of γC4. However, because both 1R1 and CAG-C4-GFP alter γC4 expression levels, they may not reflect the native physiological state. In contrast, γC4fl-only shows no significant differences in γC4 mRNA or protein levels compared with WT, minimizing confounds due to expression-level changes and providing a complementary platform to interrogate γC4 function from a distinct perspective (Fig. 1f, Supplementary Fig. 5).

Because γC4nmd mutants show substantial neonatal lethality (around 60%) yet are not uniformly lethal, and because embryonic apoptosis has been reported in prior γC4 loss-of-function models, we analyzed CC3-positive apoptosis in E18.5 brains (Figs. 2 and 3). In γΔCR1 embryos, pan-Pcdh-γ disruption caused robust and widespread CC3 activation, with apoptotic cells enriched in GAD67-positive domains. In contrast, γC4nmd embryos showed a significant but more modest increase in apoptosis, with a more restricted distribution and lower overall levels than γΔCR1 (Fig. 3c). Notably, γC4fl-only embryos exhibited CC3 levels near WT, suggesting that full-length γC4 alone is sufficient to suppress the elevated apoptosis observed in the pan-Pcdh-γ knockout background despite truncation of the other 21 isoforms. Together, these data indicate that loss of full-length γC4 can elicit measurable embryonic neuronal apoptosis, but not to an extent that results in uniformly lethal outcomes.

Furthermore, in relation to the embryonic apoptosis phenotypes, we assessed the phosphorylation levels of FAK and PYK2, kinases implicated in downstream signaling regulated by Pcdh-γ, by western blotting of P2 soluble fractions prepared from neonatal brain lysates (Fig. 4). pY397-FAK/FAK was elevated in γΔCR1 and γC4fl-only, whereas pY402-PYK2/PYK2 did not differ across genotypes. In light of models in which Pcdh-γ restrain FAK activation and downstream PLC/PKC–MARCKS signaling11,12, the elevated pFAK in γC4fl-only may reflect the widespread loss of CR across 21 Pcdh-γ isoforms, thereby weakening cluster-level inhibition of FAK. Recent evidence14 that PKC phosphorylation of a shared Pcdh-γ C-terminal motif can negatively regulate Pcdh-γ function further raises the possibility of feedback between CR loss–driven changes in FAK/PKC activity and Pcdh-γ regulation. Although protocadherin cytoplasmic domains can inhibit both FAK and PYK2, the absence of PYK2 differences in our bulk P0 dataset may reflect averaging across regions/cell types and developmental context dependence.

Functionally, full-length γC4 was also essential for establishing dendritic self-avoidance in cerebellar Purkinje cells, critical for proper circuit organization (Figs. 5 and 6). Both global and Purkinje cell-specific truncation of γC4 led to excessive dendritic self-crossing. On the other hand, Purkinje cells which only full-length γC4 expression under the other 21 Pcdh-γ disrupt γCR maintained a typical dendritic architecture. Furthermore, the phenotype of Purkinje cells with PV-Cre-dependent Pcdh-γ deletion was partially rescued by AAV-mediated postnatal overexpression of γC4 (Fig. 7). These observations refine prior models by suggesting that, in Purkinje cells, full-length γC4 may provide a key CR-dependent function required for dendritic self-avoidance, whereas the common regions of other Pcdh-γ isoforms are not strictly required for this aspect of dendritic morphogenesis. In retinal starburst amacrine cells, a single Pcdh-γ isoform can be sufficient to support self-avoidance, although isoform diversity is critical for self/non-self discrimination among neighboring cells. Although we did not directly test starburst amacrine cells, our Purkinje cell data are compatible with the concept that a limited set of functional isoforms can sustain self-avoidance in a cell-type-specific manner. Notably, Purkinje cells also show contributions from both Pcdh-α and Pcdh-γ, implying that distinct neuronal populations may integrate protocadherin inputs differently to achieve dendritic patterning.

Taken together, our results identify γC4 as a master regulator of both neuronal survival and dendritic patterning, operating through both structural interactions and intracellular signaling. These findings provide critical insight into the mechanisms by which clustered protocadherins shape brain development. Furthermore, given the parallels between the phenotypes observed in γC4 mutants—such as brain volume reduction, circuit disorganization, and lethality—and features of human neurodevelopmental disorders, including autism spectrum disorder and intellectual disability, we propose that neonatal survive γC4nmd mice may serve as a valuable model for studying such conditions. The selective vulnerability of the brainstem and cerebellum in these mutants mirrors region-specific defects seen in patients with protocadherin-related syndromes, underscoring the potential translational relevance of this work.

Methods

Animal experiments

All experiments were approved by the Animal Experiment Committees of The University of Osaka and National Institute for Physiological Sciences. All genome-edited mice were backcrossed into C57BL/6 J and maintained at the animal facility of The University of Osaka Frontier Biosciences. The mice were housed under standard conditions on a 12-h light/dark cycle with water and food ad libitum. Control and homozygous mutant mice were the littermates from heterozygous breeding for each genotype. Embryonic stages were calculated by defining noon of the day of vaginal plug observation as embryonic day 0.5 (E0.5) and the day of birth as postnatal day 0 (P0). The all Pcdh-γ knockout mouse line, ∆γCR1, have been described previously43 used as a negative control in the study. PV-Cre and Pcdh-γ floxed mouse line have been reported42,44. All mouse experiments were performed using samples from both sexes, and data were collected and analyzed without stratification by sex.

Generation of only full-length γC4 disruption and expression mice using DOMINO method

The targeted sgRNA sequence (sgRNA_gC4 + 2876 s: CCACCCTCTGATCTTCTCTA TGG) was annealed and ligated to BbsI site (GAAGAC (2/6)) of pX330-U6-Chimeric_BB-CBh-hSpCas9 vector (Addgene, #42230)48. Cloned vector was amplified by KOD-plus-Neo (TOYOBO) and purified using MinElute PCR Purification Kit (QIAGEN). The purified PCR product was used as a template to synthesize the Cas9 nuclease mRNA and sgRNA using mMESSAGE mMACHINETM T7 ULTRA Transcription Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific), then purified using MEGAclearTM Transcription Clean-Up Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific). They were injected into C57BL/6 J fertilized eggs according to a previous report48. The validations of γC4-mutation were performed using standard PCR and Sanger sequence methods of genomic DNA from the founder murine tail. After the genome editing using CRISPR/Cas9, the 2-nucleotide base-pair deletion of γC4 VCD through non-homologous end joining (NHEJ) caused splicing of premature stop codon at the Pcdhg constant exon 1 (CR1) and frame-shifting mutation. To generate mice expressing only full-length γC4 (γC4fl-only), we designed a sgRNA (sgRNA_gCR+2 s; AAGCCCCGCCCAACACTGAC TGG) and injected it into fertilized embryos. For in vitro fertilization, sperm from male heterozygous γC4nmd mice were used with C57BL/6J oocytes. The validations of γC4fl-only mice were performed PCR and direct sequence methods of mouse tail genomic DNA and we confirmed 1-nucleotide base-pair deletion in Pcdh-γ constant exon 1 (CR1). To generate γC4N∆79 mutants, the sgRNA was designed in signal peptide (sgRNA_gC4 + 99 s; GATTCGCTACCCGGTCCCAG AGG).

Quantitative RT-PCR

The mouse brains were removed at postnatal day 0 (P0), then immediately frozen with liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80 °C until use. Total RNA was extracted using RNeasy Mini kit (QIAGEN) according to the manufacturer protocol. The possibility of genomic DNA contamination was excluded by the treatment with DNase I with RDD Buffer (QIAGEN). Three μg of total RNA per sample were used to make cDNA using ReverTra Ace qPCR RT Master Mix (TOYOBO) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. qPCR was performed in triplicate (technical replicates) using primers listed in Table S2 with THUNDERBIRD Next SYBR qPCR Mix (TOYOBO) or Thermo Scientific SYBR Green qPCR Master Mix (Thermo Scientific). The relative abundance of each transcript was calculated using the ΔΔCt method, normalized to B2m and littermate controls. Briefly, cycle thresholds (Ct) were averaged across three technical replicates and the Ct of sample-matched B2m was subtracted (ΔCt). These ΔCt values were subtracted from the average ΔCt control value within littermates (ΔΔCt), and the relative expression score calculated as 2ΔΔCt. The relative expression values of each transcript in controls, γC4nmd, or γC4fl-only mutants were compared using a one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s multiple-comparisons test. The statistical data were analyzed using Prism 10 (GraphPad, San Diego, CA). Statistical significance is indicated as follows: *p < 0.05; **p < 0.01; ***p < 0.001; ****p < 0.0001. Exact P values and the underlying raw data are provided in Supplementary Data 1.

Production of rabbit anti-γC4-specific antibody

A polyclonal antibody against the Pcdh-γC4 was developed in rabbits using Pcdhgc4 (NM_033582.2, amino acids 301–342 for extracellular and 759–806 for intracellular). We subcloned the relevant cDNA fragments into the pGEX4T-1 plasmid (GE Healthcare) and expressed glutathione S-transferase (GST) fusion proteins in Escherichia coli. Immunization and affinity purification were conducted as described earlier49.

Preparation of brain lysates

Each genotype mouse brains at postnatal day 0 (P0) were homogenized with potter homogenizer in five-volume of buffer H [20 mM Tris-HCl (pH 8.0), 2 mM EDTA (pH 8.0), 0.32 M sucrose] supplemented with cOmplete protease inhibitor (Roche, Cat.# 11836170001) and PhosSTOP (Roche, Cat. # 4906837001)]. After centrifugation at 20,000 × g for 1 h at 4 °C, the pellet was solubilized with buffer S [20 mM Tris-HCl (pH 8.0), 150 mM NaCl, 1.0% Triton X-100] with cOmplete protease inhibitor and PhosSTOP for 1 h at 4 °C. After centrifugation at 20,000 × g for 1 h at 4 °C, the supernatant was collected, and the protein concentrations were determined by a BCA protein assay (Thermo Fisher Scientific).

Western blotting

Twenty-five µg of protein per genotype were boiled in SDS-Sample Buffer [50 mM Tris-HCl, 2% (w/v) SDS, 2% (v/v) 2-Melcaptothanol, 4% (v/v) Glycerol] at 100 °C for 5 min. Boiled proteins were separated using 5–11% gradient Bullet PAGE One Precast Gel (Nacalai Tesque) and transferred to nitrocellulose membranes (BIO-RAD) according to the standard protocol. Membranes were blocked 2% non-fat dry-milk or 2% BSA in Tris buffered saline containing 0.1% Tween-20 (TBS-T) or Bullet Blocking One for Western Blotting (Nacalai Tesque, #13779-01) and incubated with the following antibodies; anti-pan-Pcdh-α50,51 (1/1000), anti-pan-Pcdh-γ43 (1/1000), anti-Pcdh-γB2 (Neuro Mab, N148/30 (75-184), 1/1000), anti-Pcdh-γC3 (Neuro Mab, N174B/27 (75-237), 1/1000), anti-Pcdh-γC4 (extracellular 301–342 aa, in this study, 1/1000), anti-Pcdh-γC4 (intracellular 759–806 aa, in this study, 1/500), anti-FAK (Cell Signaling Technology, #3285, 1/1000), anti-phosphoFAK (pY397, Invitrogen, #44-624 G, 1/1000), anti-PYK2 (Proteintech, #17592-1-AP, 1/2000), anti-phosphoPYK2 (pY402, Cell Signaling Technology, #3291, 1/1000), anti-N-cadherin (BD Transduction Lab, #610920, 1/1000), and anti-β-actin (13E5, Cell Signaling Technology, #4970, 1/10,000). The membranes were washed in TBS-T at three times and incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies. The Can Get Signal (TOYOBO, NKB-101) was used for the primary and secondary antibody diluent. The chemical luminescence images were obtained and analyzed using the ChemiDoc MP Imaging System (BIO-RAD). Exact P values and the underlying raw data are provided in Supplementary Data 1.

Footprint test

Footprint patterns were recorded by placing a fresh sheet of white Japanese calligraphy paper on the floor of the runway. Immediately prior to testing, the forepaws and hindpaws of each mouse were coated with nontoxic paint (red for forepaws, blue for hindpaws). Mice were gently positioned at the entrance of the runway and released to walk voluntarily. Hindpaw prints were subsequently analyzed to quantify stride length and stride width. Exact P values and the underlying raw data are provided in Supplementary Data 1.

Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)

Adult mice (~3 months old) were deeply anesthetized and transcardially perfused with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), followed by 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA) in 0.1 M phosphate buffer (PB). After decapitation, the heads were post-fixed in chilled 4% PFA/0.1 M PB for 24 h and then stored in chilled PBS. Ex vivo MRI of mouse head was conducted using an 11.7 T vertical-bore scanner (AVANCE II 500WB; Bruker BioSpin, Ettlingen, Germany) and a 15 mm inner diameter transmit/receive volume radio frequency (RF) coil. Coronal, horizontal, and sagittal images of each mouse head were obtained using a Turbo Rapid Acquisition with Relaxation Enhancement (T2-Turbo RARE) sequence with the following parameters: echo time, 31 ms; repetition time, 3000 ms; number of signal averages, 8; RARE factor, 4; slice thickness, 0.5 mm (gapless); matrix size, 512 × 512; field of view, 20 × 20 mm. For brain volume analysis, sagittal images were quantified using the open-source software ITK-SNAP52.

Immunostaining and quantification of apoptotic cells

Embryonic day 18 (E18) mouse embryos were ice-cold anesthesia and perfused with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) followed by 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA) in PBS as reported36. After decapitation and removal of the dorsal cranium to expose the brain, the brains were post-fixed overnight at 4 °C. The brains were cryoprotected in 30% sucrose in PBS for at least 2 days at 4 °C. They were embedded in O.C.T. compound (Sakura Finetek Co., Ltd., Tokyo, Japan) and quickly frozen in isopentane cooled with liquid nitrogen. Sagittal cryosections of 20-μm thickness were cut on a cryostat (Leica CM3050, Germany) and stored at −80 °C until use. The sections were blocked 2% normal goat serum (NGS) and incubated with the following antibodies: anti-GAD67 and anti-Cleaved Caspase3. The next day, the sections were incubated with Alexa Fluor conjugated secondary antibodies (Molecular Probes) and 4’,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI). Fluorescence images were captured using BZ-X810 All-in-One microscopy (Keyence) and LSM780 laser scanning microscopy (Carl Zeiss). For quantification of CC3-positive neuronal counts and CC3-positive area, a fixed region of interest was defined for each brain region. For each ROI, fluorescence images for CC3 (Alexa Fluor 594 channel) and DAPI were acquired on the same focal plane using an VS200 slide scanner (Olympus) with 2–3 sections per region per embryo. Images were processed in ImageJ/Fiji (v1.53) with background subtraction followed by intensity thresholding. For cell counting, nuclei were identified based on DAPI signal, and CC3/DAPI double-positive objects with nuclear-sized morphology were counted. For CC3 area measurements, the total area of above-threshold CC3-positive pixels within the ROI was quantified. Small objects consistent with staining noise were excluded using a particle-size filter. Statistical analyses were performed in GraphPad Prism, one-way ANOVA with Tukey’s multiple-comparisons test.

Tetbow AAV injections and Purkinje cells analysis

The AAV plasmids utilized in this study include pAAV-TRE-mTurquoise2-WPRE (Addgene #104110), pAAV-TRE-EYFP-WPRE (Addgene #104111), pAAV-TRE-tdTomato-WPRE (Addgene #104112), and pAAV-ihSyn1-tTA (Addgene #99120)38. Tetbow components were packaged into AAV.PHP.eB using the ultracentrifugation method described in a previous paper39. Intravenous administration of AAV.PHP.eB mixture (4.0 × 1012 vg [viral genomes] tTA, 1.01 × 1011 vg tdTomato, 2.22 × 1011 vg EYFP, 1.55 × 1011 vg mTurquoise2) was performed via injection into the retro-orbital sinus of newborn mice (P1) or 4-weeks-old (PV-Cre conditional mutants). For rescue experiments, mouse L7 promoter-driven45 γC4 coding AAV (AAV.PHP.eB/mL7_Pcdh-γC4-PAtag-WPRE; 9.67 × 1012 vg) were injected with Tetbow AAV mixture at P1. Four weeks post-injection, mice were transcardially perfused with 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA) in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) under deep anesthesia with isoflurane. After the brain was removed, it was post-fixed within the same fixative for 4 h, then immersed in 25% sucrose in PBS over for two nights for cryoprotection. The sections were cut at 300 µm on a cryostat (Leica CM3050, Germany). Brain-slice samples were cleared with SeeDB2G for imaging41. Labeled and cleared Purkinje cells were imaged at NIKON Imaging Center (The University of Osaka). The images were obtained using A1R MP+ Inverted Multiphoton Microscope System (NIKON) equipped with x40 silicon lens (NA = 1.25). Acquired images were analyzed by Imaris (Oxford Instruments) to trace the Purkinje cells and measure the area and self-intersection. Also, the videos of Purkinje cells were built by Imaris. PA-tag antibody-stained Purkinje cells were imaged using Dragonfly confocal laser microscope (Oxford Instruments). Means of multiple samples were compared using one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s multiple-comparisons test for the Purkinje cell morphology and self-crossing analyses. Exact P values and the underlying raw data are provided in Supplementary Data 1.

Statistics and reproducibility

All quantitative data are reported as mean ± SEM. Comparisons between two independent groups were performed using a two-tailed unpaired Student’s t test when assumptions of normality and homoscedasticity were met; otherwise, a two-tailed Mann–Whitney U test was used. Comparisons among more than two groups were assessed by one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post hoc multiple-comparisons test. Exact sample sizes (n) are provided in the figure legends. Statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism 10. Statistical significance was defined as p < 0.05. Asterisks indicate p < 0.05 (*), p < 0.01 (**), p < 0.001 (***), and p < 0.0001 (****); ns indicates not significant.

Cellulose Paper-Based Adsorbent of Heavy Metals in Water Fabricated by One-Step Ultrafast Ligation of Diazofluorene
Authors: Medy C. Nongbe, Samuel Calderoni, Guillaume Bretel, Marie Denis, Erwan Le Grognec, François-Xavier Felpin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This study outlines the development of a cellulose paper-based adsorbent, Cell-Flu, achieved by an innovative covalent ligation of 4,5-diazafluorene motifs to cellulose paper. The ultrafast approach entails the insertion of 9-diazo-4,5-diazafluorene into the O–H bonds present within the glucose units of cellulose under catalyst-free conditions. The grafting occurs in only 15 min at 25 °C through a simple impregnation strategy. Through thorough experimentation, we fine-tuned the adsorption properties of Cell-Flu in aqueous media for Ni­(II) and Cu­(II) by adjusting pH levels, contact duration, and adsorbent dose. Additionally, we describe the capability of Cell-Flu to adsorb other heavy metals like Pb­(II), Cd­(II), and Pd­(II), uncovering its broader metal removal potential. High levels of remediation, in the range of 93–98%, were obtained for all tested metals over a period of 1 to 3 h. The interaction between Cell-Flu and metal species at the equilibrium is most accurately described by the Temkin isotherm.

Introduction

The quality of drinking water is an important public health concern in many countries due to outdated distribution networks and contamination of groundwater by growing industrial and agricultural activities. The contamination of groundwater by pollutants of various origins leads to recurrent epidemics of cholera and dysentery. Among the major chemical pollutants, coming essentially from anthropogenic activities, heavy metals are particularly problematic because they are persistent in nature and lead to long-term contamination of land and water resources. Intensifying regulatory frameworks and growing societal demand for environmental protection have catalyzed the advancement of enabling technologies and the development of innovative methodologies aimed at mitigating heavy metal contamination of water resources. A diverse array of physicochemical techniques has been developed for the remediation of heavy metal-contaminated water resources, among which electrochemical, ion-exchange resins, nanofiltration membranes, chemical precipitation and adsorption separation processes represent the most established and widely implemented methodologies. −

Among this diversity of technologies, the use of adsorbents made from biobased materials for pollutant adsorption is a highly promising approach, merging the simplicity of adsorption processes with the biocompatibility, affordability, and biodegradability inherent to biobased materials. −

Cellulose, available in large quantities from renewable sources, represents a highly promising biobased material for the adsorption of pollutants from water, owing to its intrinsic surface chemistry and structural versatility. − Cellulose is a linear polysaccharide, made of glucose units and isolated from the plant kingdom. Cellulose has many advantages over most biobased materials because it is i/a cheap material, ii/almost inexhaustible since the annual bioproduction is estimated to be about 1.5 × 1012 tons and iii/hydrophilic but not soluble in water. The inherent chemical structure of pristine cellulose offers a versatile platform that can be readily functionalized to introduce coordination sites, enabling its transformation into efficient depolluting membrane materials. Various strategies for cellulose modification have been proposed such as graft copolymerization, − direct chemical functionalization of the cellulose framework, − and composite fabrication. − These studies have yielded high-quality results using nanocellulose, microcellulose or wood pulp, but the direct modification of cellulose in the form of paper has been comparatively underexplored. −

We recently contributed to the development of new methods for the functionalization of cellulose paper and some of these methods have been used for the fabrication of depolluting membranes − and sensors. − The functionalization approaches include esterification reactions, nucleophilic substitutions of C6 alcohol functions and 1,3-dipolar cycloadditions of cellulose azide. These functionalization methods are characterized by a simplicity of execution and a good degree of substitution, but suffer from a modest time efficiency not compatible with an industrial setting in the case of a large-scale production.

In this contribution we report a powerful one-step and ultrafast strategy for the functionalization of cellulose paper using diazofluorene. The cellulose functionalized with fluorene groups proves to be an excellent adsorbent not only for Cu­(II) and Ni­(II) which are the two metals of interest in this study, but also for Pb­(II), Cd­(II) and Pd­(II), displaying a broad spectrum of applications. The use of this device requires neither training nor equipment, which makes it ideal for domestic use.

Grafting of Cellulose Paper with 9-Diazofluorene (Cell-Flu)

A solution of 9-Diazo-4,5-diazafluorene 4 in CH2Cl2 (35 μL, 1.5 M) was deposited on a piece of mercerized cellulose paper (Whatman #6, 3 cm2). After 15 min at 25 °C, the piece of paper was sonicated two times with toluene and dried under vacuum. The degree of substitution (DS = 14%) was determined by elemental analysis using the following equation 1 DS=11+MNωN×Mpc−MgcMpc×1NbN where M N is the molecular weight of nitrogen, M gc is the molecular weight of grafted cellulose, M pc is the molecular weight of pristine cellulose, ωN is the mass fraction of nitrogen within cellulose, and Nb N is the number of nitrogen atoms on the grafting molecule.

General Procedure for Evaluating the Effect of pH on Metal Adsorption

Fresh aqueous solutions of Ni­(OAc)2·4H2O and Cu­(OAc)2·5H2O (2 mL, 100 ppm) were prepared in Milli-Q water and adjusted to the desired pH values (ranging from 3 to 9) using 1 mol/L aqueous HCl and/or NaOH. Each solution was then stirred with Cell-Flu (5 g/L) on an orbital agitator at 100 rpm for 60 min at 25 °C. After the contact time, the adsorbent was separated, and the residual metal concentration in the supernatant was quantified by atomic absorption spectroscopy. Prior to analysis, the samples were diluted 20-fold to ensure measurements fell within the linear range of the calibration curves.

General Procedure for Evaluating the Effect of Contact Time on Metal Adsorption

Fresh aqueous solutions of Ni­(OAc)2·4H2O and Cu­(OAc)2·5H2O (100 ppm, 2 mL) were prepared in Milli-Q water and stirred with Cell-Flu at concentrations of 10 g/L for Cu­(II) and 5 g/L for Ni­(II) on an orbital agitator at 100 rpm and 25 °C. Experiments were conducted at the previously determined optimal pH conditions (pH = 9 for Cu­(II) and pH = 6 for Ni­(II)). At predetermined time intervals (10, 20, 30, 40, 60, 90, 120, and 150 min), samples were collected, and the remaining metal concentration in solution was quantified by atomic absorption spectroscopy. Prior to analysis, all samples were diluted 20-fold to ensure consistency with the linear range of the calibration curves.

General Procedure for Determining Adsorption Isotherms

Fresh aqueous solutions of Ni­(OAc)2·4H2O and Cu­(OAc)2·5H2O (100 ppm, 2 mL) were prepared in Milli-Q water and stirred with Cell-Flu at concentrations of 5 g/L for Cu­(II) and 10 g/L for Ni­(II) on an orbital agitator at 100 rpm for 90 min at 25 °C. Aqueous solutions of Ni­(OAc)2·4H2O and Cu­(OAc)2·5H2O were prepared at varying concentrations ranging from 5 to 1000 mg/L (5, 25, 50, 75, 100, 200, 300, 400, 500, and 1000 mg/L). After adsorption under standard conditions, the residual concentrations of Cu­(II) and Ni­(II) in the supernatant were quantified by atomic absorption spectroscopy. All samples were diluted 20-fold prior to analysis to ensure measurements were within the linear range of the calibration curves.

Results and Discussions

Preparation of Cell-Flu

In a previous contribution we described the ultrafast and straightforward covalent functionalization of cellulose paper by insertion of diazo compounds into O–H bonds present within the glucose units of cellulose under catalyst-free conditions. Following this strategy, we reasoned that the ligation of 9-diazo-4,5-diazafluorene 1 onto cellulose paper, would result in a device, namely Cell-Flu, with powerful coordinating properties via the 4,5-diazafluorene motif which is known to be an efficient ligand for transition metals (Scheme ).

General Strategy Described in This Work

9-diazo-4,5-diazafluorene 1 was prepared in three steps, as depicted in Scheme , from 1,10-phenanthroline 2 by modification of a reported procedure. Oxidative ring contraction of 1,10-phenanthroline 2 with potassium permanganate gave 4,5-diazafluoren-9-one 3 which was converted to the corresponding tosyl hydrazone 4. Base mediated elimination furnished the expected 9-diazo-4,5-diazafluorene 1 in good overall yield (34% over three steps). Diazo insertion onto mercerized cellulose paper was conducted by an impregnation method. A piece of mercerized cellulose paper (3 cm2) was impregnated by a solution of 9-diazo-4,5-diazafluorene 1 in CH2Cl2 and the resulting wet material was left to stand for 15 min. The resulting Cell-Flu was thoroughly washed with toluene under ultrasound to remove any trace of unreacted diazo compound 1 and prevent further leaching from adsorbed (and not covalently anchored) 4,5-diazafluorene motifs.

Characterization of Cell-Flu

Analysis of Cell-Flu by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) confirmed the successful grafting of diazo compound 1 onto cellulose paper. The survey scan spectrum exhibited new peaks in the N 1s region at 398.8 eV, attributed to nitrogen atoms of the 4,5-diazafluorene backbone (Figure b). Additionally, the grafting was corroborated by a pronounced increase in the components associated with C–C bonds at 284.6 eV and C–H bonds at 285.3 eV in the high-resolution C 1s spectrum of Cell-Flu (Figure d). Elemental analysis indicated that the degree of substitution (DS) was approximately 0.14. This suggests that, on average, 1.4 primary alcohol groups, which are likely the most reactive, per ten glucose units were functionalized with fluorene groups. It is worth noting that this value does not consider the active surface area, and numerous buried hydroxyl groups likely not undergo etherification.

Survey scan spectra of (a) pristine cellulose and (b) Cell-Flu. High resolution C 1s spectra of (c) pristine cellulose and (d) Cell-Flu.

The FTIR spectra of both pristine cellulose and Cell-Flu display characteristic absorption bands associated with the cellulose backbone (Figure ). Notably, the bands observed at approximately 1100, 2900, and 3400 cm–1 correspond to the stretching vibrations of C–O–C, C–H, and O–H functional groups, respectively. In the unmodified cellulose, the absorption band at 1641 cm–1 is attributed to the bending vibration of physically adsorbed water. Successful grafting of the 4,5-diazafluorene moiety onto cellulose is evidenced by the appearance of two additional bands at 1594 and 1558 cm–1, which are assigned to the stretching vibrations of aromatic CC and CN bonds, respectively, confirming the covalent attachment of the fluorene motif.

Remarkably, this grafting process did not affect the fiber network; scanning electron microscopy (SEM) of pristine cellulose and Cell-Flu revealed that the average fiber diameter remained consistent within the 5–25 μm range (Figure a,b).

SEM images of (a) pristine cellulose and (b) Cell-Flu. Image (a) was published in our previous work and reprinted with permission from ref . Copyright 2024 American Chemical Society.

The thermal stability of Cell-Flu was evaluated by thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) and compared to that of pristine cellulose paper over the temperature range of 30 to 500 °C at a heating rate of 5 °C/min (Figure S1). An initial weight loss observed below 100 °C for both materials corresponds to the evaporation of physically adsorbed moisture. Between 100 and 270 °C, the mass of both Cell-Flu and pristine cellulose remained relatively stable, indicating no significant decomposition within this temperature window. The onset of thermal degradation for Cell-Flu occurred at approximately 270 °C, slightly lower than that of pristine cellulose (ca. 300 °C), suggesting a minor reduction in thermal stability upon functionalization. However, at 500 °C, Cell-Flu retained a significantly higher residual mass (∼25%) compared to pristine cellulose (∼10%). This increased char yield is attributed to the incorporation of thermally stable 4,5-diazafluorene groups, which contribute to enhanced carbonaceous residue formation upon decomposition.

Effect of the pH and Contact Time

We began by examining how the adsorption of Cu­(II) and Ni­(II) onto Cell-Flu was influenced by the pH of the aqueous solution and the contact time between the metal ions and the adsorbent. The pH is a key factor, as it determines the form of the metal species in solution, thereby influencing the interaction between the metal and the adsorbent. In acidic and neutral aqueous solutions (3 < pH < 7), Cu­(II) exists mainly as free Cu2+. At higher pH values, the Cu­(OH)+ (pH 8) and Cu­(OH)2 (pH 9–11) species become predominant. A related speciation pattern is observed with Ni­(II) for which Ni2+ (3 < pH < 8), Ni­(OH)+ (pH 9–10) and Ni­(OH)2 (pH 11) species are predominant in solution. We determined the adsorption levels of Cell-Flu used at a dose of 5 g/L in a solution containing 100 mg/L Cu­(II) or Ni­(II) over a pH range of 3–9 at 25 °C for 60 min (Figure a). The highest remediation levels were observed at pH 9 for Cu­(II) and pH 6 for Ni­(II). This suggests that Cell-Flu has an increased affinity for Cu­(OH)2 and Ni2+. The lower adsorption level of Ni­(II) species at pH > 6 could be explained by the low solubility of Ni hydroxide species. Additionally, the drop in efficiency of Cell-Flu observed at more acidic pH most likely reflects the presence of electrostatic repulsions between Cu2+ or Ni2+ and the positively charged Cell-Flu surface due to the high proton concentration. At pH values below 6, the nitrogen atoms within the fluorene backbone are likely to become protonated, thereby diminishing their capacity to coordinate metal ions effectively. The dose of Cell-Flu was also varied and we observed that Cu­(II) species were most efficiently adsorbed at a dose of 10 g/L, while Ni­(II) species required only 5 g/L to reach a high level of remediation. An increase in Cell-Flu concentration up to 10 g/L did not result in any significant enhancement of Ni­(II) removal efficiency, suggesting a saturation of available binding sites or diffusion limitations under the tested conditions.

(a) Impact of pH on the adsorption of Cu­(II) and Ni­(II) onto Cell-Flu (5 g/L) after 60 min at 25 °C. (b) Influence of contact time on the adsorption of Cu­(II) and Ni­(II) onto Cell-Flu at concentrations of 10 g/L and 5 g/L, respectively, at 25 °C.

Following the optimization of pH and adsorbent dosage for Cu­(II) and Ni­(II) uptake onto Cell-Flu, adsorption kinetics were investigated to determine the optimal contact time (Figure b). Ni­(II) exhibited a faster adsorption rate than Cu­(II), reaching equilibrium within 60 min, whereas Cu­(II) required approximately 90 min. In both cases, remediation efficiency reached a plateau near 95%.

The kinetic profiles were best described by a pseudo-second-order model, first introduced by Ho for modeling the adsorption of divalent metals on peat (Figure ). The linear form of this model can be represented as 2 tqt=1k2qe2+1qet q e (mg/g) and q t (mg/g) denote the amounts of metal adsorbed at equilibrium and at a given time (min), respectively, and k 2 (g/(mg·min)) is the adsorption rate constant. A linear plot of tqt versus t yields a straight line with high correlation coefficients for both Ni­(II) (R 2 = 0.9986) and Cu­(II) (R 2 = 0.9641). These findings indicate that the adsorption mechanism is predominantly governed by chemisorption, likely involving valence forces through electron sharing or exchange, with minimal contribution from external mass transfer. The adsorption rate appears to be primarily controlled by the intrinsic capacity of Cell-Flu, rather than the concentration of metal ions in solution.

Adsorption Isotherms

To gain deeper insight into the equilibrium adsorption behavior of metal ions onto Cell-Flu, the experimental data were fitted to the Langmuir, Freundlich, and Temkin isotherm models, which collectively encompass a broad range of adsorption mechanisms typically observed with cellulose-based materials.

The Langmuir isotherm model assumes monolayer adsorption onto a structurally homogeneous surface possessing a finite number of energetically equivalent sites, with no lateral interactions between adsorbed species. It is particularly well-suited for systems where chemisorption dominates and adsorption energies are uniformly distributed across the surface. The linear form of the Langmuir isotherm can be represented as 3 Ceqe=Ceqm+1bqm q e and q m (mg/g) denote the amounts of metal adsorbed at the equilibrium and for a complete coverage, respectively, C e (mg/L) is the concentration of metals at the equilibrium and b (L/mg) is the Langmuir constant.

The Freundlich isotherm is an empirical model that characterizes adsorption on energetically heterogeneous surfaces and accommodates the possibility of multilayer formation. It assumes a nonuniform distribution of adsorption heat, whereby the binding energy decreases logarithmically with increasing surface coverage. The linear equation of the Freundlich isotherm is 4 ln⁡qe=ln⁡Kf+1nln⁡Ce K f (L/mg) and 1/n are Freundlich constants describing the capacity and intensity of adsorption, respectively.

While the Freundlich model assumes a logarithmic evolution of the adsorption energy, the Temkin model describes a linear decrease in the heat of adsorption as the surface coverage increases. The Temkin isotherm incorporates adsorbate–adsorbate interactions by assuming a uniform distribution of binding energy across the surface, represented by the following equation 5 qe=(RTb)ln(AT)+(RTb)ln(Ce) b (J/mol) term stands for the heat of adsorption, A T (L/g) is the Temkin isotherm constant, R (8.314 J/mol/K) is the universal gas constant and T (K) is the absolute temperature.

The linearized adsorption isotherm plots and corresponding correlation coefficients (R 2) for the Langmuir, Freundlich, and Temkin models are illustrated in Figure . The modest correlation with the Langmuir isotherm indicates that the adsorption of Cu­(II) and Ni­(II) on Cell-Flu does not strictly conform to ideal monolayer coverage or uniform site energy distribution, in good agreement with the macroporous nature of the cellulose paper grade used in this study (Whatman #6) possessing pores >3 μm. Similarly, the Freundlich isotherm, which accounts for multilayer adsorption on heterogeneous surfaces with a nonuniform energy distribution, also demonstrated moderate correlation. However, its empirical nature and lack of a defined saturation point limit its predictive accuracy for the systems studied here. By contrast, the Temkin isotherm exhibited the highest degree of fit to the equilibrium data, with R 2 values of 0.96 and 0.92 for Cu­(II) and Ni­(II), respectively. This strong correlation suggests that the adsorption process on Cell-Flu is influenced by adsorbate–adsorbent interactions, where the heat of adsorption decreases linearly with increasing surface coverage, consistent with the functionalized and polar surface of cellulose. Such behavior implies that the binding sites possess a distribution of energies and that electrostatic or chemical interactions between adsorbed metal ions modulate the adsorption energetics. The good correlation with the Temkin model which account for chemical interactions also confirms that the adsorption involves coordination of Cu­(II) and Ni­(II) by the 4,5-diazafluorene moiety. Adsorption of Other Metals

While we initially focused on the removal of Cu­(II) and Ni­(II) which are the main metals extracted from mines in Côte d’Ivoire, we also assessed the ability of Cell-Flu to adsorb other prevalent metallic pollutants such as Pb­(II), Cd­(II) and Pd­(II). Pb­(II) and Cd­(II) residues are identified by the World Health Organization as being among the 10 chemicals of major public health and finding efficient adsorbents is of general interest. The choice to study the ability of Cell-Flu to remove Pd­(II) species is more unusual because this metal is not a major pollutant of groundwater. However, in addition to Ni and Cu, significant quantities of platinum group metals, including palladium, must be extracted from the mine. For each of these metals we determined the optimal pH, contact time of adsorption and dose of Cell-Flu (Table ). High levels of remediation, in the range of 93–98%, were obtained for these three metals over a period of 1 to 3 h. These results confirm the universal nature of Cell-Flu to adsorb heavy metal in aqueous solution.

Last, we compared the performance of our cellulose-based adsorbent with those reported in the literature for materials prepared from the cellulose backbone (Table ). The adsorption capacity (identified by the q e from kinetic models) of our material generally falls within the lower range of previously reported systems. However, this moderate performance is offset by both the simplicity and the rapidity of our preparation method. Unlike many published approaches that require multiple chemical modification steps or demanding experimental conditions, the distinct advantage of our approach lies in the synthetic accessibility of Cell-Flu. The material is prepared via a one-step, ultrafast grafting of 9-diazo-4,5-diazafluorene onto the hydroxyl functionalities of native cellulose under mild conditions, without the need for prefunctionalization, catalysts, or multistep protocols. Such a streamlined synthesis is particularly advantageous for applications where rapid, low-cost, and scalable membrane fabrication is essential. In the context of point-of-use water treatment, especially in low-resource settings, operational simplicity, material robustness, and production efficiency often outweigh the need for marginal gains in adsorption capacity. In addition, adsorption kinetics are overall faster with our material compared to most literature examples, which represents a clear advantage in time-sensitive applications. Our work provides a valuable complement to existing strategies involving nitrogen-based ligand functionalization of cellulose-derived materials for the removal of heavy metals from aqueous environments. Cell-Flu offers a compelling trade-off between performance and practical applicability, particularly for decentralized or domestic remediation scenarios.

Conclusion

In summary, this work led to the development of Cell-Flu, an innovative cellulose paper-based adsorbent designed for the efficient removal of heavy metals from water. Cell-Flu was prepared through a remarkably simple and rapid functionalization process, involving the covalent ligation of the 4,5-diazafluorene moiety via ultrafast insertion of diazo derivatives into O–H bonds, completed in just 15 min at ambient temperature. This low-cost and scalable method yielded a robust membrane with excellent affinity for metal ions.

We successfully tailored the adsorption properties of Cell-Flu for Cu­(II) and Ni­(II) removal and provided a comprehensive mechanistic understanding through isotherm modeling using the Langmuir, Freundlich, and Temkin frameworks. The best fit of the Temkin isotherm suggests that adsorption on Cell-Flu involves moderate site heterogeneity and a progressive decrease in adsorption energy as surface coverage increases. This is likely due to interactions between metal species and the 4,5-diazafluorene moiety grafted on cellulose, which are not fully captured by the Langmuir or Freundlich models. Beyond these two target metals, Cell-Flu demonstrated high adsorption efficiency toward other toxic metal ions, including Pb­(II), Cd­(II), and Pd­(II), underscoring its broad-spectrum applicability for water decontamination.

Importantly, Cell-Flu combines high performance with user-friendly operationit requires no specialized equipment, is easy to handle, and is ideally suited for household use, particularly in areas affected by heavy metal contamination in drinking water. Its rapid, mild, and cost-effective fabrication strategy further enhances its appeal for decentralized or point-of-use water treatment solutions.

A “flexible” compulsory dental basic research course for undergraduate students: a three-year retrospective study
Authors: Xuejing Gan, Qianmin Ou, Zhuohong Gong, Gengbin Cai, Xinyu Liu, Ruihan Yang, Yuhang Lin, Lin Li, Xiaolan Li, Yun Hong, Zetao Chen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionSince 2015, the Guanghua School of Stomatology has established an elective dental basic research course (EDBRC). To make all students benefit from the dental basic research course without causing excessive academic burden, the “flexible” compulsory dental basic research course (CDBRC) was settled in 2020. This study intends to introduce the “flexible” compulsory teaching module and assessment system of CDBRC, and analyze its effectiveness over 3 years.Materials and methodsThe grade point average (GPA), course pressure, level of basic research knowledge and skills, and students’ research achievements were collected and analyzed between EDBRC and CDBRC. The unpaired t test was used to analyze the difference.ResultsThe “flexible” CDBRC has been successfully constructed with compulsory teaching module and hierarchical assessment system. The CDBRC has not caused significant course pressure to students compared with the EDBRC. Besides, the “flexible” CDBRC can improve the students’ GPA, basic research knowledge, and research achievements.ConclusionsThe “flexible” CDBRC can improve students’ academic performance and basic research abilities without causing significant course pressure, which can be conducted in dental schools with similar backgrounds.Trial registrationNot applicable.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12903-024-04874-7.Keywords: Compulsory dental basic research course, Dental education, Curriculum load, Basic research skills, Basic research knowledge

Introduction

Undergraduate dental basic research education (UDBRE) is an important component of dental education with a basic research theme. It includes an initial introduction to common issues related to basic research and experimental skills training [1–3]. UDBRE can stimulate students’ interest in scientific research and effectively cultivate their theoretical knowledge, skills, and critical thinking in basic research [4–7]. Currently, UDBRE is well accepted in the field of dental undergraduate education and has a positive effect on career development and academic success. However, whether UDBRE should be an elective or compulsory course has not reached a consensus.

A few dental schools have succeeded in incorporating UDBRE into compulsory courses and building compulsory dental basic research courses (CDBRCs) [8–10]. For example, the Dentistry Faculty of the University of Indonesia conducted the CDBRC in its undergraduate dental program. A total of 78.3% of the respondents agreed that it is beneficial for their professional careers, and 81.3% of the respondents preferred undergraduate research as a compulsory course to an elective one [10]. However, some difficulties exist in that the education goals of the CDBRC cannot be set too high because it needs to consider all students at various levels. If not well designed, CDBRC may increase students’ academic burden and psychological pressure.

Therefore, most UDBRE was performed in the form of an elective course, in which top-rated students can participate in scientific research to improve their basic research skills. However, the elective course may lead to some unfairness in teaching resource allocation, and students may not be motivated enough to join in the elective course owing to laziness. These factors may lead to not all students benefiting from the UDBRE, and top-rated students with great potential to grow into clinical-scientific interdisciplinary talent can be missed. Thus, reforming the CDBRC to overcome these potential problems has great pedagogical significance.

In 2015, the Guanghua School of Stomatology, Sun Yat-sen University, launched an elective dental basic research course (EDBRC), called the Innovative Undergraduate Dental Talents Training Project (IUDTTP). The IUDTTP was established to cultivate research capacity and arouse research enthusiasm through elective modes of mentor-guided forms, intensive theoretical course training and competitions. A 6-year retrospective assessment indicated that it had satisfactory effects and some potential deficiencies [2]. To further combine the advantages of compulsory and elective modes, we conducted a “flexible” CDBRC on the basis of experience from the IUDTTP in 2020. The “flexible” CDBRC has adopted systemic educational goals and extensive teaching activities, covering all the undergraduate students to benefit from the CDBRC. In addition, it sets a flexible and hierarchical assessment system that can train top-rated students in scientific research without causing an obvious academic burden.

This study intends to introduce the “flexible” CDBRC, discuss its educational goal, teaching modules and content, assessment system and analyze its effectiveness over 3 years. This study provides a hybrid teaching mode of UDBRE, in combination with the advantages of both compulsory and elective modes. The aim of the authors is to train all involved students with fundamental basic dental research skills and to provide top-rated students with opportunities to grow into complex clinical-scientific talents.

Materials and methods

Workflow of the basic dental research course

In our system, the CDBRC is a flexible compulsory course that integrates the experience of an elective course [3]. It has been under development for three years and cultivated over 300 students from 2019 to 2021.

The teaching module of the CDBRC is composed of compulsory theoretical and experimental courses, elective online courses, flexible mentor-guided forms, and elective extracurricular research activities (Fig. 1). The content of compulsory courses has not changed, whereas elective online courses have been regularly updated to provide advanced knowledge of scientific research for students. The mentor-guided forms were integrated to ensure that all the students could participate in scientific research practices. It initially required students to act as research assistants in the 2019. After a year of practice, we found that it imposed certain workloads on some students. In 2020, acting as a research assistant was optional. In 2021, the CDBRC eventually established a three-tier mentor-guided form. After entering the research tutors’ team, students can opt to complete reviews only at the first level and can voluntarily engage in simple research tasks during their free time at the second level. The top-rated students in scientific research can serve as research assistants at the third level.

The components of “flexible” CDBRC. Based on the experience of EDBRC, the “flexible” CDBRC adopted systematic teaching content and activities to cover all undergraduate students. It has settled a hierarchical assessment system, which avoids causing excessive course pressure on students

In addition, the “flexible” CDBRC adopted a flexible and hierarchical assessment system. At the basic level, the course grades were comprehensively assessed by experimental course performance, literature review, and final exams, which can ease the academic burdens for students. The advanced assessment for students includes research projects, competitions, and research paper publications.

Inclusion and exclusion of students in different subgroups

Both “flexible” CDBRC and extracurricular scientific research activities are two-semester courses for second-year dental students. The participants were divided into two groups. Group A included dental undergraduates from 2019 to 2021 who had completed the “flexible” CDBRC. Group B included dental undergraduates with better learning ability from 2016 to 2018 who did not participate in the CDBRC but had voluntarily participated in the EDBRC.

This study received ethical approval (No. KQEC-2021-38-01) from the Medical Ethics Committee of the Hospital of Stomatology of Sun Yat-sen University. This study was performed from September 2016 to July 2023, and all participants provided consent to be involved in the study. Students were included only by sending anonymous questionnaires.

Anonymous questionnaires to dental undergraduates

In 5-point Likert scale, the sample size of 58 satisfied the requirement of 10% of tolerate error, suggested coefficient of variation and pairwise correlation coefficient [11]. An anonymous questionnaire was designed and distributed to dental undergraduates (N = 173) from 2016 to 2021 (Appendix. 1). The questionnaire was categorized into five parts: basic information of participants, course pressure, understanding level of basic dental research, self-assessment of basic research abilities, and an open question. The first draft was drafted by dental faculty and students together. The dental education experts subsequently reviewed the initial draft. To distribute the questionnaire, we used WJX (www.wjx.cn, China), a free online questionnaire service. All the participants voluntarily filled out the questionnaire and had the right to refuse to answer any questions. No individual identifying information was recorded when the questionnaires were collected.

Basic information of the participants

This session was applied to record participants’ basic information, including gender and grade.

Course pressure on students

This session assessed the academic burden, psychological stress, and examination stress of the course. Likert-scale items were used to quantify the questionnaires. Points 1 to 5 refer to “completely absent” and “obvious presence”, respectively.

Understanding the level of basic dental research

The content of basic research, including academic career planning, scientific research significance, literature review, experimental design and safety, the management and analysis of research results, the publication of scientific papers, academic conferences and exchanges, achievement transformation policies, the application of undergraduate research projects, and problems and solutions in basic research, was identified. Likert-scale items were used to quantify the questionnaires, with points 1 to 5 referring to “very poor”, “poor”, “fair”, “good”, and “excellent”, respectively.

Self-assessment of basic research abilities

This session was also derived from Likert-scale items and designed to identify the students’ self-assessment of their basic research abilities, including their ability to address scientific problems, experimental design, experimental skills, data analysis, research paper writing, research project application, and academic exchange.

Subjective opinions and feedback

The final session was an open-ended question to record participants’ perceptions of and suggestions for the basic dental research course.

Documental data collection and analysis

Documental data (GPA and research achievements) were collected from the Department of Student Administration and Teaching Affairs. The GPA in the second year from 2016 to 2021 was used to assess academic pressure objectively. Research achievements were identified through the number of projects and awards received by students.

Statistical analysis

The measurement data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation (M ± SD). Statistical significance was evaluated via an unpaired t test. SPSS 26.0 (IBM Corp, State of New York, USA) was used for all the data analyses. GraphPad Prism 9.0 software (GraphPad Software, Inc., USA) was used to construct the figures. Significance is expressed as *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, and ***P < 0.001.

Results

The teaching system of the “flexible” compulsory course

The flexibility of the CDBRC is demonstrated through two main aspects (Fig. 1). First, the CDBRC integrates the compulsory teaching modules and content of the elective course, which can cater to the development and assessment of students at different levels. In addition, the CDBRC sets a flexible and hierarchical assessment system, which avoids causing obvious academic burdens to students.

Description of questionnaire participation

A total of 173 students completed the questionnaire, 90 of whom were from Group A (34 males and 56 females) and 83 of whom were from Group B (44 males and 39 females). In Group B, the number of students in the in 2016, 2017, and 2018 was 31, 24, and 28, respectively (Table 1).

The impact of a “flexible” CDBRC on academic burden

GPA and student self-assessment questionnaires were used to assess the course pressure of the “flexible” CDBRC (Table 2). The average GPA for the students from Group A was 3.191 ± 0.704, whereas that from Group B was 2.904 ± 0.771 (P < 0.001). According to the results of the Likert scale, the average academic burden score for Group A was 2.967 ± 0.905, whereas it was 2.807 ± 0.911 for Group B (P = 0.248). The scores for psychological stress were 2.833 ± 0.997 and 2.904 ± 1.001 in Groups A and B, respectively (P = 0.644). The score for examination stress in Group A was 3.156 ± 1.038 (vs. 3.060 ± 1.022 in Group B; P = 0.543). Thus, these results indicate that the CDBRC does not impose obvious course pressure on students.

Influence of “flexible” CDBRC on students’ understanding of basic research knowledge

The results of the Likert-scale questions revealed that the average scores for self-assessment of the level of understanding of basic dental research in Group A ranged from 3.300 to 3.667, which was generally higher than the scores in Group B (2.834 to 3.253; Table 3). Although there was no significant difference in the “management and analysis of research results” between the two groups, the understanding level of other options in Group A was higher than that in Group B. Therefore, these results demonstrate that the CDBRC can improve the understanding of basic research knowledge.

Discussion

Inheriting the flexibility of the previously developed IUDTTP, the CDBRC established a more systematic curriculum system and promoted the sustainable development of UDBRE. The basic goal of CDBRC is to improve students’ basic research knowledge and ability. The advanced goal is to encourage them to deeply participate in basic research practice and to foster dental clinical scientists. On the basis of the results of these studies, the “flexible” CDBRC is beneficial for UDBRE, which should be considered in some schools with similar conditions.

Teaching system development of the CDBRC

The teaching of basic dental research has certain difficulties, which are manifested in complicated and challenging teaching content involving the frontier and cross-disciplinary subject content [1, 2]. Therefore, it’s challenging to construct the teaching system of UDBRE. A major limitation of the EDBRC is that it is difficult to carry out for all students. Moreover, most EDBRC and CDBRC were carried out in the form of mentor-guided student research projects or theoretical lectures [2]. It is difficult to provide systematic teaching content, leading to deficient knowledge in some aspects. A survey of students from Pakistan medical and dental schools that had launched elective basic research programs revealed that 59.2% of the students claimed that they knew how to plan and conduct a research study, whereas only 17.7% of them knew the procedures for the publication of scientific articles [12]. Therefore, a compulsory course is required to provide all students with systematic learning without excessive course pressure. Therefore, the “flexible” CDBRC combines the systematic curriculum design and hierarchical assessment index. CDBRC requires all students to complete theoretical and experimental courses to promote their basic research knowledge and ability. Meanwhile, top-rated students are encouraged to join the research tutor team, which provides opportunities for them to perform advanced scientific research.

A “flexible” CDBRC does not cause an obvious academic burden but promotes academic performance

The mental stress of medical students has attracted much attention [13]. The sources of stress for dental students mainly include academic burdens, clinical requirements, and psychosocial factors [14, 15]. Elahi’s study suggested that academic overload and increasing clinical obligations are major barriers to undergraduate research [12]. However, our results showed that the “flexible” CDBRC did not cause an obvious academic burden for students, which may be related to the following factors. First, the course is intended for second-year dental students, who face lower pressure levels than senior students do [16]. Second, the CDBRC has flexible teaching content. Students can voluntarily choose whether to further participate in scientific research. Third, the course adopts flexible assessment in which students are eligible to pass the course.

According to the study, students who participated in the CDBRC presented higher overall GPAs and clearer career planning. Yu [17] and Scott [18] also reported that students who participated in basic research education gained higher GPAs and higher degrees of scholarship achievement [19]. These results demonstrated that CDBRC can improve students’ self-learning ability. A previous study demonstrated that the integration of UDBRE into dental education is necessary because basic research experience can help cultivate students’ critical thinking [20].

The “flexible” CDBRC promotes students’ capabilities and achievements in basic research

Many studies have shown that both the CDBRC and EDBRC help students improve their understanding of research topics and methods and enhance their research capabilities [2, 21, 22]. However, few of them provided a specific analysis and compared their teaching effectiveness. In our study, the results showed that the “flexible” CDBRC can promote students’ knowledge and capabilities of scientific research. The gaps in “Management and analysis of research results” (P = 0.2849) and “Ability of experimental design” (P = 0.1478) between Group A and Group B are not significantly different, suggesting that more related courses are needed in further course reforms of the CDBRC. In addition, the “flexible” CDBRC is appropriate for all students, and many students in Group A did not know scientific research at all before attending the course, whereas EDBRC is an optional course, and many students in Group B have some knowledge of scientific research. Therefore, the degree of improvement in students’ scientific research knowledge and abilities from the “flexible” CDBRC may be greater than that reflected by the results.

In addition, the number of projects and awards received by students in Group A is greater than that in Group B. This finding shows that the “flexible” CDBRC can help students achieve more basic research achievements, which is beneficial for cultivating innovative dental students. Olaniyi also discovered that compulsory research education helps students improve confidence in engaging in further research [23].

The challenges of the CDBRC

Although CDBRC can enhance the basic research knowledge and skills of dental undergraduates, it also has several limitations. First, the CDBRC requires a basic research environment provided by colleges. However, the lack of instructors, funds, and laboratory equipment is the major barrier for the CDBRC [10, 17, 21, 24, 25]. Other barriers include scientific textbooks, regulatory frameworks, interprofessional collaboration and access to advanced technology [21, 25, 26]. It may be difficult for some colleges in economically underdeveloped regions to provide a basic scientific research environment for undergraduates [21]. Therefore, this course needs to be reformed according to the different conditions of the college. Moreover, online education and exchanges based on the internet have great potential in the promotion of basic research education [12, 25]. In addition, according to the results of this study, the CDBRC needs to improve the teaching of relevant content, such as the management and analysis of research results and the ability of experimental design. More teaching models like problem-based learning can be tried to integrate knowledge with practice [27]. Although it does not impose a significant academic burden, students’ mental health and burden should be prioritized through mental support services, counseling, and stress management programs [25]. Finally, the CDBRC can help improve students’ academic performance; thus, more research grants are needed for students in the CDBRC to conduct more high-quality projects.

Conclusion

The “flexible” CDBRC has been successfully constructed with a compulsory teaching module and hierarchical assessment system. The students in the “flexible” CDBRC reveal better academic performance and scientific research literacy without causing an obvious academic burden for students. We highly encourage more dental schools to conduct analogous projects to achieve positive teaching outcomes for dental students.

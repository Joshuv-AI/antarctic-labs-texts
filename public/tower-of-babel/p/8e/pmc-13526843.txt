Fuzuloparib enhances radiosensitivity of gain-of-function mutant p53 R273H NSCLC by promoting SLC7A11/GSH/GPX4 axis mediated ferroptosis
Authors: Mengjia Wu, Chaoyuan Pu, Wanxuan Weng, Fengmin Yin, Lei Zhang, Chenggang Xu, Mingjian Li, Chuanhui Cao, Wei Hu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractRadiotherapy is a crucial therapeutic approach for the management of non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Nevertheless, the radioresistance of NSCLC, particularly in cases involving the gain-of-function mutant p53, substantially limits its efficacy. According to recent research, mutant p53 R273H can recruit poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP)-1 on replicating DNA to promote tumor survival. Whether mutant p53 R273H-mediated radioresistance is related to the interaction between mutant p53 R273H and PARP1, and if PARP1 inhibitors (PARPi) can increase the radiosensitivity of mutant p53 R273H-expressing tumors have not been explored. Through in vitro and in vivo experiments, we determined that mutant p53 R273H promotes radioresistance in NSCLC by partially inhibiting ferroptosis through the SLC7A11/GSH/GPX4 axis, independently of PARP1, whereas FZ counteracts this effect by partially blocking the same axis to promote ferroptosis, presenting a potential novel strategy for treating NSCLC patients with gain-of-function mutant p53 R273H.Subject terms: Cancer metabolism, Non-small-cell lung cancer

Introduction

Radiotherapy is the main therapeutic approach for the management of non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC)1,2. However, radioresistance is always the major limitation for effectiveness of radiotherapy, resulting in poor prognosis3.

Radiosensitivity can be modulated by inherent genetic mutations of tumor cells4. TP53 is a tumor suppressor gene that plays an important role in DNA damage repair, apoptosis, cell cycle, and energy metabolism5. Nevertheless, mutations in the TP53 gene are one of the most common genetic alterations and present at a high frequency in human cancers, and are associated with resistance to tumor therapies6. Approximately 80% of TP53 mutations are missense7, defined as missense mutations in TP53 DNA nucleoside sequences, resulting in the expression of p53 proteins with single amino-acid substitutions in the DNA-binding domain. Among them, six hotspot mutations, including mutations at codons R175H, R245S, R248W, R249S, R273H, and R282H, account for approximately 30% of p53 mutations in cancers8. Additionally, some missense mutant p53 (mutp53) often acquire inherent, novel oncogenic functions, termed “gain-of-function”9. Statistically, gain-of-function mutant p53 is associated with the facilitation of epithelial-mesenchymal transition, altering the extracellular matrix, regulating tumor cell invasion and metastasis10, promoting metabolic reprogramming of cancer cells and enhancing fitness toward intratumoral metabolic stress, and promoting therapeutic resistance11. Furthermore, tumors containing gain-of-function mutant p53 confer increased resistance to ionizing radiation (IR)6, including lung12, head-and-neck13, and cervical cancers14. Exploring the mechanisms underlying radioresistance caused by TP53 mutation remains pivotal for addressing this persistent clinical challenge.

Mutated genes in the signaling pathway have been the focus of extensive investigations as novel therapeutic targets for medications15,16. The gain-of-function activity of mutant p53 suggests that targeting the mutant p53-dependent signaling network can be an effective approach for treatment. Mutant p53 R273H, one of the most recurrent p53 gain-of-function hotspot mutations, is associated with DNA replication. Jill et al. demonstrated that mutant p53 R273H recruits DNA replication proteins and poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP)-1 to stressed replicating DNA, enabling replication to continue even in the presence of DNA damage. This process contributes to the survival of breast cancer cells9,11,17. PARP1 is an abundant nuclear protein that plays a role in DNA damage repair, including the repair of single-strand breaks (SSBs) and double-strand breaks (DSBs), stabilization of DNA replication forks, and modification of chromatin structure18. In response to DNA strand breaks, PARP1 synthesizes poly(ADP-ribose) (PAR) chains. XPC-RAD23B, a protein involved in DNA damage repair, forms a heterodimer with free PAR and is recruited to sites of DNA damage, thereby repairing DNA damage induced by radiotherapy19.

Thus, we speculated that radiation resistance mediated by mutant p53 R273H possibly results from interactions between mutant p53 R273H and PARP1; moreover, PARP1 inhibitors (PARPi) can be a promising approach to overcome NSCLC radioresistance. They may be a viable therapeutic alternative for treating patients with cancer. Fuzuloparib (FZ), a novel PARPi, has been approved for use in China. A phase III trial in ovarian cancer has demonstrated favorable safety and potent anticancer activity20.

In this study, we discovered that beyond its canonical role in PARP1 inhibition, the PARPi FZ reverses radioresistance in mutant p53 R273H NSCLC partially by promoting ferroptosis via the SLC7A11/GSH/GPX4 axis. Our findings highlight the potential of PARPi as a synergistic treatment in combination with radiotherapy in patients with mutant p53 R273H NSCLC.

Results

Gain-of-function mutant p53 R273H promotes radioresistance of NSCLC

To verify whether gain-of-function mutant p53 R273H influenced the sensitivity of the NSCLC cell lines to radiation, p53-null H1299 and H358 cells were infected with a wild-type TP53 (wtp53) or a mutant p53 R273H overexpression lentivirus, and transfected with a wtp53 or a mutant p53 R273H overexpression plasmid, respectively (Fig. 1A and Supplementary Fig. 1A). A clonogenic assay was performed to determine the radiosensitivity of the H1299 cells. As shown in Fig. 1B and Supplementary Fig. 1B, H1299 and H358 cells expressing mutant p53 R273H reduced radiosensitivity compared to cells expressing wtp53, as reflected by increased clonogenic survival and sensitization enhancement ratios (SER) of 0.82 and 0.89, respectively, suggesting that mutant p53 R273H has significantly inhibited the radiosensitivity of NSCLC. We further examined the cell proliferation, cell cycle, and cell apoptosis in mutant p53 R273H H1299 cells upon IR exposure. Cell viability at 24, 48 and 72 h after 2 Gy IR was analyzed using the CCK-8 assay for cell proliferation detection, which showed that wtp53 significantly suppressed the proliferation of H1299 cells following irradiation. In contrast, mutant p53 R273H can substantially reduce the IR-induced proliferation inhibition of H1299 cells to a great extent (Fig. 1C). Cell cycle distribution analyzed using flow cytometry showed a significantly higher G2/M phase arrest was observed in wtp53 H1299 cells in comparison with H1299 cells following IR, whereas the cell proportion of G2/M-phase in mutant p53 R273H H1299 cells decreased after IR (Fig. 1D). The apoptosis ratio was significantly enhanced in wtp53 H1299 cells than that in H1299 cells. In contrast, mutant p53 R273H H1299 cells exhibited a marked decrease in apoptosis following IR (Fig. 1E).

PARPi FZ reverses the radioresistance of mutant p53 R273H-mediated in NSCLC

To confirm the radiosensitizing role of the PARPi, FZ, we detected the inhibition rates of tumor cell proliferation after treatment with different concentrations of FZ for 24, 48, and 72 h, which were notably dose- and time-dependent. The IC50 of FZ at 24 h were 64 μM and 61 μM for H1299 and H358 cells, respectively (Fig. 2A and Supplementary Fig. 1C). As the concentration increase of FZ increased the inhibition rate in NSCLC cells gradually increased. The high cytotoxic effects of FZ as a single agent in NSCLC cells may mask its complete radiosensitization potential of FZ. Therefore, for the purpose of radiosensitivity, the low and safe concentrations of FZ (1/4 IC50, 16 μM in H1299 and 15 μM in H358) were applied in the following study (Fig. 2A and Supplementary Fig. 1C), without evident toxicity (Fig. 2B). Next, the colony formation assay showed that compared with the IR alone, FZ significantly enhanced the radiosensitivity of mutant p53 R273H H1299 and H358 cells, with SERs of 1.6 and 1.57, respectively (Fig. 2C and Supplementary Fig. 1D). These data indicated that FZ enhanced the sensitivity of mutant p53 R273H NSCLC to radiation. Next, we assessed the effects of FZ + IR on mutant p53 R273H H1299 cell proliferation, cell cycle, and apoptosis. Compared with IR alone, the combination treatment of FZ and IR markedly reduced cell proliferation (Fig. 2D) and increased G2/M arrest and apoptosis in mutant p53 R273H H1299 cells (Fig. 2E, F).

FZ promotes ferroptosis in irradiated mutant p53 R273H NSCLC in vitro

Studies have shown that inhibition of PARP1 activity selectively induces cancer cell death owing to mutant p53 mediated high level PARP1 association with DNA replication forks in cancer cell lines. Therefore, we assayed the activity of PARP, as shown in Fig. 3A, despite the inhibitory effect of the PARPi on PARP enzyme activity, no significant difference in PARP activity was observed between mutant p53 R273H and wtp53 H1299 cells following IR (Fig. 3A). We then detected the expression of PARP1 using western blot analysis, and there was no significant difference in the expression of PARP1 in all groups (Fig. 3B), which indicated that mutant p53 R273H-induced radioresistance in H1299 cells occurs independently of both PARP activity and PARP1 expression. Studies have demonstrated that the gain-of-function mechanism of mutant p53 and PARPi-mediated radiosensitization are both associated with ferroptosis. We hypothesized that FZ may partially mediate radiosensitivity through ferroptosis in mutant p53 R273H H1299 cells. MDA assays were used to detect lipid peroxidation. As expected, IR induced an increase in MDA levels in wtp53 H1299 cells but a decrease in mutant p53 R273H H1299 cells, which was significantly reversed by the combination treatment with PARPi FZ (Fig. 3C). Ferroptosis is an iron-dependent form of regulated cell death21. We detected the Fe2+ content in H1299 cells and found that, in comparison with the IR group, the content of Fe2+ in mutant p53 R273H H1299 cells was significantly downregulated compared to wtp53 H1299 cells. After treatment with the combination of FZ and IR, the content of Fe2+ in mutant p53 R273H H1299 cells exhibited a marked increase (Fig. 3D). To further clarify the involvement of ferroptosis in FZ + IR-induced mutant p53 R273H NSCLC cell death, we measured the protein levels of the ferroptosis marker gene22, ACSL4, and found that radiation treatment elevated the expression of ACSL4 in wtp53 H1299 cells but suppressed it in mutant p53 R273H H1299 cells. Notably, the combination of the PARPi FZ and IR reversed this suppression and significantly increased the expression of ACSL4 in mutant p53 R273H H1299 cells (Fig. 3E). Finally, TEM revealed that mutant p53 R273H H1299 cells after FZ + IR treatment exhibited shrunken mitochondria with increased membrane density and fewer cristae, which are typical morphological ferroptosis22 (Fig. 3F). Collectively, these data strongly suggest that compared with wtp53, mutant p53 R273H promotes radioresistance at least partly through inhibiting ferroptosis, and this effect can be reversed by FZ.

FZ reverses the upregulation of SLC7A11 induced by mutant p53 R273H in irradiated NSCLC

Next, we sought to understand how mutant p53 R273H mediates IR-induced ferroptosis. Previous studies have shown that, by transcriptional suppression of SLC7A11, the ferroptosis suppressor gene, p53 sensitizes cells to ferroptosis23. In contrast to wtp53, mutant p53 R273H lacks DNA-binding capacity and fails to occupy the SLC7A11 promoter region6, thereby potentially abolishing wtp53-mediated transcriptional repression of the SLC7A11 gene. Hence, we analyzed the expression of p53 in H1299 cells after IR, and found that p53 was expressed in the nucleus (Fig. 4A). The fluorescence intensity of the mutant p53 R273H group was higher than that of the wtp53 group, as supported by the nucleocytoplasmic protein separation assay in H1299 cells (Fig. 4B). These findings suggest that mutant p53 was translocated into the nucleus to exert its effects. Next, Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) analysis demonstrated that wtp53, but not the R273H mutant, binds to the promoter region of the SLC7A11 gene in H1299 cells (Fig. 4C). The expression of SLC7A11 was also measured by qRT-PCR and western blot. We observed that wtp53 downregulated SLC7A11 mRNA and protein levels in H1299 cells, whereas this downregulation was completely abrogated under the condition of p53 R273H mutation (Fig. 4D, E). Notably, this effect was effectively reversed by the combination of FZ and IR in mutant p53 R273H H1299 cells (Fig. 4D, E). These results demonstrate that SLC7A11 overexpression suppresses ferroptosis in mutant p53 R273H H1299 cells. Importantly, combination treatment with FZ and IR effectively reverses this mutant p53 R273H-driven effect.

FZ promotes ferroptosis via suppressing SLC7A11/GSH/GPX4 axis in irradiated NSCLC

Ferroptosis is controlled by intracellular oxidation and antioxidant systems. Given that SLC7A11 functions through the SLC7A11/GSH/GPX4 antioxidant axis, which is crucial for the inhibition of ferroptosis24. We further explored the expression of GPX4 in wtp53 H1299 cells and mutant p53 R273H H1299 cells. Following IR, GPX4 expression level was significantly higher in the mutant p53 R273H group than in the wtp53 group. FZ + IR treatment markedly diminished GPX4 expression (Fig. 5A). Consistent with the expression trends of SLC7A11 and GPX4, the level of GSH increased in mutant p53 R273H H1299 cells after IR, while the combination of FZ and radiotherapy markedly down-regulated its level (Fig. 5B). Destroying the antioxidant defenses promotes cellular ROS accumulation, ultimately leading to ferroptosis25. As expected, we confirmed that the ROS levels were significantly lower in mutant p53 R273H H1299 cells than in wtp53 H1299 cells, after treatment with FZ + IR resulted in higher total ROS in mutant p53 R273H H1299 cells (Fig. 5C). DNA damage is one of the main indicators reflecting the level of intracellular ROS3,26. Although mutant p53 R273H H1299 cells exhibited fewer DNA DSBs after IR compared to wtp53 H1299 cells, their physical DSBs increased following combined treatment with FZ and IR (Fig. 5D). In addition, immunofluorescence staining and western blot analysis showed that the expression of γ-H2AX, a marker of IR-induced DSBs, was significantly increased in mutant p53 R273H cells following treatment with FZ and IR (Fig. 5E, F). These results indicate that the combination of IR and PARPi FZ enhances ROS levels through inhibition of the antioxidant system.

Overall, these data support the hypothesis that FZ partially promotes IR-induced ferroptosis by inhibiting the SLC7A11/GSH/GPX4 axis, thereby contributing to FZ-mediated radiosensitization in gain-of-function mutant p53 R273H NSCLC.

FZ increases radiosensitivity of mutant p53 R273H NSCLC cells by ferroptosis in vivo

FZ increased the radiosensitivity of mutant p53 R273H NSCLC cells to IR in vitro; hence, we investigated its ability to enhance the antitumor effects of IR in vivo. We inoculated wtp53 or mutant p53 R273H H1299 cells into nude mice and treated them with IR or IR + FZ (Fig. 6A). Consistent with our in vitro results, in vivo experiments showed the mutant p53 R273H group facilitated resistance to radiotherapy compared to the wtp53 group. Compared with the wtp53 group, no tumor growth inhibition was observed in the mutant p53 R273H group, whereas the combination of FZ and IR produced potent suppression of tumor growth in the mutant p53 R273H group (Fig. 6B, C). To further confirm the ferroptosis regulation by FZ combined with IR in vivo, we analyzed the level of 4-hydroxy-2-noneal (4-HNE), a ferroptosis marker reflecting the level of lipid peroxidation, using immunohistochemistry. Compared with the wtp53 group, the mutant p53 R273H group showed significantly lower 4-HNE level, which was rescued by the IR + FZ treatment (Fig. 6D). Moreover, the protein levels of SLC7A11 and GPX4 were markedly increased in the mutant p53 R273H group compared to the wtp53 group, whereas the upregulation of SLC7A11 and GPX4 was reversed under IR + FZ conditions (Fig. 6E, F). Collectively, these results suggest that mutant p53 R273H can inhibit the radiotherapy efficacy of NSCLC, while FZ reversed the radioresistance partly by enhancing IR-mediated ferroptosis in vivo.

Discussion

Radiotherapy resistance is a major challenge in the treatment of NSCLC. Several cellular signaling pathways and factors are involved in radioresistance. Among these pathways and factors, p53 mutations are considered the most crucial regulator6. Although the role of mutant p53 in determining radiosensitivity is complex and relatively controversial, mutant p53 tumors are typically considered radioresistant. In this study, our data demonstrate that compared with wtp53, mutant p53 promotes radioresistance, cell proliferation, reduces G2/M phase arrest and cell apoptosis in NSCLC.

DSBs are the most lethal lesions induced by IR and can trigger a series of cellular DNA damage responses (DDRs), including those that help cells recover from radiation injuries, such as the activation of DNA damage sensing, early transduction pathways and DNA repair27. One of the earliest events in the DDR is the recruitment of PARP1 to diverse types of DNA lesions27,28, which can initiate and regulate DNA repair pathways, including the repair of SSBs and DSBs, stabilization of DNA replication forks, and modification of chromatin structure to protect against DNA damage and cell death caused by radiotherapy or chemotherapy29. Jill et al. have demonstrated that mutant p53 R273H promotes tumor cell survival by recruiting PARP1 to DNA replication forks to facilitate DNA repair11,18. Consequently, inhibiting PARP activity can induce cancer cell death selectively. In this study, we did not observe that mutant p53 R273H itself affects PARP activity or PARP1 protein expression, even though FZ effectively inhibited PARP activity. The above results indicate that the radioresistance induced by mutant p53 R273H may not be mediated by promoting PARP1 expression and consequently enhancing DNA damage repair capability.

Gan et al. recently reported that IR potently induces ferroptosis, which plays a critical role in IR-induced tumor suppression30. In the present study, we demonstrate that compared with wtp53, mutant p53 R273H confers ferroptosis resistance in NSCLC cells. This inhibitory effect can be reversed by PARPi FZ combined with radiotherapy. Given the significant role of ferroptosis in both mutant p53 R273H-mediated radioresistance31 and PARPi-induced radiosensitization29, understanding its underlying mechanisms has become a key focus of our subsequent research.

SLC7A11 functions as an important amino acid transporter that imports extracellular cystine in exchange for intracellular glutamate, thereby promoting cysteine supply and GSH synthesis for antioxidant defense25,32. p53 was found to transcriptionally repress SLC7A11 expression and promote ferroptosis in response to ROS-mediated stress23,30,33. Here, we demonstrate that p53 acts in the nucleus, where it binds to the SLC7A11 promoter and transcriptionally represses its expression. Generally, missense mutations can be classified into contact mutations and structural mutations. Contact mutations maintain the overall conformation of p53 but impair DNA binding (e.g., R273H). While mutant p53 proteins generally evade MDM2-mediated degradation and accumulate in the nucleus34, the accumulation exemplified by the p53 R273H mutant studied here does not confer SLC7A11 repression but rather results in its upregulated expression. Furthermore, radiation treatment triggers an additional, rapid induction of SLC7A11, likely as an adaptive survival response against ferroptosis. Since the transcription factor NRF2 regulates SLC7A11, its potential role in the overall upregulation of SLC7A11 in the context of mutant p53 R273H requires further investigation. Previous studies have demonstrated that the enhancement of ferroptosis through SLC7A11 knockdown significantly potentiates the therapeutic effect of PARPi olaparib32. Our study identifies the inhibition of SLC7A11 expression by FZ as a potential mechanism for overcoming mutant p53 R273H-associated radioresistance. The antioxidant SLC7A11/GSH/GPX4 axis is considered the central repressor of ferroptosis, which exhibits an antioxidant role by mitigating lipid peroxidation through the reduction of ROS, resulting in the inhibition of ferroptosis. Notably, we found that the combination of FZ and IR suppresses the SLC7A11/GSH/GPX4 axis, increases ROS production and corresponding DNA damage. We further tested and validated that FZ exerts radiosensitization effects in mutant p53 xenografts from H1299 tumor-bearing mice, indicating that the SLC7A11/GSH/GPX4 axis is likely involved in overcoming mutant p53 R273H mediated radioresistance.

This study contributes to our understanding of radiosensitivity and ferroptosis, however, some limitations remain. First, although we investigated the relationship between p53 gain-of-function R273H mutation and RT resistance in NSCLC, there is currently a lack of verification of clinical samples. Additionally, our findings need to be validated in additional lung cancer cell lines, gain-of-function p53 mutations and endogenous mutant p53 models.

In summary, we studied the mechanisms by which FZ enhances radiosensitivity of mutant p53 R273H NSCLC partly by suppressing the SLC7A11/GSH/GPX4 axis and promoting ferroptosis.

Methods

Cell culture and transfection

The human NSCLC cell line, p53 Null H1299 and H358 were obtained from the Shanghai Cell Bank, Chinese Academy of Sciences (Shanghai, China) and Procell Life Science & Technology Co., Ltd (Wuhan, China), respectively. Cells were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium (Gibco, Rockville, Maryland, USA) containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Gibco, Rockville, Maryland, USA) and 1% penicillin streptomycin (Cytiva, Uppsala, Sweden) at 37 °C in 5% CO2 and 95% humidity. To ensure the authenticity and purity of the cell lines, short tandem repeat (STR) profiling was conducted for cell authentication, and rigorous monthly screenings were performed to detect and prevent mycoplasma contamination.

Antibodies and reagents

The following primary antibodies were used in this study: p53 (Santa Cruz Biotechnology, Texas, USA), phospho-histone H2A.X (Cell Signaling Technology, Boston, Massachusetts, USA), PARP (Cell Signaling Technology, Boston, Massschusetts, USA), ACSL4 (Proteintech, Wuhan, China), SLC7A11 (Cell Signaling Technology, Boston, USA), GPX4 (Proteintech, Wuhan, China), 4-HNE (Abcam, Boston, Massachusetts, USA), Lamin A/C (Santa Cruz Biotechnology, Texas, USA), GAPDH (Proteintech, Wuhan, China) and β-Actin (Abcam, Boston, Massachusetts, USA). Secondary antibodies against rabbit (Absin, Shanghai, China) or mouse (Absin, Shanghai, China) conjugated with horseradish peroxidase (HRP) were used in this study.

FZ was provided by Jiangsu Hengrui Medicine Co., Ltd. FZ was dissolved in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) and diluted in RPMI-1640 to working concentrations.

Transfection and lentiviral infection

Wild-type TP53 and R273H-mutant TP53 overexpression plasmids were purchased from Feng Hui Biotechnology (Hunan, China). Transfection for transient expression was performed using the Jetprime transfection reagent (Polyplus, Illkirch, France) according to the manufacturer’s protocol.

To induce wild-type TP53 and R273H-mutant TP53 overexpression, H1299 and H358 cells were infected with lentiviral particles (GeneChem, Shanghai, China). Specifically, cells were inoculated in six-well plates, 1 day in advance. On the following day, lentiviral particles were added at a multiplicity of infection of 15. The infection medium was replaced with complete medium 16 h after infection. After 48 h, infected cells were selected using complete medium containing 2.5 μg/mL puromycin.

Radiation

Cells were irradiated at the Department of Radiation Oncology, with the same equipment used for the patients receiving radiotherapy (RT); moreover, the same radiation oncology professionals who treated the patients operated the equipment. Single absorbed doses of radiation were administered using a 6 MV photon beam (Sweden, Elekta Synergy accelerator, dose rate: 500 cGy/min). The cells were then mock-irradiated under identical conditions.

Clonogenic survival assay

Cells in each group were seeded into 6-well plates at densities of 100, 200, 400, 800, and 1200 cells/well and incubated for 12 h to allow cell adhesion. FZ or DMSO was added to the culture medium and incubated for 2 h. This was followed by irradiation with 0, 2, 4, 6, and 8 Gray (Gy). Cells were then cultured for 14 days in drug-free fresh media to form visible colonies before being fixed with methanol and stained with 1% crystal violet. Colonies containing more than 50 cells were counted using microscopy, the surviving fraction (SF) and other radiobiological parameters, including sensitization enhancement ratio (SER), were calculated. In the radiation treatment experiments, a multitarget single-hit model was fitted to the data to generate survival curves using the following formula: SF = 1−(1−e^[-kD]^N)35.

Cell viability

Cell viability was measured using Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8). Cells were seeded in 96-well plates with 1000-1500 cells/well and incubated overnight. The cells were treated with the indicated concentrations of FZ for 24, 48, or 72 h. After exposing the cells to FZ for the indicated time periods, a microplate reader was used to measure absorbance at 450 nm. Cell viability was calculated for each well, and the 50% growth inhibition (IC50) was determined for subsequent calculation of the drug concentration required for radiosensitization.

Cell proliferation assay

Cell proliferation was tested by Cell Counting Kit-8 assay. Cells were seeded at a density of 5 × 103 cells/well in 96-well cell culture plates and incubated overnight in 100 μL of RPMI 1640 medium. On the following days, DMSO and FZ were added 2 h before IR (2 Gy) with 6 Mega Volts X-rays. After 24, 48 and 72 h after irradiation, cell proliferation was assessed using a CCK-8 assay Kit. Ten microliters of CCK8 reagent was added to each well, and the cells were incubated at 37 °C for 2.5 h. The optical density (OD) at 450 nm was measured using a microplate reader (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Massachusetts, USA).

Immunofluorescence

Cells were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 10 min, washed three times in PBS (5 min for each), and then permeabilized with a 0.3% Triton solution for 10 min. After blocking with 5% BSA, cells were simultaneously incubated with antibody overnight at 4 °C, treated with Alexa 488-labeled goat anti-rabbit IgG secondary antibody at room temperature for 2 h, washed with PBS, and incubated with DAPI for nuclear staining (Beyotime, Shanghai, China). Images were obtained using a confocal microscope (Carl Zeiss, Germany).

Flow cytometry

To detect the cell cycle, the cells were cultured for 2 h, harvested, and washed twice with ice-cold PBS. After being fixed in 70% cold ethanol at 4 °C overnight, the cells were stained with PI in the presence of RNase A (Solarbio, Beijing, China) at 37 °C for 30 min. The stained cells were analyzed using a flow cytometer, and the results of the cell cycle distribution analysis were analyzed using the ModFit LT software (Topsham, Maine, USA).

To detect apoptosis, cells were harvested and washed twice with cold PBS and resuspended in binding buffer containing Annexin V-FITC (Solarbio, Beijing, China) and stained with propidium iodide (PI) staining for 15 min at 4 °C in the dark. The stained cells were analyzed using flow cytometry within 60 min. Annexin V-positive and PI-negative cells (Annexin V-FITC+/ PI−) were considered early-stage apoptosis, whereas Annexin V/PI-double-positive cells (Annexin V-FITC+/PI+) were considered late-stage apoptosis.

To investigate reactive oxygen species (ROS), after treatments, cells were harvested and washed twice with cold PBS, subjected to ROS Detection Assay Kit (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) and incubated for 30 min at 37 °C in the dark. The stained cells were analyzed using flow cytometry within 60 min.

PARP activity detection

The activity concentration of poly ADP-ribose polymerase (PARP) was measured using a PARP activity detection kit according to the manufacturer’s instructions (Meimian, Yancheng, China).

MDA detection

The level of intracellular malondialdehyde (MDA) was measured using an MDA detection kit according to the manufacturer’s instructions (Jiancheng, Nanjing, China).

Ferrous iron detection

Iron levels were measured using a Cell Ferrous Iron Colorimetric Assay Kit (Elabscience, Wuhan, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, cells were collected, lysed in buffer solution on ice for 10 min and centrifuged (15,000 × g, 10 min) at 4 °C. The supernatant was collected and 80 μL chromogenic solution was added to each sample and incubated at 37 °C for 10 min. The absorbance was measured at 593 nm using a microplate reader (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Massachusetts, USA).

Transmission electron microscopy (TEM)

The samples of cells were gently scraped using cell scrapers with 1 mL PBS and carefully transferred to 1.5 mL tubes. After centrifugation, the cell pellets were fixed with 500 μL of 2.5% glutaraldehyde at room temperature. After dehydration, embedding, and the preparation of ultrathin sections, the samples were observed under a Hitachi HT7700 TE microscope (Hitachi, Japan).

Nuclear paddle protein isolation assay and western blot

The nuclear and cytoplasmic proteins were obtained using the Nuclear and Cytoplasmic Protein Extraction Kit (Ecotop, Guangzhou, China) following the manufacturer’s instructions. Cells in the logarithmic growth phase were seeded in 6-well plates at a density of 3 × 105 cells/dish and allowed to adhere for 24 h. Cells were preincubated with or without FZ at 16 μM for 2 h and then exposed to 2 Gy of radiation. The cells were then incubated for 2 h. Ice-cold RIPA lysis buffer (Abcam, Boston, Massachusetts, USA) was used to prepare whole-protein extracts, which were then fortified with a mixture of protease inhibitors. Total proteins were separated using SDS-PAGE, transferred onto polyvinylidene difluoride membranes (Millipore, Massachusetts, USA), and blocked with 5% nonfat milk, and incubated sequentially with primary antibodies and horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibodies. Chemiluminescence was used to detect the proteins using a ChemiDoc (Bio-Rad, Hercules, California, USA), and the Bio-Rad Image Lab software was used to quantify the signals.

Quantitative real time PCR (qRT-PCR) and Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) assays

Total mRNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent (Solarbio, Beijing, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions, followed by synthesis of complementary DNA (cDNA) using oligo (dT) primers by a reverse transcriptase kit (Takara, Japan). Then, qPCR was performed using SYBR Green Master Mix wiper (Takara, Japan) on Bio-Rad Real-Time PCR instruments. The relative mRNA levels were calculated by the 2–ΔΔCT method and normalized to GAPDH. The specific primers were: SLC7A11 p1: 5′-GGC AGT GAC CTT TTC TGA GC-3’; SLC7A11 p2: 5′-TCA TTG TCA AAG GGT GCA AA-3’; GAPDH p1: 5′-GGA GCG AGA TCC CTC CAA AAT-3’; GAPDH p2: 5′-GGC TGT TGT CAT ACT TCT CAT GG-3’.

ChIP was performed as described previously36. Briefly, the cells were crosslinked with 1% formaldehyde. The DNA and proteins were broken down by ultrasonic shearing. The samples were centrifuged at 10,000 × g at 4 °C, and the supernatant was incubated with antibodies at 4 °C overnight. The complexes were washed with low- and high-salt buffers, and the DNA was extracted, precipitated, and detected by PCR. PCR primer sequences were: SLC7A11 forward: AGGCTTCTCATGTGGCTGAT; SLC7A11 reverse: TGCATCGTGCTCTCAATTCT.

Intracellular glutathione (GSH)

Total intracellular GSH from cells (six-well plate) was assayed using a Glutathione kit (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Comet assay

H1299 cells (10 × 103) were treated with IR (2 Gy) or FZ + IR 2 h before harvesting, resuspended in 0.6% low melting point agarose (UltraPure LMP Agarose (Life Technologies)), placed onto precoated slides with 1% normal melting point agarose, and coverslipped. Slides were placed on ice for 30 minutes to allow gels to solidify, coverslips were removed, and slides were placed in ice-cold lysis buffer (2.5 M NaCl, 0.1 M EDTA, 10 mM Tris-HCl, 1% TritonX-100 at pH 10) in the dark, overnight. Next (with light protection), the slides were washed for 10 minutes with ice-cold ddH2O and placed in ice-cold alkaline buffer (300 mM NaOH, 1 mmol/L disodium EDTA pH >13) for 20 min, and electrophoresed for 20 min at 30 V/300 mA protected from light. Following electrophoresis, slides were incubated with neutralization buffer (0.4 M Tris-HCl, pH 7.5) for 20 min, washed twice for 10 min with ddH2O, stained with propidium iodide (PI, 2.5 μg/mL) for a further 20 min, washed with fresh ddH2O for 20 min and placed at 37 °C to dry before the comet scoring using an Olympus BH-2-RFL-T2 fluorescence microscope (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan). The percent tail DNA was quantified using the Comet Score2.0 (Synentec, Hamburg, Germany).

In vivo radiosensitization assay

The animal studies were performed in compliance with The Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals, 8th edition (2011). The animal experimental protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Zunyi Medical University (No. ZMU21-2403-096). Nu/Nu mice (female, 4–5 weeks old) were obtained from Beijing TengXin Biotechnology Company and housed under specific pathogen-free (SPF) conditions. For tumor formation, the mice were administered a subcutaneous injection of 0.2 mL of a H1299 cell suspension (107 cells/mL) into the right thigh. The mice were weighed every alternate day, and once a palpable tumor had formed under the skin, it was measured every other day to calculate the tumor volume according to the following equation: volume = 1/2 × length × width2. On day 7, when the tumors reached an average size of 60–80 mm3, the mice were randomly assigned to different treatment groups. Radiotherapy was locally administered locally to the tumor. FZ (1 mg/kg per day, intragastric administration, days 8–14); IR (6 MV X-ray, 14 Gy/7 fractions on days 10–16). At the end of the animal experiments, the mice were sacrificed under deep anesthesia (induced by intraperitoneal injection of sodium pentobarbital at 50 mg/kg), and the tumors were surgically removed, measured, weighed, and photographed.

Immunohistochemistry analysis (IHC)

Immunostaining was carried out on paraffin tissue sections. The main procedure involved was as follows: Firstly, the sections were deparaffinized in 100% xylene and rehydrated with gradient ethanol (100%, 90%, 70%, 30% and 0%). After inactivation of endogenous peroxidase by 3% hydrogen peroxide and antigen retrieval in citrate buffer by heat.The following primary antibody dilutions were used: anti-SLC7A11 (1:1000), anti-GPX4 (1:1000), anti-4-HNE (1:50). The samples on the slides were incubated with the primary antibody at 4 °C overnight. Subsequently, the secondary antibody was applied and held at 37 °C for 20 min. The samples were stained brown with DAB solution and finally counterstained with hematoxylin for light color. Images were randomly acquired at ×200 magnification using an Olympus microscope and an immunoreactive score (IRS) system was used to semi-quantitatively assess the level of staining37. The percentage of staining positive cells was scored as follows: negative staining: 0; 1–10% staining: 1; 10–50% staining: 2; 51–80% staining: 3; and 81–100% staining: 4. The staining intensity was scored as follows: no reaction: 0; mild reaction: 1; moderate reaction: 2; and intense reaction: 3. Final IRS scores of immunohistochemistry = (scores of staining intensity) × (scores of percentage of staining positive cells).

Statistical analysis

All data are expressed as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM) and were analyzed using GraphPad Prism 9 (San Diego, California, USA). Differences between the two groups were analyzed using the unpaired Student’s t test. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) or two-way ANOVA was used to assess differences among groups. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.

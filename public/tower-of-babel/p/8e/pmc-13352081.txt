Metastasizing pleomorphic adenoma of the parotid gland: A case report with multimodal imaging and histopathological correlation
Authors: Asmae Kasimi, Mohammed Leknani, Chaimae Daoudi, Amal Bennani, Hamid Ziani, Siham Nasri, Imane Skiker
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Pleomorphic adenoma (PA) is the most common benign salivary gland tumor, predominantly arising in the parotid gland. Although usually indolent, it may rarely exhibit metastatic behavior without histological malignancy, a condition known as metastasizing pleomorphic adenoma (MPA). We report the case of a 43-year-old male with a history of right parotid pleomorphic adenoma (PA), initially treated by enucleation in 2010, followed by recurrence requiring total parotidectomy in 2019. The patient presented with a painless mass in the right parotid region associated with ipsilateral cervical swelling, a frontal scalp mass and lumbar pain. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) revealed a multilobulated lesion in the parotidectomy bed demonstrating low T1 signal intensity, high T2 signal intensity, elevated apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC ratio >1.3) and progressive contrast enhancement with a type A time–intensity curve, consistent with pleomorphic adenoma. Additional lesions with similar imaging features were identified in the right masticator space involving the lateral pterygoid muscle and along the right cervical level VIIb nodal chain. Whole-body computed tomography (CT) demonstrated an enhancing scalp mass and multiple osteolytic skeletal metastases. Histopathological analysis confirmed recurrent pleomorphic adenoma with metastatic deposits without malignant transformation. This case highlights the paradoxical behavior of MPA, the importance of complete surgical excision to reduce recurrence risk and the key role of MRI in lesion characterization and long-term surveillance. Prolonged follow-up is essential due to the potential for late metastatic spread.

Introduction

Pleomorphic adenoma (PA) is the most common salivary gland neoplasm, accounting for the majority of benign tumors, with nearly 80% arising in the parotid gland [1]. Histologically, it is a mixed tumor composed of epithelial and myoepithelial elements embedded in a variable stromal background [2]. Although PA is typically benign, it carries a small but well-recognized risk of malignant transformation into carcinoma ex pleomorphic adenoma [3].

In rare instances, PA may exhibit unexpected aggressive behavior, including regional lymph node involvement or distant metastasis despite the absence of histological malignancy [4]. This entity, known as metastasizing pleomorphic adenoma (MPA), represents a paradoxical condition in which lesions remain histologically benign while demonstrating metastatic potential [4].

The pathogenesis of MPA remains poorly understood. Several risk factors have been suggested, including incomplete surgical excision, tumor spillage, local recurrence and younger age at initial presentation [4]. Notably, histological findings alone do not reliably predict metastatic behavior [5]. Furthermore, metastases may occur after a prolonged latency period, contributing to the diagnostic challenge and uncertainty regarding prognosis [5].

Parotid gland tumors encompass a wide spectrum of benign and malignant entities that are often difficult to differentiate on clinical grounds alone [6]. In this context, imaging—particularly magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)—plays a central role in the preoperative assessment by accurately defining lesion extent, localization and tissue characteristics. A rigorous radiological evaluation is therefore essential to guide appropriate management and surgical planning.

We report the case of a 43-year-old male with a history of right parotid pleomorphic adenoma, initially treated by enucleation, followed by recurrence requiring total parotidectomy. The patient subsequently developed further recurrence with nodal, cutaneous and osseous metastases, which were histologically confirmed as metastasizing pleomorphic adenoma.

Case report

A 43-year-old male presented to our institution with a progressively enlarging painless swelling in the right parotid region associated with ipsilateral cervical swelling. The patient also reported the recent appearance of a slowly growing right frontal scalp mass and chronic lumbar back pain evolving over several months, without lower-limb weakness, sensory disturbances or sphincter dysfunction. No fever, weight loss or constitutional symptoms were noted.

His medical history was significant for a right parotid pleomorphic adenoma initially treated by enucleation in 2010 at another institution. In 2019, the patient developed local recurrence requiring total right parotidectomy. Histopathological examination at that time confirmed recurrent pleomorphic adenoma without evidence of malignant transformation.

Physical examination revealed a firm, nontender and poorly mobile mass within the right parotidectomy bed, associated with ipsilateral cervical lymphadenopathy. A palpable right frontal subcutaneous scalp mass was also noted. No overlying skin inflammatory changes or ulceration were present. Facial nerve function was preserved, with no facial palsy. Oral cavity examination showed no mucosal lesion or trismus. Neurological examination was unremarkable.

Given the history of recurrent pleomorphic adenoma and the presence of multifocal lesions, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the head and neck was performed for lesion characterization and locoregional assessment.

MRI demonstrated a well-defined multilobulated lesion within the right parotidectomy bed, showing low signal intensity on T1-weighted images and heterogeneous high signal intensity on T2-weighted sequences. Diffusion-weighted imaging revealed no true diffusion restriction, with elevated ADC values and an ADC ratio of 1.4 relative to the contralateral normal parotid gland. Considering the patient’s history, the recurrent nature of the tumor, the multifocal lesions sharing similar imaging characteristics and the absence of imaging features suggestive of carcinoma ex pleomorphic adenoma, metastasizing pleomorphic adenoma was considered the leading diagnostic hypothesis.

The patient underwent surgical excision of the parotid bed recurrence with cervical lymph node dissection, in addition to resection of the pterygoid and scalp lesions. Histopathological analysis demonstrated recurrent pleomorphic adenoma of chondromyxoid subtype composed of epithelial and myoepithelial components embedded within a myxochondroid stroma, without cytological atypia, necrosis or increased mitotic activity. Given the multifocal osseous involvement and the absence of neurological complications or pathological fracture, the bone lesions were managed conservatively with close clinical and radiological surveillance. No surgical stabilization was considered necessary.

Considering the indolent histological appearance of the lesions and the lack of evidence supporting adjuvant therapy in metastasizing pleomorphic adenoma, no postoperative radiotherapy or systemic therapy was administered.

The patient remained under regular multidisciplinary follow-up including clinical examination and imaging surveillance.

Discussion

Pleomorphic adenoma (PA) is the most common benign tumor of the salivary glands, with an incidence ranging from 2.4 to 4.9 per 100,000 persons per year [7]. Although generally indolent, PA carries a risk of malignant transformation into carcinoma ex pleomorphic adenoma, estimated at 1.5% within the first 5 years if untreated, increasing to 10% after 15 years and ranging from 0% to 23% in recurrent lesions [8]. Metastasizing pleomorphic adenoma (MPA) is a rare and paradoxical entity characterized by histologically benign pleomorphic adenoma with metastatic behavior. According to the 2023 World Health Organization (WHO) classification of head and neck tumors, it is defined as a lesion that is histologically and molecularly indistinguishable from a benign primary tumor, with no reliable histological or molecular predictors of metastasis [9]. Despite its benign microscopic appearance, MPA behaves in a clinically aggressive manner with delayed metastatic spread [5]. In large series, the mean age at diagnosis of MPA is 49.5 years [5].

The most frequent metastatic sites are bone (36.6%), lung (33.8%) and cervical lymph nodes (20.1%) [4]. Multiple metastatic sites are associated with a poorer prognosis [4,9]. Cutaneous and subcutaneous metastases are less frequent (14,3%) and often occur near prior surgical sites, supporting a role of local tumor dissemination [1,10,11]. In the present case, the presence of multiple metastatic sites reflects an uncommon but recognized aggressive pattern. In our case, metastases were identified 15 years after the initial diagnosis and 6 years after local recurrence. This is consistent with previously reported data, where McGarry and Knight described a mean latency period of 16 years for recurrence and 14.9 years for distant metastasis detection [4,12].

The absence of reliable histopathological predictors makes clinical history essential for risk stratification. The most consistently reported risk factors include local recurrence, incomplete surgical excision and intraoperative tumor spillage [4,8,13]. Several mechanisms have been proposed, including iatrogenic dissemination due to capsular rupture and tumor cell seeding during surgery with vascular or lymphatic implantation [4]. The long latency between primary tumor treatment and metastatic presentation—often spanning several years or even decades—supports these hypotheses [14]. Surgical approach is a key determinant of recurrence and metastatic risk. Simple enucleation is associated with high recurrence rates ranging from 8% to 45%, whereas this rate decreases to less than 5% after superficial parotidectomy and to approximately 0.4% following total parotidectomy [4,8,13,15,16]. Therefore, enucleation is no longer recommended [17].

Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) plays a central role in the evaluation of pleomorphic adenoma (PA), particularly in recurrent and multifocal disease, due to its superior soft-tissue contrast and functional imaging capabilities [6]. PA typically demonstrates low signal intensity on T1-weighted images and high signal intensity on T2-weighted sequences, reflecting its myxoid and chondroid stromal components [6]. Diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) usually shows high signal intensity on high b-value images, elevated apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) values and an ADC ratio >1.3 compared to the contralateral normal parotid gland [18,19]. These findings are consistent with the high-water content and loose stromal architecture of PA, in contrast to malignant salivary gland tumors, which typically exhibit restricted diffusion and lower ADC values [18]. Dynamic contrast-enhanced MRI further supports lesion characterization [20]. PA typically demonstrates progressive and gradual enhancement following gadolinium administration, corresponding to a type A time–signal intensity curve, characterized by a continuous increase in signal without early washout [21,22]. This pattern reflects low vascular permeability and abundant extracellular matrix [22]. These multiparametric MRI features are essential for differentiating recurrent PA from malignant transformation or carcinoma ex pleomorphic adenoma [23]. Malignant lesions commonly demonstrate lower ADC values (ADC ratio <1.3), early enhancement and washout <30% (type B or C enhancement curves), often associated with more aggressive imaging features [6,23].

Computed tomography remains complementary, particularly for detecting bone involvement, cervical lymphadenopathy and distant metastases. However, MRI remains superior for postoperative surveillance and for differentiating recurrent tumor from fibrosis, making it the modality of choice for long-term follow-up [24].

Management of MPA remains challenging due to its rarity and lack of standardized guidelines. Surgical excision with clear margins remains the mainstay of treatment for both primary and metastatic lesions whenever feasible [14]. The role of radiotherapy remains limited, with no clear evidence of survival benefit [5,17]. Given the long latency and risk of delayed metastasis, prolonged follow-up is strongly recommended, especially in patients with prior recurrence, incomplete excision or tumor spillage [4,5]. Although PA is classically benign, MPA highlights its unpredictable biological behavior, emphasizing the importance of radiological vigilance for early detection and optimal management.

Conclusion

Metastasizing pleomorphic adenoma is a rare entity characterized by benign histology despite metastatic potential. This case highlights the importance of adequate initial surgical management and long-term imaging surveillance. MRI plays a central role in lesion characterization and postoperative follow-up, particularly in patients with recurrent disease at risk for delayed metastatic spread.

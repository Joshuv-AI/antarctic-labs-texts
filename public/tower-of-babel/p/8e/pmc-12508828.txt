Study on the correlation and formation mechanism of flavor compounds and Maillard reaction products during the drying process of Litopenaeus vannamei
Authors: Wenwen Luo, Shixin Hua, Mengxue He, Jianan Sun
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractDrying is an essential step of extending the shelf life and altering eating qualities of Litopenaeus vannamei. However, during the drying of Litopenaeus vannamei, the formation mechanisms for flavor and Maillard reaction products (MRPs) remain inadequately explored. This study investigated the effects on physicochemical properties, flavor and MRPs following four drying treatments. Microwave drying (MD) demonstrated excellent color, drying rate and aesthetic features. Correlation analyses revealed that when pyrazines accumulated, levels of MRPs like total advanced glycation end products (AGEs) and 5-hydroxymethylfurfural (5-HMF) elevated. It was evident from using glucose-lysine simulation system that characteristic flavor components like 2-nonanone were preserved during MD without further reactions to produce pyrazines and aldehydes, as in air frying and roasted drying. Meanwhile, it prevented the formation of AGEs and 5-HMF. This study offered theoretical direction for the quality monitoring of Litopenaeus vannamei drying.Keywords: Litopenaeus vannamei; Drying; Flavor; Maillard reaction; Correlation analysis, Glucose-lysine simulation

Introduction

Litopenaeus vannamei (Penaeus vannamei) is an important aquaculture species that is cultivated and traded worldwide (Han et al., 2025), accounting for roughly 62.2 % of all shrimp yield (Hou et al., 2025). Litopenaeus vannamei is well-liked by consumers owing to its rich nutrient content. Under the action of bacteria and enzymes in its body, it is particularly vulnerable to autolysis and corruption. It is inappropriate for long-term preservation (Li, Wang, Zeng, et al., 2024). The most commonly utilized technique in commercial production to extend the shelf life of prawns, aside from frozen storage, is drying (Shi, Xiao, Yin, et al., 2024). Dehydration enhances the prawns' organoleptic qualities and creates their distinct flavor in addition to blocking the activity of germs and enzymes (Nummer et al., 2004).

Nowadays, traditional drying methods like sunlight drying, hot-air drying, roasted drying, etc. are extensively employed in industrial production (Dinçer, 2023). Although simple to operate, sunlight drying is highly dependent on the weather and cannot be improved in terms of hygiene. Moreover, its low productivity limits its application. In recent years, a few innovative methods, namely microwave drying, air frying and vacuum freeze drying have been applied to dry meat (Fan et al., 2024). The final product's characteristics, including color, texture, and flavor, are significantly influenced by the drying process. Duppeti, Manjabhatta, et al. (2022) examined the aroma profile of Litopenaeus vannamei under various processing conditions such as raw, boiled, hot-air drying, microwave drying and baking, and discovered that baked prawns had the highest concentration of aroma-active compounds, while microwave drying had the highest sum of odor activity values. Air frying is an emerging high-temperature, dry-heat processing technique that can be utilized as an alternative to deep-frying. One study found that scallops treated with air frying (160 °C) contained more soluble peptides that could be digested by the stomach and gastrointestinal tract compared to baking-treated one (Wang, Wu, et al., 2023). Additionally, drying also intensifies the Maillard reaction, resulting in the production of unwanted substances like melanoidin and AGEs. These compounds challenge the fulfillment of a healthy diet's criteria (Tanaka et al., 1994). During heating, the formation of harmful Maillard reaction products (MRPs) is tightly associated with the generation of flavor compounds. Shi, Gao, Liu, et al. (2024) confirmed through correlation analysis that aldehydes and alcohols in roasted pork are negatively correlated with Nε-carboxyethyl lysine (CEL), Nε-carboxymethyl lysine (CML), and methylglyoxal hydroimidazolone (MG-H1). The reaction process might be regulated by a variety of factors, including moisture content, heating method, and the degree of oxidation of proteins and fats during the process (Diez-Simon et al., 2019). Although an increasing number of studies have focused on controlling the Maillard reaction during meat processing. Comprehensive studies that integrate the effects of drying treatments on flavor and the formation of harmful compounds of Maillard reaction in seafood, particularly in Litopenaeus vannamei, are still lacking. This limits the research on the control and improvement of flavor and quality in the dried processing of Litopenaeus vannamei.

As shrimp is a complicated reactive system, it is affected by various parameters in drying process. It is difficult to conduct direct studies on the drying process of shrimp since it cannot be quantitatively characterized or anticipated with precision. Thus, using a simulation system to investigate the dynamic correlation mechanism and interaction between flavor, precursor chemicals, and the Maillard reaction is a useful technique (Liu et al., 2025; Taş & Gökmen, 2017). There have been studies using aqueous model systems of amino acids and reducing sugars to examine the process of Maillard reaction and the mechanism of hazardous compound formation (Li et al., 2025). Amino acids and reducing sugars are ubiquitous in actual shrimp systems (Quan et al., 2020), and moderately amplifying their concentrations facilitates the assessment of changes in intermediates and final products. One of the commonly used models for examining the phases and kinetics of the Maillard reaction with high reactivity is glucose-lysine model system (Ma et al., 2017). Studies have shown that the type of sugar has little effect on glyoxal, an important intermediate product, in amino acid-reducing sugar simulation systems (Chen & Kitts, 2011). Meanwhile, both glucose and lysine exhibit strong reactivity. The Maillard reaction rates of common reducing sugars are: glucose > fructose > sucrose. Among these, glucose is not only highly reactive, but also readily available and inexpensive (Gao et al., 2024). Lysine possesses two active sites (Nα- and Nε-amino groups), which could react with the carbonyl functional groups of reducing sugars (Li, Wang, Liu, & You, 2024).

Prior studies have concentrated on individual aspects of the flavor of dried shrimp items and traditional drying processes. There are insufficient studies on the thorough assessment and analysis between physicochemical properties, aroma and Maillard reaction products during the drying process of Litopenaeus vannamei, as well as their interactions and linkages. Therefore, the objectives of this study were (1) to conduct a comprehensive analysis of physicochemical properties, aroma compounds, and the degree of Maillard reaction of dried shrimp with different drying treatments of hot-air drying (HD), roasted drying (RD), microwave drying (MD), and air frying (AF); and (2) to explore the association and formation mechanism between key aroma compounds and Maillard reaction products, through correlation analysis (Pearson correlation and PLS-R analysis) and the glucose-lysine (Glc-Lys) simulation system. This work provided theoretical guidance and quality control for the production of superior Litopenaeus vannamei products.

Materials and methods

Materials and reagents

Litopenaeus vannamei were purchased from Shangcheng Farmers' Market (Qingdao, China). CML and CEL standards (99.9 %) were acquired from Yuanye Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). 2-Methyl-3-heptanone (99 %) and n-alkanes (C7-C40) were purchased from Aladdin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Glyoxal solution (40 wt% in water), glucose oxidase (GOD, 10 kU/32.7 mg) and peroxidase (POD, 233–300 U/mg solid) were purchased from Solarbio Science & Technology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). 5-Hydroxymethylfurfural (5-HMF, >99 %) was obtained from Macklin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Chemicals such as L-(+)-lysine (Lys), D (+)-glucose (Glc), NaCl and NaBH4 were of analytical or chemical purity, as required.

Sample preparation

A 1.5 % salt brine (shrimp-to-water mass ratio was 1:3) at 100 °C was used to submerge Litopenaeus vannamei until reddish in color, which were then drained. Drying was considered to be completed when the moisture content was less than 20 % ± 2 % (w.b.) (Sun et al., 2022).

(1)Hot-air drying (HD): A hot-air drying oven was used to dry the samples at 80 °C and 1.0 m/s of air velocity, with an air humidity of 60 %.

Hot-air drying (HD): A hot-air drying oven was used to dry the samples at 80 °C and 1.0 m/s of air velocity, with an air humidity of 60 %.

(2)Roasted drying (RD): Samples were raked in a preheated oven at a temperature of 200 °C.

Roasted drying (RD): Samples were raked in a preheated oven at a temperature of 200 °C.

(3)Microwave drying (MD): A steady 539 W microwave power output was used during the drying.

Microwave drying (MD): A steady 539 W microwave power output was used during the drying.

(4)Air frying (AF): The air fryer was preheated at 150 °C for 5 min. The treated prawns were fried at 150 °C, which were turned over every 10 min.

Air frying (AF): The air fryer was preheated at 150 °C for 5 min. The treated prawns were fried at 150 °C, which were turned over every 10 min.

The sensory analysis team consisted of 10 members (the ratio of men to women was 1:1, ages 20 to 30). The evaluation was based on GB/T 39625-2020 (2020). Each group member had accumulated extensive experience in food sensory description and had undergone training as described by Xu et al. (2023). Prior to sensory evaluation, panelists were prohibited from eating for at least 1 h. All sensory experiments were conducted in a sensory room under artificial lighting at a room temperature and humidity of 60 %. All samples were placed in odorless plastic cups with random codes. Each sample was chewed in the mouth for at least three seconds and sniffed for at least five seconds, and group members were required to rest for 2 min between each sample and rinse their mouths with ultra-pure water between each sample. The samples were rated for taste, odor, condition and color using a sensory evaluation scale (GB/T 39501-2020, 2020). The sensory evaluation criteria for each section were scored on a 25-point scale: 21–25, excellent quality; 15–20, basically excellent; 10–14, barely acceptable; 0–9, very poor.

Thiobarbituric acid-reactive substance (TBARS)

Malondialdehyde (MDA) content assay kit that was obtained from Solarbio Science & Technology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China) was used to detect TBARS (Fan et al., 2014).

HS-GC-IMS analysis

A 20 mL headspace bottle containing 2.00 g of shrimp was stirred at 60 °C (500 rpm, 20 min). 500 μL of headspace was then injected into a heated syringe (80 °C) (Bai et al., 2022). Pre-separation was finished with a FS-SE-15-CB capillary column (15 m × 0.53 mm) at 40 °C in GC-IMS (FlavourSpecR, G.A.S, Germany).

SPME-GC–MS analysis

Exactly 1.00 g of meat was weighed and transferred to a 20 mL headspace bottle, to which 3 mL of saturated sodium chloride solution was added. Subsequently, 2-methyl-3-heptanone was added as the internal standard. After placing in a thermostatic bath, the pretreated SPME fiber (50/30 μm DVB/CAR/PDMS solid-phase microextraction) was inserted and extracted at 60 °C for 30 min with magnetic stirring of 500 rpm. After completion of the incubation, SPME was desorbed by 8890-5977B GC–MS system (Agilent Technology Inc., USA) equipped with an HP-5MS (30 m × 0.25 mm, 0.25 μm, 40–260 °C/270 °C) to quantify volatile compounds (250 °C, 5 min). After being kept at 40 °C for five minutes, the oven temperature was raised to 100 °C at 5 °C/min, 180 °C at 3 °C/min, and then 240 °C at 5 °C/min for five minutes (Duppeti, Kempaiah, & Manjabhatta, 2022). Volatile compounds were analyzed using MSD Chemstation data analysis F.01.03 software. The identification of aromatic compounds was based on retention index (RI) and mass spectra (MS) compared with the NIST 23 library.

Browning and 5-HMF

Referring to the methods of Bai et al. (2023) and Zhang et al. (2021), absorbance values at 294 nm and 420 nm were detected to indicate the end product melanin and intermediate products, respectively.

The determination of 5-HMF was based on the method described by Aykın et al. (2016). The shrimp meat (1 g) was mixed with 5 mL of oxalic acid (0.15 M). After homogenization, the mixture was shaken for 30 min (4 °C). After adding 40 % trichloroacetic acid (3 mL), the resulting solution was then placed for 10 min, followed by centrifugation at 5000 rpm (15 min, 4 °C). The mixture that contained 4 mL of the supernatant and 1 mL of 2-thiobarbituric acid (0.05 M) was incubated in a water bath at 40 °C for 30 min. After cooling to room temperature, the absorbance was measured at 443 nm. The results were expressed in μg/g shrimp.

Fluorescent AGEs determination and pentosidine

Pentosidine and fluorescent AGEs were measured by the method of Bai et al. (2023). The samples were homogenized for 1 min by adding 4 mL of NaCl (1.6 M) and 5 mL of PBS (0.2 M, pH 6.7) to 1 g of shrimp and stabilized for at least 60 min. The supernatant was obtained by centrifugation (5000 rpm, 10 min) and diluted thirty times with PBS (0.2 M, pH 8.0) following stabilization. The fluorescence intensity of fluorescent AGEs and pentosidine was measured at the excitation wavelengths of 370 nm and 335 nm, respectively. The width of incision was 5, 5 nm.

Qualitative and quantitative assessment of CML/CEL

The analysis was performed according to the methodology of Yao et al. (2016) with minor modifications. Dried shrimp (100 mg) was reduced with NaBH4 in 2 mL borate buffer (pH = 9.2, 0.2 M) and 0.4 mL sodium borohydride (2 M) dissolved in NaOH (0.1 M) at 4 °C for 8 h. The oils in the samples were removed utilizing 4 mL of trichloromethane-methanol (2:1, v:v) solution, which also precipitated the proteins. Centrifugation (5000 rpm, 10 min) was performed to remove the supernatant. The precipitate was hydrolyzed by adding 4 mL HCl (6 M, 110 °C) for 1 d. The dried sample was dissolved in 20 mL of distilled water. The impurities in the MCX filter were successively removed with deionized water (3 mL) and methanol (3 mL). Target analytes retained in the filter cartridge were eluted with methanol solution containing 5 % ammonia, which was collected and dried again with nitrogen. The dried sample was dissolved in 4 mL of methanol-water (80:20, v:v) and filtered through a 0.22 μm filter.

Then 10 μL of the filtrate was injected into an LC-MS/MS system (QTRAP 6500+, SCIEX) equipped with a Waters Acquity UPLC BEH C18 column (50 mm × 2.1 mm, 1.7 μm) and connected to a triple quadrupole mass spectrometer. The electrospray ion source (ESI) was operated in positive ion scanning mode, with a capillary voltage of 5.5 kV and a dryer temperature of 350 °C. The mobile phase rate was set at 0.2 mL/min. The mobile phases A and B were acetonitrile and water (0.1 % formic acid), respectively, with a desolvation gas flow rate of 500 L/h and a cone gas flow rate of 50 L/h. The results were analyzed using the MassHunter software supplied with the system, and the CML and CEL contents were expressed as μg/g of sample (Li et al., 2025).

Lysine-glucose (Lys-Glc) Maillard reaction (MR) simulation system

Preparation of the simulation system

MR simulation solution was prepared by dissolving 0.1 M Lys and 0.1 M Glc in PBS (0.1 M, pH 7.0) and then diluted to 1 L. Next, 2 mL of the MR simulation solution was injected into shrimp. The prawns were dried at a constant microwave power output of 539 W. Samples were collected and kept in a refrigerator at −20 °C until measurement, in triplicate for each set of samples. Reactions were halted when the moisture content reached 60 % ± 2 % (w.b.), 40 % ± 2 % (w.b.), and 20 % ± 2 % (w.b.).

Lys and Glc analysis

After homogenizing the extract with water in 1:9 (w/v), it was agitated for two hours at 4 °C and centrifuged for 10 min at 8000 rpm. The supernatant was diluted 15-fold, mixed with 2 mL of OPA reagent and allowed to react at room temperature for 2 min. The absorbance at 340 nm was measured, and Lys content was calculated from the Lys standard curve (Huang et al., 2022).

Glc was determined using the enzyme-colorimetric method in GB/T 16285-2008 (2008). K₄Fe(CN)₆·3H₂O (0.085 M, 10 mL), ZnSO₄ (0.25 M, 10 mL), and NaOH (0.1 M, 12.5 mL) were combined with 5 g of meat and homogenized at 10,000 rpm. After adding water until the final volume reached 500 mL, the mixture was filtered. An enzyme solution (3 mL, containing GOD 400 U and POD 1000 U) was combined with the filtrate (2 mL). The absorbance was measured at 505 nm following 40 min of incubation at 36 °C in a water bath.

Determination of glyoxal (GO)

With minor modifications based on the method of Huang et al. (2022). Briefly, exactly 0.1 g of shrimp was weighed and added to 5 times volume of sodium acetate solution (15 g/L) and 10 times volume of hydroxylamine hydrochloride solution (2 g/L). Following homogenization, a water bath was performed at 50 °C for 20 min. The solution was transferred to a 50 mL volumetric flask and diluted with PBS (0.2 M, pH 8.0). The absorbance of the solution was determined at 233 nm after centrifugation (9000 rpm, 10 min).

Statistical analysis

Except where indicated, all experiment was carried out in triplicate. Each experimental group included at least n = 3 independent shrimp (i.e., from different individuals). SPSS 16.0 software was used to conduct statistical analyses (SPSS Inc.), and results were presented as mean ± standard deviation. Principal component analysis (PCA) and Pearson correlation analysis were performed by MetaboAnalyst 6.0. Partial least squares regression (PLS-R) analysis and fingerprints were performed using Unscrambler software (version 9.7, CAMO Software, Oslo, Norway) and VOCal 0.4.03 respectively, whereas Origin 2021 produced the other visualizations.

Results and discussion

Drying kinetics

Fig. 1a–d displayed drying kinetics and drying rate curves for different drying procedures of Litopenaeus vannamei. The drying times for HD, RD, MD, and AF were approximately 4 h, 48 min, 7 min, and 33 min, respectively (Fig. 1a, b). It could be found from Fig. 1c, d that there was no constant drying rate (DR) for the four treatments of Litopenaeus vannamei. The rapid moisture depletion during initial drying induced partial barrier formation within meat tissue. This structural alteration effectively restricted migration pathways of moisture (Kamiloğlu et al., 2023). At incipient phase of drying, elevated temperature of shrimp flesh promoted the migration of free water in its body, resulting in a rapid decrease in moisture (Speckhahn et al., 2010). Consistent with Pan et al. (2024), surface hardening due to the reduction of free water inhibited the escape of internal moisture. This progressively lowered drying rate and led the drying towards the stage of drying blockage. The heat generated by the heating tubes was transferred from the surface inwards by RD (200 °C) and AF (150 °C) using air as the heat transfer medium. The higher temperatures facilitated faster water evaporation, significantly shortening the drying time. In addition, hot airflow inside the air fryer accelerated surface moisture removal at lower temperatures, simultaneously yielding a texture similar to deep-frying (Wang, Wu, et al., 2023). Compared to HD, RD and AF, MD utilized electromagnetic waves to penetrate directly into the foodstuff, causing the polar water molecules to orientate and oscillate rapidly to generate volumetric heat (Elmas et al., 2020). The prawns were heated uniformly inside and outside, which accelerated the escape rate of water molecules (DR > 7.5 g/g min−1) and significantly shortened the drying time of prawns (Kipcak & İsmail, 2021; Liang et al., 2023).

Analysis of color and sensory evaluation

Color was an important indicator of the quality grade of dried shrimp, which impacted the acceptability by customers of product. The variations in color of dried shrimps under different drying methods were shown in Fig. 1e. The overall color difference values (ΔE) of dried samples exceeded 27 compared to fresh ones. It suggested that there was a considerable color difference (Pan et al., 2024), and the unique color of the product was formed after drying. MD and HD exhibited significantly higher L* values than RD and AF (p < 0.05). It might be attributed to the formation of burnt-brown hazardous compounds on the surface of shrimp caused by high temperatures of RD and AF, which made the shrimp darker in color. The change in a* value was primarily related to the amount of free astaxanthin in Litopenaeus vannamei (Lin et al., 2022). Litopenaeus vannamei underwent heat treatment, which denatured and destroyed the protein and separated astaxanthin from it, giving the shrimp an orange-red hue (Liang et al., 2022). However, the prolonged drying time of HD, RD and AF resulted in the loss of free astaxanthin due to thermal degradation or oxidative decomposition (Martínez-Delgado et al., 2017). Consequently, a* values for RD, AF, and HD were 25.29 %, 23.75 %, and 18.17 % lower, respectively, than that of MD (Fig. 1e). The b* values were mainly affected by the oxidation of proteins and lipids of Litopenaeus vannamei during the heat process. Consistent with the findings of Duppeti, Manjabhatta, et al. (2022), the b* value of MD was significantly larger (p < 0.05), and the change in b* value of HD was comparatively small. Ultimately, MD gave the shrimp a brighter, red color, thereby increasing the consumer's desire to purchase.

The sensory evaluation results of color, odor, condition, taste and overall preference of Litopenaeus vannamei were shown in Fig. 1f. MD and HD received the highest scores in all sensory categories, indicating that panelists widely recognized the high quality of these products. Both MD and HD had the brightest red color, according to the findings of sensory evaluation. MD was slightly firm in meat, but with a flat and crispy skin, sweet and resilient meat, that did not taste fishy. AF and RD had a burnt brown exterior, with a bitter shell. Additionally, the flesh was slightly tough, with a more pronounced charred taste. This was in line with the results of the aforementioned color properties.

TBARS

TBARS were one of the important markers of lipid oxidation (Li et al., 2020). Moderate lipid oxidation could confer good flavor properties to meat products, such as the formation of benzaldehyde and acetaldehyde with low sensory detection thresholds (Wang, Wang, et al., 2023). The TBARS values of MD, RD, and AF showed a significant increase (p < 0.05) compared to HD, with increases of 144.25 %, 76.13 %, and 22.03 %, respectively (Fig. 1g), which corresponded to the results of the b* value analysis. During the drying process, the migration of water and the release of pro-oxidant substances caused damage to the cell membranes of muscle cells. In this case, free metal ions were more likely to attack the cells and TBARS would eventually accumulate in the shrimp (Song et al., 2001). High temperatures and microwave radiation triggered the generation of free radicals, which accelerated the process of attacking unsaturated fatty acyl chains in lipids, especially the polyunsaturated chains (PUFA). It generated a range of secondary oxidants (Zhao et al., 2022) and caused varying degrees of damage to muscle structure. MD relied on electromagnetic fields, and the oxidation of polyunsaturated fatty acids was facilitated by the interaction of microwaves with lipids during the drying. The uniform volumetric heating of MD resulted in the highest degree of lipid oxidation (221.92 ± 31.50 nmol/g) of dried meat compared to the drying methods using air as a heat transfer medium (Fig. 1g), which was in agreement with the results obtained by Hur et al. (2014).

HS-GC-IMS

As an emerging non-targeted flavor compound analysis and detection method for volatile components in food products, HS-GC-IMS was widely applied (Wang et al., 2024). According to the PCA score plot (Fig. 2a), HD and MD were closest to each other and overlapped, indicating that samples were closest in terms of flavor substance composition. The different drying methods resulted in drastic alterations in the flavor of the shrimp. To observe the changes more intuitively, the ion mobility curves of the other three groups were normalized using HD as a reference (Fig. 2b). It was discovered that RD and AF induced the formation of a greater variety of flavor components in dried shrimp in comparison to HD. The signals of most flavor substances were stronger than those of the other categories, followed by MD.

Principal component analysis (PCA) (a), topographic plots (b), and fingerprints (c) of volatile compounds detected by HS-GC-IMS of dried shrimp. HD: hot-air drying; RD: roasted drying; MD: microwave drying; AF: Air frying.

Significant flavor variations across samples were noted, and a total of 29 volatile compounds were identified. Ketones (6), aldehydes (3), alcohols (4), esters (8), pyrazines (3) and other compounds (5) were among the seven categories into which they were classified based on their chemical structures and properties (Fig. 2c). Cooking-induced oxidative and Maillard reactions played an important role in flavor formation. Propyl butyrate, 2-pentanol, 2,6-dimethylpyrazine, isoamyl isobutyrate, cyclohexanone, and 1-propanol-M were prevalent in four dried samples, conferring aromas which were reminiscent of fruits like peaches, pears, and apricots, and odors of coffee or sautéed peanuts on meat.

The degree of contribution of each flavor substance to dried products was closely related to their flavor thresholds. Pertinent studies had shown that alcohols (primarily from degradation of polyunsaturated fatty acids), and aldehydes (branched chain aldehydes mainly from oxidative deamination and decarboxylation of amino acids of the Strecker's degradation pathway) had low flavor thresholds. The odor detection thresholds of aldehydes and alcohols were typically 0.1–103 ppb and 1–104 ppb respectively, playing a crucial role in improving the aroma, whereas alkanes could reach 103–105 ppb (Ren et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2022). The fruity and sweet flavors of ketones and esters, and the roasted and nutty notes of pyrazines gave dried shrimps special scent (Zhang et al., 2024). Due to the limitations of HS-GC-IMS in terms of database and quantitative methodologies, SPME-GC–MS was utilized to accurately perform qualitative and quantitative assessments of the volatiles (Wang et al., 2024).

SPME-GC–MS analysis

Volatile flavor compounds in shrimp dried by different methods were analyzed using SPME-GC–MS. As seen in Fig. 3c, a total of 77 volatile compounds, including 10 alcohols, 11 aldehydes, 5 esters, 9 ketones, 23 heterocyclic compounds, 5 acids and 14 other compounds, were identified in the four dried samples. The contribution of PC1 was 94.6 %, PC2 was 4.42 %, and the total contribution was 99.02 %, which indicated that the measurements were able to accurately reflect the overall flavor profile (Fig. 3a). Litopenaeus vannamei developed comparable flavor profiles as a result of the various drying techniques (Fig. 3a, b). The greatest quantity of volatile chemicals was detected in RD (69), followed by AF (65), MD (59), the latter being closest to HD in terms of the amount and type of flavoring substances (51).

Principal component analysis (PCA) (a), Venn diagram (b), heat map (c) and chord diagram (d) of volatile compounds detected by SPME-GC–MS of dried shrimp. HD: hot-air drying; RD: roasted drying; MD: microwave drying; AF: Air frying.

OAV was used to identify the contribution of key flavor components to the overall scent of shrimp. When OAV >1, they were considered to contribute to the flavor of the food (Wu et al., 2014) (Table 1). Aldehydes, which were mainly produced by the thermal breakdown of reducing sugars and amino acids and the oxidative breakdown of lipids, typically had low odor thresholds and significantly impacted the aroma of dried shrimp (Wang et al., 2022). It was found that 3-methylbutanal, octanal, nonanal and decanal were important compounds that made up the fragrance profile of Litopenaeus vannamei. Octanal, nonanal, and decanal as medium-length (C6-C12) straight-chain saturated aldehydes derived predominantly from the degradation of unsaturated fatty acids exhibited a pleasant fatty aroma and impart a meaty flavor to shrimp (Shi et al., 2025). 3-Methylbutanal (chocolate, coffee, malty) was closely related to oxidative deamination and decarboxylation reactions in the Strecker degradation of leucine (Song et al., 2019). The presence of its side chain conferred a distinctive flavor on RD and AF, which was consistent with the results of Shi, Xiao, Zhang, et al. (2024) and Liang et al. (2022).

Ketones had a pleasant aroma typically presenting fruity and sweet scents. Only 2-nonanone (a kind of methyl ketone) was found to contribute to the significant and distinctive creamy, fruity and chocolaty flavors of the dried shrimp. 2-Nonanone was exclusively detected in MD (OAV = 7.16). Its unique formation in MD may be due to microwave energy accelerating the oxidation and breakdown of unsaturated fatty acids (Ozkara et al., 2019). Meanwhile, the AF treatment induced shrimp to form more 1-octen-3-ol (OAV = 1.03), an unsaturated alcohol that had a powerful mushroom scent and was created when linoleic acid was broken down (Benet et al., 2015).

Pyrazines were key aroma compounds in dried shrimp, formed primarily via the Maillard reaction and subsequent secondary reactions involving Strecker degradation products (Zheng et al., 2022). Pyrazine structures were typically heterocycles containing two nitrogen atoms, and the presence of substituents on the carbon ring significantly affected their odor and threshold by altering molecular polarity, spatial configuration, and electron distribution (Ma et al., 2019). Among these, alkyl pyrazines presented pleasant roasted and nutty aromas which had an extremely low odor threshold (often at the ppb or even ppt level) (Cherniienko et al., 2022). It was found that 2,5-dimethylpyrazine, 3-ethyl-2,5-dimethylpyrazine, 2-ethyl-3,5-dimethylpyrazine and 2-ethyl-5-methylpyrazine and trimethylpyrazine were shown to have a substantial impact on the overall olfactory characteristics, imparting a strong roasted and smoky flavor to the shrimp. 2,5-Dimethylpyrazine was a product of the involvement of glycine in the Maillard process (Shipar, 2006). In comparison, hydroxyl and basic amino acids exhibited high reactivity in pyrazine formation. For instance, lysine accelerated the formation of pyrazines at a high level through deamination (Gao et al., 2024). Both AF (total OAV: 25.31–274.30) and RD (OAV: 76.97–755.26) significantly increased pyrazines content (Table 1), which in turn enhanced the aroma of the product, with a concomitant increase in bitterness. This outcome supported that high temperatures promoted the Maillard reaction (Fig. 3d). The lower pyrazine levels in MD were consistent with the findings of Smith & Barringer (Smith & Barringer, 2014).

In summary, the different drying treatments resulted in drastic alterations in the flavor of Litopenaeus vannamei. RD and AF provided the shrimp a deeper roasted flavor compared to HD, while MD developed its unique fruity flavor and was the most similar to that of HD. In this investigation, 12 volatile compounds (OAV > 1) were identified as characteristic flavor substances of dried shrimp.

Assessment of Maillard reaction

Browning, which was intimately linked to the Maillard process, was an important element influencing the quality of dried shrimp. The absorbance values at 294 nm (A294nm) and 420 nm (A420nm) represented the content of the intermediates and final products of the Maillard reaction, respectively (Geng et al., 2017; Jiang et al., 2023). When compared to HD, the A294 nm of MD, AF, and RD increased by 11.52 %, 31.01 %, and 32.88 %, respectively, while the A420nm increased by 15.72 %, 30.59 %, and 35.62 %, as seen in Fig. 4a.

HD: hot-air drying; RD: roasted drying; MD: microwave drying; AF: Air frying. The different letters indicate significant differences (p < 0.05) between different drying treatments.

5-HMF was a potentially hazardous compound that could cause gene mutations and cancer (Bogdanowicz et al., 2020). The effects of different drying methods on 5-HMF concentration of dried shrimp were shown in Fig. 4b. The 5-HMF content was significantly higher in AF (27.68 ± 0.38 μg/g) and RD (24.67 ± 0.32 μg/g) than in HD (10.06 ± 0.38 μg/g) and MD (12.40 ± 0.43 μg/g) (Fig. 4b). 5-HMF was extremely sensitive to time and temperature, as Liu et al. (2024) discovered. Its content rose dramatically as temperature and duration increased, which accounted for why AF and RD were more conducive to its formation. Compared to AF, the 5-HMF concentration in RD was considerably lower by 10.88 % (p < 0.05). This might be as a result of 5-HMF reacting more with nitrogen-containing chemicals and polymerizing due to the increased reaction time and temperature in RD, producing nitrogen-containing polymers like melanoidins (Bouzalakou-Butel et al., 2018). Melanoidins were responsible for the brown coloration of food surfaces.

AGEs could be classified into two categories: fluorescent products and non-fluorescent products (Mendes et al., 2025). An essential component of fluorescent AGEs, pentosidine was primarily produced by the interaction of pentose with amino acid residues (Ashraf et al., 2015). Compared with RD and AF, less pentosidine and total fluorescent AGEs were generated during MD (Fig. 4c), which was consistent with the results of Mei et al. (2025).

CML and CEL were the representative compounds of non-fluorescent AGEs (Shi, Gao, Liu, et al., 2024). The contents of CML and CEL differed significantly among drying treatments (Fig. 4d), although the trend for total content was consistent with that of browning, fluorescent AGEs, and pentosidine. MD (34.75 ± 0.85 mg/kg) showed substantially higher (p < 0.05) CML content than the other three drying methods, followed by AF (27.00 ± 0.25 mg/kg) and RD (25.53 ± 0.17 mg/kg), while HD (23.35 ± 0.72 mg/kg) had the lowest content (Fig. 4d). Hu et al. (2023) discovered that dicarbonyl compounds generated during lipid peroxidation were the potential source of CML when exploring the effect of traditional frying techniques on AGEs in grass carp fillets. Compared to HD (15.80 ± 0.12 mg/kg), the CEL content in shrimp from the other three drying treatments significantly increased (p < 0.05). The tendency of proteins and lipids to oxidize when heated (high temperature and high microwave radiation) could be the cause of the increase in CEL. The active amino acids were revealed by protein unfolding and took part in the Maillard process, which produced AGEs. The lowest CEL content was found in MD (21.55 ± 0.07 mg/kg), which may be due to the fact that microwave heating reduced protein decomposition at high heating rates (Wang, Chen, et al., 2023). It was followed by AF (33.70 ± 0.28 mg/kg) while RD (43.07 ± 0.12 mg/kg) had the highest content. Unlike mild heating temperature which was more favorable for the creation of CML, high temperature accelerated the development of CEL (Yu et al., 2016). Therefore, CEL content of RD was higher than that of AF. Yet, research on how protein oxidation affected the production of AGEs, the Maillard reaction, and the binding of proteins to CML and CEL had not been well documented (Zhu et al., 2020).

Correlation analysis

Compared to fresh shrimp, the drying process significantly altered the texture, color, fat oxidation, flavor, and degree of Maillard reaction of shrimp. And there were notable correlations between various indicators. Pearson correlation and PLS-R analysis were combined and applied to the correlation analysis of drying indicators. As shown in Fig. 5b, most variables were located between the two ellipses (R2 = 50 % ∼ 100 %), and PC1 and PC2 explained 61 % and 31 % of the cross-validation variance, respectively which indicated that the model had good fit and high reliability (Xia et al., 2021). The results of the Pearson correlation and PLS-R analyses were highly consistent. CEL, browning intensity, 5-HMF, pentosidine, fluorescent AGEs, and pyrazines were located in the first quadrant. TBARS, L*, b*, a*, 2-nonanone were significantly positively correlated with changes in CML content (p < 0.01). It was probably because the oxidative degradation of lipids during drying facilitated the production of CML. Changes in CEL, browning intensity, 5-HMF, pentosidine and fluorescent AGEs were positively correlated with pyrazine analogues including 2-ethyl-5-methylpyrazine, 2,5-dimethylpyrazine, and 2-ethyl-3,5-dimethylpyrazine, but were highly negatively correlated with L*, and nonanal (p < 0.01). Meanwhile, CEL, browning intensity, 5-HMF, pentosidine, and fluorescent AGEs all showed significant positive correlations with each other, as did the pyrazines (p < 0.01). The formation of pyrazines continued until the middle to late stages of the Maillard reaction. Similarly to MRPs, it increased with prolonged heating time and rising temperature (Yu et al., 2017). However, the specific intermediate reaction links remained unclear and required confirmation using isotope labelling methods to validate the proposed reaction pathways. Furthermore, there was a negative correlation between the concentration of CEL and 1-octen-3-ol, 2-nonanone, and octanal. Under aerobic circumstances, fatty acids might thermally decompose to generate hydroperoxides, aldehydes, alcohols, and ketones, which could then react to form pyrazines (Tamura et al., 2022) (Fig. 7). For example, alkyl pyrazines (such as 2-ethyl-5-methylpyrazine) could be obtained by deamination condensation of two α-aminoketones from dicarbonyl compounds with free amino acids (Ramírez et al., 2004). Research had demonstrated the negative association between the L* value and the content of pyrazines, which might be due to the intervention of the Maillard browning process (Knerr et al., 2001). Therefore, during the drying process, it was necessary to avoid excessive baking and browning of color to control the Maillard reaction when enhancing the flavor of the dried shrimp.

MD, a more practical and excellent drying treatment, was chosen in this study to explore the mechanism underlying the association between flavor and Maillard reaction process, taking into account the drying efficiency, sensory outcomes, and the content of the detrimental products of the Maillard reaction.

Glc-Lys Maillard reaction simulation system analysis

Precursors content (Glc, Lys, and GO)

The interaction between the Glc-Lys simulation system and the shrimp throughout the microwave drying process resulted in rather complex modifications of precursor compounds of Litopenaeus vannamei, in contrast to the isolated Glc-Lys Maillard reaction model. It was evident from Fig. 6a, b that at the beginning of the reaction, both Glc and Lys dramatically dropped. While Glc in MR stabilized at a low level, it was nearly depleted in the control group (PS) as the reaction progressed (Glc < 0.056 %). Aldol interactions between Glc and other precursors, including amino acids, generated intermediates such as the Amadori product (Zhang, Liu, et al., 2025). Glucose consumption in MR during the phase of acute glucose depletion, i.e., when the moisture content exceeded 60 %, was noticeably increased by 22.19 % compared to PS (p < 0.05). Lys displayed a rising trend in the late stage of the reaction (Fig. 6b). This occurred because high microwave energy induced hydrolytic denaturation and decomposition of proteins into short peptides and amino acids as the moisture content decreased (Shi, Xiao, Yin, et al., 2024). Yet, it was noteworthy that the Lys content of MR (33.78 ± 0.70 mg/g) was significantly lower compared to PS (39.86 ± 0.63 mg/g), when shrimp were dried to a moisture level below 20 %. This might be attributed to the formation of other products from Lys with dicarbonyl compounds and intermediates (Fig. 7). Combined with the modification of Glc, it was discovered that when the moisture content was less than 60 %, the Glc-Lys reaction was no longer dominant (Hu et al., 2024).

Shrimp that had been microwave-heated had much less GO than raw shrimp (p < 0.05), as seen in Fig. 6c. The pace at which GO reacted with the free amino group and the rate at which it formed controlled the change in GO during drying (Li et al., 2023). In accordance with the findings reported by Sun et al. (2024), Glc served as a precursor to GO, and its inclusion in a simulated system led to a minor rise in GO (Chen & Kitts, 2011). The GO content of MR group (0.92 ± 0.0047 mg/g) was substantially lower than that of PS (1.02 ± 0.011 mg/g, p < 0.05) when the moisture content was less than 20 %. Together with the simultaneous drop in Lys, it demonstrated that amino acids were formed in significant amounts during dry heating and that they reacted violently with GO in the later stages of the reaction (Fig. 7).

Analysis of 5-HMF and fluorescent AGEs

A negative correlation was observed between moisture content and 5-HMF accumulation during the microwave drying process (Fig. 6d). When the moisture content was below 40 %, the 5-HMF in MR was significantly higher than that in the PS group (p < 0.05, Fig. 6d). It might be because the presence of Glc and free amino acids accelerated its formation under high temperature conditions (Xiong et al., 2024). 5-HMF was an intermediate in caramelization and the Maillard reaction, directly affecting the formation of subsequent flavor compounds and AGEs (Abrantes et al., 2022).

AGEs were mainly generated in the late stages of the reaction, both in PS and MR, as shown in Fig. 6e. This might be due to the fact that the condensation of Glc with amino compounds in the early stages of the Maillard reaction was dominated by the creation of intermediates such as Amadori rearrangement products (Taş & Gökmen, 2017). Liu et al. (2025) reported that GO exhibited higher reactivity than Glc under heating conditions. An essential pathway for the creation of GO in Litopenaeus vannamei analogue system was the isomerization of the Amadori product followed by cleavage (Roldan et al., 2015). Due to the addition of Glc-Lys to MR, more Glc could be implicated in the pre-thermal reaction, resulting in the production of additional intermediates such as Amadori rearrangement products. Traces of Glc were simultaneously converted into dicarbonyl molecules (Liu et al., 2025). The intermediate and final stages of the Maillard reaction were discovered to be successively enhanced by the addition of lysine by Zhang, Tan, et al., 2025). AGEs were formed in the middle and late phases of the Maillard reaction due to the binding of dicarbonyl compounds like GO to the amino groups (Eggen & Glomb, 2021). The significant increase in AGEs in the MR group (Fig. 6e) was consistent with the consumption patterns of Lys and Glc.

Changes in flavor

The impact of Glc-Lys simulation system on the flavor of Litopenaeus vannamei under microwave drying was demonstrated in Fig. 5c and Table 2. The dried shrimp had a strong baked flavor, due to the much greater amounts of pyrazines, including 2,5-dimethylpyrazine and 3-ethyl-2,5-dimethylpyrazine, and aldehydes, such as octanal and decanal, in MR as compared to PS. The presence of the Glc-Lys simulation system created 2-ethyl-5-methylpyrazine (OAV = 5.21), which was not detected in PS. The primary sources of aldehydes and pyrazines were Strecker degradation and subsequent complex chemical reactions, which yielded various flavor compounds as well as hazardous substances like melanoidins (ALjahdali & Carbonero, 2019). Glc and Lys were both important precursor compounds for pyrazines and aldehydes. The addition of Glc and Lys in the Glc-Lys simulation system contributed to the formation of alkyl pyrazines, aldehydes, and other meat-flavor compounds, enhancing the Maillard reaction, which was confirmed by Gao et al. (2024) and Shi, Gao, Liu, et al. (2024). Conversely, the concentrations of ketones and alcohols decreased simultaneously. With the concentration of 1-octanol dropping by 68.89 %, the concentration of 2-nonone also decreased by 74.77 %, which imparted the distinctive creamy, fruity, and chocolatey flavor to microwave dried shrimp. 2-nonanone was associated with the oxidation of free fatty acids (Acquaticci et al., 2024). Lipid oxidation was encouraged by the increased energy of MD, RD, and AF in comparison to HD. Due to its brief heating period, MD might have preserved more oxidative degradation products, such as 2-nonanone. Based on the comparative results of the simulated systems, it could be observed that the synergistic effect of Glc-Lys accelerated the formation and accumulation of GO in the early stage of the reaction. As shown in Fig. 7, the decrease in 1-octanol, 2-nonanone, etc., in MR might be due to the bonding of carbonyl molecules and cracking products like 2-nonanone to form compounds like pyrazine and thiophene, which had strong coffee and barbecue scent. The middle and late phases of the Maillard reaction were mostly where this process took place. Zheng et al. (2022) also reported similar results, finding that carbonyl compounds such as GO promote pyridines formation.

Conclusion

In this study, the effects of different drying treatments on the physicochemical properties, flavor and Maillard reaction of Litopenaeus vannamei were evaluated. MD, AF and RD dramatically shortened the drying time. MD contributed to the development of a brighter reddish color, and excellent organoleptic properties in dried products. Different drying treatments led to drastic changes in the flavor of Litopenaeus vannamei, with MD and HD being the most identical in odor. MD was enriched with characteristic flavor compound such as 2-nonanone with a fruity aroma. In addition to greatly increasing the level of pyrazines and giving the dried shrimp a roasted flavor, RD and AF also caused the browning intensity, 5-HMF, fluorescent AGEs, pentosidine and CEL to rise while MD resulted in the highest content of CML. Correlation investigation revealed that there was an interaction between color, degree of lipid oxidation, flavor and Maillard reaction during drying. Lipid oxidation promoted changes in CML, 2-nonanone and color. Changes in browning intensity, pentosidine, AGEs and 5-HMF were positively correlated with pyrazines but negatively correlated with L*. Using the Glc-Lys simulation system, the association mechanism between flavor and the Maillard reaction during MD was further investigated. It was discovered that the accumulation of intermediate key compounds, including GO, dominated the pre-drying stage. AGEs were created during the late drying stage when a significant buildup of lysine interacted with GO. The Maillard reaction was mild, and MD did not react further to produce pyrazines and aldehydes, retaining the distinctive taste components like 2-nonanone as a result of higher drying speed. The results revealed the correlation mechanism by which the drying method and process affected the qualities of Litopenaeus vannamei, and provided theoretical guidance for the synergistic regulation of flavor and control of harmful substances in industrialized shrimp processing using microwave drying. However, due to the complexity of the subject, more detailed and specialized research on the formation pathways of flavor components was needed to provide guidance for the production of premium dried shrimp products.

Pancreatic fat deposition as a mediator of adrenal gland volume and type 2 diabetes: evidence from MRI
Authors: Qinhe Zhang, Ziting Zhang, Siqi Wang, Yuhui Liu, Liangjie Lin, Jing Peng, Ailian Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo assess the role of body composition mediating the association between MRI-quantified adrenal gland volume (AGV) and type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) and to clarify the underlying mechanisms involved.MethodsThis retrospective study analyzed 239 hospitalized patients (54 T2DM, 185 non-T2DM) with abdominal MRI. AGV was manually segmented on T1-weighted images. Body composition parameters, including subcutaneous adipose tissue (SAT) area and fat fraction (FF), visceral adipose tissue (VAT) area and FF, hepatic FF (HFF), pancreatic FF (PFF), and abdominal muscle (AM) area and FF, were quantified using MRI fat fraction mapping.ResultsAfter adjusting for age, sex, hypertension, and BMI, mediation analysis showed that PFF partially mediated the association between AGV and T2DM (OR = 1.036, 95% CI: 1.009–1.101), accounting for 8.7% of the total effect. Multivariate analysis identified both AGV (OR: 1.475, 95% CI: 1.235–1.762) and PFF (OR: 1.069, 95% CI: 1.023–1.116) as independent factors associated with T2DM. ROC analysis showed AUCs of 0.792 for AGV and 0.803 for PFF in differentiating T2DM. The combined AGV + PFF model achieved an AUC of 0.836 (95% CI: 0.779–0.894).ConclusionMRI-quantified AGV and PFF demonstrate potential as biomarkers for T2DM. Pancreatic fat deposition partially mediates the link between adrenal enlargement and T2DM, suggesting a novel pathway involving HPA axis dysregulation and ectopic fat deposition.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12880-026-02184-8.

Introduction

The hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis, the central regulatory network within the neuroendocrine system, plays a critical role in mediating stress responses and regulating glucose metabolism [1, 2]. Both exogenous and endogenous stressors, including environmental, physiological, and psychological factors, among others, can induce activation of the stress response system [3]. While acute, transient activation of the HPA axis serves an adaptive function, chronic, persistent exposure to glucocorticoids has been demonstrated to induce metabolic disorders [4, 5].

Chronic activation of the HPA axis leads to an increase in AGV, a phenomenon attributed to the trophic effect of adrenocorticotropic hormone on the adrenal gland [6]. Therefore, AGV measurement represents a reliable method for assessing HPA axis activation [7, 8]. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) has been demonstrated to be a suitable noninvasive method for evaluating AGV, and AGV assessed using MRI-T1-weighted imaging (T1WI) sequences has emerged as a biomarker for chronic HPA axis activation [9, 10].

Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) is a significant global health challenge, characterized by insulin resistance and β-cell dysfunction, with worldwide implications due to its high mortality and disability rates [11–13]. Body composition, especially the deposition of subcutaneous adipose tissue (SAT), visceral adipose tissue (VAT), hepatic fat, pancreatic fat, intramuscular fat, and bone marrow adipose tissue (BMAT), plays a prominent role in T2DM pathogenesis [14, 15]. Previous studies have demonstrated that patients with T2DM frequently exhibit enhanced HPA axis activity and abnormally elevated cortisol secretion [16], and elevated cortisol levels can further contribute to insulin resistance (IR) by inducing abnormalities in the distribution and content of body components [17, 18]; thus it was recognized as a significant risk factor in the development of T2DM. However, different adipose tissue depots exert distinct effects on metabolism. Among various adipose tissue depots, pancreatic fat shows a strong association with T2DM, occurring prior to hepatic fat deposition in affected individuals [14, 19].

While previous studies have separately explored the relationships between AGV, pancreatic fat, and T2DM, our study is the first to directly investigate pancreatic fat as a mediator in the AGV and T2DM relationship through MRI-based quantification. By identifying pancreatic fat as a potential mediator, our study proposes a novel pathway linking HPA axis activity to T2DM. Understanding this pathway is crucial for elucidating how chronic HPA axis activation, as indicated by AGV, contributes to the development of T2DM through specific ectopic fat deposition. Thus, our study focused on assessing this potential mediation pathway, utilizing MRI to evaluate how body composition might influence the relationship between AGV and T2DM.

Methods

Study design and participants

This retrospective study enrolled hospitalized patients who underwent 1.5 T and 3.0 T upper abdominal MRI examinations between June 2017 and March 2021. Inclusion criteria: (1) complete clinical data; (2) MRI examination, including MRI fat fraction (FF) maps as well as MRI-T1W sequences. Exclusion criteria: (1) age < 18 years; (2) pregnancy; (3) severe liver, kidney, and pancreatic diseases, as well as diseases of the abdominopelvic cavity, skeletal muscles, and lumbar vertebrae that affect the measurement of data; (4) history of severe malignant tumors, history of radiotherapy, history of organ transplantation, or history of acquired immunodeficiency syndrome, and history of systemic application of glucocorticosteroids, etc.; (5) history of alcohol addiction or drug addiction; (6) adrenal disease; (7) poor image quality precluding reliable measurements.

T2DM was defined as a fasting plasma glucose (FPG) level ≥ 7.0 mmol/L or receipt of glucose-lowering therapy. According to this definition, participants were categorized into two groups: those with T2DM and those without (non-T2DM) for comparative analysis.

Dyslipidemia was defined in some analyses as a ratio of total cholesterol (TC) to high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) of ≥ 4.5. The same definition was used for men and women.

This study was approved by the ethics committee of the First Hospital of Dalian Medical University and performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. And the waiver of informed consent was approved for the collection of data (Project Number: PJ-KS-KY-2025-608).

MRI examinations

Abdominal MRI examinations were performed in the supine position on 1.5 T (Signa HDxt, GE Healthcare, Waukesha, WI, USA) and 3.0 T MRI scanners (Signa HDxt, GE Healthcare, Waukesha, WI, USA and Ingenia CX, Philips Healthcare, Philips). Patients fasted for 4–6 h and were instructed to exhale and hold their breath for about 20 s during scanning. Participants were encouraged to practice breath control in advance to prepare for the procedure. If they were unable to hold their breath for the full 20 s, a two-breath scanning technique was utilized. MRI FF maps was performed using 1.5 T and 3.0 T iterative decomposition of water and fat with echo asymmetry and least-squares estimation quantitation (IDEAL-IQ) from GE and 3.0 T mDixon Quant from Philips. Multiple acquired echo signals were collected during a single breath-hold and reconstructed to generate aqueous phase, lipid phase, R2* map, and FF map. Data measurement

Measurement of AGV and body composition

Bilateral segmentation of adrenal gland was achieved through manual segmentation of the organ on the T1WI with United Imaging Intelligent Research Platform (uAI Research Portal), and the AGV was calculated as the number of voxels multiplied by voxel size (Fig. 1).

Fig. 1AGV measurement. A: Right; B: Left

AGV measurement. A: Right; B: Left

SAT, VAT, and abdominal muscle (AM) area and FF were measured at the L1/L2 level; and hepatic FF (HFF), pancreatic fat fraction (PFF), and BMAT FF also were measured in a manner consistent with our previous study [14] (Supplementary material).

Inter- and intra-observer variability

The intra-observer variability was assessed by repeated MRI-acquired fat and AGV measurements of 30 randomly selected patients taken by the same observer (with 3 years of experience in abdominal imaging MR diagnosis), with a minimum interval of four weeks between the two measurements. Inter-observer variability was determined by having a second independent observer (with 5 years of experience in abdominal imaging and MR diagnosis) perform repeated measurements on the same patients using the identical method. Two radiologists involved in this study were blinded to the grouping.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics 26.0 software (Armonk, NY, USA) and MedCalc 20.022 software (MedCalc Software, Ostend, Belgium), and graphs were generated using GraphPad Prism 8.4.0 software. The normality of measurements was tested using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Normally distributed measurements were presented as mean ± standard deviation, while non-normally distributed measurements were expressed as median (25th percentile, 75th percentile). Categorical variables were described as frequencies and percentages (n, %).

The agreement between the two observers on the measurements of AGV and body composition values was assessed using intra-class correlation coefficients (ICC). An ICC < 0.50 was considered as poor agreement, 0.50 ≤ ICC < 0.75 was considered as moderate agreement, and an ICC ≥ 0.75 was considered as good agreement.

Differences in measurements between the two groups were analyzed using the independent samples t-test (for normally distributed data) or the Mann-Whitney U test (for non-normally distributed data). For categorical variables, intergroup- differences were analyzed using the Chi-squared test. The associations between AGV, body composition parameters, and T2DM were evaluated using univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed to assess the efficacy of AGV and body composition parameters in identifying T2DM, with the area under the curve (AUC) of the corresponding parameters calculated. Additionally, the optimal cut-off value, sensitivity, and specificity were determined by calculating the Youden index. Delong test was used to compare the AUC values.

To analyze the correlations among the AGV and body composition parameters, partial correlation analysis after controlling for age, sex, and BMI were performed. A bootstrap method was employed for mediation effect analysis to investigate which parameter of body composition mediates the association between AGV and T2DM, after controlling for confounding factors such as age, sex, and BMI. A two-tailed P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Analysis of the partial correlation between total AGV and body composition

After controlling for age, gender, and BMI, partial correlation analysis showed that SAT area, SAT FF, VAT area, VAT FF, and PFF were associated with total AGV (Table S2).

Analysis of the mediating effect of body composition in the association between AGV and T2DM

In this study, after controlling for confounding factors such as age, sex, hypertension, and BMI, and with the presence of T2DM as the outcome variable, we conducted a dependent mediation analysis using AGV and PFF, SAT FF, VAT area and BMAT FF as independent and mediating variables. We found a significant direct effect of AGV on T2DM, with a confidence interval that did not include 0; SAT FF, VAT area, and BMAT FF showed no significant mediating effect (P > 0.05). In contrast, PFF demonstrated a significant mediating effect in the association between AGV and T2DM (β = 0.0356, 95%CI: 0.0088, 0.0964, P < 0.05), and the mediating effect accounted for 8.7% of the total effect. Theoretically, it suggests that part of the risk of T2DM due to increased AGV is due to the effect of increased AGV on PFF (Table S3 and Table S4) (Fig. 4).

Discussion

This study demonstrated that PFF partially mediates the association between MRI-quantified AGV and T2DM. Both total AGV and PFF were identified as independent risk factors for T2DM, underscoring the intertwined roles of chronic HPA axis activation and ectopic fat deposition in diabetes pathogenesis. Specifically, AGV not only exhibited a significant direct effect on T2DM but also exerted an indirect effect through PFF, with the mediating effect accounting for 9.31% of the total effect. These findings highlight the intertwined roles of chronic HPA axis activation and ectopic fat deposition in the pathogenesis of T2DM.

In the pathogenesis of T2DM, endocrine axis dysfunction involves impairments in the neural pathways of the HPA axis and defective glucocorticoid receptor-mediated negative feedback regulation [20, 21]. Glucocorticoids play a crucial role in energy metabolism, potentially inducing insulin resistance and impaired glucose metabolism, with links to HPA axis activation [22–25]. Additionally, prolonged hyperinsulinemia can stimulate adrenocorticotropic hormone synthesis and increase HPA axis activity [26]. Askani E et al. [27] reported that T2DM was significantly associated with increased AGV, independent of age, sex, dyslipidemia, hypertension, and BMI. They further noted that AGV may serve as an indirect marker of the impact of glucose metabolism and HPA axis dysfunction, which is consistent with our findings.

Consistent with prior evidence [28, 29], our results demonstrated that the T2DM group had significantly higher VAT area, PFF, BMAT FF, and AM area compared with the non-T2DM group. In contrast, SAT FF was lower in the T2DM group than in the non-T2DM group, with all differences being statistically significant (P < 0.05). Yet, in our study, the lower SAT FF may reflect adipose tissue fibrosis or inflammation, whereas the lack of difference in VAT FF could be attributable to adipocyte hypertrophy without altered tissue composition. Notably, HFF did not differ between groups, possibly due to lipid-lowering treatments or the modest sample size, though this does not detract from our primary finding regarding pancreatic fat.

The MRI-based fat quantification technique employed here allows assessment of body composition distribution and is considered the technical gold standard for fat content evaluation due to its accuracy in quantifying fat percentages across diverse organs and tissues [30, 31] and it is highly reproducible across readers, field strengths, scanning devices, and imaging platforms [32].

In the present study, correlation analysis between AGV was associated with various MRI-based body composition components, including SAT FF, VAT area, VAT FF, PFF, and AM area, consistent with prior reports of a bidirectional relationship between HPA axis activity and fat distribution [9]. In our study, the lower SAT FF observed in the T2DM group may indicate the presence of fibrosis or inflammatory changes within SAT [33, 34]. Such alterations are often characterized by an increase in extracellular matrix components, like collagen, and infiltration by immune cells, which together contribute to a reduction in the proportion of detectable fat signal [35–38]. This remodeling process can diminish fat storage capacity and alter metabolic functions of subcutaneous fat, potentially impacting insulin sensitivity. Conversely, the lack of significant difference in VAT FF between T2DM and non-T2DM groups might be attributable to adipocyte hypertrophy, a condition where fat cells enlarge to store excess lipids. This enlargement may occur without significantly altering the overall cellular composition of the tissue, thereby maintaining a consistent fat fraction. This further links increased AGV to dysfunctional adipose tissue [39, 40].

Crucially, our results demonstrated that AGV is closely associated with PFF, which exerts a partial mediating effect on the AGV-T2DM relationship. Specifically, AGV not only has a significant direct effect on T2DM but also exerts an indirect effect through PFF. This mediating effect accounted for 8.7% of the total effect, suggesting that when exploring the relationship between AGV and T2DM, in addition to focusing on PFF, other potential influencing mechanisms require further investigation.

Pancreatic fat contributes to diabetogenesis via impaired β-cell function and IR, effects that are not replicable by VAT or SAT [41]. Increased AGV may induce lipolysis via cortisol secretion, leading to elevated free fatty acids [42] and subsequent pancreatic fat deposition [43], thereby forming the AGV-PFF-T2DM pathway. These include the well-documented enhancement of HPA axis activity and cortisol secretion in T2DM patients. Elevated cortisol levels can directly induce IR through mechanisms such as stimulating hepatic gluconeogenesis and inhibiting skeletal muscle glucose uptake. Additionally, the interaction between the HPA axis and pancreatic β-cell function warrants further study.

This study has the following limitations. First, the cross-sectional design and relatively small sample size preclude causal inferences; longitudinal studies are needed to validate our findings. Second, AGV measurement relied on manual segmentation, which is time-consuming; future work should explore automated methods to improve efficiency. Third, all participants were Chinese, which may limit generalizability to other populations. Fourth, the lack of key laboratory data (e.g., cortisol levels) restricts mechanistic exploration of the AGV - T2DM association. Fiffth, previous studies has indicated that FF values are not significantly influenced by variations in imaging equipment or pulse sequences [44–47]; yet, the effect of slice thickness on FF values has not been reported. While slice thickness differed among devices in our study, our experimental data confirmed that FF measurements remain consistent irrespective of slice thickness, as detailed in the Supplementary Materials. Finally, the comparable HFF between groups may reflect the influence of lipid-lowering treatments or lifestyle interventions in the T2DM cohort, though this does not affect our primary conclusion regarding pancreatic fat.

In conclusion, this study used quantitative MRI to demonstrate that pancreatic fat deposition partially mediates the relationship between AGV and T2DM. This finding enhances our understanding of the pathogenic link involving the HPA axis and T2DM, offering novel insights for future mechanistic investigations.

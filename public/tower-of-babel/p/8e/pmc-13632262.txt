Optimization of process parameters for bitter gourd candy production using response surface methodology
Authors: H. R. Arun, G. P. Sharma, V. Kavan Kumar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In this optimization study, response surface methodology was used for optimization of process parameters for osmotic drying of bitter gourd slices. The Box-Behnken design of three variables such as syrup concentration (40, 50 and 60 ⁰Brix), syrup temperature (40, 50 and 60 ℃) and duration of osmosis (60, 180 and 300 min) which includes 17 experiments formed by using 5 central points was used to optimize input parameters. The linear, quadratic and interaction effects of three variables were analysed for the responses. For each response, i.e. water loss and sugar gain, the second-order polynomial models were developed and the fitted model’s analysis of variance (ANOVA) was performed to check the adequacy and accuracy of the fitted models. The response surfaces and contour maps showing the interaction of process variables were constructed. The analysis of variance (ANOVA) indicated that the linear terms of syrup concentration, syrup temperature and duration of osmosis were significant at the 1% level for both the responses, viz. water loss and sugar gain. The F-value indicated that the duration of osmosis was the most influential factor, followed by syrup concentration and syrup temperature for water loss and sugar gain. The optimized conditions for making bitter gourd candy was 48 0Brix syrup concentration, 47 ℃ process temperature and 190 min duration of osmosis for osmotic drying, followed by convective drying at 40 ℃ drying air temperature and air velocity of 2 m/s.

Introduction

Bitter gourd has nutritional components such as high in carbohydrates, proteins, vitamins, minerals and other nutrients that are important for good health. It has good source of protein, ascorbic acid, calcium, iron and phosphorus. Total fat content of whole fruit ranged from 2.9 to 6.4% of dry matter [1]. It also contains high amount of Vitamin A, vitamins B1 (thiamine), B2 (riboflavin), B3 (Niacin) and B9 (folic acid) [2]. Inorganic minerals such as, potassium, phosphorus, calcium, sodium, magnesium, iron, and zinc are also abundant in bitter gourd [3]. It also contains different types of amino acids viz. glutamine, glycine, asparagine, lysine, leucine, alanine, valine, proline, arginine, serine, phenylalanine, isoleucine, tryptophan, threonine, histidine, and methionine. Compared to broccoli, spinach, bananas and other vegetables the bitter gourd has higher levels of iron, beta-carotene, and potassium. Due to these compositions in the bitter gourd it has a vital role in the traditional medicines like Ayurveda to treat the diabetes, intestinal worms, blood purification, skin diseases, etc.

Fruits and vegetables are a vital addition to the human diet because they contain necessary minerals, vitamins and fibre that are necessary for good health. Due to insufficient preservation facilities, a large amount of product is wasted, since only approximately 2% of total crop is processed in the country [4]. As a result, there has been a constant trickle of novel ‘minimal’ preservation approaches emerging. Simultaneously, advances in modern technology have reignited interest in traditional preservation methods and how they may be used with contemporary technology. Food preservation mentions to the treatment and handling of food in order to prevent spoilage caused or intensified by microorganisms. Preserving food normally involves avoiding the development of bacteria, fungi and other microorganisms, as well as delaying fat oxidation, which causes rancidity [5].

Due to its high moisture content, bitter gourd is fragile and perishable commodity that is difficult to store for a long stretch at room temperature. In India, proper storage and processing of bitter gourd is needed for consumption, preservation and utilization during the off-season [6]. Dehydration of bitter gourd is a critical step in preventing post-harvest losses, specifically, when the market receives a large amount of produce early in the season, resulting in a surplus. Processing by dehydration is a technique with immense promise in the food industry for reducing food spoilage and increasing food safety [7]. It is one of the oldest vegetables known to mankind and eaten worldwide, which has the ability to prevent storage losses by processing and preservation. Bitter gourd produces a rubbery appearance and turns brown if it is not properly pre-treated and it also lacks some of its properties, such as ascorbic acid and chlorophyll, during drying [8].

Dehydration of perishables is a good alternative in developing countries such as India, which have poorly built low temperature and thermal processing facilities. Pre-treatments such as osmotic dehydration could be used for partial reduction of moisture before the drying operation [2, 9]. The osmotic dehydration method is a way to concentrate solids in foods. In this process the biological tissues are pre-treated by submerging them in an aqueous solution of an edible solute such as sugar or salt. The osmotic pressure created by the concentration gradient between the hypertonic solution and intercellular fluid acts as a driving force. As a result, there is no phase change in the water removing process and also the texture of the product is not affected [10]. The suitability of the bitter gourd candy development depending on the utilization of various parameters with different proportions. These proportions will give the large number of experiments to examine better conditions. Therefore, the study emphasis on optimization of the process parameters to reduce the experiments for making bitter gourd candy to the possible extent and with longer shelf-life.

Materials and methods

Variables under study

The independent variables like process temperature, syrup concentration, and, process time, were adjusted according to predefined values, and these parameters were varied in relation to the dependent variables, namely water loss (WL) and, sugar gain (SG).

Optimization for osmotic drying

Response surface methodology (RSM) is a significant statistical approach for investigating complicated processes; as a result, these techniques were used to optimize the process parameters for osmotic drying of bitter gourd slices. RSM is a combination of. statistical approaches the. leading experiments, emerging models, assessing the impacts of variables, and tracing optimal conditions for desired responses [11, 12]. RSM’s research entails fitting experimental sugar gain/water loss measurements to a conventional quadratic polynomial equation and then optimising the results using appropriate optimization techniques or mathematical solution. Various researchers have investigated the optimization of osmotic drying [13–15]. The process parameters syrup temperature, syrup concentration and osmosis time were optimized’ for maximum WL and optimum (targeted) SG.

RSM makes it possible to model the relationships between input elements and the intended response more accurately and efficiently, which improves the precision of process optimisation in osmotic drying. RSM reduces the number of experiments required to explore the process space by enabling the simultaneous change of several parameters, in contrast to traditional one-factor-at-a-time experimental techniques. As a result, a stronger and more trustworthy model is produced, which may subsequently be utilised to forecast and optimize the procedure for the intended results.

Results and discussions

This chapter deals with the results of the experiments carried out on osmotic drying and osmo-convective drying of bitter gourd slices. The effect of independent variables such as sugar syrup concentration, syrup temperature and, duration of osmosis for osmotic drying and drying air temperature for convective drying on dependent variables such as water loss, sugar gain, weight reduction, moisture content, drying rate and moisture diffusivity and also the quality of dried products has been presented and discussed. It also consists of statistical analysis of experimental data.

Optimization of osmotic drying process

During osmotic drying process mass transfer occurs due to semi-permeable cell membrane of the fruits and, vegetables and, mass transfer is opposed by two resistances i.e. internal and, external. The external resistance is due to fluid dynamics of solid-fluid interface and, internal due to structure of cell tissue, permeability of cell, membrane, deformation of vegetable and, interaction of mass fluxes [18].

Mass transfer is influenced by parameters like syrup concentration, syrup temperature, duration of osmosis, agitation of solution and samples to solution ratio [19]. So, optimization of, process parameters viz. ST, SC and, DO are necessary so that optimum sugar gain and maximum water loss can be achieved. During experiments, it was found, that these process parameters affect the water loss and, sugar gain but could not achieved equilibrium even after 300 min of osmosis. Therefore, for optimization of osmotic drying is to achieve maximum water loss and, targeted sugar gain of 6%.

A Box Behnken model with 3 variables and 3 levels, seventeen experiments were performed for obtaining the WL and, SG as the responses for each experiment. The experiments were replicated and experimental data was averaged for water loss and, sugar gains are stated. The sample to sucrose solution ratio was 1:5 (w/w) and, bitter gourd sample were immersed in the sugar syrup solution. The samples were removed from the solution at pre-set interval of time and excess solution adhering samples was removed by using blotted paper. The samples were weighed, in order to calculate, water loss and, sugar gain. In order to estimate constants in Eqs. (1), 17 runs were performed in random order to avoid bias. The choice. for the centre points. and, range for. the. variables were. taken through primary trails. The independent variables, like i.e. SC., ST. and, DO. with coded variables (xi), decoded variables with their levels.

Conclusions

This research concluded that the use of response surface methodology for the optimization of process parameters of bitter gourd candy development is necessary to obtain the best suitable conditions. The process temperature (40, 50 and, 60 °C) and, duration of osmosis (60, 180 and 300 min) which includes 17 experiments formed by using 5 central points was used to optimize input parameters for each response, i.e. water loss and sugar gain. Response surfaces were used to illustrate the interaction of process variables with one another. As well as maps of contours. For both answers, WL and SG, the analysis of variance (ANOVA) showed that the linear terms of SC, ST, and DO were significant at the 1% level. According to the F-value, the most important factors impacting water loss and sugar gain were the osmosis time, syrup concentration, and process temperature. According to the graphical method, the range of optimised values for the process variables was as follows: syrup concentration (48 0Brix), syrup temperature (47 ℃), and osmosis duration (190 min). The superimposed contours of the SC (C), ST (T), and DO (θ), as well as their intersection zone for maximum WL and targeted SG of 6%.

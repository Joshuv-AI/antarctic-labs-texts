In-silico identification and functional analysis of cis-regulatory elements in selected protein phosphatase 2A (PP2A) gene promoters of Arabidopsis thaliana reveals 5′-AAAG-3′ as over-represented motif under abiotic stress condition
Authors: Zaiba Hasan Khan, Yukti Singh, Saumya Agrawal, Sandhya Mehrotra, Rajesh Mehrotra
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractA strong gene regulation mechanism is essential to provide accurate temporal and spatial gene expression. This is facilitated by an exclusive arrangement of cis-regulatory elements (CREs) in the upstream regulatory regions of the genes, which collaborate with transcription factors (TFs) in regulating many biological processes, such as plant response to environmental stress. Here, we conducted a bioinformatics analysis in detail to find and profile CREs in the 1 kilobase (1Kbp) upstream regions of Arabidopsis thaliana protein phosphatase 2A (PP2A) genes. Through the plant-specific database newPLACE (SOGO), we found a broad spectrum of CREs involved in phytohormonal signaling, development, and stress responses. Furthermore, de novo motif discovery by the MEME suite identified a new and statistically significant motif, 5′-AAAG-3′, with a high enrichment throughout the PP2A promoters, indicating that it might play a role in regulating gene expression under different physiological conditions. To ascertain the functional significance of these genes further, we carried out a meta-analysis using Genevestigator, which illustrated differential expression of the PP2A genes—upregulation and downregulation—under a range of abiotic stress conditions. Overall, our results shed light on intricate PP2A gene transcriptional regulation and point out CREs as major factors governing plant development, growth, and stress acclimation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13104-025-07513-8.Keywords: Protein phosphatase 2A, Abiotic stress, cis-regulatory elements, Gene expression

Introduction

Kinases catalyse the phosphorylation of their target protein and phosphatases catalyse the dephosphorylation of their target proteins. Therefore, they act as a regulatory switch which helps in controlling the cellular functions in eukaryotic cells [38]. Protein phosphatase is also included as a part of the protein phosphatase group of enzymes, and is associated with almost all cellular functions in eukaryotic cells including abiotic and biotic stress responses [19, 29]. Protein Phosphatase 2A (PP2A) is associated with one of the most extensive families of serine/threonine phosphatase enzymes. It is a key regulatory enzyme in cells, evidence and facts show, approximately 1% of the mammalian cells are committed to PP2A only. Although the vast family of protein kinases and their activities have been known for a long time, the physiological function of protein phosphatases-the obligatory counterpart of protein kinases in cellular control circuitry-has hardly been explored. It should be noted that phosphatases, as a whole, can be categorised into three primary groups, based on substrate specificity: the Ser/Thr group, PTPs (tyrosine) and DSPs (dual-specificity). The classification of Ser/Thr phosphatases are divided into two sub-categories i.e., metal ion-dependent protein phosphatases (PPM) and Ser/Thr-specific protein phosphatases (PPP). PPM is also known by another name i.e., PP2Cs. PPP is down the line sub categorised into PP1, PP2A, PP2B (calcineurin), PP4, PP5, PP6, PP7 and protein phosphatases with Kelch-repeat domains [23]. All these Ser/Thr phosphatases and their subcategory have been found in plants, but there is no such evidence for PP2B to be found in plants.

PP2A is recognised as a holoenzyme made up of three subunits, which includes a catalytic subunit (C) that works with regulatory subunit (A) and a third subunit known as variable subunit (B). The B subunit of PP2A defines its subcellular localization and substrate specificity. The further classification of the B subunit can be done as B, B′, B′′. PP2A subunits have been identified to have crucial roles in stress regulation in plants. The signal perception tends to begin the generic stress signal transduction in plants, followed by triggering of protein phosphorylation cascades mediated through protein phosphatases and protein kinase, that ultimately targets either proteins involved in transcription factors or cellular protection that control the expression of stress-regulated genes. PP2A subunits are involved in post-translational regulation of key metabolic enzymes and signalling components in plant cells. PP2As subunits participate in an extensive range of developmental, biological and physiological activities in plants such as phytohormone transport (auxin) and signalling pathways (abscisic acid, ethylene, brassinosteroids), nitrate assimilation, blue light response, growth and development and control of cytoskeletal structure [4]. PP2A is also known to act as a regulator in ROS-dependent responses involved in pathogenesis responses and light acclimation in Arabidopsis thaliana [19, 31]. They are also known to impact the activity of a large number of enzymes engaged in certain critical metabolic pathways. We have earlier shown that protein phosphatase 2C (PP2C) like promoter is induced by ABA and not Methyl Jasmonate and Salicylic acid (SA) [3]. The Arabidopsis thaliana genome was analysed using in-silico in PP2C-like promoter (accession no AT5G59220). We have identified that the PP2C-like promoter reveals a distinct genetic arrangement of 5′-ACGT-3′ elements. The PP2C-like promoter contains three 5′-ACGT-3′ elements in close contiguity. There was a separation of 30 nucleotides between two elements, whereas the key 5′-ACGT-3′ element had five nucleotide separation from another 5′-ACGT-3′ element [25]. We have demonstrated that two 5′-ACGT-3′ elements are 25 nucleotides apart responded to ABA, while the one that is five nucleotides apart responded to SA [24, 26, 27]. We also found 5′-AACA-3′ elements which negatively interferes with the PP2C gene promoter [28]. More recently we showed that 5′-TGAC-3′ cis-regulatory element to be involved in genes downregulated by heat and salinity [6]. In the current study, we identified that PP2A gene promoters have a very high occurrence of 5′-AAAG-3′ motif.

Materials and methods

Identification of PP2A genes of Arabidopsis thaliana and retrieval of their promoter sequences for CRE analysis

We manually searched for PP2A genes in Arabidopsis thaliana from the database TAIR (The Arabidopsis Information Resource) (Table 1). Our goal was to detect various cis-regulatory elements present in the promoter sequences of these genes. We intentionally selected the size of each promoter as a contact figure of ‘1 kb’ for uniformity. Cis-regulatory elements (CRE) analysis was conducted using the New Plant Cis-regulatory DNA Element (NewPLACE) Sogo database. NewPLACE is a database containing a wide range of plant-specific motifs found in regulatory regions of genes taken from previously published reports [13, 20].

CREs were screened on the basis of their frequency of occurrence in each PP2A gene to ensure a more detailed and accurate analysis. Furthermore, we filtered the data to find CREs occurring in more than 80% of the PP2A genes.

Identification of novel motifs

We intended to identify novel motifs in the upstream regions of Arabidopsis thaliana PP2A genes, using the MEME (Multiple Expectation maximizations for Motif Elicitation) suite software, a robust tool for the de novo discovery of sequence motifs. The MEME suite software package is designed for motif analysis, and hence turns out to be beneficial for scientific studies. The high-throughput genomics and proteomics helps in validating the significance of motif analysis advancements.

In-silico analysis of PP2A genes expression

To study the expression patterns of Arabidopsis thaliana PP2A genes, we utilized the Genevestigator platform [48], which combines curated transcriptome datasets such as microarray (Affymetrix ATH1 platform) and RNA-sequencing data. We used curated microarray datasets available in Genevestigator (ATH1 Affymetrix platform). A total of 22 PP2A genes were queried. Expression data were retrieved for 10 developmental stages, including seed, germination, seedling, rosette, bolting, flowering, silique, stem, senescence, and leaf. Each stage included hundreds to thousands of samples (n = 18–2684). Gene expression values were normalized by Genevestigator and represented as a percentage of expression potential, which reflects relative abundance compared to all available conditions. We did not apply additional filtering thresholds (e.g., fold change or p-value cutoffs) as the goal was exploratory profiling. The visualized data enabled the identification of stage-specific expression trends. We examined gene expression under abiotic stress conditions, such as drought, heat, cold, salt, osmotic, and oxidative stress, using Genevestigator "Perturbations" and “Anatomy” methods for stress-responsive expression profiling. To find differentially expressed PP2A genes, we used criteria of |log2 fold change|≥ 1.0 and adjusted p-value < 0.05 on datasets with well-annotated experimental information. This enabled us to identify genes that, in response to particular environmental stressors, were markedly upregulated or downregulated.

Results

Abundant CREs and their role

183 different types of CREs were identified after analysing the 1 kb upstream promoter sequence of PP2A genes using the newPLACE (Sogo) tool. Amongst these 183 CREs, 85 CREs were found in the promoter region of AT3G59420 while 54 were found in the promoter region of AT1G15415.

Of all the discovered CREs, 11 were found in the promoter regions of all 22 PP2A genes. We classified them as abundant CREs because of their widespread existence. Figure 1 and Table 2 shows the 11 CREs that were observed along with their frequency of occurrence in all 22 PP2A genes of Arabidopsis thaliana. These 11 CREs include DOFCOREZM (5’AAAG3’), ARR1AT (5’NGATT3’), CAATBOX1 (5’CAAT3’), CACTFTPPCA1 (5’CACGTG3’), GT1CONSENSUS (5’GRWAAW3’), GATABOX (5’GATA3’), POLLEN1LELAT52 (5’AGAAA3’), GTGANTG10 (5’GTGA3’), ROOTMOTIFTAPOX1 (5’ATATT3’), WRKY71OS (5’TGAC3’) and MYCORE (5’ CNGTTR 3’). These CREs were found to be distributed throughout the promoter regions on both the + and—strands. The DOFCOREZM CRE is the most abundant with 6–29 repeats in the 1000 bp region of the PP2A promoter with a total of 361 repeats followed by ARR1AT, CAATBOX1, CACTFTPPCA1, GT1CONSENSUS, GATABOX with 277, 260, 256, 197, 177 repeats respectively.

The DOFCOREZM CRE is a known binding site for the Dof (DNA binding with Zn finger) family of TFs, which are associated with the expression of several genes in plants [44]. Dof is responsible for the regulation of plant processes including plant growth and development, seed-specific expression and both biotic and abiotic stress response. According to the probability distribution, there should only be 85 occurrences of AG motifs in a sequence of 22,000 bp in size, yet there are 361 occurrences overall, which is 275 more than the normal distribution. We can conclude that the frequency of this DOFCOREZM CRE motif is statistically higher. Only WRKY1OS and CAABOX1 are not statistically significant out of these 11.

The tetranucleotide motif CACTFTPPCA1 is used to control the expression level of genes specific to mesophyll tissue in plants [42]. Among the PP2A genes, this motif is the second most prevalent motif. In Arabidopsis, ARR1AT (ARR1-binding element) functions as a transcriptional activator and cytokinin response regulator [33]. CAATBOX1 CREs aid in tissue-specific promoter activity of the pea legumin gene LegA in tobacco [39]. GATABOX is an important motif involved in light regulation and tissue-specific expressions in plants [10]. The GT1CONSENSUS sequence acts as the binding site for the GT1 TF in light regulatory genes (Differential Regulatory Function A Necessary but Not Sufficient Element for Light-Activation, n.d. [7]). The GTGANTG10 motif functions in the promoter region of late-pollen genes of tobacco plants [36]. The POLLEN1LELAT52 motif controls the expression of pollen-specific genes in plants such as tomatoes [2]. The ROOTMOTIFTAPOX1 motif is responsible for distinctive expression patterns in the roots of several plants [8]. The gibberellin signalling pathway’s transcriptional repressor is the WRKY71OS motif, and is a well-known binding site of the WRKY71 TF in rice. The 5’-TGAC-3’-containing W-BOX (WRKY binding box) elements found in the Pathogenesis-Related 10 (PR-10) genes are the particular binding sites for WRKY71 TF [6], Z. L [46]. WRKY TF family is involved in plant defence response, along with stress stimuli response and plant growth and development. The WRKY transcription factor (TF) family is essential for plant defense responses, and biotic and abiotic stress stimulus responses, as well as plant growth and developmental regulation. WRKY TFs are central components of stress-responsive signaling pathways, most importantly through participation in the regulation of pathogen resistance genes and stress-inducible defense mechanisms [9, 35].

In the case of Protein Phosphatase 2A (PP2A) genes, while direct PP2A subunit-WRKY or MYB transcription factor interactions are not yet comprehensively detailed, novel evidence indicates PP2A to modulate stress-responsive pathways via the regulation of phosphorylation state within signaling networks where these TFs are operative. Thus, the overrepresentation of WRKY and MYBCORE motifs within PP2A promoters could imply tentative regulatory crosstalk, connecting phosphatase-mediated signaling with transcriptional regulation during stress and developmental processes [5, 32].

MYBCORE motif is the binding site for two MYB proteins ATMYB1 and ATMYB2 from the Arabidopsis thaliana plant. They regulate the genes for flavonoid biosynthesis and are responsive to water stress.

Identification of novel CREs using MEME suite

A previously unannotated, statistically significant sequence motif discovered de novo using MEME Suite that does not match any known plant CREs in current databases, regarded as novel motif. MEME suite (Multiple Em for motif elicitation) tool is used for discovering statistically significant novel motifs in a group of related DNA sequences. MEME uses a group of related DNA sequences as its input and provides multiple motifs as the output. It uses statistical modelling to analyse different aspects of each motif such as optimum width, number of occurrences, and description. Using MEME, we obtained one novel motif for the same dataset of PP2A genes. Figure 3 shows the statistical significance of the unique motif in all 22 promoters of PP2A genes with a p-value of 3.4e-015. Here, the width of this motif is 21 with a total of 58 sites in the total length of 22,000 bp promoter sequences.

In-silico analysis of PP2A gene expression

Genevestigator tool was used to perform the gene expression meta-analysis for the microarray dataset on PP2A genes from Arabidopsis thaliana (Fig. 4). A total of 9000 + samples were included in this aggregated analysis. Heatmap (Fig. 4) shows that the PP2A-A2 gene is highly expressed during all the stages of development as mentioned such as seed, germination, seedling, rosette, bolting, flowering, silique, stem, senescence, and leaf. This suggests a potentially constitutive role for PP2A-A2 in developmental regulation. Similarly, PP2A-B′δ (AT3G26030) is also expressed consistently in all states except senescence, where there was a noticeable decrease in its expression (Fig. 4). This pattern implies that PP2A-B′δ has a wide developmental function, which may reduce in the latter stage of plant life. Certain genes such as B′θ (AT1G13460), Transducin/WD40 (AT1G48870), PP2A-5 (AT1G69960), CHIP (AT3G07370), B′ζ (AT3G21650), and PUX5 (AT4G15410) depict noticeably higher expression levels during the senescence phase of plant development, indicating their possible participation in senescence-associated regulatory mechanisms. On the other hand, B′η (AT3G26020), B′δ, B′ε (AT3G54930), ACR4 (AT1G69040), and B′ι (AT5G25510) genes are consistently expressed across all developmental stages except senescence, indicating their possible reduced involvement during the senescence phase.

Heat map showing expression patterns of PP2A across 10 distinct development stages in Arabidopsis thaliana. Each cell represents the expression strength of a gene at a specific stage. Darker colors indicate higher expression levels

A similar expression analysis was performed using Genevestigator to examine the expression patterns of Arabidopsis thaliana PP2A genes under various hormonal treatments and abiotic stress conditions (Fig. 5). According to the expression data availability in Fig. 5, the B′η, B′ε, B′δ, ACR4, B′ι, B′ζ, PP2A-4(AT3G58500), PP2A-5 and B′θ genes were notably downregulated in response to the heat stimuli, indicating a potential suppression of these PP2A isoforms under elevated temperature conditions. In the case of drought, most of the genes were found to be upregulated such as CHIP, PUX5, B′ζ, B′η and B′θ, suggesting their potential role in drought response mechanisms. Conversely, the B′δ gene was consistently downregulated under multiple stress conditions, including drought, heat, osmotic, and salt stress, indicating a possible stress-sensitive regulatory behaviour. The CHIP gene was found to be upregulated in heat, genotoxic, and drought conditions. Similarly, the PUX5 gene was also upregulated in cold, drought, and heat conditions. Notably, both genes, CHIP and ACR4 were downregulated significantly following exogenous application of salicylic acid (SA), indicating their possible involvement in SA-mediated stress signaling pathways.

Discussion

Promoters are known as specific sequences of nucleotides clustered upstream of the gene coding region and are required for precise initiation and regulation of transcription. Promoters contain CREs that act as a molecular switch to determine which genes are specifically active in a particular type of cell and up to what extent. Hence, they can regulate the gene expression quantitatively at apparent time and space. Understanding the control of PP2A gene expression will be aided by identifying CREs that comprise the promoter architecture of these genes and their combinatorial interactions. If the goal is to modify a gene’s expression pattern in a desired and precise way, thorough research on cis-elements will be beneficial. To the best of our knowledge, no previous study has systematically analysed the cis-regulatory elements present in the promoter region of Arabidopsis thaliana PP2A genes and their potential role in regulating the stress response to both biotic and abiotic stimuli. This study reveals major cis-regulatory elements that are possibly involved in driving high expression in PP2A genes under various conditions.

Consequently, the present study employed an in-silico methodology to investigate the existence of cis-elements in PP2A genes. Numerous CREs that react to stress, plant hormones, and different developmental processes have been found. This paper highlights the 11 most abundant CREs, 22 unique CREs and 1 novel motif that have the potential to target transcriptional factors regulating the expression of PP2A genes.

In-silico gene-expression meta-analysis using the MEME tool suggests upregulation and downregulation of PP2A genes under various abiotic stress conditions. It highlights that the PP2A-A2 gene exhibits a high and consistent expression level throughout the developmental stages of Arabidopsis thaliana from germination to senescence indicating its possible role across the life cycle of the plant. Similarly, genes such as B′θ, Transducin/WD40, PP2A-5, CHIP, B′ζ and PUX5 have pronounced expression levels during the senescence phase implying their role in ageing, senescence and stress response. Many genes including B′η, B′ε, B′δ, ACR4, B′ι, B′ζ, PP2A-4, PP2A-5 and B′θ were found to be downregulated under heat stress highlighting their sensitivity to heat stress. On the contrary, the CHIP gene was upregulated in response to heat as well as genotoxic stress and drought insinuating its involvement in certain stress-tolerant pathways. Gene-expression meta-analysis also shed light on the involvement of CHIP and ACR genes in the SA pathways.

Furthermore, these motifs can assist in designing certain mutation/deletion experiments to validate the regulatory elements and identify the TFs regulating PP2A gene expression. By assessing the complex differential expression of these PP2A genes, we can investigate their precise role in plant growth, stress tolerance and senescence. Our study opens new avenues for investigating the regulatory elements in gene expression of different PP2A genes in important crop plants regulating their stress and immune response.

Effectiveness of manual diaphragmatic techniques in adults with non-specific low back pain: a systematic review and meta-analysis protocol
Authors: Francisco José Vera‑Serrano, Maria Jesus Vinolo-Gil, Rubén Aragón‑Martín, Carlos Pérez-Pérez, Pedro Alfonso Domínguez-Vera, Juan Antonio Díaz-Mancha
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractIntroductionNon-specific low back pain (NSLBP) is one of the leading causes of disability worldwide. Emerging evidence suggests that altered diaphragmatic function may be associated with lumbar pain, impaired trunk stabilisation and functional disability. Manual diaphragmatic techniques have been proposed as an intervention to modulate diaphragmatic tension, mobility and neuromyofascial relationships; however, their effectiveness has not yet been synthesised using rigorous systematic review methods.Methods and analysisThis protocol follows Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Protocols guidance and has been prospectively registered in PROSPERO. Randomised controlled trials evaluating manual techniques specifically applied to the diaphragm in adults with NSLBP will be eligible. The primary outcomes will include pain intensity and functional disability; secondary outcomes will include lumbar mobility, respiratory function, quality of life and adverse events. Searches will be performed in PubMed/MEDLINE, Cochrane CENTRAL, PEDro, CINAHL, Scopus, Embase and clinical trial registries without language or date restrictions. Two reviewers will independently perform study selection, data extraction and risk-of-bias (RoB) assessment using the Cochrane RoB 2 tool. Where appropriate, a random-effects meta-analysis will be conducted; the certainty of evidence will be evaluated using the Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development and Evaluation approach. Substantial clinical and methodological heterogeneity is anticipated across trials, which may limit the feasibility of quantitative data pooling.Ethics and disseminationAs this study uses data from previously published trials, ethical approval is not required. Introduction

Non-specific low back pain (NSLBP) is a complex, multifactorial condition with high prevalence and significant socioeconomic impact.1 2 According to the latest Global Burden of Disease report,3 in 2020, approximately 619 million people experienced LBP, a figure that is projected to increase to 843 million by 2050, driven mainly by population growth and demographic ageing. The same study reported that LBP was the main cause of years lived with disability worldwide, ranking above chronic pathologies with a high health burden such as cardiovascular diseases, cancer or mental disorders.3 4 Furthermore, this study included LBP as the fourth cause of disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) in people aged 10–49 years.5

Over the past three decades, the focus of clinical interventions for the treatment of NSLBP has shifted from pain management to focusing on functioning by improving activities and participation, thus emphasising the role of rehabilitation.6 In our daily clinical practice as physiotherapists, it is notable how frequently we see patients in consultation due to LBP, whether in its acute or chronic manifestation.

Emerging biomechanical and clinical research suggests that the diaphragm is associated with trunk stability through interactions with the transversus abdominis, pelvic floor and thoracolumbar fascia.7 8 The diaphragm functions not only as the primary respiratory muscle but also as a stabiliser of the lumbar spine.9 Anatomically, its lumbar portion arises from the medial and lateral crura, which insert onto the anterior aspect of the upper lumbar vertebral bodies,10 11 and its arcuate ligaments form mechanical bridges between the thoracolumbar fascia and the psoas major and quadratus lumborum muscles.12 13 This architectural continuity, detailed in recent anatomical studies,710 14,16 creates pathways for force transmission between the thorax, abdomen and lumbar spine, such that alterations in diaphragmatic mobility or tone may propagate across the thoracolumbar fascia and deep paravertebral stabilisers.7 15 16

The quadratus lumborum and psoas muscles are directly connected to the diaphragm through these fascial and bony structures.17 From a clinical perspective, this fascial continuity implies that diaphragmatic dysfunction—including hypomobility, hypertonia or aberrant breathing patterns—may be associated with myofascial compensations and altered load distribution in the lumbopelvic segment, potentially contributing to NSLBP.7 18

The WHO is taking steps to expand access to care for people with LBP, such as the Rehabilitation 2030 Initiative.19 This package of interventions for rehabilitation, including LBP, provides information on key rehabilitation interventions for this condition. In the different approaches to treating this condition, pain management is considered essential from a physiotherapy perspective, primarily through joint mobilisation, spinal manipulative therapy and physical training. Exercise tolerance management also includes physiotherapy to improve cardiorespiratory fitness and, again, physical strengthening.19

Published systematic reviews and meta-analyses20 21 agree that breathing exercise programmes are an effective intervention for reducing pain and improving disability in patients with chronic NSLBP. Both studies highlighted that interventions incorporating diaphragmatic techniques and resisted breathing produced clinically relevant benefits when applied with a frequency of 3–5 weekly sessions, lasting between 30 and 50 min, for a minimum period of 4 weeks. These findings reinforce the need to consider respiratory function when designing physiotherapy protocols for this population.

Manual diaphragmatic techniques aim to modify diaphragmatic mobility, resting length, fascial relationships and neural input, potentially affecting pain and function in patients with NSLBP.10 22 Roy 23 observed significant changes in heart rate when applying a lumbar manipulation technique in patients with or without associated LBP. Oscoz24 and Márquez25 also found alterations in the mobility of the diaphragmatic domes and improvements in spirometric parameters when performing a diaphragm stretching technique. Marizeiro et al26 observed similar improvements in thoracic expansion after applying a diaphragmatic stretching technique in the supine position. Veloso et al27 and Braga et al28 also demonstrated the effectiveness of diaphragmatic techniques in increasing thoracic mobility, particularly when applied in various positions and using multiple techniques. Additionally, Sığlan et al29 found that myofascial release applied to both the diaphragm and iliopsoas muscles significantly improved pain and disability in patients with chronic LBP, supporting the hypothesis that diaphragmatic dysfunction may be associated with lumbopelvic impairment. These findings reinforce the potential relevance of manual diaphragmatic interventions in this population. Previous reviews addressed breathing retraining or inspiratory muscle training but did not isolate manual diaphragmatic approaches. Individual randomised controlled trials report variable results; therefore, a focused systematic review restricted to RCTs of manual diaphragm techniques will clarify the current evidence base, estimate pooled effects where possible and identify gaps for future research.

Our primary objective is to present a systematic review protocol aimed at identifying and synthesising the scientific evidence on the effectiveness of manual diaphragmatic techniques in adults with NSLBP. Specifically, this review seeks to address the following research questions: are manual interventions directed at the diaphragm effective in reducing pain and disability in individuals with NSLBP compared with sham, no treatment or conventional physiotherapy? Can manual diaphragmatic techniques be considered a complementary therapeutic approach to standard physiotherapy interventions for the management of NSLBP?

Methods

To guarantee the comprehensive reporting and execution of this systematic review protocol, the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses Protocols (PRISMA-P) guidelines30 31 and methodological recommendations from the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions were followed32 (online supplemental appendix 1). The review methodology was preregistered on the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews with registration number CRD420251172616.

Eligibility criteria

The primary eligibility criteria encompass published RCTs that evaluate the effectiveness of manual diaphragmatic techniques in adults diagnosed with NSLBP. The eligibility framework follows the population–intervention–comparator–outcome structure.

Inclusion criteria

Eligible participants

Eligible participants are adults aged ≥18 years with a clinical diagnosis of NSLBP, defined as lumbar or lumbosacral pain not attributable to a specific pathology (eg, infection, tumour, fracture and radiculopathy). Both subacute and chronic cases will be considered. Studies must clearly describe the inclusion and exclusion criteria and ensure that participants are medically stable enough to undergo manual therapeutic intervention.

Intervention

Included studies must investigate manual techniques directly applied to the diaphragm or its anatomical attachments. These may include, but are not limited to, diaphragmatic release, manual stretching of the crura or domes and fascial mobilisation targeting diaphragmatic tissue. The intervention must be administered by trained physiotherapists or manual therapy practitioners and described in sufficient detail to ensure reproducibility (eg, technique characteristics, duration, session frequency and total treatment period).

For this review, ‘manual diaphragmatic techniques’ are defined as hands-on interventions in which a trained physiotherapist or manual therapy practitioner applies direct manual contact (eg, pressure, mobilisation, stretching or myofascial release) to the diaphragm itself, its crural attachments or its primary anatomical insertions—including the costal margin, diaphragmatic crura or arcuate ligaments—with the explicit therapeutic intent of modifying diaphragmatic mobility, tone or fascial relationships.

Eligible techniques include, but are not limited to, diaphragmatic myofascial release, manual stretching of the diaphragmatic crura or domes, direct costal margin mobilisation targeting the diaphragm and osteopathic diaphragmatic techniques.

The following will be excluded: (a) breathing exercises or inspiratory muscle training without manual therapist contact, (b) general thoracolumbar or spinal myofascial release without specific diaphragmatic intent, (c) self-applied techniques or device-based interventions and (d) multimodal programmes in which the diaphragmatic component cannot be reasonably isolated.

Studies in which the diaphragmatic manual component is clearly separable from co-interventions will be included; otherwise, they will be coded as ‘multimodal’ and subjected to sensitivity analysis.

Interventions that focus solely on respiratory training without manual contact (eg, breathing exercises, inspiratory muscle training devices) will not be included.

Comparator

Eligible comparators include sham manual therapy, usual or standard physiotherapy, other active physical interventions or no treatment. Studies comparing manual diaphragmatic techniques with other manual therapy methods not targeting the diaphragm will also be included if the diaphragmatic component can be isolated or analysed separately.

Co-interventions administered to either the intervention or the comparator group will be systematically recorded during data extraction and categorised as (a) potentially diaphragm-influencing (eg, core stabilisation exercises, breathing cueing and spinal manipulative therapy) or (b) non–diaphragm-influencing (eg, thermotherapy, electrotherapy and general strengthening). Trials in which both arms receive exercise-based programmes but the experimental arm additionally receives manual diaphragmatic techniques will be eligible and analysed separately within a dedicated subgroup.

Primary outcomes

Primary outcomes will encompass variables directly reflecting pain intensity and functional disability. Pain will typically be measured using validated instruments such as the visual analogue scale (VAS), numeric pain rating scale (NPRS) or pressure pain threshold (PPT) via algometry. Disability and functional impact will be evaluated using standardised questionnaires such as the Oswestry disability index (ODI) or the Roland–Morris disability questionnaire (RMDQ).

These outcomes reflect the effectiveness of manual diaphragmatic interventions in reducing pain and improving function among individuals with NSLBP.

Secondary Outcomes

Secondary outcomes will include measures of lumbar mobility (eg, modified Schober test and fingertip-to-floor distance), respiratory function (forced vital capacity (FVC), forced expiratory volume in 1 s (FEV₁), peak expiratory flow (PEF) and thoracoabdominal expansion via cirtometry) and health-related quality of life (Short-Form (SF)-12 or SF-36). Additional outcomes such as postural control, trunk muscle activity or diaphragmatic excursion measured by ultrasound will be extracted if reported. Adverse events will be defined as any unfavourable or unintended sign, symptom or disease temporarily associated with the intervention, regardless of whether it is considered to be causally related. These include, but are not limited to, increased local pain, dizziness, vasovagal response or dyspnoea occurring during or immediately following a manual diaphragmatic technique session. Adverse event data will be extracted for all included trials. If no adverse events are reported by a study, this will be recorded explicitly and coded as zero events. In the case of zero-event data across studies, a narrative synthesis will be conducted rather than quantitative pooling, and the absence of reporting will be discussed as a potential source of underreporting bias.

Study design

This review will include parallel-group RCTs. Cross-over RCTs will be eligible if they report data from the first period (pre-crossover) separately and in a usable format, or if the wash-out period is sufficiently long (at least 4 weeks) to preclude carry-over effects. If included, data will be extracted from the first period only and analysed alongside parallel-group trial data using standard methods for cross-over designs as described in the Cochrane Handbook.32

Study selection

The literature search will be conducted across the following electronic databases: PubMed/MEDLINE, Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL), Physiotherapy Evidence Database (PEDro), CINAHL, Scopus and Embase. A comprehensive search strategy combining Medical Subject Headings and free-text keywords related to the diaphragm, manual therapy and NSLBP will be applied using Boolean operators (AND, OR and NOT). The complete search strings for each database will be detailed in Appendix 2 (Search Strategy).

No restrictions will be placed on the publication date or language to maximise the inclusion of relevant studies. The same core terms and logical structure will be adapted for each database according to its specific indexing system.

To enhance the comprehensiveness of the review, reference lists of all eligible and relevant systematic reviews or primary studies will be manually screened to identify additional records. Furthermore, trial registries such as ClinicalTrials.gov and the WHO International Clinical Trials Registry Platform will be searched for ongoing or unpublished trials. When unpublished data or preprint versions are identified, corresponding authors will be contacted to request access to full datasets or online supplemental information.

Studies published in languages other than English will be managed as follows: articles will be assessed for eligibility based on their title and abstract using automated translation tools for initial screening and those selected for full-text review will be translated by a bilingual team member or a certified translation service prior to data extraction.

Selection process

Two independent reviewers will conduct the screening and selection process for all studies retrieved from the electronic databases. Each reviewer will work autonomously and remain blinded to the other’s decisions during the initial phases.

In the first stage, titles and abstracts will be screened to determine potential eligibility based on the pre-defined inclusion and exclusion criteria. Studies deemed relevant by either reviewer will proceed to the full-text assessment phase. Subsequently, both reviewers will independently evaluate the full texts of all selected articles against the established eligibility criteria to confirm their inclusion in the review.

Following this step, the reviewers will appraise the methodological quality and RoB of each eligible study and extract the necessary data for quantitative synthesis. Any disagreements between reviewers will be resolved by consultation with a third independent evaluator to ensure methodological rigour and consensus.

All members of the review team will examine the complete texts of eligible trials to validate inclusion decisions. The full selection process—including identification, screening, eligibility assessment and final inclusion—will be systematically documented using a Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses30 flow diagram, which will be presented as figure 1 in the final manuscript.

Risk of bias assessment

RoB will be assessed using Cochrane RoB 2 tool for randomised trials.33 Two reviewers will independently evaluate each study across the RoB 2 domains and provide an overall judgement (low risk/some concerns/high risk). Disagreements will be resolved by consensus or referral to a third reviewer. The results of RoB 2 will be presented in tables and figures.

Statistical analysis

A narrative synthesis will be conducted to summarise the available evidence on the effectiveness of manual diaphragmatic techniques in NSLBP management. Findings from studies with similar interventions and outcome measures will be grouped and described systematically.

If sufficient data are available and the included studies are deemed methodologically and clinically homogeneous, a meta-analysis will be performed. Data will be analysed using RevMan Web (Cochrane, 2024) or R statistical software (V.4.x or later, R Foundation for Statistical Computing) with the meta or metafor packages, as appropriate. For continuous outcomes, mean differences (MDs) with corresponding 95% CIs will be calculated; when studies use different measurement scales, standardised MDs will be employed. For dichotomous outcomes, risk ratios (RR) with 95% CIs will be estimated.

Between-study heterogeneity will be evaluated using the χ2 test (significance level set at p<0.05) and quantified with the I² statistic, which describes the percentage of total variation due to heterogeneity rather than chance. I² values of 0%–40% will be interpreted as low heterogeneity, 30%–60% as moderate, 50%–90% as substantial and 75%–100% as considerable heterogeneity. When no meaningful heterogeneity is detected, a fixed-effects model will be applied; otherwise, a random-effects model will be used to account for inter-study variability.

A quantitative meta-analysis will be considered inappropriate if the pooled I² statistic exceeds 75% and no clinically meaningful subgroup can explain the observed heterogeneity. In such cases, results will be summarised using structured narrative synthesis following the Synthesis Without Meta-Analysis guidelines. If major heterogeneity is identified or data are insufficient for meta-analysis, a qualitative narrative synthesis will be performed to describe patterns, strengths and limitations across the included studies.

Certainty of evidence

The Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) approach will be employed to determine the overall certainty of evidence across all key outcomes related to the effectiveness of manual diaphragmatic techniques in adults with NSLBP.

The GRADE process evaluates the quality of evidence according to five primary domains: RoB, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision and publication bias. Each domain will be assessed independently by two reviewers, and discrepancies will be resolved through discussion or consultation with a third reviewer.

For each critical outcome (eg, pain intensity, functional disability, lumbar mobility, respiratory function and quality of life), the certainty of evidence will be classified as high, moderate, low or very low, following GRADE guidance.34

High certainty will be assigned when evidence originates primarily from well-designed RCTs with low RoB and consistent, precise results that directly address the research question.

When multiple outcomes are reported, a prioritisation process will be used to select those most clinically relevant to physiotherapy management. A Summary of Findings table will be generated using GRADEpro GDT software to present the results and the corresponding certainty ratings in a transparent and structured format.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and members of the public were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination plans of this research protocol. This is because the study consists exclusively of secondary analysis of published data, with no recruitment of human participants or interaction with patients in any phase of the research process.

Ethics approval statement

Ethical approval is not required for this protocol because the study involves the analysis of data from previously published RCTs and does not include collection of identifiable individual data. According to institutional ethical guidelines and the Declaration of Helsinki, systematic reviews that synthesise existing evidence without direct human involvement are exempt from full ethics board review

Dissemination plan

The results of the review will be disseminated through publication in an international peer-reviewed journal and presentation at national and international scientific meetings in the fields of musculoskeletal rehabilitation and physiotherapy. A plain-language summary will also be produced to facilitate knowledge translation among clinicians and other healthcare professionals. No restrictions will be placed on data accessibility, and all extracted data will be made available in open repositories following publication.

Discussion

The purpose of this investigation is to conduct a systematic review and meta-analysis to evaluate the scientific evidence on the effectiveness of manual diaphragmatic techniques compared with sham, no treatment or conventional physiotherapy in adults with NSLBP.

LBP remains one of the leading causes of disability and work absenteeism worldwide, with significant economic and social implications.1 Recent research suggests that diaphragmatic dysfunction may play a role in postural stability, intra-abdominal pressure regulation and pain perception.10 22 As such, manual interventions directed at improving diaphragmatic mobility and function may represent a promising therapeutic strategy within physiotherapy practice.26,28

This systematic review seeks to provide a comprehensive synthesis of current evidence regarding the efficacy, safety and clinical relevance of manual diaphragmatic techniques. The findings are expected to address the following:

Clarify whether these interventions produce measurable improvements in pain intensity, functional capacity and respiratory function among individuals with NSLBP.

Clarify whether these interventions produce measurable improvements in pain intensity, functional capacity and respiratory function among individuals with NSLBP.

Identify gaps in existing research and methodological limitations in the available trials.

Support the integration of evidence-based diaphragmatic therapy into physiotherapeutic management programmes when appropriate.

Data synthesis and statistical analyses are expected to be finalised within the established project timeline. The final report will be prepared in accordance with standards,30 ensuring transparency and reproducibility in reporting.

On completion, the results will be submitted for publication in a peer-reviewed journal in the field of rehabilitation or musculoskeletal physiotherapy. Findings will also be disseminated at national and international scientific conferences, and a plain-language summary will be made available through institutional channels to promote the translation of evidence into clinical practice.

This protocol outlines the methodological framework for a systematic review that will synthesise evidence on the effectiveness of manual diaphragmatic techniques in adults with NSLBP. Recent biomechanical and neuromuscular research has highlighted the diaphragm as a contributor to trunk stabilisation and pain modulation; however, its therapeutic potential remains under-explored and dispersed across trials with heterogeneous methodology. By applying rigorous eligibility criteria, validated appraisal tools and the GRADE approach, this review is expected to provide a comprehensive and clinically relevant summary of current evidence.

This work may help clarify whether manual techniques targeting the diaphragm have beneficial effects on pain, disability and respiratory function in NSLBP and whether they can be integrated as complementary strategies within physiotherapeutic management. The review may also inform future RCTs by identifying methodological limitations, gaps in outcome reporting and areas requiring standardisation—particularly regarding intervention dosage, treatment fidelity and long-term follow-up.

Mapping the research landscape of TGF-β1 in airway remodeling (1994–2024): A bibliometric analysis
Authors: Lvzhao Liao, Qiushuang Li, Yiying Xiao, Ning Jiang, Ju Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Transforming growth factor-β1 (TGF-β1) is involved in airway remodeling in asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) through extracellular matrix deposition, pro-fibrotic signal transduction and structural changes. This study used bibliometric methods to draw the development context and current research trends of TGF-β1-related airway remodeling research. On July 18, 2024, we queried the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) to retrieve relevant records. Subsequent bibliometric analyses were performed using VOSviewer, CiteSpace, and R package “bibliometrix” to map collaborative networks, keyword co-occurrences, and emerging research frontiers. A total of 920 articles published from 1994 to 2024 were included, involving 5398 authors from 2652 institutions in 215 countries/regions. China had the highest global productivity with 290 publications. The University of California System was the leading institution with 65 publications. At the level of the individual author, Gosens Reinoud was the most productive author (with 17 publications). At the journal level, articles were mostly published in the American Journal of Respiratory Cell and Molecular Biology. Keyword analysis identified “expression,” “inflammation,” “asthma,” and “growth-factor-beta” as frequent terms. Finally, burst detection illustrated “epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT)” and “oxidative stress” as important topics that have emerged recently. This bibliometric analysis maps the main contributors, cooperation networks, and evolving research topics of TGF-β1-related airway remodeling. This field has been centered on cellular mechanisms and inflammatory pathways, while recent studies have increasingly focused on oxidative stress and epithelial-mesenchymal transition. These findings may help guide the prioritization of future airway remodeling research.

1. Introduction

Airway remodeling is the structural change of the bronchial wall due to prolonged inflammation and repeated injury.[1,2] Its main features are subepithelial fibrosis, smooth muscle hyperplasia, epithelial fragility, goblet cell metaplasia and abnormal extracellular matrix (ECM) turnover,[3] which narrow the airways, increase bronchial reactivity and lead to the irreversible decline in lung function characteristic of asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD).[4] The global burden of COPD is expected to continue to increase, with the number of cases expected to exceed 600 million by 2050.[5] Although the etiology is complex, involving genetic susceptibility, environmental exposure, and persistent inflammation, these factors ultimately converge into downstream pathways that contribute to airway remodeling.[6] Allergen sensitization and viral infection lead to remodeling in asthma, and the main causes of COPD are long-term damage from cigarette smoke and fine particles.[7] Understanding these overlapping and disease-specific mechanisms is important for developing targeted interventions.

Transforming growth factor-β1 (TGF-β1) is a key mediator of these structural changes. In asthma and COPD, increased TGF-β signaling has been reported to promote fibroblast-to-myofibroblast differentiation, extracellular deposition and airway smooth muscle proliferation.[8] Experimental studies further support this pathway: ginsenoside Rg1 attenuates cigarette smoke-induced remodeling by inhibiting the TGF-β1/Smad3 cascade,[9] while modulation of the miR-181a/phosphatase and tensin homolog deleted on chromosome 10 (PTEN) axis or NADPH oxidase 4 (NOX4) has been associated with reduced TGF-β1-mediated airway pathology in asthma models.[10,11] Genetic modifiers, such as the family with sequence similarity 13 member A (FAM13A), also involve TGF-β1 signaling in determining the disease phenotype.[12]

Although TGF-β1 has been extensively studied at the mechanism level, the overall development of this research field has not been systematically mapped. Bibliometric analysis provides a quantitative method for the study of publishing activities, collaborative networks, citation patterns and the evolution of topics within a specific literature system.[13] Although Liu et al have mapped a broader field of airway remodeling,[14] the specific bibliometric characteristics of TGF-β1-related airway remodeling research are still unclear. Therefore, this study analyzes publications from 1994 to 2024 to identify major contributors, characterize thematic changes, and provide data-driven references for future research.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Search strategies and data collection

Systematically queried the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) for the Science Citation Index Expanded (SCI-EXPANDED) and Social Sciences Citation Index (SSCI), from inception to July 18, 2024. The search strategy was designed to find records that contain both words associated with “airway remodeling” and those in “TGF-β1”: (TS = (Airway Remodeling)) AND TS = (Transforming Growth Factor beta 1 OR TGF-beta 1 OR TGF-β1). Analysis of English-language original research papers published after 1994 was carried out. The analysis is limited to English original research articles to reduce index inconsistency and improve the interpretability of bibliometric mapping. The exclusion criteria included non-English texts, non-original article types (e.g., reviews, meeting abstracts, letters, editorials), and studies that were not about the TGF-β1-mediated remodeling axis. To prevent problems with data inconsistency due to changes in the database, all the records were taken out and exported on July 18, 2024. The filtering process included exporting bibliographic information using the “Full record and cited references” and “plain text” formats. All data were collected in text format, encompassing details such as the number of publications and citations, titles, author information, institutions, countries/regions, keywords, and journals for bibliometric analysis. Two independent authors conducted the screening process. In cases of disagreement, the authors discussed the issue to reach a consensus. If needed, a third reviewer was consulted to make a final decision. As this is a bibliometric analysis based exclusively on published literature and publicly available data, it does not involve human participants, animals, or identifiable personal data. Therefore, ethical approval was not required for this study in accordance with the relevant regulations and the journal’s policy.

2.2. Statistical analysis

Three bibliometric tools were employed for data visualization and analysis: VOSviewer (1.6.20), CiteSpace (6.3.R1), and the R (4.3.3) with the “bibliometrix” package. VOSviewer was used to map collaboration networks (institutional, author, and co-authorship) and journal co-occurrence network.[15] Network nodes represent entities such as authors or institutions, with size scaled to publication count and color denoting cluster or time period. Connection strength reflects the frequency of collaboration or citation links. Additionally, VOSviewer performed keyword co-occurrence analysis to identify dominant research themes.

CiteSpace was utilized to detect keyword bursts and emerging research trends. The parameters were configured as follows: the time slicing was set from January 1994 to July 2024, and the node type selected was keywords. The threshold for keyword nodes was set to 5 before each fragment, and pruning was performed using the pathfinder and clip merge network methods. Visualization analysis was performed based on these parameters to generate a timeline of keywords in the research field.

The “bibliometrix” package facilitated the quantitative assessment of publication outputs and impact. Productivity and influence were evaluated using the H-index, G-index, and M-index. Individual and journal influence were analyzed using the H-index,[16,17] and additionally, the G-index (to account for highly cited outliers)[18] and the M-index (to normalize productivity over the course of one’s career) were introduced.[19] Journal prestige was assessed based on the 2024 Journal Citation Reports (JCR), and quartile rankings (Q1–Q4) and Impact Factors (IF) were used to describe journal-level characteristics.

3. Results

3.1. Publications overview

The first search found 986 records. Systematic screening excluded 66 publications, which included 52 reviews, 4 editorials, 2 letters, 6 meeting abstracts and 2 miscellaneous documents; thus, 920 articles remained for the final analysis (Fig. 1). The large-scale academic network in the corpus includes 5398 scholars from over 2600 institutions around the world. There were 266 relevant journals and about 25,410 cited works in the literature (Fig. 2A). The top 50 most cited papers are shown in Table S1, Supplemental Digital Content 1. At the head of this cohort, a 2003 Journal of Clinical Investigation (IF: 13.3) study found that anti-IL-5 therapy reduced ECM deposition in the bronchial subepithelial basement membrane of mild atopic asthma, and it has been cited 669 times.[20] The second most frequently cited work was published in 1997 in the American Journal of Respiratory Cell and Molecular Biology (IF: 5.9), which found that eosinophil-associated TGF-β1 mRNA expression is correlated with airway fibrosis and had accumulated 563 citations.[21]

The total number of publications each year has been on the rise since 1994. Some interest had developed by 2001, but the amount increased considerably after 2003. Productivity reached its peak in 2018 at 57 papers and remained relatively high in 2016 to 2023, often exceeding 40 per year. Although the 2024 data were incomplete, the 17 already-indexed articles showed continued investigative activity (Fig. 2B).

3.2. Journal contributions

An evaluation of the top 20 most productive journals identified different contribution profiles (Table S2, Supplemental Digital Content 2). The American Journal of Respiratory Cell and Molecular Biology was the most productive journal, with a total of 54 papers, 1975 citations, and an H index of 32. The American Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine, while ranking seventh in terms of volume (TP = 19), led all other journals for scholarly influence (TC = 2720), indicating high citation visibility. The American Journal of Physiology-Lung Cellular and Molecular Physiology also had a strong presence, ranking second in output (TP = 45) and fifth in citations (TC = 1205).

3.3. Country analysis

A total of 215 countries published articles on the role of TGF-β1 in airway remodeling, with the top 20 most productive countries generating a substantial number of publications in this field (Figure 4A and Table S3, Supplemental Digital Content 3). According to the corresponding author’s publications, China ranked first with 290 articles, followed by 155 articles in the United States and 58 articles in Japan. In terms of citation index, the United States ranked first in total citations (TC = 8469), followed by China (TC = 5053) and Japan (TC = 2355). The average number of citations was 54.6 in the United States, 17.4 in China, and 40.6 in Japan.

Figure 4B illustrated the world’s network of collaboration for TGF-β1 and airway remodeling research. The USA had a significantly higher total link strength at 146 and served as a core node for foreign collaboration; Canada (86) and the United Kingdom (67) followed much further behind.

3.4. Leading authors

Gosens Reinoud was the most productive author, with 17 publications, an H-index of 15, and 665 citations, ranking fifth in total citations (Table S4, Supplemental Digital Content 4). Halayko Andrew J. followed with 16 publications, an H-index of 15, and 594 citations, ranking seventh in total citations. Broide David H. had a high citation count relative to his publication number; although he published 15 articles and had an H-index of 14, he ranked second in total citations (TC = 1129). 3.5. Institution analysis

A total of 2652 institutions have been studying the effects of TGF-β1 on airway remodeling. The University of California System led all institutions in productivity with 65 publications and came close to the University of Groningen (64) and the University of Manitoba (48; Fig. 6A). These high-yield institutions occupy an important position in the institutional collaboration network. 3.6. Keyword analysis

Keyword analysis identified 1689 terms related to TGF-β1 in airway remodeling (Table S5, Supplemental Digital Content 5); among them, “expression” appeared most frequently (335 times; link strength = 1645), indicating that the main focus of this study is the regulation of gene transcription. Frequent references were made to “inflammation” (240), “growth factor beta” (205), and “asthma” (211), which collectively indicated that cytokine activity and allergic airway disease had a pathophysiological connection. In addition, “TGF-beta” (183) and “fibrosis” (118) also showed structural problems as a result of this signal. The terms “cells,” “activation” and “proliferation” appeared together to show that specific research was conducted on cell characteristics, and “lung,” “disease” and “hyperresponsiveness” provided the clinical foundation for this study. The prevalence of “murine models” and “mice” showed that the translational work has used preclinical systems to explore the mechanisms of this disease (Fig. 7).

Visual analysis of keyword co-occurrence network.

3.7. Burst keywords analysis

The time mapping of keyword bursts from 1994 to 2024 shows that the research focus has undergone multiple changes (Fig. 8). Early research, from the mid-1990s to around 2010, focused mainly on molecular characterization, with strong bursts for “gene expression” (1996–2014), “subepithelial fibrosis” (1997–2010), and “messenger RNA” (1997–2010). From approximately 2011 to 2017, interest increased in obstructive pulmonary disease, suggesting greater attention to disease-specific applications. More recently, from 2018 to 2024, “epithelial-mesenchymal transition” and “oxidative stress” showed strong bursts, indicating growing interest in epithelial plasticity and redox-related injury. “Cigarette smoke” also emerged as a burst term from 2020 to 2024, reflecting increased attention to environmental contributors to airway remodeling.

4. Discussion

4.1. General information

This bibliometric analysis describes the development of TGF-β1-related airway remodeling research from 1994 to 2024. The results show that the research results are mainly concentrated in the journals related to respiratory medicine, lung physiology, and molecular biology, among which the American Journal of Respiratory Cell and Molecular Biology is the main publishing platform. Its high number of publications and overall link strength show that it has played an important role in the dissemination of research in this field. This model is consistent with the biological nature of airway remodeling research and usually requires the integration of clinical respiratory diseases, cell biology, and molecular signaling.

At the national level, China contributed the most publications, while the United States showed higher citations and closer international cooperation links. These indicators should be interpreted as different dimensions of research activities: the number of publications reflects the output, the number of citations reflects the citation visibility, and the total link strength reflects the collaboration structure. Therefore, they should not be used as a direct measure of the quality of research. The difference in publication output and citation visibility suggests that future research in high-yield countries may benefit from broader international cooperation, clearer mechanistic framing, and wider dissemination in journals with stronger readership in respiratory or molecular medicine.

At the institutional and author level, the University of California System, the University of Groningen, Gosens Reinoud, Broide David H., and Righetti Renato Fraga have been identified as major contributors or network participants. These findings indicate that this field is developed around the combination of a productive institutional system and a professional research team. The relatively concentrated network of collaborations also suggests that cross-institutional and cross-national collaborations may be critical in advancing more repetitive and clinically relevant research on TGF-β1-related airway remodeling.

4.2. Emerging topics

Keyword burst analysis shows that research attention has changed over time in 3 major directions. In the early stages, the bursts of “gene expression,” “messenger RNA,” and “subepithelial fibrosis” reflect efforts to characterize the molecular and histological features of airway remodeling.[21,22] These early studies have helped to establish the association between TGF-β1 expression, eosinophil inflammation, collagen deposition, and airway fibrosis, laying the foundation for subsequent mechanistic studies.

Since the 2010s, terms related to obstructive pulmonary disease, mechanisms, and signaling pathways have become more prominent, indicating a shift in research towards disease- and pathway-oriented approaches. Recent reviews have summarized the involvement of TGF-β signaling in asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease and pointed out that its role may vary depending on the disease environment and cellular environment.[8] The emergence of the burst term “cigarette smoke” also indicates that people are paying more and more attention to environmental damage, especially in COPD-related airway remodeling. Experimental studies have shown that cigarette smoke-induced remodeling may involve the TGF-β1/Smad3 pathway, supporting the biological rationality of this bibliometric trend.[9]

Since 2018, epithelial-mesenchymal transition has continued to erupt, indicating increasing attention to epithelial plasticity and changes in cell status in small airway diseases.[23] At the same time, the outbreak of oxidative stress indicates that people pay more attention to redox imbalance as a mediator of TGF-β1-related epithelial damage and remodeling.[24,25] These trends suggest that recent research has shifted from descriptive descriptions of remodeling-related changes to pathway-oriented studies of epithelial plasticity, oxidative stress, and environmental-induced airway injury. However, keyword prominence reflects research attention rather than direct evidence of causal importance or clinical efficacy. Future studies should further elucidate the interaction of these mechanisms in asthma, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, and other chronic airway diseases and should combine experimental results with clinically relevant phenotypes.

4.3. Strengths and limitations

This study has several strengths. First, it provides a focused bibliometric map of TGF-β1-related airway remodeling research covering 30 years, enabling developments in the field to be viewed in terms of published output, collaborative networks, citation visibility, and keyword evolution. Second, the combined use of VOSviewer, CiteSpace, and bibliometrix enabled visualization of collaboration structures and temporal research trends, which may help researchers identify active contributors and emerging topics. Third, by focusing on TGF-β1 rather than general airway remodeling, this study provides a more specific perspective on signaling pathways closely related to structural airway changes.

Several limitations should also be acknowledged. First, the number of citations does not necessarily reflect the clinical importance or methodological quality of a single study. Second, since only English publications are included, relevant contributions from non-English-speaking regions may be omitted. Third, the analysis was based on WoSCC, and studies indexed only in other databases may not have been captured. Fourth, recently published studies may be underrepresented in citation-based indicators because the number of citations accumulates over time. Finally, the search strategy was deliberately limited to explicitly mentioning“airway remodeling” and “TGF-β1” records to maintain the specificity and interpretability of the bibliometric corpus. This method may rule out the use of other terms to study TGF-β1-related airway structural changes. Future bibliometric studies may incorporate broader synonyms and multi-database search strategies to further validate developments in this field.

5. Conclusion

This bibliometric analysis provides an overview of TGF-β1-related airway remodeling research from 1994 to 2024. The findings identify major contributors, journals, institutions, and collaboration networks, and show that research attention has gradually shifted from general molecular characterization toward epithelial-mesenchymal transition, oxidative stress, and related pathway-oriented mechanisms. These results provide a descriptive reference for researchers seeking to understand the development of this field and to identify potential directions for future investigation.

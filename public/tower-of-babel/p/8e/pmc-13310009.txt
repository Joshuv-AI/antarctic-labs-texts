The association between 6-thioguanine nucleotide levels, adalimumab exposure, immune response, and drug clearance
Authors: Andres Yarur, Jonathan C Lo, Bincy Abraham, Stephen Hanauer, Thierry Dervieux
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThiopurines are associated with improved infliximab pharmacokinetics (PK), but their impact on adalimumab PK and immunogenicity is not well defined. This study examined associations between 6-thioguanine (6-TGN) levels and antibodies to adalimumab (ATA), adalimumab drug levels (ADL), and adalimumab clearance (CL).MethodsUsing a large clinical laboratory database, we included adults with Crohn’s disease or ulcerative colitis treated with thiopurines and adalimumab who had RBC 6-TGN and adalimumab concentrations measured within 30 days. ATA, ADL, and CL were assessed using drug-tolerant assays and nonlinear mixed-effects modeling. A 6-TGN threshold of ≥125 pmol/8 × 108 RBCs was used. Logistic regression was performed to calculate odds ratios (ORs).ResultsWe analyzed 1194 specimens from 1100 patients; ATA positivity was 3.7%. Compared with 6-TGN ≥125 pmol/8 × 108 RBCs, lower 6-TGN was associated with higher odds of ATA formation (>1.7 U/mL; OR = 2.55), lower odds of ADL ≥10 µg/mL (OR = 0.58), and lower odds of low CL (< 0.318 L/d; OR = 0.55). Compared with every-other-week dosing, weekly dosing produced higher ADL (13.1 vs 8.9 µg/mL; P < .01) but also higher CL (0.425 vs 0.302 L/d; P < .01). Within each dosing interval, higher 6-TGN levels were associated with lower prevalence of ATA (P < .05) and a higher chance of achieving ADL ≥10 µg/mL (P < .01) and CL < 0.318 L/d (P < .01).ConclusionHigher 6-TGN levels are associated with reduced immunogenicity, higher adalimumab drug levels, and lower clearance. Thiopurine may enhance adalimumab PK and support a more favorable therapeutic response.Keywords: Therapeutic drug monitoring, pharmacokinetics, immunogenicity

Introduction

Tumor necrosis factor (TNF)-α inhibitors have been the cornerstone in the treatment of inflammatory bowel diseases (IBD). Nonetheless, patients often develop secondary loss of response due to immunogenicity.1 The use of thiopurines has been shown to augment the pharmacokinetics (PK) of anti-TNF drugs and offer better clinical outcomes.2 Furthermore, higher levels of 6-thioguanine (6-TGN), a sub-metabolite of azathioprine, have been associated with improved infliximab PK. However, there are no data on how 6-TGN levels are associated with immune response and formation of antibodies to adalimumab (ATA), adalimumab drug levels (ADL), and clearance (CL). Given the limited potential for dose optimization with adalimumab, optimizing thiopurine therapy may enhance therapeutic outcomes. The aim of this study was to assess the association between 6-TGN levels and ATA, ADL, and adalimumab CL.

Methods

We analyzed a large clinical laboratory database (Prometheus Laboratories, San Diego, CA) of adult patients with Crohn’s disease (CD) or ulcerative colitis (UC) who were treated with thiopurines and adalimumab, 40 mg every other week or weekly. We included patients who had both RBC 6-thioguanine (6-TGN) levels and adalimumab concentrations measured within 30 days of each other, using reverse-phase liquid chromatography. ATA concentrations were measured using drug-tolerant homogenous mobility shift assays. Cases of CD and UC were identified using International Classification of Disease Codes (ICD-9 and ICD-10). PK outcomes of interest included ATA status (positive >1.7 U/mL), ADL >10 µg/mL, and lower CL (<0.318 L/d, determined using nonlinear mixed effect models) based on previous studies showing their association with better clinical and endoscopic outcomes.3,4 Given that 6-TGN levels ≥125 pmol/8 × 108 RBCs have been associated with higher infliximab levels and lower rates of anti-drug antibodies,5 we used this threshold in our analysis. Descriptive statistics were reported as frequencies for categorical variables and medians (interquartile range [IQR]) for continuous variables. Logistic regression models were used to analyze the associations between exposures and outcomes of interest, and Mann—Whitney U test was used as appropriate.

Results

We identified 1194 pairs of 6-TGN and adalimumab-related data in 1100 adult patients treated with thiopurines and adalimumab at 40 mg every other week (n = 836) or weekly (n = 358). All specimens were collected between October 2013 and August 2022. The median age was 39 years (IQR: 28-53), 58% were female, and 72% had CD. The median RBC 6-TGN level was 166 pmol/8 × 108 RBCs (IQR: 104-249 pmol/8 × 108 RBCs; 32% < 125 pmol/8 × 108 RBCs). The median serum ADL was 9.8 µg/mL (IQR: 6.3-14.9 µg/mL; 49% > 10 µg/mL), and median CL was 0.333 L/d (IQR: 0.228-0.503 L/d; 46.4% < 0.318 L/d. The overall ATA positivity rate was 3.7% (45/1194) with 24 specimens (2%) presenting with ATA and ADL below the lower limit of quantification of the assay (<1.7 µg/mL).

In univariate analysis, compared to patients with RBC 6-TGN ≥ 125 pmol/8 × 108 RBCs, those with 6-TGN < 125 pmol/8 × 108 RBCs had significantly higher odds of ATA formation (OR = 2.55, 95% CI, 1.40-4.63), lower odds of ADL >10 µg/mL (OR = 0.58, 95% CI, 0.46-0.75), and lower odds of low CL (< 0.318 L/d; OR = 0.55, 95% CI, 0.43-0.70). These associations remained significant after adjusting for a diagnosis of CD in multivariate analysis (adjusted OR = 2.63, 0.58, and 0.55, respectively). Similar odds were observed using the first collected specimen (n = 1100).

Additionally, ATA formation was associated with lower CL (<0.318 L/d: OR = 0.02 95% CI, 0.01-0.18) (P < 0.01). The association between 6-TGN < 125 pmol/8 × 108 RBCs and lower odds of low CL remained significant in the subset of specimens without ATA formation (n = 1149, OR = 0.57, 95% CI, 0.44-0.74) (P < 0.01).

Among specimens with ADL <10 µg/mL and 6-TGN < 125 pmol/8 × 108 RBCs, 10% presented with ATA formation (23/231), while it was 1% of patients in those with ADL ≥10 µg/mL (2/155). In the 125-250 pmol/8 × 108 RBCs TGN range, ATA formation decreased to 6% (14/250) in those with ADL <10 µg/mL with 1% ATA observed among those with ADL ≥10 µg/mL (3/265). At TGN concentrations ≥250 pmol, ATA formation was lowest, occurring in 3% of specimens with ADL <10 µg/mL (4/129), while no patients (0%) with ADL ≥10 µg/mL demonstrated ATA (0/164).

There were some differences in adalimumab PK parameters between dosing intervals. ADL was higher among patients who received weekly adalimumab dosing (13.1 µg/mL [IQR: 8.1-18.0]) compared to those who received every-other-week dosing (8.9 µg/mL [IQR: 5.9-13.3]) (P < 0.01). CL was significantly higher among patients who received weekly adalimumab dosing (0.425 L/d [IQR: 0.310-0.653]) compared to those who received every-other-week dosing (0.302 L/d [IQR: 0.205-0.430]) (P < 0.01). On logistic regression analysis, within each interdose interval, higher RBC 6-TGN levels were associated with lower prevalence of ATA (P < 0.05) and a higher chance of achieving ADL ≥10 µg/mL (P < 0.01) and CL <0.318 L/d (P < 0.01) (Figure 1A-C).

Discussion

In this study, just as seen with infliximab, we found that 6-TGN levels were positively correlated with ADL, negatively correlated with ATA and adalimumab CL, and had negligible impact on ATA formation in the presence of high ADL. Although only hypothesis-generating, these findings suggest that optimizing the use of thiopurines, through dose adjustment or drug manipulation—may augment the PK of adalimumab and potentially provide clinical benefit, particularly among patients presenting with accelerated metabolism and lower odds of achieving concentrations commensurate with disease control (ADL> 10 µg/mL).

Interestingly, we also observed that patients receiving adalimumab weekly, compared to those on every-other-week dosing regimen, had higher drug concentrations but demonstrated higher clearance. This counterintuitive finding warrants further investigation, but weekly dosing might reflect dose intensification likely to be initiated in the presence of higher CL and insufficient neutralization of the inflammatory burden.

The limitations of this study center on the lack of granular data, including disease phenotype/severity, prior use of advanced therapy, history of immunogenicity, and inability to differentiate between proactive and reactive therapeutic drug monitoring.6

In conclusion, concomitant use of thiopurines may have a role in improving PK of adalimumab by reducing the rate of immunogenicity and maintaining higher ADL, potentially leading to more sustained therapeutic responses and improved clinical outcomes.

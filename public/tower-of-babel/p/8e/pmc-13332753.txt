Repurposing Old Drugs: Roflumilast as a Promising Therapeutic Option for Plaque Psoriasis
Authors: Shan Li, Naichao Wu, Xingren Liu, Wei Jiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPathogenic studies have revealed that phosphodiesterase 4 (PDE4) plays a key role in psoriasis inflammation. This narrative review evaluates roflumilast, a potent PDE4 inhibitor available in oral and topical formulations, as a repurposed therapeutic for plaque psoriasis.ObjectiveTo synthesize the mechanism of action, efficacy, safety, and clinical application of roflumilast, highlighting recent evidence and its position relative to existing therapies.MethodsWe systematically searched PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, and Cochrane Library from inception to early 2026 using keywords including “roflumilast”, “PDE4 inhibitor”, and “plaque psoriasis”.ResultsBoth oral and topical formulations achieve clinically meaningful improvements in PASI and IGA scores with favorable tolerability.ConclusionRoflumilast represents a convenient, cost-effective, and practical option, especially for sensitive sites and comorbid patients, although larger head-to-head trials against biologics are still needed.Keywords: PDE4 inhibitor, roflumilast, plaque psoriasis

Introduction

Psoriasis is a chronic, immune-mediated, inflammatory skin disease affecting approximately 2–3% of the global population.1,2 It is characterized by erythematous, scaly plaques that can cause significant physical discomfort, psychosocial burden, and reduced quality of life. Current treatments options include topical agents, phototherapy, conventional systemic therapies (eg., methotrexate, cyclosporine), and targeted biologics.3 Despite these advances, many patients experience suboptimal responses, tolerability issues, or high treatment costs, highlighting the need for new or repurposed therapeutic options. Among emerging oral and topical therapies, phosphodiesterase-4 (PDE4) inhibitors target have gained attention as targeted anti–inflammatory agents.4 Roflumilast is a highly selective and potent PDE4 inhibitor (reported to be 25–300 times more potent than apremilast or crisaborole),5 available in both oral and topical formulations.6

Methods

This narrative review was conducted according to standard methodology for narrative reviews. We systematically searched PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, and Cochrane Library without language restriction from inception to January 2026 using keywords including “roflumilast”, “PDE4 inhibitor”, “psoriasis”, and “plaque psoriasis”. Titles and abstracts were screened by two authors, with full-text review of potentially relevant articles. Disagreements were resolved by discussion. Relevant randomized controlled trials, real-world studies, mechanistic papers, and previous reviews were included. This review builds upon and updates prior narrative reviews (particularly Mansilla-Polo et al, 2024) by incorporating new real-world evidence published in 2025, Phase 3 foam trial (ARRECTOR), pediatric data, comparative studies, and weight-loss insights, thereby providing an updated clinical perspective on the practical utility of roflumilast.

Mechanism of PDE4 Inhibitors in the Treatment of Psoriasis

PDE4 is the major hydrolase of cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) in immune cells, transforming cAMP to adenosine monophosphate (AMP), which in turn triggers the generation of pro-inflammatory mediators.7 PDE4 was found in epidermal keratinocytes, neutrophils, Langerhans cells, and T-lymphocytes of the skin,8 exhibiting elevated PDE4 levels in psoriatic skin and peripheral blood compared to healthy individuals.9 These findings collectively establish PDE4 as a prospective and rational therapeutic target for the treatment of psoriasis. PDE4 inhibitors elevate intracellular cAMP levels by interfering with degradation, leading to reduce inflammatory cytokine release, superoxide generation, and chemotaxis.10 Furthermore, PDE4 inhibitors can increase the synthesis of anti–inflammatory mediators, such as interleukin (IL)-10.11 Consequently, PDE4 inhibitors exerts anti–inflammatory effects by increasing cAMP activity and decreasing the expression levels of key pro-inflammatory mediators of psoriasis, such as IL-17, IL-23, interferon-γ and tumor necrosis factor-α.8

Oral Roflumilast in the Treatment of Plaque Psoriasis

Oral roflumilast was approved by the FDA in 2011 for the treatment of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD).12 The first time roflumilast found to improve psoriasis was in 2017, when a report described a notable improvement of erythroderma psoriatic lesions in a patient undergoing oral roflumilast treatment for lung disease.13 This incidental finding subsequently drew widespread attention to the potential role of roflumilast in the treatment of psoriasis.

Later, a Phase 2, multi-center, placebo-controlled trial demonstrated the efficacy and safety of oral roflumilast in treating moderate-to-severe psoriasis. In this trial, 46 patients were randomized 1:1 to receive oral roflumilast 500 μg once daily or placebo for 12 weeks, followed by an open-label treatment through 24 weeks in both groups. At week 12, 35% of patients in the roflumilast group achieved the primary endpoint of a >75% reduction in Psoriasis Area and Severity Index (PASI), compared with 0% in the placebo group. Following 24 weeks of continuous treatment, 44%, 22%, and 9% of patients attained PASI75, PASI90, and PASI100, respectively. The most prevalent adverse events (AEs) included transient gastrointestinal symptoms, weight loss, headache, and insomnia.14

To elucidate the underlying mechanisms of roflumilast in skin, a double-blinded, placebo-controlled substudy was conducted using skin biopsies obtained from 24 patients enrolled in the above-mentioned trial. Transcriptomic analysis via RNA sequencing and quantitative immunohistochemistry demonstrated that oral roflumilast downregulated several key proinflammatory cytokines (eg., CXCL1, CXCL8, IL-1B, IL-17A, IL-23A, and IL-36A) and reduced immune cell infiltration—notably of CD4+ T cells and neutrophils. These findings highlight the potential of oral roflumilast as a systemic therapeutic option for psoriasis.15

In a real-world prospective cohort study, 58 patients with moderate-to-severe psoriasis received oral roflumilast at 500 μg once daily. By week 24, 63.0% of patients achieved PASI <5, 47.8% achieved PASI <3, and 21.7% achieved PASI <1. AEs associated with roflumilast in the study were generally mild, and no serious adverse events were reported. Gastrointestinal symptoms were the most commonly observed, while other reported AEs included asthenia, headache, somnolence, myalgia, and insomnia, etc.16 These real-world outcomes are consistent with previous clinical trial findings.17

Topical Roflumilast in the Treatment of Plaque Psoriasis

Roflumilast cream 0.3% was approved by the FDA in 2022 for the treatment of plaque psoriasis and gained approval for children aged ≥ 6 years in 2023.6 The efficacy and safety of roflumilast cream 0.3% have been demonstrated in multiple clinical trials.

Two phase 3 randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled trials, DERMIS-1 (N = 439) and DERMIS-2 (N = 442), were conducted to investigate the efficacy of roflumilast cream 0.3% in patients with plaque psoriasis. Participants were randomized 2:1 to receive roflumilast cream 0.3% or vehicle cream once daily for 8 weeks. The primary endpoint, defined as an Investigator’s Global Assessment (IGA) of “clear” or “almost clear” plus a ≥2‑grade improvement from baseline at week 8, was achieved in 42.4% of patients in DERMIS-1 and 37.5% in DERMIS-2, compared with 6.1% and 6.9% in the respective vehicle groups. Moreover, in comparison to the vehicle group, roflumilast groups significantly improved multiple clinical endpoints, including the proportion of Intertriginous-IGA (I–IGA) scores of 2 or higher at baseline, I-IGA clear status, PASI75, Worst Itch‑Numerical Rating Scale (WI-NRS) success, etc. The trials noted a minimal occurrence of AEs arising from the treatment, such as transient gastrointestinal symptoms, headache, hypertension, and nasopharyngitis, indicating positive clinical tolerability and safety. The positive outcomes from the DERMIS-1 and DERMIS-2 trials formed the basis for the FDA approval of roflumilast cream 0.3% for the treatment of plaque psoriasis.18

Given the importance of long-term safety and effectiveness, an open-label phase 2 trial assessed the once-daily use of roflumilast cream 0.3% over 52 weeks for plaque psoriasis treatment. In this trial, 44.8% of patients achieved an IGA score of clear or almost clear, and 66.7% achieved an I-IGA score of clear or almost clear plus 2-grade improvement. Clinical efficacy was maintained throughout the 52‑week study period, with certain patients sustaining response up to 64 weeks. Overall, 49.4% of patients experienced AEs, most of which were mild or moderate in severity and unrelated to the drug, such as diarrhea, nausea, and headache. No tachyphylaxis and minimal irritations were observed, which demonstrated roflumilast cream 0.3% represents a well-tolerated topical option suitable for long-term management of plaque psoriasis, including application on sensitive areas such as the face and intertriginous regions.19

Beyond the cream formulation, the efficacy and safety of roflumilast foam 0.3% have been evaluated for scalp and body lesions. The foam vehicle, with higher water content and free of fragrances, propylene glycol, isopropyl alcohol or ethanol (excipients that can cause irritation), effectively penetrates hair-bearing areas.20 Several trials demonstrated significant improvements in signs, symptoms, and pruritus (as early as 24 hours), with low adverse event rates.20,21

Discussion

PDE4 inhibitors elevate intracellular cAMP levels, thereby suppressing multiple pro-inflammatory pathways central to psoriasis pathogenesis. Roflumilast is currently the only PDE4 inhibitor available in both oral and topical formulations, offering unique flexibility in clinical practice. Topical roflumilast (cream and foam) offers practical advantages for site-specific treatment,19 including minimal irritation, no apparent tachyphylaxis (a common limitation of prolonged topical corticosteroid use), and suitability for sensitive areas such as the face and intertriginous regions as well as long-term maintenance therapy.20 The foam formulation facilitates application to hair-bearing scalp areas.22 Evidence for oral roflumilast in psoriasis is still emerging and derives mainly from small randomized trials and real-world cohorts. Nevertheless, several clinically relevant advantages have been observed, including weight loss (approximately 4–6 kg over 24 weeks) that may benefit the many psoriasis patients with obesity, once-daily oral dosing, affordability, and lack of routine laboratory monitoring requirements.23 Both formulations have shown meaningful clinical efficacy; however, comparative data with other established therapies remain limited. In one analysis, roflumilast cream 0.3% demonstrated comparable efficacy to tapinarof cream with a different safety profile.24 A small randomized pilot study found that oral roflumilast achieved numerically higher PASI responses compared with intramuscular methotrexate, although differences did not reach statistical significance.25 While roflumilast offers a convenient and generally well-tolerated option, particularly for patients with comorbidities or those requiring treatment of sensitive areas, larger head-to-head trials against biologics and long-term safety data are still needed to fully define its place in the current treatment landscape.

Limitations

This is a narrative review rather than a meta-analysis or systematic review, which does not include comparative research between roflumilast and other PDE4 inhibitors or psoriasis therapies.

Conclusions

In conclusion, as summarized in Table 1, both topical and oral roflumilast represent promising practical alternative for plaque psoriasis management, particularly where convenience, cost, or site-specific application is prioritized. Future research should prioritize long-term comparative effectiveness studies against other systemic agents and biologics.

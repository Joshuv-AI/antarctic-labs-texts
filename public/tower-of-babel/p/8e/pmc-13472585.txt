Effectiveness of a Structured Pharmaceutical Educational Intervention on Maternal Knowledge of Pediatric Vaccines, Immunization Schedules, and Vaccine-Preventable Diseases: A Quasi-Experimental Study
Authors: Selvaraj Hemalatha, Kandasamy Maheswari, Gaoudam Nadarajane, Panneerselvam Periasamy
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Immunization is a cornerstone of public health, preventing childhood morbidity and mortality. However, inadequate maternal knowledge often leads to incomplete immunization coverage.Aim:To evaluate the effectiveness of a structured teaching program (STP) on knowledge regarding the immunization schedule among mothers of children under five.Methodology:A quasi-experimental one-group pretest–posttest design was employed, involving 50 mothers in Sanarpalayam, Namakkal District, selected via purposive sampling. A structured questionnaire with 30 multiple-choice questions assessed knowledge. Data were analyzed using descriptive (mean, standard deviation [SD]) and inferential statistics (paired t-test, Chi-square test).Results:The mean knowledge score improved from 13.5 (SD = 4.05) in the pretest to 24.5 (SD = 5.03) in the posttest, with a 36% increase in the mean percentage score (t = 19.65, P < 0.05). Significant associations were observed between pretest knowledge and age (χ2 =11.56, P = 0.012) and education (χ2 =13.03, P = 0.025).Conclusion:The STP significantly enhanced maternal knowledge of immunization schedules, emphasizing the need for ongoing maternal education to improve vaccination coverage and child health outcomes.

INTRODUCTION

Immunization is a highly effective and cost-efficient public health intervention that prevents morbidity, disability, and mortality from vaccine-preventable diseases. The World Health Organization (WHO) estimates that timely immunization prevents over 2 million child deaths annually.[1] In India, the Universal Immunization Programme and initiatives like Mission Indradhanush aim to achieve full immunization coverage. However, coverage remains suboptimal, with only 62% of children fully immunized according to the National Family Health Survey (NFHS-5, 2019–2021), largely due to low maternal awareness and vaccine misconceptions.[2]

Mothers, as primary caregivers, play a critical role in ensuring the timely vaccination of their children. Their knowledge directly influences immunization uptake.[3] Educational interventions, such as structured teaching programs (STPs), have shown promise in addressing knowledge gaps and improving health behaviors.[4] This study evaluates the effectiveness of an STP in enhancing maternal knowledge regarding the immunization schedule among mothers of children under five in Sanarpalayam, Namakkal District, India.

OBJECTIVES

This study aimed to assess the baseline knowledge of the immunization schedule among mothers of children under five, evaluate the effectiveness of an STP in enhancing their knowledge, and examine the association between pretest knowledge scores and selected demographic variables. The study hypothesized that there would be a significant difference between pretest and posttest knowledge scores (H1) and that pretest knowledge scores would have a significant association with selected demographic variables (H2).

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study design and setting

A quasi-experimental one-group pretest–posttest design was used. The study was conducted in Sanarpalayam, a rural area in Namakkal District, Tamil Nadu, India, where access to health education is limited.

Population and sampling

The study population comprised mothers of children under five. A sample of 50 mothers was selected using non-probability purposive sampling based on the following inclusion criteria: mothers aged 18–40 years with at least one child under 5 years, who were willing to participate, and who were residing in Sanarpalayam.

Data collection tool

A self-structured questionnaire with 30 multiple-choice questions was developed to assess knowledge regarding the immunization schedule, covering vaccine types, timing, and importance. The tool’s reliability was established using the split-half method (r = 0.74). Each correct response was scored 1, with a maximum score of 30.

Intervention

The STP was a 45-min educational session delivered via lectures, visual aids, and discussions, focusing on the national immunization schedule and vaccine benefits, and addressing common myths. The intervention was conducted in a community hall by trained health educators.

Data collection procedure

After obtaining informed consent, a pretest was administered to assess baseline knowledge. The STP was delivered, followed by a posttest 1 week later to evaluate knowledge retention. Data collection occurred between March and April 2025.

Ethical considerations

Ethical approval was obtained from the Institutional Ethics Committee. Informed consent was secured, and participants were assured of confidentiality and the right to withdraw without consequences.

Data analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS version 25. Descriptive statistics (mean, standard deviation [SD], percentage) summarized knowledge scores and demographic characteristics. A paired t-test compared pre- and posttest scores, and Chi-square tests assessed associations between pretest knowledge and demographic variables. P-value <0.05 was considered significant.

DISCUSSION

This study demonstrates that the STP significantly improved maternal knowledge regarding the immunization schedule, with a 36% increase in the mean percentage score (t = 19.65, P < 0.05). The shift from predominantly inadequate (76%) to adequate (70%) knowledge levels post-intervention highlights the program’s efficacy. These findings align with Ingale et al.[4] who reported significant knowledge gains following an STP on immunization schedules. The significant associations between pretest knowledge and age and education corroborate existing literature, suggesting that younger and less-educated mothers may have lower health literacy, necessitating targeted interventions.[5]

The study’s rural setting underscores the importance of context-specific education, as rural mothers often face barriers like limited access to healthcare information.[6] The STP’s use of interactive methods (lectures, visual aids) likely enhanced engagement and retention, consistent with Goldstein et al.,[7] who advocate for tailored health communication to address vaccine hesitancy.

Limitations include the small sample size and the lack of a control group, which may limit generalizability. Future studies should employ randomized controlled designs and larger samples to confirm these findings.

CONCLUSION

The STP significantly enhanced maternal knowledge regarding immunization schedules, as evidenced by a 36% improvement in the mean knowledge scores and significant associations with age and education. These results emphasize the critical role of maternal education in improving vaccination coverage and child health outcomes, particularly in underserved areas. Ongoing educational initiatives are essential to sustain these gains and achieve universal immunization goals.

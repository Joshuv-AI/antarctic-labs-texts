Art therapy with Mandala: study protocol for a randomised controlled clinical trial on anxiety and health status among pregnant women in Northern Iran
Authors: Sana Nazmi, Fereshteh Behmanesh, Hossein Ali Nikbakht, Motahareh Govahi, Angela Hamidia
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPregnancy is a period of physiological, psychological, hormonal and social changes. Mothers experience pregnancy anxiety during this period. One treatment used to reduce anxiety is Mandala colouring. In the present study, we intend to determine the effectiveness of Mandala colouring on anxiety and health status in the second trimester of pregnancy in pregnant women.Methods and analysisIn this randomised controlled clinical trial study, 60 women with a gestational age of 14–28 weeks and 43–96 scores on the Vandenberg Pregnancy Anxiety Questionnaire will be selected by convenience sampling and will be assigned to intervention and control groups by block randomisation. Information will be collected using demographic and midwifery, Vandenberg Pregnancy Anxiety and General Health Questionnaires. For pregnant women in the intervention group, Mandala colouring will be performed for 6 days, with each session lasting 30 min. The control group will receive routine care. Analyses will be done using SPSS V.22 software.Ethics and disseminationThis study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Babol University of Medical Sciences (IR.MUBABOL.REC.1402.129). The trial will adhere to the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Findings will be disseminated through publication in peer-reviewed journals and presentation at scientific conferences.Trial registration numberIranian Registry of Clinical Trials (IRCT20180218038783N6).

Introduction

Anxiety disorders are widespread psychiatric disorders that appear throughout life, including during pregnancy.1 Anxiety is said to be an unpleasant and unknown state of fear, which is associated with symptoms such as fatigue, restlessness and heart palpitations.2 3 Pregnancy is a period in which physiological, psychological, hormonal and social changes occur that may increase the risk of emotional distress and psychiatric complications at this stage of women’s lives. Some women may experience pregnancy as a period of happiness, satisfaction and self-fulfilment. However, some people experience changes in their mental health during this period, such as anxiety.4 Anxiety during pregnancy affects about 20% of women.5 Although indicators of maternal anxiety during pregnancy are heterogeneous, symptoms and anxiety disorders are common during pregnancy, and symptoms may range from mild to severe.68

The mental state of the mother can affect the fetus, so anxiety disorders are considered one of the risk factors in pregnancy.9 10 Among the adverse fetal outcomes are low birth weight,11 premature birth, lower Apgar score and fetal growth defects.5 It is also related to causing complications in childhood as well as neurological, cognitive and behavioural development disorders.12 In addition, regarding maternal complications, it has been consistently found that prenatal anxiety is a strong predictor of postpartum anxiety and depression.13 The results of studies have shown that about 25%–50% of women who had pregnancy anxiety show symptoms of postpartum depression 2 months after giving birth.1 Regarding the distinction between prenatal anxiety and general anxiety, some studies show that pregnancy-specific anxiety may be a stronger predictor of negative maternal and fetal outcomes than general prenatal anxiety.10 14

Women with pregnancy anxiety have a strong preference for psychological treatments during pregnancy and are afraid of taking medication because of its teratogenic effects. Among the solutions that have been used to reduce anxiety during pregnancy are cognitive behavioural methods, couple counselling based on spouse support, solution-oriented counselling and relaxation methods.1517 Another method that is considered to reduce anxiety today is a type of creative therapy called art therapy.18 Art therapy can be used as an independent treatment as well as a supportive treatment along with other treatments for mental disorders. Among the cases of art therapy, we can mention painting, colouring, drawing, sculpting and modelling.19 It is believed that art therapy supports anxiety in coping with symptoms and improving their quality of life; it can also improve emotion regulation and self-structuring skills and increase self-awareness and reflective abilities.20

One of the types of art therapy used to reduce anxiety is Mandala colouring.21 From the point of view of art psychotherapists, Mandala painting is one of the basic tools of art psychotherapy that focuses on healing. As a universal archetype, Mandala facilitates the spontaneous expression of the unconscious and becomes a source of healing, meditation and relaxation.22 The results of the studies have shown that the daily colouring of Mandala improves psychological symptoms. Therefore, we can refer to the study by Khademi et al. Gençdoğan et al’s study investigated the effect of Mandala colouring on students’ exam anxiety reduction23 and observed positive results of anxiety reduction among the target groups. Anxiety disorder is highly prevalent during pregnancy and is associated with adverse maternal and neonatal outcomes. While several psychological interventions have been explored, evidence regarding non-pharmacological, accessible and low-cost strategies remains limited. In particular, few randomised controlled trials have investigated the effects of Mandala colouring on anxiety and general health during pregnancy. Given the potential of Mandala colouring as a simple, engaging and low-cost intervention, further research is warranted. The present study will be conducted to determine the effectiveness of Mandala colouring on anxiety and health status in the second trimester of pregnancy in pregnant women. It is hypothesised that Mandala colouring will significantly reduce anxiety levels and improve health status among pregnant women in their second trimester compared with the control group.

Methods and analysis

Study setting

The research population will include all pregnant women referred to Shahid Yahyanejad Hospital in Babol. Shahid Yahyanejad Hospital is one of the hospitals in Babol city where pregnancy and childbirth care are provided for women with low-risk pregnancies. Babol is a key city in Mazandaran province, located in northern Iran. The majority of the population in this area are followers of the Muslim Shiite faith.

Study design

This protocol describes a single-blind randomised controlled clinical trial with random allocation of study units into two parallel groups: intervention and control. This manuscript has been reported in accordance with the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials guidelines (online supplemental material 1). The study design also follows the consolidated standards of Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials clinical trial reports (figure 2). The trial was registered in the Iranian Clinical Trial Registry on 30 December 2023 (https://irct.behdasht.gov.ir/user/trial/73832/view). The recruitment period for this study started on 13 January 2024 and is expected to continue until 30 November 2025.

Flow of participants through the study.

Eligibility criteria

The research samples in this study will include pregnant women who met the inclusion criteria and received a written informed consent form (online supplemental material 2). The criteria for entering the study include the following: second trimester of pregnancy (14–28 weeks), singleton and low-risk pregnancy, reading and writing literacy, not taking depression and anti-anxiety and depression drugs and scoring 43–94 from Vandenberg Pregnancy Anxiety Questionnaire (VAQ) (the middle 50% of possible scores). Exclusion criteria include: multiple pregnancy suffering from a known physical and mental illness, obtaining a minimum score of 42 and a maximum score of 95 from the pregnancy anxiety questionnaire, having a stressful event in the last 6 months, such as the death of a first-degree family member, unwillingness to continue participating in the study, taking psychoactive and anti-anxiety and depression drugs. Withdrawal criteria are: withdrawal from the study, fetal death and 2 days without Mandala staining.

Interventions

After obtaining written informed consent from people who meet the inclusion criteria, the VAQ will be presented for completion. Then pregnant women who scored 43–94 will be randomly assigned to two intervention and control groups. In this way, the sealed envelopes in which two codes A and B are prepared separately and in the number of samples are given to a member of the research team who does not interfere in the sampling. After selecting the samples based on the inclusion criteria, the researcher who performs the sampling (a midwife) will call that person and get the code for each person. People who score above 94 are diagnosed as having high anxiety and will be referred to a psychiatrist. After this stage, demographic information questionnaires and General Health questionnaire (GHQ) are provided to the samples. The samples of the intervention group will be given six designs of Mandala along with coloured pencils and they will be asked to colour the designs of Mandala every day for 6 days and for at least half an hour each time. During colouring, the subject will be in a calm environment and concentrate. The six simple and attractive Mandala designs were selected and provided uniformly to all participants. Each session was planned for a minimum of 30 min, and participants could continue longer if needed. The mothers were encouraged to maintain their focus for at least 30 min, as most could finish within this time. To confirm adherence, participants were asked to present their completed Mandalas to the researcher when submitting the second-phase questionnaires after the six intervention sessions.

Both the intervention and control groups will receive identical standard prenatal care, including regular obstetric visits, routine health assessments and essential educational counselling (eg, nutrition, exercise and breastfeeding). The only difference is that the control group would not participate in the structured Mandala art therapy sessions. Both the intervention and control groups will complete the pregnancy anxiety questionnaire and the GHQ again immediately and 1 month after the intervention. Both intervention and control groups will receive routine pregnancy care. Personal data of potential and enrolled participants will be gathered by a researcher, kept confidential without names being disclosed and maintained throughout the trial for privacy protection.

Outcomes

The primary outcome of the study is the average pregnancy anxiety score, which will be evaluated using the standard VAQ.

The secondary outcome is the average general health score, which will be calculated using the standard GHQ.

Sample size

The sample size was considered according to the primary outcome of the study. For this purpose, based on previous studies21 on the primary outcome (mean and SD of the total anxiety score before and after the Mandala intervention) in the intervention group based on the following values and taking into account the effect size of 0.77 and also the confidence level of 95% and the power of 80%, using G Power V.3 software, a minimum number of 16 cases in each group is required as the initial sample. However, to perform the analysis of covariance analysis to compare the values after the intervention, considering the basic values of at least 26A sample is required in each group, and taking into account a 15% drop, a total of at least 60 samples (30 people in each group) was determined as the final sample size.

Assignment of interventions: allocation

Sequence generation

The block randomisation method with ten blocks will be used to assign the target group to one of the two intervention groups and the control group (two groups A and B). According to the block randomisation protocol (generated by Random Allocation Software), participants will be assigned to one of two groups with a 1:1 ratio, so the researcher cannot predict which group the next person will be in.

Concealment mechanism

It is possible to reveal the random sequence without hiding it, which ultimately weakens the randomisation process. In this study, to hide the random allocation, sealed opaque envelopes with a random sequence will be used. Based on the sample size of this research, a number of envelopes are prepared. Each of the random sequences created is recorded on a card, which is placed inside the envelopes in order. At the time of registration, based on the order of entry of eligible participants into the study, one of the envelopes will be opened, and the allocated group of that participant will be revealed.

Implementation

Registration of samples and obtaining informed consent for their participation will be done by the researcher. After the qualified sample is determined, the researcher contacts the supervisor to determine the group for the sample, and the supervisor determines the number assigned to sample A or B by opening the envelope.

Assignment of interventions: blinding

In this study, considering that the type of intervention is known after randomisation and the completion of the questionnaires is also self-reported, blinding cannot be done in the first stage for researchers and pregnant women. Blinding will only be done for the data analyst.

Data collection and management

The data collection instruments in this study will be demographic and obstetric information forms, pregnancy anxiety questionnaire and GHQ.

Demographic and obstetric information questionnaire

It includes questions related to age, education, occupation, spouse’s education, spouse’s occupation, length of marriage, housing status, gestational age, number of pregnancies and deliveries, and planned pregnancies.

Vandenberg Pregnancy Anxiety Questionnaire

The Pregnancy Anxiety Questionnaire will be used, which was designed by Vandenberg24 to measure pregnancy-related anxiety. This questionnaire includes 17 items covering five components: fear of childbirth, fear of giving birth to a physically or mentally disabled child, fear of changes in marital relations, fear of mood changes and their impact on the child, and fear of changes in the mother’s personal life. Responses will be rated on a 7-point Likert scale (from ‘not at all true’ to ‘completely true’), scored from 1 to 7, respectively. Subscale scores will be calculated by summing the relevant items, and the total score will be obtained by summing all items, resulting in an overall range of 17–119. Higher scores will indicate higher levels of pregnancy anxiety. For the present study, individuals within the lowest and highest 25% of scores will be excluded; therefore, pregnant women with scores between 43 and 94 will be eligible. The validity of the VAQ has been confirmed by Askarizadeh et al Reliability has been supported, with Cronbach’s alpha coefficients above 0.7 in previous studies.25

Goldberg GHQ

This questionnaire has four subscales as follows:

The first subscale (A) of physical symptoms includes items about a person’s feelings about his health status and his feeling of fatigue, and includes physical symptoms. This subscale evaluates bodily sensory perceptions that are often associated with emotional arousal.

The second subscale (B) of anxiety includes items related to anxiety and insomnia.

The third subscale (C) social functioning and perception disorder: it measures the extent of people’s ability to deal with professional demands and daily life issues and reveals their feelings about how to cope with common life situations.

The fourth subscale (D) of depression includes items related to severe depression and personal tendency to commit suicide.

The overall score of each individual is obtained from the sum of the scores of the four subscales. Questions 1–7 measure physical symptoms. Questions 15–21 measure performance impairment. Questions 22–28 measure depression. Questions 8–14 measure anxiety. Iranian studies have established the reliability and validity of the questionnaire.2628 Javanmard and Mamaghani reported the correlation coefficient for test subscales equal to 87%.28

Statistics methods

For statistical analysis after data collection and to ensure the correctness of data entry. In order to check the level of pregnancy anxiety and health status in all participants, it will be reported from the central index of the average and the dispersion index of the SD or in the form of numbers and percentages. In order to check the related statistical tests, first the normality of the data will be checked using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Also, in order to compare qualitative demographic variables in two groups, a χ2 test will be used, and if the conditions of the χ2 test are not met, Fisher’s test will be used. An independent t-test will be used to compare quantitative demographic variables in groups, such as age, gestational age, number of children and duration of marriage. Also, an ANCOVA (Analysis of Covariance) test will be considered to compare the total score of pregnancy anxiety and health status and their areas in two groups, considering the measurements before the intervention. Mean difference and CI of 95% are used to show the effect size. All analyses will be considered by SPSS software V.22 and significance level (p<0.05).

Ethics and dissemination

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Babol University of Medical Sciences (IR.MUBABOL.REC.1402.129). Prior to participation, all participants will be provided with both written and verbal information regarding the study’s purpose, procedures, potential risks and benefits. Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants. Confidentiality will be ensured through the use of anonymous identifiers for data collection and secure storage of all personal information. Participants will be informed of their right to withdraw from the study at any time without any penalty. The results of this study will be disseminated through publication in peer-reviewed journals and presentation at scientific conferences.

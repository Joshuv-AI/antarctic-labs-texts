Serotonin, cortisol, and DHEA-S levels in anxious and depressive pregnant women living with HIV
Authors: María del Pilar Meza-Rodríguez, Philippe Leff-Gelman, Diana Medina-Bastidas, Miroslava Avila-García, Ricardo Figueroa-Damián, Ignacio Camacho-Arroyo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Pregnancy in women living with human immunodeficiency virus (WLWH) represents an important challenge for maternal-fetal health. Besides, they can also present anxiety (Anx) and depression (Dep). Imbalances in serotonin (5-HT), dehydroepiandrosterone sulfate (DHEA-S), and cortisol (CORT) levels can contribute to Anx and Dep manifestations. Currently, there is not enough data about the neuroendocrine and neurochemical changes in pregnant WLWH with affective disorders. This study aimed to characterize 5-HT, DHEA-S, and CORT plasma levels in Mexican pregnant WLWH presenting Anx/Dep. Forty-two adult pregnant women were recruited during the third trimester of gestation at the National Institute of Perinatology in Mexico during 2019–2022. These patients were divided into three groups: (1) pregnant WLWH with Anx/Dep (n = 16), (2) pregnant without HIV but with Anx/Dep (n = 12), and (3) healthy pregnant women without Anx/Dep (n = 14). WLWH presented a marked reduction in 5-HT (41.33 ± 39.37 ng/dL) compared to non-infected pregnant women with Anx/Dep (220.2 ± 151.8 ng/dL) and the healthy group (370.0 ± 145.3 ng/dL). Anx/Dep infected and uninfected pregnant women showed a significant reduction in DHEA-S levels (86.58 ± 30.59 and 76.9 ± 36.7 µg/dL, respectively) compared to healthy subjects (149.7 ± 44.6 µg/dL). Anx and Dep symptoms were inversely correlated with 5-HT and DHEA-S levels. No significant differences were observed in CORT levels among the three groups (p = 0.094). Our results suggest the presence of a disbalance in 5-HT and DHEA-S levels in pregnant WLWH with affective symptoms.

Background

Major depressive disorder (MDD) is the third cause of disability worldwide [1, 2]. Pregnant women show a high prevalence of depression (Dep) [3], that ranges from 2 to 21% [4, 5] and may increase until 31% postpartum [5]. Anxiety (Anx) disorders are also highly prevalent in women [6, 7], ranging from 15.8 to 25% during pregnancy [8, 9] and until 31.7% in postpartum [10]. Anx in pregnancy has adverse consequences for maternal mental health and birth outcomes, being also a risk factor for postpartum Dep [11, 12]. High levels of stress and Anx during pregnancy have been associated with obstetric complications such as an increase in cesarean section rate and preterm delivery [10, 13].

Perinatal Dep is related to a dysfunction of serotonin (5-HT) neurotransmission and activation of the indoleamine 2,3 dioxygenase (IDO), leading to the depletion of brain 5-HT levels [14, 15]. A downregulation of 5-HT1A receptors in regions essential to the regulation of emotion, psychomotor function, and reward [15, 16] has been associated with the worsening of depressive and suicidal symptoms [15, 17].

Prenatal stress and Anx in pregnant women produced well-known effects on their neonates such as low gestational age, prematurity, and low insulin-like growth factor levels [10]. Prenatal Anx responses continue negatively affecting the offspring’s postnatal life [10, 18]. Newborns showing low dopamine and serotonin (5-HT) levels displayed greater frontal electroencephalogram activation and lower vagal tone, spent more time in deep sleep and less time in alert states, and had less motor maturity and autonomic stability [18].

The hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) and sympathetic-adrenal-medullary axes participate in the stress response [19]. The hypothalamic dysfunction promotes several hallmark symptoms of Dep associated with high circulating cortisol (CORT) levels [20]. During the third trimester, pregnant women with severe Anx exhibit increased levels of CORT and decreased dehydroepiandrosterone sulfate (DHEA-S) serum levels [21]; it is well known that DHEA-S exhibits neuroprotective, antioxidant, antihypertensive, anti-glucocorticoid, and anti-inflammatory properties [22].

Annually, over 1.5 million women living with HIV (WLWH) give birth [23, 24]. Mental disorders in pregnant WLWH have been associated with several factors [25, 26]. In low and middle-income countries the prevalence of perinatal depression among pregnant WLWH ranges from 23 to 53%, more than twice the estimated rates in the general population of pregnant women [26–31]; impressively, the prevalence of severe Anx rises to 70% in these women [32]. Pregnant WLWH displaying MDD showed poor adherence to the antiretroviral therapy, increasing the risk of vertical transmission, complicating the disease compared to pregnant WLWH without affective symptoms, and doubling the risk of death [33–35].

In people living with HIV (PLWH), there has been reported an enhanced degradation of tryptophan (5-HT precursor), activation of IDO, and depletion of 5-HT brain levels suggesting a link between increased 5-HT catabolism and intensity of depressive symptoms [36, 37]. Scarce information exists concerning the psychological components and biochemical factors implicated in mood-related disorders in pregnant WLWH. Therefore, the present study focused on characterizing 5-HT, DHEA-S, and CORT plasma levels of pregnant WLWH exhibiting Dep and Anx.

Materials and methods

Study design and patient enrollment

We performed an observational, cross-sectional study at the National Institute of Perinatology (INPer) in México City, from January 2019 to December 2022. The approval of the protocol was obtained from the Institutional Research and Ethical Committees (Project Number, 2017-3-128).

Third-trimester adult pregnant WLWH (28–40 gestation weeks) exhibiting symptoms of Anx plus Dep without psychotropic treatment who attended the prenatal HIV clinic care at the INPer, were invited to participate in the study. In addition, women without HIV displaying symptoms of Anx/Dep and healthy pregnant women without affective disorders, who were attending the Prenatal Control Outpatient Unit of the INPer, were also invited to participate. All the patients enrolled in the study signed a written informed consent. Patients were clinically and obstetrically evaluated at their admission. Women with a history of psychotropic treatment, complications like preterm labor, negative fetal outcomes at the time of recruitment, and chronic diseases, except for HIV were not included. Elimination criteria included participants with incomplete questionnaires, incomplete laboratory tests, and inconsistencies in their evaluation of mood-related disorders.

Psychology evaluation and patient clustering

Once enrolled, patients were remitted to the Neuroscience Department to evaluate their emotional status and the presence of mood-related disorders. Clinical and sociodemographic variables, including familiar dynamics, were collected and uploaded into a database during the initial interview. For evaluations, patients answered the psychometric battery designed for this purpose: (1) The Beck Depression Inventory (BDI-II) one of the most used instruments for symptoms detection of Dep, the scores are divided into 0–9 absent Dep, 10–16 mild Dep, 17–29 moderate Dep, > 30 severe Dep [38]; (2) The translated version to Spanish of State-Trait Anxiety Inventory (STAI) better known as IDARE standardized for the Mexican population [39]. For the population studied the scores of STAI-S > 43 points were compatible with the presence of anxiety-state, while STAI-T > 45 points considered an anxiety-trait [40]; (3) The score of the Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale (EPDS) calibrated for Mexican pregnant women was divided into absent of Dep between 0 and 13 points, and presence of Dep > 14 points [41]; (4) In the case of the 30-item Goldberg General Health Questionnaire (GHQ-30) [42], translated, adapted and validated for Mexican population, we considered a score of < 7 points as absence of Dep, while a score > 8 points was compatible with the presence of Dep [43, 44]; (5) The Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS) a worldwide instrument used to evaluate Anx and Dep in hospital setting, the scores of 0–7 showed absence of symptoms, 8–10 moderate symptoms, and 11–21 severe symptoms [45]; (6) The mini-mental state examination (MMSE), a rapid test of 30 points with the objective to the cognitive worsening detection, shows that a highest point means less cognitive worsening [46, 47]; the translation for Spanish speaker patients [48], shows that the scores between 27 and 30 points detected patients without cognitive worsening, 26 − 24 points are suspicious of cognitive worsening, a score of 23 − 12 points is obtained in patients with mild to moderate cognitive worsening, and patients with less that 11 − 9 points have a moderate to severe cognitive worsening. Pregnant women diagnosed with some mood-disorders received psychological management and treatment. Patients with severe symptoms were referred to the INPer Neuroscience Department.

Blood sampling

Blood samples from the participants were obtained and stored in 6 mL Vacutainer EDTA K2 tubes (Becton–Dickinson. New Jersey, USA). Sampling was performed between 7:00 and 9:00 am. Plasma was separated with a centrifuge Rotofix 32 A (Hettich, Westphalia, Germany) at 1600 × G at room temperature for 10 min. Afterward, plasma was stored in 1.5 mL microcentrifuge tubes at − 70 °C until further use.

5-HT and plasma steroid quantification

The quantification of 5-HT and steroids was performed using an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). For 5-HT, we used the 5-HT Elisa Kit (Abnova, Walnut CA. USA); CORT was measured using a Competitive Human ELISA Kit (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA USA), and DHEA-S was measured by a Human DHEA-S Elisa Kit (MyBioSource, San Diego, CA. USA). All determinations were performed by following the manufacturer’s instructions. Samples were read at a microplate spectrophotometer MultiSkan Sky (ThermoScientific, Waltham, Massachusetts, USA). Calibration ranges were: 5-HT, 10.2 − 2500 ng/dL, CORT, 50–3200 µg/dL, and DHEAS, 10–1000 µg/dL. Intra-assay covariances were 4.5% for 5-HT, 5.6 for CORT, and 6% for DHEA-S, and inter-assay covariances were 6.0% for 5-HT, 6.3% for CORT, and 8% for DHEA-S.

Statistical analysis

Demographic variables were described with measures of central tendency. Due to the reduced sample size, the normality of the variables was analyzed with the Shapiro-Wilk test. Clinical variables were analyzed with the Kruskal-Wallis test including a Bonferroni post hoc test for continuous and ordinal variables. Psychological tests, steroid and serotonin plasma concentrations were analyzed by Spearman Rho correlation coefficients. Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS v25 (IBM, New York. USA) and p values ≤ 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

Demographic characteristics

Forty-two pregnant women in the third trimester of gestation were accepted to participate in the study. Patients were clustered into three groups: (1) 16 pregnant WLWH with Anx/Dep symptoms; (2) 12 pregnant women without HIV infection but with Anx/Dep symptoms; and (3) 14 healthy pregnant women without HIV infection and no Anx/Dep symptoms.

Most participants were in the half-second decade of life (Kruskal Wallis, p = 0.245), cohabiting with their partners but without getting married (X2, p = 0.892). The most common education levels were middle and high school (X2, p = 0.024), and they use to work in home labor (X2, p = 0.345). Regarding family dynamics, the healthy group considered sufficient, while the perception of Anx/Dep groups were from regular to poor. Finally, the WLWH with Anx/Dep and pregnant women with Anx/Dep HIV- considered their family dynamic sufficient to regular (X2, p = 0.045) as shown in Table 1.

Emotional status and mood disorders

The highest Anx/Dep symptoms were observed in the pregnant WLWH as it was recorded in BDI-II (Kruskal-Wallis, p < 0.001), EDPS (p < 0.001) and HADS (p < 0.001) psychological tests (Table 2). The groups of symptomatic patients displayed significant differences (p < 0.001) in the median scores of STAI-S and STAI-T for state and trait anxiety compared with the control healthy pregnant women group (Table 2). In the same line, significant differences (p < 0.001) were found between the median score estimated for the GHQ-30 test in both infected and non-infected symptomatic pregnant women and the control group (Table 2). All groups presented similar median scores in the MMSE regarding the cognitive impairment test.

5-HT and steroid plasma levels

Infected and non-infected women with Anx/Dep presented a marked significant diminution in 5-HT and DHEA-S levels compared with the healthy group. 5-HT levels in the Anx/Dep HIV- group were 220.29 ± 151.82 ng/dL, whereas those in the control group were 370.03 ± 145.37 ng/dL. The lowest 5-HT levels were observed in WLWH with Anx/Dep (41.33 ± 39.37 ng/dL) (Kruskal-Wallis, p < 0.001). as shown in Fig. 1; Table 2. Post hoc test with Bonferroni correction showed that 5-HT levels were different between the healthy group and infected Anx/Dep (p < 0.001), also there were differences between the infected group and Anx/Dep non-infected (p = 0.013). No differences were found between the healthy group and Anx/Dep non-infected (p = 0.192). DHEA-S levels were similar and lower in pregnant WLWH (86.58 ± 30.59 µg/L) and non-infected pregnant women with Anx/Dep (76.96 ± 36.79 µg/dL) than in the healthy group (149.75 ± 44.61 µg/dL) (Kruskal-Wallis, p < 0.001). A post hoc test with Bonferroni correction was performed on the differences between the healthy group and Anx/dep uninfected (p = 0.001) and Anx/Dep HIV (p = 0.009). Anx/Dep and Anx/dep with HIV showed no differences (p = 0.361). Unexpectedly, no significant differences were observed in CORT levels among the three groups (Kruskal-Wallis, p = 0.094) (Figs. 1, 2; Table 2).

Correlations between biochemical parameters and clinician-rated instruments

The psychological tests’ mean scores were negatively correlated with 5-HT and DHEA-S plasma levels in both symptomatic pregnant WLWH and HIV- pregnant women groups (p < 0.001) (Table 3). Anx and Dep symptoms were inversely correlated with 5-HT and DHEA-S levels. High scores for state (STAI-S), and trait (STAI-T) Anx observed in both symptomatic pregnant WLWH and HIV- pregnant women groups were negatively correlated with the estimated lower 5-HT and DHEA-S plasma levels (p < 0.001) (Table 3; Fig. 3). A negative correlation was identified between psychological discomfort and plasma 5HT and DHEA-S levels; a similar situation was observed with Anx and Dep. Psychological discomfort, Anx (trait-state) and Dep, were independent of the HIV infection. Also, DHEA-S levels were not influenced by the HIV serological status (p > 0.05) (Table 3). CORT levels correlated negatively with Anx and Dep in the HADS and EDPS psychological tests (Table 3).

Discussion

The HIV pandemic continues to be a significant global health problem [49]. PLWH are confronted with chronic psychosocial stressors that may lead to chronic HPA axis dysregulation and functional changes of CORT [50, 51]. In these people, CORT has been linked to stress and HIV-related health outcomes [52], also they can exhibit aberrant functioning of the HPA axis [53, 54] with an inverse correlation between basal CORT levels and lymphocytes CD4 + counts [55], and a positive correlation with DHEA-S serum levels [53–56].

WLWH usually presents Anx and/or Dep, and posttraumatic stress symptoms. Similarly, pregnant WLWH displayed high levels of state and trait anxiety (STAI-S/T), MD (BDI-II, EDPS, HADS) and emotional distress (GHQ-30) [57]. Pregnant women with MDD exhibit an abnormal HPA axis activity with hypercortisolism, possibly due to the high levels of secreted placental corticotropin-releasing hormone, enhancing an overt transfer of glucocorticoids to the fetal compartment [20, 58, 59]. In pregnant WLWH and pregnant women without HIV who displayed high levels of Anx and Dep, we found a decrease in DHEA-S levels compared to healthy women controls. DHEA-S levels in pregnant women with severe Anx were significantly lower than in those displaying no Anx [21]. Interestingly, we just found a non-significant decrease in CORT levels in WLWH with AnX/Dep compared with healthy women and women without HIV but with Anx/Dep.

Previous studies showed that AIDS patients with adrenal insufficiency displayed lower serum CORT levels upon short corticotropin (ACTH) stimulation test and had an inverse correlation between ACTH and CORT levels compared to non-AIDS subjects, suggesting a suboptimal adrenal reserve [60]. Thus, subjects with AIDS exhibiting psychosocial stress and intense anxiety may have a reduced adrenal reserve (upon constant adrenal stimulation) and as AIDS worsens, signs or symptoms of adrenal insufficiency may become critical [60, 61].

Dysfunction of 5-HT neurotransmission and activation of the indoleamine 2,3 dioxygenase (IDO) in pregnant women with MDD, lead to the depletion of brain 5-HT levels [14, 15]. Our results support previous findings in pregnant WLWH women exhibiting Anx/Dep, which showed a significant reduction of brain 5-HT levels and high levels of depressive symptoms, with an increase in 5-HT metabolism, associated with an enhanced degradation of tryptophan, and activation of IDO [36, 37]. The lowest 5-HT levels found in pregnant WLWH with Anx/Dep suggest that HIV infection can alter the serotoninergic system impacting circulatory 5-HT levels and the intensity of Anx/Dep symptoms in pregnant women.

In the same line, the reduction of 5-HT plasma levels in the symptomatic pregnant WLWH group compared to pregnant women without HIV and the healthy control groups suggests that the first group of pregnant women is highly vulnerable to the depletion of neuronal 5-HT, inducing exacerbated mood-associated symptoms. Thus, pregnant WLWH displaying affective symptoms will require an efficient mood-disorder treatment to increase both 5-HT and DHEA-S levels, and therefore, reduce the affective symptoms.

Another significant result observed in our study was that the pregnant population did not exhibit impaired cognitive function. Thus, it appears that cognition is not linked to mood-associated symptoms. Our work demonstrates a marked disbalance in 5-HT and DHEA-S levels in pregnant WLWH with affective symptoms.

Limitations

The number of patients with Anx and Dep enrolled in the study was limited. Therefore, further exploration using molecular and immune approaches is needed in more pregnant WLWH exhibiting affective symptoms, and comparing the viral load in the recruited population, in addition to estimating Th1 and Th2 immune responses, adrenal steroids, and brain trophic factors among other mediators. All patients included in the study did not receive any psychotropic or antidepressant medication previously or at least 1 year before. It would be interesting to analyze patients on anxiolytic and antidepressant medication in future research.

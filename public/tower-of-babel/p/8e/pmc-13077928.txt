Evaluation of the diagnostic performance of iChroma serological test in high SARS-CoV-2 seroprevalence setting in Gabon
Authors: Armel Mintsa, Steeve Mbomo, Veronique M´pirah, Amel Alame Emane, Joseph Ndanga Tiagni, Marien J M Mbadinga, Ngondziga Ongala Djeff Chirack, Nzengue Jean Jerome, A C Maloupazoa Siawaya, Amandine Mveang Nzoghe, Fabrice Lotola-Mougeni, Joel Fleury Djoba Siawaya, Paulin N Essone
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAbstractCoronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) has been a major global health concern over the past few years. The African region has remained less affected in terms of mortality, however, estimating the true cumulative incidence in this region is challenging due to several limitations. Serological tests can help evaluate the SARS-CoV-2 exposure at both individual and population levels. Several serological rapid tests have been developed, but their performance at population level is not always known. This current study aimed to evaluate the diagnostic performance of the iChroma test for the rapid detection of SARS-CoV-2 antibodies. Participants included in this study were recruited during the national seroprevalence household survey. The WANTAI SARS-CoV-2 Ab Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay (ELISA), was used as reference test. All samples were evaluated using both WANTAI ELISA and iChroma. In addition, a well-characterised SARS-CoV-2 rapid test (SARS-CoV-2 rapid antibody test 2.0, Roche) was evaluated on a reduced number of sample as control for rapid test using Cobas instrument. A total of 1128 samples were analysed in this study. Of the study participants, 51,2% were women with a mean age of 36 (SD: 16). Overall, 91,6% of participants had a positive WANTAI SARS-CoV-2 Ab ELISA results. In this high-seroprevalence setting, the iChroma test showed a poor agreement with the ELISA test, with a kappa value of 28 (19–38). This rapid test demonstrated a good sensitivity but a poor specificity in this setting. In contrast, we found an excellent agreement between the Roche SARS-CoV-2 rapid antibody test 2.0. and WANTAI SARS-CoV-2 Ab ELISA with a kappa value of 92% (86–98%). In this high seroprevalence setting, the specificity of iChroma test was significantly reduced and showed poorly correlation with the ELISA results. This rapid test has never been evaluated in a large scale population setting. As previously shown in other studies, Roche showed a good diagnostic performance in correlation with ELISA. The current results revealed the poor specificity of iChroma test in high SARS-CoV-2 seroprevalence settings.Clinical TrialNo applicable.Keywords: Diagnostic, Rapide tests, Accuracy, SARS-CoV-2

Introduction

Coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) is a newly identified severe acute respiratory syndrome. The disease was first diagnosed in Wuhan (Hubei, China) in December 2019 [1, 2] and rapidly became a major global health concern during the first trimester of 2020 [3, 4]. The African region was the last to be affected and has remained less impacted in terms of mortality. However, precise estimation of the cumulative incidence of COVID-19 remains challenging due to several limitations, including incomplete contact tracing, weak routine surveillance systems, and limited laboratory capacities [5]. In absence of reliable cumulative incidence data, seroprevalence studies are crucial for understanding the dynamics of Severe Acute Respiratory Syndrome Coronavirus 2 (SARS‑CoV‑2). The virus spread rapidly across African countries: In Gabon, the first case of SARS-CoV-2 infection was diagnosed in March 2020 [6]. A nationwide seroprevalence study, conducted 20 months later, showed that the virus had spread throughout the country from major urban areas to small rural communities, and that more than 80% of the Gabonese population had developed anti-SARS-CoV-2 antibodies [5].

The detection of anti-SARS-CoV-2 immunoglobulin M (IgM) and immunoglobulin G (IgG) has been proposed as an alternative non-invasive method for the clinical diagnosis of COVID-19 [7]. Serological test may be particularly beneficial for patients with low viral load [8]. Anti-SARS-CoV-2 antibody tests can identify previously undiagnosed SARS-CoV-2 carriers with a primed immune system, including individuals for whom reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) results were falsely negative or not performed [9]. At population level, serological tests are useful for epidemiological surveillance and for more accurate estimation of the transmission dynamics of SARS-CoV-2 [10]. Several anti-SARSCoV-2 antibody tests have been developed and deployed worldwide [11]. Different methodologies are used to develop serological tests including enzyme linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), chemiluminescence assay (CLIA), lateral flow immunoassays [12]. The testing method, the target antigen and the type of antibody detected can significantly influence the performance within a given population. Consequently, these assays show variable levels of sensitivity, specificity and concordance [13]. In the present study, the WANTAI SARS-CoV-2 Ab ELISA was used as the reference test [14] to evaluate the diagnostic performance of iChroma d serological assays. The Roche SARS-CoV-2 rapid antibody test 2.0 was evaluated with a small proportion of samples as a secondary control for rapid test.

Materials and methods

Study design

The current study evaluated the diagnostic performance of the iChroma test in a high SARS-CoV-2 seroprevalence setting. Anti-SARS-CoV-2 antibodies were measured in all samples using the WANTAI SARS-CoV-2 Ab ELISA (the reference test) and iChroma assay. We have selected and evaluated the SARS-CoV-2 rapid antibody test 2.0 from Roche with a Cobas instrument, a commercially available rapid tests with known performance, using a reduced number of samples to see how the current results compared with known data on rapid tests. Samples were randomised in excel spreadsheet and the first 180 samples were selected for this secondary evaluation. All tests were performed at the National Laboratory of Public Health, Gabon.

We recently conducted a national SARSCoV-2 seroprevalence study in Gabon. This study was a household survey in which participants were recruited from all nine (9) provinces of the country. Household were randomly selected and visited, and all household members, present at the time of the visit were invited to participate. Whole blood samples were collected in dry tubes and transported to the laboratory. Serum was obtained after centrifugation. Aliquots were stored into 1.5 Eppendorf tubes at −80 °C. The Estuaire province, which includes Libreville (main city), is the most populated province in the country. More than 50% of samples collected in the original national seroprevalence study were from this province. To evaluate the diagnostic performance of the tests, we focused on this province. The current study included all samples collected in the city of Libreville and its surrounding areas. Samples were thawed at room temperature in 2024 for the different analysis.

Ethical approval

Ethic approval was obtained from the national Gabonese Ethic Committee to conduct this study. All participants provided written informed consent before inclusion.

Inclusion criteria

All samples recruited in Librevile (the capital of Gabon where more than 50% of the population leave) during the national SARS-CoV-2 seroprevalence study were included in this study.

Exclusion criteria

Samples with insufficient amount of serum were excluded in the study.

WANTAI SARS-CoV-2 Ab ELISA

The WANTAI SARS-CoV-2 Ab ELISA serological test (Beijing Wantai Biological Pharmacy Enterprise Co., Ltd, China) (13) was used as the reference test. This ELISA detects total anti–SARS-CoV-2 antibodies including IgG and IgM, in serum (Table 1). Serum samples were thawed at room temperature prior to testing. ELISA was performed according to the manufacturer´s instructions. The performance characteristics of the ELISA are summarised in Table 1. The WANTAI SARS-CoV-2 Ab ELISA tests were donated by the World Health Organisation (WHO) through the “Unity study group” [15].

Roche anti-SARS-CoV-2 assay

The SARS-CoV-2 rapid antibody test 2.0(Roche), is a sandwich immunoassay in which biotinylated SARS-CoV-2 specific recombinant antigens, ruthenium-labelled SARS-CoV-2–specific recombinant antigens, and anti–SARS-CoV-2 antibodies form a specific sandwich complex. The addition of streptavidin-coated micro particles and biotin to this complex induces chemiluminescence. An anti–SARS-CoV-2 index is calculated by comparison with the mean chemiluminescent signal of a calibrator, with a reported cut off index (COI) of 1.0 for positivity. This assay was performed on the Cobas platform

iChroma™ COVID-19 Ab test

The iChroma™ COVID-19 Ab test is a competitive immunodetection assay. When the dilution buffer is mixed with the serum sample, anti-SARS-CoV-2 antibodies (Abs) in the sample bind to the fluorescence-labelled SARS-CoV-2 spike receptor-binding domain (RBD) antigen in the detection buffer. The resulting complex migrates along a nitrocellulose matrix, where covalent RBD–Angiotensin-converting enzyme 2 (ACE2)–biotin complexes are immobilised on the streptavidin capture line (test line), thereby interfering with the binding of Abs to the fluorescence-labelled antigen. The test was performed according to the manufacturer’s instructions. After incubation, the cartridge containing the serum sample and dilution buffer was inserted into the iChroma™II, a fluorescence and europium nanoparticle scanning instrument (Boditech, Gang-won-do, South Korea).

Statistical analysis

An initial descriptive analysis was conducted to characterize the study population. Continuous variables were summarised using the mean and standard deviation (SD), while categorical variables were presented as proportions. The diagnostic accuracy of the WANTAI and iChroma assays for detecting COVID-19 was assessed using sensitivity, specificity, Negative Predictive Value (NPV), and Positive Predictive Value as metrics, with WANTAI assay serving as the reference method. Agreement between the methods was evaluated using the kappa coefficient. All statistical analyses were performed using R software version 4.1.3, ensuring robust and reliable results.

Results

The data presented here were collected between 1 and 15 November 2021 in Libreville as part of the national SARS-CoV-2 seroprevalence study. Samples from all regions of Libreville and its surroundings were included. A total of 1128 participants were enrolled, and all serum samples were tested using the WANTAI SARS-CoV-2 Ab ELISA and iChroma assay. Additionally, 180 serum samples were randomly selected and tested for SARS-CoV-2 antibodies with Roche (Fig. 1). Overall, 51,2% of participants were female, and the mean age was 36 years (SD:16).

Diagnostic performance of Ichromax and Cobas technologies

SARS-CoV-2 antibodies were detected in 91,6% (1033 positive cases) of participants using the WANTAI SARS-CoV-2 Ab ELISA, while 95 participants (8,4%) tested negative (Table 2). When these results were compared with those of the iChroma rapid test, 1056 participants (93,6%) were positives for SARS-CoV-2 antibodies. Among these, 67 participants who were initially negative with the WANTAI SARS-CoV-2 Ab ELISA tested positive with the rapid test iChroma assay and were therefore considered as false positive. No SARS-CoV-2 antibodies were detected in 72 participants (6,4%) using the iChroma rapid test. Of these, 61% (44/72) were positive with the WANTAI ELISA, indicating a high proportion of false-negative results with iChroma. Only 28 participants (39%) who tested negative with the WANTAI ELISA were also negative with the iChroma assay. The sensitivity of iChroma test was 94% (92–95%) when compared to WANTAI SARS-CoV-2 Ab ELISA. However, this high sensitivity was counterbalanced by a low specificity of 39% (28–50%) in this high-prevalence setting. The positive and negative predictive values were 96 and 29%, respectively. Overall, we observed a low level of agreement between WANTAI SARS-CoV-2 Ab ELISA and iChroma test, with a kappa value of 28% (95% CI: 19–38%).

A subset of 180 participants was evaluated with a second SARS-CoV-2 rapid antibody test 2.0 (Cobas, Roche). Agreement between the Cobas assay and the WANTAI SARS-CoV-2 Ab ELISA was high, with a kappa coefficient of 92% (95% CI: 86–98%). Among the 180 participants, 95 (52.8%) were positive by WANTAI ELISA, and all of these were also positive by Cobas, indicating no false-positive results with the Cobas assay. Of the 85 participants who tested negative by iChroma, 78 (91.8%) were also negative by WANTAI ELISA, while 7 (8.2%) were positive by WANTAI, indicating false-negative results with iChroma. The Cobas assay showed a sensitivity of 99% (98–100%) and a specificity of 91% (85 - 97), with positive and negative predictive values of 100% (95–100%) and 93% (86–97%), respectively.

Discussion

Participant recruitment was conducted in November 2021, approximately 18 months after the first SARS-CoV-2 cases were reported in Gabon. All three diagnostic tests indicated widespread circulation of SARS-CoV-2 in the country, with the vast majority of the population showing evidence of anti–SARS-CoV-2 antibodies. The rapid increase in SARS-CoV-2 seroprevalence across African countries has been well documented. Early studies conducted in 2020 reported very low prevalence in African populations [16, 17], whereas studies conducted in 2021 showed substantially higher prevalence in the region [5, 18].

The iChroma assay is a rapid diagnostic test that relies on fluorescence detection using a dedicated reader. This technology is expected to provide more robust and reproducible results; however, a previous evaluation by Ana et al. [19] did not report improved diagnostic performance for detecting anti–SARS-CoV-2 antibodies in COVID-19 patients. The test was also evaluated alongside four other rapid tests in a clinical setting in Nigeria using samples from SARS-CoV-2–infected healthcare workers, where iChroma showed the lowest sensitivity when compared with RT-PCR as the reference method [20]. In contrast, our data showed a high sensitivity for iChroma, which was consistent with that of our reference ELISA test. Indeed, 94% of participants who tested positive by ELISA were also positive by iChroma, and only a limited number of false-positive results were observed. The discrepancy in sensitivity between studies may be explained by differences in study populations. Akyala et al. evaluated recently infected individuals who may not have developed a fully mature antibody response; consequently, none of the test assessed in that study reached a sensitivity of 50%. Thus, the reduced sensitivity observed may be related to the immune status of participants rather than to the intrinsic performance of the assays.

In our study, iChroma showed reduced specificity. 61% of negative iChroma results were positive with the reference test. To our knowledge, this is the first study evaluating the performance of iChroma in a large population with high SARS-CoV-2 seroprevalence. Although the test showed a high positive predictive value, negative results should be interpreted with caution in high-prevalence settings. The elevated number of false negative could lead to over estimation of the seroprevalence in community surveys if the test is used alone. The high seroprevalence increases the PPV, a positive test result is more likely to be a true positive. Finally, we observed strong agreement between the Cobas assay and the WANTAI SARS-CoV-2 Ab ELISA. Both the sensitivity and specificity of the Cobas assay exceeded 90% when compared with the ELISA, consistent with previous reports [21–23].

Conclusion

The current study evaluated the diagnostic performance of iChroma assay, in a high SARS-CoV-2 seroprevalence setting. The specificity of this test was considerably reduced in this context. Therefore, iChroma results should be interpreted with caution in high-seroprevalence settings.

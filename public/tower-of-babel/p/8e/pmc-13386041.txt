Mixed-methods evaluation protocol for the Now & Next programme: a codesigned, family-centred peer-led caregiver support programme
Authors: Sara Cibralic, Sylvana Mahmic, Anoo Bhopti, Margaret L. Kern, Lulu Barker, Prasadhini De Silva, Eram Afroze, Kylie Aekins, Valsamma Eapen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionAs the rates of child developmental delay and disability (referred onwards as developmental needs) increase globally, so does the number of caregivers caring for a child with developmental needs. It is well known that caring for a child with disability and/or delay leads to many challenges. While historically intervention services have focused on the child, it is increasingly being recognised that a whole family intervention approach is needed to improve both child and caregiver outcomes. The Now & Next programme is a family-centred, peer-led support programme targeted at improving caregiver skills in developing strengths-based goals across child, family and personal domains, taking practical steps to achieve these goals and building a support network. In doing so, the programme aims to improve goal achievement as well as caregivers’ capacity to plan and navigate supports, sense of competence, empowerment, community support and overall quality of life.Methods and analysisThis study will use a mixed-methods design, drawing on both quantitative and qualitative components. Quantitative data will measure changes in caregiver and child outcomes compared with a control group, while qualitative data will investigate caregivers’ experiences with the programme, including their perspectives on facilitators and barriers to programme engagement. Appropriate descriptive and inferential statistics will be used to evaluate quantitative data. Qualitative data will be analysed using thematic analysis.Ethics and disseminationThe study was approved by the University of New South Wales research ethics committee (iRECS10368). Findings will be shared through conference presentations and published manuscripts.Trial registration numberACTRN12626000083392 (Trial registered on ANZCTR).

Introduction

Developmental disabilities among children have increased globally.1 In high-income countries this increase has been rapid,2 3 with disability rates among children and young people in Australia, for example, rising from 8.3% in 2018 to 12.1% in 2022.2 Caring for children with developmental needs brings added challenges and stressors and has been found to be associated with reduced caregiver health and well-being, lower income and overall poorer quality of life.4 5 While historically intervention and support services have focused primarily on the child, in recent years it has been acknowledged that a whole family approach, including improving caregiver well-being, resilience, skills and empowerment, is needed to improve child outcomes and overall family functioning.6 7

Family-focused peer support programmes are one such approach.6 8 Peer support is broadly defined as a group of people experiencing similar circumstances coming together to form a social network and support one another.6 8 Peer-led support programmes are led by trained peer worker/s (ie, person/people with the same lived experience as those attending the programme). For caregivers of children with a disability and/or delay, peer workers, given their shared experiences, are able to provide emotional support, as well as skill development and foster a sense of belonging.8 Numerous reviews have shown that peer support groups result in improvements in connectedness, mental health and overall well-being, empowerment and practical support for caregivers of children with developmental needs.6 9

The Now & Next (N&N) programme is a family-centred peer-led support programme for caregivers of children with developmental needs.10 11 The programme, which draws on positive psychology principles,12 was codesigned with families of children with developmental needs. N&N arose as a way to fill this gap by helping families develop strengths-based goal-setting and the practical steps needed to achieve these goals, connecting them with other caregivers and assisting them in exploring ways to collaborate with those involved in their child’s learning (eg, therapists, teachers) to achieve the set goals. In so doing, N&N aims to improve caregiver goal achievement, empowerment, hope and overall well-being.

Preliminary evidence to support N&N is promising. In an initial study, Lancaster et al,13 using a pre–post-test intervention design, evaluated the impact of N&N on caregiver empowerment, well-being and sense of hope in a sample of 242 caregivers (M age=41.6 years). Their findings showed significant improvements in caregiver empowerment and well-being, and these gains were maintained 6–30 months later. However, significant changes in caregivers’ sense of hope were not observed. Furthermore, most families noted achieving or partly achieving the goals they had set for their children, themselves and/or their families following the completion of the programme. The study was limited by the absence of a control group.

The present study seeks to further evaluate N&N using a mixed-methods design. A waitlist-control trial method will be implemented to explore whether N&N leads to improvements in (1) caregiver well-being and mental health (operationally defined as reduced feelings of stress and loneliness); (2) caregiver sense of hope, competence and confidence in supporting their child; (3) caregiver perceptions of their families’ overall quality of life; (4) caregiver perceptions of community support, skills and empowerment and (5) caregiver perceptions of whether their child’s/families’ goals were achieved. Qualitative interviews will be used to explore caregivers’ experiences with and perceptions of the N&N programme. The evaluation will address the following research questions:

Methods and analysis

Service setting

Plumtree Children’s Services is a community-based, not-for-profit organisation focused on providing early childhood intervention, therapy and family-centred peer-informed support to children with developmental needs (aged 0–12 years) and their families. The service is physically located in Marrickville, a metropolitan suburb of New South Wales, Australia. A variety of interventions, programmes and workshops are delivered at the centre, in everyday settings such as the home or community environment, and/or online.

Intervention

N&N is a manualised programme that is delivered online, in a group format (8–10 families per group) across 8 weekly 2.5-hour sessions. While additional caregivers or support persons are encouraged to attend groups and often do, generally only one caregiver attends a group and therefore group numbers typically range between 6 and 12 individuals, depending on attendance patterns. The programme begins with an individual vision and planning session where families are encouraged to think about an inspiring vision for their child and their family (ie, what they want their future to look like). Subsequent sessions focus on goal setting, identifying strengths, circle of control and circle of influence, empowerment, working with professionals, self-care, and a final recap session.12 A brief description of the session’s outline is provided in table 1.

Study design

This study uses a mixed-methods design, drawing on both quantitative and qualitative data to address the research questions. Quantitative data will be used to address research questions 1–7, and qualitative data will be used to address research questions 8 and 9. The trial design was informed by the PRagmatic-Explanatory Continuum Indicator Summary 2 (PRECIS-2) model,14 with pragmatic considerations applied across all trial components.

Patient and public involvement

The N&N programme was codesigned by caregivers of children with a disability.10 11 Furthermore, the programme is peer-delivered, and peer leaders form part of the research team (authors EA and KA). N&N programme peer leaders are caregivers who themselves have completed the N&N programme and have undertaken additional structured leadership training to become programme facilitators.

Participants

Participants will be caregivers of a child with a disability and/or developmental delay, have the English language reading and writing proficiency needed to complete study questionnaires and semistructured interviews, and express interest in accessing the N&N programme. Caregivers who are enrolled in the RCT will be invited to participate in a semistructured interview.

Procedure and data collection

Quantitative data collection

Design

A randomised waitlist-control trial design will be implemented to evaluate the effectiveness of the N&N intervention compared with a control group. The trial will be undertaken in New South Wales, Australia, from January to December 2026. Data will be collected at two time points: preintervention (Time 1 (T1)) and postintervention/waitlist (Time 2 (T2), 8 weeks post T1 questionnaires).

Recruitment

Recruitment will occur through advertisements placed on the Plumtree website and social media pages. Families interested in participating will be invited to submit an expression of interest via an online form or by contacting Plumtree directly via telephone. Once an expression of interest is received, a peer worker will contact the family to provide detailed information about the N&N programme, including its aims, format and expected time commitment. During this initial conversation, the peer worker will also explain the research component of the project, including what participation involves, confidentiality and data privacy considerations, and how the information will be used to inform programme evaluation. Families will have the opportunity to ask questions before deciding whether they would like to participate. Participants who express interest in taking part in the research will receive an email with a secure link to the baseline questionnaires.

Randomisation

Restricted block randomisation will be used, with block sizes of six being used. Thus, within each block of six, three participants will be allocated to the intervention condition, and three will be allocated to the waitlist condition. The order of randomisation will vary within each block. Randomisation will occur after participants complete the baseline questionnaires, with allocations being pulled out of preprepared envelopes by an administrative officer external to the research team who will be blind to participant demographic information and semistructured questionnaire data. Blinding

Given the nature of the intervention, it is not possible to blind families or peer-workers administering the intervention. Researchers responsible for data analysis will, however, be blind to participant allocation. Group allocation will be masked using coded labels (Group A and Group B). Data analysts will remain blind until primary outcomes are analysed.

Procedure

Families allocated to the intervention condition will attend the next available N&N group. N&N groups are delivered over a period of 8 weeks, once per week. To increase completion of T2 questionnaires, participants will be provided with the T2 questionnaire link during Session 7 and reminded to complete the questionnaire again during Session 8 (the final session). Waitlist condition families will wait 8 weeks following completion of baseline. They will be sent the final questionnaire link in week 7 and reminded again in week 8 to complete the questionnaire. Some families may complete questionnaires early, while others may complete them after week 8. To adjust for variability in intervention exposure/time, the number of sessions/weeks passed between prequestionnaire and postquestionnaire completion will be included as a covariate in the analysis. Following questionnaire completion, waitlist families will be offered the N&N programme. As compensation for their time, families will receive the N&N programme free of charge and a AU$20 gift voucher after the completion of the T2 questionnaire. The semistructured questionnaire takes approximately 8 min to complete. Participants will receive two reminders to complete questionnaires via email, after which they will be considered lost to follow-up. All reminder emails will include instructions for withdrawing consent from the research at any stage.

Qualitative data collection

Purposive sampling will be used to recruit caregivers who have completed the N&N programme and peer workers who have delivered the programme. Recruitment will continue until theoretical saturation is reached, meaning no new themes are emerging from the data.19

Caregivers

Caregivers will be asked on the initial waitlist-control trial consent form whether they are interested in participating in the qualitative component of the research. Those who express interest will be asked to provide their contact details (telephone number and email). It is anticipated that only a small sample of participants will provide consent to be contacted, and so all participants who express interest in taking part in interviews will be contacted by the research team.

Peer workers

Emails will be sent to peer workers by the research team inviting them to participate in a 15–30 min interview. Peer workers will be informed that the interviews are aimed at investigating their perceptions of core programme components and perceived facilitators and barriers to programme implementation.

Interviews will be conducted via telephone or videoconferencing (using the Microsoft Teams or Zoom platforms) and will be audio-recorded with participant permission. Verbal consent will be obtained from all participants at the start of the interview. A semistructured interview guide (see online supplemental file 1 for the interview guide), developed based on the clinical and research expertise of the study team, will be used to explore participants’ experiences of participating in the N&N programme (caregivers) and/or delivering the programme (peer workers). Each interview will take approximately 15–30 min to complete. Caregivers and peer workers will be invited to complete the interview once and will receive a AU$50 voucher as reimbursement for their time.

Data analysis

Quantitative data analysis

IBM SPSS Statistics V.30 will be employed for quantitative data analysis, involving descriptive and inferential statistics. The main study outcomes will be evaluated using a linear mixed-models repeated-measures design or nonparametric equivalent tests across two time points, assessing interactions within and between categories. The independent variables will include data collection time point (T1 and T2) and intervention (intervention and control). As data will be collected using Google Forms, participants will be notified if a question has not been completed before they are able to move on to the next question. This will reduce the likelihood of missing data. In line with the PRECIS-2 model,14 which notes establishing intervention effectiveness under usual care conditions, an intention-to-treat analysis will also be performed. This means that participants who drop out prior to completion of the intervention will be included in the analysis within the group they were assigned to, irrespective of the number of sessions they participated in.

Sample size and power

A priori power analysis was undertaken assuming a small-to-moderate effect (η²g=0.058, Cohen’s d ∼0.50), a=0.05, and 80% power. The Psychological Empowerment Scale score was used in the power calculation as it (1) corresponds most directly to improving caregiver empowerment and (2) has previously been used to evaluate the same programme with a similar population,13 allowing for an effect size estimate. For a two-group repeated measures design, adjusting for 20% attrition, an estimate of 100 participants (50 per group) is required to detect a small-to-medium effect.

Qualitative data analysis

Qualitative interviews will be audio recorded and transcribed verbatim using Zoom’s/Microsoft Teams’ inbuilt transcription software. The data will be stored on the University of New South Wales (UNSW) OneDrive and Teams platform; these platforms have been rated by UNSW as the most appropriate for the storage of highly sensitive research data.20 Raw data will only be accessible to key research personnel. Coding and analysis will be undertaken in NVivo V.12.21 A thematic analysis framework will be used to evaluate the data.22 This will involve coders initially familiarising themselves with the data, coding each line of data, and organising and categorising data into emerging themes and subthemes.

Ethics and dissemination

This study was approved by the UNSW ethics committee (iRECS10368). All participants will provide informed, electronic consent. Qualitative study participants will also provide verbal consent prior to participating in semistructured interviews. Protocol modifications will be submitted to the ethics committee for review and approval. Approved modifications will be updated in the trial registry. The research team will be notified of any amendments to the study protocol via email and at research team meetings (held monthly).

Study outcomes will be disseminated through published manuscripts and conference presentations. Participants who indicate a desire to receive research results will be sent a copy of the results.

Participant safety

As this study does not involve experimental or high-risk procedures and no serious adverse events are anticipated, a Data Monitoring Committee has not been established. Participant safety and trial conduct will be monitored by the research team. This will include monthly research team meetings where recruitment processes, protocol adherence and data collection are discussed and any issues addressed.

Risks to participants will be mitigated by ensuring that participants are recruited by Plumtree staff who have experience working with and obtaining consent from families of children with developmental needs. Furthermore, qualitative interviews will be undertaken by, or in the presence of, a registered clinical psychologist experienced in working with caregivers of children with neurodevelopmental disorders and/or chronic health conditions and with expertise in identifying signs of distress. In line with ethics guidelines, if any adverse or unintended effects arise, they will be reported directly to the relevant authorities and human ethics committees.

Discussion

This protocol outlines the methods that will be implemented to evaluate the N&N programme, a family-centred peer-led support programme for caregivers of children with developmental needs.11 The evaluation extends past research13 through the implementation of a waitlist-control trial design, allowing for outcomes to be compared across caregivers who engage in the N&N programme and those who do not. Furthermore, qualitative interviews will be undertaken to explore caregivers’ perspectives on and experiences with the programme.

The use of a mixed-methods design, including a waitlist-control trial method, is a significant strength of this evaluation. The evaluation, however, is not without its limitations, including a lack of intervention comparison, that a follow-up will not take place, and that only caregiver report measures are being used. Furthermore, the required English language proficiency limits the generalisability of findings beyond this cohort.

The evaluation outcomes will make important contributions to the literature on family-centred peer-led support programmes for caregivers of children with developmental needs. Results will help identify the impact of the programme on both caregiver and child outcomes. Furthermore, the use of qualitative methodologies will allow for a deeper understanding of the facilitators and barriers to engaging caregivers in the peer support programmes. The information will also help in understanding the potential future uses of the N&N programme as well as help inform future evaluations of such programmes.

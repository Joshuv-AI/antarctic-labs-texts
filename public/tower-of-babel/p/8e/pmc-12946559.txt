Integration of iaa9-3 parthenocarpy and stigma exsertion for hybrid seedless tomato breeding
Authors: Long Thien Tran, Koichi Sugimoto, Nguyen Van Quan, Tran Dang Xuan, Hiroshi Ezura
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Stigma exsertion was largely eliminated during tomato domestication to promote self-pollination and secure yield. Nevertheless, exserted stigmas can prevent self-pollination and thus have potential utility for hybrid seed production by reducing costs and improving seed purity. Despite this promise, practical applications have remained limited due to the instability of the trait and low hybrid seed yields. Here, we combined stigma exsertion conferred by Style2.1 with parthenocarpy induced by the iaa9-3 mutation to develop an elite line, APi7-4-5. This line consistently displayed long stigma exsertion together with strong parthenocarpy. Morphological and reproductive analyses demonstrated that APi7-4-5 is a highly effective maternal line for hybrid seed production without emasculation, owing to its unique features: (i) complete inhibition of self-pollination under both greenhouse and field conditions, and (ii) high receptivity to pollen before anthesis, supporting efficient pollen germination and tube growth. In addition, the presence of Style2.1 in the F₁ generation further contributes to hybrid performance by preventing self-pollination and thereby enhancing parthenocarpic fruit set in untouched flowers. This semi-dominant effect of Style2.1 leads to a higher proportion of seedless fruits and improved uniformity in F₁ hybrids. Collectively, these results establish a feasible strategy for hybrid tomato seed production without emasculation by integrating iaa9-3–mediated parthenocarpy with Style2.1–regulated stigma exsertion. Introduction

Stigma exsertion, defined as the position of the stigma above the anther, acts as a physical barrier to prevent self-pollination in bisexual flowers. This trait is common in cross-pollinating species and occasionally occurs in self-pollinating crops under environmental stress, such as high temperature (Pan et al. 2019; Saeed et al. 2007). Exserted stigmas enhance hybrid seed production in self-pollination crops through several mechanisms, including (i) expanding the area available for external pollen receipt, as there is a strong positive efficiency between pistil length and stigma area (Farrell et al. 2006), (ii) prolonging the pollination period, and (iii) improving pollen germination, fruit set, and seed production (Zhang et al. 2010). For instance, the application of stigma exsertion to increase hybrid seed production has been successful employed for over a decade in rice, a typical self-pollination crop (Tien et al. 2013).

In tomato, the stigma exsertion frequently occurs in wild species but was eliminated in commercial cultivars through centuries of domestication (Bai and Lindhout 2007). Genetic studies have identified a major QTL, se2.1, on chromosome 2 that regulates floral organ morphology (Bernacchi and Tanksley 1997). Within this locus, five genes have been characterized, including Style2.1 (Solyc02g087860), which is critical for pistil elongation (Chen and Tanksley 2004). A large promoter deletion in Style2.1 accounts for the transition from exserted (wild) to inserted stigmas (cultivated) (Chen et al. 2007). While Style2.1 is a key determinant, evidence suggests that additional regulators modulate its expression. For instance, natural accessions of S. pimpinellifolium show wide variation in stigma exsertion despite nearly identical Style2.1 sequences (Gramazio et al. 2020). Recent transcriptome studies further identified homeodomain-leucine zipper IV (HD-Zip IV) genes (WO, HD7, HD7-LIKE) as positive regulators of Style2.1, promoting cell elongation in styles (Wu et al. 2024). These findings highlight the complexity of stigma exsertion regulation in tomato.

From a breeding perspective, stigma exsertion functions as a form of functional male sterility: pollen remains viable but cannot reach the stigma efficiently. Several studies have evaluated its application in hybrid seed production to reduce emasculation costs. Early experiments using cultivars Ex-1 and Ex-2 showed that slight exsertion reduced selfing rates to < 1% in greenhouses but was less effective under field conditions (Shakya and Scott 1983). In a subsequent study, stigmas exserted by more than 2 mm were found to completely prevent self-pollination in greenhouse conditions without artificial vibration (Gorguet et al. 2008). Additionally, the timing of pollination significantly impacted selfing contamination, in which the early pollination (on flowers 3 days before anthesis) resulted in higher contamination rate compared to standard pollination (at the day of anthesis) (Scott and George 1980b). However, conventional crosses between exserted and inserted lines yielded hybrids with reduced fruit set, reflecting the semi-dominant inheritance of stigma exsertion (Scott and George 1980a).

To address these challenges, George and Splittstoesser (1983) proposed combining stigma exsertion with parthenocarpy (pat-2). This strategy aims to improve fruit set in F1 hybrids while maintaining hybrid seed purity. However, it suffered from poor parthenocarpy (53% fruit set), low style receptivity, and seedless fruit production even after hand pollination. Reduced seed yield in pat-2 mutants has been attributed to abnormal auxin accumulation prior to anthesis (Takisawa et al. 2019), suggesting that the parthenocarpy source limited system efficiency.

In our previous work, we characterized the iaa9-3 mutant, which displayed strong parthenocarpy: emasculated flowers produced seedless fruits at rates near 90% (Tran et al. 2021). Unlike other mutants, iaa9-3 retained high seed productivity when hand-pollinated, making it particularly suitable for hybrid breeding. However, frequent self-pollination in untouched flowers still resulted in seed formation, a challenge also observed with other parthenocarpic mutations, such as pat-2 (Takisawa et al. 2019), Aucsia (Molesini et al. 2009), PAD-1 (Matsuo et al. 2020), and Della (Martí et al. 2007). Furthermore, recent studies also revealed that iaa9-3 mutant show enhanced adaptability under heat stress, associated with higher chlorophyll content and elevated antioxidant activity (Mubarok et al. 2023, 2024).

We hypothesized that combining iaa9-3-induced parthenocarpy with long stigma exsertion could overcome the limitations of earlier systems. Stigma exsertion would minimize selfing, and enable hybrid seed production without emasculation, while iaa9-3 parthenocarpy would ensure stable fruit set and seedless fruit development in hybrids.

Objectives of this study were to (i) develop an elite tomato line carrying both long stigma exsertion and the iaa9-3 mutation, and (ii) evaluate its potential for hybrid seed production without emasculation. We successfully developed an elite tomato line, APi7-4–5, which exhibited stable long stigma exsertion and strong parthenocarpy. Comprehensive evaluations of this line provided evidence that integration of these traits represents a highly feasible breeding strategy for hybrid seedless tomato cultivars, including an advanced system for hybrid seed production without emasculation.

Materials and methods

Plant materials and growing conditions

Three tomato parental lines were used in this study: Aichi First (ID: TOMJPF00003, denoted as AF), wild tomato S. pimpinellifolium (ID: TOMJPF00012-1, denoted as P12), and MicroTom parthenocarpic mutant line MT-iaa9-3 (ID: TOMJPE2811-2, denoted as iaa9). All material were obtained from the National BioResource Project (NBRP) Tomato Mutant Archive, University of Tsukuba, Japan. AF is a Japanese heirloom, high-quality tomato cultivar exhibiting a typical inserted stigma; P12 consistently displays a stigma exsertion phenotype; iaa9 carries a mutation in IAA9, conferring parthenocarpy (Saito et al. 2011).

The pure line APi7-4–5 was developed at the University of Tsukuba through multiple crosses among the three parental lines, (AF x iaa9) X (P12 x iaa9), and was subsequently deposited by the University of Tsukuba in the NBRP tomato collection. A hybrid between APi7-4–5 and Ai-27-7 (an iaa9-3 mutant line derived from conventional cross between AF and MT-iaa9-3, F4 generation) was generated and evaluated under open-field conditions to assess the potential benefits of stigma exsertion for enhancing the parthenocarpic performance of iaa9-3 mutant lines. In addition, other F4 Ai lines were also used as controls to evaluate the parthenocarpic performance of Api-7-4−5 under greenhouse conditions.

Plants were grown in two locations: (i) a greenhouse at the University of Tsukuba, Ibaraki Prefecture, Japan, and (ii) an open field at Hiroshima University, Hiroshima Prefecture, Japan. In the greenhouse, all breeding steps leading to APi7-4–5 were conducted. Twenty-five-day-old seedlings were transplanted into a hydroponic system with nutrient solution (pH 7.0; EC 2.0–2.5 dS m⁻¹), supplied for six minutes every hour between 06:00 and 22:00 throughout the experimental period. Plants were spaced 1.5 m between rows and 0.2 m between individuals. For open-field trials, 30-day-old seedlings of APi7-4–5, Ai-27-7, their hybrid, and P12 were planted in two rows per bed at 60 cm × 45 cm (row × plant) spacing. Crop management, including irrigation, fertilization, and other agronomic practices, followed standard protocols (Srinivasan 2010).

Selection of exserted stigma, pathenocarpy breeding lines

Phenotyping and genotyping

During breeding, homozygous iaa9-3 plants were identified by their characteristic simple-leaf phenotype. For stigma exsertion, regulated by the dominant Style2.1 allele, homozygous individuals were confirmed through both phenotyping (exsertion) and genotyping. Genotyping employed a PCR primer pair targeting the Style2.1 promoter region, yielding amplicons of 616 bp (wild parent P12 allele) and 177 bp (domesticated parent AF allele). PCR products were separated on 1% agarose gel. The primer sequences were:

RNA extraction and RT-qPCR analysis

To further validate the dominance of the Style2.1 allele in nearly pure line APi7-4–5 (F4 generation), expression analysis was performed. Style tissues were collected from open flowers at anthesis from APi7-4–5 and the parental lines AF and P12. Total RNA was extracted using the RNeasy Plant Mini Kit (Qiagen, Hilden, Germany). RNA quality and concentration were assessed by absorbance at 260 nm with a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Japan). For cDNA synthesis, 500 ng of RNA from each sample was reverse transcribed using ReverTra Ace qPCR RT Master Mix with gDNA Remover (Toyobo Co., Ltd., Japan). Quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR) was performed on a Thermal Cycler Dice® Real Time System TP800 (Takara Co., Ltd., Japan).

Two primer sets were used: one for the target gene Style2.1 and one for the housekeeping gene Actin (Solyc11g005330).

The relative expression level of Style2.1 was normalized against Actin using the 2−ΔΔCt method (Livak and Schmittgen 2001).

Data collection and analysis

Flower structure

Flowers were harvested at multiple developmental stages, from 2 days before anthesis (−2DAA) to 2 days after anthesis (2DAA), from APi7-4–5 and P12 plants grown in either greenhouse or open field conditions. Flowers of Ai27-7 and the hybrid (Api7-4–5 x Ai-27-7) were collected on day of anthesis (0DAA) under open-field conditions. After harvesting, petals, sepals, and half of anther cone were removed to expose the inner organs. Samples were imaged alongside a scale, and the lengths of the ovary (OL), style (SL), pistil (PL), anther cone (AL), and stigma exsertion (SE) were measured using ImageJ software (Schneider et al. 2012). For each genotype, 6–10 flowers per plant were randomly selected from 5 plants in each line.

Parthenocarpic performance and seed production

Fruits were collected from Api7-4–5, Ai-27-7, and their hybrid to evaluate parthenocarpic fruit development and the inhibitory effect of long stigma exsertion on self-pollination, indicated by seed production. Fruits from untouched (nature) and self-pollinated flowers were harvested at the fully ripe stage from five randomly selected plants under both greenhouse (n = 33 parthenocarpic fruits and 66 seeded fruits) and open field (n = 38 parthenocarpic fruits and 44 seeded fruits) conditions. The following traits were recorded: natural fruit set = ratio of fruits to untouched flowers, natural parthenocarpic fruit set = ratio of seedless fruits to untouched flowers, and fruit weight: comparison of seedless (from emasculated flowers) versus seeded (from pollinated flowers) fruits. Additionally, for Api7-4–5, seed numbers were also compared between early (pre-anthesis) and late (post-anthesis) pollination to assess the potential role of long stigma exsertion in hybrid seed production without emasculation.

Pollen tube observation

To further evaluate the seed production ability of the long stigma exserted-parthenocarpic tomato line, Api7-4–5, in vivo pollen tube growth were examined at different floral stages. An excess number of pollens was applied to stigmas at two days before anthesis (−2DAA), one day before anthesis (−1DAA), at anthesis (0DAA), two, four, and eight days after anthesis (2DAA, 4DAA, 8DAA). Pistils were collected 24 h after pollination and processed following our previously described protocol (Tran et al. 2023), including fixation in ethanol (100%)–acetic acid (100%) (3:1, 12 h), softening in 5 N NaOH (12 h), staining overnight with 0.01% aniline blue in K3PO4 buffer, and observation under a UV microscope (Olympus BX50, Olympus-Life Science Company, Japan). Five pistils per treatment were examined.

Scanning electron microscopy (SEM)

To examine the stigma surface morphology of Api7-4–5, five inflorescences containing flowers at different developmental stages were harvested and kept in closed Falcon tubes to prevent hydration. Samples were immediately transported to the scanning electron microscope (SEM; TM3000 Miniscope, Hitachi Group, Japan). At the SEM facility, flowers at − 2 DAA, − 1 DAA, 0 DAA, and 2 DAA were selected (n = 3–5 per stage). The tips of the styles, including the stigmas, were excised and directly placed into the microscope chamber. Stigma surfaces were imaged at 150× magnification following the manufacturer’s instructions.

Data analysis

All data are represented as mean value ± standard error (SE) of biological replicates. Statistical significance was determined using Tukey’s Honestly Significant Difference (HSD) (α = 0.05) test for multiple group comparison and Student’s T-test assessment (P < 0.05) for pairwise comparisons.

Results

Integration of Style2.1 and iaa9-3 results in long-stigma exserted, parthenocarpic line APi7-4–5

In previous studies, we demonstrated that iaa9-3 mutant lines often produce seeded fruits in open-field conditions due to natural self-pollination (Tran et al. 2021). To re-evaluate this trait, we assessed fruit set under greenhouse conditions, where pollination vectors, such as wind and insects are limited. Across eight iaa9-3 lines, an average of 52.8% seeded fruits was observed, with values ranging from 25.3% to 89.7% (Fig. S1). These findings underscore the necessity of preventing self-pollination for the development of stable, seedless tomato lines.

We hypothesized that combining iaa9-3 parthenocarpy with stigma exsertion could suppress selfing. Accordingly, conventional crosses were performed among three parental lines: S. pimpinellifolium (P12; donor of the long-stigma allele Style2.1), Micro-Tom iaa9-3 (donor of parthenocarpy), and the cultivar Aichi First (AF; recurrent genetic background). Selection was carried out using phenotypic markers (simple leaves for iaa9-3, stigma exsertion for Style2.1) together with molecular genotyping (PCR amplification of the Style2.1 promoter). PCR analysis confirmed that line APi7-4–5 carries the Style2.1 allele from the wild donor P12 (Fig. 1b). RT-qPCR further showed that Style2.1 expression in APi7-4–5 styles was nearly two-fold higher than in AF, though significantly lower than in P12 (Fig. 1c). These results validate the successful introgression of the wild-type Style2.1 allele into the AF background and suggest that expression levels are modulated by genetic background.

Phenotypically, APi7-4–5 displayed the expected combination of traits: (i) simple leaf morphology with smooth margins and reduced leaflet number which is comparable with MT-iaa9-3 mutant line (Fig. 3d), (ii) flowers with long stigma exsertion (Fig. 3e), and (iii) parthenocarpic fruit development (Fig. 3f). Together, these findings confirm the integration of Style2.1 and iaa9-3 in APi7-4–5, establishing it as a novel line for hybrid seedless tomato breeding.

Flower morphology of the APi7-4–5 mutant line

The floral structure of the APi7-4–5 mutant line was characterized under both greenhouse and open field growing conditions (Fig. 2).

Under greenhouse conditions, flowers consistently displayed pronounced stigma exsertion, with an average of > 4 mm at anthesis and minimal variation among flowers. In comparison, the wild parent S. pimpinellifolium (P12) exhibited a stigma exsertion of ~ 1.63 mm at 0DAA, less than half that of APi7-4–5, while other two parental lines showed typical inserted stigma (AF) and flush stigma (MT-iaa9-3) phenotype (Fig. 2a). Importantly, stigma exsertion was already visible in immature flowers. At −2DAA, APi7-4–5 stigmas protruded ~ 1.86 mm, elongating rapidly to ~ 4.07 mm at anthesis, and reaching 4.31–4.44 mm at 1–2DAA (Fig. 2c).

A similar developmental pattern was observed in the open field, though overall exsertion values were lower. Stigma protrusion began at ~ 0.17 mm at −2DAA, reached 2.46 mm at anthesis, and peaked at 3.26 mm at 2DAA (Fig. 2b and c). In comparison, P12 showed ~ 1 mm exsertion at anthesis. Across all stages, stigma exsertion was significantly greater in greenhouse-grown plants than in those grown in the field (Fig. 2c). This difference may reflect environmental influence, as average greenhouse temperatures were ~ 1.5 °C higher than in the field during the 14 days preceding sampling (29.0/17.8 °C vs. 27.6/21.9 °C day/night; Fig. S2).

To identify the morphological drivers of stigma exsertion, we analyzed pistil length (PL), style length (SL), ovary length (OL), and anther cone length (AL). Pre-anthesis, stigma exsertion increased sharply (~ 2.3 mm from − 2DAA to 0DAA), driven mainly by elongation of the pistil (~ 3.1 mm) and style (~ 2.7 mm). Ovary growth also contributed (~ 0.4 mm). Post-anthesis (0–2DAA), both pistil (~ 1.1 mm) and stigma exsertion (~ 0.8 mm) showed slower elongation, while ovary growth became the dominant factor (~ 0.7 mm; Table S1).

Fruit set and fruit development in the APi7-4–5

Fruit set and fruit weight of APi7-4–5 were evaluated under both greenhouse and open-field conditions (Fig. 3). Plants showed consistently high fruit set (> 93%) in both environments, and notably, all fruits on untouched flowers were completely seedless (Fig. 3a, Fig. S1). Under greenhouse conditions, the natural fruit set of APi7-4–5 was comparable to that of the best-performing Ai lines, but its parthenocarpic fruit set was significantly higher than in other Ai mutant lines, which produced at least 25.3% seeded fruits on untouched flowers (Fig. S1). Seedless fruits also developed well, reaching ~ 68% (open field) and ~ 75% (greenhouse) of the weight of seeded fruits harvested from the same plants (Fig. 3b). These results indicate that long stigma exsertion in APi7-4–5 effectively blocks self-pollination, ensuring stable parthenocarpic fruit development under diverse growing conditions.

Effects of long stigma exsertion on seed production in the APi7-4–5

Because stigma exsertion in APi7-4–5 occurs before anthesis (Fig. 2a), flowers may be receptive to pollen prior to anthers mature, a trait potentially valuable for hybrid seed production without emasculation. To test this, controlled pollinations were performed at multiple developmental stages: −2, −1 days before anthesis (DAA), and at anthesis.

Pollen successfully germinated on stigmas at all tested stages. Strong pollen adhesion and pollen tube growth were observed at −1DAA and 0DAA, resulting in 100% seed set with no significant differences in seed number (47.7 vs. 48.3 seeds/fruit in the greenhouse, 65.1 vs. 71.6 seeds/fruit in the field; Fig. 4a–b). In contrast, flowers pollinated at −2DAA showed weaker pollen tube growth and significantly reduced seed set (24.5 seeds/fruit in the greenhouse, 40.7 seeds/fruit in the field). These results indicate that APi7-4–5 stigmas are receptive before anthesis, with optimal pollination occurring at −1DAA and 0DAA.

Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) of stigma surfaces revealed clear developmental differences in physiological maturity. At −2DAA, stigmas appeared immature, with incomplete wrinkle-like structures, likely limiting pollen retention and germination, thus reducing seed set (Fig. 4c). By −1DAA, stigma surfaces were fully developed, resembling those at 0DAA and supporting efficient pollination and seed production. In post-anthesis flowers, stigma surfaces exhibited deeper wrinkle-like structures, which likely enhanced pollen adhesion and germination capacity (Fig. 4c).

Artificial pollination on post-anthesis flowers was also conducted under greenhouse conditions. Older flowers remained receptive, with pollen grains germinating effectively, but seed numbers declined progressively with flower age. Pollination at 2DAA and 4DAA yielded 32.4 and 24.3 seeds per fruit, respectively, whereas flowers at 8DAA failed to produce seeds despite abundant pollen tube growth (Fig. 4b; Fig. S3).

Style2.1: a semi-dominant allele enhancing parthenocarpic performance in F1 hybrids

To assess the contribution of stigma exsertion to parthenocarpic performance in F1 hybrids, we generated a test cross between two iaa9-3 mutant lines: APi7-4–5 (long exserted stigma) and Ai-27-7 (inserted stigma). Among the Ai lines, Ai-27-7 displayed the highest proportion of seeded fruit production (Fig. S1). The resulting hybrid exhibited a moderately exserted stigma value (SEV = 1.38 mm) and produced 10.28% seeded fruits under open-field conditions. In contrast, the parental line Ai-27-7, with an inserted stigma (SEV = − 0.12 mm), set 87.28% seeded fruits under the same conditions (Table 1). These findings indicate that the introduction of Style2.1 from APi7-4–5 enhanced parthenocarpy in the hybrid background.

To further investigate the inheritance of Style2.1, we examined near-pure lines derived from an F4 population of a cross between Aichi First (AF) and the wild species S. pimpinellifolium (P12). Three lines—AP-27-12, AP-75-6, and AP-75-9—carrying homozygous Style2.1 alleles (from P12) displayed a pronounced long-stigma phenotype, with SEVs ranging from 3.71 to 4.52 mm, significantly exceeding that of the wild donor P12. Each of these lines was crossed with Ai-29-8, a near-pure line carrying the style2.1 allele (from AF) that exhibits a typical inserted stigma (SEV = − 0.29 mm). The resulting hybrids showed intermediate SEVs (1.48–3.51 mm) compared to their respective parents (Table S3). Taken together, these results demonstrate that the wild-type Style2.1 allele acts as a semi-dominant regulator of stigma exsertion, contributing to enhanced parthenocarpic performance in F1 hybrid tomatoes.

Discussion

A feasible system for hybrid tomato seed production without emasculation

Over four decades have passed since George and Splittstoesser (1983) first proposed combining stigma exsertion and parthenocarpy as a strategy for hybrid tomato seed production without emasculation. However, this system has not been widely adopted due to low parthenocarpic rates and poor hybrid seed yields. In this study, we revisited this concept by integrating two phenomena regulated by distinct mechanisms: stigma exsertion controlled by Style2.1 and parthenocarpy regulated by iaa9-3. Through this strategy, we developed an elite pure line, APi7-4–5, which consistently exhibited long stigma exsertion and strong parthenocarpy. Comprehensive evaluations of APi7-4–5 demonstrated that combining Style2.1 and iaa9-3 overcomes the limitations of earlier systems. The key advantages of this system are discussed below:

Complete inhibition of self-pollination via long stigma exsertion:The pronounced stigma exsertion of APi7-4–5 ensures reliable prevention of self-pollination under both greenhouse and field conditions. Previous studies indicated that approximately 2 mm exsertion can prevent self-fertilization (Gorguet et al. 2008); the consistently greater exsertion observed in APi7-4–5 (Fig. 2c) provides a sufficient physical barrier. Moreover, early pistil elongation supported by parthenocarpic ovary growth reinforces this effect, ensuring stable seedless fruit formation (Table S1).

Previous systems utilizing pat-2 for parthenocarpy encountered low hybrid seed yields due to increased auxin content in the ovary before anthesis, which in turn impaired the pollen tube penetration (Takisawa et al. 2019). Unlike pat-2, down-regulation of IAA9 does not affect auxin synthesis-related genes (Wang et al. 2005). Although tomato stigmas can physiologically receive pollen several days before flower opening, receptivity is generally low at early stages (−3DAA) (Scott and George 1980b). In APi7-4–5, clear stigma exsertion at approximately one day before anthesis provided a suitable window for pollination, achieving seed yields comparable to those of open flowers (Fig. 3b). This finding highlights that flowers at − 1 DAA are optimal for hybrid tomato seed production without emasculation.

Pediatric Dentistry through a Child's Lens—The Role of Dentist's Gender, Attire, and Clinic Ambiance in Gen Alpha's Experience: A Cross-sectional Study
Authors: Sai Vaishnavi Alahari, Sampath R Cheruku, Silla S Swathi, Ziauddin Mohammad, Murali K Dindukurthi, Anusha Reddy Gollapalli

ABSTRACT
AbstractAim and backgroundA child's first impression of the dental clinic impacts their willingness to cooperate during treatment, and factors like the dentist's gender, attire, and clinic colors strongly influence a child's comfort. Introduction

Pediatric dentistry has seen many advances in recent times, yet for successful treatment, a child's cooperation remains the greatest factor, which largely depends on the fear and anxiety the child associates with the dentist and his office. The dental office and the dentist can be presented to the child in his most preferred manner to remedy the fear associated with dental treatments.1 Building a good relationship with patients, especially children, is important for dentists. This helps reduce patient fears and provides effective, efficient care. Developing a strong rapport during the child's first visit creates a comfortable environment where the child feels relaxed. Children often judge their dentist based on looks and watch every word, movement, and gesture closely during a dental appointment.2

The child's perception of the dentist starts forming as he walks into the clinic and meets the dentist even before verbal communication begins. This makes it important to understand the impact the clinic's appearance and the dentist's attire have on the child. The topic of dental professional attire remains an area of ongoing debate.3 Most young patients find the dental setting a threatening place, and this plays a role in increasing their dental anxiety. A dental practitioner's attire, expressions, and gestures can significantly affect a child's anxiety level. Research has shown that the appearance and color scheme of the dental clinic can also influence the child's attitude and perceptions of the dental clinic.4

The emotional well-being of pediatric patients is critical to pedodontics as it holds the power to alter the course of the treatment. To foster positive emotional and social adjustment in child patients, various methods have been implemented.5 Pediatric dentists today enjoy numerous advantages compared to their predecessors. The dental environment is now designed to be more child-friendly, featuring contemporary materials and technologies that create a welcoming atmosphere for children. Additionally, trained support staff further enhance this ambiance, ensuring that both the child and their families feel at ease.6 These include modifying the color, design, and patterns of the dentist's attire, as well as the overall esthetic of the dental clinic environment.5

Color has an undeniable influence on children's lives, dictating how they sense and react to their surroundings. Everything from toys to clothing and the room's recreation conveys psychological messages through color. Colors can have varied emotional effects: yellow is often linked to happiness, blue to calmness, green to tranquility, red to excitement or anger, and black to sadness. Children are more sensitive to these effects of color than adults, and creating a positive environment with the right colors can help reduce anxiety, especially in settings like dental clinics, where managing fear is crucial.4

Several studies have explored the influence of gender on children's preferences for their pediatric dentist, although research on this specific topic is somewhat limited. Some studies suggest that children's preferences for a dentist's gender may be linked to the child's age, cultural factors, and parental influence.7 With every newer generation, the general outlook of the environment changes, and generation alpha is no exception to that. This newer generation has access to information like never before, which makes them more equipped for the challenges of today's fast-changing world. Also, gender roles being redefined and taught to children from a very young age might influence the preference of the dentist by the child.

The current study aims to evaluate children's knowledge of who a pediatric dentist is and evaluate their preference regarding the dentist's gender and attire and the dental clinic environment.

Materials and Methods

The Department of Pedodontics and Preventive Dentistry, Sri Sai College of Dental Surgery, Vikarabad, performed this cross-sectional study.

Ethical Clearance

Ethical clearance was obtained from the Institutional Ethics Committee (Ref. No. 12/SSCDS/SS/IRB/2023).

Permission for Study

The principals of the selected schools in Hyderabad and Vikarabad districts, India, were asked to provide consent for the study.

Consent

Prior written consent was obtained from the parents or guardians of all participants.

Sample Size Calculation

An interviewer-administered questionnaire study was conducted among 186 primary and middle school children using convenience sampling (Fig. 1). The sample size (N) was calculated based on N = Z²pq/d² (N = sample size, p = 0.14, q = 0.86, α = 0.05).5

Questionnaire being administered

Inclusion Criteria

Questions regarding a child's knowledge and preference for pediatric dentists were circulated among schoolchildren aged 6–12 years of both genders. Children with informed consent, with no underlying special medical conditions, who were not color-blind, and who were present on the day of the study were included.

Structure of Questionnaire

An interviewer-administered questionnaire with closed-ended questions was used along with three pictures, of which two were to be colored by the child. The questions included demographic data, a child's knowledge about the pediatric dentist, and the association between anxiety and the dental setup. The pictures included that of a male dentist, that of a female dentist, and that of a dental clinic. The children were provided with colors to shade the provided picture of a male or female dentist and clinical environment with their preferred colors. Ten color options were given, which included red, yellow, blue, green, brown, black, purple, tint, pink, and orange.

The obtained data were analyzed using SPSS 20.0.

Results

A total of 186 children participated in the survey, of which 115 were female and 71 were male.

Forty-five of them were in primary school, and 141 were in secondary school.

A statistically significant knowledge gap (p < 0.001) was observed between younger and older children regarding the knowledge of pediatric dentists, with only 13% of younger children answering the question correctly (Table 1 and Fig. 2). Sixty-four percent of children stated that a dentist's attire influenced their anxiety, while 74% believed clinic environment colors affected their comfort levels (Figs 3 and 4). Most children preferred a dentist of their own gender, but 2.2% of girls preferred a male dentist, and 12.9% of boys preferred a female dentist (Fig. 5). Blue was the most favored color for dentist's attire among both genders, followed by orange and then red in boys; in female children, blue was followed by pink, closely by a 1.5% difference, and then orange (Figs 6 and 7). For the clinic environment, boys preferred blue, then green, and then orange, while girls opted for pink, followed by red and then blue (Figs 8 and 9). Brown was the most chosen color for dental chairs (Fig. 10).

Discussion

In the current study, we have assessed the knowledge of children as to who pedodontists are, and a significant difference could be observed in the knowledge about them in children based on their age. Younger children who were in primary school had shown less knowledge as compared to children in middle school. This difference can be attributed to the difference in the cognition of the children and their developmental stages. It also reflects on their exposure through the educational program and personal real-life situations.4,8

The knowledge of pedodontists was not as evident in the younger children. Children in middle school had a better understanding of a pedodontist being a “children's dentist” as opposed to primary school children. This also aligns with the age-appropriate cognitive development of these children, pedodontists being specialists it can be difficult for younger children to have an understanding of this specific role due to it being a new concept for them to grasp.4,9

Middle school children have a higher chance of being exposed to a pedodontist through referrals or for dental care in a private setup or through peers.9

Even though there exists a branch of dentistry specialized for a child's oral health care needs, it is not represented in mainstream education, and oral health is often only associated with that of a general dentist.9

There is a need to improve the awareness about dental health care professionals in the school education system and to impart age-appropriate information about specific pediatric dental specialists.10 Parents must also be involved in explaining the specialized care a pedodontist will provide. This will also help in establishing a pedodontist as a friendly figure and reduce dental anxiety in children.4 Pedodontists must also be proactive in providing education and regular checkups by conducting camps and school visits to make their role clearer to children and to desensitize them.10

The dental environment plays a significant role in a child's behavior and cooperation toward treatment.11 On being asked “Do you think the attire of the dentist makes a difference to you?” and “Do you think the colors of the clinic environment will affect the anxiety you experience at a dental clinic,” 64% and 73% of the children answered yes to these questions, respectively.

The perception of the dental setup and the way the child sees it is of paramount importance in causing anxiety. So, there is a need for pedodontists to make the ambiance of the clinic more child-friendly and as close to the changing expectations of today's children as possible.11

Color is an inseparable component of a child's everyday life, and they perceive it as everything around them. It has been widely accepted that color plays a major role in feelings and emotions.11 The color preferences of an individual relate to the kind of emotion he experiences with the color. It relays precisely to whether they associate a positive or negative emotion with a particular color.12

Color is an integral part of our day-to-day lives, and its presence is seen in everything that we perceive. Color has a significant impact on children's lives as evidenced in their clothes, toys, sports, home accessories, and lunch boxes.8 It plays an important role in a child's physiological and psychological perception. The fact that colors play a vital role in our feelings and expressions has been widely accepted.12 The bond between color and emotion is closely coupled to color preferences. In particular, color preferences are linked with whether a color imparts positive or negative feelings.8

In the current study, we gave children 10 colors: red, yellow, blue, green, brown, black, purple, tint, pink, and orange, to color the preferred dentist's (male/female) attire, dental clinic environment: walls and the dental chair.

Along with asking if the dentist's attire and the clinic environment affected their anxiety, 64.9% of children answered affirmatively that the dentist's attire affects their anxiety level in a dental clinic, of which 49.2% were definitely yes and 15.7% were yes.

About 74.2% of children answered affirmatively the question of whether the dental office environment affects them, with 33.8% saying definitely yes and 40.4% saying yes.

A child's perception of the dental environment is also a significant factor that can cause anxiety.13 A pleasant and colorful environment relieves the anxiety of children and also projects the office ambiance as friendly. Clinicians have experientially described that the emotional status of a child affects their choice of color.8

In our study, the most preferred colors for the dentist's attire were, in male children, blue followed by orange and then red. In female children, it was blue followed by pink, closely by a 1.5% difference, and then orange. As for the colors for the walls of the clinic setup, it was blue, then green, then orange for boys. For girls, it was pink followed by red and then blue. The chair color had similar preferences in both genders, which were brown and blue followed by yellow.

For several years, clinical psychologists have offered assumptions about the emotional significance of color. A study by Boyatzis and Varghese (1994) concluded that children generally associate bright colors such as pink, blue, and red with feelings of happiness and excitement, inducing a positive reaction, while darker colors like brown and black are linked to sadness or discomfort. Girls, in particular, tend to develop a stronger preference for brighter colors and a dislike for darker hues. Boys, on the other hand, might exhibit more positive associations with dark colors compared to girls.14

Blue, specifically, which was the most preferred color for the dress color of the dentist for both boys and girls, is often associated with calmness and trust, which is very desirable in a pediatric dental office. It is noteworthy that blue was also the most preferred color for the clinic environment.15

Pink, which was the second most preferred by girls for dentist's attire and first most preferred by girls for the clinic environment, is also associated with positive emotions of gentleness, happiness, and excitement, which can have a positive effect on the children during dental visits.14

Red is seen as a color that can have the dual effect of both positive emotions such as energy and excitement and negative emotions such as a sense of restlessness or overstimulation. Girls, for environment color, second most preferred this, and it can be a double-edged sword and has to be used carefully.14

Studies have shown that children often associate the color yellow with positive emotions such as happiness and cheerfulness. The color green is generally associated with nature and tranquility. Research suggests that green can have a calming effect on children, promoting relaxation and reducing anxiety. These colors were also preferred for the dental clinic colors by boys in our study.

For the dental chair, the color brown was unanimously most preferred. Brown is a color that evokes mixed feelings among children. While it can provide a sense of warmth and security, reminiscent of the earth and nature, studies have shown that children often associate brown with negative emotions, viewing it as dull or uninviting. Therefore, when incorporating brown into environments for children, it is beneficial to balance it with more vibrant colors to maintain an engaging and welcoming atmosphere.14

The color preferences of our study align with the results of a study done by Annamary et al and Ganesh et al but are in contrast with Volpato et al.4,11,16 The contrast in results could be due to the child not being able to choose the color of their choice and having to choose from the given six photos in the study done by Volpato et al.

Pediatric dentists should consider wearing blue or pink, as these colors were the most preferred and associated with a comforting presence. Incorporating blue, green, and yellow into dental clinic walls, furniture, and equipment may reduce anxiety and create a welcoming environment.

The preference of dentist gender was the same as that of their own in most cases, but there were 2.2% girls who preferred a male dentist and 12.9% boys who preferred a female dentist. Dentists of the same gender as their pediatric patients may tend to have less resistance to assist them. This finding is similar to that of previous studies by Ravikumar et al., Alsarheed, Asokan et al., Tong et al.2,17–19

This reflects and empowers children, enhancing their comfort and cooperation during medical interactions. Research shows that children foster trust and feel valued when they can associate themselves in the role of their healthcare provider. This involvement includes explaining medical information in an age-appropriate manner and listening to the child's preferences and concerns.20

The United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child emphasizes the child's right to express their views and to be heard in healthcare settings. This recognition of autonomy supports the development of self-esteem and confidence, enabling children to better understand their preferences and see healthcare providers as partners in their well-being.21

Limitations

A larger sample size will give more conclusive results.

Our study was conducted in Telangana, India, which is a limited geographic space.

The distribution of participants could be more even in terms of age and gender.

Conclusion

The results of our current study conclude that:

There is adequate knowledge about a dentist in children, while such an understanding is lacking about pediatric dentists in children.

In the child's opinion, the dental clinic environment and the dentist's attire affect the child's anxiety level. The most preferred color by children was blue in both genders for the dress color and pink in boys and girls, respectively, for the clinical environment color.

These conclusions show us the importance of spreading awareness about pediatric dentists in young children and the usage of colors that are most affable to the child and considering their preferences so that they see pediatric dentists as trusting figures and foster a healthy relationship with them.

Further studies have to be done to arrive at more conclusive outcomes.

Clinical Significance

Pediatric dentists must recognize that clinic ambiance and attire significantly influence child behavior. The findings emphasize the importance of using color psychology to create a child-friendly dental clinic. Dentists should consider wearing blue or pink, as these colors were perceived as the most calming. Similarly, clinic walls should feature soothing colors like blue and green, while dental chairs should avoid intimidating shades. A carefully curated color scheme, along with an understanding of children's preferences, can significantly reduce anxiety and improve cooperation in pediatric dental settings. Understanding these preferences enables pediatric dentists to create child-friendly, psychologically supportive environments that enhance the overall dental experience.

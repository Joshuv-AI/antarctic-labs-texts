Intermolecular Aza Paternò-Büchi Reaction of N‑(Fluorosulfonyl)imines and Triplet-State Aromatic Alkenes
Authors: Hiroto Kimura, Yasuhiro Yamauchi, Takuya Hashimoto
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Intermolecular aza Paternò-Büchi reaction between N-(fluorosulfonyl)­imines and aromatic alkenes has been developed through the photoinduced generation of a triplet state of the aromatic alkene. The N-fluorosulfonyl group proved crucial in enabling this transformation to furnish azetidines, as no product formation was observed in the reaction using N-tosylimines. Theoretical studies revealed that the highly electrophilic fluorosulfonyl-substituted nitrogen facilitates efficient nitrogen–carbon bond formation with triplet-state styrenes. Furthermore, by taking advantage of facile derivatization of the fluorosulfonyl group, we demonstrated its reductive deprotection to generate unprotected azetidines.

Introduction

Imines are indispensable building blocks in organic synthesis because of their ready accessibility from carbonyl compounds and their facile conversion into amines, − typically via their use as electrophiles. Their reactivity is profoundly influenced by the nature of the N-substituent, making it a standard practice to screen various N-substituents until the desired transformation is achieved effectively. It is, therefore, anticipated that introducing a previously untapped N-substituent with distinct electronic and steric properties could overcome the limitations of conventional approaches and further expand the synthetic utility of imines. Motivated by our finding that N-(fluorosulfonyl)­amines exhibit markedly superior reactivity to N-tosylamines in aminations, − which we attribute to their greater electron deficiency and smaller steric demand, we became interested in N-(fluorosulfonyl)­imines as an unexplored class of imines. Another notable advantage of the fluorosulfonyl group is its straightforward deprotection and versatile derivatization through SuFEx chemistry. Our interest particularly is in the application of N-(fluorosulfonyl)­imines to open-shell chemistry, which remains underdeveloped, whereas the use of imines in closed-shell chemistry is well-established.

One emerging transformation in this context is the intermolecular aza Paternò-Büchi reaction, which provides a straightforward access to azetidines from imines and alkenes (Figure a). − Seminal studies were established using excited cyclic imines such as isoindolones, , uracils, benzoisothiazoles, and quinoxalinones, which react with ground-state alkenes. However, extending direct excitation to acyclic imines has proven challenging because, upon reaching the singlet/triplet state, they can undergo unproductive pathways such as E/Z isomerization or homolytic cleavage of the bond between nitrogen and its substituent. Maruoka and co-workers pioneeringly demonstrated that N-arylsulfonyl aldimines having an extended π-systems undergo aza Paternò-Büchi reaction with aromatic alkenes through a singlet-state pathway under 365 nm irradiation (Figure b). Recently, Schindler and co-workers successfully employed an Ir energy-transfer catalyst to generate triplet-state aromatic alkenes under 427 nm irradiation, which subsequently undergo addition to ground-state acyclic α-imino esters (Figure c). , Although Schindler’s approach significantly advanced this field, the structures of acyclic imines remain limited to α-imino esters.

We herein address this issue by employing strongly electron-deficient N-(fluorosulfonyl)­aldimines in reactions with triplet-state styrenes generated via energy-transfer catalysis, − taking into account that intermolecular [2 + 2] cycloadditions of triplet-state alkenes are facilitated by pairing electronically differentiated substrates (Figure d). − During the course of our study, Willis and co-workers reported an aza Paternò-Büchi reaction of triplet-state N-(fluorosulfonyl)­aldimines that react with ground-state aliphatic alkenes and a selected group of aromatic alkenes, a result complementary to our study in terms of mechanism and substrate scope (Figure e).

Our initial key observation was the clear reactivity difference between N-(fluorosulfonyl)­imine 1a and N-tosylimine 2a in the intermolecular aza Paternò-Büchi reaction using styrene (3a) as the two-carbon synthon and Ir1 as the photosensitizer. The reaction of 1a gave azetidine 4aa as a mixture of diastereomers (1.3:1 d.r.) in 85% isolated yield; in contrast, N-tosylimine 2a did not afford cyclized product 5aa under the same reaction conditions (Figure a). Mechanistically, the concurrent formation of a substantial amount of [2 + 2] styrene dimer unequivocally confirms the generation of triplet-state styrene. The triplet-energy (E T) value of Ir1 (60.1 kcal mol–1) lies below that of 1a (66.0 kcal mol–1), while it is above that of styrene 3a (55.0 kcal mol–1). Stern-Volmer quenching studies with Ir1 revealed that styrene 3a (K sv = 3.14 mM–1) is a more efficient quencher than N-(fluorosulfonyl)­imine 1a (K sv = 0.799 mM–1) in the reaction. In addition, the reduction potential of Ir1 (E 1/2 (M+/M*) = −0.89 V vs SCE) is more positive than that of 1a (E 1/2 red = −1.3 V vs SCE). These observations strongly support the idea that the reaction proceeds via a triplet-energy-transfer pathway from excited Ir1 to styrene 3a, while effectively precluding a single-electron transfer pathway.

To gain more insight into the influence of photosensitizers, we examined several commercially available photosensitizers under the standard conditions (Figure b). Ir2, which possesses identical E T and E 1/2 (M+/M*) values to those of Ir1, furnished 4aa in 61% yield (Entry 2). In contrast, fac-Ir­(ppy)3, which has a lower E T and a more negative E 1/2 (M+/M*) values, did not afford the desired product, and 1a was recovered (Entry 3). Given that the reduction potential of 1a is more positive than that of fac-Ir­(ppy)3, rapid electron transfer/back-electron transfer between fac-Ir­(ppy)3 and 1a is likely to suppress productive bond formation with 3a. In addition, [Ru­(bpy)3]­[PF6]2, exhibiting a lower E T and a comparable E 1/2 (M+/M*) value to that of Ir1, did not furnish the product (Entry 4). A diminished yield was also observed with 4CzIPN, of which E T is slightly lower than that of Ir1 (Entry 5). These results gave further support for the proposed triplet-energy-transfer mechanism.

Interestingly, irradiation at 427 nm in the absence of a photosensitizer gave a small amount of 4aa with a higher diastereomeric ratio of 4.0:1 (Entry 6). Direct photoexcitation at the shorter wavelength of 390 nm afforded 4aa in 60% yield with 3.6:1 dr along with 8% of the styrene dimer (Entry 7). The increased diastereoselectivity can be rationalized by the singlet-state [2 + 2] cyclization proposed by Maruoka and co-workers. We also found that N-(fluorosulfonyl)­imine 1a was essential for styrene dimerization, indicating that 1a itself serves as a triplet sensitizer to generate triplet-state styrene, thereby promoting a concurrent triplet-state [2 + 2] cyclization. Although this uncatalyzed process is mechanistically intriguing (for further discussion, see Figure ), our substrate evaluation revealed its poor scope, aside from the parent styrene (Figure ). Despite requiring a longer reaction time, the reaction conducted on a 1.0 mmol scale successfully furnished 4aa in 67% isolated yield (1.3:1 d.r.) (Entry 8).

To clarify the plausible reaction pathway and the effect of N-substituents on imines, density functional theory (DFT) calculations were carried out (Figure a). As model systems, the reactions of benzaldehyde-derived N-(fluorosulfonyl)­imine 1b with styrene 3a were examined. We initially identified two plausible transition states TS1 F and TS2 F ; in TS1 F , the terminal carbon (C2) of 3a 3adds to the nitrogen atom of 1b, while in TS2 F , the internal one (C3) adds to the carbon atom (C1) of the imine. A significant difference in the activation energy barriers of these pathways (ΔΔG ‡ = ΔG ‡(TS1 F ) – ΔG ‡(TS2 F ) = −6.6 kcal mol–1) indicates that the reaction proceeds via the N–C2 bond formation, in agreement with the theoretical studies reported by Schindler and co-workers. Following the generation of Int F , the C–C bond formation proceeds via minimum energy seam of crossing (MESX) − (ΔG = +6.1 kcal mol–1) to give azetidine 4ba. When starting from N-(phenylsulfonyl)­imine 2b, the initial activation barrier via TS1 Ph was found to be 4.8 kcal mol–1 higher than that of TS1 F , accompanied by a longer interatomic distance between the N and C2 (2.52 Å) than that of TS1 F (2.42 Å), corroborating the experimental result in Figure a (Figure b). A closer examination of the NBO charge distributions in TS1 F and TS1 Ph revealed a large charge transfer from 3a to 1b in TS1 F , not observed in TS1 Ph . Although further investigation is required, this charge transfer might be attributed to the stabilization of the transition state, rationalizing the higher reactivity of electron-deficient N-(fluorosulfonyl) imines.

With the optimized conditions in hand, we next examined the scope of the aza Paternò-Büchi reaction between N-(fluorosulfonyl)­imines 1 and aromatic alkenes 3 (Figure ). First, variations in the imine substrates were investigated. Imines derived from a variety of aldehydes successfully participated in the reaction to afford the corresponding azetidines 4ba–ga, even in the presence of steric hindrance from an ortho substituent or with a pyridinyl group incorporated. Subsequently, we evaluated the applicability of aromatic alkenes by using 1a. Electron-rich methoxystyrenes exhibited high reactivity irrespective of the substituent pattern, affording 4ab, 4ac, and 4ad in excellent yields. On the other hand, electron-deficient para-(trifluoromethyl)­styrene exhibited low reactivity, giving 4ae in 28% yield, with 1a remaining in the crude mixture (63% conversion). The same trend was observed with halo-substituted styrenes; a fluoro substituent was well tolerated to give 4af in 86% yield, whereas chloro or bromo substituents resulted in lower yields, furnishing 4ag and 4ah in 44% and 35% yield, respectively. The reactions using vinyl naphthalene 3i, vinyl thiazole (3j), and α-methylstyrene 3k provided 4ai, 4aj, and 4ak in 79%, 44%, and 82% yields, respectively. We also briefly examined direct photoexcitation at 390 nm (see Figure , Entry 7), which afforded products consistently at a higher diastereomeric ratio, albeit in yields varying from 7 to 66% (Figure , footnote a).

The effect of alkene geometry on product diastereoselectivity was next examined using trans- and cis-β-methylstyrene 3l (Figure ). Under iridium photocatalysis, both trans- and cis-3l efficiently afforded a mixture of trans,cis- and cis,trans-4al regardless of the alkene geometry, corroborating the proposed triplet-state reaction pathway. In contrast, applying direct photoexcitation at 390 nm to these alkenes resulted in a pronounced difference in the diastereomeric ratio, consistent with the findings reported by Maruoka. While the reaction of trans-3l afforded 4al with essentially the same dr, cis-3l was converted mainly to cis,cis-4al, reflecting the alkene geometry. These results suggest that the singlet-state [2 + 2] cyclization dominates in the direct photoexcitation, even though some triplet-state pathway likely operates in parallel considering the photosensitizing capability of N-(fluorosulfonyl)­imines (see discussion on Figure , Entries 6 and 7).

We then investigated the derivatization of 4aa (Figure ). The nitrogen atom could be readily deprotected with LiAlH4, followed by Boc protection to afford 6aa in 75% yield. Calcium bistriflimide-mediated SuFEx reaction developed by Ball and co-workers , enabled the transformation of 4aa into sulfamide 7aa in 91% yield, providing a straightforward route to access sulfonamide-linked scaffolds.

Conclusions

In summary, we have demonstrated that installation of a fluorosulfonyl group to imines facilitates intermolecular aza Paternò-Büchi reactions with triplet-state aromatic alkenes. The distinct reactivity of the fluorosulfonyl group not only underpins the cyclization pathway but also enables straightforward downstream transformations to unprotected azetidines and sulfonamides. These features highlight the utility of this functional group as a platform for advancing azetidine synthesis and its application.

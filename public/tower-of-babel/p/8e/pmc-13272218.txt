Stoichiometric balance and sustained rhythms
Authors: Kuan-Wei Chen, Chih-Wen Shih
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
We investigate periodic solutions of a genetic negative feedback loop model incorporating protein-sequestration-based repression. Stoichiometry is defined as the average ratio of the concentration of repressors to that of activators over one period of a periodic solution. We examine how stoichiometric balance plays a critical role in the emergence of oscillations. Using Hopf bifurcation analysis combined with numerical simulations, we establish the existence of periodic solutions. We further analyze the stoichiometric conditions associated with oscillation generation in the system. To this end, we approximate the stoichiometry by a quantity evaluated at the bifurcation point and justify that this quantity decreases with increasing activator concentration. Our results precisely characterize the stoichiometric range in which sustained oscillations occur, as inferred from the approximating quantity. The validity of this approximation is supported by the properties of Hopf bifurcation and is illustrated by numerical computations. Finally, we address the effects of differential degradation rates on the existence and stability of periodic solutions, as well as on stoichiometric balance.

Introduction

Living organisms exhibit a variety of physiological rhythms that are governed by intrinsic, self-sustained oscillators. In the modeling of circadian rhythms and other biological oscillations, transcriptional feedback loops have been extensively studied as core regulatory motifs. A critical component of these loops is the interaction between transcriptional activators and repressors. The canonical mechanism involves the transcription of repressor mRNA activated by the binding of activators, followed by translation into repressor protein in the cytoplasm. The repressor subsequently translocates into the nucleus, where it inhibits activator activity and represses its own transcription. Tight binding between activators and repressors has been recognized as essential for rhythm generation (Kim and Forger 2012; Kim et al. 2014; Kim 2016; Menet et al. 2010).

A minimal model describing such a negative transcriptional feedback loop uses three variables: the mRNA concentration (M), the corresponding protein (P), and the repressor (R). Conclusions

We have analyzed the existence and properties of oscillations in an ordinary differential equation system modeling a fundamental genetic negative feedback loop. A key feature of the model is its repression mechanism, which is based on protein-sequestration. In particular, we investigated how oscillations arise through the interaction of several parameters, including the activator concentration A and the dissociation constant \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$k_d$$\end{document}kd between the activator and repressor. We further examined how these parameters are influenced by a degradation asymmetry parameter β β, which captures the degree of disparity among the degradation rates of the system components. Our analysis combines Hopf bifurcation theory with the degenerate Routh–Hurwitz criterion. We identified distinct qualitative behaviors of the bifurcating periodic solutions for small versus large values of A, as detailed in Theorem 2.2. For small A, we also observed a saddle-node bifurcation of unstable periodic orbits emerging from the Hopf bifurcation point, confirmed numerically using the AUTO continuation software.

Building on this analysis, we examined the range of stoichiometry \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\langle S \rangle $$\end{document}⟨S⟩ that supports oscillation generation, where stoichiometry is defined as the average ratio of repressor to activator concentrations over a period. Stoichiometric balance has previously been recognized as crucial for sustaining oscillations. Our results provide an explicit characterization of the stoichiometric range associated with stable periodic behavior. Specifically, we approximated \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\langle S \rangle $$\end{document}⟨S⟩ using a quantity \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S^*$$\end{document}S∗ evaluated at the bifurcation point and at the bifurcation value. We justified that \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S^*$$\end{document}S∗ decreases monotonically with increasing A and tends to center around 1. Moreover, we showed how the range of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S^*$$\end{document}S∗ supporting the oscillations depends on the parameter β β, with the widest range occurring when β = 1β=1, corresponding to equal degradation rates. This finding aligns with previous studies suggesting that oscillations are most readily sustained when degradation is balanced.

Finally, we validated the effectiveness of the approximation of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\langle S \rangle $$\end{document}⟨S⟩ by the steady-state quantities \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\overline{S}$$\end{document}S¯ and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S^*$$\end{document}S∗, as illustrated in Fig. 10. The approximation for the case of stable periodic solutions arising from Hopf bifurcation is superior than the ones emergent from the saddle-node bifurcation.

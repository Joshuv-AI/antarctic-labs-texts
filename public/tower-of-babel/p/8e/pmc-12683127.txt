Study on anti-fatigue effect and mechanism of iron source combined with Angelica sinensis and Agrimonia pilosa on pigeons under exercise stress
Authors: Chong Ning, Wei Bu, Xiangbei Meng, Huiyu Li, Xinyu Zhang, Yang Tang, Fengqing Hu, Shan Wang, Chang Tan, Chongting Guo, Yixian Liu, Weixuan Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study aimed to evaluate the therapeutic effects of sex-specific iron supplementation combined with Angelica sinensis and Agrimonia pilosa on fatigue in pigeons. In Trial 1, 70 pigeons (35 males and 35 females) were randomly divided into seven groups and fed diets supplemented with 100 or 150 mg/kg of ferrous lactate, ferrous gluconate, or ferrous fumarate for 28 days. Blood biochemical indices were monitored weekly to identify the optimal iron source for each sex. The results indicated that female pigeons responded best to 150 mg/kg ferrous lactate, whereas male pigeons responded best to 150 mg/kg ferrous gluconate. In Trial 2, 30 pigeons (15 males and 15 females) were randomly assigned to three groups: control (basal diet), low-dose (3 g/kg A. sinensis: A. pilosa = 1:1 plus sex-specific iron), and high-dose (6 g/kg plus sex-specific iron). Blood samples were collected before supplementation, after 28 days, immediately post-exercise, and after 1 h recovery. Combined nutritional intervention mitigated the accumulation of blood urea nitrogen (BUN) and blood lactic acid (BLA), decreased lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) activity, decreased creatine kinase (CK) release into the bloodstream, reduced malondialdehyde (MDA) levels, enhanced total antioxidant activity (T-AOC) in the serum, and upregulated post-exercise antioxidant capacity in pigeons, ultimately enhancing exercise endurance. This approach shows promise as a sustainable strategy for treating pigeon fatigue.Keywords: Iron source, Angelica sinensis, Agrimonia pilosa, Pigeons, Exercise fatigue

Introduction

Exercise fatigue is a multifaceted physiological and pathological condition characterized by exhaustion, fatigue, and lack of energy (Liu et al., 2022). The definition of fatigue proposed by Chaudhuri et al. has gained widespread acceptance, describing it as a state in which initiating or sustaining autonomous activities becomes challenging. This type of fatigue can be categorized as peripheral or central fatigue (Chaudhuri and Behan, 2004). The exertion involved in pigeon performance and competition often results in fatigue, leading to overexertion, injuries, and fatality. Optimal physical conditions and endurance are typically contingent on adequate nutritional support (Kastelic, et al., 2021). Following exercise-induced fatigue, pigeons and other animals may experience muscle dehydration, weight loss, fatigue, anxiety, depression, drooping tails, and piloerection (Xu, et al., 2013; Yan, et al., 2022; Ye, et al., 2017; Zhang, et al., 2024). Most current central nervous system stimulants available in the market purport to possess "anti-fatigue" properties, primarily functioning by stimulating nerve excitability to transiently alleviate fatigue rather than truly mitigating it (Yu, et al., 2023). Once the effects of medication subside, fatigue resurfaces (van Lint, et al., 2023). Nonetheless, excessive consumption of such chemicals can result in adverse reactions such as hyperactivity, tachycardia, and potential collapse (Lakhan and Kirchgessner, 2012; Sanchez-Ramos, 2015). Developing a Chinese herbal compound is crucial for the effectively preventing and managing exercise-induced fatigue in pigeons.

Danggui (Angelica sinensis) is a traditional Chinese herbal medicine with immunoregulatory properties (Zhuang, et al., 2012). Studies have demonstrated its efficacy in alleviating fatigue and enhancing immune function (Liu, et al., 2011; Yang, et al., 2006), thereby mitigating fatigue through antiviral and immune-enhancing mechanisms (Yang, et al., 2012). Moreover, Angelica sinensis enhances exercise performance in trained animals, protecting them from physical fatigue by significantly extending exercise duration, lowering blood lactic acid (BLA) and blood urea nitrogen (BUN) levels, and ultimately alleviating fatigue (Yeh, et al., 2014). Agrimonia pilosa Ledeb, another Chinese herbal medicine, has hemostatic, antimalarial, antidiarrheal, detoxifying, and immune-enhancing properties. It is commonly used to treat tumors, trichomoniasis, diarrhea, and dysentery (Jin, et al., 2022). It possesses anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and anti-fatigue properties (Huang, et al., 2023). Studies have demonstrated its ability to extend swimming endurance in mice and lower BLA and BUN levels, thereby mitigating fatigue (Wen, et al., 2022). Furthermore, it can ameliorate metabolic disorders, such as hyperglycemia and liver steatosis, reduce the body's metabolic load, and consequently exert an indirect anti-fatigue effect (Guo, et al., 2023). Moreover, this natural herbal medicine is considered a viable alternative to antibiotics in animal feed due to its ease of extraction and residue-free nature (Bo, et al., 2023; Gao, et al., 2023). Iron is a trace element that is ubiquitous in living organisms and has been previously linked to fatigue by researchers (Akkad, et al., 2015; Holbein and Lehmann, 2023). Iron deficiency can significantly impair motor function (Lai, et al., 2017). Zheng Wang et al. reported that dietary iron levels have significant effects on plasma iron (PI), serum ferritin, lacto iron content, liver catalase (CAT) activity, and cardiac succinate dehydrogenase (SDH) activity in breeding pigeons (Wang, et al., 2023). Adequate dietary iron intake is a practical, cost-effective, and sustainable approach, with research indicating that consuming iron-fortified foods is a highly effective strategy for preventing iron deficiency (Man, et al., 2022).

The aim of this study was to investigate the impact of iron sources combined with Angelica sinensis and Agrimonia Pilosa on the growth performance and blood parameters of fatigued pigeons and to identify suitable iron sources based on sex. In addition, we aimed to elucidate the mechanisms underlying fatigue relief. Physicochemical indicators were assessed using various kits, and network pharmacology was used to analyze the pivotal targets and pathways associated with alleviating exercise-induced fatigue. This study is expected to offer novel insights and methodologies for advancing our understanding of fatigue alleviation mechanisms in avian species.

Materials and methods

Reagents

Copper sulfate, potassium sulfate, boric acid, and glucose were purchased from Tianjin City Hengxing Chemical Reagent Manufacturing Co., Ltd. Folin phenol solution was purchased from Beijing Boao Tuoda Science and Technology Co., Ltd. Sodium carbonate and aluminum trichloride were purchased from Tianjin City Yongda Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd. Methanol was purchased from Tianjin City Damao Chemical Reagent Factory Hemoglobin (HGB), soluble iron (SI), total iron-binding capacity (TIBC), transferrin saturation (TSAT), blood lactic acid (BLA), lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), total antioxidant activity (T-AOC), and malondialdehyde (MDA) kits were purchased from Beijing Solaibao Technology Co., Ltd. Creatine kinase (CK) and blood urea nitrogen (BUN) kits were purchased from Nanjing Jiancheng Biotechnology Co., Ltd. Angelica sinensis and Agrimonia Pilosa were purchased from Beijing Tongrentang Traditional Chinese Medicine Co., Ltd. Ferrous gluconate, lactate, and fumarate were purchased from Henan Zhongchen Biotechnology Co., Ltd.

Experimental animals and experimental design

Pink-gray pigeons, 50 males and 50 females, were provided by Xuyuan Pigeon Farm in Shenyang. Throughout the experiment, the temperature in the housing room was maintained at 22–26°C, and the relative humidity was maintained at approximately 60 % ± 5 %. Pigeon feed was also provided by Xuyuan Pigeon Farm. All pigeons used in this study were healthy, adult pigeons, approximately 5 months old, and were young adults.

In both trials, each “pair” consisted of one male and one female housed together. Seventy pigeons (35 pairs = 35 males + 35 females) were randomly divided into seven groups of five pairs each (5 pairs per group; n = 5 males and n = 5 females per group). The control group was fed a basal diet, and all pigeons were fed continuously for 28 days according to the dose design. Zheng Wang et al. pointed out that the optimal level of iron supplementation in pigeon diet should not exceed 193 mg/kg (Wang, Zhao, Qin, Shi, Li, Wang and Shao, 2023). The basal diet set as the control group (NC), and six dose groups, supplemented with 100 mg/kg ferrous gluconate (FG-L), 150 mg/kg ferrous gluconate (FG-H), 100 mg/kg ferrous lactate (FL-L), 150 mg/kg ferrous lactate (FL-H), 100 mg/kg ferrous fumarate (FF-L), or 150 mg/kg ferrous fumarate (FF-H), were designed. Blood samples were collected on days 7, 14, 21, and 28 of the experiment. Approximately 2 mL of venous blood was collected from the pigeon wings using 2 mL EDTA anticoagulant tubes and disposable venous blood collection needles.

In the first trial, we aimed to investigate the interaction between sex and different forms of iron supplementation in pigeons. Based on those findings, ferrous lactate was determined to be more suitable for female pigeons, whereas ferrous gluconate was more appropriate for males. Therefore, in the second trial, these sex-specific iron supplements were applied in all treatment groups to ensure optimized iron supplementation for both sexes.

Following the first experiment, an additional 30 pigeons (15 pairs = 15 males + 15 females) were randomly allocated to three groups of five pairs each (5 pairs per group; n = 5 males and n = 5 females per group): (i) Control group: basal diet only, no supplementation; (ii) Low-dose group: basal diet supplemented with 3 g/kg compound nutrient (Angelica sinensis: Agrimonia pilosa = 1:1), in addition to sex-specific iron supplementation (females: 150 mg/kg ferrous lactate; males: 150 mg/kg ferrous gluconate); (iii) High-dose group: basal diet supplemented with 6 g/kg of the same compound nutrient, with the same sex-specific iron supplementation. The experimental group ingested the test samples on day, followed by a second fasting blood collection after 28 days of continuous feeding. Subsequently, all the pigeons underwent immediate training. A third blood sampling occurred post-exercise, with a fourth sampling occurring after a 1-hour rest period following exercise. The centrifuged serum was stored at −20 °C to determine serum biochemical indicators.

Each trial was conducted three times independently under identical conditions, and the mean values were used for statistical analysis.

Determination of the growth performance of the pigeons

In this study, the individual pigeon was considered as the experimental unit for all parameters measured, including growth performance, blood biochemical indicators, and exercise response. Each male and female pigeon within a pair was analyzed separately. The initial weight of each pigeon was determined prior to testing. Body weight and food intake were measured once a week between 6:00 and 7:00 a.m. throughout the experiment, following fasting for 6 hours and prior to feeding. The average daily feed intake (ADFI), total weight gain, and feed-to-weight ratio (F/G) were calculated 28 days after the experiment (Wang, et al., 2024).

Determination of blood biochemical indicators

Serum from blood samples was collected, allowed to stand for 5 minutes, centrifuged at 4000 rpm for 5 minutes, and stored at −20 °C. CK and BUN levels were measured using test kits from Nanjing Jiancheng Institute of Biological Engineering (Nanjing, China). The levels of HGB, SI, TIBC, TSAT, BLA, LDH, T-AOC, and MDA were measured using assay kits from Beijing Solebo Technology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China).

Compound analysis and disease target identification

The bioactive compounds of Angelica sinensis and Agrimonia pilosa were collected from the Database of Systematic Pharmacology of Traditional Chinese Medicine (https://tcmspw.com/tcmsp.php) (Wu, et al., 2021). Bioavailability and drug similarity were used to screen candidate active ingredients with a bioavailability of 30 % and a drug similarity of 0.18. Subsequently, the chicken targets corresponding to the active compounds identified via the Pharmmapper and PubMed databases were normalized using UniProt (https://www.uniprot.org/). Mining disease-related target points from Genecards (https://www.genecards.org/) and OMIM (https://omim.org/) databases.

Data visualization and construction of protein-protein interaction (PPI) network

Drug-disease crossover gene screening. Two lists of drug- and disease-related targets were compiled based on the above steps. Intersection PPI was analyzed using the Stringdb database (http://string-db.org/). Finally, the "drug-compound-target-disease" network was constructed using Cytoscape 3.6.1 software (Jiang, et al., 2022).

Enrichment of gene ontology (GO) functions and enrichment of kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes (KEGG) pathways

Candidate targets were uploaded to the Database for Annotation, Visualization, and Integrated Discovery (DAVID; https://david.ncifcrf.gov/) for GO and KEGG pathway analyses to investigate the involvement of these screening targets in biological signaling pathways (Hu, et al., 2020). Subsequently, data from prior analyses were integrated, and relevant genes and proteins were juxtaposed before being used in the micro-information platform (http://www.bioinformations.com.cn/) to produce bubble plots for GO and KEGG enrichment assessments. Finally, a comprehensive analysis was conducted to draw conclusions based on the results (Chu, et al., 2024).

Data analysis

For HGB data, a linear mixed-effects model (LMM) was applied, with diet (Diet Group), sex (Sex), time (Day), and their interactions specified as fixed effects, and bird identity (BirdID) included as a random effect to account for within-subject correlations. An unstructured covariance structure was selected to model repeated measures. Results are expressed as least squares means (LSMeans) ± standard errors (SE), and significant differences among treatments are indicated by superscript letters.

In the second trial, the LMM was modified to include diet, sex, blood draw time, and their interactions as fixed effects, with BirdID as a random effect. An unstructured covariance structure was likewise applied. Data are presented as LSMeans ± SE, and superscript letters denote significant group differences. Estimated marginal means (LSMeans) and their standard errors (SE) were obtained from a linear mixed model including fixed effects of diet, sex, day, and their interactions, and random effect of individual bird to account for repeated measurements. The covariance structure of the repeated measurements was modeled assuming homogeneous variance across groups at each time point. As a result, the SE values are identical for all groups and sexes within the same day, reflecting the model-based uncertainty of the LSMeans rather than the observed sample variation. LSMeans ± SE are reported in the tables for all time points accordingly.

Serum iron (SI), total iron-binding capacity (TIBC), and transferrin saturation (TAST) were assessed only once at day 28; thus, repeated measures analysis was not applicable. Following the approach of Wang et al. (Wang, Liang, Jiang, Luo, Cheng and Long, 2024), these parameters were analyzed separately for males and females using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (M ± SD). Statistical significance is indicated as *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001, and ns = not significant, compared with the control group.

In this study, each pigeon participated in only one experiment (iron supplementation or mixed nutrition), and comparisons were conducted within the same sex. Therefore, all observations within each experiment were considered independent. Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 22.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA), and figures were generated with GraphPad Prism version 8.0 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA). The analytical approach is consistent with previously published studies and appropriate for the present experimental design.

Effects of compound nutrition on serum biochemical indices of pigeon racing before and after exercise

BUN levels increased in both female and male racing pigeons after exercise compared with the pre-exercise levels. Both the L and H treatment groups showed significantly reduced BUN levels in racing pigeons compared to those in the control group (P < 0.05). This reduction in BUN levels indicated an enhanced ability to eliminate metabolites, with group H showing a more pronounced effect (P < 0.01) (Table 2). Post-exercise serum BLA levels in racing pigeons were elevated compared to the resting levels. However, both the L and H treatment groups exhibited a significant decrease in BLA content compared to those in the control group (P < 0.05), suggesting a notable impact of the compound nutrients on reducing blood lactic acid accumulation (Table 3). The LDH levels in female and male racing pigeons pre- and post-exercise are presented in Table 4. Following exercise, there was a significant increase in serum LDH activity, indicating the release of LDH from the muscles into the bloodstream, a sign of fatigue in racing pigeons. The experimental group demonstrated significantly lower LDH activity than the control group. Although it did not fully return to normal levels after a 1-hour rest, this showed a marked improvement compared to those in the control group. Table 5 displays the T-AOC activity in the serum of female and male pigeons. Following exercise, there was a significant increase in T-AOC activity compared with that in the resting state. After exercise, the serum T-AOC activity of groups L and H surpassed that of group K, suggesting that groups L and H could enhance antioxidant capacity and mitigate fatigue. The variations in CK activity pre- and post-exercise are depicted in Table 6. Compared to those in the exercise control group, the serum CK levels in groups H and L treated with compound nutrients were notably reduced (P < 0.01), indicating that the compound nutrient treatment in group H significantly decreased CK release into the bloodstream compared to that in group L. This alleviated muscle cell damage induced by intense exercise and mitigates exercise-induced fatigue. Malondialdehyde (MDA) content in the blood of racing pigeons steadily increased post-exercise compared with that in the resting control group. Furthermore, the compound nutrient significantly lowered MDA levels in the blood of exercise pigeons compared to those in the exercise control group (P < 0.05), thereby alleviating exercise-induced fatigue (Table 7).

Network pharmacological analysis

A database search identified 22 active components in Angelica sinensis and Agrimonia pilosa. A total of 308 active-ingredient targets and 10,019 disease-related genes were identified and visualized using a Venn diagram (Fig. 3A). Key targets were identified based on degree values, revealing anti-fatigue-related proteins such as AKT1, EGFR, SRC, CASP3, PPARG, ESR1, PTGS2, and MMP9 through protein interaction network analysis (Fig. 3B and C). Details of these proteins are listed in Table 8. Common targets of drugs and diseases were subjected to GO enrichment analysis using the microinformation-mapping website (http://www.bioinformations.com.cn/), resulting in 1,103 enriched GO entries. These entries encompassed 708 biological processes (BP) related to chromatin remodeling, protein phosphorylation, and the hepatin receptor signaling pathway, 109 cellular components (CC) including the receptor complex, plasma membrane, and cytoplasm, and 286 molecular functions (MF), such as histone H2AXY142 kinase activity, histone H3Y41 kinase activity, and protein tyrosine kinase activity (Fig. 4A). The KEGG analysis highlighted significant pathway changes, including cancer pathways, neuroactive ligand-receptor interactions, and nitrogen metabolism (Fig. 4B). These findings suggest that compound nutrient treatment modulates exercise-induced fatigue in pigeons.

Discussion

Exercise-induced fatigue causes long-term exhaustion and significantly affects daily functioning in organisms. (Liu, Li, Shen and Liu, 2022) Research indicates that fatigue resulting from exercise can trigger immune deficiencies, endocrine disruptions, and cognitive decline, ultimately contributing to the development of chronic fatigue syndrome (CFS) (Liu, et al., 2023a). Athletic fatigue commonly coincides with reduced exercise performance, leading to diminished quality of life (Liu, et al., 2023b). Fatigue can compromise the kinesthetic and proprioceptive functions of joints by elevating the threshold for muscle spindle activation, disrupting sensory feedback and altering joint perception (Bestwick-Stevenson, et al., 2022). Angelica sinensis, Agrimonia Pilosa, and iron supplements have garnered attention because of their potential to enhance immune function and alleviate fatigue in animals. Nonetheless, further investigation is warranted to elucidate the precise effects and mechanisms of action of these bioactive components in attenuating growth inhibition and oxidative stress caused by exercise-induced fatigue.

Intense exercise-induced fatigue causes alterations in hemoglobin levels, a common physiological response in various organisms (Villafaina, et al., 2023). Prior research has indicated that iron plays a crucial role in ferritin and protein system functionality, and also contributes to regulating bacterial infections (Tan, et al., 2021; Taschetto, et al., 2017). Iron deficiency can result in modifications in red blood cell structure, compromising the ability of hemoglobin to facilitate intracellular oxygen and carbon dioxide transport, impacting oxygen delivery within the body, and ultimately diminishing exercise performance (van der Meer and van der Wal, 2019). In the present study, pigeons were administered ferrous gluconate, ferrous lactate, and ferrous fumarate supplements, which resulted in elevated hemoglobin levels. Notably, ferrous lactate supplementation exhibited the most favorable outcomes in female pigeons, whereas ferrous gluconate supplementation proved to be the most effective in male racing pigeons. The findings revealed variations in hemoglobin levels, serum iron content, total iron-binding capacity, and transferrin saturation between male and female subjects. These differences may be influenced by multiple factors such as hormonal levels, reproductive cycles, nutritional status, and physiological processes (Huang, et al., 2024; Murphy, 2014; Plaza-Diaz, et al., 2023; Tao, et al., 2023). In mammals, males typically exhibit higher hemoglobin levels, possibly due to their larger body size and increased muscle metabolic rate. Androgens, such as testosterone, are believed to stimulate erythropoiesis and potentially enhance iron absorption and utilization. Conversely, females undergo cyclic blood loss during reproductive processes, such as menstruation and childbirth, leading to iron depletion, which can impact hemoglobin synthesis. Non-pregnant female mammals commonly experience iron loss during menstruation, whereas pregnant females face elevated iron demands owing to fetal growth and blood loss during delivery. Consequently, females may exhibit lower hemoglobin levels at certain stages, predisposing them to anemia. In cases of inadequate iron intake or limited absorption capacity, hemoglobin levels may decrease despite heightened physiological needs (Murphy, 2014). Notably, male hepatocytes demonstrated increased mitochondrial iron content and reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels compared to those of the female hepatocytes, which exhibited reduced expression of transferrin receptor 1 (TfR1) and mitochondrial iron transporter 1 (Mfrn1) (Tao, Dar, Tian, Banerjee, Glazer, Srinivasan, Zhu, Pacifici and He, 2023). Sex specific variations extend to serum ferritin levels and neurodevelopmental outcomes. Female infants exhibit significantly higher serum ferritin levels than male infants, with ferritin levels positively associated with cognitive development scores in females (Plaza-Diaz, Ruiz-Ojeda, Morales, Martín-Masot, Climent, Silva, Martinez-Blanch, Enrique, Tortajada and Ramon, 2023). Furthermore, disparities in serum trace element and mineral levels contribute to variations in hemoglobin levels, serum iron content, total iron-binding capacity, and transferrin saturation between male and female racing pigeons (Huang, He, Zhang, Zhong, Lai and Jia, 2024).

Exercise fatigue is a multifaceted physiological occurrence closely linked to an imbalance in energy metabolism, accumulation of metabolites, and oxidative stress (Kang, et al., 2023). The damage resulting from exercise-induced fatigue can induce oxidative stress, a ubiquitous physiological reaction in various organisms (Mallett and McGrath, 2024). Previous research has established an association between MDA and SOD levels, exercise-induced fatigue, and oxidative stress (Cheng, et al., 2025). In the present study, the concurrent administration of Angelica sinensis and agrimonia notably decreased BUN and BLA levels, indicating a potential enhancement in eliminating detrimental metabolites and consequent fatigue alleviation (Lu, et al., 2023). Moreover, the diminished levels of LDH and CK further validate the protective influence of this combination on muscle injury, likely attributable to its antioxidative characteristics (Sun, et al., 2022). Oxidative stress is a pivotal contributor to exercise-induced fatigue and is characterized by an imbalance between ROS generation and the body's antioxidant capacity (de Koning, et al., 2023). The current study demonstrated that Angelica sinensis and Agrimonia pilosa substantially lowered MDA levels while elevating T-AOC, suggesting their potential to mitigate fatigue through oxidative stress modulation. MDA, a byproduct of lipid peroxidation, decreases in concentration, signifying a reduction in cellular membrane damage (Xu, et al., 2018). Additionally, increased T-AOC levels indicate that this combination reinforces the body's antioxidant defenses, thereby ameliorating exercise-induced oxidative harm (Sun, Liang, Zheng, Liu, Li, Yang and Li, 2022).

The potential molecular mechanisms underlying the anti-fatigue effects of Angelica sinensis and Agrimonia pilosa were elucidated through network pharmacology analysis. The key anti-fatigue proteins identified were AKT1, EGFR, SRC, CASP3, PPARG, ESR1, PTGS2, and MMP9. GO analysis revealed their significant involvement in chromatin remodeling, protein phosphorylation, and hepatocyte growth factor receptor signaling pathways. These processes are crucial for maintaining cellular homeostasis and energy metabolism, suggesting that modulating these fundamental biological activities by related proteins may contribute to the anti-fatigue effects. Chromatin remodeling, a critical process in the regulation of gene expression, affects the expression of genes associated with energy metabolism and oxidative stress tolerance (Lalani, et al., 2006). Protein phosphorylation, particularly by protein kinases such as AKT1 and SRC, is crucial for regulating the cellular energy balance and stress response (Zhang, et al., 2018). KEGG pathway analysis showed that the identified targets play a significant role in various pathways, including cancer, neuroactive ligand-receptor interactions, and nitrogen metabolism. The presence of cancer pathways implies a potential link between fatigue tolerance and cellular stress adaptation mechanisms, because these pathways often involve key regulators of cell survival and energy metabolism (Raninga, et al., 2023). In summary, this study utilized network pharmacology analysis to offer new insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying anti-fatigue effects. The identified targets and pathways suggest that fatigue tolerance is influenced by epigenetic regulation, signal transduction, and metabolic adaptation mechanisms. Further experimental investigations are necessary to validate these findings and to explore potential therapeutic approaches based on these molecular mechanisms.

While each pigeon was treated as an independent experimental unit and repeated measurements were performed with analytical triplicates, the relatively small sample size per sex in each group (n = 5) may limit the ability to detect small or subtle effects. The number of birds was intentionally restricted due to experimental costs and animal welfare considerations, in line with the 3Rs principle (Replacement, Reduction, and Refinement). Nevertheless, the combination of repeated measures, analytical replicates, and three independent trials provides sufficient reliability to detect moderate to large effects, supporting the main conclusions of this study. Future studies with larger cohorts could further increase statistical power and allow detection of smaller changes in biochemical and physiological parameters.

Conclusions

These results indicate that supplementation of pigeon feed with a compound nutrient mixture (6 g/kg, Angelica sinensis: Agrimonia pilosa = 1:1) combined with iron can significantly mitigate exercise-induced oxidative stress and fatigue-related biochemical alterations. Among the tested nutrients, iron supplementation showed the most beneficial effects, with ferrous lactate (150 mg/kg) being most effective in females and ferrous gluconate (150 mg/kg) in males. Network pharmacology analysis suggested that these compounds may exert their anti-fatigue effects by modulating key proteins (AKT1, EGFR, SRC, CASP3, PPARG, ESR1, PTGS2, and MMP9). Collectively, these findings provide practical guidance for nutrient supplementation strategies in modern livestock production and expand the application prospects of Angelica sinensis and Agrimonia pilosa in network pharmacology research.

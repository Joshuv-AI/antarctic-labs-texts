A fire image transmission method via automatic fire alarm system data bus for remote fire verification in unattended locations: design and experiment
Authors: Liming Li, Liwei Song, Wentao Ma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study attempts to address the technical challenges of early fire detection and rapid remote verification in unattended locations. A fire image transmission method based on the data bus of Automatic Fire Alarm System (AFAS) is proposed. The Gaussian difference algorithm is used to extract feature parameters of background image and fire image. Difference regions are accurately identified by comparing the feature extrema of these two images. An encoding strategy is adopted to perform compression encoding only on the difference regions instead of the full fire image. This significantly reduces the transmitted data volume and improves the transmission efficiency, enabling remote transmission of fire image via the existing data bus of AFAS. Experimental results show that the remote transmission time of fire image is mainly affected by the resolution of the image acquisition module, the proportion of the difference region, and the transmission distance. By adjusting the resolution of the acquisition module and the layout position of the detector, the effective transmission distance could be extended. The proposed method requires neither modifying the wiring of the existing AFAS nor building a new dedicated image transmission line, thus offering significant application advantages.

Introduction

Unmanned operation is a major feature of some electrical sites1, such as power substations, underground power utility tunnels, wind turbine nacelles. Substations are responsible for voltage transformation, power distribution, and voltage regulation through current control2. Underground power utility tunnels improve the disaster resistance of urban power systems and mitigate the impact of fires and geological hazards on power supply3. Wind turbines play a pivotal role in advancing renewable energy adoption, combating climate change, ensuring energy security, and promoting sustainable development. However, in actual situation, these mentioned electrical sites are prone to fire4. If there is no effective means to monitor the fire, serious accidents will occur5–7, causing injuries, fatalities, and property damage.

The Automatic Fire Alarm System (AFAS) serves as the “first line of defense” in the fire prevention and control system. It monitors fire characteristic parameters in real time and quickly triggers early warning signals. This wins crucial time for personnel evacuation and initial fire fighting, making AFAS a core infrastructure for reducing fire losses. Practices and researches around the world have fully confirmed that the effective operation of AFAS can greatly improve the efficiency of fire emergency disposal. It also helps reduce casualties and property losses. Statistical data from the U.S. National Fire Protection Association shows specific effects. In accidents without fire detectors, the mortality rate per 100 fires is 1.04 people. In contrast, in fire accidents with fire detectors installed, the mortality rate per 100 fires drops to 0.57 people. This represents an average mortality rate reduction of 45%8.

For the mentioned unattended electrical sites, early fire detection and rapid remote fire alarm verification have become prominent pain points in fire safety9,10, as on-site fire verification needs a certain walking/driving distance and time. Similar unattended locations also encounter similar challenges in rapid fire alarm verification. The main way of fire alarm for these places is monitored by smoke sensor or temperature sensor11. Multi-information fusion technology has been applied to industrial safety monitoring system, and substation fire monitoring system12–14 to reduce false alarm, or delay alarm.

Within these, sensor and camera-based remote monitoring have recently gained great attention15–20. Scholars have conducted a series of researches on remote fire image encoding transmission and fire alarm verification. Their works cover different aspects of the field. In the field of image encoding, Sun et al. designed an attention fusion source-channel coding scheme. This scheme captures global and local features of images through a multi-branch attention module. During decoding, it adaptively adjusts feature weights using channel state information. This not only ensures computational efficiency but also improves the transmission robustness21. Liu et al. proposed a network coding scheme based on two-dimensional CNN. The scheme extracts deep spatial structure features of images through CNN. It reduces node computational complexity and cuts down transmission bandwidth consumption by about 30% while maintaining reconstruction quality22. In the research of low-bandwidth transmission, Jiang et al. developed the ASE-JSCC framework. This framework uses a semantic extraction module and a feature selection module. It only transmits semantic features that are crucial for downstream tasks in images. As a result, it reduces transmitted data by about 80% while preserving key information23. Zhang et al. proposed a DiSC-Med framework, combining diffusion models with semantic communication for image transmission. This enables high-quality image transmission under low bandwidth24. Other scholars proposed a feature-aware non-uniform quantization method. This method dynamically allocates code rates according to the contribution of features to image reconstruction. It optimizes image quality under bandwidth-constrained conditions25. In terms of fire alarm recognition and system design, Elhanashi et al. analyzed the advantages of deep learning frameworks (such as CNN and YOLO) in fire alarm recognition. They also pointed out problems in current remote fire alarm detection, including insufficient datasets and high false alarm rates26. Udurume et al. designed a fire monitoring system for industrial scenarios. It combines lightweight communication protocols with anomaly detection technology. Sensors equipped with YOLOv5 fire detection modules collect data and transmit it to the server. The delay of this system is as low as 150 milliseconds, enabling rapid remote fire verification27. Mahgoub et al. designed an edge computing-based fire alarm system for the Internet of Things. This system alleviates the lack of remote early warning in existing fire alarm systems. Its single-node coverage area reaches 1400 square meters28.

At present, there are few practical joint alarm schemes that integrate fire detection system and video surveillance system. Moreover, the existing ones are relatively high in cost. Fire imaging has specific requirements: it has low requirements for image resolution but high requirements for real-time performance and reliability of data transmission. Therefore, developing highly compressed image algorithms has become a development trend. These algorithms aim to realize the collinear transmission of images and smoke data. They also help complete low-cost combined smoke detection and image fire alarm recognition.

Although existing researches have made significant progress, there is still room for in-depth exploration. Specifically, the dedicated image compression and transmission technology for traditional fire detection bus needs more study. This technology should balance the requirements of low image reconstruction accuracy and high anti-interference ability. In particular, there is a lack of mature technical paths. These paths are needed to achieve stable and real-time image transmission on the data bus of existing AFAS.

Therefore, this study focuses on the transmission characteristics of the data bus of AFAS. It explores an image transmission method suitable for unattended scenarios. This method adopts an encoding strategy to compress and encode the difference region between fire image and background image. Through feature-level difference recognition, only the changed parts caused by fire are transmitted. It avoids full-image encoding, which reduces transmission data volume and improves transmission speed. This fundamentally resolves the large data transmission bottleneck in remote fire verification systems. Thus, it provides a technical support for rapid remote fire verification in unattended locations.

Method

General framework

The flowchart of the fire image transmission method based on the data bus of AFAS is presented in Fig. 1. In addition to the fire detection module, the on-site fire detector is embedded with four modules: image acquisition, processing, recognition, and encoding. The image acquisition module is equipped with a camera for collecting background and fire images. It captures non-fire background images at a specific frequency to generate background image information. This information undergoes quality evaluation first. Qualified background image information is transmitted to the processing module. After denoising via a Kalman filter in processing module, the background image is transmitted to the recognition module for further comparison. Simultaneously, the background image is sent to the encoding module as well. The encoding module compresses the image into PNG format, encodes the compressed data per the bus protocol, and transmits it to the monitoring center via the data bus, where the data is sequentially decoded, decompressed and stored as an updated reference background image.

When a fire occurs, the fire detection module triggers the camera to capture a fire image and generate fire image information. The fire image also undergoes quality evaluation. Qualified fire image information is transmitted to the processing module. After denoising in processing module, it is transmitted to the recognition module. The recognition module extracts features from both fire image and the background image, then compares these two sets of features. It identifies the difference regions between fire and background images, generates recognition results based on these differences, and transmits the results to the encoding module. The encoding module performs image compression and encoding on the recognition results, then transmits them to the monitoring center via the data bus. The monitoring center decodes and decompresses the transmitted data, and synthesizes the data with pre-stored background image to obtain the final output fire image.

If the attendants of the monitoring center verify a fire through the transmitted image, they could immediately remotely activate fire suppression systems to handle the incipient fire. Otherwise, if the alarm is confirmed to be a false alarm through the transmitted image, the fire alarm will be cleared, or personnel will be dispatched to conduct further on-site verification of the alarm situation.

Experimental equipment and environment

Tests were performed in a standard combustion chamber complying with GB 4715 − 202429. The chamber’s ceiling height is adjustable between 2 m and 12 m. A polyurethane standard test fire was used as the fire source. Prior to the test, the chamber was ventilated. Ventilation stopped when the thermocouple, optical smoke density meter, and ion smoke concentration meter showed the following readings: temperature of (23 ± 5) ℃, m value < 0.02 dB/m, and y value < 0.05.

The most used fire protection two-wire bus and corresponding protocol in practical engineering were adopted. RVS twisted-pair cables with the cross-sectional area of 1.0 mm² were employed to connect the detectors and controller as the data bus, consistent with practical engineering practices. All cables were set to a length of 10 m in the tests, with the exception of the transmission distance test. For all tests other than those for studying the effect of the differential region proportion, the ceiling height of the combustion chamber was fixed at 4 m, and the distance between the fire source and the detector was adjusted to ensure that the the proportion of difference region reached approximately 30% upon fire alarm. Acquisition modules with the resolution of 1024 × 768 were used in the tests, with the exception of the resolution influence test. These configurations were adopted to ensure the consistency of experimental parameters and the repeatability of test results.

After igniting the polyurethane test fire, the m-value change curve was recorded. It was compared with the standard m-value range for polyurethane test fires specified in GB 4715 − 202429, as shown in Fig. 2. The results show that the m-value curve of the simulated test fire falls within the standard range, confirming the fire source meets the test requirements. During the test, the controller issued an audible and visual alarm upon receiving the detector’s alarm signal. At this moment, the m-value of the optical smoke density meter was recorded, and the test was stopped. The transmission time was also recorded.

Transmission implementation verification

Three transmission function verification tests were conducted firstly. Test results are shown in Fig. 3. The transmission times of the three tests were 1.52 s, 1.62 s, and 1.41 s, with corresponding m-values of 0.66 dB/m, 0.74 dB/m, and 0.88 dB/m. All detectors sent fire alarm signals and transmitted on-site fire images at the alarm moment to the controller. This verifies that the basic transmission function of the proposed method is feasible.

Influence of acquisition module resolution

The influence of the acquisition module resolution on the transmission time was analyzed. Four acquisition modules with different resolutions of 640 × 480, 1024 × 768, 1280 × 960, and 1600 × 1200 were used for the tests. Their corresponding fire image sizes were approximately 300kB, 580kB, 830kB, and 1100kB respectively.

The influence of the acquisition module resolution on the image transmission time is shown in Fig. 4, which also compares the transmission time of the proposed method and full-image transmission. Each test was conducted three times repeatedly. The figure presents the average value of the three repeated tests, as well as the range of maximum and minimum values by error bar. Except for the transmission of complete images, the full-image transmission control group adopts the same image acquisition, encoding/decoding and transmission strategies as those employed in this study. It can be seen that under the same proportion of the difference region, the transmission time of the proposed method is less affected by the image size. In contrast, the traditional full-image transmission method is significantly affected by image size. As the image size increases, the transmission time increases significantly. This difference originates from the difference region transmission strategy. The proposed method only encodes and transmits the difference region between fire and background images instead of the entire image. This effectively avoids the dominant impact of image size on transmission time, highlighting the method’s advantages in data compression and transmission.

Influence of the proportion of difference region

This section analyzes how the proportion of difference region (determined by the acquisition module’s field of view) affects the transmission time. The proportion of difference region was adjusted by modifying the combustion chamber’s ceiling height and the relative viewing angle between the fire source and the detector.

The influence of proportion of difference region on the image transmission time is shown in Fig. 5. It can be seen that the proportion of the difference region has a significant influence on the image transmission time. As the proportion of the difference region increases, the required image transmission time also increases rapidly. This is because the larger the proportion of the difference region, the more data needs to be encoded and transmitted. Additional auxiliary information (e.g., coordinates, pixel values, labels) also increases, leading to longer transmission delays. It can be concluded that on the premise of ensuring the accuracy of the fire detection module, the transmission efficiency can be effectively improved and the transmission time can be shortened by optimizing the layout position of the detector to reduce the proportion of the difference region in the entire image.

Concurrent transmission performance

To investigate the response capability of the system when multiple detectors alarm and send images simultaneously, a concurrent transmission test was carried out. Two detectors were connected to the controller through one detection bus loop.

A total of 2 groups of tests were conducted, and the results are shown in Fig. 6. It can be seen that in the first group of tests, the transmission times of the two detectors were 1.51 s and 1.44 s, and the corresponding m values were 0.72 dB/m and 0.87 dB/m respectively. In the second group of tests, the transmission times were 1.41 s and 1.56 s, and the corresponding m values were 0.82 dB/m and 0.76 dB/m respectively. The experimental results indicate that the detector developed based on the proposed method has good concurrent transmission capability. The alarm thresholds of the two detectors are close, and the fire image transmission time are nearly identical, with no obvious transmission congestion or sudden increase in delay.

Influence of transmission distance

A long-distance transmission test was conducted to evaluate the detector’s alarm and image transmission performance at extended distances. Two detectors were installed in the combustion chamber simultaneously. One detector was connected to the controller through a detection bus loop using 10 m-long RVS twisted-pair cables as the control group; the other detector was connected to the controller through another detection bus loop using RVS twisted-pair cables of 200 m, 500 m, 800 m, and 1000 m in length as the test group. In the long-distance transmission tests, no repeater/relay modules were used.

The influence of the transmission distance (the bus length from the detector to the controller) on the image transmission time is shown in Fig. 7. It can be seen that the transmission time gradually increases with the increase of the transmission distance. When the distance exceeds 500 m, the transmission time shows an accelerated growth trend. This is caused by the signal attenuation effect during bus transmission. As the transmission distance increases, the signal distortion increases, and the controller needs to perform additional signal error correction and restoration processing, resulting in a significant increase in transmission delay. When the transmission distance is 500 m, the transmission time has exceeded 10 s; when it is 1000 m, the longest transmission time reaches nearly 60 s. However, the transmission time of the control group remains basically stable. The experimental results indicate that the developed detector has a certain long-distance transmission capability. Both groups sent alarm signals and transmitted on-site fire images. However, long-distance transmission significantly prolongs image transmission time.

Based on the above analysis, the remote transmission time of fire images is mainly affected by three factors: acquisition module resolution, the proportion of difference region, and the transmission distance. To extend the effective transmission distance, on the premise of ensuring the functional accuracy of the fire detection module, two optimization strategies are recommended: reduce image size by adjusting acquisition module resolution; or reduce the proportion of the difference region by optimizing detector layout. Both can improve transmission efficiency.

Conclusions

Aiming at the technical challenges of early fire detection and rapid verification in unattended locations, this study proposes a fire image transmission method via the data bus of automatic fire alarm systems. Experimental results indicate that the detector developed based on the method proposed in this study achieves an average fire image transmission time of 1.52 s at a transmission distance of 10 m and a 30% proportion of differential regions. No significant congestion occurs during the concurrent transmission of two detectors, with a transmission time difference of less than 0.15 s. The detector could send out fire alarm signals as well as transmit on-site fire images to the controller at the moment of alarm, all within acceptable delay. The system constructed based on the proposed method exhibits favorable concurrent transmission capability. The remote transmission time of fire images is mainly affected by the resolution of the acquisition module, the proportion of difference region, and the transmission distance. Under the premise of ensuring detection accuracy, transmission efficiency and distance can be improved by adjusting the resolution of the acquisition module and the layout of detectors, so as to reduce the image size and the proportion of difference region.

The limitations of this study are as follows: the impact of complex application environments on transmission performance was not investigated; the image transmission time increases significantly when the transmission distance exceeds 500 m; no contribution analysis was performed on the components of transmission time, including the time consumed in stages such as device-side data processing, encoding/decoding, and bus transmission. Future research could focus on optimizing the anti-interference capability of the coding algorithm to adapt to the electromagnetic environments of complex industrial scenarios, extending the effective transmission distance by integrating relay modules, and quantifying the contribution of each component to the total transmission time.

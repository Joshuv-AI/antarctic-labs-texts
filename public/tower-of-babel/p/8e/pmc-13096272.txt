Integrative bioinformatics analyses identify EDNRA and BCL2L1 as candidate hub genes linking predicted MEHP targets and gastric cancer
Authors: Renjie Duan, Shanjun Tao, Aichen Yang, Changzheng Li, Xu Yang, Zhuofu Li, Xiaoxiang Zhu, Kexin Zhang, Linming Lu, Xiaoju Teng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Gastric cancer (GC) remains a major global health burden with poor prognosis. Environmental pollutants such as mono(2-ethylhexyl) phthalate (MEHP), a primary metabolite of di(2-ethylhexyl) phthalate, have been implicated in gastrointestinal carcinogenesis, yet the underlying mechanisms remain elusive. We integrated chemoinformatics, transcriptomics, and computational biology to explore potential molecular mechanisms linking predicted MEHP-associated targets to gastric carcinogenesis. MEHP-associated protein targets were predicted from multiple databases and intersected with gene signatures identified by differential expression and weighted co-expression network analyses of GC transcriptome datasets. Protein-protein interaction network construction combined with machine learning algorithms (LASSO, SVM-RFE, and random forest) identified endothelin receptor type A (EDNRA) and BCL2-like 1 (BCL2L1) as hub genes. Both genes were significantly upregulated in GC across independent cohorts and demonstrated high diagnostic performance, with EDNRA additionally associated with poor prognosis. Gene set enrichment analysis revealed that EDNRA and BCL2L1 were predominantly enriched in pathways such as calcium signaling, cell adhesion, cell cycle, and lysosome. In parallel, immune infiltration profiling indicated that these genes were closely correlated with specific immune cells, including macrophage polarization and T-cell regulation. Molecular docking combined with molecular dynamics simulations provided computational support for the structural and energetic plausibility of MEHP interactions with both hub proteins, with van der Waals and electrostatic interactions contributing to binding stability. Collectively, our findings provide systems-level evidence suggesting that MEHP-associated molecular targets may intersect with EDNRA- and BCL2L1-related signaling and immune networks relevant to gastric carcinogenesis. These findings contribute to the understanding of potential toxicogenomic links between phthalate exposure and gastric cancer and highlight EDNRA and BCL2L1 as candidate biomarkers within MEHP-associated molecular networks.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-026-04829-0.

Introduction

Gastric cancer (GC) is among the most common and deadly malignancies, ranking fifth in incidence and third in cancer-related deaths worldwide [8, 10, 12, 19, 22]. Despite improvements in both surgical techniques and systemic therapies, the prognosis of advanced GC remains dismal due to late diagnosis, high recurrence, and limited therapeutic efficacy [3, 5, 15]. The etiology of GC is multifactorial, involving complex interactions among genetic susceptibility, dietary factors, Helicobacter pylori infection, and environmental exposures [11, 24, 28]. Notably, chemical contaminants from food and the environment are increasingly recognized as critical drivers of tumor initiation and progression [4].

Mono(2-ethylhexyl) phthalate (MEHP), the primary metabolite of the widely used plasticizer di(2-ethylhexyl) phthalate (DEHP), is of particular concern because of its ubiquitous presence in both the environment and human biological samples [2, 14]. MEHP has been linked to diverse toxicological effects, including endocrine disruption, metabolic disorders, and reproductive impairment [13, 14, 27]. Emerging evidence suggests a connection between phthalate exposure and gastrointestinal malignancies, yet the potential molecular mechanisms linking MEHP exposure to gastric carcinogenesis remain poorly understood [18, 23]. Human exposure through contaminated food, water, and medical devices continues to rise, underscoring the urgency of elucidating potential molecular links between MEHP exposure and gastric tumorigenesis.

To address this gap, we applied a comprehensive multi-omics and computational framework. Putative MEHP targets were predicted from multiple chemoinformatics platforms and integrated with differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) modules derived from GC transcriptome datasets. Protein-protein interaction network construction and machine learning approaches (LASSO, SVM-RFE, random forest) were then used to identify hub genes. Their functional relevance was explored through enrichment analysis, immune infiltration profiling, and survival assessment, followed by molecular docking and dynamics simulations to assess the structural plausibility of MEHP-protein interactions.

Our findings highlight endothelin receptor type A (EDNRA) and BCL2-like 1 (BCL2L1) as key hub genes potentially linking MEHP-associated targets to gastric carcinogenesis, providing novel insights into the toxicogenomic mechanisms of phthalate exposure. These results not only deepen the understanding of environmental chemical-cancer interactions but also suggest potential biomarkers and therapeutic targets for GC associated with environmental pollutants. Materials and methods

Data collection and preprocessing

The molecular structure of MEHP was retrieved from the PubChem database (CID: 20393; https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/20393). Putative protein targets of MEHP were predicted using multiple chemoinformatics platforms, including ChEMBL (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/chembl/), SEA (https://sea.bkslab.org/), TargetNet (http://targetnet.scbdd.com/), STITCH (http://stitch.embl.de/), and SwissTargetPrediction (http://swisstargetprediction.ch/). The canonical SMILES representation of MEHP (CCCCC(CC)COC(= O)C1 = CC = CC=C1C(= O)O), obtained from PubChem, was used as the standardized input for all target prediction analyses. Predicted targets from each database were integrated, and duplicate entries were removed to obtain a final set of nonredundant MEHP-associated targets.

Transcriptome datasets of GC were obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database. The GSE54129 dataset (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?%20acc=GSE54129), containing 111 GC tissues and 21 normal gastric tissues, was selected as the primary dataset. GSE13911 (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?%20acc=GSE13911), comprising 38 GC tissues and 31 normal tissues, was used as a validation dataset. Raw data were preprocessed using the R software (version 4.4.1) with the “limma” package, including background adjustment, log2 transformation, and data normalization.

Differential expression analysis

DEGs between GC and normal tissues in GSE54129 were identified using the “limma” package in R, and p-values were adjusted for multiple testing using the Benjamini–Hochberg (BH) false discovery rate (FDR) method. Genes with |log2 fold change| > 0.5 and nominal P < 0.01 were initially retained for integrative analyses. Results were visualized with volcano plots and heatmaps generated using the “ggplot2” and “pheatmap” packages.

Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA)

To identify gene modules associated with GC, weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) was conducted using the “WGCNA” package in R. An optimal soft-thresholding power was selected according to the scale-free topology criterion. Genes were subsequently clustered into modules through hierarchical clustering combined with dynamic tree cutting. Module-trait correlations were then calculated to determine the modules most strongly associated with GC.

To evaluate the robustness of network construction, sensitivity analyses were performed by varying the soft-thresholding power (β = 7–9) and adjusting dynamic tree-cut parameters (including minModuleSize and mergeCutHeight). Across these parameter settings, the overall module architecture and identification of GC-associated modules were largely consistent.

Integration of MEHP targets and GC genes

MEHP-predicted targets, DEGs, and WGCNA-derived module genes were intersected to identify MEHP-related GC targets. The overlapping genes were imported into the STRING database (https://string-db.org/) to construct a protein-protein interaction (PPI) network with a confidence score threshold of 0.4. The PPI network was visualized and analyzed using Cytoscape (version 3.10.2).

Functional enrichment analysis

Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analyses were performed using the “clusterProfiler” package in R. Significantly enriched terms were identified with an adjusted p-value < 0.05, and results were visualized using the CNSknowall online platform (https://www.cnsknowall.com/).

Machine learning algorithms for Hub gene identification

To further refine the potential MEHP-related GC targets, three complementary machine learning algorithms were applied to the 37 overlapping genes. The least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression was performed using the “glmnet” package in R, with ten-fold cross-validation to prevent overfitting and the optimal penalty parameter λ selected based on the minimum cross-validation error. Support vector machine-recursive feature elimination (SVM-RFE) was conducted using the “e1071” package to select the most informative feature subsets. In addition, random forest (RF) analysis was implemented with the “randomForest” package, and feature importance was ranked according to the mean decrease in accuracy and mean decrease in Gini index. The intersection of candidate genes identified by these three algorithms and the top-ranked nodes in the PPI network was defined as candidate hub genes, reflecting statistical convergence across feature selection methods and topological importance within the interaction network rather than experimentally validated causal drivers.

Validation of Hub genes

The expression levels of hub genes were validated in the GSE54129 and GSE13911 datasets by comparing GC and normal tissues. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were generated using the “pROC” package to assess diagnostic performance in both datasets. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis with the log-rank test was performed using the “survival” package based on TCGA GC data from The Cancer Genome Atlas stomach adenocarcinoma project (TCGA-STAD), which were obtained via the Genomic Data Commons (GDC) Data Portal (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/projects/TCGA-STAD). Patients were stratified into high- and low-expression groups according to the median expression level.

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA)

Single-gene GSEA was conducted to explore the biological functions of the hub genes in GC. RNA-seq data from TCGA-STAD were downloaded via the GDC using the TCGAbiolinks package and preprocessed by filtering out low-expression genes followed by normalization. For each hub gene, samples were stratified into high- and low-expression groups according to the median expression level. GSEA was then performed with the clusterProfiler package, with pathways considered significantly enriched at a nominal p-value < 0.05 and a false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.25. Results were visualized using the enrichplot package.

Immune infiltration analysis

The relative proportions of 22 immune cell subtypes in GC and normal samples from the GSE13911 dataset were estimated using the CIBERSORT algorithm with 1,000 permutations. Spearman correlation analysis was then performed to evaluate the associations between hub gene expression and immune cell infiltration levels.

Molecular docking

Three-dimensional structures of EDNRA and BCL2L1 were obtained from the Protein Data Bank (PDB, https://www.rcsb.org/). For EDNRA, the cryo-EM structure (PDB ID: 8HCQ), representing the endothelin-1–bound receptor in an active-state conformation, was used. The structure of BCL2L1 was retrieved from PDB ID: 7JGW. The MEHP ligand structure was downloaded from PubChem (CID: 20393) and converted into three-dimensional format using Open Babel.

Molecular preprocessing was performed using PyMOL 3.1 and AutoDock Tools 4.2.6, including removal of crystallographic water molecules and hetero-ligands, addition of polar hydrogens, and assignment of Kollman charges for proteins and Gasteiger charges for the ligand. All structures were converted into PDBQT format.

For EDNRA, the docking grid was defined based on the experimentally resolved orthosteric ligand-binding cavity observed in the co-crystallized structure. The grid center was set at the centroid of the co-resolved ligand, and the search space was defined to encompass the orthosteric binding pocket. For BCL2L1, the grid box was positioned to cover the canonical BH3-binding groove.

Docking simulations were performed using AutoDock Vina (version 1.1.2) with an exhaustiveness value of 8. Docking conformations were ranked according to predicted binding affinity, and the lowest-energy pose was selected for subsequent molecular dynamics simulation and structural analysis. The resulting complexes were visualized using PyMOL.

Molecular dynamics simulation

To assess the dynamic stability of MEHP-protein complexes, molecular dynamics (MD) simulations were performed using GROMACS (version 2022.3). Protein topologies were generated with the CHARMM36 force field, while MEHP parameters were obtained using the CGenFF server. Each complex was solvated in a TIP3P water box with 0.15 M NaCl, followed by energy minimization, equilibration (NVT and NPT ensembles, 100 ps each), and 100 ns production runs at 300 K. Trajectories were analyzed for root mean square deviation (RMSD), radius of gyration (Rg), solvent-accessible surface area (SASA), root mean square fluctuation (RMSF), and hydrogen bonding using built-in GROMACS tools. Binding free energies were estimated via the molecular mechanics Poisson-Boltzmann surface area (MM/PBSA) method using the g_mmpbsa package.

Results

Identification of MEHP targets

The molecular structure of MEHP was retrieved from the PubChem database (Fig. 2A). Potential protein targets of MEHP were predicted using multiple resources, including ChEMBL, SEA, TargetNet, STITCH, and SwissTargetPrediction. After integrating the outputs from these databases and eliminating duplicates, a total of 183 nonredundant putative MEHP targets were obtained. The Venn diagram was used to illustrate the overlap and complementarity among different prediction platforms. To preserve sensitivity at the initial screening stage, all nonredundant targets were retained for subsequent multi-layer filtering and integrative analyses (Fig. 2B; Supplementary Table S1).

Acquisition of GC-related targets

The GSE54129 dataset was first subjected to principal component analysis (PCA), which revealed distinct overall gene expression patterns between GC and normal samples (Fig. 3A). Differential expression analysis subsequently identified 5,485 DEGs, including 2,716 upregulated and 2,769 downregulated genes (Supplementary Table S1). The distribution of DEGs was visualized using a volcano plot (Fig. 3B), while the top 50 most significantly altered genes were displayed in a heatmap to illustrate expression differences (Fig. 3C).

To further identify disease-associated gene networks, WGCNA was performed on the GSE54129 dataset. Using an optimal soft-thresholding power of 8 (Fig. 3D, E), hierarchical clustering combined with dynamic tree cutting divided the genes into 22 distinct modules (Fig. 3F). A heatmap was generated to visualize inter-module correlations (Fig. 3G). Module-trait correlation analysis demonstrated that the blue, green, pink, and turquoise modules exhibited the strongest associations with the GC phenotype, each with correlation coefficients greater than 0.7 (Fig. 3H). Moreover, significant positive correlations were observed between gene significance (GS) and module membership (MM) values within these key modules (Fig. 3I-L). Collectively, 4,905 genes from the blue, green, pink, and turquoise modules were retained as GC-related targets (Supplementary Table S1).

Identification of MEHP-related targets in GC

The intersection of MEHP-predicted targets with the DEGs and WGCNA-derived key module genes from the GSE54129 dataset yielded 37 overlapping targets potentially linking MEHP-associated proteins to gastric cancer-related molecular signatures (Fig. 4A; Supplementary Table S1). These 37 candidates were imported into the STRING database to construct a preliminary PPI network (Fig. 4B), which was subsequently visualized using Cytoscape (Fig. 4C). Nodes with higher betweenness centrality were considered more functionally significant, and the top 20 ranked proteins are listed in Supplementary Table S2.

Furthermore, GO and KEGG enrichment analyses were performed for these 37 targets. GO enrichment revealed significant associations with biological processes (BP) such as daunorubicin metabolic process, polyketide metabolic process, and aminoglycoside antibiotic metabolic process; cellular components (CC) including azurophil granule lumen, Bcl-2 family protein complex, and primary lysosome; and molecular functions (MF) such as aldose reductase (NADPH) activity, alcohol dehydrogenase (NADP⁺) activity, and aldo-keto reductase (NADPH) activity (Fig. 4D; Supplementary Table S3). KEGG enrichment analysis demonstrated that these targets were significantly enriched in pathways including chemical carcinogenesis-ROS, steroid hormone biosynthesis, and the PPAR signaling pathway (Fig. 4E; Supplementary Table S4). Based on these enriched KEGG pathways, a comprehensive “chemical-target-pathway-disease” network was constructed (Fig. 4F).

Collectively, these results suggest that MEHP-associated targets overlap with GC-related multi-target and multi-pathway networks.

Identification of hub genes associated with MEHP-related targets in GC

Using the ability to distinguish GC from normal samples in the GSE54129 dataset as the evaluation criterion, three machine learning algorithms were employed to further refine the 37 potential targets. LASSO regression identified 17 candidate genes (Fig. 5A, B), SVM-RFE identified 16 (Fig. 5C, D), and RF analysis identified 8 (Fig. 5E, F) (Supplementary Table S5). The intersection of these machine learning-derived candidates with the top 20 genes ranked by betweenness centrality in the PPI network yielded two hub genes: EDNRA and BCL2L1 (Fig. 5G). These two genes were therefore identified as key hub genes at the intersection of MEHP-associated targets and gastric cancer-related molecular networks.

Association of hub genes with GC

In the GSE54129 dataset, EDNRA and BCL2L1 were significantly upregulated in GC tissues compared with normal controls (Fig. 6A). Consistent results were observed in the validation dataset GSE13911 (Fig. 6B). To evaluate their diagnostic potential, ROC curve analysis was performed. In GSE54129, the area under the curve (AUC) values for EDNRA and BCL2L1 were 0.918 and 0.997, respectively (Fig. 6C), while in GSE13911, the corresponding AUC values were 0.838 and 0.684 (Fig. 6D). These results suggest that EDNRA exhibits robust diagnostic accuracy, whereas the performance of BCL2L1 was less consistent across datasets. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis using the log-rank test further demonstrated that high EDNRA expression was significantly associated with poor prognosis in GC patients, whereas BCL2L1 expression showed no significant prognostic impact (Fig. 6E, F).

GSEA analysis revealed that in GC, EDNRA was significantly enriched in pathways including arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy, calcium signaling, and cell adhesion molecules, whereas BCL2L1 was enriched in calcium signaling, cell cycle, and lysosome pathways (Fig. 6G, H). These results suggest that EDNRA and BCL2L1 expression is associated with multiple signaling pathways relevant to GC progression.

Immune infiltration analysis based on the GSE13911 dataset showed that, compared with normal tissues, GC samples exhibited significantly increased proportions of activated CD4 memory T cells, M0/M1/M2 macrophages, activated mast cells, and neutrophils, whereas plasma cells, resting CD4 memory T cells, and resting mast cells were significantly decreased (Fig. 6I). Correlation analysis further revealed that EDNRA expression was positively associated with M0 and M1 macrophages but negatively associated with resting mast cells and plasma cells (Fig. 6J). Meanwhile, BCL2L1 expression was positively correlated with CD8 T cells, while showing negative correlations with activated mast cells, naive CD4 T cells, and γδ T cells (Fig. 6K). These findings suggest that both genes are significantly associated with immune infiltration patterns in GC.

Molecular docking and molecular dynamics simulation analysis

Molecular docking results demonstrated that the binding energies of MEHP with both EDNRA and BCL2L1 proteins were lower than − 5 kcal/mol, suggesting favorable interactions between the ligand and the two targets (Fig. 7A, B). To further explore the dynamic stability of these interactions, 100 ns molecular dynamics (MD) simulations were performed for both the MEHP-EDNRA and MEHP-BCL2L1 complexes. The RMSD trajectories of the two complexes stabilized after approximately 20 ns, indicating that equilibrium was achieved (Fig. 7C, G). The MEHP-EDNRA complex maintained a stable RMSD of ~ 0.35–0.50 nm, whereas the MEHP-BCL2L1 complex exhibited a stable RMSD of ~ 0.60–0.70 nm. The Rg values remained steady throughout the simulations (~ 2.2–2.3 nm for EDNRA and ~ 1.5–1.6 nm for BCL2L1), suggesting no major conformational collapse (Fig. 7D, H). Similarly, SASA values fluctuated only slightly, confirming the compactness of both complexes (Fig. 7E, I). RMSF analysis showed that most residues fluctuated by less than 0.5 nm, except at terminal and loop regions, indicating overall structural stability (Fig. 7F, J). Hydrogen bond analysis revealed persistent interactions (1–3 H-bonds) in each complex, further supporting the stability of binding (Fig. 7K, N).

Binding free energy calculations using the MM/PBSA method further substantiated these findings. Residue decomposition analysis indicated that several amino acids made notable contributions to MEHP binding (Fig. 7L, O). The total binding free energies of the MEHP-EDNRA and MEHP-BCL2L1 complexes were − 21.01 kcal/mol and − 23.40 kcal/mol, respectively (Fig. 7M, P). In both complexes, van der Waals (VDWAALS) interactions and electrostatic contributions (EEL) were the major stabilizing forces, whereas polar solvation energy (EGB) contributed unfavorably to binding.

Collectively, these results provide computational support for structurally plausible and energetically favorable interactions between MEHP and EDNRA/BCL2L1 under the simulated conditions.

Discussion

In this study, we constructed an integrative bioinformatics and computational pipeline to explore potential molecular mechanisms linking MEHP-associated targets to gastric carcinogenesis. By integrating chemoinformatics-based target prediction, transcriptomic analyses, protein-protein interaction (PPI) network analysis, and machine learning-driven feature selection, we identified EDNRA and BCL2L1 as critical hub genes associated with MEHP-related molecular targets in GC. The diagnostic relevance of these genes was supported by ROC curve analyses across independent cohorts, while their prognostic value was demonstrated by Kaplan-Meier survival analyses. In addition, their immune-related associations were further substantiated through immune infiltration profiling. Furthermore, molecular docking and molecular dynamics simulations provided computational evidence supporting the structural and energetic plausibility of MEHP interactions with EDNRA and BCL2L1, suggesting that these proteins may represent potential molecular nodes linking MEHP-associated targets and GC biology. It should be noted that hub genes identified through statistical and network-based approaches reflect candidate molecular nodes and do not necessarily imply experimentally validated causal biological drivers.

EDNRA encodes endothelin receptor type A, a G protein-coupled receptor that mediates endothelin-1-induced vasoconstriction and cell proliferation [20]. Accumulating evidence indicates that aberrant EDNRA signaling contributes to tumor angiogenesis, epithelial-mesenchymal transition, and immune evasion in multiple malignancies [1, 6, 21]. Our results revealed consistent upregulation of EDNRA in GC across two independent datasets, with robust diagnostic accuracy and significant association with poor prognosis. GSEA demonstrated enrichment of calcium signaling, cell adhesion, and ECM-receptor interaction pathways in samples with high EDNRA expression, indicating potential associations between EDNRA expression and proliferative or migratory phenotypes. Importantly, immune infiltration analyses revealed a positive correlation between EDNRA expression and macrophage polarization, suggesting potential associations with macrophage-related immune processes in the tumor microenvironment. Consistent with these findings, previous studies have shown that endothelin signaling facilitates the recruitment of immunosuppressive myeloid cells [7]. However, these observations are based on transcriptomic correlations and do not establish a causal relationship between MEHP exposure, hub gene expression, and immune microenvironment remodeling. Together, these results support the hypothesis that MEHP-associated molecular targets may intersect with EDNRA-related signaling pathways relevant to gastric tumorigenesis.

BCL2L1, also known as Bcl-xL, is a member of the Bcl-2 protein family and functions as a critical anti-apoptotic regulator [17, 26]. Dysregulation of BCL2L1 is frequently observed in diverse cancers, where it confers resistance to chemotherapy by inhibiting apoptosis [9, 25]. In our analysis, BCL2L1 expression was significantly elevated in GC and displayed high diagnostic value, though its prognostic impact was less consistent compared with EDNRA. GSEA results highlighted enrichment of cell cycle and lysosome pathways in samples with high BCL2L1 expression, indicating potential associations with apoptosis-related and metabolic processes. Notably, molecular docking and MD simulations suggested a stable binding conformation between MEHP and BCL2L1, with free energy contributions dominated by VDWAALS and EEL interactions. This raises the possibility that MEHP interaction with BCL2L1 may be structurally compatible with its apoptosis-related regulatory domain; however, functional consequences require experimental validation.

Our findings underscore the importance of considering environmental toxicants such as MEHP in the etiology of GC. The observed alterations in immune infiltration—such as increased macrophage and neutrophil abundance and reduced plasma cell populations—highlight a possible link between chemical exposure and tumor immune escape. Both EDNRA and BCL2L1 exhibited significant correlations with immune cell subsets, reinforcing their roles at the interface of tumor progression and host immunity. These results are consistent with the emerging concept of toxicogenomics, in which environmental pollutants interact with host genetic networks to exacerbate oncogenic signaling.

A major strength of this study lies in its integrative framework, which combined transcriptomic analyses (DEGs and WGCNA), PPI network construction, and machine learning algorithms, followed by validation through external cohorts, immune infiltration profiling, and structural biology approaches. This multi-level strategy enabled the robust identification of EDNRA and BCL2L1 as MEHP-related hub genes, supported by computationally predicted MEHP-protein interaction patterns derived from docking and MD simulations.

Limitations

This study is primarily based on integrative bioinformatics analyses of publicly available transcriptome datasets. Although multiple independent cohorts were included, the overall sample size remains limited, and potential population heterogeneity cannot be fully excluded.

In addition, the predicted MEHP-associated targets were derived from chemoinformatics databases that rely on similarity-based algorithms and existing experimental annotations, which may introduce bias or false-positive associations. Although we applied multi-database integration followed by transcriptomic filtering (DEGs and WGCNA) and machine learning–based refinement to reduce potential noise, residual prediction bias cannot be completely excluded and may influence downstream hub gene identification.

While molecular docking and molecular dynamics simulations provided computational support for the structural plausibility of MEHP interactions with EDNRA and BCL2L1, these findings should be interpreted as in silico evidence rather than definitive confirmation of direct binding. Moreover, EDNRA is a membrane-bound GPCR whose conformational dynamics are influenced by lipid bilayers. The present simulations were conducted under aqueous conditions, which may not fully recapitulate the native membrane environment. Therefore, future studies incorporating membrane-embedded simulations and experimental validation would further strengthen mechanistic interpretation and biological relevance.

Furthermore, although we evaluated the diagnostic and prognostic relevance of EDNRA and BCL2L1, the present study does not explore the mechanistic basis underlying their potential roles in GC progression. Additional analytical strategies—such as co-expression network mapping and dimensionality reduction to assess proximity to canonical GC drivers, or transcriptomic stratification based on hub gene expression followed by comparative signature analysis—may provide deeper insight into how these genes influence disease outcome. Such approaches have been applied in other cancer contexts and represent valuable directions for future investigation [16].

Conclusion

This study identifies EDNRA and BCL2L1 as key hub genes at the intersection of MEHP-associated targets and gastric cancer-related molecular networks through integrative bioinformatics and computational analyses. Both genes showed strong diagnostic value, immune relevance, and computationally supported interactions with MEHP. These findings deepen our understanding of toxicogenomic mechanisms linking environmental phthalate exposure to GC and highlight EDNRA and BCL2L1 as potential biomarkers and therapeutic targets.

Monitoring alpine wetland of Haizishan using Otsu method and Sentinel-1 SAR in Hengduan Mountains, China
Authors: Xiaoxiong Li, Jiyun Li, Zhen Xing, Sha Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study focuses on delineating water extent within the alpine wetlands of Haizishan, Qinghai-Tibet Plateau, a meteorologically complex region. Situated within a 6,000 km² Quaternary ice sheet landscape with over 600 water bodies, this area offers a crucial setting to examine climate change impacts on high-altitude wetland systems. Employing Sentinel-1 SAR data and the Google Earth Engine (GEE) platform, we implemented an Improved OTSU algorithm, achieving > 95% accuracy in water surface delineation despite persistent cloud cover. Notably, wetland water extent expanded from 52.9 km² to 54.8 km² between 2015 and 2022, correlating with increased precipitation. This expansion, and its precipitation-driven nature, distinguishes these non-glacial-fed wetlands hydrologically from glacial water bodies in the broader region, particularly impacting shallower wetland areas sensitive to thermal variations.

Introduction

Climate change is profoundly altering the hydrological systems of the Tibetan Plateau (TP), a region often termed “Asia’s Water Tower” due to its critical role in supplying water to major continental rivers1,2. These transformations influence the plateau’s hydrology and ecosystems3 and water security for one-sixth of the global population4. Scientific attention has predominantly focused on the cryosphere’s response, particularly the rapid expansion of thousands of glacier-fed lakes associated with accelerating glacial melt5,6. While these studies are vital, they represent only one facet of a more complex hydrological transformation. Alpine wetlands, another key component of the TP’s aquatic landscape, are also undergoing significant changes7. However, their dynamics are often characterized by different hydrological processes, such as direct precipitation and groundwater interactions, rather than glacial runoff8. This fundamental distinction implies that their response to climate change—in terms of magnitude, timing, and controlling factors—may diverge significantly from that of glacial lakes, creating a critical knowledge gap in our comprehensive understanding of the TP’s overall water balance.

The Haizishan region, located in the Hengduan Mountains on the southeastern margin of the TP, provides an exemplary natural laboratory to investigate these understudied systems. Its landscape is uniquely characterized by a dense network of thousands of alpine wetlands, which, integral to the plateau’s hydrology, provide key ecological functions such as water regulation and biodiversity support9. Critically, these are not remnants of contemporary retreating glaciers but are post-glacial features that formed following the dissolution of a vast Quaternary ice cap, up to 3,600 km² 10,11. Its formation from an ancient ice cap and climate change sensitivity make these alpine wetlands an ideal case study. This distinct geological history dictates that their water budget is highly sensitive to atmospheric fluxes—namely precipitation from the Asian monsoon and temperature-driven evapotranspiration—rather than cryospheric meltwater inputs12. Despite their regional importance, the quantitative relationship between the spatio-temporal dynamics of these precipitation-fed wetlands and specific climatic drivers remains poorly constrained. Consequently, while the broader impacts of climate change on the TP’s water systems are widely discussed, the specific vulnerability and response patterns of these crucial post-glacial wetlands remain largely uncharacterized in large-scale studies13,14.

Accurately quantifying the dynamics of these scattered and often small wetlands, however, presents a significant methodological challenge. The frequent and persistent cloud cover during the Asian monsoon season, which critically coincides with the wetlands’ peak water extent, severely limits the utility of traditional optical remote sensing for constructing a consistent time series. Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) technology offers a powerful alternative, providing all-weather, day-and-night observation capabilities at high spatial resolutions suitable for this task15,16. While various algorithms exist for SAR-based water extraction, their application in complex mountain terrains like Haizishan requires careful adaptation to mitigate issues such as radar shadow and layover, which can be easily confused with water bodies17. Therefore, establishing a robust and validated monitoring workflow is a crucial prerequisite for rigorously testing any hypothesis about their climatic drivers.

This study elucidates the spatiotemporal dynamics of water extent within the Haizishan alpine wetland system. Employing an Improved OTSU algorithm for water delineation on Sentinel-1 SAR data (2015–2022), we analyze the relationship between wetland water fluctuations and climate variables (temperature, precipitation). This research offers a refined approach for monitoring water dynamics in plateau wetland systems, crucial for understanding climate change impacts on these sensitive high-altitude environments. Integrating advanced water delineation, we achieve > 95% accuracy, bi-weekly temporal resolution, and ± 1 pixel spatial precision, revealing novel insights into plateau wetland evolution. Our methodology provides a technical innovation for characterizing the hydrological spatial response of alpine wetland systems to climate drivers. Specifically, temporal analysis elucidates the operational mechanisms of these wetlands under varying hydrological regimes, enhancing our understanding of high-altitude wetland systems under climate change and offering a robust approach for continuous monitoring in a warming world.

Seasonal changes in wetland water area

We applied the STL (Seasonal and Trend decomposition using Loess) method to decompose the time series data. This series ranges from 2015 to 2022 (Fig. 3), with a frequency of 6, meaning there are 6 data points per year, with data collected every month.

We applied the STL to separate the long-term hydrological signal from the strong seasonal noise inherent in the SAR time series. Unlike simple linear regression, STL allowed us to handle the non-linear shifts and potential outliers typical of radar-derived water extent data.

Our analysis reveals that the Haizishan wetland system is overwhelmingly dominated by seasonal dynamics. The seasonal component explains 69.2% of the total variance, confirming that these shallow water bodies are highly responsive to the annual precipitation-evaporation cycle. However, beneath this strong seasonal pulsing, we detected a clear and persistent signal of expansion. The Mann-Kendall test confirmed this upward trend is statistically significant (Z = 5.45, p < 0.001), with a steady growth rate of 0.25 km² yr⁻¹. While this growth may seem modest compared to the seasonal fluctuations, it represents a consistent accumulation of water storage over the eight-year period, suggesting a fundamental shift in the catchment’s water balance.

The reliability of these findings is supported by residual diagnostics. The Ljung-Box test showed no significant autocorrelation in the model residuals (p = 0.22), indicating that our decomposition successfully captured the deterministic patterns without overfitting the data.

Mechanisms of climatic forcing on wetland dynamics

Multivariate statistical modeling elucidates the competing climatic mechanisms factors influencing the surface extent of Haizishan wetlands, revealing a system associated with immediate thermal constraints and delayed hydrological recharge. As illustrated in Fig. 4a, the inclusion of antecedent precipitation significantly enhances model performance, increasing the explained variance (R2) from 0.325 to 0.420 and reducing the RMSE to 2.36 km² compared to the concurrent precipitation model. This improvement underscores the critical role of hydrological memory in alpine wetland systems.

Analysis of standardized effect sizes (β) in the optimal model (Fig. 4b) provides quantitative evidence for the decoupling of precipitation and water extent in the temporal domain. Temperature emerges as the dominant regulating factor with a highly significant negative coefficient (β = −0.458, p < 0.01). This strong inverse relationship confirms that thermal-driven evaporation acts as the primary limiting mechanism, where rising temperatures rapidly deplete water volume in these shallow, high-altitude basins, significantly offsetting potential inputs.

Conversely, precipitation exhibits a distinct temporal hysteresis. While concurrent (same-month) precipitation shows a negligible and non-significant impact (β = −0.035, p > 0.05), antecedent precipitation (1-month lag) exerts a significant positive influence (β = +0.331, p < 0.05). This statistical contrast validates a one-month catchment concentration period, suggesting that rainfall requires time to infiltrate the soil matrix and converge via subsurface runoff before effectively recharging the wetland bodies. The magnitude of the negative thermal effect (∣β∣ ≈ 0.46∣) slightly exceeds the positive recharge effect (∣β∣ ≈ 0.33∣), implying that the Haizishan wetland system is highly sensitive to warming. However, the observed wetland expansion suggests that during the study period, the net increase in precipitation input was sufficient to overcome this evaporative loss. Consequently, while the system is currently in a surplus state, future temperature increases could potentially offset the benefits of intensified precipitation.

Impact of precipitation and temperature on the variation in wetland water body numbers

Long-term monitoring from 2015 to 2022 reveals a divergent evolutionary trajectory between different wetland size classes (Fig. 5a). While the total abundance of water bodies (“All wetlands”) exhibited high interannual volatility with no statistically significant trend (slope = -2.60 units/yr, R2= 0.04, p = 0.648), the sub-population of large wetlands (> 0.03 km²) demonstrated a statistically significant linear decline. Linear regression analysis quantifies this downward trend at a rate of approximately 3.96 units per year (R2 = 0.53, p = 0.042), suggesting a gradual contraction of permanent water bodies over the eight-year study period despite the lack of a significant trend in the aggregate population.

Seasonal dynamics further differentiate these groups (Fig. 5b). The total wetland count displays a characteristic unimodal distribution, gradually increasing from May, maintaining a plateau during the core monsoon months (June– August), and receding in October. This pattern aligns with a pulse-driven hydrological regime where ephemeral water bodies emerge during the wet season. In contrast, the mean count of large wetlands remains relatively static across the growing season, reflecting their hydrological stability. However, analysis of the Coefficient of Variation (CV) reveals a distinct instability peak for large wetlands in July (CV > 0.12), likely reflecting a transitional phase where fluctuating water levels cause marginal water bodies to cross the size classification threshold during peak precipitation inputs.

Discussion

Developing process-based coupled models for hydro-climatic interactions

Our multivariate analysis quantitatively delineates the operational regime of the Haizishan wetlands, revealing a system characterized by the antagonistic interaction between immediate thermal constraints (β = −0.45) and delayed hydrological recharge (β = +0.331). To resolve these competing factors, future research must advance from statistical correlation to physically-based coupled modeling that explicitly resolves the system’s temporal hysteresis. Specifically, the distinct one-month lag identified in our results indicates that surface water expansion does not correspond to immediate meteoric inputs, but is linked to antecedent catchment saturation. This necessitates models capable of simulating the non-linear transit times required for precipitation to infiltrate the vadose zone, elevate the phreatic surface, and converge via shallow subsurface flow paths to recharge wetland basins31.

Current simplified water balance models may underestimate this buffering capacity by assuming instantaneous runoff. Instead, advanced simulations must couple surface energy fluxes with variably saturated flow dynamics to accurately capture how soil moisture memory modulates the wetland response to precipitation pulses. Given that the magnitude of the thermal negative forcing currently exceeds the positive recharge effect (∣βtemp∣ > ∣βprecip∣), such mechanistic integration is critical for determining whether projected intensification of the hydrological cycle will be sufficient to offset escalating evaporative demand, or if these non-glacial ecosystems are approaching a hydrological tipping point under sustained warming.

Hydrological coalescence and area expansion amidst declining abundance

An intriguing divergence was observed between the temporal trajectories of wetland area and abundance. While STL analysis confirms a robust expansion in total water surface area (+ 0.25 km² yr⁻¹, p < 0.001), the number of large water bodies (> 0.03 km²) exhibited a statistically significant decline (-3.96 units yr⁻¹, p = 0.042).

This apparent paradox—expanding area coupled with decreasing counts—is elucidated by a hydrological mechanism of coalescence. As visualized in Fig. 6, we explicitly identified multiple instances where initially distinct, isolated water bodies (2015 baseline) expanded and merged into unified lacustrine features during subsequent high-water periods. This morphological evidence confirms that as the wetland system expands under increasing precipitation, adjacent water bodies merge as water levels rise, effectively reducing the discrete count of individual lakes while increasing the aggregate surface area. This morphological evolution indicates that the Haizishan system is not merely expanding at the margins but is undergoing structural connectivity changes, transitioning from isolated pools towards a more integrated hydrological network.

Comparison with other studies

While these investigations showcase multi-source satellite data for water body detection, automated monitoring remains challenged by satellite revisit frequencies, spatial resolution, and water delineation algorithms. Our study reveals contrasting hydrological response patterns in non-glacial-fed alpine wetlands of Haizishan compared to glacier-fed water bodies elsewhere on the QTP. Consistent with findings from the Qiangtang Plateau18, these wetlands exhibited a slight expansion in water extent during 2015–2022 under warming and wetting climatic conditions. Crucially, preliminary results indicate a distinct thermal response in these non-glacial-fed wetlands. Unlike glacier-fed water bodies, which typically expand with increased glacial melt under warming19,20, non-glacial-fed wetlands exhibit a high sensitivity to contract under rising temperatures, a mechanism aligning with the reported shrinkage of non-glacial water bodies on the QTP, although recent precipitation increases have currently offset this thermal loss21. These divergent responses arise from differing water sources and complex catchment-scale interactions between climate, permafrost, and hydrological processes20. As ongoing climate warming accelerates glacial recession across the Tibetan Plateau, glacier-fed water bodies are projected to transition from “resource abundance” to “resource scarcity.” In contrast, these precipitation-fed alpine wetlands may continue to expand with increasing regional precipitation. This spatial differentiation underscores not only the contrasting climate responses between water bodies with differing hydrological regimes. While our findings are specific to the Haizishan region, they suggest that similar post-glacial landscapes on the Tibetan Plateau, where precipitation is the primary driver, may undergo comparable transformations distinct from glacier-fed systems.

Performance and advantages of the adaptive SAR framework

While multi-sensor fusion strategies (e.g., NDWI-SAR integration) undeniably enhance boundary delineation capabilities22, their operational viability in the Haizishan region is fundamentally constrained by persistent atmospheric obstruction. As quantified in our data availability analysis (Table 2), the scarcity of cloud-free Sentinel-2 acquisitions during the monsoon season precludes the construction of a dense time series. Reliance on optical fusion would necessitate extensive temporal interpolation, introducing synthetic artifacts and compromising the integrity of the hydrological record23. Consequently, the adoption of a SAR-exclusive protocol is not merely a methodological preference but a prerequisite for maintaining spatiotemporal continuity in this meteorologically complex environment.

Furthermore, while Deep Learning (DL) architectures represent the current state-of-the-art for water extraction24,25, their deployment is hindered by the paucity of labeled training data characteristic of remote alpine terrains. The pronounced topographic relief of the study area implies complex geometric distortions—specifically radar shadow and layover—which present a challenge to the generalization performance of DL models trained on non-native datasets26. By contrast, the proposed Local Adaptive Otsu framework operates on statistical principles independent of extensive training sets. It presents a streamlined approach that aims to distinguish hydrological backscatter from topographic noise, facilitating computational efficiency for decadal-scale processing on the Google Earth Engine infrastructure.

While Sentinel-2 imagery serves as a robust validation source due to its high spatial resolution, we acknowledge certain limitations inherent to optical validation in complex alpine terrains. The shallow depth of many wetland margins can result in mixed pixels that are difficult to classify definitively as water or non-water. Additionally, seasonal ice and snow cover, particularly in transitional months, may occasionally mimic the spectral signature of water or obscure boundaries. Therefore, while the > 95% accuracy indicates strong model performance, the validation results should be interpreted with awareness of these optical sensing constraints.

Integrating multi-source remote sensing data

Our study, spanning 2015–2022, offers a limited temporal window for fully elucidating long-term wetland water body dynamics and their climatic sensitivities. Future research would benefit from extending the temporal scope by incorporating historical SAR data from missions like ERS-1/2, ENVISAT ASAR, and RADARSAT-1/2. Such expanded datasets would enable a more robust trend analysis and provide deeper insights into the inter-annual and decadal variability of wetland hydrological changes in this region. Furthermore, broadening the spatial extent to encompass a wider portion of the QTP, or even the entire plateau, could offer a more holistic understanding of the spatial patterns and heterogeneity of wetland system responses to climatic drivers across this critical high-altitude region.

Extending the time scale and study area

While SAR data offers unique advantages for water body monitoring in cloud-prone regions, integrating multi-source remote sensing data could further enhance the accuracy and robustness of wetland water mapping and analysis. Future research could integrate SAR data with optical imagery (e.g., Landsat, Sentinel-2), altimetry data (e.g., ICESat-2, CryoSat-2), and gravimetry data (e.g., GRACE, GRACE-FO) for a more comprehensive characterization of wetland water dynamics. For example, combining SAR-derived water extents with altimetry-derived water levels could enable the estimation of wetland water volume changes and the derivation of area-volume relationships27. Integrating SAR and optical data could also reduce uncertainties in wetland water boundary delineation and refine the classification of water body types (e.g., freshwater, saline, or mixed) based on their spectral characteristics28.

Conclusion

Methodological Validation

The SAR-based water delineation methodology demonstrated exceptional accuracy (> 95%), significantly outperforming optical sensors in cloud-prone alpine wetland environments. This approach enabled robust water body monitoring, yielding 77 usable SAR images in 2021 compared to only 16 from Sentinel-2, highlighting its efficacy under challenging meteorological conditions.

Wetland Water Extent Dynamics

From 2015 to 2022, the total wetland water extent in Haizishan expanded from 52.9 km² to 54.8 km². Seasonal dynamics revealed a pattern of water extent expansion until September, followed by a period of contraction.

Climatic Attribution

Diverging from the expansionary trajectory characteristic of glacier-fed systems on the Tibetan Plateau, our results show that the Haizishan wetlands exhibit a distinct inverse thermal response, revealing a precipitation-modulated regime functionally decoupled from cryosphere inputs. Multivariate modeling elucidates a system governed by the interplay between immediate thermal constraints and delayed hydrological recharge, where a validated one-month precipitation lag underscores the critical role of catchment hydrological memory. Crucially, the statistical dominance of temperature (β = −0.458) over precipitation (β = +0.33) indicates that warming-induced evaporative drawdown acts as a strong limiting factor. Despite this thermal constraint, the recent wetland expansion implies that the intensification of monsoon precipitation has temporarily overridden the evaporative demand. This dynamic equilibrium distinguishes these wetlands from their glacial counterparts but highlights the ecosystem’s acute vulnerability should precipitation patterns stabilize while warming continues. These findings highlight the heterogeneity of hydrological responses across the plateau and suggest that precipitation-fed systems may follow different evolutionary paths than their glacier-fed counterparts.

Materials and methods

Study site

Haizishan, a 5939 km² alpine wetland system in China’s Shaluli Mountains (Fig. 7) on the eastern Qinghai-Tibet Plateau, constitutes a significant Quaternary glacial landscape at an average altitude of 4,410 m29,30. Exhibiting a cold temperate plateau climate, the region receives 600–750 mm of 4,410 m, it represents a significant Quaternary glacier site31. Characterized by extensive wetland areas exceeding 3,200 km², and numerous water bodies whose extent varies seasonally with precipitation32, Haizishan functions as a critical hydrological source for the Sichuan Basin, accumulating precipitation and snowmelt that contribute to downstream water resources.

Data collection

We analyzed Sentinel-1 SAR data for summers 2015–2022, complemented by Sentinel-2 multispectral imagery. The SAR data (https://dataspace.copernicus.eu/explore-data/data-collections/sentinel-data) has a resolution of approximately 10 m and covers various temporal phases to capture seasonal variations. Sentinel-2 multispectral imagery provided additional spectral data with a resolution of 10 to 60 m, depending on the band. Elevation data were sourced from the SRTM V4 DEM (https://www.usgs.gov/centers/eros/science/usgs-eros-archive-digital-elevation-shuttle-radar-topography-mission-srtm-1), offering a 30-meter resolution, which is suitable for detailed topographic analysis. CHIRPS(https://www.chc.ucsb.edu/data/chirps) provided precipitation data33. This dataset provides high-resolution (5 km) daily rainfall estimates. MODIS MOD11A2 (https://lpdaac.usgs.gov/products/mod11a2v006/) (1-km resolution, 8-day composite) supplied land surface temperature34,35. Data were acquired via Google Earth Engine.

Data analysis

Otsu’s method

Sentinel-1 data preprocessing involved converting radar images from logarithmic to natural scale, applying Lee Filtering to reduce speckle noise36, and reverting to logarithmic scale. We then used Otsu’s method for thresholding, which maximizes between-class variance to optimally segment water and non-water bodies37.

Improved local adaptive Otsu thresholding

Although the global Otsu algorithm established a baseline Overall Accuracy exceeding 90%, its practical utility was compromised by the topographic complexity of the Haizishan region. The assumption of a uniform bimodal distribution proved inadequate due to the spatial non-stationarity of background backscatter induced by rugged terrain. Specifically, a single global threshold failed to balance the trade-off between detecting small-scale lacustrine features and suppressing radar shadows, leading to substantial omission and commission errors. To address this, we implemented a Local Adaptive Otsu framework. By computing optimal thresholds within sliding windows, this approach effectively accounts for local intensity variations caused by topographic shading, thereby refining the delineation of these hydrologically significant yet spatially fragmented water bodies.

Image partitioning and windowing strategy

To address the spatial non-stationarity of backscatter intensity caused by the rugged topography, the preprocessed Sentinel-1 imagery was partitioned using a sliding window approach. We defined a local window size of 1 km×1 km. This dimension was empirically selected to ensure sufficient statistical samples for histogram stability while maintaining high localization to capture small-scale terrain variations. A 50% overlap (step size of 500 m) was implemented between adjacent windows to ensure transitional smoothness and mitigate blocky artifacts at the boundaries of sub-regions.

Bimodality validation and local threshold computation

For each sub-region (Wi, j), standard Otsu segmentation is prone to failure in homogeneous areas (e.g., pure water or continuous mountain slopes) where the histogram is unimodal. To mitigate this, we introduced a bimodality validation step using the Otsu Separability Factor (η).

Spatially continuous threshold surface generation

The previous step yielded a sparse grid of optimal thresholds with data gaps in regions identified as homogeneous. To construct a spatially continuous threshold surface (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{T}_{surface}$$\end{document}) for the entire scene, a two-stage reconstruction strategy was employed:

Gap Filling: The threshold values for invalid (unimodal) windows were estimated using Inverse Distance Weighting (IDW) based on the nearest valid neighbors. This assumes that the optimal discrimination threshold for a homogeneous area is spatially correlated with its adjacent transitional zones.

Surface Interpolation: Bilinear Interpolation was applied across the filled grid to upsample the coarse threshold map to the original image resolution. This generated a smooth \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{T}_{surface}$$\end{document} with pixel-wise adaptive thresholds, effectively eliminating abrupt segmentation boundaries.

Data preprocessing

Sentinel-2 imagery was subject to a standard preprocessing workflow (Fig. 8), encompassing radiometric calibration, atmospheric correction, image registration, and fusion. To guide the selection of representative water and non-water samples within the alpine wetland environment, Digital Elevation Model (DEM) and Topographic Wetness Index (TWI) data (ASTER GDEM v2) were incorporated. The Otsu algorithm provided initial water delineation within this high-altitude wetland. Subsequent refinement using DEM and TWI data mitigated misclassifications of water/non-water areas based on TWI values. Accuracy assessment was performed via comparison with high-resolution imagery, with confusion matrices used for statistical evaluation.

Fig. 8Water body detection and analysis workflow.

Water body detection and analysis workflow.

Following image segmentation, water body information was extracted and exported. Radar imagery was classified into water and non-water areas using established thresholding techniques38–40. Seasonal modeling, utilizing R’s “fitdistrplus” package and Google Earth Engine, was performed to analyze the relationships between wetland water extent, temperature, and precipitation. Eight-year summer water level trends were decomposed using the Seasonal and Trend decomposition using Loess (STL) method. Predictive analysis was conducted using Seasonal Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average (SARIMA) modeling, selected via the “auto.arima” function.

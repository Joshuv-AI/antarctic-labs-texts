Predicting the Future of Aesthetic Surgery: An Artificial Intelligence Framework for Global Publication Forecasting
Authors: Georgios Karamitros, Georgios Bouloukakis, Emmanuel Giannas, Gregory A Lamaris, Wesley P Thayer, Galen Perdikis, William C Lineaweaver, Patrick Assi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundArtificial intelligence (AI) has transformed clinical decision making, yet its application to forecasting the evolution of surgical science remains underdeveloped. Anticipating future research trajectories represents a critical unmet need for strategic planning, workforce allocation, and innovation stewardship in aesthetic surgery.ObjectivesThe aim of this study was to develop and validate an AI-assisted forecasting framework capable of modeling and predicting global aesthetic surgery research activity.MethodsWe performed a population-level observational analysis of all PubMed-indexed aesthetic surgery publications from 2010 to 2024. A fully autonomous AI pipeline conducted large-scale data ingestion, followed by high-fidelity semantic classification of publications by research domain and country (validated accuracy >97%). Annualized outputs were analyzed using optimized exponential-smoothing and autoregressive time-series models to generate long-horizon forecasts with 95% CIs.ResultsThe framework processed 24,026 records, yielding 23,521 eligible publications across 13 journals. Exponential smoothing demonstrated superior predictive performance (R2 = 0.94, root mean square error = 166.6). Global research output is projected to increase by 21.9% by 2030, reaching 2939 publications annually (95% CI, 2612-3265). Minimally invasive and injectable research exhibited the steepest projected growth (+46.1 publications/year).ConclusionsThis study establishes AI-driven forecasting as a next-generation analytic paradigm for surgical meta-research. By integrating autonomous data ingestion, semantic intelligence, and rigorously validated time-series modeling, the framework operationalizes predictive intelligence—shifting aesthetic surgery research from retrospective surveillance to prospective trajectory mapping. The resulting system is scalable, reproducible, and continuously recalibratable, positioning AI as a strategic instrument for anticipatory research governance, resource allocation, and human-capital planning in surgical science.Level of Evidence: 5 (Therapeutic) For image description, please refer to the figure legend and surrounding text.

Artificial intelligence (AI) is redefining the landscape of surgical science, extending its reach from diagnostics and intraoperative planning to the very architecture of how research is generated, analyzed, and anticipated.1 Beyond pattern recognition and data synthesis, AI now enables predictive intelligence—transforming scientific analysis from static observation into dynamic forecasting.2 Conventional research reviews in aesthetic and reconstructive surgery have been predominantly retrospective, measuring past productivity rather than anticipating future directions.3-5 In contrast, AI-driven predictive analytics, powered by natural language processing and machine learning, can model temporal, thematic, and geographic trajectories of scientific innovation.6 By coupling large-scale automation with semantic precision, such systems can identify emerging inflection points, forecast areas of accelerated growth, and provide data-driven foresight to inform academic strategy, funding distribution, and interdisciplinary collaboration.7,8

Aesthetic surgery represents an ideal proving ground for this methodology. It is among the fastest-evolving and most economically significant domains in surgical medicine, marked by rapid technological innovation, interdisciplinary expansion, and global market growth.9 The field's breadth—spanning injectables, regenerative therapies, robotics, and digital visualization—provides a uniquely dynamic environment in which to test an AI-based predictive framework capable of mapping and anticipating scientific evolution.

Accordingly, this study introduces the first AI-powered forecasting framework in surgical research. Using aesthetic surgery as a prototype field, we developed and validated a predictive analytics system that (1) autonomously extracts and curates large-scale scientific data, (2) classifies research output across thematic and geographic domains through contextual text mining, and (3) applies advanced time-series modeling to forecast the future evolution of surgical innovation. This integrated AI architecture transforms the study of surgical research from a retrospective exercise into a forward-looking, data-intelligent system for anticipating where and how aesthetic science will evolve.

METHODS

Study Design and Conceptual Framework

We applied an AI-assisted bibliometric and forecasting framework to analyze and predict global trends in clinical and experimental aesthetic surgery research from 2010 to 2024, with projections through 2030. The model integrates automated data extraction (Karamitros−Goulas method), contextual classification, and time-series forecasting to generate a temporally dynamic and geographically resolved representation of global aesthetic publication activity.2,10 AI was operationalized at 2 distinct levels: (1) large-scale autonomous data ingestion and preprocessing, and (2) semantic text-based classification enabling high-fidelity thematic and geographic labeling prior to forecasting. The objective was to extend bibliometric analysis beyond retrospective description, transforming it into a predictive tool for anticipating future scholarly trajectories and identifying emerging thematic domains within aesthetic surgery.

Data Source and Journal Selection

The PubMed database was selected for its extensive coverage and standardized metadata.11 Thirteen high-impact journals were included, representing the clinical, experimental, and technological spectrum of aesthetic surgery research. Selection was based on 4 criteria: (1) primary aesthetic surgery focus, (2) inclusion in major indexing systems (PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus), (3) consistent publication from 2010 onward, and (4) English-language availability. The final list encompassed journals such as Aesthetic Surgery Journal, Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery, and Aesthetic Plastic Surgery. The complete journal list is presented in Supplemental Table 1, available online at https://doi.org/10.1093/asj/sjag051. This approach ensured inclusion of both surgical and nonsurgical research domains across the global aesthetic landscape.

Data Extraction and Structuring

A custom proprietary Python-based algorithm was developed to systematically retrieve publication metadata from PubMed for the years 2010-2024.2 Extracted fields included title, abstract, authorship, institutional affiliation, journal, publication year, and country of origin. The algorithm employed sustainable querying protocols and compliance with PubMed's data access policies, processing over 24,000 records in <2 h.12 This automated extraction layer functioned as the computational backbone of the AI framework, enabling scalable ingestion of high-dimensional publication data while minimizing manual intervention and selection bias. This automated pipeline enabled reproducible, large-scale extraction while maintaining the transparency and ethical standards required for bibliometric research.13

Contextual Classification of Aesthetic Surgery Research

To isolate publications directly relevant to aesthetic surgery, a contextual text-mining algorithm was applied using a curated keyword taxonomy reflecting the field's major clinical and research domains (aesthetic*, cosmetic*, breast, rhinoplasty, blepharoplasty, liposuction, injectable*, minimally invasive, laser, and fat graft*).14 The keyword taxonomy was developed through an iterative, expert-informed process incorporating established aesthetic surgery terminology, MeSH indexing terms, and previous bibliometric frameworks. Candidate keywords were pilot tested on randomly sampled records to refine inclusion thresholds and minimize false classification, followed by manual validation by 2 investigators to confirm thematic fidelity. Articles lacking thematic relevance were excluded. This semantic classification step represents the second AI layer of the framework, ensuring that downstream forecasting models were trained exclusively on domain-specific, high-relevance data rather than raw publication counts. Classification accuracy was validated through manual review of a stratified random 5% subset (∼1200 records), independently assessed by 2 investigators (G.K. and G.B.), achieving >97% agreement and a false-negative rate <3%.15 This ensured high-fidelity differentiation of aesthetic surgery publications from adjacent reconstructive or dermatologic literature.

Forecasting and Predictive Modeling

Validated records were aggregated annually and analyzed using a suite of time-series models, including linear regression, polynomial regression, exponential smoothing, and autoregressive integrated moving average (ARIMA).16-18 Each model generated annual forecasts of total publication output through 2030. Comparative performance was evaluated using root mean square error (RMSE) and coefficient of determination (R2) to identify the optimal predictive framework. Exponential smoothing (Holt's linear trend) demonstrated the lowest RMSE and the most stable parameter behavior across historical validation windows, indicating superior suitability for modeling sustained, noncyclical growth patterns characteristic of aesthetic surgery research output.19 In contrast, ARIMA models exhibited higher residual error and sensitivity to short-term fluctuations, limiting long-horizon forecasting performance.20 Exponential smoothing was therefore adopted for final forecasting analyses. Forecast uncertainty was expressed as 95% CIs.

It is important to note that while the forecasting algorithms employed in this study (linear regression, polynomial regression, exponential smoothing, and ARIMA) are classical statistical time-series models, the AI component of the framework resides in the upstream architecture rather than the forecasting layer itself. Specifically, AI was operationalized through autonomous large-scale data ingestion, rule-based decision logic, and semantic text mining that enabled high-fidelity thematic and geographic classification prior to prediction. We deliberately selected established and interpretable forecasting models to ensure transparency, reproducibility, and clinical interpretability for this first implementation of predictive analytics in surgical research. In this context, the framework is best characterized as an AI-assisted forecasting system, in which AI enables scalable data curation and semantic understanding, whereas robust statistical models provide explainable and reliable long-horizon prediction.

In addition to global forecasting, data were stratified into 8 analytic categories representing major domains of aesthetic surgery: (A) general aesthetic surgery, (B) facial aesthetic surgery, (C) breast aesthetic surgery, (D) body contouring, (E) minimally invasive and injectables, (F) regenerative surgery, (G) gender-affirming aesthetic surgery, and (H) technology and innovation. Independent autoregressive models were developed for each category to capture domain-specific growth trajectories and identify differential thematic expansion through 2030.

Geographic and economic analyses were conducted at the country level, based on first-author affiliation. National publication volume was normalized by population to estimate per-capita productivity, and countries were stratified by World Bank income classification (high, upper middle, lower middle, and low income).21 These stratifications enabled assessment of global equity and geographic diffusion in aesthetic research productivity.

Validation and Quality Assurance

Two independent validation processes ensured methodological rigor and reproducibility. Algorithmic validation automatically checked metadata completeness, duplication, and field consistency during extraction. Manual adjudication was performed for ambiguous entries by 2 investigators (G.K. and G.B.) to confirm thematic classification and geographic accuracy. Interrater reliability was calculated using Cohen's ω coefficient, yielding ω = 0.94, denoting excellent agreement. The complete data inclusion and processing workflow are illustrated in Figure 1.

Flowchart of articles included in the study. A total of 70,156 records (2010-2024) were retrieved from 13 journals. After removal of 5218 records with missing metadata, 64,938 publications underwent artificial intelligence−based text mining. Nonaesthetic articles were excluded (n = 21,065), leaving 43,873 records. A total of 19,847 publications classified as reconstructive, trauma, or oncologic were excluded, leaving 24,026 articles for analysis. An additional 505 records without verifiable first-author country were excluded, resulting in a final dataset of 23,521 microsurgery publications.

Software and Statistical Environment

All analyses were performed using Python 3.11 (Python Software Foundation, Wilmington, DE) and the pandas, scikit-learn, statsmodels, and matplotlib libraries for data handling, modeling, and visualization.22,23 Statistical significance was defined at a 2-tailed ε = 0.05. All code was version controlled and executed in a reproducible computational environment to ensure analytic transparency and replicability.

Ethical Considerations

As this study utilized publicly accessible, de-identified bibliometric data without human participants, IRB approval and informed consent were not required. The study design and reporting conform to the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines, with a completed checklist provided in the Appendix.24

After removal of duplicate or incomplete records (eg, missing metadata or affiliations), the remaining articles were processed through the AI pipeline for automated text mining, topic classification, and assignment into analytic categories using the validated framework. DISCUSSION

This study introduces a fundamentally new paradigm in surgical meta-research—an AI-driven forecasting framework that transforms aesthetic surgery scholarship from retrospective observation into predictive intelligence. By combining large-scale automation, semantic classification, and time-series modeling, we developed the first system capable of anticipating where, how, and by whom innovation in aesthetic surgery is likely to emerge.2,25,26 This methodological advance positions AI not merely as an analytic instrument but as a strategic compass for the future evolution of surgical science.

In summary, the framework provides (1) a validated AI pipeline for autonomous data extraction and thematic classification of >24,000 aesthetic surgery publications; (2) a predictive modeling system that forecasts research trajectories with high accuracy (R2 = 0.94, RMSE = 166.6); and (3) a dynamic interpretive model that identifies emerging research domains, geographic redistribution, and structural shifts in scholarly output. These features collectively redefine bibliometric inquiry as a forward-looking discipline, capable of providing actionable foresight for researchers, journals, and policymakers.

From a methodological perspective, this work occupies a distinct position within the existing literature. To date, bibliometric studies in aesthetic and surgical research have been almost exclusively retrospective, relying on manual or semi-automated data extraction followed by descriptive trend analysis. Although large-scale automated extraction techniques have been introduced previously—including our own work establishing web scraping and automated ingestion as a reproducible research technique in surgical science—these efforts have remained confined to descriptive mapping of existing scholarly activity.2,27-30 The present study advances this methodological lineage by integrating 3 analytic layers within a unified framework: autonomous large-scale data extraction, contextual semantic classification, and time-series forecasting with predictive modeling. To our knowledge, this is the first application of such an integrated architecture to forecast the future structure and thematic evolution of scientific output in aesthetic surgery or any surgical specialty. Consequently, direct methodological comparison with previous literature is inherently limited, because no existing frameworks attempt to predict the research agenda itself. Instead, the most meaningful contrast lies with traditional manual bibliometric approaches, relative to which this AI-driven system offers substantial gains in scalability, reproducibility, and temporal foresight—capabilities that enable not only interpretation of past trends but anticipation of future scientific trajectories.

From an empirical standpoint, several high-impact findings emerge. First, aesthetic surgery research demonstrates robust and sustained growth through 2030, with an estimated mean annual increase of 4.8%. The discipline's evolution is characterized by 3 concurrent transformations: (1) exponential expansion of minimally invasive and injectable procedures, (2) rapid thematic diversification into regenerative and digital technologies, and (3) progressive geographic diffusion toward Asia and upper-middle-income economies. As illustrated in Figures 6 and 7, these dynamics collectively signify a field that is not merely growing but reorganizing around new technological and socioeconomic vectors.

Qualitative pattern analysis further revealed accelerating research momentum in regenerative medicine, hybrid aesthetic procedures, and digital technologies—including AI-assisted surgical planning and outcome assessment. Concurrently, injectables and energy-based interventions have emerged as the fastest-expanding subfields, reflecting global shifts toward minimally invasive enhancement. Geographic diversification and expanding international collaboration networks indicate an impending transition from Western-centric dominance to a multipolar research ecosystem, in which emerging economies gain influence, whereas established institutions increasingly emphasize quality-driven consolidation. Collectively, these dynamics represent a structural rebalancing of global aesthetic scholarship, consistent with evolving trends reported across the contemporary literature.31,32

Three principal insights define the broader implications of this transformation. First, aesthetic surgery research exhibits accelerating global expansion but remains geographically asymmetric. Nearly 70% of publications originate from high-income nations, reflecting concentrated research infrastructure and funding.11,33 Nonetheless, upper-middle-income economies—especially China, Turkey, and South Korea—demonstrate the steepest relative acceleration, suggesting that global academic equity is gradually improving.2,34 Forecasts predict that by 2030, these nations will contribute nearly one-quarter of total global output, marking a fundamental redistribution of scientific momentum.

Second, the AI-based forecasting system establishes a methodological precedent for predictive analytics in surgical research. By analyzing 15 years of publication data, the model identifies inflection points where research acceleration occurs—offering quantifiable indicators of thematic maturity or emerging opportunity.10,12 This capability enables anticipatory research governance: institutions, journals, and funding bodies can allocate resources strategically toward burgeoning domains such as injectables, regenerative therapies, and hybrid technologies before they reach thematic saturation.

Third, thematic forecasts delineate distinct trajectories of innovation. Minimally invasive and injectable procedures continue to dominate growth, whereas regenerative and fat-based research advances through translational innovation in adipose biology, stem-cell therapy, and biomaterials.35,36 Meanwhile, technology and AI-enabled innovation—although smaller in volume—display exponential potential, signaling an imminent paradigm shift toward algorithmic aesthetic design and simulation-driven interventions.23,33

The relatively modest projected growth observed within the technology and innovation category (Category I) should be interpreted in the context of structural and taxonomic characteristics of the existing literature rather than as an indicator of limited innovation. Emerging technological advances in aesthetic surgery—including AI, digital planning, simulation, and computational modeling—are frequently embedded within broader clinical or procedural manuscripts rather than explicitly labeled as technology-focused research. As a result, keyword-based classification frameworks may undercapture early-stage or interdisciplinary innovation that has not yet coalesced into a distinct publication category. In addition, historical editorial and peer-review ecosystems within aesthetic surgery have not consistently incorporated deep technical expertise from computer science, engineering, or AI disciplines, which may have influenced how such work is framed, reviewed, and indexed. As these structural dynamics continue to evolve and as technical scholarship becomes more explicitly represented within surgical journals, future iterations of the forecasting framework—particularly those integrating semantic embeddings, citation-network analysis, or machine learning–based classifiers—are expected to more fully capture the accelerating trajectory of technology-driven innovation in aesthetic surgery.

Beyond empirical results, this study marks a methodological leap in scientific foresight. The framework processes large-scale datasets within hours while maintaining thematic accuracy exceeding 97%, merging computational speed with contextual depth.2,25 Unlike static reviews, this AI forecasting model is inherently adaptive—capable of continuous recalibration as new literature emerges. Its architecture provides a scalable template for “scientific foresight analytics,” with the potential to be deployed across the full breadth of surgical science, from high-volume specialties such as general, orthopedic, and oncologic surgery to rapidly evolving domains like regenerative medicine and digital therapeutics.34 In this broader context, AI-driven forecasting represents not merely a tool for specialty-specific prediction, but a foundational infrastructure for guiding the strategic evolution of surgical innovation at a systems level.

At a disciplinary level, this approach aligns seamlessly with the ethos of aesthetic surgery—a specialty that has consistently led in digital adoption, from 3-dimensional imaging and simulation to robotic and augmented-reality interventions.2,36 Extending this innovation to the meta-analysis of its own knowledge systems represents a natural progression. Predictive analytics offer editors, policymakers, and educators a data-driven framework for anticipating thematic shifts, guiding ethical standards, and aligning training curricula with future needs.

Beyond its analytic precision, the forecasting framework provides direct translational value for the stewardship of aesthetic surgery scholarship. By converting predictive outputs into actionable strategies, it bridges the gap between data science and editorial or institutional decision making. As outlined in Supplemental Table 2, available online at https://doi.org/10.1093/asj/sjag051, our model enables targeted recommendations across 7 strategic domains: (1) capacity building within journals to accommodate projected submission surges, (2) mentorship and infrastructure development in rapidly growing regions such as Asia and Latin America, (3) thematic prioritization of high-growth areas like minimally invasive and injectable procedures, and (4) the integration of AI-assisted peer-review systems to optimize editorial efficiency. These insights underscore the framework's potential to serve not only as an analytic instrument but also as a policy-shaping mechanism for guiding the sustainable and equitable expansion of aesthetic surgery research worldwide.

The accelerating volume and thematic complexity of aesthetic surgery research carry important implications for journals and the broader scientific communication ecosystem. Because AI-driven forecasting anticipates substantial expansion across multiple domains, editorial infrastructures will need to evolve accordingly. High-growth areas—particularly injectables, regenerative medicine, and digital technologies—will require more agile submission pipelines, expanded reviewer networks, and editors specialized in certain topics capable of managing domain-specific surges. Furthermore, the proliferation of overlapping subfields underscores the need for enhanced cross-referencing and knowledge-integration strategies. AI-assisted tools for automated topic tagging, citation mapping, and semantic linking may enable journals to curate interconnected research clusters, reduce informational silos, and support coherent synthesis across rapidly diversifying content streams.37 These adaptive strategies will be essential to maintain scientific continuity and ensure that the expanding body of aesthetic scholarship remains navigable, interpretable, and meaningfully integrated into evidence-based practice.

The emergence of predictive analytics in surgical science also necessitates careful consideration of ethical governance and responsible implementation. Forecasting models such as the one presented here are not intended to function as prescriptive instruments but as decision-support systems that augment human judgment rather than replace it. As previously articulated in work examining structural pathways for AI integration in plastic surgery, the value of AI lies in its capacity to inform strategy while remaining embedded within transparent oversight frameworks, interdisciplinary evaluation, and iterative reassessment.34 When applied responsibly, forecasting can enhance—not constrain—equitable research planning by identifying structural imbalances and informing targeted capacity building rather than reinforcing existing disparities. Importantly, the potential risk of self-fulfilling influence underscores the need for cautious, phased adoption, continuous recalibration, and explicit acknowledgment of uncertainty, principles that align with broader calls for safe and ethical deployment of predictive AI in medicine.38 In this context, AI-driven forecasting represents a practical and necessary evolution in surgical research infrastructure—one that enables proactive, evidence-informed stewardship of funding, human capital, and scientific direction while preserving the primacy of human judgment and ethical accountability.

Ultimately, this work underscores that AI is not simply a computational adjunct but a real-world catalyst—redefining how surgical science perceives and plans its own intellectual trajectory. By forecasting not only the volume but also the directionality of scholarly evolution, AI transforms research analytics into an instrument of strategic foresight. Because global collaboration intensifies and predictive systems mature, AI-based forecasting is poised to become indispensable in sustaining the intellectual, technological, and ethical evolution of aesthetic surgery in the decades ahead.

Limitations

This study has inherent limitations, although several methodological safeguards were implemented to minimize their impact. First, the analytic framework relies on PubMed as the sole data source, a deliberate choice driven by its comprehensive coverage of biomedical literature, standardized metadata structure, and compatibility with large-scale automated extraction and reproducible longitudinal analysis. Although reliance on a single database may limit capture of select publications indexed exclusively elsewhere, PubMed's consistency and accessibility enabled high-fidelity semantic classification and robust temporal modeling. Potential inconsistencies in abstracts or affiliation fields were mitigated through multistage data cleaning and exclusion of only those records lacking essential metadata. The resulting dataset demonstrated strong structural integrity, supporting reliable downstream forecasting while preserving methodological transparency and reproducibility. Second, the contextual classifier employs predefined keyword taxonomies to ensure thematic specificity. Although emerging terminology may be underdetected, manual validation across random samples yielded accuracy exceeding 97%, and future iterations incorporating deep-learning semantic embeddings (eg, Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers and Generative Pretrained Transformer-based encoders) are expected to further enhance adaptability.26

Third, forecasting models necessarily extrapolate from historical publication patterns and cannot fully account for exogenous disruptions such as regulatory shifts or global crises. To address this, we employed multiple forecasting approaches and selected the model with the strongest out-of-sample performance, ensuring robust predictions within expected temporal bounds. As such, projected trajectories should be interpreted as high-confidence estimates rather than fixed certainties. Additionally, the forecasting framework is not designed to predict entirely novel terminology or scientific constructs that are not yet represented in the literature. Rather, it models the structural evolution of research activity by learning from longitudinal patterns in publication volume, thematic allocation, and geographic distribution. Consequently, predictions reflect the anticipated directionality, magnitude, and relative growth of established and emerging research domains, rather than the semantic invention of unforeseen subject matter. Historically, several transformative developments in aesthetic surgery—such as injectable neuromodulators, dermal fillers, energy-based devices, and fat grafting—initially emerged as incremental extensions of existing surgical or medical techniques before evolving into distinct and dominant research domains. The present framework is optimized to detect and project such inflection points at the domain level, while appropriately acknowledging the inherent unpredictability associated with truly disruptive innovation.

Fourth, first authorship was used as a proxy for national research productivity. Although this approach may underrepresent collaborative or mentorship-driven contributions, it provides a standardized and widely used metric for global comparison, allowing continuity with previous literature and enabling reproducible cross-country analysis.2,27-30,39-47 An additional limitation relates to language-based selection bias. The present analysis was restricted to English-language publications indexed in PubMed, which may underrepresent research activity originating from non-English-speaking regions or countries where high-quality work is disseminated through local or regional journals. This restriction reflects current limitations of global bibliometric infrastructure rather than a conceptual exclusion of non-English scholarship. To preserve semantic consistency and classification accuracy within the AI pipeline, inclusion was limited to records with standardized English-language metadata. Accordingly, geographic forecasts should be interpreted as reflections of research visibility within dominant indexing systems rather than definitive measures of intrinsic research capacity. Recognition of this limitation is essential, particularly if forecasting outputs are used to inform strategic planning, and underscores the importance of future model extensions incorporating multilingual databases and region-specific indexing sources to enhance equity and global representativeness.

Finally, although aesthetic surgery served as the initial test field, the forecasting framework itself is discipline agnostic. Ongoing work aims to extend this architecture to other high-volume surgical specialties and to integrate additional data streams—such as citation networks, clinical trial registries, and funding databases—to further strengthen the multidimensional precision of predictive modeling. Importantly, the long-term evolution of this methodology is intertwined with broader AI adoption in surgical science. Structural, cultural, and regulatory barriers to AI integration may modulate its rate of translation, yet these same challenges underscore the value of developing rigorous, transparent, and scalable forecasting tools such as the one presented here.34

CONCLUSIONS

This study introduces a next-generation AI forecasting framework that fundamentally reshapes how aesthetic surgery research can be analyzed, understood, and projected. By uniting automated data extraction with high-fidelity semantic classification, the system delivers rapid, reproducible, and high-resolution predictions of global research dynamics—demonstrating sustained growth, accelerating thematic diversification, and expanding geographic participation through 2030.

More importantly, this work establishes a transferable paradigm for computational foresight in surgery. AI—long embedded in diagnostics and simulation—now emerges as a tool for strategic insight within the scientific enterprise itself.25,33,48 By transforming static literature patterns into predictive intelligence, the framework enables researchers, journals, and policymakers to anticipate emerging domains, allocate resources proactively, and guide the evolution of surgical innovation with unprecedented clarity.

Because aesthetic surgery continues its rapid global expansion, AI-driven forecasting has the potential to become indispensable—shaping not only how knowledge is produced but also how the future of the field is envisioned. This study demonstrates that predictive intelligence is no longer aspirational; it is operational, scalable, and poised to redefine the trajectory of surgical science.

Development and validation of a nomogram for predicting postoperative survival in patients with multifocal hepatocellular carcinoma
Authors: Bojun Liu, Gongming Zhang, Guangming Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is the fifth most common malignant tumor and the third leading cause of cancer-related death worldwide. Despite the significance of multifocality in HCC (M-HCC), most studies have predominantly treated it as a prognostic factor, lacking in-depth investigation into its prognostic implications. This retrospective study aims to integrate clinical and laboratory examinations to construct a predictive model that more accurately forecasts the prognosis of patients with M-HCC. This aims to guide personalized treatment strategies and improve overall survival rates.MethodsWe applied the Lasso and cross-validated Lasso (CV-Lasso) combined with Random Forest analysis to explore the prognostic factors influencing M-HCC. Simultaneously, we utilized the identified prognostic factors to construct a Nomogram. This Nomogram assigns scores based on the magnitude of the impact of each factor on prognosis, enabling the prediction of postoperative survival rates for patients. Subsequently, the model-derived risk scores categorized patients into low-risk and high-risk groups, and Kaplan–Meier curves were generated.ResultsThe patients, who were enrolled, were divided into training and validation sets. Factors influencing the prognosis of M-HCC were identified through Lasso, cross-validated Lasso, and Random Forest analyses, revealing seven key factors: Age, Antiviral, Child–Pugh, Cirrhosis, White Blood Cell (WBC), Platelets (PLT), and Thrombin Time (TT). Nomograms were constructed based on these factors to predict 3-year, 5-year, and 8-year survival rates. ROC curves confirmed the predictive capability of various factors, with larger areas under the curve at 5 (AUC = 0.653) and 8 years (AUC = 0.733). Kaplan–Meier curves stratified patients into low-risk and high-risk groups, showing significantly higher survival times in the low-risk group (p < 0.0001).ConclusionsPostoperative antiviral treatment for patients with M-HCC, aimed at enhancing immune function and protecting liver function, has been shown to extend patient survival. Simultaneously, factors such as Child–Pugh, age, and Cirrhosis in patients with M-HCC have been associated with a decrease in postoperative survival time.

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), ranking as the fifth most prevalent malignancy globally and the third leading cause of cancer-related deaths [1], exhibits a five-year survival rate of 18% and an 80% recurrence rate [2]. Its etiology encompasses factors like Hepatitis B Virus (HBV) and Hepatitis C Virus (HCV) infections, alcoholic liver disease, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, and hepatic nodular transformation [3–6]. Curative treatment modalities for early-stage HCC include surgical resection, liver transplantation, and radiofrequency ablation, while even with curative liver cancer resection in the early stages [7], the recurrence rate and mortality remain high [6, 8], resulting in a poor prognosis, which is largely attributed to the unique circulatory and vascular systems within the liver [8–12]. For instance, in patients with Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer (BCLC) stage 0-A tumors, curative resection is associated with a 5-year survival rate of 60%, yet the recurrence incidence can be as high as 65–70% [13].

As the poor prognosis of HCC, researchers are attempting to explore various methods to investigate factors influencing the prognosis of HCC, aiming to enhance patient survival and improve overall outcomes. With the deepening research on HCC, mounting evidence suggests that the multicentric occurrence of tumors is a crucial risk factor among the numerous factors influencing the prognosis of HCC [14–16]. Besides, the incidence of multicentric hepatocellular carcinoma (M-HCC) is increasing. On the one hand, the liver's unique portal venous system contributes to the formation of multicentric tumors through early intrahepatic metastasis of liver cells. On the other hand, advancements in diagnostic technologies have improved the detection rate of covert M-HCC, which makes M-HCC an increasingly distinct subtype with poor prognosis and high incidence within HCC [6, 17, 18]. Therefore, understanding the survival patterns of M-HCC and factors associated with survival is of paramount significance for postoperative monitoring and guiding emerging adjuvant therapeutic strategies.

Multifocality of HCC constitutes a significant component of HCC, and still remains a big challenge in the treatment of HCC, where tumor multifocality can occur simultaneously or evolve from isolated tumors, encompassing two primary types: 1) intrahepatic metastasis (IM) from the primary lesion; 2) multicentric occurrence induced (MO) by new tumors [6, 19–22]. Owing to constraints in diagnostic technology, approximately 41% to 75% of patients are initially diagnosed with M-HCC [6, 19–21], whereas the majority of occult M-HCC is typically identified intraoperatively, which indicates a higher actual incidence of M-HCC [6, 23, 24]. Etiologically, MO-HCC tends to be more related to liver background factors, whereas IM-HCC exhibits a greater reliance on tumor factors, indicating a more invasive nature of the tumors [6, 24]. Although the mechanisms underlying the formation of the two types of M-HCC are not yet fully understood, it is currently established that these mechanisms are not mutually exclusive. In other words, both types of multicentric tumors can coexist simultaneously and the recurrence rate of M-HCC is significantly higher than that of typical HCC, indicating a poor prognosis [25, 26]. The prediction and early identification of factors influencing the postoperative survival of patients with M-HCC are crucial. Based on this, it is possible to identify high-risk patients for postoperative mortality, assisting clinicians in developing personalized adjuvant treatment plans.

Therefore, this retrospective study aims to investigate the clinical characteristics, risk factors, and outcomes of postoperative survival in M-HCC. Additionally, we have developed and validated a nomogram to predict postoperative survival in M-HCC, which aims to more accurately forecast the prognosis of patients with M-HCC, providing better guidance for clinicians to tailor personalized treatment strategies and enhance postoperative survival rates.

Material and methods

Patients and samples

We retrospectively enrolled patients who underwent curative resection for liver cancer at Beijing Youan Hospital from January 2012 to January 2021. HCC was diagnosed according to the American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases (AASLD) HCC practice guideline [27]. All patients had not undergone any treatment before being diagnosed with HCC. Follow-up visits were conducted every three months postoperatively until June 2023.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria for patients with M-HCC were as follows: (1) Patients diagnosed with HCC, excluding cholangiocarcinoma and hepatocellular-cholangiocarcinoma mixed tumors; (2) No prior treatment, such as surgical resection, radiotherapy, targeted therapy, interventional treatment, immunosuppressive therapy, before the diagnosis of HCC; (3) Exclusion of patients with missing clinical or laboratory data; (4) Exclusion of patients who underwent liver transplantation during follow-up or were lost to follow-up; (5) Exclusion of patients who died from other causes, such as accidents or other diseases (Fig. 1A). To avoid bias from short-term postoperative complications, we excluded patients who died within 30 days after surgery. Due to the retrospective nature of this study, the requirement for informed consent was waived. This study was approved by the Ethical Review Committee of Beijing Youan Hospital, Capital Medical University, and conducted under the guidance of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Clinical data and laboratory parameters

All enrolled patients, whose diagnoses were determined through postoperative pathological examination, were found to have M-HCC. Routine imaging examinations were conducted simultaneously, with a dynamic observation of patients' prognosis and recovery.

Clinical and laboratory data were obtained from electronic medical records for each patient. Clinical features included age, gender, BCLC, Child–Pugh classification, Cirrhosis, histology, imaging examinations, liver cancer etiology, White Blood Cell (WBC), Platelets (PLT), Thrombin Time (TT), Antiviral and survival time. Selecting patients with early-stage HCC based on the BCLC staging system, grouping patients in A. Additional laboratory parameters were collected within 7 days before surgery, including complete blood counts. Systemic inflammatory parameters, such as Neutrophil-to-Lymphocyte Ratio (NLR), Platelet-to-Lymphocyte Ratio (PLR), and Monocyte -to- Lymphocyte Ratio (MLR) were calculated. Tumor markers and liver function indicators were collected within the 7 days preceding surgery.

Patients with HBV infection received antiviral therapy both preoperatively and postoperatively. The antiviral treatment included Entecavir (ETV), Tenofovir Disoproxil Fumarate (TDF), or Tenofovir Alafenamide (TAF). The choice of drug was based on patient-specific factors, and treatment was continued for at least 6 months postoperatively.

Preoperative HBV DNA levels were measured in all patients, and the baseline viral load ranged from < 200 IU/mL to > 10^6 IU/mL. The antiviral treatment aimed to suppress viral replication and mitigate liver damage, thus reducing the risk of recurrence.

Treatment procedures

All patients undergo preoperative imaging examinations to confirm the size, location, and number of tumors. Patients with HBV and HCV infections receive corresponding antiviral treatment in the two weeks preceding surgery. Radical hepatectomy is performed for all patients, followed by routine antiviral therapy postoperatively. After surgery, quantity, type, and completeness of resection are determined based on pathological reports. CT scans are conducted to assess treatment response and postoperative complications. All patients with HBV-related HCC were treated with antiviral therapy for at least 6 months postoperatively. The choice of antiviral agent and treatment duration were determined according to the patient's clinical condition and response.

Follow up

Follow-up was conducted for all the patients via telephone or in the Outpatient Department. The patients were subjected to follow-ups every 3 to 6 months, which comprised liver ultrasound, laboratory tests (including blood routine, biochemical function, AFP, coagulation tests, and other indicators), and physical examinations. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) or CECT scans were routinely performed every 6 months.

The trial's primary endpoint was overall survival (OS) which was defined from the date of curative treatment till the date of death from M-HCC.

Statistical Analyses

Statistical analyses were conducted using R (version 4.1.2) and SPSS (version 27.0). Initially, Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (Lasso) and cross-validated Lasso (CV-Lasso) analyses were performed on the training set using R Studio to identify factors influencing the prognosis of patients with M-HCC. While Random Forest analysis typically refers to the visual representation of the Random Forest algorithm, showcasing the ensemble of decision trees within the algorithm. This visual representation illustrates the importance scores attributed to each variable, providing insights into the factors that significantly influence the outcomes for individuals with M-HCC following surgery. The plot serves as a valuable tool for understanding the predictive contributions of different variables and aids in the interpretation and optimization of the model for improved decision-making in the clinical context. Subsequently, we applied the Lasso and CV-Lasso combined with Random Forest analysis to explore the prognostic factors influencing M-HCC.

For each identified predictive factor, nomograms were constructed to predict the 3-year, 5-year, and 8-year survival rates of patients with M-HCC. Bootstrap validation using 1000 samples was applied internally to each nomogram.

Thresholds for Age, Antiviral, Child–Pugh, Cirrhosis, WBC, PLT, and TT were determined using the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. The ROC curves were constructed based on survival status and various indicators, and the optimal cutoff values were selected where the area under the curve (AUC) was maximized. ROC curves were individually plotted for Age, Antiviral, Child–Pugh, Cirrhosis, WBC, PLT, and TT. The concordance index (C-index) was computed to assess the predictive ability of each factor, with a value of 0.5 indicating random chance and proximity to 1.0 signifying better discriminatory capacity.

Calibration curves were used to evaluate the correlation between actual outcomes and predicted probabilities, while clinical decision curves assessed the practical utility of the clinical prediction model. Finally, based on the nomogram, scores were assigned for the impact of each factor on the survival of patients with M-HCC. Patients were categorized into low-risk and high-risk groups based on their scores relative to the mean, and Kaplan–Meier curves were analyzed accordingly.

The average age of patients in the training set was 57.00 years (standard deviation 8.33), and for the validation set of patients, the average age was 56.43 years (standard deviation 9.21). The majority of patients were male (318 males in the training set, 78.52%, and 142 males in the validation set, 82.08%). All patients were Child–Pugh 0-A, with 268 Child–Pugh A stage patients in the training set (66.17%) and 109 in the validation set (63.01%). In all patients with M-HCC, there were a total of 419 cases with cirrhosis, among which 295 cases were in the training set (72.84%) and 124 cases in the validation set (71.68%). The median survival time in the training set is 4.32 years (95% CI 1.81–8.33), and for those in the validation set, it was 4.54 years (95% CI 1.72–9.71). The follow-up times for both the training and validation sets followed a normal distribution.

Factors Associated with OS

Lasso and CV-Lasso were used to screen parameters, recording the variations in coefficients of these variables (Fig. 1B, 1C). The tenfold cross-validation method was applied to the iterative analysis. Subsequently, 14 factors influencing the prognosis of M-HCC were ranked based on their impact, as depicted in the random forest plot (Fig. 1D). Finally, we obtained the intersection of variables derived from Lasso, CV-Lasso, and random forest analyses, identifying seven factors influencing the survival time of M-HCC: Age, Antiviral, Child–Pugh, Cirrhosis, WBC, PLT, and TT.

Nomogram

The seven factors identified were assigned scores based on their varying impact on the prognosis of M-HCC. Subsequently, nomograms were generated for 3-year, 5-year, and 8-year survival outcomes (Fig. 1E). The nomograms indicate that age, Child–Pugh, Cirrhosis and TT are adverse prognostic factors for M-HCC, aligning with previous research findings. Conversely, antiviral treatment (for patients with HBV and HCV infections), PLT, and WBC levels serve as protective factors, implying that patients undergoing antiviral therapy exhibit higher overall survival rates, particularly relevant to those with M-HCC related to HBV and HCV infections. Normal platelet levels signify intact liver function, reflecting the liver's compensatory capacity and indicating a lower impact of multifocal HCC on liver function and a better prognosis for patients. Low white blood cell levels suggest a compromised immune response, contributing to a diminished anti-tumor response.

Based on the scores obtained from the nomogram model predictions, the training set of patients with M-HCC was stratified into low-risk and high-risk groups (based on the relationship between scores and the median score), and Kaplan–Meier (KM) curves were plotted. It was observed that patients in the low-risk group, according to the established model, had significantly higher survival times than those in the high-risk group (p < 0.0001) (Fig. 4C). Discussion

While hepatic resection is the cornerstone of early HCC treatment, the postoperative survival rate is only 40–60% [13]. The progression of potential liver diseases, such as deteriorating liver function and advancing cirrhosis, as well as tumor biology, such as recurrence, may have adverse effects on prognosis. As the 5-year recurrence rate after liver resection for HCC is as high as 80% [6, 28], previous studies have focused on the recurrence of HCC after surgery, neglecting the recurrence and survival outcomes of multicentric occurrence of HCC postoperatively. Therefore, our research is essential as we emphasize clinical characteristics, and laboratory findings associated with the early postoperative prognosis of M-HCC. This article represents the first clinical predictive model, through a retrospective study of the ten-year outcomes of 578 patients with early-stage M-HCC, for prognostic assessment in M-HCC, it aims to enhance the detection of patient prognosis. The findings provide valuable insights to guide clinicians in tailoring personalized treatment plans, ultimately improving the postoperative survival rates for patients with M-HCC.

In our clinical prediction model, antiviral treatment, WBC, and PLT emerged as protective factors for M-HCC, consistent with previous research. Age, Child–Pugh, Cirrhosis, and TT are correlated with the adverse prognosis of patients with M-HCC. Antiviral therapy's benefits include reducing viral replication, liver damage, and cirrhosis severity, thereby lowering HCC recurrence and improving postoperative survival. Elevated WBC levels indicate a robust immune response, contradicting past findings, suggesting a positive correlation with favorable prognosis in M-HCC. PLT levels reflect normal liver function, suggesting a lower degree of cirrhosis and a better prognosis, while elevated TT levels indicate poor liver function and a worse prognosis. Past research has shown a correlation between high levels of peripheral blood N cells and poor prognosis, but our study indicates a positive correlation between high WBC and favorable prognosis in M-HCC patients, suggesting the presence of immune cells in peripheral blood with a counteractive role to neutrophils. For example, current research indicates that T lymphocytes and B lymphocytes in HCC promote antitumor immune responses. However, studies have also suggested the presence of Treg cells in T cells, which are associated with poor prognosis. While elevated levels of peripheral blood N cells are correlated with poor prognosis, our study did not identify a specific immune cell type associated with the prognosis of M-HCC. Therefore, there are limitations, and further research is needed to explore which types of immune cells are related to prognosis. Additionally, investigating the correlation between peripheral blood immune cells and immune cells within liver tumors is warranted. PLT levels serve as an indicator of normal liver function, with normal platelet levels indicating normal liver function and suggesting a lower degree of cirrhosis in M-HCC patients, resulting in a better prognosis. However, elevated TT levels are associated with poor liver function and increased susceptibility to adverse events, indicating a worse prognosis. Therefore, PLT and TT levels are two indicators reflecting the level of liver function. Age, as a crucial factor influencing disease prognosis, aligns with previous findings in HCC and other cancer studies, indicating a worse prognosis with increasing age. The Child–Pugh score plays a crucial role in liver cancer surgery, primarily used to assess the patient's liver function and overall health status. Our research indicates that postoperative overall survival is significantly higher in patients classified as Child–Pugh A compared to those in Child–Pugh B, consistent with previous studies.

Regarding Cirrhosis, our model indicates that the postoperative overall survival of patients with M-HCC combined with cirrhosis is significantly lower than that of patients with M-HCC alone. Past studies have shown that patients with liver cancer combined with cirrhosis experience severe impairment of liver function, along with complications such as portal hypertension and ascites, leading to a poor prognosis and multiple concurrent complications. Our model also validates that in patients with M-HCC combined with cirrhosis, their overall survival is markedly lower than that of patients with M-HCC alone. This suggests that, before surgical resection, we can improve liver function through conservative treatment in M-HCC patients, followed by scheduled surgery for surgical treatment. Therefore, we aim to further explore the inclusion of malignancy, vascular invasion, genetic mutation points, and other factors in the model to optimize this predictive model. The findings underscore the necessity for clinicians to strive for early intervention through radical hepatectomy and provide personalized treatment plans for patients with M-HCC.

Despite the continuous advancements in medical imaging and diagnostics, the rate of misdiagnosis for M-HCC remains notably high. Therefore, we aim to integrate the development of new imaging technologies with laboratory tests to enhance the preoperative diagnostic accuracy for patients. Simultaneously, the application of digital pathology will be employed to identify M-HCC subtypes, including IM and MO, thereby enabling the provision of personalized treatment plans to improve the overall survival of patients. M-HCC can arise from intrahepatic IM or MO [6]. The former exhibits a high degree of malignancy as well as poor prognosis6 [23–28]. In contrast, the latter is associated with lower tumor invasiveness, and antiviral treatment can significantly reduce tumor recurrence, leading to a better prognosis [23–28]. Current research suggests that MO-type M-HCC responds well to local treatment, while IM-type tumors generally exhibit average responsiveness to local treatment, and sorafenib cannot reduce their recurrence rate, resulting in a poor prognosis. However, immune checkpoint inhibitors can significantly reduce the postoperative recurrence rate for both types, improving clinical prognosis. This observation aligns with phenomena observed in the prognosis of HCC patients. Therefore, future efforts should focus on expanding the treatment modalities and postoperative anti-tumor drugs to predict the prognosis of M-HCC.

In conclusion, this study focused on investigating the clinical characteristics, risk factors, and outcomes of postoperative survival in patients with M-HCC. The research identified age, antiviral treatment, Child–Pugh, Cirrhosis, WBC, PLT, and TT as significant factors influencing the prognosis of M-HCC. A predictive nomogram was developed and validated to forecast 3-year, 5-year, and 8-year survival rates, offering a valuable tool for clinicians to tailor personalized treatment strategies. The findings underscored the importance of antiviral therapy in improving postoperative survival, emphasizing the role of early intervention and radical hepatectomy for better outcomes. The study highlighted the protective nature of normal platelet levels and a robust immune response, while identifying adverse factors such as older age, Child–Pugh, Cirrhosis, and impaired liver function. Although the model demonstrated good predictive accuracy, certain limitations, such as the retrospective, single-center nature of the study and the predominantly Asian population with HBV or HCV infections, should be considered. In addition, we were unable to conduct a reliable subgroup analysis between intrahepatic metastasis and multicentric occurrence due to limited pathological and molecular data. This is an important issue that warrants further investigation, and future prospective studies with standardized classification are needed to clarify the prognostic heterogeneity between IM- and MO-type M-HCC. Prospective, multicenter trials with diverse populations are essential for validating the nomogram's generalizability and predictive accuracy in different clinical settings.

Conclusions

This research contributes valuable insights into the postoperative prognosis of M-HCC, providing a foundation for further refinement and optimization of predictive models, ultimately aiming to enhance patient survival and guide clinicians in developing effective, personalized adjuvant treatment plans.

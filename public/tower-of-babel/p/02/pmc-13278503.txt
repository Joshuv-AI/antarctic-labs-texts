Effectiveness of Demonstration Observation Assistance Performance (DOAP) and Video Assisted Learning (VAL) in teaching blood pressure measurement among MBBS students
Authors: Romita Potsangbam, Vaishali Paunikar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:The skill of measurement of blood pressure (BP) is limited to a brief demonstration during Phase I MBBS without retraining/reinforcement in any other phases of training. Disparity of skill retention among the students along with limitation of resource leads to lack of mastery. The study aims to determine the effectiveness of Demonstration Observation Assistance Performance (DOAP) and Video Assisted Learning (VAL) as a teaching-learning method for measurement of BP in Physiology and analyse the perception of students and faculty of DOAP and VAL as a teaching-learning tool.MATERIALS AND METHODS:The cross-sectional study was conducted for a duration of 5 months from September 2024 to January 2025 on Phase 1 MBBS students of 2024 - 2025 batch enrolled in the Institute. Using non-randomized convenience sampling method, 148 students were divided into two groups A and B of 74 students each. Each group were divided into 11 subgroups for which a teacher is assigned and Group A were trained by DOAP while group B trained by using VAL in separate session. Assessment was done by Objective Structured Clinical Examination (OSCE) for each group and the groups swap to prevent disparity. Perception using 5-point Likert scale-based feedback questionnaires were taken from both the students and the teachers to evaluate skill acquisition and learner satisfaction. Statistical analysis was done using independent t test and Chi–Square/Fisher exact test.RESULTS:There was statistically significant increase (P value = 0.001) in the mean OSCE score of DOAP (8.52 ± 0.64) as compared to that of VAL (7.73 ± 1.06). Perception of both students and teachers shows DOAP as a more effective teaching learning method but has limitation of resources while VAL is more convenient and flexible.CONCLUSION:A combination of DOAP and VAL can create an integrated blended learning that uses the strength of both to enhance the learning of practical skills.

Introduction

The emphasis on change in Teaching learning method (TLM) is a novelty in CBME in which student centered TLM is given importance.[12] Accurate and precise blood pressure (BP) measurement is a complex procedure requiring multiple skill and observation and is a required skill for a clinician for diagnosis and management of hypertension.[3] Training of this certifiable skill is limited only for a brief period during phase 1 MBBS course with no retraining in any other phases of the course. Disparity of skill retention among the students requires repeated training and testing for the students to develop the necessary clinical skill.

With increasing workload and demanding manpower, there is a need for new TLM which ensures maximal learning with optimal resources. In DOAP, there is individual attention to the students, students can clear their doubts, the classes can be modified as per the student interaction and needs and students can be assessed (formative assessment) simultaneously.[45]

Medical educators are now able to develop innovative educational tools that offer a more interactive, self-paced and accessible approach to learning by allowing learners to study at their own convenience and pace.[6] Videos allow learners to watch procedure repeatedly, at their own pace, whenever they want.[7] It directs the students to pause, perform and practice with peer review and correction with pause and replay of the video.[8]

The present study was undertaken to determine the effectiveness of DOAP and VAL as a teaching learning tool for measurement of BP in Physiology and analyse the perception of students and faculty of DOAP and VAL as a teaching learning tool.

Materials and Methods

Study design and setting

This Interventional cross-sectional study was conducted in the Department of Physiology from September 2024 to January 2025.

Study participants and samplings

One hundred and forty-eight Phase 1 MBBS students of 2024-25batch were recruited in the study. Non-Randomized Convenience sampling was done and the students were divided into two groups based on their roll number. Two students, one from each group (A and B) were absent and not included in the study.

Group A (Roll no 1 to 75): 74 students (Roll no 4 absent)

Group B (Roll no 76 to 150): 74 students (Roll no 135 absent)

Eleven teachers (2 Professors, 2 Associate professor, 3 Assistant Professor and 4 Senior residents) working in the Department of Physiology was identified for the conducting the study.

Data collection tool and technique

Before the conduct of the study the study tools namely, the checklist for OSCE, video for the VAL and the questionnaire to be used were presented, discussed and validated by the Departmental Board of studies and the Medical Education Unit. The teachers were sensitized on the teaching-learning methods (DOAP and VAL) one week before the conduct of the study in the department. The purpose and aim of the study were also explained in details.

Each group (A and B) was divided into 11 interventional subgroups of 6-7 students and one teacher was assigned to each subgroup based on their roll number. The purpose of the study was explained to the students.

Group A was taught measurement of BP by DOAP session in the clinical lab. Each teacher of the assigned subgroup demonstrates the steps along with the clarification of doubts, students observe the teacher in performing the steps, students assist the teacher and the students performs independently along with the feedback from the teacher. At the end of the session, assessment of the skill was done using the Objective Structured Clinical Examination (OSCE) checklist and the score was collected for data analysis.

Video demonstrating the measurement of BP by a teacher was shared via WhatsApp group to Group B and they were directed to practice measurement of BP in the clinical lab by watching the video. The teacher assigned for each subgroup acts only as an observer without giving feedbacks and correction. At the end of the session assessment of the skill was done by using OSCE checklist and the score was collected for data analysis.

The TLM of the two groups were swapped in the next session to prevent disparity, where Group A was taught measurement of BP again with VAL and Group B with DOAP respectively. 5 points Likert scale-based feedback questionnaire for assessing the perception of DOAP and VAL as a teaching learning tool in terms of feasibility, acceptability, benefits and challenges were collected from both the students and teachers at the end of the session (As shown in [Figure 1]).

Statistical analysis

The data was entered into MS Excel sheet and analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics Base 22.0. Descriptive statistics were expressed in terms of mean, standard deviation and percentage. Analytical statistics was done by using independent t test and Chi Square test/Fisher exact test and P value less than 0.05 was taken as statistically significant.

Ethical issues

The ethical clearance from the Institutional Ethics Committee vide letter no. AC/03/IEC/JNIMS/2018-C (Proposal No. 578/97/2024) was obtained before the conduct of the study.

The open-ended responses give a wider picture and deeper insight into the specific benefits and challenges of VAL and DOAP. Both the students and teacher agree that VAL allows the students to learn at their own pace and is good for revision though there is lack of interaction and feedback. The students found DOAP more interactive and helpful in guided learning while the teachers found DOAP good for giving feedback and correction. The challenges faced in DOAP by the students is the time constraints while that for teachers is the lack of resources in terms of teachers and time. The students and the teachers maximally suggested “combination of DOAP and VAL” and “DOAP re-enforced by VAL” respectively [Figure 5].

Discussion

The present study included 51.4% (76) females with mean age of 19.47 ± 1.37 year and 48.6% (72) males with mean age of 19.68 ± 1.54 years.

In the present study, OSCE scores of DOAP and VAL suggested that DOAP is a more effective teaching learning tool leading to better performance in OSCE as compared to VAL [Table 1]. Similar findings were seen in studies conducted by Mouneghi HK et al.[9] and Devi B et al.[10]

In contrast to my findings, study by Sundeep S and Swapna K[8] showed a higher score in video assisted teaching as compared to DOAP in teaching examination of deep tendon reflex. Ahmet et al.[11] and Pilieci SN et al.[12] in their study found that video education is superior to demonstration in teaching skills to medical students. Yaqinuddin A et al.[13] found that implementing technologies in medical education were highly supportive among both the teachers and the students as it was an effective tool for acquiring relevant skill and competencies as a part of a training regime.

In the present study, perception of VAL by both the students and teachers strongly agree that it allows the students to learn at their own pace owing to its repeatability, feasibility, consistency of information, and flexibility. Our findings are similar to those of Mayer RE et al.[14] Students showed a strong preference for DOAP in enhancing learning experience due to its interactive nature allowing correction of error and feedback which is also strongly agreed by the teachers. Studies conducted by Balafoutas D et al.[15] and Gradl Dietsch et al.[16] have found that DOAP is significantly effective in procedures involving multiple sequential steps.

The study showed a significant increase in perception for DOAP by the students as a more effective teaching learning tool as compared to VAL [Table 2]. Similar finding is also seen for the teachers even though not statistically significant [Table 3].

Comparative perception between DOAP and VAL in our study showed that both students and teachers strongly agree that a combination of DOAP and VAL can enhance the effectiveness in acquiring the basic skill of measuring BP. Chilkoti G et al.[17] found that implementation of a hybrid mode provides higher satisfaction among undergraduate medical students in Basic Life Support/Advanced Cardiac Life Support teaching.

The responses to the open – ended questions showed that the use of VAL helps the students to learn and revise at their own pace,[18] while DOAP is interactive and helps the student in guided learning with feedbacks from the teachers. Each methods have its challenges as shown in Figure – 5 with VAL lacking interaction and DOAP with limitation of resources.

Our finding also showed that both the teachers and the students perceive DOAP and VAL similarly with no significant differences in their perception which might be suitable for implementation as a blended teaching in the curriculum.

Limitations and recommendations

Our study has limitations in the use of non- randomised convenience sampling as the students were already divided two batches for practical session. Further research must focus on longitudinal studies to establish the impact of these teaching-learning method during the later phases of the course.

Conclusion

VAL and DOAP are complementary teaching methods where VAL is accessible, flexible, and convenient while DOAP is indispensable for practical skill development with feedback and guided learning. Combination of these TLM can create an integrated blended learning that uses the strength of both which will enhance the learning of practical skills of the students. Standard video-based materials for clinical skill demonstrations can help in teaching as well as during revision practices before examinations and refresher learning. This will ensure consistency, uniformity, and standardized approach to develop physical examination skills among undergraduate students. The attitude of medical students towards core clinical skills can improves with knowledge and skills.

We recommend the development of standardized videos to supplement practical demonstrations and enhance the acquisition of basic clinical skills.

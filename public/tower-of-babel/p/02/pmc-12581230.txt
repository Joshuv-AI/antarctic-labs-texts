Endoscopic lavage after intraventricular haemorrhage in neonates in the UK (ENLIVEN-UK): study protocol for a national randomised-controlled trial

Background

In England, almost 500 preterm infants develop intraventricular haemorrhage (IVH) annually [1]. Over 50% progress to post-haemorrhagic ventricular dilatation (PHVD), with ventricular enlargement, distension of the periventricular white matter, raised intracranial pressure and injury to the developing brain. As well as pressure and distortion, the mechanisms of brain injury in PHVD include pro-inflammatory cytokines and free radicals from iron and hypoxanthine persisting in the ventricles for months [2]. IVH and PHVD are the commonest causes and strongest predictors of cognitive disability in prematurity [1, 3–5]. Optimising long-term cognitive and functional outcomes is important in this patient group as it has been shown to be a priority for families of children with hydrocephalus [6, 7]. Despite timely intervention, the burden of poor neurodevelopmental outcomes is worse in PHVD compared to other forms of infant hydrocephalus [8]. Standard treatment reduces pressure by allowing drainage or aspiration of CSF through a temporising device such as a ventricular subgaleal shunt (VSGS) or a ventricular access device (VAD) [9]. However, this does not address the toxicity from the blood and its breakdown products.

The Drainage, Irrigation and Fibrinolytic Therapy (DRIFT) trial was a randomised controlled trial evaluating the efficacy of CSF washout for 3 to 7 days in preterms with PHVD, performed through two ventricular catheters in neonatal intensive care units [10–12]. Although the trial was terminated early based on an interim analysis and did not show a significant difference in the primary outcome of permanent shunt or death at 6 months, long-term follow-up indicated sustained cognitive and developmental benefits in the DRIFT arm at 10 year follow-up. However, the DRIFT technique was performed in a single institution and is a complex lavage procedure requiring dedicated expertise over a long period of time, and was difficult to implement, teach and disseminate. In addition, DRIFT requires the use of fibrinolytic agents and a small proportion of babies had secondary bleeds in the ventricles following treatment. This has been the only intervention in PHVD to show statistically significant improvement in cognitive outcomes.

Neuroendoscopic lavage (NEL) is a 30-min adjunct to surgical insertion of a temporising device and allows blood, clot and its breakdown products to be washed out from the ventricles. Benefits of NEL are that it is a short, well-established, endoscopic procedure that most paediatric neurosurgeons in the UK routinely perform for other indications, and therefore have the experience to perform, and that it does not require the use of a fibrinolytic agent. Retrospective evaluations from Europe have shown promising results, with the procedure showing safety, reductions in permanent VP shunt insertion and revision rates and favourable neurodevelopmental outcomes, although none were prospective comparative trials.

The ENLIVEN-UK trial has therefore been funded to determine if the addition of NEL at the time of temporising device insertion would be safe and improve neurodevelopmental outcomes at 2 years in children with severe IVH and PHVD.

Primary objective

To establish the efficacy and safety of the addition of NEL to standard of care during temporising device insertion. It is hypothesised that the addition of NEL will be safe and will improve neurodevelopmental outcomes at 2 years in children with severe IVH and PHVD.

Methods/design

Design overview

ENLIVEN-UK is a phase III, multi-centre, randomised, assessor-blinded, controlled trial comparing NEL plus temporising device (intervention arm) with insertion of a temporising device alone (control arm) in preterm infants with IVH and PHVD, including an in-folded pilot study. This trial will aim to recruit 100 infants over 3 years across 12 or more neurosurgical units in the UK. Infants born < 37 weeks of gestation, with IVH and ventricular index ≥ 4 mm over the 97th centile for gestation despite 2 lumbar punctures or ventricular taps are eligible. Infants receive joint care by neurosurgeons and neonatal teams, as in standard practice. The pathway of the trial is presented in Fig. 1.

Research setting

The trial will be conducted across 12 or more neurosurgical units across the UK. NEL will be carried out by paediatric neurosurgeons with experience in infant neuro-endoscopy.

Study population

The trial will be open to preterm infants with IVH where the decision has already been made to undergo neurosurgical intervention for the insertion of a temporising device, who meet the eligibility criteria. A target of 100 infants will be recruited over a 3-year recruitment period.

Randomisation

Patients will be randomised to temporising device alone (control arm) or NEL at the time of insertion of temporising device (intervention arm) in a 1:1 ratio using a sealed envelope [13]. Written informed consent will be obtained pre-operatively; however, randomisation will take place immediately prior to surgery. The treating neurosurgeon, theatre staff and associated clinical team cannot be blinded to the intervention, and the increased operative time will also result in the NICU team being aware of the treatment arm. Previous parent advisory group work has identified that parents would like to be informed of the allocation, and failing to do so may result in many parents not taking part; therefore, parents will be informed post-operatively whether the intervention was received.

The trained personnel assessing the primary outcome measure at 2 years’ gestational age will be blinded to the randomisation, along with the trial statistician and clinical project manager. Parents will be informed by the study team prior to the assessment that the assessor will be blinded, and therefore not divulge this information to the assessor inadvertently, which will be reinforced by the assessor before evaluation commences.

Surgical intervention

A national collaborative consensus statement to standardise the procedure across all of the participating institutions has been produced [14], and the extent of haemorrhage evacuation will be evaluated on postoperative ultrasound. To summarise, infants allocated to the intervention arm of NEL with temporising device will undergo insertion of an endoscope into the lateral ventricle via a peel-away catheter on the side of higher blood load. Ultrasound guidance may be used at the surgeon’s discretion for optimal placement of the endoscope. Following irrigation of the lateral ventricle with Ringer’s lactate or other appropriate lavage solution, the third ventricle and contralateral ventricle (via a septostomy if required) are also irrigated until the effluent is clear. Blood clots on the ependymal surface may be gently aspirated. The endpoint of the lavage will be at the surgeon’s discretion and defined as a ‘maximal safe lavage’ when the effluent is clear and there has been an attempt to dislodge and wash out clots. Once the procedure is completed, a temporising device (either a VAD or VSGS) is inserted, at the discretion of the treating surgeon in line with local practice.

Infants allocated to the temporising device arm (control arm) will undergo the standard surgical procedure to insert either a VAD or VSGS, at the discretion of the treating surgeon, in line with their standard current practice. Both VAD and VSGS are associated with the same operative and post-operative risk, namely infection, recurring haemorrhage inside the ventricles, new haemorrhage related to ventricular decompression, catheter blockage, failure of absorption of CSF from the subgaleal pocket of a VSGS, seizures, external leakage of CSF, electrolyte disturbance and a very small risk of stroke. In our experience, all these are rare and occur in less than 5% of cases. The management of these risks will remain at the discretion of the treating neurosurgical team.

Primary outcome

The primary outcome measure will be the cognitive quotient (CQ) measured at 2 years’ corrected age (± 2 months). The CQ is measured by dividing the age equivalent cognitive score on the Bayley Scales of Infant and Toddler Development Fourth Edition (Bayley IV) [15] by the corrected age at assessment multiplied by 100. The CQ is effective at distinguishing differences in infants with severe disability, overcoming the ‘floor’ effects of using standardised scores. At 2 years’ corrected age, this measure provides an important surrogate for developmental outcomes in later life.

Data collection

Trial data are collected via a secure online electronic data capture system (OpenClinica), and pseudonymised imaging scans are transferred by sites to UCL researchers for upload to a secure platform (UCL Data Safe Haven). The database software provides a number of features to help maintain data quality, including maintaining an audit trail, allowing custom validations on data, allowing users to raise data query requests, and search facilities to identify validation failure and missing data.

Safety reporting

Foreseeable significant adverse events (SAEs) will be recorded and reported if deemed related to NEL. Unforeseeable SAEs must be reported within 24 h. Notifiable events should be reported within 5 days and include a new bleed identified on post-operative cranial ultrasound, unexpected return to theatre for temporising device dysfunction, return to theatre for insertion of an additional temporising device to treat loculated hydrocephalus or for a second endoscopic procedure, confirmed or presumed infection with the trial procedure (superficial wound or deep infection where a micro-organism is isolated or where antibiotics is commenced), new development of seizures or focal neurological deficits within 48 h of NEL, and an abandoned NEL procedure. All safety data will be reviewed closely by the Independent Data Monitoring Committee (IDMC).

Proposed sample size

The sample size required for the ENLIVEN-UK trial was calculated using individual patient data from the DRIFT trial, which showed that the distribution of cognitive quotient (CQ) in the ‘standard treatment’ arm at 2 years’ corrected age was approximately uniform in the 0–100 interval with a median of approximately 50 [11]. The distribution in the DRIFT arm was more skewed, with a median of approximately 70 [11]. This was evident at 10 years, with a median difference of 25.6 (46.7 in the control arm vs 72.3 in the DRIFT arm) [12]. We hypothesise that NEL would achieve the same effect as DRIFT. We therefore estimate a median difference of 20 points between our treatment arms. A difference of 20 points is considered clinically significant, representing approximately a 5-month difference in developmental age at 2 years and a 2-year difference at 10 years. That this was also considered a significant and valuable difference to parents as confirmed by our PPI focus group.

To reflect results shown in DRIFT, the distribution of CQ in the control arm was modelled using a Beta(1,1) distribution, equivalent to the uniform distribution. The distribution in the experimental arm was modelled to be a Beta(10, 4.25). Both distributions were multiplied by 100 to reflect the 0–100 CQ scale. Using simulations performed in R with 1000 replications, we established that 45 patients in each arm, totalling 90 patients, would provide 90% power to detect the hypothesised treatment effect, based on a Wilcoxon test with two-sided alpha of 5%. To account for a 10% loss to follow-up and other methodological challenges during the study, we aim to recruit 100 patients in this study, 50 in each arm.

Statistical analysis

The primary outcome measure will be the cognitive quotient (CQ) scale rescaled to range from 0 to 100. The outcome measure will be assessed at the infant’s 2 years’ corrected age timepoint only, and we anticipate that the shapes of the two response distributions will differ. As this continuous outcome scale is not normally distributed and the sample sizes are of moderate size, the non-parametric Mann Whitney U test will be used to compare the two independent arms. This will test the alternative hypothesis that responses in the two arms are not equal. This test will be performed two-sided and at a 5% alpha level. The test statistic for the Mann–Whitney U test is denoted by U and is derived using the sums of the ranks within arms of responses in both groups. U1 (NEL with temporising device) and U2 (temporising device alone) will then be compared. An exact p-value will be calculated rather than a normal approximation. The effect measure will be the median difference in rescaled CQ score along with a 95% confidence interval. Sensitivity analyses will be performed adjusting for the minimisation factors to assess the robustness of the primary analysis, and separately the bootstrap means will also be calculated and compared between randomised groups.

All secondary outcome measures will be analysed using methods appropriate to their data structure. As a general principle, summary statistics for continuous variables used will be mean, median, SD, lower quartile, upper quartile and reported appropriately according to distribution. Summary statistics for binary and categorical variables will be n (%). Supplemental, exploratory subgroup analyses will be considered for gestational age at birth, sex, grade of IVH and site dependent upon infant numbers within each category.

It is only possible to include those randomised infants with 2-year response data in the primary analysis. Multiple Imputation of missing values is possible, though consideration will need to be taken as to the volume and mechanism behind the missing data and its consequent effect on the interpretation of the outcome measure. All infants randomised to one of the two arms will be included in other analyses where possible, irrespective of the successful completion of the procedure as intended—following the intention to treatment principle and analysis set. Any infants withdrawn after consenting, but prior to randomisation and surgery, will be summarised clearly and openly separately. Exploratory analyses may be produced using only those that adhered to the protocol as intended (per protocol) should the analysis aid interpretation.

Health economic analysis

An economic evaluation will be conducted over the trial period with a primary analysis from the NHS and personal social services (PSS) perspective and a secondary analysis from the societal perspective that will include the impact on quality of life and productivity of parents/carers contributing to the infant’s care. A discount rate of 3.5% currently recommended for health technology appraisal in the UK will be applied to costs and health effects accruing beyond the first year. The overall economic evaluation will comprise three overarching components: (i) cost-effectiveness, (ii) health-related quality of life, and (iii) cost–benefit analysis of the impact on parents/carers.

Cost-effectiveness analysis

Mean incremental costs and incremental effectiveness of NEL plus temporising device compared with temporising device alone. Incremental costs will be measured from the NHS and PSS perspective. Incremental effectiveness will be estimated as the difference in cognitive disability at 2 years’ corrected age. Data on delivery of the NEL intervention (e.g. staff time, consumables) will be collected to calculate the cost of the intervention using micro-costing methods [16]. Additional healthcare resource use such as days in hospital, number of planned and emergency admissions will be collected at 3–6, 12, 18 months and 2 years’ corrected age through purpose designed parent-completed questionnaires (telephone assessment). Unit costs will be obtained from most recent published sources [17, 18]. Incremental cost-effectiveness ratios will be reported, and uncertainty explored using cost-effectiveness acceptability curves [19, 20].

Health-related quality of life (HRQoL)

Measuring the overall impact of a health condition requires preference-based methods. Efforts to evaluate HRQoL and calculate quality-adjusted life years (QALYs) for infants less than 12 months of age are restricted by the lack of preference-based HRQoL instruments for this group [21, 22]. Because of methodological concerns surrounding the use of preference-based measurement in early childhood, the QALY metric will not be used. However, we will assess the HRQoL (at 1 year and 2 years’ corrected age) using the TAPQOL (TNO-AZL Preschool Children’s Quality of Life) a reliable and valid instrument for measuring parents’ perceptions of HRQoL for children aged between 1 and 5 years [23].

Cost–benefit analysis of the impact on the parents/carers

Responses to EQ-5D-5L and the associated algorithm mapping the 5L descriptive system data onto the 3L valuation set, as recommended by NICE, will be used to calculate QALYs in a standard format and valued as a willingness-to-pay (WTP) for a QALY gained [24, 25]. Information on productivity losses will be collected and costed using the human capital approach [26]. As caring responsibilities of parents/carers are complementary to state-funded caring, we shall also calculate the societal value of caring provided by parents/carers.

Translational research studies

To capitalise on the trial infrastructure, two translational studies are being planned alongside this trial. These will be carried out under separate funding arrangements and will require their own ethics applications. The first is a radiological study which will involve correlation between the brain MRI scan undertaken at term-equivalent age and the primary and secondary outcomes of the trial. The second is a CSF study whereby CSF, as well as a paired blood specimen, will be taken at the time of the index procedure and also, in some infants, at the time of insertion of a permanent VP shunt. Additional CSF and blood samples may be collected at any unscheduled visits to the Neurosurgical Unit if the patient has consented to these added studies. Funding has been provided within ENLIVEN-UK to transport these specimens and store them in the Liverpool Neuroscience Biobank at the Walton Centre in Liverpool. Additional funding will be sought to correlate the presence and level of biomarkers in the blood and CSF with the extent of intraventricular haemorrhage, PHVD and trial outcomes. Separate consent will be requested for the translational studies. Consent for collection of blood and CSF, as well as storage of radiological investigations, will be requested at the same time as consent for ENLIVEN-UK.

Discussion

This protocol describes the design of an RCT to evaluate the safety and effectiveness of NEL at the time of temporising device insertion to improve neurodevelopmental outcomes in preterm infants with IVH at 2 years. This is the first RCT to investigate this intervention, and if successful, will allow rapid adoption into standard clinical practice within the National Health Service.

Trial status

ENLIVEN-UK opened for recruitment in May 2024 with a 12-month in-folded pilot with clear progression criteria. If successful, recruitment will continue for a further 2-year recruitment period until August 2026.

Dissemination of results

The communication and dissemination strategy will actively involve all participating centres, which include most paediatric neurosurgical units in the country, as well as professional bodies (British Paediatric Neurosurgical Society, International Society of Paediatric Neurosurgeons) and partner organisations and charities (Shine Charity, BANNFU). A lay summary of the results will also be produced to be disseminated to all participants, parents and carers who took part and express an interest in the findings. The results of the trial will be disseminated regardless of the magnitude and interpretation of any effect of the intervention and will be published through an open-access mechanism in a peer-reviewed journal. All individuals who have made substantial intellectual, scientific and practical contributions to the trial and the manuscript should, where possible, be credited as authors; all individuals credited as authors should deserve that designation. Findings of the trial will also be presented at national and international meetings of relevant professional bodies and research groups.

The latest version of the protocol will be made available as supplementary material upon publication of the primary publication. Applications for access to the trial dataset at the end of the trial will be considered after formal consideration by the trial oversight committees and the CI.

Development of a comprehensive model for management efficiency in agricultural enterprises amid digital transformation
Authors: Bangxi Li, Yuan Lu, Andrei Ksenofontov
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe study is devoted to assessing the effectiveness of enterprise innovation management in the context of digital transformation. The relevance of this research is determined by the need to develop a comprehensive methodology that enables diagnosing the level of companies’ innovation activity and identifying priority directions for its advancement. The methodological framework includes a vector model for evaluating innovation management, indicator normalization, as well as correlation, cluster, and regression analyses. The empirical basis of the study comprises data from 24 Chinese companies operating in the agricultural sector. For each enterprise, the level of management efficiency was determined across three dimensions: innovation performance, innovation potential, and financial support of digital innovation processes. The key findings indicate that innovation potential (0.794) and financial support of innovation processes (0.683) exert the strongest influence on the formation of the innovation management vector, whereas innovation performance demonstrates a comparatively weaker effect (0.334). Hierarchical clustering made it possible to distinguish four groups of companies by their level of innovation activity: low, moderate, significant, and high, which provides opportunities for comprehensive diagnostics and benchmarking. The developed regression models confirm the reliability of the assessments and enable forecasting changes in innovation performance depending on variations in the three key indicators. The proposed methodology can be practically applied for evaluating enterprise innovation management, determining priority development pathways, and designing strategies for digital transformation.Keywords: Agribusiness, Economic efficiency, Information systems, Performance analysis, Strategic planning, Technological changesSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Given that the modern world is at the center of a transformation driven by the rapid advancement of information and communication technologies, innovation management has become an increasingly complex task in the digital economy. The digital economy has emerged as a powerful driver of innovation, altering the way businesses and society interact. In this context, the ability of enterprises to effectively manage their innovation activities has become crucial for ensuring competitiveness and sustainable development1.

In the context of the digital economy, enterprises across various sectors often link innovation management with digital technologies and artificial intelligence. This can be attributed to entrepreneurs actively seeking effective solutions to sustain their market positions and competitiveness2.

However, not every innovation yields the anticipated results in enterprise management. This is attributable to the fact that digital transformation can generate both positive and negative outcomes3. On the one hand, it provides new competitive advantages, such as enhanced efficiency of business processes, improved product quality, and accelerated managerial decision-making. On the other hand, the implementation of digital technologies may lead to additional financial expenditures, technological complexities, cybersecurity risks, and challenges related to the protection of confidential data, as well as disruptions in the integration of existing business processes. These factors may influence the overall market performance of an enterprise and its competitiveness4.

It is important to note that the agricultural sector constitutes a significant component of China’s economy, accounting for approximately 7–8% of GDP and providing millions of jobs in rural areas5. This sector demonstrates considerable potential for the adoption of digital technologies, including drones, agricultural robots, and precision farming systems, which contribute to increased production efficiency and product quality6. The analysis of innovation activity among Chinese agricultural enterprises makes it possible to evaluate how digital technologies affect business processes and corporate performance7.

China, as one of the leading economies in the world, demonstrates a unique experience in digital transformation, making its agricultural sector a significant subject of study. The use of digital technologies in China, such as artificial intelligence, the Internet of Things, and big data analysis, is significantly altering agricultural practices. This contributes to increased productivity, enhanced resource management efficiency, and ecological sustainability of enterprises8.

A distinguishing feature of China’s agricultural sector is the active government support for digital initiatives, which provides enterprises with access to modern technologies and facilitates their adaptation to the new conditions of the global market9. Despite this, companies face numerous challenges, such as the high cost of technology implementation, unequal access to digital solutions in different regions, and the complexity of integrating innovations into traditional business processes10.

Despite the considerable body of research in the field of digital transformation, insufficient attention has been given to the specifics of innovation management in agricultural companies in China, particularly from the perspective of their long-term competitiveness. This study aims to fill this gap by offering practical recommendations for improving management approaches and enhancing the effectiveness of digital innovations.

Thus, the findings of this work will contribute to a better understanding of digital transformation processes in the specific context of China’s agricultural sector, representing a valuable contribution to the theory and practice of innovation management.

Literature review

The agricultural sector constitutes a key component of the economy in many countries, including China, and is characterized by significant potential for the adoption of digital technologies. Contemporary challenges—such as climate change, globalization, and increasing demands for efficiency and sustainable production—necessitate the integration of advanced digital solutions, including drones, agricultural robots, and precision farming systems.

Research conducted by Martínez-Caro et al.10 demonstrated that digital technologies exert a positive impact on enterprise productivity; however, their effect is largely contingent upon the development of a digital organizational culture. Their findings confirm that technological innovation, when not supported by organizational structures and culture, may fail to deliver the anticipated outcomes. Similar conclusions were reached by Saarikko et al.11, who noted that digital transformation creates new competitive advantages but may simultaneously generate additional costs and risks, particularly financial and operational.

Within the agricultural context, Bucci et al.12 showed that the application of precision farming technologies in Italy proves effective only when accompanied by adequate personnel training and investment support. Chierici et al.13 further expanded this observation, emphasizing that small innovative enterprises are often constrained in their use of digital solutions due to limited resources and insufficient integration of technologies into internal processes. In China, as highlighted by Jia et al.14, the national innovation system supports the sustainable development of small and medium-sized enterprises; nevertheless, the absence of strategic adaptation and qualified personnel leaves enterprises vulnerable to risks, thereby limiting the effectiveness of digital innovations.

Furthermore, Crupi et al.15 emphasize that most existing models of innovation management assess only isolated aspects—such as innovation performance, innovation potential, or financial support—while neglecting their interdependence. Similarly, Doyle and Cosgrove16 highlight that the seasonality of production processes and rapid technological change create additional challenges for forecasting and scaling innovations. Stich et al.17 and Peter et al.18 argue that successful digital transformation requires a comprehensive approach that integrates technology, organizational change, and financial support; however, the majority of studies overlook this integrative dimension.

Thus, a critical review of the literature reveals significant gaps: the absence of comprehensive models that simultaneously account for innovation performance, potential, and financial support; insufficient attention to seasonality and external factors; and limited integration of methods for evaluating the effectiveness of innovation management. These shortcomings underscore the necessity of developing a new integrated model that combines cluster and regression analyses to forecast the effects of changes in innovation activity indicators and to provide practical recommendations for managers of Chinese agricultural enterprises.

Based on the analysis of the literature, the objective of the research is to develop a comprehensive model for diagnosing and enhancing the effectiveness of innovation management in enterprises amid digital transformation.

Research hypotheses:

Hypothesis 1

Enterprises with a high level of innovative activity demonstrate greater efficiency in innovation management compared to enterprises with a low and medium level of innovative activity, which is manifested in a clearer orientation towards the development of innovative capacity and innovative efficiency.

Hypothesis 2

The level of innovative capacity is the main factor influencing the efficiency of innovation management, and financial support for innovation processes has a smaller impact on the overall innovation efficiency of enterprises.

Hypothesis 3

The use of a comprehensive innovation management model based on cluster and regression analysis allows for a more accurate assessment of the needs of enterprises in the development of innovative activity and the identification of effective directions for increasing their competitiveness.

The novelty of this study lies in the development of an integrated model for assessing the effectiveness of innovation management, which simultaneously incorporates innovation performance, innovation potential, and financial support for innovation. The model employs cluster and regression analyses to forecast the effects of changes in these indicators and provides practical recommendations for enhancing the effectiveness of digital transformation in agricultural enterprises in China.

Materials and methods

The conceptual basis of research

The study aims to analyze the effectiveness of innovation activities in the Chinese agricultural sector, considering the impact of digital transformation. This context suggests the research area (agricultural sector), geographical limitation (China), the main goal (the development of diagnostic methods and identification of key success factors), and the conditions (introduction of digital technologies) of the study. Today, innovation activity influenced by many factors is one of the most important indicators for enterprises. This study endeavours to form a vector model to assess innovation management efficiency by giving an example of agricultural enterprises considering three identified performance indicators: innovation performance, innovation capacity, and financial support for the company’s innovation processes.

Research design

The vector model was chosen because it allows for the integration of numerous factors influencing a firm’s innovation activity within a comprehensive model. Since a firm’s innovation activity is influenced by various factors, this approach enables a more comprehensive analysis and considers the interaction among different aspects of innovation. Additionally, the vector model can be represented graphically, facilitating information visualization and a better understanding of the interrelationships among variables. Graphs can serve as powerful tools for communicating research findings and aiding external users in understanding these results. Furthermore, the vector model enables the modelling of relationships among different variables, helping to understand how one variable influences another. This can be valuable in determining strategies for enhancing the effectiveness of innovation management.

Therefore, overall, the vector model was chosen due to its capability to conduct a comprehensive analysis of innovation management, considering various indicators and their interrelationships. It can serve as a powerful tool for studying the innovation process within agricultural enterprises.

At the first stage of modelling, the substantiation of the existing system of key indicators of the enterprises’ innovation management efficiency is provided. Each of the proposed areas of the company’s innovation activity is assessed based on the arithmetic average of normalized indicators:

For innovation performance level: innovation implementation rate (l_1), the share of spending on R&D (research and development) and other external knowledge (l_2), digital service integration ratio (l_3), and digital intensity factor (l_4);

For the innovation capacity level: the share of employees involved in research and development (l_5), the intellectual property provision ratio (l_6), the digital literacy ratio of human capital (l_7), as well as the level of digital provision of workers’ workplaces (computer technology, the Internet, and smart technologies) (l_8);

For the financial support level for innovation processes: autonomy ratio (l_9), absolute liquidity ratio (l_10), the share of spending on digital innovations in the company’s revenue (l_11), and digital investment profitability coverage ratio (l_12).

At the second stage of modeling, integral indicators are identified across three dimensions—innovation performance, innovation potential, and financial support of innovation processes—within the enterprises under study in the context of digital transformation. This stage enables the consolidation of information regarding the level of management effectiveness and provides the foundation for subsequent analysis and clustering of enterprises.

The third stage of modeling the enterprise innovation management efficiency involves a cluster analysis of a group of companies being subject to study, taking into account vector spaces that form two vectors concerning the digital transformation of businesses. Therefore, the clustering must be performed over three vector spaces: IPL-ICL, ICL-FSLIP, and FSLIP-IPL. When carrying out clustering over vector spaces, the “nearest neighbor” method, that is, “k-NN” (k-nearest neighbor), was used. This clustering method enables determining the level of similarity in keeping with the established characteristics (indicators) of the companies under study, which are proposed to be used in the system of key indicators according to the vector model for enterprise innovation management.

Methodological limitations

The study can identify factors and strategies that apply to other sectors, but several key aspects need additional consideration. Geographical context is decisive for extrapolating results, as geographical, cultural, and legislative realities can vary significantly. Similarly, sectoral specifics require an individual approach, since the specifics of different industries can affect the successful implementation of innovations. The business context is also important, as specific recommendations must be tailored to the needs and conditions of each organization. Given these aspects, it is necessary to conduct additional analysis and adaptation to specific conditions and needs before applying the results of the study to other locations and sectors. In this case, the findings will be effective and relevant.

Discussion

In the present study, the application of the vector model of innovation management made it possible to cluster enterprises according to their level of innovation activity and to identify priority directions for innovation development. The findings confirm the first hypothesis: enterprises with a high level of innovation activity exhibit greater effectiveness in innovation management, which is reflected in the more pronounced development of both innovation potential and innovation performance. For instance, companies in the highest cluster demonstrated substantial advantages in innovation potential indicators, which is consistent with the conclusions of Martínez-Caro et al.10, Peng et al.20, and Li et al.21 regarding the impact of digital transformation and organizational culture on enterprise performance in China.

The cluster analysis also confirmed the heterogeneity among enterprises, a result that aligns with the work of Alexandru et al.22, Shabanov et al.23, and Baierle et al.24, who emphasize the importance of tailoring innovation strategies to the scale, region, and specific characteristics of agricultural enterprises. Unlike previous studies, in which clustering was primarily employed to differentiate groups by financial or marketing indicators13,15, this study constructed clusters based on integrated indicators of innovation management. This approach enabled a more precise assessment of enterprises’ needs in digital transformation25–27.

Regression analysis revealed that the main factor influencing the effectiveness of innovation management is innovation potential, whereas financial support exerts a comparatively weaker effect. This partially confirms the second hypothesis and is consistent with the findings of Doyle and Cosgrove16 Stich et al.17, and Feng et al.28, who argue that human capital and organizational adaptability play a decisive role in digital transformation, while financing provides only supplementary opportunities.

It is also important to note that the vector model makes it possible to identify directions for enhancing the effectiveness of innovation management at the level of individual enterprises, thereby confirming the third hypothesis regarding the practical value of an integrated approach based on cluster and regression analysis. Unlike general models11[,18[,29 the proposed methodology integrates quantitative assessment, priority setting, and forecasting of indicator changes, which constitutes the novelty of this study.

At the same time, certain risks and limitations should be acknowledged: the implementation of the vector model requires appropriate technical infrastructure, qualified personnel, and effective change management. As the analysis demonstrates, enterprises with a low level of innovation activity exhibit limited readiness for digital transformation, a finding consistent with the conclusions of Crupi et al.15, Abbate et al.25, Farace and Tarabella30 and Robertson and Lapiņa31 who emphasize the necessity of training and the adaptation of corporate culture.

Overall, the findings demonstrate that the integrated application of the vector model, cluster analysis, and regression analysis enables a more precise evaluation of innovation management effectiveness, the identification of priority directions for innovation development, the forecasting of changes in key indicators, and the support of strategic decision-making in the digital transformation of agricultural enterprises27,32,33.

Conclusions

The results of clustering enterprises based on innovation activity indicators revealed a clear division into groups with varying levels of innovation management effectiveness, which allows for the assessment of enterprises’ needs for support and development in specific aspects of innovation activity. Enterprises with a moderate level of innovation activity exhibit similar indicators, while companies with a high level of innovation activity display greater diversity in their innovation vectors, enabling them to adapt more effectively to digital changes.

The theoretical contribution of the study lies in the development of a vector model of innovation management, which enables a comprehensive assessment of innovation effectiveness, potential, and financial support for digital processes in enterprises. The use of clustering to group companies by innovation activity levels allows for a more precise identification of directions for improving innovation strategies. Additionally, the application of regression models to evaluate the effectiveness of innovation management provides a theoretical basis for analyzing the interrelationships between various indicators that influence the success of innovation processes.

The practical contribution of the study is to provide tools for innovation management through the use of cluster analysis and regression models that allow enterprises to assess their innovation activity and develop strategies for its improvement. The results of the study emphasize the importance of digital transformation for increasing innovation efficiency and reveal the potential for strategic planning and decision-making in the face of modern economic challenges.

The value of the research lies in the development and implementation of a vector model for assessing the effectiveness of innovation management in the agricultural sector, particularly in the context of digital transformation. This allows for an examination of various aspects of companies’ innovation activities and their interrelationships. Through such a comprehensive assessment, enterprises can better identify their strengths and weaknesses in innovation activities.

The contribution of the study to existing knowledge is that it provides a new approach to analyzing the effectiveness of innovation management in the agricultural sector, especially in the context of digitalization. The proposed model can be useful for enterprises seeking to integrate cutting-edge technologies and develop innovative strategies to enhance competitiveness and efficiency.

Future research will focus on a more in-depth study of residual issues and new challenges, including a comparison of the results of digital transformation and innovation in the agricultural sector with other industries. Specifically, an expansion of the sample is planned, including enterprises of varying scales and levels of digitalization, which will enable the generation of more comprehensive and comparable conclusions.

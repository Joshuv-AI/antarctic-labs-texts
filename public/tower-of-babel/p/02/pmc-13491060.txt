Shifting Landscape of Cardiovascular Diseases in China

The comprehensive analysis by Shi et al1 of China’s cardiovascular disease (CVD) burden from 1990 to 2023, based on the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) 2023 study, arrives at a critical juncture in the nation’s epidemiologic transition. As China has undergone unprecedented economic growth, urbanization, and population aging, its CVD profile has shifted from a predominantly hemorrhagic pattern toward an ischemic predominance. The study systematically quantifies incidence, prevalence, mortality, and disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) for all major CVD subtypes and their attributable risk factors, providing the most current GBD-based assessment of China’s cardiovascular landscape. Between 1990 and 2023, age-standardized DALYs decreased by 59.7%; however, the proportion of total CVD-related DALYs attributable to ischemic heart disease (IHD) rose from 22.7% to 41.3%, underscoring the shifting burden of CVD and providing evidence-based insights to guide public health prevention strategies.

Contributions of the Study

The study offers several distinctive contributions across four result domains.1 First, the current burden assessment reveals that CVD caused 4.5 million deaths (42.4% of all deaths) and 89.3 million DALYs in 2023, with IHD, intracerebral hemorrhage, ischemic stroke, hypertensive heart disease, and subarachnoid hemorrhage as the top mortality contributors, whereas peripheral arterial disease had the highest incidence rate (199.4 per 100,000)—a frequently overlooked burden. Second, sex- and age-specific analyses demonstrate marked disparities: Men had substantially higher incidence, prevalence, mortality, and DALY rates, and the dominant CVD subtype shifted markedly with age—from endocarditis in children younger than 5 years to rheumatic heart disease at ages 5 to 14 years, to IHD consistently from the age of 15 to 49 years through ≥70 years. Third, temporal trend analysis from 1990 to 2023 reveals a diverging pattern: Age-standardized mortality declined by 58.1% and DALYs by 59.7%, yet incidence fell by only 10.2% and prevalence rose by 2.2%, suggesting that prevention efforts have not kept pace with treatment advances. The composition shifted dramatically—the proportion of total CVD-related DALYs attributable to IHD nearly doubled from 22.7% to 41.3%, whereas that of hemorrhagic (including intracerebral and subarachnoid hemorrhage) stroke declined from 44.0% to 27.6%. Joinpoint regression identified distinct temporal phases for each major subtype, including a three-phase IHD DALYs trajectory of initial decline, mid-period plateau, and resumed decline after 2016. Fourth, attributable risk factor analysis from 1990 to 2023 documents a fundamental reordering: Ambient particulate matter pollution rose from the ninth to the second-leading risk factor, whereas household air pollution dropped from second to 15th. Metabolic risk factors—elevated fasting glucose and high body mass index—increased among men but declined among women, contributing to the slower improvement in men. Sugar-sweetened beverages and processed meat had the largest increases in attributable DALYs, suggesting an emerging Western dietary pattern.

Methodological Considerations

Several methodological caveats warrant careful consideration when interpreting these findings. The GBD framework, although powerful for descriptive surveillance, uses modeling approaches that identify associations rather than their causal relationships.2 The comparative risk assessment offers broad perspectives but may not fully account for time-varying confounding, shared risk pathways or the complex interdependencies among metabolic, environmental, and behavioral risk factors.3 As national averages, the estimates may not adequately reflect the profound heterogeneity across China’s diverse regions. The newly established China CVD Surveillance System has revealed that rural areas exhibit substantially higher crude CVD incidence than urban areas (719 vs 551 per 100,000),4 a pattern that national-level GBD estimates may not fully reflect. Furthermore, the coronavirus disease-2019 pandemic from 2020 to 2023 may have introduced competing mortality, health care access disruptions, and cause-of-death misclassification that may bias recent trend estimates. Although using the GBD standard global population for age-standardization facilitates international comparison, exploring the 2020 Chinese census population could offer estimates more closely aligned with China’s uniquely rapid aging trajectory. Additionally, incidence data for specific subtypes such as hypertensive heart disease and aortic aneurysm would benefit from continued refinement within the GBD framework.

Future Directions: From Surveillance to Precision Cardiovascular Prevention

The study by Shi et al1 not only charts the historical trajectory of China’s CVD epidemic but also illuminates a roadmap for future research and policy action. First, although age-standardized CVD mortality has declined impressively, the absolute burden—11.1 million incident cases and 4.5 million deaths in 2023—remains a major public health concern, underscoring the need for sustained national initiatives. The Healthy China 2030 initiative has established ambitious targets for CVD risk-factor control, yet quantifiable reduction goals for incidence have yet to be explicitly incorporated into the current action plan.5,6 Future guideline development should explicitly incorporate incidence-based targets alongside mortality objectives, particularly for primary prevention. Given that approximately 88% of IHD and 82% of ischemic stroke events occur in the primary prevention population (ie, incident events),4 community-based strategies tailored to specific urban and rural contexts remain highly relevant. In resource-limited rural areas, where physician density remains low, approximately 2 per 1,000 population,7 and hypertension control rates are less than 15%,8,9 several scalable interventions such as salt substitution, community-based Baduanjin exercise programs (which recently demonstrated a sustained 3.1 mm Hg reduction in 24-hour systolic blood pressure at 12 weeks),10 and task-shifting to village doctors represent high-yield approaches. In affluent urban settings, expanding lipid-lowering therapy coverage is a priority, given that the current statin treatment rate is approximately 10%, although elevated low-density lipoprotein accounts for a substantial proportion of all-cause mortality, the control rate remains below 5%.11

Second, rigorous evaluation of current and forthcoming prevention guidelines is essential. The 2011 Chinese Society of Cardiology guidelines are associated with a 5.7% decline in CVD mortality and a 2.9% reduction in DALYs, as demonstrated by Bayesian causal impact analysis using synthetic controls.5,12 This quasi-experimental approach, which leverages publicly available health statistics and macroeconomic indicators to construct counterfactual scenarios, provides an alternative and useful method to assess the population-level impact of subsequent guidelines and national programs. Integrating multisource data—including environmental monitoring data (eg, PM2.5 concentrations, ambient temperature), health system indicators (eg, hospital beds per 1,000 population, primary care facility density), and socioeconomic covariates (eg, disposable income, educational attainment)—into these causal frameworks can help delineate the respective contributions of policy interventions and concurrent secular trends.5 Such analyses can identify factors beyond GBD’s core modifiable risk factors, such as health care accessibility and environmental exposures, that may drive residual regional disparities. For instance, China's Cardiac-Cerebral Vascular Disease Event Surveillance project shows that western rural China has the highest stroke incidence nationally,4 likely reflecting low health care workforce density and limited access to timely patient management and infrastructure for rehabilitation and chronic disease management.

Third, these insights should guide the development of precision prevention strategies. Risk prediction tools calibrated to Chinese populations—incorporating region-specific coefficients for ambient pollution exposure, dietary patterns, and genetic architecture (eg, methylenetetrahydrofolate reductase polymorphisms relevant to folate metabolism and stroke risk)2—can move beyond one-size-fits-all approaches. For secondary prevention, the finding that 17% of CVD events are recurrent highlights the need for strengthened postdischarge care coordination, medication adherence support, and cardiac rehabilitation, particularly in middle and central China, where recurrence rates are highest.5 Among emerging frontiers, the integration of wearable device–based cardiovascular health monitoring,13 metabolomics-guided precision nutrition,14 and climate/environmental-adaptive seasonal management15 targeting the leading attributable risk factors of blood pressure control represents promising avenues. Ultimately, translating the descriptive wealth of GBD analyses into actionable, contextually adapted prevention programs, and rigorously evaluating their impact through advanced causal inference methods will help determine whether China can slow the CVD epidemic and achieve the cardiovascular health goals envisioned by Healthy China 2030.

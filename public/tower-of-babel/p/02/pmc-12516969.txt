Longitudinal Educational Achievement and Development Study (LEADS): a protocol for a prospective study assessing medical students’ competencies

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractBackgroundLongitudinal studies provide insights into the outcomes of medical training curriculum. However, few educational cohort studies have been conducted in Iran. This study aims first to evaluate the impact of the current curriculum on medical students' medium- and long-term academic and career outcomes and, second, to identify medical students' characteristics and how they change through the doctor of medicine programme.Methods and analysisThis protocol outlines a multi-phase, prospective cohort study that will take place in Mashhad, Iran. The study will implement the Kirkpatrick model, investigating medical students' knowledge, skills, behaviour and professionalism development over 10 years. Approximately 1000 medical students will be recruited through peer invitations and social networks. Data will be collected through baseline and follow-up questionnaires, academic performance records and comprehensive test scores throughout the Doctor of Medicine (MD) programme.The data from the questionnaires will be reported using a Likert scale. Quantitative data will be described using means and SD, while qualitative variables will be presented as frequencies and percentages. We will evaluate the relationship between quantitative variables using correlation coefficients and the relationship between qualitative variables via the χ2 or Fisher exact test. All tests will be two-sided, with a significance level set as p<0.05.Ethics and disseminationAll participants will complete written informed consent before data collection. All students can withdraw from the study at any time with no consequences. Results of this study will be presented at relevant conferences and will be submitted for publication in peer-reviewed journals. Introduction

The World Federation of Medical Education Framework for Global Standards (2020) states that medical schools should define learning outcomes for each course part and by graduation.1 In accordance with this standard, the Iranian government policy focuses on developing a curriculum empowering students' capability for higher education: ‘The MD programme, while implementing the latest findings of medical education, will be able to ensure alignment with the international standards of medical education. By accomplishing these advancements, dedicated, competent and responsive to the healthcare needs, graduates will be trained’.2

The medical education curriculum in Iran spans 6.5 years and encompasses four main parts: basic science (which is a prerequisite for subsequent clinical parts, 2 years), physiopathology courses (also called the foundation of clinical medicine, 1 year), clerkship (2 years) and internship (1.5 years).3 4 Following graduation, based on the ‘law related to the service of doctors and paramedics’, medical graduates are required to do community services in health networks throughout the country for 2 years.5 This mandatory service aims to enhance their practical experience further.6

Longitudinal studies in medical education provide information on short- and long-term findings on novel strategies in curricula and complex decision-making on students' progress in academia.7 8 However, as Association for Medical Education in Europe (AMEE) guide No. 90 mentioned, a large sample size for longitudinal studies is necessary due to attrition bias.9 To our knowledge, only a few educational cohort studies have been conducted in developing countries including Iran.

We describe the design and methodology of a prospective educational cohort study. The Longitudinal Educational Achievement and Development Study (LEADS) aims to highlight, first, to evaluate the current curriculum impact on medium- and long-term academic and career outcomes of medical students and, second, to identify medical students' characteristics and how they change through the doctor of medicine programme in the Mashhad University of Medical Sciences. Further papers will focus on the analysis and interpretation of collected data.

Methods

Setting

This prospective cohort study is based at the Faculty of Medicine at Mashhad University of Medical Sciences, one of the largest research-intensive medical universities in Iran. The study will have student participation from the first semester of the MD programme until 3.5 years right after graduation. The study will recruit approximately 1000 students and this project is planned for completion in 10 years, by 2034.

Study population

Participation will be sought from medical students who enter medical school. In this study, although all eligible students will be invited to participate. The target sample size of 1000 students is based on a census approach, including all eligible students from four consecutive student intakes over a 2-year academic period, with approximately 250 students per intake. We anticipate a moderate effect size (Cohen’s d=0.3–0.5) for key educational and well-being outcomes, which with the proposed sample size, would ensure >80% power at α=0.05, even considering an expected attrition rate of up to 20%.

Eligibility criteria

Based on the inclusion criteria, medical students who entered the school of medicine and filled out the written informed consent form will be included in the study.

In order to ensure the validity of the study, certain exclusion criteria will be applied; students who transfer to another university, visiting students or students who withdraw from the programme will be excluded from the study. This is to prevent any potential bias that may arise from changes in educational programmes or environments.

Recruitment, data collection methods

This educational cohort will contribute to inter-departmental cooperation (vice-chancellors of the university, including educational, research and student services). The design of this study is multi-phase:

Phase 1

Right after registration, eligible students will be invited to attend an oral presentation and demonstration of the study and its goals. They will be given a written informed consent form. Students who do not provide informed consent are not considered participants. Students who declared their consent will be considered study participants from this point onwards. Phase 3

After graduation, the students will be followed up for at least 3.5 years, not only during the ‘social services’ period but also for 1.5 years after completing this period. They will be followed in terms of their career and which specialties they will choose for residency.

As this study is a longitudinal study with potential for loss of participants,9 especially regarding filling out the questionnaire, selected peers will be assigned as facilitators for students. These peers will receive comprehensive training on various aspects of the questionnaire, enabling them to assist and encourage their assigned students to complete questionnaires. To reduce the risk of coercion or social desirability bias resulting from the involvement of same-cohort peer facilitators, facilitators will use pre-approved, scripted messages for all participant communications (eg, reminders), minimising personal influence. These peer facilitators will also undergo a structured training programme emphasising ethical conduct, neutrality and avoidance of persuasion or suggestion.22 Moreover, a dedicated office is established at the Faculty of Medicine to ensure safe and secure data gathering. A trained personnel will provide sufficient services for this project.

Data linkage and external data sources

A secure domain has been established on the university servers to facilitate the collection and management of information for this study. This domain allows researchers to collect, manage and analyse data ensuring security via defined and blind access levels.

Data analysis

The study will be conducted as a census, inviting all incoming medical students, which represents approximately 1000 individuals. The data will be analysed using SPSS 16.0 for Windows (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, 2007). Quantitative variables will be described using mean and SD, while qualitative variables will be described using frequency and percentage. The relationship between quantitative variables will be evaluated using correlation coefficients, while the relationship between qualitative variables will be assessed using the χ2 or Fisher exact test. Intragroup changes will be examined using paired t-tests and repeated measures Analysis of Variance (ANOVA). In case of non-normal distribution of quantitative variables, the equivalent non-parametric test will be used. Regression models will be used to control for potential confounding variables. All tests will be two-sided, and the significance level will be considered as p<0.05.

Attrition and sensitivity analysis

To account for potential attrition bias, we will perform sensitivity analyses by comparing baseline characteristics (eg, gender, age, entrance exam rank, baseline GPA and psychological profiles) between participants who complete the study and those who drop out (due to transfer, withdrawal or loss to follow-up).

Ethics and dissemination

Approval for the study was granted by the Ethics Committee of Mashhad University of Medical Sciences (Reference: IR.MUMS.REC.1400.311). The study will be conducted following ethical principles and guidelines. Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants before data collection. Participants will have the right to withdraw from the study with reasonable justification at any time without any penalties. The LEADS will be responsible for publishing the results of this longitudinal study in a peer-reviewed journal in accordance with International Committee of Medical Journal Editors (ICMJE) guidelines. Furthermore, our research team plans to share the cohort findings through oral and poster presentations at relevant national and international conferences.

Substrate Modification for Atrial Fibrillation Induced by Mechanical Irritation That May Be Associated With Non‐Pulmonary Vein Foci
Authors: Koichiro Sonoda, Shunpei Hyakutake, Kentaro Furukawa, Kaishi Otsuka, Asumi Takei, Koji Maemura
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThe importance of treating non‐pulmonary vein (PV) foci is increasing with improvements in the durability of PV isolation. We describe two cases wherein non‐PV foci were induced by mechanical irritation and conventional induction in the same area, which was impossible to induce after ablation, suggesting a relationship between mechanical irritation and induction of non‐PV foci. In a recurrent case of non‐PV foci, atrial fibrillation (AF) was induced only by mechanical irritation. No recurrence has been observed since the ablation of the area. Thus, treatment of the AF induction site with mechanical irritation could be considered an effective therapy for non‐PV foci.Keywords: atrial fibrillation, catheter induced, catheter stimulation, mechanical irritation, non‐pulmonary vein, non‐PV trigger

Introduction

Atrial fibrillation (AF) recurrence following pulmonary vein (PV) isolation (PVI) is often attributed to long‐term non‐PV foci [1]. As a result, awareness of the importance of treating non‐PV foci is increasing. Methods for inducing non‐PV foci include the administration of high‐volume isoproterenol (ISP) and adenosine triphosphate (ATP), and cardioversion following AF induction [2, 3, 4]; nevertheless, induction may be challenging in certain cases. We present two cases of consistent induction by mechanical irritation and conventional methods, and one case wherein effective treatment was only possible with induction of non‐PV foci by mechanical irritation. The induction of AF by mechanical irritation was approximately 10 g or less for the ablation catheter, which was within the normal operating range for the mapping catheter.

Case Reports

Case 1

A 72‐year‐old woman was admitted for catheter ablation (CA) of paroxysmal AF (PAF). The ejection fraction (EF) was 63.4% and the left atrial dimension (LAD) was 33.2 mm on echocardiography. CARTO3 version 7 (Biosense Webster, Diamond Bar, CA) was used for three‐dimensional mapping. The left atrial (LA) voltage map with right atrial (RA) pacing under sinus rhythm (SR) revealed a low voltage area (LVA) in part of the anterior LA (Figure 1A). After PVI and superior vena cava isolation, atrial premature contractions (APC)/APC run/AF was reproducibly induced by mechanical irritation with an ablation catheter near the LVA (Figure 1F: red arrow). Mechanical irritation at other sites did not induce AF. After cardioversion, non‐PV foci were induced with the PENTARAY catheter at the same site (Figure 1G: white arrow). In the same area, delayed conduction was observed in early‐meets‐late (EML) during RA pacing (Figure 1B). Focal activation was also observed in the CARTOFINDER [5] during sustained AF (Figure 1C). A highly fragmented potential area of K‐CFAE (complex fractionated atrial electrograms) was observed (Figure 1D) [6]. Ablation was performed on the same area, including the surrounding substrate, under AF (Figure 1E); however, termination of AF was not observed. After establishing SR with cardioversion, AF was not induced by ISP 10 µg, ATP 20 mg, or mechanical irritation, and no recurrence of AF was observed in the subsequent 1‐year follow‐up.

Case 2

A 67‐year‐old woman was admitted for CA of PAF. The EF was 75.2% and the LAD was 30.7 mm on echocardiography. CARTO3 version 7 was used, the RA voltage map under the SR revealed an LVA in the septum (Figure 2A,B), not indicated in the LA. After PVI, APC/APC run and AF were reproducibly induced by mechanical irritation of multiple sites in the anterior wall, appendage, high septum, and inferior wall of the RA with the PENTARAY catheter (Figure 2I: red arrow), did not induce in the LA. AF became persistent, and non‐PV foci from the base of the RA appendage were induced following cardioversion (Figure 2J: white arrow). In addition, focal activation was observed in the anterior wall and appendage of the right atrium in CARTOFINDER during sustained AF (Figure 2C,D). A high degree of the fragmented potential area of K‐CFAE was observed diffusely in the lateral wall and posterior septum (Figure 2E,F), aligning with a part of the focal activation area in CARTOFINDER. After ablating the CARTOFINDER focal activation area and including the surrounding areas where APC/APC run appear (Figure 2G,H), AF was not induced, even following mechanical irritation with a catheter, in each RA region at the level of the APC/APC run. At the 1‐year follow‐up, no AF recurrence was observed.

Case 3

The patient was a 77‐year‐old man. PVI and cavotricuspid isthmus ablations were performed for persistent AF. Five years later, PAF recurred, resulting in the need to undergo a second CA. The EF was 56% and the LAD was 39.7 mm on echocardiography. EnSite Navx (St. Jude Medical, Minneapolis, MN) was used, during LA geometry creation, non‐sustained AF was reproducibly induced by mechanical irritation with the HD Grid Mapping Catheter of the anterior wall of the LA (Figure 3D: red arrow) and ablation catheter (Figure 3E: red arrow), not induced at other sites. Fragmentation was observed under SR in the areas where AF was induced by mechanical irritation (Figure 3B,D; green arrow). There was no PV gap, and LVA was almost absent (Figure 3A). Continuous administration of ISP 20 µg and a single dose of ATP 20 mg, and continuous bust pacing for 180 ms from RA did not induce AF. Thereafter, the AF persisted by mechanical irritation and terminated during ablation of the inducted area (Figure 3C). In addition, ablation was performed including the surrounding areas where APC/APC runs appeared during ablation. Subsequently, AF owing to mechanical irritation was no longer observed; the session was terminated. No recurrence of AF has been reported for 4 years.

Discussion

AF recurrence is likely to occur in cases of incomplete treatment of non‐PV foci [7]. In recent years, a high proportion of cases of non‐PV foci has been observed in long‐term recurrences following PVI [1], highlighting the importance of treatment of non‐PV foci. Beyond PVI includes CFAE [8] and driver/rotor mapping [9, 10]; however, their effectiveness is limited. The usefulness of ablation with a specific integrated module (K‐CFAE) and AcQMap as novel mapping methods has also been reported [6, 11]. Additionally, although non‐PV trigger ablation is considered useful [2], non‐PV foci are not induced during treatment in some cases. Recently, the effectiveness of fractionated signal areas in atrial muscle (FAAM) ablation has been reported as a new treatment of non‐PV foci [12]. The usefulness of ablation using CARTOFINDER has also been reported for persistent AF [5, 13].

Acute stretching immediately elevated the Ca2+ spark frequency in atrial myocytes, which is involved in the onset of AF, in mice [14]. Zhang et al. also indicated that improving type 2 ryanodine receptor dysfunction suppresses the induction of AF in this model, thereby highlighting the mechanistic link between sarcoplasmic reticulum Ca2+ leak induced by LA stretching and AF occurrence in pressure‐overloaded hearts.

In Cases 1 and 2, the induction sites of AF by mechanical irritation were the same as those of non‐PV foci induced by conventional induction, suggesting a relationship between mechanical irritation and conventional induction. In both Cases 1 and 2, the area specifically induced by mechanical irritation was also the focal activation area of CARTOFINDER. Notably, AF induction was suppressed by ablating the same area. Case 3 demonstrated a recurrence due to non‐PV foci without PVI gap, where AF inductions were only feasible with mechanical irritation, not ISP/ATP/cardioversion. The induction area exhibited fragmentation in the SR, which may be related to the FAAM, as reported by Hirokami et al. [12]. By ablating this area, AF, which was reproducibly induced, became uninducible. No subsequent recurrence was observed, and treatment of the AF‐induced area using mechanical irritation was considered an effective complementary method for managing non‐PV foci. In the present ablation, the area of APC/APC run appeared around where AF was induced, which was also ablated. Therefore, the substrate might have also been treated.

These findings, although based on a small sample size limiting our ability to draw firm generalizable conclusions, suggest the potential efficacy of mechanical irritation‐guided non‐PV foci therapy in the treatment of AF. Future research with a larger sample size using specialized equipment is warranted.

Conclusion

The reproducible induction of AF by mechanical irritation suggests a promising treatment of non‐PV foci. Further research, including randomized clinical trials and a meta‐analysis of published literature, is warranted to substantiate the merits of treatment of non‐PV foci by mechanical irritation.

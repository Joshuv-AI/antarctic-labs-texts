Disease characteristics and treatment status of genetically confirmed spinal muscular atrophy patients: a cross-sectional survey in China
Authors: Dingding Zhang, Fengxia Yao, Chao Ling, Shan Jiang, Xunzhe Yang, Yi Dai
Published: 2025

Background

Spinal muscular atrophy (SMA), specifically referring to 5q-SMA which results from pathogenic variants in the survival motor neuron 1 (SMN1) gene Located at chromosome 5q13, is a debilitating motor neuron disease caused by homozygous deletions or compound heterozygous mutations in the survival motor neuron 1 (SMN1) gene [1]. This mutation leads to the degeneration of spinal anterior horn motor neurons and lower brainstem motor nuclei, resulting in progressive muscular weakness and atrophy [2, 3]. The carrier frequency of SMA is remarkably high, affecting 1 in 50 individuals, with an estimated incidence of approximately 1 in 10,000 newborns in China [4, 5]. The SMN protein, which is crucial for motor neuron function, is ubiquitously expressed across various tissues and organs [6]. Insufficient levels of SMN protein initiate a cascade of pathological changes, leading not only to progressive limb and trunk muscle weakness and atrophy but also to systemic complications. These include respiratory distress, spinal deformities, joint contractures, and difficulties in swallowing, significantly impairing both the physical and psychological well-being of patients [7].

As a crucial genetic counterpart to SMN1, the SMN2 Gene contains a single nucleotide variation in exon 7 that results in the exclusion of this exon during mRNA splicing, thereby producing only limited amounts of functional SMN protein [8]. This insufficiency exacerbates the pathological manifestations of SMA, underscoring the therapeutic potential of enhancing SMN2 gene expression [9]. Recently, several disease-modifying therapies (DMTs) such as nusinersen and risdiplam have been approved in China, offering promising treatment avenues for SMA patients [10]. Both nusinersen and risdiplam function by modulating SMN2 splicing to include exon 7, thereby increasing the synthesis of functional SMN protein [11, 12]. While nusinersen is administered via intrathecal injection, risdiplam is an orally administered small molecule, providing a more convenient treatment option [13]. Internationally, gene therapy with onasemnogene abeparvovec has shown remarkable efficacy in presymptomatic infants identified through newborn screening, highlighting the importance of early intervention; however, its availability and cost remain barriers in many regions, including China [14, 15]. European surveys have highlighted that improvements in motor and respiratory functions are critical for SMA patients, with a marked preference for oral treatments due to their convenience and ease of administration [16]. However, the treatment preferences of Chinese SMA patients and the factors influencing these preferences are not yet clear.

SMA is a progressive neuromuscular disorder characterized by motor function decline, respiratory complications, and varying degrees of disability [1]. While previous studies have extensively documented improvements in motor and respiratory functions following therapeutic interventions [17, 18], there remains a gap in understanding the broader disease characteristics, treatment accessibility, and patient-specific factors influencing therapeutic decisions in the Chinese SMA population. To address this, we conducted a cross-sectional survey investigating the diagnostic and treatment landscape of genetically confirmed SMA patients in China. This study aims to (1) characterize the clinical profiles of SMA patients, including disease manifestations and functional status, (2) evaluate the current utilization of disease-modifying therapies (DMTs), and (3) identify key factors shaping treatment choices. By providing a comprehensive assessment of disease burden and therapeutic trends, this study seeks to inform clinical practice and healthcare strategies for SMA management in China.

Methods

Study design and participants

This single-center, cross-sectional survey was conducted at Peking Union Medical College Hospital using an online approach, incorporating web-based questionnaires and synchronous online telephone interviews. The dual-mode strategy was employed to enhance participant accessibility and data completeness: the self-administered web-based questionnaire efficiently collected factual and historical data, while the subsequent telephone interview facilitated the elaboration of subjective perspectives and complex decision-making processes, ensuring a comprehensive understanding of patient experiences. The questionnaires applied in the two phases were different and designed to complement each other, constituting an integrated whole for the survey. Participants were systematically identified through retrospective review of hospital medical records, with all cases genetically confirmed by the clinical genetics laboratory of our institution. Patients who met the inclusion criteria were contacted based on contact information documented in their medical records, regardless of their digital Literacy or prior engagement levels. Data collection spanned from October to December 2023. This study was conducted in compliance with the Helsinki Declaration and received ethical approval from the Ethics Committee of Peking Union Medical College Hospital, and all participants provided informed consent prior to participation. For participants under 16 years of age, consent to participate was obtained from their parents or legal guardians.

The inclusion criteria for surveyed patients were (1) patients with a genetic diagnosis of SMA confirmed by the clinical genetics laboratory of Peking Union Medical College Hospital, following the criteria outlined in the “Expert Consensus on Genetic Diagnosis of Spinal Muscular Atrophy (2020 edition) [19]”, (2) expressed willingness to participate, (3) signed informed consent forms: for patients under 12 years of age, consent was obtained from guardians; for those aged 12–17 years, consent was provided by both the patient and guardians; and for patients aged 18 years or older, consent was obtained from the patient directly.

The exclusion criteria for surveyed patients were (1) patients were unable to participate or declined to sign the informed consent forms, (2) patients were deceased.

Questionnaire preparation

The questionnaire was collaboratively developed by Peking Union Medical College Hospital and China Alliance for Rare Diseases. The development process involved referencing established guidelines and consensuses related to SMA, as well as incorporating insights from previous Chinese and international surveys on similar topics [3, 19]. The questionnaire composed of three parts: (1) General Information, (2) Complications and Motor Function, and (3) Medication Treatment Regimens (Supplementary 1 Questionnaire).

Questionnaire distribution and data collection

Prior to the initiation of the study, all personnel involved in the questionnaire survey underwent comprehensive training to ensure uniformity in understanding the research background, the methodology of questionnaire completion, and the overall objectives and significance of the survey. This training included detailed explanations of the questionnaire content, distinctions between different research methods, and mastery of the appropriate guiding language to be used during the survey.

For the distribution of web-based questionnaires, dedicated survey personnel sent links or QR codes to the patients. Patients completed the questionnaire on the online survey platform, following the provided prompts. For the online interviews, dedicated survey personnel conducted telephone interviews, during which they collected relevant information and simultaneously entered the data into the survey platform.

To ensure data integrity, questionnaires were deemed ineligible and excluded from final analysis if they met any of the following criteria: (1) all selected options in multiple-choice questions were identical or followed an obvious pattern (e.g., ABAB or AAAA), or (2) responses did not adhere to the instructions or exceeded the specified range.

Statistical analyses

The normality of continuous data was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test. For normally distributed continuous data, results are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD), whereas skewed data are presented as median and interquartile range (IQR). Independent sample t-tests were employed to compare two groups of normally distributed continuous data, while analysis of variance (ANOVA) was utilized for comparisons among three groups. For skewed continuous data, the Mann-Whitney U test was used to compare two groups, and the Kruskal-Wallis H test was applied for comparisons among three groups. Categorical data are reported as frequencies and percentages, with intergroup comparisons conducted using chi-square tests or Fisher’s exact tests as appropriate. Statistical analyses were performed using R software (Version 4.2.2).

Potential factors related with spinal deformity, motor function, and willingness to have another child were analysed using univariate and multivariate logistic regression. In the analysis of motor function, those who chose the options of not able to sit, able to sit with support, and able to sit alone were deemed as poor motor function, while those who chose the options of able to stand with support, able to stand alone, able to walk with support, and able to walk alone were deemed as good motor function. Factors with P < 0.10 in the univariate analysis will all be included in the multivariate analysis. The statistical software was IBM SPSS 22.0.

All tests were two-tailed and P < 0.05 was considered to indicate a significant difference.

Results

Characteristics of SMA patients and willingness to have another child of the patient’s parents

A total of 452 patients with Genetically confirmed SMA diagnosed by Peking Union Medical College Hospital were recruited for this survey, of which 380 had contact information. Among the 380 patients, 307 reached out successfully, while 73 without answer to the call. There were 108 patients refused to participate, 38 patients were deceased, 61 quitted during the interview (mainly due to disease severity-related challenges such as respiratory/swallowing difficulties in severe SMA cases or psychological burdens), with the remaining 100 patients completing the entire survey. The response rate was 44.3% (199/452), and the completion rate was 50.3% (100/199). Additionally, questionnaires were collected from 6 patients in the pre-survey stage. Therefore, a total of 106 patients were finally collected in this study. Further details on the patient are outlined in Fig. 1.

The patients had a median age of 18.50 years (IQR, 14.00-25.75), with 42.5% aged ≥ 20 years and 30.2% aged 15–19 years. Males comprised 53.8% of the sample. The distribution of SMA types was as follows: type 1 (10.4%), type 2 (40.6%), and type 3 (49.0%). Factors influencing spinal deformity and motor function

Motor function

Analysis of motor function attained in the past revealed that the largest proportion of patients (42.5%) were able to sit independently. Significant differences in past motor function between patients with different subtypes of SMA (P < 0.001). Specifically, 72.7% of type 1 patients achieved the ability to sit with support, 37.2% of type 2 patients were able to sit independently, and a notable 80.4% of type 3 patients were able to walk independently without assistance. Whether or not the patient used DMT had a significant effect on past motor function (P = 0.026). Among those who have used DMTs, 48.6% were able to walk independently, compared to 28.1% of non-DMT users (Supplementary Table 1).

In terms of current motor function, there was a significant difference between patients who used and did not use DMT (P = 0.034). Specifically, 28.4% of DMT users are currently able to walk independently, compared to only 15.6% of non-users. Further stratification of SMA subtypes showed that there were also significant differences in current motor function between the different SMA subtypes (P < 0.001). Currently, 45.5% of type 1 patients cannot sit, while 27.9% of type 2 patients and 1.9% of type 3 patients fall into this category. Notably, 50.0% of type 3 patients are able to walk independently, whereas this ability is absent in type 1 and type 2 patients (Supplementary Table 2).

The results of multivariate analysis further showed that female patients had worse motor function than male patients (OR: 3.175; 95% CI: 1.131–8.907; P = 0.028). Patients with poor motor function have a lower level of education compared to patients with good motor function (OR: 12.067; 95% CI: 4.363–33.370; P < 0.001) (Table 4).

Main points of treatment in SMA patients

Treatment pattern

A total of 66.0% (70/106) of patients have received treatment with the intrathecal injection of nusinersen, and 24.5% (26/106) have received the oral solution of risdiplam. Notably, 20.8% (22/106) of patients have used both nusinersen and risdiplam. A smaller subset, 8.5% (9/106), have received non-DMT medications in the past or present, including drugs Like salbutamol, while 20.8% (22/106) have not received any SMA medication treatment. Medication usage among different SMA subtypes is depicted in Fig. 2, showing the highest proportion of patients using nusinersen across types 1, 2, and 3 (72.7%, 58.1%, and 71.2%, respectively). The decision to forego DMT treatment among SMA patients is primarily influenced by the high cost of drugs (88.9%), the inconvenience of treatment (44.4%), and uncertainty regarding the timing of treatment (22.2%). Among the 22 patients who have not received any SMA medication treatment, the most cited reasons were similarly high drug prices (81.8%), inconvenient treatment regimens (50.0%), and uncertainty about when to begin treatment (22.7%).

Moreover, the study found that 81.7% of patients have not changed their treatment medication during the course of their treatment, whereas 18.3% have changed medication one or more times. The primary reasons for changing medications included lack of efficacy (41.2%), inconvenience of the treatment method (41.2%), and the pursuit of greater benefits (35.3%).

DMT medication

The age of initiation and duration of DMT also varied. The median age for starting risdiplam treatment was lower than that for nusinersen [14.50 (11.25, 18.75) vs. 16.50 (11.25, 25.75)], with 65.4% of risdiplam users having been on treatment for less than six months. In contrast, the majority of nusinersen users (74.3%) had a treatment duration of one to three years. Furthermore, the median age of initiation of nusinersen treatment was significantly younger in type 1 patients compared to type 2 and type 3 patients [10.50 (5.00, 11.00) vs. 16.00 (12.00, 24.00) vs. 20.00 (14.00, 28.00), P = 0.004].

Medication selection considerations

When selecting medications, the primary considerations among all patients included drug efficacy, safety, and cost (Fig. 3). However, 71.7% of patients reported a lack of clear understanding of the medication’s efficacy, and 51.9% were uncertain about the duration of treatment and which medication was suitable for them. Specifically, type 1 patients prioritized drug efficacy, severity of their condition, and safety; type 2 patients focused on efficacy, safety, and doctor’s recommendation; and type 3 patients considered efficacy, cost, and insurance coverage. For patients using DMT medications, the main factors were drug efficacy, insurance coverage, and safety, whereas those not using DMTs were more influenced by efficacy, doctor’s recommendation, safety, and insurance coverage.

Treatment satisfaction

Regarding the current treatment received, 51.2% of the patients were satisfied, while 48.8% expressed some dissatisfaction, which was primarily attributed to the poor efficacy of the medication, lack of significant symptom improvement, and high treatment costs that impose financial strain on their families.

For SMA treatment medications, the efficacy outcomes that patients most commonly recognized included delaying disease progression, improving disease symptoms, and enhancing quality of life (Fig. 4). Differences were observed in the recognized outcomes among patients with different types of SMA. Improvement in disease symptoms was noted primarily in terms of overall mood enhancement (46.0%), motor function improvement (23.0%), and reduction in hospitalization rates (23.0%). Notably, type 3 patients (53.3%) and patients who have used DMTs (55.6%) reported more significant improvements in overall mood. Quality of life improvements were mainly characterized by enhancements in psychological and emotional status (36.2%) and physiological function (26.1%), among other factors.

Route of administration and follow-up frequency

Notably, 70.8% of patients preferred solely oral medication therapy. This preference was consistent across patients with type 1, type 2, and type 3 SMA. Besides, the proportion of patients opting for oral treatment alone was significantly higher among those who had not used DMTs compared to those who had used DMTs (87.5% vs. 63.5%, P = 0.013).

In the past year, 50.0% of patients had more than three hospital follow-up visits, with type 3 patients exhibiting a higher follow-up rate than type 1 and type 2 patients. The proportion of patients with more than three follow-up visits was significantly greater among DMT users compared to non-users (72.1% vs. 3.1%, P < 0.001). Conversely, among patients who had not used DMT medications, 96.9% did not have any follow-up visits in the past year. The primary reasons for this lack of follow-up were high medication costs (69.4%), the inconvenience of treatment (55.6%), and perceived poor efficacy of medication (22.2%). Additionally, some patients cited the absence of an effective treatment plan or concerns about contracting cross-infections at the hospital as reasons for not attending follow-up visits.

Discussion

This single-center, cross-sectional investigation highlights several critical issues in the management of SMA in China. Despite the availability of DMTs for over five years in China, a small proportion of patients may lack understanding regarding the application of these treatments. This knowledge gap could be attributed to several factors, including the scarcity of comprehensive patient education programs, and insufficient awareness among physicians in non-specialist centers that impedes the dissemination of information about novel therapies. Moreover, those who are undergoing or have previously received DMTs often possess inadequate knowledge about these medications, including their efficacy, treatment duration, and suitability. The primary barriers to DMT utilization among aware patients include the high cost and the perceived inconvenience of treatment. This gap in understanding underscores the urgent need for healthcare providers to enhance patient and family education regarding DMT options. Furthermore, nearly half of the patients reported dissatisfaction with their current treatments, primarily due to unmet expectations regarding treatment efficacy. The presence of integrated multidisciplinary care teams, including neurologists, rehabilitation specialists, and respiratory therapists, along with comprehensive caregiver support systems, is crucial for effective management of SMA. Such coordinated long-term care, which encompasses pharmacological treatment, physical therapy, and psychosocial support, is likely to enhance patient satisfaction with treatment. These findings suggest that current therapeutic outcomes are not fully aligning with patient expectations, indicating a need for improved patient-provider communication and support systems. Addressing these educational and economic barriers is essential for optimizing treatment adherence and improving overall patient outcomes in the Chinese SMA patients.

Our findings indicate that household annual income significantly influences the decision to pursue DMTs. The primary barrier for patients not receiving DMTs is the perceived high cost of medication, which aligns with previous research demonstrating that financial constraints and medication pricing are critical determinants in treatment selection. In China, the healthcare security system is structured with basic medical insurance as its foundation, supplemented by critical illness insurance, medical assistance, and finally complemented by commercial health insurance. Similar study from Australia [20] shows that higher-cost treatments are less favored, underscoring the importance of affordability and insurance coverage in patient decision-making. Additionally, the Australian study highlighted that the general population, parents, and healthcare professionals prioritize treatments that enhance functioning and mobility, whereas higher costs, invasive delivery methods, and the risk of adverse events decrease treatment acceptance [20]. These findings collectively emphasize the need for healthcare systems to consider economic factors and patient preferences when planning and implementing SMA treatment programs. Ensuring that DMTs are both accessible and affordable is essential for improving treatment uptake and patient outcomes.

Our study provides a precise characterization of the clinical spectrum and disease characteristics of SMA by including only Genetically confirmed 5q-SMA patients, a method that enhances the reliability of our findings. Previous research often included patients without genetic confirmation [17, 21], potentially introducing biases and confounding factors that obscure the true clinical presentation and progression patterns of the disease. The genetic basis of SMA involves deletions or mutations in the SMN1 Gene, with most patients having homozygous deletions of exons 7 and 8, or just exon 7, leading to reduced levels of the SMN protein which is critical for motor neuron health [22]. The presence of the SMN2 gene, which produces a small amount of functional SMN protein, modulates the severity of the disease, making SMN2 copy number an important determinant of clinical outcomes [23]. In our study, detailed genetic data allowed us to accurately assess the disease profiles of different SMA subtypes. This approach not only provides a clearer understanding of disease progression but also supports the development of more targeted therapeutic strategies. Our findings align with international research emphasizing the importance of genetic confirmation in SMA studies to ensure accurate representation of disease characteristics and progression [2]​​. This study underscores the necessity for rigorous genetic diagnosis and comprehensive genetic profiling in SMA studies to inform clinical practice and improve patient outcomes.

Previous studies on SMA disease characteristics have primarily focused on pediatric populations, with Limited attention to adult patients and family decisions regarding the willingness to have another child. A large retrospective study from China examined 392 treatment-naive pediatric SMA patients, revealing detailed clinical presentations, genetic defects, motor function assessments, and follow-ups. This study found that higher SMN2 copy numbers were associated with better survival and preserved motor function​​ [24]. Another study on 237 infants with SMA in China highlighted the survival probabilities and onset ages of different SMA types, providing essential baseline data for understanding disease progression without proactive interventions [25]​​. Our study addresses these gaps by comprehensively analyzing the disease status, motor function, and medication treatment choices of SMA patients across different age groups, while also considering family decisions regarding repeat pregnancies and prenatal diagnosis. Our findings reveal that many families opt for prenatal diagnosis in subsequent pregnancies, demonstrating a proactive approach to managing SMA risk [26]. Additionally, we observed variations in disease progression and treatment responses among adult patients, emphasizing the need for age-specific management strategies. Notably, our study found that female SMA patients exhibited a higher risk of severe spinal deformities and worse motor function compared to males. This disparity may stem from hormonal influences on muscle and bone metabolism. Further research is needed to elucidate the relative contributions of these factors to sex-based differences in SMA progression. By including a broad spectrum of SMA patients and integrating genetic confirmation, our study provides a nuanced understanding of the disease, aiding in the development of tailored therapeutic interventions and informed family planning​​.

Previous studies have highlighted that the convenience of treatment administration significantly influences medication choices among SMA patients. A previous study emphasized that patients and families prefer treatments that are easier to administer, with oral options being favored over more invasive methods such as intrathecal injections [27]. This study corroborates these findings in China, revealing a preference for oral medication treatments among SMA patients. In addition, our study found that overall satisfaction was better among SMA patients currently receiving treatment, consistent with findings from similar research [28, 29]. A prospective observational study in Germany demonstrated that adult SMA patients reported high overall satisfaction with nusinersen but expressed lower satisfaction with its administration convenience [28]. Additionally, a case series evaluating the switch from nusinersen to risdiplam showed that patients preferred the oral administration of risdiplam, citing it as a major factor for their increased satisfaction [30]. These findings suggest that treatment convenience plays a critical role in influencing patient satisfaction. The clinical application rate of oral risdiplam remains lower than that of intrathecal nusinersen, likely due to risdiplam’s recent market introduction. However, with risdiplam’s inclusion in the National Medical Insurance Catalog in 2022, financial barriers are being reduced, potentially enhancing its clinical accessibility and adoption.

There have several limitations that must be acknowledged in this study. First, the results of the survey do not fully align SMA subtypes with developmental milestones, indicating that patients with chronic diseases such as SMA may exhibit information bias when recalling their medical histories. The fact that some of the patients were no longer contactable or had passed away, coupled with the heavier burden of the disease on the patients’ families, resulted in many patients refusing to participate in the survey. Additionally, the high attrition rate (61 patients discontinued interviews) reflects inherent challenges in rare disease research, including disease severity and low response rates in real-world settings. Furthermore, patient-reported outcomes in this study may not fully align with objective clinical assessments (e.g., HFMSE, RULM scores), highlighting the need for future studies incorporating standardized functional evaluations. Specifically, future research should aim to integrate both patient-reported outcomes and objective clinical measures to better evaluate overall disease status and functional improvements in SMA patients. Moreover, due to considerations of accuracy and ethical constraints, we did not collect data on the exact age of onset or the date of death. This omission prevents us from assessing the relationship between the age of disease onset and survival rates comprehensively. Another limitation is the single-center design of our study, which may affect the generalizability of the findings. Future multi-center studies with more comprehensive data collection are essential to validate and expand upon our findings.

Conclusion

Our study indicates that patients with different subtypes of SMA are currently experiencing various complications while maintaining good motor function. Despite these positive findings, there remains a significant need for widespread patient education to enhance awareness and understanding of DMTs. Future real-world studies should focus on collecting comprehensive outcome data, including motor function scales and patient-reported outcomes, to refine patient classification and management.

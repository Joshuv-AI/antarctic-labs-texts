Increased Risk of Complications in Total Hip Arthroplasty (THA) of Femoral Neck Fracture (FNF): A Comparative Meta-Analysis of THA Outcomes in FNF and Osteoarthritis
Authors: Ralph Maroun, Mohammad Daher, Jonathan Liu, Patrick J Kelly, Alan H Daniels, Mouhanad M El-Othmani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractDespite contradictory results of various published data on the subject, the complications of total hip arthroplasty (THA) in femoral neck fracture (FNF) compared to those in osteoarthritis (OA) are yet to be further elucidated. We queried PubMed, Cochrane, and Google Scholar from inception until October 2024 for studies that compared the surgical outcomes of THA in the management of FNF and OA. We evaluated the overall complications, such as dislocations, prosthetic joint infection (PJI), intraoperative fractures, periprosthetic fractures (PPFx), mechanical loosening, venous thromboembolism (VTE), hematoma, leg length discrepancy (LLD), and revisions. In addition, surgical parameters such as the duration of surgery and the length of stay (LOS) were also assessed. Eleven studies were included in our meta-analysis. The use of THA in FNF is associated with high risks of overall complications (odds ratio [OR] 1.58, 95% confidence interval [CI] 1.00-2.49, P=0.05), dislocations (OR 2.12, 95% CI 1.07-4.21, P=0.03), PJI (OR 1.75, 95% CI 1.50-2.05, P<0.00001), PPFx (OR 1.62, 95% CI 1.18-2.22, P=0.003), and intraoperative fractures (OR 3.82, 95% CI 1.59-9.19, P=0.003) in comparison to those in the OA group treated with THA. FNF patients had a long LOS (mean difference=3.34, 95% CI 0.69-5.99, P=0.01). There was no statistically significant difference observed in the risk of VTE, hematoma, LLD, mechanical loosening, the number of revisions, and the duration of surgery between the FNF and OA groups, which were treated with THA. There is an increased risk of complications in patients undergoing THA for FNF than in patients undergoing THA for OA.Keywords: Total hip arthroplasty, Osteoarthritis, Femoral neck fractures

INTRODUCTION

The number of total hip arthroplasty (THA) procedures has been increasing for decades due to its remarkable success rates and positive outcomes1,2). Several studies have projected that, by 2030, nearly 600,000 THA procedures will be done annually in the US3-5). Although THA has been primarily indicated for end-stage osteoarthritis (OA), it has become a preferred option for various other hip conditions, such as femoral neck fractures (FNFs)6-8). With the growing incidence of FNFs due to aging of society and urbanization, a recent trend that favors THA in the treatment of FNF in the elderly has sparked an ongoing debate9,10).

By not permitting proper pre-surgical optimization in FNF patients who require urgent surgical care, it is hypothesized that THA for FNF in such cases would be associated with a high complication rate and poor surgical outcomes when compared to the use of THA in elective cases7). Although several studies have shown increased number of perioperative complications in the FNF group undergoing THA11,12), some studies have shown no significant difference in these complications of THA between FNF and OA patients13,14). There have also been reports of conflicting results in some studies regarding duration of surgery and length of stay (LOS) associated with THA in FNF and OA patients12,14,15).

It is imperative to perform a comparative analysis of the outcomes of THA in FNF and in OA patients to better inform patients and to improve patient care by adopting the appropriate perioperative measures. Therefore, the aim of this meta-analysis is to compare THA outcomes in patients undergoing elective procedures for hip OA or under urgent conditions for FNF.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

1. Search Strategy

We followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines to search PubMed, Cochrane, and Google Scholar from inception through October 2024 for articles that compared the outcomes of THA in patients undergoing surgery for hip OA or FNF16). Keywords and Boolean terms, such as “hip replacement”, “hip arthroplasty”, “fracture”, “osteoarthritis”, and “elective” were used. Supplementary articles were added by searching through the reference lists of the identified articles. One author extracted the data, and another confirmed the choice of the included articles. The PRISMA flowchart summarizes this process (Fig. 1).

PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-Analyses) flowchart describes the selection process of the journal articles for the study.

The inclusion criteria consisted of published articles in English comparing THA complications and surgery-related parameters between OA and FNF patients. Studies based on registry data from countries other than the US were also included. The exclusion criteria consisted of non-comparative studies, studies based on national databases that might lead to patient overlap with data from medical centers of the concerned country or studies with non-relevant outcomes.

2. Data Extraction

Two reviewers independently determined the eligibility of the included studies. Extracted data included overall complications, specific complications (leg length discrepancy [LLD], dislocation, mechanical loosening, periprosthetic fracture [PPFx], intraoperative fractures, venous thromboembolism [VTE], prosthetic joint infection [PJI], and hematoma), revisions, LOS, and the duration of surgery. Microsoft Excel 2024 (Microsoft) was used to extract the data.

4. Statistical Analysis

Statistical analysis of the data in our study was performed with Review Manager 5.4 (The Cochrane Collaboration, 2020). Mean differences (MD) with 95% confidence intervals (CI) were used for continuous data, while odds ratios (OR) were calculated for dichotomous data. Using a random-effects model in the case of considerable heterogeneity (defined by P≤0.05 or I2>50%) and a fixed-effect model (if P>0.05 or I2<50%), heterogeneity was evaluated by Q tests and I2 statistics. A sub-analysis of the outcomes was performed and compared between registry and non-registry-based studies. A statistically significant result was denoted as P≤0.05.

Eleven retrospective studies, including two registry-based studies, met the inclusion criteria for our comparative meta-analysis of THA in FNF and OA7,11-15,18-22). The studies included 112,050 patients that were subclassified into 88,128 of the OA group (78.7%) with a mean age of 73.5 years and 23,922 of the FNF group (21.3%) with a mean age of 74.5 years. 2. Adverse Events

Seven non-registry and two registry-based studies comprising 104,445 patients reported overall complication data (23,295 in the FNF group and 81,160 in the OA group). A high rate of complications was seen in the FNF group (OR 1.58, 95% CI 1.00-2.49, P=0.05) (Fig. 2A). However, the two registry-based studies did not differ in complications (OR 1.09, 95% CI 0.30-3.96, P=0.90) (Fig. 2A).

With respect to specific complications, high rates of dislocations (OR 2.12, 95% CI 1.07-4.21, P=0.03) (Fig. 2B), PPFx (OR 1.62, 95% CI 1.18-2.22, P=0.003) (Fig. 2C), intraoperative fractures (OR 3.82, 95% CI 1.59-9.19, P=0.003) (Fig. 2D), and PJI (OR 1.75, 95% CI 1.50-2.05, P<0.00001) (Fig. 2E) were observed in the FNF group undergoing THA. There was no difference based on the use of THA between the FNF and OA in VTE (Fig. 2F), hematoma formation (Fig. 2G), LLD (Fig. 2H), and mechanical loosening (Fig. 2I). Most results of the sub-analyses did not differ between the registry- and non-registry-based studies. However, the risk of overall complications was significant only in non-registry based studies (OR 1.84, 95% CI 1.06-3.19, P=0.03) (Fig. 2A), and the risk of PJI was significant only in registry-based studies (OR 1.75, 95% CI 1.50-2.05, P<0.00001) (Fig. 2E). Furthermore, in registry-based studies, mechanical loosening was found to be significantly low in FNF patients undergoing THA (OR 0.28, 95% CI 0.13-0.58, P=0.0008) (Fig. 2I).

3. Revisions

Four non-registry and two registry-based studies comprising 110,288 patients reported data on the rates of revision (23,256 in the FNF group and 87,032 in the OA group). No difference was observed in the risk of revisions between the two groups (OR 1.34, 95% CI 0.78-2.31, P=0.30) (Fig. 3) even with sub-analyses specific to the type of study. 4. Surgery-Related Outcomes

Two non-registry–based studies comprising 1,082 patients undergoing THA reported data for LOS (447 in the FNF group and 635 in the OA group). Three non-registry–based studies comprising 1,203 patients reported data on the duration of surgery (487 in the FNF group and 716 in the OA group). The FNF group treated with THA had a longer LOS than that of the OA group treated with THA (MD=3.34, 95% CI 0.69-5.99, P=0.01) (Fig. 4A). There was no difference in the duration of surgery (MD=–0.56, 95% CI –8.99 to 7.87, P=0.90) (Fig. 4B) between the two groups treated with THA.

DISCUSSION

Currently, FNF and OA are the most common indications for THA procedures23). There is growing use of THA to manage FNF cases in the US24). Although several studies compared the complications in outcomes between patients undergoing THA for OA and FNF, some studies showed no difference and others showed high rate of complications in the FNF cohort12,14,15). The present meta-analysis showed a high rate of overall complications, specifically the rates of dislocations, PPFx, intraoperative fractures, and PJI along with a long LOS in the FNF population. Regarding discrepancies in the sub-analyses, the difference in the risk of overall complications was not significant in registry-based studies. This could be attributed to the heterogeneity of the patient population in registries and, therefore, their overall health status that could minimize variations in the rates of complications. Data collection in registries is mostly based on standardized collection protocols, which improves the documentation of overall complications and diminishes possible rate variations.

The FNF cohort recorded a high rate of overall complications. Generally, pre-surgical planning and optimization for elective THA, unlike that for an acute indication, can take weeks to months7). The long optimization stage permits the modulation of risk factors and proper anticipation of common complications that might explain the findings in our meta-analysis25). However, no difference was seen in the number of revisions between the two groups. The FNF population is usually considered frailer, which would explain the presence of more comorbidities. Hence, surgeons are more reluctant to perform revision surgeries, if they can be avoided26). Further, Iamthanaporn et al.27) showed that mechanical loosening was the major cause of early and late revision, a complication with fairly similar rates between our FNF and OA groups (Table 4).

The risk of dislocations, intraoperative fractures, and PPFx was high in the FNF group undergoing THA. Patients with FNF tend to have intact, or injured and loose joint stabilizing soft tissues in contrast to the potential low risk of dislocation due to soft tissue stiffness associated with OA14). Furthermore, patients with FNF are more likely to experience movement-affecting comorbidities, such as cognitive dysfunction and neuromuscular disorders12,14). Therefore, a cognitive decline could hinder the ability to avoid positions and activities that predispose the hip to dislocation11). These same comorbidities predispose patients with FNF to an increased tendency to fall, which explains the high risk of both dislocations and PPFx21,28). Patients with FNF are also more likely to experience osteoporosis compared to their counterparts without FNF29-32). This might explain the increased risk of both periprosthetic and intraoperative fractures. While the recent focus on cementation in this patient population has reportedly led to low rates of intra- and postoperative fractures29-32), it was challenging to identify the applicability of these recommendations within the included studies in our meta-analysis.

Patients with FNF tend to have more comorbidities, which explains the longer LOS needed in the delicate care of high-risk patients7,29,33). Our analysis has also shown that the group with FNF undergoing THA has a high risk of PJI. Limited planning in non-elective THA could hinder proper preoperative decolonization and skin preparation, which are necessary to reduce infection in high-risk groups undergoing THA7,34,35). A meta-analysis of 40 studies concluded that FNF is a risk factor for PJI in THA, while OA is a protective factor36). Although infection is a function of multiple variables, a suboptimal host would constitute one of the determining factors of infection in the population with FNF5). Moreover, the high risk of PJI was only significant in registry-based studies according to our subanalysis. This discrepancy between registry and non-registry subgroups may be explained by the differences in sample size and data quality. Registry-based studies typically analyze large populations that provide more statistical power to detect differences in rare outcomes such as PJI. Furthermore, registries often use specific validated criteria to diagnose PJI, while non-registry studies may use different diagnostic thresholds.

The present study has several limitations. As a systematic review, the results depend on the variables examined by the studies that meet inclusion criteria. Our study is subject to inherent biases because all the included studies in this meta-analysis are retrospective. The results of our meta-analysis are pooled since granular data were not available, which precludes performing subgroup analysis based on demographics, the type of approach, and the use of cement. Our analysis failed to fully control for confounding factors. This is relevant because the risk of complications can largely depend on patient factors, such as comorbidity or frailty5). Therefore, there is a possibility of bias in the selection of articles because, in actual practice, it is known that surgeons would prefer hemiarthroplasty in patients with significant comorbidities. This viewpoint was not considered in our meta-analysis that uniquely studied THA. There was concordance in demographics between the two groups in half the included studies in our analysis. Furthermore, the subjects of the two cohorts in our analysis differed by just one year in age. Finally, our study was potentially underpowered to detect small differences as only few studies were included in some of the analyzed outcomes.

Despite these limitations, our study provides a large cohort of patients across different settings, which permits generalization of the findings and guides perioperative discussions and expectations. Although value-based care delivery models continue to focus on the elective episode of care, the conclusions from our study are pivotal to guide future decisions and plans.

CONCLUSION

The present meta-analysis found that in spite of a lack of difference in the risk of revision, patients who underwent THA for FNF had longer LOS and a high risk of overall complications, dislocations, PPFx, intraoperative fractures, and PJI than those undergoing elective THA for OA. Our study shows that despite the proven efficacy of THA in FNF, it still poses higher risks than THA for OA with respect to adverse events. Nevertheless, our analysis could help surgeons offer advice and inform patients prior to surgery as well as establish proper perioperative care to minimize the increased risk of complications.

COVID-19 screening as an opportunity to find TB cases; experiences from an urban National Referral Hospital in Kampala, Uganda
Authors: Fred C. Semitala, Rodgers Katwesigye, Dennis Kalibbala, Mary Mbuliro, Lalitha Rejani, Darius Owachi, Edgar Atine, Stavia Turyahabwe, Moorine P. Sekadde
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundFollowing the World Health Organization’s declaration of COVID-19 as a global pandemic, several countries implemented population-wide lockdowns. However, these responses to COVID-19 caused severe healthcare system disruptions to service delivery. The TB case notification rate in Uganda decreased by 22% between January and April 2020, which coincided with a lockdown and an increase in COVID-19 cases. In this study, we tested the effect of screening all patients with both COVID-19-positive and negative symptom screen for TB at a National Referral Hospital.Design/MethodsFollowing our formative assessment, we identified potential barriers to and facilitators of integrating screening for COVID-19 and TB at Kiruddu National Referral Hospital. To address the barriers, in February 2021 we trained healthcare providers on integrated COVID-19-TB screening tools and provided COVID-19/TB screening tools/Standard operating procedures and personal protective equipment. From March 1, 2021, to June 30, 2021, we screened patients presenting to the emergency and outpatient departments for COVID-19 symptoms, and subsequently, we performed TB symptom screening for both patients with COVID-19 positive and negative symptom screen using the intensified tuberculosis case-finding (ICF) guide. We then compared the outcomes of TB symptom screening for patients initially with a positive COVID-19 symptom screen with those who initially had a negative COVID-19 symptom screen.ResultsFrom March 2021 to June 2021, we screened 1464 patients (44.3% male and 55.7% female) for COVID-19 symptoms. Out of these participants, 1252 (85.5%) screened positive for COVID-19 symptoms, while 212 (14.5%) screened negative. The majority of patients with a positive COVID-19 symptom screen, 717 (57.3%), also screened positive for TB symptoms compared to 19 (8.9%) among patients with a negative COVID-19 symptom screen. Out of the total 736 presumptive TB cases identified, 717 (97.4%) initially screened positive for COVID-19 symptoms. TB was diagnosed in 110 individuals including 104 who had positive COVID-19-symptom screen and six who had a negative COVID-19 symptom screen. All of the 110 newly diagnosed TB cases were linked to TB treatment.ConclusionsPatients who screen positive for COVID-19 symptoms should be routinely screened for TB to mitigate missed opportunities for TB case identification.

Background

The coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) outbreak led to an unprecedented global health emergency that resulted in millions of deaths around the world and in March 2020, the World Health Organization (WHO) declared COVID-19 a global pandemic [1].

In a bid to slow down the spread of COVID-19, several countries implemented population-wide lockdowns [2, 3]. However, these responses to COVID-19 caused severe healthcare systems disruptions to service delivery for other diseases, including tuberculosis (TB) [4–7].

The World Health Organization’s 2022 Global TB report reveals that in 2021, 10.6 million people contracted tuberculosis, a 4.5% increase from the previous year, with 1.6 million deaths, including 187,000 among HIV-positive people. The increase in TB cases included a 3% increase in drug-resistant disease with 450,000 new cases of rifampicin-resistant TB reported [7]. The same report notes that it is the first time in many years an increase has been reported in the number of people falling ill with TB including drug-resistant TB [7].

In Uganda, following the outbreak of COVID-19 in March 2020, there was a 40% drop in the number of persons screened for TB by June 2020, based on the unpublished Ministry of Health (MOH) programmatic data from the District Health Information System version 2 (DHIS2).

Additionally, the TB case notification rate fell from 161/100,000 in the January-March quarter of 2020 to 133/100,000 in the April- June quarter of 2020, a reduction of 22%. This coincided with an increase in COVID-19 cases and the initial national lockdown. The decline in TB case notification was highest in Kampala City, Uganda’s capital city, from 426 to 265 cases per 100,000 people [8].

In response to the COVID-19-related disruptions to TB services, the National TB and Leprosy Program in Uganda developed a TB management plan to be implemented by all health facilities. The plan included a COVID-19/TB screening algorithm (Fig. 1) to combine symptom screening for both diseases. However, this algorithm at the time did not provide for mandatory TB symptom screening for patients who presented with COVID-19 symptoms.

The symptom screening tools in this study were based on the most common symptoms of COVID-19 and TB. For COVID-19 the common symptoms are cough, loss of smell, chest pain, and nasal congestion as well as other extrapulmonary symptoms such as fever, joint pains, and headache [9]. For TB symptom screening in the general population, we utilized any of the four symptoms(that is the presence of cough of at least two weeks, evening fever, night sweats, and /or weight loss) of the WHO-recommended tuberculosis intensified case-finding (ICF) guide [10].

In this study, we determined the effect of routine screening for TB on all patients who had been screened for COVID-19 irrespective of the result of their COVID-19 symptom screen at a National Referral Hospital in Kampala, Uganda. This was a follow on study to our formative qualitative work where we explored the perspectives of healthcare providers on integrated screening for COVID-19 and TB at the same hospital [11]. Through the formative study, we identified the potential facilitators and barriers to integrated symptom screening for both COVID-19 and TB. Prior to this study, we addressed the identified barriers to integrating symptom screening of COVID-19 and TB at Kiruddu National Referral Hospital, based on the Capability, Opportunity, Motivation, Behavior (COM-B) model. Briefly, we conducted training sessions on integrated screening of COVID-19 and TB to equip the health care providers (HCP) with the necessary skills, we provided a consistent supply of personal protective equipment (PPE) to mitigate HCP fears of contracting COVID-19 infection and provided simplified standard operating procedures (SOPs) and data collection tools.

Methods

Study design and setting

We conducted this cross-sectional study between March 1, 2021, and June 30, 2021, with HCPs (Doctors, Nurses, and TB Linkage facilitators) involved in TB service delivery (TB screening and treatment) at Kiruddu National Referral Hospital (KNRH) in Kampala, Uganda. KNRH is a 200-bed capacity hospital in Makindye Division, one of the five administrative units of Kampala, the capital city of Uganda. The hospital specializes in internal medicine, burns, and plastic surgery and provides up to 500 general medical outpatient consultations daily.

The study received ethical approvals from the AIDS Support Organization (TASO) Research and Ethics Committee (TASO REC 082/2020-UG-REC-009) and the Uganda National Council of Science and Technology (HS1152ES). We performed all the methods following the relevant guidelines and regulations.

Our implementation strategy was informed by the barriers and facilitators previously identified in our qualitative study [11]. The barriers included: inadequate knowledge to integrate screening of TB and COVID-19, lack of SOPs, inconsistent supply of PPE, and the fear of contracting COVID-19 infection. We utilized the Behavioral Change Wheel (BCW) framework to develop our implementation strategy, focusing on the BCW intervention functions of education and training, enablement, and persuasion. For education and training, we trained healthcare providers (HCPs) involved in TB service delivery at outpatient and emergency departments on integrating symptom screening for COVID-19 and TB.

For enablement, we printed and distributed job aids to facilitate the integration of symptom screening for COVID-19 and TB at all screening points, ensuring the availability of standard operating procedures. For the persuasion intervention function, we enhanced infection prevention practices by procuring and distributing personal protective equipment (PPE) such as face masks, gloves, and face shields. This buffer stock ensured a consistent supply of PPE, addressing healthcare providers’ concerns about contracting COVID-19.

Study patients and sampling

We provided integrated screening services for COVID-19 and TB to all patients aged 18 years and above who presented at the outpatient and emergency departments at KNRH during the study period. The patients were consecutively selected in order of appearance according to their convenient accessibility to the study team. The sampling process came to an end when the time limit (time saturation) for the study was reached.

Study instruments and data collection

We developed a modified symptom screening tool that was aligned with the MoH-integrated COVID-19-TB screening algorithm (Appendix 1). As per the screening tool, all patients who presented at the screening points were initially screened for symptoms of COVID-19. In this modified screening tool, all patients were screened for TB using the ICF guide irrespective of the result of their COVID-19 symptom screen. For COVID-19 diagnosis the patients were tested for COVID-19 using polymerase chain reaction (PCR) tests. For TB diagnosis we performed Xpert MTB/RIF Ultra for all patients that were able to produce sputum; lateral flow urine Lipoarabinomannan (LAM) tests for patients who were HIV positive and with CD4 less than 200 cells/mm3; chest radiography (CXR) for patients who were not able to produce sputum (for HIV-negative, and HIV positive patients with CD4 cell counts > 200 cells/mm3). TB was also diagnosed in patients with clinical signs and symptoms suggestive of TB in the presence of a negative Xpert MTB result. We used Epi info version 4.6.0.2 to capture quantitative data collected from the study-screening tool. We routinely reviewed data weekly for completeness, accuracy, and consistency.

Data analysis

We summarized the descriptive statistics using Pivot tables in Microsoft Excel. We described demographic characteristics, using means and standard deviations for continuous variables, and frequencies and percentages for categorical variables. We summarized data for COVID-19 and TB symptoms and diagnoses in tables as frequencies and proportions.

Results

Participant characteristics

Between March 1, 2021, and June 30, 2021, 13,266 patients attended the emergency and outpatient departments (as recorded in the hospital registers). Of these, 1464 (11%) patients were screened for symptoms of both COVID-19 and TB using the integrated COVID-19-TB screening tool.

Out of the 1,464 patients who underwent integrated screening for both COVID-19 and TB, 302 (20.6%) patients had a documented HIV-positive status, 850 (58.1%) had a documented HIV-negative status, and 312 (21.3%) patients had an undisclosed or unknown HIV status as shown in Table 1. HIV status was self-reported and based on historical clinical data.

Integrated COVID-19 /TB screening

As summarized in Table 3, a total of 1464 patients were screened for symptoms of both COVID-19 and TB, 717 (49.0%) of the patients screened positive for symptoms of both COVID-19 and TB, (516) 35.2% screened positive for COVID-19 symptoms only, and 212 (14.5%) had no symptoms for either COVID-19 or TB, while 19 (1.3%) screened positive for TB but not for COVID-19.

Of the 717 patients who had a positive symptom screen both for COVID-19 and TB, 104 (15%) were newly confirmed to have TB, and nine of these were already receiving treatment; 20 (2.8%) were diagnosed with COVID-19.

Of the 516 patients who only screened positive for COVID-19 symptoms, 43 (8.3%) were diagnosed with COVID-19 and only one (0.2%) had TB and was already on TB treatment.

Of the 19 patients who only screened positive for TB, one was already on TB treatment, six (31.5%) were newly diagnosed as having TB, none were diagnosed with COVID-19, while the remaining 12 (63.2%) had neither COVID-19 nor TB.

Discussion

In this study, we determined the effect of routinely screening all patients with both COVID-19 positive and negative symptom screen for TB at a national referral hospital in Kampala, Uganda.

The COVID-19-TB symptom-screening algorithm (Fig. 1) that the Uganda Ministry of Health introduced to guide the integration of screening for COVID-19 and TB created missed opportunities for TB case finding. From this study 104 (94.5%) out of the 110 newly diagnosed TB cases were initially suspected to have COVID-19 and would not necessarily be required to undergo TB screening based on the algorithm in place at the time. We demonstrated that proactive screening of all patients suspected to have COVID-19 for TB using the ICF guide increases the likelihood of identifying new TB cases. This could be attributed to the similarity of the most common symptoms of COVID-19 and TB although healthcare providers were more likely to suspect COVID-19 than TB during the pandemic period [12].

Building on our previous work on the healthcare provider perspectives on the integration of COVID-19 and TB screening in the same setting [11], we were able to design well-contextualized, evidence-based stakeholder-informed strategies to overcome the identified barriers and leverage the facilitators to promote integrated screening of COVID-19 and TB.

In this study, we utilized implementation science concepts of stakeholder engagement. Some of the key research team members were from the National Tuberculosis and Leprosy Program at the Uganda Ministry of Health. This facilitated the dissemination and uptake of the study findings to inform and guide policy [13]. Following the findings of this study, the Uganda Ministry of Health revised the initial algorithm (Fig. 1) to include mandatory TB symptom screening for all patients suspected to have COVID-19 (Fig. 2).

As COVID-19 becomes a more endemic disease in many settings, the findings from this study demonstrate that there is a need for simultaneous, integrated screening and testing of COVID-19 and Tuberculosis in high-burden countries as a means to increase the diagnosis of both diseases [14, 15] and to reduce missed opportunities for TB diagnosis. This study shows that although the COVID-19 pandemic could have eroded some of the gains in TB care, it also provided an opportunity to implement innovative approaches to the integrated screening of COVID-19 and TB to improve access to screening and testing for TB [16, 17].

The strength of our study is that we utilized implementation science approaches including; stakeholder engagement [18], including policymakers in the formative assessment to understand the contextual factors that may affect their willingness to integrate screening of COVID-19 and TB. The findings of the formative assessment informed the evidence-based design of the implementation strategy for this study.

The limitation of our study is that we only conducted this in a single urban hospital and only provided integrated COVID-19 and TB screening services to a small proportion of patients who attended the outpatient department during the study period. We however believe that this population was representative of the general population attending similar hospitals at that time.

Conclusions

Using the Uganda Ministry of Health COVID-19-TB screening algorithm in its original form missed the majority of the newly diagnosed TB cases during the study period. All patients who screen positive for COVID-19 symptoms should be screened and tested for TB, particularly in high-burden TB countries.

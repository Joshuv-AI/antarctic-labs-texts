World Dementia Council Update
Published: 2026

INTRODUCTION

Representatives from the World Dementia Council (WDC) recently attended the 2026 International Conference on Alzheimer's and Parkinson's Diseases (AD/PD), a global meeting bringing together researchers, clinicians, industry, and advocates across neurodegenerative diseases. Held in Copenhagen from March 17 to 21, 2026, the conference program highlighted new developments across treatments, diagnostics, and prevention. Throughout the week, WDC members and trustees contributed to a variety of sessions, presenting new data and study findings, reflecting the breadth of work underway across the dementia field. Several themes emerged from the conference, including advances in prevention, biomarkers and diagnostics, treatment development, and the ever‐growing need to widely promote and celebrate the progress that has been made in this space.

PROGRESS ACROSS PREVENTION, DIAGNOSIS, AND TREATMENT

One of the key areas of focus throughout the conference was prevention. Sessions such as “Prevention and Resilience in Dementia and Aging,” chaired by WDC trustee and member, Dr. Miia Kivipelto, Professor of Clinical Geriatrics at Karolinska Institutet, highlighted new findings from the World‐Wide FINGERS study on the potential of tailored, multidomain interventions to reduce the risk of cognitive decline. WDC trustee and Head of the Dementia Fund at EQT Life Sciences, Dr. Philip Scheltens, explored the role of primary prevention in AD and the importance of addressing dementia risk factors.

Advancements in early detection and diagnosis were also central topics of the conference. From the new developments in blood‐based biomarkers to their real‐world implementation, presenters discussed the scientific readiness of these tools and the practical challenges that exist for widespread adoption. In the symposium “Blood Biomarkers: A Completed Chapter or Work in Progress? Practical Challenges and Solutions for Real‑World Implementation,” WDC member and symposium chair, Dr. Phyllis Ferrell, further elaborated on these points, speaking on the importance of bridging the gap between innovation and clinical adoption.

Discussions also covered treatment and therapeutic development. New data presented from the EVOKE and EVOKE+ trials on semaglutide use in early AD showed a lack of clinical efficacy for improving cognition among patients; however, broader discussions on drug discovery highlighted achievements in advancing potential treatments. The fireside debate “Industry Meets Academia: Moonshots and Milestones” featuring WDC member and managing director at Gates Ventures, Niranjan Bose, debated the future of neurodegenerative drug discovery across a range of sectors from startups to big pharma.

Beyond individual sessions, the conference indicated steady progress across several areas, including neuropathology, diagnostic tools, and the use of artificial intelligence in drug discovery. This progress is typically incremental, requiring sustained coordination across research, clinical, and policy communities. A recurring issue concerned the gap between scientific developments, public understanding, and implementation in practice. Participants identified the need for clearer communication of research findings and their implications to the broader public.

LOOKING AHEAD

AD/PD highlighted both the ongoing progress and remaining challenges in dementia research. Developments in prevention, diagnostics, and treatment are supported by international collaboration and shared learnings. With these new advancements, translation of the science into clinical practice and public understanding remains a key priority. The WDC continues to support collaboration, contribute to policy discussions, and help translate the emerging science into practice.

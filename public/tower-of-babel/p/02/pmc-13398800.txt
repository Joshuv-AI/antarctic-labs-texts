CRISPR/Cas9‐Mediated Editing of Four Ca2+‐Binding Receptor‐Like Cytoplasmic Kinases Improves Soybean Seed Size and Yield at Dense Planting
Authors: Wanhong Li, Jun Jin, Tianyue Gu, Xiaoxi Cai, Bowei Jia, Yan Wang, Dezhi Han, Xiaofei Yan, Xiping Hu, Xiaoli Sun, Sijia Lu, Mingzhe Sun
Published: 2026

Soybean serves as a crucial provider of both edible oil and vegetable protein for humans. Nevertheless, the grain yield of soybean at the population level lags significantly behind that of other staple crops (Tian et al. 2025). High‐density planting is critical for improving soybean yield (Li, Sun, et al. 2023), however, few genes have been functionally characterised to confer soybean yield under dense planting.

Receptor like protein kinase functions in controlling diverse biological processes in plants, including reproduction (Cui et al. 2022), stress response and immunity (Hailemariam et al. 2024). Previously, four Ca2+‐binding receptor like cytoplasmic kinases from soybean GmCRCK1a‐1d were reported to be involved in salt‐alkaline tolerance (Sun et al. 2021). Interestingly, they show much lower expression levels in developing seeds compared with other vegetative organs (Figure S1), indicating possible roles in seed development. The smaller seeds of GmCRCK1d overexpression (GmCRCK1d‐OX) Arabidopsis lines (Figure S2) supported the repression of GmCRCK1d on seed development. To confirm it, we generated GmCRCK1d‐OX soybean lines in the Dongnong50 (DN50) background (Figure S3). GmCRCK1d‐OX lines showed a significant decrease in 100‐seed weight, seed length, width and thickness (Figure S4). Given the high sequence similarity in both CDS and amino acid sequence among GmCRCK1a‐1d (Figure S5), we designed four gRNAs to generate GmCRCK1a‐1d mutants simultaneously via CRISPR/Cas9‐mediated editing (Figure 1a, Figure S6a,b). Finally, eight Cas9‐free homozygous lines, including four tetra mutants (represented as abcd) and four triple mutants (abd or abc), were identified in T2 generation (Figure 1a, Figures S6c, S7, S8). Interestingly, independent lines have identical deletion sizes, possibly due to the high homology among GmCRCK1a‐1d. These mutant lines had larger seeds compared with wild‐type DN50, as evidenced by a significant increase in seed length, width, thickness and 100‐seed weight (Figure 1b).

To elucidate the regulation of GmCRCK1a‐1d on soybean seed development, we performed a time‐course analysis. The results showed that seeds of GmCRCK1a‐1d mutant lines displayed slower development and smaller size at 25 DAF (days after flowering) and later (Figure S9). Then we performed phosphoproteomic analysis with the abcd #4 mutant line, to elucidate the GmCRCK1a‐1d‐mediated molecular mechanism in controlling seed development. We detected 7665 phosphopeptides corresponding to 12 453 phosphorylation sites on 4148 proteins, with 1163 differentially phosphorylated proteins that were significantly enriched in 17 KEGG pathways related to nitrogen, carbon and fatty acid metabolism (Figure S10). Notably, eight phosphoproteins are related to the ER‐associated degradation process (Figure S11a,b), which has been reported to control seed development and biosynthesis of storage protein and starch in seeds (Li, Zhang, et al. 2023; Zhou et al. 2022). Among them, decreased phosphorylation levels of two HSPs (Heat shock proteins) were verified via in vivo phosphorylation assays (Figure S11c,d). The average 100‐seed weight of the EMS mutant lines of HSP90 is smaller than the W82 background control (Table S1). We further detected the seed composition (Figure 1c), and showed that the percentages of seven amino acids (Cys, Trp, Arg, Asp, Gly, Val and Glu) and four fatty acids (C16:0, C18:1, C18:2 and C18:3) were altered in mutant lines. The total amino acid and protein contents of GmCRCK1a‐1d mutants are higher than DN50 (Figure S12a); except a decrease in total fatty acid and C18:2 contents in the abcd #4 mutant, no significant changes in oil content were detected (Figure S12b).

Planting density is critical for soybean yield (Li, Sun, et al. 2023). We then measured the yield traits of DN50 and GmCRCK1a‐1d mutants at different planting densities in Daqing (2024, T3 generation) and Heihe (2025, T4 generation). As the planting densities increased, the pod number and seed number per plant decreased; no significant differences were observed between DN50 and GmCRCK1a‐1d mutants regardless of planting densities (Figures S13–S15). Under different planting densities, GmCRCK1a‐1d mutants had higher 100‐seed weight than DN50, leading to an increase in grain yield per plant (Figure 1d,e, Figure S15c,d). Compared with DN50, plot yields of GmCRCK1a‐1d mutants at different densities were increased by 1.12–1.16 folds in Daqing and by 1.15–1.29 folds in Heihe (Figure 1f, Figure S15e). GmCRCK1d‐OX lines displayed oppositely (Figure S16 and S17).

Collectively, our results demonstrate that CRISPR‐mediated mutation of GmCRCK1a‐1d enhances soybean yield under dense planting conditions, primarily by increasing the 100‐seed weight, while simultaneously elevating seed protein content. These findings provide a potentiality of utilising GmCRCK1a‐1d gene‐editing in breeding soybean cultivars with high protein content and high yield at dense planting.

Enhancing perioperative antibiotic stewardship in China: a call for action
Authors: Zhe Du
Published: 2024

Dear Editor,

I am writing to express my appreciation for the recent publication of the study ‘Duration of surgical antibiotic prophylaxis and surgical site infection in orthopaedic surgery: a prospective cohort study’ in International Journal of Surgery 1. The study significantly contributes to the orthopedic surgery domain by dissecting the correlation between the duration of surgical antibiotic prophylaxis (SAP) and the incidence of surgical site infections (SSIs). Although the meticulous efforts and substantial contributions of this study are highly valued, we offer several constructive suggestions to pave the way for future enhancements.

First, the specificity of surgical procedures merits a more granular dissection. A nuanced stratification of the orthopedic surgeries, such as varying Gustilo classifications within the open fracture group and procedures like arthroplasty, arthroscopy, and spinal surgeries within the non-traumatic group, could unveil distinct SAP requirements and subsequent implications.

Second, the confounding factors necessitate a more rigorous mitigation strategy. Despite the employment of multivariable models and LASSO regression, the potential for unmeasured or suboptimally adjusted confounders persists. Parameters such as the holistic health status of patients, intraoperative dynamics, concomitant medication use including corticosteroids and immunosuppressants2, and iatrogenic factors post-hospitalization must be meticulously accounted for.

Third, the study’s preoccupation with one-year postoperative outcomes, while commendable, does not give due consideration to the long-term sequelae. Infections, particularly those linked to biomaterials such as prosthetic joints, may elude detection beyond the initial year post-surgery. The protracted or improper utilization of antibiotics could precipitate a rise in bacterial antibiotic resistance, with implications potentially manifesting years subsequent to the surgical intervention.

Despite the existence of clinical guidelines, the rampant overuse and misuse of antibiotics in perioperative contexts within China highlight a chasm between recommended protocols and empirical practice3. A concerted effort is essential to align clinical conduct with evidence-based directives. There is an exigent need for bolstered educational initiatives aimed at healthcare professionals to enhance their acumen regarding judicious antibiotic stewardship in perioperative scenarios. Concurrently, the fortification of surveillance mechanisms to monitor antibiotic usage and SSIs is imperative for tracking advancements and shaping policy.

In summation, the research by Yang et al.1 presents robust evidence instrumental in guiding clinical practice and policy formulation, advocating for a reappraisal of SAP duration and the imperative to adhere to established guidelines. Our suggestions are proffered with the intent to augment an already distinguished scholarly work. Moreover, the broader context of antibiotic utilization in China’s perioperative milieu demands vigilant action. Bridging the gap between guidelines and practice necessitates a multifaceted approach encompassing education, heightened awareness, and legislative amendments. By adopting these approaches, we aim to achieve a more judicious use of antibiotics, thereby enhancing patient outcomes and leading to improved prognoses.

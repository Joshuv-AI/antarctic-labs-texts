DOT1L as a Therapeutic Target: Insights into Epigenetic Regulation and Cancer Treatment
Authors: Hee-Joo Choi, Minh Tuan Nguyen, Boram Kim, Seung Hwan Kim, Jaehee Kim, Jeong-Yeon Lee, Mi Kyung Park
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractDisruptor of Telomeric Silencing 1-Like (DOT1L) has emerged as a critical epigenetic regulator in cancer, primarily because of its role as the sole histone methyltransferase responsible for histone H3 at lysine 79 methylation. This modification affects transcriptional activation, DNA repair, and cell cycle progression. Its dysregulation is associated with both hematological and solid tumors. In Mixed-lineage leukemia (MLL)-rearranged leukemia, DOT1L maintains aberrant gene expression patterns at loci such as HOXA and MEIS1, supporting leukemic stem cell survival and driving oncogenesis. In solid tumors, DOT1L influences diverse processes, including epithelial-mesenchymal transition, angiogenesis, and cell cycle regulation, contributing to tumor growth and metastasis. Therapeutic strategies targeting DOT1L using inhibitors, such as EPZ-5676, have shown promise in preclinical and clinical studies, highlighting their potential as versatile targets for precision oncology. This review summarizes the recent findings on DOT1L’s involvement in cancer development and its potential as a therapeutic target.Keywords: Histone methyltransferase, Epigenetic regulation, H3K79 methylation, Targeted cancer therapy

INTRODUCTION

Disruptor of Telomeric Silencing 1-Like (DOT1L) is a unique histone methyltransferase that plays a crucial role in epigenetic regulation and has emerged as a promising therapeutic target in cancer treatment (Kim et al., 2012b; Lee and Kong, 2015; Wong et al., 2015). As the sole enzyme responsible for catalyzing the mono-, di-, and tri-methylation of histone H3 at lysine 79 (H3K79), DOT1L regulates diverse cellular processes including development, reprogramming, differentiation, and proliferation (Kim et al., 2014). DOT1L-mediated H3K79 methylation is associated with active transcription and is implicated in various biological processes, including the DNA damage response, cell cycle progression, and embryonic development (Lee and Kong, 2015; Wong et al., 2015).

In MLL-rearranged leukemia, DOT1L is recruited to aberrant gene loci by MLL fusion proteins, causing inappropriate H3K79 methylation and activation of oncogenes. Beyond hematological malignancies, DOT1L is also implicated in solid tumors. In breast cancer, particularly in the triple-negative subtype, DOT1L facilitates epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and maintains cancer stem cell (CSC) populations, which are associated with increased tumor aggressiveness and poor prognosis (Kurani et al., 2022; Lee and Kong, 2015). Inhibition of DOT1L in these models has been demonstrated to reduce tumor growth and metastatic potential (Kurani et al., 2022). Furthermore, in neuroblastoma, a pediatric cancer, DOT1L regulates oncogenic pathways that promote tumor growth (Mao et al., 2021). Studies have shown that targeting DOT1L can decrease tumor proliferation and improve survival outcomes in preclinical models (Kurani et al., 2022; Mao et al., 2021).

The development of small-molecule inhibitors, such as pinometostat (EPZ-5676), has advanced the therapeutic targeting of DOT1L (Dafflon et al., 2017; Kurani et al., 2022). These inhibitors disrupt DOT1L’s catalytic activity, leading to decreased H3K79 methylation and altered gene expression. Clinical trials are currently underway to evaluate their efficacy against different cancer types (Kurani et al., 2022; Rau et al., 2016).

As research on DOT1L continues to expand, its complex roles in various cancer types have become increasingly apparent. Although DOT1L inhibition shows promise in certain cancers, its function may vary depending on the specific cellular context and cancer type. Given its context-dependent function as either an oncogene or a tumor suppressor, DOT1L requires careful evaluation when designing targeted therapies. In this review, we aim to summarize recent findings on DOT1L’s role in cancer progression and treatment, offering new insights to guide future research directions and the development of more targeted and effective DOT1L-based therapies.

FUNDAMENTAL BIOLOGY AND REGULATION OF DOT1L

Discovery and structural features of DOT1L

The disruptor of Telomeric Silencing 1 (Dot1), also known as KMT4, was initially identified as a regulator of telomere-proximal genes in yeast (Singer et al., 1998). It is a unique histone methyltransferase specific for H3K79 in both yeast and mammals (Feng et al., 2002; Lacoste et al., 2002; van Leeuwen et al., 2002). Human DOT1-Like (DOT1L) is distinct from many histone methyltransferases as it does not possess the typical SU(var)3-9, Enhancer of Zeste and Trithorax (SET) domain (Feng et al., 2002). DOT1L is composed of 1,527 amino acids and features both a catalytic domain and coiled-coil (C-C) domains involved in its functional interactions (Min et al., 2003). The protein’s N-terminal region (residues 1-416 amino acids) contains the catalytic domain, which utilizes its structure to transfer a methyl group from S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) to a lysine residue on H3K79 (Min et al., 2003). Additionally, DOT1L possesses C-C domains (residues 419-670) that promote the formation of complexes with various associated proteins, such as AF9 (MLLT3), AF10 (MLLT10), and general transcription factors (GTFs), which are essential for transcriptional regulation (Mueller et al., 2007; Wu et al., 2021). These cofactors lead DOT1L to specific genomic loci and increase its catalytic activity (Deshpande et al., 2014; Shen et al., 2013).

H3K79 methylation and transcriptional regulation

H3K79 methylation by DOT1L broadly correlates with open reading frames (ORFs) and transcriptional activity (Hake et al., 2006; Jonkers et al., 2014; Veloso et al., 2014). The different methylation states have distinct roles in this process. H3K79me1 (monomethylation) is associated with transcriptional activation and is often considered a preparatory mark that is converted to di- and tri-methylation during active transcription (Ng et al., 2003; Steger et al., 2008). In contrast, H3K79me2 (dimethylation) is strongly correlated with high transcriptional activation and ongoing elongation, commonly found around transcriptional start sites (TSS) where it may recruit factors like RNA polymerase II (Pol ll) (Jonkers et al., 2014; Kim et al., 2012a; Schübeler et al., 2004; Wang et al., 2008). Finally, H3K79me3 (Trimethylation), though less common, marks regions of highly active transcription. Methylation of H3K79 by DOT1L triggers a conformational change in chromatin, promoting its transition to a more open, euchromatic state. Given its critical role in gene expression, DOT1L is essential for the activation and elongation of transcription.

Regulation of DOT1L activity

DOT1L plays a pivotal role in various biological functions, including cell cycle regulation, proliferation, and DNA double-damage repair (Barry et al., 2009; Kim et al., 2012b; Tatum and Li, 2011). Its activity is tightly regulated. A crucial prerequisite for DOT1L’s catalytic activity is the monoubiquitination of histone H2B at lysine 120 (H2BK120ub), a post-translational modification conserved from yeast to humans (Ng et al., 2002; Sun and Allis, 2002). DOT1L directly recognizes and interacts with H2BK120ub through its ubiquitin-binding region, a step essential for properly positioning the H3K79 site for methylation (McGinty et al., 2008; Min et al., 2003; Worden et al., 2019). After binding to H2BK120ub, DOT1L recruits various cofactors including AF9/10 (Mueller et al., 2007), c-Myc (Cho et al., 2015), and NPM1 (Izzo et al., 2023) to gene promot- ers to facilitate transcriptional elongation. The entire process is linked to the cell cycle, with H3K79 methylation increasing during the G1 to S phase transition (Schulze et al., 2009), and is involved in DNA damage repair by recruiting proteins like RAD9/53BP1 to double-strand breaks (Giannattasio et al., 2005; Shroff et al., 2004; Wysocki et al., 2005).

THE ROLE OF DOT1L AND THERAPEUTIC APPROACHES BY CANCER TYPE

DOT1L overexpression is a common feature across multiple cancers, contributing to cell proliferation, metastasis, and therapy resistance. However, the specific mechanisms are cancer-type specific.

Hematological malignancies

In mixed-lineage leukemia (MLL)-rearranged leukemia, DOT1L is a primary oncogenic driver. MLL fusion proteins aberrantly recruit DOT1L to target gene loci like HOXA and MEIS1, causing inappropriate H3K79 methylation and maintaining leukemic gene expression. Mechanistically, DOT1L inhibits a repressive complex composed of SIRT1 and SUV39H1, thereby maintaining an open chromatin state at MLL fusion target genes (Chen et al., 2015). Therapeutically, the DOT1L inhibitor pinometostat (EPZ-5676) demonstrated significant anti-leukemic effects in preclinical models (Daigle et al., 2013). However, due to limited single-agent activity, combination therapies are being explored. Combining a DOT1L inhibitor with a menin-MLL interaction inhibitor markedly enhanced differentiation and cell killing in various MLL disease models without harming normal hematopoiesis (Dafflon et al., 2017). Similarly, combining with SIRT1 activators also showed enhanced activity against MLL-rearranged leukemia cells (Chen et al., 2015).

Breast cancer

DOT1L expression is associated with poor survival and aggressiveness in breast cancer, particularly in estrogen receptor (ER)-negative subtypes (Cho et al., 2015). In ER-positive breast cancer, DOT1L is co-recruited with the estrogen receptor to a substantial fraction of ER cistromes, influencing the transcription of key genes like ESR1 and FOXA1. DOT1L inhibition interferes with estrogen’s effects on gene expression, leading to cell cycle arrest and apoptosis, even in tamoxifen- or fulvestrant-resistant cell lines (Nassa et al., 2019). In triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), DOT1L promotes cancer progression through several mechanisms. It facilitates the epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and maintains cancer stem cell (CSC) populations (Kurani et al., 2022). It also drives angiogenesis by epigenetically regulating metadherin (MTDH) expression, which in turn enhances the NF-κB-HIF1α axis (Neeli et al., 2023). DOT1L inhibition with EPZ-5676 has been shown to decrease ALDH1+ CSC populations, reduce tumor growth, and limit metastasis in preclinical TNBC models (Kurani et al., 2022).

Gastrointestinal cancers

In gastric cancer, DOT1L expression is significantly increased and correlates with poor prognosis and worse overall survival. It promotes tumor proliferation by regulating cyclin-dependent kinases (CDK4 and CDK6) through H3K79me2, affecting the G1 phase of the cell cycle (Song et al., 2020). Additionally, it promotes progression by stimulating CD44 expression through the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway (Li et al., 2024). In colorectal cancer (CRC), DOT1L stability is enhanced by CBP-mediated acetylation at K358, which correlates positively with advanced CRC stage. This promotes H3K79 methylation of EMT-related genes such as SNAIL and ZEB1, leading to increased metastasis and invasion (Liu et al., 2020). The DOT1L inhibitor EPZ004777 induced cell cycle arrest and suppressed tumor growth in vivo. Mechanistically, DOT1L inhibition epigenetically downregulates c-Myc expression, leading to decreased expression of cell cycle factors (Yang et al., 2019). Furthermore, high levels of tumor H3K79me2 negatively correlated with RORγ+FOXP3+ Treg cells, indicating DOT1L affects the T cell subset balance (Sun et al., 2022).

Other solid tumors

DOT1L’s role extends to other solid tumors. In lung cancer, a gain-of-function mutation in DOT1L (R231Q) promotes malignant phenotypes by enriching H3K79me2 on the RAF1 promoter, thereby activating the MAPK/ERK signaling pathway. Combining a DOT1L inhibitor (SGC0946) with a MAPK/ERK axis inhibitor (binimetinib) effectively reverses this effect, providing promising insights for precise treatment (Zhang et al., 2023). In Renal Cell Carcinoma (RCC), DOT1L promotes proliferation and invasion by epigenetically upregulating STAT5B via H3K79me2. STAT5B’s downstream effector, CDK6, then mediates cell-cycle arrest (Hou et al., 2023a). It also regulates autophagy and mitochondrial fusion in renal cancer cell lines (Hou et al., 2023b). For ovarian cancer, DOT1L overexpression is associated with an advanced tumor stage and poor prognosis by regulating apoptotic and metabolic pathways and interfering with natural killer (NK) cell-mediated cancer cell eradication (Chava et al., 2021). The inhibitor EPZ-5676 significantly blocked subcutaneous ovarian cancer tumor growth in vivo (Chava et al., 2021). High expression of DOT1L in Pancreatic Cancer correlates with advanced cancer stage and inhibits apoptosis by promoting NUPR1 expression (Shan et al., 2022). Finally, in pediatric cancers such as neuroblastoma and retinoblastoma (RB), targeting DOT1L regulates oncogenic pathways and can sensitize cancer cells to chemotherapy, enhancing therapeutic efficacy in preclinical models (Mao et al., 2021).

DEVELOPMENT OF DOT1L INHIBITORS AND CLINICAL CHALLENGES

Key DOT1L inhibitors

The development of small-molecule inhibitors targeting DOT1L’s unique non-SET catalytic domain has been a major focus, with a primary strategy of displacing or blocking the methyl-donor SAM from its binding site (Flores-León et al., 2023; Yi and Ge, 2022). Key inhibitors developed to date include EPZ-5676 (Pinometostat), a first-in-class small-molecule inhibitor that has progressed to clinical trials for MLL-rearranged leukemia (Perner et al., 2020; Stein et al., 2018). It was preceded by EPZ004777, an early inhibitor that demonstrated selective killing of MLL-rearranged leukemia cells but had poor pharmacokinetic properties (Daigle et al., 2013). Other important compounds used in preclinical studies include SGC0946, notably in ovarian and lung cancer models (Chava et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2023), and novel SAM-competitive inhibitors like Compounds 10 and 11, which have improved pharmacokinetics and potent anti-leukemic activities (Perner et al., 2020).

Clinical trial status

Pinometostat has been evaluated in phase 1 clinical trials in both adult and pediatric patients with MLL-rearranged leukemias (Stein et al., 2018). The adult trial (NCT01684150) enrolled 51 patients and provided the first evidence of DOT1L target inhibition in humans, demonstrating its clinical efficacy. While generally well-tolerated, the drug showed modest clinical activity, with two patients achieving complete remission (Stein et al., 2018). The pediatric trial (NCT02141828) has been completed, but results have not yet been published (Shukla et al., 2016). Due to the modest single-agent activity observed in these trials, researchers are now exploring combination therapies (Menghrajani et al., 2019).

Therapeutic resistance and strategies to overcome it

Resistance to DOT1L inhibitors has emerged as a significant barrier in both preclinical and clinical settings. The anti-proliferative effects can be minimal or delayed, suggesting the presence of both intrinsic and acquired resistance mechanisms (Schneider et al., 2023). While the upregulation of drug efflux pumps like ABCB1 has been implicated, this does not fully account for therapeutic limitations. Increasing evidence points to the non-catalytic scaffolding functions of DOT1L, which may support cancer cell viability independent of its methyltransferase activity, thereby conferring resistance to catalytic inhibitors (Liu et al., 2024; Schneider et al., 2023). Overcoming this requires rational combination strategies and the development of novel therapeutic modalities, such as protein degraders (e.g., PROTACs) that can eliminate the entire DOT1L protein (Yim et al., 2025).

EMERGING ROLES AND FUTURE PROSPECTS OF DOT1L

DOT1L as an immune regulator

The therapeutic potential of DOT1L inhibition extends beyond oncology, particularly into the realm of immunology. DOT1L is a pivotal epigenetic modulator of both innate and adaptive immunity, primarily through H3K79 methylation activity (Kealy et al., 2024). In T-cells, DOT1L is crucial for maintaining the naïve state of CD8+ T-cells and preventing their premature differentiation into memory-like cells without antigen exposure (Kwesi-Maliepaard et al., 2020). Its inhibition can attenuate graft-versus-host disease (GVHD) while preserving the antitumor efficacy of T-cells, indicating its potential in adoptive immunotherapy and other immune-mediated disorders (Kagoya et al., 2018). In B-cells, DOT1L is essential for proper humoral immunity, including germinal center formation, class-switch recombination, and the generation of memory B-cells (Aslam et al., 2021; Kealy et al., 2020). It supports pro-proliferative germinal center gene programs and represses anti-proliferative plasma cell genes (Aslam et al., 2021). This ability to fine-tune immune cell programming suggests its therapeutic relevance in autoimmune and chronic inflammatory disorders (Kealy et al., 2024).

Regulation of cellular senescence

DOT1L also plays a direct role in mediating the senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP), a hallmark of senescent cells that affects the tissue microenvironment. During oncogene-induced senescence, DOT1L expression is upregulated and promotes the transcription of IL1A, which in turn amplifies the expression of downstream SASP factors like IL-6 and IL-1β (Leon et al., 2021). Notably, modulation of DOT1L selectively regulates the SASP independently of the growth arrest associated with senescence (Leon et al., 2021). This mechanism has pathological relevance in conditions like acute kidney injury (AKI), where DOT1L promotes cellular senescence; its inhibition reduces senescence burden and mitigates tissue injury (Yao et al., 2025).

Challenges and the dual role of DOT1L

Despite its promise, significant challenges remain. The role of DOT1L is highly context-dependent, acting as an oncogene in some cancers while potentially functioning as a tumor suppressor in others, complicating therapeutic strategies (Alexandrova et al., 2022). This complexity necessitates the identification of predictive biomarkers to guide patient selection. Future research must focus on developing more selective inhibitors with improved pharmacokinetic properties and advancing epigenetic profiling tools to optimize clinical translation (Kurani and Slingerland, 2025). The exploration of combination therapies and novel inhibitor designs will be critical to fully harness the potential of targeting DOT1L.

CONCLUSION

DOT1L has emerged as a crucial epigenetic regulator and a versatile therapeutic target. Its importance is underscored by its involvement in various oncogenic processes, from its role as a direct driver in MLL-rearranged leukemia to its influence on metastasis, angiogenesis, and CSC maintenance in a wide array of solid tumors (Alexandrova et al., 2022; Cho et al., 2015).

The therapeutic response to DOT1L inhibition varies; hematological malignancies show a more direct dependency, whereas solid tumors often require combination strategies to overcome resistance mechanisms and pathway redundancy. The development of DOT1L inhibitors has opened new avenues for epigenetic-based cancer therapy, demonstrating efficacy in both preclinical models and early clinical trials. Their generally favorable safety profile suggests potential for combination therapies that may enhance therapeutic efficacy.

Conceptual diagram of DOT1L’s dual role in cancer. This schematic illustrates the context-dependent nature of DOT1L function across different cancer types. DOT1L acts as an oncogene in MLL-rearranged leukemia, breast, lung, and gastric cancers by promoting aberrant gene expression, proliferation, and metastasis making DOT1L inhibition a viable therapeutic strategy. Conversely, DOT1L may function as a tumor suppressor in colorectal cancer and potentially other solid tumors by maintaining genomic stability and regulating cell cycle checkpoints requiring therapeutic caution to preserve these protective functions. The context-dependent duality of DOT1L necessitates predictive biomarkers and patient stratification for optimal therapeutic targeting, highlighting the need for precision medicine approaches in DOT1L-directed cancer therapy.

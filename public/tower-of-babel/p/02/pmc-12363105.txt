The effect of non-insulin dependent insulin resistance indices on the prediction in the recurrence of late atrial fibrillation after modified maze procedure concomitant with valvular heart surgery
Authors: Jianliang Liu, Min Lin, Yan Huang, Wenpeng Dong
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveSurgical ablation of atrial fibrillation (AF) is suitable for patients with AF undergoing concomitant cardiac surgery. However, the high rate of AF recurrence after surgery remains a significant clinical concern. This study examined the association between insulin resistance (IR) indices and late AF recurrence in patients undergoing modified maze surgery combined with valvular heart surgery.MethodsA retrospective analysis was performed on 293 patients undergoing open-heart valve surgery and modified maze surgery. Patients were categorized into recurrence and non-recurrence groups based on atrial fibrillation recurrence beyond the 3-month postoperative window. Multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression and restricted cubic spline (RCS) analyses were employed to assess the association between insulin resistance indices and late AF recurrence. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was used to evaluate the predictive ability of insulin resistance indices for late AF recurrence.ResultsA total of 293 patients were enrolled (131 males, 162 females). During a median follow-up of 25.00 months, 88 patients (30.03%) experienced late AF recurrence. Compared to the non-recurrence group, patients with recurrence had significantly higher baseline preoperative IR indices. Multivariate Cox regression identified recurrence during postoperative hospitalization, left atrial diameter (LAD), and IR indices were risk factors for late AF recurrence of postoperative. Multivariate adjusted RCS curves demonstrated a linear correlation between IR indices and late AF recurrence.ConclusionIR indices demonstrated statistically significant but clinically modest associations with late AF recurrence in patients undergoing concomitant cardiac valve and modified maze surgery.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12872-025-05032-9.Keywords: Atrial fibrillation, Insulin resistance, Surgical ablation, Modified maze procedure

Introduction

Atrial fibrillation (AF) is the most prevalent form of supraventricular arrhythmia [1]. It is linked to an elevated risk of mortality, stroke, and peripheral embolism [2]. The prevalence of AF varies from 2% in the general population to 10–12% among individuals aged 80 and above [3, 4]. The onset and progression of AF entail intricate interactions among electrical remodeling, structural remodeling, inflammatory responses, oxidative stress, and metabolic disturbances [5]. Prior research has indicated that advanced age, being male, a sedentary lifestyle, smoking, obesity, diabetes, obstructive sleep apnea, and hypertension all predispose individuals to AF, with each factor having been demonstrated to induce structural and electrical remodeling of the atrium [6].

The Cox-Maze IV procedure (CMPIV) is currently considered the gold standard of surgical management for AF, with excellent 10-year efficacy [7]. When performed concomitantly with cardiac valve surgery, AF ablation does not increase operative mortality or major complication rates [8]. However, postoperative AF recurrence may contribute to persistent disease burden. In a long-term follow-up study of 853 patients undergoing CMPIV, Khiabani et al. reported that 76% (647/853) received extended monitoring. Freedom from atrial tachyarrhythmia (ATA) rates among evaluable patients were 92% (552/598) at 1 year, 84% (213/253) at 5 years, and 77% (67/87) at 10 years [9]. Similarly, a prospective study by Henn et al. including 620 AF patients documented freedom from ATA rates of 92% (411/446) at 1 year, 88% (267/304) at 2 years, 87% (194/223) at 3 years, 81% (135/166) at 4 years, and 73% (102/139) at 5 years [10].

Insulin resistance (IR) refers to diminished sensitivity of target tissues to insulin, resulting in impaired glucose uptake and utilization. IR may promote atrial structural remodeling and intracellular calcium dysregulation, potentially increasing susceptibility to atrial fibrillation [11]. Previous studies in non-diabetic Asian populations with obesity have demonstrated an association between insulin resistance and AF development independent of established risk factors [12]. The hyperinsulinemic-euglycemic clamp represents the gold-standard for IR assessment, though its clinical application is limited by procedural complexity [13]. The homeostasis model assessment of insulin resistance (HOMA-IR), derived from fasting insulin and glucose levels, has been identified as an independent predictor of AF recurrence after ablation in non-diabetic patients with paroxysmal AF [14]. However, HOMA-IR requires serum insulin measurement, restricting its utility for routine clinical screening [15]. Non-insulin-based IR indices offer practical advantages. The Lipid Accumulation Product (LAP), calculated using waist circumference and serum triglyceride levels, is an indicator assessing lipid distribution and metabolic risk. It effectively reflects visceral fat accumulation, particularly in individuals with central obesity, and demonstrates strong associations with insulin resistance, metabolic syndrome, and related disorders [16]. Nevertheless, routine waist circumference measurement remains uncommon in hospitalized patients, limiting LAP implementation.Recently developed non-insulin dependent IR indices (TyG index, TG/HDL-C, METS-IR, and TyG-BMI index) utilize routinely available parameters: fasting glucose, lipid profiles, height, and weight, provides a more practical tool for clinical assessment of IR. This study investigates the relationship between these four clinically accessible IR indices and late AF recurrence in patients undergoing modified maze surgery combined with valvular heart surgery.

Methods

Patient population

Patients were retrospectively enrolled who underwent a modified maze procedure concomitant with valvular heart surgery at The First Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University (Hefei, China) between January 2021 and April 2024. Inclusion criteria comprised: (1) preoperative diagnosis of persistent or paroxysmal AF; (2) Patients undergoing concomitant surgical ablation during valvular surgery. Exclusion criteria were: (1) incomplete baseline clinical data; (2) Lack of data to calculate IR indices. The study received approval from the Institutional Review Board of The First Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University (Approval No. PJ2023-11-53), was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, and written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

Data collection and IR indices calculation

Clinical data were collected from electronic medical records, including age, sex, height, weight, medical history. preoperative fasting blood parameters, including white blood cells, hemoglobin, platelets, estimated glomerular filtration rate(eGFR), uric acid, glucose, triglyceride(TG), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol(LDL-C), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol(HDL-C), diagnostic results (echocardiography, electrocardiography), operative details (procedure duration), and postoperative outcomes (including Recurrence of AF during hospitalization).

The Body Mass Index (BMI) was calculated using the formula: weight/height2. Triglyceride-Glucose index (TyG index) was calculated using the formula: ln [fasting TG (mg/dL)×fasting blood glucose (mg/dL)/2] [17]. TG/HDL-C was calculated using the formula: TG (mg/dL) ÷ HDL-C (mg/dL) [18]. METS-IR was calculated using the following formula: ln [(2×fasting blood glucose (mg/dL)) + TG (mg/dL)]×BMI (kg/m²)÷(ln [HDL-C (mg/dL)]) [19]. The TyG-BMI index was calculated using the following formula: ln [fasting TG (mg/dL)×fasting blood glucose (mg/dL)/2]×BMI (kg/m²) [20].

Surgical ablation procedure and postoperative management

The surgical ablation pathway and its steps are outlined as follows: (1) Right atrial procedure: The right atrial appendage is resected, followed by the creation of an ablation line from the right atrial appendage to the tricuspid annulus. Ablation is then performed at the middle segment of the crista terminalis, extending to the superior vena cava, inferior vena cava, coronary sinus, and the tricuspid isthmus. (2) Left atrial procedure: The left atrial appendage is resected, and the Marshall ligament is transected. Isolation and ablation of both left and right pulmonary veins are carried out. The posterior wall of the left atrium undergoes ablation, forming a box in conjunction with the pulmonary vein isolation line.

After the surgery, patients underwent electrocardiogram(ECG) monitoring and received continuous intravenous infusion of amiodarone, which was subsequently switched to oral administration. The oral dosage was progressively decreased to 200 mg once daily to maintain sinus rhythm. The anticoagulation protocol for these patients involved subcutaneous injection of low molecular weight heparin in conjunction with oral warfarin. Once the international normalized ratio (INR) reached the desired level, the low molecular weight heparin was discontinued. Patients who underwent mechanical heart valve replacement were prescribed lifelong warfarin therapy, whereas those who underwent valve repair or biological valve replacement required warfarin therapy for at least six months.

Follow-up and definition of AF recurrence

Atrial tachyarrhythmia (ATA) episodes lasting ≥ 30 s detected via continuous bedside monitor electrocardiography or surface electrocardiography during the postoperative hospitalization period were defined as early postoperative atrial fibrillation recurrence; follow-up electrocardiograms were performed at 1, 3, 6, and 12 months postoperatively through outpatient visits, readmissions, or telephone follow-up, with the initial 3 months designated as the blanking period, and any ATA episode ≥ 30 s recorded by ECG or Holter monitoring beyond this period was defined as late postoperative atrial fibrillation recurrence.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 25.0 and R 4.4.2. Normality tests guided the choice of parametric or non-parametric tests. Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median (interquartile range, IQR).Student’s t-test was applied to normally distributed variables, while the Mann-Whitney U test was used for non-normally distributed variables.Categorical variables are reported as counts and percentages (%), with comparisons conducted using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. univariate logistic and Cox regression were used to screen covariates, and confounding factors such as gender, age, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, left atrial diameter (LAD), recurrence during postoperative hospitalization, and uric acid were adjusted for Multivariate Cox regression was included P < 0.05 and the variables considered meaningful in the previous literature, Kaplan-Meier analysis was performed according to the optimal cut-off value of IR indices, Log-rank test was performed to compare whether there was a difference between groups, and the area under the curve (AUC) of receiver operating characteristics (ROC) was calculated to evaluate the overall performance of IR indices in predicting recurrence of late AF. The relationship between IR indices and late recurrence of postoperative AF was assessed using a multivariately adjusted restriction cubic spline (RCS). Subgroup analyses were performed by gender, age, hypertension, history of diabetes, eGFR, uric acid, etc., to evaluate the predictive ability of TyG-BMI index in different subgroups, and to calculate the P value of interaction. A two-sided P-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics

A total of 293 patients were included in this study, including 131 males (44.71%) and 162 females (55.29%), excluding 33 patients who lacked indicators to calculate surrogate indicators of insulin resistance, who were automatically discharged after surgery, and who were lost to follow-up (Table 1). The median follow-up time was 25 (17, 36) months, and 88 patients (30.0%) developed recurrence of late AF after surgery. There were no significant differences in age, gender, medical history, and type of AF between the recurrence group and the non-recurrence group (all P > 0.05). The baseline preoperative IR indices in the recurrence group was higher than that of the non-recurrence group.

The predictive value of the IR indices for the risk of late AF recurrence

The ROC curves of IR indices for predicting late recurrence of postoperative AF showed that the area under the ROC curve (AUC) of TyG index, TyG-BMI index, TG/HDL-C, and METS-IR were 0.620, 0.650, 0.639, and 0.585, respectively (Fig. 1). The results of ROC curve analysis showed that the area under the curve (AUC) of TyG index for predicting postoperative AF recurrence was 0.620(95%CI: 0.546–0.693, P = 0.001), TyG-BMI index was 0.650(95%CI: 0.582–0.717, P < 0.001), TG/HDL-C was 0.585(95%CI: 0.508–0.661, P = 0.022), and METS-IR was 0.639(95%CI: 0.570–0.708, P < 0.001). Based on the Youden index, the corresponding sensitivity and specificity were determined as: TyG index: Youden index = 9.034 (Sensitivity = 0.917, Specificity = 0.341). TyG-BMI index: Youden index = 211.766 (Sensitivity = 0.673, Specificity = 0.580). TG/HDL-C: Youden index = 1.603 (Sensitivity = 0.859, Specificity = 0.364). METS-IR: Youden index = 35.867 (Sensitivity = 0.746, Specificity = 0.466).

Cox proportional risk regression analysis of risk factors for recurrence of postoperative AF

Univariate Cox regression analysis showed that recurrence of AF, left atrial diameter (LAD), and postoperative uric acid level during postoperative hospitalization were risk factors for recurrence of late postoperative AF. After adjustment, multivariate Cox regression analysis showed that recurrence during postoperative hospitalization (HR = 2.30, 95%CI: 1.50–3.54, P < 0.001) and left atrial diameter (HR = 1.47, 95%CI: 1.01–2.14, P = 0.043) were risk factors for recurrence of late AF after modified maze (Table 2).

Discussion

Research indicates that IR contributes significantly to the development and progression of AF. The underlying mechanisms include: IR’s influence on atrial electrophysiological properties and structural remodeling processes; its association with left atrial enlargement and impairment of left ventricular diastolic function; and its role in mediating abnormal deposition within epicardial adipose tissue. These pathophysiological alterations collectively contribute to an increased susceptibility to AF [12].

Our study demonstrated that after adjusting for confounders, four IR indices exhibited statistically significant linear correlations with late AF recurrence following the modified maze procedure concomitant with valvular heart surgery (all P < 0.05). Kaplan-Meier analysis based on the optimal cut-off values for these four IR indices also revealed that patients with higher IR levels had a significantly increased cumulative incidence of late postoperative AF recurrence compared to those with lower IR levels (Log-rank test, all P < 0.001). Subgroup analysis examining the relationship between the TyG-BMI index and late AF recurrence showed a significant interaction when stratified by sex, specifically in males (HR: 2.03, 95% CI 1.52–2.71, P < 0.05). This suggests that the association between the TyG-BMI index and late AF recurrence warrants particular attention in male AF patients.However, in the ROC curve analysis, the best-performing index (TyG-BMI index) yielded an AUC of only 0.650, indicating limited predictive performance for the IR indices. Whether preoperative management of IR aids in preventing late AF recurrence after this combined procedure therefore requires further investigation. The development and progression of AF involve complex pathophysiological mechanisms, and IR represents only one contributing factor. This complexity likely accounts for the limited predictive capability of single IR indices for late AF recurrence. Nevertheless, our findings demonstrate a correlation between IR and late postoperative AF recurrence, suggesting its influence should not be overlooked. Further research integrating IR with other risk factors associated with late postoperative AF recurrence is needed to explore these relationships more comprehensively.

Limitations

This study has several limitations: (1) As a retrospective, single-center cohort study with a relatively limited sample size, the findings remain susceptible to selection bias and residual confounding inherent in observational designs despite multivariable adjustment, precluding causal inference. (2) Although we identified significant correlations between IR indices and late AF recurrence, all four indices demonstrated limited predictive utility (AUC < 0.70) for late AF recurrence after the modified maze procedure concomitant with valvular heart surgery. (3) The predictive model and derived cut-off values lack external validation in independent cohorts, preventing assessment of generalizability and increasing the risk of model overfitting; future multicenter validation is needed to establish clinical applicability. (4) The absence of universal continuous cardiac monitoring during follow-up may have underestimated true recurrence rates.

Conclusion

Although all four IR indices demonstrated statistically significant linear correlations with late AF recurrence after the modified maze procedure concomitant with valvular heart surgery, none provided sufficient predictive utility for clinical application.

Circulating exosomal protein EFEMP1 and SERPINC1 as diagnostic biomarkers for epithelial ovarian cancer
Authors: Shiwen Wang, Huimin Wang, Kangyu Wang, Qianru Zhang, Xingguo Song
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT

Introduction

Ovarian cancer currently is the fifth leading cause of cancer-related deaths among women, and approximately 140 000 women die globally per year. Epithelial ovarian cancer is the most common type of ovarian cancer [1]. This debilitating disease presents subtly, 70 % were diagnosed at stage III and IV [2], thus treatment options often are limited, leading to high fatality rates and poor survival [3,4]. Initial diagnostic tests include transvaginal ultrasonography and serum cancer antigen 125 (CA-125) measurement; however, these tests are not specific and with low sensitivity. Therefore, it is beneficial to explore new diagnostic biomarkers for EOC and to reduce patient mortality [5].

Exosomes are vesicles produced by cells with a diameter of 30 to 150 nm and can be found in all biological fluids such as blood, urine and milk [6,7]. Exosomes play a crucial role in cell to cell communication by delivering bioactive molecules to recipient cells, effectively altering their biological behavior [8]. Recent studies revealed a strong connection between exosome and ovarian cancer. In ovarian cancer, cancer cell-secreted exosomes communicate with the components of the tumor microenvironment and participate in tumor occurrence and progression, paraneoplastic syndromes, immune regulation and drug resistance [9]. For example, ovarian cancer cell-secreted exosomal miR-205 promotes metastasis by inducing angiogenesis dependent on PTEN-AKT pathway [10]; Exosome-liposome hybrid nanoparticle codelivery of triptolide (TP) and miR-497 conspicuously overcomes chemoresistant ovarian cancer via promoting dephosphorylation of the overactivated PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling [11].

Previous studies have well summarized the feasibility of exosomal contents as tumor biomarkers, including nucleic acids, lipids and protein, which exist stably in biological fluids without degradation [12]. Exosomal proteins, as the genome output, provide more direct information about disease progression, possessing unique features over traditional serological markers [13]. Exosomal proteins often express higher in cancer patients, and their alteration reflects tumorigenesis and metastasis, suggesting its diagnostic and prognostic value. For example, the expression of exosomal protein HSP70 was significantly increased in ovarian cancer, breast cancer, and lung cancer samples [14]. Besides, exosomes from EOC patients had higher concentrations of TGFB1 [15], as well as Claudin 4 [16] which was associated with tumor stage and CA-125 level, indicating their diagnostic and prognostic role in ovarian cancer.

In current study, we aimed to validate whether exosomal proteins served as the potential biomarkers for EOC. We found EFEMP1 and SERPINC1 were obviously up-regulated in serum exosomes from EOC patients compared to those from both the healthy and benign individuals, possessing with the favorable diagnostic efficiency. Our data provide the evidence exosomal EFEMP1 and SERPINC1 have the potential to serve as novel biomarkers for EOC.

Materials and methods

Patients and clinical samples

163 healthy donors, 30 patients with benign ovarian tumor, and 183 EOC patient admitted in the Shandong Cancer Hospital from January 2021 to July 2024 were enrolled in current study, and their serum samples were collected. Healthy donors did not suffer from any tumor, hypertension, diabetes, immune or other basic diseases after physical examination; Patients with EOC and benign ovarian tumor didn't receive any anti-tumor treatment or suffered from hypertension, diabetes or other basic diseases before serum collection. Patients with benign ovarian tumor included teratoma, ovarian cyst, cystadenoma, and ovarian sex cord stromal tumor. The International Federation of Gynecology and Obstetrics (FIGO) staging system was used for tumor staging. Exosomes isolation

As previously described, ultracentrifugation was used to separate serum exosomes [17]. Briefly, the supernatant underwent centrifugation at 10,000 × g at 4 °C for 30 min to remove large vesicles, followed by ultracentrifugation (Beckman Coulter, Brea, CA, USA) at the condition of 100,000 × g at 4 °C for 2 h. After washing with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), the exosomes were obtained by another ultracentrifugation at the same condition.

Transmission electron microscopy (TEM)

The exosomes suspension was transferred to the TEM grids, then the grids were washed with PBS 20 min later and placed in 50 μl 1 % glutaraldehyde drops for 5 min, followed by 100 μl ddH2O washing. Subsequently, the grids were placed to 50 µl uranyl-oxalate solution (pH=7.0) for 5 min and 50 µl methylcellulose UA for 10 min, respectively. Images were captured by a FEI Tecnai G2 Spirit 120KV microscope at different magnifications and processed using ImageJ to determine the diameter of the exosomes.

qNano assay

Tunable Resistive Pulse Sensing (TRPS, Izon Science Ltd, Christchurch, New Zealand) was used to measure the size of exosomes according to the operating manual. Data processing was performed using Izon control suite v.3.3.2.2000 (Izon Science Ltd).

Cell culture

Human ovarian cancer cells A2780 were purchased from the Cell Bank of the Chinese Academy of Sciences (Shanghai, China), and cultured in DMEM (Gibco, Carlsbad, CA, USA) containing 10 % fetal bovine serum (FBS; Gibco) and antibiotics (penicillin/streptomycin, 100 U/ml) at 37 °C, 5 % CO2.

Protein extraction and western blots

A2780 cells and serum exosomes were lysed by Radioimmunoprecipitation (RIPA) lysis buffer supplemented with 0.5 mM PMSF (Beyotime, Jiangsu, China) on ice for 30 min, and the total protein concentration was determined with a BCA Protein Assay Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, MA, USA). 50 μg protein was separated and transferred onto PVDF membrane (Billerica Millipore, Massachusetts, USA), followed by blockade using 5 % evaporated skimmed milk for 1 h. Consequently, the membrane was incubated with primary antibodies including anti-GM130, anti-HSP70, anti-CD9 (all 1:500, CST, Danvers, United State), and anti-TSG101 (1:2000, PTM-BIO, Hangzhou, China), as well as anti-EFEMP1 (1:500, Abcam, Shanghai, China) and anti-SERPINC1 (1:500, Boster, Hubei, China) overnight at 4 °C and secondary antibodies bound to HRP for 1 hour at room temperature. The protein bands were visualized using ECL blotting detection reagents (Thermo Fisher Scientific).

Liquid chromatography mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) analysis

LC-MS/MS analysis were delegated to Jingjie PTM Biolab Co. Ltd (Hangzhou, PR China). Briefly, equivalent protein samples were fully digested and separated by an ultra-high performance liquid chromatography system and injected into an NSI ion source for ionization. Then, peptide segments were analyzed by the Orbitrap Explorer™ 480 mass spectrometry (Thermo Fisher Scientific). MS/MS data processing was performed by Proteome Discoverer (v2.4.1.15). Raw data were subjected to quality control, followed by quantitative analysis, differential protein screening, classification, KEGG, GO, and protein domain enrichment analysis.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay

The quantitative detection of the exosomal proteins EFEMP1 and SERPINC1 was performed by the ELISA kits (Boster, Hubei, China) according to the manufacturer's instructions. All protein samples were diluted with reagent diluent (1:10 for EFEMP1 or 1:500 for SERPINC1) before ELISA analysis. Results were read using SpectraMax i3x Multi-Mode Microplate Reader (Molecular Devices, San Jose, USA), and the standards and the samples were run in duplicate.

Statistical analysis

SPSS Statistics 25.0 software (IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY, USA) and GraphPad Prism version 9.5 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA) were used for statistical analysis. Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was used to analyze data distribution. If normal, parametric test was employed; if not, the Mann-Whitney test was used. The receiver-operating characteristic (ROC) curve was used to analyze the diagnostic efficiency, and the area under the curve (AUC), sensitivity, specificity, and 95% confidence interval (CI) were calculated. Numerical data were presented as the median and interquartile range (IQR). P values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant and all tests were two-sided.

Results

Identification of the isolated exosomes

Exosomes from EOC patients and healthy donors were randomly selected and verified by TEM, qNano, and western blots. The results demonstrated the typical round exosomal morphology with diameters ranging from 50 to 150 nm (Fig. 1A and 1B). Consistently, CD9 and HSP70, the exosomal marker proteins, were significantly expressed in exosomes but not in cellular lysates; whereas GM130, the exosomal negative control, was only expressed in the cells (Fig. 1C), indicating the isolated extracellular vesicles possessed the typical properties of exosomes and could be identified as exosomes.

(B) Size distribution of exosomes with 50–150 nm diameter was analyzed by qNano system; the samples were obtained from EOC patients and healthy donors. (C) Western blot showing expression levels of the exosomal related proteins CD9, HSP70, GM130.

Profile of differential exosomal proteins using LC-MS/MS analysis

To explore the differential exosomal proteins, exosomes were collected from 6 healthy donors and 14 EOC patients (stage I-II, n = 6; stage III-IV, n = 8) and subjected to LC-MS/MS analysis. A total of 207 proteins were screened out, in which 122 were up-regulated and 85 were down-regulated. The top 30 differential proteins based on P-values are screened and drawn in the heatmap and volcano map as shown in Fig. 2A and 2B. Besides, the differential proteins were mainly enriched in serpin, calcium-binding EGF domain and complement and coagulation cascades as demonstrated by KEGG analysis (Fig. 2C, D), and primarily regulated biological processes such as protein transport and tube morphogenesis as described by GO analysis (Fig. 2E).

Profile of differential exosomal proteins using LC-MS/MS analysis. The top 30 differential exosomal proteins with P value were shown in a (A) heat map and (B) Volcano plot (P < 0.05). (C) Protein domain enrichment analysis of the differential proteins. (D) KEGG enrichment analysis of the differential proteins. (E) GO enrichment analysis of the differential proteins.

Exosomal EFEMP1 and SERPINC1 were elevated in EOC significantly

We then analyzed the above selected exosomal proteins expression by ELISA in a small cohort. Five candidate exosomal proteins were first screened out due to their aberrant expressions and then validated by western blots. Unexpectedly, only exosomal EFEMP1 (epidermal growth factor-containing fibulin-like extracellular matrix protein 1) and SERPINC1 (serine peptidase inhibitor clade C member 1) but not ITIH4 (inter-alpha-trypsin inhibitor heavy chain H4), ITGAM (Integrin subunit Alpha M), CFHR5 (Complement factor H related 5) were upregulated in EOC patients compared with those in healthy donors (Fig. 3A-B and Fig. S1). Therefore, these two exosomal proteins were verified in a more expanded cohort subsequently, including healthy donors, benign and malignant ovarian patients. As shown in Fig. 3C, exosomal EFEMP1 and SERPINC1 were significantly elevated in EOC patients as compared to those in healthy controls and benign patients.

(H: healthy donors; C: epithelial ovarian cancer); (C) The concentration of exosomal EFEMP1 and SERPINC1 in EOC patients, healthy donors (HD), and patients with benign ovarian tumor (BT); (C) The concentration of exosomal EFEMP1 and SERPINC1 in early-stage (stage I+II), advanced stage (stage III+IV) EOC patients, and HD; (ns, no significance, ****P < 0.0001.).

Furthermore, we analyzed the expression levels of exosomal EFEMP1 and SERPINC1 among different stages of EOC patients and healthy donors. As shown in Fig. 3D, exosomal EFEMP1 and SERPINC1 were significantly elevated in early-stage EOC (stage I+II, n = 29) patients when comparing with the healthy donors, indicating their potential for early diagnostics of EOC (Fig. 3D). Nevertheless, their expressions didn't seem different between early and advanced (stage III+IV, n = 136) stage of EOC (excluding 18 patient with unclear TNM stage).

The correlation between exosomal EFEMP1 and SERPINC1 and the clinicopathological characteristics was also evaluated. As shown in Table 1, exosomal EFEMP1 but not SERPINC1 were closely related with progesterone receptor (PR) status of EOC patients, implying it might be involved in estrogen related process. Nevertheless, they were irregulated with others factors including age, BMI, menopause, tumor location, lymph node metastasis, and distant metastasis.

Exosomal EFEMP1 and SERPINC1 acted as diagnostic biomarker for EOC

Next, a ROC curve was employed to evaluate the diagnostic performance of exosomal EFEMP1 and SERPINC1 for EOC. As shown in Fig. 4A, the AUCs of exosomal EFEMP1 and SERPINC1 were 0.8071 (95 % CI: 0.7608 to 0.8534), and 0.8211 (95 % CI: 0.7755 to 0.8667), respectively. Moreover, the AUC could reach 0.8344 (95 % CI: 0.7896 to 0.8792) when they combined.

(A) The AUCs of exosomal EFEMP1 (left), SERPINC1 (mid), and their combination (right) in EOC patients relative to healthy donors; (B) The AUCs of EFEMP1 (left), SERPINC1 (mid), and their combination (right) in early stage EOC patients relative to healthy donors. AUC: Areas under the curve.

Similarly, ROC analysis of exosomal EFEMP1 and SERPINC1 in 29 patients with early-stage EOC patients and 163 healthy donors was used to determine diagnostic accuracy. As shown in Fig. 4B the AUC was 0.7963 (95 % CI: 0.6881 to 0.9045), and 0.8578 (95 % CI: 0.7762 to 0.9395), respectively, as well as 0.8627 (95 % CI: 0.7803 to 0.9451) when they combined. Taken together, these data suggest exosomal EFEMP1 and SERPINC1 in serum acted as diagnostic biomarker for early detection of tumors.

Exosomal EFEMP1 and SERPINC1 functioned in the differential diagnosis of EOC from benign ovarian tumors

We also evaluated the differential diagnostic accuracy of exosomal EFEMP1 and SERPINC1 via comparing malignant (n = 174) and benign (n = 28) patients who had the results of both CA-125 and human epididymis secretory protein 4 (HE4). The two exosomal proteins exhibited the AUCs of 0.7898 (95 % CI: 0.7116 to 0.8681), and 0.7742 (95 % CI: 0.7087 to 0.8398), respectively, as well as 0.8046 (95 % CI: 0.7405 to 0.8687) when they combined (Fig. 5A).

(A) The AUCs of exosomal EFEMP1 (left), SERPINC1 (mid), and their combination (right) in EOC patients relative to patients with benign ovarian tumor; The AUCs of exosomal EFEMP1 (B) or SERPINC1 (C) combined with CA-125 (left) and HE4 (mid), as well as the three combination (right) in EOC patients relative to benign ovarian tumor patients. (D) The combined AUC of CA-125 and HE4(left); and of additional joint with the two exosomal proteins (right) in EOC patients relative to benign ovarian tumor patients. AUC: Areas under the curve.

CA-125 and HE4 are the most widely used serum biomarkers for ovarian cancer detection, thereby they were employed into multimarker panels with the two exosomal proteins for the differential diagnosis between malignant and benign patients. As shown in Fig. 5B and Fig, S2, the AUCs of CA-125 and HE4 were elevated from 0.7373 to 0.8555, and from 0.8625 to 0.9184 by exosomal EFEMP1, respectively. The combined AUC of CA-125, HE4 and EFEMP1 was 0.9206; Similarly, the AUCs of CA-125 and HE4 were reached 0.8561 and 0.9302 when combined with exosomal SERPINC1, and their combined AUC was 0.9339 (Fig. 5C). Besides, the combined AUCs of the CA-125 and HE4 were 0.8633, and reached to 0.9372 when combined with the two exosomal proteins (Fig. 5D). Collectively, our findings suggest exosomal EFEMP1 and SERPINC1 can serve as novel serum biomarkers not only for EOC diagnosis but also for its differential diagnosis.

Exosomal proteins were corelated with coagulation index and LE-DVT

Exosomes in serum and plasma were different, thus we analyzed the expression of exosomal EFEMP1 and SERPINC1 in serum and plasma from the same patient. After paired statistical analysis, EFEMP1 seemed not different between serum and plasma, whereas SERPINC1 was significantly decreased in plasma compared to that in serum (Fig, 6A). This is because SERPINC1 is a glycoprotein that regulates blood coagulation [18], plasma is the liquid portion of unclotted blood after all the various cell types are removed. Besides, we found that exosomal SERPINC1 was correlated with coagulation indexes, including platelet count and fibrinogen (FIB) (P = 0.0015 and <0.0001, respectively; Fig. 6B-C). Importantly, exosomal SERPINC1 expression was different between LE-DVT (lower extremity deep venous thrombosis) positive and negative EOC patients, it was obviously lower in LE-DVT positive EOC patients (P = 0.0100; Fig. 6D), indicating exosomal SERPINC1 may have the potential to predict LE-DVT occurrence.

Discussion

EOC, the most common type of ovarian cancer, is the second cause of gynecologic cancer death in women around the world. The outcomes are complicated and largely depend on the timely diagnosis [19]. Thereby, sensitive and specific biomarkers to diagnose EOC patients are urgently needed, such as exosomes which have been well-established as diagnostic, prognostic, predictive or monitoring biomarkers. In the current study, we demonstrated EFEMP1 and SERPINC1 in exosomes are significantly upregulated and serve as the promising diagnostic biomarkers for EOC.

EFEMP1, an extracellular matrix glycoprotein, is widely expressed in several developing and adult tissues [20]. Its expression and role in tumor are complicated and even contradictory in different observation. For example, it has been reported EFEMP1 was increased in bladder cancer [21] and glioma [22] but decreased in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) [22] and breast cancer [23]. Besides, EFEMP1 can also act as an independent predictor. It was the potential biomarker for diagnosis and prognosis of osteosarcoma [24], and closely associated with adverse pathological features and worse outcomes in invasive urothelial carcinoma [25]. SERPINC1, a serine protease inhibitor in the coagulation cascade, can act as a tumor suppressor in the HCC [26] but promote colon cancer progression [27]. Serum SERPINC1 was reported to be a valuable marker for the prognosis of HCC patients [26] and a novel therapeutic strategy for patients with liver metastases from CRC (colorectal cancer) [28]. In our study, we demonstrated the up-regulated expression of exosomal EFEMP1 and SERPINC1 in EOC patients, possessing the favorable diagnostic accuracy.

This study identified exosomal EFEMP1 and SERPINC1 as novel biomarkers for EOC diagnosis. First, exosomal EFEMP1 and SERPINC1 were significantly elevated in EOC patients, the ROC curve analysis demonstrated they could distinguish patients from healthy donors, possessing favorable diagnostic efficiencies. More importantly, EOC is usually diagnosed at an advanced stage, thereby its early diagnosis is urgently needed. Our research demonstrated the higher expressions of exosomal EFEMP1 and SERPINC1 in early-stage patients than in healthy donors, which also possessed rather high early diagnostic efficiency. Moreover, exosomal EFEMP1 and SERPINC1 exerted the differential diagnostic accuracy to distinguish the malignant patients from the benign individuals. Taken together, our data suggest that exosomal EFEMP1 and SERPINC1 as novel biomarkers for EOC diagnosis.

Unlike EFEMP1, SERPINC1 displayed a decrease in the plasma compared that in serum. Plasma is the liquid portion of unclotted blood that is left behind after all the various cell types are removed. The most commonly used anticoagulants include heparin, ethylenediamine tetraacetic acid (EDTA) [29]. Heparin prevents coagulation by activating antithrombin III (ATIII) encoded by SERPINC1 [30], while EDTA exerts anticoagulation by chelating calcium ions, which in turn promote the heparin-catalyzed antithrombin III/thrombin reaction [31]. That can explain the decrease of SERPINC1 in plasma, indicating heparin/EDTA-anticoagulant plasma might be unsuitable for SERPINC1 detection. Moreover, SERPINC1 provides instructions for the production of ATIII, whose minor changes can increase the risk of thromboembolism [32]. Consistently, we found that exosomal SERPINC1 was correlated with coagulation indexes, including platelet count and FIB, and more importantly, was obviously lowe in LE-DVT positive EOC patients, indicating the potential for predicting LE-DVT occurrence.

Several limitations should be carefully taken into consideration in the present study. First, the current study enrolled 163 healthy donors and 183 EOC patients, the total sample sizes were relatively insufficient and might lack statistically vigorous power, more samples are needed for further validation. Second, long-term clinical follow-up data were also absent, thus we failed to analyze the prognostic value of two exosomal proteins for EOC. Finally, we were unable to obtain information of traditional tumor markers such as CA-125 and HE4 in the healthy donors, therefore, we could not analyze their combined performance for the diagnosis of EOC.

In summary, our findings revealed that exosomal EFEMP1 and SERPINC1 were significantly upregulated in EOC patients compared with those in healthy donors as well as in the benign patients, exhibiting favorable efficiency for EOC diagnostics and differential diagnostics, indicating the potential as the diagnostic biomarkers for EOC.

Quantitative records of paleotemperature in Qarhan Salt Lake, Qaidam Basin and its relationship with potassium deposits
Authors: Di Wang, Chenglin Liu, Lijian Shen, Yufei Hu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Qarhan Salt Lake, located in the Qaidam Basin on the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau in northwestern China, is the largest potash production base in China. The main potash deposit in Qarhan Salt Lake is a comprehensive deposit in which the liquid potassium resources dominated by potassium-rich and lithium-rich brine coexist with the low-grade solid potassium salt deposit composed of solid potash minerals (polyhalite, carnallite, sylvite). Polyhalite is one of the representative potassium minerals, widely distributed in the Bieletan area of Qarhan Salt Lake, coexisting with halite, and its deposition indicates that the paleo-temperature of brine in this area once fluctuated obviously. Twenty core samples from two boreholes in the Bieletan area of the Qarhan Salt Lake were subjected to fluid inclusions thermometry, and homogenization temperatures reflecting the brine temperatures during the precipitation of evaporite minerals in the Salt Lake were obtained, ranging from 12.4 to 28.9 °C and 9.2 to 19.8 °C, respectively. By scanning electron microscopy, acicular polyhalite and pompom-like aggregate of polyhalite have been identified in the sampling layer, indicating that evaporite deposition have reached the stage of potassium salt deposition. The temperature fluctuations of the fluid inclusions and the elemental chemical data are compared vertically, and the sections with significant temperature fluctuations are close to the layers where polyhalite were deposited. It shows that the paleo-temperature fluctuation impact the sedimentary environment of brine, and then affected the polyhalite deposition of Qarhan Salt Lake.

Introduction

Located on the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau in northwest China, the Salt Lake Qarhan in Qaidam Basinis one of the largest salt lake in China1 (Fig. 1), and climate conditions play key role in the formation of potash deposit in salt lakes. However, in these regions sedimentary environments with an extremely high salinity represented by extensive exist salt layers severely limit the applicability of traditional paleotemperature proxies2. Although consideratble studies of temperature variations during the late quaternary period have been conducted3–5, direct and quantitative climate records during this interval are still lack in Qarhan Salt Lake. Evaporites may retain a sensitive record of paleoenvironmental conditions, including environmental, climatic, and surface water chemistry5,6.Figure 1Schematic map of major area of Qarhan Salt Lake (modified from Zhang, 1987; Niu, 2014). Homogenization temperatures (Th) of halite fluid inclusions serve as indictor of water temperatures and approximate air temperatures without corrections for substantial pressure and provide direct and quantitative temperature inferences, thus widely used for environmental reconstructions of the geological past, especially in evaporitic environments where many other paleoclimate proxies are less appropriate2,7–9. Bieletan area is located in the west of Qarhan Salt Lake, where a large number of potash minerals are deposited, among which the most common is polyhalite10,11, The areas where potassium-bearing minerals were deposited are the most chemically evolved of all evaporites, so they are likely to preserve the most information about the ancient environment5. Recently, halite sedimentary textures composed of primary fluid inclusions can be well preserved and potash sediments were uncovered from the two boreholes GTB18 and GTB25 in Bieletan, thus providing unique opportunity for climate reconstruction and their relationship with the formation of potash deposit. The purpose of this study is to explore the influence of brine temperature change on salt mineral deposition through the combination of fluid inclusion homogenization temperature, geochemical analysis and mineralogical analysis experiments, so as to understand the evolution process of Qarhan Salt Lake more deeply. The borehole sample data from the Bieletan area reveals temperature fluctuations in brine during various stages of salt mineral deposition, leading to distinct chemical depositions within different temperature ranges. Consequently, we believe that paleotemperature fluctuations inducing changes in the brine environment hold significant implications for solid potassium salt deposit formation in Qarhan Salt Lake and offer a novel perspective on studying Quaternary evaporite mineral deposition in inland salt lakes across China.

Geological setting

Located in northwest China’s Qinghai Province, the Qaidam Basin is an enclosed non-marine basin with an area of about 120,000 km2 and an average sea altitude of 2800 m1,4. Qarhan Salt Lake is the largest salt lake in the Qaidam Basin, located in the central and eastern part of the Qaidam Basin (coordinates E94°00–E96°07, N36°40ʹ–N37°10ʹ, Fig. 1), 40–90 km from the Kunlun Mountains in the south and 18–32 km from the Qilian Mountains in the north. The Qarhan Salt Lake extends from northwest to southeast, covering an area of about 5800 km2, 170 km from east to west and 20–40 km from north to south. The typical climate of Qarhan Salt Lake is dry, little rainfall, large evaporation, large daily temperature difference, and the annual average temperature is only 5.3 ℃, the average annual precipitation is about 24.1 mm, and the average annual evaporation is over 3500 mm (Qarhan meteorologic station). The entire Qarhan Salt Lake is divided into four zones, from east to west: the Huobuxun area, Qarhan area, Dabuxun area, and Bieletan area1,10.

Since the middle Pleistocene, Sebei structure and Salt Lake structure in the north of Qarhan Salt Lake have been formed, while Yabaer structure has been formed from the late Pleistocene10,12. These structures are not obvious, the surface fluctuation is not big, and the dip angle of the two wings is small. The northwestern region of the Qarhan Salt Lake features upper Pleistocene and Holocene alluvium and diluvium, which lie in front of the Kunlun Mountains. The southern region of Qarhan Salt Lake features upper Pleistocene and Holocene alluvium and diluvium12. The exposed stratigraphy of Qarhan Salt Lake is mainly Holocene (Qh), followed by late Pleistocene (Qp). In the Holocene and late Pleistocene strata, evaporite deposits dominated by halite contain solid and liquid potassium magnesium salt deposits, which are divided into S1, S2 and S3 layers from top to bottom, separated by L1, L2 and L3 lacustrine clastic layers. The S1–S3 salt layer is mainly composed of halite, followed by gypsum and clastic minerals, and contains a small amount of potassium salt minerals such as carnallite, polyhalite and sylvite. The main minerals of L1–L3 clastic layer are silicate clastic minerals, followed by halite, gypsum and carbonate minerals12.

The GTB18 and GTB25 boreholes are industrial observation Wells in the northern part of Bieletan area for brine extraction in the area, so both are drilled to a depth of 20 m and all core samples are from quaternary strata. To avoid dissolution and reprecipitation of salt minerals in salt-bearing strata, the drilling mud was made with saturated brine extracted from nearby well that has already completed construction. The studied boreholes are 20 m in deep, and show a similar stratigraphic age of the late quaternary (14.9 ka and 15.7 ka, respectively, Fig. 2). The sedimentation rates of three boreholes CK2022, CK6 and CK1308 in similar locations in the study area were calculated by carbon 14 age13, and the sedimentation rates of GTB18 and GTB25 were calculated by comparing their lithology with the three boreholes, and the sedimentary rates are used for stratigraphic correlation (Fig. 2).Figure 2(a) Lithologic columns of borehole GTB18 and GTB25; (b) lithologic columns distribution and 14C age data in Bieletan area (modified from Liang and Huang, 1995); (c) correlation of borehole deposition rates in Bieletan area.

Research methods

Homogenization temperature of halite fluid inclusions

When the salt lake dries, the halite crystallization has two processes of accumulation and bottom growth8, and two types of primary fluid inclusions are usually captured in this halite: the cumulate crystals crystallized at the interface between the water surface and the atmosphere, and in chevron crystals formed at the bottom of the water14,15.

Among the halite crystals within the available range of core, GTB18 core 1.5 m, 2.5 m, 3.3 m, 4.1 m, 6.1 m, 7.3 m, 11.5 m, 14.3 m, 17.8 m, 18.9 m and GTB25 core 0.9 m, 2.9 m, 4.6 m, 5.8 m, 7.3 m, 10.7 m, 11.5 m, 13.5 m, 14.7 m and 16.5 m were analyzed for homogenization temperature. Primary fluid inclusion in cumulate crystals dominate these halite samples (Fig. 3), so the inclusion temperature data used in this paper are derived from cumulate crystals. The homogenization temperature of these single liquid inclusions can reflect the change of paleo-temperature of salt lake water2,7,8. In order to prevent the original temperature information in the fluid inclusions of halite from being damaged during the preparation of the inclusion sample, the traditional method of making thin sections of inclusions by machine cutting and polishing was abandoned, and the halite crystals were cut along the cleavage plane of the halite crystals with a blade, and then the cut halite flakes were treated in the same way. The purpose of using this thin section method is to obtain a halite cleavage slice with a thickness of less than 2 mm. The homogenization temperature was measured using a linkam THMSG600 cold and hot platform and observed using a Leica DM4500-P-LED polarizing microscope.Figure 3Primary fluid inclusions from borehole core samples in Bieletan area. The cooling nucleation method is proposed based on the property that fluid inclusions in sedimentary and diagenetic minerals are most commonly trapped as a single liquid phase during crystal growth14,16. Such fluid inclusions, formed at temperatures below about 50 ℃, can be chilled in a freezer to nucleate vapor bubbles whose homogenization temperature can be measured directly by heating the fluid inclusions until the nucleate vapor bubbles shrinks away6,8.

Homogenization temperature runs were undertaken in four phases, the freezing temperature parameter used is − 20 ℃, after the bubble appears, the gradient heating method is used to test. The first phase is the cooling from normal temperature to − 20 ℃, and the cooling rate is usually 15 ℃/min to achieve the purpose of rapid cooling into the subzero environment. The freezing time for the sample is more than 30 min, usually 1–2 h being the best in first phase. The second phase is from − 20 to 0 ℃, with a heating rate of 3 ℃/min; when the temperature reaches 0 ℃ and less than 15 ℃, the third phase generally adopts the heating rate of 0.5 ℃/min, which can be adjusted at different rates according to the feedback of samples and instruments, but in principle it does not exceed the heating rate of 2 ℃/min. In the fourth phase, when the temperature is above 15 ℃, the heating rate is adjusted to 0.1–0.2 ℃/min, and the actual adjustment is not more than 0.5 ℃/min (Table 1). Elemental geochemistry

Before the experiment, the core samples were pretreated with aqueous solution. The specific method was to dissolve 1 g core sample powder (more than 200 mesh) with 100 ml distilled water. The water-dissolved sample solution was diluted (25 times) and the chemical elements K+, Na+, Ca2+, Mg2+, Cl− and SO42− were analyzed using inductively coupled plasma Atomic emission spectrometry (ICP-AES). Geochemical analysis and homogenization temperature measurement of fluid inclusions were carried out at the MNR Key laboratory of Metallogeny and Mineral Assessment, Institute of Minerals Resources, Chinese Academy of Geological Sciences.

Scanning electron microscope analysis

The characteristics and residual structure of minerals are analyzed by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) to provide basic data for the inference of diagenetic environment and migration evolution history of minerals, which is an important basis for mineral identification17,18. The solid samples from two boreholes were made into thin slices and analyzed by scanning electron microscopy (TESCAN VEGA SEM). In order to ensure that the samples were not damaged as much as possible, the slices are replaced by manual grinding instead of mechanical manufacturing. SEM analysis was carried out at the Key Laboratory of Deep-Earth Dynamics, Institute of Geology, Chinese Academy of Geological Sciences, using the FEI Nova NanoSEM450.

Each homogenization temperature data point is marked in different layers, and the maximum homogenization temperature data is used to draw the temperature fluctuation curve. Analysis and discussion of experimental data results

Since the late Pleistocene, the Qarhan Salt Lake has experienced severe fluctuations of water desalination and salinization, and lake expansion and withdrawal1,3,22–25. In the past few decades, scholars have conducted paleoclimate related studies on the sediments of the salt lake in the Qaidam Basin and speculated that the lake water body has experienced the evolutionary stages of relatively stable evaporation in the early stage, low temperature drying in the middle stage and late (post glacial) stage in the past 50,000 years2,3,22,24,26. The mineral precipitation sequence formed by the evaporation of Qarhan Salt Lake brines is gypsum, anhydrite, halite, sylvite, carnallite, polyhalite, and bischofite1,5,27, and the order of precipitation of sylvite, carnallite and polyhalite is not absolute, they may have been deposited at the same time or at different times. Previous studies have shown that modern potash evaporites (carnallite-MgKCl3⋅6H2O) occur on the surface of the Qarhan Salt Lake plain, being extensively ephemeral deposit5. The formation of polyhalite depends on the calcium-rich inflow water and the mixing effect of salt lake brine1,19,20, so the formation stage of polyhalite may exist before or after the deposition of carnallite. The change in paleotemperature may have caused a change of the brine environment, allowing the polyhalite to be deposited in the salt-bearing formations. Our borehole sampling location is relatively close to the previous study area (Fig. 7), the XRD analysis results of a large number of shallow samples taken by the Chinese Academy of Geological Sciences in the Bieletan area in 2013, and the results of GTB18 and GTB25 boreholes are compared with the previous results (Table 4), indicating that there are a large number of polyhalite deposits in the salt-bearing strata in this area. Niu et al. (2015) showed that the deposit of polyhalite in the Bieletan area gradually decreased from west to east, and the solid potassium deposit in the western area near Senie Lake was mainly composed of polyhalite. In this study, we report the appearance of polyhalite aggregate (Fig. 6), which means that the evaporation and concentration of brine has undergone several stages of precipitation calcite–gypsum–halite–polyhalite. Although there is no obvious mineralogical evidence to support whether the stage of precipitation chloride (carnallite and bischofite) has been reached, the precipitation of polyhalite indicates that the area has at least undergone a stage of extreme drying. Using the homogenization temperature of fluid inclusions as an index parameter for paleotemperature fluctuations, we have obtained some effective research results in reflecting changes of brine temperature. In these boreholes homogenization temperature variation (Table 2), the average homogenization temperature of borehole GTB18 is 28.9 ℃ at the highest and 12.4 ℃ at the lowest. The temperature fluctuation is relatively stable at the depth of 2.5–4.1 m, and the homogenization temperature starts to rise significantly at the depth of 6.1 m, reaches the peak at the depth of 7.3 m, and reaches the lowest at the depth of 11.5 m. The average homogenization temperature of borehole GTB25 is 19.8 ℃ at the highest and 9.2 ℃ at the lowest. The temperature fluctuation is relatively stable at the depth of 10.7–11.5 m, and the average homogenization temperature in the depth from 0.9 to 7.3 m is fluctuates obviously, reaches the peak at the depth of 14.7 m, and reaches the lowest at the depth of 16.5 m. The geochemical analysis results show that the vertical peak fluctuation of potassium ion in GTB18 borehole core occurs in two horizons, 4.5–5.5 m and 6.3–6.7 m, respectively, and the vertical peak fluctuation of potassium ion in GTB25 borehole core occurs in three horizons, 5.1 m, 6.3–7.2 m and 16.5 m, respectively. In the XRD analysis results of the two boreholes, the polyhalite of the GTB18 borehole appeared at the depth of 4.50–4.70 m and 6.20–6.50 m with 5% and 10% content, respectively, and the polyhalite of the GTB25 borehole appeared at the depth of 5.10–5.20 m and 16.40–16.50 m with 25% and 10% content, respectively. The occurrence depth of polyhalite and its content in evaporite minerals are consistent with the results of previous XRD tests of ZCS2T2, ZCS2T4 and ZCS2T5 samples in this area (Table 4). By comparing the homogenization temperature of fluid inclusions with the results of chemical analysis and mineralogy analysis, it is found that the vertical variation of potassium ion near the zone where the temperature fluctuates obviously also fluctuates significantly, and polyhalite is more likely to be deposited as potassium minerals in the formation. Based on the results of these geological studies, it can be determined that the paleotemperature of the brine in Qarhan Salt Lake has a fluctuation range influenced by paleoclimate. Since fluid inclusions can record the temperature of brine crystallization, it is reasonable to use the homogenization temperature data of halite fluid inclusions as a parameter index of paleotemperature fluctuations. Under the background that there is a large amount of polyhalite in the solid potassium salt deposit in the Qarhan Salt Lake Bieletan area reported by predecessors, Our results further prove that the change of paleotemperature may have led to the change of brine environment, which caused a large amount of polyhalite to be deposited in the Bieletan salt-bearing strata to form the solid potassium salt deposit in this area.

Conclusion

Quantitative paleotemperature data of Bieletan area of Qarhan Salt Lake were established. The paleotemperature range of the brine in this area can be represented by the average homogenization temperatures of 12.4–28.9 °C and 9.2–19.8 °C. The minimum and maximum homogenization temperatures of GTB18 are 4.5 °C and 52.1 °C, and the both temperatures of GTB25 are 6.8 °C and 45.3 °C.

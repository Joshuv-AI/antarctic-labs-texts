Identification of novel biomarkers for hypertension and ventricular remodeling based on transcriptomics and machine learning
Authors: Zipeng Li, Bohao Zhang, Limeng Chao, Xin Tian, Wei Chen, Chang Liu, Hai Tian
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryVentricular remodeling (VR) associated with hypertension (HTN) is characterized by a complex molecular mechanism. In the context of clinical treatment, substantial challenges persist. A total of 12 differentially expressed genes were jointly obtained from different cohorts and weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA). Among them, genes associated with oxidative stress were screened out. Upon verification through learning machine, it was revealed that NR1H2 and MT1E are closely associated with the progression of HTN-VR. Their stability was validated using both internal and external datasets. The study presented the immune cell infiltration patterns associated with these key genes and the potential mechanisms underlying disease progression, which were further verified in mouse models. The research revealed that NR1H2 and MT1E serve as crucial risk markers for the progression of HTN and VR. These efforts are intended to offer valuable insights into the underlying mechanisms and uncover potential targets for clinical intervention.Subject areas: health sciences

Introduction

Hypertension (HTN) is a prevalent chronic cardiovascular disorder, with approximately 1.3 billion adults afflicted by it globally.1 The cardiac disease resulting from HTN is a leading contributor to various negative cardiovascular outcomes, posing a significant threat to public health.2,3,4 Ventricular remodeling (VR) refers to a series of alterations in the size, shape, ventricular wall thickness, and tissue architecture of the ventricle that occur as a result of myocardial injury or increased loading conditions. It represents a pathophysiological response process that is secondary to lesion repair and the overall compensatory mechanisms of the ventricle. VR caused by HTN is a common hypertensive heart disease, a process commonly considered as a compensatory hypertrophy that the heart undergoes to accommodate the elevated afterload resulting from HTN and to ensure sufficient ejection fraction.5 Nevertheless, many reports have substantiated that HTN can induce VR even in the absence of compensatory responses,6,7 which is termed pathological remodeling.8 However, the mechanism therein has not yet been elucidated. As it plays a significant role in global morbidity and mortality rates,9 revealing the specific molecular mechanism of the pathophysiological process of VR caused by HTN can provide an important theoretical basis for its prevention, diagnosis, and treatment.

As a prevalent etiological factor in VR applications,10,11 HTN has been substantiated to exert its pathogenic effects by modulating disease progression in distal organs.12,13,14 Furthermore, the entire pathophysiological process of HTN is intricately linked with the evolution of inflammation, underscoring its multifaceted role in disease development.15 More importantly, it can also induce VR by activating the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system, triggering the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines, endothelin-1, and transforming growth factor, which collectively contribute to the molecular mechanisms underlying disease progression.16 Collectively, these elements establish the pathophysiological foundation of VR. Through a series of intricate molecular and cellular events, they eventually culminate in cardiac fibrosis and subsequent functional impairment, a process that is of great significance in understanding the complex etiology and progression of VR-related cardiac pathologies.17

In this study, key genes that could potentially be used to construct a risk prediction model were identified through gene differential analysis of datasets and analyses such as weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA). Subsequently, machine learning techniques were employed to further screen for crucial risk markers of VR in the context of HTN. Moreover, the functions of these markers and their relationships with immune infiltration were investigated. Laboratory animal models are capable of recapitulating the disease progression trend of HTN-VR. To this end, we utilized norepinephrine to establish a mouse model of HTN-VR. We then measured the mRNA expression levels of key genes in peripheral blood leukocytes and the heart. Consequently, this research aims to delve into the risk markers associated with VR caused by HTN and identify potential therapeutic targets.

Results

Differential expression gene analysis

Figure 1 illustrates the overall workflow. To explore the mechanism of VR induced by HTN, we screened the transcriptome dataset GSE74144 from the GEO database. This dataset comprises peripheral blood leukocyte tissue 14 individuals with HTN and left ventricular hypertrophy, 14 hypertensive patients without ventricular hypertrophy, and 8 control subjects. We categorized the samples into a HTN group and a VR group (Figure 1).

The flowchart of this study

The dataset was partitioned into two parts, and the data were standardized (Figures 2A and 2B). After normalization, the limma package was employed to identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in both datasets. Through a comparison between the control group and the HTN group, we identified 238 genes that were differentially expressed (Figures 2C and 2D). On the other hand, we compared the datasets of the VR group and the control group, and a total of 465 DEGs were identified (Figures 2E and 2F).

Key module identification by WGCNA

To investigate the key genes related to VR caused by HTN, we utilized WGCNA to identify the important gene modules in the GSE74144 dataset. Based on the predefined topological criterion of the scale tree, the optimal soft threshold selected was 8 (Figure 3A), and this resulted in the identification of 21 unique gene modules, which formed their corresponding cluster dendrogram (Figure 3B). As shown by the heatmap linking HTN, VR, and gene modules (Figure 3C), the yellow gene module was the only one that exhibited a significant correlation with both groups at the same time (comprising 451 genes, p < 0.05). Moreover, by overlapping the DEGs and key genes identified through WGCNA analysis in peripheral blood leukocytes from both HTN and VR groups, we identified a total of 12 critical genes, which including TES, DOK2, MAPKAPK2, MT1E, GRK6, MT1B, SERINC3, NR1H2, REPS1, MYO1G, HLA-E, and SCARNA5 (Figure 3D).

KEGG of DEGs

To explore the potential functions of the screened DEGs in diseases, we utilized the “cluster-profiler” package to perform enrichment analysis on the Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathways of the key genes. In the GO analysis, we observed that metabolic pathways associated with metal ions, such as calcium and copper, were prominently highlighted. These pathways included detoxification of copper ion, stress response to copper ion, stress response to metal ion, cellular response to copper ion, response to copper ion, calcium-dependent protein kinase activity, calmodulin-dependent protein kinase activity, calmodulin binding, and calmodulin-dependent protein serine/threonine kinase activity. On the other hand, in the KEGG analysis, functional pathways such as mineral absorption, efferocytosis, cellular senescence, endocytosis, and vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) signaling pathway exhibited differences (Figures 4A–4D). Thus, these findings seem to indicate that copper and calcium ions might play a crucial role in the process of VR induced by HTN.

(E) Identification of key genes related to oxidative stress.

A wealth of evidence suggests that within the vascular microenvironment shaped by HTN, a persistent inflammatory response occurs alongside oxidative stress (OS).18,19,20,21 This OS acts both as a trigger and a result of the inflammatory process, playing a central role in the pathological response.22,23,24 As a result, we obtained the gene set related to OS from the GeneCards database (https://www.genecards.org/). Specifically, we conduct a search for “oxidative stress,” select the components of Protein Coding, RNA Gene, Functional Element, Pseudogene, Genetic Locus, and Gene Cluster, and then proceed to export this selected information. Finally, we overlapped it with the WCGNA module genes and the DEGs in the HTN and VR groups. This process led to the identification of 8 key genes: MAPKAPK2, SCARNA5, NR1H2, HLA-E, TES, MT1E, GRK6, and MT1B (Figure 4E).

Machine learning of key genes

Using the identified 8 key genes, both random forest and support vector machine (SVM)-recursive feature elimination (RFE) analyses were applied to identify critical genes within two dataset groups. The random forest method detected 7 important genes in the HTN dataset and 3 important genes in the VR dataset (Figures 5A–5D). In contrast, the SVM-RFE approach recognized 6 core genes in the HTN dataset and 8 core genes in the VR dataset (Figures 5E and 5F). Finally, a Venn diagram analysis was conducted to find the overlap among these gene subsets (Figure 5G), resulting in the identification of 2 critical genes (MT1E and NR1H2).

Immune infiltration analysis

The R package CIBERSORT was utilized to evaluate the relationship between core genes and immune cell infiltration. More specifically, MT1E showed a markedly negative association with naive B cells and resting dendritic cells (Figure 6A). In contrast, NR1H2 presented a significantly positive link with CD8 T cells and a notably negative connection with resting dendritic cells (Figure 6B). Following this, the analysis focused on evaluating the relationship of immune cell infiltration between the VR group and the normal group. After pooling the samples from GSE74144, GSE240921, and GSE24694, we compared the immune cell infiltration patterns between the control group and the VR group. The results demonstrated that the infiltration of Macrophages M0 and Neutrophils was significantly enhanced in the VR group (Figure 6C).

GSEA analysis of key genes

In addition, to explore the underlying functions they influence, we conducted gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) analysis on the two identified key genes and listed the top 5 upregulated and downregulated functional pathways related to them. The top 5 downregulated pathways linked to MT1E include adherens junction, extracellular matrix-receptor interaction, motor proteins, platelet activation, and primary immunodeficiency. The upregulated functional pathways encompass apoptosis—multiple species, glycosphingolipid biosynthesis—ganglio series, legionellosis, mucin type O-glycan biosynthesis, and tryptophan metabolism (Figures 7A and 7B). For NR1H2, the downregulated pathways involve ATP-binding cassette (ABC) transporters, ATP-dependent chromatin remodeling, cortisol synthesis and secretion, Gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) signaling pathway, and taste transduction. The upregulated functional pathways consist of asthma, biosynthesis of unsaturated fatty acids, citrate cycle (tricarboxylic acid [TCA] cycle), fatty acid elongation, and proteasome (Figures 7C and 7D).

Model stability verification

To delve deeper into the correlation between HTN and VR, we separately performed receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analyses on internal and external datasets. Within the internal GSE74144 dataset, the detection outcomes of these two genes were as follows: for NR1H2, the area under the curve (AUC) was 0.835; for MT1E, the AUC was 0.955 (Figure 8A). Furthermore, to validate the stability of the model, we downloaded additional datasets related to HTN (GSE28345, GSE28360, and GSE217007)25,26 and VR (GSE240921 and GSE249694)27,28 from the GEO database. The validation results were as follows: for the GSE28345 dataset, MT1E (AUC = 0.667) and NR1H2 (AUC = 0.75); for the GSE28360 dataset, MT1E (AUC = 0.8) and NR1H2 (AUC = 0.578); for the GSE217007 dataset, MT1E (AUC = 0.667) and NR1H2 (AUC = 0.667); for the GSE240921 dataset, MT1E (AUC = 0.603) and NR1H2 (AUC = 0.623); and for the GSE249694 dataset, MT1E (AUC = 0.667) and NR1H2 (AUC = 0.833). The situations of each dataset are shown in Table 1. Evidently, both NR1H2 and MT1E exhibit excellent specificity and sensitivity in the diagnosis of HTN and VR.

Validation of in vitro models

To construct a VR model, 7-week-old C57BL/6 mice were treated with norepinephrine. Following the intervention, in contrast to the control group, significant alterations were observed in the heart rate, systolic blood pressure and mean blood pressure of the mice in the intervention group; however, no notable difference was found in the diastolic blood pressure (Figure 9A). The hearts of the mice exhibited significant enlargement (Figure 9B), hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining accentuated the alterations in VR of mice. In the intervention group, the murine hearts exhibited interventricular septum thickening, an elevated number of inflammatory cells, and disorganized cell alignment, along with an increased incidence of necrosis and dissolution. Additionally, Masson staining indicated that myocardial fibrosis was substantially increased in the disease group compared to the control group, confirming the successful establishment of the model (Figure 9C). The western blot (WB) experiment revealed a significant upregulation of MYH7, atrial natriuretic peptide (ANP), and brain natriuretic peptide (BNP) proteins in the hearts of mice in the intervention group (Figure 9D). These findings suggest that the HTN-VR model in the mice of the intervention group was successfully established. To assess the stability of the predictive model, we observed a notable upregulation in the mRNA levels of MT1E and NR1H2 in both peripheral blood mononuclear cells and heart tissues of the VR group mice. Furthermore, we likewise noted an upregulation in the expression of MT1E and NR1H2 in the hearts of the model mice (Figure 9E). We generated the ROC standard curve using the measured gene transcription values (Figures 9F and 9G).

Discussion

At present, on a global scale, HTN is gradually becoming a major cause of cardiovascular diseases and premature death. Despite a decline in the global average blood pressure attributed to the widespread use of medications, the prevalence has been rising year after year.29 For individuals with pre-existing HTN, it is of utmost importance to retard the disease progression and prevent end-stage outcomes induced by HTN, or what may result in severe cardiovascular complications, thereby elevating both the incidence rate and mortality rate.30 In the case of patients, modifying unhealthy lifestyle habits, including high sodium intake, low-potassium diet, obesity, excessive, and alcohol consumption, represents an effective approach to the prevention and management of HTN.31 When these measures are complemented by the targeted prediction and intervention provided by transcriptomic analysis, it is possible to offer patients more accurate disease prediction tools and potential therapeutic strategies.

In this study, the dataset GSE74144 was analyzed using bioinformatics approaches. A total of 237 and 464 DEGs were respectively identified in the HTN cohort and the VR cohort through differential gene analysis. Moreover, 451 module-related genes were detected via WGCNA. By conducting a cross-analysis, the research spotlight was then placed on eight key genes associated with OS. The screening process was further optimized by means of machine learning techniques (including random forest analysis and SVM-based analysis). The credibility of the model was verified through ROC curve analysis. Eventually, the key genes MT1E and NR1H2 were successfully screened out.

Interestingly, both MT1E and NR1H2 appear to hold substantial potential research significance in the context of cardiovascular diseases. Metallothionein (MT) is a protein that is rich in cysteine.32 Besides playing a crucial role in processes such as the absorption, storage, and transportation of metals,33,34 it also appears to be a protective agent against oxidative and inflammatory injuries.35,36,37,38 The current research findings suggest that arsenic exposure can suppress the mRNA transcription of the MT1 gene, which significantly inhibits cardiac development.39 Mice deficient in Mt1/2 display cardiac dysfunction during exercise, accompanied by the emergence of cardiac fibrosis, vascular rarefaction, and functional deterioration.40 However, research specifically focusing on the association between MT1E and cardiovascular diseases remains scarce in the literature. The NR1H2 serve as a pivotal regulator of macrophage function, governing the transcriptional programs implicated in lipid homeostasis and inflammation.41,42,43 As a gene involved in cholesterol efflux, it is closely associated with cardiovascular diseases.44 Metformin elicits an adenosine 5'-monophosphate (AMP)-activated protein kinase (AMPK)-dependent and ATF1-dependent upregulation of NR1H2 in both murine bone marrow-derived macrophages and human blood-derived macrophages, thereby suppressing atherosclerosis in mice.45 Yan et al. found that controlling Rnd3 expression by modulating the binding of the NR1H2 and Rnd3 promoters, and regulating fibroblast-specific activation, can prevent cardiac remodeling in dilated cardiomyopathy.46 Teruyoshi et al. discovered that hyperglycemia activates NR1H2 to inhibit macrophage activation, which is the key to diabetes suppressing aneurysms47; these investigations have established the pivotal role of NR1H2 in the course of VR. In the present study, we discovered that the expressions of NR1H2 and MT1E were upregulated in the mouse model of HTN combined with VR, indicating that the combination of NR1H2 and MT1E holds potential diagnostic significance.

Our study aimed to explore the pathological mechanisms underlying the development of VR and HTN patients through bioinformatics analysis of transcriptomic data and to construct a predictive model. We delimited the scope of key genes through gene differential analysis and WGCNA. Moreover, we verified previous reports using KEGG analysis. These results enlighten that the OS environment induced by HTN accelerates the progression of VR.48 Regulating the equilibrium of energy and redox homeostasis is of paramount importance for mitigating pathological myocardial remodeling.49 During OS, reactive oxygen species produced by mitochondria promote cellular damage, which is an important mechanism leading to VR.50,51 Consequently, we focus on directed toward genes associated with OS functions. Notably, we identified that reactions associated with calcium and copper ions are intricately involved in this process, thereby warranting further investigation. The dysregulation of macronutrient and micronutrient homeostasis holds substantial significance in the evaluation and management of HTN and adverse VR.52 Modulating copper transport in patients with HTN can effectively ameliorate myocardial remodeling and cardiac dysfunction,53 and impairments in calcium transport and calcium dysregulation represent crucial targets for alleviating cardiac injury and structural alterations.54,55 In addition, we observed a strong association with the endoplasmic reticulum during the disease progression, suggesting that this organelle may be a central player in the pathological development. Previous studies have highlighted the link between endoplasmic reticulum alterations and cardiac structural remodeling56,57,58 and prompted its connection with metal ion metabolism.58,59 The disruption of calcium homeostasis triggers endoplasmic reticulum stress.60,61 The calcium/copper bimetallic amplifier, via cascade-enhanced endoplasmic reticulum stress and mitochondrial dysfunction, is employed for inducing programmed cell death in cancer treatment.62 GSEA suggested that the key genes are associated with functions including energy metabolism, glycolipid metabolism, and programmed cell death. These pathways may represent potential mechanisms by which the key genes influence disease progression, which also constitutes the objective of our subsequent research.

Through DEGs analysis and machine learning techniques, we were able to delineate a specific scope. Our results highlighted that NR1H2 and MT1E are pivotal genes in constructing a predictive model for VR secondary to HTN.

These genes may likewise serve as potential targets for the treatment and management of HTN and VR. To validate these findings, we utilized norepinephrine to establish an in vivo mouse model of HTN/VR. The reliability of the model was verified in peripheral blood leukocytes. It is worth noting that similar results were also observed in cardiomyocytes, which indicates the direction for the subsequent research.

However, our study is still subject to numerous limitations. First and foremost, the disease prediction model established through bioinformatics analysis in this research is confronted with the issue of insufficient sample size. Moreover, in our basic experimental validation, we developed a mouse disease model of HTN-VR and carried out molecular validation. Nevertheless, there remains a lack of validation of the model’s prediction regarding the progression from HTN to VR. Specifically, it is necessary to proving the stability of the model in predicting the occurrence of VR within the hypertensive population. Finally, with respect to the detailed mechanisms underlying disease progression, corresponding experimental evidence is lacking. The precise mechanism underlying the disease progression remains elusive. In future investigations, we intend to carry out more in-depth research and more comprehensive experimental validations to bridge this knowledge gap and further confirm our findings and hypotheses. In summary, our research offers valuable understanding into the molecular mechanisms underlying HTN and VR, offering potential therapeutic targets for future treatment approaches.

Conclusion

In conclusion, through bioinformatics and machine learning approaches, two OS-related crosstalk genes, NR1H2 and MT1E, were identified within the HTN and VR cohorts. The stability of the model was validated using external datasets. In vivo experiments successfully replicated the progression of HTN-VR disease and confirmed the expression profiles of these genes. This research not only offers a novel perspective for comprehending the progression of HTN-VR disease but also presents potential therapeutic targets, thereby contributing to the advancement of related research and treatment strategies.

Method details

The GSE74144 dataset was retrieved from the GEO database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). It encompassed the peripheral blood leukocyte samples of 14 patients with pure hypertension, 14 patients with both HTN and ventricular remodeling, and 8 control individuals.

The dataset was divided into two groups: the HTN group and the VR group. Following batch correction, differentially expressed genes (DEGs) within the dataset were detected using the R package “limma”63 and subsequently visualized with the aid of the R packages “ggplot2”64 and “pheatmap”.

Identifying the module genes of HTN and VR through WGCNA

WGCNA is a powerful tool for genomics analysis, enabling the grouping of closely related genes. We utilized the complete dataset GSE74144 as the original matrix and categorized the samples into the control group, HTN group, and VR group. Through hierarchical clustering, genes were classified into distinct modules. Subsequently, based on the variation in gene expression profiles, the soft threshold was set to 0.8 to construct a weighted gene co-expression network. By identifying the differences in gene expression patterns and applying correlation analysis with correlation coefficients, genes were organized into various modules, which facilitates the identification of potential key biomarkers. We selected all gene modules with a p-value < 0.05 to participate in the screening of key genes.

Functional enrichment analysis of Gene Ontology (GO) and genes and genomes (KEGG)

The R package “clusterprofiler”65 was utilized to conduct Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analyses on the key genes. This was done to investigate the primary pathological mechanisms involved in the disease progression.

Machine learning

To more precisely pinpoint the central genes from HTN to VR and enhance the model’s development, we performed two distinct machine learning analyses. Specifically, we utilized the random forest algorithm (RF) and support vector machine (SVM) for selecting pivotal genes in HTN and VR contexts. In detail, when conducting further screening of 12 genes using RF analysis, we selected genes with an importance value exceeding 0.8 as potential candidates for biomarkers. Subsequently, when performing an analysis of these 12 genes using SVM based on two disease cohorts, we chose the identified core genes to participate in the subsequent analysis. The key genes of HTN and VR were identified via independent cross-analyses using RF and SVM, with their intersecting results being designated as the critical genes linking HTN and VR.

Immune cell infiltration analysis

The R package “CIBERSORT”66 was employed to evaluate the infiltration status of immune cells associated with key genes. Based on the infiltration dataset “20240424mmdata1.Rdata”,66 the immune cell infiltration between the VR group and the Control group was contrasted, along with the relationship between individual genes and immune cell infiltration.

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) of key genes

The single-gene GSEA analysis for key genes was carried out using the R package “limma” to investigate the possible functions that these key genes may play during the course of disease development.

Experimental model and study participant details

Twenty male C57BL/6 experimental mice at 7 weeks of age were procured from the Animal Experiment Center of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Harbin Medical University. The experimental protocol was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Harbin Medical University No: SYDW2025-104.

All mice were randomly allocated into the model group and the control group. These mice were housed in a Specific Pathogen Free (SPF) environment, maintained under a 12-h light/dark cycle, with the temperature kept between 22°C and 24°C and humidity at 50%–60%. Following their acquisition, the mice underwent one week of environmental adaptation before being subcutaneous injected with 2.0 mg/kg norepinephrine over a period of 15 days. Mice in the control group received two daily injections of normal saline. This procedure was conducted to establish a ventricular remodeling model. Upon successful model establishment, the mice were euthanized, their peripheral blood was collected, Peripheral blood leukocytes of mice were isolated using a peripheral blood lymphocyte separation medium. The isolated cells were then cryopreserved at −80 °C for subsequent use, and their hearts were either perfused and stored at −80°C or fixed using 4% paraformaldehyde for subsequent use.

Western Blotting of mouse heart tissue

The mouse hearts were ground into a powdery state using liquid nitrogen. Ripa lysis buffer (manufactured by Seven Company in China) was employed, which was mixed with Phenylmethanesulfonyl fluoride at a ratio of 100:1 to lyse the powdered mouse heart tissue. After sufficient lysis, sample was boiled at 100 °C for 15 min for denaturation. Following separation by SDS-PAGE electrophoresis, different protein samples were transferred onto polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membranes. After incubation with ANP (Proteintech, U.S.), BNP (abcam, U.S.) Myh7 (Proteintech, U.S.), Beta Tubulin (Proteintech, U.S.) and second antibodies (Proteintech, U.S.), the immune-reactive bands were developed using chemiluminescence (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Subsequently, image analysis was conducted with the aid of ImageJ software. β-tubulin protein (Proteintech, UK) was used as an internal control.

Real-time quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR) of mouse heart and peripheral blood leukocytes

RNA extraction from the hearts and white blood cells of mice was performed using the SevenFast Total RNA Extraction Kit (SEVEN BIOTECH, China). Subsequently, cDNA synthesis was conducted with the ABScript II cDNA First-Strand Synthesis Kit (ABclonal Technology, China) in accordance with the instruction manual. For the quantitative analysis of target genes, the FastStart Universal SYBR Green Master Mix was utilized, and DNA amplification was performed via the LightCycler480 system. To measure the relative abundance of the mRNA of key genes, the delta-delta Ct (ΔΔCt) method was adopted, and an internal reference control was set for comparison. The PCR reaction conditions were as follows: Initially, an initial denaturation was carried out at 95 °C for 3 min. Subsequently, a 40-cycle annealing-extension program was implemented. Each cycle included a 5-s denaturation at 95 °C and a 30-s annealing at 60 °C. Finally, the melting stage was followed by a final extension step. NR1h2 primer sequence is Forward primer:CATCGTGGTCATCTTAGAGCC; Reverse primer:GTTGTAGTGGAAGCCCGAAG, and MT1E primer sequence is Forward primer: AGAGTGAGTTGGGACACCTTG; Reverse primer: TCCCCGAGACAATACAATGGC.

Histological staining

Upon obtaining the hearts of the mice, the paraffin-embedded cardiac tissues were sliced into 5 μm thick sections for dewaxing and rehydration processes. These sections were subsequently dehydrated using alcohol and cleared with xylene. Following this, Masson trichrome staining (Solarbio, China) and hematoxylin-eosin staining (Solarbio, China) were performed, allowing for the observation of histopathological changes in the tissues.

Quantification and statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism 9.0 and ImageJ. For statistical testing of differences between two groups, a two-tailed t-test was employed. For statistical testing of differences among multiple groups, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was utilized. A p-value of less than 0.05 was regarded as indicating statistical significance.

An improved multi-objective animated oat optimization algorithm for resource-constrained construction project organization design
Authors: Qing Xue, Chunjiang Wu, Jiyang Nie, Shijie Zhou, Hui Liu, Vasilios N. Katsikis
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Global urbanization has promoted to an increasing scale of construction projects, thereby making the optimization of construction project organization design a critical task in engineering management. However, conventional methods relying on empirical decision-making suffer from issues like low resource allocation efficiency, many difficulties in coordinating multi-objective conflicts and insufficient dynamic adjustment capabilities. To address these issues, we propose the first multi-objective extension of the Animated Oat Optimization algorithm (MOAOO), which represents a pioneering contribution in transforming the single-objective AOO into a multi-objective optimizer for construction project organization design. The developed algorithm fundamentally extends the biological mechanism of Animated Oat Optimization introducing several key innovations: (a) a novel hybrid position update rule combining Elite Reference Points and stochastic perturbations to prevent premature convergence; (b) an innovative three-layer constraint processing mechanism ensuring the generation of feasible solutions; and (c) a dual-threshold convergence monitoring system for early termination. Notably, we establish MOAOO as the inaugural multi-objective variant of AOO, integrating dynamic elite retention strategies, non-dominated sorting, and dynamic archive mechanisms to enable effective collaborative optimization of three conflicting goals. Enough experiments on ZDT test functions demonstrate that the designed MOAOO method shows competitive performance compared to advanced algorithms such as Pre-DEMO, MOEA/D-OED, and Pi-MOEA in terms of hypervolume inverted generational distance and the Spacing metrics. The indicator is improved in certain configurations. In an engineering case study, MOAOO reduces resource fluctuation by 72.7% in the compromise solution while achieving a balanced duration (279 days) and cost ($1.34 M). Moreover, the proposed algorithm converges in 118 iterations on average, thereby verifying its practical value in construction scheduling.

Introduction

The rapid pace of global urbanization and the expansion of construction project scales have intensified the need for optimized construction project organization design, which is a core aspect of engineering project management. Early methods such as Gantt charts and critical path analysis relied heavily on manual experience, leading to three key limitations: inefficient resource allocation, inability to resolve multi-objective conflicts, and lack of dynamic adjustment capabilities1,2. These shortcomings have led to project delays and cost overruns, especially in complex urban construction. Although meta-heuristics offer optimization potential, they suffer from slow convergence and local optima traps in resource-constrained scenarios3. It requires novel algorithms that combine dynamic multi-objective balancing with real-world constraints.

In recent years, the rapid advancement in artificial intelligence and metaheuristic algorithms has developed numerous new technical paradigms for construction project organization design. Metaheuristic algorithms achieve global search by simulating natural phenomena, avoiding the local optima inherent in traditional methods, and they also support customizable multi-objective weights and generate Pareto solution sets. Heuristic algorithms and their variants, derived from Genetic Algorithms (GA)3, Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO)4, and Simulated Annealing (SA)5 et al.—have been progressively applied to construction scheduling, resource allocation, risk control, and project management. Some new heuristic algorithms are also constantly being proposed. Inspired by the antenna function of long-horned beetles in nature and the search behavior of the beetles ‘random walking mechanism, Jiang and Li proposed a new algorithm called the Beetle Antenna Search Algorithm6. Khan et al.7 formulated quantum beetle antennae search, a meta-heuristic optimization algorithm and variant of Beetle Antennae Search, and used it for constrained combinatorial optimization problems. In addition, Khan et al.8 also optimized the search process of the Beetle Antennae Search Algorithm using adaptive force moment estimation and update rules, thus improving the algorithm’s local search capabilities. Studies integrating non-dominated sorting (NDS) into Rao-1 and Rao-2 algorithms9 have addressed multi-objective time–cost trade-offs in small-to-medium-scale construction scheduling10. Lin et al.11 proposed two PSO(Particle Swarm Optimization)-based models (RC-APSO and iRC-APSO) for solving static and dynamic resource-constrained problems in building projects. Zuo et al.12 developed a methodology for allocating and scheduling limited risk-related resources, enabling effective risk response strategies for project managers. Behera and Sobhanayak13 introduced a hybrid GWO(Grey Wolf Optimizer)-GA algorithm for multi-objective task scheduling in cloud computing, significantly reducing makespan, energy consumption, and cost. Pham et al.14 optimized ready-mix concrete truck scheduling by integrating Grey Wolf Optimizer (GWO)15 and Dragonfly Algorithm16, thereby enhancing supply chain coordination between batching plants and construction sites. Zohrehvandi et al.17 formulated a multi-objective mathematical model incorporating client satisfaction and employed the NSGA-II(Non-dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm II) metaheuristic18 to resolve trade-offs. Wefki et al.19 established an innovative framework integrating BIM software, genetic algorithms, 5D models, and BI dashboards. Luo et al.20 proposed a novel multi-objective particle swarm algorithm, which improves optimization efficiency through archived angle division and dynamic update strategies. Chen et al.21 proposed a multi-objective particle swarm optimization algorithm with task allocation and archive-guided mutation strategies. By assigning different evolutionary tasks to particles with different characteristics, it is used to solve the problem of low search efficiency in traditional algorithms. Saad et al.22 extended the Pareto optimizer to the multi-objective parrot optimizer, verifying the reliability of the multi-objective parrot optimizer in global and engineering optimization problems. Kalita et al.23 enhanced their elite non-dominated sorting and congestion distance mechanisms by introducing a multi-objective exponential distribution optimizer, and used them for global optimization and practical engineering design problems. In order to solve the problem of complex objective functions and mixing continuous and discrete design variables and various design constraints in engineering optimization, Brajević et al. proposed a Hybrid Sine Cosine Algorithm24. However, existing algorithms still face challenges in high-dimensional, nonlinear, and dynamically disrupted construction scenarios, such as slow convergence and susceptibility to local optima traps25. Wang et al.26 demonstrated that AOO(Animated Oat Optimization)’s dual-phase framework achieves superior robustness in high-dimensional problems. Integrating AOO with construction scheduling thus holds significant potential for multi-objective optimization, which is demonstrated in this engineering case study.

Modern engineering projects require the coordination of multiple objectives, including project duration, cost, quality, resource fluctuation, and sustainability. The flow-line construction to reduce duration and cost27 and the BIM-based comprehensive optimization of construction elements28, demonstrate the necessity of multi-objective synergy. However, these methods struggle to quantify nonlinear interdependencies among objectives. Algorithmic models can achieve multi-objective equilibrium through Pareto frontier analysis29. Furthermore, uncertainties (e.g., weather, supply chain disruptions, policy changes) necessitate dynamic adjustments to construction plans, making real-time monitoring via big data and algorithmic iteration indispensable. Chattapadhyay et al.30 employed machine learning for risk identification, assessment, and prediction in large-scale construction projects. In research by Hospedales et al.31, the application potential of meta-learning in the field of intelligent construction was investigated. Meta-learning provides the possibility of solving projects that traditional machine learning algorithms cannot solve. In addition, machine learning projects in construction engineering can also be defined on deep sets32, and a series of research has been conducted on such a paradigm. Palakonda et al.33 presented the first comprehensive comparative study investigating the impact of 25 single- and multi-objective metaheuristic algorithms, alongside various objective function formulations and encoding schemes, on the performance of network pruning for convolutional neural networks. Tursunboev et al.34 proposed a novel multi-objective optimization framework for heterogeneous federated learning (MOHFL) that employs the NSGA-II algorithm to simultaneously optimize client model sizes and selection ratios, effectively balancing global model accuracy and communication costs. In the multi-task environment of project scheduling, an imitation learning system that can be continuously improved through autonomous data collection is crucial, especially in areas such as construction engineering that rely more on expert experience35,36.Some commercial tools such as Primavera P6 and MS Project support basic resource balancing, but rely on heuristics with limited multi-objective optimization capabilities19. BIM integrated platforms, such as Autodesk HUD combined with Navisworks, support visual scheduling but lack embedded meta-heuristic optimizers for Pareto solution generation, so custom algorithm extensions are required28.

To address these challenges, we propose an improved Animated Oat Optimization algorithm (MOAOO, Multi-Objective Animated Oat Optimization) for multi-objective optimization of construction project organization design in small residences. Unlike commercial software relying on predefined rules, MOAOO generates adaptive Pareto frontiers via bio-inspired mechanisms, enabling dynamic trade-offs among duration, cost, and resource stability. Animated oat optimization algorithm

The Animated Oat Optimization (AOO) algorithm26 is a novel metaheuristic whose computational model diverges from conventional bio-inspired algorithms. Inspired by the alternating rolling and catapult movements of wild oat seeds in hygroscopic media, it innovatively establishes a synergistic computational architecture with two tiers: extensive (global) search and intensive (local) optimization. The design organically unifies exploratory optimization and precise convergence mechanisms within a unified operational framework through biomimetic principles. The algorithm comprises four phases: initialization, biological parameter mapping, exploration, and exploitation. Global and local search strategies in metaheuristic algorithms

The Traveling Salesman Problem (TSP) holds significant theoretical and practical value in combinatorial optimization38. Exact algorithms (e.g., branch-and-bound) can find globally optimal solutions through exhaustive enumeration, but their computational complexity grows exponentially with problem scales—especially when the number of cities increases, leading to significant performance bottlenecks. For large-scale TSP instances, such methods rarely converge to optimal solutions within polynomial time. In contrast, metaheuristics employ efficient search strategies tailored to problem characteristics, leveraging heuristic rules to conduct directed exploration within the solution space and progressively approximate optimality. By integrating global exploration, local exploitation, and neighborhood construction mechanisms, these algorithms substantially reduce computational resource consumption while also preserving solution quality, thereby obtaining near-optimal solutions under limited time budgets39. The balance between efficiency and precision establishes metaheuristics as the preferred approach for complex TSP instances.

Global and local search strategies constitute effective methodologies for TSP resolution. For global search, Lévy flight37- a heavy-tailed distribution-based random walk—and non-dominated sorting40- solution screening technique for multi-objective optimization- represent critical strategies for enhancing the balance between local exploitation and global exploration. Lévy flight simulate non-uniform step-length jumping patterns in natural foraging behaviors (e.g., albatross flight paths), achieving dynamic balance between localized fine-grained searches via short steps and long jumps escaping local optima. Mathematically, step generation follows Eq. (3). Fractional dynamics equations describe its anomalous diffusion behavior, and fractal dimensions linked to parameter β explain non-local jumping characteristics in biological foraging paths. Non-dominated sorting, a core multi-objective screening method, stratifies solutions via dominance relations and diversity preservation mechanisms to approximate Pareto frontier: Solution A dominates B iff A is not worse than B in all objectives and strictly better in at least one. Solutions are partitioned into non-dominated layers iteratively, starting with the first non-dominated layer.

For local search, elite memory mechanisms41 dynamically store high-quality historical solutions to build a traceable “knowledge repository” (elite pool), preventing loss of elite solutions during iterations. The core of this approach is the survival-of-the-fittest principle, with current optimal individuals directly propagating to the next generation, thereby mitigating performance degradation caused by stochastic operations. Archive mechanisms42, a key multi-objective optimization technique, dynamically maintain non-dominated solution sets by pruning dominated candidates while simultaneously retaining high-quality solutions. Diversity metrics (e.g., hypervolume or spacing) ensure convergence-diversity balance when approximating Pareto frontier.

Resource constraints

Resource consumption per period should not exceed the available capacity. The formulation establishes the fundamental temporal framework for project completion, serving as the baseline against which optimization objectives are measured. For this framework, the minimization of makespan is established as a core optimization objective, representing a classical target in project scheduling that significantly influences project delivery timelines and associated costs.

For an RCPSP instance to be feasible, both temporal constraints and resource constraints must be satisfied, and the total cost includes direct costs and indirect costs44. Construction project scheduling needs to deal with the DAG dependence logic shown in Fig. 1, and reverse adjustment may easily lead to infeasible solutions. Classical heuristic algorithms such as NSGA-II struggle to reconcile time, cost, and resource fluctuation objectives45, and it is urgent to combine biological heuristic mechanisms to enhance feasibility.

Furthermore, the Multi-mode Resource Constrained Project Scheduling Problem (MRCPSP) extends traditional RCPSP. In MRCPSP models, each activity offers multiple execution modes; Each mode corresponds to specific durations and resource requirements, enabling dynamic selection of activity modes and attributes during scheduling45. For project scheduling with reallocatable resources, Time–Cost Tradeoff Problem (TCTP) is a core optimization challenge in project management46. Its fundamental principle balances project duration and cost through resource allocation adjustments: On one hand, increased resource investment raises costs but can compress the project schedule by shortening the critical path duration; on the other hand, extending duration reduces resource expenditure and conserves budgets. Tradeoffs are resolved under linear or nonlinear assumptions via heuristic optimization. Mozhdehi et al.47 developed a resource-constrained scheduling model, with resource allocations varying within bounds to influence costs and typically assuming fixed task durations. Yang et al.48 introduced a continuous nonlinear resource allocation framework with dedicated algorithmic solutions. There are also some studies that use machine learning and reinforcement learning for solving the RCPSP problem. Sung et al. described resource allocation as a Markov process and use deep reinforcement learning algorithms to find the best resource allocation strategy49; Du et al. proposed a new multi-agent deep reinforcement learning architecture is used for solving resource limited management problems in 6G in-X Subnetworks50. The scheduling model proposed herein is a resource-availability-constrained, continuous-resource-allocation variant of RCPSP.

Although multi-objective RCPSP in engineering falls under combinatorial optimization, its topology shows Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) properties—fundamentally distinct from the Hamiltonian cycles in Traveling Salesman Problems (TSP), and it also guarantees construction logic feasibility. Specifically, duration minimization under resource constraints faces dual limitations: resource supply restrictions curtail critical paths; process logic and construction sequences impose rigid network dependencies. Unlike TSP’s complete graph structure, any reverse adjustment in such rigid networks may violate logical constraints, yielding infeasible solutions. Consequently, heuristic algorithms require adaptive enhancements (e.g., Lévy flight or non-dominated sorting) with strengthened process-constraint validation and resource-conflict detection mechanisms to ensure schedule feasibility by optimizing duration.

2.4. Research gaps and corresponding contributions

Regarding the research direction of this paper, existing research has some limitations. In terms of algorithm limitations, the improved goal in this paper, AOO algorithm, is a single-objective algorithm and does not support multi-objective optimization26; while some scholars have improved the classic single-objective algorithm to a multi-objective algorithm (such as MOPSO), there is a problem of slow convergence under resource constraints 42. In terms of model limitations, RC-APSO does not consider resource fluctuation targets11, and TCTP models ignore dynamic adjustments46. In terms of engineering applicability, most commercial tools rely on heuristic rules and lack Pareto’s cutting-edge generation capabilities19. Several multi-objective variants of classical algorithms such as MOPSO and NSGA-II exist, they frequently suffer from slow convergence or premature stagnation in highly constrained environments42. In contrast, AOO’s dual-phase framework offers a naturally balanced search dynamic, which we hypothesize can be effectively leveraged for multi-objective optimization through the integration of non-dominated sorting and elite archiving mechanisms. Thus, the transformation of AOO into a multi-objective optimizer (MOAOO) addresses a methodological gap, which also explores the applicability of a novel bio-inspired mechanism in complex engineering contexts. The MOAOO algorithm proposed in this paper combines AOO biological mechanisms and dynamic elite libraries to resolve multi-objective conflicts; triple objective functions collaboratively optimize time–cost-resource fluctuations, and three-level constraint processing ensures the generation of feasible solutions, which is better than penalty function method2.

Construction project organization design problem modeling

The proposed model addresses three objectives: duration minimization, cost minimization and resource fluctuation minimization, subject to constraints defined in this section.

Formulation of the objective function

The proposed method is a resource-constrained project scheduling (RCPS) model with continuous resource allocation and limited resource availability. The parameters of the model come from a real small residential building project in Chengdu, China. The average daily labor force availability for the project ranges from 100 to 150 workers, and the task dependence follows China’s national standard GB/T 50,901–2013. Resource fluctuation penalties are calibrated based on contractor surveys assessing overtime productivity losses. For schedule compression, minimizing total construction duration necessitates optimizing planning arrangements while simultaneously satisfying resource constraints. The specific optimization principle operates as follows: Starting from the earliest start times of all activities, resource usage is verified period by period. When resource conflicts occur at any time, it is assumed that the start time of the corresponding activity will be delayed by one day to eliminate resource overallocation. The one-day incremental adjustment aligns with industry practices for daily resource reallocation in construction scheduling, minimizing disruption while ensuring feasible resource utilization per work shift. Repeat the above process until all periods satisfy resource constraints, ultimately yielding the project duration. Regarding cost control, project costs are directly correlated with benefits. Maximizing benefits requires simultaneous consideration of schedule-related and cost-related objectives. Classical cost-based objectives focus on minimizing total project costs, yet few studies treat cost as an independent research subject. Most research incorporates it as one element in multi-objective optimization problems, with literature exploring time–cost trade-offs predominating and forming the Time–Cost Trade-off Problem (TCTP). This work extends the foundation by incorporating resource fluctuation as an objective and solving a triple-objective model (time, cost, resource fluctuation). Four solution types were selected from the obtained Pareto solution set: the minimum-duration solution, minimum-cost solution, minimum-resource-fluctuation solution, and compromise solution, to replace traditional single-objective optimization models in comparative analysis. The objective of minimizing total costs

The total cost for a project comprises two components: direct costs and indirect costs. Generally, an inverse relationship exists between activity duration and direct costs, as compressing the duration necessitates increasing the quantity of machinery and workforce allocated to relevant activities, thereby elevating the project’s direct costs. Conversely, direct costs decrease with prolonged project duration. However, under certain conditions, increasing direct costs may not directly lead to duration compression. When duration compression reaches a threshold constrained by workface limitations or project structural specifics, additional expenditures fail to enable resource augmentation for further compression, rendering duration reduction unachievable. To simplify this complex duration-dependent constraint and align with the human resource component in the resource fluctuation minimization objective, we quantify total costs as the summation of human resource expenditures (variable costs) and fixed overheads per task (defined as the sum of direct and indirect costs, with identical fixed overhead assumed for each task). Following simplified Life-Cycle Cost (LCC) principles and prior resource-constrained scheduling studies11,44, the total project cost is defined as the sum of variable labor expenditures and task-level fixed overheads. The proposed model balances computational tractability with real-world cost drivers. MOAOO adapts the single-objective Animated Oat Optimization (AOO) algorithm into a multi-objective optimization framework, handling conflicting objectives through non-dominated sorting and a dynamic elitism preservation strategy. The algorithm stores Pareto optimal solutions in a dynamic external archive and uses a crowding distance mechanism to ensure the diversity of the solution set. In each iteration, the current population is merged with the elite solution set, stratified via fast non-dominated sorting, and filtered based on crowding distance to retain high-quality solutions. The proposed framework effectively balances the synergistic optimization of three objectives: makespan, cost, and resource fluctuation, thereby overcoming the limitation of single-objective algorithms in generating the Pareto frontier. The algorithmic improvement strategies proposed in this study are elaborated in this section.

Dynamic elitism preservation mechanism and memory mechanism

The algorithm restructures the position update strategy of the AOO by introducing an Elitist Repository (ERP) and a memory mechanism (Elite Pool). The proposed framework utilizes Pareto frontier solutions obtained through non-dominated sorting to guide the population’s evolutionary direction, ensuring convergence toward high-quality solution spaces. The ERP stores current Pareto-optimal solutions by applying fast non-dominated sorting and crowding distance screening to each generation’s population. Simultaneously, the memory mechanism preserves historical elite solutions across generations via an Elite Pool to prevent the loss of high-quality solutions. New solutions are then generated based on elite solutions in the ERP, combined with physics-inspired update formulas. Enhanced archive update strategy

As a Pareto dominance-based multi-objective optimizer, MOAOO relies critically on its external archive to store non-dominated solutions. The Archive maintenance involves two core steps: (1) Ranking solutions into non-dominated fronts (with O(kN2) complexity, where k is the number of objectives); (2) For each front, preserving solutions with larger crowding distances to ensure uniformity along the Pareto frontier. The proposed dual mechanism guarantees convergence to the true Pareto frontier while maintaining solution diversity—a key advantage over weighted-sum approaches.

An enhanced archive update strategy is proposed to maintain the non-dominated solution set. When incorporating new solutions into the archive, the strategy considers the non-dominated relationship, it also controls the archive size to ensure solution distribution uniformity. Specifically, when the archive reaches capacity, the algorithm prioritizes retaining non-dominated solutions. For the same non-dominated front, solutions with smaller crowding distances are eliminated through a screening process. Building upon the NSGA-II framework18, the strategy introduces a dynamic merging mechanism and an efficient pruning algorithm, significantly improving the convergence and distribution of the solution set. Consequently, it ensures diversity and broad distribution of solutions in the archive, thereby better approximating the entire Pareto frontier. The strategy comprises the following three steps:

Crowding distance pruning is executed when the number of non-dominated solutions exceeds the archive size to preserve diversity. The crowding distance of all solutions is initialized to zero. For each objective dimension, solutions are sorted by their fitness values. Boundary solutions (those with minimum and maximum fitness values) are assigned an infinite crowding distance to ensure their retention. Intermediate solutions are assigned a crowding distance equal to the normalized Euclidean distance between their adjacent neighbors in the sorted list. Finally, solutions with the largest crowding distances are selected until the archive capacity is reached. Convergence monitoring and early termination mechanism

To enhance computational efficiency, the MOAOO algorithm incorporates a dual-threshold convergence monitoring system. The system automatically terminates iterations upon reaching a steady state by real-time evaluation of the improvement magnitude in objective functions. Specifically, if the improvement amplitudes for all objective functions consecutively remain below a predefined threshold over several generations, the algorithm is deemed to have converged, and the iterative process is terminated prematurely. Algorithm performance validation

To validate the optimization performance of the MOAOO algorithm for multi-objective problems, the ZDT1-3, DTLZ1, and WFG1 test functions are selected from the ZDT, DTLZ, and WFG benchmark problem sets for multi-objective optimization. ZDT problems in these sets are two-objective optimization problems, DTLZ and WFG problems in these sets are three-objective optimization problems. ZDT1, featuring 30 decision variables and a convex Pareto frontier, is used to validate the algorithm’s ability to approximate the global Pareto frontier, achieve uniform distribution of solutions, and maintain stability under high-dimensional variables. ZDT2, also with 30 decision variables but a non-convex Pareto frontier, specifically tests the algorithm’s capability to handle concave fronts, avoid solution gaps in the central region of the front, and escape flat regions. Similarly, ZDT3 has 30 decision variables; its Pareto frontier is disconnected, employing discrete segments to create local optimum traps. The DTLZ1 test function comprises three objective functions with a high-dimensional decision variable space and a linear hyperplane as its Pareto front. It is primarily used to validate the algorithm’s convergence capability in high-dimensional objective spaces, overcome the flat-region traps inherent in its design, and ensure approximation to the global Pareto front despite the presence of numerous local optima. The DFG1 test function is a dynamic multi-objective optimization problem where time-varying parameters induce continuous movement of the Pareto front. It is specifically designed to examine the algorithm’s ability to track the trajectory of dynamic Pareto-optimal solutions, test its rapid response to environmental changes, and evaluate its performance in maintaining good distribution and convergence while the optimal solution set continuously shifts. The function validates the algorithm’s ability to discover all disconnected front segments, capture discontinuous edges, and ensure a balanced distribution of solutions across segments. Considering that the reference point must be slightly larger than the worst point to avoid boundary effects, the reference point is set to [1.2, 1.2]. The population size is set to 400, the archive size to 200, and the maximum iteration count per function to 300. The problem dimensionality is set to 30, with variable bounds of [0, 1], and the number of objective functions is 2 on ZDT1-3, 3 on DTLZ1 and WFG1. The effects of parameter variations on different metrics.

Lévy flight parameter β

Β is the shape parameter in the Lévy flight distribution, which directly governs the capability and characteristics of global exploration. A smaller β value leads to more frequent long-distance jumps and stronger global exploration ability; however, it may result in excessive randomness and slow convergence. In contrast, a larger β value leads to a more concentrated step size distribution and more frequent short-distance movements, enhancing local exploitation capacity but increasing the risk of trapping in local optima. In this experiment, HV was selected as the evaluation metric, as it simultaneously measures the convergence and diversity of the Pareto front. Specifically, HV quantifies the dominated volume occupied by the solution set in the objective space. The core role of β is to strike a balance between exploration (identifying new frontier regions of the Pareto front) and exploitation (conducting refined searches in known regions). The balance directly impacts the HV value: effective exploration can discover broader frontier regions, increasing the width of HV; effective exploitation can find solutions closer to the true Pareto front, improving the height/depth of HV. Thus, HV is a reasonable metric to assess whether the adjustment of the β parameter successfully balances exploration and exploitation capabilities. An optimal β value should yield the maximum HV. The effects of different Lévy flight parameters β on HV are presented in Table 15 and Fig. 8a.

Elite pool size and population size

The elite pool maintains population diversity and prevents the loss of high-quality genes by reusing historical elite solutions. The population size determines the search breadth of the algorithm in each iteration: an excessively large population size leads to insufficient diversity and a high risk of premature convergence: an excessively small population size results in a slow convergence rate and high computational overhead. Both parameters fundamentally influence the algorithm’s global search capability and its potential to maintain diversity. An adequately large and well-managed elite pool, combined with an appropriate population size, can ensure that the algorithm covers a broader region in the objective space and retains high-quality solutions. It directly translates into a larger and more complete dominated volume. Therefore, using HV to measure the comprehensive effects of these two parameters is the most appropriate choice, as it reflects the overall quality of the algorithm’s final output. The effects of different Elite pool size and population size on HV are presented in Tables 16, 17 and Fig. 8b–c.

Archive size

The archive is used to store and maintain the non-dominated solution set identified during the iterative process, serving as the collection of the algorithm’s final output results. Its size limits the number of “optimal representatives” that can be retained. IGD measures the average distance from the reference points on the true Pareto front to the algorithm’s approximate front. The key aspect of the archive size lies in whether it is sufficiently large to represent the entire true Pareto front. If the archive size is too small, even if the algorithm finds numerous high-quality solutions, only a subset of them can be retained, leading to the emergence of “gaps” on the approximate front. In such cases, the distance from the reference points in certain regions to the nearest solutions in the archive will be considerable, thereby increasing the IGD value. IGD is highly sensitive to both the distribution uniformity and convergence of the solution set. An appropriate archive size should ensure that after pruning solutions, the remaining solution set can still adequately cover the true Pareto front, thus yielding the minimum IGD value. Therefore, the archive size is directly correlated with the IGD metric, as it tests the representative capability of the solution set. The effects of different archive size on IGD are presented in Table 18 and Fig. 8d.

Dynamic adjustment coefficient \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$c$$\end{document}

In the random perturbation update strategy of MOAOO, the dynamic adjustment coefficient c is an adaptive parameter that decreases with iterations. It controls the magnitude of perturbation and directly affects the local fine-tuning and distribution of solutions. The spacing metric is specifically designed to measure the distribution uniformity within the solution set, which calculates the standard deviation of the distances between individual solutions. Parameter c primarily influences the local exploitation phase of the algorithm. A well-designed value of c should help the population spread evenly over the Pareto front instead of clustering around a few points. If the value of c is excessively large (i.e., excessive perturbation persists in the later iterations), it will lead to instability in the positions of solutions and a dispersed distribution; if the value of c is excessively small (i.e., perturbation ceases prematurely), it will result in insufficient local fine-tuning of solutions, which may cluster in certain regions and cause uneven distribution. Therefore, spacing is a reasonable metric to evaluate whether parameter c can effectively guide the population to form a uniform distribution over the Pareto front. The sensitivity analyses conducted on six key parameters of the MOAOO algorithm reveal distinct patterns in their influence on optimization performance, providing valuable insights into the algorithm’s operational characteristics and robustness.

The Lévy flight exponent β demonstrates a clear optimal range of [1.3, 1.7] for balancing exploration and exploitation capabilities. Over this range, the algorithm achieves superior HV values (0.765 ± 0.009), indicating effective coverage of the Pareto front while maintaining solution quality. Values below this range (β = 1.0) result in excessive exploration, leading to inefficient convergence and reduced HV (0.762 ± 0.011), while higher values (β = 2.0) promote exploitation at the expense of exploration, increasing susceptibility to local optima (0.761 ± 0.012). The finding aligns with theoretical expectations for Lévy flight in multi-objective optimization, moderate β values facilitate both global search diversity and local refinement.

Population size analysis reveals a saturation effect beyond 300 individuals, with HV values plateauing at approximately 0.765. Smaller populations (50–100) show insufficient search diversity, resulting in suboptimal HV (0.752–0.758), while excessively large populations (500) provide diminishing returns with increased computational overhead. The elite pool size shows optimal performance at 10 individuals (2.5% of population size), balancing historical solution preservation with current population dynamics. Smaller pools (size 5) limit diversity preservation, while larger pools (size 20) may introduce stagnation through excessive reliance on historical solutions. The appropriately sized elite pool ensures dynamic equilibrium between inheriting high-quality genes and maintaining evolutionary momentum. Parameter configuration optimization significantly impacts the comprehensive performance of multi-objective optimization algorithms.

Archive size sensitivity demonstrates a clear optimum at 200 solutions for IGD performance (1.45E-3 ± 0.2E-3). Smaller archives (50–100) inadequately represent the Pareto front, leading to increased IGD values due to coverage gaps. Beyond 200 solutions, IGD performance deteriorates slightly (1.58E-3 at size 300), suggesting that excessive archive sizes may retain suboptimal solutions that dilute the quality of the non-dominated set. The finding emphasizes the importance of archive size as a critical parameter for maintaining both convergence and diversity in multi-objective optimization.

The dynamic adjustment coefficient c shows optimal performance at c = 0.5 for spacing metric (1.15E-3 ± 0.2E-3), indicating superior distribution uniformity across the Pareto front. Lower values (c = 0.1) result in insufficient perturbation, causing solution clustering and increased spacing values (1.85E-3), while higher values (c = 0.9) introduce excessive randomness, disrupting solution distribution patterns (1.51E-3). The parameter’s time-decreasing nature effectively balances early-stage exploration with late-stage exploitation, a characteristic particularly beneficial for complex multi-modal landscapes.

Discussion and engineering implications

The scalability analysis in this section demonstrates that the MOAOO algorithm can be effectively extended to solve medium-to-large-scale construction project scheduling problems involving up to 100 activities. Although computational time increases with problem scale, handling such scale problems on current ordinary computing hardware remains within an acceptable range (approximately 13.5 min), which holds practical value for the planning phase that requires multiple rounds of scheme comparison and selection.

The moderate performance degradation of the algorithm reveals its processing limits. For large or ultra-large-scale projects involving hundreds of activities, multiple resource types, and highly complex dependency relationships, further optimizations may be necessary. Potential strategies include adopting a hierarchical optimization framework, integrating problem-specific heuristic rules for population initialization or local search, and leveraging parallel computing to accelerate population evaluation. In addition, combining MOAOO with machine learning models for predicting task durations or resource efficiency represents a promising direction to address ultra-large-scale optimization problems.

In conclusion, while retaining its advantages in multi-objective optimization, MOAOO demonstrates the potential to handle engineering problems far beyond the scale of the original benchmark case. This study provides empirical evidence for its application in a broader range of real-world medium-to-large-scale construction project management scenarios.

Conclusion

We present the first multi-objective extension of the Animated Oat Optimization algorithm (MOAOO). The algorithm incorporates several innovations: (1) The inaugural transformation of AOO’s single-objective framework into a multi-objective optimizer; (2) A novel ERP-guided hybrid position update strategy that effectively prevents premature convergence; (3) An innovative three-layer constraint handling mechanism ensuring the generation of feasible solutions; And (4) the initial application of heuristic algorithms based on biomechanics to construction project scheduling. The development of MOAOO contributes to extending novel bio-inspired algorithms to multi-objective domains.

The selection of the Animated Oat Optimization (AOO) algorithm as the foundation for multi-objective extension stems from its unique biomechanical inspiration and structural merits, which align synergistically with the demands of multi-objective optimization in resource-constrained construction scheduling. Specifically, AOO’s inherent Dual-Phase Search Mechanism—alternating between rolling (local exploitation) and catapulting (global exploration)—naturally balances intensification and diversification, a core prerequisite for approximating diverse Pareto fronts in multi-objective optimization. Moreover, as validated by Wang et al.26, AOO shows superior robustness in high-dimensional spaces, making it well-suited to real-world construction scheduling problems characterized by numerous decision variables and constraints. Unlike established metaheuristics such as particle swarm optimization (PSO) or genetic algorithms (GA), AOO is a recently proposed algorithm with limited exploration in multi-objective contexts, thereby presenting a valuable opportunity to pioneer its extension and assess its performance in complex engineering domains. Additionally, AOO’s bio-inspired dispersal mechanism, which simulates the localized settling and long-range dispersal of oat seeds, aligns closely with the need to preserve solution diversity while converging toward the Pareto frontier in multi-objective optimization. Non-dominated sorting and dynamic archiving techniques can be integrated with any single-objective optimizer, AOO’s intrinsic structural properties provide a synergistic foundation for such enhancements, enabling more effective management of the complex trade-offs between duration, cost, and resource stability in the organizational design of small-residence construction projects.

In addition, the proposed MOAOO algorithm integrates a dynamic archive mechanism with crowding-distance-based pruning, which significantly improves both the distribution uniformity and convergence accuracy of the Pareto frontier. Coupled with a dual-threshold convergence monitoring system, the algorithm effectively reduces unnecessary iterations, thereby substantially enhancing computational efficiency. In the 10-activity construction case study, MOAOO successfully generates a high-quality Pareto front, offering three representative scheduling strategies: the shortest-duration solution, the minimum-cost solution, and a balanced compromise solution. These provide practical and flexible decision-making options for project managers under multi-objective constraints. Extensive experimental evaluations on benchmark functions further suggest the competitiveness of MOAOO, which achieves notable improvements in Hypervolume (HV), Inverted Generational Distance (IGD), and Spacing metrics compared to state-of-the-art algorithms such as Pre-DEMO, MOEA/D-OED, and Pi-MOEA. These results support the effectiveness, efficiency, and robustness of MOAOO in addressing complex multi-objective optimization problems. Specifically:

The proposed work establishes MOAOO as the first multi-objective variant of AOO, which also validates the potential of novel bio-inspired algorithms in addressing real-world multi-objective engineering problems.

Future work

Based on the research results and the limitations found in the research, future research can target the following critical areas to enhance the applicability and robustness of the MOAOO algorithm in construction project organization design: Expand the MOAOO framework and mathematical models to include key objectives beyond time, cost, and resource fluctuation, such as safety performance indicators, environmental impact, quality control indicators, and sustainable development goals; Rigorously evaluate MOAOO’s performance and scalability in large-scale real-world building projects involving hundreds of activities and complex network structures, investigating potential algorithmic enhancements to manage the computational complexity associated with high-dimensional solution spaces. Significantly expand resource models to accommodate multiple, heterogeneous resource types and their specific constraints. Developing a stochastic robust optimization model integrated with MOAOO is used to explicitly handle prevalent construction uncertainties; Exploring the integration of MOAOO with digital twin technology and building information modeling (BIM) platforms can applied to establish a closed-loop, real-time optimization system.

In addition to the aforementioned directions, future research could explore framework-level enhancements to the Animated Oat Optimization (AOO) algorithm itself. Potential improvements may include:

Developing adaptive mechanisms for the dual-phase search (rolling and catapulting) to dynamically adjust the balance between exploration and exploitation based on problem landscape characteristics;Incorporating learning mechanisms to enable the algorithm to memorize and reuse effective search patterns across different optimization stages;Enhancing the biomechanical model underlying AOO to better simulate complex seed dispersal behaviors in dynamic and constrained environments;Investigating hybridization strategies that integrate AOO with local search techniques or machine learning models to improve convergence speed and solution precision in high-dimensional spaces.Such foundational improvements could further strengthen the applicability and performance of AOO and its multi-objective variants in complex engineering domains.

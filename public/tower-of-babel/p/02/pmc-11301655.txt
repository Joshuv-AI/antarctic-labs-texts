Miliary sarcoidosis
Authors: Atif Saleem Siddiqui
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Key messageMiliary patterns on chest imaging of the lung can be associated with sarcoidosis.Keywords: dyspnea, pregnancy, sarcoidosis

CLINICAL IMAGE

A 24‐year‐old female with an intrauterine pregnancy of 36 weeks with no known medical comorbidities presented to the emergency department with cough and shortness of breath for 1 month. Her heart rate was 124/min, respiratory rate was 28/min, blood pressure was 110/80 mm/hg, temperature was 98.6 F, and oxygen saturation was 80% on room air. Initial laboratory data were normal. Serum Quantiferon was negative. Fungal antibodies and immunological workup results were negative. Computed tomography of the chest with contrast showed bilateral diffuse miliary infiltrates (Figure 1A), without mediastinal lymphadenopathy (Figure 1B). She required heated high‐flow oxygen and underwent a successful caesarean section. Bronchoscopy with transbronchial cryobiopsy (TBLC) and bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) was performed after the caesarean section. TBLC was preferred over a transbronchial lung biopsy (TBLB) due to its higher diagnostic yield in parenchymal lung diseases. BAL was negative for Mycobacterium polymerase chain reaction, Acid fast bacilli (AFB), bacterial and fungal cultures. TBLC revealed caseating granulomas (Figure 1C). Grocott's methenamine silver stain, fite stain and AFB stain were negative for fungi, non‐tuberculous mycobacteria and mycobacteria tuberculosis (Figure 1D–F). A diagnosis of sarcoidosis was made, and she was treated with steroids and methotrexate. Her hypoxemia resolved, and she was subsequently discharged. Common radiographic findings of sarcoidosis include peribronchovascular thickening, interstitial lung disease and symmetric hilar and mediastinal lymphadenopathy. 1 Treatment includes steroids and immunosuppressive medications. Miliary sarcoidosis is a rare presentation of sarcoidosis representing <1% of the reported cases. 2 Pregnancy has been associated with an improvement in sarcoidosis‐associated interstitial lung disease (ILD). However, pregnancy in sarcoidosis patients can increase the risk of complications such as preeclampsia, pulmonary hypertension, and venous thromboembolism. This case illustrates a rare occurrence of worsening hypoxemia and rapid progression of sarcoidosis‐associated ILD during pregnancy. In conclusion, TBLC should be considered for evaluating ILD associated with sarcoidosis, as well as other parenchymal ILDs, due to its higher diagnostic yield compared to TBLB.

(A) CT scan of the chest showing bilateral diffuse reticulonodular infiltrates with a miliary pattern. (B) CT scan of the chest with contrast, demonstrating mediastinal windows without mediastinal lymphadenopathy. (C) Histopathology of lung parenchymal tissue (haematoxylin–eosin stain), with an arrow indicating a granuloma. (D) Grocott's methenamine silver stain of lung parenchymal tissue, showing negative staining for fungi. (E) AFB stain of lung parenchymal tissue, showing negative staining for mycobacteria. (F) Fite stain of lung parenchymal tissue, showing negative staining for non‐tuberculous mycobacteria and nocardia.

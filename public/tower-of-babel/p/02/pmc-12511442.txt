JinhuaNSICU, an open accessible Neurosurgical Intensive Care Database
Authors: Lin Chen, Ziyi Huang, Xiaoli Liu, Guodong He, Binbin Ren, Fan Yang, Fengfeng Jiang, Qi Tu, Dandan Cai, Kai Zhang, Zhongheng Zhang, Gensheng Zhang, Minfeng Tong
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe Jinhua Central Hospital Neurosurgical Intensive Care Unit Database (JinhuaNSICU) is a single-center, bilingual (Chinese and English), and open public database focused on neurosurgery patients. This database has been filtered, cleaned, and de-identified, recording a variety of rich clinical information throughout the entire hospital stay of patients admitted to the NSICU. It contains 8,773 individuals with 20,530 episodes’ demographics, laboratory tests, vital signs, microbiology culture, examinations with reports, medications with extra special chinese herbal, orders, operations, and surgery with anesthesia. More than 26% of patients have records of more than three hospitalizations. Creating this neurosurgery database will bridge the existing gap in neurosurgical data and inspire more researchers to participate in neurosurgical research, which will further promote the follow-up studies, development of clinical decision support tools and sharing of data for international observation analysis.Subject terms: Stroke, Cancer in the nervous system, Trauma

Background & Summary

Neurosurgery critical care databases play a crucial role in the storage, analysis, and utilization of clinical data, which significantly enhances patient care, bolsters research efforts, and strengthens educational and administrative processes within the field of neurosurgery1–3.

The Multimedia Computer Database for Neurosurgery has been a pivotal resource, launched in 20004. It integrates clinical, imaging, and operative data, greatly facilitating clinical audits, research, education, and the preparation of scientific presentations. This database includes diagnostic information, digitized neuroimaging studies, operative details, and outcomes, which supports flexible search strategies based on anatomy, pathology, or clinical presentation for clinical research and patient tracking. The National Neurosurgery Quality and Outcomes Database (N2QOD) is a national registry designed to capture patient-centered data to improve the quality and efficiency of neurosurgical care, which was made public in 2013 and allowed to be used for any US researchers5. It facilitates the analysis and reporting of clinical data, aiding neurosurgeons, practice groups, and hospitals in enhancing care delivery and improving outcomes. The American College of Surgeons-National Surgical Quality Improvement Program (ACS-NSQIP) database is increasingly used in neurosurgical research for large-scale data collection on surgical outcomes6. It has updated all relevant data from the participating contributing hospitals from 2005 to 2021 to date, supporting any researcher from those hospitals to apply the collected data. In recent years, there have also been open-source, comprehensive ICU databases available to support research related to neurosurgery. Medical Information Mart for Intensive Care (MIMIC-IV) database from the Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center in the United States was updated in 2024, including all ICU admissions from 2008 to 20227–9. Chinese critical care database from Zhejiang Provincial People’s Hospital in China was released in 2023, including all ICU admitted patients from 2012 to 202210,11.

As previously noted, access to certain clinical databases is restricted to a handful of nations, while others are accessible exclusively by specific hospitals. Additionally, some databases include data from only a limited number of NSICU patients. Data from Asian population or China is quite rare. Moreover, due to their comprehensive nature covering a wide range of conditions, certain datasets have inadvertently neglected to collect crucial information pertinent to neurosurgery, including details on surgical anesthesia and follow-ups. In this study, we reported a Neurosurgical Intensive Care Unit Database collected from the Jinhua Central Hospital in China Zhejiang province, a tertiary care general hospital. It encompasses comprehensive records of inpatient stays, surgical procedures, and ICU treatments across multiple admissions for these patients, facilitating retrospective data analysis and the construction of research models.

Methods

Study setting and population

The study was conducted in Zhejiang Jinhua Central Hospital, Zhejiang, China from 2014 to 2023, and it was approved by the ethics committee of Jinhua Central Hospital (Approval number: 20231250101). The hospital is a tertiary comprehensive hospital in central and western Zhejiang, integrating medical treatment, scientific research, prevention, healthcare, and rehabilitation, which includes 37 clinical departments and 42 wards with a bed capacity of 2,200 to 2,400. We included all patients who entered the Neurosurgical Intensive Care Unit (NSICU) during this period, encompassing their entire hospitalization and any subsequent hospital stays at this hospital. All protected personal and health information were deidentified to support the retrospective study. Our study was implemented in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Database structure and development

The JinhuaNSICU database is stored in a comma-separated values (CSV) file format, which can be loaded into any database system. We have emulated the naming convention of MIMIC-IV and, in conjunction with the characteristics of our own data, determined the structure of this database.

We extracted and process the needed cohort and their data from our Research Collaboration Platform (RCP), which was used to support researchers from various hospital departments in conducting observational studies conveniently, such as cohort selection, visualization analysis, statistical analysis, and building machine learning models. The capability of RCP relies on the Research Database which was conducted based on merging the core information systems like Hospital Information System (HIS), Laboratory Information Management System (LIS), Electronic Medical Record (EMR), Operation Room Information System (ORIS), and Picture Archiving and Communication Systems (PACS). The extracted data were processed to acquire the final JinhuaNSICU database. The main content includes data cleaning, format conversion, de-identification, and translation. Data cleaning includes aligning patient populations, standardizing timestamps, maintaining consistent column names with the same semantics, etc. Format conversion includes renaming the unique patient ID of subject_id, the patient’s hospitalization ID of hadm_id, and the format in which various column data is saved, etc. Translation includes the patient’s demographic information, chief complaints, diagnoses, drug names, surgical names, medical orders, etc. Figure 1 illustrates the core workflow involved in building the database.

Data cleaning

We conducted comprehensive data cleaning on the electronic medical records to address non-standard, erroneous, or duplicate entries, which may stem from historical or unknown causes. This step is critical to ensure data consistency and prevent misleading conclusions in downstream analyses. Specifically:

Erroneous data

These primarily stem from limitations in the hospital’s early information systems, which lacked input validation before storing data. Common issues included full-width numerals (vs. standard digits), embedded carriage returns in strings, and incorrect/out-of-bound date formats. All such cases were standardized through formatting corrections.

Duplicate records

A clinical team reviewed all fields of potentially duplicated entries and confirmed cases were systematically removed.

Data consistency

We systematically validated values against clinically reasonable ranges and cross-field logical relationships. Records with out-of-range values, internal contradictions, or illogical associations were flagged and rectified.

Furthermore, we implemented primary indexing, data standardization, metadata management, and quality control procedures to ensure dataset authenticity, accuracy, and reliability.

De-identification

We removed all protected health information, including names, dates of birth, and health care costs, from each data sheet. Additionally, we removed other identifying details such as outpatient and inpatient ID, names of physicians, nurses, and medical directors. All specific real times were deleted, with the admission time standardized to 0. Other times, such as discharge, operation, and transfer times, were converted into minutes relative to the admission time. In order to support longitudinal observational studies, we have recorded the patient’s admission time periods in medical_record_front_page including 2013~2015, 2016~2018, 2019~2021, and 2022~2024.

Data Records

JinhuaNSICU contains data associated with 20,530 distinct hospital admissions for 8,773 individual patients admitted to NSICU between 2014 and 2023. And it can be publicly acquired at China National Center for Bioinformation (https://ngdc.cncb.ac.cn/omix/release/OMIX011033)12. Table 1 presents the basic statistics of the demographics. The median age is 60 years old, and median hospital duration is 15.7 days. The proportion of males is higher. The hospital mortality rate is 5.4% for patients who are clearly marked as deceased in the medical records or physician orders. Nearly 48% of patients have more than two hospital admissions. The median number of hospital admissions is 3 and longest time span of admission is 1.13 years.

Technical Validation

In the process of transmitting data from multiple hospital information systems to the RCP, the integrity of the data is verified. Due to factors such as the integration of different information systems and the level of refinement in data preprocessing, there may be some missing and duplicate data. Therefore, the quality of the JinhuaNSICU database largely depends on the Research Database12. We firmly believe that as the RCP continues to iterate and stabilize, the number of RCP users increases, and the feedback from users of the JinhuaNSICU database grows, the quality and usability of our research database will be further enhanced.

We compared the characteristics of open-source ICU databases that possess a larger number of NSICU patients, which were MIMIC-IV (US), INformative Surgical Patient dataset for Innovative Research Environment (INSPIRE, South Korea)13 and ours (JinhuaNSICU, China). In Table 19, we compared the demographic information including language, region, time span, number of patients, admission age, percentage of multiple admissions, hospital duration, and mortality rate. The JinhuaNSICU had a largest cohort size, the highest rate of readmission, and the longest hospital stay. In Table 20, we displayed the comparison of nine interest diseases. In MIMIC-IV, We observed that hypertension, ischemic cerebrovascular disease, hemorrhagic stroke, diabetes, aneurysm, and subarachnoid hemorrhage are significantly more prevalent than in populations from INSPIRE and Jinhua. In INSPIRE, intracranial space-occupying lesions have a high incidence rate, far exceeding that of the others. In JinhuaNSICU, the proportion of patients with traumatic brain injury is the highest. In Fig. 3, we compared the distribution of hospital stays among these databases by boxplot. The medial duration is the longest in JinhuaNSICU, followed by INSPIRE, while MIMIC-IV is relatively shorter overall. In the subgroup analysis of males, females, adults, and middle-aged elderly, a consistent pattern is maintained. In the oldest population, INSPIRE shows a relatively longer hospital stay with larger variations among patients. In Fig. 4, the kernel density plots were showed to compare the difference between them considering the total cohort and subgroups divided by gender (male and female) and age ([16, 65), [65, 80), [80,)). The most frequent hospital duration days for MIMIC-IV and JinhuaNSICU were quite close. Similar characteristics were observed in different subgroups, except for slight differences in the elderly population.

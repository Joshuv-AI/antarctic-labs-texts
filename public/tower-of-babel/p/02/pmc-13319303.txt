Thermal irreversibility and entropy generation analysis in MHD peristaltic transport of blood-based hybrid nanofluid through a curved duct
Authors: Noreen Sher Akbar, Najma Saleem, Javaria Akram, Lemessa Asefa Eressa, Taseer Muhammad, Muhammad Fiaz Hussain, Muhammad Bilal Habib
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe thermal control of peristaltic blood flow in a curved duct under a magnetic field is a relatively unexplored phenomenon. This research fills this gap by proposing the use of a blood-based hybrid nanofluid. The blood flow is assumed to be a non-Newtonian fluid, modelled by the Casson fluid model. It is mixed with gold (20 nm) and iron oxide (25 nm) nanoparticles. The equations are expressed in a cylindrical coordinate system in order to account for the curved nature of the duct. Peristaltic waves are considered to propagate along the wall. The governing equations are obtained using the low Reynolds number approximation (Reynolds number Re) and long wavelength approximation. The finite element method (FEM) with Python programming is applied for solving the resulting strongly nonlinear partial differential equations. The obtained results are also utilized to analyze the irreversibility of the system. The main findings indicate that increasing the aspect ratio enhances the blood flow velocity at the central part. It is found that the mono nanofluid has more improvement in central velocity compared to the hybrid nanofluid. Optimal control of the magnetic parameter and duct wall elasticity can enhance the flow efficiency. It also results in a reduction of thermal losses and thermodynamic irreversibilities.Keywords: Blood flow, Hybrid nanofluid, Heat transfer, Curved duct, Finite element approach

Introduction

Peristaltic motion is described as the recurring, wave-like muscle contractions that facilitate the unidirectional transport of liquids or semi-solids through organs or ducts. This process plays a significant role in various physiological functions, such as carrying and mixing the food through the digestive tract [1], transporting urine from the kidneys to the bladder [2], clearing mucus and debris in the respiratory tract [3], and assisting the movement of ova from the ovary to the uterus [4]. Peristaltic pumps [5] are widely used in food and chemical processing to transfer liquids while maintaining safety and sanitation due to their unique design and operation. Peristaltic pumps enable accurate and effective manipulation of delicate materials in hemodialysis [6], drug delivery [7, 8], and other therapeutic devices [9]. Because of the advancements in contemporary technology, simulating peristaltic flows has important uses in industrial [10] and biomedical engineering [11]. Blood flow via elastic channels is affected by heat transfer effects [12–14], which are essential for several applications in biomedical engineering, microfluidics, and food processing. Researchers can get insights that improve the designs and efficiencies of real-world systems by faithfully replicating these processes. Convective heat transmission is significantly impacted by temperature changes at the solid–liquid interface, which reflects the combined effects of convection and conduction. The impact of temperature and velocity slip on a peristaltic stream of viscous fluid via a tapered channel was examined by Rafiq et al. [15]. They looked at the right convective slip parameter values for lower fluid temperature. Similarly, heat convection along the peristaltic channel surface increases the overall heat transfer rate, as demonstrated by Abbasi et al. [16]. This model was further examined in a biomedical setting by Hina et al. [17], who looked at the effects of thermal slip in curved channel flow. Thermal slip effects can raise the fluid temperature at the top channel surface while lowering it at the lower surface, according to research by Riaz et al. [18].

The study of how electrically conducting fluids behave when magnetic fields are present is known as magnetohydrodynamics, or MHD. Blood transport through curved configurations, such as arteries and arterioles, demonstrates complex dynamics owing to curvature-induced forces and the development of secondary flows. The integration of magnetic field effects enables the applied magnetic field to interact with blood, owing to its electrically conducting nature, thus manipulating and regulating the flow via the Lorentz force. Such considerations are particularly relevant in physiological and biomedical engineering, including bloodstream regulation in therapeutic procedures [19], diagnostic processes [20], and microfluidic systems [21], where specific manipulation of electrically conducting fluids is critical. Magdy et al. [22] highlighted that by properly adjusting the magnetic parameter, it is possible to manipulate flow characteristics effectively. A mathematical model of the physiological flow of magneto-blood via a partially blocked artery was presented by Prasad et al. [23]. Hussein et al. [24] studied nanofluid dynamics and magnetic field effects on heat transfer and fluid motion in ciliated uterine tube models. They reported that integrating nanotechnology and magnetic force communicates a profound understanding of biomimetic energy structures in cilia-assisted peristaltic transport systems. Additionally, Rashid Ellahi et al. [25] emphasized the implication of incorporating magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) effects in blood motion within peristaltic corrugated conduits. Their findings reveal that the application of magnetic forces notably alters flow properties, including fluid velocity, pressure gradient, and surface friction. Such insights are particularly useful for modeling physiological transport phenomena and for optimizing the design of biomedical devices.

Due to their distinctive thermophysical properties and wide range of potential applications, research on nanofluids and hybrid nanofluids has recently gained considerable attention across various scientific and engineering disciplines. As hybrid nanofluids combine the advantages of multiple nanoparticles, they can enhance thermal characteristics while allaying some of the stability issues associated with conventional fluids and mono-nanofluids [26]. This makes them explicitly useful in applications where effective heat transfer is essential, such as in cooling systems, renewable energy technologies, and modern engineering processes. Maheshwari et al. [27] developed a model for targeted drug delivery by infusing hybrid nanoparticles of gold and iron within the blood flow. Their findings suggest that the incorporation of larger nanoparticles raises the inertia in the bloodstream. This enhanced inertia can disrupt the natural flow transition, particularly in non-Newtonian blood. Abdelsalam and Bhatti [28] reported that hybrid gold–copper nano-granules in blood transport improve thermal aspects and alter hemodynamic characteristics in stenosed arteries, representing their significance in heat transfer enhancement and potential uses in cardiovascular diagnostics and treatment. Hoskins et al. [29] explored the unique properties of iron oxide and gold nanoparticles within a multimodal platform, highlighting their potential applications as MRI contrast agents and nano-heaters. Arif et al. [30] perceived that the shape of the nanoparticles played a considerable role in improving heat transfer rate as spherical-shaped iron oxide nanoparticles increased heat transfer rate by up to 4.63%, platelet-shaped zinc nanogranules augmented heat transfer rate by up to 8.98%, and Cylindrical-shaped gold nanoparticles improved heat transfer rate by up to 10.41%. Abbasi et al. [31] conducted a study on the peristaltic transport of hybrid nanofluids through curvature-dependent channel surface and stated that the elevated curvature values can significantly enhance the heat transfer rate within the curved geometry. Kot et al. [32] studied (Ag–Al₂O₃/blood) hybrid nanofluid transport inside a curved catheterized stenosed artery and perceived increased thermal transport and regulated flow characteristics, highlighting its relevance in biomedical thermal management processes. Hybrid nanogranules in blood transport through biomimetic passages represent a favorable avenue for optimizing drug delivery, thermal management, and biomedical devices. Here are some interesting investigations highlighted in studies [33–35].

In order to optimize the efficacy and performance of thermo-mechanical devices, heat losses are obliged to be minimal. This approach not only enhances operational effectiveness but also contributes to energy conservation and sustainability in various applications such as biomedical devices, heat exchangers, self-cleaning surfaces, aerospace, and marine engineering. Thus, to discover the reduction of flow resistance and sustain optimal heat-transfer rate, considerable attempts [36–39] have been made to address the above-mentioned perspectives. Noreen et al. [40] have considered the thermo-mechanical ciliated porous channel flow to examine the entropy production of nanofluid. Saleem et al. [41] have discussed the various means of entropy reduction in hybrid nanofluid flow within a ciliated pump. Entropy analysis for bio-nanofluid transport within an electroosmotic curved channel was conducted by Narla et al. [42]. They highlighted that the small values of the curvature parameter induce an augmentation in the average entropy number near the channel center and a descent near the walls. Kumawat et al. [43] discussed the effects of heat source and chemical reaction on entropy generation inside the curved stenotic artery and reported that the probability of atherosclerosis rises in the presence of curvature. Sharma et al. [44] stated that the elevated values of radiation number and viscous heating parameter yield entropy inside a partially blocked curved artery. A numerical assessment of entropy production caused by forced convection through curved rectangular ducts filled with viscous fluid was presented by Ko and Ting [45]. Following the same idea, Liu et al. [46] have further extended the work by adding the effects of electric and magnetic fields. Recently, Riaz et al. [47] have conducted the second law study of Newtonian fluid through a curved duct. Mostapha et al. [48] investigated entropy production in a divergent conduit filled with a blood-based nano-liquid under the influence of a magnetic field and activation energy output. They reported that appropriate values of the Hartmann number and activation energy parameter contribute to the minimization of entropy creation, thereby aiding cooling performance. Further literature can be viewed through Refs. [49–52].

A careful review of the aforementioned literature divulges that limited attention has been given to the analysis of hybrid nanofluid flow within a rectangular segment of a curved duct, particularly in the context of entropy production in curved configurations such as arteries and arterioles, heat exchangers, and biomedical devices. Addressing this gap is important for enhancing thermal management and increasing flow performance in curved bioengineering systems. Motivated by this, the present study investigates entropy generation in a three-dimensional magnetohydrodynamic transport of blood-based hybrid nanofluid through a rectangular segment of a curved artery. The base fluid is characterized by a Casson fluid model, in which gold and iron oxide nanoparticles are deployed with respective volume fractions of 5% and 7%. The flow in the medium is induced by a peristaltic wave train traveling along the convectively heated axial surface of the duct. The mathematical equations of the problem are modelled in cylindrical coordinates and simplified under long-wavelength and low Reynolds number approximations. The resulting non-linear partial differential equations are solved numerically with the help of the finite element method implemented in Python. A comprehensive parametric analysis is carried out to measure the effects of key physical parameters on axial velocity, temperature distribution, Bejan number, and entropy generation, thereby highlighting the thermal performance and irreversibility characteristics of the flow system. The significance of this study is that it is the first thermodynamic irreversibility analysis (entropy generation and Bejan number) of MHD peristaltic transport of a Casson hybrid nanofluid (blood + Au + Fe₃O₄) in a curved rectangular duct. The key contribution is the determination of the effects of curvature, magnetic field, and viscous dissipation on velocity, temperature, and entropy generation, with findings of better cooling effect with the hybrid nanofluid and more reverse pumping with increased curvature. This work offers new insights into the design of energy-efficient biomedical pumps and hyperthermia devices not previously reported in the literature.

Numerical computational technique for the current problem

The governing nonlinear coupled partial differential equations are discretized over the computational domain using the finite element framework. The weak formulation is developed by introducing appropriate test (weighting) functions and applying the Galerkin approach, which transforms the governing equations into a system of nonlinear algebraic equations at the element level. The dependent variables are approximated using suitable shape functions, and the resulting elemental contributions are assembled to construct the global system of equations. Due to the inherent nonlinearity and coupling in the governing model, the final system is solved iteratively using the Newton–Raphson method. At each iteration, the residual vectors and the corresponding Jacobian (tangent stiffness matrix) are evaluated, and the solution is updated until the prescribed convergence criterion is satisfied. This procedure ensures accurate and stable numerical approximation of the physical behavior described by the model.

The thermal profile is analyzed for both species of the considered nanofluids. The reduction in temperature of the hybrid nanofluid (Blood + Au + Fe2O3) compared to the mono nanofluid (Blood + Au) is explained by the fact that the presence of iron oxide nanoparticles creates a stronger cooling effect. The incorporation of Fe2O3 enhances the thermal conductivity and heat transfer ability of the hybrid nanofluid, enabling it to move heat away more efficiently and hence retain a lower local temperature. This behavior is useful in biomedical applications like targeted drug delivery or hyperthermia therapy, where it is desirable to control cooling or undesirable excessive thermal damage. Figures 12, 13, 14 explore the effect of elastic wall properties, represented by parameters , on the temperature distribution . As these parameters increase, the temperature profile exhibits a progressive rise, with hybrid nanofluids (Blood + Au + Fe2O3) consistently showing higher temperature values compared to nanofluids (Blood + Au). Physically, the elastic wall properties enhance energy storage and dissipation, intensifying thermal effects in the fluid. Furthermore, compared to hybrid nanoparticles like Au and Fe2O3, the nanofluids' higher thermal conductivity increases heat transfer efficiency and results in higher temperature distributions. The differences demonstrate how wall elasticity and nanoparticle composition work in concert to improve thermal behavior. The effects of the Biot number, Casson fluid parameter, and cross-sectional aspect ratio on the temperature distribution for nanofluids and hybrid nanofluids are examined in Figs. 15, 16, 17. While a greater Casson fluid parameter indicates increased yield stress, which lowers flow mobility and permits more thermal energy buildup, an increase in the cross-sectional aspect ratio improves heat retention by changing the flow shape. In a similar vein, a higher Biot number indicates increased convective heat transfer at the boundary, which raises the temperature overall. These results highlight how geometry, fluid rheology, and boundary conditions work together to enhance thermal performance, with hybrid nanofluids continuously exhibiting better heat transfer properties. The effect of the curvature parameter on the temperature profile is seen in Fig. 18. The thermal profile depicts the dual behavior. A decline in fluid temperature is observed in the lower half of the channel, while there is a rise in fluid temperature in the upper half. The dual temperature behavior with the increase in curvature parameter (δ) is associated with the reduction of secondary flows: the lower half of the channel becomes warmer due to less expulsion of heat by the centrifugal effect and the upper half becomes less hot due to less recirculation and viscous dissipation near the outer curved wall. The temperature profile decreases as the Hartmann number rises, indicating increased magnetic field effects, as shown in Fig. 19. This happens as a result of the Lorentz force suppressing fluid velocity, which lowers the overall temperature distribution within the flow by increasing thermal conduction and decreasing thermal convection. The effects of the thermal radiation parameter and Prandtl number on the temperature profile are examined in Fig. 20 and 21. Figure 20 reveals that an increase in the thermal radiation parameter reduces the temperature profile. This suggests that stronger radiative heat transfer facilitates energy dissipation from the fluid, leading to a lower temperature. Together, these results highlight the interplay between heat conduction, momentum, and radiative effects in controlling thermal behavior within the studied system. Conversely, in Fig. 21, a rise in the Prandtl number enhances the temperature profile, indicating that higher Prandtl values slow heat diffusion relative to momentum transfer, thereby increasing thermal retention.

Figures 26, 27, 28, 29 illustrate how entropy generation varies according to important parameters, including the long wavelength parameter. . and elastic wall properties . Entropy production is the term used to describe the irreversible energy loss that occurs in a system as a result of heat transfer, friction, and fluid flow resistance. This leads to an increase in disorder and a drop in the overall efficiency of the system. It is shown in Figs. 26, 27, 28 that entropy generation increases with increasing elastic wall characteristics, mainly because of the increased resistance to fluid motion brought on by the walls' increased elasticity. As more energy is lost as irreversibilities, this causes greater fluid deformation and viscous dissipation, which raises the development of entropy. On the other hand, entropy generation falls in Fig. 29 as the curvature parameter rises. An increase in the curvature parameter (less curved duct) weakens the secondary flow and viscous dissipation, thus reducing frictional and thermal irreversibilities, which accordingly reduces total entropy generation.

Figures 30, 31, 32, 33, 34, 35 represent 2D and 3D contour plots to show how the cross-sectional aspect ratio (α) affects temperature distribution. The temperature distribution varies noticeably as α rises, with greater temperature values throughout the domain resulting from larger aspect ratios. This is particularly noticeable in the 3D charts, as greater aspect ratios make the temperature rise more noticeable. This implies that the distribution of heat in a system is greatly influenced by the cross-sectional shape. Thermal buildup is probably improved by larger aspect ratios, which may have an impact on total thermal management. In more intricate flow systems, the three-dimensional image makes it easier to see how aspect ratio affects heat transmission. Increased surface area exposed to the fluid due to larger values of α may result in more effective heat exchange between the fluid and the surrounding surfaces. This is especially crucial in applications where heat transfer optimization is essential, such as cooling systems and heat exchangers. Maximizing thermal efficiency in these systems requires an understanding of how variations in aspect ratio affect temperature dispersion. The impact of the Biot number (Bi) on temperature distribution is depicted in Figs. 32, 33, where there are discernible changes as Bi rises from 0.02 to 0.05. The fluid–solid boundary's thermal resistance is measured by the Biot number. Higher resistance is indicated by an increase in Bi, which improves heat transmission in the system. Indicating that greater Biot numbers enhance heat transmission between the fluid and surrounding surfaces, resulting in more pronounced thermal gradients and an overall rise in temperature distribution, Fig. 18 illustrates that higher Biot numbers are associated with higher temperature values. Lastly, Figs. 34, 35 examine how the temperature distribution is affected by the Hartmann number (Ha), which ranges from 1.2 to 2.0. The ratio of magnetic to viscous forces in a conducting fluid is represented by the Hartmann number, a crucial metric in magnetohydrodynamics. The 3D charts clearly show that the temperature drops as Ha rises. This suggests that greater Hartmann numbers stabilize the thermal distribution, most likely as a result of the magnetic field's ability to limit heat transmission and reduce fluid mobility. The inverse association between Ha and temperature is confirmed by Fig. 19, which is consistent with this tendency.

Concluding Remarks

In this research attempt, the MHD peristaltic propulsion of non-Newtonian blood-based hybrid nanofluid through a curved duct is analyzed. The flexible walls of the duct are assumed to be convectively heated. The blood’s non-Newtonian aspects are modelled through the Casson fluid model, and a hybrid composition of nanofluid is attained by suspending the iron oxide and gold nanoparticles with average diameters of 25 nm and 20 nm, respectively. The governing equations after non-dimensionalization and linearization approximation are found to be non-linear partial differential equations; therefore, the finite element method is opted for the numerical solution. The analysis of the fluid flow problem is performed through graphical results prepared for velocity, temperature, and pressure gradient. Further, the irreversibility of the proposed system is analyzed through graphical attributes of the entropy generation function and the Bejan number. The key outcomes are:

The velocity profile is found to decline for a larger curvature ratio parameter; however, the temperature profile shows dual behavior for a larger curvature ratio parameter.

So, it can be concluded that the hybrid nanofluids provide better cooling effects, and fluid flow is more facilitated in a comparatively less curved channel. Future endeavors of this study may embrace the incorporation of more realistic arterial wall properties, non-uniform magnetic fields, pulsatile pressure gradients, the deployment of ternary, and multiphase effects, in order to further augment the relevance of the model in complex biological frameworks.

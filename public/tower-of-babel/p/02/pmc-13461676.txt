Platelet Transfusion Thresholds and Associated Outcomes in Adult ICU Patients with Thrombocytopenia: A Retrospective Observational Study
Authors: Nipun Yallawar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPlatelet transfusion practices in critically ill adults vary widely, and real-world triggers often deviate from guideline-suggested thresholds.1,2 Data from Indian intensive care units on indications, pre-transfusion platelet counts, and their relationship with bleeding and mortality are limited.ObjectivesTo describe platelet transfusion triggers and indications in critically ill adults and to explore associations between transfusion characteristics, bleeding complications, and hospital mortality.Materials and methodsThis was a single-centre observational study in a multidisciplinary intensive care unit. Consecutive adult patients who received at least one platelet transfusion during their ICU stay were included. For each platelet count episode and transfusion, we recorded pre-transfusion platelet count (trigger), indication (prophylactic, therapeutic, or procedural), number of random donor platelet units, and bleeding events. Patient-level variables included demographics, primary diagnosis, comorbidities, SOFA and APACHE II scores at ICU admission, organ support (mechanical ventilation, vasopressors, renal replacement therapy), length of stay, and ICU and hospital outcomes. Descriptive statistics were used for all variables. Univariable associations were assessed with the Mann–Whitney U test for continuous variables and chi-square or Fisher's exact test for categorical variables. Multivariable logistic regression was performed to identify predictors of hospital mortality, with age, sex, APACHE II score, primary diagnosis, and minimum pre-transfusion platelet count as covariates.ResultsSixty-five transfused patients (mean age 52.3 ± 15.7 years; 61.5% male) contributed 295 platelet count episodes, of which 116 involved transfusion. The overall median platelet count was 49 ×10/L (IQR 28–89). Among transfusion episodes, the median pre-transfusion trigger was 26 ×10?/L (IQR 13–44.8), and a median of 4 (IQR 4–6) random donor platelet units were transfused. Indications were therapeutic in 40.5%, prophylactic in 37.1%, and procedural in 21.6% of episodes. Hospital mortality among transfused patients was 36.9%; any bleeding occurred in 29.2% and major bleeding in 4.6%. In univariable analysis, mechanical ventilation and the use of vasopressors were associated with higher mortality. In contrast, the minimum platelet trigger and total units transfused were not significantly different between survivors and non-survivors. In multivariable logistic regression, none of the prespecified covariates, including minimum platelet trigger, showed a statistically significant independent association with hospital mortality.ConclusionIn this single-centre ICU cohort, platelet transfusions were most commonly administered for therapeutic and prophylactic indications at a median trigger of approximately 25 × 10^9/L. Hospital mortality and bleeding complications appeared more closely related to overall illness severity and organ support than to the absolute platelet transfusion trigger.

Effect of intraoperative cell salvage on postoperative blood transfusion demand outcomes in cesarean section with placenta previa: a retrospective study with propensity score matching analysis
Authors: Guiying Yang, Dukun Zuo, Qiangting Deng, Xiaohang Bao, Ming Yang, Fang Chen, Lei Cao, Wenjun Liu, Cheng Xiao, Junyan Lai, Yan Liang, Hong Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThis study aimed to analyze the postoperative blood transfusion demand and safety of intraoperative cell salvage (ICS) without leukocyte depletion filters in cesarean sections complicated by placenta previa.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed the electronic medical records of a teaching hospital in China between July 2014 and January 2025. Women were categorized into three groups based on the intraoperative transfusion method: ICS group (ICS), allogeneic blood transfusion group (ABT), and ICS combined with ABT group (ABTICS). The primary outcome was the incidence of postoperative blood transfusions. The secondary outcomes included postoperative complications (amniotic fluid embolism, disseminated intravascular coagulation [DIC], puerperal or incision infection, and pulmonary infection), length of hospital stay, hospitalization costs, and other indicators. Outcomes among the three groups were compared using propensity score matching (PSM) analysis.ResultsIn total, 832 patients were included in the analysis, and 114 pairs (342 patients) were matched according to their propensity scores. After PSM, when there was no significant difference in intraoperative blood loss volume, the incidences of postoperative red blood cell(RBC) and plasma transfusion were significantly higher in group ABT groups than in the ABTICS and ICS groups (RBC:25.0% vs. 6.8%, P = 0.001; and 25.0% vs. 7.8%, P < 0.001, respectively; plasma: 17.2% vs. 4.3%, P = 0.001). These results are consistent with those of the non-matched cohort. Before PSM, the incidences of postoperative RBC transfusion and plasma were higher in group ABT than in the ABTICS and ICS groups (RBC: 20.1% vs. 5.4% and 20.1% vs. 7.6%, respectively; all P < 0.001; plasma: 12.7% vs. 3.1%, P < 0.001; and 12.7% vs. 6.2%, P = 0.015, respectively). There were no significant differences among the three groups in the incidence of postoperative complications (P > 0.05).ConclusionsThe use of ICS without the application of leukocyte depletion filters during cesarean section was safe and effective in reducing the need for allogeneic blood transfusion during and after the procedure. Furthermore, it has made a substantial contribution in addressing blood resource shortages and meeting the rapid transfusion needs of women with placenta previa.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12871-025-03327-0.

Background

Postpartum hemorrhage (PPH) remains one of the leading causes of maternal mortality, accounting for approximately 100,000 maternal deaths annually worldwide, representing 27.1% of total maternal mortality [1]. With the implementation of China’s two-child and three-child policies, there has been an increase in high-risk cases, such as placenta previa and placenta accreta. These conditions are associated with significant blood loss and extremely high mortality risk [2]. Blood transfusion is the most effective intervention to save the lives of these women. Transfusion methods include allogeneic blood transfusion (ABT) and intraoperative cell salvage (ICS). In China, the scarcity of blood resources poses significant challenges to allogeneic transfusion [3], making ICS a crucial method for addressing blood resource limitations.

However, potential risks associated with amniotic fluid embolism, immune reactions, and coagulopathy have restricted the use of ICS in cesarean deliveries in certain countries [4–7]. To ensure the safe application of ICS in cesarean section, current studies have suggested combining leukocyte filters with a two-suction technique to reduce amniotic fluid contamination [8, 9]. In cases of placenta previa and placenta accreta, avoiding placental tissue when selecting a uterine incision site is challenging. After uterine incision, large amounts of amniotic fluid and blood simultaneously emerge. Even with the two-suction technique, effective separation of blood from the amniotic fluid remains difficult. Additionally, the fine pore structure of leukocyte filters significantly impedes infusion rates [10, 11], greatly reducing the resuscitation efficiency in procedures requiring massive transfusions, such as those for placenta previa. For women with placenta previa and placenta accreta who require rapid massive transfusion, omitting the leukocyte filter may improve transfusion efficiency. However, the safety of ICS without leukocyte filters in cesarean sections for placenta previa remains unclear [11].

The results of current studies on the effects of ICS on coagulation function are inconsistent. Some studies have indicated that ICS significantly reduce the concentrations and activities of coagulation factors during the washing process, including fibrinogen, coagulation factor II, and factors VII, X, and XIII [12]. This has led some surgeons to decline the transfusion of salvaged blood owing to concerns about postoperative bleeding. Furthermore, the impact of ICS on cesarean delivery outcomes remains unclear, as previous studies have reported inconsistent findings regarding its effectiveness in reducing the need for allogeneic blood transfusions [13–15]. In addition, some studies have suggested that the use of leukocyte depletion filters can effectively remove all elements of the amniotic fluid, thereby preventing coagulopathy [16]. Therefore, whether the transfusion method without leukocyte filters in cesarean sections for patients with placenta previa who require rapid transfusion increases the incidence of postoperative hemorrhage and demand for allogeneic blood transfusion remains to be further investigated.

Since 2014, our hospital has employed ICS without leukocyte filters and with two suction devices for blood transfusions in women with placenta previa and placenta accreta. This study aimed to retrospectively analyze the postoperative blood demand and safety of ICS for cesarean sections complicated by placenta previa at our institution. These findings are intended to provide more accurate and comprehensive references for clinical decision-making.

Methods

Study design and women

This single-center retrospective cohort study investigated the safety and efficacy of ICS for reducing allogeneic transfusion in cesarean sections. The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Army Medical University, Chongqing, China (approval ID: 2025-084-01). Written informed consent was not obtained from any participants.

Inclusion Criteria: women were eligible if they met the following conditions: (a) age ≥ 18 years, (b) American Society of Anesthesiology status classification I–II, (c) preoperative diagnosis of placenta previa centralis (The placental tissue completely covers the internal cervical os after 28 weeks of pregnancy), and (d) undergoing cesarean section under spinal or general anesthesia with perioperative blood transfusion.

Exclusion Criteria: women were excluded if they presented with: (a) significant liver dysfunction, defined as Child-Pugh score > grade A; (b) significant kidney dysfunction, defined as preoperative urea nitrogen > 133µmol/T, and creatinine levels > 7.1 mmol/L, and glomerular filtration rate < 50 mL/min/1.73 m²; or (c) incomplete medical records.

Based on these inclusion and exclusion criteria, 832 patients who underwent cesarean section were enrolled. The women’s enrollment process is illustrated in Fig. 1.

Data source

All data were obtained from electronic medical records of a teaching hospital in China between July 2014 and January 2025. An anesthetist recorded the anesthesia-related data and intraoperative complications, and a nurse anesthetist performed the postoperative follow-up. A gynecologist recorded the baseline data and preoperative demographics. Prior to analysis, all names and other identifying information of the included women were removed. Two researchers independently extracted the data using a self-designed data collection form. In the case of discrepancies between the results of the two researchers, a third researcher conducted an independent assessment.

Anesthesia and medication

All women underwent subarachnoid or general anesthesia. Subarachnoid anesthesia was performed at the L2-L3 or L3-L4 level, with 18–22 mg of ropivacaine (AstraZeneca Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd.) administered intrathecally; the highest anesthesia level was T6. General anesthesia was induced using propofol 1–2 mg/kg, remifentanil 1–2 µg/kg, and rocuronium 0.6–1 mg/kg, with maintenance achieved using 0.8–1 MAC sevoflurane and remifentanil (0.15–0.2 µg/kg/min), and the depth of anesthesia was maintained at BIS 40–60. A double-lumen internal jugular vein cannula, 16-gauge peripheral venous cannula, and 20-gauge radial artery cannula were inserted in all women. According to the hospital’s protocol, oxytocin (20 U in 100 mL of 0.9% normal saline) was administered as an intravenous continuous infusion immediately after umbilical cord clamping to increase uterine contractions.

Transfusion selection criteria

For women diagnosed with central placenta previa, where the main placental attachment is on the anterior uterine wall or ultrasound confirms placental accreta, we recommend the following intraoperative blood management strategy: autologous blood is preferred. If the recovered red blood cell (RBC) volume exceeds 200 mL, autologous blood transfusion should be performed. If the parturient declines autologous blood use, or if autologous blood recovery is unsuccessful because of contamination, allogeneic blood transfusion should be performed. For parturients receiving autologous blood transfusion, allogeneic blood should be supplemented if blood gas analysis indicates hemoglobin (Hb) < 7 g/dL.

Postoperatively, the decision to transfuse was based on vaginal bleeding volume and hemoglobin levels. Blood transfusion is required when the hemoglobin level is < 7 g/dL, necessitating the administration of allogeneic RBCs. Typically, the transfusion ratio of plasma to RBCs is maintained at 2–3:1.

ICS and group allocation

For cesarean sections requiring ICS, we used two separate suction tubes. A separate suction tube was established to waste amniotic fluid as completely as possible before delivery of the placenta to minimize amniotic fluid contamination. Blood from the surgical field was suctioned into a sterile reservoir through a heparinized double-lumen tube and a filter using another suction tube. An ICS machine (Medtronic Inc., USA) was used for cell salvage (CS). The machine was set up and operated by an anesthetist. All operating department practitioners were trained in CS, following a comprehensive competency-based training program that included theoretical and practical skills. The suction line and reservoir were anticoagulated with a pre-prepared solution of 500 mL saline containing 25,000 IU/mL heparin. Surgical swabs were routinely washed with saline containing 50 IU/mL of heparin to maximize RBC recovery. The suction pressure was maintained at 150–200 mmHg.

Reinfusion of salvaged blood was commenced in the operating room, with the blood remaining at the patient’s bedside throughout the process. Any unused blood was discarded if it was not reinfused within 6 h of collection initiation. Routine monitoring was performed during reinfusion, which was consistent with our hospital’s protocol for allogeneic blood transfusion. Transfusions were administered using standard transfusion lines without leukodepletion filters. All CS volumes were recorded as ICS.

Women were categorized into three groups based on the intraoperative transfusion method: ICS (ICS, n = 276), allogeneic blood transfusion (ABT, n = 298), and ICS combined with ABT (ABTICS, n = 258). In the ABT and ABTICS groups, plasma, platelet, and cryoprecipitate transfusions were administered as needed during the operation.

Demographic and clinical data collection

We collected demographic data including age, body mass index, and gestational age. Preoperative comorbidities, including intrahepatic cholestasis during pregnancy (ICP), gestational diabetes, gestational hypertension, and coagulation disorders, were documented. Intraoperative data included blood loss and transfusion volume. The postoperative parameters assessed included postoperative blood transfusion volumes, Hb, white blood cell count (WBC), neutrophil percentage (NEUT%), platelet count (PLT), procalcitonin, amniotic fluid embolism, disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC), PPH, puerperal or incision infection, pulmonary infection, placental accreta, hospital length of stay, and hospital costs, etc.The primary outcome was the incidence of postoperative blood transfusion.

Statistical analysis

All data were analyzed using SPSS (version 26.0; IBM Corp.) and R software (version 4.2.1; http://www.R-project.org). Statistical significance was defined as a two-sided P-value of < 0.05. Continuous variables with a normal distribution are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD), whereas non-normally distributed continuous variables are presented as median and interquartile range (25th and 75th percentiles). Categorical variables are presented as numbers (frequencies).

Stepwise logistic regression analysis was used to evaluate the role of demographic and preoperative baseline variables in postpartum blood transfusion (yes/no). All demographic and preoperative baseline data variables listed in Table 1 were included in this model. Odds ratios (OR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI) were determined using logistic regression analysis.

All the groups were matched using propensity scores to mitigate the influence of bias and confounding variables. We conducted propensity score matching (PSM) according to the method described by Qiangting et al. [17]. We first compared the variables in Tables 1 and then performed PSM for variables with P < 0.05. The propensity score was calculated for the following baseline variables: age, operation duration, blood loss volume, gestational age, preoperative Hb level, and primiparas or multiparas (yes/no). The R packages ‘dplyr,’ ‘RMS,’ ‘foreign,’ and ‘VIM’ (version 4.2.1; http://www.R-p8roject.org) were utilized to perform PSM among the three groups for statistical analysis. Normally distributed continuous data among the three groups were compared using analysis of variance, including age, body mass index, gestational age, preoperative Hb level, duration of surgery, and intraoperative blood loss volume. Tukey’s post-hoc analysis was employed for continuous variable comparisons between each matched group, including postoperative Hb, PLT count, WBC count, and neutrophil percentage (NEUT%).

The Mann–Whitney U test was used for between-group comparisons of non-normally distributed data and for comparisons between each matched group. Categorical variables among the three groups were compared using the chi-square or Fisher’s exact tests, which were also used for comparisons between each matched group. Additionally, a two-sided P-value of < 0.01666 was set as the significance threshold for each matched group comparison of non-normally distributed data and categorical variables.

Results

Demographic and preoperative baseline data analysis

The demographic and preoperative baseline data of the three groups are presented in Table 1. Significant between-group differences were observed in the incidence of primiparas or multiparas, age, gestational age, preoperative Hb level, operation duration, and volume of blood loss during the operation (all P < 0.05). However, no significant differences were observed in the other parameters (P > 0.05). After PSM, 114 women were matched in each group. No significant differences in demographic or other baseline characteristics were observed between the groups (P > 0.05), with absolute standardized mean differences ≤ 0.2 (Fig. 2).

Risk factor analysis

As summarized in Table 2, stepwise logistic regression analysis for all included women showed that preoperative coagulation dysfunction and intraoperative blood loss volume were significant risk factors for postoperative blood transfusion (OR, 5.181; 95% CI, 1.505–17.893; P = 0.009; and OR, 1.001; 95% CI, 1.001–1.001, P < 0.001, respectively). Grouping factors were identified as protective factors against postoperative blood transfusion (OR, 0.360; 95% CI, 0.253–0.513; P < 0.001).

Intraoperative and postoperative outcomes for Non-matched women among three groups

As shown in Table 3., before PSM, the blood loss volume in ABTICS group was more than that in ABT group and ICS group (1188.6 ± 789.4 mL vs. 627.2 ± 400.6 mL, 1188.6 ± 789.4 mL vs. 753.3 ± 347 mL, respectively; all P < 0.001). The incidence of PPH was higher in the ABTICS group than in the ABT and ICS group (43.7% vs. 21.5% and 43.7 % vs. 23.6%, respectively; all P < 0.001). The incidence of postoperative RBC transfusion was lower in the ICS and ABTICS groups than in the ABT group (5.4% vs. 20.1% and 7.6% vs. 20.1%, respectively; all P < 0.001). The incidence of postoperative plasma transfusion in group ABT were significantly higher than that in groups ABTICS and CS (12.7% vs. 3.1%, P < 0.001; and 12.7% vs. 6.2%, P = 0.015, respectively). No significant differences were observed in the incidence of postoperative RBC and plasma transfusions between the ICS and ABTICS groups (P > 0.05). Hospital cost and length of hospital stay were higher in the group ABTICS than in the ICS and ABT groups (P < 0.001). Meanwhile, the incidence of placenta accreta was significantly higher in the ABTICS and ICS groups compared to the ABT group (61.2% vs. 6.7%; and 28.3% vs. 6.7%, respectively; all P < 0.001).

As shown in Table 4, postoperative NEUT% was significantly higher in group ABT compared to groups ABTICS and ICS (88.3 ± 5.2 vs. 84.7 ± 5.4; and 88.3 ± 5.2 vs. 84.6 ± 5.1, respectively; all P < 0.001). Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to compare the incidence of postoperative PPH and transfusion requirements using ICS without leukocyte filters during cesarean sections for placenta previa. This finding indicates that, despite no significant difference in intraoperative blood loss volume, the ABT group had the highest incidence of postoperative transfusion. Therefore, the use of ICS demonstrated effective blood-saving effects both intraoperatively and postoperatively. However, the incidence of postoperative complications, including PPH, pulmonary infections, puerperal or incision infections, pulmonary embolism, and DIC, was similar among the three groups, suggesting that all three transfusion methods are generally safe. This retrospective study offers important insights into how safe and effective ICS without leukocyte filters is for saving blood during cesarean sections with placenta previa. In the non-matched cohort, the incidence of PPH was significantly higher in the ABTICS group than in the ICS and ABT groups. This may be due to the significantly greater blood loss volume observed in the ABTICS group. Therefore, the intraoperative blood loss volume was the primary factor contributing to the increase in postoperative PPH. Stepwise logistic regression analysis confirmed this finding. In the matched cohort, when there was no significant difference in the intraoperative blood loss volume, the incidence of postoperative PPH did not differ significantly among the three groups. In both the nonmatched and matched cohorts, the incidence of postoperative transfusion was lower in the ICS and ABTICS groups than in the ABT group. This indicates that ICS can provide washed autologous RBCs with preserved function and viability, thus avoiding immunological reactions and storage damage, optimizing the effectiveness of blood therapy and reducing the need for postoperative blood transfusion. This finding is consistent with those of several previous studies [18, 19]. Furthermore, studies have shown that ICS exhibit elevated levels of thrombin-antithrombin III and fibrinopeptide A, indicating enhanced thrombin generation, which better preserves thrombin function and reduces the need for postoperative blood products [20]. Notably, the absence of significant differences in the incidence of postoperative transfusion between the ICS and ABTICS groups implies that the combined approach achieves comparable efficacy to autologous transfusion alone, potentially offering flexibility in clinical scenarios where the autologous blood volume may be insufficient. The higher postoperative Hb levels in the ABTICS group compared to those in both the ABT and ICS groups further support the synergistic benefits of combining autologous and allogeneic strategies, possibly owing to optimized blood volume restoration and enhanced RBC availability. However, caution must be exercised to avoid excessive Hb levels resulting from combined transfusion methods, which could lead to an unnecessary waste of blood resources. This indicates that the use of ICS can save blood not only during the operation but also during the postoperative period.

In the ABT group, NEUT% significantly increased (> 85%), whereas it was moderately elevated in the ABTICS and ICS groups, indicating that allogeneic transfusion exacerbates the inflammatory response. Allogeneic blood products can induce immunomodulatory effects including cytokine release and leukocyte activation, thereby amplifying systemic inflammation [21, 22]. Nevertheless, there were no significant differences in the incidence of postoperative complications (such as pulmonary, postpartum, or incision infections) between the groups, suggesting that the inflammatory response did not translate into adverse clinical outcomes during hospitalization. The higher trend of NEUT% in the ABT group necessitates increased vigilance regarding the risk of postoperative infections, although the complication metrics may not capture these risks.

In the matched cohort, the higher hospital costs observed in the ABTICS group than those in the ICS group may reflect the additional expenses associated with combining ICS with ABT. CS systems require specialized equipment and personnel [23], which can offset the cost savings from reduced allogeneic product usage. However, the higher postoperative Hb levels in the ABTICS group do not exclude the possibility that the increased costs were due to excessive transfusion volumes. Notably, no significant cost difference was observed between the ABT and ICS groups, suggesting that CS alone may not impose an economic burden compared with standard ABT, making it a cost-effective alternative that conserves blood resources.

Women with placenta previa or placenta accreta are at a high surgical risk, and such operations pose a serious threat to the mother’s life. Cesarean sections must be managed in a tertiary hospital by a multidisciplinary team consisting of experienced obstetricians, anesthesiologists, neonatologists, and hematologist [24]. Regardless of the blood transfusion method employed, thorough preparation of both ICS and allogeneic blood must be performed prior to cesarean section to ensure adequate blood supply and guarantee the safety of the parturient. In cases where increased allogeneic blood transfusion is not required, ICS alone is an excellent option and can be safely employed for blood transfusion therapy.

This study had limitations inherent to its retrospective design. Due to data collection constraints, we were unable to obtain precise measurements of postoperative vaginal bleeding volume and coagulation function. These parameters were indirectly evaluated based on the incidence of postpartum hemorrhage and postoperative transfusion volumes. However, these results require further investigation and validation. Moreover, long-term outcomes including maternal quality of life and neonatal outcomes were not assessed, warranting additional research.

Conclusions

The use of ICS with two suction sets without the application of leukocyte depletion filters during cesarean section is safe and effective in reducing the need for allogeneic blood transfusions during and after the procedure. This approach represents a blood-saving and relatively safe transfusion strategy for cesarean sections involving the placenta previa. Furthermore, it has made a substantial contribution in addressing blood resource shortages and meeting the rapid transfusion needs of women with placenta previa.

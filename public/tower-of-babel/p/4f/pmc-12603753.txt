Fabrication and optimization of functional electrodes via FIB structuring and AACVD-based semiconductor deposition
Authors: D.A. Vázquez-Vargas, O. Solís-Canto, P. Amézaga-Madrid
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This study presents the development of a resistive-type gas sensor through the design and fabrication of electrodes integrated with sensing material deposition. The electrodes are designed with simplicity in mind, significantly reducing fabrication time compared to conventional interdigitated electrodes that rely on complex methods like photolithography and sputtering. The manufacturing process begins with patterning electrodes on platinum-coated silicon substrates using focused ion beam (FIB) milling. Subsequently, the sensing layer is synthesized through aerosol-assisted chemical vapor deposition (AACVD). CO₂ detection tests were carried out under various conditions using a Linkam system, validating the functionality and potential of the sensor design. Several aspects of the prototype stand out:•The sensor consists of a minimalistic architecture—comprising a substrate, metallic electrodes, and a metal oxide sensing layer.•FIB-based electrode patterning allows for a streamlined process requiring little optimization. It enables nanometer-scale spacing—ranging from tens to hundreds of nanometers—substantially finer than the micrometer-scale gaps typically achieved through photolithography. This reduction in spacing may contribute to higher sensor sensitivity. Notably, electrode geometries produced by FIB remain underexplored in literature.•AACVD demonstrates excellent suitability for depositing semiconducting metal oxide films with tunable morphology and characteristics, offering promising applications in gas sensor technologies.

Background

Air quality concerns have increased the need for sensitive gas detection technologies. Pollutants such as carbon dioxide (CO₂), nitrogen oxides (NOₓ), sulfur dioxide (SO₂), and volatile organic compounds (VOCs) contribute to environmental degradation and health risks, which makes their detection at low concentrations essential [1,2]. Conventional monitoring systems often lack sufficient sensitivity or portability for real-time applications, highlighting the relevance of developing reliable and miniaturized sensors [3,4]. In particular, the rising concentration of CO₂, despite its role in photosynthesis, is linked to greenhouse effects, ocean acidification, and possible respiratory issues, underscoring the need for accurate detection across domestic, industrial, and environmental settings [5,6]. The significance of this work lies in its contribution to the advancement of gas sensor design through the integration of nanoscale electrode engineering and optimized semiconducting oxide thin films. While previous studies have demonstrated the role of sensing materials in determining sensor performance, the influence of electrode geometry and fabrication techniques has often been overlooked or addressed through complex, multi-step processes [7,8]. Here, we emphasize the potential of focused ion beam (FIB) milling as a versatile and precise approach to electrode patterning, enabling nanometer-scale control over inter-electrode spacing and geometry [9]. By coupling this method with aerosol-assisted chemical vapor deposition (AACVD) of semiconducting oxide films, the proposed strategy enhances sensitivity, selectivity, and reliability at low gas concentrations [[10], [11], [12]]. This combination not only streamlines fabrication but also establishes a scalable pathway toward miniaturized, high-performance gas sensors. Ultimately, this study provides a foundation for developing next-generation sensing devices capable of addressing critical challenges in environmental monitoring, industrial safety, and healthcare applications [3,4,13].

When electrodes are located beneath the sensing film, gas molecules diffuse into the material and react chemically, generating charge carriers that modulate resistance over time.

The response of the sensor is described by the following Eq. (1):(1)R(t)=[erfc(τt)+tτπ{1−exp⁡(−τt)}]Where τ is a term called diffusion time (τ=Yo24D), which depends on the diffusion constant of the test gas (D) and the thickness of the sensing material (Yo); and t corresponds to the time.

Research confirms that placing electrodes beneath the sensing surface enhances sensitivity [13]. Narrow electrode spacing improves detection at low gas concentrations, while wider spacing is more effective at higher concentrations [[14], [15], [16], [17], [18], [19]]. When designing electrodes via FIB, the ion beam performs sputtering by transferring momentum to atoms, whose removal enables precise patterning. The beam diameter—and thus resolution—depends on probe current, with low currents yielding finer structures [17].

Method validation

The successful removal of material from the silicon substrate enabled the fabrication of a pair of collinear electrodes, which served to monitor the resistance variation of the materials subsequently deposited by the AACVD technique. The deposited coatings adhered uniformly to the electrode surface and exhibited a homogeneous grain morphology across the entire substrate.

To confirm the role of the CuO and ZnO layers in the electrical response, control experiments were performed on samples without the deposition of these semiconductor films. The measurements verified the absence of electrical conduction between the electrodes after the FIB fabrication process, confirming that the contacts remained electrically isolated. These results demonstrate that the responses recorded in subsequent experiments arise exclusively from the interaction of the deposited CuO and ZnO layers with the target gas.

The sensor design allowed screening tests to be carried out in the presence of carbon dioxide (CO₂) as the target gas. In these experiments, nitrogen (N₂) was used as the reference gas, while the test gas consisted of a mixture of N₂ (97 %) and CO₂ (3 %). Since N₂ is a chemically inert and stable gas (N₂(g) → N₂(ads)), its exposure to the sensor does not involve electron transfer or significant adsorption on the sensor surface, resulting in a constant baseline resistance. In contrast, CO₂ directly interacts with oxygen ions previously adsorbed on the semiconductor surface, leading to the formation of carbonates or bicarbonates (CO₂(g) + O⁻(ads) → CO₃²⁻ + e⁻ or CO₂(g) + OH⁻(ads) → HCO₃⁻ + e⁻). This process decreases the concentration of charge carriers at the surface, which in turn directly modifies the resistance of the sensing material [20,21].

The samples were exposed to both gaseous environments, and the variation of the electrical resistance under these conditions was analyzed. Subsequently, the sensor signal parameters (S as the resistance variation in the presence of the reference gas (Ra) of the sensor once it comes in contact with the test gas (Rg) were calculated such that (S=Ra−RgRa*100), response time (tresp) it takes for the device to modify by 90 % its resistance once it comes in contact with the test gas) and recovery time (trec) it takes for the sensor to return to 90 % of its original resistive behavior once the test gas is removed from the environment) for different temperatures at which the measurements were performed (between 100 and 400 °C, with intervals of 100 °C), in addition, the measurements were performed with and without irradiating the surface with UV-A light (365 nm).

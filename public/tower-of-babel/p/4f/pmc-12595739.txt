Long-term outcomes of phacoemulsification and intraocular lens implantation combined with goniosynechialysis for primary angle-closure glaucoma with 360° peripheral anterior synechiae
Authors: Junhua Li, Yuchen Gu, Hengli Lian, Na Li, Liqing Wei, Wanting Wu, Tingting Li, Weihua Pan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPhacoemulsification combined with intraocular lens implantation and goniosynechialysis (PEI + GSL) represents a mainstream ​surgical technique​for the treatment of primary angle-closure glaucoma (PACG). As the disease progresses, when the extent of peripheral anterior synechiae (PAS) gradually expands to 360°, whether PEI + GSL remains effective remains uncertain. This study aimed to investigate the long-term clinical efficacy​of PEI + GSL in treating PACG patients with 360° PAS.MethodsA case‒control study was conducted on PACG patients with 360° PAS (360° PAS group) and those with PASs ranging from 90° to 180° (90°≤ PAS ≤ 180° group). Patients underwent PEI + GSL and were followed up for 1–3 years. The data collected included intraocular pressure (IOP), glaucoma medications, visual acuity, PAS extent, complications, and other relevant parameters. Kaplan‒Meier curves were used to analyse the cumulative surgical success rates in both groups.ResultsThe 360° PAS group included 31 eyes (31 patients), and the 90°≤ PAS ≤ 180° group included 32 eyes (32 patients). At the final follow-up (36 months), the IOP was 14.8 ± 3.9 mmHg in the 360°PAS group and 15.5 ± 2.9 mmHg in the 90°≤ PAS ≤ 180° group (P = 0.507). The degree of IOP reduction was 47.9% in the 360°PAS group and 33.0% in the 90°≤ PAS ≤ 180° group (P = 0.272). The mean number of glaucoma medications decreased by 2.5 in the 360°PAS group and 1.1 in the 90°≤ PAS ≤ 180° group (P < 0.001). Background

Primary angle-closure glaucoma (PACG) is a group of diseases characterized by elevated intraocular pressure due to primary angle closure, leading to changes in the optic disc and visual field damage. As one of the most prevalent causes of irreversible blindness globally, PACG remains a significant public health concern [1, 2]. The pathognomonic feature of PACG is characterized by progressive angle closure, which is predominantly mediated by the formation of the PAS. The severity of angle closure is positively correlated with the extent of PAS formation, resulting in a gradual increase in IOP and subsequent progression of glaucomatous damage.

The therapeutic objectives in the management of PACG patients encompass three principal aspects: (1) reversal or prevention of the angle-closure mechanism, (2) effective IOP control, and (3) prevention of progressive optic nerve damage [3]. Several surgical interventions have been implemented in clinical practice to achieve optimal IOP control. Among these, the combined procedure of phacoemulsification with intraocular lens implantation (PEI) and goniosynechialysis (GSL) has gained increasing acceptance and recognition among ophthalmic surgeons.

GSL is a specialized surgical technique that involves meticulous dissection and separation of the PAS from the angle wall, thereby facilitating angle reopening and the restoration of physiological aqueous outflow [4, 5]. The theoretical basis of this procedure lies in its potential to restore trabecular meshwork function through a sequential mechanism involving (1) complete PAS removal, (2) anatomical re-exposure of the trabecular meshwork, and (3) gradual recovery of the aqueous outflow pathway [6–8].

Numerous clinical investigations have demonstrated the therapeutic efficacy of combined phacoemulsification with intraocular lens implantation and goniosynechialysis (PEI + GSL) for the management of PACG. Postoperative analysis revealed a statistically significant reduction in IOP among PACG patients following PEI + GSL surgery [5, 6, 9–13], with corresponding IOP reductions ranging from 38% to 55% [14, 15]. Furthermore, quantitative analysis demonstrated a significant reduction in the extent of the PAS score, with a decrease ranging from 56% to 59.4% [9, 14]. Consistent with these findings, our research team’s previous studies have provided substantial evidence supporting the efficacy of PEI + GSL as a reliable surgical intervention for PACG management [16–19].

Current clinical evidence suggests that early intervention with PEI + GSL surgery in PACG patients prior to the development of extensive PAS significantly enhances IOP reduction efficacy [9, 13, 16, 20]. Regarding the efficacy of PEI + GSL in PACG patients with 360° PAS, the current literature remains limited. A comprehensive review of available evidence revealed only sporadic case reports addressing this specific clinical scenario, making it challenging to draw definitive conclusions about the effectiveness of the procedure in such advanced cases [21]. Our research team conducted an expanded retrospective case series study with an increased sample size, specifically investigating the therapeutic outcomes of PEI + GSL in PACG patients presenting with 360°PAS formation.

Subjects and methods

This retrospective study analysed consecutive patient data from August 2014 to August 2019 at the Hangzhou Branch of Wenzhou Medical University Eye and Vision Hospital, where patients underwent standardized PEI + GSL treatment. The study protocol adhered to the ethical principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Institutional Review Board (Approval No. H2023-038-K-36). In cases in which both eyes met the inclusion criteria, only data from the left eye were included in the analysis to maintain data consistency.

The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) age ≥ 50 years; (2) confirmed diagnosis of PACG; (3) preoperative gonioscopic evidence of PAS extending between 90° and 360°; and (4) a minimum follow-up duration of 12 months. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) had secondary glaucoma; (2) had a history of intraocular surgery; (3) had significant systemic diseases (e.g., rheumatoid arthritis or systemic lupus erythematosus); or (4) had incomplete clinical data. The left eye was systematically selected for inclusion if both eyes met the inclusion criteria.

The study cohort comprised 63 eyes of 63 PACG patients, stratified into two groups on the basis of PAS extent: Group 1 included 31 eyes (31 patients) with 360° PAS, whereas Group 2 consisted of 32 eyes (32 patients) with a PAS ranging from 90° to 180° (inclusive). All participants completed a follow-up period ranging from one to three years.

All study participants underwent comprehensive ophthalmic evaluations, including (1) best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) measurement; (2) IOP assessment via Goldmann applanation tonometry; (3) anterior segment evaluation via slit-lamp microscopy; (4) gonioscopic examination; (5) detailed fundus examination; (6) biometric measurements via IOL Master; (7) standard automated perimetry for visual field assessment; (8) ultrasound biomicroscopy (UBM) for anterior segment imaging; (9) optical coherence tomography (OCT) for retinal and optic nerve head analysis; (10) B-scan ultrasonography; and (11) documentation of the number and types of IOP-lowering medications.

For visual acuity measurements worse than counting fingers, the following standardized logMAR conversions were applied according to established protocols [22–24]: counting fingers = 2.0 logMAR; hand motion = 2.3 logMAR; light perception = 2.6 logMAR; and no light perception = 2.9 logMAR.

PEI + GSL was performed in accordance with a standardized surgical protocol. Preoperatively, initial gonioscopic evaluation was conducted to determine the location and extent of preoperative PAS. Two corneal incisions (a main incision and a side incision) were created. Subsequently, meticulous dissection of PAS was performed using a viscoelastic agent (Amvisc; Bausch & Lomb Inc., Rochester, NY, USA) under direct visualization, and this dissection achieved separation of a portion of the PAS. The subsequent surgical steps included continuous curvilinear capsulorhexis (CCC), hydrodissection, phacoemulsification, cortical cleanup, and implantation of a foldable intraocular lens (IOL) into the capsular bag. Notably, IOL implantation was omitted in eyes with preoperative no light perception (NLP). After completing these steps, residual PAS were meticulously manipulated with an iris repositor to achieve adequate separation between the iris root and the trabecular meshwork. When necessary, an additional auxiliary corneal incision was created to facilitate GSL from more optimal surgical angles. The extent of angle separation was directly confirmed intraoperatively via real-time gonioscopic (or endoscopic) visualization. If angle separation was deemed insufficient, repeated GSL was performed, with the goal of achieving nearly 360°angle separation.Notably, a small number of firm PAS persisted in some patients and could not be completely separated despite repeated dissection attempts. When intraoperative hemorrhage occurred in the anterior chamber angle, hemostasis was achieved by viscoelastic tamponade. All procedures were performed by a single experienced surgeon (W.H. Pan) to ensure consistency in surgical technique.

Postoperatively, all patients received topical dexamethasone eye drops (switched to prednisolone eye drops after 2 weeks), nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory eye drops, and 0.5% pilocarpine eye drops for 4 weeks, with gradual tapering of the dosage.

For all patients, IOP, BCVA, slit-lamp examination, fundus examination, and gonioscopy were recorded preoperatively and during postoperative follow-up. PAS were defined as adhesions of the iris tissue to the trabecular meshwork(TM), which were identified clinically as a persistently closed angle upon indentation gonioscopy, where the posterior pigmented trabecular meshwork was not visible in the anterior chamber angle. Starting from the third postoperative month, patients were routinely followed every 3 to 6 months.

All intraoperative and postoperative complications, along with any subsequent surgical interventions, were systematically documented throughout the follow-up period.

Surgical success and failure were defined as follows: the primary outcome criterion was IOP control at the final follow-up visit (≥ 12 months). Complete success was defined as an IOP of ≤ 21 mmHg without the use of glaucoma medications or additional surgical interventions (including trabeculectomy and glaucoma drainage device surgery, among major types).Qualified success was defined as an IOP of ≤ 21 mmHg with the use of up to two glaucoma medications. Failure was defined as uncontrolled IOP despite the use of more than two glaucoma medications, the need for a secondary IOP-lowering procedure, hypotony (IOP < 6 mmHg at two consecutive visits), or the occurrence of any sight-threatening complication [25].

Statistical analysis

PASS 11.0 (NCSS Statistical Software, Kaysville, UT, USA) was used for statistical sample size calculation. On the basis of the preexperimental comparison design used to detect differences between the two groups, a sample size of 29 patients per group was determined to achieve a power of 0.90 and an alpha value of 0.05. Data analysis was performed via SPSS (version 26.0; SPSS, Chicago, IL, USA), with the results presented as the means ± standard deviations (for normally distributed variables), medians with ranges (for nonnormally distributed variables), or proportions (for categorical variables).

To evaluate baseline differences between the two groups, variables such as intraocular pressure, number of antiglaucoma medications, PAS range, Student’s t test, chi-square test (or Fisher’s exact test), and Wilcoxon signed-rank test were applied, as appropriate. Independent samples t tests and Wilcoxon rank-sum tests were used to compare preoperative and long-term follow-up values for IOP (normally distributed) and BCVA (nonnormally distributed), respectively.

Kaplan–Meier curves were generated to analyse the probabilities of complete and qualified success between the two groups. Correlation analysis was performed via Pearson’s correlation coefficient. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

In both groups, the mean postoperative IOP values were significantly lower (p < 0.05) than the mean preoperative IOP values at all time points up to 3 years. At the final follow-up (36 months), the IOP was 14.8 ± 3.9 mmHg in the 360°PAS group and 15.5 ± 2.9 mmHg in the 90°≤PAS ≤ 180° group, with no significant difference between the two groups (p = 0.507).

During the 3-year follow-up period, the reduction in IOP was 47.9% in the 360°PAS group and 33.0% in the 90°≤PAS ≤ 180° group, with no significant difference between the two groups (p = 0.272).

The 360°PAS group demonstrated a reduction in the mean number of topical glaucoma medications from a preoperative level of 3 (range: 2–4) to 0 (range: 0–0) at 3 years postoperatively, representing a mean reduction of 2.5 medications (p < 0.001). Similarly, the 90°≤PAS ≤ 180° group showed a reduction in the mean number of topical glaucoma medications from a preoperative level of 1 (range: 0–2) to 0 (range: 0–0) at 3 years postoperatively, with a mean reduction of 1.1 medications (p < 0.001). A significant difference was observed between the two groups (p < 0.001).

Visual outcomes

The 360°PAS group presented a mean BCVA of 0.40 logMAR (range: 0.00–2.90 logMAR) preoperatively, which improved to 0.30 logMAR (range: 0.00–2.90 logMAR) at the final follow-up after treatment (p = 0.285, excluding patients with no light perception [NLP]). In the 90°≤PAS ≤ 180° group, the mean BCVA improved from 0.15 logMAR (range: 0.00–2.00 logMAR) preoperatively to 0.10 logMAR (range: 0.00–1.30 logMAR) at the final follow-up (p = 0.162). (Fig. 2).

PAS

The 360°PAS group comprised 31 patients (31 eyes), and statistical analysis was performed on 17 patients (17 eyes) whose gonioscopy records were available. At the final follow-up, the median PAS range was 90° (40°, 157.5°). Among these patients, four exhibited PAS ranges of ≥ 180° (180°, 180°, 180°, and 240°); however, their IOPs were all within the normal range (13.3 mmHg, 12.8 mmHg, 8.4 mmHg, and 15.0 mmHg, respectively), and none required IOP-lowering medications. Additionally, we specifically analysed postoperative recurrent peripheral anterior synechiae (re-PAS). Among these 17 patients, 14 (82.4%) developed re-PAS, with a median re-PAS range of 90° (15°, 157.5°). All patients maintained IOPs of ≤ 21 mmHg at the final follow-up.

In the 90° ≤ PAS ≤ 180° group, which included 32 patients (32 eyes), statistical analysis was performed on 27 patients (27 eyes) whose gonioscopic records were available. At the final follow-up, the median PAS range was 45°(0°, 90°). Among these patients, one exhibited a PAS range of 180°(this patient had a preoperative PAS of 180°), with an IOP of 13.8 mmHg at the final follow-up, and was managed with three IOP-lowering eye drops. Among these 27 patients, 16 (59.3%) developed re-PAS, with a median re-PAS range of 45°(0°, 90°). Only one patient had an IOP exceeding 21 mmHg, reaching 23.0 mmHg.

Additionally, we conducted a separate analysis to evaluate potential differences between patients with a postoperative PAS ≥ 180° and those with a PAS < 180°. Statistical analysis confirmed that there were no significant differences between the two groups in terms of the reduction in IOP or the change in the number of IOP-lowering medications (p = 0.053 and p = 0.857, respectively).

Surgical success

The Kaplan‒Meier curves in Fig. 3 illustrate the complete success rates of the 360°PAS group and the 90°≤PAS ≤ 180° group. The success rates in both groups gradually declined from 58.1% to 68.8% at 1 year postoperatively to 54.8% and 62.5% at 3 years postoperatively, respectively. No significant difference was observed in the complete success rates between the two groups at 1 year postoperatively (p = 0.378) or 3 years postoperatively (p = 0.537). Additionally, there was no significant difference in the rate of decline between the two groups (p = 0.999).

The Kaplan‒Meier curves in Fig. 4 illustrate the complete and qualified success rates of the 360°PAS group and the 90°≤PAS ≤ 180° group. The success rates in both groups gradually declined from 93.5% to 100% at 1 year postoperatively to 90.3% and 93.8% at 3 years postoperatively, respectively. No significant difference was observed in the complete plus qualified success rates between the two groups at 1 year postoperatively (p = 0.238) or 3 years postoperatively (p = 0.970). Furthermore, there was no significant difference in the rate of decline between the two groups (p = 0.999).

We utilized the Cox proportional hazards regression model to investigate whether baseline characteristics, including sex, age, preoperative intraocular pressure (pre-IOP), preoperative peripheral anterior synechiae (pre-PAS), postoperative peripheral anterior synechiae (post-PAS), disease duration, and the ratio of anterior chamber depth to axial length (ACD/AL), influence surgical success. However, no statistically significant risk factors were identified (p > 0.05).

Complications and reoperation

Intraoperatively, mild anterior chamber hemorrhage occurred in 8 eyes, and malignant glaucoma developed in 2 eyes (1 patient in each group). Postoperative complications included ciliary body detachment in 2 eyes, minor bleeding in 2 eyes, IOP spikes in 2 eyes, and malignant glaucoma in 1 eye (which underwent anterior vitrectomy on the 9th postoperative day). Among these patients, 2 patients required external filtration surgery.

Discussion

PACG is characterized by closure of the anterior chamber angle due to the formation of the PAS. Previous studies have indicated that when the PAS extent exceeds 180°, the IOP typically begins to rise. In cases where medical therapy is ineffective or optic nerve damage occurs, filtering surgery is recommended [4, 26]. In recent years, PEI + GSL has gradually emerged as the mainstream surgical approach because of its lower incidence of complications than traditional filtration surgery. Several studies have reported favourable surgical outcomes with PEI + GSL in patients with PACG across varying extents of PAS [10, 14–17, 27]. Therefore, what are the efficacy and safety profiles of PEI + GSL when the PAS angle increases to 360°? The literature on this topic remains limited. In this comparative study of 360°PAS in PACG, we observed a 47.9% reduction in IOP in the 360°PAS group, with a complete success rate of 54.8% and a complete plus qualified surgical success rate of 90.3%. No significant differences were found compared with the control group (90°≤PAS ≤ 180° group) (p = 0.272, p = 0.537, and p = 0.970, respectively). Additionally, we found that the preoperative IOP, preoperative PAS extent, postoperative residual PAS extent, and disease duration did not significantly influence the surgical success rate.

In 2019, Panse K et al. [28] performed PEI + GSL on 6 patients with 360° closed-angle PACG. After a 12-month follow-up, the IOP decreased from 23.5 ± 11.2 mmHg preoperatively to 12.2 ± 1.8 mmHg, representing a 33.7% reduction, with a mean decrease of 0.7 glaucoma medications. Additionally, although several studies [4, 6, 10, 13, 14, 29] have included patients with 360°PAS, they did not report data such as IOP and the number of IOP-lowering medications for this subgroup separately. As a result, these studies cannot be used to compare surgical outcomes, especially for patients with 360° PAS. Our 36-month study of 31 eyes demonstrated that PEI + GSL effectively managed PACG with 360° PAS. The postoperative IOP significantly decreased from 28.4 ± 13.5 mmHg to 14.8 ± 3.9 mmHg (47.9% reduction, p < 0.001), with a mean reduction of 2.5 glaucoma medications (p < 0.001). These findings support its clinical application in patients with 360°PAS PACG. We were also encouraged by the results. Although our procedure (PEI + GSL) does not involve goniotomy or trabeculectomy, it achieved an IOP reduction of almost 50%, which appears superior to outcomes reported in the literature for similar procedures [28]. We attribute this effectiveness primarily to two technical refinements: (1)Intraoperatively, we used an iris repositor to perform meticulous manipulation of residual PAS, with the goal of achieving adequate separation between the iris root and the trabecular meshwork. The extent of angle separation was directly confirmed intraoperatively via real-time gonioscopic (or endoscopic) visualization. If angle separation was deemed insufficient, repeated GSL was performed, with the goal of achieving nearly 360°angle separation. (2) When necessary, we created additional auxiliary corneal incisions, which allowed us to perform GSL from more favorable angles, and this approach may have significantly enhanced the completeness of PAS separation.

At the 36-month follow-up, the IOP measurements were not significantly different between the 360° PAS group (14.8 ± 3.9 mmHg) and the 90°≤ PAS ≤ 180° group (15.5 ± 2.9 mmHg) (p = 0.507). Similarly, the composite surgical success rates (complete + qualified) demonstrated comparable outcomes between the two groups (90.3% vs. 93.8%, p = 0.970). These findings align with those of previous studies that reported consistently high success rates of PEI + GSL in PACG patients across different preoperative PAS extents. Yu et al. [20] conducted a study involving 32 PACG eyes with a preoperative PAS ranging from 120° to 270°who underwent PEI + GSL. The 12-month follow-up results demonstrated a complete surgical success rate of 90.0% and an overall success rate of 100%. Moghimi et al. [14] evaluated PEI + GSL outcomes in 45 PACG eyes and reported a mean preoperative PAS of 245.7° ± 91.3°. At the 12-month follow-up, the mean IOP significantly decreased from 23.3 ± 7.3 mmHg to 14.5 ± 2.5 mmHg, indicating effective IOP control. These collective findings suggest that the extent of preoperative PAS may not significantly influence the surgical outcomes of PEI + GSL. Importantly, our results indicate that every effort should be made to achieve separation approaching 360°of PAS intraoperatively, and that this endeavor, regardless of the initial PAS extent, appears to be a critical determinant of surgical success.

In the present study, 14 patients (82.4%) in the 360° PAS group developed re-PAS, while 16 patients (59.3%) in the 90° ≤ PAS ≤ 180° group developed re-PAS. The incidence of re-PAS was considerably high in both groups; however, most patients still maintained normal IOP. This finding is also one of the important findings of the present study. We discuss this observation from the following two aspects: (1) Regarding the causes of re-PAS development: We propose that during the GSL technique used in the present study, the mechanical separation of the anterior chamber angle with an iris repositor inevitably causes mechanical trauma to the peripheral iris tissue. Additionally, some patients had a plateau iris configuration. Despite aggressive postoperative anti-inflammatory treatment, re-PAS still developed in the peripheral iris of some patients, leading to the relatively high incidence observed. (2) Regarding the reason why most patients maintained normal IOP despite the high re-PAS incidence: In the 360° PAS group, 14 patients (82.4%) developed re-PAS, with a median re-PAS extent of 90° (15°, 157.5°). All these patients maintained an IOP of ≤ 21 mmHg at the final follow-up. We hypothesize that although a relatively extensive re-PAS developed, the TM re-opened by GSL may have recovered its aqueous humor drainage function. Furthermore, the function of the re-opened TM regions may compensate for the impaired TM function in areas with post-PAS. This finding requires verification in future anterior segment OCT studies or studies focusing on the anterior chamber angle drainage pathway.

Histopathological evidence from Hamanaka et al. [30] demonstrated that persistent trabecular-iris contact and peripheral anterior synechiae in PACG may induce endothelial damage and subsequent Schlemm’s canal occlusion. This finding raises a critical clinical question regarding the potential correlation among PAS duration, disease progression, and PEI + GSL surgical outcomes. The literature review revealed several relevant findings. Zhang et al. [31] reported successful IOP control in 12 PACG patients whose disease duration exceeded one year following PEI + GSL, which was maintained throughout the 13-month follow-up period. Varma D et al. [15] reported that in their study of 29 eyes with chronic angle-closure glaucoma (disease duration >3 years), PEI + GSL surgery resulted in a significant reduction in intraocular pressure from 27.4 mmHg preoperatively to 12.2 mmHg postoperatively, with a complete surgical success rate of 86% at the 6-month follow-up. In our study cohort of PACG patients with 360° PAS and a mean disease duration of 2 years, 54.8% achieved complete surgical success, maintaining normal intraocular pressure without the need for any IOP-lowering medications. Notably, in these patients with complete surgical success, despite a postoperative residual PAS of 123.6°±66.4°, the intraocular pressure returned to normal levels, suggesting that the aqueous outflow function via Schlemm’s canal in these patients might have been restored. These collective findings from both studies indicate that the duration of disease may not represent a significant limiting factor affecting surgical success rates. However, this observation warrants further investigation through well-designed clinical studies involving PACG patients with extended preoperative disease durations.

The reported complication profile of GSL includes both intraoperative and postoperative events. Intraoperatively, anterior chamber hemorrhage was the most frequently observed complication. Postoperatively, transient IOP elevation is commonly encountered. Additionally, although less frequent, other potential complications may include anterior chamber exudation, ciliary body detachment, supraciliochoroidal effusion, and malignant glaucoma [16, 17, 32]. Our findings indicated that GSL-related complications occurred at a relatively low frequency, and the majority of these complications demonstrated complete reversibility with appropriate management.

This study had several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, the retrospective design may have introduced potential selection bias and limited the strength of our conclusions. Second, the relatively small sample size and heterogeneous follow-up durations may have affected the generalizability of our findings. Future studies should incorporate larger, well-defined cohorts with standardized follow-up protocols to validate the long-term efficacy of this surgical approach. Furthermore, the current study did not identify specific risk factors influencing surgical success rates, which warrants further investigation through prospective multicenter studies with expanded sample sizes and comprehensive risk factor analysis.

Conclusion

PEI + GSL in PACG patients with 360° PAS resulted in a 47.9% reduction in IOP, a mean reduction of 2.5 glaucoma medications, and a high overall surgical success rate (90.3%), comparable to the 90°≤ PAS ≤ 180° group (93.8%).

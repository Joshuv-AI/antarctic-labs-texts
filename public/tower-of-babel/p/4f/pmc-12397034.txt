Current perspectives and global trends of nanotechnology in advanced breast cancer: a bibliometric and visualized analysis
Authors: Hanwen Huang, Tingting Ma
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Despite advancements in diagnosis and treatment methods for advanced breast cancer (ABC), a more extensive breast cancer (BC) stage with heterogeneity in clinical features, its clinical management remains a substantial challenge. Furthermore, although research on nanotechnology (NT)-based ABC treatment has recently gained significant attention, studies intuitively examining this discipline are quite scarce. Therefore, we aimed to comprehensively assess the current status and emerging trends in NT-based ABC treatments. Studies focusing on NT application in ABC treatment were retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection (WOSCC) database. Bibliometric and visual analyses were then performed using CiteSpace and VOSviewer software. Herein, 3531 papers were included. From 2005 to 2024, annual publications on NT use in ABC treatment increased, mostly from China and the United States, accounting for 1139 and 1032 publications, respectively. Furthermore, the Chinese Academy of Sciences (CAS) was the most prominent institution, with Yaping Li as the leading author in studies on NT-based ABC treatment. Additionally, the Journal of Controlled Release had the highest number of publications while Cancer Research was the most cited journal. Moreover, the most widely studied areas were therapeutic intervention and metastatic breast cancer (MBC), while solid lipid nanoparticles (LNPs), targeted therapy, and tumor microenvironment (TME) were the latest research focal points. This study provides a comprehensive overview of NT use in ABC treatment over the past two decades. Specifically, it evaluated the contributions of countries, authors, and institutions, and identified research hotspots and emerging frontiers, providing novel perspectives and laying the groundwork for future investigations.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-03260-1.

Introduction

Breast cancer (BC) is the second most prevalent malignant tumor worldwide, and the most common cancer among women [1, 2]. Advanced breast cancer (ABC), representing a more extensive stage of BC, encompasses two forms: inoperable locally advanced breast cancer (LABC) and metastatic breast cancer (MBC) [3]. LABC with inflammatory features, extensive skin invasion, or advanced nodal involvement (bulky/fixed axillary or supraclavicular/internal mammary nodes) is generally not amenable to surgical intervention [4, 5]. MBC occurs when cancer spreads to distant sites such as bones, brains, livers, and lungs [6, 7]. Although metastasis typically develops years after primary BC diagnosis, it may also be detected at initial presentation [8, 9].

Systemic treatments for ABC include chemotherapy, targeted therapy, endocrine therapy, and radiotherapy (RT), with chemotherapy as the primary intervention [6, 10–12]. However, chemotherapy poses the risk of damage to normal cells, and should be tailored to patients’ specific conditions and individual factors [13]. By inducing intratumoral or systemic changes, chemotherapy may promote the metastatic colonization [14]. Additionally, chemotherapy could also lead to drug resistance [15, 16], which frequently results in progressive disease during neoadjuvant therapy in many LABC patients [17]. The other treatments have also been associated with specific shortcomings, with RT requiring further enhancement and the application of targeted therapy remaining limited due to the absence of clear targets in certain types of BC [18].

Given these limitations, alternative strategies are being explored to overcome the challenges associated with traditional treatments. One such approach is the use of nanotechnology (NT). The technique involves the creation and manipulation of substances at the nanoscale level using individual atoms and molecules, has recently emerged as a potential therapeutic avenue [19, 20]. In addition to their large relative surface area, nanomaterials have a strong penetration capability and can interact with various biomolecules [21]. These properties make them highly useful in tumor diagnosis and treatment. Furthermore, Nanoparticles (NPs) can carry multiple ligands that may recognize molecules present in cancer cells, enhancing the targeting ability and diagnostic accuracy [22]. Additionally, NPs could capture cancer-related proteins, circulating tumor cells (CTCs), and other tumor biomarkers [23]. Moreover, many NPs could enhance the effectiveness of computed tomography (CT), magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), and positron emission tomography (PET) [24–26]. In cancer treatment, NT is often employed as a form of drug delivery system to directly target primary tumors and metastatic areas, which may reduce drug resistance and adverse reactions (ARs) [27, 28]. What’s more, NT crucially impacts the therapeutic efficacy of approaches such as phototherapy and RT [29, 30]. Overall, NT offers new opportunities for ABC diagnosis and treatment. Multiple studies have been conducted on the use of NT in cancer treatment. For instance, Zhang et al. reported that using platelets as a bridge, NPs modified with P-selectin targeting peptides could precisely deliver drugs to metastatic sites, significantly enhancing anti-metastatic efficacy [31]. Yook et al. created a new brachytherapy method for LABC treatment using gold NPs (Au NPs) linked to the β-particle emitter 177Lu and panitumumab [32], The assessment demonstrated superior tumor growth inhibition compared to conventional therapy in preclinical models. These studies align with the current trend of integrating ligands with targeted biomolecules to enhance therapeutic precision, as evidenced by similar strategies reported in prostate cancer [33] and thyroid cancer [34]. However, due to challenges in safety, manufacturing, regulation, and funding, NT-based therapeutic research is mainly limited to in vivo and in vitro experiments, with a noticeable lack of relevant clinical studies [35], necessitating additional research efforts.

Bibliometric analysis, a mathematical and statistical method for quantitatively assessing pertinent literature, could effectively delineate the key areas of research and evaluate the contributions of authors, countries, and journals in a specific field [36]. Although multiple bibliometric analyses have explored NT use in BC treatment [35, 37, 38], ABC has received little attention. To our knowledge, no bibliometric analysis has focused exclusively on ABC and NT, this interdisciplinary field merits further investigation. Therefore, we analyzed the current status of frontier research hotspots and future directions of NT-based studies through a bibliographic review of scholarly publications on ABC from 2005 to 2024, providing valuable insights into this field.

Materials and methods

Data collection and processing

In this study, the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) database [36] was searched for pertinent articles from “January 1, 2005” to “September 17, 2024”. The search strategy (Supplementary Table 1) involved PubMed’s Medical Subject Headings (MESH) database and relevant published literature [35, 37, 39–41], yielding 3,565 articles. Only the articles and reviews published in English were included. After excluding 284 records of other types [retractions (n = 1), data papers (n = 2), corrections (n = 4), retracted publications (n = 6), editorial material (n = 15), book chapters (n = 46), early access reports (n = 50), proceeding papers (n = 68), meeting abstracts (n = 77), and non-English articles (n = 15)], 3,281 articles (2,552 research articles and 729 reviews) were included in the final analysis. Bibliometric and visualization analyses

Bibliometric and visualization analyses were conducted using specialized tools: (1) Microsoft Excel 2019 tracked annual publication trends through basic statistical analysis, (2) VOSviewer 1.6.20 generated co-occurrence networks to visualize collaborations of countries, authors and institutions as well as trends in journal citations, research fields, and keyword evolution [42], (3) CiteSpace 6.3.R3 was used to detect bursts in co-occurrence items and reference co-citations, and created dual-map overlays of journals [43], (4) Charticulator-generated chord diagrams intuitively display international collaboration strength through dynamic ribbon connections [44].

Results

Overview of bibliometric analysis

Supplementary Table 2 details an overview of this study, which involved 3,281 valid articles written by 19,773 researchers from 5,307 institutions across 85 countries between 2005 and 2024. As of the search date, these articles had been cited 159,270 times, with an average of 48.54 citations/article. The H-index of the studies was as high as 160, reflecting their significant academic value. Notably, a higher H-index indicates a greater academic impact in a particular field or research dataset [45].

Publishing trends

The number of publications and citations could indicate the scale and development trend in a particular research field. Figure 2A shows the annual publication trend in our domain of interest from 2005 to 2024. Our analysis of cumulative publication demonstrated overall growth in the targeted field, aligning strongly with the predictive model equation; y = 10.952 × 2−57.938x + 104.67 (R2 = 0.9993). Furthermore, the yearly publication count steadily increased from 7 in 2005, peaking at 343 papers in 2022, with two periods of negative or slow growth observed from 2017 to 2019 and from 2020 to 2023. As of the final target date (September 17, 2024), the number of studies published in 2024 alone was already 288. Moreover, we estimated that the total publication volume in 2024 could be equal to or slightly lower than that of 2023. There was also an annual increase in the number of citations, with a notable growth observed from 2014 (4493) to 2021 (20656) (Fig. 2B).

Analysis of Co-cited references and reference burst

Co-cited references are two or more articles cited together in other publications [46, 47]. Based on a g-index with k = 25, 1,259 co-cited references were extracted from the 3,281 articles included in this study. Table 5 lists the top ten co-cited references. A co-citation network was further generated (Fig. 8A), with each node representing a cited document. Node size correlated positively with the number of citations. Among the co-cited references, Sung’s “Global Cancer Statistics 2020: GLOBOCAN Estimates of Incidence and Mortality Worldwide for 36 Cancers in 185 Countries” report was the most frequently cited (96) [48]. The article with the second most co-citation counts (75) was Shi et al.’s. summary of the progress, challenges, and potential opportunities in cancer nanomedicine, which explored NT application prospects in cancer treatment [49]. The articles were further classified into 15 clusters (Fig. 8B) and analyzed using CiteSpace based on its key two metrics: Modularity (Q score) and silhouette (S score). Herein, the Q score was 0.8125 (> 0.3), while the average S score was 0.9288 [with the lowest and highest S scores being 0.865 and 1, respectively; both > 0.7], indicating that the clustering effect was valid and highly homogeneous. All the clusters were further divided into four categories, and Table 6 summarizes the detailed information. A citation burst is an indication that a particular paper has gained significant attention and recognition over a specific period. We created a citation burst depiction (Fig. 9), with the blue line representing the observation time interval from 2005 to 2024 and the red line representing the burst duration. The citation strengths for the top 25 papers ranged from 10.07 to 34.44. Among them, the article by Sung et al. (2021) showed the strongest citation burst [48], followed by William J. Gradishar et al.’s. conclusive paper (2005) that compared NP albumin-bound paclitaxel with polyethylated castor oil-based paclitaxel [50]. Some articles that have recently shown citation bursts include Raghu Kalluri et al.‘s study on exosomes (Science; 2020), Jayanta Kumar Patra et al.‘s work on nano-based drug delivery systems (Journal of Nanobiotechnology; 2018), and Adrienne G. Waks et al.‘s article (Journal of the American Medical Association; 2019), indicating that they are likely to continue receiving attention in the future and may become key frontiers.

Analysis of keyword

Analyzing keyword evolution and frequency changes could help identify research hot spots and trends in a specific field. After extraction and merging, we found 165 keywords with more than 30 co-occurrences each. Table 7 shows the top 20 keywords with a high frequency in research on NT use in ABC treatment, including “breast cancer” (1311), “drug delivery” (965), “nanoparticle” (868), “metastatic breast cancer” (432), and “metastasis” (387). All the keywords were further categorized into four clusters (Fig. 10A): Red (Biology and Metastasis Mechanisms of BC), Blue (Nano Drug Delivery and Targeted Therapy), Green (Chemotherapy and Clinical Research on MBC), and Yellow (In Vivo Experiments and NT Applications). Table 8 summarizes the detailed information of the categories and their representative keywords. We further analyzed the weighted appearance years of keywords in each cluster (Fig. 10B). Blue keywords, such as “peripheral blood”, “albumin-bound paclitaxel”, and “gene delivery” appeared earlier, while red keywords, such as “immunogenic cell death”, “silver nanoparticles”, and “exosomes” were more recent, indicating that they could be future research hotspots. A visualization map was further generated (Fig. 11), depicting the top 25 terms with the strongest citation bursts. The blue and red lines indicated the timeline and burst period, respectively. The most powerful burst was in 2006 with “metastatic breast cancer” as the key phrase, and lasted for nine years. Furthermore, keywords such as “tumor microenvironment”, “targeted therapy”, “triple-negative breast cancer”, and “mesoporous silica nanoparticles” experienced a citation burst after 2021 and have continued to the present. Notably, these keywords could become new research focal points.

Discussion

General bibliometric discussion

Bibliometrics is an analytical approach that could be employed to summarize evolution trends and knowledge structures in NT application in ABC treatment. Furthermore, the number of publications is a key indicator of research dynamics and future trends. Our bibliometrics analysis of the number of publications revealed that it has increased over the past two decades. From 2013 to 2017, the number of publications on clinical NT use grew dramatically, this increase may reflect the breakthroughs in drug delivery systems and the development of novel nanomaterials [51, 52]. A surge in annual publications was also observed between 2020 and 2021, this attributable to the successful application of mRNA-based vaccines delivered via LNPs for COVID-19 treatment [53–55]. Notably, publications in this field exceeded 300 in the past four years, demonstrating sustained strong growth in nanotechnology (NT) for cancer treatment research.

We also found that China and the United States were the leading contributors regarding publication volume, accounting for 1139 and 1032 articles, respectively. This phenomenon could be attributed to the substantial financial investments by both countries in the field of clinical NT [56]. Notably, although China ranked first in publication volume, the United States still dominated this field regarding citation counts and impact. This may reflect distinct research priorities and collaboration patterns across countries. For instance, China’s high publication volume but relatively lower citation impact could indicate a focus on quantity-driven research policies or domestic collaboration networks, whereas the United States’ higher citation counts may stem from its established international collaborations and long-term investment in high-impact studies.Furthermore, among the top ten most prolific authors, Yaping Li was the most productive and influential with 43 papers and 4130 citations. His research mainly focused on nanodrug delivery systems (NDDSs) and their applications in BC treatment [57–59]. On the other hand, Zhiwen Zhang led in average citations with 112.56 citations, highlighting the high academic value and wide applicability of his work. His studies primarily focused on the applications of NPs in treating various diseases [60, 61]. Seven of the top 10 institutions in terms of publication volume were from China, highlighting China’s significant involvement in NT studies related to ABC treatment. The other three institutions were from the United States. Among them, CAS was the most productive institution (197 articles), with Harvard University leading in citation count (15337 citations). Notably, a close collaboration among these institutions could facilitate resource sharing and foster innovation breakthroughs, further accelerating growth in the research field. The IF is a widely accepted and important measure of a journal’s academic influence, which is positively correlated with citation counts but negatively correlated with publication volume. A higher IF indicates that the articles published in the journal are cited more frequently, reflecting their higher academic quality. However, it is important to acknowledge the limitations of the IF as well. For instance, the IF primarily reflects short-term citation rates and may fail to adequately capture a journal’s long-term academic impact. Furthermore, it can be significantly influenced by journal operational strategies. Among the top ten journals in terms of publication volume, only three—Journal of Controlled Release (IF 2023 = 10.5), Biomaterials (IF 2023 = 12.8), and ACS Nano (IF 2023 = 15.8)—had an IF > 10.0. Meanwhile, among the top ten most cited journals, only one had an IF < 10. The Journal of Controlled Release not only published the highest number of articles (92) but also ranked as the second most cited journal, demonstrating its dual achievement in both publication scale and academic impact in NT-related ABC treatment research. This exceptional case proves that journals can indeed attain a win-win scenario by simultaneously maintaining high productivity and significant scholarly influence.

Through reference co-citation analysis, articles generally considered as important could be identified. Our analysis revealed that the top ten co-cited studies primarily focused on NP drug delivery systems and nano-diagnostic tools, with a particular emphasis on the application of NP albumin-bound paclitaxel in cancer therapy. Gradishar WJ et al’s. study published in 2005 in the Journal of Clinical Oncology was the earliest published paper among the top ten co-cited references. This Phase III study revealed that NP albumin-bound paclitaxel had superior efficacy and safety than standard paclitaxel, representing an important advancement in MBC treatment. This article also had the second-highest burst intensity, reflecting its high value in the realm of NT use for cancer treatment. Hyuna Sung et al’s. (2020) study recorded the strongest citation burst. This study comprehensively analyzed global cancer trends and established an epidemiological foundation for NT applications in ABC treatment. It is also noteworthy that a recent article, “The Biology, Function, and Biomedical Applications of Exosomes”, examined the biological functions of exosomes and their potential applications in biomedicine. Based on these valuable insights from pertinent studies, we deduced that NP drug delivery remains a topic of sustained interest, especially in NT use for ABC treatment.

Our keyword analysis results revealed that the most frequently occurring terms were related to drug delivery, NPs, and MBC, which could be the most extensively researched subfields. Drug delivery systems are crucial for enhancing therapeutic efficacy and reducing ARs. NPs can precisely deliver drugs to tumor sites, improving treatment efficiency, owing to their unique physicochemical properties. The sustained research enthusiasm in these fields stems from the clinical success of various nanomaterial platforms such as liposomes, magnetic nanoparticles, and gold nanoparticles. These findings have led to subsequent studies on higher precision nanocarrier systems, such as using artificial intelligence to optimize the size, morphology, and surface chemistry of nanoparticles, and predict the best targeting ligand-drug loading combinations [62]. Furthermore, due to its high mortality rate and complexity, MBC urgently requires the development of innovative therapeutic approaches, making it a key research focus. For example, Feng Liu et al. created a nano-electro-platform that reshapes tumor cells to generate enhanced extracellular vesicles to combat tumor spread [63]. Recent keywords with a burst citation, such as “targeted therapy”, “tumor microenvironment”, and “mesoporous silica nanoparticles” were identified, highlighting emerging areas of interest, where pioneering work on personalized nanomedicine and microenvironment modulation has driven new experimental and clinical explorations [64–66].

Core research themes

Keywords convey an article’s thematic information, distilling and encapsulating its core content. Analyzing the frequency of keyword occurrences could directly reflect the research hotspots and emerging trends within a specific academic field. In this section, we discuss the keywords that could influence the research frontiers in our domain of interest.

NPs

According to research, NPs, a series of tiny particles with dimensions typically in the range of 1 to 100 nanometers, primarily comprise metals, as well as organic and inorganic molecules [67]. In the context of breast cancer therapy, the NPs under extensive research include gold, superparamagnetic iron oxide and silica nanoparticles, which have demonstrated superior tumor-targeting capabilities in preclinical models [68]. The high surface-to-volume ratio of NPs allows for surface modification, making them highly promising for drug delivery applications and targeted treatments. Notably, PEGylation, a common surface modification for NPs, could reduce non-specific binding with plasma proteins, decreasing the likelihood of recognition and clearance by the immune system and extending the circulation time in the bloodstream [69, 70]. Furthermore, surface modification could increase the attachment of specific ligands to the surface of NPs, promoting the precise targeting of specific cells or tissues [71]. These ligands could bind to receptors on target cells, guiding the NPs to the desired location [72, 73], thus improving drug delivery effectiveness and reducing the side effects associated with systemic treatments.

These advantages of ligand-mediated targeting have been demonstrated in recent therapeutic applications. For instance, Hong et al. employed DMXAA-modified fibronectin-conjugated gold NPs (fAuNPs) to target the tumor vasculature [74]. The DMXAA-guided fAuNPs accumulated in tumor blood vessels, enabling the destruction of the tumor vasculature via photothermal therapy. This study not only validated the crucial mechanism by which ligand moification improves nanoparticle accumulation at tumor sites, but also pioneered the innovative combination of ligand targeting with localized photothermal treatment, establishing a new technological paradigm for targeted tumor vascular therapy. These approaches was later innovated by other researchers in breast cancer treatment research. Guiquan Zhang et al. developed thermo-responsive nanoparticles that leverage the synergistic effects of photothermal therapy and chemotherapy while inducing immunogenic cell death [75], thereby providing a reliable and promising strategy for breast cancer immunotherapy. Meanwhile, Silvia Vanni et al. successfully enhanced the targeted delivery efficacy of trastuzumab in HER2-positive breast cancer cells by employing PLGA nanoparticle-based drug loading technology coupled with fiber-optic light-controlled release [76].

Drug delivery

Traditional drug delivery systems (DDS) are those used to introduce therapeutic molecules into the human body via oral administration, injections, or topical applications [77]. These conventional methods have certain shortcomings, including non-specific distribution of drugs in the body, low bioavailability, rapid drug metabolism, and inability to effectively penetrate tumor cell barriers [68, 78, 79]. Moreover, the drugs may be widely distributed to healthy tissues before reaching the target site, not only reducing the therapeutic efficacy but potentially also leading to severe side effects.

(1) New Types of Nanocarriers and Nano-drug Delivery Systems

Researches on clinical NT has yielded novel insights that could be leveraged to address these issues. Encapsulating drugs in nanocarriers offers several advantages, including enhanced targeting precision, protection from degradation in the bloodstream, reduced systemic toxicity, improved therapeutic efficacy, increased drug solubility, and enhanced pharmacodynamic and pharmacokinetic properties [51, 80–82]. Consequently, researchers have developed various nanocarrier systems, including solid lipid nanoparticles (SLN), liposomes, polymeric micelles, metallic NPs, spanlastics, nanoemulsions, and nanoliposomes [83–86]. Liposomes, one of the earliest discovered carriers, could result in an enhanced permeability and retention (EPR) effect, allowing drugs to accumulate more effectively at the site of disease.

Several drug delivery systems have been developed through research. For instance, using a lipid hydration method, Mst Naznin Ara and colleagues developed an aptamer-modified nanocarrier system, Apt-PEG-LP, which could target tumor Endothelial Cells (ECs) for efficient drug delivery [87]. Furthermore, using a 532 nm wavelength Nd: YAG laser commonly employed to treat arterial occlusive diseases, Parvin Ahmaditabar designed a liposome drug delivery system, Lip/PSCS-tPA, for a controlled release and targeted delivery of a tissue Plasminogen Activator (tPA) [88]. Moreover, an increasing number of innovative nanocarriers are currently attracting the attention of researchers. Chitosan NPs can encapsulate hydrophobic anticancer molecules, thus enhancing their solubility, permeability, and bioavailability. Additionally, folate-modified chitosan NPs could bind to the overexpressed folate receptor α on the surface of ovarian and endometrial cancer cells, overcoming resistance to conventional treatments [89].

(2) Delibery and targeting strategies

There are two methods for nano-drug delivery: passive delivery and self-delivery. In passive delivery, the drug is encapsulated within the structure through the hydrophobic effect and is gradually released to the target disease area. Self-delivery involves directly conjugating the drug to the carrier, and timing of release is crucial. If the drug does not reach the target site on time or dissociates too quickly, its efficacy will be reduced; On the contrary, timely release can maximize its therapeutic effect [90]. Nano-drug targeting has two approaches: “active” and “passive“ [91]. Active targeting involves conjugating ligands like antibodies and peptides to the NP carrier, anchoring the drug to receptors at the target site. In contrast, passive targeting relies on circulating the drug carrier through the bloodstream, using properties like pH, temperature, and molecular size to direct it to the target site and accumulate in the tumor via the EPR effect. Overall, in addition to enhancing the targeting and therapeutic efficacy of drugs, nano-drug delivery systems could also provide more precise and personalized treatment options for diseases.

(3) Nano-drug delivery in ABC.

Passive targeting plays a crucial role in the treatment of advanced breast cancer. Although some studies suggest that the EPR effect may vary due to intrinsic tumor heterogeneity and individual factors [92], and there are significant differences between animal models and human solid tumors, the clinically approved nanoplatforms for the treatment of breast cancer are currently employing this effect [91]. Compared to passive targeting, active targeting offers advantages such as precise delivery and reducing of drug toxicity. Specific targeted nanoparticles recognize and bind to molecular targets associated with breast cancer growth (such as HER2/neu, MAPK) and breast cancer stem cell surface markers like CD24, CD44, thereby achieving localized delivery [93]. Based on this, researchers have also proposed emerging methods such as bioorthogonal ligand-based targeting, microneedles, stimuli-responsive delivery systems [93, 94]. Even though many emerging approaches have demonstrated considerable efficacy, the majority of current research on nano-drug delivery in ABC has not yet progressed to clinical trials, making its practical benefits difficult to assess.

MBC

In BC cases, the lung, brain, and bone marrow are the organs most susceptible to colonization by CTCs [95]. These organs differ significantly in structure and physiological characteristics, not only influencing the metastatic process but also posing unique challenges for effective treatment [96]. Increasing number of clinical trials are focusing on the application of nanotechnology in metastatic breast cancer (MBC) treatment, with nanoparticle-based DDS demonstrating significant potential in MBC chemotherapy regimens. Nano-drugs such as Doxil and Abraxane, which possess distinct physical properties and biological effects, have entered clinical trials. The combination of atezolizumab with albumin-bound paclitaxel has received FDA approval, showing therapeutic value for PD-L1-positive metastatic TNBC patients, with final survival analyses confirming prognostic benefits for this patient subgroup [97, 98]. Furthermore, a phase I single-arm trail evaluated the toxicity profile of the albumin-bound paclitaxel/non-pegylated liposomal doxorubicin combination regimen in treatment-naïve HER2-negative MBC patients, confirming its favorable efficacy with manageable toxicity, warranting further validation in larger-scale clinical trials [99]. While nano-based therapies show promise for systemic control, site-specific approaches are required for different metastatic patterns, where anatomical barriers and microenvironment heterogeneity demand tailored strategies.

(1) Lung Metastasis

Lung metastatic breast cancer (LMBC), the second most common among breast cancer metastases, remains challenging to treat [100], accounting for 60–70% of BC-related deaths attributable to lung metastasis [101, 102]. Although LMBC often occurs within five years of the initial diagnosis, noticeable symptoms typically appear only when the disease has progressed significantly, leading to a poor prognosis [103]. Therefore, early detection and effective treatment of lung metastatic lesions are critical for improving patient prognosis.

Through its interaction with the ligand CXCL12, the chemokine receptor CXCR4 might enable tumor cells expressing CXCR4 receptors to remain in specific regions [104]. According to research, CXCR4 expression is crucial for the proliferation, invasion, and formation of distant metastases in BC [105], making it a potential therapeutic target. Wang et al. combined CXCR4-enriched 4T1 cell membranes with copper-indium-sulfide Quantum Dots (QDs) NPs, creating a nano-trap, which they assessed for its efficacy [106]. The system occupied CXCL12 and effectively prevented the metastasis of BC cells to the lungs. Furthermore, Yongfeng Zhao et al. designed a PET imaging probe with high sensitivity and assessed its effectiveness in a mouse 4T1 BC model [107]. Specifically, they conjugated the targeted ligand AMD3100, a CXCR4 antagonist, with CXCR4-targeted gold nanoclusters doped with the PET isotope 64Cu, enabling prompt and more sensitive detection of CXCR4 upregulation in the pre-metastatic lung microenvironment. Additionally, Chia-Wei Yang et al. combined Indocyanine Green (ICG) and Hyaluronic Acid (HA), creating iron oxide NPs (NP-ICG-HA), which they assessed for their efficacy [108]. These NPs targeted BC cells overexpressing the glycoprotein CD44 with HA, and clear signals were displayed in the lungs during magnetic particle imaging (MPI), providing a non-invasive method for detecting BC lung metastasis.

Radiotherapy damages tumor cells and DNA due to the high-energy X-rays [109], but the existence of hypoxic tumor microenvironment may induce resistance to radiotherapy. High radiation doses may damage healthy tissues, both of which limit the clinical application of RT [110]. To address this issue, Qiyue Wang et al. designed bismuth nano-in-micro spheres using dry powder inhalation [111]. They applied the spray-drying technology to incorporate multifunctional lipid-modified bismuth-based nanoflowers (DP-BNFs) into lactose microparticles, thereby formulating a radiosensitizer (DP-BNF@Lat-MPs) with an appropriate median mass aerodynamic diameter. designed formulation was found to be form deposits on the lungs and exhibit rapid drug release following accumulation in the tumor area. Thus, cell membrane-coated nanoparticles may serve as novel drug delivery strategies [112], in which nanoparticles and nanocarriers are coated and/or camouflaged with cell membranes to enhance specific binding to receptors overexpressed in the tumor microenvironment. This allows precise targeting of tumor cells. Among them, macrophage membranes and tumor cell membranes are have been proposed to be ideal materials [95]. Cao and his colleagues developed a macrophage-based delivery system (LD-MDS) by modifying the macrophage membrane which sustains its activity while preventing the development of cytotoxicity before reaching the lung metastatic site [58]. Legumain protease can activate the modified macrophages at the lung metastatic site. The cleaved Melittin propeptide is converted into exosome-like nanovesicles that encapsulate the cytotoxic drug DM4, which then enters 4T1 cells, where it is activated and releases the toxic drug. Bacterial ghosts (BGs) are hollow shells derived from multiple biological vectors (such as red blood cells, cancer cells, and bacteria) via genetic engineering or fragmentation [113]. They lack proliferative function but retain immune response capabilities [114]. Their large inner cavity and intact outer membrane allow them to carry a variety of drugs [115]. Although cancer cell membranes can facilitate homologous recruitment, they possess antigens on their surface which have weak immunogenicity, which hinders the recognition by the immune system [116]. Dandan Ling combined the advantages of both and prepared cancer cell membrane-coated liposomal paclitaxel-loaded bacterial ghosts (LP@BG@CCM) [113]. This innovative material demonstrated a markedly enhanced lung targeting rate and effectively modulated various immune factors, highlighting its promising potential for therapeutic applications.

(2) Bone Metastasis

Bone metastasis is a complex process, that involves seeding, survival, dormancy and reactivation of disseminated tumor cells [117]. Approximately 70% of patients with ABC develop bone metastasis [118], which is generally characterized by osteolytic bone metastasis caused by abnormal activation of osteoclasts [119].

Traditional diagnostic methods, such as CT and MRI, are not sufficiently effective in detecting small bone metastatic lesions [120]. Therefore, the application of nanotechnology may overcome the aforementioned limitations. Kantamneni et al. designed a novel imaging probe, rare-earth-metal-doped, albumin-encapsulated nanoparticles (ReANCs) [121]. In their study, it was observed that the probe could detect small metastatic lesions via whole-body SWIR imaging. This nanoprobe and SWIR technology were successfully employed to visualize micro-metastatic lesions that were previously undetectable by traditional MRI. Moreover, a pH-responsive mechanism was utilized as another innovative approach. Han Qiao et al. designed a treatment and diagnostic system using ZA-anchored Gd-doped UCMSs loaded with PL and PAA (PL-Gd-UCNP@ZA-PAA, PUCZP) [122]. In acidic region where osteoclasts are more active, this system is capable of accurately targeting exposed HA, enhancing its sensitivity and specificity for the detection and treatment of early-stage bone metastasis.

Tumor invasion into the bones may cause fractures, spinal cord compression, severe bone pain, and other complications, which decrease the patient’s quality of life [123, 124]. Therefore, bone metastasis treatments need to eliminate cancer cells and control the complications associated with bone metastasis. Alendronate exhibit excellent bone-targeting ability and inhibition of osteoclasts [95]. Clinically, it is applied to treat bone metastases in breast cancer as well as reduce the development of skeletal-related events (SREs) [125, 126]. Ravi Prakash Shukla et al. conjugated bisphosphonate (ALN) and Oleanolic acid (OA) at both ends of PEG (polyethylene glycol), promoting their self-assembly into porous crystalsomes (ALN + OA@NCs) [127]. In the 4T1 cell-induced tibia model, ALN + OA@NCs markedly reduced bone loss and yielded higher accumulation at the tumor site. These findings indicate that this drug delivery strategy effectively targets breast cancer bone metastases and alleviates associated complications. Wu et al. further demonstrated that liposomes modified with alendronate and low molecular weight heparin (LMWH) for the delivery of the anticancer drug doxorubicin (DOX) significantly inhibit tumor growth and suppress tumor metastasis [128].

(3) Brain Metastasis

Breast cancer brain metastasis (BCBM) has been reported to occur in about 15% of patients [129, 130]. Moreover, patients presenting with more aggressive subtypes, such as triple-negative subtype and HER2-positive subtype breast cancer, are at risk of developing brain metastasis [131, 132]. Breast cancer with brain metastases is associated with a high mortality rate, with survival often limited to just a few months.

The blood-brain barrier (BBB) is primarily composed of endothelial cells, pericytes, and the terminal feet of astrocytes, which work together to tightly regulate the transport of molecules to the brain [133]. MBC cells disrupt BBB integrity by upregulating markers such as COX-2 and HBEGF, while simultaneously triggering neuroinflammation and recruiting microglia, which in turn release cytokines like TNF-α to further enhance BBB permeability, thereby establishing a positive feedback loop that promotes tumor metastasis and immune cell infiltration [134]. But the complex structure of BBB presents a significant challenge in the treatment of BCBM, as it prevents effective drug delivery to the brain [135].

To address this challenge, Heng Lu et al. developed an Angiopep-2-modified PEG-PLA micelle system as a dual-targeting drug delivery platform, which co-loaded paclitaxel and lapatinib to achieve both blood-brain barrier penetration and brain metastasis targeting through low-density lipoprotein receptor-related protein (LRP) recognition, with its anti-brain metastasis efficacy being validated in both in vitro and in vivo studies [136]. Zimiao Luo et al. synthesized a vascular-targeting anticancer peptide (pVAP) by combining para-hydroxybenzoic acid (pHA) and the VAP peptide (DSDNDTDRDVDADP). This peptide could modify platelet membrane-coated liposomes that encapsulated Cabazitaxel (CbTX) nanocrystals (CNC), forming a novel drug delivery system [135]. The results indicated that the delivery system achieved specific targeting to 4T1 tumor cells and could cross the BBB, thereby enhancing tumor inhibition compared to treatment with free CbTX. Bolong Tao designed a biomimetic nano-bomb ExoCAR/T7@Micelle based on CAR-NK cell-derived exosomes, which demonstrated effective treatment of HER2 + BCBM [137]. In an animal experiment, Eun-Joo Park found that the application of focused ultrasound (FUS) and circulating microbubbles to induce temporary disruption of the BBB before trastuzumab treatment led to the satisfactory therapeutic effect of BMBC [138]. Although these studies provide insights into the effective treatment of BCBM, there is still a gap before they can be applied in clinical practice. Additionally, key issues such as long-term safety and resistance management remain unresolved, which also points the way for future research.

Limitation

Although this study was performed in strict conformity to the bibliometric standards, there are some limitations that need to be acknowledged. Firstly, the data used in this research were obtained from the WoSCC database, which did not include studies indexed in other databases. Secondly, we only enrolled studies published in English, suggesting that the present results may be globally representative. However, a comprehensive analysis was conducted suggesting that our results are robust. Future studies are needed to validate our findings in studies published in other languages. Thirdly, the threshold settings used in the retrieval software may have led to the omission of some data. Furthermore, considering the time constraints, some published studies may not be captured in the current data. Additionally, high-quality articles published outside the specified time frame could not be included in this study.

Conclusion

This study applied scientometric tools to perform a system-wide analysis of the literature published from 2005 to 2024 on the use of nanotechnology in ABC. The study revealed publication trends and global distribution patterns, offering valuable insights into the evolution of research hotspots and emerging themes in the field. Over the past two decades, the number of publications on nanotechnology in ABC has increased significantly, with China and the United States leading as the primary contributors. In future, collaborative research among countries, researchers and institutions is advocated to accelerate the use of nanotechnology in ABC. As illustrated by keywords and citation analysis, pivotal research areas identified include metastatic breast cancer, drug delivery, and nanoparticles. In summary, the bibliometric and visualization analysis underscores the pivotal role of nanotechnology in the diagnosis and treatment of ABC. It is anticipated that advancements in nanotechnology will lead to significant improvements in the clinical outcomes of ABC.

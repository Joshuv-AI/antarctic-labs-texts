Beyond Google Images: Crafting impactful images for presentations or clinical use with AI
Authors: Shahul Ameen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
In presentations for teaching, research, or public education purposes, well-chosen images enhance focus, reflection, and memory retention. AI tools help create images that are culturally sensitive and inclusive and depict abstract or complex psychiatric scenarios. AI images should be clear, audience-tailored, and complementary to text and verbal explanations. Clear, detailed prompts ensure alignment with the intended vision and stylistic consistency across images. Addressing variables such as style, mood, and color helps create unique, compelling images. AI images can also be helpful in psychoeducation and psychotherapy, as they can be customized to the patient’s demographics and symptoms and can be quickly created during a consultation. They would especially benefit patients with low literacy. This article describes the leading AI image-generation tools, offers practical guidance on image creation and effective prompt strategies, and provides example prompts. It also discusses the limitations of current models and ethical considerations. Together, these insights support educators, researchers, and clinicians in using AI images responsibly and creatively to enrich their presentations and patient care.

Carefully chosen images enhance teaching, research, and public awareness presentations. When congruent with spoken content, they improve attention, encourage reflection, and support memory by associating concepts with visual metaphors.[1] A study found that while relevant images help focus and retention, extraneous ones can distract and reduce learning effectiveness.[2] For about two decades, presenters’ primary source of images was image search engines such as Google Images. However, images sourced through them have limitations. Many are copyrighted, watermarked, heavily priced, or of poor resolution. They seldom depict Indian faces or cultural contexts and sometimes portray patients in a stigmatizing manner. Images that represent complex psychiatric scenarios are tough to find. Many AI-based image generators have recently emerged [Table 1]. With appropriate instructions, AI-generated images (AIGIs) can accurately reflect Indian cultural contexts, illustrate abstract concepts [e.g., a patient with obsessive compulsive disorder (OCD) practicing cognitive defusion and preparing for value-based action, Figure 1], and maintain scientific accuracy. This is particularly important in psychiatry, where culturally sensitive visuals are crucial. AIGIs also allow consistent color schemes that suit the topic, audience, or slide backgrounds.

A review found that in health education, visual aids like pictures can improve adherence, comprehension, and recall, which can lead to better patient outcomes. Regional and reinforcing visual aids yielded the largest improvements, especially in patients with low literacy.[3] In psychoeducation, visualization of key information is a crucial psychotherapeutic element.[4] In countries like India with high illiteracy rates, AIGIs may be valuable aids in psychoeducation and psychotherapy.

This article offers tips, drawn from personal experience,[56] recent literature, and relevant websites, for creating and using AIGIs in academic slide presentations, with principles also applicable to posters. It also explores clinical applications of AIGIs in psychiatric practice, provides example images, and addresses technical and ethical considerations.

Effective prompting varies by tool, with each requiring unique structures or syntax. For example, Stable Diffusion leverages negative prompts for better results, while Midjourney uses specific parameters like --ar 16:9.

Tools such as Stable Diffusion (via its Prompt Search Engine) and Ideogram (via its Explore page) allow us to view images created by others and the prompts used, providing an opportunity to improve our prompting skills. Similarly, NightCafe and Ideogram enhance our prompts by adding extra details, allowing us to learn from these refinements to craft better prompts [see online-only Supplementary File 1 for an example].

ENSURING STYLISTIC CONSISTENCY

For consistent style and color across images, reuse similar prompts or modify only the essential elements.[7] Saving a prompt template (i.e., text describing the outcome image, with slots for variables we can change) in a separate document would be helpful. Alternatively, create a “Project” on the AI website (this option is typically available only in paid accounts). Then, upload a project file that describes the preferred style and tone (e.g., “Use a modern, minimalist style with a light color scheme, ensuring clarity and easy visibility against a white background. Always use Indian faces and attire. The images are for a professional academic presentation on [topic]. Use the 16:9 aspect ratio for slide”). With each image request, ask the AI to follow the instructions in the project file; it serves as a template to ensure uniformity and save time.

EXAMPLE PROMPTS

Prompts may be broad (e.g., “Please provide an image about stigma of mental illness”) or highly specific, depending on need. The online-only Supplementary Files 2 and 3 provide example prompts for images intended for various positions in a set of slides, along with the outputs.

CLINICAL APPLICATIONS

Online-only Supplementary File 1 features 11 sample prompts to create example images that can be used for various clinical needs, ranging from anger management to cognitive behavior therapy, along with the resulting images and prompt templates (where relevant). Most of these images can be quickly created during a session or consultation, incorporating the demographics and clinical details of the patient being seen. (Some images needed many iterations—using the provided images as references may ease the process.) These images may require fewer stylistic considerations than those for presentations. However, since these images are customized to patients’ personal details, they must be treated with the same level of privacy and confidentiality as other clinical data, adhering to India’s Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023. Patient consent should be obtained, particularly if the image is to be stored or reused.

This is a rapidly advancing field. AI tools may evolve to address many of these limitations.

In summary, the strategic use of AIGIs can make presentations more engaging, inclusive, and pedagogically sound, as well as psychosocial interventions more appealing and effective. As you are the ultimate judge of the appropriateness and effectiveness of the AIGIs, keep learning the principles of visual design and communication. Because AI models evolve rapidly, stay updated on emerging technologies and tools. Researchers should assess the effectiveness of AI-generated vs. traditional images in presentations, including a comparison of different AI tools, using variables such as audience perception and learning outcomes. Qualitative studies are needed to explore patients’ perspectives on the clinical use of AIGIs.

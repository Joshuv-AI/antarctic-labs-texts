miR-181a-5p: a key regulator of placental function in health and disease
Authors: Leisa Lopes-Aguiar, Rafaella Scandiuzzi, Gabriela Michelazzo, Murilo Geraldo, Maria Cristina Cintra Gomes-Marcondes, Fernanda Garanhani de Castro Surita, Maria Laura Costa, Than Gábor, Laís Rosa Viana
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTMicroRNAs (miRNAs) are essential post-transcriptional regulators implicated in diverse physiological and pathological processes. Recently, miR-181a-5p emerged as a critical modulator of placental function. This review synthesizes literature on miR-181a-5p at the maternal-foetal interface in normal pregnancies and complications, such as preeclampsia, gestational hypertension, gestational diabetes, and Rh-negative haemolytic disease of the foetus and newborn (Rh-HDFN). From a PubMed search, 12 articles were qualitatively synthesized. Evidence shows that early miR-181a-5p suppression is crucial for normal trophoblast proliferation via the renin-angiotensin system. Conversely, its aberrant expression characterizes gestational pathologies. In preeclampsia, miR-181a-5p upregulation suppresses trophoblast viability, migration, and invasion, increasing apoptosis. In gestational hypertension, its downregulation perturbs glycolytic metabolism. Furthermore, elevated miR-181a-5p disrupts endothelial and placental barriers in gestational diabetes and Rh-HDFN, respectively. Ultimately, miR-181a- as a pleiotropic regulator in the placenta. Decoding its molecular networks underscores its clinical potential as a biomarker and therapeutic target.KEYWORDS: MiR-181a-5p, placenta, preeclampsia, hypertension, diabetes, Rh-HDFN

Introduction

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are small non-coding RNAs comprising 17–25 nucleotides that play a crucial role in the post-transcriptional regulation of gene expression, by targeting mRNAs for degradation or inhibiting translation [1]. A single miRNA may target hundreds of mRNAs, thereby influencing the expression of a wide range of genes involved in a variety of biological processes, such as cell proliferation, differentiation, development, and apoptosis [1–4]. Aberrant expression of miRNAs, and consequently their target mRNAs, has been associated with the development and progression of multiple human diseases, including neurodegenerative disorders, obesity, cardiovascular disease, cancer, hepatitis, atherosclerosis, diabetes, preeclampsia, and others [5–8].

Among a wide number of miRNAs, miR‑181a‑5p has been extensively studied as a regulatory miRNA with altered expression in various diseases [9–14], especially in different types of cancer [15–20]. This miRNA has been shown to exhibit dual behaviour. Primarily, it can act as an oncomir by targeting early growth response 1 (EGR1) and wnt inhibitory factor 1 (WIF1) in acute leukaemia [21–23], mixed-lineage leukaemia protein 3 (MLL3) in papillary thyroid cancer [24], SMAD family member 7 (SMAD7) in ovarian cancer [25], ras association domain family member 6 (RASSF6) in osteosarcoma [26], tumour suppressor candidate 3 (TUSC3), protein inhibitor of activated STAT 3 (PIAS3) and n-myc downstream-regulated gene 2 (NDRG2) in breast cancer [27–29], and phosphatase and tensin homolog (PTEN) in cervical cancer [30]. On the other hand, it can also act as a tumour suppressor by targeting cyclin dependent kinase 1 (CDK1) in non-small cell lung cancer cells [31], notch receptor 2 (NOTCH2) in glioblastoma multiforme [32], as well as glucose-regulated protein 78 (GRP78) and matrix metalloproteinase-14 (MMP14) in cervical cancer [33,34].

It is also well established that cancer and the placenta share several biological similarities [35–39], including high tissue invasion capacity, promotion of angiogenesis, accelerated growth, and the ability to evade the immune system [36,39] The placenta is a transient and essential organ that functions as the maternal-foetal interface [40]. In this context, beyond its established role in cancer, miR-181a-5p has also been reported to be involved in normal placental function [41,42] and placental dysfunction related to gestational pathologies, such as preeclampsia [43–47], gestational hypertension [48], gestational diabetes [49], and Rh-negative haemolytic disease of the foetus and newborn (Rh-HDFN) [50].

Therefore, the present review aims to summarize the available studies describing the role of miR-181a-5p in the placenta through the regulation of potential target genes, proteins and metabolites, thereby providing a framework for future investigations.

Methods

The PubMed database was searched for all records published up to January 2026. The search strategy employed various combinations of the following terms: ‘mir-181a-5p’ AND (‘placenta’ OR ‘pregnancy’ OR ‘preeclampsia’ OR ‘trophoblast’ OR ‘gestational diabetes’ OR ‘gestational hypertension’ OR ‘small for gestational age’ OR ‘fetal growth restriction’ OR ‘miscarriage’ OR ‘premature’ OR ‘pregnancy pathology’ OR ‘placental dysfunction’ OR ‘placental pathology’). The initial search yielded 159 candidate articles. After the manual removal of duplicates, 40 articles were screened. The inclusion criteria consisted of studies evaluating miR-181a-5p in placental tissue and trophoblastic cell lines. Subsequently, review articles and studies evaluating samples from blood, non-placental tissues, extracellular vesicles, or miR-181a were excluded. Ultimately, 12 original research articles met all inclusion criteria and were included in this review.

miR-181a-5p in placental pathophysiology

Normal placental function

Throughout gestation, the placenta undergoes tightly regulated structural development and molecular shifts that reflects stage-specific functional demands [40]. Early placentation is characterized by extensive placental remodelling, which occurs under low oxygen tension levels and involves trophoblast proliferation, invasion, and the critical remodelling of maternal spiral arteries [51,52]. Mid-gestation is marked by changes in villus architecture, increased placental perfusion, and metabolic adaptations [40,51]. At term, the placenta exhibits functional maturation accompanied by inflammatory signalling pathways that are critical for labour initiation and parturition [53].

These temporally coordinated transitions are supported by dynamic gene expression programmes, including post-transcriptional regulatory mechanisms such as those mediated by miRNAs. By acting as fine-tuners of gene expression, these miRNAs ensure that proliferative networks dominate during early pregnancy. Consequently, identifying miRNAs with stage-specific expression profiles is essential to understand the molecular basis of normal placentation [54,55].

Specifically, miR-181a-5p has been linked to placental development through the regulation of the human placental renin-angiotensin system (RAS) [41,42] (Tables 1 and 2). In this context, the appropriate regulation of the human placental RAS, particularly the increased expression of the renin (REN) gene and the prorenin receptor (ATPase H+ -transporting accessory protein) during early gestation, may be crucial for a successful pregnancy outcome [56].

Wang et al. [41] investigated the expression of placental miRNAs across gestation by comparing placental villous tissues collected in early (10–11 weeks), mid-gestation (14–18 weeks), and at term (38–40 weeks). Their results demonstrated that miR-181a-5p expression was significantly lower in early gestation compared to term placentas. In contrast, REN mRNA expression and prorenin protein levels were higher during early gestation (Figure 1). To elucidate the underlying regulatory mechanism, the interaction between miR-181a-5p and its targets was assessed in trophoblast cell line of human first-trimester placenta (HTR-8/SVneo). Overexpression of miR-181a-5p resulted in a significant reduction in both REN mRNA and prorenin protein levels. These findings suggest that the physiological suppression of miR-181a-5p during early gestation permits the upregulation of placental RAS activity, thereby promoting trophoblast proliferation and supporting placental development.

Corroborating these findings, Arthurs et al. [42] demonstrated that the transfection of miR-181a-5p into HTR-8/SVneo cells significantly reduced REN mRNA expression, further validating REN as a functional target of miR-181a-5p in trophoblast cells. Moreover, miR-181a-5p overexpression markedly impaired trophoblast proliferation. Together, these results underscore a critical role for miR-181a-5p as a negative regulator of trophoblast proliferation during placental development, meaning that its downregulation enhances proliferation, whereas its overexpression suppresses it.

Given the importance of proper miR-181a-5p regulation in normal placental development, the dysregulation of this miRNA may, at least in part, drive the pathogenesis of various placental complications [57].

Placental dysfunction

Gestational hypertension and preeclampsia

Hypertensive disorders in pregnancy are important contributors to the increased risk of maternal, foetal, and neonatal morbidity and mortality [58–60]. Gestational hypertension is defined as a systolic blood pressure≥ 140 mmHg and/or a diastolic blood pressure≥ 90 mmHg on two occasions more than four hours apart after 20 weeks of gestation [61,62]. While preeclampsia shares the exact blood pressure criteria of gestational hypertension, it is primarily distinguished by the presence of proteinuria, which traditionally defines preeclampsia without severe features. However, the condition escalates to preeclampsia with severe features if blood pressure reaches severe ranges (≥160/110 mmHg) on two occasions more than four hours apart, or sustained for over 15 minutes requiring immediate antihypertensive treatment [63]. Furthermore, a diagnosis of preeclampsia with severe features is also established, even in the absence of proteinuria, by specific symptoms or laboratory values indicative of end-organ damage, such as thrombocytopenia, renal insufficiency, impaired liver function, pulmonary oedema, or new-onset neurologic symptoms [61,62,64]. Beyond these clinical manifestations, this pathology also affects the placenta. Crucially, a shallow extravillous trophoblast invasion prevents the appropriate remodelling of uterine spiral arteries, causing a reduction in placental perfusion. This localized failure triggers a pathophysiological cascade characterized by hypoxia-reoxygenation injury, increased oxidative stress, and inflammation [65].

Given this clinical and pathophysiological, stratifying hypertensive disorders into molecular clusters would enable early and accurate screening as well as personalized preventive therapies [66]. In this context, many studies have confirmed the involvement of miRNAs in preeclampsia, including placenta-specific miRNAs, and their roles in the pathogenesis, diagnosis, and prediction of the disease. Because these epigenetic regulators modulate genes associated with endothelial function, immune response, and trophoblast behaviour, their dysregulation often triggers a cascade of cellular dysfunctions at the maternal-foetal interface, such as impaired spiral artery remodelling and exacerbated inflammation [67–70]. Specifically, miR-181a-5p has been linked to placental dysfunction through perturbations in glycolytic metabolism and membrane components in gestational hypertension [48], and alterations in trophoblast proliferation, apoptosis, invasion, and migration in preeclampsia [43–47] (Tables 1 and 2).

To explore the complex molecular landscape of this condition, a recent study employed an integrative multi-omics approach to investigate placental alterations associated with gestational hypertension [48]. The study population was divided into two groups: a healthy pregnancy group (39 weeks) and a gestational hypertension group (38 weeks). The authors observed reduced placental expression of miR-181a-5p and 143 differentially abundant metabolites. Integrative miRNA-mRNA-metabolite profiling revealed that miR-181a-5p and its predicted targets, especially SAC1 like phosphatidylinositide phosphatase (SACM1L), PTEN, lysophosphatidylcholine acyltransferase 1 (LPCAT1) and lysophosphatidylcholine acyltransferase 2 (LPCAT2), were related to glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate and cytidinediphosphocholine (CDP-choline) metabolites, which impact the inositol phosphate and glycerophospholipid pathways (Figure 1). These findings highlight a specific miRNA, molecular targets, and metabolites that may play distinct roles in the pathophysiology of gestational hypertension. However, contrasting results exist; for instance, Hromadnikova et al. [71] reported no significant differences in placental miR-181a-5p expression when comparing pregnancies complicated by gestational hypertension (39 weeks) with normal pregnancies (40 weeks).

Moving from gestational hypertension to preeclampsia, the expression pattern of miR-181a-5p alters notably. Wang et al. [43] reported an upregulation of miR-181a-5p in placental samples from pregnancies complicated by preeclampsia (37 weeks) compared with control placentas (39 weeks). Consistent with these findings, Zheng et al. [47] evaluated the basal plate region of placentas from women with severe preeclampsia (34 weeks) and uncomplicated pregnancies (37 weeks), demonstrating that miR-181a-5p expression was significantly increased, whereas matrix metallopeptidase 9 (MMP9) expression was downregulated (Figure 1). Notably, MMP9 levels positively correlated with gestational age at delivery and neonatal birth weight, while miR-181a-5p exhibited a negative correlation with these clinical parameters. Consequently, higher MMP9 expression was associated with fewer adverse outcomes, whereas elevated miR-181a-5p was linked to an increased risk of complications. Together, these findings implicate the miR-181a-5p/MMP9 axis in the regulation of foetal growth and pregnancy duration, highlighting its potential as a therapeutic target in severe preeclampsia.

Adding another layer of complexity to the pathophysiology of preeclampsia, a recent study investigated the role of miR-181a-5p within mast cell-derived exosomes [46]. The authors isolated these cells from the placentas of preeclamptic women (after 28 weeks) and normotensive controls, observing a significant upregulation of miR-181a-5p in mast cells from the preeclamptic group. To better understand the effects of this upregulation, mast cell line of human blood CD34+ (LUVA) were transfected with a miR-181a-5p mimic, which led to an increased release of miR-181a-5p-enriched exosomes. When applied to HTR-8/SVneo trophoblast cells, these exosomes significantly impaired trophoblast viability, migration, and invasion. Mechanistically, bioinformatic analysis and validation assays identified YY1 transcription factor (YY1) as a direct target of miR-181a-5p. In trophoblast cells, exosomal miR-181a-5p suppressed YY1 expression, thereby inhibiting trophoblast invasiveness (Figure 1). Conversely, YY1 overexpression reversed these suppressive effects, restoring cell migration and invasion while concurrently upregulating MMP9 expression. Collectively, these findings propose that mast cell-derived exosomal miR-181a-5p impairs trophoblast function through the YY1/MMP9 pathway, highlighting its potential as both a biomarker and a pathogenic regulator in preeclampsia.

Reinforcing the suppressive effect of miR-181a-5p on trophoblast invasion and metalloproteinase activity, Huang et al. [45] evaluated placental tissues from women with preeclampsia (29 weeks) and normotensive controls (31 weeks), corroborating the upregulation of this miRNA in the disease. To investigate its biological role, in vitro experiments were conducted using a miR-181a-5p mimic in HTR-8/SVneo cells and choriocarcinoma cell line of human placenta (JAR). Functional assays revealed that miR-181a-5p overexpression inhibited cell proliferation by inducing G2/M cell-cycle arrest, which was associated with reduced levels of cyclin B1 (CCNB1) and CDK1. Furthermore, it suppressed the invasive capacity of trophoblast cells, correlating with the inhibition of the matrix metallopeptidase 2 (MMP2) and MMP9. Finally, transfected cells exhibited increased apoptotic activity, evidenced by elevated levels of cleaved caspase 3 (CASP3) (Figure 1). Collectively, these findings suggest that miR-181a-5p overexpression drives widespread trophoblast dysfunction, representing a key factor in the pathogenesis of preeclampsia.

Expanding the investigation to the maternal-foetal interface, Wu et al. [44] demonstrated that miR-181a-5p expression is significantly elevated in the decidua of patients with severe preeclampsia (36 weeks) compared to control pregnancies (37 weeks). In vitro functional assays across multiple trophoblast cell lines corroborated that the upregulation of this miRNA compromises cell invasion and migration. Mechanistically, target prediction and validation identified insulin-like growth factor 2 mRNA binding protein 2 (IGF2BP2) as a direct target of miR-181a-5p in HTR-8/SVneo cells, exhibiting reduced expression in preeclamptic tissues. The study confirmed that silencing IGF2BP2 mimics the inhibitory effects of the miRNA, while its overexpression reverses this dysfunction. Ultimately, these findings indicate that miR-181a-5p suppresses IGF2BP2 to regulate trophoblast differentiation, further establishing its involvement in the pathogenesis of severe preeclampsia (Figure 1).

Despite the prevailing evidence supporting the upregulation of miR-181a-5p in preeclampsia, it is important to notice that both Hromadnikova et al. [71] and Gusar et al. [72] observed no significant differences in placental miR-181a-5p expression when comparing preeclamptic and normotensive pregnancies. Specifically, Hromadnikova et al. [71] evaluated a substantial cohort of preeclampsia cases and controls. Similarly, Gusar et al. [72] analysed placental samples from early-onset (31 weeks) and late-onset (37 weeks) preeclampsia versus normotensive controls (30 and 37 weeks, respectively), yet also found no significant dysregulation of this miRNA.

Gestational diabetes

Gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) is defined as glucose intolerance resulting in hyperglycaemia of variable severity, with onset or first recognition during the second or third trimester of pregnancy that is not clearly overt diabetes prior to gestation or other types of diabetes occurring throughout pregnancy [73]. As the crucial interface between mother and foetus, the placenta is profoundly impacted by this altered metabolic environment. GDM dysregulates the placental transport of glucose, amino acids, and lipids [74]. Furthermore, oxidative stress and inflammation develop as consequences of this placental metabolic dysregulation [75]. Consequently, GDM is associated with adverse pregnancy outcomes and increases the risk of obesity and diabetes in both offspring and affected mothers later in life [76]. Uncertainty remains regarding diagnostic criteria, treatment effectiveness, and cost-benefit aspects; however, recent studies suggest that miRNAs may play important roles in gestational diabetes mellitus development and may serve as potential biomarkers. By modulating genes linked to endothelial homoeostasis and metabolic stress, aberrant miRNA profiles actively contribute to the vascular complications seen in diabetic placentas [67,77]. In this context, miR-181a-5p has been linked to placental dysfunction through the disruption of paracellular junctions and vascular permeability associated with altered occludin (OCLN) expression in gestational diabetes mellitus [49] (Tables 1 and 2).

To further detail these findings, Villota et al. [49] investigated placentas from normoglycemic controls (38 weeks) and pregnancies complicated by gestational diabetes mellitus managed either by dietary intervention alone (d-GDM; 39 weeks) or by metformin treatment (m-GDM; 39 weeks). Among the five miRNAs initially screened, miR-181a-5p was the only miRNA significantly upregulated in chorionic villi from d-GDM placentas compared to controls. In parallel, both OCLN (variant 2) mRNA and occludin protein (isoform A) were significantly downregulated in chorionic villi (Figure 1). To further elucidate the functional consequences of these molecular alterations, in vitro assays using umbilical vein endothelial cells of human normal pregnancies (HUVECs) demonstrated that the experimental overexpression of miR-181a-5p resulted in a marked reduction in the expression of OCLN (variants 1 and 2). Consistent with these changes, miR-181a-5p overexpression led to impaired endothelial barrier integrity, as evidenced by a progressive decrease in transendothelial electrical resistance accompanied by a concomitant increase in endothelial permeability. Collectively, these findings indicate that elevated miR-181a-5p expression in diet-controlled GDM placentas contributes to endothelial barrier dysfunction through the suppression of OCLN expression. This dysregulation ultimately compromises tight junction organization and increases vascular permeability. Therefore, lifestyle interventions alone, including dietary modification and exercise, may be insufficient to prevent occludin-related endothelial alterations in pregnancies complicated by gestational diabetes mellitus.

Rh-negative haemolytic disease of the foetus and newborn

Another placental pathology is Rh-HDFN, an immune condition in which maternal antibodies against foetal Rh antigens cross the placenta and destroy foetal red blood cells, potentially leading to anaemia and other serious neonatal complications [78]. Within this disrupted microenvironment, pleiotropic miRNAs emerge as key molecular mediators of tissue damage. Specifically, miR-181a-5p has been linked to placental dysfunction through alterations in the permeability and integrity of the placental barrier, associated with pathways involved in development, cytoplasmic organization, intracellular signalling, and angiogenesis in Rh-HDFN [50] (Table 1).

Further demonstrating the impact of miRNA dysregulation on the placental barrier, another investigation analysed placental samples from pregnancies resulting in Rh-HDFN newborns (39 weeks) and from pregnancies with healthy newborns (38 weeks) [50]. The authors reported a highly significant increase in miR-181a-5p and miR-125b-2-3p expression levels in placentas from the Rh-HDFN group, which strongly correlated with disease severity, indicating their modulatory roles. A total of 109 genes were identified as predicted targets that inversely correlated with miRNA expression. Of these, 46 were related only to miR-181a-5p, 32 were related only to miR-125b-2-3p, and 31 were common to both (Figure 1). Functional analysis revealed the association of these miRNAs with transcriptional regulation, cytoplasmic microtubule organization, epithelial fold morphogenesis, phosphatidylinositol-mediated signalling, and angiogenesis, all of which are closely related to placental function and the maintenance of the placental barrier. These findings suggest that placental miRNAs may represent novel biomarkers for monitoring the development of Rh-HDFN.

Conclusions

The current literature highlights miR-181a-5p as a critical molecular determinant of placental health and disease. During normal gestation, its tight spatiotemporal regulation, specifically its suppression during early pregnancy, is essential for proper placental development. However, its aberrant expression, predominantly its upregulation, emerges as a recurrent hallmark across multiple placental pathologies. The mechanistic evidence compiled in this review underscores the pleiotropic nature of miR-181a-5p.

Limitations and perspectives

It is important to highlight certain methodological limitations within the analysed literature. First, there are relatively few studies currently available on this topic, and the majority of these are constrained by small sample sizes, which may affect the generalizability of their findings. Furthermore, most of the included articles did not specify the exact placental region from which the samples were collected. Given the highly heterogeneous nature of the placenta, this lack of spatial specification limits a more precise understanding of the localized dynamics of miR-181a-5p.

Ultimately, decoding the complex regulatory networks of miR-181a-5p not only deepens our understanding of placental pathophysiology but also unveils its promising clinical potential. With continued research, miR-181a-5p and its downstream targets may serve as valuable non-invasive biomarkers for the early prediction of adverse pregnancy outcomes, as well as novel therapeutic targets to mitigate placenta-mediated complications.

Immunological role and prognostic value of ITGA3 and ITGA5 in oral squamous cell carcinoma
Authors: Lujuan Wang, Xuemeng Xu, Deyu Zeng, Qiu Peng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractOral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) is the most common type of oral cancer, originating from the squamous cells of the oral mucosa. The global occurrence is significant and is closely linked to smoking, alcohol use, and HPV infection, with a generally poor prognosis, highlighting the importance of early detection and treatment. Integrins are heterodimeric transmembrane receptors composed of α and β subunits, forming a recognized family of about 24 subtypes. ITGA3 and ITGA5, integrin family members, are crucial for cytoskeletal organization, cell migration, proliferation, and survival. ITGA3 acts as a cell membrane adhesion molecule, engaging with laminin family extracellular matrix proteins linked to tumors. ITGA5 and β1 combine to form a receptor for extracellular fibronectin, playing a role in malignant tumor cells and vasculature. Our study identified that ITGA3 and ITGA5 were overexpressed in oral cancer tissues, correlating with a significantly poorer prognosis for patients exhibiting high levels of these integrins. Further, we found that ITGA3 and ITGA5 were associated with the infiltration of a variety of immune cells as well as with the expression of a variety of immune checkpoints and ferroptosis-related genes. Our findings indicate that ITGA3 and ITGA5 expression levels are associated with the sensitivity to commonly used oral cancer treatments. These findings suggest that ITGA3 and ITGA5 could be potential targets for the prognosis, diagnosis, and treatment of OSCC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-16026-0.Keywords: Oral squamous cell carcinoma, ITGA3, ITGA5, HPV, ImmunotherapySubject terms: Cancer, Cell biology

Introduction

Oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) constitutes over 90% of oral cancers, making it a prevalent malignant tumor in the head and neck region1–3. Originating from the squamous cells of the oral mucosa, it is frequently located in the buccal mucosa, floor of the mouth, anterior two-thirds of the tongue, and alveolar ridge. The development of OSCC is associated with a number of factors, including excessive alcohol consumption, tobacco use, betel quid chewing, and human papillomavirus (HPV) infection4,5. These factors stimulate the oral mucosa over a long period of time, leading to abnormal cell proliferation and carcinogenesis. In terms of diagnosis, OSCC has a variety of clinical manifestations, including leukoplakia, erythema, indurated ulcers, and mycotic masses1,6,7. Confirmation of the diagnosis relies on biopsy and pathology, in which histological grading correlates poorly with clinical regression, whereas prognostically relevant indications of histological risk include free infiltration, perineural and vascular-lymphovascular infiltration, bone invasion, and tumor depth8. In addition, detection of circulating tumor cells (CTCs) in the peripheral blood has significant clinical value in early screening, adjunctive diagnosis, assessment of metastasis, and determination of malignancy, progression, and pathological grading of OSCC9,10. Treatment of OSCC is usually by surgical resection, often supplemented by cervical lymph node dissection and radiotherapy. Key prognostic factors include tumor size, lymph node involvement, and distant metastasis. In recent years, some new treatments for OSCC have shown to be safer and more effective than conventional chemotherapy11–13. Despite advancements in OSCC treatments enhancing patient prognosis, the 5-year survival rate remains under 50%, highlighting the ongoing research focus on discovering more effective treatments and prognostic assessment methods.

Integrins, heterodimeric transmembrane receptors made up of α and β subunits, include ITGA3 and ITGA5, which play significant roles in tumorigenesis and cancer progression14. ITGA3, or the α3 subunit, acts as a surface adhesion molecule interacting with extracellular matrix proteins, especially laminins, which are frequently linked to tumors14. It has been demonstrated to facilitate extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling and M2 polarization, affecting ECM stiffness and influencing tumor cell growth15,16. ITGA3 is associated with immune cell infiltration and acts as a positive prognostic biomarker for cancers such as breast cancer17. Elevated ITGA3 expression is linked to the recruitment and regulation of tumor-infiltrating leukocytes (TILs), which may affect patient outcomes. ITGA5, along with β1, constitutes a receptor for extracellular fibronectin, playing a role in malignant tumor cells and vasculature14. It has been identified as an oncogene in multiple cancers18–20such as hepatocellular carcinoma and ovarian cancer. ITGA5 expression is linked to poor prognosis in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, indicating its involvement in tumor progression and metastasis. ITGA5 could serve as an independent prognostic marker in HNSC, underscoring its potential as a therapeutic target.

In this study, we used bioinformatics analysis to identify 272 genes that are aberrantly expressed in OSCC, which are mainly enriched in signaling pathways such as HPV infection as well as ECM. ITGA3 and ITGA5 are crucial genes in the HPV signaling pathway, and elevated expression levels of these genes in OSCC patients are associated with significantly poorer overall survival (OS), disease-free survival (DFS), progression-free survival (PFS), and disease-specific survival (DSS).Our findings indicate a strong correlation between ITGA3 and ITGA5 expression and the infiltration of immune cells, including CD8 + T cells, as well as the expression of immune checkpoints like CD274.The expression levels of ITGA3 and ITGA5 were associated with several ferroptosis-related genes. Importantly, patients with differential expression of ITGA3 and ITGA5 have inconsistent sensitivity to clinically common drugs used to treat OSCC. These findings suggest that ITGA3 and ITGA5 could be potential targets for the prognosis, diagnosis, and treatment of OSCC.

Results

Differentially expressed genes and their enriched signaling pathways in OSCC

To identify genes abnormally expressed during the malignant progression of oral cancer, we analyzed datasets GSE25099, GSE23558, and GSE37991 from the GEO database. Our analysis revealed 165 up-regulated and 107 down-regulated genes consistently present in oral tissues compared to normal tissues across all three datasets (Fig. 1A). KEGG analysis21,22 revealed that the differentially expressed genes were significantly enriched in the HPV infection signaling pathway (Fig. 1B), which is closely linked to oral cancer development16,23–25. We also found that these differential genes were associated with viral defense by Gene Ontology (GO) analysis (Suppl Fig. 1 A). Protein-Protein Interaction (PPI) network analysis revealed that the differential genes predominantly clustered within the chemokine and integrin receptor families (Suppl Fig. 1B). Interestingly, when we analyzed the genes related to the signaling pathway of HPV infection, we found that the integrin family (ITGA, ITGB) is an important member of it. Moreover, we found that ITGA3 and ITGA5 are differentially expressed genes in oral cancer (Suppl Fig. 1 C).

Differentially expressed genes in OSCC. (A) Differentially expressed genes common to three oral cancer datasets GSE25099, GSE23558, and GSE37991 in the GEO database. (B) KEGG analysis of the differentially expressed gene-enriched signaling pathways.

Abnormally high expression of ITGA3 and ITGA5 in OSCC

To further confirm the expression levels of ITGA3 and ITGA5 in oral cancer tissues, we found that ITGA3 and ITGA5 are indeed significantly highly expressed in oral cancer tissues using three datasets, GSE25099, GSE23558, and GSE37991 (Fig. 2A and B). We also found that ITGA3 and ITGA5 were highly expressed in tumor tissues compared to paracancerous tissues by immunohistochemical experiments using oral cancer and its paracancerous normal samples collected from 96 patients (Fig. 2C and D). Expression levels of ITGA3 and ITGA5 were significantly elevated in oral cancer cells (CAL27, SCC9, SCC15, SCC25, and SAS) compared to normal human oral epithelial keratinocytes (HOK), as shown by Western Blot analysis (Fig. 2E). These results suggest that ITGA3 and ITGA5 are abnormally expressed in oral cancer and may be associated with the malignant progression of oral cancer.

ITGA3 and ITGA5 are highly expressed in OSCC. (A-B) Detection of ITGA3 and ITGA5 expression in datasets GSE25099, GSE23558, and GSE3791. (C-D) Expression of ITGA3 and ITGA5 in oral cancer tissues and paracancerous tissues detected by IHC assay. (E) Expression of ITGA3 and ITGA5 in oral normal cells and oral cancer cells detected by western blot assay.

Expression levels of ITGA3 and ITGA5 in different cellular subpopulations in OSCC

Tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes are closely linked to tumor progression, immune response, and prognosis26–28. We analyzed single-cell sequencing data from six oral cancer patients in the GSE172577 dataset29 to examine ITGA3 and ITGA5 expression across cellular subpopulations. ITGA3 showed high expression predominantly in keratinocytes, endothelial, epithelial, and malignant cells, while ITGA5 was primarily expressed in endothelial, mono/macro, and epithelial cells. In immune cells, ITGA3 was highly expressed mainly in Treg, Mast and CD8T cells, and ITGA5 was highly expressed mainly in Treg, NK and CD8T cells (Fig. 3A-F). The findings preliminarily indicate a potential link between ITGA3 and ITGA5 expression and immunotherapy in oral cancer.

(B, E) Distribution of ITGA3 and ITGA5 expression in different cells tsne plot, different colors represent expression abundance. (C, F) Histogram of ITGA3 and ITGA5 expression abundance in different cells.

ITGA3 and ITGA5 are associated with prognosis in patients with OSCC

Using univariate Cox regression analysis on TCGA data, we found that ITGA3 and ITGA5 significantly impact the prognosis of oral cancer patients, while age, gender, smoking, and radiation therapy do not. The p-values, hazard ratios (HR), and confidence intervals (CI) for univariate cox analysis of ITGA3 expression were 0.049, 1.12(1,1.26), respectively, whereas the p-values, hazard ratios (HR), and confidence intervals (CI) for univariate cox analysis of ITGA5 expression were 4.33*10−4, 1.21(1.09,1.34), respectively. The nomogram predicted the 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year overall survival of tumor patients Fig. 4A-B). Our analysis revealed that oral cancer patients with elevated ITGA3 and ITGA5 expression exhibited significantly poorer overall survival (OS), progression-free survival (PFS), disease-specific survival (DSS), and disease-free survival (DFS) compared to those with lower expression levels (Fig. 4C-D, Suppl Fig. 2A-B).

Differentially expressed genes regulated by ITGA3 and ITGA5

To explore the mechanisms by which ITGA3 and ITGA5 influence oral cancer progression, we examined differentially expressed genes in tumor samples with varying ITGA3 and ITGA5 expression levels using TCGA data. Our analysis revealed that ITGA3 regulates 296 differentially expressed genes, with 100 up-regulated and 196 down-regulated (Fig. 5A, Suppl Fig. 3 A). And ITGA5 can regulate 376 differentially expressed genes, including 71 up-regulated genes and 305 down-regulated genes (Fig. 5C, Suppl Fig. 3B). Our analysis of the functional enrichment of differentially expressed genes regulated by ITGA3 and ITGA5 revealed that both sets of genes were primarily enriched in signaling pathways like ECM-receptor interaction and focal adhesion (Fig. 5B and D), which are well-documented to be closely associated with tumor progression30,31.

Differentially expressed genes regulated by ITGA3 and ITGA5. (A, C) The volcano diagram shows the differentially expressed genes in the ITGA3 and ITGA5 high and low expression samples. Red dots represent significantly differentially up-regulated genes, blue dots represent significantly differentially down-regulated genes, and gray dots represent non-significant genes. (B, D) Functional enrichment analysis including KEGG pathway enrichment results and GO term enrichment results for differentially expressed genes.

Correlation of ITGA3 and ITGA5 expression with immune checkpoint expression and immune cell infiltration

Recent studies indicate that immunotherapy holds promise for treating oral cancer, particularly as neoadjuvant therapy and when combined with chemotherapy and radiotherapy23,32. We examined the association between ITGA3 and ITGA5 expression and immune cell infiltration in oral cancer using TCGA data. ITGA3 expression correlated with T cell CD8+, NK cell, T cell CD4+, and B cell infiltration, while ITGA5 expression showed significant correlation with T cell CD8+, macrophage, T cell CD4+, and B cell infiltration (Fig. 6A-B, Suppl Fig. 4A-B). Our analysis revealed significant correlations between ITGA3 expression and the immune checkpoints CD274, IGSF8, ITPRIPL1, and PDCD1LG2. Similarly, ITGA5 expression showed significant correlations with CD274, HAVCR2, IGSF8, PDCD1LG2, and SIGLEC15 (Fig. 6C, Suppl Fig. 4 C). Using the Tumor Immune Dysfunction and Exclusion (TIDE) algorithm, we found a significant correlation between ITGA3 expression and immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) response, with ITGA5 showing a similar association (Fig. 6D, Suppl Fig. 4D).

ITGA3 and ITGA5 is associated with ferroptosis

Ferroptosis is a form of programmed cell death reliant on iron, marked by lipid peroxidation. Ferroptosis plays a crucial role in tumor progression, and its induction may offer novel therapeutic strategies for cancer patients33–35. To analyze whether ITGA3 and ITGA5 are associated with ferroptosis, we analyzed the correlation of ITGA3 and ITGA5 with the expression of common ferroptosis-related genes using TCGA data, and found that ITGA3 was correlated with CDKN1A, FDFT1, GPX4, HSPA5, HSPB1, NFE2L2, SAT1, ACSL4, ALOX15, ATP5MC3, CARS1, DPP4, GLS2, and TFRC, whereas ITGA5 expression was significantly correlated with CDKN1A, FDFT1, HSPA5, HSPB1, NFE2L2, SAT1, ACSL4, ALOX15, ATL1, ATP5MC3, CARS1, CS, DPP4, GLS2, LPCAT3 and TFRC (Fig. 7A-B).

Correlation between ITGA3 and ITGA5 expression and ferroptosis.

Expression of ITGA3 and ITGA5 is correlated with drug sensitivity

Tumor resistance, characterized by reduced or lost sensitivity of tumor cells to chemotherapy, is a primary cause of treatment failure36–38. We used the Genomics of Drug Sensitivity in Cancer (GDSC) database to assess how ITGA3 and ITGA5 expression levels influence sensitivity to oral cancer drugs, specifically paclitaxel, 5-fluorouracil, gemcitabine, and cisplatin. The expression of ITGA3 was significantly correlated with the sensitivities to paclitaxel, gemcitabine, and cisplatin, while ITGA5 expression showed significant correlation with the sensitivities to paclitaxel, 5-fluorouracil, and cisplatin (Fig. 8A-D).

Discussion

The global incidence of oral cancer is increasing significantly. Advancements in treating locally advanced oral cancer remain limited, with a 5-year survival rate below 50%23,39. Oral cancer is influenced by multiple factors, such as smoking, alcohol consumption, and betel quid chewing. Investigating the determinants of oral cancer development and implementing early prevention strategies are crucial for lowering its incidence and promoting oral health40–42. In recent years, basic research on oral and maxillofacial tumors has shown abundant innovative results. Researchers have extensively investigated the molecular mechanisms of malignant tumors and the influence of various factors in the tumor microenvironment on tumor progression, providing a theoretical foundation for developing new targeted anti-tumor therapies43,44. Chemotherapy is one of the commonly used clinical treatments for OSCC, and antitumor drugs are often difficult to achieve satisfactory results in the treatment of OSCC due to the acquisition of tumor resistance. Numerous studies indicate that the abnormal activation of signaling pathways, such as Wnt, PI3K/AKT, NF-κB, and STAT, plays a crucial role in mediating drug resistance in oral cancer45–48. Despite advancements in treatment and prognosis through diverse therapeutic approaches, further identification of potential targets is necessary to enhance the five-year survival rate of oral cancer patients.

ITGA3 and ITGA5, integrin family proteins, are crucial in tumorigenesis and development49. ITGA3, a transmembrane protein, serves as a primary receptor for the extracellular matrix (ECM) and plays a crucial role in regulating tumor cell proliferation, apoptosis, invasion, and migration50–52. ITGA3 is expressed in a wide range of tumors and is able to regulate cellular signaling, which has a ITGA3 is expressed in a variety of tumors and can regulate cell signaling, which has a profound effect on tumor development. Elevated ITGA3 expression is linked to neutrophil and lymphocyte infiltration, enhances TH17 cell differentiation and expansion, and facilitates Treg infiltration, which suppresses immune responses and supports tumor progression17,53. In colorectal cancer (CRC), increased ITGA3 expression is strongly linked to Tregs marker FOXP3 and T-cell depletion markers PD-L1, CTLA-4, and PD-1, which are related to immune checkpoint therapy (ICT). ITGA3 may be crucial in recruiting and regulating tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs)54. ITGA3 regulates immune cell infiltration by promoting ECM remodeling and modulating epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) processes55. Elevated ITGA5 expression is observed in various tumors and is associated with overall patient survival, highlighting its potential as a therapeutic target and biomarker18,56,57. ITGA5 facilitates gastric cancer cell growth, migration, invasion, and strengthens cell-matrix protein adhesion. ITGA5 facilitates the FAK/Src/Rac1 signaling pathway, promoting gastric cancer progression. Integrin α5β1, formed by the binding of ITGA5 to ITGB1, functions as a receptor facilitating cell differentiation, development, and migration. Elevated ITGA5 expression is associated with tumor metastasis and potentially influences gastric cancer cells through the activation of the FAK/Src/Rac1 pathway58.

The application and mechanism of immunotherapy in tumors is the current hot research direction in the field of tumor therapy. Immunotherapy has demonstrated significant efficacy in treating various cancers, including kidney cancer, specific melanomas, and non-small cell lung cancer59–62. However, not all types of cancers can benefit from this type of treatment, for example, “cold” tumors such as prostate cancer are less responsive to immunotherapy63. Despite significant progress in the use of immunotherapy in tumor treatment, limited response rates and inevitable immune-associated adverse events (irAEs) remain major challenges that need to be addressed in future research. This study indicates that ITGA3 and ITGA5 are linked to immune cell infiltration and immune checkpoint expression, highlighting their potential as targets in oral cancer immunotherapy.

Ferroptosis is associated with cancer treatment resistance, and induction of ferroptosis has been shown to reverse drug resistance. In conventional therapeutic modalities, tumor cells may become resistant to apoptosis-inducing treatments, and ferroptosis, as an alternative cell death pathway, offers new opportunities to overcome this resistance64,65. Immune cells in the tumor microenvironment (TME) can also determine whether ferroptosis occurs in tumors. CD8 + cytotoxic T cells in the tumor microenvironment promote lipid peroxidation and trigger ferroptosis in tumors through IFNγ secretion66,67. In addition, ferroptosis in cancer cells can promote anti-tumor immune responses and activate anti-tumor immunity by releasing immunostimulatory signals68,69. There is also evidence that ferroptosis has a pro-tumorigenic role in tumor immunity. Gpx4-deficient T cells quickly gather lipid peroxides on their membranes when activated, leading to ferroptosis, potentially hindering antitumor immunity and facilitating tumor growth70. Our findings indicate a strong association between ITGA3, ITGA5, and various ferroptosis-related genes, necessitating further investigation to determine their potential role in tumor progression through ferroptosis regulation.

Materials and methods

Cell culture

Cells were sourced from the Chinese Academy of Sciences cell banks in Shanghai, China. HOK, CAL27, and SCC15 cells were maintained in high-glucose DMEM with 10% FBS and 1% antibiotic-antimycotic solution, whereas SCC9, SCC25, and SAS cells were cultured in DMEM/F12 medium with the same supplements, all at 37 °C and 5% CO2.

Western blotting

Details of Western blotting were previously described71. Briefly, HOK and various oral cancer cell lines were lysed by RIPA lysate, and 50 µg of protein lysate was collected for SDS-PAGE and subsequently detected using antibodies to ITGA3, ITGA5, and the internal reference protein GAPDH (Cell Signaling Technology, USA).

Data collection

Expression data for ITGA3 and ITGA5 in oral cancer and normal samples were primarily sourced from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and three GEO database datasets: GSE25099, GSE23558, and GSE37991. GSE25099 dataset was analyzed genome-wide for transcription using the Affymetrix GeneChip Human Gene 1.0ST array. Included in this dataset are expression data obtained from 57 OSCC patient specimens and 22 oral tissues from normal individuals. The GSE23558 dataset is including RNA profiles of 27 OSCC patients compared to 5 normal oral tissues. The GSE37991 dataset is a gene expression profiling that includes 40 OSCC patients with 40 cases of adjacent normal oral tissues.

Single-cell sequencing data analysis

The OSCC dataset (GSE172577) in.h5 format and its annotations were obtained from TISCH. The single-cell data underwent processing and analysis with R software tools MAESTRO and Seurat, followed by cell clustering and subclustering using the t-SNE method72. We based the t-SNE plot on the expression distribution of ITGA3 and ITGA5 in different cells, where different colors represent the expression abundance, with darker colors representing low expression of the gene in the cell and brighter colors representing high expression of the gene in the cell.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

Paraffin-embedded tumor tissues and their corresponding paraneoplastic normal tissues from 96 patients with oral cancer were stained and detected using antibodies against ITGA3 and ITGA5 after deparaffinized and rehydrated steps. The monoclonal ITGA3 antibody (66070-1-Ig) derived from mouse and the recombinant ITGA5 antibody (84468-4-RR) derived from rabbits were purchased from proteintech company and used at a dilution ratio of 1:200. After the antibody binds, a secondary antibody corresponding to the source of the primary antibody and labeled with horseradish peroxidase is added and incubated for 1 h at room temperature. The slices are washed to remove unbound secondary antibody. Add DAB substrate solution for color development reaction, as needed, and finally follow the color development reaction time until the color is as expected. Hematoxylin-eosin staining was performed for better observation of the tissue structure. Observe the staining results with a microscope and record the distribution and expression intensity of antigen-positive cells.

Analyzing the relationship between ITGA3 and ITGA5 and prognosis of OSCC patients

We downloaded the STAR-counts data and corresponding clinical information of HNSC tumors from the TCGA database to analyze the prognosis of HNSC patients based on the expression values of ITGA3 and ITGA573. Briefly, we used the log rank test to compare the difference in survival between patients with differential expression of ITGA3 and ITGA5 in the KM survival analysis. For the Kaplan-Meier curves, we used log rank test and univariate Cox regression to derive the p-values and hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). We used R software with version v4.0.3 for statistical analysis. Results were considered statistically significant when the p-value was less than 0.05.

Correlation analysis of ITGA3 and ITGA5 with immune response

Using the STAR-counts data of HNSC tumors from the TCGA database and the corresponding clinical information, we analyzed the lymphocyte infiltration, immune checkpoint expression, and immune response of HNSC tissues with differential expression of ITGA3 and ITGA5 by the immunedeconv software package and Tumor Immune Dysfunction and Exclusion algorithm(TIDE)74,75. We analyzed RNAseq data of IGLEC15, TIGIT, CD274, HAVCR2, PDCD1, CTLA4, LAG3, and PDCD1LG2, which are key genes associated with immune regulation, from ITGA3 and ITGA5 high- and low-expression samples, and by analyzing the expression levels of these genes, we can deeply understand the relationship between ITGA3 and ITGA5 and immune checkpoints.

Statistical analysis

Statistical significance was calculated using Prism Software (GraphPad 8.0, https://www.graphpad.com). Statistical analyses for bioinformatics analysis were performed using R software (v4.0.3). Comparative analyses between groups were performed using the Wilcoxon test. The diagnostic effect of the characterized genes was assessed by ROC curves and area under the curve calculations. Pearson’s correlation test was performed for interactions between variables. Results were considered statistically significant when the p-value was less than 0.05.

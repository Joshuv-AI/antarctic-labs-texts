Dataset of dielectric spectra and physicochemical parameters of Creole chicken breast meat
Authors: Wilson Castro, Pedro Gamboa, Lusmenia Ruiz-Mejía, Thony Arce, Himer Avila-George, Tony Chuquizuta
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis data article presents a dataset of physicochemical and dielectric measurements from Creole chicken (Pectoralis major) breast meat. Ninety-six samples from male chickens (42—45 days of growth) were collected and evaluated at two postmortem stages (8 and 24 h) under refrigerated conditions (4 °C). Physicochemical parameters include pH, color values (CIE L*a*b*), titratable acidity, and moisture content, all obtained using standardized analytical methods. Dielectric spectra were acquired with a parallel-plate sensor connected to an impedance analyzer, covering the frequency range from 40 Hz to 1 MHz. The dataset also provides categorization of samples according to widely recognized meat quality types: pale–soft exudative, red–firm non–exudative, and dark–firm dry. The resource enables further use in food quality research, particularly in the development of predictive models and non-invasive classification approaches and contributes to advancing reproducibility and open access to high-quality reference data in poultry science.Keywords: Dielectric spectroscopy, Non-destructive food analysis, Electrical permittivity, Food quality evaluation, Poultry meat processing, Spectral data analysis, Automatic meat classification, Physicochemical properties

Value of the Data

This dataset integrates broadband dielectric spectra (40 Hz–1 MHz) with key physicochemical attributes of Creole chicken breast meat at early postmortem stages. It enables researchers to: (i) explore relationships between permittivity-based profiles and conventional meat quality metrics, (ii) develop and benchmark classification or regression models for non-destructive assessment, and (iii) compare native poultry against commercial breeds in future work. The explicit organization by postmortem stage and pH-based quality class supports reproducible statistical designs and fosters method development in food science and industrial quality control, without reporting inferential outcomes within this Data in Brief.

Background

Meat quality has traditionally been evaluated through physicochemical parameters such as pH, moisture, color, and acidity, which are directly associated with consumer acceptance and technological performance in processing. In parallel, dielectric spectroscopy has gained recognition as a rapid and non-destructive technique for assessing muscle tissue properties, since its response to electromagnetic fields reflects structural and compositional characteristics.

Previous applications of dielectric methods have focused mainly on pork and broiler chicken meat, where spectral data have been used to describe postmortem changes and to establish quality-related indicators. However, comparable data for native poultry breeds are scarce. Creole chicken, in particular, is of cultural and economic relevance in several regions of Latin America, yet systematic information on its dielectric behavior and associated quality traits is limited.

The dataset compiled in this study addresses this gap by integrating dielectric spectra with standardized physicochemical measurements of Creole chicken breast meat at two postmortem stages (8 h and 24 h). It was generated to provide a structured resource for researchers in food science and related fields, enabling further exploration of quality attributes and methodological development in poultry meat analysis.

Data Description

The dataset contains dielectric and physicochemical measurements from ninety-six samples of Creole chicken breast meat (Pectoralis major), collected at two postmortem stages: 8 h and 24 h. Each sample includes spectral measurements and conventional quality attributes. The dielectric data are divided into two components: the equivalent parallel capacitance (Cp) and the dissipation factor (D), both measured at 401 discrete frequencies between 40 Hz and 1 MHz. Together with the measured thickness of each specimen, these values can be used to calculate the real permittivity (ε′) and the dielectric loss factor (ε″).

The physicochemical data include meat type classification (pale–soft exudative, red–firm non–exudative, dark–firm dry), pH at 8 h and 24 h, titratable acidity (%), moisture content (dry basis,%), and color parameters in the CIE L*a*b* space. For color, L*, a*, b*, chroma (C), and hue angle (H) were measured at 8 h, and L* was measured again at 24 h.

Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

The experimental workflow for generating the dataset is illustrated in Fig. 1. The steps are detailed below.

Experimental design

The dataset was structured to allow downstream statistical and machine-learning analyses. Ninety-six skinless, boneless male Creole chicken breasts (42–45 days) were sampled and measured under refrigeration. Each sample was evaluated at two postmortem stages (8 h and 24 h).

The primary experimental factor is postmortem stage with two levels (8 h and 24 h postmortem). In addition, a categorical organization by meat quality class is provided using pH at 24 h as the decision rule: pale–soft exudative (PSE), red–firm non-exudative (RFN), and dark–firm dry (DFD). This classification is included to facilitate grouped analyses when appropriate.

For each sample, dielectric measurements include equivalent Cp and D at 401 discrete frequencies from 40 Hz to 1 MHz, with five technical replicates, and sample thickness for permittivity calculations. Using Cp, D, and thickness, the dataset provides the real part of permittivity (ε′) and the loss factor (ε″) across all frequencies. Physicochemical variables comprise pH (8 h and 24 h postmortem), moisture content (gravimetric method), titratable acidity, and color in CIE L*a*b* at 8 h postmortem with derived chroma and hue; L* is also recorded at 24 h postmortem. These variables permit common statistical designs (e.g., one- or two-way analyses by postmortem stage and/or quality class, multivariate analyses of spectra vs. physicochemical traits, and cross-validation schemes for predictive modeling) to be conducted by data users outside the scope of this Data in Brief.

Chicken breasts

Skinless and boneless male chicken breasts (Pectoralis major) from ninety-six Creole chickens aged 42–45 days were obtained from the processing facility of Multiservice Company DIR EIRL (Chota, Cajamarca, Peru). The slaughter process included bleeding, feather removal, evisceration, and chilling at 4 °C for six hours, followed by meat cutting.

Conservation

Samples were transported to the Emerging Technologies Laboratory at the Productive Improvement Research Institute, Universidad Nacional Autónoma de Chota, in insulated expanded polystyrene containers (30 × 30 × 40 cm³) with frozen gel packs to maintain 2–4 °C. Upon arrival, the breasts were stored at 4 °C for up to eight hours. Four portions from each breast were allocated for physicochemical analysis scheduled for that day.

Dielectric profile acquisition

Fig. 2 shows the for dielectric profile acquisition and signal preprocessing. The methodology is detailed below. Slices 4 ± 1 mm thick were cut with a meat slicer (V-220T, Ventus, China), and cylindrical specimens of 5 cm diameter were prepared with a stainless-steel punch. Equivalent Cp and D were measured using a parallel-plate sensor (Keysight 16451B, Germany) connected to an impedance analyzer (Keysight E4990A, USA). Measurements were taken at 401 discrete frequencies from 40 Hz to 1 MHz, with five replicates per sample. The system was powered on for 30 min prior to use and calibrated in open-circuit and short-circuit modes. Cp values are stored in Sheet 1 of the dataset and D values in Sheet 2.

Sample thickness

The thickness (ta) of each specimen (mm) was measured and recorded in Sheet 3 of the dataset.

Physicochemical characterization

Measurements were carried out according to standardized methods. pH was determined using AOAC 981.12:2005, acidity with AOAC 900.02:2005, and moisture by gravimetric drying. Color was evaluated with a colorimeter (3Nh NR200, China) in the CIE L*a*b* space. Parameters included L*, a*, b*, chroma (C), and hue angle (H). Table 1 summarizes the variables measured at 8 h and 24 h postmortem.

Meat quality classification

Meat quality categories were defined according to pH at 24 h postmortem: pale–soft exudative (PSE, pH < 5.70), red–firm non–exudative (RFN, 5.70 < pH < 6.10), and dark–firm dry (DFD, pH ≥ 6.10). The classification is presented in Table 2.

Utility of the database

This database [7] provides dielectric profiles coupled with key physicochemical parameters for the classification of Creole chicken meat into PSE, RFN, and DFD categories. Likewise, it serves as a valuable resource for the development and implementation of models capable of discriminating meat quality using dielectric profiles in combination with machine learning techniques. Previous studies using similar types of data have demonstrated the potential of dielectric spectroscopy for meat classification in other animal species [4,8,9]. Consequently, the dataset presented here can be leveraged to design and validate predictive models, optimize feature selection strategies, and evaluate non-invasive quality assessment tools for poultry processing.

Limitations

The dataset has the following limitations:

•Dielectric properties were measured only within the frequency range of 40 Hz to 1 MHz. As a result, the dataset cannot provide information about tissue behavior outside this portion of the electromagnetic spectrum.

Dielectric properties were measured only within the frequency range of 40 Hz to 1 MHz. As a result, the dataset cannot provide information about tissue behavior outside this portion of the electromagnetic spectrum.

•Meat classification was based exclusively on pH and CIE L* values measured at 24 h postmortem. Other quality indicators, such as shear force, water-holding capacity, lipid oxidation, or protein solubility, were not included in the dataset.

Meat classification was based exclusively on pH and CIE L* values measured at 24 h postmortem. Other quality indicators, such as shear force, water-holding capacity, lipid oxidation, or protein solubility, were not included in the dataset.

•The size of the dataset is limited to ninety-six samples, all corresponding to male Creole chickens between 42 and 45 days of age. This restricts the possibility of analyzing variability across sex, age groups, or production systems.

•The dataset is provided in .xlsx format, organized into four sheets (Cp, D, Thickness, and Physiochemical). Access and processing require appropriate software, and users must calculate dielectric properties (ε′ and ε″) from Cp, D, and thickness values if needed.

Ethics Statement

The authors have read and adhered to the ethical requirements for publication in Data in Brief. The present work does not involve human subjects, experimental use of live animals, or data collected from social media platforms.

The chicken breast samples analyzed in this study were obtained from a commercial poultry processing facility (Multiservice Company DIR EIRL, Chota, Cajamarca, Peru), following standard industrial slaughtering practices. No live animals were handled, treated, or sacrificed for the specific purposes of this research. The data were generated exclusively from postmortem meat samples provided by the facility.

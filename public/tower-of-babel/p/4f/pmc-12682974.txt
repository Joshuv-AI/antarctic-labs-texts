Deep root dissection during valve-sparing aortic root replacement
Authors: Christopher R Burke, Scott DeRoo
Published: 2025

Extent of deep root dissection.

Central Message

Deep aortic root dissection to the level of the virtual basal ring is essential during valve-sparing root replacement to minimize recurrent annular dilation.

The aortic annulus is a complex anatomic structure with several important landmarks that influence aortic root reconstruction. The external ventriculoaortic junction often lies at a level above the aortic annulus in the region of the right coronary cusp. The imaginary line along the plane of the leaflet nadirs is referred to as the virtual basal ring (VBR).1 Failure to achieve complete circumferential aortic annular stabilization at the level of the VBR during valve repair may serve as a potential mode of repair failure.2 Many have abandoned partial annuloplasty techniques (ie, subcommissural annuloplasty) given concerns for high rates of repair failure, especially in the setting of a dilated annulus.3

There has been increasing emphasis of the importance of anchoring the surgical graft at the level of the VBR during valve-sparing root replacement (VSRR).2 This is especially pertinent in the area of the right-left commissure/raphe as well as the right coronary cusp (RCC), where the ventriculoaortic junction and VBR are often at disparate levels. Failure to anchor the surgical graft at the level of the VBR can lead to recurrent annular dilation and recurrent aortic regurgitation. Therefore, a deep external root dissection down to the level of the VBR may help avoid this mechanism of late failure. This allows the entire aortic annular apparatus to be protected by the surgical graft. Traditionally, dissection was carried out through the ligament that connects the right-left commissure to the pulmonary valve and into the fat pad. However, a deep root dissection requires dissection into the muscular plane of the interventricular septum down to the level of the VBR (Figure 1). Leaving a small plane of muscle on the left ventricular outflow tract side is critically important to preventing injury in this area, which jeopardizes the operation. We assess the depth of dissection by using the nadir of the RCC as a reference. The membranous septum serves as the inferior limit of dissection around the right-non commissure and dissection is stopped here to prevent injury into the septum and/or conduction injuries. Right ventricle injuries must be identified to prevent bleeding but are easily managed with suture repair. Finally, dissection into the septum can create small muscle bleeders that should be identified with cardioplegia administration and controlled. Once this dissection is complete, the annular sutures are placed at the level of the VBR and brought through the polyethylene terephthalate graft. This allows the graft to sit perpendicular to the plane of the annulus and prevents rolling in of significant right ventricle septal muscle between the annulus and graft. This helps stabilize the annulus and prevent recurrent dilation.

Case

A 41-year-old man with history of bicuspid aortic valve (BAV) and severe symptomatic aortic regurgitation, preoperative ejection fraction of 50%, and severe left ventricular dilation presented to our hospital. A Sievers 1 BAV with fusion of the right-left commissure was noted along with a 3.9-cm aortic root diameter. The aortic annulus measured 34 mm on preoperative computed tomography scan. The patient desired avoidance of anticoagulation therapy. See Video 1.

A standard median sternotomy was performed and the aortic valve leaflets were assessed for repairability. A deep aortic root dissection to the level of the VBR was performed. The right ventricular outflow tract was dissected sharply off the aortic root in the area of the RCC. A 34-mm straight polyethylene terephthalate graft (Terumo Gelweave) was selected based on sizing of the distance between the VBR and top of the posterior commissure. The base of the polyethylene terephthalate graft was then sutured by placing 8 or 9 pledgeted mattress sutures at the level of the VBR. These sutures are occasionally tied over a dilator to guide final annular size. The commissures were reimplanted in a 180°-180 orientation. We plicated the graft in the interleaflet triangles to help create neosinuses; however, caution should be exercised in certain BAV situations (eg, smaller annulus or immobile leaflets) because this could lead to higher postoperative gradients. Leaflet repair was performed first with raphe shaving and debulking to increase mobility. This was followed by serial plication of the conjoined leaflet to shorten the free margin length and increase the effective height. The remainder of the VSRR was performed in a standard fashion. Postoperative echocardiogram revealed no residual aortic regurgitation and a mean gradient through the aortic valve of 11 mm Hg. He had a routine postoperative course and was discharged home in stable condition on day 6. He did well throughout the postoperative period and 6-month echocardiogram revealed trace aortic regurgitation with a mean gradient of 10 mm Hg. This study received IRB approval (University of Washington #STUDY00014540; approved December 6, 2021). Need for informed consent was waived.

Discussion

VSRR techniques have refined significantly over time, with an emphasis on graft type (straight vs Valsalva), reimplantation orientation of BAV (180°-180° vs 210°-150°), graft sizing, and technical components such as depth and extent of the root dissection. The concept of the deep root dissection has been championed by El Khoury and colleagues2 and results in the ability the anchor the surgical graft at the level of the VBR. However, caution should be noted that this dissection does carry risk of injury to the right ventricle or left ventricular outflow tract, along with potential injury to the membranous septum and/or conduction system.2 Great care is needed to ensure hemostasis in this area because small, bleeding vessels can be encountered within the muscle.

Although excellent results of VSRR have been shown, aortic regurgitation progression and increased reintervention rate (compared with trileaflet aortic valves) has been observed following BAV repair with VSRR.4,5 One potential reason for late failure of VSRR with BAV repair is recurrent annular dilation and decreased leaflet coaptation with recurrent aortic regurgitation. The area of the RCC is particularly vulnerable to this issue given the lack of continuity with the fibrous skeleton of the heart in this region. The issue of recurrent aortic annular dilation is likely more pronounced in patients with BAV as well as patients with aortopathy. Therefore, deep root dissection is particularly important in this patient population. However, data regarding the durability of this technique are currently limited and longer-term study is needed to assess the influence on repair failure.

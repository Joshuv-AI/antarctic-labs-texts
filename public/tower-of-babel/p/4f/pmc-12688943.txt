A new era in pain management: suzetrigine’s role in reducing opioid dependence
Authors: Umaid Khan, Haris Afridi, Raghabendra Kumar Mahato, Hammad Amin Mirza
Published: 2025

Dear Editor,

Opioids have long been the cornerstone of acute pain management; however, their use is marred by significant concerns regarding safety, tolerability, and addiction potential. On 30 January 2025, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration approved suzetrigine, a novel, highly selective inhibitor of voltage-gated sodium channel NaV1.8, which is predominantly expressed in peripheral nociceptive neurons responsible for transmitting pain signals[1]. In line with the TITAN 2025 guideline on transparent and structured clinical reporting, it is essential that such advancements in pain therapeutics are communicated in a clear, balanced manner to highlight both their benefits and areas requiring further evaluation[2]. This approval marks a pivotal moment in pain therapeutics, offering an alternative that circumvents the risks associated with opioids.

Suzetrigine has demonstrated statistically significant and clinically meaningful reductions in moderate-to-severe acute pain across a variety of clinical settings, regardless of whether the pain is surgical or non-surgical in origin[3]. These findings were consistent across multiple randomized controlled trials, establishing the agent’s effectiveness as a non-opioid, non-addictive treatment option. In pivotal Phase III trials, patients undergoing abdominoplasty and bunionectomy experienced significantly reduced pain scores when treated with suzetrigine compared to both hydrocodone/acetaminophen and placebo[4]. An oral loading dose of 100 mg followed by 50 mg every 12 hours was particularly effective. The high degree of NaV1.8 selectivity – over 31 000-fold greater than for other sodium channels – minimizes off-target effects, enhancing the drug’s safety profile[5].

Importantly, suzetrigine’s mechanism of action does not involve opioid receptors, effectively avoiding the pathway responsible for tolerance, dependency, and misuse. As such, suzetrigine represents a paradigm shift in acute pain management – providing robust analgesia without the burden of opioid-related adverse effects. While its short-term efficacy and tolerability are well supported, the long-term safety of suzetrigine warrants further investigation. Nevertheless, its current clinical profile underscores its potential to significantly reduce opioid reliance, enhance patient safety, and improve treatment outcomes.

Suzetrigine offers a promising and much-needed non-opioid alternative for the management of moderate-to-severe acute pain. With strong clinical data and a novel, targeted mechanism, it may play a transformative role in reshaping pain treatment protocols and reducing the public health burden of opioid use.

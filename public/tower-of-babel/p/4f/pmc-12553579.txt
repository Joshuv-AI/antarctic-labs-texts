Potential prognostic biomarker MIER2 in colon adenocarcinoma: from data mining to validation
Authors: Chen Zhao, Xue Li, Sumin Zhu, Lihong Shi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Colon adenocarcinoma (COAD) is highly malignant tumor in the gastrointestinal tract, and reliable biomarkers for predicting its prognosis are remain lacking. MIER Family Member 2 (MIER2) has been implicated in tumor biology, yet its role in COAD remains unclear. MIER2 expression in COAD was analyzed using TCGA and UALCAN databases. Survival analysis, Cox regression, and nomogram construction were performed to evaluate its prognostic value. Functional enrichment analysis was conducted via LinkedOmics. Immune infiltration was assessed using ESTIMATE and CIBERSORT algorithms. Additionally, the effect of MIER2knockdown on the proliferation, and migration of SW480 cell was evaluated. The expression of MIER2 was significantly higher in COAD tissues and associated with a poorer OS, DSS and PFI. Multivariate analysis confirmed MIER2 as an independent prognostic factor. Co-expressed gene analysis revealed enrichment in immune-related pathways, including type I interferon signaling and macrophage activation. In addition, MIER2 expression was associated with altered immune infiltration. MIER2 knockdown suppressed SW480 cell proliferation and migration, and RNA-seq further indicated that this might be related to the intrinsic apoptotic signaling pathway. High expression of MIER2 is associated with poor prognosis and immune cell infiltration, and it serve as a novel prognostic biomarker and potential therapeutic target for COAD.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s10238-025-01876-9.

Introduction

Colon adenocarcinoma (COAD) is one of the most common malignant tumors in the gastrointestinal tract worldwide, posing a significant threat to public health due to its high morbidity and mortality [[1–4]]. Despite advancements in clinical treatments such as surgery and chemotherapy, the 5 year survival rate remains unsatisfactory, primarily due to therapeutic resistance, aggressive progression, and metastasis [[5–7]]. Therefore, a deeper understanding of the molecular mechanisms driving COAD and recognizing novel biomarkers contributed to improving diagnosis, prognosis, and further development of targeted therapies. High-throughput sequencing helps us to systematically explore gene expression profiles and signaling pathways, providing a potential approach to identify therapeutic targets and underlying molecular mechanisms in COAD [[8,9]].

MIER2 (Mesoderm Induction Early Response 2) is a member of the MIER family, characterized by regulating histone deacetylase binding activity and contributing to histone deacetylase activity [[10–12]]. Previous studies have primarily focused on MIER1, which has been implicated in tumor suppression [[13]]. However, the biological function and clinical significance of MIER2 in cancer, particularly in colorectal cancer, remain largely unexplored. Given the functional similarity within the MIER family, it is plausible that MIER2 may also participate in oncogenic or tumor-suppressive pathways.

In this study, we performed a comprehensive analysis to investigate the expression pattern, clinical relevance, and biological function of MIER2 in COAD. We integrated multiple datasets from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA), validated our findings in public platforms and tissue samples, and further examined the impact of MIER2 on patient prognosis. Functional enrichment analysis of co-expressed genes and immune infiltration profiling were also conducted to elucidate the potential mechanisms by which MIER2 contributes to COAD progression. Furthermore, cell experiments and transcriptome sequencing were employed to validate the oncogenic role of MIER2 at the cellular and molecular levels. Our findings suggest that MIER2 may serve as a novel prognostic biomarker and potential therapeutic target in COAD.

Methods

Data acquisition and preprocessing

The TCGA-COAD dataset was downloaded from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/), which includes 51 normal samples and 647 COAD samples. The original FPKM RNA-seq data were further converted into the TPM format (ref: TPM = (FPKM / sum of FPKM over all transcripts) × 10^6) and performed log2 transformation for subsequent analysis. Differential gene analysis was conducted in the COAD dataset with high and low MIER2 expression. The Wilcoxon test and logistic regression were used to analyze the correlation between clinicopathological characteristics and MIER2. Kaplan–Meier (KM) analysis and Cox regression analyses were conducted to evaluate the associations between different MIER2 expression levels and overall survival in COAD patients.

Functional enrichment analysis

To identify genes significantly co-expressed with MIER2 in COAD, the LinkedOmics database (http://www.linkedomics.org) [[14]], an open-access platform for analyzing multi-omics data within the TCGA cohort, was utilized [[15,16]]. The “HiSeqRNA” platform and the “TCGA-COAD” cohort were selected. The Spearman correlation analysis was used to assess the relationship between MIER2 and its co-expressed genes. The “LinkInterpreter” module was then used to perform GO and KEGG enrichment analyses.

Construction and evaluation of clinical nomogram

Based on the multivariate Cox regression analysis, we constructed a prognostic nomogram to predict 1-year, 2-year, and 3-year overall survival (OS) probabilities in COAD patients [[17]]. The nomogram was generated using the “RMS” package in R. The predictive accuracy and discriminative ability of the nomogram were evaluated using the concordance index (C-index) and calibration curves.

Immune infiltration analysis

To investigate the relationship between MIER2 expression and immune cell infiltration in the COAD tumor microenvironment, we applied the ESTIMATE algorithm using the “estimate” package in R to calculate stromal scores, immune scores, and ESTIMATE scores [[18,19]]. Furthermore, immune infiltration levels of various immune cell subsets were analyzed using the CIBERSORT algorithm (the number of permutations was set to 1000 and P < 0.05), and their associations with MIER2 expression levels were assessed by Spearman correlation.

Cell culture and transfection

SW480 cells were obtained from the China Center for Type Culture Collection (CCTCC, China). The cells were cultured in DMEM with 5% fetal bovine serum and 100 U/mL penicillin and streptomycin at 37 °C in a 5% CO2 atmosphere. The cells were seeded in 6-well plates and allowed to reach 50–60% confluence. Si- MIER2 were transfected using Lipofectamine 2000 according to the manufacturer’s instructions. After 48 h, knockdown efficiency was determined by RT-qPCR. The siRNA sequences used were: Si-MIER2-1: CGAGUUGGUGAAAUGCAACUU; Si-MIER2-2: GAGUUUGGACUCAUCGGCAUU.

RNA extraction and quantitative real‐time PCR

Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Then, RNA was converted into cDNA using the HiScript III first-strand cDNA synthesis kit. RT-qPCR was conducted using Hieff UNICON qPCR SYBR Green Master Mix (ABclonal, #RK21203) on a LightCycler 480 quantitative PCR instrument. The relative mRNA abundance of the target genes was expressed as 2−ΔΔCt method. For the determination of the reference gene, the GeNorm method was used [[20]]. The specific primer sequences used were: MIER2-F: GGATCTCGTTTCCTGGCTGAT; MIER2-R: TGAGGTCCCACCATGATCTC. β-actin-F: CATGTACGTTGCTATCCAGGC; β-actin-R: CTCCTTAATGTCACGCACGAT; NOTCH3-F: CGTGGCTTCTTTCTACTGTGC; NOTCH3-R: CGTTCACCGGATTTGTGTCAC; NCOR2-F: CACGAGGTGTCAGAGATCATCG; NCOR2-R: GCCATAAGCCCGTTCATGTTG; NOTCH4-F: CCTGGCTCCTTCAACTGCC; NOTCH4-R: GCAAGTAGGTCCAGACAGGT; DLL1-F: TGTGACGAGTGTATCCGCTAT; DLL1-R: GTGTGCAGTAGTTCAGGTCCT.

RNA sequencing

Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Library construction and sequencing were completed by LC Bio-tech (Hangzhou, China). The R “DESeq2” package was used to identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs). A |log2FC|≥ 0.6 and p value < 0.05 were the thresholds for identifying DEGs. The R “clusterProfiler” package was utilized to analysis on Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoko Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG).

Cell proliferation

Cell proliferation was assessed using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (Beyotime Biotechnology) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Briefly, transfected SW480 cells were seeded into 96-well plates (0.32 cm2/well) at 1 × 103 cells/well (3125 cells/cm2) and incubated for 24, 48, and 72 h. At each time point, 10 μL of CCK-8 was added to each well and incubated at 37 °C for 2 h. The absorbance of each well was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader.

Wound healing assay

Wound healing assay was performed as previously described [[21,22]]. The migratory ability of SW480 cells was evaluated by the wound healing assay. SW480 cells were seeded in 6-well plates and allowed to reach 90–100% confluence. A sterile 200 μL pipette tip was used to create a parallel scratch in each well. The wells were washed with PBS to remove the medium and floating cells, and then cultured in serum-free DMEM medium. At 0, 24, and 48 h, scratch widths were captured under a light microscope (Olympus, Japan).

Statistical analysis

The relationship between MIER2 expression and clinicopathological features was assessed through Chi-square test, Wilcoxon test and logistic regression. Survival analysis was performed by the Kaplan–Meier with Log-rank test. Univariate and multivariate analyses based on Cox regression were used to evaluate the prognostic factors. All statistical analyses were conducted within R (version 3.6.3) and GraphPad Prism 8.0 software. Data are presented as mean ± SEM, and a p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Result

High expression of MIER2 in COAD patients

To investigate the expression pattern of MIER2 in colon adenocarcinoma (COAD), we analyzed transcriptomic data from The Cancer Genome Atlas Program (TCGA) and compared MIER2 mRNA levels between tumor tissues and adjacent normal tissues. The results showed that MIER2 was significantly upregulated in COAD tissues compared to normal controls (Fig. 1A and B). This finding was further validated in the UALCAN database (Fig. 1C), which consistently demonstrated elevated MIER2 expression in COAD. Association of MIER2 expression with survival of COAD patients

Kaplan–Meier survival analysis was performed to evaluate the prognostic value of MIER2 expression in COAD patients. The results demonstrated that patients with high MIER2 expression exhibited significantly shorter overall survival (OS), Disease Survival Specific (DSS) and Progress Free Interval (PFI) compared to those with low MIER2 expression (OS: HR = 1.68, 95%CI: 1.18 ~ 2.38, p = 0.004; DSS: HR = 2.58, 95%CI: 1.59 ~ 4.17; p < 0.001; PFI: HR = 1.46, 95%CI: 1.07 ~ 1.98, p = 0.016) (Fig. 2A-C). Moreover, subgroup analysis of OS was performed based on age, gender, T stage, M stage, N stage, pathologic stage, residual tumor, history of colon polyps, histological type, and colon polyps present. Subgroup survival analysis showed that high MIER2 expression was associated with clinical prognosis in M0 stage, N0 stage, stage II, age > 65 years, female, and adenocarcinoma (Fig. 2D–I). Furthermore, we also performed a univariate Cox regression analysis (Table 3), and found that TNM status, pathologic stage, residual tumor, and MIER2 expression had independent prognostic significances in COAD, and multivariate analysis further confirmed that MIER2 remained an independent prognostic factor after adjusting for TNM status, pathologic stage, and residual tumor (adjusted HR = 2.523, p < 0.001). Enrichment analyses of genes co-expressed with MIER2

To explore the potential biological functions of MIER2 in COAD, we utilized the LinkedOmics database to identify genes significantly co-expressed with MIER2 in TCGA-COAD cohorts. Volcano plots revealed that 5155 and 4188 genes were significantly positively and negatively correlated with MIER2, respectively, and the top 50 positive and negative co-expression genes are shown in the heatmap (Fig. 4A–C). Gene Ontology (GO) biological process enrichment analysis of the biological processes indicated that co-expressed genes were primarily enriched in response to type I interferon, viral gene expression, and macrophage activation. GO cellular component analysis showed that MIER2 is primarily associated with phagocytic cup, collagen trimer, and extracellular matrix. GO molecular function analysis indicated significant correlations with bHLH transcription factor binding, transcription corepressor activity, and extracellular matrix structural constituent. Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis further indicated that MIER2 co-expressed genes were significantly enriched in glycosaminoglycan biosynthesis, staphylococcus aureus infection, Notch signaling pathway, and HIF-1 signaling pathway (Fig. 4D–G). To further validate the enrichment analysis, we examined the related-gene expression in the Notch signaling pathway (NOTCH3, NCOR2, NOTCH4, and DLL1). The MIER2 knockout significantly reduced the expression of NOTCH3 and NCOR2 (Supplementary Fig. 1). Correlation between MIER2 expression and immune infiltration

GO enrichment analysis of MIER2 co-expressed genes revealed significant enrichment in immune-related biological processes, notably the type I interferon, viral gene expression, and macrophage activation. These findings suggested that MIER2 may be functionally involved in modulating innate immune responses in COAD patients. The CIBERSORT algorithm was used to estimate the immune cell infiltration score for each sample, and found high-MIER2 expression group exhibited elevated the levels of CD8 + T cells and Tregs cells activated, decreased the level of CD4 + T cells, dendritic cells, and activated mast cells activated (Fig. 5A). Similarly, the expression of MIER2 was negatively correlated with eosinophils, CD4 + T cells, and monocytes and positively correlated with CD8 + T cells, Tregs cells, and M0 macrophages (Fig. 5B). To evaluate the relationship between MIER2 expression and the tumor immune microenvironment in COAD, we also assess stroma score, immune score, and estimate score through ESTIMATE algorithm, and the high MIER2 expression group had higher stromal scores than the low MIER2 expression group (Fig. 5C). In addition, the expression of MIER2 was positively correlated with CD8 + T cells and M0 macrophages, and negatively correlated with CD4 + memory T cells and monocytes (Fig. 5D-H). Knockdown of MIER2 suppresses cell proliferation and migration in COAD cells

To investigate the functional role of MIER2 in colon adenocarcinoma (COAD) progression, we performed functional experiments in COAD cell lines. Using the CCLE database, we found MIER2 mRNA expression significantly increased in COAD cell lines (Fig. 6A). Small interfering RNA (siRNA) targeting MIER2 was transfected into SW480 cells, and knockdown efficiency was confirmed at both the mRNA level by RT-qPCR (Fig. 6B). Cell proliferation assays using CCK-8 revealed that MIER2 knockdown significantly reduced SW480 cell proliferation compared to negative control siRNA groups (Fig. 6C). In addition, wound healing assay was conducted to assess the effect of MIER2 knockdown on cell motility. The results showed that silencing of MIER2 significantly impaired the migratory capacity of SW480 cells (Fig. 6D). Differential gene function analysis via transcriptome sequencing

To further investigate the molecular mechanisms underlying the oncogenic role of MIER2, we conducted RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) on SW480 cells transfected with si-MIER2 or control siRNA. Principal component analysis (PCA) demonstrated clear separation between si-MIER2 and control groups, indicating robust transcriptomic changes upon MIER2 knockdown (Fig. 7A). Differential expression analysis identified a total of 536 upregulated and 197 downregulated genes (Fig. 7B). Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis revealed that the upregulated gene were significantly enriched in biological processes (BP) such as intrinsic apoptotic signaling pathway, intrinsic apoptotic signaling pathway in response to endoplasmic reticulum stress, and response to endoplasmic reticulum stress. Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis further demonstrated that upregulated genes were primarily involved in lysosome, shigellosis, and protein processing in endoplasmic reticulum (Fig. 7C). The downregulated genes of GO-BP analysis significantly enriched in RNA localization, mRNA transport, and nucleic acid transport. KEGG analysis enriched in Nucleocytoplasmic transport, cell cycle, and thiamine metabolism (Fig. 7D). Discussion

In this study, we demonstrated that MIER2 is significantly upregulated in COAD. This overexpression was consistent across multiple cancer types, with particularly in COAD, and validated in both TCGA and UALCAN datasets. Although MIER2 expression was not significantly associated with most clinicopathological parameters, high expression was correlated with histological subtypes, suggesting that MIER2 may be involved in distinct pathological processes in COAD. Importantly, survival analyses revealed that elevated MIER2 expression was significantly associated with poorer overall survival (OS), disease-specific survival (DSS), and disease-free survival (DFS). These findings indicate that MIER2 could serve as a novel prognostic biomarker in COAD.

To further elucidate the biological implications of MIER2 dysregulation, we performed co-expression and enrichment analyses. Genes co-expressed with MIER2 were predominantly involved in type I interferon response, viral gene expression, and macrophage activation, suggesting a potential immunoregulatory function. These processes are intimately linked with the tumor immune microenvironment, which plays a critical role in colorectal cancer progression and therapeutic response [[23–25]]. Indeed, immune infiltration analyses indicated that high MIER2 expression was associated with altered immune cell composition, including elevated CD8⁺ T cells and regulatory T cells (Tregs), and reduced CD4⁺ T cells and dendritic cells. This complex immune landscape suggests that MIER2 may contribute to immune evasion or modulation of antitumor immunity, possibly via interferon signaling and extracellular matrix remodeling.

Functionally, silencing of MIER2 in COAD cell lines led to significant suppression of cell proliferation and migration, reinforcing the hypothesis that MIER2 may act as a pro-oncogenic factor in COAD. Previous studies have demonstrated that MIER2 promotes drug resistance in renal cell carcinoma (RCC) by inducing lipid accumulation through the regulation of PGC1A expression [[13]]. In addition, MIER2 is a transcriptional regulator that participates in key cellular processes such as proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis by modulating chromatin state and gene expression. It has been reported to interact with HDAC1/2, recruiting histone deacetylases to achieve transcriptional repression of target genes [[10]]. However, our transcriptomic analysis based on MIER2 knockdown SW480 cells further revealed the activation of pro-apoptotic pathways, including the intrinsic apoptotic signaling pathway and response to endoplasmic reticulum stress, along with the downregulation of pathways related to cell cycle progression and RNA transport. The intrinsic apoptotic pathway, also known as the mitochondrial pathway, is a tightly regulated process triggered primarily by intracellular stress signals such as DNA damage, oxidative stress, or endoplasmic reticulum stress [[26,27]]. This pathway is primarily regulated by the Bcl-2 family of proteins, which consists of both pro-apoptotic (e.g., BAX) and anti-apoptotic members (e.g., BCL-2) [[28,29]]. In response to cellular stress, the balance shifts in favor of pro-apoptotic proteins, leading to mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization, the release of cytochrome c, and the subsequent activation of caspase-9, which in turn activates caspase-3, culminating in programmed cell death [[30–32]]. These transcriptomic changes are consistent with the observed phenotypic effects and indicate that MIER2 may exert oncogenic effects by regulating genes critical for cell survival and metastasis.

Conclusion

Taken together, our study suggests that MIER2 acts as an oncogenic regulator in COAD. Its overexpression is associated with poor prognosis, altered immune infiltration, and an intrinsic apoptotic signaling pathway related to tumor progression. Future studies focusing on the upstream regulators and downstream effectors of MIER2, as well as in vivo functional validation, will be essential to fully elucidate its role in COAD pathophysiology.

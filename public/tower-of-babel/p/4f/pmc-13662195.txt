Chromosome-level genome assembly of oil-tea tree Camellia brevistyla
Authors: Hai Lin, Deyi Yuan, Shixin Xiao, Wenfang Gong, Lin Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractCamellia oleifera, belonging to the genus Camellia, is an important woody edible oil plant with high ecological and economic values. In this study, we assembled a chromosome-level genome of Camellia brevistyla that represents the closest diploid relative of polyploid C. oleifera based on current research. The genome size of the assembly was 3.10 Gb with a contig N50 size of 88.60 Mb. The BUSCO completeness score was estimated to be 98.88%, indicating high assembly completeness. A total of 2.29 Gb of the sequences were annotated as repetitive elements, accounting for 73.67% of the entire genome assembly. A total of 35,093 protein-coding genes were predicted, with the annotation completeness evaluated to be 97.03% via BUSCO analysis. In conclusion, the high-quality C. brevistyla genome is a pivotal genetic resource within the genus Camellia, which can serve as a vital diploid comparator for evolutionary and genomic studies in the more complex polyploid C. oleifera.

Background & Summary

Camellia oleifera, belonging to the Camellia genus (Theaceae), is a perennial shrub and unique woody oil seed species. The term “oil-tea camellia” collectively refers to a group of Camellia species characterized by high seed oil content1. Camellia oil, extracted from the seeds, is a high-quality edible oil rich in unsaturated fatty acids and known as “Eastern olive oil”. Furthermore, camellia oil is remarkably rich in multiple bioactive compounds, including polyphenols, saponins, and other healthy components, possessing high nutritional and health-beneficial value2. Polyploidy is frequently observed within oil-tea camellia species and the most widely cultivated species are tetraploid C. oleifera (2n = 4x = 60) and hexaploid C. oleifera (2n = 6x = 90)3. Given their vast morphological variations and complex ploidy levels, the origin and evolutionary history of polyploid C. oleifera plants have remained elusive for decades. Consistent with other transcriptomic evidence4,5, our previous work identified C. brevistyla as the closest diploid relative of polyploid C. oleifera6. Notably, C. brevistyla is also an economically important tree species within the Camellia genus due to its significant oil production7. In this study, a combination of PacBio High Fidelity (HiFi) sequencing and High-throughput Chromosome Conformation Capture (Hi-C) technologies was employed to obtain a high-quality C. brevistyla genome. The genome of C. brevistyla is a pivotal genetic resource within the genus Camellia and essential for dissecting evolutionary history and genetic variations for more complex polyploid Camellia species.

Based on k-mer analysis, the genome size was estimated to be 3.17 Gb (Fig. 1a). We used 97.84 Gb PacBio HiFi reads together with 286.48 Gb Hi-C data for genome assembly (Table 1). The draft assembly was further anchored into 15 pseudochromosomes (Fig. 1b). The final genome size was 3.10 Gb with a contig N50 of 88.60 Mb (Fig. 1c, Table 2). We identified 2.29 Gb of repetitive elements in the genome, which accounted for 73.67% of the total genome assembly (Table 3). The most abundant category of transposable elements (TEs) was long-terminal repeat retrotransposon (LTR), with LTR/Copia and LTR/Gypsy comprising 6.20% and 33.67% of the genome, respectively (Fig. 1c, Table 3). Analysis of the insertion time revealed that Gypsy and Copia superfamilies underwent continuous expansion over the past 4 million years (Fig. 1d). A total of 35,093 protein-coding genes were predicted in the assembled genome (Table 2), of which 94.24% could be functionally annotated with the eggNOG database.

Materials and Methods

Plant materials, sample collection, and sequencing

Whole-genome sequencing was performed on a single specimen of C. brevistyla, cultivated in the International Camellia Germplasm Resource Garden in Jinhua City, Zhejiang Province, China (latitude 29°06′29″ N, longitude 119°38′58″ E). High-quality genomic DNA was extracted from fresh young leaves for genome sequencing following a modified hexadecyltrimethylammonium bromide (CTAB) method8. The circular consensus sequencing (CCS) libraries were constructed with the SMRTbell Express Template Prep Kit 2.0 (PacBio) and subsequently sequenced on the PacBio Sequel II platform (Pacific Bio, CA, USA). For Hi-C sequencing, formaldehyde was used for crosslinking the fresh leaves, and the crosslinking reaction was terminated using glycine solution. The cross-linked chromatin was then digested using the MboI restriction endonuclease. The Hi-C libraries were constructed and sequenced on the NovaSeq 6000 platform (Illumina). Tissues from leaves, roots, stems, flowers, and seeds were sampled for RNA sequencing (Table 1). Total RNA extraction was performed using the RNeasy Plant Mini Kit (Qiagen, Hilden, Germany). The cDNA libraries were constructed using the TruSeq RNA Library Preparation Kit, version 2 (Illumina, USA) and sequenced on the NovaSeq 6000 platform (Illumina).

Genome survey, assembly, and scaffolding

The PacBio HiFi reads were first split into 21-mers, followed by k-mer frequency counting. The genome size was then estimated by GCE software (v1.0.2) with parameters ‘-c 30 -H 1’9. The de novo genome assembly was performed using hifiasm (v0.16.1)10 by integrating PacBio HiFi reads with Hi-C data. To ensure assembly quality, Purge_dups pipeline (v1.2.5)11 was employed to remove potential redundant sequences. The cleaned Hi-C reads were mapped to the draft assembly using BWA MEM (v0.7.17-r1198)12 with parameter ‘-SP5M’. The unique mapped reads were subsequently taken as input for HapHiC pipeline (v1.0.6)13 with parameter ‘--correct_nrounds 2’ for scaffolding followed by manual curation and refinement using Juicebox (v1.11.08)14.

Repeat sequence and gene annotation

Repeat annotation was performed using the EDTA pipeline (v2.0.1)15 with parameters ‘-u 5.62e-9 --species others’. The neutral mutation rate of 5.62 × 10−9 from C. sinensis16 was specifically applied to estimate the insertion times of LTR retrotransposons. Subsequently, the comprehensive and non-redundant repeat library generated by EDTA was used by RepeatMasker (v4.1.1)17 to annotate and mask repetitive elements across the genome. Gene structure annotation was performed by combining de novo prediction, homologous alignment, and transcriptome data. RNA-seq data were assembled using Trinity (v2.8.5)18 and gene models were predicted via PASA pipeline (v2.5.3)19. Protein sequences from closely related species (C. lanceoleosa20, C. sinensis21, C. chekiangoleosa22, and C. nanyongensis23) were downloaded and aligned to the genome using miniprot (v0.18)24 to provide homologous evidence. AUGUSTUS (v3.0.3)25, SNAP (v2006–07–28)26, and GeneMark (v4.71_lic)27 were employed for de novo prediction on the repeat-masked genome using only sequence information. Finally, all results were integrated into consensus gene models using EvidenceModeler (v1.1.1)28. Functional annotation of the predicted genes was performed using eggNOG-mapper (v2.0.1–14)29.

Data Records

The raw sequencing data, including PacBio HiFi reads, Hi-C reads, and RNA-seq, have been deposited in the NCBI Sequence Read Archive (SRA) database under accession number SRP70225930 and genome assembly has been deposited in the NCBI GenBank database under accession number GCA_057755025.131. Additionally, we have also deposited the data into the National Genomics Data Center (NGDC), China National Center for Bioinformation32. Raw sequencing data are also available in the NGDC Genome Sequence Archive (GSA)33 under accession number CRA02206334, and the genome assembly and annotation data have been deposited in the NGDC Genome Warehouse (GWH)35 under accession number GWHHIXW00000000.236.

Technical Validation

The quality of genome assembly was evaluated using multiple approaches. The completeness of genome assembly and annotation were evaluated to be 98.88% and 97.03% (Tables 4, 5), respectively, via BUSCO (Benchmarking Universal Single-Copy Orthologs, v5.6.1)37 analysis based on the Embryophyta_odb10 database. The whole-genome LTR Assembly Index (LAI) was calculated using LTR_retriever (v2.9.0)38,39, yielding a value of 15.19, which satisfied the criterion for a reference-grade genome. The Merqury (v1.3)40 pipeline was employed to assess base-level consensus accuracy with the HiFi reads. The consensus quality value (QV) of 69.29 corresponds to a single-base accuracy exceeding 99.999%.

Furthermore, the HiFi reads were mapped to the genome assembly using minimap2 (v2.26-r1175)41 with parameter ‘-ax asm5’, yielding a mapping rate of 98.30% and an average mapping depth of 27.37× (Fig. 2a), a value highly consistent with the initial sequencing depth (30×). Syntenic blocks were identified using ‘jcvi.compara.catalog’ module in the JCVI (v1.5.9) toolkit42 with parameter ‘-cscore = 0.99’.

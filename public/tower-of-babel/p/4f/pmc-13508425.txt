Exploration of an artificial intelligence-supported training mechanism for teaching competence development in graduate students of prosthodontics
Authors: Lei Jiang, Xueting Weng, Xinwen Tong, Changyuan Zhang, Run Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThis study aimed to evaluate the effectiveness of an artificial intelligence-supported training framework in enhancing the teaching competence of prosthodontics graduate students.MethodsTwenty-one first-year prosthodontics graduate students at Fujian Medical University participated in a structured, four-stage intervention over two semesters: baseline assessment, AI platform training, AI-assisted iterative teaching practice, and final evaluation. DeepSeek served as the core AI tool for lesson preparation, lecture script drafting, concept map generation, and interactive teaching design. Teaching competence was assessed at seven time points across five dimensions: teaching attitude and language, teaching content, logical structure, professional accuracy, and interactivity. Longitudinal changes were evaluated using repeated measures analysis, and students also rated the usefulness of the AI tool on a 5-point Likert scale.ResultsOverall teaching competence improved significantly from baseline (56.52 ± 4.83) to the final evaluation (77.24 ± 4.11; F = 45.32, p < 0.001, η2 = 0.69). All five dimensions showed significant gains, with the largest improvements in teaching content and logical structure. Individual growth curves demonstrated continuous progress for nearly all participants. Students reported high perceived usefulness of AI (median 5, IQR 4–5), reflecting positive engagement with AI-assisted training.ConclusionThe AI-assisted training framework was associated with improvements in prosthodontics graduate students’ teaching competence, particularly in content completeness and structural coherence. The introduction of DeepSeek, with strengths in Chinese semantic processing and professional reasoning, offers a potentially useful and localized tool for dental education. This framework may provide a strategy to help cultivate graduate students with skills in research, clinical practice, and teaching, potentially supporting the development of future high-quality faculty.

Introduction

Graduate students represent the primary pool of future university faculty [1]. Despite their typically strong academic qualifications and research capabilities, systematic training in teaching competence remains limited, particularly in medical and dental education. Previous studies have shown that research productivity does not necessarily correlate with teaching competence; conversely, teaching experience can promote the development of research skills [2, 3]. This underscores the necessity of structured pedagogical training during graduate education.

In China, this imbalance is especially pronounced in medical graduate education. Academically, most institutions require students to complete research experiments and publish scholarly articles in order to graduate; clinically, professional master’s students are guided by standardized training and exit examinations [4]. Such policy and guidance bias results in the underdevelopment of graduate students’ teaching abilities, leaving many inadequately prepared for future teaching roles. For example, in the Prosthodontics program at our institution, graduate students rarely receive systematic training in course design, classroom organization, or reflective teaching practices.

Currently, the higher medical education system demonstrates a clear deficiency in systematic teaching competence training, which presents an opportunity for the integration of emerging technologies such as artificial intelligence. AI, particularly advances in natural language processing and generative models, provides novel avenues for innovation in medical education [5, 6]. AI can assist in course planning, content structuring, interactive teaching design, and personalized feedback, thereby accelerating the development of teaching competence [7].

Based on this potential, the present study designed and piloted an AI-empowered training framework for graduate students in prosthodontics. The framework aims to enhance teaching competence through a structured cycle encompassing baseline assessment, AI-assisted training, iterative practice, and summative evaluation.

Materials and methods

Study design

This study was an educational intervention aimed at evaluating the effectiveness of an AI–assisted training mechanism in enhancing the teaching competence of graduate students in prosthodontics. The intervention was implemented over two semesters following a four-stage framework:baseline assessment, AI platform training, AI-assisted cyclical teaching practice and summative evaluation.

Participants

Participants were first-year graduate students in Prosthodontics at the School and Hospital of Stomatology, Fujian Medical University, enrolled in the 2023–2024 academic year. All students were invited to participate voluntarily and provided written informed consent prior to inclusion. Based on commonly used statistical parameters (two-tailed test, α = 0.05, power 1 − β = 0.80), the required sample size for paired comparisons was calculated using G*Power software. For a large effect size (Cohen’s d = 0.8), n = 15 was needed. A total of 21 students were included in the study finally.

Intervention framework

Baseline assessment

Each student was assigned a standardized teaching topic (e.g., “Clinical Significance and Fabrication of Temporary Restorations”) and independently prepared a 10-min PowerPoint presentation without the use of AI tools. A panel of five faculty members evaluated the presentations using the official institutional scoring rubric. To ensure scoring consistency, all evaluators participated in a calibration session prior to the baseline assessment. During this session, the faculty panel jointly reviewed the institutional rubric and scored several sample teaching presentations to harmonize their interpretation of the evaluation criteria. The rubric has been routinely used in internal teaching assessments within the institution. Inter-rater agreement was evaluated using the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC), with an ICC of 0.84, indicating acceptable reliability for the overall teaching competence score. Assessment dimensions included completeness of teaching content, logical structure, language fluency, professional accuracy, and interactivity and engagement. This step established each student’s baseline teaching competence.

During the training process, students were guided to use structured prompts to support teaching preparation and instructional design. Examples of prompts used with DeepSeek included: 1) If you were an expert in this field, how would you present or teach this chapter in a 10-min lecture for dental students? 2) Create a mind map based on the provided content to illustrate the key concepts and logical relationships. 3) Generate a structured teaching outline for this chapter, including an introduction, key concepts, key points, difficult points, integration of clinical cases, and the design of classroom interactions.

These prompts helped students organize teaching materials, clarify knowledge structures, and design interactive classroom activities. Students were also encouraged to iteratively refine prompts to obtain more pedagogically relevant outputs.

AI-Assisted cyclical teaching practice

This stage constituted the core intervention of the study and included five rounds of teaching practice. Each round lasted two weeks and was organized around a unified teaching theme. To minimize potential practice effects, different teaching topics were assigned in each round of training. In each round, students were required to use AI tools to complete the following tasks: Prepare teaching slides using DeepSeek, Doubao, and other relevant platforms; Draft comprehensive teaching design plans outlining objectives, instructional methods, interactive strategies, integration of ideological and political elements, and session summaries; Develop and refine lecture scripts to ensure clarity and coherence; Generate concept maps to visually represent the main teaching lines and relationships among knowledge points; Conduct 10-min simulated teaching presentations.Each round of training was evaluated and feedback provided by the same panel of reviewers, following the evaluation procedures used at baseline and based on the standardized scoring rubric.

Summative evaluation

Students were required to prepare teaching materials within one week on a designated topic (e.g., “Retention Principles of Complete Dentures”), which differed from those used in the baseline assessment and training phases, with the assistance of AI tools. The submitted materials included PowerPoint slides, teaching design plans, lecture scripts, and concept maps, followed by a 10-min on-site teaching presentation. The evaluation procedures were the same as previously described.

Additionally, students rated the usefulness of AI in teaching preparation and classroom performance on a 5-point Likert scale [8] (1 = not helpful, 5 = very helpful).

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics (mean ± standard deviation, SD) were computed for total teaching competence scores and the five dimensions (teaching attitude and language, teaching content, logical structure, professional accuracy, interactivity and engagement) across seven assessment time points (baseline, five practice rounds, and final evaluation), as well as for AI usefulness ratings. Repeated measures ANOVA was used to test within-subject changes over time. Mauchly’s test was performed to assess the sphericity assumption; when violated, the Greenhouse–Geisser correction was applied. All analyses were performed using SPSS 26, with two-tailed p-values < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

Results

Teaching competence total score

Across the seven assessment time points, graduate students’ overall teaching competence scores exhibited a significant upward trend (F = 45.32, p < 0.001, η2 = 0.69), increasing from a baseline of 56.52 ± 4.83 to 77.24 ± 4.11 at the final evaluation. The large effect size (η2 = 0.69) suggests that the intervention had a substantial educational impact, indicating that the AI-supported training framework contributed meaningfully to the development of teaching competence among graduate students. Within-subject contrasts revealed a significant linear trend, indicating continuous improvement in teaching competence throughout the training period (Table 1). Perceived usefulness of AI

Students’ ratings of AI usefulness in teaching preparation and classroom performance (5-point Likert scale) demonstrated a generally positive attitude, with a median score of 5 (IQR: 4–5). Specifically, 9 students (42.9%) rated AI as “5 – very useful,” 9 students (42.9%) as “4 – useful,” and 3 students (14.2%) as “3 – moderately useful.”

Discussion

This study demonstrated that an artificial intelligence (AI)–empowered training mechanism effectively enhanced teaching competence among prosthodontics graduate students. Significant improvements were observed in overall performance as well as in specific dimensions, including teaching content, structural organization, language fluency, and interactivity. Longitudinal tracking of individual growth curves further confirmed that nearly all students exhibited continuous improvement, highlighting the feasibility of integrating AI tools into postgraduate teaching training in dental education. The large effect size observed in this study further supports the educational relevance of the intervention, suggesting that AI-assisted training may play a meaningful role in accelerating the development of teaching competence among postgraduate dental students.

It should be emphasized that the present study was designed to evaluate a pedagogical training framework centered on the application of AI tools rather than to isolate the independent effect of a single AI system. The intervention integrated multiple instructional components, including prompting skills training, faculty guidance, structured lesson planning, iterative feedback, and repeated simulated teaching sessions. These elements were intentionally incorporated to facilitate students’ effective use of AI tools during teaching preparation and instructional design. Therefore, the improvements observed in teaching competence should be interpreted within the context of this integrated AI-supported educational framework.

In higher medical education, graduate students often devote substantial time to clinical and research training, while systematic pedagogical preparation remains relatively limited. Addressing this gap, AI tools can serve as accessible training aids, providing instant feedback, personalized learning recommendations, and automated content generation [9]. Wu et al. [10] reported that students trained with the ChatGPT model demonstrated enhanced clinical reasoning and decision-making skills, with conversational guidance and automated Q&A functions significantly improving both learning experiences and efficiency. Furthermore, Preiksaitis et al. [11] highlighted the broad applications of generative AI in medical education, including simulation-based training, self-directed learning, and writing assistance, emphasizing its capacity to enrich educational practices through personalized feedback and automated content generation. Similarly, Lucas et al. [12] supported the transformative potential of large language models in medical education, capable of offering innovative solutions such as personalized learning, intelligent tutoring, content generation, and clinical decision support. Collectively, these studies substantiate the effectiveness of incorporating AI into teaching competence training, as demonstrated in the present study, by compensating for young faculty’s lack of teaching experience and accelerating the development of instructional skills.

Compared with traditional passive knowledge acquisition, AI promotes active learning by fostering independent information search, inquiry, and problem-solving [13]. Such interactive pedagogical methods not only strengthen comprehension but also cultivate essential cognitive skills, including critical thinking, problem-solving, and self-directed learning. By offering personalized learning experiences tailored to individual needs and iterative practice opportunities, AI addresses the limitations of teacher availability and standardized curricula, thereby boosting learners’ confidence [14]. These elements are crucial for developing comprehensive teaching competence. Accordingly, the AI-assisted training in this study produced favorable outcomes, with students achieving multidimensional improvements and successfully transitioning from “novice instructors” to “qualified teachers” within a relatively short period.

At present, AI applications in medical education are advancing rapidly. Duan et al. [15] conducted a comprehensive investigation into medical students’ attitudes toward AI and found that they generally expressed positive views and willingness to integrate AI into their learning and teaching practices. This aligns with the self-assessment results in the present study, where graduate students rated AI usefulness positively. Moreover, generative AI has the potential to serve as a tool that explains complex concepts at an appropriate level for postgraduate learners, thereby enhancing comprehension [16]. For instance, one study reported that AI successfully reviewed trainee-generated radiology reports and produced relevant educational feedback [17]. In our study, continuous interaction with AI tools provided graduate students with iterative feedback, substantially improving their teaching competence and supporting the application potential of AI in medical education.

Unlike most prior studies that primarily relied on general-purpose large language models such as ChatGPT or GPT-4, the present study introduced DeepSeek as an auxiliary tool in a medical education context for the first time. DeepSeek demonstrated strengths in Chinese semantic processing, accurate translation of technical terminology, and structured logical reasoning, making it particularly well-suited to disciplines such as prosthodontics that demand high linguistic precision and clinical reasoning. Its application in this study not only improved the efficiency and coherence of teaching preparation but also provided empirical evidence for exploring more localized and discipline-specific AI teaching support tools. The adoption of DeepSeek can thus be considered an innovative aspect of this study, enriching the pathways for AI integration in medical education.

The findings of this study provide empirical support for the application of AI-assisted teaching in medical education. First, AI can enhance the accuracy of instructional content and the logical structure of delivery. Second, AI-assisted teaching can offer personalized learning experiences; El Saadawi [18] demonstrated that AI-based tutoring systems can interpret learner input with precision to provide customized educational experiences tailored to diverse student needs. Furthermore, AI can adapt to learner progress, delivering personalized feedback and improving learning experiences. Through such automated and individualized evaluation and feedback, AI can enhance teaching competence across multiple dimensions [19]. Looking forward, medical education should embrace AI as an integral component to cultivate healthcare professionals with strong AI literacy.

At the same time, the potential risks of AI in medical education must be recognized. As noted by Janumpally et al. [20], known challenges include inaccuracy, overreliance, authenticity and completeness, bias, and privacy and security concerns. In our study, some students scored lower in the domain of professional accuracy, primarily due to insufficient critical evaluation of AI-generated content, leading to the uncritical adoption of incorrect viewpoints. Thus, while acknowledging the benefits of AI in postgraduate education, it is equally important to remain vigilant about its risks.

This study has several limitations. First, the participants were limited to graduate students from the the School and Hospital of Stomatology, Fujian Medical University, with a relatively small sample size, which may affect external validity. Second, this study employed a One-Group Pretest–Posttest Design [21] without a separate control group. This approach was primarily chosen to ensure that all graduate students had equal access to the newly developed training program, which had been incorporated into the teaching development plan of the Department of Prosthodontics. However, the absence of a control group limits the ability to draw causal inferences. Improvements in teaching competence may partially reflect repeated teaching practice, maturation effects over time, or increasing familiarity with the evaluation rubric. Finally, prior exposure to AI tools among participants represents another potential confounding factor, as familiarity with these technologies may have influenced the intervention outcomes [22].

Conclusion

This study observed that the AI-empowered training mechanism was associated with improvements in the teaching competence of prosthodontics graduate students, with particularly notable gains in the completeness and coherence of instructional content. Longitudinal analysis indicated that most students showed sustained progress during AI-assisted training, highlighting its potential practical value in medical education. Unlike previous studies that primarily relied on general-purpose large language models, this study introduced DeepSeek for the first time, whose strengths in Chinese language processing and domain-specific reasoning may offer new possibilities for dental education.

In summary, the AI-supported training framework offers a novel approach to cultivating high-quality medical professionals equipped with research, clinical, and teaching competence. Future multi-center, multidisciplinary, and longitudinal studies will be essential to further validate its effectiveness and optimize implementation strategies.

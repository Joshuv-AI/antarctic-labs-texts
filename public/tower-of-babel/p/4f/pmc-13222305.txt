Phase III Prospective Randomised Study of Netarsudil and Concomitant Latanoprost Efficacy and Safety for Primary Open-Angle Glaucoma or Ocular Hypertension in Japan: J-ROCKET-2
Authors: Makoto Aihara
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThis prospective, phase III, randomised, multicentre, double-masked study (jRCT2031210122) assessed the intraocular pressure (IOP)-lowering efficacy, tolerability and superiority of netarsudil 0.02% + concomitant latanoprost 0.005% to placebo + latanoprost in Japanese participants with primary open-angle glaucoma (POAG) or ocular hypertension (OHT) who have inadequate IOP reduction with latanoprost monotherapy.MethodsParticipants received latanoprost once daily for 6 weeks. Eligible participants (n = 246) with POAG (n = 165) or OHT (n = 81) were randomised (1:1) to netarsudil + latanoprost (n = 122) or placebo + latanoprost (n = 124) for 4 weeks. Primary endpoint was mean diurnal (MD) IOP at week 4, with superiority demonstrated if the between-group difference in least squares mean MD IOP was < 0 and p < 0.05. Secondary endpoints included MD IOP at weeks 1 and 2. Adverse events (AEs) were recorded.ResultsAt week 4, MD IOP (standard error) was 15.29 (0.17) versus 17.65 (0.17) mmHg with netarsudil + latanoprost versus placebo + latanoprost, respectively [difference, − 2.36 (p < 0.0001)]. The differences at weeks 1 and 2 between netarsudil + latanoprost and placebo + latanoprost were − 2.47 and − 2.41 (p < 0.0001), respectively. At each visit, netarsudil + latanoprost significantly reduced MD IOP versus placebo + latanoprost (p < 0.0001). Of those receiving netarsudil + latanoprost, 64.8% reported AEs versus 21.0% receiving placebo + latanoprost. Conjunctival hyperaemia was the most reported AE in both groups (53.3% vs. 6.5%). No serious AEs were reported.ConclusionNetarsudil + latanoprost was superior to placebo + latanoprost in lowering elevated IOP in Japanese patients with POAG or OHT. Key Summary Points

Why carry out this study? Primary open-angle glaucoma (POAG) and ocular hypertension (OHT) are commonly treated with prostaglandin analogues, such as latanoprost, primarily to lower elevated intraocular pressure (IOP); however, patients do not always achieve their target IOP with monotherapy and often require adjunctive therapy to do so.This prospective, phase III, randomised study assessed the IOP-lowering efficacy of netarsudil + concomitant latanoprost to placebo + latanoprost in Japanese participants with POAG or OHT. What was learned from the study? Results show the superiority of netarsudil + latanoprost in reducing IOP versus placebo + latanoprost after 1, 2 and 4 weeks of treatment in Japanese participants who have inadequate IOP reduction with latanoprost monotherapy.Adverse events (AEs) were more common in participants receiving netarsudil + latanoprost than in those receiving placebo + latanoprost. However, no serious AEs were reported with netarsudil + latanoprost and there were no new safety signals in this study compared with previous findings.Findings from this study support the use of netarsudil + latanoprost to lower elevated IOP in Japanese patients with POAG and OHT not achieving target IOP levels on latanoprost monotherapy.

Introduction

Glaucoma is a degenerative disease characterised by the selective loss of retinal ganglion cells, followed by visual field loss [1]. It is the predominant cause of blindness worldwide and affects an estimated 70 million people [1]. The only known modifiable risk factor for lowering the likelihood of developing glaucoma and reducing progression of existing disease is reducing intraocular pressure (IOP) [2]. Current pharmacotherapies aim to lower IOP either via increasing uveoscleral outflow, such as prostaglandin analogues and alpha agonists, or by decreasing aqueous humour (AH) production, for example beta-blockers and carbonic anhydrase inhibitors [3].

Latanoprost is currently one of the most commonly prescribed prostaglandin analogues that lowers elevated IOP by primarily increasing uveoscleral outflow [4]. However, some patients with POAG or ocular hypertension (OHT) do not achieve their target IOP with prostaglandin analogues and require adjunctive therapy or an alternative agent [5]. Previous findings have shown that dual therapy regimens composed of a combination of a prostaglandin analogue with another drug class demonstrated more powerful IOP-lowering efficacy than prostaglandin analogues alone [6].

In 2017, the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approved netarsudil—a Rho-associated coiled-coil containing protein kinase (ROCK) inhibitor—for the reduction of elevated IOP in patients with OAG or OHT [7]. Netarsudil has a novel mechanism of action and lowers elevated IOP by targeting dysfunctional trabecular meshwork (TM) and lowering episcleral venous pressure (EVP) [8]. Three large phase III studies conducted in the USA, ROCKET-1 (3-month duration), ROCKET-2 (12-month duration) and ROCKET-4 (6-month duration), showed that once-daily netarsudil 0.02% led to clinically and statistically significant IOP reductions from baseline in patients with OAG and OHT [9–11].

The efficacy and safety of netarsudil/latanoprost as a fixed-dose combination (FDC) has been reported in patients with OAG or OHT in two phase III clinical trials [MERCURY-1 (12-month duration) and MERCURY-2 (3-month duration)] in the USA and Canada [4, 12, 13]. In Europe, the non-inferiority of netarsudil/latanoprost FDC to the commonly prescribed bimatoprost/timolol FDC was demonstrated in MERCURY-3 (6-month duration) [14].

Ripasudil hydrochloride hydrate (0.4%) is currently the only ROCK inhibitor available for patients in Japan, indicated for those with glaucoma or OHT who are low/non-responders to other glaucoma drugs [3]. Ripasudil, 0.4% twice daily, reduced IOP by − 2.6 to − 3.7 mmHg after 52 weeks in an open-label prospective study [15]. The most commonly reported adverse events (AEs) with ripasudil were conjunctival hyperaemia (74.6%), blepharitis (20.6%) and allergic conjunctivitis (17.2%) [15]. Netarsudil was previously demonstrated to be superior to ripasudil 0.4% in lowering elevated IOP in Japanese patients [J-ROCKET trial (NCT04620135)] [3]. After 4 weeks, mean diurnal (MD) IOP was significantly lower with netarsudil (15.96 mmHg) than ripasudil (17.71 mmHg; p < 0.0001) [3].

The most common ocular AEs with netarsudil are conjunctival hyperaemia, haemorrhage, instillation site pain and cornea verticillata [7]. In the ROCKET-1 and -2 studies, most cases of cornea verticillata presented between weeks 6 and 13 [9], most of which resolved upon cessation of therapy [11].

The efficacy of netarsudil/latanoprost FDC has previously been demonstrated in global studies enrolling mainly Western participants, but there is currently no available evidence supporting the additional IOP-lowering effect of netarsudil specifically in Japanese patients who experience inadequate IOP-lowering response with latanoprost alone. Therefore, this phase III, randomised, controlled, double-masked trial aimed to demonstrate the superiority of netarsudil 0.02% with concomitant latanoprost 0.005% (netarsudil + latanoprost) over placebo with latanoprost 0.005% (placebo + latanoprost) in IOP-lowering efficacy in Japanese patients with primary OAG (POAG) or OHT, who experienced inadequate IOP-lowering effects with latanoprost 0.005% alone.

Methods

Study Design

This phase III, multicentre, randomised study (Japan registry of clinical trials: jRCT2031210122) was conducted from 25 May 2021 to 2 May 2022 across 31 sites in Japan. The study was conducted in compliance with institutional review boards (IRBs) and the Good Clinical Practice for Drugs guidelines, and in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. IRBs included Shintokai Medical Corporation, Shin-Akasaka, Jinbo Orthopedics and the Society for the Ethics of Clinical Research. Further details are presented in Table S1. Ethical approval ID numbers are unavailable because of the location of the IRBs. All participants provided consent before the study commenced. All eligible participants entered a 6-week run-in period, in which latanoprost 0.005% was administered at 21:00 daily, followed by a qualification period of 2–7 days. This was followed by a 4-week treatment period (Fig. S1). IOP was measured using Goldmann applanation tonometry at screening (visit 1), qualification 1 (visit 2) and at three time points of 9:00, 11:00 and 16:00 at qualification 2 [day 1 (visit 3)], and weeks 1, 2 and 4 (visits 4, 5 and 6). At each time point, IOP was measured twice consecutively, and the mean value was adopted. If a ≥ 2-mmHg difference was noted between the two consecutively measured values, a third measurement was performed, and the median value was adopted.

Participants and Study Population

The key inclusion criteria (applied from visits 1 to 3) were: aged ≥ 20 years, a primary diagnosis of POAG or OHT (or a diagnosis of POAG in one eye and OHT in the other eye) and IOP of ≥ 18 mmHg and < 35 mmHg at qualification 1 (visit 2) and qualification 2 (visit 3) in at least one eye after administration of latanoprost for ≥ 6 weeks. Participants who met any of the following criteria were excluded from the study: clinically significant ocular disease that could potentially interfere with the interpretation of efficacy variables or the assessment of the safety of netarsudil in this study; and exfoliation or pigmentary glaucoma, history of angle closure or narrow-angle glaucoma. Further details on the eligibility criteria are presented in Table S2.

Masking and Randomisation

Participants who met the eligibility criteria were randomised 1:1 to either netarsudil 0.02% or a placebo ophthalmic solution using a randomisation and trial supply management system (permuted block method). Each eligible participant was assigned a drug that was numbered via a random assignment table. The investigational drug allocation code was not accessible to the investigator or the participant. In the case of a medical emergency, the investigator could access the drug allocation code.

During the 4-week treatment period, participants were instructed to instil either netarsudil or placebo ophthalmic solutions at 21:00, followed by latanoprost ophthalmic solution after ≥ 5 min. Both ophthalmic solutions were filled in the same type of transparent container with the same labelling, so the solutions were indistinguishable. Latanoprost ophthalmic solution 0.005% (preserved) was manufactured by Pfizer Japan Inc. (Shibuya-ku, Tokyo, Japan). Netarsudil ophthalmic solution was manufactured by Santen Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd. (Osaka, Japan).

Outcomes

The primary efficacy endpoint was MD IOP at week 4 of treatment. Secondary endpoints included MD IOP at weeks 1 and 2, mean IOP at each post-treatment time point, mean change and mean percentage change from (time-matched) baseline in MD IOP at each post-treatment visit, and the percentages of participants achieving a prespecified mean, mean change, and percentage mean change in MD IOP levels.

Safety analyses included the incidence of treatment-emergent AEs (TEAEs), ocular TEAEs, adverse drug reactions and serious AEs (SAEs) reported throughout the treatment period. A TEAE was defined as any untoward or unintended disease, or its sign, occurring between the point of written consent and week 4 (visit 6) or discontinuation. TEAEs were monitored throughout the study, regardless of whether they were assessed as related to the study drug or not. Spontaneous changes in symptoms of the target disease were not considered to be TEAEs. At all scheduled visits, before IOP measurements, participants were questioned regarding potential AEs. If an SAE or adverse reaction occurred and the symptom did not resolve at week 4 (visit 6) or at discontinuation of the study, the investigators followed up until the symptom was judged to have resolved to the state before the start of the study or to a degree without any medical issues. For adverse reactions that did not lead to study discontinuation, follow-up continued until the time of database lock and was optional thereafter.

Patient Involvement

Patients and members of the public were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination of the research. The protocol and informed consent forms were reviewed and approved by the IRBs listed in the Study Design, which included members of the public.

Statistical Methods

A sample size of 234 participants (117 in each group) would have 90% power to detect an MD IOP change from baseline of − 3.0 mmHg with netarsudil, and − 2.1 mmHg with placebo [between-group difference, − 0.9 mmHg; standard deviation (SD), 2.0 mmHg], after accounting for ≤ 10% of participants withdrawing from the study. The intent-to-treat (ITT) population included all participants who received at least one dose of either study drug combination. All efficacy analyses were collected from the ITT population.

Hypothesis testing was performed on the primary efficacy endpoint to assess the superiority of the IOP-lowering efficacy of netarsudil + latanoprost versus placebo + latanoprost at week 4. The predefined statistical significance level was p < 0.05. Superiority was demonstrated when the between-group difference in the least squares (LS) mean MD IOP at week 4 was < 0 and p < 0.05. In the ITT population, a linear model with MD IOP at baseline was used as a covariate, and the two treatment groups were used as factors in the model. Missing values were imputed using multiple imputation using the linear model and adopting the hypothetical strategy for intercurrent events. Statistical tests were not controlled for multiplicity.

To assess the homogeneity of the IOP-lowering efficacy of either treatment arm among subgroups, the MD IOP at week 4 was compared between treatment groups using analysis of covariance (ANCOVA). The ANCOVA model used treatment and subgroup as the main effects, MD IOP at baseline as a covariate, and the interaction of treatment and subgroup.

The per-protocol (PP) population was a sub-population of the ITT population, and included participants who had no major protocol deviations that could impact the primary endpoint results. The PP population was a secondary population used to summarise some efficacy variables. The safety population was defined as a group of participants who received at least one dose of the study drug combination and from whom safety information was gathered.

Results

Participant Disposition

The study was conducted between 25 May 2021 and 16 April 2022. In total, 246 participants were randomly assigned to either netarsudil + latanoprost (n = 122) or placebo + latanoprost (n = 124) (Table 1), of whom 243 completed the study drug treatment period (Fig. 1). Two participants in the netarsudil + latanoprost group and one participant in the placebo + latanoprost group discontinued the study. The mean (SD) age of participants was 62.6 (12.18) years, and 123 (50.0%) participants were aged ≥ 65 years. A larger proportion of participants were female (56.5%) than male (43.5%).

Efficacy

The primary efficacy endpoint was MD IOP at week 4. MD IOP [LS mean ± standard error (SE)] at week 4 in the netarsudil + latanoprost group was 15.29 (0.17) mmHg compared with 17.65 (0.17) mmHg in the placebo + latanoprost group. The superiority of netarsudil + latanoprost versus placebo + latanoprost was demonstrated at week 4 with a between-group difference (netarsudil + latanoprost minus placebo + latanoprost) in LS means (SE) of − 2.36 (0.24), 95% CI − 2.83 to − 1.88; p < 0.0001 (Table 2).

Secondary efficacy endpoints included MD IOP at weeks 1 and 2. At week 1, MD IOP (LS mean ± SE) in the netarsudil + latanoprost combination group was 15.42 (0.15) mmHg, and 17.89 (0.15) mmHg in the placebo + latanoprost group. This corresponds to a statistically significant between-group difference in LS means (SE) of − 2.47 (0.22), 95% CI − 2.89 to − 2.04 mmHg; p < 0.0001 (Table 2). At week 2, MD IOP (LS mean ± SE) in the netarsudil + latanoprost group was 15.21 (0.17) mmHg, and 17.62 (0.17) mmHg in the placebo + latanoprost group. This corresponds to a between-group difference in LS means (SE) of − 2.41 (0.24), 95% CI − 2.87 to − 1.94 mmHg; p < 0.0001, which was also statistically significant (Table 2).

At each visit, MD IOP was statistically significantly reduced in the netarsudil + latanoprost group compared with the placebo + latanoprost group (p < 0.0001) (Fig. 2). At all visits, the mean change from baseline in MD IOP was statistically significantly greater with netarsudil + latanoprost compared with placebo + latanoprost (p < 0.0001).

Safety

A summary of the AEs occurring during the 4-week treatment period can be found in Table 3. Of the 122 participants receiving netarsudil + latanoprost, 79 (64.8%) reported any TEAE compared with 26 (21.0%) participants receiving placebo + latanoprost. Ocular TEAEs were reported more frequently in participants who received netarsudil + latanoprost (63.1%) than those who received placebo + latanoprost (15.3%). There were no reports of SAEs, and most AEs were resolved during the study.

Discussion

Results from this study support the 4-week efficacy and safety of netarsudil + latanoprost in lowering elevated IOP in Japanese patients with POAG and OHT not achieving target IOP levels on latanoprost monotherapy. The primary objective, the superiority of netarsudil + latanoprost over placebo + latanoprost in lowering MD IOP at week 4 (p < 0.0001), was met. Although not controlled for multiplicity, netarsudil + latanoprost also demonstrated statistically significant IOP-lowering efficacy at weeks 1 and 2, all time points, and in change and percentage change from baseline compared with placebo + latanoprost (p < 0.0001). With the addition of netarsudil to latanoprost in participants inadequately responding to latanoprost alone, the between-group difference in IOP reduction was 2.36 mmHg (15.29 vs. 17.65 mmHg with placebo + latanoprost) after 4 weeks of treatment. The Japan Glaucoma Society stated in their fifth edition guide for glaucoma that the IOP-lowering effect of additional medications to prostaglandin analogues is often limited to 1–1.5 mmHg [16].

This phase III study is the first to present the efficacy and safety of netarsudil + latanoprost in lowering elevated IOP in Japanese patients who have inadequate IOP reduction with latanoprost monotherapy. The IOP-lowering efficacy of netarsudil/latanoprost FDC was previously demonstrated in US and Canadian patients with OAG or OHT in the MERCURY-1 and -2 studies [12, 13]. After 3 months, netarsudil/latanoprost FDC was superior to both netarsudil monotherapy and latanoprost monotherapy (p < 0.0001) [4]. These studies were the basis for US FDA approval as the first FDC with a prostaglandin analogue [4].

MERCURY-3 was a supplemental, non-inferior comparative study, aiming to compare netarsudil/latanoprost FDC with bimatoprost/timolol, a prostaglandin analogue/beta-blocker FDC used in routine clinical practice [14]. In that study, netarsudil/latanoprost was non-inferior to bimatoprost/timolol in lowering elevated IOP in a European study population [14]. The IOP-lowering efficacy of the netarsudil/latanoprost combination in the MERCURY studies, and of the netarsudil + latanoprost concomitant regimen seen in this phase III combination study, is likely partly due to the distinct mechanisms of action of the individual agents. Netarsudil lowers elevated IOP by targeting the TM dysfunction to increase AH outflow and by lowering EVP [8], whereas latanoprost primarily increases outflow via the uveoscleral pathway [8].

In this study, the MD IOP at week 4 with netarsudil + latanoprost in participants with inadequate IOP reduction from latanoprost monotherapy (15.3 mmHg) was comparable with those following netarsudil/latanoprost FDC at week 6 in MERCURY-1 (mean IOP, 15.6 mmHg) [12] and MERCURY-2 (mean IOP, 15.9 mmHg) [13]. Findings from the current study therefore support the suggested complementary dual mechanism of action of netarsudil and latanoprost observed in other patient populations [4]. The efficacy of netarsudil in Japanese patients was previously reported in the J-ROCKET study, where it demonstrated superiority in comparison with twice-daily ripasudil [3]. Netarsudil administration is required only once daily [7].

No SAEs were reported in participants receiving netarsudil + latanoprost in the current study and no new safety concerns were identified compared with previous findings. TEAEs were observed at a higher rate with netarsudil + latanoprost versus placebo + latanoprost. The most frequently reported TEAE in this study, conjunctival hyperaemia, was more common with netarsudil + latanoprost versus placebo + latanoprost. However, the reported frequency of conjunctival hyperaemia in this study was similar to among patients receiving netarsudil/latanoprost FDC in the pooled MERCURY-1 and -2 studies (53.3% vs. 58.7%, respectively) [4]. The mechanism of ocular hyperaemia can commonly be associated with the vasodilatory effects of both netarsudil and latanoprost [17]. Cornea verticillata was reported in 7.4% of participants receiving netarsudil + latanoprost after 4 weeks; the frequency of cornea verticillata among this patient population over a longer follow-up than 4 weeks is currently unknown. In the 6- and 12-month MERCURY-1 and -3 studies, 11.0% and 17.6% of participants receiving netarsudil/latanoprost FDC presented with cornea verticillata, respectively [12, 14]. Consistent with previous studies, most cases of cornea verticillata were mild in severity and self-resolving after treatment cessation [14]. Netarsudil is a cationic amphiphilic agent that was found to cause phospholipidosis in hamster ovary cells [14, 18]. From this evidence, it can be concluded that netarsudil-associated cornea verticillata occurs as a result of the accumulation of phospholipids in corneal epithelial cells.

A limitation of the study design is the relatively short duration of 4 weeks and, as such, the long-term efficacy and safety of netarsudil in Japanese patients cannot be concluded. However, a long-term 12-month study assessing the efficacy and safety of netarsudil + latanoprost in a Japanese population is currently being conducted (jRCT2031210125).

Conclusion

These findings demonstrate the IOP-lowering efficacy of netarsudil 0.02% concomitant with latanoprost 0.005% in Japanese patients with POAG or OHT, and consistent with the suggested complementary mechanism of action of netarsudil and latanoprost observed in other patient populations. No new safety signals with netarsudil and latanoprost were identified.

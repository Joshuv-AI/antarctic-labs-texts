Left Atrial Appendage Mimicking Coronary Aneurysm and Thrombus on Echocardiography in a Child with Kawasaki Disease
Authors: Anhar A. Baeshen, Riad Abouzahr, Jameel Alata
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT

In Kawasaki disease, this resemblance can cause diagnostic confusion with coronary artery aneurysm or thrombus.Clinical presentationA 7-year-old boy presented with fever lasting more than five days and cervical lymphadenopathy following a viral illness. 1. Introduction

The left atrial appendage also known as left auricle is derived from the left wall of the primary atrium, which forms during the fourth week of embryonic development. It has developmental ultrastructural and physiological characteristics distinct from the left atrium proper.

The left atrial appendage is a pouch like projection from the main body of the left atrium, which lies in the atrioventricular sulcus in close proximity to the left circumflex artery, the left phrenic nerve, and the left pulmonary veins.

1.1. Clinical summary

A previously healthy 7 year old boy with a history of viral infection presented with a fever lasting more than five days associated with lymphadenopathy. There was no history of rash, conjunctival injection, oral mucosal changes, swelling or desquamation of the hands or feet.

Kawasaki disease was suspected and an Echocardiogram was performed at the referring hospital the study suggested an abnormality of the left coronary artery (LCA) with thrombus formation. The patient was started on Aspirin once daily, Plavix once daily, and Apixaban once daily. Due to these findings he was referred to our hospital for pediatric cardiology evaluation.

The patient was asymptomatic with no tachycardia, increased work of breathing, chest pain, syncope, or cyanosis.

On cardiac examination no murmurs were detected, Peripheral pulses were synchronous in all extremities, and there was no hepatomegaly. The remainder of the physical examination was unremarkable.

Echocardiography at our center showed a mildly dilated right coronary artery (RCA) and an aneurysmal dilation of the LCA measuring approximately 8 mm (giant aneurysm) with a mobile thrombus in the proximal segment measuring about 7 × 5 mm.

The electrocardiogram demonstrated a normal sinus rhythm.

The patient underwent computed tomography angiography of the coronary arteries, This study demonstrated a normal origin, caliber and course of the coronary arteries.

The structure of concern on echocardiogram was found to be a chicken wing LA appendage type (Fig. 1).

2. Past medical history

The patient had no prior medical conditions, no history of congenital heart disease, and no previous hospitalizations.

3. Differential diagnosis

Coronary artery aneurysm related to kawasaki disease.

Coronary artery thrombosis.

Intracardiac mass.

Normal anatomic variant of the left atrial appendage.

4. Management

Following confirmation of normal coronary anatomy on computed tomography angiography, anticoagulation therapy was discontinued. The patient continued standard management for suspected incomplete Kawasaki disease with antiplatelet therapy and close clinical observation.

5. Outcome and follow up

The patient remained clinically stable with no cardiovascular symptoms. Follow-up echocardiography showed stable findings without evidence of coronary artery dilation or thrombosis. The patient continues to be monitored in the outpatient pediatric cardiology clinic.

6. Discussion

Coronary artery involvement is the most significant cardiovascular complication of Kawasaki disease and remains the primary focus of cardiac imaging in affected children. Transthoracic echocardiography is the first-line imaging modality for coronary artery assessment; however, its diagnostic accuracy may be limited by acoustic windows and the complex spatial relationships of adjacent cardiac structures.

The LAA is a vestige of the left atrium (LA) that develops during gestation. The LAA has been well described in the adult population, primarily in the setting of atrial fibrillation and thrombus formation [1].

The LAA is a narrow, finger-like muscular structure that arises from the LA and can have one to four lobes [2].

In most patients, it lies anteriorly and superiorly to the pulmonary artery and sits along the left atrioventricular groove, atop the proximal left circumflex artery [2,3].

The LAA is best visualized in the apical four-chamber views and the parasternal short-axis view when tipped inferiorly from the standard view.5,6 However, in some patients, as the one described in this report, the LAA may lie along the LCA and thus may be misinterpreted as a LCA aneurysm [4].

The left atrial appendage is a highly variable anatomical structure that can project anteriorly and lie in close proximity to the proximal left coronary artery. In certain morphologies, particularly the chicken wing type, the LAA may simulate coronary artery dilation or intracoronary thrombus on echocardiography, leading to potential misdiagnosis and unnecessary treatment.

This case highlights the importance of recognizing normal anatomic variants as potential diagnostic pitfalls in pediatric echocardiography. Multimodality imaging, particularly coronary computed tomography angiography, plays a crucial role in resolving diagnostic uncertainty, preventing inappropriate anticoagulation, and guiding optimal patient management.

Previous case reports have described the left atrial appendage mimicking coronary artery pathology on echocardiography. Das et al. reported an adult case in which a left atrial appendage was misinterpreted as a coronary artery aneurysm, later clarified by advanced imaging [1]. Similar diagnostic challenges have been described in both adult and pediatric populations, highlighting the limitations of transthoracic echocardiography when evaluating structures adjacent to the coronary arteries [2,3].

Compared with previously reported cases, our case is unique due to its occurrence in a child with suspected Kawasaki disease, where the clinical implications of misdiagnosis may lead to unnecessary anticoagulation or escalation of therapy.

7. Conclusion

The left atrial appendage can closely mimic pathological coronary findings on echocardiography in children with suspected Kawasaki disease. A thorough understanding of cardiac anatomy and the judicious use of multimodality imaging are essential to avoid misdiagnosis and unnecessary therapeutic interventions.

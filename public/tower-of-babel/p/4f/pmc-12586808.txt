Occult breast cancer with CSF TBA positivity and abnormal involuntary movements
Authors: Wenyan Zhang, Yajie Liu, Jin Zhen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractOccult breast cancer (OBC) is a rare and special type of breast cancer. The initial symptom is an enlarged axillary lymph node. Paraneoplastic neurological syndromes (PNS) are a group of syndromes that are caused by malignant tumors and damage to the nervous system in distant parts. Most patients have neurological symptoms before the primary tumors. Early diagnosis and treatment are beneficial to improve the therapeutic effect of patients. This case report describes a 54-year-old woman presenting with a one-year history of gradual-onset abnormal involuntary movements, psychiatric disorders, mental decline, and weight loss. Neurological evaluation showed that delirium, involuntary movements and muscle tension increased. Although extensive diagnostic tests were carried out, including routine and biochemical analyses of cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) and hematological examinations, no obvious abnormalities were detected except that the antinuclear antibody and tissue-based analysis (TBA) in serum and CSF were positive. Initial treatments, including glucocorticoid, antiviral therapy and immunoglobulin, were ineffective. Further systemic ultrasonography and positron emission tomography revealed enlarged hyper-metabolic lymph nodes in the right axillary and bilateral sub mental regions. Pathological examination confirmed adenocarcinoma infiltration, consistent with breast origin, leading to a diagnosis of OBC and PNS. Combination therapy was initiated with palbociclib (CDK4/6 inhibitor) plus letrozole (aromatase inhibitor), which led to the gradual improvement of involuntary movements, although cognitive impairment and indifference still existed. This case highlights the diagnostic challenges of OBC and PNS, emphasizing the importance of comprehensive diagnostic approaches and the potential for neurological symptoms to precede tumor detection. To our knowledge, this is a rare case of OBC with TBA positivity presenting with parkinsonism and cognitive disorder, contributing to the understanding of PNS complexity.

A 54-year-old woman presented with gradual-onset abnormal involuntary movements, psychiatric disorders, mental decline and weight loss over a 1-year period. Neurological evaluation revealed that delirium, involuntary movement and muscle tension increased. After anti-neurotransmitter and sedative treatment, the patient showed aggravated involuntary movements, apathy, less facial expressions, tremor of lower limbs and head, wide-based gait, small step length, bradykinesia, gait instability, less accompanying movements, decomposition of turning movements and postural instability and the Pull Test positive. Routine biochemical analysis of cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) samples showed that the protein increased slightly, which was 0.56 g/L. The hematological examination showed that the anti-nuclear antibody was positive. Serum and CSF samples were negative for antiganglioside antibodies, antineuronal antibodies (NMDA + GAB + VGKC + RI + YO + HU + AQP4 + NMO), paraneoplastic antibodies (CV2/CRMP5 + Ma1 + Ma2 + Amphiphysin + KLHL11 + PCA2 + SOX1 + Tr(DNER) + GABABR + LUZP4 + BRSK2 + CARP VIII + DACH1 + KCTD16 + PDE10A + mGluR2 + PKCγ + ROCK2 + Zic4), HSV, CMV-DNA, JCV-DNA, Japanese encephalitis virus B, metagenomic next-generation sequencing, Aerobic and anaerobic culture. Video electroencephalogram showed normal. However, serum and CSF samples were positive for tissue based assay༈TBA༉and oligoclonal bands were only found in CSF. Considering that there may be unknown antibody positive, TBA positive result cannot be completely explained by antinuclear antibody positive. Abnormal signals of bilateral anterior commissure, lenticular nucleus and caudate nucleus can be observed on magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) (Fig. 1). Based on the above clinical manifestations, the anatomical diagnosis involves the cerebral cortex and extrapyramidal system. A therapeutic regimen consisting of glucocorticoids, antiviral therapy, immunoglobulin, and vitamin supplementation was ineffective. Systemic ultrasonography and positron emission tomography (PET) revealed enlarged hypermetabolic lymph nodes in the right axillary and bilateral submental regions. The pathological findings were pT0 N3c cM0. The histopathological examination of the lymph node tissue revealed adenocarcinoma infiltration, and immunohistochemistry demonstrated the following profile: CK (+); EMA (+); GATA-3(+); Mammaglobin (+); ER (70–80%); PR (20–30%); AR (+); Ki-67 of 10%; and Her-2 (2+). The ultrasound examination of the breast showed no abnormalities. Based on the presence of metastatic adenocarcinoma in the lymph nodes with an immunohistochemical profile consistent with breast origin and the absence of a detectable primary breast tumor on imaging, the patient was diagnosed with occult breast cancer (OBC) and probable paraneoplastic neurologic syndrome [1]– [2]. Combination therapy was initiated with palbociclib (CDK4/6 inhibitor) plus letrozole (aromatase inhibitor). A follow-up brain MRI revealed no abnormalities (Fig. 1). After the above treatment, symptoms including involuntary movements, limb/head tremors, bradykinesia and gait instability gradually subsided. The patient recovered independent ambulation, bladder/bowel control, and capacity for self-care. However, persistent indifference and cognitive impairment remained, attributed to irreversible neuronal damage from the paraneoplastic neurological syndrome (PNS) secondary to occult breast cancer (OBC). Surveillance imaging at one year showed no evidence of tumor progression or metastasis.

Follow-up MRI (b) obtained one year later revealed complete resolution of these abnormalities

OBC is a rare subtype of breast cancer. The initial symptom is typically an enlarged axillary lymph node. To date, reported cases of OBC remain scarce, contributing to its diagnostic difficulty [3, 4]. Pathological examination after axillary lymph node puncture combined with immunohistochemistry and imaging examination can improve the preoperative diagnosis of the disease. Paraneoplastic neurological syndromes (PNS) are a group of disorders mediated by an immune response to a malignant tumor, resulting in neurological dysfunction in regions distant from the primary tumor site. In most patients, neurological symptoms precede the detection of the primary malignancy. Early diagnosis and treatment of PNS are crucial and can improve patient outcomes. Paraneoplastic neurological syndrome (PNS) is a rare complication of breast cancer, mediated by immune mechanisms rather than direct tumor invasion [5]. In 70–80% of cases, PNS symptoms precede the diagnosis of breast cancer by an average of 2–41 months. The primary tumor is often occult (with no palpable breast mass) and typically requires detection through axillary lymph node metastasis or imaging. Whole-body PET is valuable for identifying occult primary lesions or metastatic foci [6], while cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) antibody testing yields positive results in approximately 32% to 40% of cases [7]. Encephalomyelitis, limbic encephalitis and rapidly progressive cerebellar syndrome are classic paraneoplastic neurologic syndromes often associated with malignant tumor [2]. The patient in this report exhibited parkinsonism and was ineffective to dopaminergic medication and anti-spasticity medications. Finally, parkinsonism gradually improved after targeting OBC.

The clinical presentation in this case shares significant features with Morvan’s syndrome, a rare autoimmune disorder first described by Augustin Morvan in 1890. The patient’s initial neuropsychiatric symptoms of agitation progressing to apathy, followed by development of movement disorders including tremors and bradykinesia, along with cognitive decline, closely parallels the classic clinical triad of encephalopathy, peripheral nerve hyperexcitability, and dysautonomia that characterizes this condition [8, 9]. The temporal progression strongly suggests Morvan’s syndrome despite initially negative serological findings. Comprehensive neurophysiological evaluation with extended electromyogram monitoring may detect subtle neuromyotonic discharges to support the diagnosis. These observations are consistent with current diagnostic guidelines, which suggest that Morvan’s syndrome should be highly suspected in seronegative patients with characteristic symptoms [10].

To our knowledge, this represents a rare case of occult breast cancer (OBC) presenting with parkinsonism and cognitive impairment. Although specific neural antibodies were not detected in this case, we believe its presentation holds the potential to expand the current understanding of paraneoplastic neurological syndromes (PNS) and the complexities involved in their clinical diagnosis.

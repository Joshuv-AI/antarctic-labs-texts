Predicting the outcome of transarterial chemoembolization combined with targeted immunotherapy for unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma based on MRI radiomics
Authors: Jiaxuan Liu, Na Zhao, Yuxin Hang, Qiwen You, Yulong Tian, Haibo Shao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo establish and validate a multi-sequence magnetic resonance (MR) imaging-based radiomics model for predicting the objective response rate (ORR) of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) treated with transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) in combination with TKI and PD-1 inhibitors, aiming to maximize the efficacy of combination therapy. A total of 151 patients with unresectable HCC who received TACE combined with TKI and PD-1 inhibitors were included from two institutions between January 2019 and November 2022. Of these, 119 patients from Center 1 were randomized into the training group (n = 71) and the test group (n = 48). The 32 patients from Center 2 were used as an external validation set. The region-of-interest (ROI) and feature extraction from preoperative T2-weighted imaging (T2WI), arterial phase, and venous portal phase enhanced MRI images were manually extracted using 3D-slicer software. The most relevant radiomic features were selected using the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression. These features were then integrated with predictive clinical features into a logistic regression (LR) model to build a combined model for predicting tumor response. The predictive performance of each model was evaluated using the area under the curve (AUC) of the receiver operating characteristic (ROC). Model performance was further assessed using the calibration curve and the decision curve. Additionally, we also analyzed the relationship between LR-predicted objective response rate (preORR) and progression-free survival (PFS), as well as overall survival (OS) in patients with intermediate and advanced HCC. Univariate and multivariate survival analyses of PFS and OS were conducted to identify independent predictive factors in patients with unresectable HCC. The BCLC stage was identified as an independent factor affecting the ORR in the clinical model (P < 0.05). The AUC values based on the combined model in the training, test, and validation groups was 0.893(95%CI:0.813–0.973),0.862(95%CI: 0.755–0.965), and 0.804(95%CI: 0.614–0.971), respectively. Calibration curves and decision curves demonstrate the good calibration performance and clinical utility of the combined model. In the survival analysis, PFS and OS were significantly different between LR-predicted ORR + and LR-predicted ORR- (P < 0.05). Multivariate survival analysis showed that BCLC stage, tumor size, and radscore were independent predictors of PFS, while BCLC stage and Child-Pugh grade were independent predictors of OS. The combined model based on MRI radiomics can effectively predict the ORR in unresectable HCC with combination therapy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-18586-7.Keywords: Intermediate and advanced HCC, MRI radiomics, TACE, ORR, Targeted therapy, ImmunotherapySubject terms: Cancer, Oncology

Introduction

According to the global Cancer Statistics of 2022, hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is the most common malignant tumor of the liver and the third leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide, characterized by a high rate of recurrence and poor prognosis, which has long been a significant global clinical challenge1. The early onset of HCC is relatively insidious, and most patients are already in the intermediate and advanced stages when the lesions are found2. Patients with advanced HCC (BCLC C stage) have a poor overall survival (OS) of 6–8 months. Importantly, portal vein tumor thrombosis (PVTT) commonly occurs in patients with advanced HCC, leading to progression of aggressive disease, impaired liver function, increased recurrence rate, and reduced median survival time (2–4 months)3. Transcatheter arterial chemoembolization (TACE) is the primary recommended treatment for unresectable intermediate HCC4. Targeted drugs based on signal molecular have been successfully supported in clinical trials, and molecular targeted therapy has become a significant milestone in the management of HCC patients. Sorafenib, an antiangiogenic and anti-proliferative agent, has revolutionized the management of HCC, ushering in the era of targeted systemic therapy. Sorafenib monotherapy prolongs the OS and delays the progression in patients with advanced HCC, with good tolerability. A randomized, multicenter, open-label Phase 3 trial showed a median OS of 14.7 months with sorafenib in advanced HCC5. In contrast, lenvatinib was not inferior to sorafenib in OS and even showed superiority in all secondary efficacy endpoints6–8. The development of immunotherapy has also significantly changed the therapeutic landscape of HCC, with targeting immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) therapy against programmed cell death-1 (anti-PD-1) or its ligand (anti-PD-L1) and cytotoxic T lymphocyte-associated protein 4 (CTLA-4) has become the primary treatment for advanced HCC, offering additional options for patients9,10. The Barcelona clinical liver cancer (BCLC) staging criteria recommend systemic therapy for BCLC C stage patients with macrovessel invasion, while some countries prefer more aggressive treatment, such as the combination of TACE and systemic therapy11,12. Moreover, progress in TACE technology has plateaued, and its therapeutic effect on HCC has reached a bottleneck. Consequently, the Chinese Guidelines for diagnosis and treatment of HCC have formally proposed the strategy of “TACE-based comprehensive therapy”, emphasizing that TACE should be combined with other local therapies and /or systemic anti-tumor therapy to enhance the efficacy of TACE. Relevant studies have demonstrated that the efficacy of TACE combined with molecular targeting drugs is superior to that of single molecular targeted drugs, and the combination of TACE and immunotherapy can prolong the survival of patients with intermediate and advanced HCC, yielding better results than TACE alone13. A phase III randomized clinical trial of lenvatinib with TACE as first-line treatment for advanced HCC14 showed that lenvatinib with TACE had better objective response rates (ORR), progression-free survival (PFS), and median OS (P < 0.001). Cai M et al.15 found that TACE + lenvatinib + PD1 had better ORR, PFS, and OS than TACE + lenvatinib. Although there are many treatment options, the efficacy of combination therapy is not high in patients with advanced HCC due to drug resistance and adverse effects, and the prognosis of advanced HCC is still very poor16–18. Therefore, it is crucial to select effective treatment options while minimizing side effects. Predicting treatment outcomes before initiating therapy could greatly assist in clinical decision-making.

HCC exhibits significant intertumor heterogeneity and different biological characteristics that are closely related to prognosis and require different treatment options at each stage. Consequently, determining the best treatment method before treatment can present a challenge for clinicians. Radiomics, which extracts visually imperceptible features from medical imaging, utilizes selected radiomics features and combines a range of computer technologies, including machine learning (ML) algorithms, to analyze data and achieve personalized prediction of patients19–21. Therefore, we aimed to apply the preoperative MRI radiomic features from patients with intermediate - advanced HCC to predict the efficacy of patient TACE combined with TKI and PD-1 inhibitors, thereby identifying a patient population that may benefit from combination therapy.

Materials and methods

Patient collection

This study collected data from two central cases:1Center 1 retrospectively collected MR-enhanced images, clinical baseline data, and follow-up data of patients receiving TACE combined with TKI and PD-1 inhibitors at the First Hospital of China Medical University from January 2019 to November 20222. Center 2 retrospectively collected MR-enhanced images, clinical baseline data, and follow-up data of patients receiving TACE combined with TKI and PD-1 inhibitors at Shengjing Hospital of China Medical University from May 2019 to November 2022. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients, and the study was approved by the ethics committees of the First Hospital of China Medical University and Shengjing Hospital of China Medical University (ethics number: AF-SOP-07-1.2-01). The patient population has not been previously reported. All the analyses were conducted in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations.

Inclusion criteria for cases: (1) Patients with a pathological or clinical diagnosis of intermediate or advanced HCC (BCLC: stage B and C). (2) Receiving TACE combined with TKI and PD-1 inhibitors; TKI drugs receiving sorafenib and lenvatinib at 400 mg/day and 12 mg/day; immunoagents receiving intravenous sintilimab and camrelizumab at 200 mg/dose once every 3 weeks (3) The initial treatment is TACE, with no prior history of surgery, ablation, targeted therapy, or immunotherapy; (4) The time interval between preoperative MRI imaging examination and TACE treatment should not exceed 2 weeks 4. Complete clinical, radiographic, review, and follow-up data.

Exclusion criteria for cases: (1) Patients with incomplete clinical information or lost to follow-up; (2) Lack of MRI imaging before TACE surgery; (3) Presence of other primary tumors in addition to HCC.

Patients from Center 1 who met the inclusion and exclusion criteria were randomized in a 6:4 ratio to the training and test groups. The patients from Center 2 were designated as the external validation group. The patient recruitment diagram is shown in Supplementary Fig. 1.

MR acquisition parameters

Using a 3.0T MRI (MAGNETOM Verio, Siemens Healthcare, Erlangen, Germany; Ingenia, Philips, Amsterdam, Netherlands; Siemens Healthcare, Erlangen, Germany), all patients underwent Gd-EOB-DTPA-enhanced MRI scans and were fasted for 6 h before MR, ranging from the top of the diaphragm to 2 cm below the liver tissue. The MRI sequence includes the following contents: T2-weighted imaging and dynamic T1-weighted enhancement. The MR acquisition parameters are detailed in Supplementary Table 1.

TACE procedure

Two interventional radiologists with over five years of experience in TACE from each institution carried out all TACE procedures. The common femoral artery was punctured with a Seldinger. The origin of the hepatic artery and the branch of the tumor-feeding artery were shown by angiography of the common hepatic artery and the superior mesenteric artery using a 5 F-RH catheter (Terumo). A microguide wire directs the microcatheter (APT, 2.4–130 cm) to superselect the tumor-feeding artery. Lipiodol and chemotherapeutic medications were then administered to the patient. Complete stoppage of blood flow from the tumor-feeding artery and the absence of tumor contrast staining on angiography following final embolization were considered the endpoints of the TACE procedure.

Patient review and follow-up

Within 2 weeks after TACE, patients received regular intravenous PD-1 inhibitors and oral sorafenib and lenvatinib. The sorafenib dose was administered at 0.4 g twice daily. For patients weighing more than 60 kg, lenvatinib was administered at 12 mg once daily; for those weighing less, the dose was 8 mg daily. The PD-1 inhibitors, camrelizumab and sintilimab, were administered intravenously at 200 mg per dose every 3 weeks. Treatment was discontinued if patients experienced disease progression or intolerable adverse effects. Drug doses were reduced or interrupted in patients experiencing grade 3 or higher treatment-related adverse events.

Two radiologists, each with over 10 years of experience in imaging diagnosis, were blinded to the clinical case data of all patients. Compared to the RECIST 1.1 and mRECIST methods, the RECIST 1.1 standard primarily focuses on tumor size, whereas the mRECIST standard emphasizes tumor activity. Since patients often exhibit stable tumor size and central necrosis post-TACE, the application of mRECIST standard is more appropriate for this study. According to the mRECIST criteria22, complete response (CR) defined as the disappearance to all target lesions during the hepatic artery phase, and the short diameter of all the original pathological lymph nodes (including target and non-target lesions) must be less than 10 mm; partial response (PR) is defined as a 30% decrease in the maximum diameter of the target lesion; disease stability (SD) means the change of disease between PR and PD; while progressive disease (PD) is characterized by 20% increase in the maximum diameter of the target lesion and the absolute increasing in the total diameter of more than 5 mm, or the appearance of new lesions.

The primary outcome in this study was objective response rate (ORR), which includes complete and partial response (CR and PR), with a tumor reduction of more than 30% sustained for more than 4 weeks. To assess the HCC response to combination therapy, all patients were assigned into two groups: those with objective response (ORR+) (CR and PR patients) and those without objective response (ORR-) (SD and PD patients), with the ORR assessed at 12 weeks23. PFS is defined as the time from the initiation of treatment to the occurrence of disease progression (PD) or death. If no disease progression or recurrence occurs by the follow-up date, the data were censored based on the last follow-up date. OS was defined as the time from the start of TACE combined with targeted immunotherapy to death or the last follow-up.

Feature extraction and feature selection

The collected T2WI, arterial phase (AP) and venous phase (VP) images were imported into the open-source software 3D-Slicer software (www.slicer.org, version 4.13.2) for tumor segmentation and radiomics feature extraction. T2WI, AP, VP images were resampled to a uniform voxel of 1*1*1 mm size by a linear interpolation algorithm, while images were intensity normalized.Two radiologists (more than 10 years of experience in imaging diagnosis), blinded to clinical case data from all patients, used the 3D-Slicer software to delineate the tumor region of interest (ROI). First, radiologist 1 and radiologist 2 manually delineated the primary tumor region of ROI in the T2WI, AP and VP images; one week later, radiologist 1 re-delineated the ROI of the primary tumor to obtain the intraclass correlation coefficient of the ROI twice.The ROI includes the tumor and its areas of necrosis and bleeding. Lesions located at the margin were distinguished from other organs, and for multiple HCC lesions, the ROI was placed on the largest lesion. Finally, three ROI were generated per patient, namely T2WI, AP, and VP ROI.Then, In the 3D-Slicer software, Select “Radiomics” in the “Informatics” plate to extract the feature, Features include First Order features (n = 486), Shape (n = 42), Gray Level Dependence Matrix(GLDM, n = 378), Gray Level Co-occurrence Matrix(GLCM, n = 648), Gray Level Size Zone matrix(GLSZM, n = 432),Gray Level Run Length Matrix(GLRLM, n = 432),Neighborhood Gray Tone Difference matrix (NGTDM, n = 135), And 851 features were extracted per VOI, Three sequences, In total, 851 * 3, 2,553 features.Work flow chart is shown in Fig. 2.

The workflow of this study, including ROI segmentation, extraction of radiomics features, feature selection, model construction and validation.

We initially screened the radiomics features using the intra-class and inter-class correlation coefficient and retained only features with a correlation coefficient greater than 0.75. 30 MR images were randomly selected and masked by two radiologists. A week later, the radiologist1 repeated the same procedure.An ICC greater than 0.75 was considered as a mark of satisfactory inter- and intra-reader reproducibility.Then, the remaining ROI masking was performed by reader 1. We then used LASSO regression combined with five-fold cross-validation to select the most predictive radiomics features. LASSO regression uses the penalty coefficient lambda to improve the performance of the model, which can significantly reduce some coefficients or even reduce its absolute value to zero, which makes it extremely useful in feature screening and parameter dimension reduction.

Model construction and evaluation

We used the selected radiomics features to construct the radiomics score, namely the radiomics model. To improve the performance of radiomics model, clinically independent risk factors were combined with radiomics model to construct a combined model. Univariate and multivariate analyses were performed for clinical indicators to identify clinical indicators that were significantly associated with the ORR in patients with TACE plus targeted immunotherapy. Then, a combined model nomogram was established by combining clinical indicators and radscore. ROC curves were drawn, and AUC, accuracy, sensitivity and specificity were used to evaluate the predictive ability and discrimination performance of the clinical model, radiomics model and combined model nomogram. Comparisons between the ROC curves were performed using the DeLong’s test. The agreement between the actual and predicted values was assessed using the calibration curve, and the Hosmer-Lemeshow tests for the fit of the combined nomogram. The decision curve (DCA) was used to assess the net clinical benefit of the different models at different thresholds.

Statistical analysis

For continuous clinical variables, if meeting normal distribution, independent sample t-test was used to compare differences between ORR + and ORR-, otherwise, Mann-Whitney U test was used to compare differences between the two groups. Categorical variables were assessed using the chi-square test or the Fisher’s exact test. The performance of each prediction model was evaluated by receiver operating characteristic curve (ROC) analysis. We compared the prediction performance of the different models using the Delong’s test. The calibration curve and Hosmer-Lemeshow test were used to evaluate the degree of deviation from the actual results. Decision curve analysis (DCA) was used to verify the clinical benefit of the fusion model. P < 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant.We used the R language (https://www.r-project.org, version: 4.3.2) and SPSS26.0 for the statistical analysis.

Result

Clinical baseline characteristics

A total of 119 patients (mean age: 65.94 ± 10.76 years, 95 males and 24 females) were enrolled in Center 1, while 32 patients (mean age: 61.44 ± 8.904 years, 24 males and 8 females) were enrolled in Center 2. The basic clinical characteristics and laboratory indicators of the patients are summarized in Table 1. In the training and test groups, univariate revealed showed that BCLC stage was statistically significant in both ORR- and ORR + groups (P < 0.05). Furthermore, BCLC stage was identified as an independent predictor of ORR in multivariate analysis. ALB levels were statistically significant between the training group ORR- and ORR + groups (P = 0.022), but there no significant difference was found in the test group (P = 0.653). In both the ORR- and ORR + groups, factors such as age, gender, HBV, Child-Pugh grade, AFP level, TBIL, ALT, AST, PLT, TKI types, and tumor number showed no statistical differences in both the training set and test set (P > 0.05). We constructed a clinical model based on BCLC stage. The AUC value of the clinical model in the training group was 0.659 (95%CI:0.545–0.773), with sensitivity and specificity of 0.704 and 0.614, respectively. In the test group, the AUC value was 0.723 (95%CI:0.607–0.839), with sensitivity and specificity of 0.895 and 0.552, respectively. The AUC value in the validation group was 0.590 (95%CI:0.415–0.766), with sensitivity and specificity were 0.647 and 0.533, respectively.

Development and evaluation of the radiomics model

We extracted a total of 2,553 radiomics features from the three regions of interest (ROI) of each patient. Initially, 504 features were eliminated based on intra-class and inter-class correlation coefficient screening, leaving 2,049 features. Then, the λ value corresponding to the minimum error was selected as the parameter using LASSO regression (Supplementary Fig. 2), and 1 T2WI, 4 arterial phase (AP) and 1 venous portal phase (VP) features were selected, resulting in a total of 6 radiomics features. The radiomics features and coefficients are shown in Supplementary Table 2.

The radiomics model was constructed using these six selected radiomics features, and the radiomics score (rad-score) was calculated based on the regression coefficients. We constructed the radiomics model according based on the rad-score, The AUC value of the radiomics model in the training group was 0.881 (95%CI:0.798–0.965), with sensitivity and specificity values of 0.889 and 0.818, respectively. The AUC value in the test group was 0.848 (95%CI:0.731–0.964), sensitivity and specificity were 0.947 and 0.724, respectively. The AUC value in the external validation group was 0.804 (95%CI:0.636–0.972 ), with Sensitivity and specificity were 0.941 and 0.667, respectively.

Assessment and risk stratification of PFS and OS

By September 2024, a total of 151 patients receiving TACE combined with TKI and PD-1 inhibitors were assessed. Of these, 93.3% (141/ 151) experienced disease progression, with a median PFS of 9 months [quartile range (QR): 5–16 months)] and a range PFS of 1–29 month. Additionally, 37.1% (56/ 151) of patients had died, with a median OS of 27 months (QR: 24–29 months) and a range OS of 10–40 month. Discussion

TACE is one of the primary treatments for unresectable intermediate and advanced HCC; however, not all patients can benefit from it24. TACE combined with targeted immunotherapy represents the main treatment option for advanced, incurable HCC25. However, HCC is characterized by significant inter-tumor heterogeneity, requiring different treatment options at each stage. This variability makes personalized treatment and prognosis analysis challenging for clinicians. Therefore, accurately assessing tumor response before TACE is crucial for determining the course of treatment for HCC. In this study, we constructed a radiomics model based on T2WI, AP and VP sequences of contrast-enhanced MRI (CE-MR) to predict the tumor response to TACE combined with TKI and PD-1 inhibitors in HCC patients. A combined nomogram integrating clinical predictors and radiomics scores was developed and validated and demonstrated satisfactory performance, calibration, and clinical utility, which may assist in personalized treatment decisions in patients with HCC.

In this study, first-order features, shape features and higher-order features (including glcm, gldm, ngtdm, glszm and glrlm) were extracted from three ROI for radiomics analysis. Six radiomics features, including three first-order and three higher-order features were selected. These features indicate that tumor intensity and intratumoral texture differences are significantly associated with ORR following combined therapy. The first-order features describe the distribution of voxel intensity within the image region, revealing connections connection between the maximum and median of tumor texture in the first-order features and tumor objective remission. Busyness represents the rapid changes in intensity between pixels and their neighbors, and Size Zone Non-Uniformity reflects the variability in the volume of the measured size regions, with smaller values indicating greater homogeneity. Long Run Low Gray Level Emphasis represents the joint distribution of long-range run lengths with lower gray values. According to the correlation coefficient, the higher Busyness and Size Zone Non-Uniformity faster changes in heterogeneit between regional volume, lower homogeneity, and a higher the rad-score, suggesting a greater likelihood of ORR+. Conversely, Long Run Low Gray Level Emphasis suggests a more concentrated distribution of long-range run lengths with low gray values, indicating a lower likelihood of ORR+. High-order features such as glcm, gldm, ngtdm, glszm, and glrlm reflect the tumor composition and structural heterogeneity, further supporting the role of texture analysis in assessing ORR in intermediate and advanced HCC. In this study, the AUC value in the training group was 0.881(95%CI:0.798–0.965), in the test group was 0.848 (95%CI:0.731–0.964), and in the external validation group was 0.804 (95%CI:0.636–0.972), demonstrating the strong predictive efficacy of the radiomics model in predicting tumor response to TACE combined with TKI and PD-1 inhibitors. Several studies have shown that radiomics features, such as texture analysis contribute to the efficacy evaluation and prognosis in patients with unresectable HCC undergoing TACE26,27.

Multivariate analysis revealed that BCLC stage (HR = 0.232, 95%CI: 0.059–0.908, P = 0.036) and rad-score (HR = 2.815, 95%CI: 1.575–5.032, P = 0.001) were independent risk factors for ORR in intermediate and advanced HCC. The presence of large vascular invasion in BCLC-C is associated with poor prognosis, easy recurrence and shorter survival28. This finding is consistent with other studies, such as those by Chen M et al.29, Kong C et al.27 who also identified that BCLC stage as an independent clinical risk factor for tumor response after TACE. Kong C et al.27 constructed a fusion model based on radiomics score, AFP, Child-Pugh grade and BCLC stage to predict tumor response after TACE. The AUC in the training and validation groups was 0.861 and 0.884, respectively, which is similar our findings. Liu QP et al.23 developed a combined model based on MR radiomics score, BCLC stage, and albumin bilirubin grade predicted an AUCs of 0.813 and 0.781 in the training set and test sets, respectively. Our combined model, incorporating BCLC stage and radiomics score, achieved higher AUC values in the training group was 0.893(95%CI:0.813–0.973), the test group was 0.862 (95%CI:0.755–0.965), and external validation group was 0.804 (95%CI:0.644–0.974), surpassing many recent studies (AUC range: 0.781–0.813)23,30. Although the AUC value of the combined model showed no significant improvement compared to the radiomics model in our study, it better aligns with clinicians’ decision-making processes by integrating clinical variables (BCLC staging). It also follows the concept of “clinical radiomics fusion”, which not only enhances the interpretability of the model, but also provides a comprehensive risk assessment and supports personalized treatment planning31,32. Furthermore, elevant literature also suggests that the combined models demonstrate enhanced robustness across different medical centers and equipment configurations, while being capable of incorporating novel biomarkers (such as pathomics) in subsequent analyses33.

Additionally, we found that predicted ORR+ of the combined model correlated with patients’ PFS and OS. Patients with Predicted ORR+ had longer PFS and OS, while those with predicted ORR- had shorter PFS and OS. Based on multi-sequence radiomics features and clinical indicators, this study provides a convenient, feasible and non-invasive tool for individualized survival prediction of HCC patients receiving TACE combined with TKI and PD-1 inhibitors, enabling the identification patients likely to benefit from the combination therapy.

The Child-Pugh grade and BCLC staging system have been endorsed by several guidelines for prognosis and treatment stratification of HCC patients34,35. Tumor size has also been shown in many studies to be associated with the prognosis and survival of intermediate and advanced HCC36. Kong C et al.27 found that Child-Pugh grade (HR = 2.846, 95% CI: 1.054–7.680; P = 0.039) and BCLC stage (HR = 2.904, 95% CI: 1.136–7.426; P = 0.036) were associated with tumour response after TACE. Liu K et al.36 found that tumor size and BCLC stage were independent risk factors for PFS after TACE for unresectable HCC. These studies align with our findings. Our study demonstrated that BCLC stage, tumor size, and rad-score were independent predictors of PFS, while BCLC stage and Child Pugh grade were independent predictors of OS. Subgroup analysis indicated that patients with BCLC-B, small tumor size, and rad-score-low risk had longer PFS, while those with BCLC-C, large tumor size, and rad-score-high risk had shorter PFS (P < 0.05). Patients with BCLC-B and Child Pugh A, they had longer OS, while patients with BCLC-C and Child Pugh B had shorter OS (P < 0.05). PFS and OS, key indicator in evaluating treatment efficacy and predicting prognosis, play crucial role in clinical practice. This study employed univariate and multivariate analyses to identify independent predictors of PFS and OS in intermediate and advanced HCC patients, which can aid clinicians in selecting patients who will benefit from TACE combined with TKI and PD-1 inhibitors.

However, this study has some limitations. First, the patient sample size was limited to two centers, and larger samples sizes are needed to verify the predictive efficacy and fit of the model in the future studies. Second, this is retrospective study, and patients selection bias may be present. To minimize selection error, patients were divided into training, test and external validation sets, and further multi-center prospective studies are needed. Third, tumor segmentation was manually delineated by the radiologists, time-consuming and laborious, with potential individualized differences. Finally, this study primarily used pure radiomics to predict the efficacy of TACE combined TKI and PD-1 inhibitors, which may provide limited information. In recent years, advancements in pathomics and genomics have been led to more comprehensive and accurate reflections of tumor heterogeneity, potentially improving the prediction of treatment benefits in TACE combined systemic therapy.

In conclusion, this study developed a combined radiomics model that effectively predicts objective response in patients with unresectable intermediate and advanced HCC, aiding in the selection of a patient population likely to benefit from TACE combined with TKI and PD-1 inhibitors.

Characteristics of debris flow development in Daxilada watershed and its hazard analysis on Lexi Expressway
Authors: Na He, Yuyang Li, Linjuan Xu, Zhiquan Yang, Filip Gurkalo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe occurrence of frequent debris flow catastrophes in the mountainous regions of southwest China has necessitated the inclusion of debris flow disaster analysis and prevention as an essential component in the planning and construction of mountainous roadways. Daxilada watershed is located in the south of Mabian Yi Zuzizhixian, Leshan City, Sichuan Province, and the proposed Leshan-Xichang Expressway (Lexi Expressway) will pass through the upper reaches of Daxilada watershed. It is essential to consider that the presence of debris flows within the Daxilada watershed could have detrimental effects on the construction and functionality of the proposed Luoshanxi Bridge. This study examined the Daxilada watershed as a case study and analyzed the factors contributing to debris flow formation in the area. This analysis was based on field investigations, remote sensing interpretation, and experimental analysis. Additionally, the study utilized the Massflow software to simulate debris flow movement. It integrated the simulation results to determine the potential hazards the Daxilada Gully debris flow posed to the line project. This study found that Daxilada Gully meets debris flow formation conditions. The simulation results demonstrated that during the debris flow activity, there would be a maximum deposition depth of 2.1 m in the proposed Engineering Agency, which may lead to siltation and blocking disaster of the line project. Concerning the parameters related to the debris flow with a frequency of one in a hundred years, in conjunction with the outcomes obtained from numerical simulation, it would provide the design basis of the cross-flow cross-section of the proposed bridge. In a quantitative analysis of the blockage situation in the gully, debris flow deposits have the potential to cause damage to the line project. Debris flow deposits block the gully, but the risk of blockage is small. The study results have specific reference values for the debris flow prevention and control project of Lexi Expressway and offer valuable insights for the prevention and mitigation of similar disasters in relative projects.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-73305-y.Keywords: Debris flow, Lexi Highway, Daxilada Gully, Formation conditions, Numerical simulation, Engineering hazardsSubject terms: Ecology, Environmental social sciences, Natural hazards

Introduction

The southwestern part of Sichuan Province is mainly a mountainous area with complex topographic conditions, active geological structure, abundant rainfall, and wide distribution of geologic hazards. Consequently, this area has been recognized as a high-risk zone for the outbreak of debris flow disasters. Frequent debris flow disasters have significantly harmed the construction and safe operation of mountainous highways, seriously restricting the construction and development of mountainous areas in China. For example, in 2019, Ganluo County, located in Sichuan province, experienced persistent heavy precipitation, resulting in flash floods and debris flow calamities. These events caused significant structural damage to numerous residential buildings, impaired road infrastructure, and destroyed bridges. The overall economic impact incurred by the county amounted to approximately 1.5 billion yuan1. On August 20, 2019, a debris flow disaster occurred in Min Gully in Yinxing Township, Wenchuan County, Sichuan Province. The debris flow resulted in significant damage to the drainage gullies situated at the mouth of the gully, as well as the road bridges. Additionally, during the peak of the flood, the debris flow temporarily obstructed the Minjiang River, leading to the Taipingyi Hydropower Station’s submersion, which was approximately 200 m upstream. As a consequence, two workers were reported missing2. On June 17, 2020, a debris flow disaster in Danba County, Sichuan Province, was attributed to intense and brief heavy rainfall. As a result of these conditions, debris flows ensued, obstructing the primary channel of the Xiaojinchuan River and the subsequent building of a debris flow weir, along with the creation of debris flow dams. The destructive consequences of dam breaches resulting from severe flooding led to extensive devastation in numerous villages, towns, and the downstream section of the G350 national highway3. On June 26, 2020, significant flash floods and debris flows occurred in the villages of Damawu and Dabaozi, located in Yihai Town, Mianning County, Liangshan Prefecture, Sichuan Province. These natural disasters had a substantial impact, affecting nearly 10,000 individuals. Among them, 16 fatalities were reported, while six individuals remain missing. The floods and debris flows caused severe damage to embankments, roads, and power lines, spanning over 50 km. The direct economic losses resulting from these damages amounted to approximately 740 million yuan4.

Recently, a significant amount of scholarly research has been undertaken domestically and internationally on highway debris flows. As a case study, Wei Xingyu utilized the Gancao gully along the Lu-Shi highway, evaluating the Gancao gully’s hazard through field investigations and laboratory tests. The evaluation was based on analyzing the formation conditions of the debris flow in this area5. Fang Liuyang employed the Flow-2D program to conduct a simulation and analysis of the dynamic evolution of the Haibaluo debris flow on the Xiangli Motorway. Subsequently, the hazardous region of the Haibaluo debris flow was categorized based on the outcomes of the simulation. Furthermore, an assessment was conducted to evaluate the impact of the debris flow on the roadway6. Hu Guisheng proposed a low-frequency debris flow identification system for transportation arteries in western Sichuan by identifying and calculating the stability of the hollow region and analyzing the rounding degree of stones in the accumulation fan7. The risk of rockfalls and debris flows along the Besham to Chilas portion of the Karakoram Highway was evaluated by Sajid Ali, who subsequently categorized them into four distinct risk zones8. Wang Yuechen proposed the preparation method based on scenario-objective-capability because of the lack of theoretical methods for the preparation of highway disaster emergency plans in China9. Song Yabing examined the avalanche-slip-type gully debris flow in Langga Village along the Sichuan-Tibet Highway. The research employed a combination of field investigations, remote sensing interpretation, indoor tests, and model calculations to analyze the movement characteristics and formation evolution processes of debris flows. Based on the findings, a prevention and control mode for this particular type of debris flow was proposed10. Ahmed M. Youssef conducted a comprehensive analysis of the debris flows occurring along the Al-Hada Highway in the western region of the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia. This study aimed to ascertain the volume of these debris flows and evaluate their influence on the road infrastructure. Additionally, Youssef proposed suitable measures and solutions for these vulnerable areas to mitigate and prevent potential debris flow risks in the future11. Although significant progress has been made by domestic and international experts in the field of research related to highway debris flows, with the construction of mountainous highways, the incidents of highway debris flows causing hazards are still occurring and even intensifying. Therefore, the research on highway debris flow still needs to be continued. Based on previous research, Daxilada Gully in Mabian Yi autonomous county, Sichuan province, was chosen as a case study. It aimed to analyze debris flow hazards on the proposed Luoshanxi Bridge of the Lexi Expressway. This analysis was conducted through numerical simulation, considering the conditions contributing to debris flow formation. It is imperative to establish a comprehensive reference framework to facilitate the design of a debris flow prevention and control project for Lexi Motorway and offer guidance for disaster prevention and mitigation in similar projects.

Overview of the study area

Topography and geomorphology

The Daxilada watershed is situated in the southern region of Mabian Yi autonomous county, within Leshan city, located in the province of Sichuan, China (Fig. 1). A leaf-shaped structure characterizes the topology of the gully within this watershed. The topography of the region exhibits a pronounced elevation gradient, with the eastern portion characterized by higher terrain and the western portion characterized by lower terrain. The maximum elevation in the area reaches 1893 m, while the minimum elevation is 861 m, resulting in a substantial relative height difference of 1030 m. The watershed encompasses an area of 14.08 km2. The primary gully within the watershed spans 7.09 km, with an average longitudinal slope of 415‰, and the longitudinal slope of the main gully has a longitudinal slope of 112‰. Daxi Lada gully basin’s slope distribution ranges between 0° and 60°, of which 35°–60° slope range section is mainly distributed with the south side of the basin and the main gully near to its mouth. Because of the intermittent uplift of the new tectonic movement in the watershed and channel erosion cutting, channel erosion downcutting is serious, mainly for the “V” shaped valley, with a general width of 2–10 m. The basin has a high degree of vegetation coverage, up to more than 75%, and the vegetation type of trees, shrubs, and half of each. The basin contains many branch gullies, precisely 17 on both the right and left sides of the main gully. Due to the rugged topography, the study area exhibits a high occurrence of avalanches and landslides. This results in an abundant supply of loose solid material, contributing to debris flow formation. Additionally, the gully in the region has a significant longitudinal slope, facilitating the accumulation of water and material sources. Meteorological hydrology

The research site is situated inside a region characterized by a subtropical humid monsoon climate. This area is affected by both monsoonal patterns and mountainous topography, resulting in ample precipitation, clearly defined seasons, and a notable three-dimensional climate. Based on the meteorological station data, the average annual rainfall in Mabian Country is 976.0 mm, of which May to September is the rainy season every year, with the rainfall amounting to 795.00 mm, accounting for 81% of the average annual rainfall, which is the period of high incidence of geologic hazards, such as landslides and mudslides. The maximum annual rainfall was 1290.5 mm, the maximum daily rainfall was 205.2 mm (July 20, 1960), the maximum three-day rainfall was 225.0 mm (July 19–21, 1960), the maximum 1-hour rainfall amounted to 67.6 mm (1972 and 1982), and the maximum 10-minute rainfall amounted to 25.0 mm (1972 and 1982).

Formation conditions of the debris flow in Daxilada watershed

Seismic acceleration of source accumulation in the study area

The study area is situated in the eastern periphery of the south-central segment of the north-south seismic belt, which experiences significant seismic activity in China. Specifically, it is a region inside Sichuan characterized by a high occurrence of medium-to-strong earthquakes. The area experienced a peak acceleration of ground shaking at a magnitude of 0.15 g, and the ground-shaking response spectrum had a distinctive period of 0.40 s, corresponding to the Modified Mercalli Intensity value of VIII.

In order to evaluate the potential influence of earthquakes on the study area, seismic data near the study area from 2004 to 2023 through the National Earthquake Science Data Center (NESDC) was collected. Based on this data, the relationship equation13 between the radius of susceptibility R and the magnitude of the earthquake M was used, and distance D between the study area and the array was calculated. If R-D ≥ 0, the seismic event was selected as the one with an influential earthquake, but if R-D ≤ 0, this seismic effect was removed.

These materials are predominantly concentrated along the sides of the main and branch gully channels. Conversely, there is a limited presence of avalanche and slide material sources in the upper and middle regions of the watershed (Fig. 4a).

Rainfall meets the critical rainfall for debris flow initiation

The studied area exhibits significant longitudinal slopes in its gullies and valleys, creating favorable conditions for rainwater accumulation and the transportation of loose sediments. Additionally, the watershed contains numerous well-distributed sources, further enhancing the potential for debris flow commencement. Debris flows are highly probable to transpire when the precipitation in the designated research region reaches critical rainfall26.

Previous studies have shown that slope, rainfall intensity, precipitation, soil particle composition, soil density, etc., significantly impact debris flow initiation, and the underlying condition is a decisive factor affecting debris flow initiation. The induced rainfall required for debris flow initiation varies significantly under different underlying conditions, and the mode and scale of slope failure are significantly different. The soil composition of the slope (soil fractal dimension value) is an important part of the underlying condition, which determines soil structural characteristics27. The experimental results revealed that the rainfall intensity of debris flow initiation with different fractal dimension values is different, and the soil with smaller fractal dimension values requires higher rainfall conditions28. Under the same rainfall conditions, gravel soils with fractal dimension greater than 2.4 are prone to form debris flow, and the 10-minute and 1-hour critical rainfall required for debris flow initiation gradually decreases with the increase of fractal dimension. Correlation analysis shows that the fractal dimension significantly correlates with the 10-minute and 1-hour critical rainfall. There is a good exponential relationship between fractal dimension and 10-minute and 1-hour critical rainfall21. The relevant formulas are Eqs. (5) and (6) in the paper. Debris flow hazard simulation and engineering hazard analysis

The Lexi Highway, as currently planned, is set to traverse the upper sections of the primary channel of the Daxilada gully, as well as the confluences of its No.1 and No.2 branch gullies. Notably, debris flow in the Daxilada gully has the potential to pose risks and hazards to the proposed project. Hence, the simulation aimed to examine the scenario wherein the debris flow advances towards the intended bridge to forecast the potential ramifications of the debris flow on the project. The simulation software employed in this study is Massflow30, which is a highly efficient computational simulation software for mountain disaster dynamics based on the theory of depth-integrated continuum mechanics, utilizing the improved MacCormack-TVD finite-difference method, which also takes into account the complex topography and geomorphology and has the features of second-order accuracy and adaptive solution domain.

Simulation parameter acquisition and calculation

Topographic data acquisition and processing

In order to conduct numerical simulations using the Massflow software, it was necessary to get topographic data about the designated study area. The digital elevation model (DEM) data near the study area was acquired using satellite retrieval. ArcGIS was employed to process the Digital Elevation Model (DEM) data to get the raster data about the elevation characteristics within the designated study region. The raster data of the elevation information of the study region was converted into the ASCII file format using ArcGIS, enabling compatibility with the Massflow software.

Selection of simulation parameters

The simulation employed a methodology rooted in the study of debris flow to accurately model the movement of debris flows. Debris flow initiation points were strategically established in the higher sections of the main gully and the No.1 and No.2 branch gullies. The peak flow rates recorded at each initiation point were 13.63 m3/s, 8.03 m3/s, and 9.63 m3/s. The hydrological coefficients of the cross-section were 0.72, 0.85, and 0.88. The area of the Daxilada watershed was 14.08 km2, and based on the experiences, the debris flow duration was set at 30 min (1800 s).

The Voellmy model was selected for simulation, which was improved from the Coulomb model and was suitable for mud and debris flow hazards, mainly considering the turbulence coefficient, which could limit the higher particle flow velocity32. Due to the lack of historical disaster data in the basin, the friction coefficient and turbulence coefficient are set as 0.12 and 300 respectively according to the field investigation results and the characteristics of diluted debris flow in the study area combined with previous research results33.

Analysis of simulation results

The thickness of the maximum deposition depth at different moments of the debris flow can be obtained through simulation (Fig. 5). It can be seen that in 0–400s, the debris flow started to form in the main gully and branch gullies, and the deposition was mainly concentrated in the vicinity of the initiation point of the debris flow; in 400–800s, the debris flow in the gullies of each branch gully converged to the main gully, and there was a noticeable deposition appeared at the point where the proposed project was located; in 800 –1200s, the debris flow started to move in the main gully, and at this time, the point where the proposed project was located became the maximum deposition depth of debris flow in the location; 1200–1400s, the debris flow continued to move downstream, and the thickness of debris flow deposition at the site of the proposed project began to decrease; 1400–600s, the debris flow formed a significant deposition in the gully turn downstream of the proposed project site, and the deposition at the proposed project point was significantly reduced; 1600–1800s, with the end of the maximum calendar time for possible debris flow in the watershed, the debris flow eventually formed a significant deposition in the gully turn downstream of the proposed Project site; gully turned downstream of the project site, and a small amount of debris flow deposition would accumulate at the proposed project site.

Based on the findings from the field investigation and the outcomes obtained from simulations, it was known that debris flow would accumulate in the gully at the proposed bridge engineering point of Lexi Highway when the debris flow occurred. By adding the point where the proposed project is located in Massflow (grid position X:179, Y:148), the relationship curve between the deposition depth in the gully of the proposed project site and the simulation time can be obtained, and Origin can decipher the data in the obtained relationship curve (Fig. 8).

Data interpretation shows that at 1180 s, the debris flow deposition depth of the proposed project site reached the maximum value of 2.1 m. At 1800 s (the end of the simulation), the debris flow deposition depth of the proposed project site was 0.88 m. Therefore, when the debris flow occurs, it may form impact and siltation hazards to the proposed bridge, and it may even lead to the blockage of the gully. Therefore, further quantitative calculations combined with the site investigation data were needed to determine whether the deposition and influence of the debris flow would threaten the proposed project and provide construction and prevention recommendations for the proposed project.

Engineering hazard analysis

Analysis of bridge overflow sections

Based on the simulation findings about the debris flow, it was determined that the thickness of the debris flow in the vicinity of the proposed bridge had the potential to reach a maximum of 2.1 m. In the event of a debris flow, the planned bridge possesses sufficient overflow capacity to prevent obstruction or burial by the debris flow. Therefore, it is necessary to analyze the cross-flow section of the proposed bridge and calculate the minimum clear height and clear width to satisfy the safe cross-flow of the debris flow in combination with the relevant specifications.

The clear height of the bridge was determined by a combination of the maximum debris flow buildup thickness, debris flow wash-up height, and safety height. Analysis of trench blockages

Based on the site investigation of the Daxilada watershed, the presence of larger boulders with invited trees in the main gully cross sections 1 and 2 (Fig. 7c and d) may block the gully to form a barrier dam when debris flows. During debris flow development, dams breaking in gullies create greater destructive forces and a more comprehensive range of damage downstream. Therefore, the proposed project site gully was analyzed for the possibility of plugging, and the risk of plugging was evaluated.

Based on the findings derived from the morphological investigation technique, it was evident that the debris flow exhibited a significant alteration in its flow pattern within the two portions situated upstream and downstream of the envisaged bridge (the flow increased from 13.63 to 22.14 m3/s, which was nearly one times higher). According to the research of related scholars, gully blockage could lead to the upstream and downstream debris flow and other parameters of the blockage location increasing exponentially34,35. Moreover, the simulation results of the Massflow software showed that when the debris flow occurs, there would be a maximum of 2.1 m deposition in the proposed construction site. Therefore, the debris flow channel blockage would occur in the proposed Agency.

In order to assess the potential risk of a barrier dam forming due to blockage in the gully, it is necessary to conduct a thorough examination of the gully’s volume and the quantity of material sources that could contribute to the blockage.

The hazardous material source is located in the Clearwater section of the upper Daxilada gully, 300 m above the proposed Luoshanxi bridge and to the left of the main gully of the Daxilada gully. According to the site mapping, the volume of the source is 25.54 × 104 m3, mainly composed of gravel soil.

Regarding the site investigation, the slope in the region tends to be stable, and the human activities in the nearby area are relatively small. In subsequent engineering construction, it is necessary to reduce the excessive development of the bottom of the slope in order to prevent the stability of the slope from being reduced and causing harm.

Conclusions

The research area exhibits a significant relative elevation variation of 1030 m. Additionally, the gullies and valleys within the area have steep longitudinal slopes, which facilitate the deposition of water and sediment. These variables create ample potential energy for the occurrence of mudslides. Based on the computed data, the study region has experienced 12 earthquakes. A large number of earthquakes can mobilize soil mass in the study area. The research area experienced a significant increase in landslides and collapses, resulting in a greater availability of loose solid materials contributing to mudslides. According to the field mapping, there were 193.3 × 104 m3 of loose solid material sources in the study area, among which the total amount of avalanche and landslide deposition solid material sources was the most, about 119.6 × 104 m3. The gradation characteristics of the deposition material in the study area were analyzed, and the calculated sub-dimensional values of the main gully, the No.1 branch gully, and the No.2 branch gully were 2.48, 2.59 and 2.56, which were the range of the sub dimensional values of the prone to debris flows. The critical rainfall for debris flow commencement in the study region was determined based on the sub-dimensional values. The rainfall observed in the study area satisfies the criteria for critical rainfall required to initiate a debris flow. A comprehensive analysis indicated that the Daxilada watershed exhibits the necessary circumstances conducive to developing debris flow.

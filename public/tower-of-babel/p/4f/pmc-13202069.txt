KLF4 overexpression in CAR T cells enhances antitumor potency by preventing exhaustion
Authors: Haoqi Wang, Xianglong Meng, Yuanyuan Feng, Anqi Su, Wentao Qin, Junjie Shen, Kunyu Lu, Lingling Gu, Yujie Ma, Yaxing Wang, Yuqing Mao, Dongdong Ji, Lu Zong, Meijuan Zheng, Yingying Du, Jingjing Cong, Jing Li, Xuefu Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundChimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell therapy is a promising immunotherapy for patients with refractory or relapsed lymphoma or leukemia. However, inefficient activity and insufficient persistence of CAR T cells impede the durability of its therapeutic efficacy.MethodsKrüppel-like factor 4 (KLF4)-overexpressing CAR T cells were generated and assessed for function, exhaustion, and transcriptional changes using in vitro stimulation models and RNA sequencing, with antitumor efficacy validated in mouse models.ResultsWe report that KLF4 overexpression enhances the durability of CAR T-cell antitumor efficacy. KLF4 overexpression enhanced CAR T-cell activity and promoted the differentiation into effector CAR T cells. Furthermore, KLF4 overexpression prevented CAR T-cell exhaustion induced by continuous tumor antigen stimulation. RNA sequencing data confirmed that KLF4 overexpression led to the upregulation of genes involved in effector functions and the downregulation of genes associated with exhaustion in CAR T cells. Notably, KLF4-overexpressing CAR T cells delayed tumor growth, prolonged the survival of mice with hematological malignancies, and exhibited reduced exhaustion and enhanced effector functions in vivo relative to normal CAR T cells.ConclusionsOur findings highlight the potential of KLF4 overexpression as a therapeutic approach to counteract CAR T-cell exhaustion and enhance antitumor activity, underscoring its therapeutic promise in clinical immunotherapy.

Introduction

Chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell therapy has emerged as an immunotherapy for patients with refractory or relapsed hematologic malignancies, demonstrating remarkable efficacy owing to its high specificity and cytotoxicity.14 However, durable remission is not always achieved in patients undergoing CAR T-cell therapy. Clinical studies indicate that around half of patients who receive CD19-directed CAR T cells for hematological malignancies eventually experience a relapse.59 This is primarily due to impaired activity caused by the tumor immunosuppressive microenvironment and insufficient persistence caused by CAR T-cell exhaustion,10 11 underscoring the critical need to improve CAR T-cell activity and persistence in vivo.

T-cell exhaustion is a state of T-cell dysfunction characterized by impaired effector functions, sustained expression of inhibitory receptors, and diminished proliferative capacity.1214 This exhaustion is primarily driven by persistent antigen exposure in the tumor microenvironment.1517 T-cell exhaustion is governed by a complex network of transcriptional factors. Several transcription factors, such as TOX, NR4A, and BATF, have been identified as key drivers of the exhaustion program.1822 Conversely, some transcriptional factors can counteract exhaustion and maintain T-cell functionality.2325 Krüppel-like factor 4 (KLF4) is a zinc-finger transcription factor that plays diverse roles in cell differentiation. For instance, KLF4 has been shown to regulate Th17 differentiation by directly binding to the IL-17a promoter.26 KLF4-deficient OT-I cells generated more functional memory T cells compared with wild-type OT-I cells in responses to Listeria monocytogenes-OVA.27 In contrast, KLF4 upregulates AP-1 family factors and confers transient effector characteristics to tumor-specific CD8+ T cells.28 However, the impacts of KLF4 on the activity and exhaustion of CAR T cells remain largely unexplored.

Here, we aimed to investigate the roles of KLF4 overexpression in CAR T-cell function and immunotherapy. We demonstrated that KLF4-overexpressing CAR T cells exhibit enhanced effector function, decreased terminal exhaustion and improved tumor control in vitro and in vivo. These findings highlight KLF4 as a promising therapeutic approach for enhancing the efficacy of CAR T-cell therapy.

Methods

Human samples

Peripheral blood samples from patients with diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) were obtained from the Anhui Provincial Cancer Hospital, and samples from healthy donors were collected at the First Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University. All procedures were approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Anhui Medical University (Approval No. 81250534) and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to sample collection.

Animals

Male NOD/ShiLtJGpt-Prkdcem26Cd52Il2rgem26Cd22/Gpt (NCG) mice, 6–8 weeks old, initial body weight 20–25 g, were used for in vivo experiments. All procedures were conducted in accordance with the NIH Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals and were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Anhui Medical University (Approval No. LLSC20231069).

CAR construction and lentivirus production

Second-generation CARs were constructed with distinct promoters. The CD19-CD28 CAR was driven by the human cytomegalovirus (CMV) promoter and comprises a human CD19-specific single-chain variable fragment (scFv) fused to the intracellular signaling domains of CD28 and CD3ζ. The CD19-4-1BB CAR was driven by the EF1α promoter and comprises a human CD19-specific scFv fused to the intracellular signaling domains of 4-1BB and CD3ζ. The coding sequences of KLF4 and truncated nerve growth factor receptor (tNGFR) were synthesized by Universal Biotech (Anhui, China). 293T cells were cultured to 80–90% confluence to generate lentiviral particles carrying CARs. Cells were co-transfected with three plasmids using the jetPRIME reagent (Polyplus, Marguerite Perey, France): the packaging vector psPAX2, the envelope plasmid pMD2.G, and the CAR plasmid. The medium was changed 6–8 hours after transfection, and the lentiviral supernatant was collected at 48 and 72 hours. The harvested supernatant was filtered through a 0.45 µm membrane filter (BS-PES-45, Biosharp, China) to remove cellular debris.

CAR construction and retrovirus production

MSGV retroviral vectors were used to generate the indicated CAR constructs. The CAR comprises a human CD19-specific scFv linked to the intracellular signaling domains of CD28 and CD3ζ. The coding sequences of c-Jun, KLF4, and tNGFR were synthesized by Universal Biotech. The 293GP packaging cell line was cultured to 80–90% confluence prior to transfection. Cells were co-transfected with the MSGV retroviral vector plasmid encoding the CAR construct and the RD114 envelope plasmid using jetPRIME transfection reagent, according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The culture medium was replaced 6–8 hours after transfection. Retroviral supernatants were collected at 48 and 72 hours post-transfection and filtered through a 0.45 µm pore-size membrane filter to remove cellular debris. The filtered supernatants were either used immediately or stored at −80°C until use.

CAR T-cell manufacturing

Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were isolated by density gradient centrifugation using Ficoll-Paque Plus (GE HealthCare) and counted using an automatic cell counter (Countstar, Shanghai, China). PBMCs were plated in 24-well plates and incubated overnight at 4°C with anti-CD3 (5 μg/mL, clone OKT3, BioLegend, USA) and anti-CD28 (5 µg/mL, clone CD28.2, BioLegend, USA). Activated T cells (1×106 cells/well) were cultured for 24 hours prior to transduction. Activated T cells were transduced with lentivirus in the presence of 8 μg/mL polybrene (Solarbio) at a multiplicity of infection of 10. Plates were centrifuged at 800×g for 1.5 hours at 37°C to enhance transduction efficiency. The medium was replaced 6–8 hours post-infection, and the cells were maintained in a medium supplemented with interleukin (IL)-2 (200 IU/mL). CAR expression was assessed on day 4 post-transduction using flow cytometry (CytoFLEX SRT, Beckman Coulter) with green fluorescent protein (GFP) as a reporter.

Western blot

CAR T cells (1×106) were collected by centrifugation and lysed in 100 µL of radioimmunoprecipitation assay (RIPA) lysis buffer per 1×106 cells on ice for 30 min. Protein samples were mixed with loading buffer, boiled at 100°C for 10 min. Proteins were separated by sodium dodecyl sulfate–polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) at 90 V for 30 min, followed by 120 V for 60 min. Proteins were transferred to polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membranes (Millipore, Billerica, Massachusetts, USA) at 400 mA for 30 min and blocked in 50×rapid blocking solution for 30 min. The membranes were incubated overnight at 4°C with KLF4 polyclonal antibody (sc-166190, Santa), washed three times with 1×Tris-buffered saline with Tween 20 (TBST), and then incubated with near-infrared goat anti-rabbit fluorescent secondary antibody for 60 min at room temperature in the dark. After three washes with 1×TBST, the membranes were visualized using a fluorescence imaging system.

Quantitative real-time PCR

Total RNA was extracted from cultured cells using TRIzol reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific) following the manufacturer’s instructions. RNA concentration and purity were assessed by spectrophotometry. For reverse transcription, 1 µg of total RNA was converted to complementary DNA using the PrimeScript RT Reagent Kit (Takara Bio). Quantitative real-time PCR was performed on a LightCycler 96 instrument (Roche) using TB Green Premix Ex Taq II (Takara Bio). The thermal cycling conditions were: 95°C for 30 s, followed by 40 cycles of 95°C for 5 s and 60°C for 30 s. Primers for human KLF4 (forward 5’-CCCACATGAAGCGACTTCCC-3’, reverse 5’-CAGGTCCAGGAGATCGTTGAA-3’) and the endogenous reference gene β-actin; forward 5’-CCTGGCACCCAGCACAAT-3’, reverse 5’-GCCGATCCACACGGAGTACT-3’) were used. The stability of β-actin Ct values across samples was confirmed to ensure consistent RNA input. Relative KLF4 expression was calculated using the 2ˆ−ΔΔCt method, with β-actin as the internal control. Data are presented as mean±SD from at least three independent experiments.

CAR T-cell cytotoxicity assay

CAR T cells were seeded at 1×106 cells per well in 96-well U-bottom plates. Target cells (Raji, Daudi, SU-DHL-4, and Nalm-6) were labeled with 5 µM CellTrace Violet (C34557, Thermo Fisher, USA) and co-cultured with CAR T cells at effector-to-target (E:T) ratios of 1:1, 1:2, and 1:4, with a final volume of 200 µL per well. Wells without CAR T cells were used as negative controls. The plates were incubated at 37°C with 5% CO₂ for 24 hours. After incubation, 7-Aminoactinomycin D (7-AAD, 420404, BioLegend, USA) was added, and the viability of the target cells was measured by flow cytometry. Cytotoxicity was calculated using the following formula: cytotoxicity (%) = (experimental group 7-AAD + % – negative control group 7-AAD + %) / (negative control group 100% – 7 AAD + %.

Flow cytometry

The cells were harvested, washed, and stained with Zombie dye to exclude dead cells. Intracellular cytokine production, proliferation, apoptosis, exhaustion, and differentiation/memory phenotypes were evaluated after co-culturing with the target cells. The following antibodies were used: APC/Cy7 anti-human CD3 (clone UCHT1; 300470), APC/Fire 750 anti-human CD8 (clone SK1; 344746), PE/Cyanine7 anti-human CD8 (clone MQ1-17H12; 500326), PerCP/Cyanine5.5 anti-human CD4 (clone RPA-T4; 300529), PE anti-human IL-2 (clone SK1; 344712), BV421 anti-human GZMB (clone GB11; 563389), APC anti-human IFN-γ (clone 4S.B3; 502512), PE anti-human TNF-α (clone MAb11; 502909), PerCP/Cyanine5.5 anti-human Ki-67 (570281), PE anti-human PI (KGA1107-50), APC anti-human Annexin V (KGA1107-50), PE anti-human LAG-3 (clone 11C3C65; 369306), APC anti-human CD39 (clone A1; 328210), BV605 anti-human TIM-3 (clone F38-2E2; 345017), BV421 anti-human PD-1 (clone EH12.2H7; 329920), PE anti-human CD45 (clone HI30; 304058), PerCP/Cyanine5.5 anti-human CD56 (clone 5.1H11; 362506), Brilliant Violet 421 anti-human CD14 (clone HCD14; 325628), Brilliant Violet 605 anti-human CD11c (clone 3.9; 301636), APC/Fire 750 anti-human HLA-DR (clone L243; 307658). Data were acquired using a CytoFLEX flow cytometer (Beckman Coulter) and analyzed using FlowJo software.

Cytokine detection by ELISA

CAR T cells were co-cultured with Raji tumor target cells at a 1:1 E:T ratio (1×106 cells cells/well) for 24 hours. Supernatants were analyzed using ELISA kits (Dakewe Biotech, Beijing, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The catalog numbers were as follows: interferon (IFN)-γ (Cat. No. 1110002), tumor necrosis factor (TNF)-α (Cat. No. 1117202), IL-2 (Cat. No. 1110202), and granzyme B (GZMB) (Cat. No. 1115802).

RNA sequencing processing and data analysis

CAR T cells were co-cultured with Raji cells at a 1:1 E:T ratio and subjected to repeated antigen stimulation. Cells were stimulated for either three or six cycles, with each stimulation lasting 48 hours. Two independent RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) experiments were performed, corresponding to CAR T cells after three and six stimulation cycles, respectively. The transcriptome sequencing and analysis were conducted by OE Biotech (Shanghai, China). Differentially expressed genes and signaling pathways associated with CAR T-cell function, exhaustion, memory, proliferation, glycolysis, oxidative phosphorylation, and apoptosis were analyzed. Data were further characterized using, hierarchical clustering heatmaps, Gene Ontology (GO), Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG), and Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA).

Seahorse assay

CAR T-cell glycolytic activity was assessed by measuring the extracellular acidification rate (ECAR) using a Seahorse XF-96 Analyzer (Seahorse Bioscience). Cells (2×105 per well) were seeded in Seahorse 96-well plates and incubated for at least 45 min at 37°C without CO₂. ECAR (mpH/min) was measured following sequential treatment with 10 mM glucose, 1 mM oligomycin, and 50 mM 2-deoxyglucose (Sigma-Aldrich). Data from at least three independent experiments were analyzed.

In vivo efficacy of CAR T cells in xenograft models

On day 0, NCG mice were intravenously injected with either Raji-Luc or Nalm6-Luc cells to establish systemic Burkitt lymphoma and B-lineage acute lymphoblastic leukemia (B-ALL) xenograft models, respectively. For the Burkitt lymphoma model, CAR T cells were administered via intravenous injection on day 8, when the bioluminescent signal reached approximately 1×10⁷ and no significant differences were observed among groups. For the B-ALL model, CAR T cells were administered on day 6, when the bioluminescent signal reached approximately 1.2×10⁷ and no significant differences were observed among groups. Tumor burden was monitored using bioluminescence imaging, and mouse survival was recorded separately. The study was continued until the experimental endpoint, and tumor growth and survival data were recorded at each time point.

H&E staining

Following CAR T-cell therapy, mice were euthanized, and the heart, liver, spleen, lung, and kidney tissues were harvested. The tissues were fixed in 4% neutral-buffered formaldehyde, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned. Paraffin sections were stained with H&E and imaged. The scale bars are indicated in the corresponding figures.

Serum biochemical analysis

Blood samples were collected at the indicated time points and allowed to clot at room temperature for 30 min, followed by centrifugation at 3,000×g for 10 min to obtain serum. Serum levels of alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), blood urea nitrogen (BUN), and creatinine (CREA) were measured using commercially available clinical chemistry reagents (Mindray Chemistry Reagents for BS series; catalog numbers: ALT reagent 105–000814-00, AST reagent 105–000815-00, BUN/urea reagent 105–003177-00, CREA reagent 105–004612-00). Serum cytokine levels, including IL-2, IL-6, TNF-α, and IFN-γ, were measured using ELISA kits (Dakewe Biotech, Beijing, China) with the following catalog numbers: IL-2 (Cat. No. 1110202), IL-6 (Cat. No. 1110602), TNF-α (Cat. No. 1117202), and IFN-γ (Cat. No. 1110002), according to the manufacturers’ instructions.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism V.9.0 (GraphPad Software). Experiments included biological replicates or independent mouse cohorts, and were independently repeated at least three times. Data are presented as mean±SEM. Comparisons between two groups were performed using two-tailed unpaired Student’s t-tests. Survival curves were analyzed using the log-rank (Mantel-Cox) test. Statistical significance was defined as *p<0.05, **p<0.01, and ***p<0.001, respectively.

Results

KLF4 overexpression enhances CAR T-cell antitumoral activity in vitro

To evaluate the impact of KLF4 overexpression on CAR T-cell activity, we generated KLF4-overexpressing CD19.28ζ CAR T cells by co-transducing human T cells with a lentiviral vector encoding a second-generation CD28-based CAR together with either KLF4 (KLF4OE CAR T cells) or a tNGFR (CAR T cells) as a control (online supplemental figure S1A,B). This approach resulted in robust KLF4 expression in CAR T cells (figure 1A and B). Compared with controls, KLF4OE CAR T cells showed no significant difference in Ki-67 expression under IL-2 culture conditions, but exhibited an increase in Ki-67 expression following stimulation. Consistently, KLF4OE CAR T cells demonstrated pronounced expansion on repeated tumor stimulation (figure 1C–E), highlighting their enhanced antigen-driven proliferative capacity. Notably, KLF4OE CAR T cells displayed reduced apoptosis following stimulation (figure 1F). Furthermore, KLF4OE CAR T cells exhibited a higher frequency of cells producing IFN-γ, TNF-α, IL-2, or GZMB compared with controls (figure 1G, online supplemental figure S1C). In parallel, KLF4OE CAR T cells displayed an increased proportion of effector T cells (figure 1H). To assess cytolytic activity, KLF4OE and control CAR T cells were co-cultured with B-cell lymphoma targets (Raji, SU-DHL-4, Daudi, Nalm-6) at the indicated E:T ratios. KLF4OE CAR T cells exhibited enhanced tumor lysis, accompanied by increased secretion of IL-2, IFN-γ, TNF-α, or GZMB in the culture supernatant (figure 1I–M). Collectively, these data indicate that KLF4 overexpression significantly enhances the antitumor activity of CAR T cells in vitro.

KLF4 overexpression prevents CAR T-cell exhaustion

To evaluate the impact of KLF4 overexpression on CAR T-cell exhaustion, CAR T cells were subjected to three consecutive rounds of stimulation with Raji tumor cells at 48 hours intervals (figure 2A). Notably, KLF4OE CAR T cells exhibited reduced exhaustion, as indicated by the lower expression of programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1), cluster of differentiation 39 (CD39), lymphocyte activation gene 3 (LAG-3), and T cell immunoglobulin and mucin domain-containing protein 3 (TIM-3) (figure 2B–E), and a higher frequency of cells producing IFN-γ, TNF-α, or GZMB (figure 2F–H). Collectively, these findings indicate that KLF4 overexpression prevents CAR T-cell exhaustion induced by continuous tumor antigen stimulation.

Given that clinical CAR T cells are typically generated from patient-derived T cells,8 29 we next engineered KLF4OE CAR T cells from the T cells of patients with DLBCL. Compared with the controls, KLF4OE CAR T cells demonstrated stronger tumor killing capacity (figure 2I–K) and produced higher levels of IFN-γ, TNF-α, IL-2, and GZMB (figure 2L). Additionally, KLF4OE CAR T cells reduced the expression of exhaustion markers (figure 2M and N). These data indicate that KLF4 overexpression also effectively enhances the antitumor activity of DLBCL patient-derived CAR T cells, highlighting the clinical translation of KLF4OE CAR T cells.

KLF4 overexpression increases CAR T-cell glycolysis

We next elucidated the metabolic property of KLF4OE CAR T cells. Metabolic analysis revealed a significantly elevated ECAR and ATP production in KLF4OE CAR T cells (online supplemental figure S2A,B). Notably, KLF4OE CAR T cells exhibited elevated mitochondrial membrane potentials and lower mitochondrial reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels (online supplemental figure S2C,D). Transcriptomic analysis revealed that GSEA demonstrated enrichment for glycolysis-related genes and downregulation of oxidative phosphorylation pathways (online supplemental figure S2E,F). Moreover, KLF4 overexpression upregulated genes associated with glycolysis, including HK2, ENO1, PGK1, and PFKFB4, and downregulated genes related to oxidative phosphorylation, such as MFN2, SLC25A11, and CPT1A (online supplemental figure S2G). These findings indicate that KLF4 overexpression enhances the glycolysis of CAR T cells.

KLF4 overexpression induces the exhaustion-resistant transcriptional feature of CAR T cells

To further investigate the transcriptional impact of KLF4 overexpression on CAR T cells subjected to repeated tumor stimulations, we sorted KLF4OE CAR T cells and tNGFR controls after three stimulations and performed transcriptomic sequencing. Among the differentially expressed genes, KLF4 overexpression upregulated genes associated with effector function (TBX21, IL-2 and PRDM1), cytotoxic molecules (GZMB and IFNG), and T-cell proliferation (CD3E, MKI67, CD6 and CD28). In contrast, genes encoding inhibitory receptors (CTLA4, LAG3, HAVCR2, TIGIT) and apoptosis regulators (BCL10, FASLG and TNFSF10) were downregulated (figure 3A and B). GO and KEGG pathway enrichment analyses revealed strong enrichment of genes involved in glycolysis, leukocyte differentiation, and regulation of cytokine production (figure 3C and D). Furthermore, GSEA indicated that effector function and proliferation-associated genes were upregulated, whereas genes related to exhaustion and apoptosis were downregulated by KLF4 overexpression (figure 3E–H). Collectively, these results indicate that KLF4 overexpression promotes an effector transcriptional program in CAR T cells.

KLF4 overexpression enhances the efficacy of CAR T cells against Burkitt lymphoma

Given that KLF4 overexpression enhances CAR T-cell activity and resists exhaustion in vitro, we next evaluated the therapeutic efficacy of KLF4OE CAR T cells in vivo. CAR T cells (1×106) were adoptively transferred into mice bearing Raji-luc tumors (figure 4A). Mice treated with KLF4OE CAR T cells exhibited better tumor control and body weight maintenance, and prolonged survival compared with the controls (figure 4B–E, online supplemental figure S3A). Importantly, no tissue damage or abnormal hematological changes were observed (figure 4F and G). To confirm this, high doses of CAR T cells (1.5×106) were intravenously administered (figure 4H), which enhanced therapeutic efficacy and achieved tumor eradication (figure 4I–L, online supplemental figure S3B), without detectable tissue damage (figure 4M and N). Collectively, these findings demonstrate that KLF4 overexpression improves the antitumoral efficacy of CAR T cells in vivo.

KLF4 overexpression prevents CAR T-cell exhaustion in vivo

Next, we evaluated the differentiation and function of KLF4OE CAR T cells in vivo (figure 5A). KLF4OE CAR T cells exhibited superior anti-leukemic activity compared with tNGFR controls (figure 5B). Higher numbers of CAR T cells were detected in the peripheral blood of KLF4OE CAR T cell-treated mice (figure 5C). Importantly, KLF4OE CAR T cells showed reduced exhaustion, as indicated by the lower expression of PD-1, CD39, LAG-3, and TIM-3 (figure 5D–G), and increased effector functions, as indicated by higher levels of IFN-γ and TNF-α (figure 5H–I). These findings demonstrate that KLF4 overexpression enhances CAR T cell activity and prevents CAR T-cell exhaustion in vivo.

KLF4 overexpression enhances the efficacy of CAR T cells against B-lineage acute lymphoblastic leukemia

To further demonstrate the antitumor efficacy of KLF4OE CAR T cells in vivo, we established a B-ALL Nalm6 xenograft model. In addition, the transcriptomic analysis revealed that KLF4 overexpression resulted in increased c-Jun expression (figure 3B). Given that c-Jun overexpression has been reported to enhance CAR T-cell resistance to exhaustion,30 31 we also compared the antitumor efficacy of CAR T cells with c-Jun overexpression. Food and Drug Administration-approved 1928ζ gamma-retroviral vector was used to construct CAR (online supplemental figure S4A). CAR T cells were adoptively transferred into mice bearing NALM-6-luc tumors (figure 6A). KLF4OE or c-JunOE CAR T-cell treatment resulted in superior tumor control and prolonged survival compared with control CAR T cells. Meanwhile, KLF4OE CAR T cells exhibited better antitumor efficacy than c-JunOE CAR T cells (figure 6B–E).

The 4-1BB costimulatory domain is known to promote a memory-like phenotype in CAR T cells.32 To determine whether KLF4 overexpression enhances the functionality of CD19.BBζ CAR T cells, we generated KLF4OE CD19.BBζ CAR T cells (online supplemental figure S4B). CAR T cells were subsequently adoptively transferred into mice bearing NALM-6-luc tumors (figure 6F). Compared with control-treated mice, those receiving KLF4OE CD19.BBζ CAR T cells exhibited superior tumor control, better maintenance of body weight, and prolonged survival (figure 6G–J). Collectively, these findings demonstrate that KLF4 overexpression enhances the antitumor efficacy of CAR T cells and prevents their exhaustion in vivo.

To evaluate the antitumor efficacy of KLF4OE CAR T cells in an immunocompetent-like setting and their effects on the host immune system, humanized NCG mice were generated by reconstitution with PBMCs and subsequently injected with Nalm6-Luc tumor cells, followed by adoptive transfer of CAR T cells (online supplemental figure S5A). Compared with control groups, those receiving KLF4OE CAR T cells showed improved tumor control, better maintenance of body weight, and longer survival (online supplemental figure S5B–E). Notably, KLF4OE CAR T cells exhibited a significantly higher proportion and absolute number compared with the control group (online supplemental figure S5F–G). The proportions of human immune cell subsets were not significantly different between the two groups, indicating KLF4OE CAR T cells did not affect the host immune environment (online supplemental figure S5H). Similarly, serum cytokine levels were comparable between the two groups (online supplemental figure S5I). In addition, histological examination of major organs revealed no apparent abnormalities (online supplemental figure S5J). Together, these results show that KLF4 overexpression enhances the antitumor activity of CAR T cells without causing abnormal systemic cytokine responses.

KLF4 overexpression improves the efficacy of repeatedly stimulated CAR T cells

To further investigate the effects of KLF4OE CAR T cells under prolonged repeated antigen stimulation, KLF4OE and control CAR T cells were isolated after six stimulation cycles and subsequently subjected to transcriptomic profiling or tumor therapy. RNA-seq data demonstrated that KLF4 overexpression enhanced the expression of genes linked to effector activity, including GZMB and IFNG, together with proliferation-associated genes (CD3E, MKI67 and CD28). In contrast, transcripts encoding inhibitory receptors (CTLA4, LAG3 and HAVCR2) and pro-apoptotic factors (BCL10, MCL1 and FASL) were reduced (figure 7A–D). Meanwhile, the CAR T cells were adoptively transferred into tumor-bearing NCG mice to evaluate their in vivo antitumor efficacy (figure 7E). The results showed that, compared with the control group, mice receiving KLF4OE CAR T cells exhibited reduced tumor burden, more stable body weight, and prolonged survival (figure 7F–I). Collectively, these findings indicate that KLF4 overexpression enhances resistance to exhaustion and antitumor efficacy of CAR T cells.

Discussion

CAR T-cell therapy has achieved clinical success in the treatment of hematologic malignancies. However, its therapeutic potential remains limited by inefficient activity and insufficient persistence. In this study, we demonstrated that overexpression of the transcription factor KLF4 is a potent strategy for enhancing the antitumor efficacy of CAR T cells. We provide comprehensive evidence that KLF4 overexpression reprograms CAR T cells across multiple layers. KLF4 overexpression enhances their effector functions, promotes a favorable metabolic shift towards glycolysis, and prevents their exhaustion, ultimately leading to improved tumor control in vivo. This study highlights KLF4 overexpression as a therapeutic approach for improving CAR T-cell immunotherapy.

T-cell exhaustion remains a principal obstacle to the success of adoptive cell therapy.3335 Recent efforts have focused on targeting exhaustion-inducing factors such as TOX, NR4A and MYB,19 21 36 37 our approach centers on reinforcing a naturally occurring protective mechanism. KLF4 is a zinc finger transcription factor involved in regulating cell growth, proliferation, differentiation, invasion, autophagy, and embryogenesis.3840 Previous studies have reported that deficiency of KLF4 inhibits the proliferation of TCR-stimulated activated CD8+T cells.41 Our findings align with and extend the study by Nah et al, which identified KLF4 as a sustainer of a functional subset of exhausted CD8+T cells during chronic viral infections.28 Crucially, we demonstrated that this concept translates effectively to CAR T cells. The lower expression levels of inhibitory receptor (PD-1, LAG-3, TIM-3, and CD39) and the better cytokine-producing capacity following repeated antigen stimulation suggest that KLF4 overexpression is associated with a delay in exhaustion of CAR T cells. Notably, we observed that KLF4 overexpression increased c-Jun expression. It has been reported that overexpression of c-Jun in CAR T cells enhances their resistance to exhaustion and improves antitumor efficacy, supporting its role as a key regulator of T-cell function.30 42 Our findings raise the possibility that c-Jun acts as a downstream effector of KLF4. Compared with c-Jun overexpression, KLF4 overexpression appears to exert broader effects. In in vivo models, KLF4OE CAR T cells demonstrated more pronounced antitumor efficacy than c-JunOE CAR T cells, suggesting that KLF4 may confer additional advantages beyond c-Jun upregulation. Collectively, these results identify KLF4 as a promising strategy to enhance CAR T-cell antitumor efficacy by limiting exhaustion.

Some studies indicate that KLF4 can regulate mitophagy and mitochondrial dynamics, suggesting a relationship between the transcription factor and cellular metabolism.43 44 We found that KLF4 overexpression increased CAR T-cell glycolysis, which is consistent with the report that KLF4 overexpression in induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) promotes glycolysis while reducing the tricarboxylic acid cycle through TCL1 activation.45 The upregulation of key glycolytic enzymes (HK2, ENO1 and PGK1) suggests a plausible mechanism involving direct transactivation of these metabolic genes by KLF4. Furthermore, KLF4 overexpression mitigated oxidative stress and improved mitochondrial fitness, as evidenced by reduced ROS levels and increased membrane potential. Together, these findings suggest that KLF4 enhances metabolic activity and mitochondrial function in CAR T cells, which may contribute to their improved functional performance. Future studies employing rescue experiments are required to establish the role of metabolic reprogramming in KLF4OE CAR T cells.

The translational potential of our findings is highlighted by the enhanced efficacy of KLF4OE CAR T cells derived from patients with DLBCL. This indicates that the beneficial effects of KLF4 are recapitulated in a clinically relevant setting, overcoming the potential limitations associated with the use of cells from healthy donors. However, the efficacy of KLF4OE CAR T cells against solid tumors remains an open and critical question for future research.

In summary, we identified KLF4 as a powerful ally in the fight against CAR T-cell exhaustion. By integrating KLF4 into CAR T-cell engineering, we can potentially create more resilient and potent immunotherapies for cancer treatment. This work not only provides a promising strategy for clinical translation but also deepens the understanding of the molecular interplay between transcription factor, metabolism, and T-cell fate, paving the way for further innovative approaches in CAR T-cell immunotherapy.

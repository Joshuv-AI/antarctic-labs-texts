CRGWO-DWA: A structured global–local collaborative planning framework for smooth UAV navigation in dynamic environments
Authors: Yong He, Qiang Gao, Qing Huang, Boji Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractUAV path planning in dynamic environments requires balancing global path quality, local obstacle avoidance, and motion smoothness. To address the premature convergence and unsmooth paths of the Grey Wolf Optimizer (GWO) and the local-optimum tendency and evaluation imbalance of the Dynamic Window Approach (DWA), this paper proposes CRGWO-DWA, a structured global–local collaborative planning framework. In the global planning stage, CRGWO enhances population diversity and search performance through piecewise linear chaotic initialization, periodic regeneration of the control parameter, and adaptive leadership weighting, while cubic spline interpolation is used to generate a smooth reference path. In the local planning stage, DWA is improved using min-max normalized evaluation and redesigned weighting to enhance trajectory discrimination and responsiveness under dynamic obstacle interference. Experimental results show that CRGWO achieves the best or tied-best performance in most reported benchmark metrics, reduces path length and redundant turning nodes in static environments, and produces more compact and smoother paths in three-dimensional scenarios. In dynamic multi-obstacle environments, CRGWO-DWA enables safe and smooth UAV navigation with stable velocity profiles. These results indicate that the proposed framework provides an effective solution for UAV path planning in dynamic environments.Keywords: grey wolf optimizer, dynamic window approach, unmanned aerial vehicle, path planning, dynamic obstacle avoidanceSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) have been widely applied in search and rescue, reconnaissance, emergency response, logistics, infrastructure inspection, and precision agriculture1–6. In these scenarios, autonomous navigation is essential for both safety and efficiency, and path planning is one of its key enabling technologies7. For practical UAV missions, path planning involves not only generating a collision-free route8, but also ensuring real-time obstacle avoidance, kinematic feasibility, trajectory smoothness, and stable motion execution. In dynamic environments, these requirements are tightly coupled, making it difficult for a single planning strategy to simultaneously achieve high global path quality and reliable local responsiveness9.

UAV path planning is generally classified into global planning and local planning10. Global planning generates an optimal or near-optimal route from the start to the goal based on prior environmental information, whereas local planning adjusts the trajectory online according to sensor feedback and environmental changes11,12. This division reflects an inherent trade-off13: global planners provide long-horizon guidance and improve overall path quality, but they are often less responsive to dynamic disturbances; local planners are computationally efficient and highly reactive, but without reliable global guidance they are prone to myopic decisions and local optima. Therefore, robust UAV navigation in dynamic environments requires an effective global–local collaborative framework rather than isolated improvement of either stage.

Existing global planning methods mainly include graph-search, sampling-based, evolutionary, and swarm intelligence approaches14. Graph-search algorithms such as A* and Dijkstra produce deterministic solutions, but their computational cost increases significantly in large-scale or high-resolution environments15. Sampling-based methods such as RRT and PRM provide flexible exploration, yet the generated paths often require post-processing to improve smoothness and quality. Evolutionary and swarm intelligence algorithms, including genetic algorithms, particle swarm optimization, ant colony optimization, and Grey Wolf Optimizer (GWO), have attracted considerable attention for their strong global search ability and adaptability16,17. Among them, GWO is particularly suitable for UAV path planning because of its simple structure, few control parameters, and fast convergence18–21. However, conventional GWO may still suffer from insufficient population diversity and premature convergence, leading to suboptimal path quality and redundant turning points.

For local planning, the Dynamic Window Approach (DWA) is widely adopted because it explicitly considers velocity and acceleration constraints and can directly output executable motion commands with high computational efficiency22,23. However, conventional DWA still has notable limitations in dynamic environments. Its multi-criteria trajectory evaluation is sensitive to manually assigned weights and may become unbalanced across different scenarios. In addition, under dynamic obstacle interference, DWA can fall into local optima and produce unstable or weakly discriminative velocity decisions. Although recent studies have improved DWA by integrating global planners, learning-based methods, or modified sampling strategies, achieving reliable obstacle avoidance together with smooth and stable motion output remains challenging, especially when global guidance and local replanning are not effectively coupled24,25.

Existing GWO-based path planners mainly enhance global search performance by modifying initialization strategies, convergence factors, hybrid search operators, or adaptive learning mechanisms26–28. Although these methods can improve global optimization quality, they still focus primarily on path generation itself and do not explicitly address how globally optimized discrete waypoints can be transformed into dynamically executable, trackable, and smooth navigation trajectories. Existing hybrid global–local planners are able to combine long-range guidance with reactive obstacle avoidance29,30, but the coupling between the two stages is often relatively loose, where the global planner mainly provides a nominal route and the local planner still relies heavily on independent decision-making under dynamic disturbances. As a result, path continuity and local responsiveness are not always jointly guaranteed. In addition, recent learning-based planners, including reinforcement-learning-based path planning methods and RL-enhanced local planners, have shown strong potential in dynamic decision-making, adaptive parameter adjustment, and generalization to complex environments31–33. However, these methods usually depend on large-scale training and relatively high computational cost, and their decision process is often less interpretable.

To address the above issues, this paper proposes CRGWO-DWA, a structured global–local collaborative planning framework for UAV navigation in dynamic environments. Unlike loosely coupled hybrid planners, the proposed framework unifies global path planning, path smoothing, guidance-point generation, and local dynamic replanning within a single planning pipeline, thus improving the coordination between global guidance and local obstacle avoidance.

The main contributions of this study are summarized as follows.

(1) A structured global–local collaborative planning framework is established for UAV navigation in dynamic environments, in which global path optimization, spline-based path refinement, guidance-point generation, and local dynamic replanning are explicitly integrated into a unified workflow.

(2) A refined global planning method, termed CRGWO, is developed by incorporating piecewise linear chaotic initialization, periodic regeneration of the control parameter, and exponentially adaptive leadership weighting, thereby improving population diversity, search efficiency, and global path quality.

(3) A practical DWA-based local replanning strategy is introduced by applying min–max normalization and adjusted weights to improve the comparability of heterogeneous trajectory-evaluation terms under dynamic obstacle interference.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces the algorithmic foundations of GWO and DWA. Section 3 presents the proposed CRGWO-DWA framework in detail. Section 4 reports the benchmark evaluation results and UAV path-planning experiments. Finally, Sect. 5 concludes the paper.

Global path refinement via cubic spline interpolation

To bridge the gap between discrete global planning and executable UAV motion, cubic spline interpolation is employed to refine the waypoint sequence into a smooth global reference path. In UAV path planning, path smoothness refers to the geometric continuity of the planned path and the reduction of abrupt direction changes between adjacent segments. Improving path smoothness is essential because it helps reduce unnecessary maneuvering and provides a more suitable reference for downstream trajectory tracking and local replanning. In the proposed framework, discrete waypoints are first generated by CRGWO during the global planning stage, and cubic spline interpolation is then applied to connect these waypoints and produce a smooth reference path for downstream local replanning and motion execution.

Min–max normalized DWA evaluation

To improve the consistency of local trajectory evaluation in dynamic environments, the DWA scoring function is reformulated using min–max normalization and redesigned weights. In the original DWA, the heading, clearance, and velocity terms may have different numerical scales, which can cause one term to dominate the final score and weaken the balance of trajectory evaluation. Therefore, sum normalization is replaced by a min–max normalization scheme to improve cross-indicator comparability.

Compared with sum normalization, min–max normalization maps each indicator to [0,1], improving cross-indicator comparability and reducing sensitivity to baseline shifts. This process enhances the robustness of the DWA algorithm, especially in dynamic and uncertain environments, where changes in scale or baseline can affect the evaluation. From the stability perspective, this reformulation prevents any single criterion from dominating the final score merely because of its numerical scale, thereby improving the consistency of local decision-making across different obstacle configurations. Since the normalization is applied only to the already computed evaluation matrix, the additional computational cost is linear in the number of sampled trajectories and evaluation terms, and thus does not change the dominant complexity of the DWA rollout-and-evaluation procedure.

Overall, the normalized evaluation improves the consistency of DWA-based local decision-making without changing the dominant computational complexity.

Overall workflow of the CRGWO-DWA framework

The overall workflow of the proposed CRGWO-DWA framework is illustrated in Fig. 7. First, CRGWO is used in the global planning stage to generate a collision-free path from the start to the goal. The resulting discrete waypoints are then refined by cubic spline interpolation to form a smooth global reference path. After that, the start point, goal point, and representative waypoints along the reference path are extracted and organized into a sequence of local guidance points for the subsequent DWA-based replanning process. During the local planning stage, candidate velocity commands are sampled and evaluated according to the improved DWA scoring function, with particular emphasis on motion feasibility, obstacle clearance, and target tracking. At each control cycle, the highest-scoring command is selected so that the UAV can track the reference path while reacting safely to dynamic obstacles. In this way, the proposed framework strengthens the cooperation between global guidance and local obstacle avoidance, enabling safe, smooth, and stable UAV navigation in dynamic environments.

Results and discussion

To validate the effectiveness of the proposed CRGWO-DWA framework, this section presents experiments from two perspectives: benchmark evaluation of CRGWO for global optimization, and UAV path-planning simulations in grid-based environments. Specifically, the experiments first assess the convergence behavior and robustness of CRGWO in benchmark optimization tasks, and then evaluate the performance of the proposed global–local collaborative planning framework in static and dynamic path-planning scenarios. In this way, the experimental design clearly distinguishes the role of CRGWO in global path generation from the role of CRGWO-DWA in dynamic obstacle avoidance and motion execution.

Dynamic obstacle avoidance results of the CRGWO-DWA framework

This subsection evaluates the effectiveness of the proposed CRGWO-DWA framework in dynamic obstacle avoidance scenarios. Specifically, CRGWO is first used to generate a smooth global reference path, after which representative waypoints along that path are extracted as guidance points for the improved DWA during local replanning. In this way, the proposed framework strengthens the cooperation between global guidance and local obstacle avoidance, thereby alleviating a key limitation of purely local planners, namely their susceptibility to local optima, while preserving the real-time responsiveness of DWA. In the dynamic replanning experiments, trajectory smoothness refers to the continuity of the executed UAV motion, which is mainly reflected by the absence of abrupt fluctuations in the linear and angular velocity profiles.

To this end, dynamic obstacle encounters are explicitly simulated during flight. Dynamic obstacles are deliberately placed in path-critical regions along the nominal route so that the UAV trajectory would otherwise intersect or conflict with the planned path, thereby creating representative interference scenarios that require local replanning. This setup enables a direct evaluation of the proposed framework in terms of local decision-making, reactive obstacle avoidance, and trajectory execution in dynamic environments. As in the previous subsection, experiments are conducted in both simple and complex environments. To further validate the structured global–local coupling mechanism, a comparison was conducted between the proposed structured CRGWO-DWA framework and a loosely coupled CRGWO-DWA pipeline, denoted as Structured CRGWO-DWA and LC-CRGWO-DWA, respectively. For a fair comparison, both methods used the same CRGWO-generated global path, the same improved DWA local planner, and the same dynamic obstacle settings. The main difference lies in the way global planning information is transferred to the local replanning stage. In the loosely coupled pipeline, DWA uses sparse global waypoints as local targets, whereas the proposed structured pipeline transfers the smoothed global reference path and extracted guidance points to the DWA-based local replanning stage.

The results in Table 10 show that the proposed structured CRGWO-DWA pipeline achieves smaller tracking deviations than the loosely coupled pipeline in both dynamic environments. Specifically, in the simple environment, the average tracking deviation decreases from 3.933 m to 0.786 m, and the maximum tracking deviation decreases from 4.151 m to 1.662 m. In the complex environment, the average tracking deviation decreases from 4.206 m to 0.412 m, and the maximum tracking deviation decreases from 4.957 m to 1.625 m. These results indicate that the explicit transfer of the smoothed reference path and extracted guidance points provides more effective global guidance for DWA-based local replanning. Therefore, compared with the loosely coupled pipeline, the proposed structured coupling mechanism improves the consistency between the executed trajectory and the global reference path.

Overall, the results in both environments demonstrate the effectiveness and computational feasibility of the proposed CRGWO-DWA framework for dynamic obstacle avoidance. By coupling a smooth global reference path generated by CRGWO with improved DWA-based local replanning, the framework enables the UAV to maintain safe obstacle clearance, preserve path continuity, and generate stable velocity commands in the presence of dynamic disturbances.

Conclusions

This paper proposed CRGWO-DWA, a structured global–local collaborative planning framework for UAV navigation in dynamic environments. The framework integrates CRGWO-based global path optimization, spline-based reference path smoothing, guidance-point generation, and DWA-based local replanning into a unified pipeline, thereby strengthening the coordination between long-range path guidance and local dynamic obstacle avoidance.

The experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method under the considered benchmark and simulation settings. CRGWO shows competitive benchmark performance and achieves the best or tied-best results in most reported metrics, although the magnitude of improvement varies across different functions and compared algorithms. In static path-planning scenarios, CRGWO reduces path length and redundant turning nodes compared with standard GWO. In dynamic multi-obstacle environments, the complete CRGWO-DWA framework enables safe and smooth UAV navigation with stable velocity profiles, indicating improved local responsiveness and motion executability.

Nevertheless, the current experiments are still based on grid-based simulation environments and simplified dynamic-obstacle models. Future work will extend the proposed framework to more diverse and realistic scenarios, including larger-scale three-dimensional environments, more complex dynamic obstacle interactions, and higher-fidelity UAV simulation platforms.

Recent highlights from the International Journal of Cardiology: Heart & Vasculature: Valvular heart disease
Authors: Francesco Pelliccia, Artur Dziewierz, Marco Zimarino, Dobromir Dobrev
Published: 2025

Valvular heart disease (VHD) is one of the most common cardiovascular diseases today. In some patients it may be highly symptomatic, and eventually lead to sudden cardiac death [[1], [2], [3]]. It is important to early identify significant valve disease because intervention can improve quality of life and increase life expectancy. In most patients, the diagnosis of significant VHD can be made based on a physical examination, yet nearly half of the patients who could benefit from intervention are not being recognized or referred. There have been major improvements in both the diagnosis and treatment of patients with VHD, with noninvasive echocardiography available to confirm the presence and severity of VHD, better and more durable surgical procedures, and the advent of catheter-based therapies.

Here, we highlight papers published recently in the International Journal of Cardiology: Heart & Vasculature which advance the understanding of VHD, delineate diagnostic and prognostic features, and provide perspectives for improved patient treatment.

Aortic valve disease

In recent years, lipoprotein(a) (Lp(a)) has been increasingly recognized as a significant contributor to the pathogenesis of aortic stenosis (AS) [4], prompting a focused investigation into innovative pharmacological strategies to target this lipoprotein and its associated risks. Khan et al. published an interesting review delving into the mechanistic intricacies of Lp(a) involvement in AS pathophysiology and its potential as a therapeutic target [5]. The review underscores the essential role of cardiologists and physicians in recognizing and addressing Lp(a) as a modifiable risk factor in AS management. Furthermore, it explores promising avenues of novel drug approaches, including emerging pharmacotherapies and targeted interventions, aimed at modulating Lp(a) levels and attenuating AS progression. By navigating the complexity of Lp(a) modulation and its implications for AS management, this review aims to bridge critical gaps in understanding and clinical practice, ultimately optimizing treatment strategies and improving patient outcomes in the realm of AS therapeutics [5].

Troger et al. aimed to detect sex-differences in severe AS, especially concerning flow-patterns, via phase-contrast cardiac magnetic resonance imaging (CMR) [6]. Forty-four patients with severe AS underwent transthoracic echocardiography, cardiac catheterization and CMR. Aortic valve area (AVA) was determined in all modalities, with CMR yielding geometrical AVA via cine-planimetry and functional AVA via phase contrast CMR. Geometrical AVA showed no sex-differences, whereas functional AVA differed significantly between sexes in all three modalities. In men, no significant methodology dependent biases in functional AVA-measurements were found between individual modalities. Conversely, in women, measurements obtained at cine-planimetry CMR and phase-contrast CMR differed significantly. Thus, the authors concluded that in patients with severe AS, functional AVA showed marked sex-differences in all modalities, whilst geometrical AVA did not differ.

The hybrid aortic repair consisting of root replacement and endovascular arch repair is an optimal alternative for patients unfit for circulatory arrest. However, an artificial aortic valve prosthesis might impede the endovascular procedure. Ma et al. described their experience with the branching retrograde externalized guidewire technique in 112 patients undergoing aortic root/valve replacement combined with aortic arch repair, and discussed its utility and efficiency [7]. The cardiopulmonary bypass time and cross-clamped time were significantly shorter in the group undergoing branching retrograde externalized guidewire than in the conventional group. Less operative red blood cell consumption was achieved in the BREG group. The 30-day mortality was similar between the two groups.

Antibiotic prophylaxis in bicuspid aortic valve patients is currently a matter of debate. Although international guidelines no longer recommend it, some studies indicate a high risk of infective endocarditis. Pereira et al. evaluated the risk of native valve infective endocarditis in bicuspid aortic valve patients and compare to individuals with tricuspid aortic valve [8]. A total of 8 cohort studies were selected, with a total of 5351 patients with bicuspid aortic valve. During follow-up, 184 patients with bicuspid aortic valve presented with infective endocarditis, with an incidence rate of 48 per 10,000 patients-year and a 12-fold higher risk compared with general population, after adjusted estimates. Thus, this systematic review and meta-analysis suggests that patients with bicuspid aortic valve may have a significantly higher risk of native valve infective endocarditis.

Although the prevalence of VHD is high in patients with systemic lupus erythematosus, the predictive factors of concomitant VHD have not been fully evaluated. Usuku et al. studied 288 patients and divided them into two groups: the VHD group and non-VHD group [9]. Multivariate logistic regression analysis revealed that age and the platelet count were significantly and independently associated with having VHD associated with systemic lupus erythematosus, particularly in middle-aged and older patients.

Inadequate auscultation skills among clinicians delay VHD diagnosis and treatment. Artificial intelligence provides a solution to compensate for the insufficiency in auscultation ability by distinguishing between heart murmurs and normal heart sounds. Jiang et al. studied 499 patients and found that sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy of the deep-learning model for identifying various VHDs ranged from 71 % to 100 % [10]. The best diagnostic performance was observed for mitral stenosis, with a sensitivity of 100 %, a specificity of 100 %, and an accuracy of 100 %.

Transcatheter aortic valve replacement

Awad et al. performed a systematic review and meta-analysis to synthesize available evidence to guide clinical decision-making by comparing the performance of transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) and surgical aortic valve replacement (SAVR), specifically in patients with severe AS and a small aortic annulus [11]. Eleven studies with 3670 patients were included. TAVR significantly increased the risk of 2-year new permanent pacemaker implantation and major vascular complications than SAVR. However, TAVR significantly decreased the risk of patient-prosthesis mismatch and post-operative AF. Also, SAVR reduced the risk of paravalvular leak. Therefore, TAVR and SAVR remain valid alternatives, and decisions should be based on anatomy of the annulus and aortic root, operative risk, and comorbidities.

Usuku et al. assessed the association between left atrial (LA) stiffness and outcomes in patients undergoing TAVR [12]. In 279 patients with AS who underwent TAVR between 2015 and 2023, multivariate Cox proportional hazard analysis identified LA stiffness as significantly and independently associated with all-cause death after adjusting for body mass index, trans-aortic valve velocity, AF, log-transformed high-sensitivity cardiac troponin T, tricuspid regurgitation (TR), and LV global longitudinal strain. Thus, the authors concluded that estimation of LA stiffness provides significant prognostic value in patients with AS undergoing TAVR, even after adjusting for multiple predictive factors.

Socioeconomic status is an important determinant of healthcare outcomes in many settings, but few studies have evaluated the impact of SES among patients with AS. Zhou et al. studied 2462 patients who underwent TAVR and found that patients with a lower economic condition were younger, had more comorbidities, and were less likely to have private health insurance or receive care in private hospitals [13]. Also, patients with a lower socioeconomic status presented more often with advanced markers of disease (lower AVA, lower dimensionless index, increased pulmonary hypertension) and were more likely to undergo urgent TAVR, but faced longer waiting times for elective TAVR. Despite these pre-procedural differences, mortality and complication rates were similar across groups with different economic conditions. In multivariable analyses, socioeconomic status was not an independent predictor of mortality or major adverse cardiovascular events at 30 days or 12 months.

Despite a decade of investigations, the evidence of the long-term advantages of TAVR over SAVR for AS remains scarce. Biancari et al. studied patients with EuroSCORE II <4 % who underwent TAVR or SAVR for AS from the prospective observational OBSERVANT study [14]. Propensity score matching resulted in 355 matched pairs of patients who underwent TAVR or SAVR. Difference in terms of survival between the propensity matched cohorts became significant 3 years after the procedures. Ten-year cumulative incidences of repeat aortic valve procedure were 2.6 % after SAVR and 1.1 % after TAVR. The results of this prospective observational, non-randomized study showed that 10-year survival of low-risk patients who underwent TAVR with early generation prosthesis devices was lower than SAVR.

TAVR is considered more effective than SAVR for patients with a small aortic annulus, however, the comparative efficacy of different transcatheter heart valves (THVs) remains uncertain. Ahmed et al. evaluated 10 studies with 2960 patients [15]. Balloon expandable valves (BEVs) were associated with a significantly smaller indexed effective orifice area and a higher transvalvular mean pressure gradient. The risk for prosthesis-patient mismatch was significantly higher for patients receiving BEVs than those receiving self-expandable valves (SEVs). Although nonsignificant differences were observed between BEVs and SEVs regarding 30-day and 1-year all-cause mortality, 30-day stroke rates, vascular complication, paravalvular leak, and permanent pacemaker implantation, patients receiving BEVs were associated with a significantly increased risk of 1-year cardiovascular mortality compared to those receiving SEVs. In patients with small aortic annulus, BEVs demonstrated worse hemodynamic performance as determined by the higher risk of moderate and severe prosthesis-patient mismatch compared to SEVs. Moreover, the use of BEVs was associated with a higher risk of 1-year cardiovascular mortality.

TAVR and SAVR have different levels of invasiveness, which can result in different levels of functional status after the procedure. Stavridis et al. performed a systematic review and meta-analysis selected 10 studies showing direct comparison between TAVR and SAVR regarding postoperative functional status [16]. TAVR patients had a significantly lower incidence of postoperative delirium compared to SAVR patients. No significant differences were found in secondary outcomes between the groups. Thus, the authors concluded that TAVR is associated with a lower incidence of postoperative delirium compared to SAVR without compromising length of stay or other major clinical outcomes.

Trans-femoral represents the main access for TAVR. Although there are various technical strategies to conduct TAVR (pacing modality, secondary arterial access, primary access puncture etc.), the optimal technique is not established. Romagnoli et al. assessed the impact of systematic use of less-invasive totally-endovascular TAVR in terms of feasibility, safety, and main access complication management using VARC-3 outcomes definitions in 1022 consecutive TF-TAVR patients who were approached using the less invasive technique [17]. Technical success was achieved in 97 % patients. Access-related major vascular complications were observed in 12 patients and VARC-3 ≥ type 2 bleedings in additional 12 patients. At 30-day, all-cause death occurred in 17 patients. This value was significantly lower than expected on the bases of the mortality predicted not only by EuroSCORE II and STS-PROM score, but also by STS-ACC mortality score. Thus, the authors concluded that systematic use of less-invasive totally-endovascular TAVR is feasible and is associated with an extremely low rate of access-related bleeding and vascular complications which may drive to outcome improvement.

Conventional stented, rapid deployment and new-generation stented valves are now available for SAVR. New-generation devices feature advanced tissue treatment for theoretical prolonged durability and a new stent design able to expand in case of future transcatheter valve-in-valve. D’Onofrio et al. analyzed data of 2589 patients from two national multicenter registries and one Institutional database [18]. In isolated SAVR, aortic cross clamp time was shorter for Intuity group. Overall mortality was 2 %, 1.7 % and 0.5 % in Magna Ease, Intuity and Inspiris groups, respectively. Permanent pace-maker implantation rate was lower for Inspiris. Median mean gradients were 13, 10 and 10 mmHg for Magna Ease, Intuity and Inspiris, respectively. D’Onofrio concluded that Inspiris shows low rates of permanent pacemaker implantation and its transaortic gradients are similar to rapid-deployment valves and lower than Magna Ease.

TAVR has shown clear survival benefits in severe AS. However, patients unable to recover LV function remain at risk with poor long-term survival. Moya et al. studied a total of 110 patients with severe AS referred for TAVR and found that patients with a baseline global work index < 2323 mmHg% had significantly worse outcome following TAVR [19]. The addition of biomarkers did not yield any further advantages, whereas incorporating the global work index cut-off value of 2323 mmHg% significantly enhanced the predictive value. Thus, the authors concluded that our findings provide evidence for the enhanced usefulness of myocardial work analysis in the initial risk stratification of patients with severe AS referred for TAVR. Specifically, a baseline global work index <2323 mmHg% demonstrates an independent predictor associated with increased incidence of all-cause mortality and heart failure (HF) hospitalization following TAVR.

Bleeding is frequent during TAVR, especially when performed through a transapical approach, and is associated with a worse prognosis. Radico et al. tested the implication of red blood cell transfusion and the optimal transfusion strategy in this context [20]. Among 11,265 participants in the multicenter TRITAVI (Transfusion Requirements in Transcatheter Aortic Valve Implantation) registry, 548 patients (4.9 %) who received transapical TAVR at 19 European centers were included. The primary endpoint of the study, i.e. the 30-day occurrence of all-cause mortality, occurred in 47 (8.6 %) patients. At multivariable cox regression analysis, blood transfusion was an independent predictor of 30-day mortality as well as baseline ejection fraction and acute kidney injury. Based on these results, Radico et al. concluded that red blood cell transfusion is an independent predictor of short-term mortality in patients undergoing transapical TAVR, regardless of major bleeding.

TAVR is being currently employed in patients with low surgical risk and severe AS. The durability and extended outcomes of TAVR as compared to SAVR in low-risk patients remains uncertain. Rahman selected randomized controlled trials (RCT) comparing outcomes of TAVR vs. SAVR in low surgical risk patients having severe AS using online databases [21]. In 6 RCTs comprising 5122 subjects, there was a significant reduction in all-cause death and composite of all-cause mortality and disabling stroke in patients undergoing TAVR. At intermediate-term follow-up, there was no significant difference in survival and composite outcome.

Rangel et al. investigated the change in severity of mitral regurgitation (MR) after TAVR and its effect on 5-year mortality [22]. A total of 820 patients were included in this study. Of these, 167 patients showed an improvement in MR grade. Systolic pulmonary artery pressure and NT-proBNP decreased in patients who had an improvement. There was no significant difference in 5-year mortality for MR at baseline or reduction in mortality for patients with an MR improvement compared to patients with worsening or equal MR status.

Blood levels of NT-proBNP has been suggested as a prognostic tool for the selection of patients for SAVR. Marqvard Sørensen et al. examined how levels of NT-proBNP pre-TAVR is associated with one-year rates of HF admission and mortality following TAVR [21]. Using Danish nationwide registries, the authors identified all patients undergoing TAVR from 2014 to 2021 who had at least one recorded NT-pro-BNP measurement within one year before TAVR. The authors selected 1140 patients undergoing first-time TAVR with a recorded NT-pro-BNP; 846 with a low NT-proBNP (<420 pmol/L) and 294 (25.8 %) with a high NT-proBNP (≥420 pmol/L). A high versus low NT-proBNP was associated with increased one-year cumulative incidence of HF-admissions and all-cause mortality. A high NT-proBNP was associated with higher rates of outcomes irrespective of previously known AF, HF, chronic kidney disease, and hypertension.

Owing to the minimally invasive nature of TAVR, TAVR seems to be preferred in patients with cancer; however, related research on the clinical efficacy and safety of TAVR in patients with cancer and severe AS is limited, and outcomes are inconsistent. Osawa et al. evaluated the clinical outcomes of patients with cancer who underwent TAVR [23]. They analyzed 15 studies (195,658 patients) published in PubMed and Cochrane Library databases between January 2022 and January 2023. Patients with cancer had a lower risk of short-term mortality, but a higher risk of long-term mortality than those without cancer. Patients with cancer had a lower incidence of postprocedural stroke and acute kidney injury but a higher incidence of pacemaker implantation than patients without cancer.

Red blood cell distribution width is calculated in every blood count test and reflects variability in erythrocyte size. High levels mirror dysregulated erythrocyte homeostasis and have been associated with clonal hematopoiesis as well as higher mortality in several conditions. Stachel et al. determined the impact of preprocedural red blood cell distribution width levels on functional outcomes after TAVR [24]. The authors studied 176 consecutive patients receiving TAVR between 2017 and 2021 and found that 43 patients (24 %) had distribution width ≥ 15 %. Of note, red blood cell distribution width was an independent predictor of the composite endpoint when accounting for all baseline differences in multivariable regression. The authors concluded that elevated preprocedural red blood cell width identifies patients at risk for impaired functional outcome after TAVR and may represent a useful low-cost parameter to guide intensity of outpatient surveillance strategies.

The clinical impact of baseline MR on the outcomes after TAVR is not well validated. Zheng et al. assessed the clinical impact of baseline MR on outcomes after TAVR in 120 consecutive patients with severe AS undergoing TAVR [25]. Overall survival in patients with nonsignificant and significant baseline MR was not significantly different, while patients from the improved MR group after TAVR demonstrated a significantly higher survival than unchanged or worsened MR group during 2-year follow-up. NYHA functional class had generally improved at 1 year, with only 8 % of patients with nonsignificant MR and 17 % of patients with significant MR in class III or IV. In conclusion, significant baseline MR was not associated with the increased risk of all-cause mortality 2 years after TAVR. Significant baseline MR was improved in most patients at 1 year after TAVR. Patients with unchanged or worsened MR had an increased all-cause mortality.

Information regarding the outcomes of TAVR in men is limited. Nishida et al. investigated short- to mid-term outcomes and prognostic predictors in this population [26]. The data of 519 men were analyzed from 1693 consecutive patients with symptomatic severe AS who underwent TAVR at six hospitals between April 2010 and July 2020. In multivariate analysis, the serum albumin level, AF and STS score were independently associated with all-cause mortality following TAVR. In conclusion, the authors demonstrated that serum albumin levels, AF, and STS score were independently associated with all-cause mortality following TAVR in men.

The prognostic implications of post-operative AF in conjunction with TAVR is sparsely examined. Petersen et al. examined the impact of first-time detected post-operative AF after TAVR on all-cause mortality and HF [27]. Overall, the authors identified 6807 patients surviving 30 days beyond TAVR: 4229 without AF, 2283 with history of AF, and 291 with first-time detected post-operative AF. Compared with patients without AF, adjusted analysis yielded increased associated hazard ratio of all-cause mortality in patients with history of AF and in patients with first-time detected post procedural AF. Further, they observed increased associated HRs of HF admissions in patients with history of AF and in patients with first-time detected postoperative AF.

Vascular injury and bleeding complications remain frequent after TAVR. Whether the access-site of preprocedural coronary angiography affects TAVR-related complications is not known. Al-Kassou et al. evaluated the impact of transradial versus transfemoral access for preprocedural coronary angiography on outcomes in patients undergoing subsequent TAVR [28]. The authors included 1002 patients undergoing transfemoral TAVR, of whom 39 % had undergone radial and 60 % femoral access for pre-TAVR angiography. Results showed that transradial access for preprocedural coronary artery angiography was associated with significantly lower rates of vascular complications following subsequent TAVR as compared to transfemoral access. However, despite the tendency to lower major bleedings with transradial access, no significant association was detectable between the access-site of coronary angiography and TAVR-related bleeding complications.

Kofoed Petersen et al. showed that age is not significantly associated with pacemaker implantation after TAVR, but that there is still a need for further investigation on differences in other TAVR-related complications across age groups [29].

Kumar et al. showed that patients with AF and severe AS undergoing TAVI with CHA2DS2-VASc and HASBLED scores ≥ 3 have a high incidence of stroke and bleeding-related events compared to those without and may benefit from consideration for left atrial appendage occlusion and TAVR [30].

Finally, Petrou et al. performed a systematic review to update the existing body of evidence regarding the cost-effectiveness of TAVR [31]. The authors identified 33 economic evaluations of transcatheter aortic valve implantations. Results were not consistent, ranging from dominant to dominating. Moreover, the models were sensitive to an array of variables. The methodological quality of the studies was good. Thus, this systematic review highlighted areas which merit further research regarding the pillars of cost-effectiveness analysis such as modeling, the extrapolation of available data and the uncertainty of the evidence. The clinical implication is that a thorough assessment of the patient should proceed any decision-making.

It is well known that epicardial adipose tissue (EAT) is associated with the development of atrial fibrillation (AF) [32]. Li et al. investigated whether EAT density is associated with the development of new-onset AF after SAVR [33]. Of 143 patients undergoing SAVR, 55 patients (38 %) developed AF after surgery. Male and older patients with smoking history were more likely to develop post-operative AF. On univariable analysis, patients who developed AF had significantly more EAT than patients without evidence of AF. On multivariable analysis, EAT and age were independent risk factors for new onset AF. Furthermore, EAT was significantly associated with age and cardiac structure changes, such as cardiac left ventricular (LV) end-diastolic and end-systolic volumes and NT-proBNP before surgery [33].

Mitral valve disease and repair

Kempton et al. reviewed the pathophysiology of pulmonary hypertension in LV dysfunction and MR, the utility of pulmonary arterial compliance as a measure of right ventricle (RV) afterload, and its calculation for clinical use and interpretation, underlining the utility of pulmonary arterial compliance as an adjunct for assessing pulmonary vascular hemodynamics [34]. Pulmonary hypertension is a common and prognostically important complication of MR and mean pulmonary arterial pressure and pulmonary vascular resistance are traditionally used to diagnose pulmonary hypertension. However, these indices measure static rather than pulsatile load, leading to an incomplete representation of pulmonary vascular load. Pulmonary arterial compliance is one method for quantifying pulsatile load, and is both a stronger predictor of prognosis in LV dysfunction, as well as a more sensitive measure of early pulmonary vascular dysfunction than pulmonary vascular resistance. With the expansion of transcatheter mitral and tricuspid valve therapies, there is renewed interest to more accurately define the load imposed by the pulmonary vasculature on the RV, especially in the early phase, prior to the onset of chronic pulmonary hypertension.

Yeo's Index, product of the mitral leaflet separation index and dimensionless index, is a novel measure of the severity of rheumatic mitral stenosis (MS). Leow et al. assessed Yeo's index in 237 patients with rheumatic MS with or without mixed valve disease.

There were 113 patients with isolated MS and 124 patients with mixed valve disease [35]. Overall, Yeo's index ≤ 0.26 cm showed 93 % sensitivity and 87 % specificity for identifying severe MS (mitral valve area ≤ 1.5 cm2). In isolated MS, Yeo's index ≤ 0.26 cm showed sensitivity of 94 % and specificity of 90 % for identifying severe MS, while in mixed valve disease sensitivity was 90 % and specificity 86 %.

Efficacy of balloon mitral valvuloplasty in low gradient severe rheumatic MS is not very well defined. Yusuf et al. evaluated the outcomes of mitral valvuloplasty in low gradient severe rheumatic MS [36]. Sixty patients were divided into normal-flow/low-gradient (N = 40) and low-flow/low-gradient (N = 20) groups. The authors showed that symptomatic improvement following balloon mitral valvuloplasty was better seen in the normal flow group because of greater decrease in mitral gradient in comparison to the low flow group. Results of balloon mitral valvuloplasty were suboptimal in low flow group because of higher sub-valvular obstruction, increased age and higher prevalence of AF.

Mitral annular disjunction (MAD), i.e. the embryologic fibrous separation between mitral annular ring and basal LV myocardium, plays a controversial role in arrhythmic mitral valve prolapse (MVP). Hussain et evaluated 100 MVP patients undergoing CMR imaging (52 MVP patients with posterior MAD) and found that MVP patients with MAD were more likely to have an abnormal basal inferolateral/ papillary muscles LGE [37]. Posterior MAD longitudinal disjunction gap was a predictor of ventricular tachycardia. Thus, the authors concluded that abnormal LGE involving basal inferolateral myocardium and papillary muscles may provide etiologic substrate for arrythmia in MVP patients.

Percutaneous mitral valve repair has evolved to be a standard procedure in suitable patients with MR not accessible for open surgery. Sauter et al. analyzed the influence of the number and positioning of the clips implanted during the procedure on MR reduction [38]. They studied 410 patients with severe MR undergoing mitral valve repair using the MitraClip® System and found an enhanced reduction of MR predominantly in degenerative MR patients who received more than one clip. Implantation of only one clip led to a higher MR reduction in patients with functional MR in comparison to patients with degenerative MR. Also, in patients with functional MR, after 6 months, the reduction of MR was more stable with an increased number of implanted clips, which suggests that this specific patient collective may benefit from a higher number of clips.

Triscupid valve disease and replacement

Computed tomography (CT) plays a crucial role in assessing RV outflow tract morphology. Faure et al. emphasized the role of CT in evaluating also the coronary artery relationship in percutaneous pulmonary valve implantation [39].

Transcatheter tricuspid valve replacement is a novel therapeutic approach to treat tricuspid regurgitation (TR), which is the most common tricuspid valve condition. This timely issue has been discussed by Zaman who highlighted how the landscape of TR with transcatheter tricuspid valve replacement is expanding [40].

Also, Chung et al. assessed the prevalence, clinical significance, and economic impact of TR in 21,051 patients with TR, with or without HF [41]. They found that patients with TR and HF or very severe TR are more likely to be hospitalized, use more healthcare resources, and face higher financial burdens, as compared with patients without HF and/or without severe TR.

The prognostic value of functional TR in patients with pulmonary arterial hypertension and chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension remains undetermined. Veen et al. quantified the prognostic role of TR in relation to RV dysfunction on clinical outcomes and secondarily the evolution of TR and RV dysfunction over time [42]. They studied 76 pulmonary arterial hypertension and 44 chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension and found that the cumulative effect of moderate-to-severe TR and moderate-to-severe RV dysfunction was associated with the endpoint in univariable joint-models. In a multivariable joint-model with both the evolutions of TR and RV dysfunction only TR remained significant. Based on these results, the authors concluded that persistent moderate-to-severe tricuspid valve regurgitation during follow-up predicts adverse outcomes and might be a better predictor of lung transplantation and mortality compared to RV dysfunction.

Conclusions

Although VHD is increasingly becoming a challenging clinical problem the newer pharmacologic and interventional options significantly affect the natural history of the condition, similarly to what has been observed with ischemic heart disease in the last decades [[43], [44], [45], [46], [47]]. The International Journal of Cardiology: Heart & Vasculature welcomes submissions that advance the fundamental and clinical understanding of this growing area.

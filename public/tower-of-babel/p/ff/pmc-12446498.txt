Non-invasive diagnostic value of pericoronary fat attenuation index for identifying culprit lesions in acute coronary syndrome
Authors: Fengfeng Yang, Zhengyang Li, Haoran Cai, Jing Zhu, Huijia Liu, Yang Zhao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesThis study aimed to determine the efficacy of fat attenuation index (FAI) as a non-invasive diagnostic tool in the precise identification of culprit lesions in individuals diagnosed with acute coronary syndrome (ACS).MethodsA retrospective analysis of 230 patients with non-ST-segment elevation ACS. PCAT attenuation (FAIstandard) was measured in the proximal 40-mm segment of each major coronary artery. Furthermore, the average PCAT attenuation of the identified lesions was designated as FAIlesion. The average PCAT attenuation across the complete length of coronary artery, referred to as FAIaverage, was computed. Plaque characteristics (volume, composition) were analyzed via coronary computed tomography angiography. Multivariable logistic regression identified predictors of culprit lesions, and diagnostic performance was assessed using area under the curve (AUC) and decision curve analysis.ResultsCulprit lesions exhibited significantly elevated levels of PCAT attenuation across the parameters of FAIstandard, FAIaverage, and FAIlesion. FAIlesion demonstrated superior diagnostic accuracy versus FAIstandard and FAIaverage, and also emerged as the strongest independent predictor (Odds ratio = 2.598, P < 0.001). In training and test sets, a composite model integrating FAIlesion with additional indices demonstrated enhanced diagnostic efficacy for the detection of culprit lesions in patients with ACS (AUC = 0.960, 0.803). Low-attenuation plaque volume (<30 HU) was independently associated with culprit lesions (OR = 3.12, P = 0.002).ConclusionFAIlesion, a superior non-invasive biomarker for high-risk ACS lesions compared to traditional FAI, enables earlier precise risk stratification through clinical integration.Keywords: Non-invasive diagnostic, Pericoronary fat attenuation index, Acute coronary syndrome, Culprit lesions, Coronary computed tomography angiography

Introduction

Acute coronary syndrome (ACS) represents a major public health concern due to its association with sudden myocardial ischemia, which can lead to substantial morbidity and mortality [1]. Currently, conventional diagnostic approaches for ACS predominantly rely on invasive procedures, particularly coronary angiography. While these techniques are considered reliable, they often involve considerable risks [2], [3], including procedural complications, high costs, and patient discomfort. Consequently, there is an urgent need to develop non-invasive diagnostic modalities capable of accurately identifying individuals at risk of ACS while minimizing associated risks and financial burdens [4].

Recent advancements in imaging methodologies suggest that pericoronary adipose tissue can play a crucial role in the pathogenesis of coronary artery disease [5], [6], [7]. Several studies have identified correlations between the pericoronary fat attenuation index (FAI) and the inflammatory mechanisms associated with atherogenesis [8], underscoring the potential role of FAI as an innovative biomarker for the detection of responsible lesions in individuals with ACS [9]. The prospect of employing the FAI as a diagnostic instrument is rooted in its capacity to represent localized coronary inflammation, a notable element that influences plaque instability and the occurrence of subsequent cardiovascular incidents.

In this context, the present study aimed to determine the efficacy of FAI as a non-invasive diagnostic tool in the precise identification of culprit lesions in individuals diagnosed with ACS.

Materials and methods

Patients

This study enrolled 267 individuals diagnosed with non-ST-segment elevation ACS (NSTE-ACS) who underwent coronary computed tomography angiography (CCTA), followed by invasive coronary angiography (ICA) between January 2023 and January 2025. All patients underwent ICA within three days post-CCTA. This study was reviewed and sanctioned by the ethics committee of our institution. Informed consent, documented in writing, was secured from all participants and their guardians.

NSTE-ACS includes non-ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI) and unstable angina (UA). NSTEMI is characterized by the presence of ischemic symptoms accompanied by elevated cardiac biomarkers, while lacking ST-segment elevation on electrocardiogram (ECG). Conversely, UA is defined by the occurrence of new or worsening chest pain (either upon exertion or at rest) within a two-week timeframe, without any associated biomarker release. The present excluded 21 patients who had undergone prior coronary revascularization (including coronary stenting or bypass grafting), 8 with a history of myocardial infarction, 5 whose CCTA images were of suboptimal quality, and 3 patients with significant lesions located in the diagonal branches. Consequently, our analysis focused on patients with NSTE-ACS exhibiting major coronary artery stenosis of 50 % or greater. Ultimately, 230 patients were included in the study analysis. The dataset was divided randomly, allocating 70 % and 30 % of the data to the training and test sets, respectively.

We gathered and examined data related to several personal characteristics, encompassing age, gender, hypertension status, hyperlipidemia, diabetes mellitus, smoking behavior, alcohol consumption, familial occurrences of coronary artery disease, electrocardiogram results, as well as levels of low-density lipoprotein, high-density lipoprotein (HDL), B-type natriuretic peptide, glucose, cholesterol, and triglycerides.

Coronary CTA

Data were collected using a 256-multidetector row computed tomography (CT) scanner (Revolution Apex; GE Healthcare). For patients whose heart rates exceeded 80 beats per minute, oral metoprolol (25–50 mg) was administered to ensure a reduction in heart rates to below 70 beats per minute prior to imaging. A non-ionic iodine-based contrast medium (350 mg/L) was injected using a high-pressure injector, with the volume of contrast administered varying from 60 to 70 mL, depending on the patient's body mass index. CT imaging was performed using the following parameters: gantry rotation time of 280 ms, a tube voltage of 120 kV, and an average radiation exposure of 1.0 ± 0.5 mSv. Each subject was positioned supine, and connected to an ECG monitor, after which a ventricular layer monitoring scan was performed. A prospective ECG-gated trigger sequence scan was subsequently carried out using a contrast agent group injection tracking method, demonstrating the common application of a bolus-triggered approach. The quality of the images was categorized into four distinct grades, with only those classified as good to excellent being selected for further analysis. Two experienced senior radiologists independently reviewed the images, with any discrepancies resolved through collaborative discussions.

FAI analysis

Pericoronary adipose tissue (PCAT) attenuation was assessed using semi-automated software (UIH-AI-Cardiac-Clinical-v3.0, United Imaging Healthcare). In this context, PCAT was characterized in cases where all voxels situated within a radial distance from the coronary outer wall equivalent to the vessel's diameter exhibited CT attenuation values ranging from -190 to -30 Hounsfield Units (HU). The PCAT attenuation itself was quantified as the mean CT attenuation of adipose tissue within the specified region of interest. Following the methodology outlined by Oikonomou et al. [8], PCAT attenuation (referred to as FAIstandard) was measured in the proximal 40-mm segment of the left anterior descending coronary artery (LAD) and the left circumflex coronary artery (LCx), as well as in the proximal segment ranging from 10 to 50 mm of the right coronary artery. Furthermore, the average PCAT attenuation of the identified lesions, designated as FAIlesion, was assessed along the length of the culprit and non-culprit lesions. To gauge the degree of panvascular inflammation, the average PCAT attenuation across the complete length of each major coronary artery, referred to as FAIaverage, was computed. Representative images illustrating the FAI analysis are displayed in Figure S1. This analysis was carried out by two readers, each possessing over five years of experience in cardiac CT, who were unaware of the clinical characteristics, ICA data, and prior clinical CCTA interpretations. All analyses conducted in this study were performed in a blinded manner.

CCTA-based plaque quantitative characteristics

The total volumes of the identified plaques, calcified plaques (＞350 HUs), non-calcified plaques (31–350 HUs), and low attenuation plaques (-30 to 30 HUs) [10], were assessed via a semi-automated process with per lesion and per patient. Mean Hounsfield Unit (HU) values, overall lesional volume, and the relative proportions of plaque volumes were determined using a semi-automated threshold-based segmentation technique (UIH-AI-Cardiac-Clinical-v3.0, United Imaging Healthcare) focusing on the culprit and non-culprit lesions.

ICA

ICA was conducted using a Toshiba digital subtraction angiography X-ray machine (Infinix, Toshiba). A 6-Fr sheath catheter was introduced following percutaneous puncture of the right radial artery via the Seldinger technique. Subsequently, angiographic catheters targeting the left and right coronary arteries were advanced to their respective openings. Comprehensive visualization of all coronary artery branches was achieved from various angles and projection positions. The degree of coronary stenosis in each lesion was evaluated, and the associated Gensini scores were computed accordingly. In accordance with the clinical guidelines [11], the culprit lesion was identified by the cardiologists who performed the procedure based on electrocardiographic and coronary angiographic findings. The non-culprit lesion was strictly defined as the most stenotic lesions identified by ICA in the absence of any identifiable culprit lesions. For patients presenting with multiple lesions, only the single most severe stenosis meeting these criteria was selected for analysis. This procedure was executed by two cardiologists, each possessing a minimum of 10 years of experience, who remained unaware of the results from the CTA and FAI.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using R software (version 4.0.1) in conjunction with MedCalc 15.6 software. Continuous variables are expressed as means and standard deviations or medians with interquartile ranges. In contrast, categorical variables were evaluated using chi-square tests. To illustrate interrelations among model variables, we created heatmaps and column charts, thereby providing clear visual representations of the principal analytical results. Comparisons were conducted utilizing Student's t-test, paired-samples t-test, or the Mann-Whitney U test, as deemed necessary. Comparisons of categorical data involved the chi-square or Fisher's exact tests, as appropriate. Univariable and multivariable analyses were performed to identify significant predictors of culprit lesions and PCAT attenuation. The efficacy of the multivariable models was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic curves and decision curve analysis (DCA) curves. A threshold for statistical significance was established at P < 0.05.

Comparison of PCAT attenuation across quantification methods

Notable distinctions were identified in the FAIstandard, FAIaverage, and FAIlesion metrics between the groups of culprit and non-culprit lesions (P < 0.05) (Fig. 1). Specifically, FAIlesion exhibited a strong correlation with FAIstandard (r = 0.72; P < 0.001) and FAIaverage (r = 0.76; P < 0.001) (Figure S2). Bland-Altman analysis indicated only statistically insignificant systematic bias between FAIstandard and FAIaverage. In contrast, considerable variations were observed when evaluating the FAIlesion characteristics against the FAIstandard and FAIaverage (Fig. 2). The paired sample t-test confirmed significant differences between FAIlesion with FAIstandard (P < 0.01) and FAIaverage (P < 0.001) (Fig. 3). FAIlesion exhibited a markedly higher level than either FAIstandard and FAIaverage. Conversely, no significant difference was observed when FAIstandard was compared with FAIaverage (p = 0.57).

Discussion

This investigation represents the first effort to directly compare coronary inflammatory activity between culprit and non-culprit lesions in patients with ACS, using PCAT attenuation as an innovative inflammatory biomarker. The principal findings of this study are as follows: 1) Culprit lesions exhibited significantly elevated levels of PCAT attenuation across the parameters of FAIstandard, FAIaverage, and FAIlesion, indicating a heightened degree of inflammatory activity; 2) Among the indicators assessed, FAIlesion exhibited the highest diagnostic accuracy for identifying culprit lesions, with an AUC of 0.928, and also emerged as the strongest independent predictor (Odds ratio = 2.598, P < 0.001); 3). A composite model integrating FAIlesion with additional indices demonstrated enhanced diagnostic efficacy for the detection of culprit lesions in patients with ACS.

Vascular inflammation has recently emerged as a crucial component in the advancement of atherosclerosis and the mechanisms underlying ACS [12]. Prior research has further suggested that ACS predominantly results from the rupture of plaques rather than their erosion [13], [14]. Based on this premise, we proposed that lesions in patients with ACS are characterized by heightened perivascular inflammation, commonly linked to plaque rupture and high-risk attributes, including extensive lipid deposits, fragile fibrous caps, and macrophage infiltration [15], [16], thus serving as a significant biomarker for vascular inflammation [9], [17]. By monitoring the changes in CT attenuation within pericoronary adipose tissue, investigations have shown that the reduction of vascular inflammation leads to notable modifications in local adipogenesis [18], [19]. Thus, FAI facilitates the non-invasive identification of vascular inflammation via CCTA. Inflammatory stimuli within the perivascular area influence adipocyte differentiation and alter the lipid-to-water ratio, resulting in increases in the attenuation values in the adjacent adipose tissue [20]. This study demonstrated that the attenuation of PCAT is considerably greater in proximity to culprit lesions than non-culprit lesions in patients with ACS, particularly regarding the parameters of FAIstandard, FAIaverage, and FAIlesion.

Prior investigations have identified PCAT situated near the proximal RCA as a crucial biomarker for the assessment of coronary inflammation [21], [22]. Nevertheless, it is essential to acknowledge the potential for significant variations in the inflammatory characteristics of PCAT across different coronary vessels, such as LAD and LCx arteries. These variations may be particularly pronounced in the context of heterogeneous plaque vulnerability distributions, indicating that PCAT located at the RCA proximal region may not adequately represent the inflammatory heterogeneity present throughout the entire coronary system [23]. Recent research trends have advocated for a comprehensive analysis of multi-vessel PCAT to enhance the biological representativeness and clinical predictive capabilities of different inflammatory markers [9], [24]. We posited that manifestations of PCAT would be more prominent at lesion sites exhibiting stronger correlations with inflammation levels. Although we referred to established measurement protocols when assessing pericoronary adipose tissue inflammation at lesion sites, these traditional methodologies may encounter limitations when addressing diffuse or focal lesions. Therefore, in the present study, we not only measured proximal PCAT in all participants, but also calculated the FAI at lesion sites, in addition to the average PCAT attenuation throughout the vessel. The results of these analyses demonstrated positive correlations between FAIlesion and FAIstandard (r = 0.72), as well as between FAIlesion and FAIaverage (r = 0.76). Bland-Altman analyses revealed minimal bias between FAIstandard and FAIaverage, which is consistent with prior findings indicating that proximal PCAT reflects systemic coronary inflammation [25]. However, significant discrepancies were noted in the Bland-Altman comparisons between FAIlesion-FAIstandard and FAIlesion-FAIaverage. Further statistical comparisons revealed notable differences between FAIlesion and FAIstandard/FAIaverage, while no significant difference was found between FAIstandard and FAIaverage. We hypothesize that the culprit lesion site would exhibit heightened inflammatory activity, leading to relative elevations in FAIlesion values. This hypothesis supports our assertion that PCAT presents a more advantageous anatomical position and a stronger inflammatory correlation within culprit lesions [26]. In our findings, FAIaverage and FAIlesion emerged as independent predictors of culprit lesions in patients with ACS, with the latter showing the strongest predictive capacity (odds ratio = 2.598, P < 0.001). Given that the adipose tissue can secrete bioactive substances that influence adjacent vascular walls through endocrine and paracrine mechanisms [27], our results are consistent with histological evidence of localized inflammation in patients with ACS [28].

In the subgroup analysis, culprit lesions were found to possess a markedly elevated volume of low attenuation (<30 HU) compared with non-culprit lesions among patients with NSTEMI and UA (P < 0.05). This observation reinforces the significant pathological involvement of the necrotic core—a characteristic indicative of high-risk plaques—in the pathogenesis of ACS, which is consistent with previous research findings [29], [30]. Nonetheless, the efficacy of identifying culprit lesions solely through quantitative plaque composition analysis currently remains inadequate. Our analysis revealed a moderate level of diagnostic accuracy for the identification of culprit lesions, achieving an AUC of 0.682 (95 % CI: 0.599–0.765), which is numerically inferior to that obtained via FAI-based evaluation.

The ramifications of these results have significant relevance for clinical practice, particularly in the context of managing ACS. The capability to precisely recognize high-risk individuals utilizing FAI metrics and biochemical indicators may facilitate more focused therapeutic approaches. Our findings further indicate that individuals exhibiting elevated FAIlesion are markedly more predisposed to culprit lesions, thus providing critical insights for early intervention strategies that could enhance patient outcomes.

Limitations

This study has several limitations primarily attributable to its retrospective cohort design, which may introduce biases related to selection and data collection processes. While CCTA reveals crucial anatomical information, the dependence on previously collected imaging data could restrict the applicability of our results to more diverse populations. Moreover, the reliability of the pericoronary FAI as a biomarker may be influenced by variations in imaging protocols and patient characteristics that were not consistently regulated across the study cohort. Furthermore, this study's focus on a specific patient demographic may limit the generalizability of our findings to other populations, including those with varying comorbidities or risk factors. This limits generalizability to the broader ACS population, particularly those with STEMI or requiring emergent intervention. Additionally, the lack of long-term clinical validation assessments raises several questions regarding the enduring predictive capability of the pericoronary FAI metrics over longer durations. Future investigations should therefore focus on incorporating larger, multi-center cohorts and evaluating the diagnostic effectiveness of the FAI over prolonged follow-up periods to enhance understanding of its practical application in clinical settings.

Conclusion

In summary, this study highlights the potential of pericoronary FAI as a promising non-invasive biomarker for detecting culprit lesions in patients with ACS. Pericoronary FAI adds clinically-relevant prognostic information beyond traditional risk factors. Among the pericoronary FAI variants examined, FAIlesion emerged as the most accurate discriminator of future ACS events and the only variant that retained independent predictive power in fully-adjusted models. FAIlesion provides a non-invasive tool to improve risk stratification in ACS, enabling targeted therapies for high-risk patients and reducing reliance on invasive procedures. These findings support integrating FAIlesion into routine CT reporting to refine individual risk stratification and guide earlier preventive therapy. Future studies should focus on prospectively validating this biomarker and investigating the biological mechanisms connecting the pericoronary adipose tissue with coronary artery disease, ultimately aiming to enhance patient outcomes in the management of ACS. In addition, criminal lesions in NSTEMI and UA patients have significantly increased low attenuation volume (<30HU) compared to non-criminal lesions, thereby enhancing clinical relevance for coronary CTA interpretation and plaque-vulnerability assessment.

Predictive value of a combined nomogram model integrating ultrasound and cytology for 2025 ATA risk stratification of lymph node negative papillary thyroid carcinoma
Authors: Xiachuan Qin, Xiaoling Liu, Fanding He, Weihan Xiao, Xianjun Ye
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesThis study aimed to predict the 2025 American Thyroid Association (ATA) risk stratification of lymph node negative (N0) papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) patients using preoperative ultrasonic and cytological features to provide guidance for clinical treatment strategies.MethodsIn this multicenter, retrospective study, 500 N0 PTC patients who underwent total thyroidectomy with lymph node dissection were included across 2 institutions from September 2018 to February 2024. Patients were categorized into low, intermediate-high recurrence risk groups based on the 2025 ATA risk stratification system. Univariate logistic regression analysis was performed to assess the relationship between ultrasonic and cytological features and 2025 ATA risk stratification. Significant features (P < 0.05) were then incorporated into a multivariate logistic regression model to identify independent predictors of risk stratification. A Nomogram was constructed using predictors from the final multivariate logistic regression.ResultsPapillary like arrangement, Escape like arrangement, Nucleolus, Size, Echo, Margin, and Extracapsular extension (ECE) were identified as independent predictors of 2025 ATA risk stratification. A Nomogram model was developed based on these predictors, demonstrating good discrimination with a C-index of 0.799. The calibration curve further demonstrated excellent consistency between the predicted and actual preoperative 2025 ATA risk stratifications. Additionally, the Nomogram displayed a C-index of 0.778 in the testing cohort.ConclusionsRisk stratification in N0 PTC patients correlates with factors such as Papillary arrangement, Escape like arrangement, Nucleolus, Size, Echo, Margin, and ECE, emphasizing the necessity of closely monitoring patients presenting with these risk factors. Additionally, the Nomogram model integrating seven preoperative risk factors specifically tailored for solitary N0 PTC patients was devised, showcasing notable predictive accuracy for preoperative 2025 ATA risk stratification.Keywords: Papillary thyroid carcinoma, ATA risk stratification, Cytology, Ultrasound, Nomogram

Introduction

Thyroid carcinoma, particularly of the highly differentiated type, stands out as the most prevalent malignancy within the endocrine system, with an estimated 570,000 new cases diagnosed worldwide annually [1]. In the United States alone, approximately 44,000 new cases of thyroid cancer are reported each year [2]. Papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) notably represents the most common subtype among differentiated thyroid malignancies, constituting approximately 90% of thyroid cancer diagnosed of 2017 [3]. For high risk PTC cases, there is a widely held belief in the necessity of total thyroidectomy accompanied by lymph node dissection to mitigate the risk of recurrence [4, 5]. Conversely, in instances of low risk PTC, active surveillance [2, 6], or minimally invasive interventions such as ablation, may be preferable [4, 7]. However, the current landscape of thyroid cancer treatment faces criticism for both overdiagnosis and overtreatment. Furthermore, ongoing debates persist regarding the efficacy and potential complications associated with routine prophylactic central neck dissection [8–10]. These controversies stem from the limitations of existing preoperative diagnostic modalities in accurately stratifying patient risk. The risk stratification for PTC relies on the 2025 ATA Thyroid Cancer Guidelines, which propose a four-tiered clinical-pathological risk stratification system categorizing patients as low, low-intermediate, intermediate-high and high risk of recurrence. Precise risk stratification is paramount for customizing treatment plans effectively. Unfortunately, certain risk factors crucial to stratification are only discernible following the receipt of the final histological report.

Ultrasound is widely recognized as the foremost diagnostic tool for evaluating thyroid nodules owing to its accessibility, high resolution, safety profile devoid of radiation, and provision of multidimensional real-time imaging capabilities [11]. It serves as the primary imaging modality for assessing thyroid nodules, enabling confirmation of nodule size, location, ultrasonic features, and the associated risk of malignancy [5, 12]. Fine-needle aspiration (FNA) biopsy is considered a vital adjunct to ultrasound examination and is acknowledged as the most accurate and cost-effective method for evaluating thyroid nodules. The ATA risk stratification system acknowledges the limitations of individual diagnostic modalities. Consequently, current tumor examinations increasingly rely on multimodal data to address complex clinical issues. Ultrasound examination offers a macroscopic view of tumors, whereas cytological examination provides a microscopic perspective. Integrating both macroscopic and microscopic tumor information enhances predictive efficacy through complementary data. Studies have demonstrated that combining cytological with ultrasonic features can significantly increase the cancer risk assessment of indeterminate thyroid nodules to nearly 80% [13]. Therefore, we posit that integrating ultrasonic and cytological features of tumors can more accurately stratify preoperative risk, aid in predicting the extent of initial disease, and facilitate the determination of the medical management plan for PTC.

Materials and methods

The study was performed in agreement with the Declaration of Helsinki. This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board. The requirement for informed consent was waived due to the retrospective study design. A total of 396 cases of PTC were retrieved from the thyroid ultrasound image repositories of 2 medical centers spanning from September 2018 to February 2024. All enrolled patients underwent thyroidectomy along with prophylactic lymph node dissection. Additionally, an external testing cohort comprising 104 patients who underwent ultrasound examination at one medical center was included. Inclusion criteria were: (1) Patients undergoing thyroidectomy with lymph node dissection; (2) Postoperative pathological diagnosis of PTC;3. Thyroid ultrasound and FNA examination performed within one week before surgery; 4. Preoperative ultrasound indicated no abnormal lymph nodes in the neck. Exclusion criteria were: 1. Multifocal PTCs; 2. Patients under 18 years old; (3) Missing or poor-quality ultrasound images; (4) Missing or poor quality of cytological images.

Histopathological analysis of surgical specimens serves as the reference standard. Data collection includes recording the number and maximum size of distant metastases, extracapsular extension, vascular invasion, and cervical lymph node metastases [5]. Patients were then strictly categorized into low, low-intermediate, intermediate-high and high risk risk of recurrence based exclusively on the 2025 ATA risk stratification system [14].

low risk (tumor confined to the thyroid, complete resection, no extrathyroidal extension, no vascular invasion, no high-risk histological subtypes, and node-negative or ≤ 5 micrometastatic lymph nodes), low–intermediate risk (microscopic invasion of perithyroid soft tissues, presence of high-risk subtypes or vascular invasion, or ≤ 5 metastatic lymph nodes < 3 cm), intermediate–high risk (microscopic invasion of posterior thyroid structures, lateral cervical lymph node metastasis < 3 cm, vascular invasion ≥ 4 foci, or aggressive histological subtypes), and high risk (gross extrathyroidal extension, incomplete resection, distant metastasis, metastatic lymph nodes ≥ 3 cm or with extranodal extension, extensive vascular invasion, or poorly differentiated carcinoma).Due to limitations in patient numbers, low–intermediate risk, intermediate-high risk and high risk patients are combined, and further subdivided into low risk and intermediate to high risk groups. Ultrasound image analysis

Ultrasound images are routinely acquired, with examining physicians blinded to the patient’s clinical information. Specifically, all patients undergo neck ultrasound examination within one week preceding their surgery. Two ultrasound physicians, each possessing 7 and 8 years of experience in thyroid ultrasound diagnosis, respectively, independently interpret all preoperative ultrasonic features without access to clinicopathologic results. In the event of disparities between the interpretations of the two radiologists, a third senior ultrasound physician, with 16 years of experience in thyroid ultrasound diagnosis, conducts a review of these features and renders the final decision. The imaging features of nodules that are considered include: (a) Size, (b) Echo (Assessment of the nodule’s echogenicity compared to adjacent thyroid tissue, categorized as high, isoechoic, or hypoechoic. Hypoechoic nodules are further defined as markedly hypoechoic if their echogenicity is lower than that of adjacent strap muscle echogenicity. High and isoechoic cases are grouped together due to their low frequency), (c) Shape (regular or irregular), (d) Margin (burr or smooth), (e) Aspect ratio (aspect ratio ≥ 1; aspect ratio < 1), (f) Location (Determined from preoperative ultrasound longitudinal images, with each lobe divided into three regions (upper, middle, and lower). Tumor location is then classified accordingly); j. Calcification (absence of Calcification, microcalcifications (≤ 1 mm in diameter), and large calcifications (> 1 mm in diameter)); and h. Extracapsular extension (ECE) (Identified through ultrasound findings suggestive of perithyroidal involvement, characterized by the absence of normal thyroid tissue between the border of the cancerous nodule and the thyroid capsule. Extra perithyroidal invasion is defined by normal thyroid contour bulging and loss of the echogenic envelope.) [15–17]. Additionally, clinical features considered include gender, age, and the presence of concomitant chronic lymphocytic thyroiditis (CCLT).

To assess the reproducibility of ultrasound feature interpretation, the inter-observer agreement was evaluated. A random subset of 50 patients was independently reviewed by the two sonographers mentioned above. Cohen’s kappa coefficient (κ) was calculated for each categorical ultrasonic feature. The agreement ranged from substantial to almost perfect: Size (κ = 1.000, machine-measured), Location (κ = 0.936), Echo pattern (κ = 0.817), Margin (κ = 0.911), Shape (κ = 0.901), Aspect ratio (κ = 0.905), Calcification (κ = 0.904), CCLT (κ = 0.834), and ECE (κ = 0.790). These results indicate excellent consistency in the interpretation of preoperative ultrasound features.

Cytology image analysis

FNA biopsies are routinely performed in daily clinical practice, with the examining physician blinded to the patient’s clinical information. Specifically, all patients undergo FNA cytology examination within one week prior to surgery. Two thyroid cytologists, one possessing 5 years of experience and the other 6 years of experience in diagnostic thyroid cytology, respectively, independently interpret the preoperative cytologic features without access to clinicopathologic results. In instances where there is a discrepancy between the interpretations of the two cytologists, a third senior thyroid cytologist, with 10 years of diagnostic thyroid cytology experience, conducts a review of these features and renders the final decision. Cytologic features of the nodule included: (a) True papillary arrangement, (b) Papillary arrangement, (c) Whirlpool arrangement, (d) Escape like arrangement(Nail gland cells in FNA samples exhibit a “scattered” or deviated arrangement from the true nipple or papillary structure, with increased gaps between cells or arranged in a radial/scattered pattern), (e) Nuclear enlargement, (f) Nuclear elongation, (g) Nuclear crowding, (h) Nuclear overlap, (i) Nuclear mold, (j) Nucleus outline, k. Nuclear sulcus, l. Intranuclear pseudoinclusions, m. Dust core, n. Nucleolus (The nucleolus score is based on salience and number per cell.Prominent nucleolus: The nucleolus is significantly higher than the surrounding chromatin, making it easier to observe.Quantity score: ≥ 1 positive nucleus in each cell, multiple nucleoli can be recorded), o. Sand body, p. Multinucleated giant cells [18, 19].

To ensure the reliability of cytological feature assessment, inter-observer agreement was analyzed. The two cytologists mentioned above independently re-evaluated a random sample of 50 cytology specimens. Cohen’s kappa coefficient (κ) was computed for each cytological characteristic. The agreement was substantial for all features, with κ values ranging from 0.82 to 0.92: Papillary arrangement (κ = 0.920), Whirlpool arrangement (κ = 0.880), Escape-like arrangement (κ = 0.848), Nuclear Elongation (κ = 0.820), Nuclear overlap (κ = 0.878), Nuclear mold (κ = 0.901), Nucleus outline (κ = 0.820), Nuclear sulcus (κ = 0.920), Intranuclear pseudoinclusions (κ = 0.878), and Nucleolus (κ = 0.878). These findings confirm high reproducibility in the cytological feature extraction process.

Statistical analysis

To assess the reproducibility of feature interpretation, inter-observer agreement for ultrasound and cytological features was evaluated using Cohen’s kappa coefficient (κ) in a randomly selected subset of 50 patients.

To determine which ultrasonic and cytological features could serve as potential risk factors for 2025 ATA risk stratification, a univariate analysis was conducted in the training cohort to screen for variables with p < 0.05. Then, these variables were further subjected to multivariate logistic regression analysis to identify independent predictive factors (p < 0.05). Subsequently, a Nomogram was developed by combining cytological and ultrasonic features. The discriminative ability of the predictive Nomogram was evaluated using the Harrell consistency index (C-index) [20] which has a similar meaning to the Area Under the Curve (AUC). The highest value of the C-index is 1, indicating perfect discrimination, while the lowest value is 0.5, representing random discrimination. Calibration curves were used to assess the degree of match between the predicted probabilities from our Nomogram and the actual probabilities from ATA risk stratification. The DeLong test method was used to compare the AUC of different models [21].To evaluate the clinical effectiveness of the combination column chart, decision curve analysis (DCA) was performed by calculating the net benefit of the threshold probability range of the training and testing cohorts. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 26.0 (SPSS, Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) and R version 4.2.3 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). The RMS package (version 6.5-0) was specifically used for regression modeling and nomogram construction.

Results

The demographics and baseline clinical characteristics of the patients are presented in Table 1. This study included a total of 500 patients with clinically lymph node-negative PTC who underwent thyroidectomy combined with lymph node dissection. The consistency rate for risk stratification classification between the 2015 and 2025 ATA guidelines in this cohort of lymph node negative papillary thyroid carcinoma patients was 92.3%, with a discordance rate of 7.7%.Among them, 133 patients were male, and 367 patients were female. The average age of the patients was 43.2 ± 12.6 years old, and the average tumor size was 12.1 ± 7.6 mm. There were 216 cases classified as intermediate to high risk and 284 cases classified as low risk. The training cohort consisted of 396 patients, including 106 males and 290 females, with an average age of 43.2 ± 13.0 years old. The intermediate to high group in the training cohort had 177 cases, while the low risk group had 219 cases. The average tumor size in this group was 12.4 ± 7.8 mm. The testing cohort consisted of 104 patients, including 27 males and 77 females, with an average age of 43.2 ± 11.3 years old. The intermediate to high group in this group had 39 cases, while the low risk group had 65 cases. The average tumor size in this group was 10.9 ± 7.0 mm.

Predictive model for ultrasound-cytology features to predict risk stratification of PTC patients

Independent predictors from ultrasound and cytology were included in a multivariate logistic regression, which showed that: Papillary like arrangement (P = 0.012), Escape like arrangement (P = 0.021), Nucleolus (P < 0.001), Size (P < 0.001), Echo (P = 0.034), Margin (P = 0.003), and ECE (P < 0.001) were the independent predictors for determining risk stratification in patients with PTC (Fig. 2). Following this, we synthesized the above seven independent predictors to establish an ultrasound-cytology Nomogram model (Fig. 3A). The Nomogram model showed that Size was the strongest predictor of risk stratification in PTC patients, followed by Nucleolus, Echo, ECE, Escape like arrangement, Margin, and Papillary like arrangement.

Discussion

The ATA risk stratification system is commonly used to estimate the risk of disease recurrence [5, 14], and DTC is managed postoperatively based on its recurrence risk pattern, categorized into low, low-intermediate, intermediate-high and high categories. Individual features are considered to accurately stratify the recurrence risk profile. Minimally invasive interventions are viable options for the lowest risk disease requiring interventional treatment. However, for high nodules, a more aggressive approach involving surgery combined with lymph node dissection is typically chosen [22]. This study additionally validated the consistency of risk stratification for patients with cN0 PTC between the 2015 and 2025 editions of the ATA guidelines, demonstrating an agreement rate of 92.3%.The 2025 revised guidelines primarily refined and subdivided the intermediate-risk category by splitting it into low-intermediate risk and intermediate-high risk. The criteria for low risk and high risk remained essentially unchanged. The core stratification logic—including tumor invasion extent, lymph node status, histological subtypes, vascular invasion, and surgical margin status—was highly consistent with the 2015 guidelines. In this study, we collected preoperative ultrasonic and cytologic features from 396 patients and performed univariate and multivariate analyses to make a systematic analysis of the relationship between ultrasonic and cytologic features and risk stratification of PTC. Our findings showed that Papillary arrangement, Escape like arrangement, Nucleolus, Size, Echo, Margin, and ECE were independent predictors for determining the risk stratification of patients with PTC. In addition, we developed a Nomogram model leveraging the identified metrics, as it allows for the integration of all these variables to individually estimate the risk stratification of each PTC patient. The Nomogram model, utilizing ultrasonic and cytologic features, exhibited strong discriminatory power, yielding an AUC of 0.799 for the ROC curve within the training cohort. Furthermore, in an external testing cohort comprising 104 PTC patients, the ROC curve achieved an AUC of 0.778. This model enables swift and straightforward estimation of the risk stratification for PTC patients. The study to amalgamate preoperative FNA and ultrasonic features for predicting the preoperative risk stratification of PTC offers clinicians a reliable tool.

High-resolution ultrasound is commonly regarded as the preferred method for preoperative assessment of thyroid tumors [23]. However, its effectiveness can be limited in visualizing deeper anatomical structures or those obscured by bone or airborne acoustics [5], resulting in lower diagnostic efficacy for ATA risk stratification. Numerous studies have utilized ultrasonic features to diagnose key risk stratification factors such as lymph node metastases and ECE [24–26], These metrics rely on ultrasonic features to determine tumor aggressiveness. In our research, features including Size, Echo, Margin, and ECE were identified as independent predictors for PTC risk stratification. It should be noted that in this study, echogenicity was analyzed with “low/marked hypoechoic” set as the reference category. The results showed that both “low echoic” and “high/isoechoic” patterns were associated with a lower risk of chronicity (odds ratio < 1). The apparent discrepancy with the TI-RADS principle arises primarily from the difference in the reference point: the TI-RADS system typically uses “high/isoechoic” as the baseline reference, whereas our model employed the widely recognized highest-risk feature—“marked hypoechogenicity”—as the reference standard. This approach more directly reveals a gradient of decreasing risk from marked hypoechogenicity to high/isoechogenicity, which is inherently consistent with the risk stratification logic of TI-RADS. While ultrasonic features often reflect the macroscopic aggressiveness of a tumor, they may overlook crucial microscopic biological information. In Chen, B. D’s study, the echoes of nodules often represent a higher likelihood of CLNM occurrence [27]. Cytologic images, on the other hand, contain subtle and complex details [19]. Recent studies have leveraged cytological features from FNA to assess tumor aggressiveness, with findings suggesting that different cytological findings correlate with distinct clinical tumor phenotypes. Notably, TIR3A/TIR3B cytology predicts a more inert behavior compared to TIR4/TIR5 cytology. Laura Croce posits that varying cytology examinations correspond to diverse clinical tumor phenotypes and that tumor cytology responds to tumor aggressiveness [28]. Our analysis of tumor cytological features through both unifactorial and multivariate approaches revealed a Papillary arrangement, Escape like arrangement, and Nucleolus as independent predictors of PTC risk stratification at the microscopic level.

Nomograms are widely employed predictive models in oncology, integrating key factors to visualize the probability of clinical outcomes [29]. We developed a Nomogram by amalgamating the crucial clinical features outlined earlier. The Nomogram illustrates the relative contribution of each factor to risk stratification, with Size emerging as the strongest predictor, followed by Nucleolus, Echo, ECE, Escape like arrangement, Margin, and Papillary like arrangement. The Nomogram enables the integration of all features, allowing for individualized estimation of ATA risk for each patient. Compared with the current ATA.

risk stratification system, the ultrasound cytology line chart model constructed in this study has significant incremental value. The risk stratification of ATA depends on postoperative pathological results, and some high-risk factors such as lymph node metastasis or extra thyroid invasion can only be confirmed after surgery, leading to delayed decision-making and the risk of excessive or insufficient treatment. The research results show that the model has higher predictive accuracy in both the training and validation groups, outperforming single ultrasound or cytology models.Our Nomogram achieved a C-index of 0.799, signifying accurate prediction of PTC risk stratification; the calibration curve indicated good alignment with actual conditions. To validate the model’s stability, we collected data from 104 PTC patients across two hospitals for testing; the Nomogram model also performed well in the testing cohort (AUC = 0.778), confirming the robust diagnostic efficiency of the model. Leveraging the quantitative risk stratification provided by our Nomogram, clinicians can precisely identify PTC risk. Patients with a high Nomogram score may necessitate prophylactic cervical lymph node dissection and rigorous postoperative evaluation to enhance prognosis, whereas those with a low risk Nomogram score may consider active surveillance or ablative therapy.

Finally, this study systematically analyzed the key updates and model applicability of the 2015 and 2025 versions of the ATA guidelines for differentiated thyroid cancer [5, 14].The core frameworks of risk stratification in the two versions remain highly consistent, both using tumor size, extrathyroidal extension, vascular invasion, lymph node status, histological subtypes, and surgical margins as core evaluation dimensions. The ultrasound features and cytological indicators included in this study directly correspond to the core risk factors in the guidelines, and their pathophysiological associations are not affected by guideline updates, ensuring the inherent predictive value of the model. The main update in the 2025 guidelines is the refined subdivision of the intermediate-risk group, which was further divided into low-intermediate risk and intermediate-high risk, while the criteria for low-risk and high-risk categories remained substantially unchanged. Therefore, this model can serve as a reliable tool for preoperative risk stratification in lymph node negative PTC patients, helping clinicians make individualized treatment decisions based on the latest evidence-based medicine recommendations.

Our study presents several limitations that warrant consideration. Firstly, due to its retrospective nature, the selection bias caused by including surgical patients is inevitable. Secondly, our study exclusively focused on patients diagnosed with PTC, thus potentially limiting the generalizability of our conclusions to other thyroid cancer subtypes. Our study excluded multifocal PTC, which to some extent limits the generalizability of the research results.In this investigation, we identified several key morphological features including Size, Nucleolus, Echo, ECE, Margin, Escape like arrangement, and Papillary like arrangement as pivotal factors for risk stratification among patients with PTC. Leveraging these factors, we developed a predictive model for PTC risk stratification, which exhibited robust discrimination, calibration, and clinical utility. Our model offers clinicians a reliable tool for accurately assessing PTC risk.

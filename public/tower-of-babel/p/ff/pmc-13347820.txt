ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionRigid thoracoscopy is an established diagnostic instrument for pleural diseases; however, it is limited by challenges related to manoeuvrability, patient comfort and operator learning curve. A novel dual-function semi-rigid thoracoscope with associated semi-rigid forceps has been engineered to integrate the diagnostic efficacy of rigid biopsy forceps with the ergonomic advantages of a semi-rigid system. The Dual-functIon Semi-rigid thoraCoscOpy Versus rigid thoracoscopy for the diagnosis of plEuRal disease trial was designed to evaluate whether this device achieves a diagnostic yield that is non-inferior to that of traditional rigid thoracoscopy.Methods and analysisThis study is designed as a prospective, randomised, open-label, controlled, non-inferiority trial. A cohort of 92 patients presenting with undiagnosed exudative pleural effusion across five centres in China will be randomly allocated to undergo pleural biopsy using either a dual-function semi-rigid thoracoscope or a conventional rigid thoracoscope. The primary endpoint of the study is the diagnostic yield. The key secondary endpoints include procedural analgesic requirements, specimen quality, operator-assessed device performance, postprocedural pain and safety. A non-inferiority margin of 10% will be applied, with analyses conducted on both the intention-to-treat and per-protocol populations.Ethics and disseminationThe study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of China-Japan Friendship Hospital (2026-KY-033). Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants before enrolment. The trial will be conducted in compliance with the Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice guidelines. The results will be submitted for publication in a peer-reviewed journal.Trial registration numberNCT07114783.Keywords: Biopsy, RESPIRATORY MEDICINE (see Thoracic Medicine), Bronchoscopy

Introduction

Medical thoracoscopy using rigid and semi-rigid techniques is a useful, effective and safe method for investigating and managing pleural diseases.1 2 Previous guidelines suggest that 25% of exudative pleural effusions remain undiagnosed after thoracentesis and closed pleural biopsy.3 4

Among all biopsy methods using medical thoracoscopy, rigid thoracoscopy was found to be superior to semi-rigid thoracoscopy (both flexible forceps biopsy and cryobiopsy); however, the diagnostic yield was similar once pleural biopsy could be successfully performed. This may be because the rigid forceps can only be equipped with a rigid thoracoscope. An intrapatient study compared the specimen size, depth and diagnostic yield of rigid forceps, flexible forceps and cryoprobes using rigid thoracoscopy in 80 patients. They found that rigid forceps biopsies had the significantly largest size and depth and the highest diagnostic yield.5

Although rigid thoracoscopy is well-performed by experienced surgeons, rigid instruments also cause several concerns. A rigid thoracoscope must travel in a straight line and has limited manoeuvrability when examining the posterior and mediastinal aspects of the thoracic cavity, particularly if the lung remains partially or fully inflated.6 The operator inevitably angles the scope by leveraging it against the underlying ribs. The pressure and angle of the rigid instrument over the periosteum are believed to cause pain. The larger trocars used during rigid thoracoscopy can also cause greater discomfort in terms of periprocedural pain. A previous study reported that rigid thoracoscopy was associated with a higher need for sedatives and analgesics.7 Additionally, the learning curve of rigid thoracoscopy is not natural for respiratory physicians who are already trained in bronchoscopy because the devices do not have similar designs.

To overcome these concerns, we designed and developed a dual-function semi-rigid thoracoscope (UE FET-680, UE Corporation, Zhejiang, China) with a straight working channel that allows rigid forceps through it to perform pleural biopsy. We hypothesised that the yield of dual-function semi-rigid thoracoscopy would be non-inferior to that of rigid thoracoscopy since rigid forceps of the same size could be used to perform pleural biopsy. However, evidence supporting this hypothesis is lacking.

Methods and analysis

This protocol was designed in accordance with the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials (SPIRIT) Reporting Guidelines.8

Design

DISCOVER-2 is a randomised, open-label (not blinded), controlled, non-inferior trial with two parallel groups. The overall study design is summarised in figure 1. Participants will be recruited from five sites in China (China-Japan Friendship Hospital, Shandong public health clinical centre, Hainan Affiliated Hospital of Hainan Medical University, Central Hospital of Xiaogan and the Second People’s Hospital of Gansu Province). The participating sites were selected based on the availability of dual-function semi-rigid thoracoscopy with semi-rigid forceps and rigid thoracoscopy with rigid forceps. Eligible participants will be randomly assigned to one of two interventional groups: a dual-function semi-rigid thoracoscopy group or a rigid thoracoscopy group, with an equal allocation ratio of 1:1. This trial is planned to start in August 2026 and end in December 2027. As the time of manuscript submission, the trial is not yet recruiting participants

Participants and recruitment

This trial will recruit consecutive patients scheduled to undergo pleural biopsy via local anaesthetic thoracoscopy at participating sites. Potentially eligible patients will be identified from inpatient wards, outpatient clinics, or referrals for evaluation of undiagnosed pleural disease. Screening will be performed by trained study investigators according to the predefined eligibility criteria. Patients who meet the preliminary eligibility criteria will be informed of this clinical trial by a physician and will provide written informed consent prior to their participation during the preoperative visit. The example of the participant consent form is shown in the supplementary material. No financial or non-financial incentives will be offered to participants for trial participation.

Grouping

Randomisation

A computer-generated randomisation list will be employed for randomisation in this trial. The list will be generated using the sealed envelope simple randomiser (https://www.sealeden-velope.com/simple-randomiser/ v1/lists). Randomisation will be stratified by participating centre, with randomly varying block sizes of 4 and 6 within each centre. All participants will be allocated to the dual-function semi-rigid thoracoscopy and rigid thoracoscopy groups in a 1:1 ratio. Screening, informed consent, eligibility assessment, chest CT imaging, and other preprocedural tests will be completed before allocation is revealed. An independent who is not involved in participant recruitment, intervention delivery or outcome assessment will generate the randomisation list and prepare sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes separately for each centre. The randomisation plan will be concealed in these envelopes. The relevant envelope will be opened by a physician on the day of surgery after participant eligibility has been confirmed.

Reference standard

The final diagnosis will be established by the treating physician according to all accessible clinical, radiological, microbiological and pathological information. The final diagnosis will be used for the interpretation of non-diagnostic biopsy results and for secondary diagnostic performance outcomes, including disease-specific sensitivity. An independent diagnosis verification panel will be conducted at the major participating centre (China-Japan Friendship Hospital) to verify the final diagnoses. Panel members will not be involved in the thoracoscopic procedures and will be blinded to treatment allocation. Discordant cases will be reviewed by the panel. If consensus cannot be reached, the final diagnosis will be determined by majority decision, and the disagreement will be recorded. Patients without a specific benign or malignant diagnosis after 12 months of follow-up will be classified as having idiopathic disease. Authors with inventor status of the dual-function semi-rigid thoracoscope will not serve on the independent diagnosis verification panel or participate in final diagnosis adjudication.

Outcomes

Primary outcome

Diagnostic yield is the primary outcome of this trial and is defined as the percentage of participants for whom the allocated thoracoscopic pleural biopsy provides a specific histopathological diagnosis.9 The classification of the biopsy result will be based on the histopathological result of the allocated biopsy and will not use subsequent follow-up information or the final reference diagnosis. Malignant pleural disease and other defined histopathological diagnoses will be classified as diagnostic results. Non-specific pleuritis, fibrinous pleuritis, non-interpretable specimens and cases in which no biopsy is obtained will be classified as non-diagnostic results.

Secondary outcome

The secondary outcomes are procedural sedative/analgesic agents, diagnostic sensitivity for specific diseases, sampling quality, duration of procedure, operator’s view of the procedure, post-thoracoscopic procedure pain and safety. Postprocedural length of hospital stay and direct medical costs will also be recorded as secondary exploratory outcomes.

Procedural sedative/analgesic requirements will be quantified as the total dose of each sedative or analgesic agent administered during thoracoscopy.

Disease-specific diagnostic sensitivity is defined as the proportion of participants with a specific final diagnosis according to the reference standard whose allocated thoracoscopic pleural biopsy provides a specific histopathological diagnosis supporting the same disease.9 Participants with non-specific biopsy findings who are subsequently diagnosed with malignant pleural disease or tuberculous pleuritis during follow-up will be classified as false-negative biopsy results for the corresponding disease.

The sampling quality includes the size, depth and interpretability of the specimen. The largest cross-sectional area of the processed specimens on microscopic glass slides will be measured using ImageJ software (National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, Maryland, USA) and will be provided in mm2.10 Each sample will be measured twice, and the arithmetic mean of both measurements will be calculated. A thoracoscopic biopsy specimen, including the fatty tissue of the thoracic wall, is considered a deep biopsy and is therefore of high quality.5 The tissue depth of each specimen will be recorded. The interpretability of specimens is assessed as: ‘easily interpretable’ (enough tissue with all elements required for diagnosis), or ‘interpretability with some difficulty’(less tissue or fewer diagnostic elements, diagnosis less reliable), or ‘interpretable with great difficulty’ (little tissue or scant diagnostic elements, low reliability of diagnosis), or ‘non-interpretable’ (diagnosis not possible).11

The operators will record the quality of the image, ease of manoeuvring, ease of obtaining a biopsy sample and expectation that the biopsy sample would reveal a definitive histological diagnosis on a Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) from 0 to 100.7 All participants will be requested to report their degree of post-thoracoscopic pain using a VAS questionnaire ranging from 0 to 100 while at rest and coughing at 2, 6, 12, 24 and 48 hours postoperatively.12 Pain assessment will be performed by trained research personnel who are not involved in the procedure and are blind to treatment allocation. The post-procedural complications described in a previous study were classified as major (including empyema, major haemorrhage (drop in haemoglobin by 10 g/L or requiring blood transfusion), persistent air leak >3 days and re-expansion pulmonary oedema) and minor (subcutaneous emphysema, operative site infection, non-infective fever and minor haemorrhage).13

Postprocedural length of hospital stay will be defined as the number of days from the date of thoracoscopy to hospital discharge. Direct medical costs during the index hospitalisation will be extracted from hospital billing records, including procedure-related costs, anaesthetic and analgesic medication costs, chest tube and drainage-related costs, postprocedural imaging and management costs and postprocedural hospitalisation costs.

Statistical considerations

Sample size

A previous randomised controlled trial reported that the diagnostic yield of rigid thoracoscopy was 98%.7 In that trial, the diagnostic yield was 97.8% with rigid thoracoscopy and 73.3% with conventional semi-rigid thoracoscopy in the intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis, corresponding to an absolute difference of 24.5 percentage points. However, among patients in whom pleural biopsy was successfully performed, the diagnostic yields were 100% and 94.3%, respectively, with an absolute difference of 5.7 percentage points. Therefore, the 10% margin was selected as the prespecified non-inferiority margin. It is clearly smaller than the clinically unacceptable overall performance gap observed with conventional semi-rigid thoracoscopy, while being slightly wider than the small difference observed after successful biopsy. Based on a non-inferiority design, assumptions of 90% power, 5% significance level, 10% non-inferiority limit and 10% dropout rate resulted in a sample size of 92 individuals (figure 2). The calculation was performed using PASS 2024.

Baseline statistics

Baseline characteristics will be summarised by allocated procedure using descriptive statistics. Continuous variables will be presented as mean with SD or median with IQR, as appropriate. Categorical variables will be presented as numbers and percentages. No formal statistical testing will be performed for baseline characteristics.

Analysis populations

Two distinct analytical cohorts will be identified. The ITT population will encompass all randomised participants analysed according to their initial treatment assignment, irrespective of protocol adherence, completion of procedures or subsequent withdrawal. This cohort will serve as the primary set for analysing the primary efficacy endpoint and for all procedure-related information, specimen quality and safety analyses. Conversely, the per-protocol (PP) population, used for secondary efficacy (mainly diagnostic sensitivity) analyses, will comprise participants from the ITT cohort who have a definitive final diagnosis established by the pre-specified reference standard. This cohort is crucial for calculating disease-specific performance metrics because the validity of such metrics necessitates a confirmed disease status against which the test results can be compared.

Primary outcome analysis

Diagnostic yield analysis will be conducted on both the ITT and PP populations. Primary non-inferiority analysis will be performed within the ITT population. For participants with nondiagnostic final diagnoses, supportive analyses will similarly be conducted on the PP population to evaluate the robustness of the primary findings. The diagnostic yield will be calculated for each population and intervention group. The risk difference between the dual-function semi-rigid thoracoscopy and rigid thoracoscopy groups, along with the two-sided 95% CI, will be determined. Non-inferiority of the dual-function device will be established if the lower limit of the 95% CI for the risk difference exceeds the prespecified non-inferiority margin of −10%.

Secondary outcome analysis

Secondary outcome analyses will use the population that is most suitable for each specific endpoint. For each target disease, a 2×2 contingency table will be constructed by cross-tabulating the thoracoscopic biopsy results against the final reference diagnosis. Sensitivity will be calculated for each intervention group based on the ITT population and 95% CIs. The ITT population will be used for between-group comparisons of specimen quality and procedural metrics. To address repeated measurements within individual participants, data will be aggregated at the participant level prior to conducting statistical tests. Specimen size, treated as a continuous variable, will be determined by calculating the mean cross-sectional area of all biopsies obtained from each participant. These participant-level means will subsequently be compared between intervention groups using an independent samples t-test or the Mann-Whitney U test if the assumption of normality is not met. Specimen depth, treated as a categorical variable, will be categorised according to histopathological evaluation. Participants whose specimens are classified as ‘Deep’ in at least 50% of cases will be designated at the patient level as having achieved ‘adequate sampling depth’. The proportion of participants achieving adequate sampling depth will be compared across the intervention groups using the χ² test or Fisher’s exact test, as deemed appropriate. The duration of the procedure, operator assessment scores and postprocedural pain scores will be compared between the groups using independent sample t-tests or their non-parametric counterparts. Postprocedural length of hospital stay and direct medical costs will be summarised by treatment group and compared using the same approach as other continuous outcomes. Because cost data are expected to be skewed, they will be reported as medians with IQRs.

Potential centre-level variation will be assessed in a sensitivity analysis. Diagnostic yield will be summarised by study centre and allocated procedure. A logistic regression model including allocated procedure and study centre as fixed effects will be used to assess the robustness of the primary analysis.

Statistical software and significance

After database lock, the anonymised dataset will be analysed by independent statisticians who are not involved in device development, trial intervention delivery or endpoint adjudication. Statistical analyses will be conducted using R software (V.4.3.1 or later). The primary non-inferiority test will employ a one-sided significance level of α=0.025. For hypothesis tests related to secondary endpoints and subgroup interactions, statistical significance will be determined using a two-sided p<0.05. Effect estimates, including risk differences, mean differences and proportions, will be presented along with their respective 95% CIs.

Data collection and management

Data will be collected prospectively and managed using a centralised web-based Electronic Data Capture system to ensure standardised and real-time data entry and validation across all participating sites. Source data will be entered directly into electronic case report forms within this system. To minimise assessment bias, postoperative pain and other assessor-collected secondary outcomes will be assessed by research personnel who are not involved in the procedure and are blinded to group allocation. The total procedural dose of each sedative or analgesic agent and operator assessment scores will be recorded in the electronic case report form immediately after the procedure. Authors with inventor status and the device company will not be involved in database management or have access to the trial database before database lock. An independent Data Monitoring Committee, which will not include authors with inventor status or representatives of the device company, will periodically review the quality and completeness of the data as well as participant safety. On completion of the study and resolution of all queries, the final trial database will be locked. Subsequently, a fully anonymised dataset will be securely transferred to independent statisticians for final analysis, thereby ensuring the separation of data management and analysis functions. Data on postprocedural length of hospital stay and direct medical costs will be collected from medical records and hospital billing systems.

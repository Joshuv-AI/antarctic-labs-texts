Protocol for the efficient and inducible generation of CRISPR-Cas9-edited human cortical neurons from the iCas9-iNgn2 hPSCs
Authors: Navroop K. Dhaliwal, Octavia Yifang Weng, Yun Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
SummaryGeneration of CRISPR-Cas9-edited cortical neurons from human pluripotent stem cells (hPSCs) enables the study of gene functions and neural disease mechanisms. Here, we present a protocol for developing iCas9-iNgn2 hPSC, an inducible cell line that allows the simultaneous induction of the neuralizing transcription factor Ngn2 and the Cas9 nuclease to rapidly generate edited human cortical neurons. We describe the steps of the protocol from transducing iCas9-iNgn2 with guide RNA-containing lentivirus to producing edited cortical neurons in about 2 weeks.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Dhaliwal et al.1

Before you begin

Using CRISPR/Cas9 for genetic editing of different cell types in vitro has been a powerful tool to study the molecular regulation of human physiology. However, the limited availability of human cell types and tissues has previously hindered this investigation in the physiologically relevant setting. To that end, human pluripotent stem cells (hPSCs) have recently become a widely used resource to generate cell types that are otherwise inaccessible to researchers. Human cortical neurons can be generated rapidly by transducing hPSCs with lentivirus containing the transcription factor neurogenin2 (Ngn2).2 However, delivering CRISPR/Cas9 to these hPSCs-derived neurons can be inefficient and toxic. To address this challenge, our lab has developed a dual-targeted hPSC line (iCas9-iNgn2) that contains doxycycline-inducible transgene cassettes for Cas9 and the Ngn2 in the AAVS1 “safe-harbor” loci.3,4 Using a previously generated iCas9 hPSC line that contains a doxycycline-inducible Tet-On Cas9 expression cassette in the AAVS1 safe harbor locus,3 we inserted a second doxycycline-inducible Tet-On Ngn2 expression cassette into the other AAVS1 locus of the iCas9 line (Figure 1A).1 Because the Tet-On Ngn2 cassette we used contained a Puro sequence which made the newly engineered line puromycin resistant,4 we next removed the Puro sequence from the correctly targeted hPSCs using two single-guide RNAs (sgRNAs) flanking the Puro sequence (Figure 1A).1 Similar donor plasmid to introduce Ngn2 into the AAVS1 locus has been previously described and can be obtained from Addgene.5 In the iCas9-iNgn2 hPSCs, the expression of Cas9, combined with single guide RNA (sgRNA) delivered by lentivirus, allows efficient genome editing. Simultaneously, the expression of Ngn2 enables rapid differentiation of hPSCs into human cortical neurons.Figure 1Generation schematic and overall timeline of using iCas9-iNgn2 hPSC tool(A) Schematic diagram on the 2-step CRISPR editing strategy to generate iCas9-iNgn2 hPSC.(B) Schematic diagram describing the main steps (transduction, selection, induction, and maturation), the overall timeline, and key culture media with added components.

Our recent study used the iCas9-iNgn2 hPSCs to generate gene-edited human cortical neurons using 1–2 sgRNAs against 1–2 target genes (e.g., PTEN, RPTOR, RICTOR) cloned into one lentiviral plasmid.1 However, this tool is generalizable and can also be robustly used with a lentivirus library containing any number of sgRNAs based on the design and question of the study. Here, we present the step-by-step protocol describing the transduction, induction and validation of the iCas9-iNgn2 hPSC line to generate neurons edited for one target gene in an overall timeline of about two-week (Figure 1B). For complete details on the phenotypic analyses of the gene-edited human neurons, please refer to the original paper (Dhaliwal et al.1).

Expected outcomes

The iCas9-iNgn2 hPSC tool and its differentiation protocol is used extensively with single-target and dual-target sgRNAs system in our recent study.1 This tool and protocol has been validated using different sgRNAs and differentiation experiments. This protocol is designed to efficiently generate edited, mature, and functional human neurons in as little as two weeks. Furthermore, assessing sgRNA efficiency beforehand is crucial because sgRNA efficiency impacts the overall editing outcome of the iCas9-iNgn2 system. This protocol generates a polyclonal population of neurons with various indels, as demonstrated by Synthego ICE analysis traces published in our recent study.1 Therefore, performing a western blot to evaluate the protein amount is recommended. We have observed an expected indel percentage range of 60%–90% based on Synthego ICE analysis. Phenotypic analyses should ideally be performed after a substantial change in protein level is confirmed.

Limitations

The iCas9-iNgn2 hPSC tool has been generated in the male H1 genetic background. Similar genetic engineering may be needed to introduce the inducible Cas9 and Ngn2 cassettes into additional female hPSCs for future investigation. The efficiency of the protocol is dependent upon many factors such as sgRNA design, lentiviral titers, cell counts for transduction, puromycin selection and doxycycline dosage. Thus, it is imperative to follow all the steps and timings carefully. The iCas9-iNgn2 hPSC line has been tested extensively with many sgRNA designs successfully but we acknowledge slight variability in editing efficiency depending upon the sgRNA design.

The spontaneous differentiation of iCas9-iNgn2 hPSC during the maintenance and expansion (Figure 6D), related to steps 1, 2, and 7.

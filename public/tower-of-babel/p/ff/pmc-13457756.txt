Anesthetic Efficacy of Lignocaine Added with Magnesium Sulfate during Pulpectomy in Children with Irreversible Pulpitis: A Prospective, Split-mouth, Randomized Controlled Trial
Authors: Chilukuri Raghuram, Deepika Pai, Kalyana C Pentapati, Nekkanti Sridhar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAim and backgroundPain control is one of the major challenges of pediatric dentistry. Pain control after an inferior alveolar nerve block (IANB) during pulpectomy is often challenging in pediatric patients. Hence, this study aims to investigate the effect of addition of magnesium sulfate (MgSO4) to lignocaine hydrochloride on pulpal anesthesia achieved after administration of IANB for pulpectomy in children with irreversible pulpitis.Methods32 patients fulfilling the inclusion criteria were selected in this split-mouth study design. Patients received either test or control solutions at two different appointments as an inferior alveolar nerve block. The test solution contains MgSO4 added to lignocaine hydrochloride, whereas the control solution contains sterile water added to lignocaine hydrochloride. Pain assessment was performed using the Heft–Parker visual analog scale (HP-VAS) and the faces, legs, activity, cry, and consolability (FLACC) scales. Comparison of variables was done using a paired t-test and a Wilcoxon signed-rank test.ResultsPain scores recorded in the test side were significantly lower compared to those recorded in the control side during access opening (FLACC scores on the test side and control side were 2.00 ± 0.76 and 2.47 ± 0.62; HP-VAS scores in the test and control side were 25.72 ± 5.75 and 28.44 ± 5.64). Pain scores during pulp extirpation are: HP-VAS on the test side is 36.68 ± 1.07, and on the control side is 55.74 ± 1.69; FLACC scores on the test and control sides are 2.05 ± 0.94 and 2.61 ± 0.57, respectively.ConclusionMagnesium sulfate, when added to lignocaine local anesthesia (LA), was successful in enhancing the depth of pulpal anesthesia.Clinical significanceAddition of magnesium sulfate enhances the depth of local anesthesia while treating primary teeth with chronic irreversible pulpitis.Keywords: Inferior alveolar nerve block, Lignocaine hydrochloride, Magnesium sulfate, Pulpectomy, Pulpal anesthesia

Introduction

Inferior alveolar nerve block (IANB) is recommended for performing invasive dental procedures of mandibular teeth in the pediatric population. Unfortunately, patients with inflamed pulp tissue may pose greater challenges for achieving successful, in-depth local anesthesia (LA).1 This can be attributed to several hypotheses, such as ion trapping, altered membrane excitability, and elevated levels of tetrodotoxin-resistant channels (TTXr) in inflamed pulp.2

An unsatisfactory level of pulpal anesthesia in children can lead to a negative attitude towards future dental treatment.3 Methods like supplemental buccal infiltration and intrapulpal anesthesia are employed to enhance pulpal anesthesia but are considered as second injection procedures, making children further uncooperative.2,4 Hence, there is a need for modification of the local anesthesia solution.

The most acceptable explanation for reduced LA efficacy in pulpal inflammation is the inflammatory activation of peripheral nociceptive free terminals and associated central mechanisms.5 Being involved in neuropathic and inflammatory pain, central sensitization is responsible for secondary hyperalgesia, which is an increased pain sensitivity outside the area of injury, and tactile allodynia, where even a slight touch leads to a pain response. Central sensitization is an expression of activity-dependent plasticity, resulting from an increase in synaptic strength mediated primarily by N-methyl-D-aspartate (NMDA) glutamatergic receptors.5

Magnesium (Mg) ions block NMDA receptors noncompetitively, helping in achieving successful pulpal anesthesia.5 Magnesium sulfate (MgSO4) was safely used as an adjuvant in LA for several surgeries, such as tonsillectomies in pediatric patients, and was found to decrease the requirement for postoperative analgesia without causing any side effects.6

Magnesium sulfate as an adjuvant to lignocaine for IANB was successful in achieving improved pulpal analgesia in treating irreversible pulpitis of permanent mandibular second molars without causing any side effects.5

Therefore, this study aimed to investigate the effect of the addition of magnesium sulfate to lignocaine hydrochloride on pulpal anesthesia achieved after administration of the inferior alveolar nerve block while performing pulpectomy in children diagnosed with irreversible pulpitis.

We propose an alternate hypothesis that the depth of pulpal anesthesia achieved with IANB administered with magnesium sulfate added to lignocaine hydrochloride will be better compared to that of IANB administered without the addition of magnesium sulfate to lignocaine hydrochloride.

Materials and Methods

The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee–Kasturba Medical College and Kasturba Hospitals Ethics Committee, Manipal Academy of Higher Education, Manipal, with IEC number 67/2022 and registered with Clinical Trials Registry-India (ctri.nic.in) with enrolment no: CTRI/2023/01/049239.

All procedures in our study were performed in accordance with the Helsinki Declaration of 1964, while following the CONSORT (Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials) guidelines.

Sample Size Calculation

Based on findings by Mousavi et al.5 on pain during access cavity after administration of lignocaine LA with MgSO4 added as an adjuvant in IANB for mandibular permanent second molars with irreversible pulpitis, the effect size of 1.4 was estimated. At 95% power with 95% confidence interval, an estimated sample size of 30 would be required to detect a significant difference between the test and control. Given a 10% attrition rate in our study, a sample size of 35 was determined.

A total of 111 patients were screened who visited the department of Pediatric and Preventive Dentistry, Manipal College of Dental Sciences, Manipal, on an outpatient basis from August 2022 to October 2023. 42 patients that have fulfilled the inclusion criteria of our study, with their parents consenting for participation in study, were included, of which 7 patients were eliminated from study as they failed to achieve adequate anesthesia after LA administration. Among the remaining 35 patients, 3 patients were excluded as they failed to show up for the treatment of a tooth belonging to one of their arms. The final sample size of this study, therefore, comprised 32 patients (Fig. 1).

Inclusion Criteria

Thirty-two healthy cooperative children between the age group of 6–9 years with ability to understand pain record scales and those demonstrating positive or definitely positive behavior (as per Frankl's behavior rating scale of 3 or 4), with an indication of pulpectomy in mandibular primary second molars of both sides of the jaw, were included.

Inclusion criteria for pulpectomy:

Mandibular primary second molars having symptoms of irreversible pulpitis, having a lingering pain response to cold test, showing a prolonged response when the Endo-frost (Roeko Endo-Frost by Coltene Whaledent, India) cold spray stimulus is removed.

Mandibular primary second molars having symptoms of irreversible pulpitis, having a lingering pain response to cold test, showing a prolonged response when the Endo-frost (Roeko Endo-Frost by Coltene Whaledent, India) cold spray stimulus is removed.

With moderate to severe pain as recorded by HP-VAS reading of moderate (55–114 mm) and severe (>114 mm) pain after the cold test.

With moderate to severe pain as recorded by HP-VAS reading of moderate (55–114 mm) and severe (>114 mm) pain after the cold test.

The intraoral periapical radiograph shows no radiographic changes in the periodontal ligament region and no root resorption.

The intraoral periapical radiograph shows no radiographic changes in the periodontal ligament region and no root resorption.

Exclusion Criteria

Patients having a history of allergies to Mg or LA or other medications, uncooperative patients, patients having a history of significant medical conditions such as cardiac/hepatocellular/bleeding disorders, patients taking beta blockers or other opioid medication preoperatively, and patients with orofacial infections such as facial abscesses were excluded from this study.

Exclusion criteria for pulpectomy:

Primary mandibular second molars with signs of irreversible pulpitis, with no pain, and with a normal response after performing the cold test.

Primary mandibular second molars with signs of irreversible pulpitis, with no pain, and with a normal response after performing the cold test.

Teeth showing enlargement in the periodontal ligament region and/or the presence of periapical radiolucency in intraoral periapical radiograph of primary mandibular second molars.

Teeth showing enlargement in the periodontal ligament region and/or the presence of periapical radiolucency in intraoral periapical radiograph of primary mandibular second molars.

In this prospective, split-mouth, double-blinded, randomized controlled trial, investigator 1 has administered LA, performed access opening and pulp extirpation, and also collected the postinterventional data, whereas investigator 2 has prepared the anesthetic solution and has taken the informed consent from parents and assent from patients. Investigator 1 and patients, both were blinded to the anesthetic solution tested. Randomization was done by investigator 2 using a research randomizer software to allocate the teeth into two groups, test side and control side. Solutions were prepared in a sterile environment by investigator 2 just before LA administration. 20 gm of MgSO4 powder (I.P. grade MgSO4–20 gm by OOM Laboratories, India) was mixed with 1.66 L of water to make a 10% MgSO4 solution.

Preparation of Test Solution

0.18 mL of anesthesia from a cartridge containing 1.8 mL of 2% lignocaine with 1:80,000 epinephrine (Lox 2% NEON Laboratories limited, Andheri, Mumbai, India) was drawn using a 40 unit insulin syringe (BD GlideTM with TBL, India) accounting to 7.2 units and replaced with 0.18 mL of 10% W/v MgSO4, giving a final concentration of “1.8 mL cartridge of 1.8% lignocaine with 1:88,000 epinephrine along with 1% W/v MgSO4.”

Preparation of Control Solution

0.18 mL of anesthesia from a cartridge containing 1.8 mL of 2% lignocaine with 1:80,000 epinephrine was drawn using a 40 unit insulin syringe (accounting to 7.2 units) and replaced with same amount of (0.18 mL) of sterile distilled water (IP grade, pyrogen free by Deogratias Parenteral, India), leading to a final concentration of “1.8 mL cartridge of 1.8% lignocaine with 1:88,000 epinephrine along with distilled water.”

The procedure was explained in detail to guardians/parents of the patients fulfilling the inclusion criteria and a written informed consent and assent was then obtained. Each child was weighed before the treatment and the maximum recommended dose (MRD) of MgSO4 to be used was determined.

As the study follows a split mouth design, LA administration and indicated treatment was performed only on one of the sides in one visit. On the other side, LA was administered on a subsequent visit with time interval of 4–7 days. In the control side, a topical anesthetic (procaine topical gel by Pascal International, USA) was applied to the site of injection. After its application, 0.18 mL of sterile distilled water combined with “1.8 mL cartridge of 1.8% lignocaine with 1:80,000 epinephrine” was administered as IANB at a rate of 1 mL/min using 27-gauge needle following standard recommended technique for IANB administration.

Investigator 1 administered the anesthetic solution and collected HP-VAS and FLACC pain scores for administration of LA. Children were told to place a mark on linear line of HP-VAS scale in accordance with level of pain felt by them, ranging from no pain to intense pain. The FLACC scale constitutes five sections that are face, legs, activity, cry and consolability. The signs, actions and behaviors of patients under these sections indicate distress. Body and legs of the patient are not covered by a drape so that activity of the patient is observed by the investigator and tenseness and tonicity of patients body is also assessed. Each section is scored on a scale ranging from 0 to 2, which best suits the behavior of the child that was described in the scale, with a total score ranging from 0 to 12.

Within 15 minutes of IANB administration, profound lip numbness was considered as the indicator of IANB success.5 The access opening and pulp extirpation began after ascertaining the objective signs of soft tissue anesthesia. Access opening was carried out using an access opening bur (Dentsply-bur #164, Dentsply India Pvt. Ltd.) while opening of the pulp chamber and widening the access cavity was done by an endo-z bur (Dentsply- bur #165, Dentsply India Pvt. Ltd.) with a noncutting tip design. All canals were patented and barbed broach was used for the pulp extirpation. The pain experienced by the patients during access cavity preparation and pulp extirpation was recorded using the HP-VAS scale and FLACC scale.

If the child reports extreme discomfort during the treatment, then it is reported as failure to achieve adequate anesthesia, and the child is excluded immediately from the study. Further treatment of these excluded patients was carried out as per standard treatment protocols. The sequence of events followed for treatment in the test side is the same as that of those followed in the control side, with the difference being the addition of Magnesium sulfate to the LA solution. On the test side, after application of topical anesthesia (procaine topical anesthetic gel), 0.18 mL of 10% W/v MgSO4 combined with “1.8 mL cartridge of 1.8% lidocaine with 1:80,000 epinephrine” was administered as IANB.

Parents were briefed on potential postoperative complications (pain, lip or tongue biting, bleeding, and hematoma) at the end of both treatment appointments. Guardians/parents of the participants were contacted 2 hours after LA administration to ask for the duration for which the anesthetic effects had lasted, and were contacted 24 hours later to obtain information about any postanesthetic events.

Statistical Analysis

Data were analyzed using Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) version 20. A p-value of < 0.05 was considered as statistically significant. Variables were compared using paired t-test and Wilcoxon signed-rank test according to the distribution of data.

Results

The primary objective of our study was to evaluate and compare extent of pulpal anesthesia achieved by IANB using lignocaine hydrochloride with and without addition of MgSO4 on primary mandibular second molars diagnosed with irreversible pulpitis. The secondary objective was to evaluate onset, duration, and adverse effects after IANB with lignocaine hydrochloride is administered in combination with and without addition of magnesium sulfate for treatment of irreversible pulpitis in primary mandibular second molars.

Mean age of our study participants was 7.50 ± 1.27 years. The mean weight of participants was 24.97 ± 4.35 kg. Out of 32 study participants, 15 were female (46.9%) and 17 were male (53.1%). In accordance with the inclusion criteria of our study, all the patients included, have shown Frankel's positive (71.9%) or definitely positive (28.1%) behavior.

Preoperative pain scores after cold testing were statistically insignificant among test and control sides. Subjective evaluation of pain during LA administration as recorded by HP-VAS scale was significantly higher in test side as compared to that of control side. There is statistically significant difference in the pain scores among test and control sides during access opening and pulp extirpation. The average pulp extirpation scores from all canals were significantly low in test side as compared to control side (Table 1).

Discussion

IANB is considered the gold standard for anesthetizing the mandibular region. But it is not always successful in achieving adequate pulpal anesthesia. According to clinical trials, 44–81% of patients with irreversible pulpitis did not receive enough pulpal anesthesia after IANB.7–9 According to Ardakani et al.,10 the success rate of IANB in 7–9-year-old children was 59.1% on left side and 35.6% on right side; these values are much lower than the overall success rate of IANB.

In inflamed pulps, anesthetic failure is said to be increased by 8-fold.1 There are two mechanisms for hyperalgesia: central and peripheral. Both these mechanisms are involved in chronic inflammation. In peripheral mechanism, there is activation of transient receptor potential vanilloid type 1 (TRPV1). which are sensitive for changes in pH and heat of pulp. Their activation also leads to an increased release of calcitonin gene-related peptide, substance P and also activation of NMDA receptors, leading to activation of central mechanism of hyperalgesia.1

Several methods have been tried to increase the success rate of IANB, such as introduction of alternate techniques of injection,11 various anesthetic solutions,9 supplemental injections,8,12,13 giving the patients several premedications prior to IANB14 and adding adjuncts to various local anesthetic solutions leading to several formulations.15

According to Malamed SF,16 2% lignocaine is gold standard for use in pediatric and adult patients and has been widely used for IANB. Articaine was not used in our study as there were reports of long-term numbness leading to the incidence of soft tissue injuries, especially in children.17 Acute bronchospasm in pediatric age groups has been treated with IV magnesium at doses of 10–25 mg/kg and also up to 100 mg/kg.6 Indicated specific dosage of MgSO4 in pediatric population for LA is 2–5 mg/kg.18

The analgesic effects of MgSO4 have been vastly studied. During tonsillectomy procedures, peritonsillar infiltration using MgSO4 (2–5 mg/kg) in combination with LA has been shown to decrease perioperative pain.19 Epidural MgSO4 (50 mg), when used as an adjuvant during caudal blockade, has improved and also prolonged the effect of analgesia, along with enhancing functional recovery.20,21 There were no cases reported in literature as “clinical hypermagnesemia” that are related to overdosing. Due to its excellent safety profile and optimal cost-effectiveness, MgSO4 is also called a super adjuvant in pediatric local anesthesia.18

To our knowledge, in the literature, there are only two studies5,22 that evaluated effect of MgSO4 in enhancing anesthetic efficacy. Both these studies were done in adult population and have concluded that MgSO4 has enhanced depth of pulpal anesthesia achieved in symptomatic irreversible pulpitis.

All the included teeth in our study had a positive response to cold test with a lingering pain response of moderate to severe pain as recorded by HP-VAS scale, correlating to signs of chronic irreversible pulpitis. Seven patients (16.66%) were excluded from our study due to failure of IANB which was assessed based on the subjective report of profound lip numbness given by the patient, as adopted in similar study by Shahi et al.23 and Mausavi et al.5 This value is in line with success rate of IANB in general, which is in the range of 30–97% as reported by Kanaa et al.24 and in line with the failure rate of IANB even with accurate injection which is 15–20% as reported by Ardakani et al.10

In our study, HP-VAS and FLACC scales were used to benefit from advantages of both scales, in both subjective and objective pain evaluation as felt by the patient.5,6,22 Children aged above 6 years were included in our study due to their comparative ability to tolerate pulpectomy procedures in their first visit, and care was taken to equalize the number of patients receiving test or control solution in their first appointment. In our study, 17 (53.125%) patients received the test solution as IANB on their first appointment, and 15 (46.875%) patients received the control solution as IANB in their first appointment. In order to increase the validity of reported pain scores, patients between the ages of 6 and 9 years were included in our study.

As the approximate failure rate of IANB is 0–48.6% and the most common cause of this failure is the technique of administration of IANB,5 to maintain uniformity and optimal efficiency, the block was administered by a single skilled operator (investigator 1) in our study.

The concentration of MgSO4 used in our study was based on previously established reports.5,6,18 In order to retain the chemical properties of the drug, the experimental anesthetic solutions–both test and control solutions were freshly prepared. No patient in our study reported any adverse effect (e.g., paraesthesia) due to addition of MgSO4 as an adjuvant. This observation was similar to that in previous studies that used MgSO4 as an adjuvant in local anesthesia.5,6,22

Results of subjective pain evaluation in our study revealed that there was a significant difference in the pain levels reported during LA administration between the two sides. Patients experienced slightly more pain when LA mixed with MgSO4 was deposited on the test side. On the contrary to this, the objective pain assessment results during deposition of LA solution were higher on the control side as compared to the test side. But this difference is not significant. These contradicting observations can probably be explained by the fact that MgSO4, being a mild tissue (skin) irritant,25,26 causing a slightly higher discomfort, which is localized and cannot be reflected in the movements of the patient while recording the FLACC scale values. Also, the amount of MgSO4 used is very minimal in this study to only cause a localized discomfort during LA deposition.

In our study, the subjective and objective pain scores while access opening suggested a significant difference in pain levels between both sides, indicating that MgSO4 has lowered the level of pain experienced during access opening on the test side. The results of our study indicate that the addition of MgSO4 to lignocaine has successfully enhanced the depth of pulpal anesthesia achieved when given as IANB. This was reflected in average pulp extirpation values in both HP-VAS and FLACC scales.

The possible explanation for the significant decrease in pain levels during access opening and pulp extirpation on the test side can be that magnesium has a key function in antinociception as a naturally occurring physiological calcium antagonist at many voltage-gated channels. Magnesium is added to the solution to dull the activation of NMDA receptors, which blocks excitatory postsynaptic currents and reduces C-fiber stimulation. MgSO4 eliminates hypersensitization and decreases central sensitization by blocking the activation of the NMDA receptor in the brain's dorsal horn via the amino acid transmitters, notably glutamate and aspartate.22

Hence, the alternate hypothesis of this study that the depth of pulpal anesthesia achieved with administration of IANB containing MgSO4 added to lignocaine hydrochloride will be superior as compared to conventional IANB containing lignocaine hydrochloride without MgSO4, is accepted.

The results of our study were in accordance with the study by Mausavi et al.5 who suggested that adding MgSO4 resulted in enhancement of anesthesia when given as IANB in patients undergoing root canal treatment for irreversible pulpitis in mandibular molars. The results of our study were also in accordance with the results of a study conducted by Chandrasekaran et al.22 In their study, patients were given MgSO4 added to LA as IANB, where MgSO4 was used in two concentrations—75% and 150%, and compared to a control group. The anesthetic efficacy was highest when 150 mg MgSO4 was used, second highest in 75% MgSO4 group and least in conventional group.

From the observations of our study, it was found that MgSO4 added to lignocaine local anesthesia took slightly longer time for the onset of its effects, which was recorded by taking the subjective signs such as lip numbness into effect. But this difference was statistically insignificant. Duration of anesthesia was recorded by asking the patient to report the time when the anesthetic effect on the soft tissue wears off. The control solution acted for slightly longer than the test solution, but this difference was also statistically insignificant.

These observations were not in line with those recorded by Chandrasekaran et al.22 who reported that MgSO4 addition has shortened the onset time and prolonged the duration of action of anesthesia. They evaluated by using an electric pulp tester (EPT) every 1 minute for onset time and every 15 minutes for duration of LA action. The results obtained in both these studies cannot be directly compared due to differences in study design, inclusion criteria, age of the participants, volume of MgSO4 in test solutions prepared, and mode of evaluation.

Although some studies have adopted usage of EPT to assess the onset time and duration of action of anesthesia, we do not consider subjective assessment of lip numbness alone as a limit of this study as traditionally a positive report of lip numbness from the patient is considered as standard of care in achieving successful anesthesia after IANB administration. Furthermore, keeping the age group of participants of our study into consideration, EPT was not used every 15 minutes as it can lead to decrease in patient compliance.

Some limitations of the study should be acknowledged. Final sample size could be higher, and patients from different geological backgrounds could have been included. A multicentric study in future would give a higher power value to the results. As the results of this study depend on recording pain felt by the children, along with subjective and objective assessment of pain, physiologic parameters such as heart rate, oxygen saturation could have also been checked to assess and compare the physiologic discomfort experienced by the patient on both sides. As MgSO4 is a new compound being introduced as an adjuvant to local anesthetic, acceptability of patients to MgSO4 could have been evaluated. Furthermore, despite standardization of treatment and inherent split-mouth study design, inherent physiological variability of pain perception among patients would have introduced some heterogeneity.

The results of our study showed an increase in the pulpal anesthesia achieved after administration of magnesium sulfate added to lignocaine hydrochloride as IANB. MgSO4 had no side effects when used in children. It showed physical compatibility and chemical stability when mixed with lignocaine hydrochloride.27 Hence, we recommend the use of MgSO4 as an adjuvant to lignocaine hydrochloride to obtain superior pulpal anesthesia when given as IANB in children during pulpectomy procedures.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this clinical trial shows that the addition of 0.18 mL of 1% MgSO4 to 1.8% lignocaine local anesthesia, resulting in 1:88,000 epinephrine and when delivered as IANB, was successful in enhancing the depth of pulpal anesthesia by decreasing the pain perceived by children during access opening and pulp extirpation stages of pulpectomy on primary mandibular second molars with symptomatic chronic irreversible pulpitis. Moreover, the addition of magnesium sulfate to lignocaine local anesthesia did not affect the onset and duration of action of anesthesia when given as IANB. No adverse effects have been reported, suggesting that magnesium sulfate can be safely used as an adjuvant to lignocaine local anesthesia to achieve profound pulpal anesthesia during pulpectomy procedures. There is a need for more studies with larger sample sizes to be carried out in children, along with different concentration doses and with different local anesthesia solutions, for improved evidence in the literature before its routine use in the regular dental practice.

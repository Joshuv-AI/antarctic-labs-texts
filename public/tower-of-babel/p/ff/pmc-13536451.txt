Introduction

Tooth intentional replantation refers to the deliberate extraction of a tooth, extraoral endodontic manipulation and repair, and tooth replantation into the extraction socket.1 For tooth that have unsatisfactory result with nonsurgical retreatment or apical surgery, intentional replantation and dental implant are viable options.2 Intentional replantation is regarded as a final strategy to preserve natural teeth affected by inaccessible endodontic and periodontal disease.3 It is indicated for cases of failed nonsurgical endodontic treatment and apical microsurgery, perforations and external root resorption, and compromised teeth or periodontal defects due to radicular grooves, as well as for patients unwilling to undergo prolonged or expensive treatments.4 Compared with dental implant, Intentional replantation is of lower treatment times and reduced economic costs. It is an alternative in endodontic surgery when there are important anatomical limitations such as the maxillary sinus, mental nerve, or inferior alveolar nerve.5

Scientometrics/bibliometrics is an objective and reliable method of providing an overview of the current studies on a particular research field based on the citation and scientific domains.6–8 A previous bibliometric analysis included 171 articles and reviews on intentional replantation published before Feb 2023 and analyzed the authorship, countries and institutions of origin, journals, study design, and disease type.9 Recently, an expert consensus mentioned that more than 300 papers on intentional replantation have been published.5 A further bibliometric analysis would be important for understanding of application area and scientific domains of this technique as well as for the communication between dentists and researchers. Therefore, the purpose of this study was to analyze the scientometric characteristics and research trends of all the papers on tooth intentional replantation, with emphasis on the analysis of the keywords that can reflect research directions and topics of concern.

Materials and methods

According to the methodology described previously,6–8 all the papers on tooth intentional replantation in the Scopus database were retrieved on 28 Feb 2026. We used medical subject terms “Replant* OR Reimplant*” AND “Intentional*” in title, abstract and keywords in literature search, without restriction to paper type and year of publication. In literature search, the asterisk indicates a wildcard used to search for all endings including fifth or more root words. Only English literature was included because it is an international knowledge-exchange language. The scientometric characteristics of all the eligible papers were recorded for the following information: title, keyword, citation count, publication year, journal of publication, authorship, affiliation, and country/region of origin. Data search and extraction were performed independently by two investigators, and any discrepancy of results was resolved in a consensus symposium. The years of publication were divided into before 2017 and after 01 Jan 2017, so that the number of papers can be to some extent compared in the analysis of research trends. Microsoft Office Excel 365 was used for index model building, and the Bibliometrix Biblioshiny R-package software was used for bibliometric statistics. In this descriptive study, variables were presented as numbers and percentages. No comparisons were made, and thus no P-values were set.

Results

Citation characteristics

With the search strategy algorithm, a total of 356 papers on intentional replantation were retrieved in the Scopus database. As presented in Fig. 1A, the most type of papers on intentional replantation was article (n = 156), followed by case report (n=145) and review (n=19). The total citation count (after removal of self-citations) was 5562 (5190) and the h index was 42 (41) for all the papers. To further concretize the trends of scientific output, we assessed the annual number and accumulated citations of the papers during 2006–2025 (Fig. 1B). The annual number of the papers on intentional replantation ranged from 2 to 28 during 2006–2025. The accumulated citations (after removal of self-citations) of the papers ranged from 61 (56) to 610 (573) during this period. Research characteristics

Based on the frequency of the keywords in all the papers on intentional replantation (Fig. 2A), a list of the common keywords is automatically recognized by the database. The most common study design was case report, followed by follow-up study, review, retrospective study, and controlled study. The most related tooth disease was tooth fracture, followed by tooth periapical disease, periodontal disease, tooth pulp disease, tooth injury, and so on (Fig. 2B). Before 2017 and after 2017, there have always been the same common keywords such as root canal therapy, treatment outcome, follow up, cone beam computed tomography, reimplantation, tooth extraction, diagnostic imaging, apicoectomy, root resorption, tooth ankylosis, tooth avulsion, periodontal ligament, autotransplantation, prognosis, root canal filling material, resin, tooth radiography, tooth resorption, mouth hygiene, calcium derivative, periodontal pocket, bone regeneration, and alveolar bone loss.

Besides, there were more papers regarding animal experiment, mice, cost benefit analysis, systematic review, and meta-analysis.

Discussion

Scientometric analysis is a reproducible, systematic, and transparent process that provides an overview of the current studies related to a particular field. This scientometric study attempted to analyze the bibliometric characteristics and research trends of all the papers on intentional replantation retrieved from the Scopus database. The increasing annual number citations of the papers, particularly in the past 4 years, suggest that this technique has governed increasing attention and investigation. Bibliometric characteristics including journals of publications, contributing authors, and institutions and countries of origin were identified in sequence. These would aid clinicians and researchers in choosing target journals, finding potential collaborators or partner institutions, as well as promoting mutual understanding and more reciprocal cooperation regarding research. For instance, Huang et al.9 previously reported that the most productive journals in the field of intentional replantation were Journal of Endodontics, Dental Traumatology, and Oral Surgery Oral Medicine Oral Pathology Oral Radiology. After 2017, we observed that the most productive journals were Journal of Endodontics and International Endodontic Journal. The other two journals mentioned by Huang et al.9 were no longer productive. The possible reason might be that the scholars regarding intentional replantation would prefer to select endodontic journals to publish their studies.

The strength of this scientometric study was to carry out the analysis of chronological comparison (before 2017 versus after 2017) based on all the common keywords of the papers on intentional replantation. The trend of bone substitutes, boron compounds, and boron derivative before 2017, has changed to aluminum compounds, aluminum derivative, and silicate after 2017. The trend of treatment keywords such as anti-bacterial agents, antiinfective agent, curettage, guided tissue regeneration, post and core technique, and retrograde obturation before 2017, has changed to apical surgery, endodontic surgery, surgical extrusion, periodontal regeneration, treatment failure, and platelet-rich fibrin after 2017.2–4,10 Besides, there were more systematic review and/or meta-analyses of the effectiveness and outcome of intentional replantation after 2017.11–14 It is worth noting that current studies on intentional replantation mainly relies on case reports and series, and there is a lack of high-level evidence such as large-sample prospective cohort studies and randomized controlled trials.15–18 Regarding the limitations of the current study, we only searched all the English papers from the Scopus database and thus may overlook important research published in other languages and other databases. Moreover, the more recent papers could not accumulate a large number of citations at the time of this study.

In summary, this scientometric study elucidated the current scenario and research trends in the field of intentional replantation with emphasis on the analysis of the keywords. Accordingly, finding the scientometrics would elucidate the comprehensive identification and recognition of the important research topics concerned, and help in improving in reciprocal collaboration and communication for investigations on intentional replantation. In order to obtain more convincing conclusions, further studies with larger sample sizes and longer observation periods are needed.19,20

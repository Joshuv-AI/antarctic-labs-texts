Treatment outcomes and associated factors among road traffic injury patients in emergency departments of public hospitals in Awi Zone Northwest Ethiopia
Authors: Henok Demelash, Gashaw Alamineh Wondem, Muluken Teshome, Esuyawkal Mislu, Shimelis Tadesse, Aysheshim Belaineh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractGlobally, road traffic injuries (RTIs) cause numerous tragedies such as serious economic loss to the community and death of young people. In Ethiopia, a large proportion of serious injuries result from RTIs and have become major causes of death in hospitals. However, there is insufficient research conducted on treatment outcomes of road traffic injuries and associated factors in the study area. The primary aim of this study was to determine the magnitude of poor treatment outcomes and identify associated factors among patients in the emergency departments of public hospitals in Awi Zone, Northwest Ethiopia. A facility-based cross-sectional study was employed in Awi Zone public hospitals, northwest Ethiopia. With a sample of Medical charts of 461 RTI patients were reviewed and data were collected between January 1, 2022 and June 30, 2024. Data were collected by using data collection checklist. Four nurses and one health officer were employed as data collector and supervisor respectively. Data were entered using Epi Data version 4.7 and cleaned, coded, and analyzed using SPSS version 27. Bivariate analysis was computed and variables with p-value < 0.25 were included in multi-variable logistic regression. The significant of statistical associations were tested using odds ratio and 95% confidence interval (CI) and p-value < 0.05. Finally, the results were presented in texts, tables, and graphs. For this study, 461 study subjects of RTIs victims were enrolled. Among these, 49 (10.6%) patients had poor treatment outcomes. Patients aged 31–50 years [AOR = 0.091, 95% CI: 0.019–0.443], patient age > 50 years [AOR = 0.114, 95% CI: 0.021–0.606], absence of complication [AOR: 0.021; CI: (0.002–0.208)], and patients who received first aid [AOR: 0.340; CI: (0.123–0.938)] were significantly associated with poor treatment outcomes. The study showed a high rate of prognosis but still the poor outcome was not underestimated. Age, absence of complications, and first aid service were statistically significant factors that affect treatment outcomes. Therefore, health care providers should prioritize those RTI victims with complication, not received first aid service, and younger age groups.Keywords: Trauma care, road traffic injury, emergency department, clinical outcomes, Hospital based study, low resource settingsSubject terms: Diseases, Health care, Medical research, Risk factors

Introduction

Road traffic injury (RTI) is an accident that occurs on a way or street open to public traffic, resulting in one or more persons being killed or injured, and involving at least one moving vehicle1. In today’s world, RTI is treated as a major epidemic of non-communicable disease and it results in permanent disability, through amputation, head injury, or spinal cord injury2. This causes numerous family tragedies and represents a serious economic loss to the community and the death of young people3.

According to World Health Organization (WHO) reports, more than 1.24 million people die every year and 20–50 million people are injured or disabled; thus making the loss of 518 billion US dollars globally4. RTI was “the eighth leading cause of death globally”5. Based on current trends, it is projected to be the fifth leading cause of death globally by 20306. Every day in Africa, about 2,400 individuals die due to road traffic crashes7.

In low and middle income countries (LMICs), such as African countries, deaths from RTIs are 40% higher than in all other LMICs and 50% higher than the world average8. In Sub-Saharan Africa, there are challenges to delivering high-quality emergency medical services due to patient overload, poor integration with other health services, limited and inefficient services, poor clinical documentation, and a shortage of physicians and necessary supplies3,9.

Different types of RTIs are motorcycle accidents, animal collisions, pedestrian accidents, bicycle accidents and car accidents1,10. The most common injury is car injury which injured and killed the large number of people10. RTIs patients are often disabled or die due to lack of access to pre-hospital services11, delayed presentation12, not received first aid service before arrival to hospital13, and poor comprehensive emergency care system14.

Developing countries have high burden in road traffic-related deaths and injuries, primarily due to an increase in motor vehicle numbers, poor enforcement of traffic safety regulations, inadequacy of public health infrastructure, and poor access to health services15. The problem of RTIs among trauma patients in Ethiopia also has a significant magnitude. A previous systematic review and meta-analysis showed that 31.5% of prevalence was found16.

Even though RTI was one of the most common causes of morbidity and mortality among almost all age groups, treatment outcome and associated factors of RTI is not well addressed in the study area. In addition, the treatment outcome of RTI may be varied from place to place and time to time due to different factors. Therefore, this study will contribute valuable information on treatment outcomes of RTI and identify associated factors.

The finding of this study will help generate evidence for decision makers, policy planners, future researchers and program evaluators to develop successful evidence based preventive strategies on trauma care. Therefore, the purpose of this study was to determine treatment outcomes and identify associated factors of road traffic injury at Awi Zone Public Hospitals, Northwest Ethiopia.

Methods and materials

Study area and period

The study was conducted in Awi zone public hospitals and data were collected between January 1, 2022 and June 30, 2024. The administrative town of Awi zone is Injibara. Injibara is 114 km far from Bahir Dar and 449 km away from Addis Ababa, the capital city of Ethiopia. Awi Zone public hospitals of Amhara region were bordered in the west by Benishangul Gumuz region, in north-by-north Gondar zone and in east by west Gojam zone. According to the population projection of 2024, Awi zone population was expected to be 1,401,487 of these about 686,789 males and 714,758 females. Eighty percent of the population are rural inhabitants, while 20% are urban dwellers. In the Awi zone, one general hospital and four primary hospitals render services to all populations of the zone and the nation at large. All hospital are organized into various wards, including medical, surgical, gynecology and obstetrics, orthopedics, oncology, pediatrics, and an intensive care unit (ICU). It also has adult and pediatric emergency departments and multiple outpatient departments. Available imaging services include conventional radiology, ultrasonography, and computed tomography. All hospitals provide emergency health care services. In emergency department (ED), the physician-to-patient ratio is 1 to 46,721 (Source: Awi Zone Health department, 2024).

Study design

A facility based cross sectional study was employed.

Population: source and study population

All RTI patients who were admitted in the ED of Awi zone public hospitals were the source population, whereas randomly selected road traffic injured patients who were admitted in the emergency department of public hospitals in Awi zone, between January 1, 2022 and June 30, 2024 were the study population.

Eligibility criteria: inclusion and exclusion criteria

All road traffic injured individuals who were admitted to public hospitals in Awi zone, from January 1, 2022 to June 30, 2024 with complete patient charts were included in this study. On the other hand, RTI victims who died before arrival, immediately referred to other hospitals, and incomplete cards (if treatment outcome status was unknown) were excluded from the study.

Sample size determination

Sample size for the first specific objective was determined using a single population proportion formula with the assumption of 95% level of confidence interval (CI) and 4% margin of error. Sampling techniques and procedures

All five public hospitals in Awi zone were included in the study. The Hospitals were Injibara General Hospital, Chagni Primary Hospital, Dangla Primary Hospital, Jawi Primary Hospital, Agew Gmjabet Primary Hospital. Sampling frame was prepared for all Hospitals and the sample was allocated proportionally for each hospital with respective to the number of admitted road traffic injured patients. Data collection instrument and procedure

Data were collected using extraction tool (checklist) adopted from the Federal Ministry of Health emergency registration logbook and related literature14–17. Frequency, characteristics and hospital outcomes of road traffic injuries and their victims. The questionnaires were prepared in English. Then trained data collectors (4 nurses) collected data. The data collection process supervised by one health officer, during data collection. The supervisor cross-checked for completeness and consistency of collected data on a daily basis and record keepers sorted out all the trauma cases from logbooks and medical records. The data collectors also traced and collected data from randomly identified charts of trauma cases using checklist.

Data quality assurance

In order to maintain quality of the data, data collectors and supervisors were trained in data collection procedures. The checklist or formats were carefully designed and prepared in English. Before actual data collection time the checklist was pretested for validity and reliability by trained data collector experts in Bura primary hospital, which is outside the study area. Then possible adjustment or modifications were made on the tool. The collected data was reviewed and checked for completeness and consistency by principal investigator on a daily basis.

Data processing and analysis

The data was checked, cleaned, coded and data were entered using Epi data 4.6 version and exported to Statistical Package for Social Science (SPSS) version 27 statistical software for analysis. Descriptive statistics such as percentage, frequency, mean, and standard deviation were presented using graphs and tables. Bi-variable and multivariable logistic regression models were computed to assess the association between the independent variables and dependent variable. Significant of statistical association was tested using odds ratios and its 95% confidence interval (CI) and p value (< 0.05). Variables with p-value < 0.25 in the bi-variable logistic regression were considered for multi-variable analysis. Variables having a p-value < 0.05 in the multi-variable logistic regression model, considered as statistically significant factor that associated with the dependent variable. Besides, the goodness of fit of model was checked using Hosmer–Lemeshow test. Multicollinearity was assessed the relationship between independent variables, and checked by using a variance inflation factor (VIF) at a cut off (VIF < 5).

Treatment outcome of road traffic injured patients

Of the total RTI victims, about 411 (89.4%) were discharged with improvement and 49(10.6%) discharged with poor treatment outcome (4.2% died in hospital and 6.4% had disability).

Factors associated with treatment outcome of RTI

The regression analysis showed that patients aged 31–50 years had 91% lower odds of poor outcome compared with those patient’s age < 15 years [AOR = 0.091,95% CI:0.019–0.443,P = 0.003]. In addition, patients aged > 50 years had 89% lower odds of poor outcome compared with those patient’s age < 15 years [AOR = 0.114, 95% CI: 0.021–0.606, P = 0.011]. Moreover, in this study patients without complications were 98% less likely to develop poor outcomes compared with those who developed complications [AOR: 0.021; CI: (0.002–0.208), p = 0.001]. Furthermore, this study findings showed that patients who received first aid before reaching the hospital were 66% less likely to develop poor outcomes compared with those who did not receive first aid [AOR: 0.340; CI: (0.123–0.938), p = 0.037] (Table 5).

Discussion

This study revealed a 10.6% prevalence of poor treatment outcomes (death or permanent disability) among 461 RTI victims admitted to Awi Zone Public Hospitals between January 2022 and June 2024. Among these, 4.2% died in hospital and 6.4% were discharged with permanent disability. The death outcome observed in this study was higher as compared to the studies conducted in hospitals of India (1.33%)23, (2%) in Iran24, Zewuditu Memorial hospital (1%)25. This might be due to the studies were conducted in five public hospitals in Awi zone and also many victims were linked from another health facility, due to this patient overload and life-threatening cases may lead to higher deaths. The death reported in this study was lower than the study conducted in tertiary care center Tanzania (17.5%)26 and in previous study of Ethiopia, Jimma specialized hospital (12.6%)27. This might be due to timing of the study, when people were not freely moving from place to place due to security problem.

Regarding factors that associated poor treatment outcomes, age was a significant factor. Patients age between 31 and 50 years (A = 0.091) and > 50 years (AOR = 0.114) had markedly lower odds of poor treatment outcomes compared to patients of age < 15 years. These findings contrast with other studies reporting such as studies done in public and private hospitals at Adama28, Tanzania29, and Iran30 had higher poor treatment outcomes in age group of 25 and 44. The plausible reason might be higher exposure or physiological vulnerability. In addition, patients < 15 years of age group were school-age children who may be more vulnerable for RTI during walking to school and low access of care transportation when students go to school.

Moreover, the present study showed that presence of complication was a significant factor for poor treatment outcome (AOR = 0.021). This finding is in line with previous study conducted in Tibebe Gion specialized hospital, Ethiopia32. This might be due to when patients after trauma do not come to the hospital immediately, and most of the time severe cases were admitted in the emergency room.

Furthermore, first aid service was found to be a significant factor for poor treatment outcomes. The study findings revealed that patients who got first aid were 66% less likely to develop poor treatment outcome ((AOR: 0.340; CI: (0.123–0.938)). This might be justifying the facts that first aid is basic life-supporting care in stabilizing patients that would decrease complications. This implies that getting timely life supporting first aid during RTI would help to improve treatment outcome of patients and decrease the occurrence of RTI related death.

Strength and Limitation

Strength

The study included all public hospitals in the zone, improving generalizability.

Limitation

The retrospective nature of the study relies on secondary data extracted from medical records, which could be incomplete or inconsistently documented. As a result, some important clinical variables such as injury severity, Glasgow Coma Scale, initial hemoglobin level, blood pressure on arrival, comorbidity, and substance use could not be reliably assessed, potentially leading to information bias. Additionally, treatment outcomes were based on hospital admission records, and long-term outcomes after discharge could not be evaluated.

Moreover, the proportion used for sample size determination for this study might underestimate our sample size because of the difference of study settings. Furthermore, initial haemoglobin level in acute trauma the level of hemoglobin would not show the degree of injury.

Conclusions

The present study showed a high rate of prognosis following RTI and demonstrated that obtaining first aid prior to hospital entrance was strongly associated with good treatment outcomes, whereas being younger, age older than 50 years, and presence of complication were significantly associated with poor treatment outcomes. However, the current study highlights the importance of involving health care providers to prioritize patients who are younger age (less than 15 years old), older than 50 years, have additional complications, or did not receive first aid prior to hospital arrival throughout their management approaches in order to improve RTI treatment outcomes. To reduce the problem of RTI in Awi Zone of Amhara Region, policy makers and traffic authorities should also enforce strict laws and regulation for drivers. Other stakeholders should also take the necessary actions.

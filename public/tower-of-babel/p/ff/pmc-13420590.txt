Case Description

A 28-year-old man with alcohol use disorder was admitted with confusion. His vital signs were notable for sinus tachycardia and elevated blood pressure. He was disoriented to time and place, and demonstrated incoherent speech, consistent with alcohol withdrawal and possible delirium tremens. Laboratory evaluation showed severe lactic acidosis and ketoacidosis, hyperkalemia (potassium: 6 mEq/dL), elevated creatinine (1.5 m/dL), and elevated hepatic transaminases. Head computed tomography revealed an acute left frontoparietal subdural hematoma. Echocardiography demonstrated a severely dilated left ventricle with global hypokinesis and an ejection fraction of <20%. Telemetry revealed frequent premature ventricular complexes. Electrocardiogram (Figure 1) showed sinus rhythm, marked prolonged QTc, and unusual pattern of alternating T-wave morphologies with 3-beat periodicity. He also had frequent premature ventricular complexes in a pattern of quadrigeminy, with the QRS complex falling on the T wave (R-on-T phenomenon). Shortly after electrocardiogram acquisition, the patient developed torsades de pointes that degenerated into ventricular fibrillation, necessitating emergent defibrillation. Discussion

Torsades de pointes is a polymorphic ventricular tachycardia classically associated with QT interval prolongation, and it represents a life-threatening manifestation of ventricular electrical instability. The condition is typically multifactorial, arising from the convergence of predisposing substrate abnormalities and acute triggering factors. This case illustrates how alcohol withdrawal, severe metabolic derangements, acute neurologic injury, and underlying cardiomyopathy can synergistically precipitate malignant ventricular arrhythmias.

T-wave alternans (TWA) on electrocardiogram is identified by a repetitive pattern in T-wave amplitude or morphology, commonly with 2-beat periodicity and rarely with 3-beat periodicity, as demonstrated in this case. TWA reflects beat-to-beat variation in action potential duration and early after depolarizations that increase dispersion of repolarization, creating regional differences in refractoriness that facilitate reentry.1

Although QTc prolongation is present in many at-risk patients but rarely progresses to torsades de pointes, macroscopic TWA represents a rare but highly specific marker of imminent ventricular arrhythmia. QTc prolongation establishes the arrhythmogenic substrate through delayed repolarization, whereas TWA indicates critical dysfunction in intracellular calcium handling that actively triggers arrhythmia initiation.2

The recognition of TWA as a marker of arrhythmogenic risk has led to the development of microvolt TWA testing, a noninvasive tool used for risk stratification of sudden cardiac death in selected patient populations.3

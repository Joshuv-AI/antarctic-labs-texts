Simplified calculation method for the critical load bearing of market-based profile emergency steel pier
Authors: Zi-yu Liu, Shi-tong Chen, Mo-mo Zhi, Kai Zhang, Hong-tao Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
A novel emergency steel pier, assembled from market-available profiles, has been developed to address the challenges associated with the "regular stockpiling and rapid emergency deployment" of bridge pier repair equipment. These challenges often involve significant labor and financial costs for manufacturing and maintenance. The buckling modes of the columns and pier-girder connections were considered. Using energy methods and plate-shell theory, an analytical formula for the critical load-bearing capacity of the steel pier under emergency conditions was derived. Numerical simulations were conducted for comparison and validation, resulting in an optimized and simplified method for calculating the critical load capacity, which is suitable for engineering applications.The study shows that the critical load-bearing capacity of the emergency steel pier exhibits distinct patterns under the influence of column buckling modes and pier-girder connection methods. In cases where the column is fixed at one end and hinged at the other, or fixed at both ends, the critical load for axisymmetric buckling is affected by factors such as elastic modulus, Poisson's ratio, height, radius, wall thickness, and the number of buckling waves. When considering non-axisymmetric buckling, the number of circumferential buckling waves becomes an additional factor. A simplified method for calculating the critical load-bearing capacity was developed. The simplified formula depends only on a correction factor, modified buckling stress, the number of columns, and cross-sectional area. This makes it highly practical for emergency engineering applications.

Introduction

The level of bridge construction reflects a country's technological advancement and overall national strength, and the emergency response capability of bridges is equally important [1,2]. Bridge piers, as the main supporting structures of bridges, are prone to damage from events such as earthquakes, wars, and collisions. Damage to piers can lead to the disruption of bridge traffic, making the rapid repair and restoration of piers in emergency situations crucial [3,4]. In response to the need for emergency pier repairs, China has developed several types of emergency repair equipment for bridge piers, such as the Type 65 Military Railway Pier, Type 83 Lightweight Military Railway Pier, and prefabricated highway steel bridge piers. Other countries focus more on repair equipment for the superstructure of bridges, with limited reports on emergency pier repair equipment development [5]. Overall, both domestically and internationally, the availability of emergency repair equipment specifically for bridge piers is insufficient [6,7].

An analysis of the existing emergency pier repair equipment reveals that they are standardized and follow a design approach based on "prefabrication, stockpiling during peacetime, and rapid deployment during emergencies" [8]. Due to the unpredictability of natural disasters, it is challenging to accurately estimate the quantity and scale of equipment needed to meet emergency bridge pier repair demands. Insufficient stockpiling can result in inadequate emergency response capability, while excessive stockpiling requires substantial financial and human resources, leading to waste. Therefore, there is an urgent need to develop a novel emergency steel pier that can be assembled from readily available market profiles.

For new pier emergency repair materials, conducting critical load-bearing capacity analysis can determine the upper safety threshold of the structural load-bearing capacity [9,10]. Currently, scholars have conducted extensive research on the calculation methods of critical load-bearing capacity for piers [11,12]. Wu et al. derived the calculation formula for the critical load-bearing capacity of high rigid piers in cantilever and completed bridge states based on elastic stability theory and the energy method, but they did not consider the impact of vertical displacement on piers [13]. Zhou et al. derived the calculation formula for the critical load-bearing capacity of combined piers composed of double thin-walled piers and single piers based on the Euler critical force differential equation of a single cantilever column fixed at one end and free at the other, but they did not consider the wind load effect on the pier body [14]. Cheng et al. derived the calculation formula for the critical load-bearing capacity of semi-rigid node supports using the variational method and found that the critical load-bearing capacity decreases with the increase in support height [15]. Feng used the energy method to derive the calculation formula for the critical load-bearing capacity of double-limb thin-walled high rigid piers, but the calculation ignored the axial deformation energy and shear deformation energy of the piers [16]. Lan et al. derived an approximate analytical formula for the critical load-bearing capacity of irregular double-column high piers based on the stiffness activation principle of compressed columns and verified the accuracy of the formula through examples and ANSYS calculations [17]. Lin et al. conducted stability performance research on steel-concrete composite columns, considering factors such as the height-to-width ratio of the section and connecting rods, and determined the stability coefficient based on finite element calculation results, thereby obtaining the calculation formula for the critical load-bearing capacity of steel-concrete composite columns under axial compression and bending [18]. In summary, the reasonable determination of the critical load-bearing capacity of piers requires consideration of the structural characteristics and related influencing factors of the piers.

This paper develops a novel emergency steel pier based on market profiles, incorporating a "temporary-permanent" transition function. Building on this foundation, considering the buckling modes of columns and the connection methods between piers and beams, it derives an analytical formula for the critical load-bearing capacity of the steel pier under emergency conditions using the energy method and plate-shell theory. This formula is validated through comparison with numerical simulation results, aiming to propose a simplified calculation method for the critical load-bearing capacity of steel piers suitable for rapid repair scenarios, ensuring the safe application of piers in emergency situations.

Emergency repair steel pier design based on market profiles

The elevation view of the emergency repair steel pier based on market profiles is shown in Fig. 1(a). The basic components include standard segments and top segment. The standard segments consist of columns, inner sleeves, outer sleeves, and connecting systems, as illustrated in Fig. 1(c). Compared to the standard segments, the top segments incorporate a support platform, as shown in Fig. 1(b). The profiles composing the components include steel pipes, I-beams, channel steels, steel plates, etc., all utilizing market-standard profiles. Considering that steel pipes are the primary vertical load-bearing structure, and accounting for their common length specifications, the top and standard sections are preliminarily designed to be 6 m in length, which can meet the repair needs of medium to low-height piers. The internal columns of each segment are arranged in a square pattern and can be configured as needed. The columns are connected by braces, with steel outer and inner sleeves installed on the outside and inside of the columns, respectively. The purpose of the inner and outer sleeves is to serve as external molds for future concrete pouring if there is a need to convert the structure from temporary to permanent. If no such conversion is required, the sleeves can be omitted. Additionally, the columns between segments are connected using a combination of annular flange bolting and guided plug-in connections. The sleeves between segments are joined by annular flange bolting, while the connection between the outer sleeves and columns is secured through bolting and welding. The support platform at the top segment is a prefabricated concrete element, which connects downward to the pier cap bracing and the capping steel plate. The support platform has pre-drilled holes for different types of bearings.Fig. 1Structure diagram of emergency repair steel pier based on market profiles/cm.Fig. 1

The key feature of the novel emergency steel pier is its ability to be assembled from market-available profiles. Unlike existing railway and highway emergency repair piers, the components can be sourced from the market during an emergency, eliminating the need for large-scale stockpiling. This approach provides a technical reserve during peacetime, addressing the labor and financial issues associated with the traditional "stockpiling and emergency deployment" model of manufacturing and maintenance for repair equipment.

Theory calculation of critical load-bearing capacity of steel piers

The newly developed emergency steel pier primarily relies on a steel structure for load-bearing. The load from the bridge girder is distributed to the surrounding columns via the support platform at the top segment and the pier cap bracing. The load is then transferred downward through the standard segment columns to the foundation. As the upper load is mainly borne by the columns, the critical load-bearing capacity of the emergency steel pier largely depends on the buckling mode of the columns. Under load, the columns may exhibit two types of buckling: axisymmetric and non-axisymmetric. Axisymmetric buckling refers to buckling waves forming only along the axial direction, with the cross-section remaining a thin-walled circular ring and the deflection function only related to deformation in the x-direction [19]. Non-axisymmetric buckling refers to buckling waves forming along both the axial and circumferential directions, with the cross-sectional shape becoming an elliptical ring and the deflection function related to both x and s directions [20]. Therefore, the subsequent analysis focuses on axisymmetric and non-axisymmetric buckling of the columns. When analyzing, the second-order effects of the columns are considered, with the critical load taken as the upper limit of the pier's load-bearing capacity. This can be used for approximate calculations of second-order effect moments. Additionally, considering that the top support platform of the steel pier should meet the installation requirements of both fixed and movable supports, the analysis simulates the constraint effects of different support types by altering the boundary conditions at both ends of the columns. Specifically, two types of constraints are considered: one end fixed and one end hinged, and both ends fixed.

Calculation method of critical load-bearing capacity for axisymmetric buckling

In order to determine the critical load-bearing capacity of the steel pier, it is essential to initially hypothesize the deflection function of column buckling. Subsequently, critical load derivation analysis is performed based on the energy method. During the analysis, the impact of upper beam stiffness on the critical load-bearing capacity of the steel pier is disregarded, while axial compression deformation and membrane stress in the column are taken into account.

Calculation method of critical load-bearing capacity for non-axisymmetric buckling

Considering that under the action of loads, columns may buckle axially and circularly simultaneously, resulting in the cross-section of the column becoming an elliptical thin-walled ring. This further affects the critical bearing capacity of the steel pier for emergency repairs. Therefore, based on the axial symmetric buckling foundation, further research is conducted on the critical bearing capacity of the steel pier under non-axial symmetric buckling conditions.

Simplified calculation of critical bearing capacity of the steel pier

Due to the neglect of nonlinear factors in the previous derivation process of membrane strain energy and external work, the column buckling stress continues to increase beyond the yield strength of the steel. Therefore, firstly, the buckling stress formula is corrected. Subsequently, through comparison and verification with numerical simulation results, a simplified calculation method for the critical bearing capacity of the steel pier is obtained, which is suitable for engineering applications.

Correction of column buckling stress

Given that the buckling stress of the column is related to the number of buckling waves, in order to obtain an analytical solution for the buckling stress of the column applicable to engineering applications, the determination of the number of buckling waves m and n is first carried out. On this basis, a corrected formula for buckling stress is derived. Specifically, when analyzing, the determination of the number of buckling waves and the buckling stress are carried out separately for axisymmetric and non-axisymmetric buckling of the column.

Buckling stress correction formula

Based on existing studies that comprehensively consider structural safety and economic costs, it is recommended to use steel columns of specifications with an outer diameter D = 630 mm and wall thickness t = 10 mm for the new emergency repair steel pier [21]. Based on this, columns with a height h = 6 m made of Q235 profile steel are chosen, with material properties: yield strength fy = 235 MPa, elastic modulus E = 206 GPa, and Poisson's ratio μ = 0.3.

Firstly, an analysis is conducted for the column undergoing axisymmetric buckling with one end fixed and the other end hinged. By substituting the relevant parameters into equations (43), (15), the obtained yield strength exceeds 235 MPa, indicating that the buckling stress of the steel pier calculated using the given formulas is too high, surpassing the yield strength of the steel at 235 MPa. Specifically, the elastic modulus (E) in equation (15) only accounts for the elastic stage and does not consider the yield, strengthening, and necking stages of the steel. This causes the calculated buckling stress of the column to continue increasing beyond the yield strength of the steel. However, in practice, once the steel yields, its corresponding tangent modulus rapidly decreases. In summary, whether the column undergoes axisymmetric or non-axisymmetric buckling, the appropriate buckling stress formula should first be selected based on the pier-beam connection type. If the calculated value σcr exceeds the yield strength fy of the steel, then the buckling stress equals the yield strength fy of the steel. If the calculated value σcr is less than or equal to the yield strength fy, then it represents the buckling stress σcr.

Steel pier critical bearing capacity simplified formula

Due to the complexity of the steel pier critical bearing capacity formula derived earlier involving multiple influencing parameters, for obtaining a simplified calculation method adaptable to rapid repair scenarios, factors such as wind load, bolt hole, connection systems, pier height, and pier-beam restraint methods are considered. Numerical simulation is conducted to verify the reliability of theoretical results based on the derived formula, while simultaneously optimizing the calculation method.

Constraints are applied in the model based on the actual connection methods between components. The connections between columns and the internal connections of inner sleeves adopt a tie constraint, while the bolts, columns, and bearing beams use "face-to-face" contact. Threaded contact is applied to simulate the annular flanges between segmental columns. For "face-to-face" contact, normal behavior is defined using "Hard" contact, and tangential behavior is described by the Coulomb model. In large deformations, the "hard" contact between the circular neck of the bolt and the bolt hole may lead to stress concentration and issues with non-convergence. Therefore, the bolt shank, nut, and washer are modeled as a single integrated component. The column and connecting system are welded together. The weld geometry is neglected in the model, and neither residual stress from welding nor material property changes in the heat-affected zone are considered. Initial geometric imperfections are also not introduced. Additionally, the base of the repair steel pier is considered fixed.

In the finite element model of the assembled emergency steel pier, the columns, bearing beams, bracing systems, and bolts are modeled using 3D 8-node first-order reduced-integration elements (C3D8R). However, using C3D8R elements can lead to hourglass effects during analysis, resulting in a reduction in structural stiffness. The hourglass effect occurs when the 3D 8-node first-order reduced-integration elements bend, and due to the single integration point at the centroid, it becomes difficult to capture element strain in the thickness direction, manifesting as deformation without strain. To mitigate this, multiple layers of mesh are applied in the thickness direction of the plates during analysis, with an even number of four layers. While increasing node density and reducing mesh size can improve the accuracy of the finite element model, it also reduces computational efficiency. Therefore, selecting an appropriate mesh size is critical. Smaller mesh sizes are used in key areas of the finite element model, such as high-strength bolts and flanges, where contact is concentrated and stress concentration and large deformations are likely to occur. In regions with uniform loading and far from critical nodes, such as the ends of the bearing beams, larger mesh sizes are applied. These additional terms are related to the circumferential buckling wave number (n). Analysis of Fig. 7 shows that regardless of whether the pier-beam connection is fixed at both ends or fixed at one end and hinged at the other, the correction factor for the critical load capacity of the steel pier is less than 1 when the pier height increases from 6 m to 42 m. This indicates that the finite element results are smaller than the theoretically derived critical load capacity. The discrepancy arises primarily because, in the theoretical derivation, buckling failure is defined through a deflection function, and buckling stress is corrected by considering strength failure. However, the accuracy of the selected deflection function may be insufficient, leading to a mismatch between the predicted and actual buckling modes, which affects the accuracy of the steel pier's critical load capacity.

In contrast, finite element analysis takes into account more factors present in actual engineering conditions, such as segmental bolting, bolt hole gaps, bracket welding, and the redistribution of internal forces. These factors influence the overall mechanical behavior of the steel pier, making the finite element results more representative of real-world conditions and explaining why the calculated values are lower. Therefore, while the finite element results are smaller than the theoretical values, they more accurately reflect the actual load-bearing capacity of the steel pier under practical conditions.

Although theoretical derivation provides a simplified method for calculating critical load capacity, actual engineering factors affecting load capacity are diverse and complex. Finite element simulations capture more real-world details, especially those related to segmental connections and joint details, making the results more valuable for practical engineering applications. This indicates that the differences between theoretical derivation and numerical simulation are not simply errors but stem from how each model reflects actual working conditions. In engineering practice, both theoretical and numerical analyses should be combined to ensure structural safety and reliability.

Conclusions

This study developed a novel emergency steel pier assembled from standard market profiles. The calculation method for the critical load capacity of the steel pier was investigated, considering column buckling modes and pier-beam connection types under emergency conditions. Based on finite element analysis, a simplified correction method for calculating the critical load capacity was proposed. The main conclusions are as follows.(1)Under both one end fixed-one end hinged and both ends fixed conditions, the axisymmetric buckling critical load capacity of the steel pier is influenced by the column's elastic modulus, Poisson's ratio, height, radius, wall thickness, number of columns, and axial buckling wavelengths. For non-axisymmetric buckling, the influence of circumferential buckling wavelengths must also be considered. The number of buckling wavelengths is determined by the column's radius, wall thickness, Poisson's ratio, and height. Axial buckling wavelengths increase with column height, and when the diameter-to-thickness ratio increases, the wavelengths rise discretely in a stepped manner.(2)Regarding the buckling mode of the column, compared to axially symmetric buckling, the critical bearing capacity of the steel pier is higher when considering non-axially symmetric buckling under the same conditions. Additionally, since the axially symmetric buckling formula is conservative and the non-axially symmetric formula is complex, it is recommended to perform calculation analysis based on axially symmetric buckling in engineering applications.(3)Based on the formula for the critical bearing capacity of steel piers, the influencing factors are further optimized to obtain a simplified calculation method for the critical bearing capacity of steel piers. The simplified critical bearing capacity only requires considering the correction factor, corrected buckling stress, number of columns, and cross-sectional area. The adjustment coefficient should be determined according to the pier-beam connection method, with 0.80 for one end fixed and one end hinged, and 0.85 for both ends fixed.(4)The initial deformation of the columns and residual stresses from welding affect their buckling performance. Initial deformation may reduce the critical buckling load capacity, while residual stresses from welding can cause localized stress concentrations, impacting the buckling load. Therefore, further refinement of the calculation method is needed to improve the accuracy of the load capacity analysis for emergency steel piers. Additionally, specialized experimental studies will be conducted to further enhance the calculation method for the critical load capacity of emergency steel piers.

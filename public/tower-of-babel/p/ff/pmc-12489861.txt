PGC1A Restores Mitochondrial Health to Attenuate EMT During Lens Epithelial Fibrosis via Regulating TFAM
Authors: Jingqi Huang, Peiyi Jiang, Baoxin Chen, Mi Huang, Jieping Chen, Jiani Wang, Shan Huang, Yizhi Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
PurposeEpithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) of lens epithelial cells (LECs) is a predominant pathological driver for fibrotic cataracts. This study explores the role and mechanism of peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor γ coactivator 1-α (PGC1A), a key mitochondrial regulator, in EMT of LECs.MethodsRNA-sequencing analysis was applied to reveal biological changes during human lens epithelial fibrosis. Primary rabbit LECs were treated with TGFβ2 to induce EMT. Mitochondrial alterations were evaluated by MitoTracker staining, transmission electron microscopy, mitochondrial membrane potential assay, ATP content assay, and reactive oxygen species (ROS) assay. Loss- and gain-of-function studies were performed to uncover roles and mechanisms of PGC1A in EMT of LECs. Changes of PGC1A, EMT markers, and mitochondrial regulators were analyzed by Western blot, immunofluorescence staining, and RT-qPCR. Cell migration was assessed using the cell scratch assay. Ex vivo whole rat lenses were treated with TGFβ2 to induce fibrotic cataract to evaluate the potential therapeutic effect of PGC1A on lens fibrosis. Lens epithelial fibrosis was examined by hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) and immunofluorescence staining.ResultsPGC1A was decreased with significant mitochondrial dysfunction during TGFβ2-induced EMT of LECs. PGC1A silencing promoted EMT by enhancing TGFβ2-Smad2/3 signaling, accelerating subcapsular fibrotic plaque formation. PGC1A upregulation protected LECs from TGFβ2-induced EMT by restoring mitochondrial health and energy metabolism. Mechanistically, PGC1A inhibition decreased mitochondrial transcription factor (TFAM), which mediated protective effects of PGC1A on mitochondria and LECs. Further, ZLN005, a PGC1A agonist, attenuated fibrotic lens opacity via preventing LECs from EMT.ConclusionsPGC1A safeguards LECs against EMT by restoring TFAM-mediated mitochondrial energy metabolism under TGFβ2 stress, offering potential targets for the treatment of lens epithelial fibrosis.

Lens epithelial cells (LECs) are the principal cells of the crystalline lens throughout life by supporting its development and homeostasis. During embryonic development, LECs are determined to form lens fibers and lens epithelium, constructing a transparent lens under different ocular environmental signals.1 The lens epithelium is a well-organized monolayer of LECs beneath the lens anterior capsule, constituting the protective barrier, metabolic center, and transport site to maintain homeostasis and transparency of the adult lens.2

Activation of TGFβ signaling, triggered by injuries such as ocular trauma, intraocular surgeries, and metabolic disorders or inflammation, could induce aberrant epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) of LECs.3 This conversion from an epithelial to mesenchymal phenotype disturbs the morphology and intercellular junctions of LECs, directly damaging integrity and function of the lens epithelium. Moreover, acquisition of mesenchymal features, including intracellular cytoskeleton remodeling and extracellular matrix (ECM) deposition along with invasive migration, ultimately leads to the formation of subcapsular plaques consisting of multilayered and disorganized mesenchymal-like cells. This process contributes to the development of fibrotic cataracts, including posterior capsule opacification (PCO) and anterior subcapsular cataract (ASC).3,4 However, as other fibrotic disorders that result in organ failure, there are currently no clinically effective interventions for lens fibrosis.5

Programmed degradation of nuclei and membrane-bound organelles including mitochondria during lens fiber maturation is a necessity for lens transparency.6 As a result, LECs are the major cells in the lens that contain functioning mitochondria, making them the most metabolically active cells in the mature lens. Mitochondria are bioenergetic powerhouses in eukaryotes, producing adenosine triphosphate (ATP) via oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS) to support cell metabolism and viability.7 Factors including genetic defects, environmental stresses, and systemic disorders, could affect mitochondrial activity and functionality, thereby altering cell behavior and tissue function. Dysfunction of mitochondria manifests in various diseases across organs including fibrotic disorders.7–9 Mitochondrial metabolism is emerging as a critical regulatory mechanism for maintaining epithelial integrity and actively repressing epithelial fibrosis in the kidneys and lungs.10–14 Our RNA-sequencing (RNA-seq) analysis first uncovered significant mitochondrial dysfunction as well as dramatically decreasing peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor γ coactivator 1-α (PGC1A), a master regulator of mitochondrial biogenesis and energy metabolism,15–17 during TGFβ2-induced lens epithelial fibrosis. However, whether and how mitochondria respond to TGFβ2 stress to regulate EMT of LECs during the development of fibrotic cataracts remains to be investigated.

Here, we revealed that loss of PGC1A promoted TGFβ2-smad2/3 signaling transduction to aggravate EMT of LECs and lens epithelial fibrosis, which accelerated subcapsular fibrotic plaques formation. Conversely, PGC1A upregulation restored mitochondrial health and respiration, thereby attenuating TGFβ2-Smad2/3 signals and EMT of LECs. Mechanistically, mitochondrial transcription factor A (TFAM), a key effector of mitochondrial biogenesis and energy metabolism through its contributions to transcription for essential proteins of the OXPHOS machinery,18 mediated the protective effect of PGC1A on LECs against TGFβ2-induced EMT. Furthermore, ZLN005, an agonist targeting PGC1A, inhibited EMT of LECs and fibrotic plaques formation via restoring mitochondrial bioenergetic function. Together, this study unveils an indispensable role of PGC1A/TFAM-mediated mitochondrial energy metabolism in LECs fate transition under TGFβ2 stress, providing potential strategies for the intervention of fibrotic cataracts.

Methods

RNA-Seq Data Analysis

Our RNA-seq data of human lens epithelial explants (HLEEs) was acquired as previously described (Project Accession No. PRJCA010973, GSA Accession code No. HRA002775).19 HLEEs were isolated and cultured with or without 10 ng/mL TGFβ2 for 48 hours. After quality control, high throughput bulk RNA-seq was conducted with a high sequence platform (NovaSeq 6000; Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA). Differential gene expression analysis between TGFβ2-induced HLEEs and normal HLEEs with three biological replicates was performed using DESeq2, which uses a negative binomial distribution-based model. To control the false discovery rate, P values were adjusted using the Benjamini-Hochberg method. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs; P < 0.05 and |log2FC|≥1) were identified and subjected to Gene Ontology enrichment analysis.20

Isolation, Culture, and Treatment of Primary LECs

All procedures involving animals adhered to the ARVO Statement for the Use of Animals in Ophthalmic and Vision Research and were approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Sun Yat-Sen University. Primary LECs were isolated from 2-month-old New Zealand White Rabbits and cultured in Minimum Essential Medium (MEM; 11095080; Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; 10099141C; Thermo Fisher Scientific, Thornton, AU) and 1% non-essential amino acids (11140050; Life Technologies, Carlsbad, CA, USA). To induce EMT, primary rabbit LECs were treated with 10 ng/mL TGFβ2 (302-B2-010; R&D Systems, Minneapolis, MN, USA) for 0 to 3 days. To identify the effects of TGFβ2 on mitochondria in LECs, primary rabbit LECs were treated with or without TGFβ2 for 2 days. To upregulate PGC1A to inhibit TGFβ2-induced EMT in LECs, ZLN005 (20 µM, HY-17538; MedChemExpress, Shanghai, China) or an equal volume of dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) was added to the culture medium 24 hours before treatment with or without TGFβ2 for 2 days.

In Vitro Transfection With Plasmid and siRNA

For gain-of-function studies, pcDNA3.1-PGC1A (oePGC1A) and negative control (oeNC) plasmids were obtained from Dahongbio. For loss-of-function studies, siRNAs targeting PGC1A (siPGC1A), TFAM (siTFAM), and negative control (siNC) were purchased from Ribobio. Following the manufacturer's instructions, plasmid (1.25 ng/mL) or siRNA (100 nM) was mixed with Opti-MEM and transfection reagents using Lipofectamine 3000 (l3000015; Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) and added when primary rabbit LECs reached 70% confluency. After transfection for 24 hours, primary rabbit LECs were treated with or without 10 ng/mL TGFβ2 for 2 days.

Ex Vivo Whole Lens Culture and Treatment

Intact whole lenses were isolated from 6-week-old Sprague-Dawley rats and cultured in Dulbecco's Modified Eagle Medium (C11965500BT; Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) supplemented with 1% FBS. Any opaque lenses due to dissection were discarded and only transparent lenses were used for further experimentation. After siRNA (100 nM) transfection or ZLN005 (20 µM) treatment for 24 hours, lenses were treated with or without 10 ng/mL TGFβ2 for 4 days. Culture medium was renewed every other day throughout the culture period. Each group contained lenses from separate animals (n = 3). After the incubation period, lenses were photographed using a stereoscope (S8AP0; Leica, Wetzlar, Germany), and then either fixed with 4% PFA for hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) and immunofluorescence staining or subjected to Western blot in triplicates.

MitoTracker Staining

Morphological changes of mitochondria in primary rabbit LECs were evaluated by Mitotracker Deep Red (M22426; Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA). Live primary rabbit LECs were washed with Hank’s Balanced Salt Solution (HBSS) three times and then incubated with pre-warmed staining solution containing 200 nM MitoTracker probe for 30 minutes at 37°C. After the HBSS were washed three times, the staining solution was replaced with fresh pre-warmed media. Live LECs were observed and imaged using a confocal microscope (LSM980; Zeiss, Oberkochen, Germany). Confocal images were analyzed by mitochondrial analyzer in ImageJ software.

Transmission Electron Microscopy

Primary rabbit LECs were washed twice with PBS and fixed in 2.5% glutaraldehyde overnight at 4°C, followed by a PBS wash 3 times and being postfixed with 1% osmium tetroxide for 2 hours at 4°C. Samples were dehydrated with gradient ethanol and embedded in epon resin, and polymerized at 60°C for 48 hours. The blocks were ultrathin-sectioned at 80 to 100 nm with a diamond knife using an ultramicrotome (UC7; Leica, Wetzlar, Germany). Ultrathin sections were stained with uranyl acetate for 20 minutes and lead citrate for 12 minutes. Images were captured using a transmission electron microscopy (Tecnai G2 Spirit; FEI, Hillsboro, OR, USA) and then analyzed by ImageJ software to measure the ratio of cristae number to the mitochondrial area (µm²).

Mitochondrial Membrane Potential Assay

Mitochondrial membrane potential was assessed using MT-1 MitoMP Detection Kit (MT13; Dojindo, Kumamoto, Japan). According to the manufacturer's instruction, live primary rabbit LECs were treated with MT-1 working solution (MT-1 Dye diluted in the culture medium at 1:1000) for 30 minutes at 37°C. Following an HBSS wash twice, the images were obtained with the imaging buffer using a confocal microscope (LSM980; Leica, Wetzlar, Germany). The fluorescent intensity was analyzed using ImageJ software.

Mitochondrial or Glycolytic ATP Content Assay

Mitochondrial and glycolytic ATP of primary rabbit LECs were detected by the Glycolysis/OXPHOS Assay Kit (G270; Dojindo, Kumamoto, Japan) according to the manufacturer's protocol. Primary rabbit LECs were incubated with or without 1.25 µmol/L oligomycin for 3 hours at 37°C. After incubation with ATP working solution at 25°C for 10 minutes, the relative light unit was assessed using a microplate reader (Infinite 200 Pro; Tecan, Mannedorf, Switzerland) to determine glycolytic and mitochondrial ATP levels.

Reactive Oxygen Species Assay

Intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels in primary rabbit LECs were measured using an ROS Assay Kit (S0034S; Beyotime, Shanghai, China) according to the manufacturer's protocol. LECs were washed with pre-warmed PBS, and then incubated with 10 µM fluorescent probe DCFH-DA free of light at 37°C for 20 minutes. After incubation, LECs were washed three times with HBSS to remove extracellular DCFH-DA. Fluorescence images were captured immediately using a fluorescence microscope (DM3000; Leica, Wetzlar, Germany).

Protein Isolation and Western Blot

Primary rabbit LECs and rat lens epithelium were lysed in RIPA buffer (P0013C; Beyotime, Shanghai, China) with PMSF and PhosStop (04906837001; Roche, Basel, Switzerland). Proteins were separated by SurePAGE gels (GeneScript Biotech, Nanjing, China) and transferred onto PVDF membranes (IPVH00010; Merck Millipore, Burlington, MA, USA). After blocking with 5% BSA, the membranes were incubated with primary antibodies overnight at 4°C, followed by secondary HRP-conjugated antibodies (Cell Signaling Technology, Danvers, MA, USA) at a dilution of 1:2000 for 2 hours at room temperature. Targeted protein bands were visualized with immobilon Western chemiluminescent HRP substrate (WBKLS0500; Merck Millipore, Burlington, MA, USA) using a ChemiDoc imaging system (12003153; Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA). The intensity of protein bands was determined and further normalized to β-actin using ImageJ software.

Primary antibodies used in this study included: PGC1A (sc517380, 1:200; Santa Cruz), FN (ab137720, 1:1000; Abcam), α-SMA (ab7817, 1:2000; Abcam), ZO-1 (617300, 1:1000; Invitrogen), E-cad (14472, 1:1000; CST), MFN1 (14739, 1:1000; CST), MFN2 (9482, 1:1000; CST), DRP1 (8570, 1:1000; CST), phospho-Smad2 (3108, 1:1000; CST), phospho-Smad3 (9520, 1:1000; CST), Smad2/3 (8685,1:1000; CST), TFAM (176558, 1:10000; Abcam), NRF1 (ab175932, 1:3000; Abcam), NRF2 (sc28312, 1:200; Santa Cruz), and β-actin (3700, 1:3000; CST).

RNA Extraction and Real-Time quantitative Polymerase Chain Reaction

Total RNA was extracted using RNeasy Mini Kit (74104; Qiagen, Hilden, Germany) and converted to first-strand cDNA with PrimeScript RT Master Mix (RR036A; Takara, Kutsatsu, Japan). The qPCR was performed using the QuantiNova SYBR Green PCR kit (208054; Qiagen, Hilden, Germany) on a QuantStudio 7 Flex system (Applied Biosystems, Waltham, MA, USA). Relative RNA expression was quantified using the comparative 2-ΔΔCt method and normalized to β-actin. Primers used in this study were synthesized by Tsingke Biotech: Ocu-PGC1A-F: ACAAACAATGACCCTCCGCA; Ocu-PGC1A-R: GTTGTTGGTTTGGCTTG-CAC.

Cell Scratch Assay

Cell migration was evaluated using a cell scratch assay. Primary LECs were seeded onto 6-well plates and grown to 100% confluency. A scratch was made using a sterile 200 µL pipette tip. After removing detached cells by PBS wash, LECs were cultured in FBS-free medium. Scratch areas were imaged 10 hours after the scratches were made using a light microscope. The percentage of the healing area to the entire scratch area was analyzed using ImageJ software.

Immunofluorescence Staining and Hematoxylin and Eosin Staining

Primary LECs were fixed with 4% PFA for 20 minutes. Lenses were fixed with 10% neutral buffered formalin, dehydrated in gradient ethanol, and embedded in paraffin. After antigen retrieval, freshly-cut lens sections were blocked in 3% BSA with 0.3% Triton X-100 at room temperature for 1 hour, and incubated with primary antibodies at 4°C overnight, followed by incubation of fluorochrome-conjugated secondary antibodies (1:1000, CST) free of light for 1 hour at room temperature. After counterstaining with DAPI for 5 minutes, the images were captured using a microscope (DM3000; Leica, Wetzlar, Germany). Primary antibodies included: FN (ab137720, 1:100; Abcam), α-SMA (ab7817, 1:100; Abcam), ZO-1 (617300, 1:100; Invitrogen), E-cad (14472, 1:100; CST), and Smad2/3 (8685,1:400; CST).

For H&E staining, lens sections were prepared as described above and stained with hematoxylin solution (BA-4097; BASO Diagnostics, New Taipei City, Taiwan) and eosin solution (BA-4098; BASO Diagnostics, New Taipei City, Taiwan). Images were obtained using a microscope (DM3000; Leica, Wetzlar, Germany).

Statistical Analysis

All experiments were repeated at least three times. All data were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism 9. Two-tailed unpaired Student's t-test was used for comparisons between the 2 groups, and 1-way ANOVA was applied to compare multiple groups. In 1-way ANOVA, the normality was verified via Shapiro-Wilk test and the homogeneity of variances was verified via Brown-Forsythe test. Significance was accepted at the 95% confidence level (* P < 0.05, ** P < 0.01, and *** P < 0.001).

Results

Mitochondria Were Dysregulated Accompanied With Decreased PGC1A During TGFβ2-Induced Lens Epithelial Fibrosis and EMT of LECs

To clarify whether TGFβ2 would influence mitochondrial function in LECs, we screened out DEGs (Fig. 1A) during TGFβ2-induced human lens epithelial fibrosis for enrichment analysis of biological process (Fig. 1B). According to the gene ontology (GO) analysis, mitochondria in fibrotic lens epithelium not only exhibited disrupted morphology and organization with increased mitochondrial membrane permeability and release of cytochrome C, but also showed dysfunction in energy metabolism with decreased OXPHOS and enhanced glycolysis, impaired respiratory chain complex assembly, and compromised mitochondrial transmembrane transport (see Fig. 1B).

Next, we validated alterations of mitochondrial homeostasis with cellular model of EMT in primary rabbit LECs treated with TGFβ2. As shown in Figures 1C to 1E, the decrease of epithelial markers such as intercellular junctions formed by E-cadherin (E-cad) and zonula occludens-1 (ZO-1) indicated impaired lens epithelial integrity. Concurrently, aberrant fibronectin (FN) deposition in ECM and cytoskeleton remodeling with increased α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA) further confirmed TGFβ2-induced mesenchymal transition of LECs. Meanwhile, TGFβ2 induced morphologic alterations in mitochondria in LECs, showing tubular mitochondria fragmenting into donut or spherical shapes with decreased mean branch length and increased fragments (Fig. 1F). At a higher magnification, TEM showed that TGFβ2-treated LECs contained swelling mitochondria with fewer intact cristae, which are intact and abundant in respiratory enzymes for OXPHOS21 in normal LECs (Fig. 1G). We then assessed alterations of mitochondrial function in energy metabolism during EMT of LECs. The mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP; ΔΨm) generated by proton pumps in respiratory electron transport chain is a key indicator for mitochondrial activity and is essential for ATP generation via OXPHOS.22 As expected, TGFβ2 diminished ΔΨm in LECs (Fig. 1H). Additionally, in consistency with GO analysis, mitochondria-derived ATP decreased whereas glycolytic ATP increased in LECs under TGFβ2 stress, symbolizing a metabolic shift from OXPHOS toward glycolysis during EMT of LECs (Fig. 1I). Moreover, fusion and fission are key events in regulating mitochondrial morphology, which is dynamically balanced to support OXPHOS.23,24 Echoing morphological derangement and respiratory defect, we found that the fusion protein mitofusin 2 (MFN2) instead of mitofusin 1 (MFN1) decreased, whereas the fission protein dynamin-related protein 1 (DRP1) increased during EMT of LECs (Figs. 1J, 1K). Consistent with our RNA-seq analysis, which showed elevated response to oxidative stress in TGFβ2-treated human lens epithelium (see Fig. 1B), TGFβ2 significantly increased ROS levels in primary rabbit LECs (Supplementary Fig. S1A).

As revealed in RNA-seq during human lens epithelial fibrosis (see Fig. 1A), PGC1A, the master regulator of mitochondrial biogenesis and energy metabolism, was also dramatically downregulated at both mRNA and protein levels in a time-dependent manner over the 1 to 3 day period during TGFβ2-induced EMT of primary rabbit LECs (Figs. 1L–O). Moreover, PGC1A was significantly decreased as LECs within the monolayered epithelium in rat whole lenses transformed into disorganized clumps of mesenchymal cells within fibrotic plaques during lens epithelial fibrosis (Fig. 1P).

Taken together, these results demonstrated that mitochondrial homeostasis was disrupted in morphology, organization, and bioenergetic function accompanied with PGC1A decrease during TGFβ2-induced lens epithelial fibrosis and EMT of LECs, implying mitochondria as signaling organelles in regulating LECs fate under TGFβ2 stress.

Knockdown of PGC1A Aggravated TGFβ2-Induced EMT of LECs by Enhancing Smad2/3 Activation

To identify the regulatory role of mitochondria in EMT of LECs under TGFβ2 stress, loss-of-function studies were conducted using siRNAs to downregulate PGC1A in primary rabbit LECs (Figs. 2A–C). PGC1A knockdown intensified TGFβ2-induced nuclei elongation, declined in epithelial identities and junctions (E-cad, ZO-1), increased in cytoskeleton rearrangement (α-SMA), and had an abnormal ECM accumulation (FN) in LECs (Figs. 2D–F). Loss of intercellular anchoring due to mesenchymal transition could result in detachment of epithelial cells from each other and the basement membrane, which facilitates cell migration.25 Consequently, PGC1A knockdown boosted migratory capacity of LECs upon TGFβ2 stimulation (Figs. 2G, 2H). The above results indicated that PGC1A depletion promoted loss of epithelial phenotype and EMT progression in LECs.

To investigate the intrinsic pathway by which PGC1A relayed TGFβ2 signals to promote EMT of LECs, we assessed the activation of Smad2/3, the principal intracellular transducers of TGFβ signaling pathway,26,27 which can be phosphorylated to form heteromeric complexes with Smad4 and translocate into the nucleus to regulate EMT-related genes in ASC and PCO.28,29 It has also been verified previously that Smad2/3 were activated in LECs upon TGFβ2 treatment.30–32 Here, we found that PGC1A silencing enhanced Smad2/3 phosphorylation to increase its nuclear translocation in LECs (Figs. 2I–K). Together, decrease of PGC1A, the master mitochondrial regulator, promoted EMT of LECs by increasing Smad2/3 signal transduction upon TGFβ2 stimulation.

Deficiency in PGC1A Accelerated Fibrotic Plaques Formation Beneath the Anterior Lens Capsule by Boosting EMT of LECs

EMT of LECs is a key mechanism underpinning fibrotic cataracts. We continued to investigate whether PGC1A deficiency could promote lens epithelial fibrosis through EMT of LECs using an ex vivo fibrotic cataract model with TGFβ2-treated whole rat lenses. While the lenses remained transparent with TGFβ2 treatment after 2 days, the lenses with PGC1A silencing under TGFβ2 treatment displayed white plaques, which became more widespread than TGFβ2-treated lenses after 4 days (Fig. 3A). These results suggested that PGC1A silencing accelerated fibrotic plaques formation beneath the anterior lens capsule during lens epithelial fibrosis. Histologically, H&E staining confirmed that PGC1A depletion promoted formation of subcapsular fibrotic clumps in whole rat lenses, evidenced by aggravated nuclei elongation, cell depolarization, excessive proliferation, and abnormal invasion into the lens cortex (Fig. 3B). TGFβ2-induced invasive fibrotic plaques in whole rat lenses were characterized by EMT of LECs with expression of mesenchymal proteins (FN and α-SMA) and loss of intercellular junctions (E-cad and ZO-1), disrupting the integrity of the lens epithelium as a protective barrier, which was further exacerbated by PGC1A depletion (Figs. 3C–E). Collectively, deficiency in PGC1A accelerated lens epithelial fibrosis by promoting EMT of LECs.

Overexpression of PGC1A Rebuilt Mitochondrial Homeostasis in LECs Under TGFβ2 Stress

To illustrate the effect of PGC1A on mitochondrial health involving morphology and activity in energy production under TGFβ2 stress, primary rabbit LECs were transfected with plasmids to overexpress PGC1A prior to TGFβ2 treatment (Figs. 4A, 4B). As shown in Figure 4C, PGC1A overexpression in LECs maintained mitochondrial tubular network and prevented TGFβ2-induced sphericity and fragmentation with increased mean branch length and decreased fragments. Besides, cristae structure disintegrated by TGFβ2 in LECs were stabilized as PGC1A expression increased (Fig. 4D). In addition, PGC1A upregulation protected mitochondrial respiration activity as evidenced by recovered ΔΨm under TGFβ2 stress (Fig. 4E), and revived mitochondrial ATP synthesis to suppress TGFβ2-induced metabolic switch from OXPHOS toward glycolysis in LECs (Fig. 4F). Correspondingly, PGC1A rebalanced fusion and fission in mitochondrial organization to adapt the alterations in morphology and respiratory function in response to TGFβ2 by upregulating MFN2 and inhibiting DRP1 (Figs. 4G, 4H). Moreover, overexpression of PGC1A effectively reduced the TGFβ2-induced elevation of ROS in LECs (Supplementary Fig. S1B). Overall, overexpression of PGC1A rebuilt mitochondrial homeostasis in morphology, organization, bioenergetic function, and redox balance in LECs under TGFβ2 stress.

Together, overexpression of PGC1A protected LECs from EMT and migration by suppressing TGFβ2-Smad2/3 signaling.

Collectively, upregulation of PGC1A restored mitochondrial health and function, consequently preventing LECs from EMT triggered by TGFβ2.

TFAM was Inhibited by TGFβ2 Stress and PGC1A Silencing

PGC1A has been reported to regulate mitochondrial biogenesis and energy metabolism through targeting multiple transcription factors, such as nuclear respiratory factor 1/2 (NRF1/2) and TFAM.15 Here, we found that TFAM instead of NRF1/2 was downregulated by TGFβ2 in primary rabbit LECs in a time-dependent manner (Figs. 6A, 6B). TFAM is a mitochondrial signaling molecule orchestrating the replication, transcription, and maintenance of mtDNA, which encodes 13 essential respiratory complex subunits devoted to ATP production via OXPHOS and is therefore central for mitochondrial biogenesis and bioenergetic function.18 We also revealed that PGC1A inhibition downregulated TFAM at both mRNA and protein levels (Figs. 6C–E) whereas PGC1A overexpression upregulated TFAM in LECs (Figs. 6F–H).

We then investigated whether TFAM served as the downstream effector of PGC1A to relay TGFβ2 signals in LECs, and found that PGC1A overexpression in the absence of TFAM failed to suppress Smad2/3 signal transduction (Figs. 7H–J) to prevent EMT of LECs, as confirmed by highly expressed mesenchymal markers and abolished epithelial identity and junctions (Figs. 7K–M).

The above results revealed that PGC1A regulated mitochondrial activity in ATP production and EMT of LECs through the key mitochondrial transcription factor TFAM, indicating TFAM as an indispensable downstream effector of PGC1A for lens epithelial maintenance via protecting mitochondrial respiration in LECs.

Discussion

LECs play a dominant role in the establishment and maintenance of lens transparency. Lens epithelial fibrosis resulted from EMT of LECs lays the foundation of fibrotic cataractogenesis,3,4 and there are currently no clinically effective strategies to reverse fibrosis.5

As the major cells containing functional mitochondria in mature lenses, LECs are the most metabolically active cell population. Growing evidence reveals mitochondria as not only the cellular power sites, but also signaling organelles to alter cell states and tissue functions in response to extrinsic or intrinsic stresses.34 Metabolic alterations are emerging as a key node in EMT during developmental and pathological processes across multiple organs.9,35–37 Approaches targeting metabolic dysregulation are expected to prevent fibrotic diseases, some of which are being investigated in ongoing clinical trials (NCT02538536, NCT03421431, and NCT02030977).38–41 Here, we investigated the role and mechanism of mitochondria in EMT of LECs during lens epithelial fibrosis under TGFβ2 stress.

First, we found that TGFβ2 disrupted mitochondrial morphology and structure, which inevitably caused functional breakdown including metabolic reprogramming in ATP synthetic pathway with decreased OXPHOS and enhanced compensatory glycolysis. We then explored the effect of mitochondrial dysfunction on EMT of LECs through functional studies of PGC1A. Here, we found that PGC1A was decreased during lens epithelial fibrosis and loss of PGC1A promoted TGFβ2-Smad2/3 signaling to aggravate EMT of LECs, which destroyed lens epithelium integrity and accelerated subcapsular fibrotic plaques formation. Conversely, PGC1A upregulation restored mitochondrial health with enhanced biogenesis and mitochondrial respiration, which then prevented EMT of LECs via repressing Smad2/3 activation. These findings demonstrated that mitochondria respiratory defects induced by TGFβ2 could aggravate EMT of LECs through Smad2/3 activation.

Next, how PGC1A regulates energy metabolism under TGFβ2 stress to alter LECs fate draws our attention. Multiple downstream transcription factors have been identified to be regulated by PGC1A and involved in the expression of both nuclear- and mitochondrial-encoded genes to adapt mitochondrial respiration.15 In our study, expression of TFAM, instead of NRF1/2, was decreased by TGFβ2 and PGC1A inhibition during EMT of LECs. TFAM, the key transcription factor for mitochondrial biogenesis and bioenergetics, is essential for energy conversion by modulating the expression and maintenance of mtDNA, which encodes core subunits of respiratory complexes and drives ATP synthesis via OXPHOS.18 TFAM deficiency has been demonstrated to promote skin and renal fibrosis via inducing OXPHOS defects.42,43 Here, we found that TFAM inhibition exacerbated EMT of LECs by promoting TGFβ2-Smad2/3 signals and further mechanistic studies identified TFAM as the downstream effector of PGC1A for maintaining mitochondrial energy metabolism to protect LECs from TGFβ2-induced EMT.

Intriguingly, how does alterations in mitochondrial energy metabolism regulate mesenchymal transition of LECs? The switch from OXPHOS to glycolysis as a mitochondrial metabolic alteration was noted in EMT during tumor metastasis since a century ago.44 This reprogramming is also increasingly confirmed to be crucial in EMT during development and fibrotic disorders.45,46 Altered metabolic intermediates such as acetyl-CoA and nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) could modulate EMT regulators through epigenetic modifications.47–49 In addition, microenvironmental alterations such as pH decrease triggered by acidic product accumulation from enhanced glycolysis has been reported to promote EMT by boosting TGFβ secretion and activation.50,51

Given the above, our study implies the essential role of mitochondrial respiration in maintaining lens epithelial integrity under TGFβ2 stress. In previous studies, mutations in mtDNA or nuclear-encoded genes such as optic atrophy 3 (OPA3), which is associated with defective OXPHOS, could result in developmental cataracts,52–54 suggesting that defective respiration also affect the differentiation of LECs. Altogether, our study expands the critical effect of PGC1A/TFAM-mediated mitochondrial energy metabolism on protecting mature LECs from EMT, emphasizing the importance of mitochondria in both the establishment and maintenance of lens transparency.

Beyond cellular metabolism, mitochondria are essential in maintaining redox homeostasis by producing antioxidants and regulating redox modifiers such as NAD+ and glutathione, which broadly influences cell fate and tissue health.7,55–59 RNA-seq analysis showed a significant increase in the response to oxidative stress in human lens epithelium upon TGFβ2 stress (see Fig. 1B), which was consistent with the elevated ROS levels during TGFβ2-induced EMT of primary rabbit LECs (see Supplementary Fig. S1A). In lens disorders, oxidative stress not only disturb the homeostasis of lipids and proteins in the lens,60,61 but also disrupts the viability and function of LECs.62 Our previous work has identified oxidative stress as a pathological trigger for EMT of LECs by impairing lysosomal degradation in high glucose-induced cataract.63 Wang et al. and Li et al. also reported that oxidative stress could promote EMT of LECs.64,65 In addition to orchestrating mitochondrial biogenesis and metabolism, PGC1A is an essential mediator for mitochondrial redox homeostasis by regulating expression of antioxidants in response to multiple stresses.66–68 Here, we also found that PGC1A upregulation could reduce TGFβ2-induced elevation of ROS levels in primary rabbit LECs (see Supplementary Fig. S1B). The interaction between PGC1A-mediated mitochondrial homeostasis and oxidative stress in maintaining mature LECs features upon TGFβ2 stress also deserves deep investigation in the future.

Mitochondria also have a central role in intrinsic apoptotic cell death.69,70 Whereas TGFβ2 treatment for 2 days induced EMT of LECs, prolonged TGFβ2 stimulation (up to 4–6 days) led to increased proportion of apoptotic LECs (Supplementary Fig. S2), probably due to further damage of the mitochondria or increased ROS levels in LECs (Supplementary Figs. S3, S4). Interestingly, high levels of apoptosis have been identified in nearly all types of fibrosis and apoptosis has been implied as a key player in the propagation of organ fibrosis71 including PCO.72 Besides, the concomitant EMT and apoptosis have been reported in TGFβ2-induced fibrotic cataract73 and other fibrotic disorders.74 However, little is known about the underlying mechanism for dual fate alterations of LECs concerning EMT and apoptosis under TGFβ2 stress. Here, we found that EMT and apoptosis of LECs are sequentially modulated events as TGFβ2 exposure extended: EMT predominates at early stages (2 days) and apoptosis emerges during later phases (4–6 days) of TGFβ2 treatment. Mechanistically, mitochondria control apoptosis through the release of cytochrome C following mitochondrial membrane disruption, which subsequently activates cytosolic proteolytic caspases to initiate apoptotic cell death upon stress stimulation.75 Our RNA-seq data exhibited mild upregulation in cytochrome C release from mitochondria and in mitochondria membrane permeability after 2 days treatment with TGFβ2 (see Fig. 1B). In addition, the sustained TGFβ2 exposure up to 4 to 6 days aggravated mitochondrial fragmentation and further decreased MMP (see Supplementary Fig. S3B), indicating potential increase of cytochrome C release and mitochondria membrane permeability in LECs in later stages of TGFβ2 treatment. Regardless, both EMT and apoptosis of LECs are undoubtedly detrimental to lens transparency. Mitochondria-mediated regulation might be one of the crucial mechanisms underlying the multiple effects of TGFβ2 on LECs fate determination. Altogether, further explorations in multifaceted mitochondria will expand the knowledge of mitochondria as signaling organelles in LECs health and lens transparency.

Deciphering the role and mechanisms of mitochondrial dysfunction in EMT is essential for developing therapeutic strategies for fibrotic disorders. Finally, we explored the potential of a pharmaceutical approach targeting PGC1A in mitigating EMT of LECs and found that ZLN005, a small-molecule activator of PGC1A, attenuated EMT, and fibrotic lens opacity via upregulating TFAM and restoring mitochondrial respiration. Nevertheless, therapeutic efficacy observed in ex vivo lens culture system requires confirmation in combination with in vivo assessments. In the future, in vivo animal models will be applied to confirm the scientific findings of this study and explore more potential drug candidates based on mitochondria protection.

In summary, our study unveils the novel role and mechanism of mitochondrial dysfunction in EMT of LECs during lens epithelial fibrosis (Supplementary Fig. S5). We revealed that TGFβ2 stress suppressed PGC1A and its downstream effector TFAM, leading to mitochondrial respiratory dysregulation with energy metabolic shift toward glycolysis, which aggravated EMT of LECs and lens opacity by promoting smad2/3 signals transduction. Restoration of PGC1A/TFAM-mediated mitochondrial health provides potential strategies for preventing fibrotic cataracts as well as other fibrotic disorders.

A serological survey of influenza D virus infection in cattle in Hokkaido, Japan
Authors: Wataru SEKINE, Misa KATAYAMA, Kosuke OHIRA, Ayano ICHIKAWA, Yuta WAKABAYASHI, Akiko TAKENAKA-UEMA, Shin MURAKAMI, Taisuke HORIMOTO
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractInfluenza D virus (IDV) is an emerging pathogen associated with bovine respiratory disease complex. To update the epidemiological status of IDV in Hokkaido, Japan, we tested 73 serum samples collected in 2023 from cattle with respiratory disease or diarrhea. Hemagglutination inhibition (HI) assays were performed using three IDV strains representing distinct phylogenetic clades: D/swine/Oklahoma/1334/2011, D/bovine/Yamagata/10710/2016 (Y16), and D/bovine/Yamagata/1/2019 (Y19). Overall, 34 of the 73 samples (46.6%) were seropositive. Antibodies against Y16 were detected most frequently (42.5%), whereas the highest mean HI titers were observed against Y19 among the three strains. These results indicate that IDVs belonging to both the Y16- and Y19-lineages are circulating among cattle in Hokkaido, consistent with previous epidemiological findings.Keywords: cattle, hemagglutination inhibition, Hokkaido, influenza D virus, seroprevalence

Influenza D virus (IDV) was first identified in swine in Oklahoma, the United States, in 2011 [2, 3]. It is now recognized as a novel member of the family Orthomyxoviridae with a broad host range [8], including cattle, swine, camelids [11, 18], and small ruminants [16, 17]. Among these species, cattle are considered the primary host [2, 3, 20], and accumulating evidence suggests that IDV plays a significant role in the bovine respiratory disease complex (BRDC) [1, 9, 14]. Since BRDC is one of the major causes of economic losses in the cattle industry worldwide [6, 15], understanding the prevalence and circulation of IDV is of substantial veterinary and economic importance. Based on phylogenetic analyses of the hemagglutinin-esterase-fusion (HEF) gene sequences, IDV is currently classified into five distinct lineages: D/OK, D/660, D/Yama2016, D/Yama2019, and D/CA2019 [7]. Previous study has demonstrated the presence of antigenic diversity among IDV lineages. Hemagglutination inhibition (HI) assays have shown that homologous virus–serum combinations generally yield the highest HI titers, whereas titers against heterologous IDV lineages are typically reduced, often by several-fold. Among these lineages, antigenic distances have been observed between the D/Yama2016 and D/Yama2019 lineage viruses, although the antigenicity of D/Yama2016 appears to be closely related to that of the D/OK lineage [7].

In Japan, serological evidence of IDV was first reported by Murakami et al. in 2016 [10], who demonstrated a nationwide distribution of IDV antibodies in cattle, with an overall seropositivity of 23.6% and a higher prevalence of 28.1% in Hokkaido [5]. In addition, IDVs have been isolated from cattle in Hokkaido, revealing the presence of two distinct phylogenetic lineages [4]. However, since these reports were published, epidemiological information on IDV circulation in Hokkaido, the largest cattle-producing region in Japan, has remained limited. To update the current situation, we conducted a serological survey of cattle sampled in Hokkaido in 2023.

A total of 73 serum samples were collected from cattle with respiratory disease or diarrhea. The sampled animals consisted of 43 dairy cows, 21 beef cattle, 7 F1 crossbred cattle, and 2 Jersey cattle, ranging in age from 1 year and 2 months to 2 years and 4 months. Detailed farm-level information was not available; the number of farms from which the samples originated was unknown. Samples were collected by veterinarians from the East Regional Center of NOSAI Hokkaido with the informed consent of the farm owners responsible for the animals, in accordance with ethical guidelines. All protocols and sampling procedures used in this study were approved by the Animal Experiment Committee of the University of Tokyo (approval number: P25-125).

Serum samples were treated with receptor-destroying enzyme (RDEII; Denka, Tokyo, Japan) at a 1:3 ratio prior to testing. The mixture (12 µL) was incubated at 37°C for 16 hr, followed by heat inactivation at 56°C for 30 min. Subsequently, 18 µL of phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) was added, followed by addition of 3 µL of turkey red blood cells (Japan Lamb Group, Tokyo, Japan). After adsorption at 24°C for 1 hr, the samples were centrifuged at 1,000 × g for 5 min, and the supernatants were collected for further analysis. HI assays were performed by mixing 25 µL of serially diluted serum (from 1:40 to 1:640) with 25 µL of virus suspension containing 4 hemagglutinating (HA) units, followed by incubation with 50 µL of 0.7% turkey red blood cell. Three IDV strains representing distinct phylogenetic clades were used [7]: D/swine/Oklahoma/1334/2011 (OK) [3], D/bovine/Yamagata/10710/2016 (Y16) [13], and D/bovine/Yamagata/1/2019 (Y19) [12]. The HI titer was defined as the highest serum dilution that completely inhibited HA, and seropositivity was defined as an HI titer of ≥40, in accordance with previous studies of IDV [17, 19].

Of the 73 serum samples tested, 34 were seropositive against at least one IDV strain, yielding an overall seropositivity rate of 46.6%. The breed-specific seropositive rates were 37.2% in dairy cattle, 61.9% in beef cattle, 57.1% in F1 crossbred cattle, and 50.0% in Jersey cattle. However, the observed differences in seropositivity rates among cattle types should be interpreted with caution, as they may be influenced by confounding factors such as age distribution, herd size, housing conditions, management practices, and exposure risk. At present, there is limited biological or epidemiological evidence to support breed- or production-type-dependent differences in susceptibility to IDV. Antibodies were detected in 22 samples against OK (30.1%), 31 against Y16 (42.5%), and 23 against Y19 (31.5%) (Table 1). The majority of seropositive samples reacted with more than one strain, suggesting antigenic relatedness among these lineages and resulting in cross-reactivity among strains (Fig. 1). Notably, Y19 was associated with the highest HI titers, with several sera exhibiting titers of 320 and one reaching a titer of 640, although Y16 showed the highest frequency of detection (Table 1). Although higher HI titers against Y19 may suggest relatively recent exposure to D/Y19-lineage viruses, this interpretation is based solely on serological data and should therefore be made with caution. The higher detection of antibodies against Y16 may reflect multiple factors, including cross-reactive antibody responses among lineages or past infection histories, and cannot be conclusively explained by the present study.

The relatively high seroprevalence observed in this study, compared with the 28.1% reported in 2016 [5], may reflect ongoing and potentially increasing circulation of IDV in Hokkaido. Our previous study has reported antigenic differences in the HEF protein between D/Y16- and D/Y19-lineage viruses [7]. Taken together, these findings suggest that both D/Y16- and D/Y19-lineage viruses may be co-circulating in cattle in Hokkaido.

Our findings are consistent with international reports of high IDV seroprevalence in cattle, particularly in North America and Europe, where prevalence rates often exceed 50% [21]. The detection of high HI titers against Y19 suggests that this lineage may currently be dominant in cattle populations in Hokkaido (Table 1). IDV infects not only cattle and small ruminants but also a variety of wildlife species [8], indicating a broad host range and raising concerns about a potential risk of future zoonotic transmission.

In conclusion, this study provides updated serological surveillance data on IDV in Hokkaido, Japan. Nearly half of the cattle tested in 2023 were seropositive for IDV, with particularly high HI titers observed against the Y19 virus. These results suggest that both D/Y16- and D/Y19-lineage IDVs are currently circulating among cattle in Hokkaido. Ongoing monitoring, including molecular characterization of circulating strains and expanded serological surveys, will be essential to better understand the epidemiology of IDV in Japan and to evaluate its potential impact on animal health and the cattle industry.

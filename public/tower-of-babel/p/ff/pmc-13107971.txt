Distinct transcriptional regulation of the Wnt and TGFβ signaling families associated with wound healing and fibrotic outcomes to lens injury
Authors: Catherine Lalman, Kylie R Stabler, Joshua Disatham, Lisa A Brennan, Marc Kantorow, Janice L Walker
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPosterior capsule opacification (PCO) is a fibrotic complication of cataract surgery caused by aberrant repair of residual lens cells. It remains unclear how the lens injury response shifts toward a pathological repair process. Here, we performed a transcriptional analysis of two major pro-fibrotic pathways, the TGF-β superfamily and Wnt signaling pathways to determine how they are differentially regulated during regenerative wound healing versus fibrosis in a lens injury model. For these studies, RNA sequencing was performed on an ex vivo lens explant system that models post-surgical healing within regenerative and fibrotic microenvironments, from 0 to 3 days post-injury. Fibrotic conditions were marked by increased expression of TGF-β ligands, receptors, SMAD3, and extracellular regulators, LTBP1 and THSB1 consistent with strong activation of canonical TGF-β signaling. In contrast, BMP signaling during wound healing, was characterized by early induction of BMP receptors, SMAD1, and downstream ID genes, indicative of robust activation while in fibrosis, BMP ligands and receptors increased but downstream signaling was blunted potentially by elevated expression of BMP inhibitors. Inhibition of ID proteins using a pan-ID inhibitor, impaired both wound closure and myofibroblast formation, supporting a role for ID protein function in both contexts. Distinct Wnt ligands and FZD receptors were upregulated in regenerative vs. fibrotic repair contexts to drive canonical/noncanonical Wnt signaling. Fibrosis favored upregulation of the Wnt/Ca2+ pathway and multiple RSPO’s associated with Wnt activation. These findings reveal new insight into the transcriptional regulation of the TGF-β and Wnt family pathway components and modulators associated with programming distinct outcomes to lens injury.Keywords: Lens, PCO, Fibrosis, Injury, Wound healing, TGFβ, Wnt

Introduction

Posterior capsule opacification (PCO) remains a frequent fibrotic complication of cataract surgery, occurring in up to 50 % of adult patients (Ursell et al., 2020; Wormstone et al., 2009). Following surgical trauma, residual lens cells may proliferate, migrate, and undergo a transition to myofibroblast cells, leading to the generation of α-smooth muscle actin (αSMA)-positive myofibroblasts that remodel the extracellular matrix (ECM), particularly through the deposition of collagen I, thereby compromising lens transparency (Eldred et al., 2011; Shu and Lovicu, 2017; Wormstone et al., 2009). Deciphering mechanisms that shift the outcome of cataract surgery from normal wound healing to fibrotic repair can inform strategies to mitigate PCO. Two key signaling pathways that play major roles in regulating both wound healing and fibrosis in many tissue types are Wnt and Transforming growth factor beta (TGFβ) (Burgy and Konigshoff, 2018; Eldred et al., 2011; Kiritsi and Nystrom, 2018; Lichtman et al., 2016; Lovicu et al., 2016; Taiyab et al., 2016; Valcourt et al., 2005). The canonical Wnt/β-catenin and canonical TGFβ/Smad and noncanonical TGFβ/Rho/ROCK signaling pathways play well-established roles in promoting lens fibrosis (Eldred et al., 2011; Korol et al., 2016; Shu and Lovicu, 2017; Taiyab et al., 2016; Valcourt et al., 2005). However, there is a lack of in depth understanding at the molecular level of how components of Wnt and TGFβ families are transcriptionally regulated in response to lens injury linked to normal lens wound healing vs. pathological scar inducing environments. Such studies can illuminate distinct points of transcriptional regulation of these pathways in response to lens injury that may promote a fibrotic instead of normal wound healing outcome and inform the development of potential strategies to prevent this negative outcome to lens injury.

Among the key regulators of fibrosis are the Wnt signaling pathways, which are activated following lens injury. Canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling activity is minimal in the postnatal lens epithelium but becomes rapidly induced after lens injury, as demonstrated by Wnt reporter mice (Wang et al., 2018b). Wnt-driven epithelial mesenchymal transition (EMT) can be reversed by DKK1 (Liu et al., 2017) or by targeting β-catenin/CBP interactions (Taiyab et al., 2016). In the canonical pathway, Wnt ligand binding to Frizzled and LRP5/6 stabilizes cytoplasmic β-catenin, which then translocates to the nucleus to regulate genes controlling proliferation, adhesion, and migration (Clevers and Nusse, 2012). Noncanonical Wnt pathways, including the planar cell polarity (PCP) and Wnt/Ca2+ branches, act independently of β-catenin to coordinate cytoskeletal dynamics, polarity, and calcium signaling via RhoA, JNK, and NFAT activation (Liu et al., 2022; VanderVorst et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2023). PCP signaling regulates lens fiber elongation and alignment during development, but its role in fibrotic outcomes remains poorly understood (Chen et al., 2008; Sugiyama et al., 2010). Wnt/Ca2+ signaling has been implicated in fibrosis in other tissues (Zhang et al., 2023), yet its contribution in the lens is largely unexplored.

TGF-β signaling is another central regulator of fibrosis. Canonical TGF-β signaling is initiated by ligand binding to TGFβRII and ALK5 (TGFβRI), leading to SMAD2/3 phosphorylation, complex formation with co-SMAD4, and nuclear translocation to regulate transcription of profibrotic targets such as αSMA and COL1A1 (Saika et al., 2004b; Valcourt et al., 2005). The SMAD-dependent pathway has been shown to mediate EMT in the lens and up regulate the synthesis of fibrotic markers α-SMA and collagen (Basta et al., 2021; Meng et al., 2018a; Saika et al., 2004b). In parallel, TGF-β activates noncanonical pathways, including MAPK, ERK, JNK, p38, PI3K/AKT, and Rho/ROCK, which reinforce EMT and promote cytoskeletal remodeling and contractility (Bakin et al., 2000; Cho et al., 2007; Zhang, 2009). Specifically, in the lens, TGF-β-mediated activation of Rho/ROCK leads to formation of actin stress fibers and translocation of MRTF-A, further driving αSMA expression and fibrosis (Cho and Yoo, 2007; Korol et al., 2016; Pellegrin and Mellor, 2007). Experimental overexpression of TGF-β1 induces anterior subcapsular cataract and fibrotic plaques, even in SMAD3-deficient backgrounds (Banh et al., 2006), while adenoviral expression of SMAD7, an inhibitory SMAD, blocks EMT in lens injury models (Saika et al., 2004a).

Compared to TGFβ, far less is understood in the lens about the role of the TGFβ superfamily member activin. Activins signal through ACVR1B (ALK4) or ACVR1C and type II receptors (ACVR2A, ACVR2B), activating SMAD2/3 similar to TGF-β (Zhang et al., 2024; Zhang, 2009). In contrast, the BMP branches of the TGF-β superfamily play important roles in lens development and repair. BMPs are essential for lens morphogenesis, fiber differentiation, and regulation of EMT (Belecky-Adams et al., 2002; Furuta and Hogan, 1998; Shu et al., 2017). BMPs bind type I receptors (e.g., ACVR1, BMPR1A/B), then recruit type II receptors (e.g., BMPR2) to activate SMAD1/5/8, which also complex with SMAD4 to drive transcription (Szilágyi et al., 2022). Notably, BMP7 and BMP4 have been shown to antagonize TGF-β–induced EMT in lens explant and in vivo models (Shu and Lovicu, 2021; Shu et al., 2017, 2021). BMP pathway outcomes are highly context-dependent, shaped by regulators such as extracellular antagonists (Noggin, Follistatin, Cerberus), intracellular repressors (SMAD6, SMAD7, SMURF1/2), and downstream effectors including the ID (inhibitor of differentiation) proteins (Saika et al., 2006; Shu et al., 2021; Zhang, 2009; Zhu et al., 2011). Overexpression of ID2 and ID3 has been shown to suppress EMT in the lens, suggesting BMPs act at multiple regulatory levels to restrict fibrotic signaling (Saika et al., 2006).

Notably, crosstalk between TGF-β and Wnt pathways is well documented. TGF-β signaling can induce Wnt ligand and receptor expression, enhancing β-catenin signaling (Guan and Zhou, 2017). In parallel, SMAD3 can stabilize β-catenin and cooperate with it at shared gene promoters to drive fibrotic transcription programs (Saika et al., 2004b). This convergence supports sustained fibrotic responses and promotes autocrine feedback loops that perpetuate ECM remodeling. Conversely, BMP signaling has been shown to antagonize TGF-β-driven EMT in part through induction of ID proteins, which suppress mesenchymal gene expression and fibroblast activation (Saika et al., 2006).

Although these signaling pathways are well established in the lens and other tissues, their precise regulation during regenerative wound healing versus fibrotic remodeling remains unclear. In particular, how TGF-β, BMP/Activin, and Wnt pathways are selectively engaged, or restrained, by specific ligand–receptor interactions and modulatory proteins following lens injury is not fully understood. Using a post-cataract surgery model in which we can separately study regenerative wound healing vs fibrotic repair we investigated the molecular regulation of the Wnt and TGFβ family signaling pathways in response to a clinically relevant wounding, cataract surgery. Using this model, we can study the response to cataract surgery injury leading to wound healing across the cell denuded posterior lens capsule, which is completed by day 3 post-injury (Menko et al., 2014; Walker et al., 2015). At the same time cells migrate off the edges of the wound capsule post-cataract surgery explant onto the tissue culture substrate, where a fibrotic response ensues resulting in transition to αSMA + myofibroblasts and the accumulation of collagen I fibrillar matrix and by day 3 post-injury (Basta et al., 2021; Walker et al., 2018). RNA was isolated on Time 0 (T0), 1 h (1 h), Day 1 (D1), Day 2 (D2) and Day 3 (D3) post-wounding of the lens, separately on cells undergoing wound healing compared to cells inducing fibrosis. RNA-seq analysis was performed, and the data set was surveyed for components of the Wnt and TGFβ families revealing new insights into regulation of these pathways, including their modulators to reveal points of regulatory control and potential mechanisms by which Wnt and TGFβ drive lens fibrosis vs. regulation of these signalling family pathways with normal wound healing. Our studies revealed specific upregulation of the BMP axis and a role for ID proteins in regulating the response of lens wounding.

Materials and methods

Animals

All animal procedures were approved by the Thomas Jefferson University Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) and conform to the ARVO Statement for the Use of Animals in Ophthalmic and Vision Research. Embryonated White Leghorn chicken eggs were obtained from Poultry Futures (Lititz, PA) and incubated at 37 °C until the appropriate developmental stage.

Preparation and treatment of ex vivo wounded lens epithelial explants

Post-cataract surgery explants were prepared as previously described (Walker et al., 2007, 2015). Briefly, embryonic day 14 (E14), lenses were isolated, and the fiber cell mass removed by hydroelution through an incision in the anterior capsule. To create a post-cataract surgery explant a few more cuts were made to the anterior epithelium, and the explants were flattened and pinned cell-side up in 35 mm dishes. Explants were cultured in complete medium—M199 (Gibco 11150–059) with 10 % FBS (Gibco A56704–02), 1 % L-glutamine (Corning 25–005-CI), and 1 % penicillin–streptomycin (Corning 30–002-CI)—at 37 °C and 5 % CO2. For treatments, post-cataract surgery explants were treated with vehicle (DMSO, Cell Signalling Technology 12611) or AGX51 (MedChem Express HY-129241) (40 μM and 60 μM doses) starting at T0 or 1-day post-injury as indicated, and treatments were replaced each day. Doses for AGX51 were chosen taking into consideration the IC50 and the literature (Wojnarowicz et al., 2021).

Phase microscopy and wound healing

Wound healing images were captured day 0, day 1, day 2, and day 3 post-injury using Nikon SMZ800 microscope. Open wound areas were measured using NIS elements image-analysis software and the % of wound closure was calculated for each day compared to Day 0.

Western blot analysis

Ex vivo post-cataract surgery cultures were extracted in RIPA (5 mM EDTA, 150 mM Sodium Chloride, 1 % IEGPAL, 1 % Sodium/Deoxy-cholate, 1 % SDS 20 % Solution, 50 mM Tris-HCl) with protease/phosphatase inhibitor cocktail (Cell Signaling Technology 5872, Aldrich, Danvers, MA, USA). 20 μg of protein was loaded per lane and lysates were separated by 4–20 % Tis-Glycine mini protein gels, 1.0 mm wedgewell (Invitrogen, XP04202BOX). Gels were transferred overnight onto PVDF membranes. Membranes were blocked in 5 % milk for 1 h, incubated overnight with primary antibody to αSMA (Protein Tech 23081–1-AP). Blots were re-probed for 1 h in GAPDH (Protein Tech 10494–1-AP). Following primary antibody incubation, HRP-conjugated mouse or rabbit secondary antibodies (Biorad, Hercules, CA, USA) were incubated for 1 h. Subsequently, membranes were exposed to CCL + chemiluminescence detection reagent (ThermoFisher Scientific, 34580) and imaged using Protein simple (FluorChem M, FM0418). For western blot quantification, blots were analyzed in Alpha view (3.5.0.927), mean intensity of bands was calculated and the ratio of αSMA/GAPDH was calculated.

Immunofluorescence staining

Ex vivo post-cataract surgery explants were fixed in 3.7 % formaldehyde in PBS, permeabilized in .25 % Triton X-100 (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO), blocked, and immunolabeled on day 1 for N-cadherin (NCD-2, ThermoFisher Scientific 13-2100, Waltham, MA) or on day 3 for alpha smooth muscle actin (αSMA) (ABCAM AB5964, Waltham, MA) and FN-EDA (Santa Cruz, IST-9, sc-59826, Dallas, Texas). Subsequently, ex vivo explants were incubated with rhodamine or Alexa Fluor 488-conjugated secondary antibodies (Jackson ImmunoResearch Laboratories, West Grove, PA). To label F-actin, cultures were labeled with Alexa Fluor 633-conjugated phalloidin (ThermoFisher Scientific A22284, Waltham, MA). Primary and secondary antibodies were incubated for 30 min at 37°. Images were collected with a confocal microscope (LSM 510; Zeiss, Thornwood, NY).

RNA sequencing and bioinformatics

For RNA-seq studies, three independent experiments, six explants were harvested at the following times: immediately after wounding (T0), 1 h, and on days 1–3 post-wounding. RNA was isolated separately from wound healing on lens capsules vs. extracapsular zone (ECZ) regions. Total RNA from each sample was submitted to Novogene for library preparation and Illumina sequencing. Novogene performed QC, including 1 % agarose gel electrophoresis, Nanodrop spectrophotometry for purity and concentration, and RIN assessment on an Agilent 2100—to ensure high data quality. They provided FPKM values, lists of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) with log2fold change (log2f), p-values, and adjusted p-values, plus initial bioinformatics analyses. Sequencing data for T0 and 1-h samples are deposited under GEO accession number GSE239368 and D1-D3 were deposited under GSE298481.

Statistical analysis

Adjusted p-values (padj) based on the Benjamini and Hochber’s method were provided by Novogene. Genes were considered differentially expressed (DEGs) based on their calculated padj. An analysis of individual molecules that were either upregulated or downregulated at each time point was analyzed by creating clustered heatmaps and KEGG pathway diagrams using the SRplot program (Tang et al., 2023). All FPKM values of genes contained within each heatmap are included in Supplemental file 1. Log2f graphs were created by calculating the log2f change of each gene using the FPKM values provided by Novogene. Using Python’s matplotlib and GraphPad Prism v9.0.0 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA), we created graphs that grouped log2f values by differentially expressed gene (DEG) on day 1 through day 3 fibrosis vs. wound healing with adjusted p-values <.05. Genes that were not statistically significant on a particular day across the time course were indicated by a grey bar to visualize the temporal trend of gene expression. Additionally, genes not differentially expressed as well as DEGs that were not significantly expressed across all three days were not included on the graph. Log2f and adjusted p-values for DEGs are included in Supplemental file 2. Data quantification for western blot analysis and wound healing was performed using Alpha view and GraphPad Prism Software (9.0.0 (121)). Data was normalized and presented ±SEM from 3 independent experiments and an unpaired t-test analysis was performed for all quantified western blot and wound healing experiments.

Results

Impact of lens wounding on Wnt and TGF-β pathway gene expression during regenerative wound repair and the induction of fibrosis

Our well-established clinically relevant ex vivo post-cataract surgery explant model was used to provide a comprehensive comparative analysis of gene expression patterns for the Wnt and TGFβ signaling pathways to gain insight into how these pathways may be regulated due to lens injury during regenerative wound healing versus induction of fibrosis. In response to lens injury caused by mock cataract surgery, a dual response was initiated, where regenerative wound healing occurs across the cell-denuded lens capsule, leading to wound closure by day 3, and fibrosis occurs in migrating cells that move onto the tissue culture substrate, driven by endogenous TGFβ (Fig. 1A (Basta et al., 2021),). N-cadherin cell-cell junctions demarcate the collectively migrating wounded epithelial cells on the lens capsule basement membrane (Fig. 1A). Leader cells, marked by CD44, guide this migration; in the fibrotic environment of the ECZ, these CD44+ cells deposit fibrotic matrix, including fibronectin-EDA and serve as myofibroblast precursors, which begin to transition into αSMA expressing myofibroblasts on day 3 post-injury (Fig. 1A and (Basta et al., 2021; Walker et al., 2018). Using this model, RNA was collected right after injury at T0, 1 h post-injury to capture early changes in gene expression prior to any significant migration, as well as on D1, D2, and D3 post-injury in both regenerative wound and fibrotic microenvironments (Fig. 1A). Pearson’s correlation analysis demonstrated strong correlation coefficients among biological replicates within both the wound healing and fibrosis groups, supporting the internal consistency and reliability of the dataset (Fig. 1B). Clustered heatmaps were created using normalized FPKM values of Wnt and TGF-β pathway components based on KEGG terms for these pathways and the literature (Fig. 2). DEGs that were significantly expressed, with adjusted p-values <.05, were included with the log2f values. Any DEG that did not significantly change on a specific day show the log2f value in grey to allow temporal gene expression changes over all three days to be visualized comparing fibrosis to wound healing (Fig. 2). This analysis included examining regulation of the following: 1) canonical (Wnt/β-catenin) and non-canonical Wnt pathways (PCP and Wnt/Ca2+), 3) canonical and non-canonical TGFβ pathways, 4) TGFβ superfamily pathway members, activin and BMP, as well as 5) the activators and repressors that control signaling of the Wnt and TGFβ pathways. Together these studies aim to map the transcriptomic regulation of the Wnt and TGFβ family pathways in response to lens injury associated with normal and pathological outcomes (Fig. 2).

Distinct regulation of Wnt ligands and frizzled receptors in response to lens injury

To begin our analysis of the Wnt pathway, we first focused on examining the changes in gene expression of Wnt ligands and their respective FZD receptors, critical initiators of Wnt signaling, in response to lens injury (T0, 1 h) and associated with wound healing and fibrotic outcomes (D1-D3). Of the 19 Wnt ligands and 10 FZD receptors known to exist, 12 Wnt ligands and 7 FZD receptors were differentially expressed in response to lens injury (Fig. 3A). Most of the Wnt ligands identified have the potential to signal through both the canonical and non-canonical Wnt pathways in a context dependent manner (Wnt2, Wnt4, Wnt6, Wnt7A, Wnt7B, Wnt9A and Wnt10 A) (Fig. 3A–C). Others expressed were mainly canonical (Wnt1) or non-canonical WNT ligands (Wnt2B, Wnt5A, Wnt5B, and Wnt11) (Fig. 3A–C). FZD receptors can bind to multiple distinct Wnt ligands, interactions are modeled for differentially expressed frizzled receptor and Wnt ligands (Fig. 3C), some of which are associated more with canonical (FZD2, FZD10) vs. noncanonical (FZD6 and FZD4) Wnt signaling. At T0, a group of Wnt ligands and FZD receptors were already highly expressed: Wnt1, Wnt2B, Wnt5A, Wnt7A, Wnt7B, and Wnt4 and FZD2, FZD7, and FZD10 (Fig. 3A), likely reflecting roles of Wnt/FZDs previously described in the lens (Iongh, 2006; Wang et al., 2018b; Wu et al., 2025). The majority of these Wnt and FZD genes decreased in expression from T0 to 1 h post-injury (Fig. 3A). At 1 h post-injury there was a sharp increase in the non-canonical Wnt ligand, Wnt11, its major receptor FZD4 as well as FZD1 (Fig. 3A). Subsequently expression of these genes decreased D1-D3 (Fig. 3A).

Additionally, we compared expression of Wnt and FZD genes each day (D1-D3) between fibrosis and WH. In the regenerative wound-healing microenvironment on the lens capsule compared to fibrosis, multiple Wnt ligands were upregulated following injury (downregulated with fibrosis). Wnt7A, Wnt7B, Wnt5A, and Wnt2B (Fig. 3A–C) accompanied by elevated expression of their respective high affinity Frizzled receptors. Wnt5A binds to FZD6, whose transcripts increase from D2 through D3 of wound healing (Fig. 3A–C). FZD4, FZD9, and FZD10 have the highest fold change with wound healing (Fig. 3B). By contrast, under pro-fibrotic conditions on the rigid tissue culture substrate, a distinct set of Wnt ligands and FZD receptors were induced; Wnt1, Wnt5B, Wnt10 A, Wnt6 and Wnt9A expression are elevated, along with frizzled receptors FZD1 and FZD7 (Fig. 3B). These findings reveal distinct spatial and temporal control of Wnt signaling, indicating elements are in place to specifically activate distinct canonical and noncanonical Wnt pathways in pro-fibrotic and pro-regenerative environments in response to injury.

Distinct upregulation of canonical Wnt signaling components and transcriptional machinery with fibrosis compared to wound healing

Next, we surveyed the RNA-seq data set for canonical Wnt/β-catenin pathway components. The key Wnt/β-catenin downstream component, DVL3 showed increased expression with fibrosis compared to wound healing from D1-D3 (Fig. 4A and B). Dishevelled (DVL) is critical to disrupting the β-catenin destruction complex (GSK3β/axin/APC) to stabilize β-catenin (Kang et al., 2022). Once stabilized, β-catenin can accumulate and enter the nucleus to interact with (TCF/LEF) to regulate Wnt target gene expression. Several β-catenin co-activators and transcriptional regulators, including TCF3, TCF7, TCF12, TCF7L2, and TBL1XR1, (a component of SMRT/N-CoR co-repressor complex) were upregulated in fibrosis vs. wound healing (Fig. 4A and B). Expression of TBL1XR1, TCFL2, TCF7, TCF7L1 first markedly increase 1 h post injury, then decrease with wound healing (Fig. 3A). LEF1, another key downstream transcription factor, was also significantly upregulated at T0 (Fig. 4A). Although LEF1 expression declines thereafter, it remains elevated during wound healing when compared to fibrosis (Figs. 4A and 3B).

The canonical Wnt gene target MYC exhibits temporally distinct expression profiles in wound healing versus fibrosis. Myc was strongly induced within 1 h post-injury in the wound-healing context (Fig. 4A), consistent with a role in promoting proliferation and matrix remodeling (Portal et al., 2022), then expression declines rapidly thereafter. Our findings indicate an overall enriched increase in DVL3, TCF-related transcriptional drivers, and Wnt target genes with fibrosis vs. wound healing suggesting enhanced canonical Wnt signaling is associated with a fibrotic response to lens injury.

Impact of lens wounding on transcriptional regulation of WNT/PCP and WNT/Ca2+ signaling pathways

We next examined how non-canonical Wnt signaling, including both the planar cell polarity (PCP) and calcium-dependent branches, diverges between wound healing and fibrotic conditions. Our findings so far showed that during wound healing, Wnt5a serves as the primary PCP ligand, while under fibrotic conditions, Wnt5b replaces Wnt5a as the principal PCP ligand (Fig. 3A and B). We also examined expression of Receptor Tyrosine Kinase-like Orphan receptor (ROR) and related to receptor tyrosine kinases (Ryk), which act as co-receptors with FZDs to bind Wnt and influence downstream signalling (Roy et al., 2018). ROR2 expression was elevated specifically on D2 and D3 of induction of fibrosis (Figs. 5A–4B). In contrast, while co-receptors ROR1 and RYK were highly expressed at T0 and 1 h post-injury before subsequently declining, ROR1 remained elevated in expression with wound healing compared to fibrosis (Figs. 5A–4B). These findings suggest that non-canonical PCP Wnt signaling is engaged in both contexts but likely operates through distinct receptor complexes during wound healing versus fibrosis.

DVL3, a shared downstream component of canonical and non-canonical Wnt (Fig. 2), and the gene that encodes the scaffold protein VANGL1 both rise concurrently with fibrosis (5 A,B). Gene expression of the vangle interacting protein Prickle1, becomes elevated on Days 2 and 3 of fibrosis, providing a mechanism for sequestering Dishevelled and VANGL proteins and modulating PCP signaling in a profibrotic context. This interaction likely facilitates the establishment of planar cell polarity in a profibrotic context. As well, DAAM2, which directly interacts with DVL to regulate activity of Rho GTPases and actin cytoskeleton remodeling, is up regulated with fibrosis on D3 (Fig. 5A and B). Lastly, several genes involved in actin cytoskeletal reorganization, including RHOA, ROCK1, and ROCK2, are upregulated during fibrosis compared to wound healing (Fig. 5A and B). In parallel, MAPK9, a JNK2 family kinase that facilitates cytoskeletal remodeling, is similarly up regulated on Day 3 of fibrosis (Fig. 5A and B).

During repair, the PCP pathway component ANKRD6 (also known as Diversin), which can act as a molecular switch that promotes Wnt/PCP while inhibiting Wnt/β-catenin, was highly expressed T0 and 1 h post-injury (Fig. 5A). In this regenerative context, we note DAAM1 increases T0 to 1 h, and throughout wound healing. Prickle2 and MAPK10 (JNK3), which transduces PCP signals into cytoskeletal reorganization, are up regulated as well (Fig. 5A and B). DAAM1, which diverts Dishevelled from canonical β-catenin activation toward Rho GTPase signaling, also remains persistently upregulated in wound healing compared to fibrosis (Fig. 5A and B), attenuating canonical Wnt signaling through PCP crosstalk. Thus, distinct components of PCP Wnt signaling are in place to potentially promote Wnt/PCP signaling during wound healing and fibrosis and with the potential to enhance or dampen canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling.

The Wnt/Ca2+ signaling arm was selectively activated during fibrosis, with minimal activation observed during wound healing. Wnt5b, FZD7, and ROR2, all components of the PCP pathway that showed expression with fibrosis, also contribute to Wnt/Ca2+ signaling (Figs. 3 and 5A,B). PLCB1, the phospholipase C β1 enzyme that links Frizzled receptors to IP3 production and intracellular Ca2+ release, is up regulated from Day 2 to Day 3 of fibrosis (Fig. 5A and B). Downstream components of this pathway also show induction of the regulatory and catalytic subunit of calcineurin PPP3CA on day 3, and NFATC1 and NFATC2, Nuclear factor activated T-cell family TFs activated downstream of calcium and calcineurin are elevated on Days 2 and 3 of fibrosis (Fig. 5A and B). Calcineurin promotes dephosphorylation of NFATC1 and NFATC2, enabling their nuclear translocation and activation of fibrotic gene programs (Li et al., 2023). In contrast, the same Wnt/Ca2+ signaling components remain at baseline or show minimal expression during wound healing, indicating little to no up regulation of this pathway in the regenerative wound healing state.

The consequence of lens injury on gene expression of Wnt activators and repressors

To understand how signaling is restrained or promoted in response to lens injury we assessed the transcriptional regulation of extracellular and intracellular inhibitors and activators of the Wnt signaling pathway during wound healing and fibrotic repair. Key extracellular Wnt inhibitors include DKKs, which bind LRP5/6 co-receptors to block Wnt binding (Kardooni et al., 2018), as well as secreted Frizzled-related proteins (sFRPs), Cerebus (Cer1), and Wnt Inhibitor Factor 1 (WIF1) which act as Wnt antagonists to compete with Wnt ligands to bind frizzled receptors (Chi et al., 2011; Cruciat and Niehrs, 2013; Li and Amar, 2006). In contrast, R-spondins (RSPO) are a family of Wnt signaling activators not directly but through interaction with leucine-rich repeat-containing G-protein coupled receptors (LRG) receptors (He et al., 2023; Park et al., 2018).

To potentially prevent excessive Wnt activation during wound healing, several extracellular inhibitors are induced early post-injury. SFRP1, which sequesters Wnt ligands through its cysteine-rich domain, is elevated on Day 1 and remains high through Day 3 of wound healing, thereby preventing ligand–receptor interactions. Similarly, CER1 and DKK3 expression increase with wound healing (Fig. 6A and B). In fibrosis, none of these inhibitors show significant induction, except for distinct members of the sFRP family. SFRP2 increases D3 during fibrosis, while SFRP4 increases specifically on D2 and D3 (Fig. 6A and B). SFRP5 increased D2, however by D3 expression begins to decrease with fibrosis (Fig. 6A and B). As well, several Wnt pathway potentiators, including RSPO1, RSPO2 and RSPO3 are strongly induced at 1 h before decreasing on Day 1. Furthermore, RSPO2 and RSPO3 are slowly up regulated with fibrosis starting on Day 2 and continuing through Day 3 (Fig. 6A and B). RSPO1 is the only RSPO upregulated with wound healing vs. fibrosis (Fig. 6A and B). Thus, Wnt activation may be favored with fibrosis, also taking into accounted limited upregulation of distinct classes Wnt inhibitors potentially allowing for greater unchecked Wnt signaling in the fibrotic microenvironment.

We also analyzed the inhibitory machinery that governs β-catenin stability in the canonical Wnt pathway under wound healing versus fibrotic conditions. Within the β-catenin destruction complex, Axin2, which functions as the scaffold protein anchoring the core kinase GSK3B, facilitate β-catenin phosphorylation, is up regulated at 1 h and remains elevated with wound healing (Fig. 6A and B). On the other hand, several components related to β-catenin destruction complex specifically are paradoxically up regulated during fibrosis. APC, which serves as a scaffold that binds β-catenin for phosphorylation and degradation, shows elevated expression starting Day 2 and persisting through Day 3 of fibrosis (Fig. 6A and B). GSK3B, a kinase that phosphorylates β-catenin, along with CSNK1E, which primes β-catenin for subsequent GSK3B action, both increase on Days 2 and 3 of fibrosis (Fig. 6A and B).

In contrast, components of the ubiquitin ligase complex responsible for targeting phosphorylated β-catenin for proteasomal breakdown are downregulated during fibrosis compared to wound healing. BTRC, an F-box protein that is involved in targeting β-catenin for ubiquination, declines in expression during fibrosis. As well, RBX1, which recruits the E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme, also shows decreased levels on Days 2 and 3 (Fig. 6A and B). On the other hand, CXXC4, which binds Dishevelled and promotes its ubiquitination, is up regulated on Days 1–3 of wound healing.

Additional negative regulators of β-catenin mediated transcription include NDK1, a negative regulator of β-catenin mediated transcription and TLE4, a transcriptional co-repressor of the Wnt pathway, were expressed shortly after injury at 1 h, and with fibrosis through D2 and D3 (Figs. 6A–5C). Finally, β-catenin transcriptional repressor CBY1 showed elevated expression with fibrosis D2 and D3 (Figs. 6A–5C). These findings reveal that multiple levels of regulation of the Wnt signaling pathway in regenerative and fibrotic repair environments. Upregulation of TGF-β pathway genes correlates with the potential of enhanced activation of this pathway during fibrosis compared to normal wound healing

We next evaluated how both the canonical TGFβ/Smad-mediated axis and the non-canonical TGFβ-MAPK, Rho-Rho kinase are reprogrammed in response to injury to promote fibrosis. During fibrosis, both receptors and ligands of the canonical TGF-β pathway are markedly elevated. Transcripts for TGFβR2 rise on Day 2 of fibrosis while TGFβ1 and TGFβ3 increase concomitantly (Fig. 7A and B). Notably TGFβ2, the predominant TGF-β isoform in lens tissue, is induced more gradually and becomes significantly up-regulated only on Day 3 (Fig. 7A and B), consistent with the established timing of myofibroblast differentiation (Walker et al., 2018). Canonical SMAD3 gene transcripts remain elevated from Day 1 through day 3 (Fig. 7A and B), reflecting the potential for persistent Smad mediated transcriptional activity in a profibrotic context. Concurrently, non-canonical TGF-β effectors associated with cytoskeletal reorganization also show robust induction: RHOA, ROCK1 and ROCK2 expression each increase with fibrosis, mirroring early cytoskeletal changes required for myofibroblast transition (Fig. 7A and B). Additional, upregulated noncanonical effectors with fibrosis include, MAPK9 (Jun2) and MAPK14 (p38) (Fig. 7A and B). In contrast, with regenerative repair, other non canonical effectors, which may act dependently or independently of TGFβ signaling, H-RAS, TRAF4, PIK3CB, PIK3CG, and MAPK10 (JNK3), and AKT2 were upregulated with wound healing (Fig. 7A and B). These findings, indicate a specific sustained upregulation of TGF-β signaling components to drive a fibrotic response to lens injury.

Distinct transcriptional regulation of activin and BMP receptor pathways during fibrosis versus wound healing

We also examined how the Activin and BMP branches of TGF-β superfamily signaling are differentially regulated in regenerative versus fibrotic repair environments. From D1-D3 of fibrosis, we observed an increase in expression of INHBA, which encodes activin A and the downstream effector SMAD3, suggesting activation of the activin pathway signaling with fibrosis (Fig. 8A and B and Fig. 7A and B). In contrast, components of the BMP signaling pathway, including ligands, receptors, and downstream effectors were differentially regulated in regenerative wound and fibrosis environments, suggesting the potential to initiate distinct BMP signaling pathways in each environment. During wound healing, there was up regulation in in BMPR1A (type I), and ACVR2B (type II) receptors as early as 1 h after injury. Additionally, BMPR1B (type I) and BMPR2 (type II) showed increased expression from T0 to day 1, accompanied by increased expression of SMAD1, a downstream BMP effector (Fig. 8A and B). To further characterize BMP pathway activity, we, analyzed the expression of ID genes to evaluate how BMP-driven programs are engaged during wound healing versus fibrosis. IDs (inhibitor of differentiation), are bHLH transcription factors tightly linked to cell cycle regulation, differentiation, and cell commitment (Norton, 2000). In the wound healing environment, expression of BMP-inducible ID proteins ID1 and ID2 is already elevated at T0 and remain so at 1 h before dipping slightly on D1 and rebounding thereafter (Fig. 8A). In contrast, ID3 expression is lower at time zero and rises significantly by 1 h, before following a similar dip-and-rebound pattern. When comparing fibrosis to wound healing, ID1 shows the largest fold increase followed by ID2, ID3 and ID4, which all remain elevated through wound healing (Fig. 8B).

In the fibrotic environment, BMP ligands, BMP2, BMP4, BMP7 along with receptors ACVR1, BMPR1A, BMPR2 were elevated (Fig. 8A and B). Notably, BMP4 and BMP7, both canonical ligands for ACVR2B and ACVR1 and antagonists of TGF-β signaling, were consistently induced during fibrosis (Fig. 8A and B). However, their signaling output appears diminished, as indicated by the lack of downstream upregulation of SMAD1 and ID protein induction (Fig. 8A and B). These findings suggest that although similar BMP components are present in both environments, the amplitude and downstream responsiveness of BMP signaling differ significantly between regenerative wound healing and fibrosis.

Distinct regulation of activator and repressor expression to control TGFβ superfamily signaling in response to lens wounding

To identify how TGFβ signalling pathways can be negatively and positively regulated in regenerative and fibrotic environments to help predict injury outcome we examined transcriptional regulation of TGFβ family activators and repressors. During wound healing, multiple regulatory mechanisms are engaged to limit TGF-β activity. ZFYVE9 (encodes Smad Anchor for receptor activation (SARA)) is up regulated on Day 3, where it serves multiple functions (Fig. 9B). SARA can be antagonistic to TGFβ however the mechanisms remain to be elucidated (Rozes-Salvador et al., 2018) and is also described to facilitate the recruitment of SMAD2/3 to the TGF-β receptor complex (Tsukazaki et al., 1998), indicating a transient activating role (Tang et al., 2010). Decorin, which binds and sequesters TGF-β ligands in inactive complexes, increases notably on Day 3, contributing to late-phase suppression of ligand-receptor interactions. Additionally, BAMBI, a pseudo receptor that inhibits TGF-β receptor activation and is typically induced by canonical Wnt signaling, is upregulated, indicating a broader loss of inhibitory control over the TGF-β superfamily during wound healing (Fig. 9B). In the regenerative repair environment, multiple inhibitors of BMP signaling are also induced with some remaining highly expressed wound healing vs. fibrosis including CER1, BMPER (Fig. 9A and B).

In contrast, fibrotic conditions show a more complex regulatory profile. LTBP1, which sequesters latent TGF-β in the extracellular matrix until activation, is consistently elevated across Days 1–3 of fibrosis (Fig. 9B). Expression of thrombospondin THBS1, a matricellular proteins that activates latent TFGβ, occurs from Day 1 through Day 3, suggesting active modulation of cell extracellular matrix interactions that favor fibrosis (Fig. 9B). While SMURF1, SMURF2, and BMP7, all canonical TGF-β pathway inhibitors, are upregulated with fibrosis days 2 through 3, TGFBR1 and TGFBR2 transcripts also remain elevated, suggesting that inhibitory input is not sufficient to suppress pro-fibrotic signaling. SMAD6, a weaker feedback inhibitor compared to SMAD7, becomes up regulated on Day 3, hinting at partial feedback repression of the pathway. Additionally, transcriptional negative regulators, SKI and SKIL were upregulated (Fig. 9B) suggesting another level of negative regulation for the TGβ pathway.

Despite the upregulation of BMP ligands and receptors, including BMP2, BMP4, BMP7, BMPR1A, and BMPR2, with fibrosis, the expression of extracellular BMP inhibitors was elevated. Follistatin (FST), Chordin (CHRD), Noggin-2 (NOG2), each of which blocks BMP signaling at the ligand or receptor-binding level, are upregulated fibrosis relative to wound healing (Fig. 9B). These studies reveal a complex negative and positive environment post-injury to regulate BMP and TGFβ signaling pathways. Impact of the pan-ID antagonist AGX51 on the lens injury response

Our RNA-seq findings revealed that the downstream BMP signaling effector, SMAD1 and bHLH transcriptional repressors, ID1, ID2, ID3 and ID4 increased in expression in regenerative vs. fibrotic settings in response to lens injury (see Fig. 8). Taken together with the known role of BMPs as TGFβ antagonists, suggests a potential BMP-SMAD1-ID signaling preference with regenerative vs. fibrotic repair. Also, our recent supervised machine learning study, using the RNAseq data set D1-D3 from the ex vivo post-cataract surgery model, identified ID1 as a potential important gene candidate associated with regenerative wound repair (Lalman et al., 2025). Confoundingly, our RNA-seq findings also showed an increase in BMP ligands and receptors with fibrosis, however this coincided with an increase in expression of BMP antagonists to potentially limit BMP signaling (see Figs. 8 and 9). To better understand the function of BMP pathways, specifically the role of ID proteins in regulating the response to lens injury we perturbed ID protein function using AGX51, a potent and specific pan-inhibitor to IDs (ID1, ID2, ID3, and ID4), which targets ID proteins for degradation to block ID function (Wojnarowicz et al., 2021).

For these studies, ex vivo post-cataract surgery cultures were treated from time 0, with vehicle or increasing doses of AGX51, to block ID function immediately in response to injury and throughout the injury-repair process. Wound healing was observed from time 0 through day 3 and showed that treatment with AGX51, led to a dose-dependent decrease in wound healing (Fig. 10A). Under these conditions, starting treatment at time 0, AGX51 treatment also blocked transition to a myofibroblast since expression of the myofibroblast marker, αSMA was inhibited on day 3 post-injury (Fig. 10B). However, it is important to note that treatment with AGX51 from day 0 also led to decreased movement off the lens capsule onto the pro-fibrotic tissue culture substrate, which subsequently will prevent the pro-fibrotic response from occurring. To ensure that cells had a chance to begin their migration off the lens capsule and onto the pro-fibrotic tissue culture substrate, we also added AGX51 on D1 post-injury. This allowed us to determine the effect of AGX51 treatment on cells within the pro-fibrotic tissue culture environment and their ability to transition to a myofibroblast phenotype separate from the defect in migration off the lens capsule. AGX51 treatment from day 1 post-injury inhibited αSMA expression on day 3 post-injury (Fig. 10C). These findings reveal a role for ID transcriptional regulators in modulating the response to lens injury to navigate both regenerative and fibrotic repair outcomes.

Discussion

Our RNA-seq profiling of the lens injury response reveals how injury modulates expression of TGF-β and Wnt family pathway components within the same cells exposed to either a pro-reparative or a pro-fibrotic microenvironment. Additionally, our results identify candidate regulators that may promote or suppress fibrotic signaling, which demonstrates the importance of both the cellular microenvironment and the balance between pathway activators and repressors in determining the trajectory of the wound response. These studies provide new mechanistic insight into how canonical Wnt, and canonical and noncanonical TGFβ pathways, with established roles in promoting lens fibrosis, become aberrantly regulated at the transcriptional level to potentiate signaling to drive a pathological response to lens injury (see Fig. 11, model). In addition, we provide new evidence for the specific upregulation of the Wnt/Ca2+ signaling axis with the induction of lens fibrosis, a pathway not yet linked to PCO development. Finally, we demonstrate that ID signaling functionally contributes to both regenerative wound healing and fibrotic repair following lens injury. Together, these data offer a comprehensive view of how TGF-β and Wnt signaling pathways are transcriptionally regulated in distinct repair contexts, providing new molecular insight that can be utilized to inform the development of future strategies to prevent a pathological outcome to lens injury.

Microenvironment-specific regulation of canonical/non-canonical Wnt-frizzled pathways in response to lens injury

The findings presented here demonstrate that the microenvironmental context governs distinct Wnt signaling programs that drive either regenerative lens repair or fibrotic remodeling. Our data suggest that different branches of both canonical and non-canonical Wnt signaling are selectively activated depending on the tissue outcome (Fig. 11A, model). For instance, in the wound-healing context, Wnt7A and Wnt7B are upregulated, consistent with studies that show Wnt7A expression increases in response to corneal injury to mediate repair (Wu et al., 2025) and that Wnt7A/7 B mediate regenerative responses across several systems (Chen et al., 2023; Dong et al., 2017; Lin et al., 2010; Wang et al., 2018a; Wu et al., 2025). In parallel, the upregulation of Wnt5A and its receptor FZD6 suggests activation of the planar cell polarity (PCP) axis, which may support epithelial organization, cytoskeletal remodeling, and coordinated collective cell migration during repair. Further reinforcing a polarity-centered regenerative program, we observed sustained expression of DAAM1 throughout the wound healing response. This is consistent with prior studies linking Wnt/PCP signaling to the regulation of collective cell movement and spatial orientation in both embryologic development and cancer (VanderVorst et al., 2019, 2023). Together, these findings support the hypothesis that the regenerative lens environment activates a polarity- and organization-centered Wnt program, distinct from the signaling profile associated with fibrosis.

By contrast, induction of fibrosis is accompanied by upregulation of Wnt6, Wnt9A and Wnt10 A, Wnt ligands all associated with fibrosis in other tissue types (Luo et al., 2018; Oda et al., 2016; Teiken et al., 2018) The fibrotic lens epithelium also engages a remodeled PCP-like program, substituting Wnt5a with Wnt5b and shifting receptor preference potentially to FZD1 and/or FZD7, frizzled receptors previously linked to pro-fibrotic Wnt activity (Guan and Zhou, 2017). A study by Nabhan et al. showed specific FZD expression associated with distinct cell types in the lung, which can be specifically targeted with agonists to promote regeneration of alveolar epithelium (Nabhan et al., 2023). In this study agonists to FZD5 and FZD6, specific to lung epithelium could promote regeneration in response to lung injury in the mouse without inducing fibrosis and FZD1 and FZD7 expression specific to fibroblasts was associated with fibrosis (Nabhan et al., 2023). Together with our findings, which show similar regulation of FZD with regenerative (FZD6) and fibrosis (FZD 1,7), we speculate that distinct FZDs associated with regenerative repair may be specific targets to modulate the lens injury response away from a pathogenic response.

Wnt/Ca2+ signaling emerges as a pathway selectively upregulated during fibrotic, but not regenerative, repair of the lens (Fig. 11A, model). Unlike the canonical β-catenin or planar cell polarity (PCP) branches that dominate in wound healing, fibrosis is marked by the simultaneous upregulation of Wnt5b, FZD7, and PLCB1, suggesting a shift toward calcium-mediated signaling downstream of Frizzled activation. This pathway is further supported by the induction of PPP3CA, which encodes a subunit of calcineurin, a phosphatase complex known to activate NFAT-dependent transcription (Li et al., 2023). In other tissue systems, NFAT signaling has been strongly linked to inflammatory responses, ECM production, and the induction of myofibroblasts, reinforcing its likely contribution to the fibrotic phenotype observed in the lens (Bogdanova et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2023). Notably, upregulation of this signaling axis is largely absent during regenerative wound repair, implying that non-canonical Wnt/Ca2+ signaling is selectively co-opted under pathological conditions. Together, these findings point to a molecular divergence in Wnt signaling branch activation, with fibrosis favoring a calcium-dependent transcriptional program that is distinct from the Wnt-canonical and polarity-driven responses engaged during regenerative repair.

Potential mechanisms to drive an unchecked canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway in fibrosis vs. wound healing

Canonical Wnt signaling is tightly regulated at multiple levels, and our transcriptomic data indicate the possibility that distinct extracellular, intracellular, and transcriptional mechanisms are at play (Fig. 11A, model). Extracellular control of Wnt activity diverged significantly between regenerative and fibrotic settings. In wound healing, multiple families of secreted inhibitors such as SFRP1 and DKK3 were prominently induced. Also, CER1 elevated already at T0 remained high 1 h post-injury. CER1 and SFRP all encode Wnt antagonists that directly bind to Wnt to prevent downstream signaling (Chi et al., 2011; Cruciat and Niehrs, 2013; Li and Amar, 2006) Unlike other DKK family members (DKK1, DKK2 and DKK4), there is no clear evidence that DKK3 acts as a Wnt antagonist; instead, it was shown to modulate TGFβ signaling (Busceti et al., 2017; Kardooni et al., 2018; Li et al., 2017; Romero et al., 2016). Consistent with studies in a mouse cataract surgery model (Wang et al., 2018a,b), we find DKK3 expression decreased with fibrosis in response to lens injury. Considering DKK3’s role in antagonizing TGFβ, perhaps an increase in DKK3 with wound healing limits TGFβ signalling compared to fibrotic repair. Furthermore, upregulation of multiple families of Wnt antagonists likely act as a negative feedback mechanism to limit Wnt activation during regeneration repair.

In fibrosis, by contrast, the only family of secreted inhibitors that were upregulated were SRFP2, SRFP4 and SRFP5. However, several R-spondins, including RSPO2 and RSPO3 which enhance Wnt signaling by stabilizing Frizzled–LGR receptor complexes, exhibited a biphasic expression pattern (Park et al., 2018). These R-spondins are transiently induced early after injury, likely to boost initial Wnt activity, and are reactivated during the fibrotic phase. In contrast, only RSPO1 is elevated during regenerative lens wound healing. The seemingly paradoxical upregulation of sFRPs during fibrosis may serve to limit Wnt signaling, thereby restraining the extent of the fibrotic response, or to prevent Wnt activation in specific cell populations. Our previous studies showed that addition of exogenous TGFβ could exacerbate the fibrotic reaction leading to greater accumulation of αSMA + myofibroblasts and fibrotic ECM, showing cells within the fibrotic region are capable of a more aggressive response (Basta et al., 2021). Yet, even under these pro-fibrotic conditions, subsets of wounded lens epithelial cells, particularly those lacking CD44 expression, remained protected from fibrotic transformation (Basta et al., 2021). These findings suggest that protective mechanisms exist within the epithelium to buffer against excessive Wnt or TGF-β signaling, which may help explain the transcriptomic patterns observed here. Taken together, the balance between secreted Wnt inhibitors such as sFRPs and potentiators such as R-spondins appears to modulate the intensity and spatial range of Wnt signaling in the fibrotic microenvironment. This regulatory balance may contribute to sustained Wnt activation while also providing spatiotemporal control that limits excessive fibrotic remodeling.

Our transcriptomic data suggest that distinct intracellular mechanisms regulate β-catenin stability in regenerative versus fibrotic environments (Fig. 11A, model). In fibrosis, key components of the β-catenin destruction complex, including APC, GSK3B, and CSNK1E are upregulated, which would typically enhance β-catenin turnover. However, this increase is not accompanied by a corresponding rise in expression of the ubiquitin ligase components required for proteasomal degradation. Notably, BTRC (β-TrCP) and RBX1 are downregulated during fibrosis. This mismatch suggests a bottleneck in the ubiquitin-proteasome system, allowing phosphorylated β-catenin to evade degradation and accumulate in the nucleus, despite upstream destruction complex activity. Though this dysregulation of the canonical Wnt ubiquitination process has not been extensively explored, in a study done by Lin et al., it was discovered that overexpression of NEDD4L, an E3 ubiquitin ligase, enhances β-catenin degradation and thereby limits fibroblast proliferation, invasion and myofibroblast differentiation (Chen et al., 2021). As well, in the context of cancer, a study published by Blees et al., demonstrated that erioflorin inhibits β-TrCP1, preventing ubiquitination and degradation of its targets, including β-catenin (Blees et al., 2012). In contrast, wound healing exhibits a more tightly regulated β-catenin degradation system. For example, AXIN2, a scaffold protein critical for destruction complex assembly, and CXXC4, a Dishevelled-binding protein that promotes β-catenin clearance, are both upregulated. This coordination likely supports efficient β-catenin turnover and prevents excessive activation of the Wnt pathway (Lu et al., 2013; Schaefer et al., 2020). Additionally, fibrosis is associated with upregulation of transcriptional regulators such as TLE4 and CBY1, which may reflect compensatory mechanisms aimed at dampening Wnt-driven transcription and limiting the fibrotic response. Together, these molecular differences likely underlie the more effective β-catenin clearance and restrained canonical Wnt signaling observed during regenerative repair compared to fibrosis.

Taken together, these findings suggest that multiple tiers of Wnt regulation, including extracellular modulators, intracellular degradation machinery, and transcriptional repressors are dynamically rewired in response to the surrounding tissue context (Fig. 11A, model). In regenerative wound healing, these mechanisms work in concert to constrain Wnt signaling, ensuring timely resolution of the repair program. In contrast, fibrosis is marked by dysregulation at several of these levels, leading to sustained β-catenin activity, activation of non-canonical Wnt pathways, and promotion of myofibroblast differentiation and extracellular matrix accumulation. In particular, numerous studies on fibrosis in the lens as well as in other tissues, have highlighted the importance of extracellular regulation as a key checkpoint in modulating fibrotic outcomes. Targeting secreted Wnt ligands, Frizzled receptors, or extracellular antagonists such as DKK1 and sFRPs has been shown to significantly alter the trajectory of tissue remodeling, further underscoring the therapeutic relevance of this regulatory tier (Kardooni et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2018b).

Distinct mechanisms that may underpin regulation of TGF-β signaling associated with fibrosis

TGF-β signaling in fibrosis engages both canonical and non-canonical branch to coordinate fibrotic remodeling in the lens (Lovicu et al., 2002, 2016; Valcourt et al., 2005). On the other hand, we previously demonstrated that regenerative repair in the post-cataract surgery model does not require TGFβ signaling (Basta et al., 2021). Our transcriptomic studies validate a preferential activation of TGFβ signaling with the induction of lens fibrosis compared to wound healing and provides new clues into how this pathway becomes aberrantly regulated to drive this pathological response (Fig. 11B, model). Amplification of signaling through this pathway is supported by upregulation of TGFβ ligands, TGFβ1 and TGFβ2, their receptors TGFβR1 and TGFβR2 and SMAD3 supports activation of TGFβ and sustained transcriptional output of TGF-β target genes involved in fibrosis (Lovicu et al., 2016). In parallel, non-canonical effectors are upregulated that can contribute to cytoskeletal remodeling. Induction of RHOA, ROCK1, and ROCK2 allow for activation of actomyosin contractility tightly linked to myofibroblast transition (Korol et al., 2016). Other non-canonical effectors upregulated and previously linked to driving lens fibrosis were MAPK1, MAPK9 (JNK 2) and MAPK14 (p38) (Lovicu et al., 2016; Madala et al., 2012; Sato et al., 2002). These changes are complemented by the induction of thrombospondin 1, which mediates latent TGF-β activation. Simultaneously, sustained expression of LTBP1, which tethers latent TGF-β to the extracellular matrix (ECM), likely reflects an increase in both ligand production and its sequestration for controlled, context-dependent activation (Caja et al., 2018). Together, these coordinated canonical and non-canonical TGF-β signaling branches form a feedforward network that sustains pathway activation and enforces a persistent fibrotic state in the lens epithelium. In the fibrotic context, strong upregulation of INHBA and its receptor ACVR1 suggests enhanced Activin–SMAD2/3 signaling, which is known to promote mesenchymal transition, ECM production, and fibroblast activation (Zhang et al., 2024).

Consistent with the dispensable role for TGFβ in wound healing, the transcriptomic landscape of the regenerative environment suggests the presence of a coordinated inhibitory network that acts to limit TGF-β signaling and prevent progression toward fibrosis. One such regulator is ZFYVE9, which facilitates SMAD2/3 recruitment to the TGF-β receptor complex but later acts as a transcriptional repressor, demonstrating a temporally dynamic mode of pathway regulation (Runyan et al., 2009). Additionally, upregulation of DKK3, along with decorin, an extracellular matrix proteoglycan known to sequester active TGF-β ligands, highlights the presence of layered inhibitory mechanisms that function at both intracellular and extracellular levels (Wang et al., 2018b).

In fibrosis, however, the regulatory landscape becomes more complex and surprisingly permissive of sustained TGF-β signaling, despite the upregulation of classical inhibitors. Negative regulators such as SMURF1, SMURF2, BMP4, and BMP7 are all elevated, yet their expression appears insufficient to suppress ongoing TGF-β pathway activity, which remains essential for fibrotic progression (Jiang et al., 2024; Shu and Lovicu, 2021; Shu et al., 2021). Notably SMAD6, which preferentially inhibits BMP signaling, is selectively upregulated by day 3. This shift may contribute to the maintenance of TGF-β–driven fibrosis while reducing BMP-mediated counter-regulatory effects. Additionally, however, the upregulation of inhibitory regulators in fibrosis may reflect attempts to constrain the fibrotic response or may act in a cell type-specific manner to preserve regenerative potential in certain epithelial subpopulations. Together, these findings point to a context-dependent regulation of TGF-β signaling that shapes divergent outcomes in wound healing versus fibrosis.

Roles for BMP/ID signaling in modulating the response to lens injury

Distinct modulation of the BMP branches of the TGF-β superfamily is associated with divergent injury outcomes supporting regenerative repair in some contexts and promoting fibrosis in others (Fig. 11B, model). Wound healing was characterized by a transient yet robust activation of the BMP pathway immediately post-injury. Within 1 h, there is coordinated induction of BMPR1A, BMPR1B, BMPR2, and ACVR2B, accompanied by elevated expression of SMAD1 and BMP-responsive ID genes. This early surge supports a pro-regenerative program that counters TGF-β mediated fibrotic signaling and promotes epithelial maintenance and repair (Miyazawa and Miyazono, 2017; Perez-Shibayama et al., 2024). In contrast, fibrotic repair is marked by the upregulation of multiple BMP ligands, including BMP2, BMP4, and BMP7, as well as receptors BMPR1A, BMPR2, and ACVR1. However, this upregulation is not accompanied by a corresponding increase in downstream BMP effectors such as SMAD1 or the ID family of transcriptional regulators, suggesting that the signaling cascade may be actively restrained. Supporting this idea, we observed increased expression of BMP antagonists, including CHRD, NOG2, and FST, during fibrosis. These inhibitors may serve to tightly regulate BMP activity, preventing it from fully counteracting TGF-β signaling and thus allowing fibrotic progression to continue.

To validate the functionality of BMP signaling in both regenerative and fibrotic contexts, we targeted ID function, known to be induced downstream of BMPs using AGX51, a small-molecule inhibitor that targets ID proteins for destruction. We were unable to identify ID antibodies with high enough homology to cross-react with chicken tissue to be able examine changes in ID protein expression following AGX51 treatment. Since AGX51 treatment is known to elevate ID mRNA levels while blunting expression of protein (Wojnarowicz et al., 2021) this also precluded us from using ID mRNA as a readout for the experiment. Nonetheless, blocking ID function with AGX51 significantly slowed wound healing and inhibited the emergence of α-SMA positive myofibroblasts, indicating a role for ID proteins for both epithelial repair and fibrosis initiation. The role of BMP signaling in the lens repair response appears complex. There is a strong literature that supports BMPs play an antagonistic role to TGFβ to block fibrosis in the lens (Saika et al., 2006; Shu and Lovicu, 2021; Shu et al., 2017). The study by Shu et al. showed that agonists (ventromorhins) to activate BMP signaling failed to block fibrosis in the lens but addition of BMP4 could block lens fibrosis suggesting that BMP activation mechanisms are not all equal (Shu et al., 2021). How BMP-ID pathways differ between signaling regenerative and fibrotic repair is not clear. ID transcriptional regulators are typically expressed in highly proliferative undifferentiated cell populations (Roschger and Cabrele, 2017); taken together with our previous studies demonstrating that transition to a fibrotic phenotype is dependent upon S phase progression (Basta et al., 2023) suggests a role for ID proteins in regulating proliferation/differentiation status required for induction of fibrosis in response to lens injury. Notably, IDs can also be induced in response to TGFβ signaling (Liang et al., 2009; Ma et al., 2014) and dual inhibition of ID1 and ID3 was able to prevent pulmonary fibrosis (pre-print(Antar et al., 2025)). Yet in other contexts, IDs play a protective role against fibrosis. For instance, AAV-mediated delivery of id3 gene therapy to rabbit eyes prevented alkali trauma-induced corneal fibrosis (Gupta et al., 2022). Since AGX51 targets all ID proteins whether distinct IDs play repair specific roles in navigating lens wounding outcomes and in response to BMP and/or TGFβ signals remains to be determined.

Future studies and limitations

Our studies provide a comprehensive evaluation of potential mechanisms that may underpin how Wnt and TGFβ pathways are regulated in regenerative and fibrotic repair microenvironments in response to lens injury as well as pinpoint potential therapeutic targets for intervention strategies, thereby laying the foundation for future studies. Because our RNA-seq was performed on bulk tissue rather than single cells, we could not assign gene expression changes to specific cell populations and explain some of the paradoxical patterns in pathway regulation that we observe, a subject for future studies. A limitation of the transcriptomic analysis is whether the changes we observe occur at the protein level. Additionally, it would be important to perform functional experiments to determine whether specific identified pathway changes lead to selective suppression of regenerative vs. fibrotic repair. Also, our studies in the post-cataract surgery model, while providing insight into inherent processes to the lens, lack the context of the eye environment which may influence regulation of these pathways. For instance, the aqueous humor is a large reservoir of TGFβ (Allen et al., 1998; Cousins et al., 1991; Tripathi et al., 1994), which can influence regulation of the pathways and the cataract surgery response. Lastly, while we examined these pathways separately, it is well known that Wnt/TGF-β families crosstalk, another level of regulation that can impact lens injury outcomes.

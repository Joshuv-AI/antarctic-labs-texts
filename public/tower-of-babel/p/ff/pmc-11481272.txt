To assess the availability of antibiotics for online sale and comparison of e-pharmacies on key characteristics influencing safe and rational use of antibiotics: an exploratory analysis
Authors: Puneet Kaur, Navjot Kaur, Jasbir Singh, Jasmeen Kaur
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundGrowing antimicrobial resistance represents serious public health threat. With the increasing presence of online/e-pharmacies in India, public access to medicines has increased. Easy access coupled with lack of adequate and authentic drug information can undermine rational antibiotic use.ObjectivesThe present study aimed at exploring the availability of antibiotics for online sale and comparing the e-pharmacies on key characteristics influencing safe and rational use of antibiotics.MethodsA cross-sectional study was conducted over a period of 3 months using the websites of 10 popular e-pharmacies in India. Availability of antibiotics for online sale was assessed in context of the National List of Essential Medicines, 2022 and WHO-AWaRe (Access, Watch, Reserve) categories. Three separate questionnaires were developed to extract and compare relevant information from e-pharmacies pertaining to their safety and authenticity (in light of the expected standards laid down in draft e-pharmacy rules, 2018), availability of drug product and consumer awareness information for promoting prudent antibiotic use. Data were summarized using descriptive statistics.ResultsOut of the 17 antibiotics studied; antibiotics belonging to all WHO-AWaRe categories i.e. Access (83.33%), Watch (88.33%) and Reserve (78%) groups were available through e-pharmacies. Wide variation exists among the e-pharmacies regarding compliance to studied parameters under three questionnaires.ConclusionsIncreased access to Watch and Reserve groups of antibiotics can translate into antibiotic misuse. It is therefore crucial that the regulatory gaps concerning e-pharmacies are addressed urgently and consumers are educated regarding safe and rational use of antibiotics.

Introduction

In India, an ‘e-pharmacy’ has been defined as business of distribution or sale, stock, exhibit or offer for sale of drugs through a web portal or any other electronic mode.1 Online/e-pharmacies/webportals offer several advantages such as providing easy and round the clock access to an unlimited number of drugs for the housebound, free access to drug information and easy comparison of medicine prices from the comfort of one’s home.2 However, these advantages may prove counterproductive if the drug product and consumer awareness information is inadequate coupled with a lack of patient–physician or patient–pharmacist interaction to allow for safe use of drugs. Further, the USFDA found that 95% of websites selling prescription medicines were found unsafe and unlicensed.3 They may serve as proxies for stealing personal information and could result in financial fraud.4

The Indian e-pharmacy market is expected to grow and reach a value of ∼₹90 billion by 2027.5 The rapid expansion of e-pharmacies along with wider internet penetration has increased public access to medications, which may include antibiotics. In contrast to other medicines, antibiotics are unique in the sense that their use in individuals can affect antibiotics’ effectiveness in the community.6 It is therefore pertinent that antibiotics are sold with adequate safeguards and are used judiciously. To emphasize appropriate use of antibiotics and kerb antimicrobial resistance, the WHO has developed the AWaRe framework, which groups antibiotics under the Access (A), Watch (Wa) and Reserve (Re) categories.7 Access group antibiotics are considered essential for treating common infections and should be widely available e.g. Amoxicillin. By contrast, the Watch and Reserve group antibiotics are recommended only in restricted cases and therefore should be used sparingly when all other antibiotics fail. Similarly, the National List of Essential Medicines (NLEM) specify which antibiotics and antibiotic formulations should be available at different levels of healthcare so as to promote their safe and prudent use.8 The regulatory framework governing sale of drugs through e-pharmacies in India is still evolving as the draft e-pharmacy rules, 2018, have not been implemented yet.1 The mushrooming of e-pharmacies in this evolving regulatory framework could lead to antibiotic overuse and its dire consequences. Data regarding availability of antibiotics for online sale in India and adequacy of information on e-pharmacies concerning key characteristics influencing safe and rational use of antibiotics are lacking.

Materials and methods

This was an exploratory cross-sectional analysis conducted over a period of 3 months. The popular e-pharmacies in India were selected from the top 10 results retrieved through the Google search engine using the keywords ‘buy antibiotics online in India’.9 Availability of antibiotics was assessed by conducting searches on the e-pharmacy websites using generic names of studied antibiotics. A total of 17 antibiotics belonging to each of the WHO-AWaRe categories were selected. WHO-AWaRe categorization represents a validated tool that informs the appropriate use of antibiotics. Further, NLEM, 2022 was used as a tool to select individual antibiotics under different AWaRe categories that are available for use in India.

Three questionnaires, each addressing one specific domain, were developed by the study’s authors, N.K. and J.S., after a thorough review of the published literature to capture relevant information affecting safe and rational use of antibiotics through e-pharmacies.1,10,11,12,13 The ‘e-pharmacy characteristic(s)’ Questionnaire included 18 questions pertaining to safety and authenticity of e-pharmacies. Online pharmacies were expected to be 100% compliant to the studied parameters [1 to 9 and 15 in Table S1 (available as Supplementary data at JAC-AMR Online)] as per the draft e-pharmacy rules, 2018. The ‘Adequacy of antibiotic product information’ Questionnaire consisted of four questions with multiple subparts pertaining to different domains of drug product information. The ‘Consumer awareness information’ Questionnaire contained 12 questions on the information provided by the e-pharmacies to consumers for safe use of antibiotics. All the questions were closed (not open-ended).

Data extraction was done by two study authors, P.K. and J.K., which was further cross-checked by a third author, N.K. Ethical approval was not applicable as the study is based on data available in the public domain. Data were summarized and presented using descriptive statistics.

Results and discussion

Availability of antibiotics online

Antibiotics (n = 17) belonging to all AWaRe categories i.e. Access (83.3%), Watch (88.3%) and Reserve (78%) were available for online sale through e-pharmacies. The higher availability of Watch antibiotics parallels similar trends of availability in community pharmacies.14,15 This increased availability of Watch antibiotics is worrying as it can translate into their upfront use leading to both overuse and misuse. Such irrational use can further promote the emergence of antimicrobial resistance.16

E-pharmacy characteristics

All (10/10) of the studied e-pharmacies adhered to certain criteria as laid out in the draft rules, which boosts public confidence.1 These included disclosure of their location and/or telephone in India, explicit information on the confidentiality of a consumer's personal and financial information and having a grievance redressal policy in place. However, although most of the e-pharmacies were mentioned as being registered under the Company Act of 2013, none of them displayed information regarding registration status with any state board of pharmacy or central licencing authority. Further, wide variation exists among e-pharmacies in information disclosure governing compliance to applicable law(s) such as the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940, Information Technology Act, 2000, Consumer Protection Act, 1986, and Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Act, 1985, which is an area of concern. While the draft rules stipulate sale of drugs must happen under the supervision of a registered pharmacist, only one e-pharmacy had a pharmacist on board and their registration status was unavailable.1

While it was encouraging to note that all e-pharmacies (100%) required prescription as a must for dispensing antibiotics, only 70% provided any information regarding ‘what constitutes a valid prescription?’. None of the e-pharmacies explicitly stated mechanisms to prevent uploading of the same prescription multiple times and to alert the consumers in case of inadvertent placement of multiple or excessive antibiotic orders. This increased access to antibiotics can lead to stockpiling, self-medication and sharing left-over medicine with others. Although these pharmacies made mention of keeping records as per the law, none of them were specific about keeping records of prescribers, patients and drugs dispensed as mandated by law for the sale of schedule H1 drugs. Adequacy of antibiotic product information

Most e-pharmacies displayed details related to pharmaceutical dosage form (90%); indications (70%), adverse drug reactions (ADR) (70%) and contraindications (60%). Only 50% of the websites provided information on drug use for special populations, and 40% included details on expiry date and storage conditions. None of the e-pharmacies enquired after any history of allergy, pregnancy or comorbidities before placement of antibiotic orders. No information on drug disposal was provided. The presence of references to scientific literature pertaining to displayed information helps consumers find unbiased evidence-based information. Only 40% pharmacies provided these references and the date on which the provided information was last updated. Further, none of the e-pharmacies displayed registration/licence status of the authors/reviewers of antibiotic information. While 80% of the websites advocated consultation by physician in case of adverse effects, none provided a pharmacovigilance number to facilitate ADR reporting.

Consumer awareness information

Lack of knowledge and inadequate counselling of patients regarding medicine usage can result in improper usage of medications, especially antibiotics.17 Numerous studies have shown that consumers lack knowledge regarding what are antibiotics, when to take them, when to stop, when not to use antibiotics and so on.18,19,20 Further, consumers tend to store the left-over medications or their empty packs as a guide to later use without a prescription for similar symptoms in future. WHO recommends public education as one of the key interventions to rationalize the use of antibiotics.18 Since e-pharmacies serve as virtual interfaces, we assessed whether the information is provided to consumers on various aspects for promoting appropriate use of antibiotics. For instance, if an antibiotic X is classified under the WHO Watch or Reserve groups, this could alert the consumer to not to use it upfront or to seek professional advice. We noted a wide variation in the amount of consumer awareness information provided by e-pharmacies. For example, only 50% e-pharmacies advised consumers ‘to not to use antibiotics for viral infections such as a sore throat’. Moreover, this information was provided in the FAQ section, which might not be opened by the consumers at all. We therefore advocate for provision of a standard set of consumer awareness information points in an easy-to-understand language in a prominent place that the consumers need to check before they can order an antibiotic. To the best of our knowledge, ours is the first study from India to explore the availability of antibiotics online and compare e-pharmacies on various parameters that have a bearing on the rational use of antibiotics. However, since this was an exploratory pilot study, sample size was limited to only 10 e-pharmacies. Nevertheless, we developed three questionnaires to capture relevant information for safe procurement and use of drugs through e-pharmacies and pilot tested them. Because we did not procure any antibiotics from the e-pharmacies, issues regarding their quality, logistics services and so on cannot be commented on.

Although the Indian drug regulator, the Central Drugs Standard Control Organization, issued draft rules for e-pharmacies in 2018, these have not been implemented yet. Since regulating drugs and medicines is crucial to public health and safety, the drug regulatory authorities should roll out comprehensive norms for the efficient and legitimate functioning of e-pharmacies as soon as possible. Also, consumer education programmes should be developed to promote responsible use of antibiotics through online pharmacies.

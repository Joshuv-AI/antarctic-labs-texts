Using the improved YOLOv11 model to enhance computer vision applications for building crack detection algorithms
Authors: Xiaohu Gao, Chunmei Cao, Xiaojing Yi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
With the acceleration of urbanization, building crack detection has become an important task for ensuring the safety of structures. Traditional detection methods face challenges such as low efficiency and high error rates. Deep learning, particularly YOLO series algorithms, has become an effective technology for addressing these issues. This study introduces an enhanced model derived from the YOLOv11 algorithm, designed to improve both the precision and real-time efficiency of building crack detection. The improved YOLOv11 model introduces innovative designs, such as the C3K2-SG module, FPSConv module, and Inner_MPDIoU loss function, which significantly improve crack feature extraction, fine-grained feature fusion, and small target detection accuracy. The C3K2-SG module improves crack detection in complex backgrounds, the FPSConv module optimizes the detection of cracks at various scales, and the Inner_MPDIoU loss function improves the localization of small crack targets. Experimental results show that the enhanced model reaches a detection accuracy (mAP@0.5) of 88.6%, marking a 4.6% increase over the original YOLOv11. Furthermore, the precision and recall rates increased by 3.5% and 7.6%, respectively. The model also provides a detailed comparative analysis of four typical crack types (vertical cracks, horizontal cracks, multi-level cracks, and complex cracks). Compared to YOLOv11, the improved model demonstrates significant advantages in detecting small targets and complex cracks, with an average improvement of 0.12 in detection accuracy. This model offers an efficient and accurate solution for building crack detection and has broad application prospects, especially in intelligent inspection and building safety monitoring.

Introduction

With the acceleration of global urbanization, the construction industry is booming, and the increase in the number of concrete buildings has been accompanied by a rise in the incidence of engineering accidents. Concrete structures are prone to cracking under the influence of factors such as load and corrosion. These cracks not only affect the aesthetic appearance of the buildings but also severely threaten their load-bearing capacity, durability, stability, and safety. However, the complex orientations and fine features of concrete cracks pose challenges for detection. Traditional manual visual inspection methods suffer from poor safety and high subjectivity, necessitating the development of stable and highly accurate detection methods to enhance the safety of building structures1–3.

Currently, in both practical and simulated tests, existing manual crack recognition technologies based on visual and hand-drawn crack patterns and location information are no longer adequate for actual applications, often leading to omissions and errors4–6. For example, cracks in concrete are often overlooked. In the past, crack detection in buildings in China mainly relied on manual inspection.However, this approach relies significantly on the subjective evaluation of workers, which can result in problems like missed detections, false positives, inefficiency, and incomplete identification of cracks. As a result, current detection methods are not well-suited to address practical requirements7. With the rapid development of digital imaging, computer vision, and data mining technologies, deep neural network-based crack detection has proven to be an effective and widely used method. Finally, after large-scale crack detection and learning, cracks in buildings can be detected more efficiently8.In contrast to conventional manual techniques, crack detection utilizing digital imaging and deep data analysis offers more objective and dependable outcomes, enabling accurate identification and measurement of crack shapes and parameters. This saves a significant amount of labor and resources while also improving work efficiency. Its application in surface crack detection of building components offers significant advantages9,10.

Current crack detection techniques using digital imaging technology can be classified into four primary types: methods for identifying objects using sensor technologies, visual analysis techniques, computational learning strategies, and neural network frameworks.

Sensor-based detection methods rely on the physical performance feedback from sensors to analyze cracks, such as using piezoelectric ceramic transducer patches11, laser displacement sensors12, and fiber optic grating sensors13. However, over time, sensors adhered to the surface of concrete are prone to detachment or deformation, leading to reduced detection cycles and increased costs. Therefore, image processing detection methods were proposed. Image processing detection methods often use morphological operations14, binarization15, and other techniques to detect cracks. Some studies also use edge detection operators such as the Prewitt operator16, Canny operator17, and Laplacian operator18,19 to segment crack images. However, these image processing techniques are sensitive to environmental noise such as lighting and shadows. In practical engineering applications, there is a greater emphasis on combining these methods with machine learning detection methods20, such as histogram + neural networks21, Hough image transform + support vector machines22, and binary image + K-means clustering23. However, these methods require pre-extraction of crack features and cannot achieve end-to-end detection. Additionally, if the extracted crack features are incorrect, the machine learning method will learn erroneous features, ultimately leading to incorrect crack detection.

In recent years, several researchers have leveraged deep learning techniques for concrete crack detection. Object recognition techniques driven by deep learning are mainly classified into two-step and single-step approaches. Two-stage algorithms, such as R-CNN24 and Faster R-CNN25, split the detection process into two phases: generating region proposals and performing classification. For instance, Young-Jin et al.26,27 applied Faster R-CNN for training and detecting concrete crack images, while Lu Deng et al.28 introduced a crack detector based on R-CNN. On the other hand, one-stage algorithms, like the SSD29 and YOLO30 series, streamline the detection process by directly identifying objects in images, leading to faster detection speeds. For example, Maeda et al.21 developed a crack detection algorithm using the SSD network, successfully identifying eight types of cracks from a large dataset of road damage images with high precision. Majidifard et al.31 proposed the YOLO model and validated its target localization capabilities using a dataset of pavement images. Later, Mandal et al.32 introduced the YOLOv2 model, which enhanced the recognition of multiple crack types, improving both computational efficiency and accuracy, and was particularly effective in detecting and locating alligator cracks in pavement images. LLi et al.33 used YOLOv3 to detect surface cracks in sluice gates. YOLO series algorithms, with their faster detection speeds and accuracy, better meet the needs of real-world engineering applications. With continuous optimization of the algorithms, YOLOv434 achieved a balance between speed and accuracy, but issues such as poor localization accuracy and suboptimal small target detection still remain.

Through comprehensive analysis, it can be observed that although the current deep learning algorithms in road crack detection have made some progress, there are still several shortcomings. These can be summarized as follows: low model detection accuracy, misdetection and missed detection of small targets, and poor generalization ability in complex scenarios. To address these issues, improvements were made based on the YOLOv11 framework. YOLOv11 algorithm

YOLOv11 (You Only Look Once version 11) is the latest real-time object detector that redefines possibilities with faster detection speed, higher accuracy, and efficiency35,36. It has gained significant attention in various object detection fields, such as industry, healthcare, and security, making it suitable for building crack detection as a benchmark algorithm. YOLOv11, the latest version in the YOLO series, introduces several architectural advancements while retaining its efficient real-time detection capabilities. These improvements aim to enhance detection accuracy and model flexibility. One such innovation is the inclusion of the C3K2 module in the backbone network, which strengthens feature extraction by employing convolution kernels of different sizes (e.g., 3 × 3 and 5 × 5) along with a channel separation technique. This approach allows the model to more effectively handle complex characteristics and boosts its ability to detect objects across various scales. Furthermore, YOLOv11 integrates the SPPF module, an enhanced variant of the traditional Spatial Pyramid Pooling (SPP) module. SPPF enhances the model’s capability to identify objects across various sizes by capturing spatial information at multiple levels, ensuring high-precision detection across images with targets of diverse sizes. Furthermore, the C2PSA module combines channel and spatial focus mechanisms, enhancing the expressive capacity of the feature map. By adding parallel spatial attention within the feature maps, the C2PSA block helps the model focus more on critical regions, thereby enhancing detection accuracy in complex environments. Improved YOLOv11 model

To address issues such as feature information loss, low detection accuracy for small target cracks, and frequent false negatives and false positives during the building crack detection process37, three improvements were designed based on the YOLOv11 framework to enhance the performance of building crack detection. First, the C3K2-SG module is introduced into the Backbone network. This module utilizes a point-movement mechanism to achieve effective and flexible feature extraction, enabling precise identification of target defects in complex backgrounds. Secondly, to capture more detailed features and minimize model complexity, the FPSConv module is introduced as a replacement for the SPPF module. Unlike the traditional pooling operation in SPPF, convolution operations are more effective at preserving fine details in intricate images. Lastly, the Inner_MPDIoU loss function is developed, concentrating on various regression samples to improve the model’s ability to identify targets. C3K2-SG module

Due to the difficulty of distinguishing slender crack targets from complex backgrounds and the potential blurring caused by camera shake during inspection, these issues pose significant challenges to structural safety detection38. Moreover, as the network depth increases, repeated convolutions often generate redundant and irrelevant information, which easily suppresses the fine-grained features of small cracks. To address these issues, the C3K2-SG module was designed. By integrating the Self-moving Points Convolution (SMPConv)39 with the Convolutional Gated Linear Unit (CGLU)40, the module allows weight parameters to move adaptively and selectively pass through information channels, thereby filtering redundant responses and focusing on informative crack-related features. FPSConv module

YOLOv11 retains the previous SPPF41 module. To address the issue that pooling operations in SPPF may lose some detailed features, this paper focuses on this problem and designs the FPSConv module. The original pooling operation in SPPF is replaced with a shared convolution42 operation. This design significantly enhances the model’s feature extraction capability while reducing storage and computational overhead. Compared to using independent convolution layers, shared convolution layers save space and improve computational efficiency. The parameter sharing in convolution layers is also known as weight sharing43. As shown in Fig. 5, the convolution operation involves sliding a 3\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\times$$\end{document}3 convolution kernel over a 6\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\times$$\end{document}6 input image, performing a weighted sum operation on each pixel and its neighboring pixels at each sliding position. The resulting computed value, F1, represents the pixel in the convolved image at that position. Since the convolution kernel’s parameters remain unchanged, the same convolution kernel is used to convolve different positions of the input data, and the same set of weights is applied multiple times during the dot product operation. This greatly reduces the model’s parameter count and enhances its capacity for generalization.Fig. 5Shared convolution schematic.

Shared convolution schematic.

To enhance accuracy in building crack detection using the improved model, this paper substitutes the three pooling layers in the original SPPF module with three shared convolutions, referred to as FPSConv, to capture more detailed information from the feature maps. The three shared convolutions are set with different dilation factors of 1, 3, and 5, respectively. This allows for the extraction of features at different scales, where low dilation factors capture local details, and high dilation factors capture contextual global information, corresponding to the multi-scale feature fusion capability of the pooling operation. Additionally, the design retains two 1\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\times$$\end{document}1 convolution layers at the front and back, which efficiently integrate the number of channels and perform feature fusion operations44. This approach not only minimizes the number of parameters but also enables the convolution operations to capture more detailed features during the feature extraction process. In practical inspection scenarios, the FPSConv module can more quickly and accurately identify tiny cracks on buildings. Inner MPDIoU loss function

In the YOLOv11 model, the CIoU loss function is used as the primary regression loss function. The computation process is illustrated in Eq. (1).1\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \text {CIoU} = d + \alpha \cdot v + (1 - \text {IoU}) \end{aligned}$$\end{document}In this equation, d denotes the distance between the centers of the predicted box and the ground truth box, c represents the length of the diagonal of their smallest enclosing rectangle, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha$$\end{document} and v are the aspect ratio parameters. The CIoU loss function considers multiple factors such as the overlap between bounding boxes, the distance between their centers, and the aspect ratio to optimize the prediction results. However, in the case of small and irregular cracks, such as those found in building structures, the CIoU loss function struggles due to its limited ability to capture directional information and issues with accurately computing the overlap, especially when cracks have non-rectangular or fine shapes.

To address this, we propose the Inner MPDIoU loss function, inspired by the InnerIoU loss function45, which introduces a scale factor to dynamically adjust the supplementary bounding box during the loss computation. This adaptation allows the model to more precisely capture the true bounding box of small cracks, especially those with irregular shapes, by better aligning with the actual features of the cracks. This approach enhances the model’s generalization capabilities and accelerates its convergence during training.

The MPDIoU loss function46 calculates the similarity between bounding boxes using the minimum point distance, incorporating three primary components: the overlap or non-overlap of regions, the distance between their centers, and the variations in size and proportions. To handle small and irregular cracks, the loss function dynamically adjusts the bounding box size based on the target’s characteristics, reducing the risk of inaccurate localization for small cracks. The procedure begins by calculating the ratio of overlap to union between the predicted and ground truth boxes, then normalizing the distance between their top-left and bottom-right corners to derive the final result. Dataset

The crack image dataset used by the single model is from the Middle East Technical University (METU) in Turkey, consisting of concrete crack images for classification47,48 (https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/5y9wdsg2zt/2). While there are some surface differences in the base images, such as in the categories of plaster and painted surfaces, the overall semantic background is consistent.The images were captured under consistent lighting conditions on the same day, taken from the walls and floors of multiple concrete buildings across the METU campus. The photos were shot with a camera positioned approximately 1 meter from the target, facing it directly. A total of 450 high-resolution images (4032\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\times$$\end{document}3024 pixels) were selected and processed to create the crack image dataset used in this chapter. The dataset was split into three subsets: 16,000 images for training, 2000 for validation, and 2000 for testing, maintaining a distribution ratio of 8:1:1.

Related experiments of the Inner_MPDIoU loss function

The Inner_MPDIoU loss function combines the principles of InnerIoU and MPDIoU, providing a significant improvement in the detection accuracy of small cracks. Its novelty is in the incorporation of a system that dynamically modifies the dimensions of the auxiliary bounding boxes, allowing for flexible resizing based on the changing scales of the targets. This approach ensures a more accurate fit to the actual target bounding box, enhancing the model’s generalization capacity and accelerating its convergence. Additionally, the MPDIoU loss function incorporates three key components: the overlap and non-overlap regions, the separation between the central points, and the variations in width and height, which enhance both the accuracy and resilience of the model across different situations.

During the experiment, the Inner_MPDIoU loss function was compared with other loss functions, with performance evaluated using the mAP_0.5 and mAP_0.5:0.95 metrics. As shown in Figs. 7 and 8, the improved loss function gradually converged after 150 training epochs, reaching a higher mAP value, which demonstrates the clear advantages of the Inner_MPDIoU loss function compared to traditional loss functions. Particularly in the detection of complex cracks, the improved loss function exhibited superior robustness and accuracy, effectively reducing false positives and false negatives.

To summarize, the development of the Inner_MPDIoU loss function significantly enhanced both the accuracy and stability of the building crack detection model, while also boosting its training efficiency. Conclusion

This research presents an upgraded YOLOv11 model that integrates the C3K2-SG module, FPSConv module, and Inner_MPDIoU loss function. These novel improvements enhance the model’s capacity to capture characteristics, optimize the fusion of multi-scale information, and increase the accuracy in detecting small cracks. Furthermore, the model maintains a high frame rate, ensuring its real-time applicability in building crack detection tasks. These advancements provide a more efficient, precise, and robust solution for detecting building cracks, particularly in complex environments and for identifying various types of cracks, outperforming the original YOLOv11 model.

To evaluate the impact of the suggested enhancements, ablation experiments were performed, and the impact of each modified module was thoroughly analyzed. The experimental results indicated that the C3K2-SG module significantly boosted both detection accuracy and recall. Specifically, by adding the C3K2-SG module to the baseline YOLOv11 model, accuracy increased from 90.9 to 91.7%, and recall improved from 77.0 to 80.5%. The introduction of the FPSConv module further enhanced detection speed and accuracy, with the mAP@0.5 value risin g from 84.0 to 85.2%. Lastly, the inclusion of the Inner_MPDIoU loss function significantly enhanced the model’s capacity to detect small cracks, achieving an mAP@0.5 of 88.6%, a 4.6% improvement over the baseline. These findings indicate that the integration of the C3K2-SG module, FPSConv module, and Inner_MPDIoU loss function notably optimized both the detection performance and processing speed, validating the effectiveness of the suggested method.

To further confirm the superiority of the enhanced YOLOv11 model, comparisons were made with existing state-of-the-art object detection algorithms. The experimental results showed that YOLOv11 outperformed other popular algorithms, such as YOLOv5, YOLOv7, and YOLOv8, across various evaluation metrics. YOLOv11 achieved a detection precision (P) of 88.9%, recall (R) of 67.0%, and an mAP@0.5 of 74.0%. In contrast, YOLOv5 had an mAP@0.5 of 73.6%, YOLOv7 reached 73.1%, and YOLOv8 obtained 74.4%. Although YOLOv8 showed a marginally higher mAP, its detection speed was slower.

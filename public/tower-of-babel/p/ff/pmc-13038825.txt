Identification of potential clinical markers associated with prognosis of neuroblastoma
Authors: Fengming Ji, Jianjun Zhu, Haoyu Tang, Chenghao Zhanghuang, JinRong Li, Yu Hang, Bing Yan, Zhen Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimThis study retrospectively analyzed the data of patients with neuroblastoma (NB) to explore the risk factors and independent risk factors affecting the prognosis of patients.MethodA retrospective analysis was conducted on the data of patients with NB diagnosed and treated at Children’s Hospital of Kunming Medical University from January 2014 to December 2024. Kaplan–Meier (KM) survival analysis and the Cox Proportional Hazards Model were used to ascertain the risk factors and to identify independent risk factors associated with overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS), respectively.ResultA total of 231 patients were retrospectively analyzed. The results of Cox Proportional Hazards Model showed that age, tumor volume of onset (TVO), LNF (lactate dehydrogenase-neuron specific enolase-ferritin), metastasis site, DNA ploidy, and recurrence were independent risk factors affecting the OS. TVO, lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), neuron specific enolase (NSE), ferritin, LNF, image-defined risk factors (IDRFs), metastasis site, and DNA ploidy were independent risk factors affecting the DFS of patients.ConclusionReliable biomarkers are crucial for effective patient stratification and personalized treatment strategies for NB. Early identification of high-risk patients and the initiation of aggressive treatment can help improve OS and DFS in patients.Keywords: Neuroblastoma, Pediatrics, Risk factor, Overall survival, Disease-free survival

Background

Neuroblastoma (NB) is a biologically heterogeneous embryonic malignant tumor that originates from neural tissue. It is the most common extracranial solid tumor in children. Predominantly affecting children under the age of five, it accounts for approximately 15% of childhood cancer-related mortality [1, 2]. NB originates from cells of the sympathetic nervous system, predominantly within the adrenal medulla or along the sympathetic nerve chain. Its origins are commonly traced to the adrenal glands, paraspinal regions, chest, and pelvis [3, 4]. NB shows diversity in clinical manifestations, tumor characteristics, treatment responses and prognosis [5]. The 5-year survival rate of low-risk patients is 85%, while for high-risk patients, even after comprehensive treatments, the 5-year survival rate of patients is still less than 50%, which brings huge challenges to the treatment of NB [6].

Tumor markers support early detection and diagnosis, dynamically evaluate therapeutic efficacy to direct treatment, and predict recurrence and metastasis 3–6 months in advance [7]. Accurate diagnosis and staging of NB rely on a variety of tools: imaging, histological assessment of the primary tumor and metastatic lesions, bone marrow (BM) biopsy, and detection of certain biomarkers [8]. Reliable biomarkers will help classify patients into different risk categories, identify high-risk patients at an early stage, and guide physicians to tailor personalized treatment plans for patients based on the severity of the disease. By dynamically monitoring the levels of biomarkers, it is possible to assess treatment efficacy in real-time, determine the degree of tumor remission, detect tumor recurrence or drug resistance, and facilitate personalized treatment adjustments [9]. In this study, by analyzing the impact of clinicopathological factors on overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS), we aim to emphasize their importance in refining risk stratification schemes and ultimately improving patient outcomes.

Materials and methods

Patients

Clinical data of all children diagnosed and treated for NB at the Children’s Hospital of Kunming Medical University from January 2014 to December 2024 were collected. All patients were closely followed up, with the follow-up deadline being March 31, 2025. The study followed the Declaration of Helsinki guidelines and the research protocol was approved by the ethics committee of the Children’s Hospital of Kunming Medical University.

Inclusion criteria and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria: (1) all diagnostic and therapeutic interventions for patients were conducted at the Children’s Hospital of Kunming Medical University. (2) The clinical records of the patients are comprehensive. (3) Patients with disease recurrence received ongoing subsequent treatments.

Exclusion criteria: (1) Preoperative chemotherapy, surgery, or postoperative chemotherapy was not administered at Children’s Hospital of Kunming Medical University. (2) The patient voluntarily discontinued treatment during the therapeutic process. (3) The cause of death for deceased patients was unrelated to NB.

LNF score calculation rule

The LNF score is a composite prognostic assessment system based on serological tumor burden markers (lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) - neuron specific enolase (NSE) -ferritin) that reflects systemic tumor metabolic activity and systemic inflammatory response status. The optimal cut-off values for LDH, NSE, and ferritin were individually determined using the maximum youden index. Each indicator exceeding its corresponding cut-off value was assigned 1 point (otherwise 0 points), yielding a total score ranging from 0 to 3; higher scores indicate a more significant tumor burden and systemic inflammatory response.

Statistical variable

Statistical variables include the following contents: age, gender, primary site, tumor volume of onset (TVO), LDH, NSE, ferritin, LNF, IDRFs (image-defined risk factors), recurrence, INSS (International Neuroblastoma Staging System) stage, risk classification, pathological type, paired-like homeobox 2B (PHOX2B), MYCN amplication, aberration of 11q, DNA ploidy, metastasis site. TVO was calculated based on contrast-enhanced CT scans obtained at initial hospital admission, using the formula: TVO = length × width × height (cm³). Serum levels of LDH, NSE and ferritin were measured from the first laboratory tests performed prior to any therapeutic intervention.

Statistical analysis

This study conducted data analysis using 22.0 SPSS statistical software. Kaplan–Meier survival analysis was conducted to identify the risk factors that affect OS and DFS. The risk factors affecting the prognosis of NB patients were screened for Cox proportional hazards model analysis, and finally the independent risk factors affecting the prognosis of NB patients were determined. The optimal cut-off values of TVO, LDH, NSE, and ferritin for OS and DFS were determined by the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve. A P value < 0.05 indicates a statistically significant difference.

Patients

A total of 231 patients were included in this study, including 134 males and 97 females, with an average age of 36.46 ± 28.30 months. The INSS stage II: III: IV was 21:79:58:75. During the follow-up period, a total of 41 patients died and 43 patients experienced tumor recurrence. The OS rates of 231 patients at 1 year, 3 years and 5 years after surgery were 98.8%, 89.8% and 80.7% respectively, and the DFS rates at 1 year, 3 years and 5 years were 98.3%, 85.2% and 78.5% respectively.

Discussion

NB is a highly heterogeneous disease, ranging from spontaneous regression to aggressive progression. This heterogeneity is primarily reflected in two aspects: intertumoral heterogeneity and intratumoral heterogeneity. Intertumoral heterogeneity arises from substantial differences in tumor histology. The histology and differentiation status of NB are defined by the relative proportions of neuroblasts/ganglion cells and reactive stromal schwann cells [10]. Intratumoral heterogeneity is characterized by the presence of multiple phenotypes in tumor cells, including adrenergic phenotypes, mesenchymal phenotypes, as well as different states of differentiation and the presence of infiltrating stromal cells and immune cells [11, 12].

MYCN amplification, aberration of 11q, diploid DNA status, and segmental gains of 17q are all common chromosomal instability events in NB. These molecular aberrations contribute to the high degree of genetic, cellular state, and developmental stage heterogeneity in NB [13]. This complexity may originate in early embryogenesis and gradually evolve with the developmental process, serving as an important characteristic of the tumor’s high invasiveness and poor prognosis [14–16]. Although this study found that MYCN amplification, 11q deletion, and DNA ploidy were all significantly associated with poorer OS and DFS, the final multivariate analysis only confirmed diploid DNA as an independent prognostic factor. This discrepancy largely reflects an important challenge in the current practice of precision oncology: the economic accessibility of genetic testing. The development of high-throughput sequencing technology has led to the discovery of numerous new genomic and molecular biomarkers, providing new insights into the diagnosis and treatment of NB. However, techniques for detecting specific gene mutations and chromosome segment changes remain costly and technically challenging [17]. Moreover, molecular marker testing usually requires a relatively long testing period, whereas clinical pathological features can be rapidly obtained after surgery or biopsy, helping doctors to promptly formulate surgical, radiotherapy, or chemotherapy plans. Therefore, it is of great practical significance to develop and refine a staging system based on clinicopathological features.

The INSS and the International Neuroblastoma Risk Group Staging System (INRGSS) are staging systems for NB based on clinical, radiological, and surgical assessments [18]. The INSS is a postoperative classification framework that primarily ascertains the stage of localized and regional neuroblastoma tumors, evaluating the tumor’s relation to midline structures, lymph node involvement, and the extent of the initial surgical excision [19, 20]. INRGSS introduced in 2005, stratifies neuroblastoma tumors on the basis of International Neuroblastoma Risk Group definition (INRG) criteria and the presence of metastatic disease, independently of surgical resection extent. Presently, INRGSS enjoys widespread adoption among cooperative research groups in North America and Europe [20, 21]. The INRGSS staging system, which does not rely on the extent of surgical resection, allows for preoperative assessment and quantification of disease risk, thereby providing a more timely basis for the selection of treatment plans [22, 23]. This study found that IDRFs were significantly associated with DFS in NB patients (P = 0.004). This result supports the advantage of the INRGSS staging system in preoperative risk assessment and provides a basis for neoadjuvant chemotherapy or multidisciplinary surgical strategies for patients with positive IDRFs. Future work should combine dynamic imaging and molecular markers to further refine the risk stratification model for IDRFs.

Prognosis assessment in neuroblastoma is multidimensional and complex, influenced by the tumor’s biological characteristics and the host’s response to the disease. Beyond histological indicators such as MYCN amplification and DNA aneuploidy, serum and urine biomarkers—characterized by their non-invasive nature, reproducibility, and cost-effectiveness—serve as powerful adjuncts for risk stratification and treatment efficacy monitoring in neuroblastoma. The INSS advocates for the inclusion of urinary catecholamine metabolites (VMA/HVA) in routine neuroblastoma diagnostic criteria; given their marked elevation in over 90% of cases, their quantification is a pivotal tool for timely clinical detection of the disease [18]. However, individual levels of VMA or HVA have not shown independent prognostic value across the entire patient population [24]. LDH, NSE, and ferritin, which are indicative of neuroblastoma tumor burden, can be influenced by cellular lysis, specific secretion, or metabolic requirements. These biomarkers are particularly elevated in cases of metastatic spread or vigorous proliferation [25]. LDH is a critical metabolic enzyme within the tumor microenvironment, where it modulates lactate concentrations within neoplastic cells, thereby facilitating immune evasion and angiogenesis. Consequently, LDH affects the activities of immune cells and the functions of stromal cells in the vicinity [26]. NSE is a significant biomarker for neuroblastoma. It plays a role in the glycolytic pathway and is predominantly present in neural and neuroendocrine tissues. Elevated levels of NSE in disease staging are frequently associated with advanced disease stages, including increased tumor size, distant metastasis, and bone marrow involvement [7, 27]. Ferritin, predominantly secreted by macrophages, may be abnormally synthesized by NB cells. Acting as an iron storage protein, ferritin is implicated in tumor immune suppression, angiogenesis, and iron-independent cell proliferation. Studies have consistently demonstrated that elevated serum ferritin levels at diagnosis correlate with advanced disease stages in NB [28]. In the study by Zhang et al. [29], ferritin was identified as the sole independent risk factor affecting the overall survival of NB patients. In a cohort study involving 1,053 NB patients conducted by Morgenstern et al. [30], serum LDH levels greater than 1,250 U/L were significantly associated with “ultra-high risk” disease progression. Yang et al. [31] showed that in neuroblastoma patients with metastatic disease (INRG stage M/MS), levels of LDH and ferritin were markedly elevated compared to patients without metastasis. However, prior studies predominantly investigated single factors or, at most, combinations of two factors without establishing consistent thresholds. For the first time, we integrated LDH, NSE, and ferritin into the 0–3 point LNF scoring system and validated, using a multivariate Cox model, that this integration significantly influences the overall survival rate of neuroblastoma patients, irrespective of INSS stage, MYCN status, or DNA ploidy. This study introduces the LNF score, which integrates data from three distinct biological pathways—including cellular destruction, specific secretion, and metabolism/inflammation—to address the underrepresentation of biological heterogeneity inherent in singular biomarkers. Multivariate analysis corroborates that the LNF score is a prognostic factor, independent of INSS stage, MYCN status, and age, with risk escalating proportionally to the score. The INSS relies on postoperative pathologic outcomes and lymph node dissection findings and is thus applicable to patients who have undergone surgery. Conversely, the INRGSS requires intraoperative details and high-quality radiological images. In contrast, the LNF enables risk re-stratification within 24 h post initial diagnosis utilizing only standard laboratory tests, which is indispensable for primary healthcare facilities with limited resources. Given the convenience and cost-effectiveness of testing for these three markers, the LNF score holds promise as a rapid prognostic tool in primary care settings. Its clinical application can be further optimized through ongoing monitoring and multicenter validation studies in the future.

TVO is a macroscopic measure that reflects tumor burden. In NB, an increase in TVO is often associated with higher MYCN copy numbers, elevated Ki-67 indices, and enhanced angiogenesis levels, pointing to a more aggressive biological phenotype. Furthermore, larger tumors are more likely to infiltrate critical blood vessels and the spinal canal, resulting in reduced surgical resection rates and increased chemoresistance. Consequently, TVO can potentially integrate both biological aggressiveness and anatomical resectability, augmenting traditional staging systems and offering enhanced guidance for clinical decision-making processes [32, 33]. The study by Angeles et al. [34] indicated that a reduction rate of tumor size greater than 60% after radiotherapy for cervical cancer is associated with a favorable prognosis. Zhang et al. ‘s [35] research also pointed out that adjusting the dose of cisplatin based on tumor volume changes is feasible and effective for the radical treatment of advanced cervical cancer in elderly patients. In this study, TVO significantly impacted both OS and DFS. Although believed meaningful for NB treatment, the analysis of TVO changes has been limited to survival analysis due to data constraints, focusing on the relationship between tumor volume and NB prognosis.

Limitations of this study include the following aspects: First, this study employs a single-center retrospective analysis approach, with a sample confined to patients from a single tertiary hospital, which may lead to selection bias and information bias. Moreover, due to the retrospective nature of the study design, confounding factors could influence the outcomes, potentially over or underestimating the effects of predictive factors. Additionally, the survival prediction model in this research was only evaluated through internal validation and did not leverage an independent external dataset to verify its generalizability. Therefore, caution is necessary when interpreting the model’s applicability across different regions, ethnicities, or healthcare systems, especially the potential impacts of varying treatment strategies for neuroblastoma on predictive outcomes. Furthermore, the study spans a lengthy period, during which there have been significant changes in the diagnosis and treatment protocols for neuroblastoma, potentially causing temporal bias. Despite adjustments made using a multivariable Cox regression model for the year of diagnosis, the impact of therapeutic advancements on survival outcomes cannot be entirely ruled out.

Conclusion

NB exhibits substantial tumor heterogeneity, highlighting the critical need for the identification of diagnostic biomarkers. These biomarkers facilitate effective patient stratification and enable the development of personalized treatment strategies. By pinpointing specific biomarkers, patient stratification can be pursued with greater precision. In cases of high-risk patients, aggressive combination therapies become a viable option. Conversely, for low-risk patients, the risk of overtreatment is mitigated, thereby diminishing treatment-related side effects, enhancing survival rates, and improving quality of life. Furthermore, biomarker identification aids in tracking tumor recurrence and drug resistance, enabling timely adjustments to treatment regimens and bolstering therapeutic efficacy. Our findings are anticipated to augment the IINRG classification framework, refine risk stratification for patients with neuroblastoma, and inform personalized treatment decisions.

SVRS: self-supervised 3D voxel reconstruction network from stereo vision
Authors: Zhengyang Zou, Yunxia Wu, Hailan Zhang, Qian Xu, Ruifeng Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThree-dimensional voxel reconstruction based on stereo vision is essential for environmental perception in autonomous robots. Existing pseudo-LiDAR methods recover voxel grids by estimating depth maps and projecting them pixel by pixel, leading to high computational cost and boundary over-smoothing. To overcome these issues, we model the inverse relationship between 2D pixels and 3D voxel grids and propose a Self-supervised 3D Voxel Reconstruction network from Stereo vision (SVRS). Specifically, we represent a given 3D scene as multi-scale uniform cubic voxel grids and introduce a novel Pixel-Voxel Projecting Module (PVPM). PVPM projects the 3D position of each voxel grid into index coordinates, which establishes implicit stereo–voxel correspondences and converts dense pixel features into sparse voxel representations. Furthermore, we explore an Octree-based Encoder-Decoder Architecture (OEDA) to reconstruct multi-scale voxel grids via hierarchical spatial partitioning, avoiding the influence of dense empty grids on sparse occupied grids via a coarse-to-fine manner. Finally, SVRS leverages off-the-shelf stereo matching methods within a self-supervised training framework. Experiments on the DrivingStereo dataset show that SVRS achieves competitive reconstruction accuracy while improving inference speed by up to 14 over advanced pseudo-LiDAR approaches and 3 over real-time approaches.Keywords: Stereo vision, Environmental perception, Voxel reconstruction, Octree architectureSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Stereo-based 3D scene reconstruction and completion have attracted considerable attention due to their promising applications in various fields, such as service robots, autonomous driving, and virtual reality1,2. As an important branch of stereo-based 3D perception, 3D voxel reconstruction partitions objects and surfaces into uniform binary voxel grids and demonstrates promising performance. However, the high-quality voxel reconstruction of a full 3D scene remains a challenging task due to the noisy, high computational demands and partial geometric data.

In the early studies, stereo-based 3D voxel reconstruction frameworks can be divided into three stages: estimate disparity from rectified images with stereo matching methods, pseudo-LiDAR3,4 generation using calibration parameters and 3D spatial partitioning5,6. Compared with other processes, stereo matching is the key content to solve 3D voxel reconstruction.

Traditional stereo matching algorithms usually rely on manual feature extraction methods7, combined with global optimization constraints such as smoothness, occlusion relationships, and left-right consistency to predict disparity. However, these manually modeled pixel features have limitations when dealing with low-texture areas, often resulting in low disparity prediction accuracy. Recently, benefiting from the development of the cost volume concept and the improvement of learning ability, deep stereo matching methods have made significant progress in terms of accuracy8–10, efficiency11–13, and robustness. Therefore, existing stereo matching methods have been able to provide an accurate database for depth estimation and 3D spatial partitioning.

Nevertheless, the 3D voxel reconstruction from stereo images is still limited by the following issues. Firstly, stereo matching estimates coordinate differences of corresponding points, which inevitably introduces over-smoothed boundaries because of smoothing supervision. These over-smoothed points will generate artifacts when projected into 3D space, thereby causing false positive problems14. Secondly, although the existing methods have met the real-time requirements in the depth estimation stage, they still need to project the depth map pixel by pixel into voxel grids, which also results in a consumption problem. Moreover, down-sampling the depth map and projecting low-resolution depth into 3D space is a trade-off solution, but it may lead to the omission of small target detection.

Most recently, BEVFormer maps multi-view 2D image features into a unified bird’s-eye view (BEV) spatial representation15,16, providing more intuitive and efficient environmental perception capabilities. However, these methods are extremely vulnerable for height dimension. To address this limitation, TPVFormer17 expands the BEV to top-down, front-view, and side-view perspectives, using a tri-perspective view (TPV) to represent the 3D scene; VoxFormer18 further improves the perception capabilities of autonomous driving by dividing the scene perception into two stages: voxelized representation and 3D semantic scene completion. Both of these methods are still based on the projection of 2D features onto a 3D environment and are limited by the high requirements of the Transformer models, thus still facing significant challenges in practical applications. In other research directions, Knyaz et al.19 presents a single-view voxel model prediction method that uses generative adversarial networks. And StereoVoxelNet20 employs a deep neural network to speed up efficiency but is only used for the obstacle occupancy detection task.

To address the aforementioned limitations, this paper considers leveraging the stereo geometric principles and the stereo camera calibration parameters and further learning the implicit mapping between individual 3D voxel grids and dense 2D disparity maps. This idea adopts geometric prior constraints to calculate the space and replaces explicit traversal with implicit mapping, fundamentally reducing unnecessary computations and redundant operations. Furthermore, LiDAR has difficulty collecting dense 3D grid voxel label on a large scale. Therefore, in this paper, we consider using pseudo-LiDAR labels for auxiliary training.

Specifically, we propose a novel method for Self-supervised learning 3D voxel reconstruction network from stereo vision (SVRS). SVRS end-to-end predicts 3D voxel reconstruction within the preset range of stereo images, aiming to enhance the computational efficiency from 2D images to 3D environments, as shown in Fig. 1. Firstly, we propose Pixel-Voxel Projecting Module (PVPM) that leverages 3D positional information of each 3D voxel grid and stereo projection to index stereo correspondence information, further providing implicit representation of 3D voxel reconstruction. PVPM is the key module to map individual 3D voxel grids and dense 2D disparity, which eliminates the inefficiencies associated with redundant pixel-wise processing inherent in existing methods8,12,21, significantly reduces the computational burden of the model, and mitigates the issue of false positives caused by excessive boundary smoothing.

Overview of our proposed SVRS. We leverage off-the-shelf stereo pipeline to supervise the cost volume and the 3D voxel reconstruction predictions of our methods. PVPM and OEDA are the key components of SVRS and are used for converting 2D stereo features into 3D voxel grids.

Furthermore, we explore a fully 2D CNNs Octree-based Encoder-Decoder Architecture (OEDA) to reconstruct 3D voxel grids from implicit representation of PVPM. OEDA employs hierarchical spatial partitioning, effectively avoiding the interference of empty regions on sparse predictions, and enables progressive multi-scale prediction from coarse to fine.

In our experiments, we employ DrivingStereo which provides LiDAR Ground Truth and stereo calibration files to evaluate the proposed SVRS. Extensive experiments demonstrate that, compared with conventional methods, SVRS achieves performance comparable to state-of-the-art methods while maintaining an inference time of only 60 ms. Moreover, it effectively reduces the false positive problem and significantly enhances the practicality of stereo vision for mobile robotics applications.

Related works

Camera based 3D reconstruction

3D reconstruction from images is a fundamental challenge within the domains of 3D vision, aiming to predict 3D geometry of objects or scenes from single or multiple 2D images. Traditional computer vision methods resolve this challenge by shape-from-shading22, structure-from-motion23, or structure from stereo7. In recent years, deep-learning-based computer vision methods such as multi-view stereo (MVS)24,25, Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF)26,27 and Gaussian Splatting28–30 have made impressive progress with the breakthroughs in artificial intelligence related technology. More recently, voxel-based 3D scene semantic completion (SSC) methods18,31,32 have also started to receive attention after the release of SemanticKITTI33 dataset, and are gradually developing into the mainstream methods for reconstructing the surrounding environment of autonomous driving. To be specific, this approach estimates whether each voxel is occupied and assigns semantic information. MonoScene34 is the pioneering work that estimate both geometry and semantics of scene from a single-view RGB image. However, this method lifts 2D semantic information to incorrect 3D space due to the absence of reliable depth guidance. VoxFormer18 and Xiao et.al32 introduce the category-agnostic query proposal and category-aware sparse to-dense generation. In these methods, they adopt MobileStereoNet35 or DiffusionDepth36 to provide depth prior from stereo images or single image. Although these methods achieve SOTA performance, they do not learn the 3D occupancy from images end-to-end and produce inadmissible latency when back-projecting depth to 3D space. OccDepth37 and StereoScene38 introduce stereo soft feature assignment module or improve the cost volume module of stereo matching to learn 3D occupancy prediction end-to-end. However, these two methods are still inherited from the dense cost volume3,4 module and only use a single-dimensional U-shaped structure for reconstruction and refinement.

Stereo matching

Traditional stereo matching methods rely on local information for establishing image relationships7,39, thereby exhibiting limited effectiveness in low-texture regions. With the advances of deep convolutional neural networks, the quality of depth predicted from stereo images has steadily improved and has led to a remark able improvement in downstream 3D vision applications such as object detection, surface reconstruction and semantic scene completion. MCCNN40 replaces the hand-craft feature representation with CNN, which combined deep learning with stereo matching methods for the first time. However, this type of methods that replace the traditional pipeline with learning methods cannot take advantage on large-scale datasets. Therefore, DispNetC21 introduce cost volume to establish the feature information interaction of stereo vision. GC-Net41 is the first to propose 3D CNNs based stereo depth prediction framework, where potential stereo corresponding 2D features are mapped to a 3D cost volume through a concatenation operation. The promise of this method inspired GwcNet9 proposes group-wise correlation and MobileStereoNet35 proposes lightweight aggregation method to improve feature similarity measurement, which are significant for our proposed GVAC. In the exploration of real-time, AAnet11 proposes adaptive aggregation to accelerate the similarity aggregation of the cost volume. BGNet42 design a novel edge-preserving cost volume upsampling module based on the learned bilateral grid, which operates time-consuming modules at low dimensions. More recently, 2D-CNN-based methods such as Raft-Stereo12 and IGEVStereo43 utilize correlation to produce matching cost volume which is subsequently optimized through ConvGRU44 refiner for depth prediction. Even though the state-of-the-art stereo matching models achieving high accuracy in disparity estimation (2D), but the operation that back-project disparity to 3D space will generate artifacts which increase the false positive rate of 3D occupancy prediction. In addition, the real-time disparity estimation models still produce inadmissible latency when back-projecting to 3D space, not to name the sacrifice in prediction accuracy. In this paper, our method is inspired from the cost volume of stereo matching and presents a novel 3D voxel reconstruction framework that employs deep neural network to output sparse voxel grids from stereo images directly.

Method

Overview

In this section, we present SVRS to directly outputs binary 3D voxel reconstruction from stereo images. Target at achieving these contents, a Mobilenet-like45,46 feature extraction network is first designed to reduce memory consumption. Then, the PVPM is proposed to establish the connection between each voxel grids and the stereo feature map. Subsequently, OEDA is introduced for progressive multi-scale predictions. For supervision stage, we use off-the-shelf stereo matching methods to predict disparity, where the estimated disparity is used to supervise the PVPM and is then projected to the 3D space for generalizing 3D voxel reconstruction pseudo ground truth.

Feature extraction

As a pioneer work, MobileNet-V1 employs depth wise and point-wise convolutions to produce an output with the same size as the output of a standard convolution but with lower computational complexity. Later, MobileNet-V2 was introduced, which formulates its block with point-wise, depth-wise, and point-wise layers. The number of input and output channels are specific for each layer, such that the channel dimension is expanded with an expansion factor t within the block. For non-downsampling layer, a skip connection is included as well, making it a so called Inverted Residual block.

In our methods, we use a combination of MobileNet-V1 and MobileNet-V2 to extract feature maps at 1/4 original image resolution. And then, we use residual structure of MobileV1 three times, and use dilated convolution with parameter 1 in the last time. The outputs of these three times (channels are 64,128,128 respectively) are concatenated to obtain the final output of channel 320. Finally, we leverage structure of MobileV1 to fuse concatenated features from different scales and map the channel dimension from 320 to 32.

OEDA

We use an Encoder-Decoder module to produce multi-scale 3D predictions. Inspired by the previous work which predict voxel reconstruction from single-view image, we encode Voxel Volume to a low resolution along the depth dimension using 2D CNNs and then upsample it with 2D transposed convolution layers, as shown in Fig. 2.

Overview of our proposed OEDA. The four inputs represent the implicit representations indexed in each of four scales.

An octree is a tree-like data structure48 in which each node has eight children. Benefiting from its tree-like data structure, an octree is naturally hierarchical. In previous methods, researches commonly use deconvolution to upsample coarse predictions and then add to current layer to predict current fine predictions, which called U-Shape37 structures.

It is worth noting that the occupied locations of 3D voxel grids are still sparse, which represents that the coarse-to-fine refinement process will make the sparse occupied location information severely interfered by the empty location information. To solve this problem of U-Shape structures, we use an octree structure to filter the interference at empty locations. Specifically, 3D voxel reconstruction partitions objects and surfaces into uniform binary voxel grid cells, which only contain 0 or 1. Therefore, we add multi-level supervision in the coarse-to-fine process, and multiply the occupancy prediction with the current feature before deconvolutional upsampling. In this way, we pre-set the empty locations to 0 before upsampling to the next level, which minimizes the interference of dense non-occupied locations on sparse occupied locations, as shown in Fig. 3.

Finally, to determine whether the voxel grids after upsampling are occupied and to relate the discrete 0 and 1 with the differentiable 3D predictions, this method uses 0.5 as the threshold parameter20, performs binary processing on the output, and uses the output for supervision.

Experiments

In this section, we introduce datasets, implementation details and evaluation metrics in turn. Then we compare our method with SOTA approaches. And finally, we conduct ablation studies to demonstrate the effectiveness of the key models in our method and verify the reasonability of model parameter settings.

Dataset

DrivingStereo50 is a large-scale dataset for stereo matching in autonomous driving scenarios. And DrivingStereo contains over 180k images covering a diverse set of driving scenarios, which is hundreds of times larger than the widely used KITTI datasets. Most importantly, DrivingStereo provides binocular calibration extrinsic parameters and high-quality disparity labels extracted from multi-frame LiDAR data, which is import for our experiments that compare with stereo matching methods. From Table 1, we can observe that our method significantly improves the ability to reconstruct 3D voxel grids and achieves several times faster inference speed compared to previous methods. Remarkably, our method achieves about 14 times inference speed improvement compared to high-performance methods Raft-Stereo, and is also about three times faster than the second-fastest method. Moreover, our method has a decrease in the Recall metric compared to the Raft-Stereo-based method, but a substantial increase in the IoU and Precision metrics. We analyze that this phenomenon stems from two reasons. Firstly, PVPM abandons the dense pixel-wise projection of baseline methods (e.g., Raft-Stereo) and establishes an implicit mapping between 3D voxels and 2D cost volume through four-scale indexing coordinates to realize sparse feature retrieval. This strategy effectively filters invalid empty voxels and reduces computational overhead (the key to achieving 14 speedup over Raft-Stereo), but brings an inherent trade-off in small object detection. During the sparse indexing process of PVPM, these fine-grained small object features are easily ignored or not retrieved by the voxel grid, leading to the corresponding 3D voxels of small objects not being correctly marked as occupied. In addition, Raft-Stereo-based methods restore the dense point cloud and then voxelizes one by one. It represents all the potentially occupied grids as “occupied” states, which improves the “completeness rate”, but has a high false positive rate. As a result, the Precision metric of Raft-Stereo-based methods have significantly decreased. However, SVRS aims to solve this problem, and therefore performs poorly on the Recall metric.

As shown in the “Time(ms)” column of Table 1, SVRS achieves an inference speed of 60 ms (16.7 fps), which is 14 faster than the advanced pseudo-LiDAR method Raft-Stereo (860 ms) and 3 faster than the real-time method BGNet (270 ms after edge filtering). The key to achieving this effect is fundamentally overturns the serial and intensive computing paradigm of the pseudo-LiDAR method. Among them, the PVPM proposed in this paper establishes an implicit mapping between 3D voxels and 2D cost volumes through geometric priors in three dimensions. It directly transforms the 2D pixel-based dense computation of the pseudo-LiDAR method into a 3D voxel-based sparse computation, thereby eliminating the invalid computations in empty areas at the root cause. Furthermore, based on the U-Shap architecture and parallel reasoning capabilities, OEDA significantly reduces the computational complexity of voxel reconstruction. Therefore, SVRS is able to significantly increase the reasoning speed while maintaining accuracy.

The content within the blue boxes indicates the artifacts that appear on the object boundary. The results show that SVRS achieves stronger predictions with a faster inference speed.

SVRS consistently outperforms conventional stereo matching approaches in terms of IoU, Recall, and Precision. This performance improvement stems from the synergy between the proposed PVPM and OEDA. PVPM employs a reverse indexing mechanism to sparsely retrieve features directly from the stereo cost volume, thereby avoiding the artifacts and noise caused by boundary smoothing in traditional per-pixel projection and significantly enhancing reconstruction fidelity. Concurrently, OEDA leverages a coarse-to-fine multi-scale prediction strategy with void-region suppression, effectively capturing scene structural completeness while maintaining high inference efficiency. The combination of these designs allows SVRS to strike an optimal balance between false-positive suppression and miss detection reduction, resulting in a notable improvement in overall IoU. These results demonstrate that SVRS achieves superior accuracy, completeness, and efficiency in 3D voxel reconstruction.

Conclusion and future work

This paper presents a self-supervised 3D Voxel Reconstruction Network (SVRS) designed to enhance the efficiency of 3D voxel reconstruction from stereo vision while effectively mitigating false positives caused by edge artifacts. The key innovations of this work are threefold. First, by establishing a geometric projection relationship between 3D voxel grids and 2D stereo features, the proposed Pixel–Voxel Projecting Module (PVPM) effectively addresses the limitations of existing methods in handling redundant pixels in stereo depth maps. Moreover, PVPM converts dense pixel correspondence information into a sparse voxel implicit representation, thereby reducing computational overhead and alleviating false positives resulting from boundary over-smoothing. Second, an Octree-based Encoder–Decoder Architecture (OEDA) is developed, which leverages hierarchical spatial partitioning to achieve progressive multi-scale prediction from coarse to fine. This design suppresses the influence of void regions on sparse predictions and ensures both accuracy and efficiency in 3D voxel reconstruction. Third, a dual-mode training strategy is introduced. It supports not only supervised learning with LiDAR point clouds, but also self-supervised learning using stereo depth maps derived from off-the-shelf stereo matching methods. In the self-supervised mode, the Sobel operator is applied to filter edges in the left image to alleviate edge artifacts, while pseudo-LiDAR point clouds are voxelized to generate pseudo labels for model supervision.

Experimental results on the DrivingStereo dataset demonstrate that, compared with existing stereo matching–based methods, SVRS achieves substantial improvements across multiple performance metrics. Specifically, the inference speed increases by at least 260%, and the IoU under supervised training is significantly enhanced. Moreover, all evaluation indicators in the self-supervised setting reach or surpass those of existing methods, particularly in alleviating boundary artifacts around object boundaries.

However, although SVRS effectively alleviates the false positive problem, its performance in the Recall metric is slightly lower than that of some existing methods. This trade-off highlights the inherent balance between Accuracy and Recall in 3D voxel grid prediction. Future work will focus on optimizing this accuracy–recall trade-off to further enhance the overall performance of end-to-end 3D voxel reconstruction.

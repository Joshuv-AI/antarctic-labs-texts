Dendrophenol modulates the LRRK1/PIK3AP1 signaling axis: a novel strategy to mitigate inflammation and pyroptosis in gouty arthritis
Authors: Xianjun Chen, Lin Shi, Qingjiang Pang, Rongyao Yu, Chenghao Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundGouty arthritis (GA) is caused by hyperuricemia and the articular deposition of urate crystals, for which current treatments remain suboptimal. Considering the anti-inflammatory properties of Dendrobium extracts, especially Dendrophenol (Den), this study conducted an in-depth investigation into its effects on GA.MethodsThrough methylthiazolyldiphenyl-tetrazolium bromide (MTT) test, the impact of Den on bone marrow-derived primary macrophages (BMDMs) was assessed to ascertain its non-toxic dosage range. The GA cell model was established, subjected to Den (0.5 µM) treatment, and transfected with leucine rich repeat kinase 1 (LRRK1) overexpression plasmid, after which the pyroptosis and inflammatory indices were detected. Overexpression of LRRK1 reversed these regulatory effects of Den. Bioinformatics analysis determined that PIK3AP1 was the downstream target of LRRK1. Background

Gout is a metabolic disease closely related to hyperuricemia and urate crystal deposition. When the blood uric acid rises to a certain degree, sodium urate crystals form and deposit locally in the joints, inducing local inflammatory response and tissue destruction, ultimately leading to gouty arthritis (GA) [1, 2]. At present, therapeutic options for GA face challenges including efficacy limitations and drug side effects, driving the exploration of more effective alternatives. Consequently, natural products, with wide sources and minimal side effects, have thus attracted significant research interest [3, 4].

Dendrobium extracts have been confirmed to improve acute GA by inhibiting the toll-like receptors (TLRs)/myeloid differentiation factor 88 (MyD88)/nuclear factor kappa-B (NF-κB) pathway in the synovium of the ankle joint [5]. Dendrophenol (Den), as an component in Dendrobium, has many effects such as anti-apoptosis and anti-inflammation [6], indicating that Den may serve as the essential bioactive constituent responsible for the therapeutic efficacy of dendrobium. According to SwissTargetPrediction website, polo-like kinase 1 (PLK1) is one of the targets of Den. PLK1 inhibitors can suppress the activation of NOD-like receptor family pyrin domain containing 3 (NLRP3) inflammasome and subsequent interleukin-1β (IL-1β) production in macrophages stimulated by NLRP3 activator [7, 8]. Roco family kinase leucine rich repeat kinase 1 (LRRK1) acts as the substrate of PLK1 and plays a role in the downstream signal of PLK1 [9]. In addition, Fangji Huangqi Decoction (FHT) has been reported to reduce the levels of IL-1β and NF-κB proteins through LRRK1, improve uric acid excretion ability, and thus decrease serum uric acid levels [10]. Therefore, we hypothesized that Den regulates LRRK1 to inhibit macrophage inflammation and improve GA.

Pyroptosis is a type of programmed cell death induced by inflammasome, which plays an important role in the pathogenesis of GA [11, 12]. Previous studies have proved that Sipeimine mitigates osteoarthritis (OA) progression by inhibiting phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K)/protein kinase B (AKT)/NF-κB pathway, NLRP3 inflammasome activation and cell pyroptosis [13]. Bioinformatics analysis showed that phosphoinositide-3-kinase adaptor protein 1 (PIK3AP1) is not only a related target of GA, but also a predictive interaction target of LRRK1. PIK3AP1 is instrumental in the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway, regulation of which suppresses the expressions of downstream gene AKT and immune inflammatory gene interleukin − 6 (IL-6) in gastric epithelial cells (GES-1) [14]. A close relationship between PIK3AP1 and the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway, as well as cellular inflammation and pyroptosis, has also been demonstrated in thyroid cancer and osteoarthritis [15, 16].

Based on the above research background and findings, we speculated that Den may inhibit macrophage inflammation and pyroptosis via regulating LRRK1/PIK3AP1 signaling pathway, thereby improving GA. The purpose of this study is to explore the mechanism of Den in GA, and to provide a new theoretical direction for future research on GA treatment strategies.

Materials and methods

Animals and cell culture

A total of 3 male C57BL/6 mice, aged 6 weeks, were obtained from Hangzhou Medical College (China). The mice were kept in a regulated environment (a 12-hour light/12-hour dark cycle, a constant temperature of 25 ± 1 °C, and a relative humidity of 55% (± 10%)). Laboratory chow and water were available ad libitum to all animals involved. As previously noted, bone marrow from the C57BL/6 mice was isolated to produce bone marrow-derived primary macrophages (BMDMs) [17]. BMDMs were cultivated in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM, 12491015, ThermoFisher, USA) supplemented with 1% penicillin/streptomycin (P/S; C0222, Beyotime, China), and 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, 10100139, ThermoFisher, USA).

Isolation of BMDMs

After the mice were anesthetized with pentobarbital sodium (STY667, Zzsiji, China) and euthanized through cervical vertebrae dislocation, the body surface was disinfected. In the ultra-clean bench, the bilateral femurs and tibias were aseptically separated, the surface tissues were removed, and the bone marrow was rinsed with DMEM containing P/S until being transferred to a centrifuge tube. Following filtration through a cell sieve, the single-cell suspension was obtained. After centrifugation at 1000 rpm for 5 min, the cells were resuspended in DMEM containing 10% fetal bovine serum and 10 ng/mL macrophage colony-stimulating factor (M-CSF), and adjusted to a concentration of 1 × 10⁶ cells/mL. The cells were then inoculated into petri dishes and placed in a 5% CO₂ incubator at 37℃. On the third day of culture, the supernatant was removed to eliminate non-adherent contaminating cells and the medium was replaced with fresh medium. The culture was continued until days 6–7. The spindle-shaped or irregular morphology of adherent cells signified the generation of mature BMDMs.

Cell processing and grouping

To assess the effect of Den (A420955, Sangon Biotech, China) (0, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 5 µM) on BMDMs, the methylthiazolyldiphenyl-tetrazolium bromide (MTT) assay was performed to screen the optimal stimulation concentration of Den in cells. The cells were first stimulated with lipopolysaccharide (LPS, 500 ng/mL, ST1470, Beyotime, China) for 4 hours, and then treated with Den (0.5 µM) for 1 hour [3]. Cells were divided into the following groups: Control (conventional culture), LPS (500 ng/mL LPS) [3], LPS + Den (500 ng/mL LPS + 0.5 µM Den), LPS + Den+negative control (NC) (500 ng/mL LPS + 0.5 µM Den + NC), LPS + Den+LRRK1 (500 ng/mL LPS + 0.5 µM Den+LRRK1 overexpression plasmid), LPS + Den+NC+short hairpin RNA (sh) NC (500 ng/mL LPS + 0.5 µM Den + NC+shNC), LPS + Den+LRRK1 + shNC (500 ng/mL LPS + 0.5 µM Den+LRRK1 overexpression plasmid+shNC), LPS + Den+NC+short hairpin RNA targeting PIK3AP1 (shPIK3AP1) (500 ng/mL LPS + 0.5 µM Den + NC+shPIK3AP1), and LPS + Den+LRRK1 + shPIK3AP1 (500 ng/mL LPS + 0.5 µM Den+LRRK1 overexpression plasmid+shPIK3AP1). In brief, LRRK1 overexpression plasmid and shPIK3AP1 (5’-GCUAACCGAAUCCCUGAAGAATT3’) were bought from GenePharma (China), and transfected into cells using Lipofectamine 3000 (L3000001, Invitrogen, USA) for 24 h. Then, 0.5 µM Den was added for 1-hour treatment, and the follow-up experiments were performed.

Bioinformatics analysis

The Online Mendelian Inheritance in Man (OMIM, https://omim.org/), GeneCards (http://www.genecards.org/), and Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) databases were applied to identify target genes associated with GA. Dataset GSE242872 was retrieved from the GEO database for differential gene analysis between the experimental group and the control group, and all differential genes were collected as targets. LRRK1 interaction targets were investigated in the Search Tool for the Retrieval of Interacting Genes/Proteins (String, https://cn.string-db.org/), Biogrid (https://thebiogrid.org/), and GeneMANIA database (https://genemania.org/). Potential targets were identified through the junction of GA-related target genes and LRRK1 interaction targets. The 14 genes (13 intersection genes plus LRRK1) were entered into the String database to generate the protein-protein interaction (PPI) network. Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis were performed for these genes using the clusterProfiler package.

MTT test

BMDMs with good growth status during logarithmic growth period were inoculated (5,000/well) into 96-well plates (FCP962, Beyotime, China) and cultured in a 5% CO2 cell incubator at 37℃ for 24 h. Then the cells were treated with different concentrations of Den (0, 0.25, 0.5, 1 or 5 µM) for 48 h. Next, 10 µL MTT (C0009S, Beyotime, China) was added to each well. After incubation at 37℃ with 5% CO2 for 4 h, cells were cultured with 150 µL dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, HY-Y0320C, MCE, USA). The absorbance was measured at 570 nm by a multifunctional microplate reader (Varioskan LUX, Thermo Fisher, USA).

Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR)

Total RNA was extracted using Trizol (15596026, Invitrogen, USA) from the cells. Reverse transcription (4368813, Applied Biosystems, USA) was performed to generate the complementary DNA (cDNA). The specific primers (Sangon Biotech, China) LRRK1 (forward: 5’-GCCATCGGCCTCTAGTTCTC-3’, reverse: 5’-AATCAGCAGTTGGGGGATGG-3’), PIK3AP1 (forward: 5’-CAGGGGTGCCCAGAGGA-3’, reverse: 5’-GGGTCTCTGTGTTGTAGGGC-3’) and glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) (forward: 5’-AATGGGCAGCCGTTAGGAAA-3’, reverse: 5’-GCGCCCAATACGACCAAATC-3’) were subsequently added to the cDNA. The PCR amplification system was assembled following the guidelines of SYBR GreenER™ qPCR SuperMix (1176202 K, Invitrogen, USA), resulting in a total reaction mixture volume of 50 µL. Samples were collected at the end of the cycle for quantitative fluorescence PCR after amplification using a specified reaction protocol (QuantStudioTM 3, A28137, Applied Biosystems).

Flow cytometry

The cells in each group were digested with pancreatic enzyme (C0201, Beyotime, China) and treated with fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC)-labeled Caspase-1 antibody (NB100-56565 F, Novus Biologicals, USA) and propidium iodide (PI, HY-D0815, MCE, USA) at 4℃ for 30 min in the dark. Then, the cells were centrifuged at 700 rpm for 5 min and suspended in 300 µL phosphate buffered saline (PBS, C0221A, Beyotime, China). The proportion of Caspase-1+ and PI+ cells was detected by FACSCalibur flow cytometer (BD Biosciences, USA).

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

Inflammatory cytokines, tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α; BMS607-3TEN, Thermo Fisher, USA) and IL-1β (BMS6002-2, Thermo Fisher, USA) in cells were measured by ELISA kits. Ingredient concentrations were calculated using the kit’s formula. 100 µL of primary antibody was added to each well of a white board and incubated for 4 h at 37 °C. Sealing liquid was added for 1-hour culture, and then cells were washed for 3–5 min. The remaining liquid was removed from each well by tapping the plate for an hour at 37 °C. Next, 100 µL of cell supernatant was added to each well, and the mixture was washed three times for 5 min. Incubation with substrate and enzyme-labeled antibody was conducted at 37 °C for an hour. The reaction was ultimately halted by the terminating solution. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm utilizing a microplate reader.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism 8.0. Measurement data are expressed as mean ± standard deviation. Multi-group comparisons were conducted using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s post hoc test. A P-value below 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Den improved GA by regulating LRRK1

Initially, we evaluated the effects of Den on BMDMs using the MTT assay to determine a non-toxic dosage range. The data indicated that Den at 1 and 5 µM markedly reduced cell viability (Fig. 1A, P < 0.01), whereas Den at 0.25 and 0.5 µM exhibited barely impacted cell viability (Fig. 1A). In the subsequent trials, we chose Den at the highest dose of 0.5 µM, which was non-cytotoxic, for intervention. After the establishment of the GA model, Den treatment and transfection of LRRK1 overexpression plasmid were carried out. LRRK1 levels were significantly up-regulated after LPS treatment, and later were diminished by Den intervention, while overexpression of LRRK1 reversed the effect of Den intervention (Fig. 1B, P < 0.001). In addition, Den treatment inhibited pyroptosis and down-regulated IL-1β and TNF-α, which were offset by overexpression of LRRK1, as evidenced by promoted pyroptosis (Fig. 1C-F, P < 0.001).

B The level of LRRK1 was detected by quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR). Glyceraldehyde − 3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) was an internal parameter. C-D Propidium iodide (PI)/caspase-1 staining was used to measure pyroptosis by flow cytometry. E-F The levels of interleukin-1β (IL-1β) and tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) were examined by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). Each experiment was repeated 3 times. **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001

PIK3AP1, a downstream target of LRRK1, co-participated in GA development

900 GA-related target genes and 186 targets interacting with LRRK1 were retrieved in the database (Fig. 2A), and their convergence yielded 13 targets (Fig. 2A). Fourteen genes (13 intersection genes plus LRRK1) were entered into the String database to generate the PPI network (Fig. 2B). KEGG enrichment analysis showed that these genes were mainly enriched in the B cell receptor signaling pathway, T cell receptor signaling pathway, and Fc epsilon RI signaling pathway (Fig. 2C). GO results demonstrated that these genes were mainly enriched in the immune response-activating signaling pathway, cytoplasmic side of plasma membrane and structural constituent of cytoskeleton (Fig. 3A-B). We subsequently investigated the impact of LRRK1 overexpression on PIK3AP1 expression in cells, and noticed that LRRK1 overexpression abrogated Den intervention-induced remarkable down-regulation of PIK3AP1 mRNA, and elevated its mRNA level (Fig. 3C, P < 0.001).

Den improved GA by mediating LRRK1/PIK3AP1 axis

To ascertain the regulatory influence of PIK3AP1 on GA, we created PIK3AP1-silencing cells. The data revealed that LRRK1 overexpression augmented PIK3AP1 mRNA levels in Den-treated model cells, which was counteracted by PIK3AP1 knockdown (Fig. 4A, P < 0.01). Moreover, LRRK1 overexpression reversed Den treatment-induced inhibition of cell pyroptosis, IL-1β level, and TNF-α level, while PIK3AP1 knockdown negated the reversing effects of LRRK1 overexpression (Fig. 4B-E, P < 0.05). These findings indicated that Den repressed inflammation and pyroptosis of GA cells through the modulation of LRRK1/PIK3AP1.

Discussion

In this study, we explored the regulatory mechanism of Den on inflammation and pyroptosis of GA cells, and found that in this LPS-stimulated macrophage model, Den improved GA by regulating LRRK1/PIK3AP1 axis, highlighting a novel and promising cellular pathway for future therapeutic exploration against GA.

The pathogenesis of GA is closely linked to inflammation and pyroptosis, a programmed cell death mediated by inflammasome [18, 19]. In GA, deposition of sodium urate crystals can activate inflammasome, such as NLRP3 inflammasome [20, 21]. The NLRP3 inflammasome activates caspase-1, which cleaves Gasdermin D (GSDMD) and forms GSDMD pores, leading to the release of inflammatory factors such as IL-1β and IL-18 [22]. These inflammatory factors further recruit immune cells, intensify local inflammatory response, and promote the development of GA. Inflammation is a key pathogenic component of GA, as evidenced by the marked elevation of pro-inflammatory cytokines, IL-1β and TNF-α, in synovial fluid and affected tissue in patients [23]. IL-1β can induce synovial cells and chondrocytes to synthesize matrix metalloproteinases, resulting in the degradation of cartilage and bone tissue [24, 25]. TNF-α can trigger vascular endothelial cells to express adhesion molecules, promote white blood cell adhesion and infiltration, and aggravate inflammation [26]. Meanwhile, the inflammatory response activates NF-κB and other signaling pathways, and further promotes the expression and release of inflammatory factors, forming a vicious cycle to deteriorate GA [27].

The present work elucidated a novel mechanism by which Den disrupted the vicious cycle in macrophages. In contrast to recent findings that Den suppresses vascular calcification and the release of inflammatory cytokines by activating interleukin 13 receptor subunit alpha 2 (IL13RA2)-dependent inhibition of signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3) and attenuating the WNT family member 3 (WNT3)/β-catenin signaling pathway [28], our data revealed a distinct, macrophage-centric pathway centered on the Roco family kinase LRRK1. LRRK1, as a substrate of PLK1, plays a role in the downstream signal of PLK1 and is intimately associated with bone diseases [29]. PLK1 inhibitors has been reported to diminish NLRP3 inflammasome activation and the ensuing IL-1β generation in macrophages activated by NLRP3 stimulators, implying that LRRK1 may impact inflammatory response in GA through the regulation of NLRP3 inflammasome activation [13]. Our study is the first to position LRRK1 as a direct functional target of Den in inflammatory signaling. We demonstrated that Den blocked the vicious cycle between pyroptosis and inflammation in GA cells. Mechanistically, Den significantly down-regulated LRRK1, and crucially, overexpression of LRRK1 reversed the inhibitory effects of Den on pyroptosis and inflammatory factor release, establishing LRRK1 as pivotal to Den’s mechanism.

A key advance of this study is the identification of PIK3AP1 as the critical downstream effector linking LRRK1 to a major pro-inflammatory signaling cascade. We identified PIK3AP1 through bioinformatics and confirmed it as a downstream target of LRRK1. PIK3AP1, a downstream target of LRRK1, is essential in the PI3K/AKT signaling cascade. Beyond its vital role in promoting cell survival and proliferation, activation of PI3K/AKT pathway is a key regulator of inflammatory response in the inflammatory state [30]. Our data demonstrated that Den intervention markedly reduced mRNA level of PIK3AP1. The causal relationship within this axis was solidified by genetic experiments: knockdown of PIK3AP1 mimicked Den’s effect by inhibiting pyroptosis and inflammation and also mitigated the phenotypic consequences of LRRK1 overexpression.

However, we must acknowledge that there are limitations in this study. First, the mechanism research is based on an LPS-induced macrophage model. Although this model can simulate a general inflammatory state, the pathways involved may differ from those specifically activated by MSU crystals, a core pathological trigger of GA. Hence, the applicability of the conclusions in MSU-specific models remains to be verified. Second, whether there is a direct interaction between Den and LRRK1, as well as the specific molecular mode (such as phosphorylation) by which LRRK1 regulates PIK3AP1, has not yet been clarified, and the mechanism needs in-depth exploration. In addition, the use of a single concentration and time point for Den prevents the determination of its dose-effect relationship, time-effect dynamics, and key pharmacological parameters (such as EC50). Finally, all conclusions are derived from in vitro experiments, and verification of complex phenotypes such as joint swelling and synovial inflammation in animal models is essential.

Conclusion

In summary, this study reveals a new mechanism by which Den regulates LRRK1/PIK3AP1 to improve GA. Mechanistically, Den inhibits LRRK1 and its downstream PIK3AP1, and reduces the release of pyroptosis and inflammatory factors, thereby alleviating inflammatory response in GA. Although this study provides mechanistic evidence at the cellular level, these findings warrant further validation in vivo. Our future studies are aimed at establishing appropriate animal models of GA to assess the therapeutic efficacy, safety profile, and target specificity of Den, thereby laying the groundwork for its potential application in humans.

A novel multi-module neural networks strategy of human emotion recognition in the human-robot interaction
Authors: Khalid Zaman, Ammad Ul Islam, Gan Zengkang, Muhammad Bilal, Ayman Alharbi, Sayyed Mudassar Shah, Sohail Asghar, Hongzhao Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractNew technologies in human emotion recognition (HER) have drawn considerable attention to use in the fields of security, intelligent customer service, healthcare, educational, human-robot interaction (HRI), and adaptive system training. To identify human emotions, our model incorporates MobileNetV3, Vision Transformer (ViT), RegNet and SE-ResNeXt into a unique deep ensemble classification structure. A Novel Multi Module Neural Networks (MMNNs) architecture is designed in this research for HER for practical application the main purpose of is to identify the human emotions. An innovative approach to improve the performance of HER by integrating MMNNs with Transfer Learning (TL) to train CNNs is researched. The MMNNs classification model is trained by combining features from four CNN models using feature pooling. The key novelty of the model is the novel DEtection TRansformer (DETR) which enhances the CNN learning block. It consists of a CNN that learns low dimensional feature representation, an encoder decoder transformer and a simple Feed Forward Network (FFN) that outputs the final detection prediction, which ultimately boosts face recognition efficiency and accuracy. The MMNNs results are validated on AffectNet, CK + and a custom-made dataset (CMD) achieving accuracy of 91.07%, 87.03% and 96.98% respectively which is further increased by data augmentation technique to 95.09%, 89.15% and 98.13% respectively.Keywords: Multi-Module Neural Networks (MMNNs), Human-Robot Interaction (HRI), DEtection TRansformer (DETR), Custom-Made Dataset (CMD), Human Computer Interaction (HCI)Subject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Combining deep learning with traditional hybrid functionalities

The go-when emotion recognition systems are concerned are features generated based on deep learning models like convolutional neural networks (CNNs), and the classic handcrafted features, including think Local Binary Patterns (LBP) and Histogram of Oriented Gradients (HOG). Although such conventional approaches as LBP are exceptionally popular, they cannot manage to detect the complex patterns and cannot follow them when the lighting is altered or the face of the person is obstructed by a part of it.

In this project, assembled a hybrid feature combination methodology that combines the traditional manually-crafted features with the flair deep-learning features. In principle, we worked with LBP and HOG and superimposed them on the response of a sequence of ensemble CNN models and trained them jointly. Problems such as occlusions, naughty face angles and uneven light distribution has been a nightmare to emotion recognition. However, by such a combination, the system is in a better place to grind through these challenges. The hybrid model is much more flexible in various settings, and this fact implies that it can be useful in a variety of datasets and real-life situations.

Live processing and performance effectiveness

Most emotion detection systems are highly efficient, however they tend to be slow enough to be used in real-time, which could not be utilized in things like humanoid robotics or autonomous vehicles. In order to produce predictions, deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs), e.g., DenseNet and InceptionV3, will simply require large resources, and in many cases require high-performance graphics processing units (GPUs). There is also a usual latency issue of these models, which makes them unsuitable in real-time emotion recognition.

The application of the MobileNetV3 will resolve this issue by improving the performance of our ensemble model. MobileNetV3 is created to work effectively with limited parameter allowing the model to perform faster without affecting accuracy. As such, our method works in real time with no latency systems and includes human robot interaction in robotics, medical diagnosis, and autonomous vehicles. Our methodology is ideal where precision and cost are the minimized parameters as it can be fitted in real-life devices with less processing power.

Personalized data and improved conditional generalization

FER-2013 and CK + are the two most commonly used datasets in emotion recognition, but they are not always the most diverse and do not display the best results in a realistic setting. As an example, the majority of pictures in FER-2013 are made up of static photos with little changes in terms of light, objects, or facial expressions.

The present work stands out against many others because it uses several datasets to train and evaluate the selected model, one of them being a custom-made-dataset (CMD). This data includes aspects like changing facial expression, partial obscuration, and changing light conditions in attempts to recreate real world conditions of human-robot interaction. This is the greatest improvement that was made over the previous research since the model is shown to be more robust and applicable to real-life situations after training on this dataset. The model has more flexibility because it has better generalization performance and is able to perform effectively with wide range of real world human-robot interaction systems.

The proposed methodology

The proposed MMNNs design for HER utilized one of the ensembles learning techniques for improving the accuracy and speed of the emotion recognition process. To developed Convolutional Neural Networks such as RegNet, MobileNetV3, ViLT, and SE-ResNeXt’s most efficient features were consolidated in this system is illustrated in Fig. 2. These models were selected manually because of their effectiveness at extracting high-dimensional input data features. Each model has a unique contribution to make in the feature extraction operation. MobileNetv3 tries to utilize the available parameters more effectively. MobileNetV3 balances its scaling method resulting in it having the optimal depth, breadth, and resolution. Because it can easily generate powerful feature maps. In the case of an application that requires detecting emotions on a real-time basis, the MobileNetV3 would be the most suitable when it comes to feature extraction. Used Vision and Language Transformers (ViLT) to get the whole image from images that are considered to be a sequence of patches. Transformer, the key part of ViLT, helps the model to comprehend the intricate relation between the image patches. This is required to get the fine-grained phrases that can be written on the whole face. The scalable design of RegNet guarantee that the speed and complexity of the model find the perfect balance. As a useful member of the ensemble, it manages to identify key features, copes with large datasets successfully, and is robust in a range of real-world scenarios. The SE-ResNeXt model’s channel recalibration strategy has become notorious due to the huge significance it imparts to the feature representation by its focus on the most effective channels. The model can implement the Squeeze-and-excitation (SE) mechanism through channel level feature map transformations in order to tune expressive areas such as the eyes and lips. This feature addition simplifies the recognition of emotion particularly under difficult and fast conditions.

In order to assemble the benefits of every advanced model, the ensemble model used in the present study consists of MobileNetV3, ViLT, RegNet, and SE-ResNeXt. Even though ensemble models require more power, they are known to outperform because of the combination of various types of data. The more models, the more calculations, and therefore the consumption of more memory, and more time to solve the problem. In order to make the ensemble workable in real-time applications, used the simplified architecture known as the MobileNetV3. This causes the reduction of complexity.

A feature vector is developed that represents a lot of information regarding the human use of emotion by combining the feature that is given to each CNN model in comparison to the input data. Once the fusion is done, emotional classification is improved further by the application of classifiers such as CNN, Gated Recurrent Units (GRU), and Multilayer Perceptron (MLP)9. GRU is less expensive to long sequence data, especially the long one due to the fact that it maintains the illustrations of vector representation of data as a sequence and also because it uses less space in memory compared to the LSTM networks. All the classifiers’ output undergoes calibration process which allows for combining results and identifying the ultimate category of emotions. Because the structure is elastic, it is simple to merge much of the primitive classifiers that are trained on diverse or same set of features. This is on the premise of hierarchical ensembles in an effort to ensure a more accurate and stable system.

The MMNNs proposed for robots in HER perform excellently, particularly in the multi-modal architecture in which contextual information (i.e., text or audio) and visual information are important. The Proposed MMNNs outperforms the state-of-the-art models such as MobileNet V3, ViLT, RegNet, and SE-ResNeXt on all 5 datasets. Due to the ability of MMNNs to handle a large variety of varieties of data, a significant competitive edge can be achieved, namely, over such data sets as Emotic, ArmanEmo, and G-REx, where both the image and the text (or audio56) must be taken into account in the case of proper emotion recognition. In complex examples, MMNNs achieves higher F1-scores and is much better and precise. Since multi-modal input maintains a deeper context to recognize the emotion it would, therefore, be the most suitable option to be applied in the real-world emotion identification program in the HRI. Such marvelous performance is most pronounced on numerous and challenging datasets showing that multi-modal models such as the Proposed MMNNs are required and essential in order to enhance the accuracy and capability of the HE detection systems so that these models can deal with greater amounts of data and information.

Conclusions

The deep ensemble classification model used in this paper maximizes emotion detection in the human-robot interaction accuracy. A powerful framework is developed, which can be used to handle the complexities of changing conditions, when MobileNetV3, ViT, RegNet, and SE-ResNeXt are combined. To make the model stronger and more likely to recognize emotions under challenging conditions, used strong face recognition of the DETR. It is shown that in a different type of emotions within the datasets, augmentation technique is able to be applied effectively in order to eliminate the class imbalance problems, which are widespread in emotion detection dataset and processes are able to detect consistently such emotion as Fear and Disgust.

The MMNNs model is trained by combining features from CNN models using feature pooling. the key section of our architecture is the novel DETER block is used to enhance the CNN learning block. The MMNNs thus ended up being more powerful on the entire part. It was observed that the MMNNs performed better than all the conventional models when tested on standard benchmark datasets and CMD. Seven basic human emotions, improving the accuracy of this classification even further, took place in the form of the ensemble technique. This study has the potential to become the cornerstone of emotion identification architecture in HRI as it offers an effective and scalable approach of real-time identification of emotions.

Researchers can look into other options of improving the model to fulfill the real-time needs in the future. They might use it with datasets of various data types, Western music genres, gesture recognition, speech recognition, and ways to reduce its costs without the need to diminish its application in such systems as autonomous vehicles and medical diagnostics. It, therefore, would prove more viable and flexible in a broader context of situations. In spite of the higher accuracy of the ensemble model, the future work will aim at investigating lightweight convolutional neural network (CNN) designs based on MobileNetV3 and other resource-efficient models to improve the inference speed and resource consumption especially in real-time Human-Robot Interaction (HRI).

Orthotopic renal autotransplantation: A step forward during postchemotherapy retroperitoneal lymph node dissection for germ cell tumor
Authors: Akash Mor, Ajit Gujela, Sachin Patel, Vineet Kumar, Gagan Prakash
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTWe present the first and a rare case of orthotopic renal auto-transplantation in the management of postchemotherapy residual retroperitoneal mass encasing the left renal vessels but sparing the parenchyma in a 24-year-old patient with Stage IIIC nonseminomatous germ cell tumor after 4 cycles of chemotherapy. Immediate postoperative and 6 months’ follow-up renograms confirmed the maintained glomerular filtration rate of the transplanted kidney with no residual disease. This case report shows the feasibility of renal autotransplantation in carefully selected patients as they are young and have long-term survival.

INTRODUCTION

Post chemotherapy retroperitoneal lymph node dissection (RPLND) is an integral part of the management of testicular germ cell tumors. Germ cell tumors (GCTs) have a long-term overall survival of >90%, and a better understanding of the survivorship issues has highlighted the cancer unrelated causes of mortality in these patients. Consequently, one should consider saving the kidney if the renal parenchyma is not involved by the tumor and the oncological clearance is not compromised. One such additional surgical approach is the renal autotransplantation. This approach has been described earlier in the literature on three occasions and ours is the fourth case of renal auto-transplantation and the first instance where an orthotopic renal auto-transplantation was performed during the RPLND for GCT.

CASE REPORT

A 24-year-old man married with one child presented with a left testicular swelling clinically suspected to be testicular cancer. A high inguinal orchidectomy and cross-sectional imaging confirmed the diagnosis of nonseminomatous germ cell tumor (yolk sac and immature teratoma), Stage IIIC, intermediate risk as per the International Germ Cell Cancer Collaborative Group.

Due to the heavy disease burden and poor pulmonary functions, a decision was taken to start vincristine, ifosfamide, and cisplatin (VIP) in a multidisciplinary meeting. After 4 cycles of VIP, the tumor markers normalized and there was a residual retroperitoneal mass measuring 12 cm × 10 cm × 12 cm the left renal vessels (single artery and single vein with no abnormal branching), the ureter, the aorta (360° angle of contact) and the pancreas as shown in the computed tomography (CT) scan [Figure 1]. A preoperative DTPA scan showed a glomerular filtration rate (GFR) of 30 ml/min for the left kidney. Considering his age, a good anticipated long-term survival, and an uninvolved renal parenchyma a decision about considering renal autotransplantation was taken in the multidisciplinary tumor board and then the same was discussed with the patient and his family.

(a) Contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) showing renal vein (blue) coursing through the mass (brown) (b) Contrast-enhanced CT showing renal artery (red) coursing through the mass (brown) with compressed ureter resulting into mild hydronephrosis with abutment of the renal pelvis

After the midline laparotomy and exploration of the retroperitoneal space, the tumor was removed en bloc with the left kidney on an ice bed, sparing the pancreas. This involved a preplanned dissection of the origin of the renal artery and the vein with a length sufficient for a tension-free anastomosis. Bulldog clamps were applied proximally over the. renal artery and the renal vein. The ureter was divided 5 cm distal to the pelviureteric junction. The kidney was perfused on the ice slush with histidine–tryptophan–ketoglutarate solution [Figure 2]. The warm ischemia time was 30 min. The bench dissection included separation of the en-bloc tumor from the renal hilum and dissection and refashioning of the vessels at the hilum to prepare them for the anastomosis. As the length of the proximal and the distal stumps were long enough to allow tension free anastomosis, an orthotopic transplantation was preferred. The orthotopic auto-transplantation was performed by anastomosing the refashioned hilar artery to the aortic arterial stump with 7-0 Prolene, the renal vein to the venous stump with 7-0 Prolene, and an end-to-end uretero-ureteric anastomosis with 5-0 polydioxanone. Intraoperatively, a ureteric stent was placed which was removed after 6 weeks. This amounted to a cold ischemia time of 1 h and 30 min. The kidney color and turgidity were satisfactory after the anastomosis. A template nodal clearance was performed and nephropexy concluded this step of surgery.

The patient was discharged on the postoperative day 9 after an uneventful stay and the confirmation of the renal function on a Doppler scan. The histopathology showed a mature teratoma and no further systemic therapy was warranted. On a 6-month follow-up, the tumor markers were normal, and the GFR of the left auto-transplanted kidney was 28 ml/min, and there was no residual tumor in the retroperitoneum [Table 2]. The CT scan showed normal enhancing parenchyma without any stricture and normal opacification of the renal vessels. The patient has completed 12 months of follow-up and is doing fine.

DISCUSSION

Post-chemotherapy for NSGCT, 20%–40% of the patients have mature teratoma, and 45%–65% of the patients have necrosis in the specimen of the RPLND.[1] These tumors tend to grow along and around the vessels in the retroperitoneum. Consequently, various series report the incidence of vascular injuries in up to 1.6% and the need for additional procedures in 29.3% of the patients.[2] The need for nephrectomy as an additional procedure, as reported in various series, ranges between 4.6 and 7.3%.[2] A vascular dissection is always attempted, but chances of having an injury would depend on the degree of encasement and the desmoplastic reaction which cannot be accurately predicted on the preoperative imaging.

The concept of renal auto-transplantation during the RPLND has been described in three case reports in the literature [Table 1]. Our case was based on the similar grounds, however, we transplanted the dissected tumor-free kidney orthotopically, rather than in the pelvis.

CONCLUSION

Bench surgery and orthotopic or heterotopic renal autotransplantation are feasible and should be considered in the armamentarium of RPLND for NSGCTs provided a sufficient stump length is available. Adoption of this approach by global centers with centralized care for the GCTs will help in establishing the long-term oncological safety of this additional procedure and its impact on survivorship.

Efficacy and safety of rituximab versus intravenous cyclophosphamide for systemic sclerosis-associated interstitial lung disease: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Rioko Hiura, Masahiro Ayano, Ayumi Uchino, Junki Hiura, Naoyasu Ueda, Atsushi Tanaka, Seiji Yoshizawa, Shotaro Kawano, Fumiaki Sagawa, Shun-Ichiro Ota, Akie Hirata, Sho Fujimoto, Naoya Nishimura, Ayako Takaki-Kuwahara, Yasutaka Kimoto, Koichi Akashi, Hiroaki Niiro
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe efficacy of rituximab (RTX) for the treatment of systemic sclerosis-associated interstitial lung disease (SSc-ILD) has not been fully established. This study compared the efficacy and safety of RTX and intravenous cyclophosphamide (CY) for SSc-ILD.MethodsThis retrospective study compared the efficacy and safety of RTX (20 patients) and CY (30 patients) after adjusting for the stabilised inverse probability of treatment weighting based on propensity scores. The efficacy endpoints were the absolute changes in forced vital capacity (FVC) and serum Krebs von den Lungen-6 (KL-6) levels from baseline to 6 and 12 months after treatment. The incidence of progression based on the definition of progressive pulmonary fibrosis was also recorded. The safety endpoint was the frequency of adverse events.ResultsThe clinical characteristics of the two groups were well-balanced after adjusting for confounders (i.e., FVC, nintedanib use, and newly diagnosed cases). From baseline to 6 and 12 months after the start of treatment, the median FVC increased by 50 and 60 ml in the RTX group and 40 and 15 ml in the CY group, respectively, with no difference after adjustment. The changes in serum KL-6 levels and the incidences of progression were identical in both groups after adjustment. The overall adverse events were similar in both groups after adjustment.ConclusionsRTX demonstrated comparable safety and efficacy as CY in patients with SSc-ILD. Thus, RTX may be an alternative to CY for the treatment of SSc-ILD.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13075-025-03654-0.

Background

Systemic sclerosis (SSc) is a systemic autoimmune rheumatic disease (SARD) characterised by immune dysregulation and progressive fibrosis that causes lesions in the skin and internal organs [1, 2]. Among these patients, SSc-associated interstitial lung disease (SSc-ILD) is an important cause of death, with a prevalence of up to 30% and 10-year mortality rate of up to 40% [3], and thus its treatment and management are crucial.

Cyclophosphamide (CY) and, more recently, mycophenolate mofetil (MMF) have demonstrated their efficacy for SSc-ILD in previous studies [4–6] and have been recommended for its treatment [2, 7]. However, CY can cause adverse events such as gastrointestinal symptoms and gonadal dysfunction, and its long-term administration is associated with an increased risk of malignancy [5, 8], thereby highlighting the need for alternative treatments.

Rituximab (RTX) is a chimeric monoclonal antibody that eliminates CD20-positive B cells [9]. B cell depletion therapy is effective in several systemic autoimmune rheumatic diseases, such as lupus nephritis [10, 11] and ANCA-associated vasculitis [12]. Chronic B cell activation is a known component of the pathogenesis of fibrosis in SSc [13]. Since RTX acts selectively on B cells, it is expected to address the pathogenesis of SSc while having less metabolic and cytotoxic-related adverse effects. These features position RTX as a potential alternative to CY in the treatment of SSc-ILD, with a few randomised control trials comparing their efficacy and safety. One Japanese multicentre clinical trial in patients with diffuse cutaneous SSc demonstrated that RTX improved skin sclerosis and forced vital capacity (FVC) [14], but the efficacy for ILD was only evaluated as a secondary endpoint. Another comparative study of patients with SARD-ILD, including SSc-ILD [15], demonstrated that there was no significant difference between both drugs in the 24-week rate of change in FVC from baseline, but an evaluation limited to patients with SSc-ILD was not performed in detail. Therefore, the efficacy and safety of these two drugs for SSc-ILD have not been fully established.

To address this knowledge gap, this study evaluated the efficacy and safety of RTX and CY in patients with SSc-ILD in real clinical practise.

Methods

Patients

This retrospective study included Japanese patients given RTX or CY for SSc-ILD between January 1, 2018, and June 30, 2024, at Kyushu University Hospital and 5 other affiliated hospitals. SSc was diagnosed based on the 2013 European League Against Rheumatism/American College of Rheumatology classification criteria [16], while ILD was diagnosed by a cardiothoracic radiologist based on high-resolution computed tomography (HR-CT) findings. Patients who received treatment with RTX or CY within 12 months prior to this study were excluded. Concomitant administration of other immunosuppressive drugs, antifibrotic drugs, and glucocorticoids was permitted if there were no recent changes. RTX was administered at 375 mg/m2 (body surface area) per dose, for a total of 4 doses given 1 week apart, with maintenance dosing adjusted at the discretion of the attending physician. CY was administered at 500 mg/m2 per dose, for a total of 6 doses given 4 weeks apart, with the dose per course and maintenance dosing adjusted at the discretion of the attending physician.

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Kyushu University Hospital (Fukuoka, Japan; approval number 23391) and conducted according to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Due to the retrospective study design, all study information was disclosed at the site of the related facilities. The requirement for patient consent was waived as per the ethical committee guidelines.

Clinical and laboratory assessment

Patient information was collected from the medical records after study approval and de-identification of data, ensuring that identity of the patients could not be ascertained in any way. The following information was collected: demographic data, laboratory findings, drug therapy, adverse events, and outcomes. Disease duration was defined as the period from the first non-Raynaud’s phenomenon onset to the start of treatment. Pulmonary arterial hypertension was diagnosed when the mean pulmonary arterial pressure was 25 mmHg or higher on right heart catheterization according to the 2018 Nice classification [17]. Gastrointestinal lesions were defined as upper gastrointestinal lesions (e.g., lower oesophageal peristalsis, gastroesophageal reflux disease, reflux oesophagitis, oesophageal stricture, etc.) or lower intestinal lesions with subjective symptoms.

The nonspecific interstitial pneumonia (NSIP) pattern was determined based on the 2013 American Thoracic Society/European Respiratory Society international classification [18]. SSc-ILD was further classified as either extensive or limited disease based on the staging system [19]. The percentage of predicted FVC was expressed as the percentage of predicted values based on the prediction equations of the Japanese Respiratory Society [20]. HR-CT and pulmonary function test were followed up every 6–12 months in accordance with the Japanese guideline [21].

Outcomes

The efficacy endpoints were the absolute changes in FVC and serum Krebs von den Lungen-6 (KL-6) levels from baseline to 6 and 12 months after the start of treatment. The incidences of progression, death, and the introduction of long-term oxygen therapy were also evaluated. Progression was defined based on the definition of progressive pulmonary fibrosis, including the progression of ILD on HR-CT, which was determined based on the radiologist’s diagnosis, and an absolute decrease of more than 5% in %FVC in one year [22]. The safety endpoint was the frequency of adverse events.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were summarised as their mean ± standard deviation or median and interquartile range. Categorical variables were reported as frequencies and percentages. Differences between the two groups were analysed using Student’s t-test for normally distributed continuous variables, Mann–Whitney U-test for non-normally distributed continuous variables, and Pearson’s chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables.

Inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) based on the propensity score was used to balance comparison groups regarding the patient clinical characteristics [23–25]. The propensity score for the exposure of RTX was estimated by a logistic regression model including the following confounding factors shown to be significant based on clinical experience: FVC, nintedanib use, and newly diagnosed case. Then, using stabilised IPTW [26], weighted pseudo samples of both treatment groups with the same distribution of measured baseline variables were produced. Between-group differences in confounding factors were examined using standardised mean differences in both unweighted and weighted samples; the absolute standardised mean differences < 0.25 were considered as good balance [27].

The cumulative incidence of progression was analysed using Kaplan–Meier survival curves, with the log-rank test used for group comparisons. The incidences of death and the introduction of long-term oxygen therapy were calculated as the number of events per person-year of observation, with Fisher’s exact test used for group comparison. The hazard ratios after stabilised IPTW-weighting were estimated using the Cox proportional hazards model. Sensitivity analyses were limited to patients with extensive disease.

All tests were two-tailed, with p < 0.05 considered significant. All statistical analyses were performed with EZR (Saitama Medical Center, Jichi Medical University; http://www.jichi.ac.jp/saitama-sct/SaitamaHP.files/statmedEN.html; Kanda, 2013 [28]), a graphical user interface for R (The R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria, version 4.4.1), which is a modified version of the R commander (version 2.9–1) designed to add statistical functions frequently used in biostatistics.

Results

Patients

The analysis included a total of 50 patients (RTX group, n = 20; CY group, n = 30), with more recent cases observed in the RTX group (Table 1 and Supplementary Table 1). None of the patients were excluded from the study. The median (interquartile range) number of drug administrations was 4 (4–5) for the RTX group and 6 (6–7) for the CY group. The mean age ± standard deviation of all patients was 58.8 ± 13.9 years, and 36 (72%) patients were female. The median (interquartile range) disease duration was 2.1 (0.3–7.2) years, and this was longer in the RTX group. There were no significant differences in age, sex, or organ involvement between the two groups. Regarding the evaluation of ILD, both groups were similar in terms of SSc-ILD stage, FVC, and serum KL-6 levels. Notably, 65% of the RTX group and 53.3% of the CY group had %FVC less than 70%. Regarding treatment, the CY group had a higher prednisolone dose and lower nintedanib use compared to the RTX group. There were significantly fewer newly diagnosed cases in the RTX group. After propensity score weighting, the clinical characteristics of both groups were well-balanced for all baseline covariates and an absolute standardised mean difference of less than 0.25, especially for confounding factors. Safety

Table 4 shows the number (%) of adverse events within 1 year of initiating treatment in both groups. A few cases of infusion reactions occurred in the RTX group, but all were mild and did not require prolonged hospitalisation. Cytomegalovirus infection was not observed in either group. Aside from infusion reactions and infections, thrombocytopenia occurred only in the RTX group, while other adverse events occurred only in the CY group. After adjustment for stabilised IPTW based on the propensity score, the adverse events other than infusion reactions were similar between both groups. Sensitivity analysis

The sensitivity analyses were limited to patients with extensive disease, including 13 and 15 cases in the RTX and CY groups, respectively. FVC increased at 6 and 12 months in both groups, which demonstrated a similar absolute change (Table 5). Adverse events other than infusion reactions were also similar between the two groups (Table 5). Discussion

RTX and CY had similar efficacy and safety in terms of the changes in FVC and serum KL-6 levels, the cumulative incidence of progression, and the frequency of adverse events.

In this study, FVC increased after treatment initiation in both groups. A previous study comparing the efficacy and safety of RTX and CY for SSc-ILD only had a follow-up period of 6 months [29], but the 12-month follow-up period used in this study allowed for a better comparison of FVC changes. Even when the analysis was restricted to patients with extensive disease, most of whom would have been excluded from clinical trials due to their low baseline FVC [14, 15], the changes in FVC were not significantly different between the two groups. The greatest strength of this study was the inclusion of advanced cases of SSc-ILD in real clinical practise, and it was demonstrated that the efficacy of RTX was not inferior to that of CY. Given that RTX has demonstrated comparable efficacy to CY in the treatment of SARD-ILD and has been recommended as an alternative to CY for remission induction therapy of ANCA-associated vasculitis [30], the results of our study further support the potential of RTX as an alternative to CY for SSc-ILD.

The overall number of reported adverse events and serious adverse events in this study were nearly identical between both groups, consistent with previous reports demonstrating that both drugs had equivalent safety in patients with ANCA-associated vasculitis [31, 32] and RTX was even safer in patients with SARD-ILD and SSc-ILD [15, 16]. Additionally, although this study included patients with low baseline FVC and almost all infections were identified in this subset of patients, the safety of both drugs was similar even when restricted to such patients. These results indicate that RTX can be a safe alternative to CY, and it can be safely used even in patients with more advanced diseases.

This study had several limitations. First, this was a small multicentre study with a relatively fewer number of cases. Our findings need to be validated by further studies on efficacy and safety that include more cases. Second, this was a retrospective observational study with some bias included. For instance, the proportion of first-episode cases and patients treated with nintedanib differed between the two groups. Although we adjusted for stabilised IPTW based on the propensity score to reduce these biases, there may have been residual differences that were not examined (e.g., clinical characteristics of the patients). Third, there were several cases with missing blood test data, pulmonary function test results, and patients' subjective symptoms, which can be attributed to the retrospective observational study design. Additionally, the observation period coincided with the COVID-19 pandemic, which resulted in fewer pulmonary function tests being performed. Further studies should employ a prospective observational study design to minimise the effects of bias and missing data. Fourth, RTX for SSc was not covered by insurance in Japan until September 2021, and thus, many patients in the RTX group started treatment only after that date (Supplementary Table 1). Thus, the observation period for all patients in the RTX group was less than 3 years, which is insufficient for assessing long-term prognosis. Long-term observation is needed to fully evaluate the long-term efficacy, safety, and prognosis after treatment with these drugs. Finally, MMF for SSc-ILD was not covered by insurance in Japan until February 2024, making it difficult to compare RTX and MMF, which is another first-line immunosuppressive drug.

Conclusions

RTX demonstrated comparable efficacy and safety compared to CY in patients with SSc-ILD. Our findings suggest the potential of RTX as an alternative treatment option for the treatment of SSc-ILD.

Cost-effectiveness of sugemalimab plus chemotherapy versus placebo plus chemotherapy as first-line treatment for advanced oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma in China
Authors: Chunyan Yan, Zhengxiong Li, Jiayan Li, Ruiqin Huang, Shuqing Li, Ruigang Diao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveSugemalimab plus chemotherapy significantly prolonged progression-free survival and overall survival in patients with advanced oesophageal squamous-cell carcinoma (ESCC). However, considering the high cost of sugemalimab, we evaluated the cost-effectiveness of sugemalimab plus chemotherapy in the first-line treatment for advanced ESCC.DesignA partitioned survival model with a 3-week cycle length and the lifetime time horizon was constructed to assess the cost-effectiveness of sugemalimab plus chemotherapy versus placebo plus chemotherapy as first-line treatment for advanced ESCC. The key survival data, drug costs, and utility values were obtained from the GEMSTONE-304 trial, YAOZHI database and the published literature. Total costs, quality-adjusted life-years (QALYs) and incremental cost-effectiveness ratios (ICERs) were measured. One-way and probabilistic sensitivity analyses were performed to evaluate the uncertainty and model stability. In addition, subgroup and scenario analyses were conducted.SettingThe Chinese healthcare system perspective.ParticipantsA hypothetical Chinese cohort of patients with unresectable, locally advanced, recurrent or metastatic ESCC.InterventionsSugemalimab plus chemotherapy versus chemotherapy.Primary outcome measureCosts, QALYs, ICERs.ResultsCompared with the placebo plus chemotherapy, the sugemalimab plus chemotherapy provided an additional 0.36 QALYs, with an incremental cost of $44 376.88. The result of ICER was $119 400.69/QALY, which was significantly higher than the willingness-to-pay (WTP) threshold of three times per capita gross domestic product ($38 024.68/QALY) in 2023. Sensitivity analysis revealed that the cost of sugemalimab, the discount rate and the utility values were the most influential parameters on the base-case analysis results. Subgroup analyses showed that programmed cell death - ligand 1 subgroup ICERs exhibited dose-dependent efficacy: the ICERs for sugemalimab plus chemotherapy versus placebo plus chemotherapy were $138 739.23/QALY and $108 051.81/QALY in advanced ESCC patients with 1 ≤ combined positive score (CPS) <10 and CPS ≥10. Scenario analysis indicated that even with patient assistance programme, the total cost of the sugemalimab plus chemotherapy group ($23 474.25) would be $15 030.18 higher than that of the placebo plus chemotherapy group ($8444.07), which had an ICER of $40 440.28/QALY. When the price of sugemalimab decreased by 50% and 85%, the probabilities of sugemalimab plus chemotherapy being cost-effective were 0% and 100%, respectively.ConclusionFrom a Chinese healthcare system perspective, sugemalimab plus chemotherapy as first-line treatment for advanced ESCC might not be a cost-effective treatment option at the WTP threshold of $38 024.68/QALY.

Introduction

Oesophageal squamous-cell carcinoma (ESCC) is a malignant tumour originating from squamous cells of the oesophageal mucosa, which is the most common subtype of oesophageal cancer worldwide.1 It forms through the uncontrolled growth of abnormal cells that may eventually invade tissues outside the oesophagus or metastasise to other parts of the body via the blood and lymphatic systems. Globally, ESCC accounts for approximately 84% of all oesophageal cancer cases and is particularly common in Asian populations, which account for almost 90% of all oesophageal cancer cases.2 Advanced ESCC usually has a poor prognosis and short median survival, posing a serious threat to patients’ lives. Systemic chemotherapy is the mainstay of treatment for advanced or metastatic ESCC, and commonly used chemotherapy agents include 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) or paclitaxel plus platinum agents.3 However, despite these treatment options, the overall prognosis of advanced ESCC remains poor, with a median overall survival (OS) usually <1 year,4 highlighting the urgent need for new treatment strategies and antineoplastic agents.

With advances in immunotherapy, immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have become an important first-line treatment option for advanced ESCC.57 Among existing programmed cell death - ligand 1 (PD-L1) inhibitors, sugemalimab has garnered significant attention due to its unique mechanism of action and robust clinical evidence. Sugemalimab is a full-length, fully human IgG4 monoclonal antibody that not only effectively blocks the programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1)/PD-L1 pathway but also retains Fc-mediated antibody-dependent cellular phagocytosis effects. This dual mechanism may activate macrophages to produce antitumour activity, theoretically conferring potential advantages. More importantly, results from the Phase III GEMSTONE-304 study demonstrated that sugemalimab in combination with chemotherapy significantly extended progression-free survival (PFS) and OS in patients with advanced ESCC, with manageable safety.8 As a result, sugemalimab has become the first and only PD-L1 inhibitor worldwide to achieve positive results in a Phase III trial for first-line treatment of advanced ESCC and to receive regulatory approval for this indication, addressing a clinical gap in PD-L1-targeted therapy for this patient population. In addition, since PD-L1 monoclonal antibody acts on tumour cells and does not stimulate T-cells, it has an advantage over PD-1 monoclonal antibody in reducing the risk of immunotherapy-related adverse effects, especially for those with autoimmune diseases, which can greatly reduce the risk of death.

However, while innovative drugs deliver clinical breakthroughs, their high costs also impose a heavy economic burden on healthcare systems. Preliminary pharmacoeconomic evaluations suggest that the current incremental cost-effectiveness ratios (ICERs) of sugemalimab may exceed conventional willingness-to-pay (WTP) threshold, which was set at three times per capita gross domestic product (GDP).9 This situation underscores the urgency and importance of conducting an in-depth and precise cost-benefit assessment of the therapy. Currently, there remain gaps in this field of research, such as a lack of biomarker-based subgroup analyses, insufficient evaluation of the long-term survival benefits of immunotherapy and limited systematic exploration of innovative payment models. Therefore, this study aimed to systematically evaluate the cost-effectiveness of sugemalimab in the treatment of ESCC and to thoroughly investigate the aforementioned underexplored areas, thereby providing a scientific basis for rational drug use, optimised allocation of healthcare resources and informed healthcare policy decision-making.

Methods

The Consolidated Health Economic Evaluation Reporting Standards are used to report this study, and their updated reporting criteria are followed (online supplemental Table 1).10

Patients and treatment

This was a simulation-based study which involved the hypothetical cohort of patients based on characteristics reported in GEMSTONE-304 and elsewhere. Enrolled patients received sugemalimab 1200 mg or placebo intravenously every 3 weeks (Q3W) until disease progression, unaccepted toxicity or reaching maximum treatment duration (24 months) per protocol, in combination with six cycles of the fractional polynomial (FP) regimen (5-FU 800 mg/m2, D1-4; cisplatin 80 mg/m2, D1; Q3W).11 In the GEMSTONE-304 trial’s intention-to-treat (ITT) population, post-progression immunotherapy was administered to 78 (21.8%) and 51 (28.0%) patients in the sugemalimab-chemotherapy and placebo-chemotherapy groups, respectively. Similarly, second-line chemotherapy was given to 145 (40.5%) and 88 (48.4%) patients in these respective groups. Therefore, we assumed that the proportion of patients receiving different post-progression treatment regimens in the two cohorts of this study was consistent with that observed in the GEMSTONE-304 trial. Additionally, based on the recommendations of the Chinese Society of Clinical Oncology (CSCO) guidelines (2023), we assumed that patients with disease progression in this study could receive camrelizumab as the PD-1 inhibitor12 or docetaxel as chemotherapy,13 while the remaining patients would receive best supportive care (BSC). BSC was used when the disease had not progressed by the maximum treatment duration.14

Model construction

Several studies have indicated that partitioned survival analysis can be directly modelled using OS and PFS data from clinical trials and is capable of directly reflecting the time-dependent nature of event rates.15 16 Thus, this study used TreeAge software (TreeAge Pro 2020, https://www.treeage.com) to construct a three-state partitioned survival model that included PFS, progressive disease (PD) and death.17 The objective was to evaluate the economics of sugemalimab plus chemotherapy compared with placebo plus chemotherapy as a first-line strategy for the treatment of unresectable, locally advanced, recurrent or metastatic ESCC in China. In this model, we set the cycle length to 3 weeks based on dosing intervals in the GEMSTONE-304 trial, and the time horizon of the study was set as a lifetime horizon. All simulated patients were assumed to enter the model in the PFS state; they could transition to the PD state or the death state. The proportion of patients in different health states was obtained from the PFS curve and the OS curve. The basic-case analysis in this study focuses on total costs, quality-adjusted life-years (QALYs) and ICERs. The model structure is shown in figure 1. According to the recommendations of the “China Guidelines for Pharmacoeconomic Evaluations (2020)”, the discount rate for cost and health outcomes of this study was set at 5%,18 and the threshold of WTP was set at three times per capita GDP of China in 2023 ($38 024.68/QALY).19 20

Clinical data inputs

First, we used GetData Graph Digitiser software (V.2.24, https://getdata-graph-digitiser.com/) to extract data points from PFS and OS curves. Then we reconstructed individual patient data (IPD) using R software (V.4.2.3, http://www.r-project.org). Finally, Weibull, exponential, Gompertz, generalised gamma, gamma, log-logistic and log-normal distributions were used for fitting and extrapolating the PFS and OS curves based on the reconstructed IPD.21 The optimal fitting distributions for each group were chosen based on the visual inspection and lowest value of the Akaike information criterion and Bayesian information criterion.22 Additionally, we compared the median OS and PFS reported in the GEMSTONE-304 clinical trial with the median survival times calculated based on the seven fitted distributions. We further sought evaluations from clinical experts regarding the clinical plausibility of the results from different fitted distributions. Finally, the log-logistic distribution was chosen to fit the OS and PFS of the sugemalimab plus chemotherapy and placebo plus chemotherapy groups. The shape parameters (γ) and scale parameters (λ) of the optimal fitting distributions were summarised in online supplemental Table 3), and the goodness-of-fit results were shown in online supplemental Table 2). Long-term survival data were presented in online supplemental Figures 1 and 2.

Cost inputs

This study was conducted from the perspective of the Chinese healthcare system. Therefore, only the direct medical costs, including costs of drug treatment, costs of routine follow-up, costs of laboratory tests and radiological examinations, costs of hospitalisation, costs of BSC and costs for management of adverse events (AEs), were considered. Among them, the prices of drugs, such as sugemalimab, camrelizumab and docetaxel were obtained from the average winning bids of the YAOZHI database (https://data.yaozh.com/) in 2023.23 To calculate the dosage and costs of chemotherapy drugs, we assumed that the body surface area was 1.72 m2 for the ITT patients in this study.24 Other costs, such as the cost of BSC, routine follow-up, laboratory tests and radiological examinations, hospitalisation and daily care, were obtained from published literature.25 26 To simplify the model, only serious AEs with grade ≥3 and an incidence of ≥3% were included in this study.27 28 Additionally, the costs of AEs in this model were considered by multiplying the single-treatment costs by the incidence of AEs and assumed to be incurred only during the first cycle. All costs were converted to US$ at the average exchange rate in 2023 (1$=7.05 CNY). The cost-related data were shown in table 1.

Utility inputs

Each health state was assigned a health utility preference ranging from 0 (death) to 1 (perfect health). The health utility values in this partitioned survival model were 0.75 for the PFS state and 0.60 for the PD state, which were obtained from multiple economic evaluation.28 29 The disutility values for AEs of grade ≥3 were considered in this study, which were also extracted from published literature.25 26 All utility-related parameters were also shown in table 1.

Sensitivity analysis

In this study, the robustness of the model was examined using one-way sensitivity analysis (OWSA) and probabilistic sensitivity analysis (PSA). Each parameter was subjected to OWSA with upper and lower limits of 80%–120% of the base value, and the results were presented in the form of tornado diagrams.30 31 The discount rate was based on the “China Guidelines for Pharmacoeconomic Evaluations (2020)”, and the floating range was set from 0% to 8%.20 A total of 1000 second-order Monte Carlo simulations were run, following the parameter range and distribution, for the PSA.32 The data of costs followed the gamma distribution, whereas health utility values and AE rates followed the beta distribution. The results of the PSA are presented in the form of scatter plots and cost-effectiveness acceptability curves. The cost-effectiveness acceptability curve was illustrated to represent the probability that sugemalimab plus chemotherapy would be considered cost-effective at various WTP thresholds.

Subgroup analysis

To explore the influence of PD-L1 expression on the cost-effectiveness of first-line sugemalimab plus chemotherapy for advanced ESCC, we conducted cost-effectiveness analyses in different PD-L1 sub-populations using the Kaplan-Meier survival functions extracted from the GEMSTONE-304 trial. Patients were stratified according to CPS as follows: (1) 1 ≤ CPS < 10 (sugemalimab plus chemotherapy, n=163; placebo plus chemotherapy, n=83) and (2) CPS ≥10 (sugemalimab plus chemotherapy, n=154; placebo plus chemotherapy, n=78). A separate evaluation of the CPS<1 cohort was precluded by the limited sample size (sugemalimab plus chemotherapy, n=41; placebo plus chemotherapy, n=21), which yielded insufficient power for robust economic inference.

Scenario analysis

The following three scenario analyses were performed in this study:

Scenario analysis 1: sugemalimab is more expensive as it is not currently covered by China’s Medicare. This study analysed the cost-effectiveness of a patient assistance programme (PAP) scenario, as drug price significantly affects the results. The PAP of sugemalimab is ‘2+2, 2+25, 1+3’ means that patients can receive two cycles of free drug after purchasing two cycles, then obtain twenty-five additional cycles of assistance after purchasing another two cycles. ‘1+3’ means that patients can purchase one cycle of sugemalimab at their own expense and receive assistance until reaching maximum treatment duration (24 months). It is assumed that all patients in this scenario are eligible for PAP of sugemalimab.

Scenario analysis 2: we examined the effect of sugemalimab on ICERs at different prices. At the same time, considering the possible price reduction of sugemalimab in the future, we plugged into the model a 50% or 85% reduction in the price of sugemalimab, respectively.

Scenario analysis 3: we applied flexible parametric models, including FP, restricted cubic spline and Royston-Parmar (RP) spline models, to fit and extrapolate survival curves for both treatment groups, assessing whether the choice of modelling approach would substantially influence the outcomes. Ultimately, the RP spline model was selected for the final survival models of sugemalimab plus chemotherapy and chemotherapy groups, with detailed parameters provided in online supplemental Table 5.

Results

Base-case analysis results

The results of the base case were shown in online supplemental Table 4). The total cost and effectiveness of the placebo plus chemotherapy regimen was $8444.07 and 0.79 QALYs, while the total cost and effectiveness of the sugemalimab plus chemotherapy regimen was $52 820.95 and 1.16 QALYs. compared with the placebo plus chemotherapy regimen, the sugemalimab plus chemotherapy regimen provided an additional 0.37 QALYs, along with a total cost increase of $44 376.88. The result of ICER was $119 400.69/QALY, which was significantly higher than the WTP threshold ($38 024.68/QALY) set in this study (online supplemental Table 6).

Sensitivity analysis results

The OWSA was performed based on the lower and upper limits of the model variables in table 1, and the result was shown in figure 2. The factors that had the greatest impact on the ICER were the cost of sugemalimab, the discount rate, the utility values of PD and PFS. None of the parameter changes made the ICER less than the WTP threshold, confirming that the cost-effectiveness analysis results in this study were robust.

Subgroup analysis results

In the subgroup of patients with a PD-L1 expression level of 1 ≤ CPS < 10, the sugemalimab plus chemotherapy regimen resulted in 0.30 additional QALYs compared with the placebo plus chemotherapy, but at an increased cost of $42 283.67 and an ICER of $138 739.23/QALY (online supplemental Table 7). In the subgroup of patients with PD-L1 expression level of CPS ≥10, sugemalimab plus chemotherapy resulted in 0.44 more QALYs than placebo plus chemotherapy at an increased cost of $47 058.74, with the ICER of $108 051.81/QALY (online supplemental Table 7).

Scenario analysis results

Scenario analysis 1: even in the scenario of PAP, the total cost of the sugemalimab plus chemotherapy group ($23 474.25) would be $15 030.18 higher than that of the placebo plus chemotherapy group ($8444.07). The ICER was $40 440.28/QALY, which was still higher than three times GDP per capita in China. However, in the subgroup of PD-L1 expression level of CPS ≥10, the ICER was $36 295.73/QALY, which was lower than the WTP threshold of 3 times GDP per capita in China ($38 024.68/QALY) (online supplemental Table 7). When PAP of sugemalimab was considered, the probabilities of sugemalimab plus chemotherapy being cost-effective were 30.00%, 3.80% and 64.60% in advanced ESCC patients with ITT, PD-L1 1 ≤ CPS < 10 and CPS ≥10, respectively (figure 3).

Scenario analysis 2: the price reduction of sugemalimab has no effect on the cost or outcomes of the placebo plus chemotherapy group. When sugemalimab reduced prices by 50%, the total cost of the sugemalimab plus chemotherapy group was $31 772.10, and the ICER was $62 766.53/QALY. But when sugemalimab reduced prices by 85%, the total cost of the sugemalimab plus chemotherapy group was $17 037.95, and the ICER was $23 122.74/QALY, which was lower than the WTP threshold of 3 times GDP per capita in China. The probabilities of sugemalimab plus chemotherapy being cost-effective were 0% and 100%, respectively (figure 3).

Scenario analysis 3: the results of the fitting for the sugemalimab plus chemotherapy and placebo plus chemotherapy groups for the flexible parametric models were shown in online supplemental Figure 3) and online supplemental Table 4). When using the RP spline model, the sugemalimab plus chemotherapy group had an increase of 0.37 QALYs compared with the placebo plus chemotherapy group, but at an increased cost of $44 444.83, with an ICER of $120 302.74/QALY (online supplemental Table 7). The results from the flexible parametric models showed good consistency with the base-case analysis results, indicating that the survival model selection did not significantly influence the results and conclusions.

Discussion

With the intensive development of antitumour drugs, ICIs represented by PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors have greatly improved the prognosis and survival of patients and met the treatment needs of cancer patients, which has become an important means of cancer treatment. However, improved outcomes for cancer patients often come at the cost of high treatment costs.33 The huge economic burden faced by cancer patients has a large impact on both the quality of survival and life satisfaction of patients. In addition, the high cost of cancer treatment also poses a challenge to China’s medical insurance fund. Therefore, it is necessary to find high-value, innovative oncology drug treatment options that are reasonably priced and affordable to patients.

Based on this, from the perspective of the Chinese healthcare system, this study predicted the long-term survival of ESCC patients based on a standard parametric survival model to evaluate the long-term economic outcomes of sugemalimab plus chemotherapy as first-line treatment for advanced ESCC patients. Compared with the placebo plus chemotherapy group, sugemalimab plus chemotherapy achieved an additional 0.37 QALYs with a total cost increase of $44 376.88, which resulted in an ICER of $119 400.69/QALY gained in advanced ESCC patients. Additionally, we employed flexible parametric survival models to extrapolate long-term survival outcomes in this study. The resulting ICER was $120 302.74 per QALY, which is closely aligned with the result obtained from the standard parametric model. Thus, the results showed that the sugemalimab plus chemotherapy regimen improved patients’ health outcomes compared with the placebo plus chemotherapy regimen, but at an increased cost. At the current prices and WTP threshold ($38 024.68/QALY), sugemalimab plus chemotherapy might not be cost-effective. The results of the OWSA showed that the price of sugemalimab was the most influential parameter, but it had no effect on the overall economic result when it fluctuated within the set range of this study. However, sugemalimab has been approved and marketed for a relatively short period of time, and it is normal for cost-effectiveness analyses using its initial market price to find that it is not economically viable in combination with chemotherapy for the first-line treatment of ESCC. There have been six studies evaluating the economics of sugemalimab plus chemotherapy for the first-line treatment of patients with metastatic non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) in China.3439 The results of five of these studies have shown that in the first-line treatment of metastatic NSCLC, sugemalimab combination therapy was not economically advantageous, which was consistent with our results.

Currently, in addition to sugemalimab, multiple PD-1 inhibitors have been approved in China for first-line treatment of advanced ESCC, including camrelizumab, toripalimab and sintilimab. Interestingly, from the perspective of Chinese payers, different PD-1 inhibitors demonstrate entirely distinct cost-effectiveness profiles in the first-line treatment of advanced ESCC. Consistent with our findings, the study by Zhang et al24 showed that the ICER for camrelizumab+chemotherapy versus chemotherapy alone was $46 671.10 per QALY, which exceeds WTP threshold. Two other studies by Wu et al40 and Zheng et al,41 which analysed the cost-effectiveness of pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy versus placebo plus chemotherapy in first-line treatment of oesophageal cancer in Chinese patients, yielded results consistent with our study, with ICERs of $115 391.84/QALY and $41 805.12/QALY, respectively, both exceeding the WTP thresholds. Conversely, studies by Liu et al27 and Shao et al42 both demonstrated that, compared with chemotherapy, the ICERs for sintilimab combined with chemotherapy were $21 782.24 per QALY and $30 409.44 per QALY, respectively, both below the WTP threshold. Additionally, the study by Fang et al43 demonstrated that compared with chemotherapy, toripalimab plus paclitaxel and cisplatin also represents a cost-effective option, with an ICER of $28 348.42 per QALY, which falls below WTP threshold. The inconsistencies in these research findings may be attributed to significant cost variations among different PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors. In recent years, to compete in China’s anti-tumour drug market, an increasing number of ICIs have entered the National Reimbursement Drug List (NRDL) through price reductions. For instance, after inclusion in the NRDL, the price of sintilimab decreased by 86.2% compared with its initial launch price, while toripalimab saw a reduction of 88.7%.44 Therefore, this study further analysed the economic impact of combining sugemalimab with chemotherapy for advanced ESCC patients after an 85% price reduction for sugemalimab. The results showed that with an 85% price reduction for sugemalimab, the total cost for the sugemalimab plus chemotherapy group was $17 037.95, with an ICER of $23 122.74/QALY, which is below the WTP threshold. The probability of cost-effectiveness for this combination regimen as first-line treatment for advanced ESCC reached 100%. This indicates that to enhance the cost-effectiveness of sugemalimab plus chemotherapy in first-line treatment for advanced ESCC, substantial price reductions through medical insurance negotiations may be necessary to facilitate its inclusion in the NRDL.

Based on the results of the OWSA, it can be seen that the utility values of PD and PFS are also important factors affecting the results of the analysis. In the present study, patients with advanced ESCC obtained lower incremental QALYs (0.37 QALYs), which is consistent with the results of previous related studies. This may be because the survival benefits between different treatment options are relatively small in the treatment of patients with advanced cancer. In terms of ICIs in first-line combination therapy for patients with ESCC, most published economics studies have shown that immunotherapy in combination with chemotherapy produces an incremental QALY that is usually <1, and therefore, this can also lead to higher ICER results.24 27 45 46

PD-L1 is frequently overexpressed in ESCC and serves as a significant positive predictive biomarker for the efficacy of ICIs.47 48 Thus, PD-L1 expression is strongly associated with therapeutic outcomes of immunotherapy in ESCC. Previous economic studies suggest that PD-1 inhibitors are potentially sensitive in patients with higher PD-L1 levels, with greater survival benefits and better health outcomes than in the overall population.424951 The results of the subgroup analysis in this study showed that compared with placebo plus chemotherapy, sugemalimab plus chemotherapy gained an additional 0.30 and 0.44 QALYs at incremental costs of $42 283.67 and $47 058.74, yielding ICERs of $138 739.23/QALY and $108 051.81/QALY in advanced ESCC patients with PD-L1 expression levels of 1 ≤ CPS < 10 and CPS ≥10, respectively. This suggests that sugemalimab may be more effective in patients with PD-L1 expression levels. However, under the WTP threshold in this study, there was still a 0% probability that sugemalimab plus chemotherapy was cost-effective compared with placebo plus chemotherapy in either the ITT population or in advanced ESCC patients stratified by PD-L1 expression. When the sugemalimab PAP was included, the economic results in the PD-L1 CPS ≥10 subgroup were reversed, indicating that sugemalimab plus chemotherapy became the cost-effective option under the charitable-donation scenario. It is recommended that clinical management of advanced ESCC should tailor treatment regimens according to PD-L1 expression levels.

Finally, the WTP threshold is a key parameter for assessing cost-effectiveness. Although both the WHO and China Guidelines for Pharmacoeconomic Evaluations recommend 1–3 times GDP per capita as the WTP threshold, there is general agreement that the threshold for cancer treatment should exceed that for general diseases. Therefore, existing studies suggested that a moderately higher WTP threshold may be more appropriate for evaluating the cost-effectiveness of cancer therapies in real-world settings.52 53 For example, although WTP is defined as $50 000/QALY in the US, the WTP threshold is usually set between $100 000/QALY and $150 000/QALY when cost-effectiveness evaluations of anticancer drugs from the perspective of the US healthcare system.54 At present, some Chinese scholars have also proposed that anticancer drugs warrant a higher WTP relative to other drugs. In our study, it was found that when the WTP threshold was set at $150 000/QALY, the probability that it is cost-effective for Chinese patients with advanced ESCC rose to 98.4%. This bodes well for improving the economic prospects of antineoplastic drugs, driven by future economic development and policy improvements.

Limitation

This study still has some limitations. First, since the original data could not be obtained from the published literature, we reconstructed the IPD based on the OS and PFS Kaplan-Meier curves from the GEMSTONE-304 trial. By fitting the reported parametric distributions, we extrapolated clinical benefit data beyond the observed trial period. Although this approach is not perfect, it broadly reflects the actual survival data observed in the clinical trial. Additionally, we employed flexible parametric models to validate the modelled and observed data. Second, because the clinical trial cited in this study (GEMSTONE-304) did not publish Kaplan-Meier curves for time to treatment discontinuation, we were unable to simulate actual discontinuation or death times. Third, the GEMSTONE-304 clinical trial did not announce a subsequent treatment plan. We assumed that the patients’ subsequent treatment plan would be camrelizumab or docetaxel according to the CSCO Oesophageal Cancer Diagnosis and Treatment Guidelines, which may also be different from the treatment plans in the real world. Finally, because GEMSTONE-304 did not collect health-utility data, the utility values of PFS and PD as well as disutilities for AEs in this study were obtained from other literatures, which may lead to some bias in the results of the cost-effectiveness analysis. However, the results of the OWSA showed that the variation of various utility parameters within their floating ranges had little effect on the overall economic results, so the results of this study still have some reference value.

Conclusion

In summary, from the perspective of the Chinese healthcare system, sugemalimab plus chemotherapy might not be considered cost-effectiveness in the first-line treatment for patients with advanced ESCC. Even in the scenario of PAP, the probabilities of sugemalimab plus chemotherapy being cost-effective were 30.00%, 3.80% and 64.60% in patients with advanced ESCC in the ITT, PD-L1 1 ≤ CPS <10 and CPS ≥10 subgroups, respectively. Thus, substantial price reductions could improve cost-effectiveness of sugemalimab. After the 85% price reduction of sugemalimab, the probability of sugemalimab plus chemotherapy being cost-effective was 100% in patients with advanced ESCC. The findings may provide evidence that informs future price negotiations for sugemalimab in China.

Innovative 3D Navigation Module for Precise Unilateral Pedicle Screw Combined with Contralateral Translaminar Facet Screw Placement in Lumbar Spine Surgery
Authors: Xu Chen, Hanhao Dai, Jun Luo, Huaizhi Zhang, Chao Song, Zhibo Deng, Yanyan Zhang, Xing Li, Jianhui Dai, Jie Xu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThe incidence of degenerative diseases of the lumbar spine has increased in recent years. Unilateral pedicle screw combined with contralateral translaminar facet screw fixation offers the advantages of less trauma, better stability, and fewer complications. However, the surgical difficulty and suboptimal pinning accuracy of translaminar facet screw placement in clinical practice limit its use. Therefore, in this study, we designed a novel suspended 3D‐printed navigation module to facilitate fast and accurate intraoperative screw placement. The aim of this study is to investigate the digital design, precise implementation, and evaluation methods for placing unilateral pedicle screws in the lumbar spine combined with translaminar facet screw placement using a new suspended 3D navigation module.MethodsThis retrospective study included 46 patients with single‐level lumbar lesions who underwent spine surgery at the Affiliated Hospital of Putian University between June 2022 and December 2023. The suspended navigation module was designed digitally. Preoperative screw placement was simulated using 3D printed models, followed by an intraoperative accurate screw placement facilitated by the navigation module and a postoperative evaluation of the accuracy of screw placement. The absolute difference in three‐dimensional coordinates of the inlet and outlet points of the preoperative design and the postoperative screw‐nail channel served as the precision index.ResultsIn a study involving 46 patients, surgery was successful with 92 pedicle screws and 46 translaminar facet screws placed without any penetration of the cortex. The difference in coordinates before and after screw insertion was minimal, with entry points varying between 1.21 to 1.36 mm and exit points between 1.97 to 2.46 mm. When screw accuracy met certain thresholds, there was no significant difference between preoperative design and postoperative coordinates, indicating precise replication of the surgical plan.ConclusionThe new suspended 3D navigation module enables the precise placement of unilateral pedicle screws in the lumbar spine combined with translaminar pedicle screws for precise surgery.

Introduction

Traditional surgical approaches for lumbar degenerative diseases and instability involve procedures such as posterior lumbar decompression, pedicle screw fixation, and interbody fusion.1, 2 However, these methods, particularly posterior lumbar interbody fusion (PLIF) and transforaminal lumbar interbody fusion (TLIF), are associated with significant trauma and complications.3, 4, 5, 6 Minimally invasive TLIF (MIS‐TLIF) presents advantages such as reduced trauma, less bleeding, and quicker recovery.7, 8, 9

Internal fixation is crucial for stability post‐surgery, but choosing between unilateral pedicle screw (UPS) and bilateral pedicle screws (BPS) remains contentious due to their respective drawbacks. UPS has low biomechanical strength and poor rotational resistance and has some disadvantages in terms of clinical complications and biomechanical stability compared with BPS10, 11, 12; BPS provides extremely strong internal fixation, which can maintain excellent mechanical stability for the spine. However, because of its overly strong internal fixation, it produces stress masking, which affects intervertebral fusion13, 14 and may also accelerate lumbar degeneration of adjacent segments.15, 16, 17 These clinical concerns warrant attention.

To address these concerns, Huang and Mao's team developed a hybrid method (UPS + TLFS), which combines UPS with contralateral translaminar facet screws, reducing trauma and achieving satisfactory outcomes.18, 19 However, challenges persist due to the complexity of vertebral plate anatomy and the reliance on operator experience during pedicle screw placement, limiting their widespread use.

Therefore, we designed a new suspended 3D‐printed navigation module for MIS‐TLIF, which facilitates fast and accurate intraoperative pedicle screw placement. The purpose of this study is to:(i) design a novel suspended 3D navigation module to assist in the placement of unilateral pedicle screws combined with contralateral translaminar facet screws in the lumbar spine；(ii) verify the safety and accuracy of screw placement on the 3D printed model before surgery; and (iii) use the 3D navigation module for precise screw placement during surgery and evaluate the effect of screw placement postoperatively, to provide better guidance for the clinical development of this operation.

Materials and Methods

Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

Inclusion Criteria

(i) Patients with single‐segment lumbar spine lesions, unilateral lower extremity symptoms with or without lower back pain, and ineffective conservative treatment for 3–6 months; (ii) Patients with lumbar degenerative disc disease, single‐segment lumbar degenerative slips (Meyerding I degree), giant lumbar herniated discs, and lumbar herniated discs with spinal stenosis (lateral fossa type, which does not require total laminectomy or bilateral lateral fossa decompression); (iii) Patients with imaging compatible with clinical symptoms and signs; (iv) Using 3D printed navigation module assisted screw placement.

Exclusion Criteria

Patients with bilateral lumbar isthmus clefts; Patients with lumbar fracture instability; Patients with severe osteoporosis; Patients with severe obesity (BMI ≥ 35); and Patients with inconsistency between imaging data and clinical presentation.

Patient Information

We retrospectively enrolled and evaluated 46 patients with single‐segment lumbar lesions who underwent suspended 3D navigation module‐assisted surgical treatment between June 2022 and December 2023. All the surgeries were performed by the same group of physicians. The ethics committee approval was obtained from the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Putian University (No: 202165).

Preoperative Digital Design

Computed Tomography (CT) Scanning and 3D Reconstruction

CT scans were conducted with a layer spacing of 0.625 mm, voltage of 120 kV, and current of 54 mA. DICOM images (512 × 512 pixels) were generated and imported to Mimics 14.0 software (Materialise, Belgium), for three‐dimensional reconstruction (Calculate 3D). This process enabled the creation of a three‐dimensional model of the lumbar spine, facilitating observation from multiple angles and directions (Figure 1A,B).

Suspended 3D navigation module design. (A,B) 3D reconstructions. (C,D) The designed pedicle screw path and translaminar facet screw path. (E) Rear view of the navigation module and screw path in the transparent view. (F) Side view of the module and screw path in the transparent view.

Optimal Screw Path Designation

The Create Cylinder function was used to create two 7.0 mm pedicle screws and one 5.0 mm translaminar facet screw for the virtual nail trajectory simulation. The screws were placed in the optimal position by sequential movement of the navigation (Figure 1C,D).

Suspended 3D Navigation Module Designation

The navigation module substrate was designed based on the visible bone surface encountered in the MIS‐TLIF surgical approach. The navigation module consists of positioning modules, guideposts, and support columns. Through Boolean subtraction, a prototype of the navigation module was generated with unnecessary parts removed and module edges trimmed, resulting in a navigation module equipped with guideposts (Figure 1E,F).

Preoperative Simulation Surgery

3D Printed Solid Models of the Lumbar Spine and Navigation Module

The suspended 3D navigation module was firmly affixed onto the posterior bone surface of the lumbar spine. Pressure was applied to secure the module in place, and a Kirschner pin guide was carefully inserted into the bone through the navigation module channel. Following drilling to the appropriate depth using an electric drill, the navigation template was removed. Subsequently, the hole was reamed with a hollow tap and screwed to the measured length and diameter of the screw in the direction of the nail channel (Figure 2A,B). Postoperative radiographs and CT scans were performed to evaluate the effect of screw placement.

Operation Method

Following successful anesthesia, the patient was positioned prone on the operating table. A longitudinal incision, approximately 4 cm in length, was made 2 cm to the left of the L4/5 spinous process line to expose the inferior articular process of the upper vertebra, superior articular process of the lower vertebra, and lamina. The soft tissue covering the base of the navigation module was removed by observing the 3D printed solid model. Subsequently, the suspended 3D‐printed navigation module was securely placed against the bone surface. The guidewire was prepositioned after firmly positioning the navigation module. Upon confirming the accuracy with C‐arm fluoroscopy, pedicle screws were placed on the same side as the incision. Following this, the connecting rod was installed and secured. After reduction, the intervertebral space was addressed and decompressed, and the nerve roots were released. Interbody fusion cage insertion followed. Subsequently, the TLFS guidewire, drill, and tap were removed, and screws of appropriate length were inserted through the incision into the opposite lamina and tightened for fixation (Figure 3C).

3D Registration and Data Acquisition

Postoperative thin‐slice CT scans were obtained, and 3D reconstruction was performed using the Mimics software. This construction combined the postoperative lumbar spine with the 3D models of all screws (Simulation\Merge). The resulting STL file was exported to a preoperatively designed mask. Preliminary alignment with the originally designed 3D model of the lumbar spine was performed using the Registration\Point Registration command, and automatic alignment was performed using the Registration\Global Registration command to maximize the overlap of the two models and observe the alignment effect. If necessary, we used the Move or Rotate commands to fine‐tune the alignment manually (Figure 4B).

We used the Simulation\Split command to split the postoperative model into lumbar vertebrae and screws (Figure 4C). We displayed the position of the postoperative screws in the preoperative lumbar vertebrae (Figure 4D). We observed the correspondence between the preoperative designed nail trajectory and postoperative screws (Figure 4E). Furthermore, we constructed the preoperative and postoperative screw entry and exit points at the intersection between the nail paths and originally designed vertebral bone surfaces using MedCAD\Point and exported the 3D coordinate values of each screw point to Excel using the Export txt command. Finally, we used the Export text command to export the 3D coordinate values of each nail point to Excel (Figure 4F).

Statistical Analysis

The absolute value of the difference between the preoperative and postoperative data was taken and screened at intervals of every 0.1 mm, starting from 0.4 mm. The number of qualified nails placed (number of occurrences), as well as the number of unqualified nails placed (number of nonoccurrences), was counted under different precision requirements, and the four‐cell table chi‐square test was performed with SPSS Version 20.0 (SPSS Software, Chicago, IL, USA), and a nonsignificant difference was considered as p > 0.05.

Results

Effect of Screw Placement in Preoperative Simulated Operation

In the preoperative simulation surgery, the navigation module has a good match effect with the 3D model of lumbar spine. Postoperative radiographs and CT showed that the postoperative effect of navigation module assisted screw placement was highly consistent with the preoperative design (Figure 2C–F).

Patients

Forty‐six patients with single‐segment lumbar spine lesions were included in this study, with 26 cases of lumbar disc herniation, 12 of lumbar spinal stenosis, and eight of lumbar spondylolisthesis. The mean age was 57 years (43–67 years), with 22 males and 24 females. All of the patients were followed up.

Accuracy of Screw Placement in Clinical Surgery

The procedure was successful in all 46 cases, with the navigation module securely anchored. A total of 92 pedicle screws and 46 translaminar facet screws were successfully placed without bone penetration. The absolute difference in the X, Y, and Z coordinates of entry points before and after pedicle screw insertion points were 1.21 ± 0.59, 1.24 ± 0.63, and 1.27 ± 0.59 mm, respectively. The corresponding values for exit points were 1.97 ± 0.80, 2.15 ± 0.83, and 2.04 ± 0.85 mm, respectively. The absolute difference in the X, Y, and Z coordinates of entry points before and after transforaminal pedicle screw insertion points were 1.21 ± 0.58, 1.36 ± 0.69, and 1.30 ± 0.65 mm, while for exit points were 2.42 ± 0.91, 2.46 ± 0.97, and 2.40 ± 0.95 mm, respectively. When the entry point accuracy of the pedicle screw was ≥2.2 mm, and the exit point accuracy was ≥3.5 mm, and when the entry point accuracy of the translaminar facet screw was ≥2.4 mm and the exit point accuracy was ≥3.8 mm, there was no significant difference in the coordinates before and after the operation (Table 1). Intraoperative Indicators

The operative time was 113.43 ± 8.26 min, intraoperative bleeding was 136.26 ± 36.52 mL, and intraoperative radiation exposure time was 52 ± 12 s.

Efficacy

At the final follow‐up, the JOA score increased from preoperative 9.3 ± 2.8 to 23.9 ± 3.3. The treatment efficacy was evaluated according to the criteria of the JOA Lower Back Pain Scoring System: excellent in 29 cases, good in 15, and possible in two, with an excellent rate of 95.7%.

Complications

No screw penetration through the cortex and no complications related to nail placement were observed. Furthermore, no adverse events, such as nail fracture, screw loosening, or infection, occurred by the end of the follow‐up period.

Discussion

Main Findings

The study demonstrated that the 3D‐printed navigation module improves the accuracy of screw placement, has the advantages of fewer complications and better efficacy, and helps to promote recovery of lumbar spine function in patients. Additionally, it eliminates the need for repeated intraoperative fluoroscopy and reduces radiation exposure to patients and surgeons.

Internal Fixation of TLFS+UPS Provides Stable Fixation

The TLFS proposed by Magerl in 1984 20 involves placing a tension nail from the root of the contralateral spinous process through the vertebral plate, the articular process, to the base of the transverse process. TLFS also requires fewer internal fixations, lowers the risk of screw loosening, and offers moderate modulus of elasticity. 21 Schleicher et al. 22 investigated the biomechanics of lumbar flexion, extension, lateral bending, and rotation and found comparable strength between TLFS+UPS and BPS fixation, with no significant difference. Gong et al. 23 demonstrated through finite element analysis that TLFS+UPS provided superior stability and reduced pressure compared to UPS fixation or BPS fixation. Luo et al. 24 found TLFS+UPS achieved fixation strength similar to BPS while reducing the impact on adjacent segment mobility, theoretically lowering the incidence of adjacent segment degeneration. Sethi et al. 25 followed up 19 patients with internal fixation of TLFS+UPS for 15–54 months and observed improved clinical outcomes and reduced treatment costs. In our study involving 46 patients, TLFS+UPS + interbody fusion preserved the normal status of the contralateral paravertebral muscles, contralateral interspinous and supraspinous ligaments, and posterior lumbar ligamentous structure. At the 12‐month follow‐up, all cases showed well‐positioned pedicle and facet screws with successful interbody fusion and no adjacent segment degeneration was observed, which was consistent with the results observed by Sethi et al.

TLFS Placement Is Challenging

However, the surgical technique for TLFS placement requires a high level of expertise, including a deep understanding of anatomy and surgical skills. Screw placement accuracy is often suboptimal due to reliance on the surgeon's experience and tactile feedback, along with the need for frequent fluoroscopy and repeated adjustments. Shim et al. 26 performed percutaneous placement of TLFS under radiographic guidance in 20 patients with lumbar degenerative diseases. Of the 65 screws placed, seven deviated from the facet bone, but no neurological complications occurred. Therefore, TLFS placement represents a clinical challenge.

Benefits of 3D Printed Navigation Modules

The 3D printing‐based navigation module offers design flexibility, radiation‐free operation, and cost‐effectiveness. Unlike traditional C‐arm fluoroscopic screw placement methods, this technology allows detailed preoperative planning on a computer and simulation of screw entry point, guide pin angle, and screw placement length. A 1:1 lumbar spine model with the navigation module was created using 3D printing, facilitating preoperative simulation and intraoperative guidance for screw placement. This approach reduces the difficulty of surgery and is clinically beneficial.27, 28 While Shao et al. 29 introduced a 3D‐printed navigation module for UPS + TLFS surgery, their design lacked consideration for minimally invasive surgical approaches, like the MIS‐TLIF procedure. Their module required extensive intraoperative exposure of bone surfaces, limiting its suitability for MIS‐TLIF and lacking an assessment of screw placement accuracy. Therefore, we designed a new suspended 3D‐printed navigation module suitable for MIS‐TLIF surgery, and the results of the study showed that the navigation module was easy to operate intraoperatively and achieved accurate screw placement.

The Precision Evaluation of Screw Placement in the 3D Navigation Module

To assess the accuracy of nail placement compared to preoperative design, this study utilized the absolute difference in entry and exit point coordinates before and after surgery. The precision evaluation index was determined based on the qualification rates across different accuracy levels. As illustrated in Figure 5, a broader accuracy range was related to a lower accuracy requirement, resulting in a higher qualification rate for the entry and exit pinpoints. At specific precision levels, surgical outcomes were considered consistent with the design if no statistically significant differences were found. The results indicate a high level of consistency between postoperative outcomes and preoperative design in the Mimics software, allowing for precise implementation of the surgical design and affirming the precise execution of the surgical plan facilitated by the new suspended 3D‐printed navigation module. In addition, according to a meta‐analysis by Lu et al., 30 3D‐printed guided molding technology is more accurate and safer than hand‐drawn technology. Zhu et al. 31 reported no statistically significant difference in nail placement satisfaction between a 3D printing group (97.18%) and a robotic group (96.8%); however, the nail placement time for the 3D‐printed personalized guide was shorter. Based on these findings, we believe that the 3D‐printed module can achieve fast and accurate screw placement.

Design Essentials of the 3D Navigation Module

The accuracy of the nail placement depends on the alignment capability of the navigation module with a specific position on the bone surface. For the 46 patients in this study, a preoperative three‐dimensional lumbar spine model of the target segment of the lumbar spine was reconstructed using Mimics software to simulate pedicle screw placement and design the navigation module, which consisted of three components: the seat module, nail channel, and support column. The design of the seat module is based on the bone surface structure accessible during surgery, which is a combination of the concavity and convexity of the bone surface and the range of soft tissues that can be easily peeled off to avoid the attachment points of the tendon, vital blood vessels, and nerves. The position of the screws in the pedicle and vertebral plate can be visually observed using Mimics software to optimize the optimal diameter, length, and orientation of the screws. The suspended column is designed to detach from the surgical approach's limited area. It allows suspension without direct bone contact, thereby reducing the area of bone detachment.

3D Navigation Module Operating Points During Surgery

Accurate screw placement requires precise surgical design and implementation. Some of the principles for achieving accurate implementation are highlighted below. Positioning the navigation module during surgery: During surgery, it is important to remove the soft tissues covering the base of the navigation module and identify the unique positioning location. Once the optimal positioning site is identified, guide pins for pedicle screws are inserted first, followed by those for facet joint screws. In cases where facet joint screw angles are steep, a minor percutaneous incision may be necessary prior to guide pin insertion.

Limitations and Prospects

The limitations of our study include the following: The accuracy of nail placement using the floating navigation module relies heavily on proper positioning, as a mismatch between the navigation module and bone surface may result in screw misalignment and subsequent iatrogenic injury. The sample size was relatively small, and the follow‐up data were not comprehensive. Therefore, further research with larger sample sizes in clinical practice is required for more reliable results. Once the use of individualized 3D‐printed navigation templates for the lumbar spine is well‐established, future research should focus on templates for other minimally invasive and higher surgical risk areas, such as percutaneous navigation templates, cervical or thoracic spine navigation templates. This will help reduce intraoperative tissue invasion, decrease pain and risk of surgery, further reduce the cost of surgery, and accelerate patient recovery.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this study effectively evaluated nail placement outcomes using precision and accuracy criteria, showing a high level of consistency between actual surgeries and preoperative designs. Ultimately, the use of the hanging 3D navigation module facilitated precise screw placement, indicating its potential value for clinical application.

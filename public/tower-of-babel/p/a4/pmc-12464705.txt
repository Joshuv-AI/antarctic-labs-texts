Transcriptional landscape of the kidney in chickens infected by virulent or avirulent infectious bronchitis virus☆
Authors: Mengjiao Huang, Lin Chen, Yufei Ma, Budunzhu Luo, Xinran Li, Qiujin Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractInfectious Bronchitis Virus (IBV), a γ-coronavirus, is responsible for respiratory disease, renal injury, and reproductive disorders in poultry. This study presents a comparative transcriptomic profiling of chicken kidney infected with either the virulent GD17/04 strain or the attenuated 4/91 vaccine strain of IBV. RNA sequencing and functional enrichment analyses revealed that both strains initially activate antiviral sensing pathways. However, the virulent strain subsequently provoked sustained and amplified immune activation, involving NF-κB signaling, Jak-STAT signaling, and adaptive immune responses, culminating in severe renal dysregulation. In contrast, infection with the vaccine strain elicited a tempered immune response, accompanied by enhanced metabolic homeostasis and tissue repair processes. Through WGCNA, we identified a virulence-specific immune module (turquoise) and a renal development-associated module (yellow) linked to virus-induced nephropathy for GD17/04, alongside a balanced immune regulatory module (brown) for the 4/91 strain. Intersection analysis further revealed that the virulent strain induced a broader spectrum of immune activation, whereas the vaccine elicited a more restrained response, largely encompassed within that of the virulent infection—including shared pathways such as NOD-like receptor signaling. Among 69 core genes commonly upregulated in a virulence- and time-dependent manner by both strains, protein-protein interaction analysis identified RSAD2, ZNFX1, and TRIM25 as central hub genes. Functional investigations established TRIM25 as a pro-viral host factor: its knockdown significantly suppressed viral replication, whereas its overexpression exacerbated viral propagation. This work provides the first WGCNA-based transcriptional landscape of renal infection by IBV, elucidating virulence-dependent immune signatures and underscoring the pivotal role of TRIM25 in viral pathogenesis.Keywords: Infectious bronchitis virus, Transcriptomics, Immunity, Virulence

Introduction

IBV, an early discovered coronavirus belonging to the γ-family, is a highly contagious pathogen that poses a significant threat to the global poultry industry. Characterized by its ability to infect multiple organ systems, IBV primarily targets the respiratory tract, kidneys, and reproductive organs of chickens (Jing et al., 2023). The pathogenicity of IBV is strain-dependent, with virulent strains such as GD17/04 causing severe renal lesions and systemic damage (Huang et al., 2021). In contrast, attenuated vaccine strains, such as 4/91, induce milder immune responses while still conferring protection. Currently, vaccination remains the most effective control measure (Alluwaimi et al., 2020; Smialek et al., 2016). While vaccines are widely utilized to mitigate the impact of IBV, the emergence of novel variants and the incomplete cross-protection between strains highlight the necessity for a deeper understanding of host-pathogen interactions and the molecular mechanisms underlying virulence and attenuation.

A recent study has focused on the respiratory tropism of IBV, particularly through tracheal transcriptome profiling conducted both in vitro and in vivo, emphasizing the modulation of host immune responses (Kelsey et al., 2024). Previous studies have provided valuable yet partial insights into IBV nephropathogenesis. Early transcriptome profiling of IBV-infected chicken kidneys revealed differentially expressed genes but offered relatively limited functional annotation and did not include comparative analysis across strains with differing virulence (Cong et al., 2013). A subsequent study primarily focused on apoptosis induction during nephropathogenic IBV infection, contributing mechanistic insight into cell death processes but offering less coverage of broader immune alterations (Hui et al., 2017). The role of the TLR7/NF-κB signaling axis in renal injury was reported using RT-qPCR, highlighting a specific immune mechanism; however, this approach did not employ system-level transcriptomic analyses (Li et al., 2022a). More recently, single-cell RNA sequencing analysis was applied to characterize infected cell types and intercellular communication, which systematically examined cells in kidney, bursal, and tracheal tissues following IBV infection, emphasizing similarities and differences in viral expression patterns across target organs (Chengyin et al., 2024). Systematic comparisons of transcriptional responses between virulent and vaccine strains within the kidney are lacking, thereby limiting insights into the molecular determinants of virulence and vaccine-induced protection.

To address this gap, this study employs RNA sequencing to delineate the dynamic transcriptional landscape of chicken kidneys infected with the virulent GD17/04 strain and the attenuated 4/91 vaccine strain. By integrating functional enrichment analysis, WGCNA, and protein-protein interaction networks, we identify virulence-specific immune modules and core regulatory genes governing renal pathology. Intersection analysis highlights shared and strain-specific pathways, revealing that the virulent IBV engages a broader spectrum of immune pathways compared to the vaccine strain. Notably, 69 core genes were consistently perturbed by both strains, including TRIM25, which has been confirmed as a central regulator of IBV replication through experimental validation.

Materials and methods

Virus, animals, and experimental infection

The IBV-GD17/04 strain (GenBank number MT701511) was isolated in China in 2017 and has since been maintained in our laboratory (Huang et al., 2021). The 4/91 vaccine strain was obtained from MSD Animal Health Products Co., LTD. Ten-day-old SPF chickens were purchased from Guangdong Dahuannong Animal Health Products Co., LTD. For a comprehensive description of the experimental infection procedure, please refer to our previously published proteomics study (Huang et al., 2023). Chickens in group G were inoculated with the GD17/04 strain, while those in group V received the 4/91 vaccine strain. Group C, serving as the control group, was administered an equivalent volume of PBS in the same manner. At 3, 5, and 7 days post-inoculation (dpi), the kidneys of the chickens were harvested and immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen for subsequent analysis.

All live virus experiments were conducted in an enhanced animal biosafety level 2 (ABSL-2+) facility at the National Research Center for Veterinary Medicine (NVC). Animal studies were carried out following the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals issued by the Ministry of Science and Technology of China and were approved by the NVC Ethics Committee.

Sequencing experimental procedure

Total RNA was extracted from chicken kidney using Trizol reagent (Invitrogen), in accordance with the manufacturer's protocol. To eliminate potential DNA contamination, the isolated RNA was treated with DNase I (Thermo Fisher Scientific). The concentration and purity of the extracted RNA were assessed using a Nanodrop 2000, while RNA integrity was evaluated through agarose gel electrophoresis, with the RIN value determined using an Agilent 2100. For the construction of a single library, a minimum of 1 µg of total RNA was required. The RNA concentration needed to be ≥ 35 ng/μL, with an OD260/280 ratio of ≥ 1.8 and an OD260/230 ratio of ≥ 2.0. Eukaryotic mRNA 3′ ends possess a poly(A) tail structure. By employing magnetic beads with Oligo(dT) that base pairs with poly(A), mRNA can be isolated from total RNA for transcriptome analysis. The mRNA was fragmented using a fragmentation buffer, and small fragments of approximately 300 bp were separated using magnetic bead screening. Subsequently, double-strand cDNA synthesis was performed, utilizing six-base random hexamers to invert the mRNA template. The double-stranded cDNA structure possesses a sticky end, which was converted into a blunt end by adding the End Repair Mix. An 'A' base was added to the 3′ end for the Y-shaped joint connection. PCR enrichment was conducted to generate the final cDNA sequencing library. The libraries were sequenced as paired-end 150-base reads on an Illumina Novaseq 6000, following the manufacturer's protocols.

Sequencing data analysis and gene expression analysis

Billions of Illumina sequencing reads, referred to as raw reads, underwent quality control and statistical analysis to yield clean reads. These clean reads were subsequently compared to the reference genome to generate mapped data for further analysis. The software HISAT2 (v2.2.1) (Kim et al., 2015) was utilized for sequence alignment analysis. For sequencing coverage assessment, RSeQC (v2.3.6) was employed to evaluate the uniformity of sequencing results, represented as sequence coverage across the 5′ to 3′ regions of all genes in the sample. StringTie (v3.0) (Pertea et al., 2015) was used to assemble the mapped reads, annotate known transcripts, and identify potential new transcripts for functional annotation based on the reference genome. RSEM (v1.3.3) with default parameters was employed to calculate gene expression levels in each sample and to perform principal component analysis (PCA). Differential gene expression analysis was conducted using the DESeq2 package in RStudio, where DESeq2 algorithms normalized the data and identified differentially expressed genes (DEGs) based on the filtering parameters: |log2 (fold change)| ≥ 1 and p-value ≤ 0.05. Bar charts and volcano plots for DEGs were generated using R language packages.

GO and KEGG enrichment analyses

Functional enrichment analysis of related genes was conducted, including GO enrichment and KEGG enrichment. The Goatools was utilized to perform GO enrichment analysis on the related genes, aiming to identify the main GO functions associated with these genes. Fisher’s exact test was employed, and a GO function was deemed significantly enriched when P-adjust < 0.05. R script was used for the KEGG pathway enrichment analysis, following the same calculation principle as the GO functional enrichment analysis. A KEGG pathway function was considered significantly enriched when P-adjust < 0.05.

Weighted gene co-expression networks analysis (WGCNA)

The WGCNA algorithm is used to construct gene expression networks, available at https://horvath.genetics.ucla.edu/html/CoexpressionNetwork/Rpackages/WGCNA/. Initially, the gene expression data underwent preprocessing steps. This involved background correction and standardization, followed by filtering out abnormal and small variant genes. The correlation intensity among the treated genes was then distributed without scaling. The mean expression value in the parameters was set to 1, and the coefficient of variation was set to 0.1. Subsequently, genes were classified, with those exhibiting similar expression patterns grouped into modules. In the parameters, the network type was set to be signed, the soft power value (β value) was determined based on the previous step of data preprocessing, and the minModuleSize was set to 30. Additionally, the minKMEtoStay was set to 0.3, and mergeCutHeight was set to 0.25. Key modules were identified by performing association analysis with phenotypic data using the Spearman correlation coefficient calculation method.

Screening and analysis of key genes

Venn analyses, available at jvenn (inrae.fr), were used to show the overlap of genes and identify the common and unique genes between gene sets. Genes with similar expression patterns are usually functionally related. Gene expression pattern clustering analysis was performed using RSEM (v1.3.3) software for selected gene sets, including heat maps and subcluster trend maps. Additionally, protein interaction network analysis was performed using the STRING website and subsequently enhanced using Cytoscape (v3.10.1) software.

Confirmation of DEGs by RT-qPCR

Using Primer Premier 5.0 software, we selected the Mx1, OASL, IFI6 (also known as ISG12 in chickens), PKR (also known as EIF2AK2 in chickens), MDA5 (also known as IFIH1 in chickens), IRF1, IRF7, and TRIM25 genes from the DEGs to design their RT-qPCR primers. The GAPDH gene was chosen as the internal reference gene. The sequences of these primers are presented in Table 1. RNA extraction from ground tissue fluid was performed using TRIzol reagent (Invitroge) according to the manufacturer's instructions. Reverse transcription was conducted following the guidelines of the ReverTra Ace qPCR RT Master Mix with gDNA Remover Kit (TOYOBO). RT-qPCR was executed using Hieff™ qPCR SYBR® Green Master Mix (Yeasen, China) on a Light-Cycler 480 PCR system (Roche). The cycling conditions were set as follows: 95°C for 5 minutes, followed by 40 cycles of 95°C for 10 seconds, 58°C for 10 seconds, and 72°C for 30 seconds, with subsequent incubations at 95°C for 5 seconds, 60°C for 1 minute, and 95°C for 15 seconds. The data were analyzed using the 2−△△t method, and the log2 values were compared with the transcriptome sequencing results.

The transfection of siRNA and overexpression vector for TRIM25 and IBV infection in vitro

According to the chicken TRIM25 gene (ENSGALG00000003144, Transcript ID: ENSGALT00000049075), three sequences of siRNA were designed and synthesized by GenePharma (China), as detailed in Table 2. To construct the overexpression vector, the TRIM25 sequence containing a Flag tag was inserted into the pcDNA3.1(+) vector. DF-1 (UMNSAH/DF-1) cells were obtained from the National Infrastructure of Cell Line Resource (Beijing, China) and cultured in DMEM with a pH of 7.2 (Invitrogen), supplemented with 10% FBS in a 5% CO2 incubator. Once a confluence of 60-80% was achieved, the cells were transfected with siRNA targeting chicken TRIM25 or the Flag-TRIM25 vector using jetPRIME (Polyplus, France). After a 48-hour transfection period, RNA and protein were extracted for subsequent RT-qPCR and Western blot analyses. To assess the efficiency of gene silencing, RT-qPCR was performed using primers specific to the TRIM25 gene (listed in Table 1), as previously described. A non-targeting siRNA (siNC) was used as the negative control, and GAPDH as the reference gene for normalization. Gene expression levels were quantified using the 2⁻ΔΔCt method, and statistical comparisons were made by one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) in software GraphPad Prism (v10.1). An anti-TRIM25 rabbit polyclonal antibody (Proteintech) was utilized to detect TRIM25 protein levels, while an anti-Flag mouse monoclonal antibody (Proteintech) was employed to confirm the overexpression of TRIM25.

Results

Transcriptomic quality qnalysis and the identification of DEGs

As outlined in Fig. 1, chickens were inoculated via the intraocular-nasal route with either the 4/91 vaccine strain or the GD17/04 strain at a dose of 10⁵ EID₅₀, while control groups received PBS. Viral challenge success was confirmed as previously described (Huang et al., 2023). Kidney samples were collected, homogenized, and lysed for RNA extraction and subsequent transcriptome sequencing. Summary statistics of raw and quality-controlled sequencing data are provided in Table S1. Clean reads were aligned to the reference genome (Table S2). Sequencing uniformity was assessed via coverage analysis across the 5′–3′ gene regions (Fig. S1A), and transcript length distribution is shown in Fig. S1B. All assembled transcripts were annotated against six functional databases (NR, Swiss-Prot, Pfam, EggNOG, GO, KEGG), with annotation statistics summarized in Fig. S1C. Gene expression was quantified using TPM, and the distribution is visualized in Fig. S1D. PCA showed clear separation between groups and high intra-group reproducibility (Fig. S1E).

Workflow. Forty-five 10-day-old specific pathogen-free (SPF) chickens were equally divided into three groups. Each chicken in the infected groups was administered either the GD17/04 or 4/91 vaccine strains at a dose of 105 EID50 via oculonasal administration, while PBS was administered as a negative control. Five chickens from each group were euthanized at 3, 5, and 7 dpi. Kidney tissue from the same region was homogenized, followed by total RNA extraction, transcriptome sequencing, and bioinformatics analysis.

DEGs were identified at each time point using criteria of |log₂FC| > 1 and P < 0.05 (Fig. S1F). Volcano plots (Fig. 2) display log-fold change against statistical significance, with red and blue dots indicating up- and down-regulated DEGs, respectively. The top 10 most significant genes (by P-value) are labeled; unnamed genes/transcripts are omitted. In the GD17/04 group, EGR1 was significantly down-regulated at 3 dpi, while B2M, PCK1, TRIM25, PDK4, BAG3, STAT1, and ZNFX1 were up-regulated. At 5 dpi, up-regulated genes included B2M, CTSS, BLB1, STOML1, MOV10, MYO1F, SAMHD1, and PTPRC. At 7 dpi, CTSS, BLB1, STOML1, MOV10, and KCNJ5 were up-regulated, whereas CYP1A2, HERPUD1, HSPA5, and FN3K were down-regulated. In the 4/91 vaccine group, EGR1, HSPA2, FOS, DUSP1, HSPB9, JUN, ATF3, and CYR61 were down-regulated at 3 dpi, while CYP11A1 was up-regulated. At 5 dpi, HINTW, OASL, and LY6E were up-regulated. At 7 dpi, TMEM266, HSPA5, DHTKD1, HMGCS2, and NRG4 were down-regulated, and SLC22A7 and PIGR were up-regulated.

GO enrichment analysis of DEGs

The most significantly enriched biological processes for the virulent GD17/04 strain and attenuated 4/91 vaccine strain across 3, 5, and 7 dpi are summarized in Fig. 3.

In chicken kidneys infected with the virulent GD17/04 strain, DEGs were strongly associated with immune activation throughout the infection course. Key enriched terms included immune and interferon response regulation, leukocyte migration and chemotaxis, innate immunity through pattern recognition receptors, and adaptive immune processes involving T and B cell differentiation. Notably, eosinophil chemotaxis and response to interferon-gamma were consistently enriched across all time points, with expanding gene numbers indicating prolonged immune stimulation.

In contrast, infection with the 4/91 vaccine strain elicited a distinct transcriptional response, enriched for defense response to organisms, reaction to biotic and hormonal stimuli, as well as nucleic acid metabolic and ribonuclease activities by 5 dpi. At 7 dpi, processes shifted toward synaptic signaling, extracellular matrix organization, and endonuclease-related functions.

These results underscore a virulence-dependent divergence in host responses: the virulent strain drives sustained and broad immune activation, while the vaccine strain modulates alternative biological pathways, including metabolic and structural adaptations, aligning with its attenuated phenotype.

KEGG pathway enrichment analysis was performed to identify key signaling and metabolic pathways associated with DEGs in chickens infected with either the virulent GD17/04 strain (G) or the attenuated 4/91 vaccine strain (V) across three time points (3, 5, and 7 dpi). The top 30 significantly enriched pathways (adjusted P < 0.05) are summarized in Fig. 4.

For the GD17/04 strain, DEGs were markedly enriched in immune and inflammatory pathways throughout the infection. At 3 dpi, prominent pathways included Toll-like receptor signaling, Jak-STAT signaling, cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction, chemokine signaling, antigen processing and presentation, and phagosome formation. Numerous viral infection pathways (e.g., HPV, CMV, EBV, HSV, HIV) were also enriched. By 5 dpi, additional pathways emerged, such as NF-kappa B signaling, intestinal immune network for IgA production, and various autoimmune and infectious disease pathways (e.g., inflammatory bowel disease, lupus, asthma, tuberculosis). At 7 dpi, natural killer cell mediated cytotoxicity, B cell receptor signaling, and Fc receptor-mediated phagocytosis were notably enriched. Pathways related to phagosome, antigen presentation, autoimmune diseases, and cell adhesion molecules were consistently enriched across all time points, with an increasing number of genes over time.

For the 4/91 vaccine strain, the enriched pathways reflected broader and more diverse biological processes. At 3 dpi, DEGs were associated with NOD-like receptor signaling, Toll-like receptor signaling, steroid hormone biosynthesis, neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction, and metabolic pathways such as linoleic acid and tyrosine metabolism. By 5 dpi, enrichment was observed in metabolic pathways (glycerolipid, galactose, fructose and mannose metabolism), TGF-beta signaling, and graft-versus-host disease. At 7 dpi, significant pathways included Staphylococcus aureus infection, complement and coagulation cascades, PI3K-Akt signaling, PPAR signaling pathway, extracellular matrix-receptor interaction, and synaptic vesicle cycling.

These results highlight a clear distinction between the two strains: the virulent GD17/04 strain induces sustained and intense immune activation pathways, while the vaccine strain modulates a wider array of processes, including metabolic, endocrine, and neural pathways.

WGCNA for GD17/04 strain infection

Gene co-expression networks were constructed using WGCNA on gene expression data from control and GD17/04-infected groups. A soft power threshold of β = 3 was selected based on scale-free topology criteria (Fig. 5A). A total of 3,927 genes were clustered using a TOM-based dissimilarity measure and dynamic tree-cutting, resulting in six distinct co-expression modules, each represented by a unique color (Fig. 5, Fig. 5). Functional enrichment analysis for each module is summarized in Fig. 5D. The blue module, down-regulated in both groups over time, was enriched in developmental terms, including epithelium and tube development, muscle contraction, extracellular matrix organization, and Wnt signaling. The brown module exhibited similar expression between control and infected samples and was linked to cell cycle progression, chromosome organization, microtubule activity, and DNA replication. The green module was involved in metabolic processes such as heme binding, carboxylic acid metabolism, amino acid catabolism, steroid and drug metabolism, and oxidoreductase activities. Likely, these genes are minimally associated with the physiological changes induced by viral infection.

The turquoise module, containing the largest number of genes, up-regulated in the GD17/04 infected group over the course of the infection, was significantly enriched in immune-related processes, including type I and interferon-gamma responses, myeloid leukocyte activation, monocyte and neutrophil chemotaxis, T cell proliferation and differentiation, T cell receptor signaling, and complement activation. In contrast, genes in the yellow module were up-regulated over time in controls but down-regulated after infection, and were associated with kidney development processes such as nephron tubule morphogenesis, planar polarity, and cell adhesion.

Given their expression dynamics, the yellow and turquoise modules were hypothesized to be most relevant to GD17/04 infection. The yellow module’s down-regulation may reflect virus-induced nephropathy, while the turquoise module is associated with the host immune response triggered by the virus. The turquoise module was predominantly associated with cell proliferation processes, including cell cycle regulation, DNA replication and recombination, chromosome organization and segregation, kinetochore function, microtubule dynamics, mitotic and meiotic events, and extracellular matrix organization. Genes in the blue module were enriched in metabolic functions, particularly related to endoplasmic reticulum stress response, carboxyltransferase activity, progesterone and steroid metabolism, amino acid and tryptophan catabolism, aromatase activity, NAD biosynthesis, as well as macrophage activation and skeletal muscle differentiation.

Notably, the brown module—up-regulated at 7 dpi—showed significant immune-related enrichment, encompassing leukocyte adhesion, migration and differentiation, lymphocyte and T cell activation, virus response, defense mechanisms, immune signal transduction, and cytokine signaling. Seemingly, the brown module in WGCNA for the control and vaccine groups (denoted as V and C) corresponds to the turquoise module in WGCNA for the control and GD17/04 infected groups, both of which are associated with the immune response to IBV infection. To verify this conjecture, Venn analysis was conducted (Fig. 6E). The genes in the brown module of WGCNA (V and C) are predominantly mapped to the turquoise module in WGCNA (G and C), though only 194 overlapping genes, indicated by a purple dotted circle, suggesting that 4/91 vaccine triggers a less aggressive immune response. DEGs from the brown module, including ZNFX1, IFIT5, IRF1, IRF7, and OASL, were identified via Venn analysis (Fig. 6F) and visualized in a heatmap (Fig. 6G). Chicken immune response to infection with highly pathogenic GD17/04 strain and 4/91 vaccine strain

To elucidate intuitively the signaling pathways involved in the host response to virulent and attenuated IBV strains, KEGG enrichment analysis was performed on the DEGs identified in the brown module of WGCNA (V and C) and in the turquoise module of WGCNA (G and C), in Fig. 7A. Shared pathways included influenza A, hematopoietic cell lineage, graft-versus-host disease, chemokine signaling, NOD-like receptor signaling, type I diabetes mellitus, and measles. Consistent with earlier findings, the virulent GD17/04 strain activated a broader spectrum of immune pathways. Venn analysis identified 69 common DEGs across infections (Fig. 7B), which may represent core marker genes for IBV infection (see Table S5). A circular heatmap of these genes is shown in Fig. 7C, with cluster analysis revealing a major cluster of 59 genes. Expression levels of 59 genes across control (C), vaccine (V), and GD17/04 (G) groups are visualized in Fig. 7D. Notably, their expression increased with both infection duration and viral virulence.

However, TRIM25 is an RNA-binding ubiquitin E3 ligase with central but poorly understood roles in the innate immune response to RNA viruses (Lucía et al., 2024).

To confirm the differential expression of the IBV-induced genes identified in the sequencing data, we selected eight DEGs from the turquoise module in WGCNA and validated their expression using RT-qPCR. RNA was extracted from the kidneys of chickens infected with GD17/04, which displayed more significant changes in the DEGs. TRIM25 promotes the proliferation of IBV in vitro

As previously mentioned, TRIM25 was found to be upregulated following infection with the GD17/04 and 4/91 vaccine strains, ranking among the top 10 DEGs based on the magnitude of upregulation or downregulation, as illustrated in Fig. 2. Furthermore, TRIM25 is identified as one of the core genes within the protein interaction network (Fig. 7F), suggesting its significant role in regulating IBV infection. To further investigate the function of TRIM25 in IBV replication, we designed siRNA specifically targeting TRIM25 and constructed an overexpression vector for Flag-TRIM25. The interference effect of three individual siRNAs for chicken TRIM25 was assessed using RT-qPCR and Western blot analysis. As shown in Fig. 9A and B, TRIM25 interference sequence 2 significantly inhibited TRIM25 at both the mRNA and protein levels. After transfection with TRIM25 siRNA or the Flag-TRIM25 overexpression vector for 24 hours, DF-1 cells were infected with GD17/04 at an MOI of 0.5 for 18 hours. DF-1 cells were lysed to detect the IBV-N mRNA levels using RT-qPCR, which revealed that interference with TRIM25 suppresses IBV replication, while overexpression of TRIM25 promotes IBV proliferation (Fig. 9C). This phenomenon is corroborated by Western blot results, demonstrating that IBV-N protein levels increased with TRIM25 overexpression but decreased with TRIM25 interference (Fig. 9D). In summary, the infection of the host by IBV may necessitate the involvement of TRIM25.

Discussion

The kidney is the primary target organ for nephrogenic variant IBV infection (Li et al., 2022b). Additionally, SARS-CoVs can directly induce kidney disease (Cheng et al., 2020; Shao et al., 2020), underscoring the importance of investigating the relationship between coronaviruses and innate immunity in the renal context. Our integrated analysis, which combines RNA sequencing, WGCNA, and protein-protein interaction network approaches, provides several critical insights into renal immunopathology and host defense against IBV.

Strain-specific immune signatures define virulence and attenuation. Although both the virulent GD17/04 and attenuated 4/91 strains initially activated innate sensing pathways such as NOD-like receptor signaling, their transcriptional responses diverged markedly. GD17/04 induced sustained activation of NF-κB, Jak-STAT, and adaptive immune pathways, correlating with severe renal injury and hyperinflammation. This hyperinflammatory state likely drives the renal tissue damage observed clinically and is potentially linked to genes within the yellow module.

The significant downregulation of the yellow module, enriched for genes critical in kidney development and homeostasis, following infection with the virulent GD17/04 strain, provides compelling insights into the molecular pathogenesis of IBV-induced nephropathy. Beyond the well-characterized hyperimmune activation triggered by IBV, our analysis suggests that the virus actively disrupts renal developmental and metabolic programs, impairing the kidney’s capacity to repair and maintain structural integrity.

Several key genes within this module have established or putative roles in renal physiology whose suppression may directly exacerbate tissue damage. For instance, SLC39A, which facilitates manganese and iron transport, is crucial for mitigating oxidative stress in renal tubules. Its downregulation likely promotes ferroptosis, an iron-dependent form of cell death increasingly implicated in tubular injury (Choi et al., 2024). Similarly, RASSF10, a regulator of RAS signaling frequently epigenetically silenced in renal carcinomas (Richter et al., 2020), may contribute to fibrotic signaling when suppressed, potentially activating profibrotic pathways that drive nephropathy progression (Panizo et al., 2021). The adhesion molecule CDH10 (cadherin-10) is essential for maintaining epithelial integrity; its reduced expression could compromise tubular barrier function, facilitating epithelial-mesenchymal transition and interstitial fibrosis (Jinawath et al., 2017).

Furthermore, metabolic genes within the module point to broad disruption of renal homeostasis. HPD catalyzes tyrosine metabolism, FBP2 regulates gluconeogenesis, and IYD participates in the synthesis of thyroid hormones, which in turn regulate the iodine cycle (Han et al., 2020; Huangyang et al., 2020; Yang et al., 2020). All of these were down-regulated after GD17/04 infection. Dysregulation of these pathways may lead to toxic metabolite accumulation and impaired energy supply in renal cells, exacerbating functional decline. Although not all genes in the module have well-defined renal roles—such as NWD2, STAC, and STRIP2—their co-downregulation within a development-associated network suggests collective involvement in maintaining nephron structure and function. For example, DVL3, a mediator of Wnt signaling, is critical for tubular repair and regeneration; its suppression likely impedes normal healing responses after viral injury.

The collective downregulation of these genes indicates that virulent IBV infection induces a state of “developmental regression” in renal tissue, disrupting pathways essential for differentiation, metabolism, and structural resilience. This may significantly amplify damage initially caused by hyperinflammation and direct viral cytopathy. In particular, the inhibition of metal ion homeostasis (SLC39A8), energy metabolism (FBP2, HPD), and thyroid hormone synthesis (IYD) converges to create a hostile microenvironment conducive to oxidative stress, impaired repair, and chronic fibrosis—hallmarks of IBV nephropathy.

Nevertheless, the exact mechanisms through which IBV targets these genes remain unclear. Epigenetic modification, viral protein-host factor interactions, or global transcriptional disruption may be involved. Future studies should validate the functional roles of key yellow module genes—such as SLC39A8, CDH10, and DVL3—using in vitro and in vivo models to clarify their contributions to viral pathogenesis and explore their potential as therapeutic targets for mitigating IBV-related kidney injury.

The identification of 69 core genes consistently perturbed by both strains, including CMPK2, CCL19, and others, particularly highlights RSAD2, ZNFX1, and TRIM25 as core hub genes, revealing universal host factors involved in IBV infection.

CMPK2, a key enzyme in mitochondrial nucleotide synthesis, reduces ATP production and mitochondrial DNA copy number upon deficiency, leading to intracellular inorganic phosphate accumulation (Zhao et al., 2022). In the context of IBV infection, mitochondrial dysfunction may exacerbate renal cell calcification, which aligns with the pathological calcium deposition observed in IBV nephropathy. CCL19, a lymphoid tissue chemokine, enhances mucosal immunity and serves as an adjuvant in DNA vaccine design; for instance, an HSV-2 gD vaccine fused with CCL19 significantly boosts IgA secretion and lymphocyte recruitment in rectal and genital mucosa (Yan et al., 2021). In IBV infection, CCL19 may coordinate mucosal immunity by activating gut-associated lymphoid tissue, thereby limiting viral spread along the enteric-renal axis.

RSAD2 encodes viperin, which has been reported to suppress IBV replication and cholesterol synthesis in Vero cells (Yu et al., 2024). ZNFX1 is an interferon-stimulated double-strand RNA sensor, and it has been reported that ZNFX1 deficiency predisposes individuals to severe viral infections and a multisystem inflammatory disease (Stefano et al., 2021). The fatal COVID-19 case in an infant prompted an early diagnosis of ZNFX1 deficiency in a sibling (Raed and Motasem, 2023). Additionally, ZNFX1 has been reported to play a dual role in viral sensing: it recognizes viral RNA to activate the MAVS pathway while suppressing NLRP3 inflammasome hyperactivation to prevent immunopathology (Huang et al., 2024). However, its role in IBV infection has not been reported yet. IBV may exploit mutations to evade ZNFX1-mediated immune surveillance, thereby exacerbating renal inflammation.

TRIM proteins, identified as E3 ubiquitin ligases, participate in various viral infections through ubiquitylation, ISGylation, and SUMOylation processes (Wei et al., 2024). Although our study provides functional evidence that TRIM25 promotes IBV replication, its precise mechanism of action during γ-coronavirus infection remains unclear. While it is well-established that TRIM25 activates RIG-I through K63-linked ubiquitination to promote interferon production (Liu et al., 2019), its functions vary significantly across viruses. For instance, it exhibits antiviral activity against influenza A virus infection by binding to and destabilizing viral mRNA (Choudhury et al., 2022) and inhibits influenza A virus by targeting viral ribonucleoproteins (Meyerson et al., 2017). It acts as a host restriction factor against infectious bursal disease virus by targeting VP3 for degradation (Wang et al., 2021). During SARS-CoV-2 infection, TRIM25 binds viral RNA and higher expression correlates with reduced disease severity (Lee et al., 2021; Tavakoli et al., 2024). MERS-CoV N protein suppresses the production of both type I and type III IFNs via sequestering TRIM25 (Chang et al., 2020). The SARS-CoV N protein was found to bind to the SPRY domain of TRIM25 E3 ubiquitin ligase, thereby interfering with the association between TRIM25 and RIG-I and inhibiting TRIM25-mediated RIG-I ubiquitination and activation (Hu et al., 2017).

The pro-viral role of TRIM25 in IBV suggests a unique mechanism. This may be related to the lack of RIG-I in chickens. We propose several hypotheses: (1) modulation of viral RNA stability or translation through its RNA-binding capacity (Álvarez et al., 2024), contrasting its role in IAV; (2) subversion of ubiquitination to degrade host antiviral proteins; (3) interference with innate immune sensors like MDA5 via alternative ubiquitination or complex formation; and (4) interaction with IBV proteins, analogous to IAV NS1-mediated inhibition of RIG-I ubiquitination (Koliopoulos et al., 2018), but resulting in enhanced replication. Notably, TRIM25 co-enriches with ISGs (e.g., RSAD2, MX1) yet facilitates IBV replication, highlighting its complex, non-binary role influenced by viral and host contexts.

By mapping virulence-dependent immune modules and identifying TRIM25 as a central regulator of IBV replication, this study transforms our understanding of nephropathogenic IBV. The transcriptional atlas provides a foundation for developing targeted antivirals, improved vaccines, and biomarkers for renal pathology surveillance in poultry industries worldwide.

VACTERL association: a case report highlighting multisystem anomalies and the critical need for multidisciplinary care in Palestine
Authors: Mo’ath Iyad Milhem, Saja Zaki Al-Junaidi, Abedallah Emad Jabareen, Mai Zayed Housheya, Walaa Murad Khamayseh, Mohammad Iyad Milhem
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Introduction:VACTERL association is a rare congenital disorder characterized by multisystem anomalies. Neonates are particularly vulnerable, and early recognition with coordinated multidisciplinary care is crucial, especially in resource-limited settings.Case presentation:A Palestinian female neonate was born with respiratory distress, greenish aspiration, and failure to pass meconium. Clinical and imaging findings revealed type 3 tracheoesophageal fistula (TEF), polycystic kidney disease, cloacal anomaly, and persistent pulmonary hypertension (PPHN). Following stabilization in the NICU, the patient developed abdominal distension and oliguria. Ultrasound confirmed bilateral enlarged, cystic kidneys, and obstruction. Urgent surgical exploration revealed a 4 cm anterior gastric wall perforation, which was repaired, and a double-barrel colostomy was performed. TEF ligation and chest tube placement were also completed. Despite intensive care and surgical intervention, the neonate succumbed at 52 hours of life due to severe cardiovascular instability.Clinical discussion:This case illustrates the complexity and variability of VACTERL presentation. The presence of severe anomalies like polycystic kidneys and PPHN contributed to the poor prognosis. Early antenatal diagnosis, coordinated surgical care, and NICU support are essential for survival in such cases.Conclusion:This case underscores the life-threatening nature of VACTERL association, highlights the importance of early diagnosis, prenatal screening, multidisciplinary care, and parental counseling, and emphasizes the challenges faced in resource-limited settings.

Introduction

VACTERL association is a complex, non-genetic condition characterized by a constellation of congenital anomalies, including vertebral defects (V), anal atresia (A), cardiac defects (C), tracheoesophageal fistula (T), renal anomalies (R), and limb abnormalities (L). The diagnosis requires at least three of these features, without alternative syndromic or genetic explanations[1,2].

The etiology remains unclear, but both genetic and environmental factors may contribute. Clinical presentation is heterogeneous, ranging from minor defects to life-threatening anomalies. Vertebral anomalies include hemivertebrae, fused or absent vertebrae, scoliosis, caudal regression, and spina bifida. Anal anomalies include imperforate anus or cloacal malformations. Cardiac anomalies vary from minor septal defects to severe structural defects. TEF may be associated with esophageal atresia, often presenting with polyhydramnios or absent gastric bubble prenatally. Renal anomalies include vesicoureteral reflux, unilateral agenesis, dysplastic or multicystic kidneys, duplicated collecting systems, hydronephrosis, and horseshoe kidney. Limb malformations may involve radial defects, polydactyly, or oligodactyly[3].

Early diagnosis, comprehensive imaging, and genetic evaluation are critical to identify associated anomalies and plan coordinated management, particularly in resource-limited settings where delays can worsen outcomes[4,5].

In this case report, we present the case of a neonate female with multiple anomalies, consistent with VACTERL association: type 3 tracheoesophageal fistula, polycystic kidneys with right ureteral dilation, cloaca and persistent pulmonary hypertension (PPHN). Through these reports we aim to underscore the importance of good evaluation for associated anomalies and the role of coordinated care in improving outcomes due to complexity of management, highlighting the importance of antenatal diagnosis to prepare birth in a tertiary center, well equipmented and prepared to manage complex cases. Case presentation

Obstetric history

The patient was the third child of a healthy Palestinian mother. The pregnancy was uncomplicated, with routine antenatal care and no significant prenatal ultrasound findings reported. The delivery was full-term via spontaneous vaginal birth.

Neonatal assessment

At birth, the neonate weighed 3620 g, measured 50 cm in length, and had a head circumference of 35 cm. Apgar scores were 8 and 9 at 1 and 5 minutes, respectively.

NICU course

The neonate received oxygen via nasal cannula, intravenous fluids (D5%), and antibiotics. Paracetamol was administered for fever. Nasogastric tube insertion and close monitoring of vital signs were performed. Lumbar puncture was done as part of sepsis evaluation.

Surgical intervention

At 34 hours of life, due to worsening abdominal distension and suspected peritonitis, the patient underwent urgent upper midline laparotomy. Outcome

Despite intensive postoperative care and mechanical ventilation, the neonate developed bradycardia and cardiac arrest. Cardiopulmonary resuscitation with repeated adrenaline administration was unsuccessful. Death occurred at 52 hours of life.

Discussion

Diagnostic challenges

VACTERL association is rare, occurring in approximately 1 in 10 000–40 000 live births. The variability of phenotypic expression complicates diagnosis. In our patient, the combination of TEF, cloacal malformation, multicystic kidneys, and PPHN illustrates the heterogeneous presentation[6].

Surgical management dilemmas

The emergent gastric perforation necessitated urgent surgery. The midline incision was chosen for optimal exposure given peritonitis. TEF ligation was performed concurrently due to the critical condition. Delays in intervention are often unavoidable in resource-limited settings, particularly when stabilization of cardiovascular and respiratory status is required[6].

Prognosis and PPHN

Persistent pulmonary hypertension is a major contributor to mortality in VACTERL neonates. Literature reports 24% mortality among affected neonates with cardiovascular compromise, consistent with our case.

Resource-limited setting challenges

Limitations in prenatal detection, neonatal intensive care, and immediate surgical resources significantly impact outcomes. This case emphasizes the importance of multidisciplinary care and timely referral to tertiary centers[7].

Literature comparison

Recent studies (2020–2024) continue to report high morbidity and mortality associated with multiple congenital anomalies, especially when PPHN and renal obstruction coexist. Our case contributes to the scarce literature documenting VACTERL cases complicated by gastric perforation in resource-limited contexts[8].

Conclusion

VACTERL association is a complex, life-threatening condition requiring early diagnosis, multidisciplinary management, and parental counseling. Prenatal screening and planned delivery at tertiary care centers can improve outcomes. In resource-limited settings, awareness of possible complications such as gastric perforation and PPHN is essential for timely intervention.

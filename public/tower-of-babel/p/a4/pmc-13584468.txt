Microsatellite instable early gastric cancers; a distinct molecular profile with HER2 over-expression and increased multiple lesion risk
Authors: Na Rae Lim, Woo Chul Chung
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground/aimsMicrosatellite instability (MSI) associated gastric cancers (GCs) have a large burden of mutational risk. In advanced gastric cancers (AGCs), the pre-treatment evaluation of MSI and over-expression of HER2 / EGFR could be crucial for prognosis. However, the significance of MSI state, HER2, and EGFR over-expression in early gastric cancer (EGC) are still under investigation.MethodA consecutive study was undertaken from March 2020 to March 2022, and we analyzed 325 gastric cancer tissues (165 EGCs; 160 AGCs) from the patients who had received gastrectomy or endoscopic submucosal dissection.ResultsThe frequencies of MSI and HER2 over-expression were 21.8% (71/ 325), and 14.5% (47/ 325), respectively. The frequency of EGFR over-expression was 48.9% (159/ 325). The infection rate of Helicobacter pylori was 50.8% (165/325), and the infection rate in EGC (58.8%, 97/165) was significantly higher than that in AGC (42.5%, 68/160) (P < 0.01). EGFR overexpression was more frequent in AGC than in EGC (P = 0.03), and there was no statistical difference for MSI and HER2. In MSI positive GCs, HER2 over-expression (P = 0.03) and H. pylori infection (P = 0.03) were more frequent than in microsatellite stable GCs. HER2 over-expression was outstanding in MSI EGCs (P < 0.01), and multiple lesions were observed more clinically.ConclusionThe frequency of H. pylori infection and HER2 over-expression occurred more frequently in MSI positive GCs. In particular, HER2 overexpression was more evident in MSI-EGC. MSI-EGC is a group of diseases with unique molecular biological characteristics and may show rapid tumor growth potential, so special treatment measures may be needed.Keywords: Gastric cancer, Microsatellite instability, Human epidermal growth factor receptor

Introduction

Gastric cancer (GC) is a heterogenous disease classified in four molecular subtypes according to the Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA). TCGA project uncovered four molecular subtypes of gastric cancer: Epstein-Barr virus, microsatellite instability (MSI), genomically stable, and chromosomal instability (CIN) [1]. Among them, MSI is defined as a hyper-mutable phenotype that occurs in the presence of a deficient DNA mismatch repair machinery. Loss of expression or defects in one or more mismatch repair machinery elements can raise the mutational risk, and it is an important driving force in cancer formation or progression. Determining MSI status before treatment of GC, especially in advanced gastric cancer (AGC), is important because it is associated with prognostic and predictive value. It has been reported that cases with MSI-high have less serosa invasion and lymph node metastasis and a relatively good prognosis [2]. However, the meaning of MSI in EGC is still difficult to clearly state and is under research.

As it was discovered that GC patients with human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2), and epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) can be the target of therapy. The over-expression of HER2 and EGFR showed unfavorable prognostic indicator [3]. HER2 is a GC subtype included in the CIN category of the TCGA, and HER2-positive GC accounts for 10–20% of GC cases [4, 5]. Along with HER2 signaling, it influences cell motility, development, cell adhesion, angiogenesis, and organogenesis. In addition, the EGFR is a cytoplasmic tyrosine kinase domain composed of a 170,000 kDa transmembrane glycoprotein. Among GCs, 2–35% of cases are reported to have EGFR protein overexpression and/or gene amplification [6, 7]. Based on the limited research and reviews known to date, over-expression of HER2 and EGFR genes was significantly correlated with disease recurrence and poor prognosis in patients with GCs. Furthermore; evidence suggests that HER2 overexpression is higher in high-grade gastric dysplasia compared to low-grade dysplasia [8, 9], and based on this, it is inferred that HER2 is involved in the early stages of gastric cancer and tumor growth.

This study aimed to compare clinical features and HER2 and EGFR overexpression according to MSI status in tissues of AGC and EGC patients. In particular, we aimed to investigate the significance of MSI in early gastric cancer.

Materials and methods

A consecutive study was undertaken from March 2020 to March 2022, and we analyzed 325 gastric cancer tissues (165 EGC; 160 AGC) from the patients who had received gastrectomy or endoscopic submucosal dissection. Clinical data such as age, gender, weight, and height at the time of surgery were collected, and histopathologic features - depth of invasion, and histologic type were analyzed. Pathological cancer stage was interpreted according to the evaluation protocol recommended by 8th edition of American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM classification. EGC defined as tumor cells were confined to mucosa and submucosa regardless of lymph node metastasis. Histological classification was performed according to the 5th edition of the World Health Organization (WHO) classification of digestive system tumors (2019). Tumor differentiation was categorized as well-differentiated (tubular adenocarcinoma, well to moderately differentiated), moderately differentiated, or poorly differentiated (including poorly cohesive carcinoma, signet ring cell carcinoma, and poorly differentiated tubular adenocarcinoma). The classification of diffuse atrophic gastritis was defined as open type if it included the gastric cardia according to the Kimura-Takemoto classification. The diagnosis of H. pylori infection was based on two or more positive results from tests including the rapid urease test (RUT), polymerase chain reaction (PCR), urea breath test (UBT), histologic evidence by silver staining, and a serologic test.

This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of St. Vincent’s hospital of Catholic university of Korea (approval number: VC24RASI0079) on April 11, 2024. Data collection commenced on the same date. Written informed consent was waived by the committee due to a retrospective nature of our study. The researchers had access to information that could identify individual participants during data collection. However, to protect participant privacy, each participant was assigned a unique management number, and all other identifying information was anonymized. This anonymization process was completed before data analysis began, ensuring no personally identifiable information was used during the analysis phase of the research.

Evaluation of MSI state

Evaluation of MSI state

We used the PCR- fragment analysis to analyze whether there is MSI. This test is a test that separates genomic DNA from normal and cancerous tissues, amplifies five microsatellite markers (BAT-25, BAT-26, D2S123, D5S346, and D17S250) using the PCR method, and then analyzes instability using the fragment analysis method using the ABI3500xL Dx from Applied biosystems. According to the National Cancer Institute Bethesda panel criteria, if instability is shown in two or more of the five markers, it is defined as MSI-high, if instability is shown in one marker, it is MSI-low, and if instability is not observed in any markers, it is defined as microsatellite stable.

(2)HER2 and EGFR over-expression

HER2 and EGFR over-expression

Immunohistochemical analysis for HER2 and EGFR was performed on tumor sections. We performed immunohistochemical staining using tissue microarray sections and a BenchMark XT autostainer (Ventana Medical Systems, Tucson, AZ, USA), according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The commercially available antibodies used for immunohistochemistry of EGFR (1:50; BD Biosciences, Rockville, MD, USA). HER2 was detected using mouse monoclonal antibody, clone 45MI (Novocastra, Newcastle upon Tyne, England). The immunohistochemical results were scored semi-quantitatively using a four-point scale: 0, no immunoreaction; 1+, faint or equivocal immunoreaction in < 10% of cells; 2+, unequivocal, strong immunoreaction in < 30% of cells; and 3+, unequivocal, strong immunoreaction in > 30% of cells. Tumors with 1+, 2 + or 3 + expression were interpreted as positive, while tumors with no expression were interpreted as negative. HER2 overexpression positivity was defined as 3 + in immunohistochemistry, and EGFR overexpression was defined as 3 + in immunohistochemistry.

(3)Statistics

Statistics

All statistical analyses were performed with the SPSS program (SPSS version 25.0; IBM, Chicago, IL, USA). Values are expressed as mean ± standard deviation. Categorical variables were tested by a Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. P-value < 0.05 was considered significant.

MSI gastric cancer (GC) vs. microsatellite stable (MSS) GC

MSI gastric cancer (GC) vs. microsatellite stable (MSS) GC

Comparative analysis was performed on 71 MSI GCs and 254 MSS GCs. In MSI GC, the patients were relatively older, had a higher frequency of being female, had a higher frequency of lesions being located in the antrum and had a high frequency of having diffuse atrophic gastritis. Poorly differentiated cancer was statistically more frequent in MSS-GC (P = 0.01) (Table 2). In MSI positive GCs, HER2 over-expression (P = 0.03) and H. pylori infection (P = 0.03) were more frequent than in MSS GCs. On the other hand, the frequency of EGFR overexpression was not statistically different (P = 0.07).

(3)MSI AGC vs. MSS AGC

MSI AGC vs. MSS AGC

Thirty-six MSI-AGC and 124 MSS-AGC patients were analyzed. Similar to MSI-GC overall, MSI-AGC patients were significantly older than MSS-AGC patients (P < 0.01) (Table 4). H. pylori infection was more frequently observed in MSI-AGC than in MSS-AGC (P < 0.01). Poorly differentiated cancer was significantly more frequent in MSS-AGC (P < 0.01). There were no statistically significant differences in HER2 over-expression (P = 0.46) or EGFR over-expression (P = 0.29) between the two groups. In contrast to MSI-EGC, HER2 over-expression was not associated with MSI status in AGC, suggesting that the clinical significance of MSI may differ between EGC and AGC.

(4)Combined MSI and HER2 expression subtypes in EGC

Combined MSI and HER2 expression subtypes in EGC

To further characterize the molecular heterogeneity within EGC, we classified patients into four subtypes based on combined MSI and HER2 expression status: MSI/HER2+ (n = 12), MSI/HER2- (n = 23), MSS/HER2+ (n = 11), and MSS/HER2- (n = 119) (Table 5). Multiple lesions, including synchronous or metachronous gastric neoplasms, were predominantly observed in MSI-positive groups (MSI/HER2+: 50.0%; MSI/HER2-: 21.7%), whereas they were rare in MSS groups (MSS/HER2+: 9.1%; MSS/HER2-: 5.9%). Notably, EGFR over-expression was markedly elevated in the MSS/HER2 + subtype (81.8%) compared to the other three subtypes (33.3%, 30.4%, and 42.9%, respectively). Due to the limited number of cases in each subtype, formal statistical comparisons between groups were not performed.

Discussion

MSI-GC is reported in 15–20% of patients showing the characteristics is mainly associated with older age, female sex, antral tumor location, and intestinal type, and is also known to be closely related to H. pylori infection [10–12]. Our results also showed the same results. In addition, recently, MSI GC makes them promising candidates for immunotherapy treatment. It is characterized by high mutation rates and the production of frameshift-peptide neoantigens, which increase the number of lymphocytes infiltrating the tumor and surrounding the tumor, creating a highly immunogenic environment [13]. Therefore in patients with AGC, identifying the MSI status test before treatment can suggest the most effective treatment. However, for EGC, it is difficult to find any usefulness or data for determining MSI status through these tests.

EGC patients need to be followed up for a certain period of time for recurrence, similar to AGC, because recurrence is found, and the incidence is reported to be approximately 1.4–1.9% [14]. MSI is known to have a lower risk of lymph node metastasis and a lower incidence of serosal invasion in AGC. Although MSI status is not yet clear in predicting the prognosis of EGC, previous studies have shown that lympho-vascular invasion occurs more frequently in EGC with high MSI, indicating that endoscopic treatment alone may be insufficient [15]. Based on these results, the research team expects that MSI status will help guide the appropriate treatment of EGC. In addition, what was notable in the previous study results was that in the case of lymph node-positive EGC, that is, in the case of N1 stage EGC, the TNM stage was classified as Ib, and the recurrence rate was reported to be 3.66%, and in the case of N2 stage, the TNM stage was classified as II, and the recurrence rate increased to 28.6% [14, 16], and many of these are expected to be related to MSI and HER2/EGFR overexpression.

HER2 acts as a networking receptor that mediates signaling to cancer cells, causing them to proliferate. HER2 gene amplification occurred during the early stages of gastric cancer and showed heterogeneity in several cases. Therefore, HER2 overexpression in AGC is frequently characterized by high grade, advanced stage, and high Ki-67 marker index, while HER2 overexpression in EGC is expected to be involved in tumor progression [8]. However, the importance of HER2 overexpression in EGC should not be overlooked because HER2 overexpression is significantly associated with tumor recurrence in lymph node-negative EGC [17]. The 5-year survival rate of patients with HER2-negative pN0 EGC (91.1%) is significantly higher than that of patients with HER2-positive pN0 EGC (81.8%) [18]. In addition, HER2 overexpression appears to be significantly associated with the development of micrometastasis in pN0 EGC. In summary, HER2 testing and differential treatment and/or follow-up strategies are inevitably important when diagnosing gastric cancer. In particular, assessing HER2 status in EGC may help identify patients at low risk of developing lymph node metastasis. In patients with undifferentiated EGC, multivariate analysis showed that HER2 overexpression was a good predictive marker for lymph node metastasis in patients with undifferentiated EGC [19]. It is noteworthy that the results of this study showed a correlation between MSI and HER2 overexpression. Combined with various previous results, it seems that some MSI cases in the early stage of EGC show HER2 overexpression, which may be the reason for their unexpectedly rapid growth or relapse (Fig. 4). However, this study is regrettable in that the number of subjects with HER2 overexpression in MSI EGC is small and the long-term prognosis was not confirmed. In terms of clinical outcomes, the significance of MSI EGC in relation to simultaneous or metachronous recurrence is unique. Although it is related to H. pylori infection, MSI status is not corrected even after eradication, and is associated with simultaneous or metachronous recurrence [20]. The results of this study clearly confirm its significance in cases that underwent endoscopic procedures. Therefore, in patients who underwent endoscopic mucosal resection or dissection, MSI status of early gastric cancer acts as an important predictor of metachronous recurrence. Although clear evidence has not yet been obtained, early surgical treatment seems to be a good clinical choice in such cases.

In conclusion, this study confirmed that MSI is related to HER2 overexpression, and it was a more prominent phenomenon in EGC than in AGC. Although long-term follow-up examinations have not been performed clinically, it is expected that tumor growth will be faster than expected because it is related to synchronous or metachronous recurrence and related to HER2 overexpression. In such cases, MSI-EGC should be recognized as a special disease group and more active measures should be taken.

A note on equidistribution on a product of Shimura curves and André–Oort
Authors: Francesco Maria Saettone
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this short note we show that Galois orbits of CM points equidistribute on a product of r⩾2 non-isomorphic Shimura curves by applying the adelic toral-packet equidistribution theorem of Aka–Luethi–Michel–Wieser. As a consequence, we deduce André–Oort for the product of those curves, previously studied by Edixhoven and Yafaev, replacing GRH by a Linnik-type splitting condition at two auxiliary primes.

Introduction

Nowadays, despite being commonly referred to as a conjecture, the André–Oort conjecture is a theorem by the work of many (see [19] for a recent survey), proved unconditionally via the Pila–Zannier strategy, which was first introduced for a new proof of Manin–Mumford in [16] and then exploited by Pila for the first unconditional proof of André–Oort for an arbitrary product of modular curves in [15]. Nonetheless, other conditional results in the case of product of two modular curves were obtained earlier, under GRH, by Edixhoven in [8] and in [9]. Under the same hypothesis, the two dimensional case was extended to Shimura curves by Yafaev in [20].

In this note we consider the case of r⩾2 non-isomorphic Shimura curves S1,⋯,Sr, and, by an equidistribution result for special points on their product

we obtain as a corollary the corresponding case of the André–Oort conjecture dropping GRH and assuming instead a mild congruence condition on two auxiliary primes (also known as a Linnik-type condition). The equidistribution input is the theorem of Aka–Luethi–Michel–Wieser [2, Theorem 1.8], which in turn relies on Einsiedler–Lindenstrauss’ joining theorem [10] together with some character-spectrum analysis.

Ergodically, the situation we consider is especially simple: since the curves S1,⋯,Sr are pairwise non-isomorphic, the product S1×⋯×Sr contains no graphs of Hecke correspondences. Consequently, the only positive-dimensional special subvarieties are fibers of CM points, and all joinings are trivial. A similar consideration on disjointness plays a crucial role in [2], where the authors prove a simultaneous equidistribution on the supersingular locus of modular curves. Other simultaneous equidistributions were proved unconditionally in [6] and [17] using Ratner’s theorem1.

Whenever joinings other than the trivial one appear, the situation becomes radically subtler. The majestic work of Khayutin [12] on the mixing conjecture exploited a striking combination of ergodic and analytic number theory especially to deal with the non-trivial joinings coming from Hecke correspondences, and it is conditional on the non-existence of an exceptional Landau–Siegel zero, together with a Linnik-type condition. The latter was removed, under GRH and the Ramanujan conjecture, in the work of Blomer–Brumley–Khayutin [3] for the self-product case r=2.

For r⩾2, let G1,⋯,Gr be r non-isomorphic forms of PGL2 and let S1,⋯,Sr be the Shimura curves relative to G1,⋯,Gr.

Theorem 1.1

Consider a sequence of CM points (zn)n in S1×⋯×Sr such that for all n, the coordinates of zn have the same CM field En. Fix two odd distinct primes p, q and assume that, for all natural numbers n, the primes p, q split in En. If the sequence (zn)n is strict2, then its Galois orbits become equidistributed in S1×⋯×Sr as the discriminants of En tend to infinity.

The above theorem yields, under the splitting condition on the En’s, a special case of a general (and, to the best of the author’s knowledge, still broadly open) folklore conjecture first written down by Shou-Wu Zhang in [22, Conjecture 2.2]. It is not hard to see that the equidistribution of Galois orbits of CM points is a refinement, and actually implies, the André–Oort conjecture (see, for instance, [22, Remarks.(1), p.3660]).

For r=2, it seems plausible that the techniques of [3, Theorem 2.2] could be adapted to remove the Linnik-type condition and to make the equidistribution effective, since the absence of Hecke graphs should allow one to drop several of their hypotheses.

In any case, Theorem 1.1 implies the following (conditional) case of the André–Oort conjecture.

From now on we will assume that all the Shimura curves we consider have maximal level structure, since this is sufficient for the André–Oort application considered here. This assumption also verifies automatically the norm-surjectivity hypothesis in the equidistribution theorem [2, Theorem 1.8]; see Theorem 2.4 below. Similarly, the assumption that the Shimura curves are defined over Q rather than over a totally real number field is irrelevant both to the diophantine and to the ergodic setting.

Lemma 2.2

Let X and X′ be smooth projective geometrically irreducible curves over a field F, and let p1:X×X′⟶X, and p2:X×X′⟶X′ be the projections on the first and second factor respectively. Let C⊂X×X′ be an irreducible curve such that p1(C)={x0} for some closed point x0∈X(F), and assume that p2|C is non constant. Then C={x0}×X′.

Proof

Set C′:=p1-1(x0)={x0}×S′ and let k(x0) denote the residue field at x0. Then C′ is an integral projective curve isomorphic to X′×SpecFSpec(k(x0)). Consider iC:C↪S×X′. Since p1∘iC is constant, ιC factors uniquely through C′, giving a morphism φ:C→C′. Because p2|C is nonconstant, the morphism φ is nonconstant. Hence it induces an inclusion at the level of function fields F(C′)↪F(C). Both F(C′) and F(C) have transcendence degree 1 over F, so the field extension F(C)/F(C′) is finite. Thus φ is a finite morphism between integral projective curves. We also have that iC is a closed immersion, so φ is a closed immersion as well. A finite morphism that is also a closed immersion between integral curves is an isomorphism, so C≃{x0}×X′ as closed subschemes of X×X′. □

Therefore, if one takes r=2 and X=S1, X′=S2 and assumes that C contains at least one CM point (and we actually assume it contains infinitely many of them), then C has to be a special subvariety.

Remark 2.5

Using the language of adelic double quotients and the equidistribution theorem above, it seems possible to prove the equidistribution of the reduction of Galois orbits of special points to the special fiber (S1×⋯×Sr)×Spec(F¯p). Naturally, the behaviour of B at p influences deeply the geometry of the special fiber at p. For individual equidistribution results covering both the case where Bp is unramified and the case where Bp is ramified, we refer to [18] and its citation orbit.

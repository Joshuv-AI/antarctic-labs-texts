Efficacy of BMP-2 in rotator cuff repair: a randomized controlled trial based on radiological and clinical outcomes
Authors: Xu-Yong Gong, Yu-Xia Yang, Jin-Young Park, Mei-Juan Yuan, Tao Bao, Yao-Jia Lu, Hang Yao, Wen-Yong Fei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTo develop an injectable recombinant human bone morphogenetic protein 2 (rh-BMP-2) hybrid material and evaluate its clinical efficacy in promoting tendon-bone interface healing in patients with rotator cuff injuries through clinical outcome assessments and MRI-based imaging analysis.Materials and methodsThis study included patients with rotator cuff tears who had an inadequate response to conservative treatment. The injectable rh-BMP2 hybrid material was synthesized. Ultimately, a total of 92 patients completed the final follow-up and were included in the analysis, with 46 patients in the experimental group (receiving the BMP-2 hybrid injection) and 46 in the control group (receiving no injection).Clinical evaluations were conducted preoperatively, at 1 and 3 months postoperatively for the Visual Analog Scale (VAS), and at 3 months and 1 year postoperatively for shoulder range of motion (shoulder flexion, external rotation, internal rotation), the American Shoulder and Elbow Surgeons (ASES) score, and Constant-Murley score. At the final follow-up (1 year), the abduction strength ratio and MRI signal-to-noise quotient were recorded.ResultsAt the 1-year final follow-up, the BMP-2 group showed significantly better outcomes across multiple key measures compared to the control group. The BMP-2 group showed superior outcomes in ASES score (93.58 ± 5.21 vs. 90.68 ± 7.17, p = 0.03), Constant-Murley score (87.17 ± 5.55 vs. 83.93 ± 6.18, p = 0.01), and Shoulder flexion (168.70°±12.83° vs. 163.70°±13.12°, p = 0.05). External Rotation range was significantly better in the BMP-2 group (68.70°±12.25° vs. 62.61°±12.33°, p = 0.02). The abduction strength ratio favored the BMP-2 group (82.25%±6.55% vs. 77.63%±10.23%, p = 0.01), and the MRI signal-to-noise quotient was significantly lower in the BMP-2 group (2.03 ± 0.59 vs. 2.26 ± 0.52, p = 0.05), indicating better tendon healing quality. VAS scores at 1 months were significantly lower in the BMP-2 group (1.24 ± 0.48 vs. 1.63 ± 0.69, p = 0.00).ConclusionsThe material can sustained release BMP2, which effectively promotes tendon-bone healing after rotator cuff repair, improves the function of the affected shoulder and reduces the rate of retear. It can provide a new possibility for improving outcomes after rotator cuff repair.Trial registrationThe questionnaires and methodology of this study have been registered with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (Registration number: ChiCTR2600118568.Registered on 09/02/2026.Retrospectively registered).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12891-026-09618-y.

Background

Despite technical refinements in rotator cuff repair, retear rates remain high (10–90%), which is largely attributable to poor healing at the tendon-bone interface [1, 2]. Tendon-bone healing involves two distinct mechanisms: the native direct stops, characterized by a transitional 4-layer structure (tendon–uncalcified fibrocartilage–calcified fibrocartilage–bone) that ensures efficient mechanical load transfer, and the indirect stops, formed through a biological process culminating in Sharpey’s fibers as an early healing marker [3]. Direct stops, with their ability to step up force transmission through the layering of tissue structures, have greater strength against tension than indirect stops [4].

Bone morphogenetic protein 2 (BMP-2) was the first BMP identified to induce osteogenesis and is considered one of the most potent osteogenic factors, and has also been shown to have the ability to promote the production of collagen type I, collagen type II, and cartilage-aggregating proteoglycans [5, 6]. Based on the biological activities in osteogenesis, cartilage, and tendon induction, more and more studies have shown that BMP-2 can significantly improve tendon-bone healing ability [7–9].

However, clinical studies on the effect of BMP-2 on rotator cuff tendon-bone interface healing remain limited. This study evaluated its impact by analyzing postoperative follow-up data after arthroscopic injection of BMP-2 into the interface during repair.

Methods and participants

Study design

This prospective, randomized controlled trial was conducted at Northern Jiangsu People’s Hospital between January 2022 and June 2024. The study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Northern Jiangsu People’s Hospital (Ethics approval number: 2022ky115). The reporting of this randomized trial adheres to the CONSORT guidelines.

Inclusion criteria

Aged between 18 and 75 years. Diagnosis of a symptomatic rotator cuff tear confirmed by both preoperative MRI and intraoperative arthroscopy. Patients with an inadequate response to at least 3 months of structured conservative treatment (e.g., physical therapy, NSAIDs, and activity modification). Ability to understand and provide informed consent for the study protocol. Willingness and ability to comply with the standardized postoperative rehabilitation protocol and complete the scheduled follow-up assessments.

Exclusion criteria

Combined with shoulder instability, shoulder arthritis, periarthritic fracture dislocation, or nerve injury. MRI evaluation of rotator cuff muscle atrophy and fat infiltration, the supraspinatus muscle tangential sign is positive, or the Goutallier grade > III. Shoulder previous surgical history. Those who were mentally disturbed.

The primary objective of the trial was to evaluate the efficacy of Bone Morphogenetic Protein-2 (BMP-2) in promoting tendon healing following arthroscopic rotator cuff repair. Eligible patients were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to either the experimental group (receiving the BMP-2 hybrid injection) or the control group (receiving no injection). The randomization sequence was generated by an independent statistician using SPSS software (version 25.0), and allocation was concealed with sealed, opaque envelopes. Although the surgical team was unavoidably unblinded, both the patients and the radiologists responsible for assessing postoperative MRI and clinical outcomes remained blinded to group assignment throughout the study.

A total of 132 patients with rotator cuff injuries were screened for eligibility. After exclusion of 38 patients (20 not meeting inclusion criteria, 12 declining participation, and 6 for other reasons), 94 were randomized into two treatment groups: 47 to the experimental group receiving BMP-2 hybrid injection and 47 to the control group receiving no injection. During follow-up, one participant from each group was lost to follow-up, resulting in a final analysis cohort of 46 patients per group (92 total participants). (Fig. 1; Table 1)

Experimental group: The experimental material was prepared as follows: First, 0.5 mg of lyophilized rhBMP-2 bone repair material (Hangzhou Jiuyuan Gene Engineering Co., Ltd.) was reconstituted with 1 mL of sterile saline according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Next, the reconstituted rhBMP-2 solution was thoroughly mixed with 1 mL of medical chitosan solution by gentle pipetting until a homogeneous gel composite was achieved. Finally, the resulting composite was loaded into a syringe equipped with a long needle for application. The mixture was then injected into multiple points at the tendon-bone interface of the repaired rotator cuff footprint area (Fig. 2).

Postoperative patients in both groups used the same rehabilitation training program and were guided by the same team of rehabilitation instructors specializing in Sports Medicine. The rehabilitation protocol progressed in phases: initial immobilization in an abduction brace with CPM-assisted passive motion (0–6 weeks), followed by active-assisted and active exercises (e.g., wall climbing, pulley training) from 6 to 12 weeks. Strengthening with resistance bands commenced after 3 months, with a full return to normal activities permitted after 6 months.

Surgical techniques

All surgical procedures were selected as arthroscopic surgery. All patients were operated under general anesthesia with endotracheal intubation in the lateral decubitus position. The operation was performed by the same senior surgeon. During surgery, we routinely performed subacromial decompression. The rotator cuff and the greater tuberosity of the humerus were freshly treated with a burr. Medial row repair was performed by inserting one or two 4.5-mm diameter absorbable suture anchors (Smith & Nephew) into the rotator cuff stump. The anchor thread was sutured to the rotator cuff stump using the suture hook over thread technique, keeping the distance between the sutures equal and evenly distributed on the rotator cuff stump, and the same thread was knotted to fix the proximal rotator cuff stump in the medial aspect of the footprint area of the greater tuberosity. For lateral fixation, one or two rows of 4.5 mm × 24.0 mm Push-Lock anchors (Arthrex, USA) were used. The sutures were then configured in a suture-bridge technique, whereby the caudal threads between the different knots were crisscrossed and fixed on the lateral aspect of the greater tuberosity. The suture tension was maintained appropriately to ensure the torn rotator cuff completely covered the footprint area.

Clinical evaluation

Postoperative follow-up was carried out in the sports medicine clinic for all patients. To ensure standardization, a senior consultant specializing in sports medicine performed all clinical measurements and outcome score evaluations. Based on established clinical trajectories where visual analogue scale (VAS) scores plateau after 3 months, we therefore limited postoperative VAS assessments to the 1-month and 3-month time points [10]. We assessed shoulder function using the American Shoulder and Elbow Surgeons (ASES) and Constant-Murley score, along with range of motion, at 3 months and 12 months postoperatively. At the 12-month follow-up, we measured scapular plane abduction strength with a dynamometer (Cools et al.; accuracy: 0.01 lb) in both arms and recorded the affected-to-healthy side strength ratio.

MRI scan and evaluation

Signal intensity (SI) on Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is thought to reflect the healing of the repaired tendon. The signal/noise quotient (SNQ) eliminates the effect of background noise, and therefore it is considered a reliable method of measuring SI [11]. All patients underwent a standardized MRI examination of the operated shoulder at the final follow-up visit (12 months postoperatively) using a 3.0-T MRI scanner (Siemens Magnetom Skyra) with a dedicated shoulder coil. The imaging protocol included oblique coronal T2-weighted fat-suppressed sequences (TR/TE:3000–4000/50-80ms; slice thickness: 3 mm; FOV: 160 × 160 mm) to optimally visualize the repaired tendon and assess for fluid signal. In this study, Sugaya classification [12] and MRI signal intensity analysis was used to assess the degree of healing of the tendon-bone interface area in all patients. All MRI scans were assessed independently by two blinded musculoskeletal radiologists. For the purposes of this study, we defined a standardized region of interest (ROI) termed the “tendon-bone interface zone” on the oblique coronal T2 fat suppression MRI sequences. This zone was delineated as the area extending approximately 3 mm above and below the tendon-bone interface, confined between the medial and lateral rows of suture anchors. In this area, the Sante DICOM Viewer 3D Pro software program issued by “Sante soft LTD” was used to select a circular area of 15 pixels as the region of interest (ROI), and the average signal value in this area was recorded. In order to quantify the standardized SI of the tendon, a circular area of 15 pixels was also selected as the background signal intensity measurement area at the anterolateral 2 cm position of the shoulder. The signal-to-noise quotient (SNQ) of the two specially defined regions was calculated according to the following formula: SNQ = ROI signal intensity / background signal intensity. The results were expressed as SNQr (SNQ of the ROI) and SNQb (SNQ of the background). Three consecutive slice images containing the suture anchors were chosen for measurement. All SNQ evaluations (including ROI placement and intensity measurement) were performed independently by two musculoskeletal radiologists blinded to patient group allocation. The inter-observer reliability for the mean SNQ measurement was excellent, with an intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) of 0.95. The values from both observers were then averaged to obtain the final mean SNQ value for each case [13] (Figs. 4 and 5).

Statistical analysis

SPSS.25 statistical software was applied for analysis. The Shapiro-Wilk method was used to test the normality of the data, in which the measurements that conformed to normal distribution were expressed as (x ± s), and the data were subjected to independent samples t-test; the measurements that did not conform to normal distribution, such as the size of the rotator cuff tear, the forward flexion, the external rotation, the internal rotation mobility, the sugaya scores, and the signal ratios, were expressed as [M (Q1, Q3 )], with M is the median, Q is the quartile, and Mann-Whitney U test was used between the two groups. The Mann-Whitney U test was used for the grade data; the χ2 test was used for the count data, and the difference was considered to be statistically significant at P ≤ 0.05.

Results

This randomized controlled trial demonstrates that augmentation of arthroscopic rotator cuff repair with BMP-2 leads to significantly superior clinical and structural outcomes after approximately one year compared to standard repair alone. The two groups demonstrated excellent baseline comparability, with no statistically significant differences in all preoperative demographic characteristics and outcome measures (P > 0.05, Table 1).

At the 3-month postoperative assessment, no significant intergroup differences were observed in shoulder range of motion (flexion, external rotation, internal rotation), ASES scores, or Constant-Murley scores (P > 0.05, Tables 2 and 3). Despite this comparable functional recovery, the BMP-2 group exhibited superior early pain control, with significantly lower VAS scores at the 1-month assessment (1.24 ± 0.48 vs. 1.63 ± 0.69, P = 0.00,Table 3).

After a mean follow-up of 14 ± 2.39 months (range: 10–19 months), the BMP-2 group demonstrated significantly superior outcomes across multiple domains. Functional assessment revealed better ASES (93.58 ± 5.21 vs. 90.68 ± 7.17, p = 0.03) and Constant-Murley scores (87.17 ± 5.55 vs. 83.93 ± 6.18, p = 0.01, Table 3). Range of motion measurements showed greater improvement in shoulder flexion (168.70° ± 12.83° vs. 163.70° ± 13.12°, p = 0.05) and external rotation (68.70° ± 12.25° vs. 62.61° ± 12.33°, p = 0.02), while internal rotation remained comparable between groups (8.80 ± 1.03° vs. 8.63 ± 1.03°, P = 0.34, Table 2).

The BMP-2 group also achieved better abduction strength recovery (82.25% ± 6.55% vs. 77.63% ± 10.23%, P = 0.01, Table 3). Most importantly, quantitative MRI assessment at approximately 1 year postoperatively confirmed enhanced structural healing in the BMP-2 group, evidenced by a lower signal-to-noise quotient (2.03 ± 0.59 vs. 2.26 ± 0.52, p = 0.05) and more favorable tendon integrity on Sugaya classification (P = 0.04, Table 4).

These results indicate that while BMP-2 augmentation provides early pain relief, its most significant benefits on functional recovery and structural healing become evident at longer-term follow-up.

Discussion

In this study, the BMP-2 material was administered after finalizing the repair via multiple dispersed injections across the tendon-bone interface. Radiological assessment revealed a more extensive healing zone in the BMP-2 group. This improved structural restoration, as seen on imaging, may facilitate a more favorable biomechanical force distribution, which could explain the enhanced functional outcomes observed in these patients. Consequently, this treatment was effective in enhancing postoperative functional outcomes and promoting superior structural healing on MRI.

Unsuccessful healing is often caused by gap formation at this interface because of failed osteo fibroblastic integration [14]. Considering that long-term mechanical unloading in chronic rotator cuff tears is often accompanied by bone loss at the interface [15], which often leads to loosening or extraction of the anchor nail, resulting in failure of the surgical intervention [16]. To objectively assess the tendon-bone interface, we quantitatively analyzed the signal intensity within a defined region of interest using MRI. This method, which reduces the potential for subjective reading errors, revealed that the BMP-2 group achieved superior structural healing, characterized by a more normalized signal profile. The significantly lower Signal-to-Noise Quotient (SNQ) in the tendon-bone interface region were lower in the experimental group than in the control group, responding to the fact that the experimental group had a better recovery of the tendon, bone surface and bone bed in this region than the control group, which had a role in promoting the healing of the tendon-bone interface of the rotator cuff.

A growing number of studies have demonstrated that rhBMP-2 significantly accelerates tendon-bone fusion during tendon-bone repair, promotes the healing of the direct tendon-bone stop, and improves its biomechanical strength [17–20]. However, BMP-2 is difficult and expensive to extract, and due to its rapid degradation and loss rate, the effect of the local growth factor is not effective and long-lasting [21]. The experiments proved that the suitable carrier of BMP not only can keep the protein in sufficient time to guide the osteogenesis in its application position, but also maintain the structural integrity and biological activity of the protein [22]. Compared to the rapid degradation and loss of BMP-2 in conventional topical applications, the release of BMP-2 is further delayed because the material is mixed with medical chitosan [23]. With the degradation and uptake of the carrier, rhBMP-2 is slowly released, forming a rhBMP-2 concentration gradient around the implanted material, which increases the time of action between rhBMP-2 and target cells. While achieving the effect of having a good encapsulated growth factor and solving the problems of short half-life and rapid degradation of BMP-2 factor, it also takes into account the biocompatibility, biodegradability, absorbability and convenient handling of the encapsulated material. Our strategy was therefore designed to fully utilize the biological role of BMP-2 to enhance the healing process.

XilinC et al. demonstrated that BMP-2 is involved in regulating the cartilage while promoting bone regeneration and bone resorption and improve the mechanical properties of subchondral bone, which effectively protects the cartilage and further reduces postoperative pain [24]. Analysis of the results of VAS scores of the patients in this study revealed that two groups were statistically significant at the 1-month postoperative review (P = 0.001). The reduction of pain enables the patients to cooperate with the rehabilitation training earlier and more actively during the postoperative recovery process. Biologically, in supraspinatus tendon tears, early exercise promotes the expression of fibroblast growth factor and the synthesis of type III collagen at the tendon-bone interface early in the repair process [25], resulting in a virtuous cycle. However, after 3 months postoperatively, patients in the control group (who did not receive the BMP-2 injection) also experienced a substantial reduction in pain, likely due to the natural progression of healing following successful surgical repair. Consequently, the difference in VAS scores between the two groups was not statistically significant beyond this time point. This is consistent with the findings of Williams RC et al. [26], who reported that pain reached its peak intensity at 6 weeks after arthroscopic rotator cuff repair and that it tended to diminish thereafter.

This study has several limitations. First, the sample size was relatively small, which may limit the statistical power of our findings. Future studies with a larger cohort are needed to validate our results. Second, the follow-up period was limited to approximately 12 months. A longer-term follow-up (e.g., 3‑5 years) would be valuable to more comprehensively analyze the potential differences between the two surgical techniques. Third, the study did not perform a subgroup analysis based on tear type (e.g., full‑thickness vs. partial‑thickness tears). Therefore, our results cannot differentiate whether the effect of BMP‑2 varies across different tear patterns. Future studies with stratified designs are warranted to identify the patient subgroups that may benefit most from this augmentation technique. Fourth, we did not perform a quantitative analysis of the BMP‑2 concentration gradient at the injection site. Investigating a range of concentrations in future work could help determine the optimal dosage and further refine the treatment protocol.

Conclusion

This study confirmed that in patients with rotator cuff tears, the application of BMP-2 material can promote faster and more robust healing of the tendon-bone interface, enhance the function of the affected shoulder, and ultimately improve the clinical prognosis.

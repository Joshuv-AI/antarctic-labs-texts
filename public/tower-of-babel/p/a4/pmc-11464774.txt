Investigating fractal fractional PDEs, electric circuits, and integral inclusions via (ψ,ϕ)-rational type contractions
Authors: Khaled Aldwoah, Syed Khayyam Shah, Sadam Hussain, Mohammed A Almalahi, Yagoub A S Arko, Manel Hleili
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe fixed point theory has been generalized mainly in two directions. One is the extension of the spaces, and the other is relaxing and generalizing the contractions. This paper aims to establish novel fixed point results of rational type generalized (ψ,ϕ)-contractions in the context of extended b-metric spaces. This will allow us to analyze generalized rational type contraction in a more relaxed and diversified framework in the light of the characteristics of (ψ,ϕ). Some existing rational-type contractions have been recalled in this direction, and others are defined. New fixed point results have been established by utilizing the properties of ψ and ϕ and applying the iteration technique. Moreover, the established results are employed to investigate the stability of fractal and fractional differential equations and electric circuits. For the reliability of the established results, examples and applications to the system of integral inclusions and system of integral equations are presented.Keywords: Fixed point, Integral inclusion, Integral equations, RLC-circuits, Fractal fractional differential equations, Extended b-metric space, Partial differential equations, Fractional derivativeSubject terms: Applied physics, Mathematics and computing

Introduction and preliminaries

Differential Equations pose a significant part in recognizing, modeling and understanding a wide range of real world natural and artificial systems. The importance of differential equations, notably fractal fractional differential equations, can be determined by their capacity to describe the evaluation of systems with time by connecting functions with their differentials. Fractal fractional differential equations have attracted researchers due to their utilization in the fields of mathematics, physics, and biology. Equations of this type help analyze and describe mathematical dynamic systems in physics, engineering, biology, economics, etc.

The development of the concept of fractal geometry into the equation of fractal fractional partial differential equations (PDEs) offers a natural extension to the traditional fractional PDEs. This development plays a significant role in delineating complex systems exhibiting irregular geometries, investigates the stability of complex-valued neural networks, stochastic delay, Ulam type stability and multi-scale capabilities1–7. Many processes are part of these systems. For example, flow through porous stuff, turbulent flow, and diffusion in varied environments. Fractal fractional partial differential equations PDEs provide a more comprehensive representation of natural systems’ intricate geometry and scale invariance.

Equations of this nature effectively represent the complex details and unchanging patterns in natural and artificial systems8–10. These types of equations can be demonstrated and investigated with different approaches. One noteworthy approach is the fixed-point theory(FPT). FPT has been attracting researchers for the last decade because of its diversity and significance in the analysis of real-world problems and systems.

Furthermore, the applications of FPT also contribute to the advancement of electrical engineering, notably in circuitry, i.e., the design and efficiency of the circuits. These integral inclusions usually come into existence as the result of mathematical modeling of systems, like those in population dynamics, control theory, and mathematical biology. The current manuscript presents the generalized (ψ,ϕ)-Rational-type contractions in the framework of EMb. The (ψ,ϕ)-contractions offer a generalization of the traditional contractions for a broader scope of analysis of convergence and the existence of fixed points in a broader context. Rational-type contractions expand the context of FPT by accommodating more intricate and realistic mappings, notably in the framework of nonlinear analysis. The rational-type contractions in the light of ψ and ϕ provide a more flexible framework compared to traditional contractions in order to ensure the existence and uniqueness of equations. Likewise, EMb offers a flexible domain and a natural setting for studying such contractions. By exploring (ψ,ϕ)-Rational-type contractions in the context of EMb, we establish novel results that are significant in many areas of mathematics and engineering regarding their applications. The established results are employed to guarantee the stability of some significant problems like fractal fractional PDEs, electric models, and integral inclusions and equations.

Conclusion

This paper aims to introduce (ψ,ϕ)-rational-type contraction in the context of extended b-metric spaces, exploring its versatility and flexibility across several domains of mathematics and engineering. The authenticity of the results has been checked by addressing fundamental problems, notably Integral inclusions, RLC electric circuits, and fractal fractional partial differential equations. Examples are provided to strengthen the validity of the obtained results. This exploration promises to advance theoretical research and practical applications, offering new avenues for innovation in mathematics and its interdisciplinary applications.

In the future, researchers can utilize these contractions to establish novel results in the framework of complex-valued extended b-metric spaces, disclosed extended b-metric spaces, and super-metric spaces. The established results will be useful in investigating the stability of systems of integral equations, integral inclusions, fractal-fractional PDEs, mathematical models, disease models, etc.

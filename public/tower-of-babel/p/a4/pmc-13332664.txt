Recognizing Early Career Translational Investigators
Authors: Matthias Nahrendorf
Published: 2026

JACC: Basic to Translational Science is committed to supporting and promoting early career investigators who are pursuing translational science. To achieve this, JACC: Basic to Translational Science will provide biographical information and a photograph of the first or senior authors who are early career investigators in the issue where their paper appears. Additionally, this biographical information will be shared on our social media platforms at the time of publication.

We are proud to recognize and showcase the work of the following early career investigators, whose research is featured in this issue of the JACC: Basic to Translational Science.

Vinay Penna BA

Paper: Interleukin-1β Drives Disease Progression in Arrhythmogenic Cardiomyopathy

Vinay Penna will obtain his MD/PhD from Washington University in St Louis School of Medicine in May of 2027. He completed his thesis work in the laboratory of Dr. Kory Lavine, focusing on the role of the innate immune system in the pathogenesis of arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy. He intends to further his clinical training in internal medicine and pursue a career as a physician scientist.

Noreen Ahmed, PhD

Paper: Targeted Intra-Atrial Delivery of GMP-Grade Human Extracellular Vesicles Prevents Inflammation-Driven Postoperative Atrial Fibrillation in Pigs

Noreen Ahmed, PhD is a Postdoctoral Fellow in the laboratory of Dr. Darryl Davis at the University of Ottawa Heart Institute, where she focuses on the development and understanding of extracellular vesicle-based therapeutics for cardiovascular disease. Her research centers on understanding how exosome-mediated signaling can modulate inflammation and fibrosis, with an emphasis on advancing clinically translatable strategies. She is particularly interested in bridging basic science discoveries with therapeutic development to address unmet needs in cardiac injury and remodeling. Through her work, Dr. Ahmed aims to contribute to innovative biologic therapies that improve outcomes in cardiovascular disease.

Jose Manuel Condor Capcha, PhD, MSc

Paper: LDLR-OPN Interaction Drives COVID-19 Myocarditis Through Monocyte Recruitment

Jose Manuel Condor Capcha, PhD, MSc, is a faculty member in the Department of Medicine, Division of Cardiology at the University of Miami Miller School of Medicine. He earned his PhD in pathophysiology from the University of Sao Paulo, Brazil. His research focuses on uncovering the molecular mechanisms underlying viral myocarditis, combining in vivo and in vitro models with advanced molecular approaches to investigate the low-density lipoprotein receptor–osteopontin complex and Gasdermin D–mediated pyroptosis in myocarditis. His work aims to identify novel therapeutic targets to prevent or reverse viral cardiac inflammation.

Paul Spangler, MD

Paper: Atrial Fibrillation Increases Proarrhythmic Mechanisms in the Ventricle

Paul Spangler, MD, is a physician-scientist in the Department of Cardiology at the University of Regensburg, Germany. He completed his medical training at the University of Regensburg, Germany, including clinical rotations at the University of California-San Diego, and Inselspital Bern. Paul is currently a postdoctoral fellow and resident in Internal Medicine and Cardiology at the University Medical Center Regensburg. His work focuses on atrial and ventricular arrhythmias, CaMKII signaling, and inflammation-driven arrhythmogenesis. His research has been supported by the Otto Hess Fellowship of the German Cardiac Society, and he is currently funded through a clinician scientist program by the German Research Foundation (DFG).

Yixuan Liu, MS

Paper: Feasibility of Magnetic Resonance Imaging–Guided Pulmonary Artery Stenting in a Commercial Wide-Bore 0.55-T Scanner

Yixuan Liu is a PhD candidate in Biomedical Engineering at The Ohio State University, working under the mentorship of Dr. Orlando Simonetti and Dr. Aimee Armstrong. He received his MS from Johns Hopkins University and his BS from the University of Minnesota Twin Cities. His research focuses on magnetic resonance imaging–guided interventional procedures at low field strength, with an emphasis on real-time image reconstruction using parallel imaging and AI. He is a recipient of a 2-year American Heart Association Predoctoral Fellowship. His work aims to develop AI tools that advance the clinical translation of magnetic resonance imaging– interventions.

Worawan B. Limpitikul, MD, PhD

Paper: Increased Arrhythmic Risk in Obesity Is Transduced by Adipose Tissue-Derived Extracellular Vesicles

Dr. Worawan B. Limpitikul completed her MD and PhD degrees at the Johns Hopkins University School of Medicine, where she studied the regulation of cardiac voltage-gated calcium channels under Drs. David Yue and Gordon Tomaselli. Her graduate work elucidated mechanisms underlying a novel calmodulin-related long-QT syndrome and developed precision therapeutic strategies for this disease. She subsequently completed her internal medicine residency at the University of California-San Francisco, and her cardiovascular disease fellowship at Massachusetts General Hospital, where she investigated obesity-related arrhythmias under Dr. Saumya Das. She is currently completing her clinical cardiac electrophysiology fellowship at Massachusetts General Hospital.

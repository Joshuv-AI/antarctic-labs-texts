To the Editor:

Fibrotic diseases contribute to nearly 45% of all deaths in the developed world, affecting vital organs such as the lungs, liver, heart and kidneys. Transforming growth factor (TGF)-β is a central driver of fibrosis, promoting the activation of fibroblasts into myofibroblasts and the excessive deposition of extracellular matrix [1]. The cell-type-specific activation of p21-activated kinase 2 (PAK2) in TGF-β signalling has been recognised for some time [2, 3], but its role in fibrotic diseases has only recently come to light [4]. With great interest, we read the recent article by Watanabe et al. [5], describing a novel cellular mechanism by which PAK2 may drive fibrotic remodelling and its implication for potential therapeutic targeting in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF). This study offers valuable insights into the distinct fibroblast populations within fibroblastic foci (FF) and dense fibrosis (DF) regions of IPF through integrated spatial and single-cell transcriptomic analysis. The authors identify PAK2 as a key fibrogenic kinase activated in FF and surrounding areas, and demonstrate that its inhibition attenuates fibrosis in a murine model, suggesting PAK2 as a promising therapeutic target.

IPF is a progressive and fatal fibrotic lung disease characterised by persistent myofibroblast activation and accumulation at sites of tissue injury, and excessive extracellular matrix deposition. In this study the authors anatomically segmented the IPF lungs into FF, transitional regions between fibrotic and morphologically preserved parenchyma, characterised by proliferating fibroblasts and myofibroblasts, and DF, which represents mature fibrotic scarring that replaces normal alveolar architecture and compromises gas exchange. They identified previously characterised CTHRC1+ collagen-producing fibroblasts [6], as well as a novel WNT5A+CTHRC1+ myofibroblast population that was enriched in both FF and DF regions. Prior studies have shown that CTHRC1+ fibroblasts are uniquely enriched in fibrotic lungs, produce pathological extracellular matrix, and exhibit a highly migratory phenotype with strong engraftment potential in fibrotic niches [6]. Building on this concept, the authors report that FF are predominantly populated by CTHRC1+ fibroblasts, whereas DF regions exhibit a higher proportion of WNT5A+CTHRC1+ myofibroblasts, suggesting a transitional state that may underlie fibrotic remodelling (figure 1a). This is supported by our previous work showing that WNT5A is specifically and consistently expressed in both developing and fibrotic myofibroblasts [7], as well as earlier studies demonstrating that WNT5A is upregulated in IPF and promotes fibroblast-to-myofibroblast differentiation via activation of TGF-β signalling [8]. The identification of WNT5A+CTHRC1+ myofibroblasts in both FF and DF highlights their potential role in bridging early fibrogenic activity with advanced scar formation. While these observations are highly insightful, the study would be further strengthened by clarifying the molecular regulatory mechanisms driving the transition from FF to DF, particularly whether this progression is mediated through WNT5A-dependent activation of TGF-β signalling. Additionally, building on prior findings that fibroblast-derived WNT5A promotes lung regeneration by inducing Axin2 expression in adjacent alveolar type 2 stem cells after injury [9], a compelling yet unresolved avenue for future investigation is to delineate the broader role of WNT5A in maintaining lung homeostasis and orchestrating repair processes in the context of fibrotic lung diseases.

Strategy for targeting p21-activated kinase (PAK) 2 in pathogenic fibroblasts of idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF). a) Schematic illustration of representative fibroblast subpopulations in normal lung, fibroblastic foci and dense fibrosis regions, highlighting the relevance of PAK activation in IPF progression. b) Major cell type annotations of a published single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) atlas (GSE135893) on healthy and IPF lungs. c) UMAP (uniform manifold approximation and projection) visualisation of the expression of PAK2 and PAK1 in the scRNA-seq atlas, demonstrating broad expression across major cell compartments. d) Split violin plots comparing PAK2 and PAK1 expression levels among major cell types in healthy versus IPF lungs.

Another significant contribution of the study is the identification of transcriptional programmes distinguishing FF from DF regions through histological segmentation and spatial transcriptomics. The authors highlight PAK2 as a key kinase enriched in FF and surrounding areas, with PAK2 target gene signatures strongly aligning with cells in the FF region. PAK2 belongs to the p21-activated kinase (PAK) family, a group of serine/threonine kinases involved in regulating diverse cellular processes. PAK2 has been suggested to function as a Smad-independent effector of TGF-β signalling in fibroblasts to drive their proliferation and morphological transformation [3]. In liver fibrosis, PAK proteins, particularly PAK1 and PAK3, act as key intracellular mediators of pro-fibrotic signalling in myofibroblasts, and their inhibition effectively attenuates liver fibrosis [4]. These findings underscore the potential role of PAK kinases in modulating TGF-β-driven myofibroblast activation across fibrotic diseases. In this study, pharmacological inhibition of PAK signalling using FRAX486 and FRAX597 attenuated TGF-β-induced myofibroblast differentiation in vitro and reduced lung fibrosis in bleomycin-treated mice, positioning PAK2 as a promising therapeutic target (figure 1a).

While the pharmacological targeting of PAK2 shows promising potential in treating pulmonary fibrosis, its broad expression across multiple lung cell types, including epithelial, endothelial and immune populations (figure 1b–d) [10], raises important considerations for therapeutic specificity. Future studies investigating the role of PAK2 in alveolar regeneration, vascular integrity and immune modulation in fibrotic models and IPF patients would greatly enhance the translational relevance of these findings. Such efforts may also guide the development of more targeted therapeutic strategies that minimise potential off-target effects or toxicity in non-fibroblast compartments. Additionally, although PAK2 expression appeared higher than that of PAK1 (figure 1c and d) or PAK3, as the authors stated and discussed in the study, the inhibitors used may still impact other PAK isoforms, which warrants further specificity and safety evaluation in future therapeutic development.

In summary, this elegant and comprehensive study reported by Watanabe et al. [5] offers important insights into the spatial and molecular heterogeneity of fibroblasts in IPF. By identifying fibrotic fibroblast subsets, particularly the WNT5A+CTHRC1+ population, and uncovering a central role for PAK2 in driving pro-fibrotic signalling, the authors provide a compelling framework for therapeutic intervention. Their findings support the concept that targeting specific fibroblast populations within defined anatomical niches may be key to halting or reversing fibrosis. As the field moves forward, these data lay a strong foundation for the development of more precise and cell-type-targeted antifibrotic therapies.

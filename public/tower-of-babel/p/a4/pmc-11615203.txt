Reliability evaluation of electric-gas-thermal coupling systems considering the post-fault operation modes of user-level integrated energy system
Authors: Bo Zhang, Jia Li, Lu Zhang, Wei Tang, Yan Sun
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
SummaryThe distribution network with user-level integrated energy systems (UIESs) represents a new form of future energy systems. Traditional reliability evaluation methods for distribution networks with multiple UIESs are no longer applicable due to their multi-energy coupling and island operation. To address this, a reliability evaluation method for electric-gas-thermal coupling systems with UIESs is introduced. Post-fault operation modes of UIESs are proposed considering the multi-energy cooperation and joint island operation. The criteria of fault correlation matrixes are further established to quickly determine the energy supply state of the loads and the operation mode of the UIESs. Moreover, a static island division model and a dynamic load reduction model for the UIES are embedded in the reliability assessment. The effectiveness and practicality of the proposed reliability evaluation method are verified, as the results accurately calculate reliability indices and offer guidance for micro-energy grid operations under various fault conditions.Subject areas: Electrical engineering, Energy systems

Introduction

User-level integrated energy system (UIES) is a small integrated energy system (IES) with multi-energy complementarity, energy conversion, and mutual supply capabilities. The distribution network with multiple UIESs is the new form of the future energy grid, which meets the requirements of the development of low-carbon energy and the efficient utilization of various energy sources. Although the operation modes of IESs can be switched flexibly and inter-grid multi-energy support can be utilized to ensure reliable energy supply for important loads during system failures,1 the integration of a large number of UIESs to the distribution network has a great impact on the reliability of the network’s energy supply. Therefore, how to accurately evaluate the energy supply reliability of a distribution network with multiple UIESs is of great significance.

A machine learning model combining random forest feature selection and extreme gradient boosting regression was proposed for the reliability evaluation of electric-gas coupling systems under scenarios of random component failure probability.2 A day-ahead security-constrained unit commitment model was proposed for the integrated electricity and natural gas system to schedule the operation and reserve simultaneously considering reliability requirements.3 With the energy flow between electric power systems and gas systems and the charging and discharging characteristics of energy storage systems fully considered, Zeng et al. proposed a reliability evaluation method for electric-gas coupling systems based on sequential Monte Carlo simulation.4 Based on the Stackelberg game model, Wang et al. proposed a post-emergency dispatch model for the electric-thermal coupling system, which improved the energy supply reliability of the coupling system under the framework of the multi-energy coupling market.5 Chen et al. proposed a rapid reliability evaluation method for electric-gas coupling systems based on the decoupling calculation of the reliability of each energy subsystem.6 Three failure models of natural gas pipelines were established using the virtual node method and additional probability distribution functions, and the important sampling method based on cross-entropy was adopted to improve the calculation efficiency of the reliability evaluation of the electrical-gas coupling system.7 Koeppel et al. analyzed the mutual dependency among the energy subsystems in the micro-energy grid, i.e., electricity, gas, and heat subsystems, and proposed a reliability evaluation model of the micro-energy grid.8 The weather impacts were considered in the reliability assessment of the micro-energy grid.9 Zhao et al. proposed a reliability evaluation method for UIESs based on a fault correlation matrix, which can quickly calculate the network reliability index through simple algebraic operations and Boolean operations.10 However, it does not consider multi-energy cooperation between IESs, making it unsuitable for evaluating the reliability of distribution networks with multiple IESs. Pan et al. measured the quality differences of various energy sources such as electric energy, gas, and thermal energy through the exergy and proposed an energy supply priority strategy and a reliability evaluation index system for micro-energy grids.11 The energy conversion, transmission, and storage in the micro-energy grid were described concisely and linearly based on the energy hub model, and a multi-parameter linear programming method was proposed with an adaptive critical area set to improve the computational efficiency of the system reliability calculation.12 Ding et al. proposed the multi-state transition matrix of the micro-energy grid and extended the general generating function method to the matrix form, so that the reliability of the micro-energy grid system with multi-energy coupled and multiple states is evaluated.13

However, the aforementioned studies on the influence of energy conversion device failure and multi-energy cooperation capability on system reliability are aimed at the interior of large-scale IESs or micro-energy grids and are not applicable to the reliability assessment of electric distribution systems with multiple IESs.

For the reliability evaluation of the electric-gas-thermal coupling system, which is connected to the UIESs, Meng et al. proposed the reliability evaluation index considering the satisfaction of users and user groups, but users’ active participation in the failure recovery process was not fully considered in the evaluation process.14 A reliability assessment method was proposed for multi-energy coupling systems based on layered decoupling, in which the impact of energy distribution network failure on the energy input of the micro-energy grid was measured, but the transition of the micro-energy grid operation mode after the fault and the mutual support among multiple micro-energy grids were not considered.15 Hong et al. considered the coordinated operation of multiple micro-energy grids after a fault and established a load reduction model with the aim of minimizing the total energy reduction, but did not consider the joint optimal energy supply of various loads in the isolated island.16

The existing reliability evaluation methods applied in distribution networks with multiple UIESs have the following shortcomings.

(1)The fault scenarios of the distribution network with multiple UIESs are diverse, and it is necessary to further consider the impact of the failure of various energy networks and equipment external to the IESs on the internal loads. The reliability of the distribution network may be further improved or more accurately evaluated by taking into account the fault support capability among multiple micro-energy grids and their energy supply to external loads.

The hourly load reduction at each load point is calculated according to the proposed static island division model and the dynamic load reduction model.

Compared to scenario 1, in scenario 2, the micro-energy grid adopts an incomplete island-independent operation mode under the coupling and mutual cooperation of various energy subsystems, and the amount of load reduction at the internal load points 1, 2, and 5 (corresponding to micro-energy grids 1, 2, and 3) is decreased to 0 during the entire fault repair period. However, the amount of load reduction at load points 3 and 4 in scenario 2 remains the same as in scenario 1 because no joint island is formed to support the external electric loads, which indicates the energy in the micro-energy grid was not fully utilized.

Comparing scenario 1 and scenario 3, the micro-energy grid is essentially a micro-grid in the independently operated energy subsystems. In scenario 3, the micro-energy grid adopts the complete island joint operation mode to form a joint island to supply the load in the affected area. Under this operation mode, the amount of load reduction at load point 1 decreases, but that at load point 2 increases as it is less important. In the event of an energy supply shortage, the reduction of important load shedding is achieved by sacrificing the energy supply reliability of less important loads.

Based on the coupled operation of energy subsystems, the formation of joint islands enables the characteristics of the mutual cooperation of micro-energy grids, and the redundant energy among multi-energy systems to be fully utilized in scenario 4. The amount of load reduction of the internal loads and the external loads is reduced to zero. Under the synergistic effect of coupling mutual cooperation and joint islands, the overall load shedding of micro-energy grid islands is greatly reduced during the recovery process of a single fault.

The concept of the island operation mode of the micro-energy grid proposed in this paper is an extension of the island concept in the traditional single-energy system, and it is adaptable to the analysis of the island operation mode of the traditional system. The island load reduction calculation method proposed in this paper is also adaptable to UIESs under different energy usage scenarios and equipment configurations.

Discussion

This paper studies the reliability evaluation of electric-gas-thermal coupling systems with a large number of UIESs connected to energy distribution networks. A failure mode set of electric-gas-thermal coupling systems is established considering the scenario of single-point fault superposition in multi-energy grids. Various energy subsystems can perform multi-energy mutual cooperation through IESs after a fault in coupling systems, and multiple IESs can form a joint island to support multi-energy loads on the island.

Based on these two types of support characteristics, post-failure operation modes of IESs suitable for electric-gas-thermal coupling systems with multiple IESs connected are proposed. Moreover, in order to describe the impact of faults on the load of the coupling system and the connectivity of the IES energy supply path, a correlation matrix for the electric-gas-thermal coupling system including the load correlation matrix and the IES correlation matrix is established. Criteria are then proposed to quickly and preliminarily determine the energy supply state of the loads and the operation mode of the IESs after a fault.

The case study shows that both the multi-energy coupling operation and the joint island operation of the micro-energy grids can effectively improve the overall energy supply reliability. The reliability indices are accurately calculated using the proposed method and provide a reference for the decision-making of the operation mode of the micro-energy grids under different fault states.

Limitations of the study

Incorporating economic factors into the load shedding strategy during micro-energy grid island operation would enhance its practical applicability. Further research is needed to quantify the multi-agent interests, including the distribution network, gas network, thermal network, IES operators, and users, and to achieve a balance between reliability and economy.

Additionally, users can actively participate in demand response by adjusting their load demands, thereby improving overall system energy supply reliability. Further research is required to define and classify load resources that users can contribute to demand response, as well as to develop incentive strategies that optimize user participation in the demand response process.

Method details

Problem description

The UIES is composed of an intelligent power distribution system, centralized heat system, etc. Some typical UIES structures in commercial areas, industrial areas, and residential areas. Multiple energy networks are deeply coupled, and they can be connected to the public energy network or operate independently. The UIES is one of the important development trends of the integrated energy system. During normal operation, the UIES is usually connected to the energy distribution network such as the power distribution network and the gas network. If the system fails, it can operate in an isolated operation mode.

Considering the complex failure modes of the electric-gas-thermal coupling system, as well as the fault support characteristics of mutual cooperation among electric, gas, and thermal energy subsystems and energy supply of multi-IES, an operation mode of IES is proposed for multi-energy networks under single-point fault superposition.

The matrix method is applied to describe the impact of faults on the load in the electric-gas-thermal coupling system and the connectivity of the IES energy supply path, and the criteria are established to determine the load energy supply state and the IES operation mode. The fast preliminary judgment of load energy supply states and IES operation modes is therefore realized, and the speed of system reliability evaluation is improved.

Failure mode set and IES operation modes

Failure mode set of multi-energy coupling systems

According to the fault components and the fault handling methods, the failure modes of the electric-gas-thermal coupling system with UIES connected are categorized as UIES component failures and energy distribution network component failures. The load-side faults are not considered in this paper as they lead to direct load shedding, do not involve scenarios related to multi-energy cooperation and joint island operation. The two types of faults are independent of each other, and are handled in different ways.

Failure of the UIES components

The UIES component failure includes distributed generation device failure, energy coupling device failure, and energy storage device failure. Since the internal energy supply line of the UIES is relatively short and its failure rate is low, its failure is not considered in this paper. The scope of influence of UIES component failure is limited to the interior of the micro-energy grid, and usually does not spread to the upper-level energy supply network. The fault handling method is to disconnect the faulty component from the system.

Failure of energy distribution network components

Due to the characteristics of coupling and mutual cooperation among energy subsystems, the scenario of simultaneous failure of different energy distribution networks needs to be considered. In addition, multi-point failures in a single energy subsystem can be transformed into single-point failures for fault handling, so multi-point failures of a single energy subsystem are not considered. The failures of energy distribution network components are further divided into two types: single-point failures in a single network and single-point failures in multiple energy networks.

Single point failure in a single network is that a failure only occurs in one energy distribution network and there is only one faulty component. For example, failure of a distribution line occurs in the electric distribution network.

Single-point failures in multiple energy networks are when failures occur in two or more energy distribution networks, and only one fault occurs in each network. For example, a line failure occurs in the electric distribution network, and a line failure occurs in the heat network simultaneously.

Operation modes of the UIES

The multi-energy coupling system island takes the UIES as the energy supply core, and the operation mode of a micro-energy grid is determined according to its energy interaction state with the energy distribution network and other UIESs.

When the UIES is normally connected to the energy distribution network and can obtain all types of energy supply from the distribution network, it is in the grid-connected operation mode. When the UIES can only obtain some types of energy supply from the distribution network, it is in the incomplete island operation mode. When the UIES cannot obtain the energy supply from the distribution network at all, it is in the complete island operation mode.

When a micro-energy grid can be connected with other UIESs through the energy distribution network, and then jointly supply the island area through joint optimized operation, the micro-energy grid is in the joint island operation mode. When a single micro-energy grid independently supplies the island area, the micro-energy grid is in the independent island operation mode.

The post-fault operation modes of UIESs are designed to address single-point fault superposition scenarios in a multi-energy grid by leveraging the multi-energy cooperation and joint island operation capabilities of UIESs. The proposed operation modes of the micro-energy grid after a fault include grid-connected operation mode, incomplete island independent operation mode, complete island independent operation mode, incomplete island joint operation mode, and complete island joint operation mode. The operation mode of the micro-energy grid and the area of the island after a fault need to be determined through fault impact analysis and the division of the island.

Correlation matrix and criteria

Correlation matrix

The consequences of UIES component failures are reflected in the micro-energy grid optimization operation and load reduction process by adding component failure constraints. The failure of energy distribution network components affects the connectivity of the energy supply path, and will affect the operation mode of the micro-energy grid and the energy supply for a large number of loads. It is necessary to analyse the consequences of energy distribution network component failures in detail from the perspective of topological connectivity.

The connection relationship between the load and the upper-level energy station and the UIES affects the energy supply status of the load, and the connection relationship between the UIES and the energy distribution network and other UIESs affects its operation mode. The load correlation matrix A and the micro-energy grid correlation matrix B are defined to describe the connection status of the load and the micro-energy grid under each fault mode, so as to analyse the fault impact.

Load correlation matrix A

The loads connected to the upper-level energy station are normally supplied by the upper-level energy station, and the loads connected to the UIES can be supplied by the micro-energy grid. The connection relationship between the load and the upper-level energy station and the UIES determines the energy supply status of the load. In order to describe the connection relationship and then determine the energy supply status of the load, a load correlation matrix A is established.

Assuming that there are I electric loads, J gas loads, K heat loads, and M UIESs in an electric-gas-thermal coupling system, the dimension of its load correlation matrix A1 under a certain fault mode is (I+J+K)×(M+1).

From the first row to the I-th row of the matrix, each row vector describes the connection relationship between each electric load and the UIES, and the upper-level substation. If the i-th electric load is connected to the m-th UIES, the matrix element A1(i, m)=1; if the i-th electric load is not connected to the m-th UIES, then A1(i, m)=0; if the i-th electric load is connected t A1(i, m+1)=0.

Similarly, from the (I+1)-th row to the (I+J)-th row, each row vector describes the connection relationship between each gas load and the UIES and the upper-level gas gate station. Each row vector from the (I+J+1)-th row to the (I+J+K)-th row describes the connection relationship between each heat load and the UIES and the upper-level heat station. Each column vector of the matrix describes the connection relationship between each UIES and load.

Micro-energy grid correlation matrix B

The micro-energy grid connected to all upper-level energy stations is in grid-connected operation mode, and that connected to some upper-level energy stations is incomplete island operation mode. The micro-energy grid that is disconnected from all upper-level energy stations is in complete island operation mode. Moreover, micro-energy grids connected to other micro-energy grids can operate in joint island mode. A micro-energy grid correlation matrix B is established in order to describe its connection relationship with the upper-level energy station and other micro-energy grids, and to determine its operation mode.

Assuming that there are M UIESs in an electric-gas-thermal coupling system, the dimension of the micro-energy grid correlation matrix B1 under a certain failure mode is M×(3M).

Each row vector of the matrix describes the connection relationship between each UIES and the upper-level energy station and other UIESs.

From the first column to the M-th column of the matrix, each column vector describes the connection relationship between each micro-energy grid and the upper-level substation and other micro-energy grids through the distribution network. If the m-th micro-energy grid is connected to the upper-level substation, the element B1(m, m)=1; if the m-th micro-energy grid is not connected to the upper-level substation, B1(m, m)=0. If the m1-th micro-energy grid and the m2-th micro-energy grid are connected through the distribution network, the element B1(m1, m2)=1; if the m1-th micro-energy grid and the m2-th micro-energy grid are not connected via the distribution network, B1(m1, m2)=0.

From the (M+1)-th column to the 2M-th column of the matrix, each column vector describes the connection relationship between each micro-energy grid and the upper-level gas station and other micro-energy grids through the gas network. If the m-th micro-energy grid is connected to the upper-level gas station, the matrix element B1(m, M+m)=1; if the m-th micro-energy grid is not connected to the upper-level gas station, B1(m, M+m)=0. If the m1-th micro-energy grid is connected to the m2-th micro-energy grid through the gas network, the matrix element B1(m1, M+ m2)=1; if the m1-th micro-energy grid and the m2-th micro-energy grid cannot be connected through the gas network, B1(m1, M+ m2)=0.

From the (2M+1)-th column to the 3M-th column of the matrix, each column vector describes the connection relationship between each micro-energy grid and the upper-level thermal station and other micro-energy grids through the thermal network. If the m-th micro-energy grid is connected to the upper-level thermal station, the matrix element B1(m, 2M+m)=1; if the m-th micro-energy grid is not connected to the upper-level thermal station, B1(m, 2M+m)=0. If the m1-th micro-energy grid is connected to the m2-th micro-energy grid through the thermal network, the matrix element B1(m1, 2M+ m2)=1; if the m1-th micro-energy grid and the m1-th micro-energy cannot be connected through the thermal network, B1(m1, 2M+ m2)=0.

Criteria for determining the energy supply state of loads

Through the failure mode impact analysis, the energy supply status of each load is determined as one of the following states: normal, outage and supplied by the micro-energy grid island. The amount of load reduction under the normal state and the outage state can be determined directly. The loads that can be supplied by the micro-energy grid island have the energy supply path for being supplied, but whether they are ultimately supplied by the micro-energy grid depends on the energy supply capacity of the grid during the fault repair period. If the energy supply capacity of the micro-energy grid is insufficient, the load cannot be included in the island or is reduced. Therefore, the reduction amount of the load that can be supplied by the micro-energy grid island needs to be determined after island division and dynamic load reduction calculation.

Criteria for determining the operation mode of UIESs

During the fault repair period, the operation modes of the micro-energy grid include grid-connected operation, incomplete island-independent operation, complete island-independent operation, incomplete island joint operation, and complete island joint operation. It can be determined by the fault impact analysis that if a network is in the grid-connected operation mode, incomplete island mode, or complete island mode. Reliability evaluation embedded with static island division and dynamic load shedding models

The static island division of the micro-energy grid determines its operation mode and the initial range of the island at the beginning of the repair period, which is the initial condition for the dynamic load reduction calculation. The dynamic load shedding of micro-energy grid islands minimizes the reduction of important loads during the repair period by optimizing component output and load reduction. The dynamic load-shedding model is universal and applicable to all four operation modes. The methodology focuses on the reliability evaluation of existing electric-gas-thermal coupling systems, with the number of UIESs based on the existing system configuration.

The impact of different micro-energy grid operation modes on load shedding is reflected in the selection of dynamic load shedding model parameters. When the micro-energy grid is in the incomplete island operation mode, the input power of the coupling devices whose energy input is in the considered form is set to 0. When the micro-energy grid is in the complete island operation mode, the support among the sources in the same energy form is given priority to reduce the loss in the energy conversion process, and the input power of all energy coupling devices is set to 0. The number of micro-energy grids in the island, M, is set to 1 for independent operation modes.

The big-M method is applied to linearize the energy storage output constraints, and Matlab and Cplex are used to solve the optimization model. The reduction factor βiθ(t) is obtained for each load i at each time t during the fault repair period, and then the energy not supply duration and the amount of sequential load reduction at each multi-energy load point are obtained.

Reliability evaluation index and evaluation method

The reliability of the electric-gas-thermal coupling system in terms of energy supply shortage is measured by the expectation of energy not supplied of electricity EEENSe, the expectation of energy not supplied of gas EEENSg and the expectation of energy not supplied of heat EEENSh.

Considering the dynamic process of load shedding and the sequencing of energy storage, sequential Monte Carlo simulation is used for the reliability assessment. When the sequential Monte Carlo simulation encounters a system failure, it first quickly determines the energy supply status of the loads and the operation mode of the UIES after the failure based on the fault correlation matrix. During the fault duration, the static island division model and the dynamic load reduction model are then used to calculate the load reduction during the fault, obtaining the reliability indices for each load point and the system as a whole.

Asia Is Not the West: Mean Nocturnal Baseline Impedance Must Be Interpreted in a Device- and Region-specific Context
Authors: Tae Hee Lee
Published: 2026

TO THE EDITOR: Lee et al1 report that, in Korean patients off proton pump inhibitors (PPIs), a mean nocturnal baseline impedance (MNBI) threshold of 2167 Ω best predicted pathological acid exposure time (AET ≥ 4%) and that MNBI decreased with increasing esophagitis severity.1 Their findings add to a growing body of evidence that Asian cohorts often differ from Western cohorts in both AET and MNBI, challenging a one-size-fits-all application of the Lyon 2.0 supportive bands.2,3

Two practice points can improve the interpretability and transportability of MNBI. First, the measurement system matters. In the consensus analysis by Sifrim et al,3 the 5th percentile MNBI values differed between Diversatek and Laborie systems even in healthy subjects: 1384 and 1395 Ω at 3 cm and 5 cm above the lower esophageal sphincter for Diversatek, and 1542 Ω and 1794 Ω for Laborie at the same levels.3 Thus, identical physiology may yield different absolute values depending on the catheter and software. Methodology (manual three 10-minute nocturnal windows as originally described by Martinucci et al4 versus simplified recumbent-period extraction), posture, and PPI washout further modify MNBI.3 Consistent with these system effects, the United Kingdom study by Hoshikawa et al5 using Laborie reported GERD thresholds of 1785 Ω at 3 cm and 1943-2034 Ω at 5 cm above the lower esophageal sphincter, depending on whether conventional or simplified MNBI was used, values that are lower than the classic Western figure near 2300 Ω.3,5 Representative device- and region-specific MNBI thresholds are summarized in Table.

Second, Asian data are heterogeneous across countries. In Vietnam (Laborie, conventional MNBI, off PPIs), MNBI showed strong diagnostic performance for abnormal AET greater than 6%, with a receiver operating characteristic–derived cutoff of 1243 Ω (sensitivity 0.79, specificity 0.93), and the Lyon supportive bands (< 1500 Ω; > 2500 Ω) performed well in this setting.6 By contrast, Chinese cohorts demonstrate that high MNBI does not invariably exclude GERD. In a Laborie cohort of 434 patients with typical reflux symptoms (103 with conclusive GERD defined by AET > 6% or Los Angeles grade C-D), thresholds of 2061 Ω and 2432 Ω differentiated GERD from non-GERD and supported exclusion, respectively.7 In a separate VersaFlex cohort of 259 patients, approximately one-third of those with AET ≥ 4% still had MNBI above 2500 Ω.8 These findings highlight that device- and country-specific cutoffs are required for Asian practice rather than simple adoption of Western norms.

Lee et al1 anchored GERD to AET ≥ 4% or LA grade B–D, in line with the 2020 Seoul consensus for Asian populations, and then identified an empirical MNBI threshold of 2167 Ω (sensitivity 0.86, specificity 0.77).1,9 The clinical message is to treat MNBI as supportive evidence within the Lyon 2.0 framework rather than a stand-alone discriminator.2 We therefore recommend that reports and laboratories specify device or brand, sensor position, extraction method (three 10-minute nocturnal windows versus simplified analysis), body position and sleep quality, and PPI washout, and that they derive local, device-specific receiver operating characteristic cutoffs. MNBI should be co-interpreted with AET, post-reflux swallow-induced peristaltic wave index, total reflux episodes, Los Angeles grade, and esophagogastric junction metrics.2,3

Histopathological and Immunohistochemical Study of Hypopigmented Patches in Trichrome Non-Segmental Vitiligo
Authors: Mona Ibrahim Abdel Hafiz, Ghada Abdel Badea Omar, Nafissa Mohamed El-Badawy, Radwa Osama Mohamed Kamel
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionVitiligo is a multifactorial autoimmune disease. Introduction

Vitiligo is an autoimmune disorder affecting 0.5–2% of the world’s population, characterized by the loss of melanocytes from the epidermis and resulting in white patches of skin. These macules typically exhibit a symmetrical distribution and are more prominent in individuals with darker skin tones. Vitiligo significantly impacts quality of life and self-esteem [1]. The pathophysiology of vitiligo is complex and multifactorial, with proposed mechanisms including autoimmune, cytotoxic, biochemical, oxidant-antioxidant imbalances, viral, and neurological factors, ultimately leading to melanocyte loss [2].

Trichrome vitiligo is a variant characterized by three distinct pigmentary zones: an achromic center, an intermediate zone of hypochromia, and surrounding unaffected skin [3]. This coloration may reflect alterations in melanin granule distribution rather than melanocyte number (Figure 1). Adjacent to the trichrome area, apparently normal skin exhibits increased pigmentation and, histologically, a greater concentration of melanin granules [4].

Both humoral and cell-mediated immune responses contribute to melanocyte destruction, implicating both innate and adaptive immunity in the etiology of vitiligo [5]. Cytotoxic CD8+ T cells, targeting melanocytes, infiltrate perilesional skin, preferentially localizing to the epidermis and dermis. Interferon-γ plays a critical role in vitiligo pathogenesis, facilitating CD8+ T cell recruitment via a positive feedback loop [6,7]. CD4+ T lymphocytes are crucial to the activation, activity, and survival of self-reactive CD8+ T lymphocytes; however, isolated CD4+ T lymphocytes have been shown to be insufficient to induce melanocyte death in autologous skin explants [8].

Objectives

The primary objective of this study was to characterize the distinct histopathological and immunohistochemical features of non-segmental trichrome vitiligo, with a specific focus on CD4+ and CD8+ T lymphocytes within the different pigmentary zones of a single lesion. This research aimed to enhance our understanding of the pathogenesis and disease activity in this specific vitiligo subtype, providing a more detailed insight into the dynamic process of melanocyte destruction and immune cell infiltration.

Methods

This observational, cross-sectional study enrolled thirty patients with trichrome non-segmental vitiligo recruited from the outpatient dermatology clinic of Al-Zahraa University Hospital, Cairo, Egypt. Diagnosis was confirmed by clinical presentation and Wood’s lamp examination. The study was conducted over a two-year period, from September 2021 to September 2023, following approval by the ethics committee of research of the Faculty of Medicine for Girls at Al-Azhar University (Approval number: 202104824). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to enrolment.

Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

Patients of both sexes, aged 18 to 65 years, with active or stable trichrome non-segmental vitiligo were included. Exclusion criteria included pregnancy or breastfeeding, a history of malignancy or radiation therapy, immunosuppression, long-term systemic corticosteroid therapy, other autoimmune diseases, or prior vitiligo treatment (topical, systemic, or phototherapy) within the preceding three months.

Clinical Assessment

Comprehensive patient history was obtained, including age, disease onset, course, duration, past medical history, and associated skin or systemic diseases. Vitiligo Disease Activity (VIDA) score [9] was used to assess disease activity during dermatological examination, categorizing patients into stable and active groups. VIDA scoring is as follows: +4 (active ≤6 weeks), +3 (active 6 weeks – 3 months), +2 (active 3–6 months), +1 (active 6–12 months), 0 (stable ≥1 year), −1 (stable with spontaneous repigmentation ≥1 year). Lower scores indicate reduced activity.

Tissue Biopsy and Processing

Under local anesthesia, four-millimeter punch biopsies were obtained from affected vitiliginous skin, trichrome areas, and adjacent apparently normal skin from each patient. Tissue samples were formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded, and sectioned for histopathological and immunohistochemical analysis.

Histopathology and Immunohistochemistry

Tissue sections underwent immunohistochemical staining to assess the expression of CD4+ and CD8+ T lymphocytes. Following antigen retrieval using a microwave-based citrate buffer, sections were incubated with primary mouse monoclonal antibodies against CD4 (ready-to-use, Clone 4B12, DAKO, code No IR649) and CD8 (ready-to-use, Clone 4B11, DAKO, code No IR646). Detection was performed using a biotinylated secondary antibody, streptavidin-peroxidase complex, and diaminobenzidine (DAB) as the chromogen. Sections were counterstained with hematoxylin and evaluated by light microscopy.

Immunohistochemical Scoring

The number of CD4+ and CD8+ T lymphocytes was assessed semi-quantitatively by an expert consultant pathologist. Five high-power fields (HPF) (400× magnification) representing hot spots were examined per section. For comparative analysis, cases were categorized according to the number of cells/HPF into two groups: Low (<40 cells/HPF) and High (≥40 cells/HPF).

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using the Statistical Package for Social Science (IBM SPSS), version 27. Quantitative data are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) for normally distributed data, and as median and interquartile range (IQR) for non-parametric data. Qualitative variables were summarized using frequencies and percentages. The chi-square test was used to compare qualitative data between groups. Fisher’s exact test was used as an alternative when the expected count in any cell was less than 5. For comparisons of non-parametric quantitative data between two independent groups, the Mann-Whitney U test was applied. The Friedman test was used for comparisons across more than two related groups with non-parametric data. Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient was used to assess the strength and direction of monotonic relationships between variables. Logistic regression analysis was performed to assess factors associated with activity of disease. All statistical tests were two-tailed, with a 95% confidence interval (CI) and a 5% level of significance. A p-value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Patient Data

The study cohort comprised 30 participants (22 females, 73.3%; eight males, 26.7%). Their ages ranged from 18 to 51 years (mean 33 ± 9.08). According to disease activity, 18 patients (60.0%) were categorized as having active vitiligo, while 12 patients (40.0%) presented with stable disease. Disease duration ranged from six months to 25 years, with a median (Q1, Q3) of 4.50 (3 – 10) years.

Immunohistochemical Findings

The study revealed a statistically significant difference in CD4+ T cell count across vitiliginous, trichrome, and apparently normal skin areas (P<0.001), with significantly higher values in the vitiliginous area than the apparently normal skin, and both elicited significantly higher values than the trichrome area. CD8+ T cell count was significantly different across the three skin areas (P<0.001), being significantly lower in the vitiliginous area than in the trichrome and significantly higher in both than in the apparently normal skin area. As for CD4:CD8 ratio, it also showed a statistically significant difference across skin areas (P<0.001), being significantly higher in both vitiliginous and apparently normal skin areas as compared to the trichrome area (Table 1, Figures 2, 4, 5).

Histopathological Findings

Histopathological examination of biopsies from the three areas revealed a statistically significant thinning in epidermal thickness in the vitiliginous area compared to both the trichrome and apparently normal skin areas (P<0.001). Degenerative changes at the dermoepidermal junction (DEJ), characterized by vacuolization, were significantly more pronounced in the vitiliginous and trichrome areas compared to the apparently normal skin areas (P<0.001). Dermal lymphocytic infiltration was also significantly higher in the vitiliginous and trichrome areas compared to the apparently normal skin area (P<0.001) (Figures 7, 8).

Discussion

Trichrome vitiligo lesions exhibit an intermediate zone of hypochromia situated between the achromic centre and the surrounding unaffected skin [3]. Numerous studies on vitiligo suggest that autoreactive, melanocyte-specific, cytotoxic T cells contribute to the gradual depigmentation of human skin [10]. This study emphasises the immunological role in the destructive mechanism impacting melanocytes in trichrome vitiligo.

This study compared the histopathological and immunohistochemical changes of hypopigmented patches in trichrome non-segmental vitiligo to the same structure in the apparently normal skin around the patches to understand the pathogenesis of the disease.

Our study found a statistically significant increase in the number of CD4+T lymphocytes in vitiligo and apparently normal skin areas compared to the trichrome area. The number of CD8+ T lymphocytes was significantly higher in the trichrome area compared to vitiligo and apparently normal skin areas. In addition, the median CD8+ T cell count was higher in the dermis/DEJ than in the epidermal layer in all areas (vitiligo, trichrome, and apparently normal skin). Increased levels of CD8+ T lymphocytes in the trichrome area suggest a link between CD8+ T cells and vitiligo depigmentation.

The current study is consistent with the findings of Wijngaard et al. [11] and Wankowicz-Kalinska et al. [12], who found that the highest numbers of CD8+ T cells were present in the perilesional area (the area adjacent to the vitiligo area), in the dermal compartment, and in the epidermis/papillary dermis of perilesional skin in apposition to residual melanocytes. Additionally, Wańkowicz-Kalińska et al. [12] reported that T cells present in the normal skin of healthy donors are typically characterised by an equal distribution of CD4 and CD8 subsets.

Our findings were also in line with those of Abdallah et al. [13], who found that CD4+ T lymphocytes were significantly higher in lesional vitiligo skin than in perilesional skin, and CD8+ T lymphocytes were significantly higher in marginal skin than in lesional skin.

Furthermore, these findings were in agreement with those reported by Benzekri and Gauthier [6], who found clusters of CD8+ T lymphocytes in the epidermis and dermis of the hypopigmented trichrome area. Additionally, they proposed that the clinical appearance of the perilesional skin of vitiligo lesions is indicative of the underlying depigmenting process.

However, a study conducted by Aslanian et al. [14] demonstrated that the CD4+ T lymphocyte count did not exhibit a significant difference in the epidermis or dermis between the active border of depigmented lesions and the adjacent apparently normal skin.

In the current study, there was a significant positive correlation between CD8+ T cells in the trichrome area and the disease activity determined by the VIDA score. By determining and evaluating the relationship between CD4+ and CD8+ T lymphocytes in vitiligo patients and the activity of the disease, this will help to understand the pathophysiology of vitiligo and assist in its therapy by investigating the association between the activity of the illness and the levels of various T-cell subsets.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to evaluate the correlation between CD4+ and CD8+ T lymphocytes in trichrome vitiligo skin tissue biopsies and the clinical activity of the disease by VIDA score. A study done by Hegab and Attia [15] reported a negative correlation between peripheral CD4+ T cells by flow cytometry and vitiligo disease activity by VIDA score. Also, there was no significant correlation between CD4:CD8 ratio in the serum of vitiligo patients and VIDA score in the study performed by Eltayesh et al. [16].

Furthermore, in this study, CD4+ and CD8+ T-cells were predominantly elevated in active vitiligo compared to stable vitiligo, resulting in a significant decrease in the CD4+:CD8+ ratio in active cases compared to stable cases. Our findings demonstrated the immunological function of T cells in the destructive mechanism affecting melanocytes in vitiligo.

In agreement with Pichler et al. [17], the stable group showed a significantly higher median CD4:CD8 ratio than the active group. Similarly, Abdallah et al. [13] reported that active vitiligo showed higher levels of CD4+ and CD8+ T lymphocytes than stable vitiligo. Both active and stable vitiligo had a significant increase in CD4+ T lymphocytes in lesional skin compared to marginal skin and a significant increase in CD8+ T lymphocytes in perilesional skin compared to lesional skin.

According to the current data, CD8+ T lymphocyte population is high in the trichrome area in active vitiligo cases compared to stable cases, giving a high VIDA score. This means that the VIDA score in our cases can correlate with histopathological changes and the clinical picture of active cases of vitiligo, particularly in the presence of trichrome sign.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first time that we have considered the significance of the VIDA score in immunohistopathological investigations of vitiligo associated with trichrome sign, as there is no reference in the literature regarding this correlation.

In the current study, there was a statistically significant increase in the epidermal thinning in vitiligo area (60.0%) compared to the trichrome area (33.3%) as well as in apparently normal skin area (6.7%). Widespread vacuolar degeneration in the basal and suprabasal layers may be the cause of the observed thinning, which would result in the loss of normal epidermal architecture. Also, dermal lymphocytic infiltration was significantly higher in vitiligo (96.7%) and in trichrome areas (100.0%) than in apparently normal skin areas (20.0%).

Our findings were in agreement with those reported by Khan et al. [18], who found thinner epidermis in 53% of vitiligo specimens. Furthermore, these findings were consistent with Bakry et al. [19], who observed epidermal thinning in 77.8% of vitiligo skin biopsies and perivascular lymphocytic infiltration in the dermis of vitiliginous and perilesional skin biopsies in all cases studied (100.0%). Also, Abdou et al. [20] reported the presence of perivascular lymphocytic infiltrate in vitiliginous skin in 73.3% of their cases. Kim et al. [21] and Gamil et al. [22] found perivascular lymphocytic infiltration in only 41% and 50%, respectively, of vitiliginous skin biopsies.

From our data, the histopathological and immunohistopathological changes occurring in the skin in cases of trichrome vitiligo are significant in elucidating the clinical presentation of the disease. The trichrome sign of vitiligo represents a transitional phase in the activity of the disease. Although this sign is classified as a special type of vitiligo and is considered a sign of activity from a clinical perspective, still, the literature has limited and unclear references on this sign.

The limitations of this study are its cross-sectional design, insufficient data, and scientific publications related to trichrome vitiligo as well as the small sample size, which is attributed to the relatively low incidence of trichrome vitiligo in our population. Additionally, there could also be a chance of recall bias in the history given by patients regarding the duration of activity, which may have resulted in the misclassification of active versus stable vitiligo.

Our reliance on immunohistochemistry for the characterization of CD4 + and CD8+ populations provide a semi-quantitative rather than a strictly fluid-quantitative assessment. Although flow cytometry provides a more accurate numerical count of CD4 + and CD8 + populations, it necessitates tissue dissociation, which makes spatial distribution analysis impossible. Given that our research objective focused on the spatial orientation of the infiltrate specifically at the dermoepidermal junction, the preservation of tissue architecture via formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) samples was prioritized. We sought to mitigate the inherent subjectivity of IHC scoring by utilizing a standardized protocol under the supervision of a consultant dermatopathologist. In conclusion, while we acknowledge the strengths of alternative technologies, we remain confident that our immunohistochemical approach provides the most clinically relevant spatial data for this specific study. Future studies should seek to overcome these limitations by employing a larger sample size, utilizing a longitudinal study design.

Conclusion

CD8+ T-lymphocytes in the trichrome area appear to play a dominant role in vitiligo disease activity, with CD4+ T lymphocytes exhibiting secondary significance. Histopathological findings support an active cellular immune response within both the trichrome and apparently normal skin of vitiligo patients. These findings suggest a cellular event occurs within the epidermis prior to visible depigmentation, potentially indicating immune compromise even in clinically pigmented skin. The trichrome area represents a crucial therapeutic target; preserving melanocytes within this region may improve treatment outcomes.

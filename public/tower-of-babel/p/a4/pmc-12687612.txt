Thoughts on the prerequisite courses for microbiology
Authors: Yijun Kang, Min Shen, Yanzhou Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThis paper conducts an in-depth investigation and analysis of 25 microbiology course outlines from 23 domestic universities in China, focusing on the structure of prerequisite courses. The study finds that microbiology course outlines typically include basic course information, objectives, content, teaching methods, resources, assessment, and scheduling. Reasonable prerequisite course settings are vital for clarifying logical relationships among courses in talent training programs, organizing key and challenging knowledge systems, and enhancing university course quality. The paper proposes recommendations for microbiology prerequisite course design, including identifying core prerequisites, ensuring logical sequence rationality, setting appropriate numbers of prerequisites, and considering professional specificities. These suggestions offer a reference for optimizing microbiology course outline design and improving biological talent cultivation quality. The paper also explores the selection frequency and number of prerequisite courses. The top three selected courses are biochemistry, general biology, and genetics, while the least selected are biostatistics, physiology, and ecology. It is recommended that universities carefully consider course logical sequences and students’ actual learning needs when setting microbiology prerequisite courses to ensure students build a solid knowledge foundation and smoothly transition to microbiology learning.

PERSPECTIVE

The curriculum outline is a guiding document for course instruction, clearly outlining course objectives, content, teaching methods, assessment approaches, and other requirements. It provides a clear direction for both teaching and learning (1). Typically, a curriculum outline includes sections on basic course information, objectives, content, teaching methods, resources, assessment, and scheduling. “Basic course information” encompasses course name, code, credits, hours, target audience, prerequisite courses, and subsequent courses. Reasonable prerequisite and subsequent course settings are crucial for clarifying logical relationships among courses in talent training programs, organizing key and challenging knowledge systems, and enhancing university education quality (2).

Microbiology is a highly scientific discipline with an extensive knowledge system spanning biochemistry, genetics, ecology, immunology, and other fields (3) (Fig. 1). According to the American Society for Microbiology’s microbiology course teaching guidelines, microbiology instruction should cover six primary concepts: evolution, cell structure and function, metabolic pathways, information flow and genetics, microbial systems, and the impact of microorganisms. Students should also master scientific thinking methods and basic microbiological experimental skills (4). Therefore, instructors must ensure students possess the necessary foundational knowledge before microbiology instruction begins. Furthermore, microbiology serves as a tool science, playing a vital role in learning other professional courses like medicine, environmental science, genetics, and food science. In practice, instructors often encounter difficulties due to students’ lack of understanding in certain knowledge areas, hindering teaching progress. This necessitates microbiology teaching practitioners to re-examine and rationally plan microbiology prerequisite courses to ensure students are adequately prepared for smooth progression into in-depth microbiology learning.

Knowledge system of microbiology course.

Against this backdrop, this paper first conducts an in-depth survey and analysis of 25 microbiology course outlines from 23 universities, focusing on prerequisite course settings to understand the preferences, strengths, and weaknesses of prerequisite course design across universities. Subsequently, based on the microbiology course’s knowledge system and structure, it provides a detailed analysis of required prerequisite courses. Considering microbiology learning patterns and common practices in domestic universities, this paper rationally designs microbiology prerequisite courses to construct a more scientific and systematic curriculum system. The research findings offer significant reference value and practical implications for optimizing microbiology curriculum outline design and enhancing biological talent cultivation quality.

Analysis results based on 25 microbiology courses

Microbiology is a foundational course for life science majors, spanning disciplines like science, engineering, agriculture, medicine, and teacher education (4). This study collected and analyzed publicly available microbiology course outlines from a diverse range of institutions, including top-tier comprehensive universities (specifically, 985 and 211 Project universities in China) and local undergraduate institutions.

We applied the following inclusion criteria: (i) the course must be mandatory for biological science-related majors; (ii) the syllabus must be publicly accessible with detailed content descriptions; and (iii) the course must be undergraduate level. We excluded abbreviated syllabi, courses intended for non-majors, and outlines lacking a detailed topic breakdown. We manually reviewed and categorized each syllabus using content analysis based on a predefined coding framework that identified key topics, learning outcomes, and structural elements. This qualitative approach enabled thematic comparisons across programs and institution types.

The final sample consisted of 25 required core courses aimed at biological majors. These represented five major categories: biological science (including biological education, 10 courses), biotechnology (seven courses), bioengineering (three courses), agriculture (including aquaculture and food science, five courses), and pharmacy (one course). All courses corresponded to “General Microbiology”—typically a second or third-year subject designed for majors—rather than introductory or non-majors courses.

Analysis of prerequisite course selection frequency (Fig. 2) reveals that biochemistry (21 courses, 33.33%), general biology (nine courses, 14.29%), and genetics (nine courses, 14.29%) are the most frequently chosen. Biostatistics (one course, 1.59%), physiology (one course, 1.59%), and ecology (two courses, 3.17%) are the least chosen. This indicates that educators generally consider biochemistry, general biology, and genetics as microbiology prerequisites.

Frequency of prerequisite courses for microbiology.

The number of prerequisite courses ranges from 1 to 4 (Fig. 3A). Specifically, 10 courses set two prerequisites, the most frequent number, followed by six, five, and four courses, setting four, three, and one prerequisite, respectively. As microbiology is typically a mandatory foundational course for biological majors, often scheduled in the second to fourth semesters, prerequisite course selection is relatively limited. This leads some universities to prioritize chemistry-related courses as prerequisites.

Notably, although biochemistry and genetics are widely regarded as ideal microbiology prerequisites, the survey finds that in many universities, these courses are scheduled in the same semester as microbiology. This weakens logical coherence between prerequisites and subsequent courses and causes practical inconveniences, potentially leaving students without the necessary biochemistry and genetics foundations for microbiology learning. Therefore, universities must be more cautious in considering course logical sequences and students’ actual learning needs when setting microbiology prerequisites to ensure students build a solid knowledge foundation and smoothly transition to microbiology.

Prerequisite course settings also vary significantly across majors (Fig. 3B). Biological science majors have the most complex prerequisite course selections, with 11 different courses appearing. Biochemistry and genetics have the highest selection rates, likely because biochemistry helps students understand microbial structure, biochemical processes, and metabolic pathways, while genetics is crucial for microbial genetics, variation, and diversity mechanisms. Biological science majors typically require genetics courses, while other majors like bioengineering may use molecular genetics or alternatives. Bioengineering majors have simpler prerequisites: biochemistry, general biology, and organic chemistry. Agricultural and pharmaceutical major microbiology courses each have four prerequisites. Agricultural majors prioritize biochemistry > general biology = organic chemistry > genetics, with genetics, important for agricultural breeding, often a mandatory prerequisite. Pharmaceutical majors lean towards biochemistry and organic chemistry, aligning with their major characteristics. Biotechnology majors also have complex prerequisites, with up to 10 courses, but their distribution is similar to biological science majors (Fig. 3B). Biological science focuses on exploring natural laws and life mysteries through experimental research on life phenomena, while biotechnology combines modern life science theories with multidisciplinary principles to pre-design, transform, and utilize organisms, involving genetic engineering and protein engineering. Both belong to biology, with biological science at the upper life science industry chain and biotechnology in the middle, with bioengineering downstream. Both emphasize theory and practice, but biological science leans more towards theoretical research, while biotechnology leans more towards applied basic research and technology development, explaining their similar prerequisite course settings. Based on the analysis, the following conclusions are drawn.

(i) Prerequisite course selection and frequency: Biochemistry, general biology, and genetics are the most commonly selected microbiology prerequisites. Biostatistics, physiology, and ecology are less frequently selected.

(ii) Variation in prerequisite course numbers: Microbiology prerequisites range from one to four courses, with 10 courses setting two prerequisites, reflecting curriculum flexibility and diversity across universities.

(iii) Course logical sequence issues: Despite biochemistry and genetics being ideal prerequisites, they are often scheduled concurrently with microbiology in many universities, weakening logical coherence and causing learning inconveniences.

(iv) Major-specific prerequisite differences: Prerequisite course selection varies across biological science, biotechnology, bioengineering, agricultural, and pharmaceutical majors, reflecting major-specific teaching focuses and curriculum designs.

Recommendations for establishing microbiology prerequisite courses

Identifying core prerequisite courses

Biochemistry is essential for understanding microbial structures, biochemical mechanisms, and metabolic pathways and should be a core prerequisite. While general biology covers a broad range of topics—including animals, plants, microorganisms, and ecology—its content lacks the depth needed to adequately support microbiology learning. Therefore, it is not suitable as a stand-alone prerequisite. Its overall effectiveness as a foundational course for biological majors also warrants further evaluation.

International guidelines, such as those from the ASM Curriculum Guidelines for Undergraduate Microbiology (5 March 2024) or related publications (5), do not specify prerequisites for individual microbiology courses. Harvard Medical School’s medical microbiology program emphasizes basic molecular and cellular biology. While international standards often outline prerequisites for a full microbiology major, such majors have become rare in China over the past 20 years.

Ensuring logical course sequence

Academic program directors should schedule prerequisite courses before microbiology to maintain curricular coherence and strengthen students’ foundational knowledge. When reviewing training programs, departments must avoid circular dependencies and ensure a logical and efficient learning sequence.

Determining the number of prerequisite courses

Given the foundational nature of microbiology and its placement early in the curriculum, limiting prerequisites to one to two core courses (e.g., biochemistry and genetics) ensures necessary background knowledge without overburdening students.

Implementing and evaluating prerequisite structures

To support practical implementation, we propose the following model for integrating prerequisite courses.

Curriculum mapping

Establish a cross-departmental process to align syllabi between prerequisite and microbiology courses. This will help eliminate content gaps and reduce unnecessary overlaps.

Content integration

Integrate key topics from prerequisite courses (such as metabolic pathways from biochemistry) directly into microbiology lesson plans. This will reinforce conceptual connections and improve knowledge retention.

Continuous improvement

Introduce a feedback loop that includes regular student surveys and learning outcome assessments. The data collected will allow for ongoing refinement of prerequisite course design, ensuring it remains responsive to both disciplinary advances and student needs.

In summary, designing effective prerequisite structures for microbiology involves defining core courses, organizing them in a logical sequence, keeping their number manageable, and tailoring them to major-specific goals. It is also essential to integrate content across related courses and establish regular evaluation cycles. Adopting international best practices—especially those from leading global institutions—can help align Chinese universities with world-class standards in microbiology education. A well-planned prerequisite system supports student success and promotes coherence across the curriculum. It is important to note that the proposed framework is based on curriculum analysis and deductive reasoning. While it offers a reasoned approach, its practical effectiveness should be further validated through empirical evidence such as student performance metrics and long-term studies.

Pulsed field ablation (PFA) produces myocardial injury primarily through electroporation.1,2 Although acute voltage reduction after PFA application is commonly interpreted as evidence of lesion formation, the relationship between acute voltage reduction and irreversible tissue injury remains incompletely understood. An acute low-voltage area observed immediately after PFA may include not only irreversibly injured myocardium but also transiently affected tissue related to reversible electroporation or tissue stunning. However, clinical data describing serial voltage mapping during the early phase after PFA remain limited.

The lattice-tip ablation catheter (Sphere-9; Medtronic, Minneapolis, MN), used with the Affera mapping system (Medtronic, Minneapolis, MN), delivers monopolar PF energy.3,4 Because electric-field characteristics may differ among PFA platforms according to catheter geometry, electrode configuration, and energy-delivery mode, serial assessment of acute voltage-map changes with this catheter may help characterize early voltage changes specific to this system.

Case report

An 80-year-old man with long-standing persistent atrial fibrillation underwent first-time catheter ablation using the Affera system with a Sphere-9 catheter. Catheter ablation was performed under general anesthesia using propofol, dexmedetomidine, and a neuromuscular blocking agent. After cardioversion restored sinus rhythm, a voltage map of the left atrium (LA) was created during high right atrial pacing. After baseline mapping, a single PFA application was delivered to the posterior carina region of the LA. This site was selected because it had preserved voltage (≥0.5 mV), sufficient surrounding tissue to evaluate the lesion dimensions, and stable catheter contact. Catheter–tissue contact was confirmed using the Affera impedance-based catheter contact assessment and fluoroscopic images. Intracardiac echocardiography was not used specifically for contact confirmation at this focal site.

Repeated voltage maps were acquired at 1, 3, 5, 10, 15, and 20 minutes after the application using the Sphere-9 catheter and identical mapping settings (Figure 1). Each map was acquired over 1 minute. The low-voltage area was assessed on serial posteroanterior views with slight cranial angulation. The lesion border was defined using the prespecified bipolar voltage threshold of <0.5 mV. The maximum and minimum diameters were measured on the Affera system at each time point (Figure 2). The dimensions of the low-voltage area were measured 5 times by a second independent observer, and the mean values were recorded. Values are presented as mean ± standard deviation. No formal hypothesis testing was performed because the measurements represented repeated assessments of a single lesion rather than independent samples. Formal intra- or interobserver reproducibility testing was not performed.

Time-dependent changes in the dimensions of the low-voltage area after a single PFA application. The maximum and minimum diameters of the low-voltage area were measured at each time point. Values represent the mean ± standard deviation of 5 repeated measurements by a second independent observer. The maximum diameter decreased from 24.1 ± 1.0 mm at 1 minute to 11.1 ± 0.3 mm at 15 minutes and remained stable at 20 minutes. No formal statistical comparison was performed because the measurements represented repeated assessments of a single lesion.

The acute low-voltage area was largest at 1 minute after PFA application and gradually decreased over time. The measured dimensions were 24.1 ± 1.0 × 17.2 ± 0.9 mm at 1 minute, 21.8 ± 0.5 × 17.9 ± 0.3 mm at 3 minutes, 15.2 ± 0.5 × 13.5 ± 0.3 mm at 5 minutes, 14.5 ± 0.3 × 12.4 ± 0.5 mm at 10 minutes, and 11.1 ± 0.3 × 10.2 ± 0.4 mm at 15 minutes, with no further apparent change at 20 minutes (11.1 ± 0.3 × 10.2 ± 0.7 mm). Therefore, in this single lesion, regression continued up to 15 minutes and appeared to stabilize between 15 and 20 minutes.

Discussion

This case illustrates that an acute low-voltage area observed immediately after PFAS application with a lattice-tip ablation catheter may partially regress within minutes. The finding is mechanistically consistent with the possibility that immediate voltage reduction may reflect a mixture of irreversible tissue injury and transient electrophysiological effects, such as reversible electroporation or tissue stunning. Accordingly, early recovery of electrical activity may remain a concern even after apparently successful acute lesion creation. Banai et al. evaluated time-dependent regression of the low-voltage area after PFA application using a pentaspline PFA catheter.5 However, their observations may not be directly applicable to lattice-tip PFA because lesion characteristics may differ according to catheter geometry, electrode configuration, and energy-delivery mode. In the present observation, the low-voltage area gradually regressed for approximately 15 minutes after a single PFA application. This temporal pattern suggests that remapping performed too early in the acute phase may overestimate the extent of persistent voltage-defined lesion formation when using a lattice-tip PFA catheter. A waiting period of approximately 15 minutes after PFA application may help provide a more realistic assessment of lesion persistence in selected settings with the Sphere-9 catheter.

This observation should be interpreted cautiously. It was obtained from a single patient and a single focal lesion at the posterior carina. Lesion evolution may differ according to atrial region, contact quality, local tissue characteristics, and PFA platform. In addition, voltage-defined lesion dimensions are influenced by mapping density, annotation, and the voltage threshold used to define lesion borders. Therefore, this case should be considered hypothesis-generating rather than definitive evidence of lesion reversibility after PFA.

Conclusion

Immediate post-ablation voltage mapping after lattice-tip PFA may overestimate the extent of persistent voltage-defined lesion formation in selected settings. Serial remapping showed partial regression of the acute low-voltage area during the first 15 minutes after a single PFA application. Further systematic studies involving larger numbers of patients, multiple atrial regions, and standardized quantitative mapping methods are warranted to clarify the clinical significance of acute low-voltage area regression after lattice-tip PFA.

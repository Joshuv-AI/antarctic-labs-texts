Reply to letter to the editor
Authors: Micha T Maeder, Laura A Rechsteiner, Philipp K Haager, Hans Rickli
Published: 2025

To the editor,

We appreciate the interest of Dr. Hu and Dr. Zhou in our recently published article on the role of the pulmonary artery pulsatility index (PAPi) in patients with aortic stenosis undergoing valve replacement 1 and the interesting comments in this context. We would like to thank the editors for the opportunity to address the three issues raised by Hu and Zhou.

Potential adverse effect of a high pulmonary artery capacitance (PAC): We re‐analysed the data as suggested by Hu and Zhou and found no evidence for an adverse prognostic effect of PAC > 6.0 mL/mmHg (Figure 1 ). The group of patients with PAC > 6.0 mL/mmHg was relatively small (n = 28) but inspection of Figure 1 shows that mortality was clearly highest in patients with PAC < 3.0 mL/mmHg, while it was comparable for patients with PAC 3.0 to 6.0 mL/mmHg and those with PAC > 6.0 mL/mmHg. Hu and Zhou refer to the work by Zern et al. 2 and Dupont et al. 3 as a basis for their suggestion regarding a potential adverse effect of a high PAC. However, Zern et al. 2 did not report PAC at all (in fact we estimated the values in the discussion of our paper 1 ), and Dupont et al. 3 showed that heart failure patients in the highest PAC quartile had the best outcome.

Potential prognostic effect of PAPi in high risk subgroups: we re‐analysed the data as suggested by Hu and Zhou and found no evidence for an adverse prognostic effect of a low PAPi in patients with stroke volume index (SVI) < 30 mL/m2 (Figure 2 ). In fact, there was a statistically significant difference in mortality between the four PAPi quartiles in patients with SVI < 30 mL/m2 but this was due to a higher mortality in patients in the second PAPi quartile, whereas patients in the first quartile had similar mortality as patients in the third and fourth quartile. We interpret this as a chance finding. When analysed as a continuous variable, there was no association between PAPi and mortality among patients with SVI < 30 mL/m2 [hazard ratio 1.02 (95% confidence interval 0.90–1.14) per dimensionless unit; P = 0.80].

Potential inaccuracy of the assessment of cardiac output with the use of the indirect Fick calculation: we fully agree with the authors, and this limitation is explicitly discussed in the limitation section of our paper 1 with the use of the same reference 4 as provided by Hu and Zhou. We acknowledge that this may have had a certain impact on the calculation of SVI and PAC. A quantification of the potential error in our population is not possible because no simultaneous direct Fick was performed. However, the advantage of the PAPi lies in the fact that only effectively measured pressures are needed for its calculation (i.e., right atrial pressure and pulmonary artery pressure), and thus, PAPi values are not affected by the method of cardiac output assessment. In this context, Hu and Zhou also refer to the effect of the pulmonary artery wedge pressure on the pulmonary artery time constant (i.e., the product of PAC and pulmonary vascular resistance), 5 an aspect we also discussed in our paper. 1 We therefore presented data showing that there was no survival difference between patients in different quartiles for the mean pulmonary artery wedge pressure (Figure 4 in our article). 1

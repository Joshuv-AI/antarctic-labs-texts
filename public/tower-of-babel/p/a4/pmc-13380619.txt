Genomic Analysis of Hypervirulent Klebsiella pneumoniae ST1-KL122
Authors: Yu Yang, Zhien He, Yuanyuan Dai, Huaiwei Lu, Yujie Li, Yuchen Zhang, Baolin Sun, Shusheng Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground: Hypervirulent Klebsiella pneumoniae (hvKp) is often characterized by hypervirulence and hypermucoviscosity, traits classically associated with the virulence regulators rmpA and rmpA2. However, some clinically virulent isolates lack these canonical regulators, suggesting the existence of non-canonical pathways driving hypervirulence.Objective: This study aimed to investigate the genetic basis of hypervirulence and hypermucoviscosity in clinical K. pneumoniae isolates that lack the rmpA and rmpA2.Materials and Methods: A total of 20 clinical isolates were collected. Virulence was assessed using the Galleria mellonella infection model. The hypermucoviscous phenotype was determined using the string test and mucoviscosity assay. Whole-genome sequencing (WGS) and comparative genomic analysis were performed to identify genetic variations, with a particular focus on an ST1-KL122 isolate (1744) that exhibited hypervirulence and hypermucoviscosity despite the absence of rmpA and rmpA2.Results: All 20 isolates induced at least 25% mortality in G. mellonella. Most isolates displayed a hypermucoviscous phenotype, except for isolates 2355, 2324, and 1951. WGS confirmed the absence of rmpA and rmpA2 in isolates 2355, 2324, 1951, and the hypermucoviscous isolate 1744. In the hypervirulent and hypermucoviscous ST1-KL122 isolate 1744, comparative genomic analysis identified two nonsynonymous mutations in the wzc gene, which encodes a tyrosine kinase involved in capsular polysaccharide (CPS) export: c.1397A>C (p.Asn466Thr) and c.1721A>T (p.His574Leu).Conclusions: Our findings provide evidence that mutations in the wzc suggest a potential non-canonical regulatory mechanism that may drive CPS-mediated hypermucoviscosity and hypervirulence in rmpA/rmpA2-negative hvKp. Further verification in more clinical strains is warranted.Keywords: Comparative genomic analysis, Hypervirulent Klebsiella pneumoniae (hvKp), RmpA, RmpA2, Wzc

1. Background

Klebsiella pneumoniae, an opportunistic pathogen, can cause pneumonia, sepsis, liver abscess, bacteremia, and meningitis ( 1 - 3 ). Notably, it is the second leading cause of bloodstream infections among gram-negative bacteria, following Escherichia coli ( 4 ). Based on virulence, Klebsiella pneumoniae is classified into two main phenotypes: classical K. pneumoniae (cKp) and hypervirulent K. pneumoniae (hvKp) ( 5 , 6 ). cKp typically causes infections in immunocompromised individuals, whereas hvKp differs from cKp by causing infections at multiple sites and promoting metastatic spread in otherwise healthy individuals ( 5 , 7 ). Several studies have shown that nearly 50% of hvKp infections occur in young, healthy people ( 8 , 9 ). The mechanisms driving the widespread dissemination of hvKp remain elusive ( 10 - 12 ). Previous studies have shown that the hypermucoviscous and hypervirulent phenotypes of hvKp are positively associated with pathogenicity ( 13 - 15 ). The rmpA and rmpA2 genes, which regulate the mucoid phenotype, enhance the production of capsular polysaccharides, leading to both hypermucoviscosity and hypervirulence xe "citationID"( 16 , 17 ). These genes are typically located on the hypervirulence plasmid ( 12 , 18 ). Earlier research indicated that certain K. pneumoniae isolates exhibited reduced mucoviscosity and virulence due to consecutive mutations in rmpA and rmpA2 ( 19 ). However, multiple studies have reported hvKp isolates lacking both rmpA and rmpA2 that still display a hypermucoviscous phenotype ( 15 , 20 , 21 ). These findings suggest that other virulence-related genes may contribute to the high pathogenicity of hvKp.

2. Objectives

Herein, we conducted a mucoviscosity assay and used the Galleria mellonella infection model to evaluate 20 hvKp isolates. Whole-genome sequencing and comparative genomic analysis were performed to determine the genetic background of hvKp. Our results showed that the ST1-KL122 1744 isolate still exhibited hypermucoviscosity and hypervirulence characteristics. This novel virulent profile may be associated with mutations in the wzc gene, independent of rmpA and rmpA2.

3. Materials and Methods

3.1. Bacterial Isolates

Twenty clinical isolates of Klebsiella pneumoniae exhibiting mucoid colony morphology were collected from infected patients at the First Affiliated Hospital of the University of Science and Technology of Anhui Province, China, between March and April 2022. All isolates were stored at −80 °C in 40% (v/v) glycerol.

3.2. Growth Curve

The growth of Klebsiella pneumoniae was monitored in Luria-Bertani medium. Overnight cultures were diluted to an OD600 of 0.02 and incubated in 96-well plates at 37 °C with shaking at 220 rpm. The optical density at 600 nm was measured every 30 minutes until peak growth was reached.

3.3. Mucoviscosity Assay

The string test and mucoviscosity assay were performed to assess the mucoviscous of Klebsiella pneumoniae ( 22 - 24 ). In the string test, hypermucoviscosity was defined as the formation of a string ≥5 mm in length when stretched with a sterile inoculation loop ( 25 - 27 ). For the mucoviscosity assay, K. pneumoniae was cultured overnight in LB medium at 37°C with shaking at 220 rpm. The cultures were then adjusted to an OD600 of 1.0. The diluted cultures were centrifuged every minute for 5 minutes at 2,350 g. The OD600 of the supernatant was measured at each time point.

3.4. G. Mellonella Infection Model

The Galleria mellonella model was used to assess the virulence of Klebsiella pneumoniae ( 28 - 30 ) Larvae weighing 0.3–0.4 g were purchased from Tianjin Huiyude Biotechnology Co, Ltd. They were kept in darkness and used within three days of purchase. The larvae were divided into a standard isolate group and a clinical isolates group. In the standard isolate group, ten larvae were injected with 10 μL of NTUH-K2044 bacterial suspension (106 CFU.mL-1) into the central region of the second proleg. In the clinical isolates group, for each isolate, ten larvae were injected with 10 μL of bacterial suspension (106 CFU.mL-1) using the same approach. After injection, the larvae were incubated at 37 °C, and mortality was recorded every 12 hours for three days. Each experiment was performed in triplicate.

3.5. Whole Genome Sequencing, Assembly, and Annotation

Second-generation sequencing was performed on all isolates. Briefly, whole-genome sequencing of Klebsiella pneumoniae was conducted at Background Genomics Institute using the Illumina HiSeq 4000 platform (Nuosai Jiyin Zu Research Center Limited Company, Beijing, China), generating 150-bp pairedend reads. Raw sequencing data were quality-filtered using fastp to remove adapters and low-quality reads (Q30 score > 90%) ( 31 ). Illumina reads were assembled with Unicycler v0.4.8 and annotated using Prokka 1.14.6 ( 32 , 33 ).

3.6. Genome Profiling and Comparative Genomic Analysis

Acquired antimicrobial resistance (AMR) genes were identified using ABRicate v1.0.1 (https://github.com/tseemann/abricate) by comparing the genome sequences against the ResFinder and NCBI databases ( 34 ). Virulence factors were detected using Kleborate and ABRicate by aligning the genomic sequences to the Virulence Factor Database (VFDB). Multilocus sequence typing (MLST) was performed using MLST 2.1 (https://github. com/tseemann/mlst), while capsular typing was conducted with Kleborate ( 35 ). Comparative genomic and phylogenetic analyses were conducted using the HarvestTools kit and BacWGSTdb. SNPs were identified using Snippy (https://github.com/tseemann/snippy).

3.7. Statistical Analyses

All statistical analyses were performed using Prism software (GraphPad Software, La Jolla, CA, USA). The error bars shown in the figures represent the standard error of the mean (SEM). Statistical significance is indicated as *P < 0.05 and **P < 0.01. Each experiment was repeated at least three times.

3.8. Nucleotide Sequence Accession Number

The whole-genome sequencing data have been deposited in the NCBI database under BioProject accession number PRJNA937035 (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/bioproject/).

4. Results

4.1. The Clinical Characteristics of 20 K. Pneumoniae Isolates

Twenty clinical Klebsiella pneumoniae isolates were collected from patients at a tertiary hospital between March and April 2022. Most of the patients in this investigation were hospitalized for conditions unrelated to lung disease or associated pneumonia (n=16), while three patients were diagnosed with liver abscesses. Sixty-five percent of the isolates were collected from male patients (n=13), and 35% from female patients (n=7). Among these isolates, the host age for 10 was over 60 years, while 5 isolates were from patients older than 40 years. The remaining isolates were from patients younger than 40 years. The majority of the isolates were obtained from sputum (n=8, 40%) and drainage fluid (n=5, 25%) in a clinical setting, with the remaining isolates collected from blood (n=3, 15%), bile (n=2, 10%), and urine (n=2, 10%). 4.3. K. Pneumoniae Isolates Exhibit Hypervirulence

To assess the virulence of the 20 Klebsiella pneumoniae isolates, a Galleria mellonella infection model was used. As shown in Figure 2, NTUH-K2044, recognized as a standard hvKp, induced a 90% mortality rate in Galleria mellonella by 72 hours post-injection, demonstrating its hypervirulence phenotype. The K. pneumoniae isolates also exhibited a hypervirulence phenotype. All collected K. pneumoniae isolates caused a mortality rate of over 25% in Galleria mellonella 72 hours post-injection (Fig. 2A-2D).

To assess the mucoviscosity of the 20 collected Klebsiella pneumoniae isolates, string tests and mucoviscosity assay were performed. 4.5. Some K. Pneumoniae Isolates Lack Essential Virulence Factors Of Rmpa and Rmpa2

Whole-genome sequencing was used to determine bacterial typing and identify virulence genes. MLST and K-typing revealed the following sequence types among the 20 hvKp isolates: ST23 (KL1, n = 5), ST367 (KL1, n = 2), ST86 (KL2, n = 2), ST65 (KL2, n = 2; NA, n = 1), ST218 (NA, n = 1), ST412 (KL57, n = 3), ST1 (KL122, n = 1), ST60 (KL5, n = 1), ST133 (KL116, n = 1) and ST1897 (KL39, n = 1) (Fig. 4 and Table 2). Virulence genes of the 20 Klebsiella pneumoniae isolates were further detected. As shown in Figure 4, all isolates lacked the clb gene, which encodes colibactin. Three siderophore-related genes, Ybt, Iro, and Iuc, were detected. Ybt, which encodes yersiniabactin, was absent in 8 isolates (1744, 1951, 2355, 0758, 0926, 1302, 1406, and 1529). Iro, which encodes salmochelin, was absent in 4 isolates (1744, 1951, 2355, and 2324). Iuc, which encodes aerobactin, was lacking in 5 isolates (1744, 1951, 2355, 2324, and 2960). Notably, rmpA and rmpA2, which are capsular polysaccharide-related genes (CPS-related genes), were absent in 4 isolates (1744, 1951, 2355, and 2324), while the other 16 isolates harbored rmpA and rmpA2.

4.6. Mutations in the Wzc Gene of Isolate 1744 Lacking Rmpa And Rmpa2

To further investigate the hypervirulence and hypermucoviscosity phenotypes in the ST1-KL122 isolate 1744, which lacks the rmpA and rmpA2 genes, we performed a comparative genomic analysis of SNPs. Three ST1-KL122 hvKp isolates, JAFFTH (GenBank: JAFFTH01.1), EH13 (CP089097), and DAGKTJ (DAGKTJ01.1), were obtained from the NCBI database and used as reference isolates. As shown in Figure 5A, two missense mutation SNPs in the wzc gene were identified in isolate 1744 compared with JAFFTH, EH13, and DAGKTJ. The first missense mutation occurred at the 1397th nucleotide position, where adenine (A) was replaced by cytosine (C). The second missense mutation occurred at the 1721st nucleotide position, where adenine (A) was replaced by thymine (T) (Fig. 5B). Correspondingly, the first missense mutation resulted in a substitution of asparagine (Asn) with threonine (Thr) at position 466 of the wzc protein, and the second missense mutation led to a substitution of histidine (His) with leucine (Leu) at position 574 (Fig. 5C).

Both identified missense mutations (N466T and H574L) are located in the Cterminal cytoplasmic region of wzc, which contains conserved functional domains. Their positions indicate a potential role in modulating wzc activity and regulation.

5. Discussion

Our study aimed to investigate the genetic basis of hypermucoviscosity and hypervirulence in infection-associated hvKp isolates. All isolates caused a mortality rate exceeding 25% in Galleria mellonella within 72 hours of injection. With the exception of isolates 2324, 2355, and 1951, all isolates showed greater hypermucoviscosity than the hypervirulence reference isolate Klebsiella pneumoniae NTUH-K2044. Whole-genome sequencing revealed that four isolates (1744, 2324, 1951, and 2355) lacked the capsular polysaccharide genes rmpA and rmpA2. Comparative genomic analysis of isolate 1744 identified two missense mutations in the capsular polysaccharide regulatory gene wzc. These findings suggest that wzc mutations may contribute to hypermucoviscosity and hypervirulence in K. pneumoniae isolates lacking rmpA and rmpA2.

NTUH-K2044 is a widely recognized standard isolate of highly virulent Klebsiella pneumoniae ( 36 - 38 ). The Galleria mellonella model is commonly employed to assess bacterial virulence ( 29 , 30 ). In the present study, eight of the collected isolates caused higher mortality in Galleria mellonella than NTUH-K2044. Although the remaining twelve isolates induced lower mortality compared to NTUH-K2044, all still caused mortality rates exceeding 25%. This level of virulence is consistent with mortality rates reported for hypervirulent K. pneumoniae in several previous studies ( 15 , 21 ). These results indicate that all collected isolates exhibited a hypervirulence phenotype. The string test and mucoviscosity assay are commonly used to assess bacterial mucoviscosity ( 22 - 24 ). Although the OD600 values of three hvKp isolates (2355, 2324, and 1951) were lower than that of NTUH-K2044, the remaining seventeen isolates showed higher OD600 values compared to the reference isolate. These findings suggest that all collected isolates possess a hypervirulence phenotype, and most, except for isolates 2355, 2324, and 1951, also exhibit a hypermucoviscosity phenotype.

The hypervirulent and hypermucoviscous phenotypes of Klebsiella pneumoniae are primarily driven by virulence-associated genes ( 8 , 11 , 39 ). Among these, siderophore-encoding genes facilitate high-affinity iron acquisition from the host environment, promoting bacterial survival, proliferation, and systemic dissemination during infection ( 16 , 40 , 41 ). Moreover, the rmpA and rmpA2 genes function as transcriptional activators of CPS biosynthesis, which plays a central role in mediating the hypermucoviscosity and hypervirulence phenotypes characteristic of hvKp ( 16 , 17 ). Herein, isolates 1744, 1951, 2324, and 2355 were found to harbor five to eight siderophore-related genes. Notably, these hypervirulent K. pneumoniae isolates lacked both rmpA and rmpA2, which are commonly associated with hypermucoviscosity and hypervirulence. These findings suggest the potential involvement of alternative genetic determinants contributing to the hypermucoviscosity and hypervirulence phenotypes observed in these isolates.

Whole-genome sequencing and comparative genomic analysis were conducted to further investigate the mechanism underlying the hypermucoviscosity and hypervirulence phenotype observed in the collected isolates. Among them, isolate 1744 was the only one exhibiting both phenotypes in the absence of rmpA and rmpA2. Single-nucleotide polymorphism analysis of isolate 1744 revealed two missense mutations in the wzc gene (c.1397A > C, p.Asn466Thr; c.1721A > T, p.His574Leu). Previous studies show that wzc encodes a tyrosine kinase essential for CPS synthesis ( 42 ). Mutations in wzc can lead to CPS overproduction and hypermucoviscosity. In isolate 1744, the identified wzc mutations, especially H574L located within the kinase domain, may impair autophosphorylation or interaction with the phosphatase wzb. This disruption could affect CPS chain regulation and contribute to the hypervirulent phenotype observed in this isolate. If validated across a broader collection of isolates, these mutations may provide a genetic basis for developing molecular markers to identify hypervirulent rmpA/rmpA2-negative isolates.

6. Conclusion

In summary, our study suggests that hypermucoviscosity and hypervirulence phenotypes in hvKp can occur even in the absence of rmpA and rmpA2 genes. The identification of wzc mutations in isolate 1744 offers new insights into the genetic mechanisms underlying these phenotypes, highlighting the need for a more nuanced understanding of hvKp pathogenesis. Future research should aim to broaden the diversity of clinical isolates, investigate alternative virulence pathways, and develop more accurate diagnostic strategies to better address the global threat posed by hvKp infections.

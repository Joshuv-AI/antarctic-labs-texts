Micro-CT dynamic changes and molecular mechanisms in a pulmonary nodule mouse model induced by Benzo[a]pyrene and lipopolysaccharide
Authors: Dong Shao, Yakun Zhao, Keyu Zhang, Yujia Zheng, Peng Zhao, Yange Tian, Miao Zhou, Jiansheng Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPulmonary nodules are clinically critical due to their high prevalence and association with early lung cancer. A mouse model is ideal for pulmonary nodule research. However, existing models perform poorly in simulating the slow occurrence and progression of nodules in humans. Establishing a stable, reproducible, and clinically relevant mouse model is essential for elucidating molecular mechanisms and providing a preclinical platform, motivating this study. This study aimed to establish a mouse model of pulmonary nodules induced by Benzo[a]pyrene (B[a]P) and lipopolysaccharide (LPS), and to clarify their dynamic changes using Microcomputed Tomography and molecular mechanisms.Methods C57BL/6J mice with equal numbers of males and females were instilled intratracheally with B[a]P once a week for 4 times, followed by intratracheal instillation of LPS once every 2 weeks for 5 times. The primary outcomes of this study were the evaluation of the incidence, number, and diameter of nodules by Microcomputed Tomography and the classification of nodule types through hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining. Secondary outcomes included the evaluation of nodules on the surface of the lungs. Then, the gene expression in lung nodules was detected using RNA sequencing, and the core genes of inflammatory nodule progression and malignant transformation were validated.ResultsAt week 20, the incidence, number, and diameter of nodules were 25%, 1.00±0.00, and 0.65±0.09 mm, respectively. Histopathological analyses indicated that these nodules were inflammatory. By week 28, the incidence, number, and diameter of nodules had increased to 41%, 1.45±0.28, and 0.98±0.12 mm, respectively. Histopathological results showed that 16.7% of nodules were classified as adenocarcinoma in situ (AIS), and the remaining were inflammatory. At week 36, the incidence, number, and diameter of nodules further increased to 70%, 1.71±0.36, and 1.07±0.14 mm, respectively. Histopathological results showed the proportion of AIS further increased to 30.8% among nodules. Overall, the incidence, number, and diameter of nodules significantly increased over time, and the proportion of AIS among nodules also increased. The RNA sequencing results showed that nuclear factor-kappa B was persistently expressed throughout nodules progression, promoting inflammatory nodules to AIS, validated by real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) and immunohistochemistry (IHC). Furthermore, enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) results confirmed that persistent secretion of interleukin-6 (IL-6) and tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) establishes a pro-inflammatory microenvironment, promoting inflammatory nodule formation and malignant transformation to AIS.Conclusions B[a]P and LPS could induce the formation of inflammatory nodules, and individual nodules gradually progress to AIS. Nuclear factor-kappa B persistently expressed throughout nodules progression may play a role in this process.

Highlight box

Key findings

• Benzo[a]pyrene and lipopolysaccharide induce mouse pulmonary nodules that progress from inflammatory lesions (week 20) to adenocarcinoma in situ (week 36), with rising incidence, number, and diameter. Persistent nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB) activation and interleukin-6/tumor necrosis factor-alpha secretion drive this malignant transition.

What is known and what is new?

• Pulmonary nodules are a common clinical finding, and long-term carcinogen exposure and persistent inflammation are two major risk factors.

• This novel model integrates two core human risk factors (carcinogen and chronic inflammation) and recapitulates the slow, dynamic progression of human pulmonary precancerous lesions, overcoming rapid-progression flaws of existing models.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• This novel model offers a reliable preclinical platform for exploring nodule progression mechanisms and testing early chemopreventive strategies. The NF-κB-mediated inflammatory-tumorigenic axis provides actionable targets for preventing nodule malignant transformation, with translational value for clinical at-risk nodule management.

Introduction

Pulmonary nodules are focal, rounded, hyperdense lung shadows <3 cm in diameter and the detection rate is increasing (1). In North America and Europe, the prevalence of pulmonary nodules is 23% and 29%, respectively (2). Although the majority of pulmonary nodules do not eventually progress into lung cancer, patients experience high levels of psychological stress during long-term follow-up, and some suffer from depression and anxiety (3). Currently, treatment measures for lung cancer are diverse and include surgical procedures, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, targeted therapy, and immunotherapy (4). However, there is still a lack of effective treatment for pulmonary nodules (5). Animal models serve as critical tools for assessing the efficacy and safety of pharmacological interventions before the initiation of clinical trials. Therefore, establishing an animal model for pulmonary nodules is very important.

Pulmonary nodules are mostly chronic inflammation, granulomas, adenocarcinoma in situ (AIS), or lung cancer. Currently, the most widely used animal model is lung cancer, including chemical induction, genetic mutation, and tumor transplantation (6). In addition, there are granuloma animal models that can be induced by microorganisms, such as propionibacterium acnes (7) and mycobacterium tuberculosis (8). However, the aforementioned models progress rapidly, which is inconsistent with the clinical characteristic that most nodules progress slowly. More importantly, these models fail to reflect the dynamic changes of nodules. Thus an animal model of pulmonary nodules is needed to be established so that the dynamics of the nodules could also be observed.

The formation of pulmonary nodules is complex and is often caused by multiple factors, including cooking fumes, smoking, bacterial inflammation, and others. Cigarette smoke contains over 60 carcinogens, including Benzo[a]pyrene (B[a]P), which is also commonly found in cooking fumes (9,10). B[a]P is widely recognized as a carcinogen and is also associated with the formation of pulmonary nodules. Bacterial inflammation are also known to contribute to the formation of pulmonary nodules, and research has demonstrated that the inflammatory response plays a crucial role in this process (11,12). Lipopolysaccharides (LPS) can activate monocyte macrophages, endothelial cells, epithelial cells, and other cell types through cellular signal transduction pathways in vivo, leading to the synthesis and release of various cytokines and inflammatory mediators (13,14). Consequently, a novel pulmonary nodule model was developed using a combination of B[a]P and LPS, with multiple observations conducted at an early stage.

In the present study, a mouse model of pulmonary nodules induced by B[a]P and LPS was established. The incidence, number, and diameter of the nodules at various stages were assessed using micro-computed tomography (micro-CT). Additionally, the pathological characteristics of nodules were analyzed. Subsequently, RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) was performed to detect different types of pulmonary nodules. Ultimately, the core genes in the progression of pulmonary nodules were also validated. We present this article in accordance with the ARRIVE reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2026-1-0300/rc).

Methods

Animals and reagents

Wild-type C57BL/6J mice (SPF, aged 6–8 weeks) were obtained from SPF (Beijing) Biotechnology Co., Ltd., with the animal license number SCXK (Jing) 2019-0010. An equal number of male and female mice were used. Each cage of animals was provided with free access to food and water and was housed in standard mouse housing cages with a temperature range of 20 to 25 ℃. B[a]P (CAS No. 50-32-8), Tricaprylin (CAS No. 538-23-8), and LPS (CAS No. 297-473-0) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich, USA. Isoflurane (approval number 031217015) was purchased from Hebei Jindafu Pharmaceutical Co. All animal experiments were performed under a project license (No. DWLL202208007) granted by Ethics Committee of Henan University of Traditional Chinese Medicine, in compliance with institutional guidelines for the care and use of animals.

Construction of a mouse model and experimental protocol

A small sample of mice was selected because the pulmonary nodules mouse model was evaluated in vivo for the first time in the present study. Eighty C57BL/6J mice were randomly divided into two groups (B[a]P and LPS group, n=50 and Vehicle control, n=30). Random numbers were generated using the standard = RAND() function in Microsoft Excel. Following a 7-day acclimation period to the housing environment, mice in the B[a]P and LPS group were intratracheally instilled with B[a]P at a dosage of 40 mg/kg, dissolved in 50 µL of glyceryl trioctanoate, once a week for 4 times from week 1 to week 4. In contrast, mice in the Vehicle control received 50 µL glyceryl trioctanoate. Beginning at week 6, mice in the B[a]P and LPS group were intratracheally instilled with LPS at a dose of 125 µg/kg, dissolved in 50 µL of saline, once every 2 weeks for 5 times from week 6 to week 14, while the Vehicle control were administered 50 µL of saline. Details of model construction are provided in Figure 1A. To ensure accurate intratracheal instillation B[a]P and LPS, a single mouse was anesthetized and then suspended in a vertical position to align the trachea, and the occurrence of rales post-instillation indicated successful administration. All instillations were conducted under anesthesia with isoflurane. Mice were exposed to B[a]P and LPS between 9:00 and 11:00 a.m. daily. Twice-daily morning and evening examinations were performed on animals by investigators. Health status was assessed based on weekly body weight and food/water intake. Animals that died before the scheduled experimental endpoint or that did not complete the data acquisition were excluded from the final data analysis to avoid incomplete or missing data. To assess the timing of nodule formation, a cage of animals was randomly selected for micro-CT imaging at week 19 of the experiment (Figure 1A). Lung nodule-bearing mice were defined as those exhibiting focal, hyperdense, round or oval shadows on Micro-CT scans. As depicted in Figure S1, no nodules were observed in the Vehicle control group, whereas the B[a]P and LPS group exhibited a distinct nodule. Pathological analysis revealed mononuclear cell infiltration. To further investigate the progression of nodule, the mice underwent micro-CT imaging at weeks 20, 28, and 36, and were euthanized under anesthesia with pentobarbital sodium (100 mg/kg) at weeks 20, 28, and 36. Details regarding the experimental protocol and animal allocation are provided in Figure 1.

In vivo micro-CT analysis

Mice were anesthetized using isoflurane, with the gas flow adjusted to 500–700 mL/min and the induction concentration set to 3–4%. The anesthetic was allowed to fill the induction box for approximately 1 minute. Mice were placed in the induction box, and full anesthesia was confirmed when they were turned onto their sides. At this point, the maintenance concentration was adjusted to 2–2.5%. The mice were then removed from the induction box and positioned prone on a specialized plate of the high-resolution in vivo micro-CT (PINGSENG Healthcare Inc., NMC-200, Kunshan, Jiangsu, China). The parameters were adjusted to perform lung CT scanning for about 5 minutes, after which the lung CT images were reconstructed using the iterative algorithm in Recon software. The reconstructed CT images were imported into 3D Slicer (http://www.slicer.org) for conversion into a 3D volume for visualization. The scanning parameters included a tube voltage of 60 kV, a tube current of 0.15 mA, and a pixel size of 0.5 mm × 0.5 mm × 0.5 mm.

Histopathological analysis

The lung tissue was placed in 4% paraformaldehyde overnight for fixation. Following fixation, the lung tissue was embedded in paraffin and sectioned into 4 µm thick slices for staining with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E). The pathology of nodules was classified as inflammation and AIS by two experienced pathologists. Microscopically, AIS appears as a pure lepidic growth of tumor cells along pre-existing alveolar structures and lacks lymphatic, vascular, stromal, or pleural invasion.

RNA sequencing

RNA sequencing was performed at Novogene (Beijing), including RNA extraction, RNA detection, library construction, and sequencing. After genome alignment, gene saturation analysis, and gene quantification, the EdgeR method [false discovery rate (FDR) <0.05] was used for difference analysis to screen DEGs among each group of samples. Then, Gene Ontology (GO) functional enrichment analysis and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis were performed. RNA sequencing was performed on lung tissues from three pathological stages. A total of 6 normal lung tissues were collected from mice at 28 weeks, 4 inflammatory nodules were obtained at 28 weeks, and 4 AIS tissues were harvested at 36 weeks. Total RNA was isolated from lung tissues and assessed for quality before sequencing. RNA purity was determined by the A260/A280 ratio (acceptable range: 1.8–2.0), and RNA integrity was evaluated using the RNA Integrity Number (RIN ≥7.0) on an Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer. Qualified RNA samples were used for cDNA library construction. Library concentration and fragment size were strictly checked to meet sequencing standards. Raw RNA-seq reads were filtered with fastp software to remove adapter sequences, ambiguous bases, and low-quality reads. Reads with Q30 below 80% were excluded to guarantee high-quality clean data for subsequent differential expression analysis.

Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction

Total RNA was extracted from nodules using TRIzol® reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, USA). RNA purity and concentration were determined via a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA), with an A260/A280 ratio of 1.8–2.1. Reverse transcription conditions were: 37 ℃ for 15 min (reverse transcription), 85 ℃ for 5 s (reverse transcriptase inactivation), and holding at 4 ℃. Q-PCR was performed on a StepOnePlus™ Real-Time PCR System (Thermo Fisher Scientific) to quantify the mRNA expression of target genes. Thermal cycling conditions were optimized as follows: initial denaturation at 95 ℃ for 30 s, followed by 40 cycles of 95 ℃ for 5 s and 60 ℃ for 30 s. Relative mRNA expression was calculated using the 2-ΔΔCt method. Primers were designed via NCBI Primer-BLAST (sequences in Table 1), synthesized by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai, China).

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

The expression of P-P65 and NLRP3 in nodules was detected using IHC. Initially, the sections were blocked at room temperature for 30 minutes with goat serum. Subsequently, they were incubated overnight at 4 ℃ with rabbit anti-mouse antibodies at a dilution of 1:500 (Servicebio, Wuhan, China). Following this, the sections were incubated at 37 ℃ for 50 minutes with biotinylated goat anti-rabbit antibodies at a dilution of 1:100. The quantification of positive IHC staining was assessed based on brown staining, and the area of optical density (AOD) was calculated using the formula AOD = integrated optical density (IOD) SUM/area SUM, as analyzed under high-power fields using Image-Pro Plus 6.0 software.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay

Bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) was prepared by injecting 1 mL PBS into the bronchus for perfusion, followed by aspiration back into a centrifuge tube. This procedure was repeated three times, after which the BALF was collected and stored at −80 ℃.

The levels of interleukin-6 (IL-6) and tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) in the BALF were measured using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The mouse ELISA kits for IL-6 and TNF-α were provided by Elabscience, Wuhan, China.

Statistical analysis

The experiments were conducted in triplicate, and the results are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Statistical analyses were performed using the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS 26.0, Chicago, USA). Model assumptions were checked using the Shapiro-Wilk normality test and Levene’s Test for homogeneity of variance. If this assumption was met, Multiple sample groups were compared using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). Data were further analyzed with the post hoc least significant difference (LSD) test under the assumption of homogeneity of variance; if this assumption was not met, Dunnett’s test was employed. The significance level was set at α=0.05, with P<0.05 considered statistically significant.

Results

Pulmonary nodules induced by B[a]P and LPS progressed gradually

Before week 20, five mice died among the B[a]P and LPS group (two mice died at week 12, and another three died at week 14) during the experiment and thus were excluded from the final data analysis. The total number and the exact number in each experiment are summarized in Figure S2.

At week 20, micro-CT showed no nodules in mice of the Vehicle control (n=10) (Figure 2A). In contrast, among the B[a]P and LPS group (n=28), the nodules were observed, with the incidence, number, and diameter of nodules were 25%, 1.00±0.00, and 0.65±0.09 mm, respectively (Figure 2B). By week 28, Micro-CT showed mice of the Vehicle control (n=10) still had no nodules (Figure 2A), however the incidence, number, and diameter of nodules had increased to 41%, 1.45±0.28, and 0.98±0.12 mm, respectively, among the B[a]P and LPS group (n=27) (Figure 2B). At week 36, Micro-CT showed mice of the Vehicle control (n=10) still had no nodules (Figure 2A), the incidence, number, and diameter of nodules further increased to 70%, 1.71±0.36, and 1.07±0.14 mm, respectively, among the B[a]P and LPS group (n=10) (Figure 2B). Overall, the incidence, number, and diameter of nodules significantly increased from the 20th to the 36th week.

Nodules on the surface of the lungs

At week 20, no nodules were observed on the surface of the lungs in mice of the Vehicle control (n=10) (Figure 4A). However, among the B[a]P and LPS group (n=18), with the incidence and number of nodules were 16.6%, and 1.00±0.00 mm, respectively (Figure 4B). Same as 20 weeks, no nodules were observed in mice of the Vehicle control (n=10) at 28 and 36 weeks (Figure 4A). However, among the B[a]P and LPS group (n=17), the incidence and number of nodules increased to 29.4% and 1.4±0.4, respectively at 28 weeks. Among the B[a]P and LPS group (n=10), the incidence and number of nodules increased to 50% and 1.8±0.37, respectively at 36 weeks (Figure 4B).

Histopathological findings

At week 20, the alveolar structure of mice of the Vehicle control (n=10) was intact, and there was no obvious inflammation or other pathological changes. In contrast, the mice among the B[a]P and LPS group (n=18) were observed to have fractured and fused alveolar lumens, a small amount of alveolar hemorrhage, as well as interstitial edema, and aggregation of inflammatory cells into clusters (Figure 5A). Similar to week 20, the alveolar structure of mice of the Vehicle control (n=10) was intact, and no obvious inflammation or other pathological changes at 28 and 36 weeks. However, compared with week 20, mice among the B[a]P and LPS group at weeks 28 (n=17) and weeks 36 (n=10) showed more severe disruption of lung tissue structure and greater numbers of inflammatory cells clustered into clusters (Figure 5A), in addition to the AIS observed (Figure 5B).

(C) The proportions of nodule subtypes (number of nodule subtypes/number of all nodule). AIS, adenocarcinoma in situ; H&E, hematoxylin and eosin.

Subsequently, the proportions of inflammatory and AIS were calculated among nodules. As shown in Figure 5C, all nodules induced by B[a]P and LPS were inflammatory nodules at week 20. At week 28, inflammatory nodules and AIS accounted for 83.3% and 16.7% among nodules, respectively. By week 36, inflammatory nodules and AIS accounted for 69.2% and 30.8% among nodules, respectively. Overall, the pathological nature of nodules exhibited a gradual progression from inflammatory lesions to AIS between week 20 and week 36.

RNA sequencing reveals the molecular mechanism of inflammatory nodule progression to AIS

To elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying inflammatory nodule progression and malignant transformation, RNA sequencing was performed on 6 normal lung tissue from 28w, 4 inflammatory nodules from 28w, and 4 AIS tissues from 36w. Principal component analysis (PCA) showed that three groups clustered independently without overlap, indicating distinct molecular phenotypic among them (Figure 6A).

Pairwise comparisons were conducted using DESeq2 with criteria of |log2FC| >1 and P value<0.05, yielding two sets of core differentially expressed genes (DEGs). A total of 4,017 DEGs (2,730 upregulated and 1,287 downregulated) were identified in Inflammation vs. Control (Figure 6B), with upregulated genes mainly including pro-inflammatory factors [e.g., IL-6, TNF-α, interleukin-1β (IL-1β)] and inflammatory receptors [e.g., toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4), nuclear factor kappa-B1 (Nfkb1), v-rel avian reticuloendotheliosis viral oncogene homolog A (Rela)] (Figure 6C). For AIS vs. Inflammation, 4,966 DEGs were detected (1,165 upregulated and 3,801 downregulated) (Figure 6D), among which upregulated genes were concentrated in oncogenes [e.g., myelocytomatosis oncogene (Myc), cyclin D1 (Ccnd1)], anti-apoptotic genes [e.g., B-cell lymphoma 2 (Bcl-2)], and inflammation-cancer crosstalk regulatory genes (e.g., IL-6, Nfkb1, Rela), while downregulated genes mainly included tumor suppressor genes [e.g., Tumor protein p53 (Trp53)] and apoptosis-related genes [e.g., Caspase-3 (Casp3)] (Figure 6E).

GO and KEGG enrichment analyses were performed to identify core biological pathways involved in nodule progression. In Inflammation vs. Control, upregulated DEGs were mainly enriched in inflammatory response, immune cell infiltration and positive regulation of IL-6/TNF production (Figure 6F). Among the top 15 KEGG pathways, 5 were inflammation-related, including IL-17, nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB), TNF, Toll-like receptor, and NOD-like receptor pathway (Figure 6G). These results indicate that B[a]P and LPS induce chronic pulmonary inflammation and nodule formation by activating the inflammation-related pathway. For AIS vs Inflammation, upregulated DEGs were mainly enriched in cell cycle regulation, DNA damage response, EMT, and lipid metabolism (Figure 6H); downregulated DEGs were mainly enriched in immune response (Figure 6I). Core KEGG pathways included metabolic pathways, pathways in cancer, PI3K-Akt, p53, TNF, and NF-κB-oncogene regulatory pathway (Figure 6J).

Venn diagram analysis revealed 3,838 overlapping DEGs (P value <0.05) between the two comparisons (Figure 6K). Notably, TNF-α and NF-κB were persistently expressed throughout disease progression (Figure 6L), suggesting them as core molecules regulating lung nodule progression and malignant transformation.

Validation of core genes by qPCR and IHC

To validate the expression of core regulatory genes, qPCR was performed to detect 7 key genes (Nfkb1, Rela, Myc, Ccnd1, Bcl-2, Trp53, and Casp3). Q-PCR analysis showed that inflammation-cancer crosstalk regulatory genes (Nfkb1 and Rela), oncogene genes (Myc, Ccnd1), and the anti-apoptotic gene Bcl-2 were gradually upregulated from the Control to Inflammation and AIS. In contrast, the tumor suppressor gene Trp53 and apoptosis gene Casp3 were progressively downregulated across the three groups (Figure 7A).

To further clarify the role of the NF-κB pathway in inflammatory nodule-to-AIS progression, IHC staining was performed to detect the expression of P-P65 and NLRP3. The results showed that the expression of P-P65 and NLRP3 showed a time-dependent increase with disease progression (Figure 7B).

Detection of IL-6 and TNF-α levels in BALF

To confirm persistent inflammatory activation in the lung microenvironment during inflammatory nodule-to-AIS progression, ELISA was used to detect IL-6 and TNF-α concentrations in BALF from mice at 20, 28, and 36 weeks. IL-6 and TNF-α levels in BALF showed a time-dependent increase with disease progression (Figure 7C). This time-dependent elevation was consistent with their progressive mRNA upregulation in lung tissues (qPCR verified) and sustained NF-κB activation (IHC confirmed). Persistent secretion of IL-6 and TNF-α establishes a pro-inflammatory microenvironment, promoting inflammatory nodule formation and malignant transformation to AIS.

Discussion

Pulmonary nodules represent a critical clinical challenge owing to their high incidence and malignant transformation potential, underscoring the imperative for robust animal models to dissect the underlying pathogenic mechanisms (15). Herein, we established a mouse model induced by intratracheal instillation of B[a]P combined with LPS, which recapitulates the dynamic progression from inflammatory nodules to AIS of human lung precancerous lesions—validated by micro-CT, histopathological grading, and RNA-seq. This model addresses the limitations of existing single-agent induction or genetically engineered models, which often fail to recapitulate the synergistic effects of environmental carcinogens and chronic inflammation in human pulmonary nodules (16).

Co-treatment with B[a]P and LPS is a strategy to establish inflammation-driven malignant transformation models (17-19). B[a]P exerts genotoxic effects and initiates early oncogenic damage, while LPS continuously activates inflammatory signaling and maintains a chronic pro-inflammatory microenvironment. The combination of the two can synergistically facilitate the progression from chronic lung inflammation to inflammatory nodules and even early cancerous lesions. The model’s time-dependent progression is consistent with the natural history of human lung lesions, with histopathological findings providing definitive phenotypic validation. At week 20, HE staining identified exclusively inflammatory nodules—characterized by alveolar lumen disruption and fusion, interstitial edema, focal hemorrhage, and clustered inflammatory cell infiltrates—which is consistent with early lung tissue injury induced by B[a]P and LPS (20). LPS is a well-characterized inducer of acute lung injury via triggering pro-inflammatory signaling cascades, and its sustained stimulation contributes to chronic inflammatory microenvironments that promote tumor initiation (21). By week 28, histopathological assessment confirmed that 16.7% of nodules had progressed to AIS, accompanied by exacerbated lung tissue architectural disruption and increased inflammatory cell aggregation. By week 36, the proportion of AIS nodules further increased to 30.8%, indicating a gradual malignant transition, which aligns with the notion that pre-cancerous lesions undergo stepwise molecular and phenotypic changes before invasive transformation.

RNA-seq deciphered the molecular underpinnings of this progression, with PCA confirming distinct molecular phenotypes in control, inflammatory nodules, and AIS (no intergroup overlap). Pairwise comparisons (|log2FC| >1, P<0.05) identified 4017 DEGs in the Inflammation vs. Control group, which were enriched in NF-κB/Toll-like receptor (TLR) pathways and IL-6/TNF production—consistent with the upregulation of pro-inflammatory factors (IL-6, TNF-α, IL-1β) and receptors (TLR4, Nfkb1, Rela). NF-κB signaling is a central regulator of inflammatory responses and has been implicated as a key mediator in linking chronic inflammation to lung tumorigenesis (22). For the AIS vs. Inflammation comparison, 4,966 DEGs were shifted toward cell cycle regulation, DNA damage response, and oncogenic signaling, featuring upregulated Myc/Ccnd1 (cell proliferation), Bcl-2 (anti-apoptosis), sustained NF-κB activation, and downregulated Trp53/Casp3 (tumor suppression/apoptosis). Venn diagram analysis (3,838 overlapping DEGs) confirmed NF-κB and TNF-α as persistent core regulators, bridging inflammatory responses and malignant transformation, which is supported by studies showing NF-κB activation in pre-cancerous lung lesions and its essential role in tumor initiation.

Q-PCR validated the RNA-seq findings, showing gradual upregulation of Nfkb1/Rela (core NF-κB components), Myc/Ccnd1 (oncogenes), and Bcl-2 (anti-apoptotic gene), alongside progressive downregulation of Trp53 and Casp3 across the control-inflammation-AIS spectrum—confirming a pro-tumorigenic molecular signature. IHC further clarified the functional activation of NF-κB: time-dependent upregulation of P-P65 (the activated form of NF-κB) and NLRP3, linking NF-κB to inflammasome-mediated inflammatory amplification. Recent studies have demonstrated that the crosstalk between NF-κB and NLRP3 inflammasome drives inflammatory responses and promotes tumor progression in NSCLC (23). ELISA complemented these findings by demonstrating time-dependent elevation of IL-6 and TNF-α in BALF, which reinforced NF-κB activation to form a pro-inflammatory feedback loop—establishing a causal link between microenvironmental inflammation and AIS transformation, consistent with prior reports that IL-1β/IL-6-enriched microenvironments accelerate pre-cancerous lesion progression (24). The elevated secretion of IL-6 and TNF-α plays a central role in shaping a pro-inflammatory microenvironment in our model. Upstream, chronic exposure to BAP combined with LPS induces metabolic activation of B[a]P, leading to oxidative stress, DNA adduct formation, and subsequent activation of NF-κB and MAPK signaling pathways. These pathways transcriptionally upregulate IL-6 and TNF-α expression, forming a persistent pro-inflammatory loop. Downstream, sustained release of IL-6 and TNF-α promotes inflammatory cell recruitment, enhances inflammatory cell infiltration, inhibits anti-tumor immune surveillance, and accelerates epithelial cell proliferation and genetic instability. Consequently, the pro-inflammatory microenvironment gradually evolves into a tumor-promoting milieu, which drives the progression from inflammatory nodules to carcinoma in situ. Therefore, the metabolic activation of B[a]P-initiated inflammatory signaling axis (NF-κB-IL-6/TNF-α) serves as a critical driver in inflammation-mediated lung nodule malignant transformation.

The novelty of this model lies in the integration of radiological, pathological, and molecular validation. Micro-CT enables non-invasive longitudinal monitoring of nodule dynamics, a key asset for preclinical intervention trials. Histopathological grading (inflammatory nodules vs. AIS) provides a gold-standard phenotypic classification, while RNA-seq uncovers pathway-level mechanisms—validated by qPCR, IHC, and ELISA—forming a multi-layered validation chain. Unlike genetically engineered models that rely on specific gene knockouts, this model recapitulates dual human lung cancer risk factors: environmental carcinogen exposure (B[a]P) and chronic inflammation (LPS), with the NF-κB/NLRP3/IL-6/TNF-α axis serving as actionable targets for preventing the malignant transformation of inflammatory nodules. Targeting this axis during the pre-cancerous stage has been shown to effectively inhibit lung tumor development, highlighting the translational value of our model (25).

Notwithstanding its strengths, this study has limitations. Although we observed sustained upregulation of NF-κB, IL-6 and TNF-α throughout the lesion progression, our current evidence only demonstrates a close correlative relationship rather than definite causality. Further mechanistic researches, including gene knockout or specific pharmacological intervention, are still needed in the future to verify the causal relationship and molecular mechanism. While RNA-seq identified crosstalk among the NF-κB, and p53 pathways, mechanistic studies (e.g., pathway inhibition or gene knockout assays) are needed to dissect their intricate interactions, as these pathways often form complex regulatory networks in lung tumorigenesis. Long-term follow-up beyond 36 weeks is required to confirm whether the model progresses to invasive adenocarcinoma, which would better mimic the full spectrum of human lung cancer progression from pre-cancerous lesions to invasive disease. Additionally, the applicability of this model to different subtypes of pulmonary nodules (e.g., solid vs. ground-glass nodules) warrants further exploration, as distinct nodule subtypes may exhibit different molecular profiles and progression kinetics. Another limitation of this study is that all pathological observations were terminated at 36 weeks. Whether longer induction duration (more than 40 weeks) could drive carcinoma in situ to further progress into invasive lung cancer remains unclear. Future studies with prolonged modeling time points are required to fully clarify the complete progression spectrum of BaP/LPS-induced lung lesions.

In conclusion, we established a stable B[a]P and LPS-induced mouse model of pulmonary nodules that exhibits time-dependent progression from inflammatory lesions to AIS—comprehensively validated by Micro-CT, histopathology, RNA-seq, qPCR, IHC, and ELISA. Persistent NF-κB activation, coupled with NLRP3 inflammasome activation and IL-6/TNF-α secretion, drives the malignant transformation process, consistent with the central role of inflammatory signaling in lung pre-cancerous progression. This multi-validated model provides a valuable preclinical platform for elucidating the mechanisms of pulmonary nodule progression and testing early chemopreventive or therapeutic interventions targeting the inflammatory-tumorigenic axis.

Conclusions

B[a]P and LPS could induce the formation of inflammatory nodules, and individual nodules gradually progress to AIS. NF-κB persistently expressed throughout nodules progression may play a role in this process.

Recent Advances in Electrochemical Preparation and Deposition of Alloys
Authors: Jingya Xu, Yu Jiang, Zheng Huang, Qi Wang, Shijie Li, Wei-Li Song, Yiwa Luo, Dongbai Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This paper reviews advances in electrolytic methods for alloy preparation, with a focus on three primary techniques: aqueous electrolysis, molten salt electrolysis, and ionic liquid electrolysis. Recognized for cost-effectiveness and environmental benefits, aqueous electrolysis is widely employed for the deposition and alloying of metals with relatively positive reduction potentials (e.g., Cu, Ni, Sn, Zn). Molten salt electrolysis offers straightforward operation and a wide electrochemical window, making it particularly suitable for synthesizing high-melting-point and highly reactive metals and their alloys, including aluminum and titanium alloys. Ionic liquid electrolysis offers a broad electrochemical window and can be conducted at low temperatures, enabling the preparation of complex multicomponent alloys. Additionally, postelectrolytic heat treatment plays a crucial role in enhancing the microstructural and mechanical properties of the alloys. The incorporation of advanced four-dimensional (4D) visualization techniques further provides dynamic insights into microstructural evolution during electrodeposition, offering valuable guidance for the optimization of electrochemical processes and the design of novel alloy materials.

Introduction

In modern materials science, the process of alloy preparation plays a decisive role in determining the compositional homogeneity, microstructure evolution, and ultimate properties of alloy materials. Conventional techniques, such as melting, powder metallurgy, and mechanical alloying, are often complex and energy-intensive. In contrast, electrolysis provides a more straightforward and environmentally friendly alternative, offering precise control over alloy composition and microstructure. This paper explores three primary electrochemical methodsaqueous electrolysis, molten salt electrolysis, and ionic liquid electrolysisemphasizing their specific applications and advantages for alloy synthesis. Furthermore, heat treatment plays a critical role as a postprocessing step, significantly enhancing the microstructural characteristics and mechanical properties of electrodeposited alloys. −

Additionally, Four-dimensional (4D) characterization combines spatial resolution with temporal evolution, enabling real-time correlation of interfacial dynamics, process parameters, and microstructural changes. This technique reveals key phenomena such as nucleation, dendritic growth, and interfacial stability, highlighting the roles of kinetic processes and diffusion limitations during electrodeposition. 4D characterization advances the understanding of multicomponent electrochemical deposition and provides insights for optimizing electrolysis cell design. Overall, 4D visualization serves as an enabling tool that links process parameters with alloy microstructural evolution, rather than a standalone characterization technique.

This review provides a comprehensive foundation for advancing electrolytic alloy preparation, ultimately paving the way for its expanded use in advanced materials for aerospace, electronics, and related fields.

Electrolytic Preparation and Deposition of Alloys in Aqueous Solutions

The electrochemical preparation of alloys via aqueous electrolysis has been extensively reported in the literature and is generally based on the cathodic reduction of metal ions in aqueous electrolytes accompanied by anodic oxidation reactions. Owing to the relatively narrow electrochemical window of water, this approach is primarily applicable to metals with more positive reduction potentials, such as Cu, Ni, Sn, and Zn. Numerous studies have demonstrated that aqueous electrolysis offers several advantages over conventional alloy production routes, including cost-effectiveness, environmental compatibility, and high controllability of alloy composition. , These advantages are largely attributed to the use of readily available aqueous electrolytes, which reduce material costs and limit the generation of hazardous byproducts. In addition, systematic investigations have shown that key operational parameterssuch as current density, pH, and temperatureplay critical roles in governing alloy composition, microstructural characteristics, and associated properties, including mechanical performance and corrosion resistance. − As a result of these combined merits, aqueous electrolysis has been widely adopted for the preparation of functional and structural alloys, with reported applications spanning aerospace, electronics, and engineering materials. , Taken together, existing studies indicate that aqueous electrolysis represents a mature and versatile platform for alloy preparation within its electrochemical constraints, providing a useful reference framework for comparison with molten salt and ionic liquid electrolysis discussed in the following sections.

Cu Alloys

Cu alloys are widely utilized in electronic devices, construction materials, and transportation due to their excellent electrical conductivity, corrosion resistance, and favorable mechanical properties. , Owing to copper’s relatively positive reduction potential, aqueous electrolysis was initially used for the deposition of pure copper. Advances in electrochemistry and materials science have successfully extended its application to the fabrication of a wide range of Cu-based alloys. −

Representative studies have demonstrated the preparation of synthesizing Cu–Sn alloys via aqueous electrolysis by regulating electrochemical parameters and electrolyte ratios. For example, Ding et al. reported the electrodeposition of Cu–Sn alloys on stainless steel substrates using a deep eutectic solvent (DES)–based aqueous electrolyte. As shown in Figure a, uniform Cu–Sn alloy coatings were obtained under optimized current density and metal ion concentration, indicating synchronized reduction of Cu and Sn rather than phase-separated deposition. The corresponding electrochemical behavior is summarized in Figure b, where distinct cathodic and anodic features reflect Cu–Sn codeposition. These features provide mechanistic insight into the competitive reduction and alloy formation process in aqueous media. Microstructural characterization in Figure c demonstrates that the codeposition regime is associated with a narrow particle size distribution and uniform surface morphology, which correlates with the dominant codeposition peak observed in the voltametric response. Phase analysis presented in Figure d further confirms the formation of Cu–Sn intermetallic compounds without detectable elemental Sn, validating the effectiveness of aqueous electrolysis in producing compositionally homogeneous Cu–Sn alloys. −

Sn Alloys

Sn alloys have a low melting point, strong ductility, and good wettability, which give them important applications in electronic packaging, thermal interface materials, and anticorrosion coatings. The aqueous electrolysis allows for exceptional control over both the composition and microstructure of tin-alloy films and coatings. ,

Kazimierczak et al. demonstrated the feasibility of fabricating ternary Sn–Zn–Bi alloy coatings via aqueous electrodeposition using a citrate–EDTA complexing system. The electrochemical configuration employed to achieve stable codeposition is schematically illustrated in Figure a. The voltametric responses, summarized in Figure b, reveal distinct cathodic and anodic features associated with the reduction and dissolution of Sn, Zn, and BI. These features indicate that the Cit–EDTA electrolyte effectively enables their simultaneous deposition despite differences in standard reduction potentials. , These electrochemical characteristics confirm that controlled codeposition of the ternary alloy can be achieved through appropriate electrolyte design.

Morphological features of the deposited Sn–Zn–Bi alloy are shown in Figure c, where the coating is composed of polyhedral particles with cauliflower-like or near-spherical morphologies. Such microstructural characteristics are commonly associated with Zn- and Birich electrodeposits and reflect the competitive growth behavior during multicomponent electrodeposition. Phase analysis presented in Figure d confirms the coexistence of crystalline Sn, Zn, and Bi phases, demonstrating good mutual compatibility among the constituent metals during aqueous electrodeposition. Additionally, Otto et al. investigated the effect of heat treatment on the microstructural evolution of Sn–Cu alloys. Their findings underscore the critical roles of temperature and duration in governing grain size and texture development. Specifically, a lower annealing temperature of 380 °C held for 900 s resulted in incomplete recrystallization, as illustrated in Figure e. Ni Alloys

Nickel alloys are valued for functional coatings and structural applications due to their exceptional corrosion resistance and mechanical properties. A key method for their preparation is aqueous electrolysis, which allows for the cathodic codeposition of nickel with other metals. This process yields alloy coatings with tailored compositions and highly uniform densities. ,

Ahmadkhaniha et al. successfully prepared Ni–Co–Mo-W alloy coatings via aqueous electrolysis. The electrolyte contained NiSO4·7H2O, CoCl2·6H2O, CuSO4·5H2O, Na2MoO4, and Na2WO4 were used as metal sources, with citric acid and boric acid serving as complexing agents and pH buffers. A schematic of the electrolytic setup is presented in Figure a, demonstrating the stable system established for the codeposition of these quaternary alloys. Microstructural analysis using scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) (Figure b) revealed that the coating surface exhibits predominantly columnar growth, with all elements uniformly distributed throughout the deposit. Figure c shows a cross-sectional SEM image of a coating obtained at 8 A/dm2 for 2 h, having an average thickness of 8.5 ± 1 μm. The results suggest that increasing coating thickness leads to elevated internal stress, subsequently promoting the formation of microcracks. X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis (Figure d) indicated that the alloy structure remained stable after heating to 700 °C, with identifiable phase-related diffraction peaks. Upon further heating to 900 °C, these peaks sharpened considerably, indicating significant grain growth within the coating.

In heat treatment research, Lee et al. analyzed the microstructure and crystal texture evolution of Ni alloys at different annealing temperatures. Figure e shows the microstructural changes of the Ni alloy as the annealing temperature is increased from 900 °C to 1000, 1100, 1125, and 1150 °C. Grain orientation analysis conducted via EBSD is presented in Figure f, which shows the IPF map and corresponding pole figure along the rolling direction (RD). The results indicate that at 1000 °C, the microstructure is dominated by micrometer-sized ⟨112⟩ grains, indicating partial recrystallization. When the temperature reaches 1100 °C, abnormal grain growth of ⟨001⟩-oriented grains occur, reflecting a pronounced coarsening behavior. As the temperature further increases to 1125 and 1150 °C, the primary texture in the RD shifts from {110} ⟨112⟩ to {210} ⟨001⟩, demonstrating that high-temperature annealing promotes significant grain rearrangement and the development of new preferred orientations. These findings underscore the crucial role of heat treatment temperature in governing the microstructure, grain size, and texture evolution of Ni-based alloys, providing valuable insights for tailoring their mechanical properties and functional characteristics.

Electrolytic Preparation and Deposition of Alloys in Molten Salts

Molten salt electrolysis is widely recognized as an effective method for preparing alloys composed of high-melting-point and highly reactive metals. This is due to its broad electrochemical window and high-temperature operating conditions. These characteristics make it particularly suitable for alloy systems involving elements such as aluminum, magnesium, titanium, zirconium, and hafnium. As a result, molten salt electrolysis has gained increasing attention in recent materials research. In this method, metal ions migrate within a high-temperature molten electrolyte under the influence of an electric field. They are then coreduced at the cathode to form alloy deposits. , Commonly used molten salt systems, such as fluorides, aluminates, and oxides, offer high thermal stability and good electrical conductivity. These properties create a robust electrochemical environment for alloy deposition. , Systematic studies have shown that by adjusting electrolyte composition and operating parameters, molten salt electrolysis allows for relatively uniform codeposition. This also enables effective control over alloy stoichiometry, which in turn affects microstructural features and the resulting properties. − In conclusion, the literature demonstrates that molten salt electrolysis provides a versatile platform for alloy synthesis. It extends the range of accessible elements beyond aqueous electrolysis to include more reactive systems. Representative examples, such as Al-, Mg-, and Ti-based alloys, highlight both the capabilities and the current limitations of this approach, which are discussed in the following section.

Al Alloys

Al alloys are widely used in the aerospace and transportation sectors due to their lightweight, high strength, and excellent corrosion resistance. Traditional methods for producing aluminum alloys, such as casting, forging, and extrusion, are often associated with high energy consumption and challenges in impurity control. In contrast, molten salt electrolysis offers a greener and more efficient alternative. This technique enables the high-temperature reduction and uniform codeposition of aluminum with other alloying elements, paving the way for the fabrication of high-purity, high-performance aluminum alloys. −

Yang et al. successfully synthesized a uniform Al–Si alloy via molten salt electrolysis using the NaF-AlF3–Al2O3–SiO2 system, as illustrated in Figure a. According to the CV results (Figure b), points B and C correspond to the deposition potentials of Si and Al. A photograph of the electrolytically obtained Al–Si alloy (Figure c) shows a smooth surface, free of the needle-like structures often observed in conventional casting, indicating a uniform and dense morphology. − The XRD patterns in Figure d reveal that the alloy is mainly composed of Al and Si phases, and the diffraction intensity of Si notably intensified with increasing SiO2 addition in the electrolyte. Furthermore, SEM and EDS analysis (Figure e) revealed a finely dispersed and interwoven distribution of Si within the Al matrix, with elemental mapping clearly illustrating the spatial homogeneity of both Al and Si.

Liu et al. performed heat treatment on the 2195 Al–Li alloy to optimize its grain structure. SEM imaging (Figure f) of the alloy plate after annealing at 300 °C shows a significant reduction in the density of fine secondary-phase particles, indicating a clear trend toward grain coarsening and microstructural homogenization. The ODF analysis of solution-treated samples annealed at 300 °C (Figure g) identifies Cube {110} ⟨100⟩ and Goss {110}⟨100⟩ as the dominant texture components, suggesting that annealing promotes preferential grain orientation rearrangement and enhances specific textural strengths. Furthermore, EBSD analysis (Figure i) elucidates the grain morphology and orientation characteristics, collectively confirming that significant recrystallization occurs during annealing at 300 °C, resulting in notable grain growth and a tendency toward orientation concentration.

Mg Alloys

Mg alloys are widely used in aerospace, automotive lightweight applications, and electronics due to their low density and high specific strength. However, the high chemical reactivity and relatively low melting point of Mg pose significant challenges in alloy processing. Traditional preparation methods often involve high energy consumption, pronounced oxidation tendencies, and limited compositional uniformity. In contrast, molten salt electrolysis effectively mitigates oxidation and impurity incorporation common in conventional smelting, enabling the production of Mg alloys with well-controlled compositions and homogeneous microstructures, thereby offering a novel technological pathway for developing high-performance Mg-based materials. ,

Jang et al. employed a MgCl2–KCl-AlF3 molten salt system with lanthanum chloride introduced as the alloying source. By regulating key parameters such as temperature, current density, and metal ion concentration, they successfully synthesized Mg-based alloys via molten salt electrolysis. The electrolysis principles and experimental schematic are illustrated in Figure a. According to the cyclic voltammetry analysis (Figure b), peak A corresponds to the deposition of Al on the electrode surface. Peaks B′ and B represent the oxidation and reduction of the Mg–Al alloy. Peaks C′/C are associated with the Mg redox couple, and peak D reflects the formation of the Al–La intermetallic phase. Figure c shows a macroscopic view of the Mg–Al–La alloy sample obtained by constant-current electrolysis at 800 °C. XRD analysis (Figure d) identified the primary phases as Al11La3, β-Al12Mg17, and α-Mg. SEM imaging combined with EDS elemental mapping (Figure e) further corroborated the uniform distribution of alloying elements, confirming the successful synthesis of a homogeneous Mg–Al–La alloy.

In the preparation of Mg alloys, heat treatment serves as a critical postprocessing step that profoundly influences microstructural evolution and enables precise tailoring of mechanical properties. Through careful control of heat treatment parameters, such as temperature, duration, and cooling rate, it is possible to regulate phase distribution, refine grain size, and manipulate the precipitation or dissolution of secondary phases. Consequently, the strength, ductility, and corrosion resistance of the alloy can be significantly enhanced.

Kurz et al. investigated the microstructural evolution of ZMX210 alloy during heat treatment. The EBSD orientation map (Figure f) of the sheet material heat-treated at 375 °C reveals that nearly complete recrystallization occurs after 30 min, resulting in significant grain refinement and a more uniform morphological distribution. Grain Orientation Spread (GOS) analysis (Figure g,h) further demonstrates a substantial increase in the proportion of recrystallized grains (GOS < 1°) alongside a marked reduction in deformed grains (GOS > 1°). These results confirm that the applied heat treatment effectively promotes recrystallization and mitigates internal stresses, thereby contributing to enhanced plasticity and toughness of the alloy.

Ti Alloys

Titanium alloys exhibit high specific strength, excellent high-temperature resistance, and outstanding corrosion resistance, but their high chemical reactivity poses significant challenges for conventional preparation routes, often resulting in high costs and impurity contamination. Molten salt electrolysis addresses these issues by enabling the coreduction of multiple metal ions in an oxygen-free or low-oxygen environment, thereby effectively preventing high-temperature oxidation and contamination. This approach offers a promising technical pathway for the controlled preparation and microstructural engineering of high-performance Ti alloys. ,

Zhu et al. proposed a novel electrolytic method for preparing Ti–Al–V alloys by introducing Ti2O3 and V2O3 in the KCl-LiCl-MgCl2–AlCl3 system, and the electrolysis principle is schematically illustrated in Figure a. CV results (Figure b) indicate a stepwise reduction mechanism: V2+→V, Ti2+→Ti,Ti2+/V2+/Al3+→Ti–Al–V alloy. XRD, SEM, and EDS (Figure c,d) show that when using an Al anode, the addition of Ti2O3 and V2O3 leads to the formation of fishbone-like Al3Ti, Al3Ti0.8V0.2, and Al3Ti0.666V0.333 alloys, along with densely packed metallic Al particles. The resulting alloy exhibits a uniformly distributed multiphase microstructure.

Ding et al. systematically investigated the influence of heat treatment on the microstructure of Ti–Al–V alloys. Figure e shows the macroscopic view of the as-prepared Ti–Al–V alloy sample prior to heat treatment. As shown by the inverse pole figure (IPF) in Figure f, annealing at 700 °C resulted in significant grain refinement, further demonstrating an increased grain orientation spread in the heat-treated sample. This indicates a more complex boundary configuration and improved crystallographic uniformity. According to the pole figure (Figure g), the maximum texture intensity decreased from 38.82 in the as-prepared state to 29.39 after annealing, confirming that the heat treatment effectively weakened the texture strength and promoted a more random grain orientation distribution. This homogenization of microstructure contributes to enhanced mechanical performance, particularly in terms of ductility and fracture resistance.

Electrolytic Preparation and Deposition of Alloys in Ionic Liquids

Ionic liquid electrolysis has been increasingly reported as an effective route for alloy preparation by employing highly conductive ionic liquids as electrolytes for the electrochemical reduction of metal ions. Compared with conventional aqueous or organic solvents, ionic liquid electrolytes exhibit superior thermal stability and wider electrochemical windows. These properties extend the deposition potential range and enable the synthesis of complex multicomponent alloy systems, including Zn-, Al-, Ti-, W-, and Cu-based alloys. , According to existing studies, these characteristics contribute to reduced deposition overpotentials and improved deposition efficiency. They allow precise regulation of alloy composition and microstructural features through controlled electrochemical parameters. , The tunability of ionic liquid chemistry further provides flexibility in tailoring metal–ion coordination environments, which has been shown to influence nucleation behavior and growth morphology during alloy deposition. Overall, the literature indicates that ionic liquid electrolysis occupies an intermediate position between aqueous and molten salt electrolysis, combining relatively mild operating conditions with an expanded electrochemical window. Representative examples reported for various alloy systems illustrate both the potential and current limitations of this approach, which are discussed in comparison with aqueous and molten salt electrolysis in the following sections.

Zn Alloys

Li et al. successfully fabricated Zn–Ni alloy coatings with tunable composition using a modified DES-water hybrid electrolyte, as illustrated in Figure a. The electrodeposition was performed in a three-electrode system with a ChCl-urea-based DES, where an external potential drove the coreduction of Zn2+ and Ni2+ onto a carbon steel substrate. The Zn–Ni codeposition was effectively controlled within a selected potential window, yielding alloy coatings with homogeneous composition.CV tests revealed two distinct reduction peaks corresponding to Ni2+ (c1) and Zn2+ (c2), as shown in Figure b. With increasing water content, the reduction potentials of both metals shifted positively, and the reduction peak currents increased. , XRD analysis (Figure c) indicates that the Zn–Ni alloy deposited from electrolytes with 0 and 1 wt % water is primarily composed of the α phase. At 3 wt % water, a mixed α + γ phase structure appears, while a single γ phase forms at 5 wt % water or higher. SEM imaging (Figure d) of the coating electrodeposited at −1.2 V with 5 wt % water reveals a fine-grained, cluster-like morphology. These results confirm that this DES-based system can effectively produce well-adhered Zn–Ni alloy coatings with clearly defined structural characteristics.

Heidarzadeh et al. investigated the microstructural characteristics of Zn alloys. After a nonequilibrium heat treatment at 810 °C for 1 h, the material formed a dual-phase structure of α + β. The IPF orientation map (Figure e) shows that the β phase preferentially nucleates and grows along the primary α grain boundaries, forming coarse β colonies, while exhibiting a finer dispersion within the α grain interiors. This morphology reflects significant spatial dependence in phase precipitation behavior. The pole figure analysis (Figure f,g) further reveals a distinct orientation relationship (OR) between the α and β phases in the primary α grain boundary region:{111} FFC// {110} BCC and ⟨110⟩⟨111⟩ FFC// BCC, indicating that the interfacial β phase nucleates and grows following a specific crystallographic matching mechanism. This interface-driven OR not only governs phase distribution but also directly influences the alloy’s deformation compatibility and anisotropic mechanical behavior. These findings provide an important theoretical basis for understanding plastic deformation modes, crack propagation tendencies, and mechanical stability in Zn alloys under practical service conditions.

Al–Ti Alloys

Xu et al. successfully fabricated Al–Ti alloy coatings using a 1-butyl-3-methylimidazolium chloride (AlCl3–BMIC) ionic liquid electrolyte, with the corresponding electrolysis mechanism depicted in Figure a. The CV results in Figure b indicate that the reduction peaks A and B, along with their corresponding oxidation peaks A′ and B′, are attributed to the Ti (IV)/Ti (III) and Ti (III)/Ti (II) redox couples, respectively. Peak C corresponds to the deposition of Al, while peak D represents the codeposition of the Al–Ti alloy. − XRD analysis was performed on deposits containing 5.3 to 11.4 atom % Ti (Figure c), revealing that all diffraction peaks correspond to a face-centered cubic (fcc) structure, confirming the formation of a single-phase Al–Ti alloy. The SEM image in Figure d illustrates the surface morphology of the Al–Ti coating electrodeposited on a low-carbon steel substrate, showing a smooth and dense microstructure free of pores or cracks. These results demonstrate that the ionic liquid electrolysis system enables the preparation of structurally coherent and highly adherent alloy coatings.

4D Visualization of Electrodeposition and Nonuniformity

During the electrolysis process, the migration, discharge, and nucleation of ions at the electrode interface collectively govern the microstructure and macroscopic properties of the resulting deposits. Differences in solvation structures, ionic transport characteristics, and interfacial reaction kinetics among various electrolytes lead to distinct evolution of the local electrochemical environment during metal electrodeposition. Such spatiotemporal nonuniformity significantly influences the density, crystallinity, and mechanical stability of the deposited materials. With the rapid advancement of in situ characterization and multidimensional imaging techniques, four-dimensional (4D) visualization methods have emerged, enabling simultaneous resolution of the temporal, spatial, structural, and compositional dynamics during electrodeposition. These approaches open new avenues for revealing the mechanism of multiscale nonuniformity formation, regulating growth kinetics, and optimizing the structural properties of electrode materials.

Based on the insights gained from 4D characterization, a conceptual framework for evaluating electrolytic cell design quality can be established. Key criteria for this evaluation include (i) the uniformity of current distribution, which can be visually monitored through 4D data to assess the spatial stability of deposition; (ii) the temporal evolution of microstructural features such as dendrite growth and phase transformations, which are directly influenced by electrochemical conditions and electrode geometry; and (iii) the stability of the deposition front, as shown by 4D tracking of morphological changes during long-term operation. By correlating these real-time, dynamic insights with the electrochemical environment, 4D data provide valuable feedback for optimizing electrode design, electrolyte composition, and process parameters. This framework helps to refine cell configurations for enhanced deposition uniformity, process stability, and ultimately the mechanical performance of the deposited alloys.

Conclusions and Prospects

Electrolytic alloy preparation has become a versatile method for tailoring alloy composition and microstructure under controlled conditions. This review summarizes the key characteristics of aqueous, molten salt, and ionic liquid electrolysis, focusing on the applicable element systems, electrochemical features, and typical microstructures for each approach. Postelectrolytic heat treatment plays a critical role in bridging as-deposited microstructures with desired mechanical and functional properties, highlighting the integrated nature of electrochemical processing and microstructural control.

A comparative analysis of microstructures produced by different electrolytic systems reveals distinct recrystallization behaviors. Alloys from aqueous electrolytes typically have fine-grained or nanocrystalline structures with high defect densities, promoting rapid recrystallization and grain growth during heat treatment. In contrast, molten salt electrolysis often results in coarser grains with stronger crystallographic textures, leading to recrystallization controlled by texture inheritance. Ionic liquid electrolysis, conducted at moderate temperatures, yields highly oriented or metastable deposits, with recrystallization pathways sensitive to both texture and compositional homogeneity. These differences emphasize the need to tailor heat treatment strategies based on the deposition method to optimize alloy performance.

Emerging 4D characterization techniques provide complementary insights into electrolytic alloy deposition. While not the primary focus of this review, 4D techniques offer time-resolved observations of microstructural evolution under electrochemical conditions, aiding in the identification of nonuniform deposition and mass transport limitations. These insights support the optimization of operating conditions and electrolytic cell design, enhancing mechanistic understanding rather than serving as a standalone research focus.

Despite significant progress, several key challenges must be addressed to enable the broader adoption of electrolytic alloy production. The modeling and prediction of multicomponent electrodeposition behavior are hindered by the complex interactions between electrochemical kinetics, mass transport, and phase formation. Additionally, scaling up electrolytic processes presents challenges such as maintaining compositional uniformity, ensuring electrode and electrolyte stability, and integrating these processes into industrial manufacturing. Environmental and economic factors, including energy efficiency, electrolyte sustainability, and process cost, must also be considered to assess the competitiveness of electrolytic alloy production.

The future of electrolytic alloy preparation will depend on advances in electrolyte design, process optimization, and multiscale modeling, supported by characterization tools like 4D techniques. Integrating mechanistic understanding, predictive modeling, and engineering considerations will be key to translating laboratory-scale methods into scalable, economically viable, and environmentally sustainable alloy manufacturing technologies.

Challenges for health workforce in the Iranian healthcare system: a scoping review
Authors: Rahim Khodayari-Zarnaq, Shabnam Ghasemyani, Khorshid Mobasseri, Maryam Naghshi, Neda Kabiri
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHuman resources are a vital component for addressing current and emerging healthcare needs and strengthening health systems. This scoping review aimed to map the challenges facing the healthcare workforce in Iran.MethodsThis review followed the JBI methodology for scoping reviews. The following databases were searched: MEDLINE, Embase, PubMed, CINAHL, PsycInfo, Scopus, ISI Web of Science, and the Cochrane Library. Two independent reviewers selected studies, extracted data, and conducted a qualitative content analysis using a deductive process to generate codes, which were grouped into categories. The search was conducted on May 2024.ResultsThe literature search yielded 1,418 articles. Title and abstract screening reduced this number to 92, with 34 articles meeting the inclusion/exclusion criteria. Through qualitative analysis, challenges in Iran’s healthcare workforce were categorized into three levels: 1) macro-level (societal and system-wide context), 2) meso-level (organizational context), and 3) micro-level (individual context).ConclusionsThis scoping review summarizes challenges faced by the healthcare workforce in Iran across macro-, meso-, and micro-level contexts. The findings can inform healthcare policymakers in designing targeted and effective interventions to strengthen human resources programs. This evidence may guide strategic planning, resource allocation, and reforms to enhance Iran’s healthcare system.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12913-025-13167-w.

Introduction

The complex epidemiological, demographic, and societal challenges in today’s global environment—particularly in health-related crises like the COVID-19 pandemic—highlight the urgent need for responsive public health systems across nations [1]. As health is a fundamental right that all individuals should have universal access to, advancing health systems has become a global priority [2]. To enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of healthcare services and ensure equitable, high-quality service delivery, countries must continuously monitor, evaluate, and reform their health systems [3]. On the other hand, health systems vary in context, ranging from public and private healthcare providers to the types of services offered, such as primary healthcare, acute care, chronic care, and aged care [4].

Human resources are a vital component for strengthening health systems to meet current and emerging healthcare needs [5]. The global impact of the COVID-19 pandemic underscored staffing shortages as the leading cause of disruptions to essential health services worldwide [6]. The global shortage of skilled healthcare professionals remains a critical barrier to achieving health equity and universal health coverage [7]. As defined by Lopes, workforce shortage refers to ‘not having the right number of people with the right skills in the right place at the right time, to provide the right services to the right people’ [8]. A 2013 World Health Organization report projected a shortage of 12.9 million skilled health professionals—including physicians, nurses, and midwives—by 2035 [9]. This shortage stems from imbalances between supply and demand. On the supply side, contributing factors include low enrollment in health-related academic programs, inadequate workforce planning, staff migration, burnout, aging populations, and early retirement. On the demand side, rising needs driven by advances in biomedicine, societal changes, and evolving healthcare priorities exacerbate the gap [10].

In Iran, there was approximately 1 healthcare worker per 118 people in 2015, with the majority employed in the public sector. The country’s healthcare workforce density includes 1.14 physicians, 0.33 dental practitioners, and 1.87 nurses and midwives per 1,000 people [11]. Nursing shortages in Iran have been a persistent, multifaceted challenge for decades, driven by reluctance to enter or remain in the profession, high attrition rates, inadequate salaries, low social status, and negative public perceptions of nursing [12]. Since the COVID-19 outbreak, burnout among frontline healthcare workers has escalated, significantly accelerating workforce emigration [13]. By the end of the pandemic’s second wave in Iran, over 3,000 physicians had emigrated to high-income countries such as Germany, Italy, and Canada. Similarly, nurse emigration surged by 300% during the pandemic [14].

Despite the pivotal role of the healthcare workforce in the health sector, the challenges they face in Iran remain inadequately understood and underexplored. Given the broad and exploratory nature of our research question, alongside the goal of mapping challenges facing Iran’s health workforce, a scoping review emerged as the most appropriate methodological approach. This scoping review aimed to map the current challenges facing the health human workforce in Iran. It provides valuable insights for healthcare policymakers seeking to implement effective human resources programs.

Review question

What are the challenges in the health human workforce within the Iranian health sector as described in the literature?

Methods

This review was conducted in accordance with the JBI methodology for scoping reviews [15, 16] and followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses extension for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA-ScR) [15].

Based on the JBI methodology for conducting scoping reviews, we formulated our research question using the PCC framework, which stands for Population, Content, and Context.

We considered reports employing a variety of study designs, including experimental, quasi-experimental, analytical observational, descriptive studies, case reports, qualitative research, opinion papers, editorials, and commentaries. Abstracts were also included, and authors were contacted to verify the availability of full texts when necessary. Studies were excluded if they exclusively addressed clinical outcomes not related to workforce issues or lacked sufficient information to extract relevant data. Both English and Persian language studies were included.

Search strategy

A preliminary limited search of MEDLINE was conducted to develop a comprehensive search strategy. Both free-text terms and controlled vocabularies were employed across the included databases. The reference lists of all included studies were screened to identify any additional relevant research. The following databases were searched: MEDLINE (via Ovid), Embase, PubMed, CINAHL (via EBSCO), PsycInfo (via EBSCO), Scopus, ISI Web of Knowledge, and the Cochrane Library. Additionally, Google Scholar was searched to identify any other pertinent studies. The search was conducted without any time limitations in May 2024. The PubMed search strategy is as follows:

Study selection

After compiling all identified citations in EndNote 20.4 (Clarivate Analytics, PA, USA), duplicates were removed. Two independent reviewers screened the titles and abstracts against the inclusion criteria. Studies meeting these criteria were retrieved in full and assessed in detail. Any disagreements were resolved through discussion, or, when necessary, a third reviewer independently appraised the conflicting papers.

Data extraction

Data extraction was performed independently by two reviewers using a Microsoft Excel spreadsheet specifically developed for this review. Following the JBI methodology for scoping reviews, the extraction focused on basic descriptive data such as authors, citation, title, year, type of evidence source (e.g., primary research, evidence synthesis, guideline, policy document), study design, context, and setting. Key data related to challenges, including the policymaking level, specific challenges or emerging trends, and aspects of these challenges, were also extracted. The extraction tool was pilot-tested, with two independent reviewers extracting data from three studies, followed by a consensus discussion. No modifications to the tool were deemed necessary.

Data analysis and presentation

A qualitative content analysis, utilizing a deductive process, was employed in this scoping review to develop codes that were subsequently grouped into categories, facilitating the identification of consensus across the data and synthesizing the results. Two independent reviewers thoroughly read and re-read all extracted data, coding the information according to the established categories. The challenges were categorized at three levels: 1) macro, societal and system-wide context 2) meso, organizational context, and 3) micro, individual context. This multi-level approach aligns with recent research by Figueroa et al., which used a similar framework to illustrate priorities and challenges for health leadership and workforce management globally [4].

Results

Findings of study selection

The initial literature search yielded 1,418 articles. Following title and abstract screening, this number was reduced to 92. Ultimately, 34 articles met the inclusion criteria and were included in the review. The main reasons for exclusion after full-text review were: 1) the research did not align with the aims of the scoping review, and 2) the study lacked extractable data.

At the organizational level, workforces faced challenges including human resource management challenges [11, 17, 21, 23, 24, 26, 28, 30, 38]; changes in organizational structures and performance metrics [16, 18, 21, 22, 24, 32, 34, 36, 38–41]; and intensification of work among front-line and middle health workforce [21, 24, 28, 32, 33, 38, 42–45].

In Iran, healthcare workforce are affected by challenges at the system and organizational level. Discussion

This scoping review highlighted the challenges that human workforce in the health system of Iran face with. Challenges were categorized in three levels of Macro-Meso-Micro. Healthcare services in Iran are provided across a range of public and private health sector actors delivering primary, secondary and tertiary services and human workforce education and training.

At the macro level, systemic issues such as workforce burnout, exacerbated by high workloads, unfair financial support, and moral distress, dominate Iran’s healthcare landscape [50, 51]. The COVID-19 pandemic amplified these stressors, with studies reporting elevated rates of anxiety, depression, and distress among healthcare workers [52]. Globally, burnout negatively impacts patient outcomes [50], and Iran’s health system is no exception.

Structural imbalances, including suboptimal nurse-to-patient ratios, persist due to workforce shortages and cultural barriers [53]. For instance, patient preferences for same–gender nurses complicate staffing logistics [53], while staff migration driven by inadequate salaries further depletes the workforce [54–58]. Macro-level interventions, such as revising nurse-to-patient ratios, improving work-life balance through flexible shifts [59], and reforming financial incentives, are critical to retaining skilled professionals.

At the organizational level, ineffective performance evaluation systems and misaligned payment models are critical barriers. Hamilton et al. [60] emphasize that no single method suffices for assessing clinical performance, necessitating multifaceted approaches. Value-based payments and pay-for-performance schemes show promise in reducing disparities [61, 62], but their efficacy depends on contextual factors like funding levels and technical support [63]. Policymakers must balance equity and efficiency, ensuring incentives motivate high performers without marginalizing others [64].

The predominant micro-level issue is the leadership gap among physician-managers. Most Iranian hospital leaders are clinicians lacking formal management training, perpetuating role ambiguity and burnout [65]. Das and Kelly’s systematic review [66] corroborates this, identifying identity crises and credibility loss among physician-managers globally. Integrating management education into medical training could bridge this gap.

At all levels, the interplay between systemic, organizational, and individual factors underscores the complexity of addressing workforce challenges in Iran’s health system. A multilevel approach is essential to tackle these issues comprehensively. Macro-level reforms must prioritize equitable resource distribution, robust workforce planning, and policies to retain skilled professionals [67]. At the organizational level, adopting innovative performance assessment tools and aligned payment models can enhance efficiency and equity [68]. Finally, addressing the leadership gap requires systemic investments in management training for healthcare professionals, fostering a cadre of physician-managers equipped to navigate the demands of modern healthcare systems [69].

These interventions are particularly urgent in the post-COVID-19 era, where the resilience of healthcare systems has been tested globally [70]. Strengthening Iran’s healthcare workforce will not only improve patient outcomes but also enhance the system’s capacity to respond to future crises [71]. Policymakers, educators, and healthcare leaders must collaborate to create a sustainable and supportive environment for healthcare workers, ensuring the long-term viability of Iran’s health system [72].

This scoping review has several limitations. First, although we conducted a comprehensive search across multiple databases, some relevant studies might have been missed. Also, due to the nature of scoping review, this study did not assess the quality of included articles, which may affect the strength of the conclusions drawn.

Conclusion

In this scoping review, we categorized the challenges faced by the human workforce in Iran’s healthcare system into three levels: Macro, Meso, and Micro. Iran’s healthcare services are delivered through a diverse mix of public and private providers across primary, secondary, and tertiary levels, with ongoing education and training for health professionals. Macro-level challenges mainly revolve around high workload and burnout among health workers, despite the presence of many skilled professionals. These issues are influenced largely by health policies affecting the entire system. Addressing workload and burnout should be a priority for policymakers, as these are the most significant macro-level challenges impacting workforce sustainability. Meso-level challenges include the need to improve performance evaluation systems and develop payment structures linked to staff performance. These are critical areas where policy interventions can have a meaningful impact to enhance workforce motivation and efficiency. Micro-level challenges identified in this review relate to the changing role of hospital managers. Notably, many hospital managers are physicians who often lack a holistic view of the health system, which can hinder effective leadership and operational effectiveness at the individual level.

Policy implications and future research

The findings of this review can support healthcare policymakers in designing targeted, effective human resource policies, resource allocation, and reforms to strengthen Iran’s health workforce. Future research should explore the root causes of workforce challenges at each level—macro, meso, and micro—and evaluate the long-term impacts of policies through longitudinal and intervention studies.

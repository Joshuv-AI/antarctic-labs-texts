Ten-year follow-up outcomes of resected stage II non-small cell lung cancer patients
Authors: Wenrui Sun, Di Li, Yueren Yan, Han Han, Jinsong Bai, Zelin Ma, Fangqiu Fu, Yang Zhang, Haiquan Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundStage II non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) comprises a diverse group of cancers with varying characteristics. This study aims to report the long-term outcomes of stage II NSCLC and gain insights into the potential benefits of adjuvant chemotherapy.MethodsLung cancer patients who underwent surgery between November 2007 and December 2013 were reviewed. Clinicopathological characteristics were collected retrospectively. Post-operative survival and recurrence status were analyzed.ResultsA total of 416 patients with stage II NSCLC were enrolled. The median follow-up period after surgery was 116 months. The overall survival rate at 5 and 10 years was 55.0%and 40.7%, respectively, while the recurrence-free survival rate at 5 and 10 years were 53.9% and 49.0%, respectively. Patients with stage II NSCLC receiving adjuvant chemotherapy had improved survival compared to those not receiving (log-rank P<0.001). Further subgroup analysis confirmed that the survival benefit from adjuvant chemotherapy was limited in IIB-N1/2a. For sub-group patients with high-risk factors (HRFs) in IIA or IIB-N0, adjuvant chemotherapy would improve survival as well.ConclusionsOur study presents long-term follow-up results for patients with stage II NSCLC, and our findings suggest that adjuvant chemotherapy is recommended for patients with stage IIA or IIB-N0 NSCLC who have HRFs, as well as for patients with stage IIB-N1/2a NSCLC.

Introduction

Lung cancer is a critical global health concern and was responsible for over 1.8 million deaths worldwide in 2022 (1). The major histological type of lung cancer is non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC).

According to the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines, surgical resection is considered the standard treatment strategy for stage II NSCLC. The 5-year overall survival (OS) rate ranges from 52% to 61% for stage IIA, and from 43% and 47% for stage IIB (2-5) under curative surgical resection. Patients with stage II NSCLC are still at significant risk of death even after curative surgical resection (6). To improve the prognosis of these patients, adjuvant chemotherapy has been administered during the postoperative period (7-9). The use of adjuvant chemotherapy for NSCLC remains a contentious topic. Several clinical trials and meta-analyses were conducted to have investigated the benefit of adjuvant chemotherapy after surgical resection for NSCLC (10-21), with some demonstrating improved survival with adjuvant chemotherapy and others not. Several high-risk histopathologic features, such as lymphovascular invasion (LVI), visceral pleural invasion (VPI), and high-grade histological findings, have been proposed as potential indications for adjuvant chemotherapy in NSCLC patients (22). However, there is still little evidence supporting these recommendations.

In this study, we analyzed our long-term follow-up results to elucidate the survival benefit of adjuvant chemotherapy in various heterogeneous groups with stage II NSCLC. Furthermore, we comprehensively analyzed the definite role of adjuvant chemotherapy in this patient population. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2024-2197/rc).

Methods

Patients

Lung cancer patients who underwent surgery at the Department of Thoracic Surgery, Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center between November 2007 and December 2013 were reviewed. Clinicopathological information, including sex, age, operative procedures, histologic findings, and postoperative adjuvant chemotherapy regimens, was collected from the medical records. All pathologic slides were re-reviewed by 2 certified pathologists. Postoperative pathologic diagnosis was made according to the 9th edition of the International Association for the Study of Lung Cancer/American Thoracic Society/European Respiratory Society classification (2). Eventually, after re-evaluation, pathological stage II NSCLC patients were enrolled and analyzed.

High-risk factors (HRFs) are factors that have been associated with a poorer prognosis. In line with previous studies (22), we defined HRF as including at least one of the following factors: LVI, VPI, or poor differentiation.

Follow-up strategy

For all the patients, follow-up started from the date of surgery. Physical examination, chest computed tomography (CT) scans, ultrasonography of the abdominal/cervical/supraclavicular regions, and magnetic resonance imaging or CT scans of the brain were obtained approximately every 4 months for the first 3 years after surgery, every 6 months for 3 to 5 years, and annually from then on. Whole-body bone scans were obtained at least once per year. OS was counted from the date of surgery to death or last follow-up. Recurrence-free survival (RFS) was defined as the time from diagnosis to the date of recurrence or death or last follow-up. All patients were regularly followed up after an operation. Patients who died from other causes were considered to be censored with no event when calculating RFS. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee and Institutional Review Boards of Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center (IRB2008223-9, date: July 14, 2020), and all patients were exempt from informed consent because of the retrospective nature of this study.

Statistical analysis

Data were presented as means, medians, or counts and percentages, as appropriate. Characteristic differences were compared by Student t-test, the Mann-Whitney U test, or Pearson’s Chi-squared test, as appropriate. Kaplan-Meier method was used to estimate OS and RFS, with a log-rank test to evaluate significant differences. Multivariate Cox regression models were used to explore independent prognostic factors. Variables with a P value less than 0.1 in univariate analysis were used in the multivariate survival analysis. All tests were bilateral. All statistical analysis was performed using IBM SPSS 26.0 (IBM-SPS Inc.), GraphPad Prism 8.0, and RStudio software version 1.3.1093 (RStudio Inc.).

Results

Patient characteristics

A total of 416 NSCLC patients were included in this study, of whom 305 (73.3%) were men, and the mean age was 60.5 years. Of the patients, 173 (41.6%) were classified as stage IIA and 243 (58.4%) were classified as stage IIB. Among them, 194 (46.6%) patients had adenocarcinoma, 183 (44.0%) patients had squamous carcinoma, and 39 (9.4%) had other pathological subtypes. In terms of treatment, 297 (71.4%) patients received adjuvant chemotherapy, while 96 (23.1%) patients did not. The clinicopathological characteristics of the 416 patients are listed in Table 1.

Long-term follow-up results of stage II NSCLC

After a median follow-up of 117 months, 200 out of the 416 included patients with stage II NSCLC had died (77 with stage IIA and 123 with stage IIB). The Kaplan-Meier survival analysis showed that the 5- and 10-year RFS rates were 53.9% and 49.0%, respectively (Figure 1A,1B). The specific recurrence situation is shown in Table S1. As for OS, the Kaplan-Meier survival analysis showed that the 5- and 10-year OS rates were 55.0% and 40.7%, respectively (Figure 1C,1D). The 5-year OS and 5-year RFS of resected stage IIA/IIB NSCLC were 62.2%/49.4%, 61.4%/47.3%, respectively (Figure 1E-1H). The 10-year OS and 10-year RFS of resected stage IIA/IIB NSCLC were 49.2%/33.7%, 55.4%/43.4%, respectively (Figure 1E-1H). According to multivariate analysis, LVI (P=0.03) and worse tumor node metastasis (TNM) classification (P=0.02) staging were significantly correlated with poorer OS, whereas video-assisted thoracic surgery (VATS) (P=0.009) was significantly correlated with a better OS compared with muscle-sparing thoracotomy (MST) (Table 2). This is likely because VATS significantly reduces surgical trauma to the body, leading to superior outcomes compared to MST in terms of postoperative pain, complication rates, hospital stay duration, functional recovery, and long-term quality of life—ultimately improving patient prognosis.

The role of adjuvant chemotherapy in survival stratified by TNM staging system

After excluding patients with missing or insufficient data on adjuvant chemotherapy status, we found that adjuvant chemotherapy was associated with better OS [hazard ratio (HR) 0.578, 95% confidence interval (CI): 0.414–0.756, P<0.001] and RFS in patients with stage II NSCLC (Figure 2A, Figure S1, Table 2). And adjuvant chemotherapy can delay the occurrence of recurrence (Figure S2A-S2C). The clinicopathological characteristics of the adjuvant and non-adjuvant groups are listed in Table S2 and there were no statistically significant differences in baseline clinicopathological characteristics between the two groups. Detailed information regarding adjuvant chemotherapy regimens, including drug combinations and number of cycles, was systematically collected for patients with adjuvant chemotherapy (Table 3).

Then the cohort was divided into two subgroups, stage IIA and IIB, according to the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) 9th edition TNM staging system. Adjuvant chemotherapy provided a significant survival (HR 0.475, 95% CI: 0.326–0.703, P<0.001) and recurrence benefit only in patients with stage IIB but not stage IIA (HR 0.724, 95% CI: 0.401–1.386, P=0.29) (Figure 2B,2C and Figure S3A).

Based on the TNM staging system, stage IIB consists of T3N0M0, T2N1M0 and T1N2aM0, so we have divided the 243 patients with stage IIB into 93 (37.8%) patients with stage IIB-N0 and 150 (62.2%) patients with stage IIB-N1/2a. No significant survival benefit was found between the two groups in stage IIB-N0 (HR 0.723, 95% CI: 0.374–1.358, P=0.21) (Figure 2D), whereas adjuvant chemotherapy significantly improved survival and recurrence (HR 0.407, 95% CI: 0.254–0.612, P<0.001) (Figure 2E and Figure S3B) in stage IIB-N1/2a.

The role of chemotherapy in stage IIA/IIB-N0 NSCLC patients with or without HRF

As mentioned above, adjuvant chemotherapy did not bring significant survival benefits in stage IIA and IIB patients with the absence of lymph node metastasis. Some analyses demonstrated that HRF was thought to be associated with the impact of adjuvant chemotherapy on survival. Hence, we performed a further analysis on correlation between adjuvant chemotherapy and HRF. The Kaplan-Meier survival analysis demonstrated adjuvant chemotherapy only significantly improved survival benefit in patients with stage IIA/IIB-N0 and at least one HRF, but not in stage IIA/IIB-N0 without HRF (Figure 3A,3B and Figure S3C). A multivariate model of overall survival was also constructed. In multivariate analysis of cases with stage IIA/IIB-N0 and at least one HRF, adjuvant chemotherapy was associated with a significant survival benefit (HR 0.414, 95% CI: 0.263–0.759, P=0.002, Table S3). Whereas, there was no evidence found that adjuvant chemotherapy was associated with a survival benefit in stage IIA/IIB-N0 without HRF (HR 3.384, 95% CI: 0.977–11.497, P=0.059, Table S4).

Discussion

In order to improve the prognosis of patients with stage II NSCLC and reduce the risk of postoperative death, doctors often consider postoperative adjuvant chemotherapy. However, the specific use of adjuvant chemotherapy is still controversial. We elucidated the role of adjuvant chemotherapy in patients with stage II NSCLC stratified by stage, lymph node zones with metastases and high-risk clinicopathological features based on long-term follow-up results.

The relationship between the effectiveness of adjuvant chemotherapy and several attributes of the individual tumor has been investigated previously (23,24). A randomized controlled trial suggested that the adjusted risk of death was significantly reduced in patients treated with adjuvant chemotherapy compared with controls for stage IB–IIIA NSCLC (11). Besides, a study conducted by Ramsden et al. (25) noted that adjuvant chemotherapy was associated with a significant survival benefit in stage II NSCLC and improved the median overall survival from 23.3 months in the operation group to 65.1 months in the adjuvant chemotherapy group. Subsequently, Byung et al. demonstrated similar findings in the cohort with completely resected stage II-N1 lung adenocarcinoma (26). In line with the above analysis, we obtained similar results in our cohort. We found that adjuvant chemotherapy was only associated with a significant survival benefit in stage II NSCLC and stage IIB-N1/2a NSCLC, but not in stage IIA/IIB-N0.

Previous studies have elucidated tumor size as a significant predictor of adjuvant chemotherapy efficacy in patients with node-negative NSCLC. In a previous population-based study from the National Cancer Database (NCDB), adjuvant chemotherapy was associated with a survival benefit in all patients with tumors over 3 cm, including tumors between 3.1 and 3.9 cm (27). Whereas, the unplanned subset analysis of CALGB 9633 demonstrated a survival benefit associated with adjuvant chemotherapy in patients with node-negative tumors of at least 4 cm (13). However, the above studies did not consider other characteristics, such as high-risk clinicopathological features, which may also be associated with the findings. A retrospective study of NCDB suggested that survival benefit was associated with adjuvant chemotherapy only in patients with 4–5 cm tumors with at least one HRF or tumors above 5 cm (22). In comparison with the above findings, our conclusions in stage IIA/IIB-N0 NSCLC are similar and different. In our cohort of patients with stage IIA/IIB-N0 NSCLC, an advantage for survival was associated with adjuvant chemotherapy only in patients with at least one high-risk feature, but not found in whole patients with stage IIA/IIB-N0 NSCLC or patients without high-risk features. Differences in results between previous studies and this study may be due to differences in study period, study population enrolled, type of adjuvant chemotherapeutic agent used, and the duration of follow-up. However, our evidence supports a more accurate indication of adjuvant chemotherapy, which is restricted to patients with at least one HRF (which included only 68.7% of the tumors in this size category in our cohort). We believe that this refinement is not trivial because many patients may be able to avoid the potential toxic, undesirable and negative effects of adjuvant chemotherapy if it is not associated with a survival benefit.

Importantly, we demonstrated long-term follow-up results of stage II NSCLC. It is essential to evaluate the long-term follow-up of patients in these kinds of trials, whether chemotherapy is associated with a sustained survival benefit and to identify any late toxicities that may be attributable to adjuvant chemotherapy.

There are still a few limitations in this retrospective study. The data on adjuvant chemotherapy for a small number of patients were not available, because they did not receive adjuvant chemotherapy at our hospital or were lost. Secondly, this is a retrospective study, so whether to conduct adjuvant therapy has a certain bias. The oncologist may choose whether to administer adjuvant therapy based on the patient's underlying condition. Although this study included some confounding data available to us, there may be other factors affecting the results of this study.

Conclusions

We reported the long-term follow-up results of stage II NSCLC. The 10-year OS and 10-year RFS of resected stage II/IIA/IIB NSCLC were 40.5%/49.2%, 48.5%/55.3%, and 33.7%/43.8%, respectively. Moreover, adjuvant chemotherapy is recommended for stage IIA/IIB-N0 patients with at least one HRF and stage IIB-N1/2a patients.

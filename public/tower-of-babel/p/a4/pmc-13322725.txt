Characteristics of the top 100 most cited prepsoas (anterior to psoas/oblique lumbar interbody fusion) lumbar interbody fusion articles: A bibliometric study of the literature
Authors: Rehan R. Khan, Rohith Ryali, Brian S. Tao, Daman P. Dhunna, Margaret Li, Nader El-Hajj, Matthew T. Kim, Jude Abiad, Tony Tannoury, Chadi Tannoury
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTPurpose:This study sought to characterize the most influential literature on prepsoas approaches to fusions, anterior to psoas (ATP) and oblique lumbar interbody fusion (OLIF), and identify the global research trends, citation patterns, and article levels of evidence for these two popular minimally invasive approaches.Methods:The Clarivate Web of Science databases were searched for phrasings for ATP and OLIF fusions. The 100 most-cited articles were screened for inclusion, and study characteristics were extracted, including citation count, country of origin, journal, year of publication, and level of evidence (LOE). Citation density and citations per year (CPY) were calculated. Network analyses were conducted on author keywords. Correlation of LOE and citation metrics was assessed with Spearman analysis, and multivariable regression was performed to identify predictors of citation impact.Results:The top 100 prepsoas articles had 6323 citations (mean 63.0 ± 73.0, range 18–494) with a mean CPY of 10.2 ± 7.5. Most were retrospective cohorts (n = 35). Their classification was level III (n = 42), level IV (n = 26), or level V (n = 16), with only a single randomized controlled trial. There was no significant relationship between LOE and the number of citations (ρ =0.024, P = 0.823). China (n = 45) was the most commonly represented country, followed by the United States (n = 17) and South Korea (n = 13).Conclusion:Despite their rapid global adoption, higher-level evidence prepsoas literature remains very limited, showing the need for prospective trials and systematic reviews to further validate their safety and efficacy.

INTRODUCTION

With the advances of minimally invasive surgical (MIS) approaches to the lumbar spine, lumbar interbody fusions (LIF) have undergone significant adoption over the last decade. These MIS approaches have expanded the surgical armamentarium for managing a wide range of spinal pathologies, including complex deformity correction, with targeted access to anterior LIF (ALIF) and lateral transpsoas fusions (LLIF/XLIF).[1234]

Similarly, prepsoas MIS techniques, including the anterior to psoas (ATP) and oblique LIF (OLIF), have gained prominence for combining the benefits of anterior and lateral exposures while mitigating the risk of psoas injury, lumbar plexus injury, and the resulting anterior thigh pain.[567] The ATP approach employs an oblique retroperitoneal corridor, typically through a single small anterolateral abdominal incision, allowing generous access to the spine between T12-S1.[78] By contrast, OLIF emphasizes utilization of the native corridor with minimal vessel manipulation, whereas ATP involves deliberate but controlled vascular mobilization under direct visualization, creating a wider and more expandable window at challenging levels such as L4-S1.[8] These technical nuances enable the placement of large-footprint cages, anterior column release, and multilevel fusion with reliable vascular control, while maintaining the MIS advantages of reduced morbidity.

Despite the rapid adoption of MIS-ATP and OLIF, no bibliometric study has systematically evaluated their global research landscape. Bibliometric analysis, a quantitative approach to assessing citation patterns, publication trends, and collaborative networks, offers a valuable lens to identify influential studies, map international contributions, and highlight emerging themes within a field. By analyzing the 100 most-cited articles on ATP and OLIF fusion, this study aims to characterize worldwide research trends, highlight landmark contributions, and trace the evolution of these two very popular prepsoas approaches within the broader spine literature.

METHODS

Search strategy and study selection

To obtain articles for study analysis, we queried all databases in the Clarivate Web of Science (WoS) on September 17, 2025, including the WoS core collection, Arabic Citation Index, Book Citation Index, Chinese Science Citation Database, Russian Science Citation Index, and Scientific Electronic Library Online. To improve the sensitivity of queries, no filters were utilized, and all publication dates were considered for study inclusion.

Using the Boolean search formula: “anterior to psoas” OR “MIS-ATP” OR “antepsoas” OR “antepsoas oblique” OR “anterior oblique” OR “prepsoas” OR “OLIF” AND “fusion,” our query output returned 300 articles. Articles were then screened by two reviewers (R.R.K. and B.S.T) to identify the 100 most-cited articles satisfying the inclusion criteria of retaining a primary focus on minimally invasive LIF, using either OLIF or the ATP approach to spinal fusions. WoS results that were abstracts without an accompanying full-text article were excluded.

Data extraction of article characteristics

Study characteristics for the 100 most-cited articles were collected, including: author names, country of origin, journal name and impact factor, publication year, total number of citations, and level of evidence (LOE). The country of origin was determined by the location of the institutional affiliation of the first author, and official Clarivate reports were used to source the most recently available impact factor of publication journals. When no consensus was reliably reached on the inclusion or exclusion of an article or regarding the information extracted for articles, final decisions were made at the discretion of the senior author.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using Python 3.10.0, with figure plots generated using the matplot and plotly libraries and network analyses performed using the network package. The correlation of the study’s LOE and the number of citations received was assessed using Spearman’s coefficient analysis. If the LOE was not explicitly reported by the journal of publication, the 2015 Journal of Bone and Joint Surgery guidelines were utilized to assign LOE.[9] Multivariable linear regression, adjusted for study design and LOE, was subsequently used to identify relationships between journal impact factors on the total number of citations received by articles per year. RESULTS

The top 100 prepsoas (ATP/OLIF) articles accrued 6323 citations in total, averaging 63.0 ± 73.0 citations/article (range, 18–494). Citation rate averaged 10.2 ± 7.5 CPY (range, 1.8–39.4). The top six prepsoas articles consisted of over 200 citations.

Characteristics of top ten most cited articles

The top cited article (n = 494) was by Silvestre et al. (2012, Asian Spine Journal), which was a retrospective study analyzing 179 patients who had the OLIF procedure. This study established OLIF as a reproducible, minimally invasive retroperitoneal approach with reduced morbidity compared to traditional anterior techniques. The second most-cited study (n = 315) was by Woods et al. (2017, The Spine Journal), which provided the first detailed description of OLIF25 and OLIF51 in 137 patients across 340 operated levels. It reported a 97.9% fusion rate and an overall complication rate of 11.7%. The third most-cited article (n = 292) was by Fujibayashi et al. (2015, Spine), which prospectively evaluated 28 patients undergoing OLIF with supplemental posterior fixation. It demonstrated significant increases in thecal sac area, disc height, and segmental angle, with a 72% clinical recovery rate and no major complications. Collectively, these three studies defined the early safety profile, technical reproducibility, and indirect decompression efficacy of prepsoas fusion techniques. DISCUSSION

MIS-ATP and OLIF have rapidly gained popularity and serve as widely accepted prepsoas approaches for lumbar fusion.[5710] Recent findings in literature indicate that both approaches are associated with the reduced incidence of intraoperative lumbar plexus injuries and postoperative neurological effects, while demonstrating strong fusion success rates and significant improvement in postoperative disability and pain scores.[81112] Although both MIS-ATP and OLIF are known to carry some risks for patients, major complications appear rare.[813] For instance, Tannoury et al. found no major vascular or direct visceral injuries in a cohort of 940 patients managed with MIS-ATP, attributed to the direct visualization of retroperitoneal vasculature afforded by the technique.[8] Furthermore, Mehren et al. observed a vascular complication rate of 0.37% amongst a cohort of 812 patients undergoing OLIF, an incidence significantly lower than the 1.7%–7.1% incidence of vascular complications with traditional ALIF approaches, as shown by Manunga et al. and Tigre et al., respectively.[131415] With their strong safety and efficacy profiles, the expanding body of literature on the MIS-ATP and OLIF techniques warrants bibliometric examination to better characterize and anticipate global research trends.

In this bibliometric analysis study, we evaluated the top 100 most-cited articles on the MIS-ATP and OLIF techniques. In assessing studies with 6323 total citations and 63.0 (±73.0) citations on average, this study provides insights into the most influential literature impacting the increasing popularity and evolution of MIS-ATP and OLIF. Compared to findings by Zhang et al., where the average citation count of the top 100 most-cited articles on the conventional fusion procedure of lateral interbody fusion (LIF) was 98.03, however, the lower citation counts of literature on MIS-ATP and OLIF suggest these techniques remain relatively understudied, partly due to the more recent development and refinement of these techniques.[16]

This current study indicates the rise in popularity of MIS-ATP and OLIF surgeries, with the strongest literature adding the newest information to the field. Among MIS-ATP and OLIF researchers, the author (first author or co-author) who contributed to the largest number of publications within the top 100 most-cited works was Dr. Jin-Sung Kim with 5 publications and 365 citations.[1718192021] Of the authors with at least 3 publications and the greatest citation to publication (CTP) ratio, Dr. Kazuhisa Takahashi was credited with 3 publications and 527 citations for a CTP of 175.67 citations.[222324] Notably, Dr. Kim’s most-cited literature demonstrated a clear literature line built upon identifying the strengths and minimal weaknesses of the OLIF procedure as compared to MIS direct lateral LIF and MIS transforaminal LIF.[1819] As one of the first to assess the impact of OLIF on minimizing muscle and nerve manipulation, blood loss, and operative times, while improving disc height restoration and fusion rates, Dr. Kim’s work focuses on complications with real-world intraoperative and postoperative significance to surgeons considering adoption of new procedures.[18] By addressing multiple perioperative and clinical outcomes within only a few foundational works, these studies offer a wide breadth of significant citable findings and keywords, further boosting the visibility and relevance of the literature. Similarly, Dr. Takahashi’s works provided early, rigorous radiographic and clinical evidence supporting the viability of mini-open OLIF, showing significant indirect decompression and symptom improvement with low complication rates.[222324] In addition, as Takahashi’s two most-cited works were published in 2015 and 2017, with CPYs of 17.5 and 28.5, respectively, suggesting that while highly-significant and foundational works, total citation counts likely reflect a longer citation accrual time window.[2324]

Furthermore, regarding the journals in which the most-cited articles were published, our study identified Asian Spine Journal, Spine, World Neurosurgery, The Spine Journal, and the Journal of Orthopaedic Surgery as the five most published journals. These findings are in line with prior bibliometric literature which reports similar journal of publication trends for analyses on lumbar spinal stenosis and minimally invasive endoscopy in spine surgery.[2526] Similarly, our study identified China, the United States, and South Korea as the top three countries of origin for most-cited literature on MIS-ATP and OLIF. These findings are consistent with previous spine bibliometric analyses by Lin et al., where they report the top three contributing nations to be China, followed by South Korea and the United States.[27] These similar findings within the field of spine surgery not only reveal the journals and regions consistently contributing to high-impact developments in literature, but also help identify primary academic hubs of innovation [Table 4], enabling the identification of potential opportunities for topic leaders to collaboratively conduct and enhance MIS-ATP and OLIF research.

Analysis of common keywords also provides insight into principal topics of interest around which most high-impact research currently clusters.[28] Strongest clustering was noted for interbody fusion, which is to be expected due to MIS-ATP and OLIF fundamentally being interbody fusion-centric topics [Figure 4]. Nonetheless, extensive focus on adjacent segment degeneration and lumbar degenerative disease indicates a general trend towards optimizing procedures to avoid adverse postoperative outcomes following lumbar fusion procedures.[2829] In addition, a focus on the anterior longitudinal ligament is indicative of a desire to optimize the sagittal alignment and stability of the spine.[3031] The detection of such trends may help identify specific areas where most critical research has been taking place, and may allow postulation that adjacent topics of current clinical debate may subsequently become new frontiers in MIS-ATP and OLIF research.

Finally, 84 of the top 100 most-cited papers were of level III (n = 42), level IV (n = 26), or level V (n = 16) evidence [Figure 5], with no significant relationship between levels of evidence and citation count (P = 0.82). While the gold standard for research has long been RCTs, systematic reviews, and meta-analyses, the relative paucity of such studies in our cohort points to larger trends in orthopedic research and literature. As discussed by Hartz and Marsh, retrospective studies are more easily conducted in orthopedics with a greater ability to study larger and more representative populations undergoing real-world experiences and interventions in clinical practice.[3233] With MIS-ATP and OLIF adoption continuing to grow, numerous widely available retrospective studies assessing complications and indications integral to surgical decision-making may presently confer greater utility than the causal benefits incurred by RCTs.

Along with these observations and trends, our study does have strengths and limitations that warrant further discussion. First, a notable strength is the most-cited article being Silvestre’s 2012 publication. While the OLIF technique was discussed by Mayer as a minimally invasive approach through which to perform spinal fusion, Silvestre is credited with officially coining, developing, and popularizing the technique.[10] The inclusion of this foundational paper, which is cited by almost all publications describing OLIF, serves as a reliable benchmark for literature focusing on the OLIF procedure. Nonetheless, as Mayer’s landmark 1997 paper did not coin the terms “MIS-ATP” or “OLIF,” this sentinel paper, which is strongly cited within OLIF and MIS-ATP-focused literature, was not included in the query return.[34] However, while this exclusion may similarly fail to capture earlier papers that did not use now widely-accepted procedural names and terminology, our utilization of multiple phrasings of “antepsoas,” “antepsoas oblique,” “anterior oblique,” and “anterior to psoas,” and “prepsoas” strives to capture the majority of commonly-used phrasings beyond the names of the procedures (ATP and OLIF) alone. An additional limitation of this study is related to the methodological reliance on a single bibliographic database, WoS. While Scopus, Google Scholar, and other platforms maintain their own citation indexing systems, there are many inherent discrepancies among their reported citation counts due to their respective journal coverages and resource inclusion. Thus, despite some inherent limitations, we elected to use WoS due to its breadth of indexed publications and integration of many databases along with the WoS Core Collection. Furthermore, a fundamental shortcoming of traditional citation count analyses is that they overvalue the contribution of earlier-published works to the overall literature. In order to partially adjust for this, we used the CPY metric as a secondary tool for ranking the speed at which authors accrued citations, providing a lens into the true contribution of a paper to the field. A similar limitation, as discussed by Rosenblatt et al., points to the potential for a “snowball effect,” whereby PubMed and other database search algorithms primarily highlight frequently-cited articles causing certain papers to accrue considerably more citations, leading to self-selection biases and confounding the true contribution of individual papers to the field.[35] Finally, an inherent limitation of many research databases is the bias towards favoring English-language publications, potentially limiting and underrepresenting the global knowledge of MIS-ATP and OLIF. Thus, while we utilized the WoS Arabic Citation Index, Chinese Science Citation Database, and Russian Science Citation Index, it remains possible that current understandings of MIS-ATP and OLIF are not fully represented.

CONCLUSION

This bibliometric study on the top 100 most-cited articles on the presoas techniques of MIS-ATP and OLIF highlights the rapid growth of prepsoas spinal fusions on a global scale. This is due to a milieu of reasons, from the minimally invasive nature, low documented postoperative outcomes, operative access, and more. Although more established fusion approaches had higher overall citation counts, there was a consistent increase in these prepsoas publications and citations. Significant contributions to this space have primarily emerged from Asian and North American surgeons and institutes, as primarily retrospective cohorts and technical notes, although there was a scarcity in higher levels of evidence work, such as randomized control trials and meta-analysis, highlighting future room for growth in these rapidly growing techniques. In all, our study lays the foundation for understanding the current trends and research landscape and potential future avenues for OLIF and MIS-ATP research.

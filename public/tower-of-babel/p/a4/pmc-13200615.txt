Diagnostic concordance between incisional biopsies and final excision specimens in maxillofacial Lesions: a 14-Year retrospective study
Authors: Sahar Kadri, Eya Moussaoui, Asma Cheikh, Lamia Oualha
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThe aim of this study was to evaluate the concordance between the histopathological diagnoses obtained from biopsies of maxillofacial lesions and the definitive histopathological diagnoses of the excised specimens, and to identify factors associated with diagnostic discordance.Materials and MethodsA retrospective cross-sectional study was conducted on 203 patients with maxillofacial lesions who underwent surgical excision following biopsy between 2005 and 2019. Sociodemographic, clinical, and histopathological data were collected and analyzed. Biopsy diagnoses were compared with the final excision diagnoses using Cohen's kappa coefficient and appropriate statistical tests.ResultsThe histopathological diagnosis of the excised specimen was consistent with the biopsy diagnosis in 87.8% of cases (p = 0.001). No significant differences were found between biopsy and excision diagnoses when the biopsy was performed by a specialist or a resident (p = 0.29), or when the histopathological evaluation was conducted by a resident or a specialist pathologist (p = 0.59). Likewise, no significant differences were observed according to whether the biopsy specimen consisted of a single fragment or multiple fragments (p = 0.65).ConclusionCareful identification and management of factors contributing to diagnostic discordance may improve the reliability of biopsy diagnoses and consequently enhance patient management.Keywords: biopsy, diagnosis, jaw neoplasms, histopathology, maxillofacial surgery

Introduction

Maxillofacial lesions comprise non-traumatic and non-congenital pathologies affecting the skin, mucosa, and bones of the cervicofacial region (1). They constitute a significant public health concern because of their increasing prevalence and the high costs associated with treatment (2, 3). These lesions may result in functional impairment, aesthetic deformities, pain, psychosocial distress, and, in severe cases, life-threatening complications (4). Appropriate management depends largely on the accuracy of the initial diagnosis, which is primarily established through biopsy examination (1). Therefore, evaluating the diagnostic concordance between incisional biopsy findings and final excision specimen diagnoses, as well as identifying factors associated with diagnostic discrepancies, is of considerable importance.

Worldwide, only a limited number of studies have investigated the concordance between biopsy and final histopathological diagnoses of maxillofacial lesions and explored factors influencing diagnostic reliability (5, 6). To the best of our knowledge, no such studies have been conducted in North Africa, particularly in Tunisia. This highlights the importance of the present study, which aims to improve surgical management, reduce complications, and minimize treatment-related costs. Indeed, patients with these lesions frequently require hospitalization and surgical intervention, creating a substantial financial burden on the Tunisian healthcare system.

The aim of the present study was to assess the concordance between histopathological diagnoses obtained from biopsies and those of final excision specimens in patients with maxillofacial lesions treated in the Department of Maxillofacial Surgery at Sahloul Hospital between 2005 and 2019.

Materials and Methods

Ethical approval

This study did not involve direct patient participation. All data were collected retrospectively from admission records, surgical files, and histopathology reports. The data were fully anonymized at the source, and no personally identifiable information was included in the manuscript. The collection and use of sociodemographic, clinical, medical, and histopathological data were conducted in accordance with institutional and international ethical standards, ensuring strict protection of patient anonymity and data confidentiality. The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of the Faculty of Dental Medicine of Monastir, Tunisia (Approval No. 2025018).

Study design

A retrospective descriptive cross-sectional study was conducted to evaluate the concordance between initial biopsy diagnoses and definitive histopathological findings following surgical excision in patients with maxillofacial lesions. This study design was selected because it enables the assessment of diagnostic agreement and associated contributing factors using routinely collected clinical and histopathological data over an extended period.

The study included all patients hospitalized for maxillofacial lesions who underwent both biopsy and subsequent surgical excision in the Department of Maxillofacial Surgery at Sahloul Hospital over a 14-year period, from January 2005 to December 2019.

During the study period, 470 patient records were initially reviewed. After the application of strict inclusion criteria requiring complete clinical data, available biopsy specimens, and corresponding definitive histopathological reports, 203 cases were retained for final analysis. Cases with missing or incomplete information, including absent biopsy details, inadequate specimen transport conditions, or insufficient tissue for reliable histopathological evaluation, were excluded to ensure the validity and reliability of the study findings.

Data collection

Data were collected retrospectively from medical and pathology records using a structured data extraction form developed on the basis of previously published studies. The variables collected included patient-related variables (age at diagnosis and sex); clinical variables (relevant medical history and lifestyle habits, including smoking and alcohol consumption when available); biopsy-related variables (anatomical site of the biopsy, number of biopsy fragments, initial histopathological diagnosis, level of clinical experience of the clinician performing the biopsy, and grade of the pathologist interpreting the specimen); and histopathological variables (level of experience of the clinician performing the surgical excision and the pathologist interpreting the excisional specimen, definitive histopathological diagnosis of the excised lesion, and concordance between the biopsy diagnosis and the final diagnosis).

Statistical analysis

Data entry and statistical analyses were performed using the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) software, version 25.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Descriptive statistics were used to summarize patient characteristics, clinical data, and histopathological findings. Quantitative variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation for normally distributed data, or as median and interquartile range for non-normally distributed data. Qualitative variables were presented as absolute frequencies and percentages.

The primary outcome measure was the level of diagnostic concordance between the initial incisional biopsy diagnosis and the definitive histopathological diagnosis obtained after surgical excision. Diagnostic agreement was evaluated using Cohen's kappa coefficient (κ). Because κ values may be influenced by disease prevalence, the prevalence effect was also taken into account during interpretation, as a highly prevalent outcome category may lead to underestimation of agreement despite high observed concordance.

Diagnostic accuracy was calculated as the proportion of concordant diagnoses between biopsy and excision specimens. Secondary analyses were performed to investigate factors potentially associated with diagnostic concordance, including the number of biopsy fragments, anatomical site of the lesion, and the level of experience of both the clinician and the pathologist.

Comparisons between categorical variables were performed using the Chi-square test or Fisher's exact test, as appropriate. Continuous variables were compared using Student's t-test or corresponding non-parametric tests when distributional assumptions were not satisfied. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant, with results reported at a 95% confidence interval.

Diagnostic concordance between incisional biopsies and final excision specimens was calculated as the proportion of biopsies in which the initial histopathological diagnosis agreed with the final excisional diagnosis. Data analysis demonstrated an overall concordance rate of 87.7% between the histological diagnoses from incisional biopsies and those from final excision specimens (178 out of 203 cases). Discordance was recorded in 12.3% of cases (n = 25). This level of agreement was statistically significant, with a Cohen's Kappa value of 0.50 and a p-value of 0.001.

Among the 125 biopsies performed by specialist physicians, 112 cases (86.6%) were concordant with the definitive histopathological diagnosis, while 13 cases (10.4%) were discordant. Among the 78 biopsies performed by resident physicians, 66 cases (84.6%) were concordant and 12 cases (15.4%) were discordant. Statistical analysis using Pearson's Chi-square test showed no significant difference in concordance between specialists and residents (χ² = 1.0, p = 0.29).

Among 198 biopsy specimens interpreted by specialist pathologists, 174 cases (87.9%) were concordant with the definitive histopathological diagnosis, while 24 cases (12.1%) were discordant. Among the 5 biopsies interpreted by resident pathologists, 4 cases (80%) were concordant and 1 case (20%) was discordant. Statistical comparison using Fisher's exact test showed no significant difference in concordance between specialist and resident pathologists (p = 0.59, Fisher's exact test = 0.48).

Finally, for the number of biopsy fragments, among 89 biopsy specimens consisting of multiple tissue fragments, 77 cases (86.5%) were concordant with the definitive histopathological diagnosis, while 12 cases (13.5%) were discordant. Among the 97 biopsies consisting of a single fragment, 86 cases (88.7%) were concordant and 11 cases (11.3%) were discordant. Statistical analysis using Pearson's Chi-square test indicated no significant difference in concordance between single-fragment and multiple-fragment biopsies (χ² = 0.19, p = 0.65).

Discussion

The objective of this study was to evaluate the concordance between histopathological diagnoses obtained from biopsies of maxillofacial lesions and the definitive histopathological diagnoses of excised specimens in patients treated in the Department of Maxillofacial Surgery at Sahloul University Hospital between 2005 and 2019 (203 cases). The results demonstrated that the histopathological diagnosis of the excised specimen was consistent with the biopsy diagnosis in 87.7% of cases (178/203). Several studies have reported the prevalence of biopsied lesions or the concordance between clinical and histopathological diagnoses (7, 8, 9). However, studies specifically investigating factors influencing concordance between biopsy and final excision diagnoses in maxillofacial lesions remain limited (10).

Regarding demographic distribution, maxillofacial lesions were more common in men (68.5%), with a mean age of 59.80 ± 18 years. These findings are partially consistent with previous reports (7, 8, 9). While several studies described a predominance among women (7, 8, 9), others similarly reported a higher prevalence among men (11, 12, 13, 14).

In the present study, the gingiva was the most frequent anatomical site involved (51.5%), consistent with the findings reported by Takashima (15) and Assadat Hashemi (16). Likewise, a Brazilian study conducted by André Luis Costa reported the gingiva as the most commonly affected site (20.73%) (7).

Malignant tumors were the predominant lesions in our study population, accounting for 92.11% of cases. In contrast, André Luis Costa reported non-neoplastic proliferative lesions as the most frequent category (29.6%) in a Brazilian cohort (7). Fulciniti reported a predominance of benign lesions (73%), with malignant lesions representing 27% of cases (17), while Gbolahan similarly found 71.4% benign and 28.6% malignant lesions (18). The high prevalence of malignant and potentially malignant lesions in our series may be explained by the tertiary referral role of the Department of Maxillofacial Surgery at Sahloul University Hospital, which receives complex cases from a large catchment area. In addition, benign-appearing lesions are frequently managed by direct excision without prior biopsy.

The concordance rate between biopsy diagnosis and final excision diagnosis in the present study was 87.7%, which is comparable to rates reported in the literature (10, 19). Chen et al. reported concordance rates of 89.3% for maxillofacial lesions (19) and 88.9% for oral lesions (10). Previous studies have attributed discordance to sampling errors, insufficient biopsy material, inflammatory changes, tissue artifacts, and interobserver variability among pathologists (2, 3, 18).

Sampling error has been identified as the leading cause of diagnostic discordance in several studies (10, 17, 18, 19). Chen et al. reported that biopsies taken from non-representative areas accounted for 60% of discordant cases (10). However, this issue appeared less pronounced in studies evaluating fine-needle aspiration biopsies of maxillofacial lesions (17).

In the present study, we also assessed whether clinical experience influenced diagnostic concordance by comparing biopsies performed by specialists and residents. No statistically significant difference was observed between biopsy and final excision diagnoses according to operator experience.

With regard to the number of biopsy fragments, the concordance rate was 84.6% for single-fragment biopsies and 86.5% for multiple-fragment biopsies; however, this difference was not statistically significant. These findings are consistent with those of Chen, who reported higher concordance rates with multiple biopsy fragments (83.3%) (10). Similarly, Lee et al., in a study on oral leukoplakia, demonstrated that concordance improved from 56% with a single biopsy to 88.1% when multiple fragments were obtained (5). Based on these findings, Chen recommended adopting map-guided, multi-fragment biopsy techniques to improve diagnostic accuracy in extensive lesions (10).

Some studies have suggested that diagnostic variability may result from interobserver differences among pathologists (18). However, no statistically significant association was identified in our study.

In our series, the presence of inflammation was not significantly associated with diagnostic discordance. In contrast, Chen et al. reported that inflammatory changes contributed to discordance in 3.4% of cases (10). This discrepancy may be explained by the fact that our study did not differentiate between specific and non-specific inflammatory alterations. In the literature, inflammation has been reported to interfere with histopathological interpretation by inducing reactive cellular atypia (10).

This study used a retrospective descriptive cross-sectional design and included all hospitalized patients who underwent surgical treatment for maxillofacial lesions preceded by incisional biopsy in the Department of Maxillofacial Surgery at Sahloul University Hospital between 2005 and 2019. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study conducted in North Africa addressing this topic and represents an exhaustive analysis spanning a 14-year period.

Nevertheless, the final sample size remained limited despite the long study period because of the strict inclusion criteria requiring complete clinical records, available biopsy specimens, and corresponding definitive histopathological reports. Cases with incomplete or missing information, including inadequate specimen transport conditions, insufficient tissue for analysis, or nonspecific inflammatory changes, were excluded to ensure the reliability and accuracy of the findings. These factors, together with the inherent limitations of retrospective studies, may limit the generalizability of the results. Furthermore, certain biopsy-related variables, such as fragment number, depth and size of the biopsy, and detailed clinical characteristics, were unavailable or incompletely documented in some records, which may have contributed to discordant cases. Nonetheless, the present study provides valuable insight into diagnostic concordance and the factors influencing biopsy outcomes in maxillofacial lesions.

We therefore recommend improving the traceability and completeness of medical records and developing a standardized clinical and histopathological reporting form containing all relevant diagnostic information to facilitate communication between clinicians and pathologists. Such standardization would also support future prospective investigations. Conclusion

Within the limitations of this study, biopsy appears to be a reliable diagnostic method for maxillofacial lesions, demonstrating a high level of concordance with the definitive histopathological findings of excisional specimens. Diagnostic accuracy was not significantly influenced by the operator's level of experience or by technical factors related to the biopsy procedure. These findings support the continued use of biopsy as a dependable tool for clinical decision-making. Nevertheless, meticulous biopsy technique and adequate tissue sampling remain essential to optimize diagnostic accuracy and improve patient management.

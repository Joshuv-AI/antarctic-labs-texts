Enhanced recovery after surgery from 1990 to 2024: A bibliometric and visual analysis
Authors: Hamiye Seyrekli, Melike Karabörk, Oğuz Gündoğdu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:This study aimed to analyze publications from 1990 to 2024 to examine the relationship of Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS) with countries, institutions, authors, journals, references, and keywords.Methods:We searched for ERAS-related publications between 1990 and 2024 in the Web of Science Core Collection (WOSCC) database. The strategy included the following search terms: “TS=ERAS” OR “enhanced recovery” OR “enhanced recovery after surgery” OR “enhanced recovery after surgery eras” OR “enhanced recovery program” OR “enhanced recovery protocol” OR “eras protocol” OR “eras r” OR “fast track” OR “fast track surgery” OR “fast-track” OR “fast-track surgery.” The search was restricted to articles and reviews in all languages. All results were filtered using these criteria on WOSCC and exported as a plain text file. Visual analyses were performed using Microsoft Word and VOS viewer 1.6.20.Results:A total of 4,275 publications and 111,922 citations were identified. Overall, 101 countries contributed to ERAS research, with the United States ranking first. The institution with the highest number of publications was Harvard University. Nicolas Demartines ranked first with 51 publications, while H. Kehlet was the most frequently cited author. Among the journals publishing on ERAS, Transplantation had the highest number of publications. The most cited source was Enhanced Recovery After Surgery: A Review. Keyword analysis revealed that “enhanced recovery after surgery” was the primary research focus within the existing literature.Conclusions:The findings of this study demonstrate that, particularly since 2008, there has been a steadily growing interest in ERAS worldwide.

Introduction

The concept of Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS) was first introduced in the late 1990s in Denmark by Kehlet and colleagues from the University of Copenhagen and was subsequently incorporated into clinical practice.[12] ERAS is a multidisciplinary, evidence-based approach to perioperative care designed to accelerate recovery for surgical patients throughout all perioperative phases.[3] These programs aim both to reduce complication risks and to facilitate faster return to daily activities.[45]

ERAS protocols have been shown to reduce complication rates and hospital length of stay by up to 50% in non-cardiac surgeries compared to conventional perioperative care. Developed based on scientific evidence across multiple surgical specialties, ERAS protocols are now widely represented in the literature.[3] Owing to ongoing refinements, ERAS has been implemented in more than 250 centers across over 25 countries.[67] Furthermore, guidelines for more than 30 different specialties have been published and periodically updated.

The ERAS approach fundamentally includes the following[8]:

Providing patients with preoperative information and education,Utilizing advanced anesthesia, analgesia, and surgical techniques to minimize surgical stress, pain, and discomfort,Enhancing postoperative recovery with strategies such as early mobilization and enteral nutrition.

Despite its rapid expansion, systematic and quantitative studies evaluating ERAS globally remain limited.[89] Bibliometric analysis can therefore provide insights into the global landscape, future directions, and major research themes of ERAS.

Bibliometric analysis applies statistical and mathematical techniques to scientific publications. With its transparent, systematic, and reproducible nature, it enhances the quality of literature reviews and enables mapping of research fields and influential studies independently of subjective evaluations.[10]

The aim of this study was to conduct a comprehensive bibliometric analysis of ERAS-related articles published between 1990 and 2024, to provide researchers with a deeper understanding of current research trends.

Search Strategy

We searched for ERAS-related publications between 1990 and 2024 in the Web of Science Core Collection (WOSCC) database. The strategy for this study included the following search terms: “TS=ERAS” OR “enhanced recovery” OR “enhanced recovery after surgery” OR “enhanced recovery after surgery eras” OR “enhanced recovery program” OR “enhanced recovery protocol” OR “eras protocol” OR “eras r” OR “fast track” OR “fast track surgery” OR “fast-track” OR “fast-track surgery.” The search was limited to articles and reviews in all languages. All results were filtered using these criteria on WOSCC and exported as a plain text file. Visual analyses were performed using Microsoft Word and VOS viewer 1.6.20. The following bibliometric information was collected: publication year, country, journal, number of citations, authors, disciplines, institutions, and subjects.

Data collection

All results were searched on WOSCC using the above strategy and exported as plain text literature in TXT or CSV format. The literature search was performed in May 2025.

Data analysis

Visual analysis was performed using Microsoft Word 365 and VOS viewer.[11] Countries, institutions, references, author distributions, and keywords from the extracted data were visually analyzed on VOS viewer 1.6.20. Since all raw data used here were obtained from public databases, no ethical review was required.

Results

Publication output and temporal trends

The data screening from 1990 to 2024 included a total of 4,275 publications and 111,922 citations. Both the annual number of articles and citation frequency have increased over the past two decades. As shown in Figure 1, a steady increase in the number of publications has been observed since 2008, reaching a peak in 2024. Similarly, a general increase in the number of citations is also notable. This trend indicates a growing interest in the field of ERAS and suggests that the topic has increasingly gained recognition within the scientific community.

Discussion

The data we obtained indicate that research on ERAS has steadily increased over the past two decades [Figure 1]. Publications related to ERAS began to rise in 2008 and showed marked acceleration after 2016. Between 2021 and 2023, both the number of publications and citations remained relatively stable. As of the most recent year examined, 2024, no significant increase was observed compared to previous years, with 422 publications and 12,746 citations recorded. Overall, it can be concluded that interest and research activity related to ERAS have shown a consistent upward trend over time.

The data we identified correlate well with the results of earlier bibliometric analyses.[69] Compared to previous bibliometric analyses, this study offers a broader and more comprehensive perspective. It spans a wider time frame, includes publications in multiple languages rather than being limited to English-only literature, and incorporates data from the most recent year (2024). These features allow our study to provide more current and globally representative insights into ERAS-related research trends. This expanded scope strengthens the reliability of our findings and enhances their relevance for guiding future research directions.

In this bibliometric analysis, we not only conducted a quantitative evaluation of countries based on publication volume, citation counts, and total link strength [Table 1], but also explored the collaborative dynamics between them [Figure 2], recent co-citation patterns [Figure 9], and co-authorship networks with associated link strengths [Figure 10]. These findings provide a comprehensive overview of global research interactions in the ERAS field.

These results offer valuable insights into both the current state and the evolving trajectory of ERAS literature worldwide. The integration of temporal dimensions into our analyses further enables a nuanced understanding of shifts in research focus, collaboration intensity, and influential contributors, thereby strengthening the interpretability and relevance of our findings.

According to bibliometric analysis, a total of 78 countries has contributed to ERAS-related research. The United States ranked first in terms of the number of publications, followed by China. The United States also had the highest citation count, with the United Kingdom ranking second. Despite the large volume of ERAS-related publications originating from China, the country ranked only eighth in terms of total citations and did not appear among the top 10 countries in total link strength. This suggests that although China is active in ERAS research, its academic visibility and international collaboration in this field may still be limited, a finding consistent with prior bibliometric studies. Canada ranked fourth in terms of publication volume, with a significant number of studies originating from McGill University. Notably, McGill University also emerged as the most cited institution overall, highlighting its strong academic influence and contribution to the development of ERAS protocols.[6] In our bibliometric analysis, a total of 101 countries were found to have contributed to ERAS-related research. Like previous studies, the United States ranked first in terms of the number of publications, followed by the United Kingdom. However, in contrast to earlier findings where China held the second position, our results indicate that China has now shifted to third place. The United States remained the most cited country, with the United Kingdom once again ranking second in citation count.

Compared to other recent bibliometric studies, our analysis additionally reveals a notable increase in both the citation count and total link strength of the Netherlands.[6] This suggests a growing influence and research capacity of the Netherlands in the field of ERAS.

Regarding institutional contributions, Harvard University produced the highest number of publications, whereas the University of Edinburgh received the highest number of citations. Most of the work from Edinburgh appeared to focus on abdominal surgery, reflecting a specialized domain of expertise. Furthermore, McGill University demonstrated the highest total link strength among institutions, indicating a strong network of international collaborations.

In the analysis of authors, the most cited individual was Dr. Henrik Kehlet, who is affiliated with the University of Copenhagen in Denmark. He has played a pioneering role in the conceptual development of ERAS through his research published in over 800 articles.[12] Kehlet’s work has primarily focused on the physiology and treatment of acute pain, the transition from acute to chronic pain, fatigue, and postoperative organ dysfunction.[131415] Gustafsson UO, who is affiliated with Karolinska University, is among the most frequently co-cited authors. He is a surgeon specialized in colorectal surgery, as well as laparoscopic and robotic surgery. His research focuses on the impact of ERAS on perioperative and postoperative outcomes, perioperative interventions, glucose metabolism, and surgery, robotic surgery, and its outcomes, as well as the effects of microbiota on psychiatric outcomes and gastrointestinal diseases. He is the lead author of international ERAS guidelines, a board member of SWERAS, and the head of research design.[16]

In our analysis, the most prolific author was Demartines Nicolas, who is affiliated with Lausanne University Hospital (CHUV) in Switzerland. His work primarily focuses on oncologic surgery, particularly in the fields of hepato-pancreato-biliary, colorectal, and upper gastrointestinal surgery. He is a former treasurer of the ERAS Society and currently serves as the head of the ERAS Implementation Program.[1718] Additionally, Olle Ljungqvist, based at Örebro University in Sweden, is among the most highly cited authors in recent years. He introduced the concept of preoperative oral carbohydrate loading instead of preoperative fasting in elective surgery. Ljungqvist has published over 300 original articles, book chapters, and reviews on topics such as surgical nutrition, surgical metabolism, surgical stress, and ERAS.[319]

The journal with the highest number of ERAS-related publications was Transplantation, with a total of 138 articles. The most cited journal was the World Journal of Surgery, which received 7,279 citations. The primary topics covered in these journals included surgery, transplantation, gastroenterology, and cardiovascular surgery. Although the World Journal of Surgery ranks third in terms of the number of publications, its significantly higher citation count compared to other journals indicates that it is a strong and influential source for ERAS research.

The most highly cited source is the article titled “Enhanced Recovery After Surgery: A Review” by Ljungqvist et al.,[3] which has received 2,278 citations. The article emphasizes the importance of ERAS guidelines and highlights several evidence-based modern care modifications. These include the shift from overnight fasting to preoperative carbohydrate drinks two hours before surgery, the use of minimally invasive approaches instead of large incisions, fluid management aimed at maintaining balance rather than administering large volumes of intravenous fluids, avoidance or early removal of drains and tubes, early mobilization, and the provision of drinks and food on the day of surgery. The review underlines that ERAS protocols can reduce hospital length of stay by 30% to 50% and achieve similar reductions in postoperative complications, readmissions, and healthcare costs. The components of the protocol are noted to mitigate the surgical stress response to preserve anabolic homeostasis. Ultimately, the article concludes that ERAS represents an evidence-based care improvement process for surgical patients. The implementation of ERAS programs leads to significant improvements in clinical outcomes and cost-effectiveness, establishing ERAS as a prime example of value-based care in surgery.

Like a recently conducted bibliometric study, the most frequently used keyword in our analysis was “ERAS.”[6] Other commonly co-occurring keywords included “Colorectal Surgery” and “Enhanced Recovery.” Keyword analysis suggests that ERAS-related studies have been more extensively conducted in the contexts of colorectal surgery, transplantation, breast reconstruction surgery, as well as in topics related to postoperative pain and analgesia.

This study has several limitations. Only the Web of Science Core Collection (WOSCC) database was used, which may have limited the total number of included publications. There may be additional studies related to ERAS protocols that were not captured due to the specific keywords used in our search strategy. Some productive authors, institutions, or keywords may not have been clearly represented in our visualizations. Furthermore, most of the studies on ERAS are observational in nature, which limits the overall level of evidence.

Conclusions

We believe that this study makes a timely contribution to the literature by analyzing the global development of ERAS awareness between 1990 and 2024 across countries, institutions, authors, journals, references, and keyword frequencies. Furthermore, we anticipate that research on this topic will continue to expand in the coming years.

Comparison of BIOCHIP Mosaic Based Indirect Immunofluorescence and Oral Direct Immunofluorescence in Patients with Oral Mucosal Pemphigus
Authors: Amrithaa Muralitharan, Reena Rai, Sakthisankari Shanmugasundaram
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Background:Oral mucosal lesions in pemphigus vulgaris may precede cutaneous lesions and can cause diagnostic confusion. Diagnosis can be made by histopathology, direct immunofluorescence (DIF), and indirect immunofluorescence (IIF). DIF of the oral mucosa is an invasive procedure and difficult to perform in patients with severe mucosal ulcer, and studies have shown that BIOCHIP-IIF can be used to detect desmoglein 1 and 3 in the serum of patients with pemphigus.Aim:To compare BIOCHIP mosaic-based IIF and oral DIF in patients with oral mucosal pemphigus.Materials and Methods:All cases of oral mucosal pemphigus which were diagnosed by clinical examination and histopathology were included in the study. Oral DIF and BIOCHIP-IIF samples were collected, and the results were analyzed.Results:Out of 36 patients, DIF was positive in 30 patients (83.3%) and BIOCHIP-IIF in 28 patients (77.8%). On comparing BIOCHIP-IIF with DIF, sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value, and negative predictive value of BIOCHIP were 80%, 33.3%, 85.71%, and 25%, respectively. Chi-square test showed no statistically significant difference between oral DIF and BIOCHIP-IIF (P value is 0.5143). Among the 28 patients who had positive BIOCHIP, 27 patients (96.4%) showed desmoglein (Dsg) 3 positivity, and 16 patients (57.1%) showed Dsg 1 positivity. The sensitivity and specificity of Dsg1 were 53.3% and 100%, respectively. The sensitivity and specificity of Dsg3 were 76.6% and 33.3%, respectively.Conclusion:BIOCHIP-IIF can be used as an alternative to oral DIF in the diagnosis of oral mucosal pemphigus.

Introduction

Pemphigus vulgaris (PV) is a blistering autoimmune disorder affecting the skin and mucous membranes, and oral lesions may precede the cutaneous lesions. The pathogenic mechanism underlying this disorder is acantholysis, where there is separation of the keratinocytes mediated by auto-antibodies against keratinocyte desmosomal proteins, desmoglein (Dsg) 1, which is primarily responsible for the skin and Dsg 3 for the mucosal involvement. Diagnosis is made by histopathology and direct immunofluorescence (DIF), which is the gold standard technique. DIF of the perilesional skin shows a fish net pattern, demonstrating the presence of IgG antibodies and complements (C3). Indirect immunofluorescence (IIF), enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), and the new BIOCHIP-IIF are serological diagnostic techniques which demonstrate positive circulating intercellular anti-Dsg antibodies in 80-90% of patients with PV.[1] BIOCHIP is an IIF multiplex method, and the diagnosis of PV is made based on reactivity of Dsg3 and/or Dsg1.[2] Patients may present with only oral erosions, posing diagnostic difficulties, as oral ulcers can be a presenting clinical sign of many other diseases. Early diagnosis is very important in preventing the long-term morbidity and mortality seen in the patients with PV. DIF is an invasive procedure causing discomfort in patients with severe painful oral ulcers. Hence, BIOCHIP-IIF, a serological test, can be used in these patients without causing discomfort and pain. The purpose of the study is to compare oral DIF and BIOCHIP-IIF in patients with oral mucosal pemphigus.

Materials and Methods

This is a cross-sectional study conducted from June 2021 to December 2022 in the departments of dermatology and pathology in a tertiary care hospital. Institutional ethics committee approval was obtained before starting the study (Project no. 21/050). Sample size calculation was done according to the formula - n = 4pq/d2. The estimated sample size was 30.

All patients diagnosed as oral mucosal pemphigus by clinical examination and histopathology were enrolled in the study. Patients with active skin lesions, patients in remission, and those with other autoimmune bullous disorders were excluded from the study. Histopathology of all the patients included in the study showed suprabasal blister with acantholytic cells.

Biopsy of the perilesional oral mucosa for DIF was obtained, and an intercellular staining (ICS) pattern with IgG and/or C3 was considered as diagnostic of pemphigus. The dermatology mosaic 7 BIOCHIP (Euroimmun, Germany) was used in this study which consists of 6 substrates constituting the primate oesophagus section, human salt-split skin (SSS) section, transfected cells with Dsg1, Dsg3, C-terminal globular domain of BP 230, and recombinant purified BP 180-NC16A domain [Figure 1]. A total of 5 ml blood was collected in a red vacutainer tube (without anticoagulant), kept at room temperature for 30 minutes, and then, the sample was centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 10 minutes. The supernatant serum was transferred to a container and sent to detect Dsg 1 and Dsg 3 antibodies by BIOCHIP-IIF according to standard method. BIOCHIP-IIF in pemphigus shows fine granular fluorescence of ICS staining pattern in primate oesophagus and fine granular cytoplasmic fluorescence in Dsg 1 and Dsg3 transfected cells.

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS software, the sensitivity and specificity values were determined, and the results were compared with the DIF of the oral mucosa.

Results

Thirty-six patients met the inclusion criteria for the study, which included 28 females and 8 males. Out of 36 patients, 30 (83.3%) had positive DIF [Table 1] and 28 (77.8%) had positive BIOCHIP [Table 2]. Out of the 28 patients with positive BIOCHIP-IIF, 24 patients (85.7%) also showed DIF positivity, and 4 patients (14.2%) had negative DIF [Table 3]. Among the 8 patients whose BIOCHIP was negative, 6 patients (75%) showed DIF positivity and 2 patients (25%) were negative for DIF [Table 3]. Considering DIF as the gold standard, the sensitivity and specificity of BIOCHIP were found to be 80% and 33.3%, respectively. The positive and negative predictive values of BIOCHIP were 85.71% and 25%, respectively. P value calculated using Chi-square test was 0.5143. Among the 28 patients who had positive BIOCHIP, all the patients (100%) showed reactivity in primate oesophagus, 27 patients (96.4%) showed Dsg 3 positivity, and 16 patients (57.1%) also showed Dsg 1 positivity in addition to the reactivity observed in the primate oesophagus and Dsg 3 [Figures 2 and 3]. SSS, BP 180, and BP 230 substrates did not show any reactivity. The sensitivity and specificity of dsg1 were 53.3% and 100%, respectively, with Chi-square test showing a P value of 0.016. The sensitivity and specificity of Dsg3 were 76.6% and 33.3%, respectively, with Chi-square test showing a P value of 0.606.

Discussion

Oral mucosal pemphigus can precede cutaneous lesions, resulting in delay in the diagnosis, so it is important to initiate treatment early to prevent morbidity.

In a study by Arunprasath et al.[3] BIOCHIP-IIF was compared with DIF in various autoimmune bullous diseases, including PV. BIOCHIP mosaic results showed a concordance of 100% in the diagnosis of PV with DIF in this study. Similarly in our study, BIOCHIP-IIF results were concordant with DIF.

Yang et al.[4] conducted a study in Australia for the validation of BIOCHIP in the diagnosis of PV and bullous pemphigoid (BP). The sensitivity and specificity of the Dsg3 substrate in PV were 60.9% and 73.6%, respectively. However, the present study was conducted in patients with only oral mucosal pemphigus, and the sensitivity and specificity of Dsg 3 were 76.6% and 33.3%, respectively. The Dsg3 substrate in BIOCHIP showed a higher sensitivity in oral pemphigus patients compared to the previous studies.

Tirumalae and Kalegowda[5] performed a study to assess the role of BIOCHIP-IIF test in cutaneous vesiculobullous diseases, and Dsg3 was the commonest positive substrate in PV (86%). Dsg3 positivity is usually present in patients with predominant mucosal involvement as seen in our study in 96.4% of the patients.

Özkesici et al.[6] conducted a study to assess the value of the BIOCHIP-IIF technique in the diagnosis of PV and BP in Turkish patients. Among the 45 PV patients, the results of BIOCHIP were compared with either DIF or ELISA. The sensitivity and specificity of BIOCHIP in the diagnosis of PV were found to be 91.1% and 97.1%, respectively. However, the present study in oral pemphigus showed a sensitivity and specificity of 80% and 33.3%, respectively.

van Beek et al.[7] conducted a multicentre prospective study on multivariant diagnostics of autoimmune bullous dermatoses using the BIOCHIP technology. Using the BIOCHIP mosaic, 98.8% sera were diagnosed as PV in agreement with the DIF results. The study concluded that in cases where biopsy may be inappropriate for medical or practical reasons, analysis of serum autoantibodies by the BIOCHIP mosaic or the multistep procedure is nearly equivalent to DIF. The study observations correlated well with our study in patients with oral mucosal pemphigus.

Asokan et al.[8] conducted a study where salivary Dsg1 and 3 ELISA were compared with oral DIF in oral mucosal pemphigus patients. The sensitivity of Dsg 1 and 3 ELISA was 31.8% and 40.9%, respectively. In our study, the sensitivity of Dsg 1 and 3 in BIOCHIP was 53.3% and 76.6%, respectively, which was higher compared to ELISA.

BIOCHIP-IIF is a non-invasive, serological diagnostic technique where simultaneous identification of target antigens and characteristic staining patterns aids in the diagnosis of the disease, compared to the invasive technique DIF, which is difficult to perform in patients having extensive mucosal ulcers. There is no study comparing BIOCHIP-IIF and oral DIF in the diagnosis of oral mucosal pemphigus. However, larger studies need to be conducted in patients with oral mucosal pemphigus.

Conclusion

BIOCHIP-IIF can also be used to diagnose oral mucosal pemphigus in patients with severe distressing ulcers where an invasive procedure like DIF is not possible.

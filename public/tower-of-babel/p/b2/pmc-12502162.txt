SQSTM1/p62 as a prognostic indicator in pancancer and nasopharyngeal carcinoma
Authors: Li-li Chen, Zong-yao Zhang, Bo Wu, Mei-zhen Lu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThis study evaluated SQSTM1/p62 expression patterns and its prognostic value across multiple cancers, with a focus on nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC).Results descriptionSQSTM1/p62 showed significantly higher mRNA and protein levels in tumors versus normal tissues across cancer types. High SQSTM1/p62 expression correlated with poorer OS and DSS in multiple cancers. Among 86 NPC patients (median follow-up 46.8 months), high SQSTM1/p62 expression (69.8% vs CNP 45% in controls, P = 0.036) independently predicted reduced DSS (HR = 4.51, 95% CI: 1.40–14.52). The unadjusted and multivariable-adjusted models consistently revealed a stable relationship between SQSTM1/p62 expression and patient prognosis. No significant interaction was observed between SQSTM1/p62 expression and age, TNM stage, or treatment methods (induction chemotherapy, concurrent chemoradiotherapy, adjuvant chemotherapy). In summary, elevated SQSTM1/p62 expression correlates with poor clinical outcomes in pancancer analyses and serves as an independent prognostic factor for NPC.

Introduction

Nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) is a distinct malignancy with a high prevalence in Southeast Asia, particularly in southern China [1]. It poses significant clinical challenges due to its aggressive behavior and frequent late-stage diagnosis in affected patients. Despite advances in imaging techniques and therapeutic modalities, including intensity-modulated radiotherapy and combined chemotherapy, the prognosis of patients with NPC remains poor [2]. Distant metastases and treatment resistance drive mortality [3, 4]. Consequently, there is an urgent need to identify reliable biomarkers and therapeutic targets to improve early diagnosis and guide personalized treatment, potentially providing personalized approaches for managing NPC.

SQSTM1/p62, an autophagy-related receptor protein, plays a crucial role in mediating selective autophagy and in regulating cellular homeostasis [5]. It is involved in various signaling pathways that influence tumor progression and resistance to therapy [6]. Elevated expression of SQSTM1/p62 has been implicated as a marker of poor prognosis in multiple cancers, including gastric and hepatocellular carcinomas [7, 8]. However, its prognostic significance in NPC remains inadequately explored, representing a critical knowledge gap in this malignancy.

This study aimed to examine the clinical implications of SQSTM1/p62 expression in NPC. To establish a broader context, an initial assessment of SQSTM1/p62 expression patterns and their prognostic significance was conducted across multiple cancer types. Following this preliminary analysis, a detailed investigation was undertaken to determine the expression levels of this biomarker and their correlation with patient outcomes, specifically in NPC. The findings from this study offer valuable perspectives on the clinical utility of SQSTM1/p62 in NPC management.

Materials and methods

Gene expression and prognosis analysis

The RNA-seq data from TCGA-GTEx (v32.0) included 33 cancer types, with ComBat-seq correcting batch effects [9]. Normalization was performed using TPM (transcripts per million) for cross-dataset comparability. Protein expression data were retrieved from CPTAC database using mass spectrometry-based quantification. Using the mean ± 2 SD of SQSTM1/p62 expression as the cutoff threshold, we classified the samples into high- or low-expression groups. Using an online platform (https://grswsci.top) we analyzed the associations of SQSTM1/p62 with overall survival (OS) and disease-specific survival (DSS) via Kaplan‒Meier survival curve analysis and univariate Cox regression analysis.

Patient selection, ethical review, and follow-up

The study cohort comprised biopsy-proven NPC cases undergoing active treatment, with untreated patients serving as exclusion criteria. A total of 130 NPC patients were consecutively enrolled at Taizhou Central Hospital (Taizhou University Hospital) between 2016 and 2020 (Fig. 1). Of these, 37 patients were excluded owing to incomplete data, resulting in 93 eligible participants. During the follow-up period, 7 participants were lost, leaving a final cohort of 86 patients included in the study. Twenty patients with chronic nasopharyngitis (CNP) were included in the control group. This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Taizhou Central Hospital (Taizhou University Hospital) (Approval No. 2019-SC-019) in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Given the retrospective cohort design, the requirement for informed consent was waived by the ethics committee. Follow-up evaluations were conducted every three months during the first two years and every six months from the third to the fifth years. OS was calculated from the date of diagnosis until death or the last follow-up. The DSS was calculated from the date of diagnosis to the date of cancer-specific mortality or the last follow-up.Fig. 1Patient selection flowchart. Immunohistochemistry and staining evaluation

Following established protocols, [10] we performed immunohistochemistry using a 1:200 dilution of the SQSTM1/P62 antibody. Immunohistochemical scoring was based on a combination of the proportion score (0–4) and staining intensity score (0–3), and all samples were independently evaluated by two pathologists. Samples with immunohistochemical scores ≤ 5 were classified as having low expression, whereas those with scores > 5 were categorized as having high expression. Scoring discrepancies were resolved through pathologist consensus.

Statistical analysis

A descriptive analysis of the study participants was conducted. Categorical variables are presented as proportions (%), whereas continuous data are expressed as the means ± standard deviations. The cutoff values for continuous variables were determined via receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis. To compare SQSTM1/P62 expression between tumor and normal tissues, grayscale values were measured via ImageJ software and visualized via GraphPad Prism 8. The correlations between clinical covariates and SQSTM1/P62 expression were analyzed via the chi-square test or Fisher's exact test. Kaplan–Meier analysis identified prognostic factors (P < 0.05) for multivariate analysis. To evaluate the stability of the association between SQSTM1/p62 expression and prognosis, unadjusted and multivariate-adjusted models were used. Stratification and interaction analyses were performed on the basis of age. The likelihood ratio test was used to assess the interaction effects. All the statistical analyses were conducted via Free Statistics software (version 1.9).

Results

SQSTM1/p62 expression in tumor and normal tissues across cancers

Analysis of SQSTM1/p62 expression revealed significant differences between tumor and normal tissues across multiple cancer types (Fig. 2). On the basis of the TCGA-GTEx data, SQSTM1/p62 mRNA levels are significantly elevated in tumor tissues compared with normal tissues in most cancers, including breast cancer (BRCA), colon adenocarcinoma (COAD), lymphoid neoplasm diffuse large b-cell lymphoma (DLBC), esophageal carcinoma (ESCA), glioblastoma multiforme (GBM), head‒neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC), kidney chromophobe (KICH), kidney renal clear cell carcinoma (KIRC), kidney renal papillary cell carcinoma (KIRP), brain lower grade glioma (LGG), liver hepatocellular carcinoma (LIHC), lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), ovarian serous cystadenocarcinoma (OV), pancreatic adenocarcinoma (PAAD), rectum adenocarcinoma (READ), skin cutaneous melanoma (SKCM), stomach adenocarcinoma (STAD), thyroid carcinoma (THCA), and thymoma (THYM) (P < 0.05). Similarly, protein-level analysis via CPTAC data revealed a consistent trend, with significantly greater SQSTM1/p62 protein expression observed in cancers such as BRCA, COAD, HNSCC, KIRC, LIHC, LUAD, lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC), PAAD, and uterine corpus endometrial carcinoma (UCEC) (P < 0.05). Univariate logistic regression analysis of the survival of cancer patients

To clarify the impact of SQSTM1/p62 expression on survival outcomes, we conducted Kaplan‒Meier survival curve analysis for OS (Fig. 3). Elevated SQSTM1/p62 expression was associated with a reduced OS risk (protective effect) in adrenocortical carcinoma (ACC), BLCA, cervical squamous cell carcinoma and endocervical adenocarcinoma (CESC), sarcoma (SARC), and THCA patients (P < 0.05). In contrast, higher SQSTM1/p62 expression was associated with worse OS outcomes (hazardous effect) in GBM, KIRC, KIPR, LIHC, acute myeloid leukemia (LAML), LGG, pheochromocytoma, paraganglioma (PCPG), testicular germ cell tumors (TGCTs), and THYM (P < 0.05). According to the Cox regression forest plot (Fig. 4), SQSTM1/p62 expression is protective in ACC (HR = 0.411, 95% CI: 0.258–0.655), DLBC (HR = 0.341, 95% CI: 0.117–0.998), and SARC (HR = 0.665, 95% CI: 0.516–0.859) patients. Conversely, it is hazardous in KIRP (HR = 1.744, 95% CI: 1.301–2.338), LGG (HR = 1.606, 95% CI: 1.063–2.427), LIHC (HR = 1.307, 95% CI: 1.112–1.536), and THYM (HR = 3.505, 95% CI: 1.327–9.257).Fig. 3SQSTM1/p62 expression and OS in pancancer analysisFig. 4SQSTM1/p62 hazard ratios for OS across cancers

In summary, pancancer analyses revealed that SQSTM1/p62 has tumor specific, bidirectional prognostic effects. Although methodological variations exist across different analytical approaches (including logistic regression, Kaplan–Meier survival analysis, and Cox regression analysis), remarkably consistent prognostic patterns are observed in specific cancer types such as ACC and HNSCC. These findings highlight the urgent need for additional external validation studies. Special emphasis should be placed on malignancies where SQSTM1/p62 has hazardous effects.

SQSTM1/p62 expression and its prognostic value in HNSCC and NPC.

Our findings highlight the clinical significance of SQSTM1/p62 across multiple cancer types, particularly in HNSCC. Compared with those in normal tissues, the mRNA and protein expression levels of SQSTM1/p62 were significantly elevated in HNSCC tissues (Fig. 2). Survival analysis revealed that elevated SQSTM1/p62 expression was associated with an adverse prognosis in patients with HNSCC (Table 1, Figs. 3, 4, 5, 6). In public databases, NPC data are typically categorized as HNSCC datasets. Thus, the cohort of 86 NPC patients served as an independent external validation to evaluate the clinical utility of SQSTM1/p62.

Patient characteristics

The baseline demographic and clinical characteristics of the patients are summarized in Table 2. The median follow-up time was 46.8 months. The sex distribution did not significantly differ between the low and high SQSTM1/P62 expression groups (P = 0.457), with males being predominant in both groups. The mean age of the overall population was 55.3 ± 12.2 years, and the mean body mass index (BMI) was 23.2 ± 3.1. Using ROC curve analysis, the optimal cutoff values for age and BMI were determined to be 57 years and 21.82, respectively. No statistically significant difference was found in age, BMI, or histological type between the groups (P > 0.05). Both groups showed balanced distributions of T, N, and M categories as well as TNM stage (P > 0.05). In addition, treatment modalities including induction chemotherapy (50.0% vs 44.1%), concurrent chemoradiotherapy (79.1% vs 82.4%), and adjuvant chemotherapy (26.7% vs 35.3%), were similarly administered between groups without significant differences (P > 0.05). Prognostic value of SQSTM1/p62 in NPC

Given that all deceased patients in this study succumbed to tumor progression, DSS was employed as the survival metric. The results of the univariate and multivariate analyses are summarized in Table 3. Univariate Cox regression identified three significant prognostic factors for DSS: high SQSTM1/P62 expression (HR = 3.50, 95% CI 1.16–10.6, P = 0.027), age ≥ 57 years (HR = 4.74, 95% CI 1.57–14.32, P = 0.006), and adjuvant chemotherapy (HR = 0.11, 95% CI 0.01–0.85, P = 0.034). Considering the clinical·significance of·TNM stage, it was included in the multivariate analysis. After adjusting for confounding variables, both high SQSTM1/P62 expression (HR = 3.53, 95% CI 1.16–10.74, P = 0.026) and older age (HR = 3.92, 95% CI 1.27–12.11, P = 0.017) retained independent prognostic significance, whereas adjuvant chemotherapy showed borderline significance (HR = 0.15, P = 0.070). We further assessed the robustness of the SQSTM1/p62-prognosis association via multivariable-adjusted models (Table 4). After three rounds of adjustment for sex, age, BMI, pathology, TNM stage, induction chemotherapy, concurrent chemoradiotherapy, and adjuvant chemotherapy, the hazard ratios for DSS were 3.75 (1.23–11.44), 4.50 (1.37–14.73), and 4.25 (1.25–14.40), respectively. Discussion

As a key autophagy regulator, SQSTM1/p62 demonstrates cancer-specific expression patterns linked to clinical outcomes [11, 12]. In the present study, pancancer analysis revealed that SQSTM1/p62 is overexpressed in various malignancies, including HNSCC, and is associated with poor OS and DSS. In NPC, elevated SQSTM1/p62 expression is significantly linked to poor prognosis and serves as an independent predictor of reduced DSS. Subgroup and multivariable-adjusted analyses further confirmed a robust association between SQSTM1/p62 expression and adverse outcomes. These findings underscore its potential as a reliable biomarker for prognosis and therapeutic guidance in both pancancer and NPC.

Bioinformatics database-driven analysis (e.g., TCGA, GEO) provides a powerful approach for screening and validating key differentially expressed genes [13, 14]. In this study, TCGA-GTEx and CPTAC databases were employed to validate the pancancer significance of SQSTM1/p62 at the gene and protein levels, respectively. Our findings revealed a complex and cancer type-specific relationship between SQSTM1/p62 expression and patient prognosis. Autophagy may play both pro- and antitumor roles in different tumor types [15]. Several studies support the notion that the inhibition of autophagy or high expression of SQSTM1/p62 is associated with poor prognosis. A prognostic model constructed on the basis of eight mitophagy-related genes, including SQSTM1/p62, has demonstrated the ability to accurately predict the prognosis of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) patients [16]. The overexpression of SQSTM1/p62 in HCC is closely associated with decreased OS and adverse clinicopathological features [17]. In DLBC, elevated SQSTM1/p62 levels were significantly associated with advanced Ann Arbor staging, higher IPI scores, extranodal disease spread, and an increased Ki-67 proliferation index (p < 0.05) while also predicting a lower 2-year PFS rate [18]. However, opposing arguments are supported by other studies. A meta-analysis demonstrated that autophagy activation may play a pivotal role in conferring radioresistance, ultimately leading to reduced survival outcomes [19]. High SQSTM1/p62 expression is associated with favorable OS in patients with CRC [20]. The prognostic value of SQSTM1/p62 appears to vary depending on the cancer type.

Pancancer analysis and clinical evaluation revealed significantly elevated expression of SQSTM1/p62 in HNSCC and NPC, which was linked to poorer survival rates. These results are supported by numerous studies. High SQSTM1/p62 expression is significantly associated with poor prognosis and an increased risk of metastasis [21]. Increased SQSTM1/p62 expression is linked to lymph node involvement and distant organ metastasis [22]. The underlying regulatory mechanisms were investigated. The inhibition of autophagy by elevated MIR106A-5p drives the aggressive phenotype of NPC [23]. By targeting SQSTM1/p62, Lnc-MEG3 inhibits invasion, migration, and epithelial‒mesenchymal transition (EMT) processes [24]. SQSTM1/p62 promotes EMT by modulating the NF-κB pathway, which contributes to metastasis [21]. SQSTM1/p62 promotes NPC cell proliferation, migration and invasion via ERK pathway activation [22].

Targeting key regulatory factors associated with tumor progression represents a potential therapeutic strategy [25–27]. The inhibition of autophagic flux contributes to the development of resistance to chemotherapy [28]. Downregulation of SQSTM1/p62 augments the efficacy of cisplatin in NPC cells [21]. Theaflavin unexpectedly promotes apoptosis in NPC by inducing autophagy in vitro [29]. Berberine induces autophagy, thereby inhibiting the growth and metastasis of NPC cells [30]. AD facilitates the SQSTM1/p62-driven selective autophagic degradation of PD-L1 through the inhibition of STAT3 phosphorylation in non-small cell lung cancer [31]. Harnessing autophagy offers a potential approach to augment chemotherapy and immunotherapy responses in oral cancer [32]. Platyphyllenone promotes autophagy and apoptosis via the AKT and JNK MAPK signaling pathways [33]. Ursolic acid activates autophagy and apoptosis [34]. SQSTM1/p62 expression is often downregulated upon the induction of autophagy in these studies. Further validation through functional studies and prospective clinical trials is needed to establish whether SQSTM1/p62 could serve as a druggable target for NPC therapy.

Limitations

This study has several limitations that warrant consideration. First, the modest sample size (n = 86) derived from a single-center cohort may limit statistical power, as reflected in the wide confidence intervals for SQSTM1/p62. Second, the retrospective design of the study may have introduced inherent biases, such as selection bias and incomplete data. To address these issues, future investigations should prioritize prospective multicenter studies involving larger patient populations to confirm the prognostic significance of SQSTM1/p62. In addition, functional and mechanistic studies will provide further evidence to be incorporated in future refinements.

Conclusions

Pancancer analyses demonstrate that elevated SQSTM1/p62 expression is significantly associated with adverse clinical outcomes. Importantly, it serves as an independent prognostic factor for NPC patients. Our findings underscore its potential clinical utility in NPC prognosis prediction, though further validation through larger cohort studies is warranted.

Dosimetric Evaluation of Hippocampus Sparing Intensity Modulated Radiation Therapy in Patients With Stage T1-T2 and Stage T3-T4 Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma
Authors: Xiaofeng Zhou, Kui Wu, Niharika Prasad, Sanjay Jaiswal, Biao Jiang, Xia Li, Wenzheng Sun, Lingli Mao, Kanghua Huang, Minghan Shi, Shen Li, Qichun Wei
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo compare the hippocampus (HPC) dose reduced by HPC-sparing intensity modulated radiation therapy (IMRT) plans between nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) patients of stages T1-T2 and T3-T4, and to investigate the correlation between the dose of the HPC and the volume of PTVnx70 (the planning target volume of the primary tumor in the nasopharynx that received 70 Gy).Methods and MaterialsFifty-eight NPC patients were retrospectively evaluated. HPC-nonsparing IMRT or sparing IMRT for each patient was designed according to the protocol for NPC. Dose-volume histogram was used to evaluate the IMRT plans for each patient. The difference in values of HPC parameters (eg, Dmin[NS] – Dmin[S]) between HPC-sparing and nonsparing plans in the stage T1-T2 group and stage T3-T4 group were compared. The correlations between the dose of the HPC and the volume of PTVnx70 were analyzed.ResultsThere was no significance between HPC-sparing and nonsparing IMRT plans. Compared with the HPC-nonsparing plans, the HPC-sparing plans significantly decreased both dosimetric and volumetric parameters for the HPC (P < .05), except for Dmin, D98%, and V5. The medians of Dmedian[NS] – Dmedian[S], Dmean[NS] – Dmean[S], D40%[NS] – D40%[S], V30[NS] – V30[S], V40[NS] – V40[S] and V50[NS] – V50[S] in the T1-T2 group were significantly lower than in the T3-T4 group (P < .05), respectively. Both dosimetric and volumetric parameters for the HPC were positively correlated with the volume of PTVnx70 in HPC-sparing and HPC-nonsparing plans (P < .05). The volume of PTVnx70 was positively correlated with Dmedian[NS] – Dmedian[S], Dmean[NS] – Dmean[S], D40%[NS] – D40%[S], V40[NS] – V40[S] and V50[NS] – V50[S] (P < .05).ConclusionsHPC-sparing IMRT plans may play a more significant role in decreasing Dmedian, Dmean, D40%, and V30-V50 of HPC in NPC patients with stages T3-T4 than those in stages T1-T2. PTVnx70 volume of NPC patients is positively correlated with all dosimetric and volumetric parameters of HPC and the reduction of specific dosage parameters by HPC-sparing IMRT plans.

Introduction

Nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) is a malignant tumor with a high incidence in China. NPC patients need comprehensive treatment based on radiation therapy (RT), with or without chemotherapy. Intensity modulated radiation therapy (IMRT) has replaced 2-dimensional RT as the standard treatment for patients with NPC and has achieved long-term clinical benefits, including disease-free survival and overall survival.1 However, because of the anatomic proximity of the nasopharynx tumor, the hippocampus (HPC), nondelineated in the target volume, is still exposed to a specific dose of radiation.2 Previous clinical studies have shown that radiation-induced HPC damage in patients after cranial irradiation may lead to neurocognitive decline, particularly deficits in learning, memory, and spatial processing.3 Gondi et al4 demonstrated that reducing the dose of HPC can effectively protect patient's memories and quality of life. Therefore, protecting the HPC during RT for patients with NPC is crucial for enhancing their quality of life.

According to the RTOG 0615 treatment protocol,5 the prescribed dose for primary nasopharyngeal tumors is 70 Gy in patients with NPC. The dose of HPC varies based on the T stage of the primary nasopharynx tumor. Therefore, in this study, the impact of 2 planning methods of IMRT (HPC-sparing and HPC-nonsparing) on the dose received by HPC is studied in patients with different T-stage NPC.

IMRT protocol

All enrolled patients underwent 3-dimensional magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) using a 3T MR scanner (GE Medical Systems). The protocol of MRI examination included axial T1- and T2-weighted images, as well as axial, sagittal, and coronal postcontrast T1-weighted images. For all enrolled patients, the head, neck, and shoulders were immobilized in a hyperextended position by a perforated, thermoplastic mask. A contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) scan with a 3.75-mm slice thickness was used for simulation and radiation planning and was transferred into the Eclipse Treatment Planning System.

We have designed 2 radiotherapeutic plans: conventional HPC-nonsparing IMRT or HPC-sparing IMRT for each NPC patient recruited in our study. According to the consensus on the target's delineation for NPC among Chinese experts in 20106 and the RTOG 0615 protocol,5 the gross tumor volume (GTV), clinical target volume, and planning target volume (PTV) were delineated based on the patient's MR images, nasopharyngoscopy, and clinical examination. In our institution, GTV includes primary tumors in the nasopharynx (GTVnx) and metastatic lymph nodes in the neck (GTVnd). The PTV of the primary tumor in the nasopharynx (PTVnx70) was defined as GTVnx with an added 0.3-cm margin and received 70 Gy in 32 fractions. High-risk subclinical disease (CTV60) was defined as GTV with an added 1-cm margin. PTV60 was defined as CTV60 with an added 0.3-cm margin and received 60 Gy in 32 fractions. Lower-risk subclinical disease (CTV54.4), with a 0.3-cm margin, received 54.4 Gy in 32 fractions. All plans were normalized such that at least 95% of the PTV was covered by the prescribed dose. The volume of PTV receiving more than 110% of the prescription dose did not exceed 20%, and receiving less than 93% of the prescription dose did not exceed 1%. No more than 110% of the prescription dose was outside of the PTV. Organs at risk (OAR) were delineated and respected the dose constraints described in the consensus on the target's delineation for NPC among Chinese experts in 20106 and the RTOG 0615 protocol.5 It includes lenses, eyes, optic nerves, optic chiasm, temporal lobes, brain stem, cochleas, and spinal cord. Patients treated with HPC-sparing IMRT received a maximum dose of 40 Gy and a mean dose of 20 Gy in the HPC.

To maintain consistency, both HPC-sparing and HPC-nonsparing plans were developed by the same medical physicist using the standard 9-field coplanar beam IMRT technique with a dynamic multileaf collimator (dMLC) and a simultaneously integrated boost. All plans were to be delivered by 6 MV photon beams of a Varian Trilogy (Varian Medical Systems).

HPC contouring

The axial postcontrast T1-weighted images were imported into the Eclipse Treatment Planning System (Varian Medical Systems) and fused with the planning CT images. A senior radiation oncologist and physicists completed the fusion with the assistance of a licensed neuroradiologist. The HPC is located between the inferior temporal corner and the ambient cistern, posterior to the amygdala, and inferior to the crus and splenium of the corpus callosum. On the T1-weighted image, the HPC is hypointensive and similar to gray matter. According to the method described in previous studies,7,8 HPC contouring began at the most anterior portion of the lateral ventricle, continued posterocranially along the medial edge of the temporal horn, and terminated at the lateral edges of the quadrigeminal cisterns before the appearance of the crus of the fornix. Treatment plan evaluation

A dose-volume histogram (DVH) was used to evaluate the target and OAR doses. The following parameters were used to assess the 2 IMRT plans:

The conformity index (CI) is the ratio of prescription isodose coverage volume to target volume9: CI = Vpres/VT, where Vpres is the volume receiving a dose greater than or equal to the prescription dose, and VT is the volume of PTV. If the CI is equal to 1 and the target volume is completely contained within the prescription isodose volume, the target volume is completely covered and irradiated. If the CI is less than 1, the target volume is only partially covered and irradiated; If the CI is more than 1, the target volume is liberally covered.

Target coverage (TC) is the ratio of the target volume receiving at least the prescription dose (VT, pres) to the entire target volume (VT)10: TC = VT, pres/VT. If TC is equal to 1, the target is completely covered.

Homogeneity index (HI) is the ratio of the dose difference between the most significant dose delivered to 2% of the target volume (D2%) and the dose to 98% of the target volume (D98%) to the median dose(Dmedian)11: HI = (D2% – D98%)/Dmedian. Smaller values of HI indicate more homogenous target volume irradiation. The HI equal to 0 indicates complete homogeneity of dose within the target.

The CI, TC, and HI for PTVnx70 and PTV60 were reported in 2 IMRT plans.

HPC-sparing and HPC-nonsparing IMRT plans comparison

The dosimetric parameters for PTVnx70, PTV60, and the OARs were calculated and recorded in both HPC-sparing and nonsparing IMRT plans in the treatment planning system. Dmean, Dmedian, Dmin, Dmax, D2%, and D98% of PTVnx70 and PTV60 were calculated and recorded for the 2 plans, respectively. Dmax, Dmean, and Dmin of the OARs were calculated and recorded, respectively. To analyze the HPC dose, a series of dosimetric parameters (Dmean, Dmedian, Dmax, D2%, D40%) and volumetric parameters (V20, V30, V40, and V50) were calculated and recorded. All of the above-mentioned indices and DVH parameters of HPC-sparing and HPC-nonsparing plans were compared. The difference values of HPC parameters (eg, Dmedian[NS] – Dmedian[S]) between HPC-sparing [S] and HPC-nonsparing [NS] plans were calculated and compared in the early-T stage group (T1-T2 group) and the advanced-T stage group (T3-T4 group). The relative decrease of dosimetric and volumetric parameters of HPC between HPC-sparing IMRT and HPC-nonsparing IMRT plans was also calculated and compared in the early-T stage group (T1-T2 group) and the advanced-T stage group (T3-T4 group). The dosimetric and volumetric parameters mentioned in the present study were as follows:•Dmin, the minimal dose of the PTV, OAR, or HPC•D98%, the dose delivered to 98% of the PTV, OAR, or HPC•Dmedian, the median dose of the PTV, OAR, or HPC•Dmean, the mean dose of the PTV, OAR, or HPC•D40%, the dose delivered to 40% of HPC•D2%, the dose delivered to 2% of PTV or HPC•Dmax, the maximal dose of the PTV, OAR, or HPC•Vn, percentage of HPC volume receiving ≥ n Gy; eg, V30, percentage of HPC volume receiving of 30 Gy

For median and maximum values, the relative decrease results were obtained by normalizing the difference in values with the value of HPC-nonsparing plans. For other values, differences were normalized with standard deviations.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed with the Statistical Package for Social Sciences software package, version 19.0 (IBM Corp.). We used the paired t-test to compare the paired sample, and data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation. The Pearson coefficient was used to analyze the correlations between dosimetric and volumetric parameters among the volume of PTVnx70. We used the Mann-Whitney U test to compare the unpaired sample, including the difference in values of HPC parameters between HPC-sparing and HPC-nonsparing plans in T1-T2 group and T3-T4 group, and the data were expressed as medians (25th-75th percentiles). One-way ANOVA and post hoc least significant difference test were used to compare the mean difference value of dosimetric and volumetric parameters for the HPC among different T-stage groups (T1 group to T4 group). A 2-tailed P value < .05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

PTV dosimetric analysis

The clinical information and volume data are summarized in Table 1. The mean volumes of PTVnx70 and PTV60 were 52.10 cm3 (range, 4.93-248.05 cm3), and 525.26 cm3 (range, 308.33-1166.29 cm3), respectively. Both HPC-sparing and nonsparing IMRT plans of all patients meet the protocol criteria. The mean values and standard deviation for DVH parameters of the PTVnx70 and PTV60 in IMRT plans with or without HPC-sparing are shown in Table E1. For both PTVnx70 and PTV60, there was no significant difference in Dmax, D2%, Dmean, Dmedian, D98%, or Dmin between the HPC-sparing and HPC-nonsparing plans. There was also no significant difference in CI, TC, or HI (Table E2).

OAR dosimetric analysis

Table E3 summarizes the mean Dmin, Dmax, and Dmean of the lenses, eyes, optic nerves, optic chiasm, temporal lobes, brain stem, cochlea, and spinal cord of the HPC-sparing and HPC-nonsparing plans for all patients. Dosimetric parameters of certain OARs, including the lenses, eyes, optic nerves, and temporal lobes, were significantly lower in the HPC-sparing plans than in the HPC-nonsparing plans. The maximum dose to the eyes decreased from 31.34 Gy (HPC-nonsparing plans) to 29.76 Gy (HPC-sparing plans) (P < .001) at an average of 5.04%, and the Dmean of the eyes decreased from 8.70 Gy (HPC-nonsparing plans) to 8.45 Gy (HPC-sparing plans) (P < .001) at an average of 2.87%. A similar tendency was found for the optic nerves: Dmax and Dmean of the HPC-nonsparing plans were significantly higher than those of the HPC-sparing plans (P < .05). However, the HPC-sparing plans contributed to Dmean of the brain stem increasing from 30.01 Gy to 31.06 Gy (P = .001) at an average of 3.50%, and the Dmax of the temporal lobes increased from 63.49 Gy to 63.84 Gy (P = .012) at an average of 0.55%. There was no significant difference in the doses for the other OARs between the HPC-sparing and HPC-nonsparing plans.

HPC dosimetric analysis

The mean volume of the HPC was 3.17 cm3 (range, 2.82-3.9 cm3) (Table 1). As shown in Table E4, the average minimum dose (Dmin) to the HPC was 5.10 Gy in HPC-nonsparing plans, and 5.07 Gy in HPC-sparing plans (P = .480); the average dose delivered to 98% of the HPC (D98%) was 6.11 Gy in HPC-nonsparing plans and 6.09 Gy in HPC-sparing plans (P = .804). In comparison to HPC-nonsparing plans, HPC-sparing plans decreased the average Dmedian, Dmean, D40%, D2%, and Dmax by 23.81% (20.33 vs 15.49, P = .000), 22.30% (21.52 vs 16.72, P = .000), 27.55% (23.56 vs 17.07, P = .000), 18.26% (42.56 vs 34.79, P = .000), and 13.38% (49.84 vs 43.17, P = .000), respectively.

The average proportion of the HPC volume irradiated by ≥ 5 Gy (V5) was 89.10% in HPC-sparing plans and 89.51% in HPC-nonsparing plans (P = .126). The average V10, V15, V20, V30, and V40 were 70.93%, 50.48%, 29.05%, 10.53%, and 3.89% in HPC-sparing plans, significantly lower than 74.45%, 62.03%, 49.08%, 27.63% and 10.91% in HPC-nonsparing plans (P = .000). The average V50 was 1.55% in HPC-sparing plans, significantly lower than 3.16% in HPC-nonsparing plans (P = .002) (Table E4).

Compared with the HPC-nonsparing plans, the HPC-sparing plans significantly decreased both dosimetric and volumetric parameters for the HPC (P < .05), except for Dmin (P = .480), D98% (P = .804), and V5 (P = .126).

The Pearson correlation analysis revealed that both dosimetric and volumetric parameters for the HPC have positively correlated with the volume of PTVnx70 in HPC-nonsparing plans and sparing plans (P < .05) (Table E5, Table E6, Fig. E1A and Fig. E1B).

Different parameters change in different T-stage groups

Dosimetric parameters (Dmedian-Dmax) and volumetric parameters (V20-V50) in patients of stages T1-T2 were lower than those of stages T3-T4 (P < .05) (Table 2 and (Table 3). The difference in the value of dosimetric and volumetric parameters for the HPC are listed in Table 4 and Table E7. The medians of Dmedian[NS] – Dmedian[S], Dmean[NS] – Dmean[S], and D40%[NS] – D40%[S] were 1.292 Gy, 3.406 Gy and 3.960 Gy in the T1-T2 group, significantly lower than 7.506 Gy, 5.292 Gy and 8.700 Gy in the T3-T4 group (P < .05), respectively. However, the medians of D2%[NS] – D2%[S], and Dmax[NS] – Dmax[S] were 8.525 Gy and 8.017 Gy in the T1-T2 group, significantly higher than 5.995 Gy and 3.810 Gy in the T3-T4 group (P < .05), respectively (Table 4 and Fig. 2a). The median of difference value of Dmin between HPC-sparing [S] and HPC-nonsparing [NS] plans (Dmin[NS] – Dmin[S]) was 0.012Gy in T1-T2 group and 0.007 Gy in T3-T4 group (P = .8500) (Table E7). Discussion

The present study's findings demonstrate that HPC-sparing IMRT plans result in a decrease in the radiation dose in the HPC in patients with NPC. Furthermore, HPC-sparing IMRT plans contributed to a more significant reduction in some dosimetric parameters (Dmedian, Dmean, and D40%) and volumetric parameters (V30-V50) of HPC in patients of stage T3-T4 than those of stage T1-T2. The reduction of Dmedian, Dmean, D40%, V40, and V50 between HPC-nonsparing IMRT plans and HPC-sparing IMRT plans were positively correlated with PTVnx70.

With the increasing survival rate and favorable outcomes of NPC, the adverse effects of postchemoradiation therapy, especially the neurofunctional deficits, have attracted increasing attention. The previous study revealed, for the first time, solid enhanced nodular lesions as the earliest MRI abnormalities of radiation encephalopathy following NPC RT.12 Several other studies,13,14 investigated radiation-induced brain damage and neurofunctional deficits in patients with NPC treated with RT.

HPC is one of the 2 regions of the brain where multipotent stem cells are located and play a vital role in neurocognitive function. The HPC is located in the medial temporal lobe and receives an unnecessary radiation dose because of its anatomic proximity to the nasopharynx tumor. There was no specific dose restriction for HPC in IMRT protocols for NPC such as RTOG 0225,15 RTOG 0615,5 and the International Guideline on Dose Prioritization and Acceptance Criteria in Radiation Therapy Planning for Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma.16

Increasingly studies have demonstrated the crucial role of radiation-induced early hippocampal injury17,18 and late hippocampal atrophy.19,20 Specifically, hippocampal atrophy is correlated with cognitive impairment.21,22 Therefore, it is necessary to pay more attention to radiation-induced HPC damage. Khodayari et al2 and Han et al10 demonstrated that HPC in patients with locally advanced NPC receives a significant radiation dose, and HPC-sparing IMRT planning significantly decreases this dose. In the present study, we decreased the hippocampal dose and maintained other OAR (brain stem, optic nerve, cochlea, etc.) dose restrictions in all patients while not affecting TC and homogeneity. The mean values of all dosimetric and volumetric parameters except V50 in HPC-nonsparing IMRT plans are lower than those in previous studies.2,10 In comparison to HPC-nonsparing plans, HPC-sparing plans significantly decreased the average Dmedian, Dmean, D40%, and Dmax by 23.81%, 22.30%, 27.55%, and 13.38%, respectively. The mean values of parameters and decreasing reduction by HPC-sparing plans are lower than in previous studies.2,10 A possible explanation could be that half of the enrolled patients were of early-stage T1-T2. The correlation analysis revealed that both dosimetric and volumetric parameters for the HPC were positively correlated with the volume of PTVnx70 in HPC-nonsparing and HPC-sparing plans.

To compare the HPC dose reduced by HPC-sparing IMRT plans between patients in stages T1-T2 and T3-T4, we analyzed the difference in values of dosimetric and volumetric parameters. HPC-sparing IMRT plans contributed to a more significant reduction in specific dosimetric parameters (Dmedian, Dmean, and D40%) and volumetric parameters (V30-V50) of HPC in patients of stage T3-T4 than those of stage T1-T2, both absolute decrease and relative decrease. This finding indicates that HPC-sparing IMRT plans may play a more significant role in decreasing Dmedian, Dmean, D40%, and V30 to V50 in patients with stages T3-T4 than those of stage T1-T2. The Pearson correlation analysis revealed that: (1) the volume of PTVnx70 was positively correlated with Dmedian[NS] – Dmedian[S], Dmean[NS] – Dmean[S], D40%[NS] – D40%[S], V40[NS] – V40[S] and V50[NS] – V50[S] (P < .05, and correlation coefficient > 0.2); (2) the volume of PTVnx70 was negatively correlated with Dmin[NS] – Dmin[S], D98%[NS] – D98%[S], V10[NS] – V10[S] and V15[NS] – V15[S] (P < .05, and correlation coefficient < –0.2). In other words, it is easier to decrease Dmedian, Dmean, D40%, V40, and V50 in a patient whose volume of PTVnx70 is higher and harder to decrease Dmin, D98%, V10, and V15 in a patient whose volume of PTVnx70 is higher. We may need to investigate the cut-off points of PTVnx70 that influence the dose to the HPC in NPC patients. The values of Dmedian, Dmean, D40%, V40, and V50 are more important than Dmin, D98%, V10, and V15 to preserve the neurofunction of HPC. The reduction of Dmax was not significantly correlated with PTVnx70.

Statistical significance was found in the difference value average of dosimetric and volumetric parameters for the HPC among the T4 group versus the T1 group, the T2 group, and the T3 group in the present study. We conclude that the variable volume and different local invasion of the primary tumor in the nasopharynx resulted in this statistical significance in the difference value average of dosimetric and volumetric parameters for the HPC. We will launch another research on this issue with a larger sample size in each T-stage group.

The present study revealed that Dmean was positively correlated with the volume of PTVnx70 in both HPC-nonsparing IMRT plans (r = 0.6758, P < .0001) and HPC-sparing IMRT plans (r = 0.7042, P < .0001); the difference value of Dmean between HPC-nonsparing and sparing IMRT plans, Dmean[NS] – Dmean[S] was positively correlated with the volume of PTVnx70 (r = 0.2832, P = .0312). A similar condition was found for the volumetric parameter, V40. Seibert et al19 documented that the mean hippocampal dose was negatively correlated with hippocampal volume loss (r = –0.24, P = .03); mean hippocampal volume was significantly reduced 1 year after high-dose (>40 Gy) RT but not after low-dose (<10 Gy) RT. The rate of atrophy in the high-dose hippocampi was also significantly higher than in the low-dose hippocampi. Hippocampal atrophy is a hallmark feature. It is correlated with cognitive decline.21,22 Hence, it is reasonable to deduce that Dmean and V40 are correlated with HPC volume loss and cognitive decline. To the best of our knowledge, the present study is the first to investigate the correlation between the dose of the HPC (dosimetric and volumetric parameters) and the volume of PTVnx70. The present study is also the first to investigate the correlation between the HPC-sparing IMRT plans to reduce the dose of the HPC (eg, Dmean[NS] – Dmean[S]) and the volume of PTVnx70. In the future, we will investigate a cut-off point for the volume of PTVnx70, which influences the dose of the HPC in NPC patients. We also need to consider HPC as an OAR and consider Dmean and V40 as the dose constraint parameters of HPC.

In the present study, we constructed HPC-sparing IMRT rather than HPC-sparing volume-modulated arc therapy (VMAT). We made HPC-sparing IMRT plans to decrease a part of dosimetric parameters for certain OARs, including the lenses, eyes, optic nerves, and temporal lobes. However, the HPC-sparing IMRT plans contributed to the Dmean of the brain stem increasing from 30.01 Gy to 31.06 Gy (P = .001) at an average of 3.50%, whereas Dmax and Dmin of the brain stem in HPC-nonsparing IMRT plans did not differ significantly from Dmax and Dmin of the brain stem in HPC-sparing IMRT plans. To our knowledge, Dmax of the brain stem is more important than Dmean to preserve the function of the brain stem. In a study enrolling 11 locally advanced NPC patients, Gu et al23 demonstrated that both HPC-sparing VMAT (HS-VMAT) and HPC-sparing IMRT with a dynamic multileaf collimator (HS-dMLC IMRT) could significantly reduce the radiation doses on the HPC, having little impact on the TC. HS-VMAT showed more advantage in decreasing radiation doses on the brain stem (V10, V20, V30, V40, and V50) as compared to HS-dMLC IMRT, whereas Dmax and Dmean in HS-VMAT and HS-dMLC IMRT were not reported. Although HS-VMAT showed more advantages than HS-dMLC IMRT in decreasing radiation doses on the brain stem, HS-dMLC IMRT is more accessible than VMAT in China, especially in underdeveloped areas. Therefore, HPC-sparing IMRT (dMLC) provides an option in many radiation oncology centers.

Neurocognitive impairment has emerged as one of the most concerning adverse effects of cancer after chemoradiation therapy. Previous clinical and animal studies have suggested that radiation-induced HPC damage may play a crucial role in neurocognitive impairment after cranial irradiation.24, 25, 26, 27 One of the mechanisms by which radiation induces neurocognitive impairment has been proposed to relate to synaptic plasticity and long-lasting damage to hippocampal neurogenesis.28 HPC damage can result in memory impairment. Various pharmacologic or nonpharmacological interventions for neurocognitive impairment have been tested in clinical studies. However, no clinical protocols or guidelines for the management of patients with cancer therapy-induced neurocognitive impairment are available. As a pharmacologic intervention, lithium protects the HPC against hippocampal neuron apoptosis, and donepezil reduces atrophy of the basal forebrain cholinergic system, a network projecting to the cortex, entorhinal cortex, and HPC.29 As a nonpharmacological intervention, pluripotent stem cells as a source for cell-replacement therapies may contribute to HPC function recovery.30 Increasing studies on decreasing the dose to the HPC and understanding the basic mechanisms underlying radiation-induced HPC damage in patients with NPC will alleviate neurocognitive impairment, and novel treatment strategies will be identified.

Our study findings should be interpreted in light of the following potential limitations. Firstly, we did not compare the effectiveness of HPC-sparing IMRT and HPC-sparing VMAT. In this study, HPC-sparing IMRT significantly decreased the radiation dose of the HPC but had little influence on the TC and other OARs. Taking the accessibility and convenience of IMRT into consideration, HPC-sparing IMRT (dMLC) is acceptable and useful in practice. Secondly, we did not investigate a cut-off point for PTVnx70 volume, which influences the HPC dose in NPC patients. Obtaining this cut-off point is favorable for reducing the dose of the HPC and neuro-cognitive protection in NPC patients. In the future, we will construct HPC-sparing IMRT and HPC-sparing VMAT to reduce the dose of the HPC and investigate a cut-off point of PTVnx70 volume influencing the dose of the HPC in NPC patients.

Conclusions

HPC-sparing IMRT plans can significantly decrease the HPC dose and have little influence on the TC and the dose of other OARs in NPC patients. The PTVnx70 volume of NPC patients is positively correlated with all dosimetric and volumetric parameters of the HPC and the reduction of specific dosage parameters by HPC-sparing IMRT plans. The results of our study indicate that HPC-sparing IMRT plans may play a more significant role in decreasing Dmedian, Dmean, D40%, and V30 to V50 in patients with stages T3-T4 than those in patients with stages T1-T2. It is advantageous to spare the HPC in delivering IMRT plans in NPC patients, especially in patients in stages T3-T4.

Synergistic effects of zinc oxide and selenium nanoparticles in drinking water on broiler chicken growth, antioxidant, and immunity responses
Authors: Hardi Ahmed Karim AL-JAF, Saman Abdulmajid Rashid
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Highlights•ZnO and Se nanoparticles improved growth and immune responses in broiler chickens.•Combined ZnO and Se NPs enhanced serum protein and antioxidant enzyme levels.•ZnO and Se NP supplementation reduced liver and kidney stress markers in broilers.•Jejunal morphology was improved by ZnO and Se NPs.

Introduction

Nanotechnology represents one of the most transformative advances in modern science, with broad applications in medicine, environmental sustainability, agriculture, and animal production. At the nanoscale (1–100 nm), materials acquire unique physicochemical properties, including a larger surface area, enhanced solubility, and superior biological reactivity compared with their conventional counterparts (Hatab et al., 2024; Roco & Williams, 1999). These features offer new opportunities to enhance nutrient utilization and address longstanding challenges in poultry nutrition, particularly in intensive broiler chicken production systems where oxidative stress, immune suppression, and poor feed efficiency persist as significant barriers to optimal productivity (Guo et al., 2025; Hafez, 2005).

Zinc is a critical trace element involved in numerous enzymatic processes, protein synthesis, tissue development, and immune regulation. Conventional supplementation with inorganic salts such as zinc oxide (ZnO) and zinc sulfate, or organic complexes like zinc methionine, is effective but often limited by poor absorption or high cost. Recent advances in zinc oxide nanoparticles (ZnO—NPs) have provided a practical alternative. Their small size and large surface area improve intestinal absorption and bioavailability, translating into better growth performance, enhanced intestinal morphology, and stronger immune and antioxidant responses in broilers (Fathi et al., 2016; Hidayat et al., 2020; Khajeh Bami et al., 2018).

Selenium plays an equally indispensable role as a constituent of selenoproteins such as glutathione peroxidase, protecting cells from oxidative damage, regulating thyroid hormones, and supporting immune competence (Jianhua et al., 2000; Yang et al., 2024). Traditional supplementation with sodium selenite or selenium yeast is hampered by limited stability and variable absorption. Selenium nanoparticles (Se-NPs), however, exhibit greater stability and efficiency, leading to improved antioxidant enzyme activity, reduced oxidative stress, and higher disease resistance in poultry (Alagawany et al., 2021; Khajeh Bami et al., 2021).

Although Zn and Se have distinct physiological roles, their functions are complementary. Zinc supports protein metabolism and gut health, while selenium provides antioxidant protection and immune regulation. Their co-supplementation has been shown to exert synergistic effects in aquaculture (Fasil et al., 2021; Ghazi et al., 2022; Swain et al., 2019), yet data in broilers remains insufficient. Moreover, most research has focused on feed incorporation, which may limit homogeneity and increase nutrient interactions. Delivery through drinking water provides an alternative strategy, ensuring more uniform intake and potentially greater bioavailability (Mohammed et al., 2023; Shinohara et al., 2018). Based on these gaps, the study examined the combined effects of ZnO- and Se-NPs administered via drinking water on growth performance, intestinal morphology, antioxidant status, and humoral immunity in broiler chickens. By integrating dual-nanoparticle supplementation with an alternative delivery route and evaluating multiple physiological outcomes, this work offers insight with direct implications for improving poultry health and productivity under modern production challenges.

Materials and methods

Experimental design

The experimental protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of the University of Sulaimani (Administrative Issue No 7/12/2192, dated October 13, 2024). All procedures complied with established guidelines for the ethical treatment of animals in research. A total of 252 one-day-old unsexed broiler chicks (Ross 308) were distributed into six treatment groups. Each treatment included six replicates, with seven chickens per replicate (n= 7). Bird management

The battery cage system (120 cm length × 60 cm width × 80 cm height) was used for breeding under controlled environmental conditions, including an initial temperature of 33 °C, relative humidity of 50–60 %, and an age-adjusted lighting program (20–24 h light, 1–4 h dark). All birds were vaccinated against Newcastle Disease Virus (NDV) at 7 days of age using the Lasota (eye drop) and Clone (injection) strains. A basal diet provided to the birds was formulated based on National Research Council NRC (1994) guidelines to meet standard nutritional requirements during the experimental period (Table 2). Birds had ad libitum access to feed and drinking water. Body weight (BW), feed consumption, residual feed, and mortality were recorded weekly. Feed intake (FI) was calculated based on the weekly feed consumption and residual feed. BW gain (BWG) and feed conversation ratio (FCR) were determined using these data. Preparation of Zn and Se NPs

ZnO—NP powder (35–45 nm and purity: 99+ %) and Se-NPs (10–45 nm and purity: 99.9 %), synthesised in a 1000 mg/L aqueous colloidal solution, were procured from US Research Nanomaterials, Inc (Houston, USA). Inorganic ZnO and sodium selenite were obtained from the local veterinary clinic (VAPCO Company, Jordan). To ensure stability and prevent agglomeration of NPs when preparing 35 and 70 mg/L of ZnO—NPs powder in drinking water, 0.5 % citric acid was added during preparation. Specifically, 3.5 g of ZnO NPs was suspended in 1 L of distilled water, followed by vigorous stirring at 600 rpm for 10 min and ultrasonic bath treatment for 20 min. To prepare the working solutions, the ZnO nanoparticle was diluted with distilled water to final concentrations of 35 mg/L and 70 mg/L, respectively, for administration via drinking water (Shinohara et al., 2018).

Blood collection

At the end of the study (35 days of age), blood samples were randomly collected from six chickens per treatment via the brachial wing vein. Approximately 5 mL of blood was drawn into sterile Yellow Gel clot-activator tubes, allowed to clot at room temperature (22–25 °C), and then centrifuged at 5000 rpm for 10 min to obtain serum, which was stored at −20 °C for subsequent analyses. The activities of antioxidant enzymes, including glutathione peroxidase (GPx; Cat. No E0298Ch), superoxide dismutase (SOD; Cat. No E0011Ch), and malondialdehyde (MDA; Cat. No E0171Ch) obtained from BT‑Laboratory (Shanghai Korain Biotech Co.), were measured using ELISA kits (Aayo RT-2100 microplate reader), following the manufacturers’ protocols. All incubations were conducted at 37.0 ± 0.5 °C. Biochemical parameters were analysed using commercial kits and an automated analyser (BIOLIS 50i). These included serum total protein, albumin, and globulin concentrations, as well as liver enzymes, alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and aspartate aminotransferase (AST). Kidney function was assessed through blood urea nitrogen (BUN) and creatinine levels.

Histological examination

Histological samples from the jejunum were collected from one chicken per replicate (six chickens per treatment), randomly selected at 35 days of age. Tissue samples were preserved in 10 % formalin and embedded in paraffin. Sections of 5 µm thickness were obtained on glass slides, stained with hematoxylin and eosin, and processed according to the standard procedures (Dogra et al., 2023). Villus height (VH), villus width (VW), and crypt depth (CD) were measured using OPTICA microscopy and ProView image analysis software (OPTIKA S.r.l., Ponteranica, Italy). All images were obtained using a 4 × objective lens (Fig. 1). For the assessment of morphometric indices, including VH, VW, and CD, five intact and properly oriented villi were selected from each cross-section of the intestinal tissue slide. Immune response assessment

Humoral immunity was assessed by measuring hemagglutination inhibition (HI) antibody titres against NDV in broiler chickens at 28 and 35 days post-vaccination. One chicken per replicate (six chickens per treatment) was randomly selected, and blood samples were collected from the brachial vein. Serum was separated by centrifugation at 5000 rpm for 15 min and stored at −20 °C for analysis. The HI test was conducted using the standard microtiter plate method, as described by (Ebrahimi et al., 2015; Seidavi et al., 2014). Specifically, 25 μL of phosphate-buffered saline was put into each well in a 96-well V-bottom plate. Subsequently, 25 μL of serum was added to the first well of each row and two-fold serial dilutions were performed in 96-well plates. An equal volume (25 μL) of NDV antigen, containing four hemagglutination antigen units was then placed in all wells and the plates were further incubated for 30 min at room temperature. Then, 25 µL of 1 % chicken RBCs were added and then plates were incubated for another 30 min before reading. The HI titers were expressed as the reciprocal of the highest dilution that inhibited hemagglutination.

Statistical analysis

All data were analysed using XLSTAT software (version 14, 2019) following a completely randomised design. Treatment effects were evaluated using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). When significant differences were identified (p< 0.05), treatment means were compared using Duncan's Multiple Range Test (Duncan, 1955). Results are presented as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM).

Result

Growth performance

As shown in Table 3, the dietary inclusion of ZnO and Se NPs significantly affected broiler chicken performance (p< 0.05). Particularly, BW at 35 days was significantly influenced by the inclusion of Zn and Se NPs in their drinking water. The highest BW was observed in treatment T5 (2512.7 ± 71.8), followed by T3 (2484.8 ± 65.9), both of which were significantly higher than those of the negative (T1) and positive (T2) controls. The positive control (T2) showed significantly higher BW (P = 0.001) compared with that of negative control, which did not receive Zn and Se supplements through drinking water. BWG over the 1–35 day period followed a similar pattern (P = 0.001). T5 achieved the highest BWG, and T3 exhibited superior performance compared with that of T1. Conversely, in terms of total feed intake (FI), T5 and T3 consumed the most feed throughout the study. Despite the elevated intake, these treatments also maintained the most efficient feed conversion ratios (FCR), indicating a favourable balance between feed intake and growth. In contrast, T4 had high (P = 0.001) FI (3855.2 ± 22.9) but displayed a relatively poor (P = 0.043) FCR (1.76 ± 0.06) and lower BW (2240.4 ± 74.8) compared with those of T5 and T3. Intestinal morphology

As shown in Table 4, morphometric analysis of the jejunum revealed significant effects of NP supplementation on VH (P = 0.003), while no statistically significant differences existed among treatments in VW, CD, or the VH:CD ratio. Discussion

Trace minerals play essential roles in the growth, development, and maintenance of birds. Particularly, Zn is an essential trace element playing a pivotal role in poultry growth, health, and immune function (Raza et al., 2023; Yin et al., 2022). Similarly, Avery and Hoffmann (2018) reported that Se is a key component of selenoproteins and antioxidant enzymes protecting tissues from oxidative damage and supports metabolic functions. Here, the superior growth performance observed in the combined ZnO—NP and Se-NP supplement treatments (T5 and T3) could be attributed to multiple factors. Both Zn and Se are known to enhance appetite, nutrient digestibility, and growth hormone activity when provided at optimal levels (Naz et al., 2016; Surai, 2018). Particularly, Zn plays a crucial role in the function of digestive enzymes and maintaining intestinal integrity, which can result in improved feed conversion and weight gain (Ogbuewu & Mbajiorgu, 2023). This indicates that the NPs provided these nutrients in easily absorbed and utilised forms, enhancing the overall growth. The nanosized particles likely increased the surface area and solubility of Zn and Se in the gut, boosting their uptake compared with those of the conventional sources (Abdelnour et al., 2021; Almeida et al., 2024). Furthermore, the positive control receiving inorganic Zn and Se (T2) showed improved performance compared with that of the negative control, however, it was outperformed by the nano-supplemented treatments, indicating a clear advantage of NP delivery. The combination of Zn and Se was particularly effective, likely because these micronutrients work together: Zn supports protein and energy metabolism, while Se prevents oxidative stress (El-Ratel et al., 2025; Fasil et al., 2021; Ghazi et al., 2022). This synergistic effect may explain why T3 and T5 not only had the highest body weight and weight gain but also maintained superior feed efficiency despite higher feed intake, indicating that nutrients were more effectively converted into tissue growth rather than being wasted through metabolic stress. Contrarily, high-dose ZnO—NPs + Se-NPs treatment (T4) led to poorer growth and feed efficiency, which initially appears counterintuitive given the enhanced antioxidant and immune status of that treatment. One possible explanation is that excessive ZnO—NPs (70 mg/L) in drinking water may have induced mild toxicity or metabolic disturbances (Zhang et al., 2018). It is also possible that the energy cost of detoxification and maintaining homeostasis under high Zn burden diverted metabolic resources away from growth, thereby explaining why performance declined despite stronger antioxidant responses (Zaefarian et al., 2019). High Zn levels can interfere with the absorption of other minerals and may disrupt the balance of the gut microflora or affect intestinal function, leading to reduced nutrient assimilation (Santos et al., 2021; Skalny et al., 2021).

Improvements in intestinal morphology, particularly increased VH, were observed in birds receiving balanced ZnO—NPs and Se-NPs supplementation. A taller VH is generally associated with a greater surface area for nutrient absorption and is often correlated with enhanced growth performance. Ali et al. (2017) demonstrated that supplementation with ZnO—NP at a dosage of 40 mg/kg significantly improved intestinal morphology in broiler chickens, specifically by increasing the thickness of the lamina propria muscularis in the small intestine. While, plays a structural role in maintaining gut integrity and mucosal development and was shown to stimulate intestinal villus growth and brush border enzyme activity in poultry (Mohammed et al., 2023). In this experiment, the combination of ZnO—NPs and Se-NPs at (T3 and T5) resulted in significantly lengthened villi compared with that of the other treatments, which likely facilitated more efficient nutrient absorption. Longer villi provide a greater absorptive surface, supporting better feed efficiency and weight gain, while Se-NPs likely contribute by protecting rapidly dividing intestinal epithelial cells from oxidative damage. The presence of Se-NPs in particular may have enhanced this protective role due to their greater bioavailability (Chen et al., 2024). Conversely, the shortest villi were observed in treatments receiving the highest ZnO—NP dose (70 mg/L) in combination (T4 and T6). It is possible that excess Zn in the lumen has an irritating or anti-proliferative effect on the intestine. High dietary Zn was reported to reduce VH in some cases, likely by altering microbial fermentation or inducing local stress in enterocyte absorption (Kumar et al., 2021; Akhavan-Salamat & Ghasemi, 2019; Zhang et al., 2018). Although Se-NPs was included in T4 and T6, it appeared that it could not fully counteract the negative impact of excessive Zn on gut morphology (Wang et al., 2023). This reduction in villus height in T4 and T6 may also help explain why these treatments, despite showing strong antioxidant responses, failed to achieve optimal growth performance: a compromised absorptive surface limits nutrient utilisation efficiency, illustrating the delicate balance between beneficial and harmful levels of Zn.

Supplementation with Zn and Se NPs significantly boosted antioxidant enzyme activities, including SOD and GPx, demonstrating the effectiveness of these NPs in strengthening the defence of the birds against oxidative stress. Tainer et al. (1983) found that Zn is a key component of the Cu/Zn-SOD enzyme, and the availability of sufficient Zn increases SOD activity in tissues and the blood. This explains the reason for all Zn-supplemented treatments, particularly those in the high dose of ZnO—NP, exhibited significantly higher SOD levels than those of the negative controls. Similarly, Se is a vital component of the active site of glutathione peroxidase; delivering Se in a bioavailable form directly enhancing GPx synthesis and activity (Zoidis et al., 2018). Birds with Se-NP supplements, especially those with high Se (T4), showed increased GPx levels, which is in line with better peroxide neutralisation. The interaction of Zn and Se supplements, particularly T4 had the highest SOD and GPx activities, indicating an additive or synergistic effect optimising both parts of the antioxidant enzyme network (Ghazi et al., 2022; Guo et al., 2025). It makes sense that these two works together, as SOD and GPx teams reduce oxidative stress. SOD first converts superoxide radicals to hydrogen peroxide, and then GPx converts peroxide into water. This indicates an enhanced defense mechanism against oxidative stress, wherein SOD and GPx operate collaboratively to neutralize free radicals and safeguard tissues from lipid peroxidation. By increasing both enzymes, the ZnO and Se NP treatments helped to quench reactive oxygen species more effectively throughout this process. Contrastingly, the reduction in MDA levels across all supplemented treatments reflects the positive outcome of improved antioxidant defences. MDA is a by-product of lipid peroxidation, a lower MDA level indicates that cellular membranes undergo less oxidative damage (Oke et al., 2024). Observations in T4 and T6 suggest that high-dose NP treatments effectively prevent lipid peroxidation, stabilizing membrane integrity in metabolically active tissues such as the liver and intestine. This protective effect is likely linked to the improved serum protein synthesis and reduced AST activity noted in these treatments, as healthier cells require fewer repairs and maintain more efficient metabolism.

Chicken serum total protein, albumin, Globulin, and albumin to globulin levels were significantly higher in birds administered with doses of ZnO—NPs and Se-NPs, particularly in T3 and T5. This boost likely stems from improved nutrient absorption and protein metabolism due to the combined effects of Zn and Se. Since albumin is produced in the liver, it helps maintain osmotic balance and facilitates nutrient transport, and its higher levels may be linked to the improved growth performance observed in these treatments compared with that of the control treatments and high-dose ZnO—NP treatments (Eid et al., 2022; Mohammed et al., 2023; Qaid et al., 2021; Zálešáková et al., 2025). This may reflect enhanced immunoglobulin synthesis, which aligns with the improved antibody titers measured in these birds. The shift in the albumin-to-globulin (A/G) ratio across treatments illustrates the physiological balance between growth-related protein synthesis (albumin) and immune-related protein production (globulins). An elevated A/G ratio in T3 and T5 suggests that these treatments prioritised anabolic processes and tissue accretion, whereas the relatively higher globulin levels in T6 indicate stronger immunological activity, potentially at the expense of growth (Filipović et al., 2007).

Lower AST levels in supplemented treatments compared with those of the negative control indicated reduced liver stress or damage. Specifically, a direct relationship was observed between elevated levels of Zn and Se NPs and decreased stress. Zn and Se have protective effects on the liver, possibly through antioxidant mechanisms and membrane stabilisation (Mohammed et al., 2023; Mohd Yusof et al., 2023). This protective effect probably results from improved antioxidant status in nano-supplemented birds, as reduced oxidative stress lessens lipid peroxidation in hepatocyte membranes, preventing enzyme leakage into the bloodstream. Furthermore, in serum creatinine levels, which indicate kidney filtration efficiency, some minor increases were observed in high growth performance treatments (PC, T3 and T5). These increases may be linked to a higher metabolic turnover or renal stress, possibly. This is because of the high mineral load resulting from the rapid growth rate. The low creatinine levels in the negative control and T6 exhibited minimal growth performance, which is consistent with the observed negative effects of high Zn on growth and intestinal morphology (Zálešáková et al., 2025).

The enhancement of NDV antibody titres in ZnO—NP and Se-NP supplemented birds demonstrates the positive impact of these micronutrients on the humoral immune response. Hidayat et al. (2020) demonstrated that Zn is well documented to influence immune cell development and function and is required for thymic hormone activity, lymphocyte proliferation, and antibody production. Adequate Zn levels improve both cellular and humoral immunity in poultry, as Zn deficiency is known to impair B-cell and T-cell responses (Ng et al., 2022). The results showed that treatments that acquired ZnO—NP and Se-NP from T4 to T6 significantly increased the humoral immune response of broiler chickens to NDV (Deb Sarker et al., 2017; Hosein et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2023). Protection against viral challenges depends on increased antibody production. Well known for their immunomodulating effects, Zn and Se are essential for lymphocyte proliferation and antibody synthesis (Dalgaard et al., 2018; Hidayat et al., 2020; Shojadoost et al., 2020). Thus, the combination of ZnO- and Se-NPs likely provided a dual benefit that Zn directly stimulated immune cell growth and differentiation, while Se preserved immune cell viability by reducing oxidative stress. The higher bioavailability and targeted delivery of these Nano minerals results in stronger immune cell activation and memory responses (Azab et al., 2019; Hatab et al., 2024; Swain et al., 2019). The stronger antibody titers in T4 and T6 match the elevated globulin levels, which mainly represent immunoglobulins and immune proteins. This suggests that improved protein metabolism and antioxidant status support humoral immunity. This may indicate that when nutrient absorption and growth efficiency are compromised by high Zn, more metabolic resources are diverted towards immune function, a phenomenon sometimes described as a trade-off between growth and immunity under stress conditions (Zerjal et al., 2021). Similarly, Ali et al. (2019) reported that zinc levels beyond 80 mg/kg depressed growth while still enhancing immune responses.

Conclusion

This study showed that supplementing broiler drinking water with zinc oxide and selenium nanoparticles improved growth, feed efficiency, gut morphology, antioxidant activity, and immune responses. Birds given the combined treatments had longer villi, higher antioxidant enzyme activities and stronger antibody titers. The best results were achieved with moderate doses (35 mg/L ZnO—NPs + 0.15 mg/L Se-NPs and 35 mg/L ZnO—NPs + 0.3 mg/L Se-NPs), which consistently supported performance and health. In contrast, higher ZnO—NP levels reduced growth and villus height despite strong antioxidant activity, suggesting a threshold where excess zinc becomes harmful. Overall, ZnO- and Se-NPs can be valuable supplements for broiler production, but their benefits depend on maintaining the right balance between dose and physiological tolerance.

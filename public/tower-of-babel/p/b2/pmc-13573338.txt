Identification of distinct subgroups in Chinese patients with Behçet’s syndrome via cluster analysis of immune cells and clinical features
Authors: Jiachen Li, Shanzhao Jin, Feng Sun, Miao Shao, Xia Zhang, Wenhao Lin, Xiao Tan, Xiumei Yang, Xiaolin Sun, Yaping Luo, Zhanguo Li, Tian Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesThis study aimed to use machine learning to explore Behçet’s syndrome (BS) heterogeneity by integrating immunocyte subpopulations and clinical characteristics.MethodsWe prospectively enrolled BS patients and recorded their demographic and clinical characteristics. Various peripheral immune cells were analysed using flow cytometry. Unsupervised machine learning was used to perform cluster analysis based on the clinical manifestations and immune cell subsets. Patients were followed up for one year to evaluate treatment response and remission rates. RNA sequencing was performed in patients with clustered BS and healthy controls.ResultsUnsupervised machine learning categorized 201 BS patients into four clusters with distinct clinical and immunological features. Cluster 1 showed isolated mucocutaneous lesions, low inflammation, and high remission, with transcriptomic enrichment in IFN-γ, IL-6, and JAK-STAT pathways. Cluster 2 featured arthritis, elevated inflammatory levels, and responded well to TNF-α inhibitors, with transcriptomic enrichment in TNF and B-cell activation pathways. Cluster 3 had cardiovascular involvement, reduced CLA+ Tregs, and also responded to TNF-α inhibitors, with transcriptomic enrichment in coagulation, platelet activation, and MAPK pathways. Cluster 4 demonstrated neurological involvement, elevated CD161⁺ Tregs, low remission, and a better response to mycophenolate mofetil, with transcriptomic enrichment in T-cell activation and NF-κB pathways.ConclusionUnsupervised clustering of BS patients revealed four distinct subtypes with significant clinical and immunological heterogeneity, which may provide a foundation for mechanistic studies and personalized treatment.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s13075-026-03851-5.

Introduction

Behçet’s syndrome (BS) is a rare autoimmune disorder characterized by systemic vasculitis affecting all blood vessel sizes [1, 2]. It presents a wide clinical spectrum, commonly involving mucocutaneous, articular, ocular, gastrointestinal, cardiovascular, and neurological systems. The disease follows a chronic, relapsing course with considerable variability in severity and organ involvement [1–3].

The pathogenesis of BS is not fully understood and involves a complex interplay of genetic predisposition, environmental triggers, and immune dysregulation [1, 2, 4–6]. Multiple immune cell subsets play key roles. Patients show expansion of helper T cell 17 (Th17) in peripheral blood, which mediates inflammation via interleukin-23 (IL-23)-dependent recruitment of neutrophils and macrophages [7–9]. Th1 cell function is systemically enhanced [4, 10], and an elevated Th17/regulatory T cell (Treg) ratio disrupts immune tolerance, contributing to systemic damage [11–13]. Intestinal dysbiosis, characterized by reduced butyrate-producing bacteria, may exacerbate this imbalance [14]. However, few studies have systematically examined the roles of specific immune cell subtypes across different clinical phenotypes or their contribution to disease heterogeneity in BS.

Machine learning provides powerful tools for disease subtyping and characterizing heterogeneity. While prior studies have used cluster analysis to identify distinct clinical subtypes in BS [15–20], there are currently no studies that combine immune cell subtypes and clinical characteristics to conduct cluster analysis on BS. Therefore, this study aimed to combine different immune cell subgroups and clinical characteristics of patients to explore the different subgroups of BS and analyse the clinical characteristics, internal mechanisms, and treatment outcomes of each subgroup.

Methods

Study participants and ethics

This prospective study consecutively enrolled patients with BS who were diagnosed and followed up at the Department of Rheumatology and Immunology, Peking University People’s Hospital, between September 2021 and May 2024. Inclusion criteria comprised patients aged 18–70 years meeting the 2014 International Criteria for Behçet’s Disease (ICBD) [21] with active disease. Disease activity was assessed using Behçet’s Disease Current Activity Form (BDCAF) [22] and all patients had a BDCAF score of ≥ 1 at enrolment. Those with overlapping autoimmune diseases were excluded.

Clinical features

The demographic and clinical characteristics of patients with BS were recorded at the initial clinical visit, and included sex; age; disease duration; systemic involvement, such as mucocutaneous, articular, ocular, cardiovascular, gastrointestinal, and neurological manifestations; specific clinical features; and initial treatments. Treatments included glucocorticoids (GCs), immunosuppressants (ISs), immunomodulators (IMs), and targeted agents. Specifically, ISs comprised methotrexate, azathioprine, mycophenolate mofetil, ciclosporin A, cyclophosphamide, and iguratimod; IMs included thalidomide, hydroxychloroquine, and low‑dose interleukin‑2. Targeted therapies consisted of TNF‑α inhibitors, IL‑6 inhibitors, and JAK inhibitors. The severity of BS was assessed using the Krause score [23].

Flow cytometry

Using flow cytometry, we assessed the levels of various immune cell subsets in peripheral blood samples collected from patients at the time of enrolment. The analysed populations included CD4⁺ T cells and their subsets, such as cytokine-producing T cells [tumour necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), interferon-γ (IFN-γ), IL-2, IL-4, IL-17], naïve T cells, effector T cells, Tregs and Treg subsets [Foxp3⁺ Tregs, cutaneous lymphocyte antigen positive (CLA⁺) Tregs, CD161⁺ Tregs], as well as follicular helper T cells (Tfh). CD8+ T, B, and natural killer (NK) cells were also examined. Detailed methodologies for flow cytometry are provided in the Supplementary Materials and the gating strategies for the involved immune cell subsets are shown in Figures S1–S4.

Follow up

All enrolled patients were followed up for one year. Follow-up data were collected from the hospital medical record system and included changes in clinical manifestations, imaging records, medication use, disease activity assessment, and whether remission was achieved. Remission was defined as (1) resolution of BS-related manifestations, (2) normal levels of inflammatory markers (C-reactive protein [CRP] and erythrocyte sedimentation rate [ESR]), and (3) no new-onset imaging/endoscopic findings [24–26]. Table S1 presents a detailed description of remission in patients with involvement of different systems.

RNA sequencing

Peripheral blood samples were collected from 19 patients with BS and 5 healthy controls (HCs). RNA sequencing was performed to identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs) that were subsequently subjected to Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis. Detailed experimental and analytical procedures are provided in the Supplementary Materials.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS (version 23; SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA), GraphPad Prism (version 10.1.2; GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA), and R software (version 4.3.3; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Continuous data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median (interquartile range, IQR) based on distribution. Categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages. Group comparisons utilized Student’s t-test or Wilcoxon rank-sum test for continuous variables, and χ² or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables. Comparisons across multiple groups employed ANOVA or Kruskal–Wallis test. Survival analysis was conducted with Kaplan–Meier curves and log-rank test. Unsupervised machine learning was applied for patient classification. All tests were two-sided, with p < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

Results

Immune cell subsets and clinical characteristics of BS patients

This study enrolled 201 patients diagnosed with BS (104 men, 97 women). The cohort had a median age of 37.0 years (30.0, 50.0) years and a median disease duration of 96.0 (24.0, 180.0) months. The median BDCAF score at enrolment was 4.0 (3.0, 5.0). Oral ulcers were the most prevalent manifestation, occurring in 192 (95.5%) patients. Genital ulcers were present in 125 patients (62.2%) and skin involvement was observed in 137 patients (68.2%), including pseudofolliculitis (n = 95, 47.3%), erythema nodosum (n = 52, 25.9%), and pustules (n = 15, 7.5%). Articular involvement was identified in 64 (31.8%) patients, including those with arthralgia (n = 64, 31.8%) and arthritis (n = 43, 21.4%). Ocular involvement occurred in 49 (24.4%) patients and manifested as uveitis (18.4%), retinal vasculitis (6.0%), conjunctivitis (5.5%), or keratitis (2.0%). Gastrointestinal involvement was present in 50 patients (24.9%), including gastrointestinal ulcers (18.9%), haemorrhages (6.5%), and oesophageal lesions (5.0%). Cardiovascular involvement was identified in 62 patients (33.5%) and included venous embolism (n = 32, 15.9%), arterial aneurysm (n = 20, 10.0%), arterial thrombosis (n = 9, 4.5%), arterial stenosis (n = 34, 16.9%), and valvular disease (n = 8, 4.0%). Neurological involvement was observed in 28 patients (13.9%), with parenchymal and nonparenchymal neurological involvement in 21 (10.4%) and 12 (6.0%) patients, respectively. Fever was reported in 36 patients (17.9%) (Table 1).

A total of 201 patients fulfilling the 2014 ICBD criteria were enrolled in this study. To evaluate the robustness of the inflammatory clustering model, we restricted the analysis to the 179 patients who also met the 1990 International Study Group classification criteria for Behçet’s disease [27]. Repeat unsupervised clustering analysis on this subset yielded the same four subtypes with characteristics identical to those of the original model, indicating that the clustering model possesses high stability (Figure S5, S6).

Different disease severity and prognoses of four BS clusters

Disease severity across the four Behçet’s disease clusters was evaluated using the Krause scoring system. The results demonstrated a significantly lower severity in Cluster 1 (3.0 [3.0, 5.0]) than in the other three clusters, while Cluster 4 (7.5 [6.0, 9.0]) exhibited higher severity than Cluster 3 (5.0 [4.0, 7.0]). No statistically significant differences were observed between Cluster 2 (6.0 [5.0, 8.0]) and Clusters 3/4 (Fig. 4A). During the 1-year follow-up assessing remission status, disease activity changes, and medication usage, significant inter-cluster differences emerged in remission rates (P = 0.0138). Cluster 1 achieved the highest remission rate (61.1%), Cluster 4 achieved the lowest rate (26.9%), and Clusters 2 (44.4%) and 3 (49.0%) demonstrated intermediate remission rates (Fig. 4B). Throughout the follow-up period, Cluster 1 maintained the lowest BDCAF scores, whereas Cluster 4 consistently showed the highest scores (Fig. 4C).

Bubble plots were used to visualise medication utilisation rates and corresponding remission rates across clusters. The size of each bubble corresponds to the frequency of drug usage within each cluster. Thalidomide was the most frequently used drug in Cluster 1 (62.5%), whereas GCs were the most common in Clusters 2 (53.7%), 3 (71.4%), and 4 (69.2%). Chi-square tests revealed distinct medication–remission associations: TNF-α inhibitor usage correlated with higher remission rates in Clusters 2 (P = 0.003) and 3 (P = 0.013), mycophenolate mofetil use was associated with improved remission in Cluster 4 (P = 0.047), while no significant drug-remission associations were identified in Cluster 1 (Fig. 4D). To adjust for confounding factors, we included age and sex in a multivariate logistic regression analysis. For Cluster 4, because there were only 7 patients who achieved remission and the univariate analysis showed that sex had a stronger association with remission than age in this cluster, only sex was included in the multivariate analysis. The results indicated that the use of TNF-α inhibitors remained associated with higher remission rates in Clusters 2 and 3, and the use of mycophenolate mofetil remained associated with a higher remission rate in Cluster 4 (Table S4-6).

Of the 201 patients, post-treatment blood samples for flow cytometry were obtained from 42 (24 male, 18 female; median age 37.0 [29.8, 44.3] years; median test-retest interval 4.0 [3.0, 9.3] months). These included 14 in Cluster 1, 8 in Cluster 2, 9 in Cluster 3, and 11 in Cluster 4. Patients in each cluster were divided into BDCAF reduction and non-reduction groups based on post-treatment score changes. Of note, all 11 Cluster 4 patients with paired data were in the reduction group.

Across all four clusters, no significant differences were detected in naive CD4⁺ T cells or Tregs before and after treatment. As depicted in Figure S5, in Cluster 2, the ratio of IFN-γ⁺ CD4⁺ T cells to CD4⁺ T cells was significantly reduced in the six patients exhibiting a post-treatment decrease in BDCAF score, whereas no significant change was observed in the non-reduction group, implying that IFN-γ⁺ CD4⁺ T cell levels in Cluster 2 patients may diminish with declining disease activity. In Cluster 3, a trend toward an increase in CLA⁺ Tregs was noted in the BDCAF reduction group, though it did not achieve statistical significance, likely attributable to the limited sample size. Additionally, CD4⁺ T cells in this group showed a significant post-treatment decrease, while no significant change occurred in the non-reduction group, suggesting that CD4⁺ T cells in Cluster 3 patients may decrease concomitantly with reduced disease activity. Moreover, in Cluster 4, CD161⁺ Tregs exhibited a significant post-treatment decrease, indicating that CD161⁺ Tregs may also decline in this cluster as disease activity lessens.

RNA sequencing profiling uncovers cluster-specific transcriptional signatures

To further elucidate the distinct pathogenic pathways across different clusters, we selected five patients from Cluster 1, five from Cluster 2, five from Cluster 3, four from Cluster 4, and five HCs for peripheral blood RNA sequencing to identify DEGs. Detailed information on the 19 patients with BS is provided in the Table S7. In comparison with HCs, a total of 620 up-regulated and 1,084 down-regulated genes were identified in BS patients. Figure 5 displays the top 50 up-regulated and down-regulated genes in BS patients ranked by fold change. Up-regulated genes were predominantly associated with immune-inflammatory responses, including C4BPA, HLA-DRB5, LTB4R2, IGHV, and FAM83A, as well as tissue damage and repair processes involving ABCB11, MT3, CHSY3, and RSPO2. Down-regulated genes possessed immunoregulatory functions, including THBS2, AVP, SVEP1, and OLA1P2. The down-regulation of these genes may collectively contribute to the aberrant inflammatory responses and tissue damage observed in BS patients.

Discussion

Behçet’s syndrome (BS) is a clinically heterogeneous disorder with varied patterns of organ involvement and progression [1, 2]. Dysregulation across multiple immune cell subsets underscores their central role in disease pathogenesis [4]. In this study, we employed a machine learning-based clustering approach integrating immunocyte subset profiles and clinical features to stratify a cohort of Chinese patients with BS. We identified four distinct subgroups: mucocutaneous, articular, cardiovascular, and neurological. Each showed unique clinical and organ involvement, as well as diverse immune cell patterns. These clusters also exhibited differences in prognosis, treatment response, and transcriptomic pathway activity, supporting immunophenotypic clustering as a tool for refined disease classification and personalized management.

Previous studies from multiple countries have reported cluster analyses of adult BS patients [16–20]. Compared with previous cluster analyses in adult BS, our study included a smaller number of patients, all from a single centre in Beijing. This may have introduced some chance variation in the overall clinical characteristics of our cohort, as well as potential selection bias. A higher prevalence of cardiovascular involvement was observed in our study compared with previous reports. A possible explanation is that Beijing is home to two leading cardiovascular centres in China — Anzhen Hospital and Fuwai Hospital — which may have attracted a larger number of BS patients with cardiovascular involvement to seek treatment in Beijing.

In previous studies, gastrointestinal involvement in BS was rare in the Eastern Mediterranean region [18, 20, 28], so cluster analyses there did not identify a gastrointestinal subtype. In contrast, gastrointestinal involvement is more common in East Asia [16, 17, 19], where a gastrointestinal subtype was previously identified based on clinical features. In the present study, patients with gastrointestinal involvement were evenly distributed across each cluster. We speculate that this failure to identify a gastrointestinal-dominant subtype may be because the included immune cell subsets were more strongly correlated with joint, cardiovascular, and neurological involvement. Similarly, the proportion of ocular involvement in East Asian patients has been reported to be lower than that in patients from the Middle East and the Mediterranean region. Consistently, the proportion of ocular involvement in our study was also relatively low, which may explain why a distinct ocular-dominant subtype did not emerge.

Meanwhile, we found no statistically significant differences in the HLA-B51 positivity rate among the subtypes. Previous studies have suggested that HLA-B51 positivity may be associated with ocular involvement, whereas patients with gastrointestinal involvement tend to have a lower positivity rate [29]. The lack of pronounced differences in ocular and gastrointestinal involvement across the groups may explain why the HLA-B51 positivity rate did not stand out in any particular subtype. In Cluster 2, patients exhibited a relatively high proportion of both uveitis and haemorrhage of digestive tract. We speculate that the coexistence of relatively high proportions of uveitis and gastrointestinal involvement in this cluster may be attributed to the fact that Cluster 2 is characterized by an elevation of CD4+ T cells producing multiple cytokines, including TNF-α, IFN-γ, and IL-2, which have been shown in previous studies to be potentially associated with ocular and gastrointestinal involvement in BS [30, 31]. The innovation of this study compared to previous cluster analyses of BS lies in the fact that our analysis integrated both immunocyte subsets and clinical features, yielding deeper mechanistic insight into distinct subgroups and their underlying pathophysiology.

Cluster 1 was characterised by mucocutaneous involvement without major systemic manifestations, along with low disease activity and severity. Immunophenotypically, these patients exhibited reduced frequencies of pro-inflammatory cytokine-producing T cells (TNF-α+, IFN-γ+, and IL-2+) and lower overall levels of effector T cells. Given the established pathogenic roles of these cytokines in BS [32, 33], these findings indicate an overall lower inflammatory burden in Cluster 1. Cluster 1 exhibited elevated levels of Tregs and naïve T cells. Tregs play a crucial role in immune responses, helping maintain self-tolerance and prevent excessive autoimmunity [11, 34]. Naïve T cells serve as precursors to effector T lineages—such as Th1, Th2, and Th17 [35, 36]; consistently, Pearson correlation analysis in our study indicated a negative association between naïve T cells and T cells producing TNF-α, IFN-γ, and IL-2. Together, these immunophenotypic features suggest a comparatively lower degree of immune dysregulation and reduced autoimmune activation in Cluster 1, which is consistent with the observed lower disease severity and activity.

Cluster 2 was primarily defined by articular involvement, with all patients exhibiting joint symptoms and demonstrating higher disease severity and activity compared to Cluster (1) This cluster showed elevated frequencies of TNF-α+, IFN-γ+, and IL-2+ T cells, indicating a pro-inflammatory state. TNF-α mainly produced by macrophages, T cells and NK cells, plays a central role in driving inflammatory responses and is implicated in autoimmune arthritis [3, 37]. The abundance of TNF-α+CD4+ T cells likely contributes to the joint symptoms in this cluster. Previous studies have also linked TNF-α to the pathogenesis of uveitis and gastrointestinal lesion in BS [38, 39], which is consistent with our observation of a high prevalence of uveitis and haematochezia in Cluster (2) IFN-γ is a key pro-inflammatory cytokine known to interact synergistically with TNF-α [40]. Previous research by Zheng et al. demonstrated that IFN-γ promotes inflammatory responses and immune injury in BS through the activation of C1q-high monocytes [32]. Additionally, Wang et al. reported a significant association between IFN-γ and ocular lesions in BS [41]. IL-2 activates and stimulates the proliferation of CD4+ T cells [42]. Elevated levels of IL-2+CD4+ T cells in Cluster 2 suggest a heightened inflammatory state in these patients. Cluster 2 represents a unique phenotype characterised by prominent joint involvement in the background of mucocutaneous manifestations with a hyperinflammatory state in BS.

Cluster 3 was characterized by a higher prevalence of cardiovascular manifestations involving both arterial and venous systems. Immunophenotypically, this cluster is characterised by the distinctive dominance of CLA⁺ Tregs. CLA is a homing receptor expressed on T cells that facilitates endothelial adhesion and tissue migration via E-selectin binding [43]. CLA⁺ Tregs are a specialized regulatory subset involved in immune homeostasis [44]. Hirahara et al. demonstrated that CLA⁺ Treg cells induced by anti-CD3 in peripheral blood can suppress the proliferation of CD4⁺CD25⁻ T cells [45]. Reduced levels of CLA⁺ Treg cells may contribute to immune dysregulation and autoinflammatory responses in patients with BS. Furthermore, previous studies have revealed that CLA mediates the adhesion of T cells to inflamed vascular endothelial cells through interactions with E-selectin ligands [46]. CLA⁺ Treg cells exhibit immunoregulatory functions, and a reduction in CLA⁺ Treg cells may lead to vascular endothelial injury, thereby contributing to cardiovascular involvement in patients. Cluster 3 corresponds to a subgroup of patients with BS characterised by cardiovascular involvement and reduced levels of CLA⁺ Tregs.

Cluster 4 was characterised by patients who exhibited neurological abnormalities and mucocutaneous manifestations. Among all the clusters, Cluster 4 had the highest disease severity and activity levels. Additionally, CD161⁺ Treg cells are significantly elevated in Cluster 4 compared to the other groups. CD161 is a cell surface marker encoded by the killer cell lectin-like receptor B1 (KLRB1) gene and its ligand LLT1. The CD161–LLT1 interaction has been found to play a functional role in various immune cells such as NK and Th17 cells [47, 48]. While Treg cells are traditionally known as an immunoregulatory subset, research by Pesenacker et al. indicates that CD161⁺Treg cells can secrete pro-inflammatory cytokines including IFN-γ and IL-17, and may contribute to inflammatory responses [49]. CD161⁺ Treg cells have been implicated in the pathogenesis of several autoimmune diseases [50–52]. We propose that elevated levels of CD161⁺Treg cells may contribute to the more severe overall disease presentation and neurological involvement observed in Cluster 4 patients. Cluster 4 comprised a subset of patients with BS exhibiting a more severe disease phenotype characterised by neurological involvement and elevated levels of CD161⁺ Tregs.

Over a 1-year follow-up period, cluster 1 had the highest remission rate, whereas Cluster 4 had the lowest, consistent with the overall disease severity in each group. Notably, TNF-α inhibitors showed better therapeutic efficacy in Clusters 2 and 3, consistent with the elevated levels of TNF-α⁺ CD4⁺ T cells in Cluster 2. This finding is supported by previous studies indicating that TNF-α inhibitors are effective in BS patients with articular and cardiovascular involvement [1, 3, 53]. Notably, Cluster 2 contained high proportions of patients with ocular (29.6%) and gastrointestinal (29.6%) involvement. Given the high efficacy of TNF-α inhibitors for these manifestations [1], this probably accounts for the significant association between TNF-α inhibitor use and the higher remission rate in Cluster 2. In Cluster 4, mycophenolate mofetil demonstrated promising therapeutic efficacy, consistent with previous literature indicating its effectiveness in treating neurological involvement in BS [54, 55]. The results of this research could help inform treatment strategies for BS patient in different subgroups. During follow-up, peripheral blood samples were collected from 42 patients after treatment for flow cytometric analysis. The results showed that as disease activity decreased following treatment, abnormalities in characteristic cell subsets of different subtypes exhibited a trend toward normalization, suggesting that effective treatment may reverse the autoimmune abnormalities associated with each subtype. However, due to the limited sample size, this finding requires further validation in future studies with larger cohorts.

We investigated the intrinsic mechanisms of each subgroup using RNA sequencing. Compared to the HC group, the differentially expressed genes in Cluster 1 patients were predominantly enriched in pathways related to interferon response, IL-6 activation, and JAK-STAT signalling, indicating that the transcriptional profile of Cluster 1 is characterized by extensive activation of inflammatory pathways relative to healthy individuals. Previous literature has reported elevated levels of salivary cytokines such as IL-6 and IFN-γ in the oral mucosa of patients with aphthous ulcers, and the inflammation mediated by these cytokines can lead to mucosal damage, including aphthous ulcers [56, 57]. The JAK-STAT pathway serves as a downstream effector for multiple cytokines, including IL-17, IL-6, and IFN-γ [58]. These findings are consistent with the skin and mucosal involvement observed in this subtype and provide molecular-level evidence supporting its underlying immune mechanism. In Cluster 2 patients, PBMCs showed significant enrichment in TNF-related signalling pathways, consistent with the upregulation of TNF-α+CD4+ T cells and the favourable therapeutic response to TNF-α inhibitors observed in this subgroup. Although humoral immunity is not typically considered to play a central role in BS, notable enrichment of pathways associated with immunoglobulin and B-cell responses was observed in Cluster 2. Furthermore, immune cell profiling revealed a marked increase in circulating Tfh cells in this cluster. Tfh cells are known to promote humoral immunity by activating B cells via ICOS and IL-21 [59], suggesting that humoral immune mechanisms may also contribute significantly to the pathogenesis of Cluster 2. In Cluster 3, upregulated genes were found to be enriched in pathways associated with coagulation, platelet activation, MAPK signalling, and T cell activation, suggesting a pronounced interaction between coagulation and inflammation in this subgroup, along with heightened activation of the MAPK pathway. Platelet activation may promote coagulation while simultaneously releasing inflammatory mediators, thereby exacerbating vascular inflammation [60]. Moreover, activation of the MAPK pathway can further amplify pro-inflammatory signals and contribute to the progression of vascular injury [61]. These findings provide insights into the underlying mechanisms of vascular involvement in BS. In Cluster 4, PBMCs exhibited a transcriptional profile characterized by enhanced T cell activation, Th17-related pathways, and canonical NF-κB signalling. This suggests that T cell-driven inflammation and activation of pathways such as NF-κB may contribute to glial cell activation and disruption of the blood–brain barrier, collectively promoting neurological injury.

Previous RNA-seq studies have shown enrichment of NF-κB, TNF, IL-1, IFN-γ and chemokine pathways in BS [62, 63]. Our study replicated these findings but further revealed that the predominant pathways differ across clinical subtypes, suggesting that the inflammatory pathways activated in BS differ depending on the pattern of systemic involvement at the transcriptomic level.

This study had several limitations. First, the analysis focused on specific immune cell subsets rather than a comprehensive range, which may limit the generalisability of the clustering results to the entire immune landscape of BS. Second, the patients were recruited from a single centre of China and the sample size was relatively small, potentially introducing selection bias. The overall clinical characteristics of the patients in this study differ to some extent from those reported in previous studies from the Middle East, Europe, Japan and other areas. This limits the generalisability of our clustering model. Third, the findings were not externally validated. Future studies involving larger multicentre cohorts with broader immune cell profiles, including both internal training and external validation sets, are warranted to further elucidate these aspects. Forth, RNA sequencing was conducted solely on PBMCs obtained at the initial stage, without subsequent assessment of post-treatment alterations in transcriptomic characteristics among patients of various subtypes. Further experimental exploration is therefore required to address this limitation.

Unsupervised machine learning identified four distinct clusters of patients with BS based on their clinical manifestations and immune cell subsets. The four clusters of patients with BS exhibited heterogeneity in terms of systemic involvement, disease severity, immune cell subset distribution, prognosis, and gene expression profiles. Through cluster analysis, we explored the relationships between distinct clinical features, clinical outcomes, and various immune cell subsets in patients with BS. This study sheds light on the immunological characteristics of different patient subgroups, offering a potential theoretical foundation for elucidating the pathogenesis of BS, and may help inform the development of targeted treatment strategies and tailored therapeutic approaches for different patient populations.

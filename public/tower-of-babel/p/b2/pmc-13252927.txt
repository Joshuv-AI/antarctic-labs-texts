Platelet-Derived Growth Factor Isoform Imbalance Is Associated with Innate Inflammation and Corneal Epitheliopathy in Dry Eye Disease
Authors: Zeenal Dabre, Hyung-Geun Moon, Tanya Sheth, Bhuvanachandra Pasupuleti, Christine Mun, Kiera Byrne, Jessica Mun, Diana Hernandez, Nancy Ascencio, Jack Shehadi, Michael Flavin, Simon Kaja, Sandeep Jain
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionTear-deficient dry eye disease (DED), including Sjögren’s syndrome and ocular graft-versus-host disease (oGVHD), is often refractory to therapy. We tested whether platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF) isoform imbalance, quantified as a PDGF-AB/BB: PDGF-AA ratio (“PDGF Ratio”), is associated with innate activation and corneal epitheliopathy.MethodsTear samples from 151 participants were profiled across two independent assay runs: discovery (n = 79) and replication (n = 72). Data were harmonized as within-plate rank percentiles. Plate-adjusted mediation tested whether the PDGF Ratio mediated associations between tear-deficient status and an innate NF-κB composite (IL-18/IL-6/IL-8/TNF-α) and National Eye Institute (NEI) scale graded corneal staining. A parallel model for staining included Schirmer scores to distinguish trophic from aqueous volume deficiency.ResultsThe PDGF Ratio differed significantly across diagnostic groups (Kruskal-Wallis p < 0.001), driven by concurrent PDGF-AA depletion (median rank 0.69 healthy vs. 0.19 oGVHD) and PDGF-AB/BB enrichment (0.18 vs. 0.62; both p < 0.001). High ratio co-occurred with higher innate cytokine ranks and lower EGF and IFN-γ. In pooled mediation, the ratio accounted for 62.8% of the tear-deficient association with innate inflammation (indirect 0.168; 95% CI 0.118–0.225) and 34.2% of the association with corneal staining (indirect 0.117; 95% CI 0.055–0.190). The staining pathway remained significant after accounting for Schirmer (indirect 0.113; 95% CI 0.050–0.185).ConclusionTear-deficient DED exhibits a reproducible PDGF isoform imbalance that statistically accounts for substantial portions of innate activation and corneal staining. These findings support the PDGF Ratio as a coherent candidate biomarker and therapeutic axis for isoform-selective rebalancing.

Introduction

Severe tear-deficient dry eye disease (DED), including Sjögren’s syndrome and ocular graft-versus-host disease (oGVHD), is characterized by tear film instability and hyperosmolar stress, a dysregulated ocular surface immune milieu, and corneal epitheliopathy that drives ocular pain, visual disturbance, and reduced quality of life [1–7]. The prevailing model posits that environmental stressors and/or systemic autoimmunity activate ocular surface and lacrimal antigen-presenting cells, which prime autoreactive Th1 and Th17 cells [8, 9]. These lymphocytes migrate to the ocular surface and lacrimal functional unit, releasing cytokines such as IFN-γ and IL-17 that disrupt epithelial junctional integrity and barrier function [10, 11]. However, clinical evidence suggests that disease can evolve into a distinct phase of chronic epithelial injury and structural remodeling that is often refractory to topical T-cell-targeting drugs, lubrication, and steroids [12, 13]. This therapeutic resistance supports the possibility that additional downstream mechanisms sustain chronic, treatment-resistant pathology beyond the initiating Th1/Th17-mediated process and motivates combinatorial approaches for recalcitrant DED [14].

We previously reported that extracellular DNA (eDNA; a damage-associated molecular pattern [DAMP]) and neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs), along with neutrophils and elevated innate cytokines including IL-6, IL-8, and TNF-α, are present on the ocular surface of symptomatic tear-deficient DED, including Sjögren’s syndrome and oGVHD [15, 16]. Others have also reported elevated innate cytokines including IL-6, IL-8, TNF-α, and IL-18 in tear fluid of independent cohort of patients with Sjögren’s syndrome and oGVHD patients [17–20]. These cytokines (IL-6, IL-8, TNF-α) and pro-IL-18 are transcriptional targets of nuclear factor kappa-light-chain-enhancer of activated B cells (NF-κB) [21, 22], but they differ in posttranslational processing and secretion: IL-6, IL-8, and TNF-α are directly secreted, whereas IL-18 requires inflammasome-mediated cleavage following NF-κB-dependent priming [23–25]. NETs and DAMPs have emerged as potent, noninfectious agonists capable of initiating and sustaining robust NF-κB signaling [26–29]. Consistent with nucleic acid DAMP sensing, we have reported increased ocular surface expression of TLR9 and MyD88 in tear-deficient patients with excessive eDNA and innate inflammation components [15], and others have reported that DAMP signaling through TLR9 stimulates secretion of IL-8 from neutrophils [30, 31] and that DAMPs stimulate NF-κB resulting in TNF-α production [32]. Increases in the activation of NF-κB and the expression of MyD88, IL-6, and IL-18 have also been reported in murine models of tear-deficient DED [33, 34]. Taken together, these reports point to potential associations between tear-deficient DED, eDNA/NETs, TLR9/MyD88, NF-κB, and IL-6/IL-8/TNF-α. In tear-deficient DED patients, tear eDNA abundance correlated most strongly with corneal fluorescein staining (objective epithelial injury) rather than symptoms or conjunctival staining, and corneal staining correlated most strongly with Schirmer scores [35]. In our clinical trial adding pooled human immunoglobulin eye drops to standard chronic therapies (artificial tears, ointments, cyclosporine, lifitegrast, steroids, eyelid hygiene), eDNA and IL-8 decreased significantly, while the reduction in corneal staining, though statistically significant, had a small effect size and was less than the minimum clinically important difference [36, 37]. Together, these observations suggest that upstream dampening of eDNA/NET-associated inflammation may be insufficient once a chronic downstream program is established. We therefore treated an NF-κB-associated innate cytokine signature (IL-6, IL-8, TNF-α, IL-18) as a biologically grounded “innate activation” axis and examined whether a parallel disruption in trophic homeostasis could help maintain that axis in chronic, refractory tear-deficient DED. We propose that the dysregulation maintaining this state involves an imbalance in growth factor-mediated repair signals that couple to a self-sustaining innate inflammatory loop.

Within this framework, we focused on the platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF) system because it sits at a mechanistic intersection between injury sensing (NETs/DAMPs), NF-κB-driven inflammation, and tissue remodeling. PDGF polypeptide chains (PDGF-A, PDGF-B, PDGF-C, PDGF-D) form homodimers (PDGF-AA, PDGF-BB, PDGF-CC, PDGF-DD) and a heterodimer (PDGF-AB) [38]. These isoforms signal through the related receptor tyrosine kinases PDGFRα and PDGFRβ, which have distinct tissue distributions and physiological roles [39–41]. Ligand composition biases receptor engagement: PDGF-AA predominantly signals through PDGFRαα, whereas PDGF-AB and PDGF-BB engage receptor dimers containing PDGFRβ (αβ and/or ββ). Several studies suggest that PDGFRα signaling (via PDGF-AA) can be homeostatic and promote survival [42–44], whereas PDGFRβ signaling (often via PDGF-BB) is linked to inflammatory, angiogenic, and fibrotic remodeling [45]. Notably, PDGF signaling is context- and dose-dependent, and both PDGFRα and PDGFRβ participate in wound repair; pathological outcomes may reflect sustained or dysregulated signaling dynamics rather than receptor identity alone. Importantly, multiple reports document reciprocal coupling between DAMP/NET exposure, NF-κB activity, and PDGF expression. NF-κB-mediated induction of the PDGF-B gene drives the production of B-chain subunits, thereby increasing the availability of both PDGF-BB and PDGF-AB ligands. [46]. In prostate cancer cell lines, exposure to extracellular DAMP increases NF-κB activity [29]. In bronchial epithelial cells, DAMP receptor signaling activates NF-κB, which translocates to the nucleus and increases secretion of PDGF-AB and PDGF-BB [47]. In vascular smooth muscle cells, PDGFRβ signaling can increase expression of MCP-1/CCL2 (a canonical NF-κB-regulated innate mediator) via increased release of DAMPs [48]. In cancer models, NF-κB activity is required for PDGF-BB-induced fibroblast activation, creating a feed-forward loop in which PDGF activates the pathway that sustains its own downstream effects [49]. Together, these cross-organ findings suggest a conserved circuit, wherein chronic innate activation (DAMPs/NF-κB) drives PDGF-B output, which in turn reinforces inflammatory and remodeling programs.

NETs further reinforce this axis. In rheumatoid arthritis, NETs significantly upregulate PDGF-B and PDGFRβ in fibroblast-like synoviocytes [50]. In endothelial cells, repression of NF-κB decreases PDGF-B expression, whereas NF-κB activation increases PDGF-B transcription; this regulation is abolished by NF-κB inhibitors, supporting PDGF-B as a direct transcriptional target of NF-κB [51]. A TLR9-PDGF-B pathway has also been reported in systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), wherein TLR9 stimulation increases PDGF-B expression [52]. Because SLE is an autoimmune condition associated with tear-deficient DED [53], these findings strengthen the plausibility that nucleic acid sensing (TLR9/MyD88) could couple to PDGF-B in ocular surface autoimmunity. Consistently, MyD88 activation (via TLR4) has been reported to increase IL-6, TNF-α, and PDGF-BB levels [54]. Taken together, these reports support interlocking interactions among DAMPs, NETs, NF-κB, and PDGF.

In the eye, PDGF ligands (PDGF-A and PDGF-B) and receptors (PDGFRα and PDGFRβ) are expressed in human corneal epithelial cells and stromal fibroblasts [55, 56], with PDGF-A and PDGF-B detected at high levels in the epithelial basement membrane [56]. Transcriptional changes in primary human corneal stromal fibroblasts grown under quiescent and proliferating culture conditions revealed significant upregulation in genes linked to TNF-α signaling via NF-κB and identified PDGFRβ as a critical hub gene [57]. PDGF-BB is typically undetectable in healthy tears and becomes measurable following epithelial disruption, such as after photorefractive keratectomy [58]. PDGF-BB signaling predominantly through PDGFRβ can induce inflammatory cytokine production in stromal lineages: it stimulates IL-8 secretion by human corneal fibroblasts [59], and in orbital fibroblasts it stimulates IL-6 and IL-8 secretion by activating the NF-κB pathway [60].

Based on this framework, we hypothesized that the relative abundance of PDGF-B-containing isoforms (PDGF-AB and PDGF-BB) compared with PDGF-AA, summarized as the (PDGF-AB + PDGF-BB): PDGF-AA ratio, functions as a mediator of ocular surface pathology. We employed a discovery-replication design across two independent assay runs with nonoverlapping participants (plate 1, discovery: n = 79; plate 2, replication: n = 72; total n = 151, one eye per participant) and applied mediation analysis to test whether PDGF isoform imbalance (PDGF-AB/BB:PDGF-AA) statistically transmits the association between tear-deficient status and (1) an NF-κB-associated innate cytokine signature and (2) clinical signs of epithelial injury.

Methods

Participant Recruitment

Inclusion criteria included all patients more than 18 years of age who visited the University of Illinois, Chicago (UIC) Dry Eye and oGVHD clinic for initial visits or follow-up established visits. Exclusion criteria included patients who were unable to provide informed consent. A total of 151 enrolled participants were analyzed.

Study Population and Diagnostic Criteria

This study evaluated tear analyte profiles across distinct DED subtypes, including Sjögren’s disease (primary and secondary), meibomian gland dysfunction (MGD), oGVHD, and severe non-autoimmune tear-deficient DED alongside healthy controls. Primary Sjögren’s disease was diagnosed based on the 2016 ACR-EULAR classification criteria [61], requiring a cumulative score of ≥4 derived from ocular surface staining, Schirmer test results, salivary flow rates, lip biopsy focus scores, and anti-SSA/Ro serology. Secondary Sjögren’s disease was diagnosed in patients with an underlying autoimmune connective tissue disease (e.g., rheumatoid arthritis, SLE, or scleroderma) who presented with reduced tear production defined as a Schirmer test result of ≤5 mm over 5 min.

Diagnosis of oGVHD was established using the International Chronic Ocular Graft-versus-Host-Disease (ICCGVHD) Consensus Group criteria [62], which incorporate Ocular Surface Disease Index (OSDI) scores, Schirmer I test values, corneal staining, conjunctival injection, and systemic GVHD status. Definite oGVHD was defined as a score ≥6 in the presence of systemic chronic GVHD or a score ≥8 in its absence.

MGD was diagnosed based on the presence of ≥2 sign of MGD on history/exam, ocular symptoms, and/or ocular surface imaging, including LipiView meiboscale >2.0, lipid layer thickness <60 nm, poor expressibility and/or poor quality of meibomian secretions with expression, eyelid margin telangiectasia, fast tear breakup time consistent with tear film instability, and/or clinical ocular rosacea. The tear-deficient DED category included patients without underlying autoimmune disease and with severe aqueous deficiency, defined as a Schirmer test of ≤5 mm. Healthy controls were included only if they had no prior diagnosis of DED, no current symptoms of ocular dryness, and no use of any topical eye drops in the preceding 6 months. A slit-lamp examination was performed to confirm a normal tear meniscus height and the absence of ocular surface and eyelid disease. Schirmer’s test and corneal staining were not performed; only demographic information and ocular surface washing (OSW) samples were obtained.

The analysis was conducted in two complementary parts: groupwise distribution analyses including four diagnostic groups (healthy, MGD, Sjögren’s, and oGVHD), whereas for mediation analysis, tear-deficient status was modeled as a binary exposure (tear-deficient phenotypes vs. non-tear-deficient comparators). For mediation analyses, “tear-deficient status” was defined a priori as Sjögren’s disease, oGVHD, or tear-deficient DED, and compared with non-tear-deficient comparators (MGD and healthy controls). MGD was included as a clinically examined, non-tear-deficient disease comparator to distinguish aqueous-deficient biology from evaporative mechanisms.

Ophthalmological Examination

All patient cohorts underwent a standardized ocular surface evaluation. Symptoms were assessed using the OSDI, with scores categorized as normal (0–12), mild (13–22), moderate (23–32), or severe (33–100) [63]. Aqueous tear production was measured via the Schirmer I test without anesthesia for 5 min; values of ≤5 mm were considered indicative of severe tear deficiency.

Corneal epithelial damage was quantified using lissamine green staining (5 µL of 1% solution) and graded according to the National Eye Institute (NEI) scale (0–15 points) [64]. Conjunctival staining was similarly graded on a 0–3 scale for nasal and temporal regions. Ocular surface inflammation was assessed using the matrix metalloproteinase-9 test (InflammaDry, RPS Diagnostics), scored semiquantitatively as 0 (negative), 1.0 (faint positive), 2.0 (positive), or 3.0 (strong positive).

Tear Sample Collection and Multiplex Analysis

OSWs were collected as previously described [16, 37]. Others have also reported OSW for analysis of cytokines in tears of DED patients [18, 65–67]. Briefly, 50 µL of preservative-free artificial tears (Refresh Optive, Allergan) was instilled into the inferior fornix. The OSW was subsequently collected within 30 s using 25-µL glass microcapillary tubes (Drummond Scientific), transferred to PCR tubes at 4°C, and transported to the laboratory for storage at −80° C until analysis. Although samples were collected bilaterally, one eye per subject was included in analyses to avoid inter-eye correlation; when disease severity was asymmetric, the more severely affected eye was selected. OSW cytokines, chemokines, and growth factors were quantified using a bead-based multiplex immunoassay (Human Cytokine/Chemokine/Growth Factor Panel A 38-Plex; Millipore; Cat# HCYTA-60K-PX38) on the FLEXMAP 3D system (Luminex, MilliporeSigma), as described in prior publications [68, 69]. The panel measured 38 analytes; however, analysis in this manuscript focuses on prespecified trophic and innate axes, including PDGF-AA, PDGF-AB/BB, EGF, IL-18, IL-6, IL-8, TNF-α, IFN-γ, and IL-17A. Samples were loaded into 96-well plates, and assays were performed according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Raw median fluorescence intensity (MFI) values were exported for statistical processing as described in the Data Preprocessing and Statistical Analysis sections. Samples were analyzed across two independent assay runs performed with different reagent lots: plate 1 (discovery cohort, lot 4297791; n = 79) and plate 2 (replication cohort, lot 4337970; n = 72).

Study Design and Data Preprocessing

To evaluate the robustness of the PDGF Ratio as a potential candidate biomarker, we used a discovery-replication design across two independent assay runs (plates) comprising 151 unique participants, with tear analytes measured from a single eye per participant. No participants were assayed on both plates: plate 1 (n = 79) served as the discovery cohort, and plate 2 (n = 72), processed in a separate run, served as the independent replication cohort for prespecified rank-based endpoints. Concentration estimates were generated for within-plate interpretability, but were not pooled across plates and were interpreted cautiously near the lower limit of quantification (LLOQ).

Raw fluorescence readings were background-corrected on a per-analyte basis to generate net MFI (NetMFI). Specifically, for each plate and analyte, blank (background) wells were measured in RAW MFI, and sample NetMFI values were obtained by subtracting the analyte-specific blank signal from each sample’s RAW MFI (yielding the analyte NetMFI values used for analysis).

Because NetMFI values can approach zero or become negative after background subtraction, particularly for analytes near the blank, we applied an analyte- and plate-specific stabilization prior to log transformation. For each analyte on each plate, the limit of blank (LoB) was estimated from the distribution of that analyte’s RAW blank MFIs (duplicates on plate 1 and triplicates on plate 2) using a robust one-sided 95% noise floor: LoB_analyte = median(blank_RAW) + 1.645 × MAD(blank_RAW), where MAD(blank_RAW) is the median absolute deviation from the median. We applied an analyte- and plate-specific LoB-derived offset and then computed log signals (stabilized log intensities) for all non-blank samples as ln(NetMFI + LoB_analyte); values with (NetMFI + LoB_analyte) ≤0 were treated as missing.

PDGF isoform balance was defined on the stabilized log scale as ln(PDGF-AB/BB + LoB_AB/BB) − ln(PDGF-AA + LoB_AA), which is algebraically equivalent to ln[(PDGF-AB/BB + LoB_AB/BB)/(PDGF-AA + LoB_AA)] but is numerically more stable near the blank. This approach prevents undefined logs and extreme ratios driven by near-zero denominators while preserving interpretability as a log-ratio measure of isoform imbalance.

To facilitate pooled analysis, all continuous variables (PDGF Ratio, analyte signals, Schirmer scores, and staining) were transformed into within-plate rank percentiles on a 0 to 1 scale. Samples were ranked strictly within their respective assay plate to remove systematic calibration offsets. On this harmonized scale, estimates represent percentile-point shifts in the population hierarchy. Pooled models included a fixed effect for plate to absorb residual technical variation. Because the primary modeling scale was within-plate rank percentiles, analyses were driven by relative ordering rather than absolute magnitude; for the analytes used in the primary and secondary models, within-plate rank percentiles were identical whether derived from NetMFI or from ln(NetMFI + LoB_analyte); in the data reported, within-plate rank percentiles are derived from ln(NetMFI + LoB_analyte).

NetMFI values were converted to assay-measured concentrations (pg/mL) using plate-specific 4-parameter logistic fits to the manufacturer’s standards and inversion of the fitted curves. Measurements below the LLOQ (defined as the lowest standard concentration) were imputed as LLOQ/2 for visualization and descriptive summaries on log scales; in tables, “% below LLOQ” denotes the fraction of samples requiring this imputation for a given analyte (and for the PDGF-AB/BB:PDGF-AA ratio, when either component required imputation). Because the two assay runs differed in reagent lots, standard ranges/dilutions, and sample input volumes (plate 1: 5 µL OSW per well; plate 2: 15 µL OSW per well), concentration estimates are reported as within-plate assay-based values and are not pooled across plates; primary inferential models rely on within-plate rank-percentile harmonization. For PDGF-AB/BB on plate 2, low-end quantification was constrained by proximity of the lowest standard signal to blank, and therefore, concentration estimates near the LLOQ are interpreted cautiously; LoB-stabilized and rank-based analyses provide the primary evidence for PDGF isoform imbalance.

General Statistical Analysis

Continuous variables were assessed for normality using Shapiro-Wilk tests and Q-Q plots; due to non-normal distributions, nonparametric methods were utilized. Continuous data are summarized as median (interquartile range). Between-group differences were tested with the Mann-Whitney U test for two independent groups, the Wilcoxon signed-rank test for paired data, and the Kruskal-Wallis test for three or more groups with Dunn’s post hoc comparisons and multiplicity control (Holm or Benjamini-Hochberg, as appropriate). Associations between continuous variables were quantified using Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient (rho) with two-sided p values; ties were handled by average ranks. To visualize monotonic relationships consistent with the rank-based analyses, scatterplots were generated using rank-transformed variables (rank[X] vs. rank[Y]) with an ordinary least-squares line fit, while groupwise distributions on the raw scale are presented as boxplots with medians and interquartile ranges. Because Spearman’s ρ is rank-based, correlations are invariant to monotone transformations within the plate; rank percentiles were used to harmonize assay runs rather than to alter correlation structure. Statistical significance was defined as alpha = 0.05 (two-sided). Exact p values are reported throughout, except for values less than 0.001, which are denoted as p < 0.001.

Mediation Analysis [70]

Mediation analyses were performed within a standard single-mediator framework on the within-plate rank-percentile (0–1) scale. Tear-deficient status (Sjögren’s disease, oGVHD, or severe non-autoimmune tear-deficient DED vs. MGD/healthy controls) was the exposure (X), the LoB-stabilized PDGF-AB/BB:AA log-ratio (“PDGF Ratio”) was the mediator (M), and outcomes (Y) were (1) an Innate_NFκB composite (mean rank percentile of IL-18, IL-6, IL-8, and TNF-α) and (2) NEI corneal staining rank percentile. In pooled analyses, a fixed effect for assay plate was included; per-plate models were fit separately and therefore omitted the plate term. The mediation system comprised three linear regressions on the rank-percentile scale: the a path (M ∼ X + plate), the total effect c (Y ∼ X + plate), and the joint model (Y ∼ M + X + plate) yielding the b path and the direct effect c′. The indirect effect was computed as a × b; when the indirect and total effects had the same sign, the proportion mediated was reported as (a × b)/c. Uncertainty for a × b, c, and c′ was quantified using nonparametric percentile bootstrap 95% confidence intervals (5,000 resamples), stratified by plate in pooled analyses to preserve the discovery/replication structure [71–73]. Effects are interpreted as percentile-point shifts on the 0–1 rank-percentile scale.

Secondary, Serial, and Parallel Mediation Models

To estimate nonredundant contributions of aqueous volume and trophic balance, we fit a parallel mediation model in which outcomes were modeled as a function of PDGF Ratio, Schirmer score, tear-deficient status, and plate (pooled analyses). Mediator models regressed PDGF Ratio and Schirmer score separately on exposure and plate, and indirect effects were computed as the product of the corresponding a and b paths for each mediator; uncertainty was summarized using plate-stratified percentile bootstrap confidence intervals. Because trophic factors plausibly precede isoform imbalance, we also evaluated serial (“trophic chain”) models on the same 0–1 rank-percentile scale in which PDGF-AA (and, in sensitivity analyses, EGF) was modeled as an upstream mediator that transmits part of the exposure effect to PDGF Ratio, which then related to the outcome. The specification of parallel and serial multiple-mediator models followed the path-analytic framework described by Hayes [74]. Analyte-wise mediation was additionally performed for individual cytokines within the Innate_NFκB composite with Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate control. Multiple-mediator specifications followed the path-analytic framework described by Hayes [75].

Orthogonal and Relative-Importance Analyses

To assess whether isoform balance adds information beyond either PDGF isoform alone, we performed plate-adjusted Spearman partial-rank correlations conditioning on PDGF-AA or PDGF-AB/BB. To reduce concerns about algebraic coupling with the Ratio denominator, we additionally examined “residualized Ratio” signals by regressing the Ratio on PDGF-AA and plate and relating the resulting residuals to outcomes. Potential effect modification was assessed by including a PDGF-AA × Ratio interaction term in the plate-adjusted innate rank-scale model. Finally, to apportion isoform contributions, we fit plate-adjusted ordinary least-squares models on rank percentiles with PDGF-AA and PDGF-AB/BB entered simultaneously and partitioned explained variance using Shapley (relative-importance) shares based on the game-theoretic approach to regression described by Lipovetsky and Conklin [76], corroborated by hierarchical (sequential) regression to quantify incremental ΔR2 when each isoform was added second (nested partial F tests).

Computational Implementation

General statistical analyses and visualizations were performed in GraphPad Prism. Mediation modeling and advanced statistical procedures were implemented in Python (version 3.11.4). All code used for the reported analyses was reviewed, refactored as needed, and executed by the study team to confirm all reported estimates.

Results

Study Cohorts and Baseline Clinical Characteristics

A total of 151 participants were included across five cohorts: Sjögren’s syndrome (n = 40), definite oGVHD (n = 28), MGD (n = 37), severe non-autoimmune tear-deficient DED (n = 15), and healthy controls (n = 31) (Table 1). The tear-deficient cohorts (Sjögren’s syndrome, oGVHD, and tear-deficient DED) were older than healthy controls (median age 62.5, 61.5, and 56.0 vs. 46.0 years), while the MGD cohort had the highest median age (72.0 years). Sex distribution differed by cohort, with Sjögren’s syndrome predominantly female (7.5% male) and higher proportions of males in oGVHD (50%) and tear-deficient DED (60%). Symptom burden was greatest in Sjögren’s syndrome and oGVHD (median OSDI 55.3 and 47.7; median visual analog scale [VAS] 5.0 and 4.5) relative to MGD (median OSDI 31.8; VAS 4.0), while the tear-deficient DED subgroup showed intermediate OSDI but higher symptom intensity by VAS (median OSDI 34.1; VAS 6.0). Consistent with aqueous tear deficiency, Schirmer’s I values were markedly reduced in Sjögren’s syndrome, oGVHD, and tear-deficient DED (median 2.0, 0.5, and 3.0 mm, respectively) compared with MGD (median 21.0 mm), and corneal staining was more severe in tear-deficient cohorts (median 7.0–8.0 in Sjögren’s/oGVHD; 4.0 in tear-deficient DED) than in MGD (median 1.0). Healthy controls contributed age/sex information and OSW samples only; symptom questionnaires and ocular surface clinical examinations were not performed in this group.

PDGF Isoform Imbalance Replicates across Assay Runs

To characterize the immunological profile associated with the PDGF Ratio, we examined PDGF-AA, PDGF-AB/BB, and the PDGF-AB/BB:PDGF-AA ratio across two independent assay runs: discovery (plate 1; n = 79; Fig. 1) and replication (plate 2; n = 72; Fig. 2). In both runs, the directionality and diagnostic group ordering for PDGF-AA, PDGF-AB/BB, and the Ratio were concordant when visualized on the log-stabilized NetMFI scale (ln[NetMFI + LoB_analyte]; top panels) and on the derived within-plate rank-percentile scale (0–1; middle panels), indicating that the observed isoform pattern was reproducible and not driven by plate-specific calibration differences. Because the severe non-autoimmune tear-deficient DED subgroup was present only on plate 1, diagnosis-stratified comparisons were restricted to healthy, MGD, Sjögren’s, and oGVHD (represented in both assay runs), while the tear-deficient DED subgroup was retained in the binary tear-deficient definition used for mediation analyses. For downstream inference, we therefore used within-plate rank percentiles as the primary analysis scale (0.50 denotes the within-plate median) and pooled these harmonized values across plate 1 and plate 2 for statistical testing (Fig. 3, top row), which recapitulated the plate-specific directionality of PDGF-AA, PDGF-AB/BB, and the Ratio; concentration-scale summaries (bottom panels of Fig. 1, 2) are presented later.

PDGF Ratio Is Elevated in Tear-Deficient Disease

The PDGF-AB/BB:PDGF-AA ratio differed significantly across diagnostic groups in each assay run on the stabilized log scale (Tables 2, 3) and remained strongly separated on the pooled within-plate rank-percentile scale used for primary analyses (Fig. 3, top row; Table 4). On this pooled scale, the Ratio differed significantly across healthy, MGD, Sjögren’s, and oGVHD (Kruskal-Wallis H = 68.15, p < 0.001; Table 4). Median Ratio ranks increased from healthy controls (0.24) to Sjögren’s syndrome (0.68) and oGVHD (0.81) (Fig. 3; Table 4), reflecting a large upward shift on the percentile-rank scale. This shift reflected two concurrent changes: a decrease in PDGF-AA (median rank 0.69 in healthy to 0.19 in oGVHD) and an increase in PDGF-AB/BB (0.18 in healthy to 0.62 in oGVHD) (both p < 0.001; Fig. 3; Table 4). Together, these data indicate that PDGF isoform imbalance in tear-deficient disease reflects both PDGF-AA depletion and relative enrichment of PDGF-AB/BB.

High PDGF Ratio Aligns with the NF-κB-Associated Innate Cytokine Signature

Distribution and correlation analyses identified a reproducible inflammatory pattern associated with the elevated Ratio (Figure 3 and 4; Tables 4, 5). Innate, NF-Κb-linked cytokines were higher in Sjögren’s and oGVHD (Fig. 3; Table 4). IL-8 (CXCL8) showed a strong disease association (p < 0.001), with median rank increasing from 0.13 in healthy to 0.81 in oGVHD; similarly, IL-18 increased from 0.12 to 0.85 in oGVHD (p < 0.001) (Table 4). IL-6 and TNF-α also demonstrated higher ranks in tear-deficient disease groups, supporting the use of an innate composite dominated by NF-κB-associated cytokines (Fig. 3; Table 4). Consistent with these distributional shifts, the Ratio correlated positively with the innate composite and individual cytokines across plates and in pooled analyses (Fig. 4; Table 5).

Trophic Factors Are Depleted in the High-Ratio State

In contrast to the innate upregulation, trophic growth factors were reduced in tear-deficient phenotypes. EGF was depleted in parallel with PDGF-AA (p < 0.001; Fig. 3; Table 4) and was inversely associated with the Ratio in pooled correlation analyses (Fig. 4; Table 5), indicating broader reduction in trophic support signals in the high-Ratio state. IFN-γ showed a marked reduction in oGVHD relative to healthy (median rank 0.62 to 0.23; p < 0.001; Table 4), whereas IL-17A did not differ significantly across these diagnostic groups (p = 0.21; Table 4) (Fig. 3; Table 4). The Ratio also showed monotonic associations with clinical measures of tear deficiency and epithelial injury (Schirmer and corneal staining; Fig. 4; Table 5). This pattern, high innate cytokines and PDGF isoform imbalance together with low trophic growth factors and reduced IFN-γ, suggests that the high-Ratio state aligns with an innate-weighted inflammatory milieu and trophic depletion rather than an adaptive cytokine-dominant profile. These descriptive findings motivated formal mediation analyses to quantify whether PDGF isoform imbalance statistically accounts for part of the tear-deficient association with innate activation and epithelial injury.

Plate-Specific Concentration Estimates Support PDGF Isoform Imbalance

Plate-specific concentration estimates (pg/mL) derived from standard curves corroborated the PDGF isoform imbalance seen on the stabilized and rank-based scales and are therefore reported separately for plate 1 and plate 2 in the four diagnostic groups visualized in Figures 1 and 2 (bottom panels) (healthy, MGD, Sjögren’s, oGVHD; Tables 6, 7). On plate 1, PDGF-AA showed marked depletion from healthy to oGVHD (median 93 vs. 6.5 pg/mL), while PDGF-AB/BB shifted from predominantly below the LLOQ in healthy (88% below LLOQ; median 4.8 pg/mL) to largely quantifiable in tear-deficient phenotypes, yielding a substantial increase in the PDGF-AB/BB:PDGF-AA ratio (median 0.073 in healthy vs. 19 in oGVHD; Table 6; Fig. 1, bottom). On plate 2, PDGF-AA depletion replicated (median 150 vs. 36 pg/mL), whereas PDGF-AB/BB concentration estimates were constrained by substantial low-end censoring (80% below LLOQ in healthy and 42–55% below LLOQ across tear-deficient groups), limiting discrimination by PDGF-AB/BB alone; nonetheless, the PDGF-AB/BB:PDGF-AA ratio remained higher in tear-deficient phenotypes (median 0.14 in healthy vs. 2.8 in oGVHD; Table 7; Fig. 2, bottom). Overall, the concentration-scale summaries reinforce PDGF-AA depletion and support interpreting PDGF-AB/BB primarily as a shift toward detectability (particularly on plate 2), consistent with the primary LoB-stabilized and rank-based findings.

Mediation Framework for the PDGF Ratio Pathway

Our biological question was whether PDGF isoform balance, operationalized as the stabilized PDGF-AB/BB:AA log-ratio (“Ratio”), accounts for part of the association between tear-deficient status and two clinically relevant outcomes: an innate cytokine composite (Innate_NFkB; mean of IL-18, IL-6, IL-8, TNF-α) and NEI corneal staining. In statistical terms, mediation decomposes the overall association (the total effect, c) into (1) an indirect path (a × b) that runs through the mediator (tear deficiency → Ratio → outcome) and (2) a direct path (c′) that captures any remaining association after the mediator is included. A statistically nonzero indirect path indicates that part of the tear-deficient association with the outcome is statistically consistent with a pathway operating through the Ratio (Fig. 5).

Mediation framework for PDGF isoform balance (PDGF-AB/BB:AA ratio) in tear-deficient disease. The schematic depicts a standard single-mediator model in which X is tear-deficient status, M is the stabilized PDGF isoform balance (PDGF Ratio), and Y is the outcome (innate composite, left; corneal staining, right). Coefficients are reported on the pooled, plate-adjusted within-plate rank-percentile (0–1) scale; uncertainty for c, c′, and a × b is summarized using percentile bootstrap 95% confidence intervals (5,000 resamples). Left panel (innate composite): n = 151. Right panel (NEI corneal staining): n = 120 (healthy controls excluded because corneal staining was not assessed; non-tear-deficient controls are therefore derived from MGD).

PDGF Ratio Mediates the Tear-Deficient Association with the Innate Signature

In pooled, plate-adjusted mediation models on the within-plate rank-percentile scale (0 to 1, where 0.10 corresponds to a 10 percentile-point shift), tear-deficient status was associated with higher PDGF isoform imbalance (Ratio), and this Ratio accounted for a substantial portion of the tear-deficient association with the innate cytokine composite. For the innate cytokine composite outcome, the total association of tear-deficient status with the outcome (c, total effect) was 0.268 (95% CI 0.193 to 0.342). Tear-deficient status was associated with an increase in the PDGF Ratio (a path 0.360), and higher PDGF Ratio was associated with higher innate composite after accounting for tear-deficient status (b path 0.467). The resulting indirect effect (a × b) was 0.168 (95% CI 0.118 to 0.225), indicating that approximately 0.168 of the 0.268 total shift in innate composite rank percentile (about 17 of 27 percentile points) was statistically consistent with the pathway through the PDGF Ratio. The corresponding proportion mediated was 0.628 (62.8%). The residual direct effect of tear-deficient status after accounting for the Ratio (c′) was 0.100 (95% CI 0.013 to 0.187), indicating that a smaller but nonzero portion of the tear-deficient association with innate inflammation remained outside the Ratio pathway. Taken together, the Ratio-mediated pathway accounted for roughly two-thirds of the tear deficiency association with Innate_NFkB on the rank-percentile scale.

PDGF Ratio Partially Mediates the Tear-Deficient Association with Corneal Staining

For corneal staining (NEI) as the outcome, the PDGF Ratio again mediated part of the tear-deficient association, but the mediated fraction was smaller and the direct component was larger. The total effect of tear-deficient status on corneal staining rank percentile was 0.342 (95% CI 0.249 to 0.433). Tear-deficient status was associated with higher PDGF Ratio (a path 0.330), and higher Ratio was associated with higher corneal staining after accounting for tear-deficient status (b path 0.355). The indirect effect (a × b) was 0.117 (95% CI 0.055 to 0.190), corresponding to a mediated proportion of 0.342 (34.2%). The direct effect remained substantial (c′ 0.225; 95% CI 0.113 to 0.336), indicating that most of the tear-deficient association with corneal staining was not explained by the Ratio and is consistent with additional non-PDGF pathways contributing to epithelial damage. Thus, for staining, the Ratio-mediated pathway accounted for about one-third of the tear deficiency association, with a larger residual component outside the Ratio pathway.

Interpreting the Mediation Paths (a and b)

The “a” path quantifies how much tear-deficient status shifts the mediator. Here, a equals 0.360 for the innate model and 0.330 for the staining model, meaning that tear-deficient eyes tended to sit about 0.33 to 0.36 higher on the 0 to 1 PDGF Ratio percentile scale than non-tear-deficient controls (roughly a 33 to 36 percentile-point upward shift in the Ratio within plate). The “b” path quantifies how strongly the mediator tracks the outcome after accounting for tear-deficient status. Here, b equals 0.467 (innate) and 0.355 (staining), meaning that a one-unit increase across the full 0 to 1 Ratio percentile range corresponds to a 0.467 increase in innate composite percentile (about 47 percentile points) and a 0.355 increase in staining percentile (about 36 percentile points), after accounting for tear-deficient status. In other words, the indirect effect reflects the expected outcome shift attributable to the combination of (1) tear deficiency increasing the Ratio (a) and (2) higher Ratio tracking higher outcome ranks (b). We next assessed whether this mediation signal was distributed across individual cytokines and reproducible across plates.

Mediation Is Consistent across Individual Innate Cytokines

As a sensitivity check, we repeated the single-mediator model using each Innate_NFkB component cytokine as the outcome (IL-18, IL-6, IL-8, and TNF-α), with tear-deficient status as the exposure and the PDGF Ratio as the mediator (pooled, plate-adjusted models; 5,000 bootstrap resamples). The indirect effect through the PDGF Ratio was significant for all four cytokines after correction for multiple testing (Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate q = 4.0 × 10^−4 for each), with indirect effect sizes on the rank-percentile scale ranging from 0.152 to 0.197 (IL-18: 0.197, 95% CI 0.145–0.259; IL-6: 0.163, 95% CI 0.106–0.225; IL-8: 0.161, 95% CI 0.104–0.225; TNF-α: 0.152, 95% CI 0.085–0.226). The proportion of the total effect accounted for by the PDGF Ratio was substantial for IL-18 (56.4%), IL-6 (63.8%), and IL-8 (48.8%), indicating that the composite-level mediation signal reflects a consistent pattern across multiple innate cytokines rather than being driven by a single analyte. For TNF-α, the indirect effect exceeded the total effect (proportion mediated 111.0%) because the estimated direct effect was slightly negative (an inconsistent or suppression pattern), which supports the presence of a robust mediated pathway while making the “percent mediated” less interpretable for that specific cytokine.

Mediation Signal by Plate (Discovery versus Replication)

We also examined per-plate replication to assess stability across assay plates. For the innate outcome, plate 1 showed a significant mediated effect (0.147, 95% CI 0.083–0.229; p < 0.001; n = 79), and plate 2 similarly showed a significant mediated effect (0.183, 95% CI 0.088–0.265; p < 0.001; n = 72). For NEI staining, plate 1 was directionally positive and significant (0.098, 95% CI 0.029–0.196; p = 0.003; n = 63), whereas plate 2 was directionally positive but not significant (0.078, 95% CI −0.007–0.176; p = 0.073; n = 57). Taken together, directionality was consistent across plates, with stronger evidence for a Ratio-mediated pathway for innate inflammation than for staining in Plate 2; pooled models summarize the average path while acknowledging this heterogeneity. Overall, the mediated pathway was more consistently supported for Innate_NFkB than for staining across plates.

Dissecting PDGF-AA versus PDGF-AB/BB Contributions to the PDGF Ratio

Because the PDGF Ratio is constructed from PDGF-AB/BB and PDGF-AA, we tested whether its associations with inflammation and epithelial injury were primarily driven by one isoform. In pooled, plate-adjusted Spearman partial-rank analyses (plates 1 and 2), the Ratio remained strongly associated with the innate composite after conditioning on PDGF-AA (partial ρ = 0.679, p < 0.001) and remained significant, though attenuated, after conditioning on PDGF-AB/BB (partial ρ = 0.515, p < 0.001) (Table 8). Complementary residualization showed that the component of the Ratio not explained by PDGF-AA remained strongly associated with innate (ρ = 0.700, p < 0.001), whereas the component not explained by PDGF-AB/BB was more weakly associated (ρ = 0.377, p < 0.001). In contrast, for corneal staining, the Ratio remained associated after conditioning on PDGF-AB/BB (partial ρ = 0.518, p < 0.001) but was not associated after conditioning on PDGF-AA (partial ρ = 0.145, p = 0.113); the same pattern was seen with residualized Ratio components.

PDGF-AA Modifies the Ratio-Innate Association

An interaction model further showed that PDGF-AA significantly modified the Ratio–innate relationship (Ratio × PDGF-AA β = −0.610, p = 0.001), indicating that higher PDGF-AA blunts the association between Ratio elevation and innate activation. Collectively, the conditioning/residualization results indicate that the Ratio-innate association is robust to adjustment for either isoform, whereas the Ratio-staining association is sensitive to PDGF-AA adjustment, an observation further clarified by the isoform relative-importance analysis below. These findings motivated a formal relative-importance decomposition of PDGF-AA versus PDGF-AB/BB contributions (Shapley analysis), presented next.

Relative Isoform Contributions to the Innate Signature

We modeled each outcome on the within-plate rank-percentile scale using plate-adjusted ordinary least-squares regression with PDGF-AA and PDGF-AB/BB entered simultaneously and then partitioned explained variance using Shapley (relative-importance) shares, corroborated by hierarchical ΔR2 (nested partial F tests). For the innate cytokine composite (n = 151), PDGF-AB/BB showed a strong positive association with the outcome (β = 0.637), whereas PDGF-AA showed an inverse association (β = −0.298). In this joint model, PDGF isoforms together explained a large fraction of outcome variability beyond plate (full model R2 = 0.676 above plate-only). Shapley decomposition of the model R2 indicated that most of this explained variance was attributable to PDGF-AB/BB (Shapley share 79%, ΔR2 = 0.532) rather than PDGF-AA (Shapley share 21%, ΔR2 = 0.144). Consistently, in nested (hierarchical) models, adding PDGF-AB/BB after PDGF-AA increased R2 by 0.496 (partial F test p < 0.001), whereas adding PDGF-AA after PDGF-AB/BB increased R2 by 0.109 (p < 0.001), indicating that PDGF-AB/BB contributes the dominant incremental explanatory information for innate inflammation on this rank scale (Fig. 6).

Relative Isoform Contributions to Corneal Staining

For corneal staining (NEI; n = 120), the pattern was reversed. PDGF-AA showed a strong inverse association with staining (β = −0.513), while PDGF-AB/BB showed a smaller positive association (β = 0.192). The joint model explained a more modest fraction of variability beyond plate (full model R2 = 0.316 above plate-only). Shapley decomposition showed that PDGF-AA accounted for the majority of the explained variance (Shapley share 87%, ΔR2 = 0.275), with a smaller contribution from PDGF-AB/BB (Shapley share 13%, ΔR2 = 0.041). Hierarchical models corroborated this: adding PDGF-AA after PDGF-AB/BB increased R2 by 0.263 (partial F test p < 0.001), whereas adding PDGF-AB/BB after PDGF-AA increased R2 by 0.030 (p = 0.027). Taken together, these results indicate that the isoform most informative for innate inflammatory rank variation is PDGF-AB/BB, whereas the isoform most informative for epithelial staining rank variation is PDGF-AA (in the protective direction), with PDGF-AB/BB contributing a smaller, secondary component. This isoform-specific association pattern mirrors the mediation patterns above (Innate_NFkB dominated by AB/BB; staining dominated by AA depletion).

Serial Mediation Models for Trophic → Ratio Pathways

Serial “trophic chain” mediation analyses were consistent with an ordered pattern in which tear-deficient status was related to outcomes through sequential changes in an upstream trophic factor followed by a shift in PDGF isoform balance (PDGF Ratio). For the innate cytokine composite outcome (n = 151), tear-deficient status was associated with lower PDGF-AA and lower EGF, and in each model lower trophic factor levels were associated with a higher PDGF Ratio, which in turn was positively associated with the innate composite in the outcome model (Fig. 7). The resulting serial indirect effect through the PDGF-AA than PDGF Ratio chain was significant (serial indirect = 0.348; 95% CI 0.253 to 0.474; p < 0.001), and the corresponding EGF than PDGF Ratio chain was also significant, although smaller in magnitude (serial indirect = 0.069; 95% CI 0.035 to 0.119; p < 0.001). For corneal staining (NEI) as the outcome (n = 120), tear-deficient status was again associated with lower PDGF-AA and lower EGF and with higher PDGF Ratio in the corresponding models (Fig. 8); however, the serial indirect effect through PDGF-AA than PDGF Ratio was not distinguishable from zero (serial indirect = 0.066; 95% CI −0.040 to 0.180; p = 0.212), whereas the serial indirect effect through EGF than PDGF Ratio remained significant (serial indirect = 0.040; 95% CI 0.013 to 0.080; p = 0.003). Collectively, these serial models are consistent with a “trophic depletion → Ratio elevation” sequence for Innate_NFkB, while staining showed stronger evidence for an EGF-linked chain than for a PDGF-AA-linked chain.

Parallel Mediation Separates Ratio and Tear Volume Pathways

In a complementary parallel mediation model for corneal staining (n = 119) that jointly included PDGF Ratio and Schirmer score (both on the rank-percentile scale) as mediators, the indirect pathway through PDGF Ratio was statistically supported (indirect via Ratio = 0.113; 95% CI 0.050 to 0.185; p < 0.001), whereas the indirect pathway through Schirmer was directionally consistent but not significant at this sample size (indirect via Schirmer = 0.093; 95% CI −0.018 to 0.214; p = 0.099) (Fig. 9). The combined indirect effect across both mediators was significant (total indirect = 0.206; 95% CI 0.087 to 0.332; p < 0.001), and the residual direct effect of tear-deficient status on staining after accounting for both mediators was attenuated and borderline (direct effect = 0.131; 95% CI −0.007 to 0.271; p = 0.068). On the 0 to 1 rank-percentile scale used for modeling, an indirect effect of 0.113 corresponds to an approximately 11 percentile-point upward shift in staining rank attributable to the PDGF Ratio pathway. Thus, when Ratio and tear volume were considered simultaneously, the Ratio pathway remained supported while the Schirmer pathway was less certain at this sample size.

Within-Subject Asymmetric-Eye Corroboration

To provide within-subject supportive evidence that the PDGF isoform imbalance reflects local ocular surface pathology rather than systemic spillover, we analyzed three subjects with marked inter-eye discordance in clinical severity (two oGVHD pairs[(Fig. 10, 11] and one MGD pair [Fig. 12]), using the contralateral eye as an internal control while applying the same analyte-specific LoB stabilization and log transformation pipeline used in the main cohorts. Across all three within-subject comparisons, the clinically more severe eye demonstrated a concordant “trophic imbalance/innate activation” shift: the stabilized PDGF isoform balance increased in every pair (Δ stabilized log-ratio = +0.704 to +1.559, corresponding to an approximately 2.0- to 4.8-fold increase on the ratio scale), driven by PDGF-AA depletion (Δ ln[PDGF-AA+LoB] = −0.352 to −1.382) accompanied by a smaller but consistent PDGF-AB/BB increase (Δ ln[PDGF-AB/BB+LoB] = +0.178 to +0.413) (Table 9, 10). In parallel, the severe eye showed higher LoB-stabilized innate cytokine signals for each component of the NF-κB composite (IL-8, IL-6, IL-18, and TNF-α all increased in all three pairs), with a corresponding increase in the within-plate rank-percentile Innate_NFkB composite (Δ = +0.286 to +0.691). Clinically, tear volume was lower in the more severe eye in every pair (Δ Schirmer = −7 to −25 mm), and the two oGVHD pairs exhibited large concomitant increases in corneal staining (Δ NEI staining = +9 and +10), whereas the MGD pair showed molecular shifts despite absent staining in both eyes. Because systemic factors (genetics, systemic inflammatory milieu, and systemic medications) are shared within each subject, these paired-eye findings provide a controlled rationale for presenting the case series as supportive evidence that PDGF isoform imbalance and the coupled innate signature track with local disease severity on the ocular surface. Overall, these within-subject comparisons recapitulate the cohort-level pattern: PDGF-AA depletion and an elevated Ratio co-occur with higher innate signaling in the more clinically affected eye.

Discussion

In tear-deficient DED, most prominently in oGVHD, our results define a coupled trophic-deficient/innate-activated phenotype in which PDGF isoform imbalance (PDGF-AA depletion with relative PDGF-AB/BB enrichment) co-occurs with depletion of other repair-associated growth signals (including EGF) and elevation of an NF-κB–associated innate cytokine signature (IL-6, IL-8, IL-18, TNF-α), alongside corneal epitheliopathy. Across two independent assay runs (plate 1 discovery; plate 2 replication), tear-deficient phenotypes showed a reproducible upward shift in the PDGF-AB/BB:AA “Ratio.” While reduced EGF has been reported in oGVHD [77, 78], to our knowledge PDGF-AA depletion has not been widely highlighted in tear-based profiling, suggesting a potentially underrecognized trophic lesion in severe tear-deficient disease. On the harmonized within-plate rank-percentile scale, higher Ratio co-occurred with higher innate cytokine ranks and statistically accounted for part of the association between tear deficiency and both Innate_NFκB (NF-κB-associated innate cytokine signature) and corneal staining. Isoform-specific relative-importance results further suggested a differential association pattern, with PDGF-AB/BB contributing most of the PDGF-attributable variance in Innate_NFκB and PDGF-AA contributing most of the PDGF-attributable variance in staining. The functional distinction between PDGF-AA and PDGF-AB/BB aligns with their divergent roles in ocular surface biology: PDGF-AA is commonly considered a maintenance-associated ligand (primarily via PDGFRα) [42–44], whereas PDGF-AB/BB (reflecting AB and BB dimers in this assay) is more injury-associated and becomes more detectable following epithelial disruption [58, 79]. An independent study performed in Brazil using Luminex multiplex quantification of undiluted tears likewise observed PDGF-AA predominance over PDGF-AB/BB in healthy controls (mean PDGF-AB/BB:PDGF-AA ratio ≈0.38), directionally consistent with our healthy OSW ratios (≈0.07–0.14); magnitude differences may reflect sampling matrix (undiluted tears vs. 50 µL OSW), eye-drop exposure exclusions, and dynamic range/low-end behavior near assay limits across platforms [68].

On the absolute (NetMFI and standard-curve pg/mL) scales, this isoform-shift phenotype is dominated by PDGF-AA depletion across both plates, while PDGF-AB/BB in controls is largely near the assay floor and is therefore most interpretable as a shift toward detectability in tear-deficient disease. The most robust absolute-scale signal across both plates was PDGF-AA depletion. Median PDGF-AA NetMFI declined approximately 9- to 10-fold from healthy controls to oGVHD in plate 1 (1,824 to 193 NetMFI) and approximately 4.5-fold in plate 2 (195 to 43 NetMFI), and plate-specific standard-curve estimates showed concordant directionality (median PDGF-AA 93→6.5 pg/mL in plate 1; 150→36 pg/mL in plate 2). In contrast, PDGF-AB/BB in healthy controls was frequently at or below the LLOQ (88% in plate 1; 80% in plate 2) and shifted toward greater detectability in tear-deficient groups, while remaining near the low-end quantification floor in a subset of samples (e.g., 42–55% below LLOQ across plate 2 diagnostic groups). Accordingly, PDGF-AB/BB in this dataset is best interpreted as showing a distributional shift toward detectability (from “often unquantifiable” toward “more often quantifiable”) rather than uniformly large absolute enrichment.

Despite these low-end constraints for PDGF-AB/BB on a concentration scale, multiple analyses support the Ratio as an informative summary of isoform imbalance on the primary rank-based modeling scale. In relative-importance models for Innate_NFκB, PDGF-AB/BB accounted for 79% of the explained variance within the joint PDGF model (Shapley share of model R2 beyond plate), whereas PDGF-AA accounted for 21%, indicating that variation in PDGF-AB/BB carried the dominant incremental information for innate signaling on this scale. For corneal staining, the pattern reversed: PDGF-AA depletion accounted for 87% of the explained variance within the joint PDGF model, consistent with a stronger trophic association with epithelial integrity. Because the assay reports a combined PDGF-AB/BB signal (AB and BB dimers), mechanistic inferences specific to PDGF-BB should be framed cautiously and, where feasible, validated with orthogonal measurements in future work.

Finally, the asymmetric fellow-eye analyses provide within-subject supportive evidence that the PDGF/innate pattern tracks with local clinical severity under a shared systemic background. In each of three discordant pairs, the more clinically affected eye showed a higher stabilized PDGF Ratio driven by PDGF-AA depletion, accompanied by higher innate cytokine signals. EGF changes were not directionally consistent across the oGVHD pairs, reinforcing PDGF-AA depletion and the Ratio as the most reproducible trophic-associated lesion in this small internal control series. These paired-eye findings remain hypothesis-supporting (selection for asymmetry; cross-sectional sampling), but they reduce the plausibility that the Ratio signal is purely a systemic epiphenomenon.

Mediation as a Statistical Pathway and Clinical Implications

The mediation analyses were designed to quantify how much of the observed tear-deficient association with inflammation and epithelial injury is statistically consistent with a pathway through PDGF isoform imbalance on the within-plate rank-percentile scale. In pooled models, the PDGF Ratio mediated 62.8% of the tear-deficient association with the Innate_NFκB composite (indirect effect 0.168 on the 0–1 scale; ∼17 percentile points) and 34.2% of the association with corneal staining (indirect effect 0.117; ∼12 percentile points). Substantial residual direct effects remained for both outcomes, emphasizing that PDGF imbalance is not sufficient to explain tear-deficient disease biology and that additional, non-PDGF pathways contribute meaningfully to innate signaling and epithelial damage.

Two observations are clinically relevant. First, the larger mediated fraction for Innate_NFκB suggests that PDGF isoform imbalance and this innate cytokine axis are closely linked on this modeling scale in tear-deficient phenotypes, consistent with a model in which growth factor disruption may help sustain an innate-weighted inflammatory milieu rather than merely reflecting it. Second, for staining, the indirect pathway through the Ratio remained supported in a parallel mediation model that included tear volume (Schirmer) as a co-mediator (indirect via Ratio 0.113, 95% CI 0.050–0.185; p < 0.001), whereas the Schirmer pathway was directionally consistent but imprecise at this sample size (indirect via Schirmer 0.093, 95% CI −0.018–0.214; p = 0.099). This pattern is consistent with, but does not prove, a “two-conduit” framework in which aqueous deficiency and trophic balance perturbation represent partially separable contributors to corneal epitheliopathy.

Causality cannot be inferred from cross-sectional mediation; however, these decompositions help frame testable therapeutic hypotheses. Current standard approaches largely address desiccation (tear supplementation/retention) and downstream inflammatory signaling (topical corticosteroids, immunomodulators) [5, 14, 80], but they do not directly target growth factor depletion or PDGF isoform balance. Our results therefore motivate evaluation of whether interventions that restore trophic support (e.g., PDGF-AA-linked supplementation or upstream trophic restoration) and/or restrain AB/BB-linked signaling can shift the Ratio and covary with improvements in innate cytokine ranks and epithelial staining. Importantly, the analyte-wise mediations indicate that the indirect pathway was reproducible across IL-18, IL-6, IL-8, and TNF-α, but the TNF-α model showed an “inconsistent/suppression” pattern (slightly negative direct effect), making “percent mediated” less interpretable for that cytokine despite evidence of an indirect component.

Broader Relevance and Therapeutic Precedents

PDGF signaling is a canonical regulator of stromal activation and tissue remodeling [44, 46], and it is pharmacologically tractable in other clinical contexts [81, 82]. In the ocular surface setting, prior work has largely framed PDGF as an injury-response and remodeling pathway [45, 58, 59, 83, 84]; our data extend that conceptual space by linking PDGF isoform imbalance to an innate-weighted tear cytokine profile and by quantifying a nontrivial statistical pathway from tear-deficient status to innate activation and corneal staining through the PDGF-AB/BB:AA ratio. Importantly, the isoform-specific results sharpen how PDGF may relate to distinct aspects of disease biology: PDGF-AA behaved as a trophic-associated signal that tracked epithelial integrity (dominant PDGF contribution to staining variance), whereas the AB/BB-linked signal carried the dominant incremental information for Innate_NFκB on the rank scale. This “division of labor” motivates isoform-selective modulation (“rebalancing”) as a better-aligned translational hypothesis than indiscriminate PDGFR blockade, because nonselective inhibition could suppress homeostatic/trophic signaling alongside injury-associated signaling.

Mechanistically, these patterns are directionally consistent with published distinctions between PDGF-AA and PDGF-BB/AB in tissue biology: PDGF-AA is commonly described as a maintenance-associated ligand (often via PDGFRα) [42–44], whereas PDGF-BB-dominant signaling (often via PDGFRβ in other systems) has been linked to fibroblast activation programs and NF-κB-linked cytokine outputs, including IL-6 and IL-8 [46, 60]. In that context, the co-occurrence we observe, higher PDGF Ratio alongside higher IL-6/IL-8/IL-18 ranks, supports biological plausibility for a PDGF-linked innate circuit in severe tear-deficient disease, while remaining agnostic about causal direction. At the same time, interpretation of the AB/BB component must remain cautious: the assay reports a combined PDGF-AB/BB signal, and low-end quantification constraints were evident, most prominently on plate 2, where a high fraction of healthy samples were below LLOQ and low-range concentration estimates were correspondingly less discriminative across groups. For that reason, the most robust plate-level information is the shift in detectability and the behavior of the Ratio, rather than fine-grained inference from low-range AB/BB concentration differences.

From a therapeutic perspective, the key translational implication is not that this study proves a treatment strategy, but that the data identify a coherent, testable axis that is distinct from aqueous volume alone. PDGF pathway modulation has precedents (including anti-PDGF strategies explored in retinal disease and systemic agents with PDGFR activity used in oncology and fibrotic disease) [81, 85–87], but those approaches largely involve pathway inhibition. In contrast, the pattern here, PDGF-AA depletion coupled with relative AB/BB-linked enrichment, suggests a different hypothesis worth testing: isoform-selective rebalancing, i.e., restoring trophic-associated PDGF-AA-linked signaling while restraining AB/BB-linked signaling, rather than globally suppressing PDGFR activity. Our data suggest that restoring trophic PDGF-AA while selectively restraining injury-associated AB/BB signaling may be required to resolve the refractory state in tear-deficient DED. Whether such an approach improves epithelial outcomes and/or innate cytokine outputs in tear-deficient DED remains an empirical question that will require assays capable of better resolving low-end PDGF-AB/BB and interventional studies designed to test whether changing the Ratio co-moves with changes in staining and innate signaling.

Study Limitations and Future Directions

Because this study is cross-sectional, the mediation results should be interpreted as a statistical decomposition of association rather than evidence of temporal ordering or causality; longitudinal sampling and interventional studies will be required to test whether PDGF-AA depletion and PDGF Ratio elevation precede (and predict) subsequent changes in innate cytokines and epithelial injury. Low-end quantification also constrains interpretation of PDGF-AB/BB in controls: a substantial fraction of values lie near the assay’s LLOQ, and in plate 2 the lowest PDGF-AB/BB standard approached blank signal, limiting confidence in absolute concentration differences at the floor even though rank-based and LoB-stabilized analyses are robust to monotone scaling. Future studies using more sensitive, low-volume platforms (e.g., ultrasensitive immunoassays or targeted LC-MS/MS) will be important to better resolve baseline PDGF-AB/BB and strengthen translation from rank-based signals to absolute concentration biology. Furthermore, from this study, we are unable to determine whether the measured PDGF-AB/BB signal represents free protein or protein bound to soluble receptors. Beyond these assay-related limitations, NET-associated proteins (e.g., histones) were not quantified in this multiplex dataset and should be evaluated in future studies using orthogonal assays to integrate NET burden with growth factor imbalance. In addition, OSW collection introduces dilution, so assay-derived concentrations should be interpreted as within-plate assay-based values rather than direct tear meniscus concentrations; methods that directly sample very small volumes of undiluted tears (including microfluidic approaches operating at nanoliter scale, as in tear osmolarity systems) could enable more definitive concentration reporting and cross-study comparability. Finally, because participants were recruited from a tertiary referral clinic enriched for severe and autoimmune-associated disease, external validation in broader and earlier stage populations will be needed to define generalizability and to determine where along the severity spectrum PDGF isoform imbalance emerges. Where feasible, future prospective designs should also standardize sampling relative to topical/systemic therapies to reduce treatment-related confounding. Importantly, clinical biomarker validation (including thresholds, predictive performance, and external prospective cohorts) was not an objective of this study and remains necessary. Together, these steps would allow a tighter bridge from robust rank-based inference to absolute concentration biology and therapeutic targeting.

In summary, across two independent assay runs, our data suggest that PDGF isoform imbalance in moderate-to-severe tear-deficient DED is linked to an innate-weighted inflammatory milieu and corneal epitheliopathy, and is statistically consistent with a trophic/inflammatory axis that may help sustain refractory disease. These findings position the PDGF Ratio as a coherent biomarker candidate and a testable mechanistic hypothesis, one that motivates longitudinal validation and isoform-targeted studies aimed at restoring trophic balance while constraining injury-associated PDGF signaling.

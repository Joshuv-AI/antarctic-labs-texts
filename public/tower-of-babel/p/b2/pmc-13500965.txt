Response to “Letter Regarding: Dual-Incision Achilles Tendon Repair Using Krackow Sutures With Over-the-Top Knot Placement for Early Rehabilitation Without Immobilization: A Retrospective Case Series”
Authors: Kaho Yasuda, Akifumi Fujita, Gantaro Minami, Hiromitsu Moriuchi
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We sincerely thank Spiezia and colleagues for their thoughtful comments regarding our recent article describing dual-incision Achilles tendon repair using Krackow locking sutures with over-the-top knot placement. We appreciate their constructive discussion and the opportunity to further clarify several aspects of our study. 1

We agree that the relatively prolonged postoperative hospital stay in our cohort may limit the generalizability of the rehabilitation protocol. As discussed in our article, inpatient supervision likely facilitated close clinical monitoring and early rehabilitation, and caution is warranted when extrapolating these findings to health care systems in which Achilles tendon repair is routinely managed on an outpatient basis. The present study was intended to report the clinical outcomes achieved using a combined surgical technique and rehabilitation protocol rather than to isolate the independent effect of postoperative immobilization.

We also agree that objective assessment of tendon healing using serial ultrasonography or magnetic resonance imaging would further strengthen the evaluation of repair integrity. For this reason, the absence of systematic imaging assessment and direct biomechanical evaluation was acknowledged as a limitation of our study. Future prospective studies incorporating structural imaging and objective functional assessments are warranted to further validate these findings.

Regarding functional recovery, we acknowledge that the median Tegner Activity Scale (TAS) score decreased from 7 to 6. However, because the TAS is an ordinal scale, summary statistics alone may not fully reflect individual patient recovery. Therefore, we reported both patient-level recovery and mean TAS values. Among the 35 patients who had reached at least 12 months of follow-up, 33 (94%) returned to their preinjury activity level, whereas only 2 patients demonstrated a decrease of 1 point. We believe these patient-level findings provide important complementary information regarding postoperative functional recovery.

We thank Spiezia and colleagues once again for their valuable comments. We agree that prospective comparative studies, particularly those performed in outpatient settings and incorporating objective imaging assessments, will be important to further establish the reproducibility and generalizability of this technique.

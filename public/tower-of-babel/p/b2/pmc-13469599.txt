Scalable distributed control for hybrid AC-DC microgrids with adaptive load management
Authors: Satyaveer Singh Negi, Adel Rawea, Prakash Dwivedi, Sourav Bose, Yahya Alward
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractHybrid AC-DC microgrids provide a critical architecture to integrate distributed energy resources, energy storage, and DC loads by minimizing power conversion stages, thereby enhancing overall system efficiency and reliability. This paper proposes a scalable hybrid AC-DC microgrid utilizing the untapped potential of isolated distributed solar lighting systems having their own solar PV panels and battery storage. The proposed hybrid microgrid consists of a 50 kW solar PV plant and 50 units of solar lighting systems. Each solar lighting system has a 200 W photovoltaic panel, a 48 V and 2 kWh battery capacity, and a bidirectional converter. This forms an aggregated 10 kW distributed energy resource having grid-forming capability supporting islanded-mode operation. A virtual impedance-based droop control mechanism is implemented for solar lighting distributed converters to enhance their current sharing accuracy and system stability. The proposed hierarchical control architecture encompasses primary and secondary control, with a 60 kW bidirectional interlinking converter facilitating power flow between the 220 V DC and 220 V AC sides of the hybrid microgrid. In islanded mode, the excess power is managed with the help of an electronic load controller for dump loads. This proposed control strategy enables intensity control of solar LED lights, reducing the lighting load by up to 20 percent during high demand periods to support the grid. The simulation is carried out in MATLAB and results show stable microgrid voltage and proportional current sharing among distributed converters. The microgrid achieves a seamless transition from grid-connected to islanded operation even after unintentional islanding, where the results demonstrate that system restores itself to normal operation within 2 cycles with the current overshoot in acceptable limits.Keywords: DC Microgrid, AC-DC interlinking converter, Virtual impedance, Droop control, Hierarchical control, MPPT, Energy storage, Load management, Electronic load controller, Dump loadSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering

Introduction

There is a need for providing electricity in rural areas and with the renewable energy resources such as solar-PV which can be easily installed near load centers there is a demand for incorporating grid forming technologies to operate microgrid in islanded conditions. DC microgrids offer some advantages by directly adding DC loads without the need of extra converter and incorporate solar photovoltaic systems with the grid while minimizing conversion losses. The increase in DC loads, particularly LED lighting systems, DC pumps and electronic devices, given the inherent DC nature of photovoltaic (PV) systems and battery storage, makes DC microgrids an obvious choice for adding PV resource on DC microgrid systems1.

There are some challenges in operation of microgrids such as current sharing accuracy, voltage regulation, and load management during critical operating conditions. Recent comprehensive reviews9 establish virtual impedance-based droop control as a mature solution for addressing these challenges, particularly in DC microgrids where line impedance variations significantly affect power sharing performance. Advanced implementations with hybrid energy storage systems17 have demonstrated successful integration achieving automatic power sharing through virtual resistance. The investigation of droop control strategies for DC microgrids have been done in several literature1, emphasizing on state-of-charge balance3 and voltage sharing4. There are novel approaches proposed for average voltage sharing control and frequency injection-based decentralized control5. The hierarchical control framework2 has been established as a general approach to coordinate droop-controlled microgrids.

Recent studies in hybrid AC-DC microgrids focus on bidirectional interlinking converter control strategies10,11. Lin et al.16 proposed a distributed power management strategy for multi-paralleled bidirectional interlinking converters, demonstrating improved reliability and power sharing. Recent advances include unified decentralized frameworks using dual active bridge converters with galvanic isolation21 and robust sliding mode control designs22 that maintain power stability under load variations. Enhanced normalization methods24 and generalized bidirectional droop control architectures25 further improve power flow management during mode transitions. However, practical implementation challenges remain regarding computational complexity and real-time performance under varying load conditions.

Energy management in DC microgrids with distributed solar generation has been extensively studied. Qi et al.14 presented distributed hybrid energy storage control based on improved MPPT algorithms, while Gugulothu et al.15 proposed comprehensive energy management strategies for standalone DC microgrids with multiple energy sources. The integration of electronic load controllers for excess power management has been systematically reviewed23, revealing methodologies to enhance generator efficiency and extend lifespan by reducing dump load utilization from 100% to 80% of rated capacity. The coordination of dump loads13 with battery storage and renewable curtailment represents a critical design consideration for islanded operation. These works highlight the importance of coordinated control between generation, storage, and load management systems.

The control architecture proposed in this paper implements a two-level hierarchical structure: primary and secondary controls, where each control layer is operating at different time scale providing short time and long time load management. The system addresses the challenge of managing distributed solar LED lighting system with dc-dc converters having MPPT function and connected to their own battery storage maintaining the stable DC bus voltage and sharing current using virtual impedance for droop control.

This paper proposes a droop control strategy based on virtual impedance for a DC microgrid with intelligent solar lighting system with the ability to adjust its power consumption autonomously to support microgrid. The microgrid system has distributed solar LED lights with individual MPPT controllers and 48 V battery storage, a centralized 50 kW PV plant, a 60 kW bidirectional interlinking converter for AC-DC coupling and an electronic load controller with 10 kW dump load capacity.

While existing literature on droop-controlled DC microgrids1,9,17 addresses current sharing and voltage regulation, these works rely on dedicated distributed energy resources rather than exploiting the untapped potential of already-deployed solar lighting infrastructure. Communication-based approaches7 improve accuracy but introduce cost and reliability concerns. Hierarchical frameworks2,3 provide coordination but do not consider intelligent load-side participation from distributed LED systems. The present work distinguishes itself from these prior studies through the following specific novel contributions:

Aggregation of isolated solar lighting units as a grid-supporting resource: Each solar LED unit, equipped with its own PV panel and battery, is aggregated into a scalable 10 kW distributed energy resource with grid-forming capability. This approach repurposes existing infrastructure rather than requiring dedicated converter assets.

Interlinking converter control

The bidirectional interlinking converter provides the grid connectivity to the DC Microgrid and allows power exchange between the 220 V DC microgrid and 220 V AC grid. Following recent advances in unified decentralized frameworks21 and robust control strategies22, the converter implements dual-mode operation with enhanced normalization methods24 for power flow control. The control strategy incorporates generalized droop characteristics25, coupling DC voltage, AC frequency, and active power in a three-dimensional control plane to enable autonomous bidirectional power flow without communication infrastructure.

The bidirectional converter has 60 kW, 220 V DC/220 V AC, 50 Hz operating capability and operates in dual modes based on grid availability. In grid-connected mode, synchronization checks verify voltage within 10% nominal and frequency within 0.5 Hz of 50 Hz. Interlinking Converter Topology.

Interlinking converter power stage topology

The bidirectional interlinking converter consists of a two stage topology to allow power exchange between the 220 V DC microgrid and 220 V AC grid. This configuration enables bidirectional power flow via a 800 V DC link in the intermediate stage.

Stage 1: DC-DC Bidirectional Converter

The first stage has a bidirectional buck-boost converter, using a half-bridge configuration with two switching devices and a series inductor. The converter operates to manage power flow in both directions. In buck mode the power flow from 800 V DC to 220 V DC, the upper switch operates with PWM while the lower switch provides the freewheeling path. In boost mode the power flows from 220 V DC to 650 V DC, the lower switch operates with PWM while the upper switch controls the output. The inductor stores and releases energy during switching cycles. This enables bidirectional current flow and seamless power reversal between the input and output terminals.

Stage 2: Three-Phase Voltage Source Inverter

The second stage converts 800 V DC to three phase AC at 220 V phase voltage. This stage has a three-phase two-level VSI with six IGBTs (Sa+, Sa-, Sb+, Sb-, Sc+, Sc-) in three half-bridge legs. The VSI operates in inverter mode allowing power flow from 800 V DC to 220 V AC for grid export. It works in rectifier mode to allow power flow from 220 V ac to 650 V DC for grid import.

Battery management system

Distributed battery management coordinates the charging and discharging of 50 individual 48 V, 2 kWh battery units while ensuring longevity and safety.

The battery management system coordinates 50 units (48 V, 41.5 Ah, 2 kWh each) with SoC limits of 20–90% and temperature compensation. Charging modes adapt to SoC: (i) bulk charge at 0.3 C (12.5 A) for SoC < 20%, (ii) constant current at 0.2 C (8.3 A) for 20–80% SoC, (iii) constant voltage at 52 V for 80–90% SoC with current tapering, and (iv) float charge at 51 V with 0.05 C trickle above 90% SoC. Power limiting ensures charging does not exceed available system power, with safety limits at 58 V (overvoltage protection) and 1 C maximum current (41.5 A). SoC estimation employs coulomb counting with 0.95 coulombic efficiency.

Discussion

The simulation results show the effectiveness of the proposed hierarchical control strategy for DC microgrids with distributed solar LED lighting systems. The virtual impedance-based droop control results in accurate current sharing without needing communication; it is scaled to 50 distributed units. The steady-state voltage regulation of ±5% and fast transient response of less than <20 ms settling time confirm the effectiveness of the two-layer hierarchical architecture.

The adaptive LED load management strategy results in extending of battery SoC and longer runtime in distributed systems. The demonstrated 24% power reduction during low DC bus voltage conditions translates to 31.6% runtime extension for affected groups, while maintaining 80% illumination levels. This approach is better than load shedding schemes that result in complete service interruption.

The reactive power variation less than 50 VAR at 50 kW active power reduces distribution network losses and voltage drop. The bidirectional interlinking converter manages power flow in both grid-connected and islanded modes, with seamless transitions validated through load change and grid disconnection.

The proposed approach offers several advantages for Microgrid: (1) no communication required between distributed units, reducing system cost and improving reliability; (2) inherent redundancy through distributed control, with no single point of failure; (3) scalable architecture with 50 units is shown and is extensible to larger systems; (4) adaptive load management extending battery operational time without any human intervention.

Real-Time Implementation Challenges: While the simulation results demonstrate effective control performance, several practical challenges must be considered for real-time deployment. The computational burden of the proposed control is intentionally kept low: each distributed converter executes only local arithmetic operations on SoC, bus voltage, and output current, requiring no iterative solvers or inter-unit communication. This makes the algorithm suitable for low-cost microcontrollers with sampling rates of 10–100 kHz. As the number of units scales beyond 50, the secondary control PI (which aggregates average bus voltage from measurement points) introduces a modest increase in communication overhead, but the primary droop layer remains fully decentralized and unaffected by scale.

Despite these challenges, the system maintains voltage stability within ±5%, stable current sharing, and seamless transition between operating modes without requiring communication between distributed units.

The proposed control strategy inherently prioritizes power contribution from higher SoC units while protecting lower SoC units, ensuring both system stability and battery longevity.

Measurement noise and sensor inaccuracies represent a practical limitation of the proposed control. Voltage and current sensors in low-cost solar lighting systems typically exhibit noise levels of ±0.5–1% of full scale and DC offsets of up to 0.3%. In the droop control context, a 1% current measurement error at 20 A rated current (0.2 A error) produces a virtual impedance deviation of , which is negligible relative to . Communication delays are not present in the primary control layer due to its communication-free design. For secondary control, which operates on a 100 ms period, delays of up to 20 ms (typical of low-bandwidth supervisory links) have negligible effect on the slow voltage restoration dynamics. These factors confirm robustness of the proposed scheme under realistic sensing conditions, though hardware-in-the-loop validation is recommended as a next step to quantify performance under full noise and delay profiles.

Conclusion and future work

This paper presents a hierarchical control strategy for grid-connected DC microgrids with intelligent solar lighting and bidirectional AC-DC interfacing. The system integrates distributed LED converters, solar PV plant, bidirectional interlinking converter, and electronic load controller for islanding operation.

This study shows a virtual impedance-based droop control strategy that achieves current sharing among two groups based on the SoC condition of their respective batteries, without relying on communication infrastructure. The system also features a bidirectional interlinking converter control capable of seamless transitions between grid-connected and islanded modes in under 100 ms. Furthermore, a hierarchical power management scheme was implemented to effectively coordinate distributed generation, battery storage, dump loads, and intelligent LED dimming.

Future research will focus on implementing deep reinforcement learning to autonomously tuned virtual impedance and droop gains in real-time. Subsequent work will also extend to the development of tertiary control layers for predicting solar PV irradiation data and the integration of advanced fault detection and isolation algorithms. Finally, these proposed strategies will undergo hardware validation using real-time platforms and full-scale prototypes. The proposed architecture demonstrates potential for rural electrification and islanded communities requiring reliable distributed solar integration.

Exploring new traveling wave solutions by solving the nonlinear space–time fractal Fornberg−Whitham equation
Authors: A. Nazari-Golshan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Complex and nonlinear fractal equations are ubiquitous in natural phenomena. This research employs the fractal Euler−Lagrange and semi-inverse methods to derive the nonlinear space–time fractal Fornberg–Whitham equation. This derivation provides an in-depth comprehension of traveling wave propagation. Consequently, the nonlinear space–time fractal Fornberg–Whitham equation is pivotal in elucidating fundamental phenomena across applied sciences. A novel analytical technique, the generalized Kudryashov method, is presented to address the space–time fractal Fornberg–Whitham equation. This method combines the fractional complex approach with the modified Kudryashov method to enhance its effectiveness. We derive an analytical solution for the space–time fractal Fornberg–Whitham equation to elucidate how various parameters influence the propagation of new traveling wave solutions. Introduction

Partial differential equations (PDEs) play a crucial role in elucidating phenomena across a spectrum of applied sciences, spanning plasma physics, computer science1,2, biomedical research3–7, intelligent predictive networks8–11, biology, and more12–15. Over recent decades, there has been a notable shift in focus among scientists and researchers toward recognizing the superior descriptive power of fractional differential simulations compared to traditional approaches. The ideas related to fractal differentiation have historical connections with prominent figures like Liouville and Riemann16,17. Fractional differential equations have found wide-ranging applications in fields such as mathematics, engineering, science, and conceptual modeling. These applications arise from specific practical needs and prompt extensive scholarly interest in their exploration18–23. We believe that much forthcoming advancement will stem from applying fractional differential equations to diverse fields. Currently, the primary focus in fractal calculus revolves around identifying suitable applications for describing fractional operators. Researchers have employed analytical and numerical techniques to achieve this objective and generate exact and approximate results. Recent efforts in this direction have been made by various authors, including utilizing methods such as the DTM and the generalized Taylor’s formula for addressing a class of nonlinear PDEs of fractional order24,25. The SIM and the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$G^{\prime } /G$$\end{document}G′/G–expansion technique have also been introduced to tackle the fractal Gardner equation26. Various scientists have explored different solution types for fractional differential equations through diverse methodologies27–29. Several analytical techniques have been employed to solve nonlinear fractional PDEs. These methods encompass the tanh-coth method30,31, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$G^{\prime } /G$$\end{document}G′/G–expansion method26,32,33, homotopy perturbation method34,35, two-scale approach36–38, the variational method39–41, and Adomian decomposition method42–44.

The nonlinear space–time fractal Fornberg–Whitham (STFFW) equation is a type of nonlinear fractal PDE (NLFPDE) used to describe the propagation of nonlinear structures in various media, incorporating fractal derivatives. Consequently, researchers have shown keen interest in this equation and have applied various analytical and numerical methods to solve both nonlinear and fractional nonlinear versions of the Fornberg–Whitham (FW) equation. For instance, Abidi and Omrani45 explored the analytical solution of the Fornberg–Whitham (FW) equation using the HAM and the ADM. Gupta and Singh46 employed the HPM to approximate solutions of the nonlinear time fractional FW (TFFW) equation. Sakar and Edogan47 used HAM to derive an analytical solution for the nonlinear TFFW equation and compared their results with those obtained using ADM. Sartanpara et al.48 applied the q-homotopy analysis technique to the TFFW equation in the context of Caputo’s sense. They also conducted a convergence analysis of the method and assessed its term approximations to demonstrate its accuracy.

The novelty of this paper is to study the nonlinear space–time fractal Fornberg−Whitham (STFFW) equation using the generalized Kudryashov method (GKM) for the first time. The manuscript begins by utilizing the fractal Euler−Lagrange technique (FELT) and the semi-inverse method (SIM)49–52 to derive the nonlinear STFFW equation. Subsequently, a modified Kudryashov method53–56 is generalized to obtain precise solutions for the nonlinear STFFW equation. This extension, the generalized Kudryashov method (GKM), combines a fractional complex approach with the modified Kudryashov method53–56. This approach transforms the STFFW equation into a set of ODEs, which are subsequently solved using the GKM. This innovative method offers a new approach to deriving traveling wave solutions of the nonlinear STFFW equation. The manuscript is constructed as follows: Section “Methodology” shows the methodology. Section “Derivation of nonlinear space–time fractal Fornberg–Whitham (STFFW) equation” is mainly concerned with the derivation of the STFFW equation. Considering the GKM is devoted to solving the STFFW equation in section “Solution of nonlinear STFFW equation”. In section “Results and discussions”, the new traveling wave solution has been investigated. Section “Conclusions” shows the conclusions of our investigation.

Acquiring analytical solutions is crucial to gaining a deeper understanding of these characteristic phenomena. Conclusions

The primary objective of this study is to derive and examine new traveling wave solutions for the nonlinear space–time fractal Fornberg–Whitham (STFFW) equation. Initially, the STFFW equation is derived using the fractal Euler−Lagrange and semi-inverse methods, techniques applicable in various scientific disciplines, conceptual modeling, and wave propagation. Subsequently, we combine the fractional complex approach and the modified Kudryashov method, known as the GKM, to derive new traveling wave solutions for the STFFW equation. We also examine the influence of various parameters, including \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha$$\end{document}α,\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\upbeta ,$$\end{document}β, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$b_{1}$$\end{document}b1, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$k$$\end{document}k, on these new traveling wave solutions. Our findings showed that changing the time fractional parameter \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\left( \beta \right)$$\end{document}β from 1 to 0.8 significantly changes the soliton height in case 2, while the changes in case 1 are barely noticeable. On the other hand, including time fractional orders does not alter the solitary wave solution of the STFFW question. Consequently, the space fractional parameter can be used to control the height of the new traveling wave, providing a practical method to adjust wave dynamics without the need for other parameters. Ultimately, this research encourages, motivates, and inspires future research in numerical and analytical results using other powerful methods, such as the explicit Runge–Kutta method, Adams method, and some stochastic techniques3,4,8–10.

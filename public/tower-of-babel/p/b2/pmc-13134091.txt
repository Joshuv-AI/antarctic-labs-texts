Epidemiological investigation of PCV2 and PCV3 in three provinces of Northeast China in 2025
Authors: Yuan Shi, Zhou Han, Qingwei Shi, Chun Lu, Mengyue Zhang, Zhishuan Ren, Jintao Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPorcine circovirus type 2 (PCV2) and porcine circovirus type 3 (PCV3) have emerged as significant pathogens jeopardizing the swine industry in China in recent years, and caused substantial economic losses to pig farms.ObjectiveThis study aimed to conduct an epidemiological investigation and genetic evolutionary analysis of PCV2 and PCV3 in three provinces of Northeast China in 2025.MethodsA total of 322 clinical samples suspected of PCV infection were collected from 25 cities across the three provinces of Northeast China. All samples were tested for PCV2 and PCV3 by a duplex nested PCR. The genomes of selected PCV2 and PCV3 strains were sequenced for the subsequent homology and phylogenetic analysis.ResultsAmong the 322 samples, the number and prevalence of PCV2-positive samples were 68 (21.12%), and the number and prevalence of PCV3-positive samples were 122 (37.89%). The highest positive rate for PCV2 was in Jilin Province, and the highest positive rate for PCV3 was in Liaoning Province. The positive rate of PCV2 and PCV3 were both higher in winter than that in other seasons. Homology and phylogenetic analysis performed on 17 sequenced PCV2 genomes revealed the nucleotide identity ranging from 96.20% to 99.90%, and all strains were classified as the genotype PCV2d. The 17 sequenced PCV3 genomes showed the nucleotide identity between 98.60% and 99.90%. Among these PCV3 strains, 5 strains belonged to the PCV3a, and 12 strains belonged to the PCV3c. The results of the amino acid sequence analysis of Cap showed that the 17 PCV2 strains exhibited multiple amino acid mutations within the antibody recognition domain. Among these, 16 strains harbored variations in the immunodominant decoy epitope. Additionally, amino acid mutations were detected in the nuclear localization signal of 5 strains. The results of PCV3 amino acid analysis showed that the residues at the position 24 and 27 were consistent with the phylogenetic analysis. Beyond these, only four amino acid mutations were observed and all of them were not located within the reported antibody recognition domain of the PCV3 Cap.ConclusionThe currently predominant strains circulating in three provinces of Northeast China in 2025 were PCV2d and PCV3c. Seasonal analysis indicated a higher prevalence in winter for both PCV2 and PCV3. PCV2 Cap displayed amino acid variations at multiple key sites, while the amino acid of PCV3 Cap is relatively conserved. This study provided the crucial data for understanding the epidemiology of PCV2 and PCV3 and for the development of PCVAD control strategies in these regions.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12917-026-05422-x.Keywords: Porcine circovirus type 2, Porcine circovirus type 3, Epidemiology investigation, Homology analysis, Phylogenetic analysis

Introduction

Porcine circovirus (PCV) belongs to the genus Circovirus within the family Circoviridae. It is a circular, non-enveloped, single-stranded DNA virus [1]. Currently, the porcine circovirus genus comprises four species: PCV1, PCV2, PCV3 and PCV4. Tischer et al. were the first to report PCV1 in 1974, considering it a cell culture contaminant with no pathogenicity [2]. In 1998, Meehan et al. isolated a novel circovirus distinct from PCV1, designated PCV2, which shared 80% nucleotide sequence identity with the PCV1 and exhibited certain pathogenic potential [3]. In 2016, Phan et al. identified another circovirus, different from both PCV1 and PCV2, in three pigs exhibiting cardiac and multisystemic inflammation, and named it PCV3 [4]. In 2019, Zhang et al. discovered the fourth circovirus in Hunan Province, China, and designated it PCV4 [5]. Currently, the predominant epidemic strains in China are PCV2 and PCV3. PCV1 is non-pathogenic [2], while PCV4 currently has a low prevalence rate in China, and related research on it remains limited [5].

The genome length of PCV2 is mostly between 1766 and 1768 nt [6, 7]. In 2018, Franzo et al. classified PCV2 into eight genotypes: PCV2a to PCV2h [8], and Wang et al. in 2020 reported a new genotype, PCV2i [9]. The PCV2 genome contains 11 open reading frames (ORFs). Among these, ORF1, ORF5, ORF7, and ORF10 are located on the positive-sense strand, while the remaining seven are on the negative-sense strand [10]. The PCV3 genome is approximately 2000 nt in size [11]. According to the domestic and international research, the genotyping method proposed by Fu et al. has gained general recognition. This method classifies PCV3 into PCV3a, PCV3b and PCV3c [12]. The PCV3 genome contains three major open reading frames (ORF1–ORF3). ORF1 and ORF3 are located on the positive-sense strand, whereas ORF2 is situated on the negative-sense strand [4]. Currently, the most studied open reading frames of PCVs are ORF1 and ORF2. ORF1 encodes replication-associated proteins (Rep and Rep’) [13].The ORF2-encoded capsid protein (Cap) is the only structural protein of PCVs, which mediates viral attachment to host cell receptors and is closely associated with viral infection [14].

PCV2 primarily causes reproductive disorders, postweaning multisystemic wasting syndrome (PMWS), porcine respiratory disease complex (PRDC), and porcine dermatitis and nephropathy syndrome (PDNS), as well as congenital tremors in piglets [15]. PCV3 is mainly associated with PDNS, reproductive failure, and inflammatory responses in the heart and multiple systems [16]. The clinical symptoms of PCV2 and PCV3 infections are similar, including dyspnea, fever, lethargy, reduced appetite and wasting. The most common manifestation is multisystemic inflammation, with histopathological lesions often characterized by myocarditis and systemic peri-vasculitis. Postmortem examination findings typically reveal significant pulmonary lesions [17]. Moreover, co-infection with other viruses can exacerbate the clinical severity, increase mortality rates, and cause substantial economic losses to pig farms [18].

Since its discovery in 1998, PCV2 has spread widely across various countries. Currently, three genotypes (PCV2a, PCV2b and PCV2d) are prevalent globally. According to a study by Li et al. (2021), PCV2b was the dominant strain from 2002 to 2008, but from 2009 to 2019, PCV2d became the predominant genotype, suggesting a potential ongoing shift toward PCV2d [19, 29]. PCV3 has been detected worldwide since its identification. Subsequently, the virus has also been found in wild boars, ticks, mosquitoes, and dogs, and this indicates that these animals may serve as reservoirs and highlight the complexity and challenges in controlling its transmission [20–22]. Based on a study by Ku et al., during 2016–2020, PCV3c showed the highest detection rate in 15 cities in China, while PCV3a and PCV3b also had relatively high prevalence [23]. Given the dynamic and variable prevalence patterns of PCV2 and PCV3, this study aimed to investigate the epidemiological status of PCV2 and PCV3 and perform genetic evolutionary analysis in three provinces of Northeast China. The findings are expected to provide data support for the prevention and control of PCV2 and PCV3 in these region.

Materials and methods

Sample collection

A total of 322 tissue and blood samples suspected of PCV2 and PCV3 infection were collected from 25 cities across three provinces—Heilongjiang, Jilin and Liaoning in 2025 (Table 1). All samples were stored at −80 °C for subsequent detection. The sources and types of the samples were described in detail in Supplementary Table S1. All samples used in this study were kindly provided by the pig farms. The animals were euthanized via intravenous injection of pentobarbital sodium into the ear vein at a dose of 150 mg per kilogram of body weight. Euthanasia for research sampling was performed in a soundproof room to prevent distress to the remaining pigs. After sample collection, the animals were transferred to a harmless treatment facility for proper disposal.

Sample preparation and nested PCR detection

Approximately 50 mg of tissue samples were ground to a fine powder using liquid nitrogen and homogenized in 150 µL of phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) in a sterile 1.5 mL microcentrifuge tube. Total DNA was extracted from the homogenates or blood samples using a commercial DNA extraction kit (TIANGEN, Beijing, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. A duplex nested PCR assay developed in our laboratory was applied for the detection of PCV2 and PCV3. The primer sequences were listed in supplementary Table S2. The duplex nested PCR was verified to possess good specificity and reproducibility, and the detection limit of PCV2 and PCV3 was 9.36 × 101 copies/μL and 6.85 × 10⁰ copies/μL respectively. The 20 µL reaction mixture consisted of 10 µL of Premix Taq™ Master Mix (Takara Bio, Dalian, China), 1 µL (10 µM) of each forward and reverse primer for both viruses, 4 µL of DNA template, and 2 µL of RNase-free ddH₂O. The first-round amplification was performed under the following conditions: initial denaturation at 95 °C for 5 min; 40 cycles of denaturation at 94 °C for 1 min, annealing at 62 °C for 30 s, and extension at 72 °C for 80 s; followed by a final extension at 72 °C for 10 min. For the second-round PCR, 1 µL of the first amplification product was used as template under the same reaction conditions except that the extension time was adjusted to 42 s per cycle. The PCR products were analyzed by electrophoresis on a 1.5% agarose gel and visualized using a fully automated gel imaging system (Bio-Rad, USA).

Full genome amplification of PCV2 and PCV3

Full-length primers were used to amplify PCV2 and PCV3 from positive samples. Amplified products were analyzed using the gel imaging system, and sequenced by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai) Co., Ltd. The obtained sequences were spliced for further analysis.

Genetic homology and phylogenetic analysis of PCV2 and PCV3

Reference sequences of PCV2 and PCV3 were downloaded from NCBI (Table S3). Homology and phylogenetic analysis of the genome and ORF2 gene of PCV2 and PCV3 were conducted using MegAlign and MEGA11 software, respectively.

Amino acid sequence analysis of PCV2 and PCV3

Sequencing results and reference strain sequences were inputted into MEGA11. Nucleotide sequences were translated into amino acid sequences, which was followed by the multiple sequence alignment. Finally, the amino acid sequences of PCV2 and PCV3 Cap were analyzed using Jalview software.

Results

Detection results and distribution of positive samples

322 samples were tested by a duplex nested PCR for PCV2 and PCV3 (Fig. S1). Among these samples, the number of positive samples and prevalence of PCV2 were 68 and 21.12% respectively, and the number of positive samples and prevalence of PCV3 were 122 and 37.89% respectively (Table S4). The highest positive rate for PCV2 was in Jilin Province, and the highest positive rate for PCV3 was in Liaoning Province (Fig. 1A). The positive rate of PCV2 and PCV3 were both higher in winter than that in other seasons (Fig. 1B).

Genome amplification and homology analysis of positive samples

The genome of PCV2 and PCV3 from the positive samples were amplified by PCR in two segments and three segments respectively (Fig. S2). These amplified fragments were sequenced and spliced to obtain the genome sequence, and different sequences from different farms and regions were selected for subsequent analysis. Finally 17 PCV2 genome sequences and 17 PCV3 genome sequences were obtained, and their sequences were deposited in GenBank under accession numbers PX661853-PX661886. Homology analysis of the PCV2 genome revealed that the nucleotide identity among the 17 PCV2 genomes ranged from 96.2% to 99.9% (Fig. 2A). Compared with the reference strains, the 17 sequenced strains exhibited the highest homology with the PCV2d reference strain ranging from 97.7% to 99.6% (Fig. 2B). In contrast, the nucleotide identity among the 17 PCV3 genomes ranged from 98.6% to 99.9% (Fig. 3A). Alignment of the obtained PCV3 genome sequences with reference strains indicated that the strains from Heilongjiang and Liaoning Provinces shared the highest homology with the PCV3c reference strains ranging from 99.0% to 99.7%, while the strains from Jilin Province showed the closest homology with the PCV3a reference strain ranging from 99.4% to 99.7% (Fig. 3B).

Phylogenetic analysis of PCV2 and PCV3 genomes

The phylogenetic analysis of the genome and ORF2 gene of the obtained PCV2 and PCV3 strains was conducted using MEGA11 software. The results demonstrated that all 17 PCV2 genome sequences were classified as genotype PCV2d. Phylogenetic analysis based on ORF2 yielded a consistent classification (Fig. 4). According to two amino acid mutations (A24V and R27K) in the Cap, PCV3 can be divided into PCV3a, PCV3b and PCV3c. Among the 17 PCV3 sequences obtained in this study (Fig. 5), 5 strains from Jilin Province were grouped into the PCV3a, while the 7 strains from Heilongjiang Province and 5 strains from Liaoning Province belonged to the PCV3c. The phylogenetic tree results for PCV2 and PCV3 were consistent with the homology analysis findings.

Amino acid sequence analysis of PCV2 and PCV3 Cap

As shown in Fig. 6A, the 17 PCV2 strains from the three provinces exhibited multiple amino acid mutations within the antibody recognition domain. Among these, 16 strains harbored variations in the immunodominant decoy epitope [24], with complex amino acid substitutions observed (169R → 169G/S/W). Additionally, amino acid mutations were detected in the nuclear localization signal of 5 strains [25]. These results indicated that amino acid variations existed at several key sites of PCV2 Cap. In contrast to PCV2, the amino acid sequence of PCV3 Cap was relatively conserved (Fig. 6B). The residues at the position 24 and 27 were consistent with the phylogenetic analyses (PCV3a: 24A and 27R; PCV3c: 24 V and 27 K). Beyond these, only four amino acid mutations were observed (77S → 77N, 152A → 152 T, 156S → 156 T/N, 168R → 168I), none of which were located within the reported antibody recognition domain of the PCV3 Cap [26].

Discussion

PCV is the primary pathogen responsible for porcine circovirus associated diseases and is currently widespread in China, with PCV2 and PCV3 being the most prevalent. A study by Cui et al. showed that between 2020 and 2021, the positive rates for PCV2 and PCV3 in six swine herds in China were 72.1% and 26.3%, respectively. The predominant genotype were PCV2d and PCV3a, although all three PCV3 genotypes were circulating [27]. Another study reported that the positive rates of PCV2 and PCV3 in Xinjiang between 2022 and 2024 were 71% and 25% respectively, and this indicated that PCV2 still maintained a higher prevalence compared to PCV3 [26]. Chen et al. also reported that between 2023 and 2024, the positive rates for PCV2 and PCV3 across 23 provinces in China were 75.14% and 17.60% respectively. PCV2d remained the dominant genotype, while all three PCV3 genotypes were in circulation [28]. This study investigated the prevalence of PCV2 and PCV3 in three provinces of Northeast China. The results showed that the positive rates of PCV2 and PCV3 were 21.12% and 37.89% respectively. The prevalence of PCV3 was higher than that of PCV2. We speculated that this might be attributed to the effectiveness of widespread PCV2 vaccination programs, whereas no commercial vaccine was currently available for PCV3. Combined with the complex transmission routes, PCV3 became prevalent in recent years, and this highlighted the urgent need for the development of the commercial PCV3 vaccine. Genetic evolution analysis revealed that all sequenced PCV2 strains from three provinces were PCV2d, and this indicated that PCV2d strains remained the predominant genotype circulating in Northeast China. Regarding PCV3, PCV3a was found exclusively in Jilin Province, while PCV3c was prevalent in both Heilongjiang and Liaoning Provinces, and this indicated that PCV3c has become the predominant genotype in Northeast China. In summary, these results enriched the data on the epidemiology for PCV2 and PCV3 in Northeast China.

The Cap is the sole structural protein of circoviruses and is closely associated with the recognition of viral receptors on host cells and viral infection. Consequently, mutations in Cap can alter viral replication and the host's ability to recognize the virus [29]. For PCV2, its antibody recognition domains are located within amino acid residues 51–81, 113–139, 161–208, and 227–233, while an immunodominant decoy epitope exists within residues 168–180 [24]. A total of six amino acid mutations were identified in these regions in this study, one of which was located within the immunodominant decoy epitope. This mutation may affect the host's ability to recognize the virus and could potentially lead to changes in vaccine-induced protection [30]. Previous study has shown that the N-terminal nuclear localization signal (NLS) region of the PCV2 Cap functions as a cell-penetrating peptide and significantly impacts viral infection. The core epitopes of this NLS, NLS-A (1MTYPRRRFRRRRRRRRRS17) and NLS-B (21QILRRRPWLVHP RHRYRWRRK41) are key research targets [25]. Among the strains obtained in this study, five strains were found to harbor one or two amino acid mutations within the NLS regions. These mutations (particularly those involved in arginine residues) might affect the nuclear localization function of the protein, and thereby altered the virus's ability to enter host cells. In contrast to PCV2, the amino acid sequence of PCV3 Cap was relatively conserved. The 24th and 27th amino acid residues existed specific variations, and these specific variations might alter the immunogenicity of PCV3 and potentially facilitate immune evasion [31]. Beyond these, only four additional amino acid mutations were observed, and none of these mutations were located within the reported antibody recognition domains of the PCV3 Cap [26]. Whether these mutations affect viral virulence or immune escape need further investigation.

In summary, PCV2d and PCV3c have become the predominant strains circulating in three provinces of Northeast China. The amino acid variations were identified within the antibody recognition domains of both PCV2 and PCV3 Cap, and this suggested these mutations may potentially influence antigenicity; however, functional studies are required to confirm this compared to other strains. At present, the primary vaccines against PCV2 infection are based on PCV2a and PCV2b strains [32], while no commercial vaccine exists for PCV3 [17]. Our findings emphasized the urgency of developing more specific PCV2d vaccines and a commercial PCV3 vaccine. At the same time, this study provided the valuable data to understand the epidemiology and prevention and control strategies of PCV2 and PCV3 in Northeast China.

Conclusion

This study revealed that PCV2d and PCV3c were the predominant genotypes in three provinces of Northeast China in 2025. Cap amino acid analysis indicated that PCV2 strains exhibited more complex amino acid mutations, while PCV3 strains showed a relatively simpler mutation pattern. This study provide the basic data for the epidemiology and vaccine development of PCV2 and PCV3.

Access to fertility care for LGBTIQ+ and unpartnered persons: a systematic review protocol
Authors: Maria P Velez, Houda Feguery, Bailey Milne, Alessia Nicole Paparella, Lianne Gonsalves, James Kiarie, Gitau Mburu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionInfertility is a major public health concern, and many individuals face substantial barriers to accessing fertility care. Across jurisdictions, regulations, eligibility criteria and funding frameworks for fertility care vary considerably, particularly in relation to lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender, intersex and queer/questioning people (LGBTIQ+), as well as unpartnered individuals. Moreover, existing definitions and prevalence estimates of infertility are largely derived from studies of heterosexual couples which may underestimate the need for fertility care among other populations. This protocol describes a systematic review designed to synthesise current evidence on the need for and access to fertility care among LGBTIQ+ populations and unpartnered individuals as well as the policies and regulations that govern such care.Methods and analysisThis protocol follows the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Protocols (PRISMA-P) guidelines. Systematic searches were conducted in MEDLINE (Ovid), Embase, Web of Science, Scopus, Cumulative Index to Nursing and Allied Health Literature (CINAHL) and Global Health, as well as grey literature sources including ProQuest Dissertations & Theses, ECRI Guidelines Trust, Canadian Medical Association Journal (CMAJ) Guidelines, National Institute for Health and Care Excellence, fertility societies guidelines and Google Scholar. Eligible studies will include quantitative, qualitative, mixed-methods, narrative synthesis and typology mapping designs. Two reviewers will independently perform title/abstract and full-text screening, data extraction and quality assessment. The Critical Appraisal Skills Programme checklist will be used for qualitative studies and the Joanna Briggs Institute tools for quantitative studies. Completeness and credibility assessments will be applied to narrative syntheses and typology mappings. The primary outcomes will include measures of fertility care need, indicators of access and policy frameworks influencing service availability.Ethics and disseminationThis study does not involve human participants. The protocol received an exemption from Research Ethics Board review by the McGill University Health Centre Research Ethics Board. Findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications, conference presentations and professional networks and shared with the WHO to inform evidence-based policy.PROSPERO registration numberCRD420251126056

Introduction

Fertility care is an important component of sexual and reproductive healthcare, and all individuals have the right to access it. Recent WHO guidance has further emphasised infertility and fertility care as integral components of equitable reproductive health services and universal health coverage.1 Infertility is commonly defined as the failure to achieve a pregnancy after 12 months or more of regular unprotected sexual intercourse—a benchmark that has guided global surveillance and policy development for decades.2 Recent conceptual frameworks have broadened this understanding to also encompass any impairment of an individual’s capacity to reproduce, either alone or with a partner.3 4 This more inclusive approach acknowledges that biological or situational fertility challenges may affect lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender, intersex and queer/questioning (LGBTIQ+) people, as well as individuals of diverse partnership statuses and that medical interventions may be required to achieve parenthood even in the absence of a traditional infertility diagnosis.35 Collectively, these definitions reflect an evolving consensus that infertility is a condition with biological, social and contextual dimensions and that access to fertility care is an integral component of sexual and reproductive health and rights.6

Despite these conceptual advances, equitable access to fertility care remains limited in many settings. Disparities persist due to variations in legal frameworks, eligibility criteria and funding policies across jurisdictions, which may restrict access based on partnership status, sexual orientation, gender identity, gender expression or sex characteristics of intended parents.579 Beyond such structural barriers, LGBTIQ+ populations and unpartnered individuals frequently encounter stigma, discriminatory practices and a lack of culturally competent care.1012 In many jurisdictions, access to fertility care remains closely tied to traditional definitions of infertility and assumptions of cisgender heterosexual partnered reproduction. As a result, eligibility for publicly funded fertility services, donor gametes, gestational carriage or fertility preservation may vary substantially according to sexual orientation, gender identity, partnership status and local legal frameworks. These disparities may contribute to inequities in access to parenthood pathways and reproductive autonomy across populations and settings.

Evidence regarding fertility care needs, access to services and governing policy frameworks for these populations remains limited, fragmented and inconsistently synthesised. Limited reviews have examined selected aspects of fertility care among specific populations, such as cultural competence in fertility care in LGBTIQ+ populations or fertility preservation for transgender individuals, specifically.10 11 However, no comprehensive systematic review has synthesised evidence on fertility care needs, access and related policy frameworks across LGBTIQ+ and unpartnered populations globally. This broader approach is expected to provide a more comprehensive understanding of fertility care access and policy inequities across jurisdictions. Given the fragmented and multidisciplinary nature of the literature, a systematic review methodology was chosen to comprehensively identify, appraise and synthesise evidence across quantitative, qualitative and policy-focused studies. Accordingly, this review will synthesise existing evidence on the need for and access to fertility care for LGBTIQ+ populations and unpartnered individuals and describe the typology of policies and regulations governing such access globally.

Methods

Protocol and registration

This systematic review protocol follows the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Protocols (PRISMA-P) checklist.13 The completed systematic review will be reported in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) statement.14 The protocol has been prospectively registered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO; registration ID: CRD420251126056).15 At the time of protocol submission, the literature searches had been completed, while screening, data extraction, quality appraisal and synthesis remained ongoing.

Search strategy and data sources

The following databases were searched for relevant studies on 18 November 2025: The Cochrane CENTRAL Register of Controlled Trials and Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews (via Wiley), CINAHL (via Ebsco), Embase Classic+Embase (via Ovid 1947 to 17 November 2025), Global Health (via Ovid 1910 to 2025 week 46), GlobalIndex Medicus (WHO), MEDLINE (via Ovid 1946 to 17 November 2025), PubMed (from 4 November 2025 to present) and Web of Science (via Clarivate Analytics).

The MEDLINE strategy was applied to all databases, with modifications to search terms as necessary. No language restrictions were applied during the database search stage to maximise sensitivity. However, only English-language studies were included during screening and eligibility assessment. Studies published from 2000 onwards were eligible to align with contemporary policies and practices. In addition, a grey literature search was conducted on 19 November 2025, using ProQuest Dissertations & Theses, ECRI Guidelines Trust, CMAJ Guidelines, National Institute for Health and Care Excellence, American Society for Reproductive Medicine Guidelines, European Society of Human Reproduction and Embryology Guidelines and Google Scholar. All detailed search strategies can be found in online supplemental appendix 1

The MEDLINE search strategy was peer-reviewed by two independent librarians and adapted for each database, incorporating both controlled vocabulary (eg, Medical Subject Headings and Emtree) and free-text terms. The strategy combined concepts related to infertility, fertility care, assisted reproduction, LGBTIQ+, unpartnered or single individuals and health policy. Duplicates were removed using EndNote’s duplicate-checking function followed by manual verification and confirmation within Covidence.

Eligibility criteria

The inclusion criteria will be defined using the Population–Concept–Context framework.16

Population

This review will include studies featuring LGBTIQ+ and/or unpartnered people seeking fertility care. Eligible participants will include same-sex couples, trans and gender-diverse individuals, intersex persons and/or single people pursuing parenthood. Although these populations are heterogeneous, they were included because they may encounter similar structural and policy-related barriers to fertility care and parenthood pathways outside traditional cisgender heterosexual partnered frameworks.

Studies focusing exclusively on cisgender heterosexual populations or the general population without relevant, disaggregated data on the populations of interest will be excluded. Studies that include extractable data on any individual population within the LGBTIQ+ and unpartnered umbrella will remain eligible (eg, an article exploring care for transgender people only).

Concept

The review will examine fertility services as part of sexual and reproductive healthcare, including assisted reproductive technologies, gamete or embryo donation or preservation and gestational carriage or ‘surrogacy’. Eligible studies will report outcomes related to fertility needs or access, or describe policies, regulations or funding mechanisms that influence access to fertility care for included populations. Both quantitative and qualitative evidence will be included, encompassing descriptive, thematic or mixed-methods analyses of fertility interest, service utilisation or policy environments. Case reports, editorials, opinion pieces and conference abstracts without sufficient data will be excluded.

Context

Studies conducted in any geographic or health-system setting (public or private) will be eligible. Policy analyses and reports from governmental and non-governmental organisations will also be included. To align with contemporary policies and clinical practices, the review will include studies published from January 2000 onward. Only studies published in English will be included, in accordance with the search parameters.

Study design

Eligible study designs will include quantitative (non-randomised) studies, qualitative studies, mixed-methods studies and policy analyses. Systematic and scoping reviews will be reviewed for reference-mining but excluded from synthesis to prevent duplication. Outcomes of interest will include quantitative measures of fertility care needs and utilisation and typology mapping of national and regional policy frameworks.

Outcome of interest

The review will focus on three primary outcomes:

Reported indicators of fertility care need among LGBTIQ+ and unpartnered individuals, including measures such as fertility intentions, interest in future pregnancy or reported demand for fertility services.

Reported indicators of fertility care need among LGBTIQ+ and unpartnered individuals, including measures such as fertility intentions, interest in future pregnancy or reported demand for fertility services.

Access to fertility care for LGBTIQ+ and unpartnered individuals assessed through indicators such as the proportion of individuals or couples using fertility services (eg, donor insemination, in vitro fertilisation, gamete donation, gamete preservation and gestational carriage) and contextualised by jurisdiction, clinic or region.

Access to fertility care for LGBTIQ+ and unpartnered individuals assessed through indicators such as the proportion of individuals or couples using fertility services (eg, donor insemination, in vitro fertilisation, gamete donation, gamete preservation and gestational carriage) and contextualised by jurisdiction, clinic or region.

Typology of policies and regulations governing fertility care for LGBTIQ+ and unpartnered individuals characterised through a narrative synthesis of national and regional policy frameworks. Typologies will be categorised as permissive, restrictive, or absent based on available evidence. Permissive policies will be defined as those that explicitly allow, recognise, protect or facilitate access to fertility care for LGBTIQ+ individuals and unpartnered persons. Restrictive policies will be defined as those that impose explicit or implicit barriers that limit, exclude or disadvantage LGBTIQ+ individuals and unpartnered persons in accessing fertility care. Policies categorised as absent will refer to frameworks that regulate fertility care without explicitly addressing or differentiating access for LGBTIQ+ individuals and unpartnered persons.

Study selection and screening process

All records retrieved from database and grey literature searches will be imported into EndNote (Clarivate Analytics) for initial deduplication, followed by manual verification to ensure accuracy. The deduplicated references will then be uploaded into Covidence (Veritas Health Innovation, Melbourne, Australia) to facilitate the screening and review process.

Screening will be conducted in two stages:

Title and abstract screening.

Title and abstract screening.

Full-text screening of potentially eligible studies.

Full-text screening of potentially eligible studies.

At each stage, two reviewers will independently screen all citations and full-text articles against the predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria. Discrepancies will be resolved by discussion and consensus; if disagreements persist, a third reviewer will adjudicate. Reasons for study exclusion at the full-text stage will be recorded in Covidence, and the complete screening process will be documented in a PRISMA 2020 flow diagram.

In addition to the database searches, the reviewers will perform manual reference-list screening (‘snowballing’) of included articles and relevant reviews and citation tracking of key publications to identify additional eligible studies. Where necessary, authors or experts may be contacted to clarify data or identify ongoing or unpublished studies.

Data extraction will be performed independently by two reviewers, with disagreements resolved by consensus or third-reviewer adjudication when necessary. Where essential data are missing or unclear, study authors will be contacted for clarification.

All extracted data will be verified for completeness and accuracy before synthesis. Extracted datasets and forms will be retained to ensure transparency and reproducibility.

Risk of bias and quality assessment

The methodological quality and risk of bias of all included studies will be assessed independently by two reviewers using standardised critical appraisal tools appropriate to study design.

For quantitative studies, the Joanna Briggs Institute Critical Appraisal Tool will be applied to evaluate methodological rigour, data quality and transparency in reporting. For qualitative studies, the Critical Appraisal Skills Programme checklist will be used to assess study credibility, relevance and transferability.

For narrative syntheses and typology mapping, a completeness and credibility assessment framework will be applied to evaluate the comprehensiveness of reporting and the plausibility of interpretations. Formal methodological quality appraisal tools designed for empirical studies will not be applied to policy and framework documents, which will instead contribute to the narrative synthesis and typology mapping components of the review. This approach will help ensure consistency and methodological integrity across diverse study types.

Quality assessments will be conducted independently by two reviewers, with disagreements resolved through consensus or third-reviewer adjudication. Where relevant, the results of the quality appraisal will be summarised narratively and presented in tabular format to support interpretation of findings.

Data synthesis

This review was designed as a systematic review rather than a scoping review because its objective is not only to map the existing literature but also to critically appraise and synthesise evidence regarding fertility care needs, access and governing policy frameworks among LGBTIQ+ and unpartnered populations. A comprehensive synthesis will be undertaken to summarise the findings from the included studies. Given the expected heterogeneity in study design, outcomes and reporting, both quantitative and qualitative data will be synthesised using appropriate methods.

For quantitative data, descriptive statistics will be summarised to report published indicators of fertility need (eg, proportion of individuals expressing fertility intentions) and access (eg, proportion using donor insemination, in vitro fertilisation, gamete donation, gamete preservation or gestational carriage). Where sufficiently homogeneous data are available, meta-analyses will be conducted to derive pooled estimates using random-effects models. Statistical heterogeneity will be assessed using the I² statistic and explored through subgroup analyses based on sexual orientation, gender identity, partnership status or region.

For qualitative and mixed-methods studies, findings will be synthesised narratively, focusing on themes directly relevant to fertility need, access and policy environments. No interpretive synthesis of barriers or facilitators will be performed in keeping with the review’s objective scope.

For policy and regulatory data, a typology mapping approach will be used to categorise national or regional frameworks governing fertility care as permissive, restrictive, or absent. Policies will be summarised in tabular and graphical formats to illustrate global variation and to identify jurisdictions with explicit inclusion or exclusion of LGBTIQ+ and unpartnered populations

All analyses will be performed using RevMan V.5.4 (The Cochrane Collaboration) for quantitative synthesis and Microsoft Excel (Microsoft Corp., Redmond, WA, USA) for the organisation and typology mapping of policy data. Reference management and screening will be conducted using EndNote (Clarivate Analytics) and Covidence (Veritas Health Innovation, Melbourne, Australia).

Results will be presented using a combination of tables, summary figures and narrative text to ensure clarity and comparability across data sources.

Patient and public involvement

Patients or members of the public were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination plans of this research.

Ethics and dissemination

As this study involves the synthesis of data from previously published studies and publicly available sources, no primary data collection will be undertaken. The protocol received exemption from Research Ethics Board review from the McGill University Health Centre Research Ethics Board.

The results of this systematic review will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publication, conference presentations and knowledge exchange with relevant stakeholders, including fertility care providers, researchers and policymakers. Findings will also be shared with the WHO Department of Sexual and Reproductive Health and Research to support the development of evidence-informed guidance on equitable access to fertility care.

Any amendments to the protocol will be documented in PROSPERO and reported in the final manuscript.

Discussion

Equitable access to fertility care is an integral component of reproductive health and rights, yet many people—especially LGBTIQ+ and unpartnered persons—continue to face barriers related to law, cost and social norms. This review will synthesise evidence on fertility care needs, access and policies affecting these populations, providing a basis for more inclusive and rights-based approaches.

Expanding access must be accompanied by safeguards that ensure safety and quality. Infertility and assisted reproduction may entail increased health and psychosocial risks, underscoring the need for clinical oversight, counselling and long-term follow-up.1719 Equity initiatives should therefore be embedded within systems that guarantee accountability and high standards of care.

By mapping global policies and regulations, this review will identify gaps and highlight best practices for safe and inclusive fertility care. Strengths include its comprehensive search strategy, standardised methodology and collaboration with the WHO. Anticipated limitations include language restriction and heterogeneity in definitions across studies.

Ultimately, this work will inform evidence-based policies that advance equitable and safe access to fertility services for all people.

Background

Immunotherapy with immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) has significantly improved the survival for patients with metastatic melanoma. ICI therapy with single agent anti-PD-1 (nivolumab, pembrolizumab) or doublet combination of nivolumab with ipilimumab or relatlimab induce objective responses of 45–60% of patients, and 10-year melanoma specific survival of 96% for those patients who had been alive and progression-free at 3 years [1–3]. However, there is scope and a need to further improve the response rate and survival.

One of the promising treatment combinations for metastatic melanoma is the use of radiotherapy with immune checkpoint inhibitors [4–7]. There have been multiple reports of the synergy between radiation and immunotherapy in preclinical studies and early phase clinical trials [7–11]. This combination improves local response compared to immunotherapy alone without worsening the toxicity associated with each agent alone, although response rates alone may not translate to meaningful clinical benefit in low volume metastatic disease, where overall survival and progression-free survival are more relevant endpoints than local control of individual lesions. Despite this, radiation may have the potential to convert tumours considered immunologically “cold” into “warm” through the combination of three processes: (1) Changes in the balance of cytokines by increasing the production of immunostimulatory cytokines which overcome the immunosuppressive tumour microenvironment, (2) Recruitment, of antigen-presenting cells and immune effector cells in the tumour microenvironment, (3) Positive regulation of antigen expression, antigen processing, histocompatibility molecules, and costimulatory signals, thereby increasing tumour immunogenicity [12].

There is a significant body of data to suggest a reproducible clinical benefit can be achieved when stereotactic body radiotherapy (SBRT) is used with immunotherapy in a tightly sequenced treatment combination, in contrast to independently timed use of either treatment alone, for the management of a variety of malignancies. In metastatic non-small cell lung cancer, a pooled analysis of two randomised trials showed that the addition of radiotherapy improved the out-of-field response rate, progression-free survival, and the overall survival [13]. In the Stereotactic Ablative Radiotherapy for Comprehensive Treatment of Oligometastatic Tumors trial (SABR-COMET) of 1–5 oligometastases in all solid tumour histologies, the 8-year overall survival was 27.2% in the experimental SBRT arm (SBRT to all sites) versus 13.6% in the standard of care arm (HR, 0.50; 95% CI, 0.30–0.84; P = 0.008) [14].

At present, patients with metastatic melanoma receive various treatment modalities in different combinations and sequencing, including a drug alone approach, drug with radiotherapy for symptom control, SBRT to persistent disease as salvage therapy to metastases do not respond to or progress after initial drug therapy, and surgery. There is an ongoing randomised trial examining the role of upfront stereotactic radiosurgery to asymptomatic melanoma brain metastases in patients receiving concurrent combination of ipilimumab and nivolumab [15] (Clinical Trials.gov Identifier: NCT03340129). However, there is no prospective randomised trial data on the role of SBRT with immunotherapy in patients with extracranial oligometastases.

We hypothesise that for patients with extracranial melanoma oligometastases, upfront concurrent SBRT with immunotherapy is safe and prolongs survival through enhanced anti-tumour immunity due to the potential synergy of the combination therapies, compared to immunotherapy alone.

Objectives and endpoints

Objectives

The primary objective of the AXIOM trial is to examine the efficacy upfront SBRT in patients with 1–5 extracranial melanoma oligometastases treated with concurrent immunotherapy. Secondary objectives are to determine safety of the upfront SBRT with immunotherapy, health-related quality of life (HR-QoL) and translational investigation.

Endpoints

The primary endpoint is overall survival defined as time from randomisation to death from any cause. Secondary endpoints are overall progression-free survival, new disease progression-free survival (time from randomisation to development of new lesions per RECIST 1.1), overall response rate, local control of baseline oligometastases, safety and tolerability, and requirement for salvage radiotherapy in immunotherapy-alone arm. Secondary endpoint quality of life will be assessed by EORTC QLQ-30 and EQ-5D-5 L questionnaires. These will be completed at baseline, week 12 and then 12 weekly to the end of 3 years, then 26 weekly to the end of 5 years, disease progression of death whichever occurs first. Exploratory translational endpoints are predictive and prognostic biomarkers of response, resistance, or toxicity from tissue, blood, and stool samples.

Study design

AXIOM is a phase II, multicentre, open-label, two-arm parallel group, randomised controlled, non-comparative trial comparing concurrent SBRT with immunotherapy versus immunotherapy alone in patients with 1–5 extracranial melanoma metastases. The efficacy of immunotherapy in this patient population is well established and therefore this randomised controlled trial design is non-comparative with the control group. The randomisation ratio of 2:1 will provide more information on the response and safety of SBRT and immunotherapy, whilst limiting the number of patients randomised to the ‘control’ group, in order to provide minimum but contemporary immunotherapy efficacy and safety data. Sample size and statistical analysis

The trial aims to enrol 86 patients in Arm A (SBRT and immunotherapy), which provides > 80% power at a 5% significance two-sided level to detect a 3-year overall survival (OS) rate of 70%. This represents a minimum absolute reduction in the mortality rate by 15%, decreasing from 45% to 30%. The study lasts for a period of 6 years of which patient accrual occurs in the first 3 years. Due to the heterogeneity of immunotherapies in historical data, a contemporary control will be included in the trial to confirm the historical efficacy assumptions. This contemporary arm (Arm B) will enrol 43 patients. Therefore, the total enrolment for the trial will be 129 patients. After a minimum 2 years of follow-up, a total 46 deaths from any cause are expected (26 in arm A and 20 in arm B). No formal statistical comparison will be conducted between arms for primary and secondary efficacy outcomes. An independent Data Safety Monitoring Committee (DSMC) will be formed to assess the futility of concurrent SBRT with immunotherapy. One interim analysis will be conducted once 23 deaths are observed (half of the expected number). During the interim analysis, the DSMC will review other outcomes including: unacceptable SBRT acute toxicity (any CTCAE Grade 4 toxicity), recruitment less than 30% of the complete sample size in 3 years from first patient and a clearly more effective therapy becomes available.

Demographic and baseline characteristics will be summarised using descriptive statistics. Survival will be assessed from the time of randomisation in both treatment arms. Time to event analyses will be conducted with the Kaplan-Meier method for each treatment arm. Median time to events and 95% confidence intervals will be reported for each treatment arm and each outcome measure. Patients who have not yet progressed or have not yet died at the time of data cut will be censored from the time to event analyses at the last known date of follow-up. Descriptive statistics of safety will be presented using National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0 by treatment group. The EORTC QLQ-C30 and EQ-5D-5 L will be used to assess changes in the patient-rated quality of life during and after treatment related to baseline in each cohort.

Discussion

Prior to the advance of immunotherapy, favourable long-term survival has reported with surgery alone in highly selected patients with oligometastatic melanoma. The Southwest Oncology Group phase II trial of complete resection for stage IV melanoma (N = 64) reported a 4-year overall survival of 31% and relapse free survival of 13% [17]. The placebo arm of the phase 3 Malignant Melanoma Active Immunotherapy Trial (MMAIT-IV) showed 5- and 10-year overall survival rates of 43.3% and 33.3%, respectively, following complete resection of ≤ 5 metastases [18, 19]. Patients with metastatic melanoma now have more effective systemic options, and the tailored incorporation of local therapy (surgery and/or RT) to the oligometastases holds promise for improving the outcome.

The overall survival benefit seen in the phase II SABR-COMET trial has led to two ongoing phase 3 randomised trials of SABR with standard of care (palliative radiotherapy with or without systemic anti-cancer therapy) vs. standard of care alone for patients with 1–3 solid cancer metastases (SABR-COMET 3) [20] and 4–10 metastases (SABR- COMET 10) [21]. In addition, there are a number of single histology trials examining the role of SBRT in addition to standard of care systemic therapy (NCT04498767/oligorare, NCT02417662/SARON, NCT06726421/STROKER, NCT04495309/OLIGOMA).

The AXIOM trial addresses the specific question on the whether the addition of SBRT to standard-of-care ICI immunotherapy can improve outcomes compared to immunotherapy alone in patients with M1a-c melanoma. The trial design allows the clinician choice of ICI immunotherapy and this will enhance the generalizability of results. Built into the AXIOM trial the exploratory biological correlatives of tissue, blood and stool sample to determine mechanisms of response, innate and acquired resistance, and toxicities to immunotherapy and combination immunotherapy with SBRT. This will be valuable in increasing our understanding of oligometastases as well as which patients benefit from SBRT. As a phase 2 non-comparative trial, direct statistical comparison between arms is not planned, which may limit definitive conclusions about the relative efficacy of the combination approach. The study is powered based on historical controls, and changes in standard-of-care immunotherapy regimens could affect interpretation of results.

Conclusions

The results of AXIOM will inform the design of larger phase III trials and may identify patient subgroups most likely to benefit from combination SBRT and ICI therapy. The biomarker analyses could guide development of predictive algorithms for treatment selection. Additionally, the safety and quality of life data will inform optimal sequencing and timing of combined modality therapy.

Development and Validation of a UV–Vis Spectrophotometric Method for the Determination of Hispidulin, Verbascoside, and Isoverbascoside in Sesewanua (Clerodendrum Fragrans Willd.) Fraction and Its Application to Dissolving Microneedle Formulations
Authors: Zulfiayu Sapiun, Siti Nur Fatimah S. Mohamad, Arlan K. Imran, Vyani Kamba, Prisca Safriani Wicita, Sitti Rhomlah Jahja, Fitriah Ayu Magfirah Yunus, Mukhriani Mukhriani, Rizka Puji Astuti Daud, Faradiba Abdul Rasyid, Mohamad Aprianto Paneo, Ysrafil Ysrafil, Masrif Masrif, Paulus Pangalo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Cardiovascular disease (CVD) remains a leading cause of global mortality, with thrombosis representing a major underlying pathological mechanism. Clerodendrum fragrans Willd. (Sesewanua), traditionally used for cardiovascular disorders, contains bioactive compounds with potential anticoagulant and cardioprotective effects, including Hispidulin (HIS), Verbascoside (VER), and Isoverbascoside (ISO). This study aimed to develop and validate a UV–Vis spectrophotometric method for the quantification of HIS, VER, and ISO in Sesewanua fraction (SF) and its dissolving microneedle (SF-DMN) formulation, and to apply the method for drug content determination, ex vivo permeation, and skin retention studies. Method validation was performed according to ICH guidelines, assessing specificity, linearity, accuracy, precision, repeatability, limit of detection (LOD), and limit of quantification (LOQ). The method demonstrated excellent linearity (R2 ≥ 0.9989), acceptable LOD and LOQ values, high accuracy and precision (%RSD < 15%; <20% at LOQ), and good repeatability (%RSD < 2%). Application of the validated method showed that HIS, VER, and ISO were successfully quantified in SF, with HIS identified as the dominant constituent. In the SF-DMN formulation, all three compounds exhibited high recovery values (96–99%), indicating efficient encapsulation and formulation stability. Ex vivo permeation studies revealed controlled and sustained drug release, while skin retention analysis confirmed the presence of all compounds within skin tissue after permeation. Overall, the validated UV–Vis spectrophotometric method is reliable and suitable for routine analysis and supports the development of Sesewanua-based dissolving microneedle systems for transdermal drug delivery.

Introduction

Cardiovascular disease (CVD) remains the leading cause of mortality worldwide, including in Indonesia. , The burden has also increased substantially, reaching approximately 659,000 deaths per year. Thrombosis is one of the major pathological mechanisms underlying CVD. , Anticoagulant agents such as heparin are widely used in clinical practice to prevent clot formation. However, heparin is associated with notable limitations, including adverse effects and heparin induced thrombocytopenia, restricting its long-term use. These limitations underscore the need for alternative agents that may provide protective effects against the early events of coagulation and thrombosis, including those with antioxidant activity.

Plants have long been a valuable source of therapeutic compounds, including in the discovery of anticoagulant agents. Previous research by Tiaravista et al., (2023) demonstrated the activity of Acmella oleracea L. as an antithrombotic agent in FeCl3 induced rats. In addition, research by found that the extract of Heteropyxis natalensis (H. natalensis) exhibits strong antithrombotic activity through multiple mechanisms. These findings align with observations in Indonesia, where various native plant species particularly on the island of Sulawesi have shown anticoagulant potential. One such plant is Clerodendrum fragrans Willd., locally known as Sesewanua, which has long been used as a traditional herbal remedy for cardiovascular disorders. −

Several studies have reported that Sesewanua leaf extract contains three major bioactive compounds hispidulin (HIS), verbascoside (VER), and isoverbascoside (ISO) that contribute to its pharmacological properties. ,, These compounds exhibit activities relevant to cardiovascular protection, including attenuation of vascular inflammation through inhibition of the NF-κB, MAPK, and AKT signaling pathways, reduction of oxidative stress, and improvement of mitochondrial function, ultimately helping prevent structural alterations in cardiac tissue. −

Based on mechanistic evidence and long-standing traditional use, Sesewanua represents a promising candidate for development as an anticoagulant agent. In this study, Sesewanua leaves were extracted and fractionated. The selected fractions exhibited promising activity but were limited by poor water solubility, which led to low bioavailability and limited suitability for oral formulations. These limitations highlight the need for more effective drug delivery strategies.

Although the oral route is widely used and convenient, herbal compounds often undergo extensive first-pass metabolism and degradation in the gastrointestinal tract, reducing their therapeutic efficacy. Therefore, alternative delivery approaches are necessary to overcome these barriers. The transdermal route offers several advantages, but its effectiveness is limited by the stratum corneum, which acts as a strong barrier to the permeation of poorly soluble or large molecules. Dissolving microneedles (DMNs) present a suitable solution to this challenge. Several studies support this approach, including successful formulation of Oryza sativa L. extract into a DMN system composed of PVP K90 and HPMC E50, red beet and parsley into soluble microneedles made from sodium hyaluronate, and Vitex agnus-castus and Tamarindus indica into 3D-printed polymeric microneedles.

The DMN transdermal delivery system consists of arrays of microscale needles capable of penetrating the stratum corneum to create micropores that facilitate drug permeation without causing pain. , This route allows for drug administration while avoiding first-pass metabolism. DMNs are typically fabricated from polymers that provide mechanical strength, biocompatibility, and the ability to dissolve within the skin, making them effective carriers for drug loading and controlled release. − In this study, the Sesewanua Fraction (SF) was incorporated into a DMN delivery system.

During drug formulation development, quantifying the active compounds in both the extract fraction and the DMN formulation is essential, including evaluating drug permeation and retention in the skin. Similar approaches have been described in previous studies, such as the work by Neda et L., (2020), who successfully validated an analytical method for Trigonelline, Diosgenin, and Nicotinic Acid in various preparations of Fenugreek seed extract. Comparable validations have also been reported for Prucalopride succinate tablet formulations, metformin HCl in pharmaceutical preparations, and herbal combinations such as Coptis teeta Wall., Andrographis paniculata, and Nyctanthes arbor-tristis. These examples emphasize the importance of identifying and quantifying reliable marker compounds to ensure formulation quality and consistency. ,

However, currently there is no validated UV–Vis spectrophotometry method for determining HIS, VER, and ISO compounds in either the Sesewanua (SF) fraction or DMN formulation. This validated method is essential to ensure consistent pharmacological activity and support the further development of Sesewanua as a potential phytopharmaceutical agent.

Validation of analytical methods is fundamental to ensuring the accuracy, precision, and reproducibility of measurement results. In research involving natural product-based therapeutics, method validation plays a crucial role in guaranteeing the quality, safety, and efficacy of formulated products. In general, analytical method validation commonly refers to the use of High Performance Liquid Chromatography (HPLC). HPLC is widely recognized as a reference method due to its high sensitivity, specificity, and precision. However, despite its excellent analytical performance, HPLC requires relatively high operational costs, including expensive instrumentation, solvents, and maintenance, as well as complex and time-consuming analytical procedures. In the present study, UV–Vis spectrophotometry was employed as an alternative analytical technique because it offers several practical advantages, including lower cost, shorter analysis time, and simpler operational procedures. In addition, several studies have compared the results of analytical method validation obtained using UV–Vis spectrophotometry and HPLC and reported that the results were not significantly different, indicating that spectrophotometric methods can provide reliable analytical performance. − Studies on herbal extracts have also shown that UV–Vis results are comparable to or show good correlation with HPLC for certain compounds, suggesting that UV–Vis spectrophotometry can be considered a suitable and simpler method for routine analysis. Based on these considerations, UV–Vis spectrophotometry was selected for analytical method validation in this study.

This study aims to develop and validate a UV–Vis spectrophotometric method for quantifying HIS, VER, and ISO in SF and SF-DMN formulations. The validation process was carried out in accordance with ICH guidelines, including assessment of specificity, linearity, accuracy, precision, repeatability, limit of detection (LOD), and limit of quantification (LOQ). , The validated method was subsequently applied to determine drug content in SF and SF-DMN and to evaluate ex vivo permeation and drug retention in skin tissue.

Materials and Methods

Materials

The fraction of Sesewanua (Clerodendrum fragrans Willd.), analytical grade hispidulin (Sigma-Aldrich), verbascoside and isoverbascoside (USP, Rockville, MD, USA), were used in this study. Supplementary information, comprising polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP K30), poly­(vinyl alcohol) (PVA), methanol, ethanol, dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), and phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), were acquired from Merck. Deionized water was used Waterone and Pluronic F127 (Sigma-Aldrich) were employed. All chemicals utilized in this study were of analytical grade and obtained from accredited commercial suppliers.

Preparation of Standard Solutions

Stock solutions of hispidulin (HIS), verbascoside (VER), and isoverbascoside (ISO) were prepared at a concentration of 1000 μg/mL using methanol, DMSO, and ethanol as solvents, respectively. ,, Prior to preparation of PBS calibration solutions, each compound was first dissolved in its corresponding organic solvent to ensure complete solubilization. Subsequently, serial dilutions were prepared using PBS pH 7.4 as the diluting medium.

The selection of organic solvents for each compound was based on their solubility and stability, referring to solvents previously reported as suitable in earlier studies. This approach allows consistent application for drug quantification in fractions and stability testing. In addition, PBS pH 7.4 was selected as the aqueous medium to standardize the conditions used in various analyses, including drug quantification in the DMN matrix, ex vivo drug release studies, drug extraction recovery from skin tissue, and drug retention, allowing direct comparison of results obtained from these evaluations.

The phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) solution was prepared by dissolving 0.25 g of PBS powder in 50 mL of deionized water.

Determination of Maximum Absorption Wavelength

The maximum absorption wavelength (λmax) of each compound was determined by scanning the UV absorption spectra of the working solutions within the range of 200–400 nm using a UV–Vis spectrophotometer (Shimadzu UV-1900i). The wavelength corresponding to the highest absorbance was recorded as λmax.

Method Validation

Linearity and Range

Linearity was evaluated using calibration standards prepared from stock solutions of each analyte, within the concentration ranges established during stock and working solution preparation. The absorbance of each standard was measured at its respective λmax using a UV–Vis spectrophotometer (Shimadzu UV-1900i) with triplicate determinations for each concentration. The mean absorbance values were plotted against concentration to construct calibration curves. Linear regression equations and correlation coefficients (R2) were obtained using Microsoft Excel. ,,

Limit of Detection (LOD) and Limit of Quantification (LOQ)

The Limit of Detection (LOD) defines the minimal concentration of an analyte that can be reliably identified, albeit not quantified. In contrast, the Limit of Quantification (LOQ) signifies the lowest concentration that can be quantitatively assessed with sufficient precision and accuracy. ,, The following equations were used for calculation: 1 LOD=3.3×σS 2 LOQ=10×σS

In this case, σ reflects the standard deviation of the response, while S defines the slope of the calibration curve.

Specificity

Specificity was assessed by comparing the UV absorption spectra of HIS, VER, ISO, blank DMN, and SF-DMN samples, which were scanned over the wavelength range of 200–600 nm in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS, pH 7.4). , The purpose of this test was to ensure that no interference occurred at the λmax of each analyte from excipients or other matrix component.

Precision and Accuracy

Precision and accuracy were evaluated using four quality control (QC) levels: high (HQC), medium (MQC), low (LQC), and at the limit of quantification (LOQ), during both intraday and interday analyses. The relative error (%RE) and relative standard deviation (%RSD) were calculated to represent accuracy and precision, respectively. ,

Repeatability

Repeatability was assessed in two solvent systems. For the organic solvent system, each analyte was prepared at the following concentrations: HIS (37.5 μg/mL), VER (25 μg/mL), and ISO (25 μg/mL). For the aqueous system, analytes were prepared in PBS at concentrations of HIS (15 μg/mL), VER (30 μg/mL), and ISO (40 μg/mL). Each sample was analyzed in six replicates (n = 6).

Preparation of Sesewanua Fraction-Dissolving Microneedle (SF-DMN)

The preparation of SF-DMN was adapted from the method described by Sapiun et al. (2024). The SF-DMN formulation consisted of SF (10%), PVP K30 (30%), PVA (5%), Pluronic F-127 (0.5%) (40%), and distilled water (15%). PVP and PVA were placed in a vial and preheated in an oven at 80 °C for 1 h (Dryland-80, inSciencePro). Subsequently, SF and Pluronic F-127 were added to the vial. The mixture was vortexed (Vortex Mixer MX-S, 2500 rpm, DLAB) for 5 min and sonicated (Ultrasonic GT Sonic) for 2 h until a homogeneous solution was obtained.

The homogeneous mixture was poured into a microneedle mold and centrifuged (LC-04S Centrifuge, Oregon, China) at 3500 rpm for 3 × 30 min. The filled molds were then dried at 37 °C for 48 h. After drying, the microneedle arrays were carefully removed from the molds and stored under suitable conditions until further analysis for mechanical strength, insertion, drug content and permeation studies.

Characterization of Sesewanua Fraction-Dissolving Microneedle (SF-DMN)

Characterization of SF-DMN includes evaluation of morphology, mechanical strength, and insertion ability. Morphology visualized using a scanning electron microscope (SEM) and needle dimensions were observed using an optical microscope to measure the initial needle height.

Mechanical Strength Test

Mechanical strength testing was performed using the compression method with eight layers of Parafilm arranged on a glass plate as a skin model. SF-DMN was placed on the surface of Parafilm, then loaded with 32 N for 30 s. After loading, the needle height was measured again to determine the percentage of needle height reduction.

The insertion capability is evaluated based on the needle’s ability to penetrate the Parafilm M layer. After loading, each Parafilm layer is observed to count the number of holes formed.

Application of the Validated UV–Vis Spectrophotometric Method

Stability of SF and SF-DMN during Storage

The effect of storage conditions on the concentration of hispidulin, verbascoside, isoverbascoside, and total flavonoids in the Sesewanua fraction (SF) was evaluated. The SF samples were stored under refrigerator (REF) and freezer (FZ) conditions, and their concentrations were analyzed using UV–Vis spectrophotometry at predetermined time intervals (0, 7, and 14 days).

Quantification of Hispidulin, Verbascoside, and Isoverbascoside in the Sesewanua Fraction

An accurately weighed amount of the SF was placed into microtubes for the determination of HIS, VER, and ISO content. Each fraction sample is dissolved in the appropriate solvent: methanol for HIS, DMSO for VER, and ethanol for ISO. The samples were vortexed for 5 min and sonicated for 10 min to ensure complete dissolution. The absorbance of each solution was then measured using a UV–Vis spectrophotometer. The concentrations of HIS, VER, and ISO were calculated using the linear regression equations obtained from the respective calibration curves.

Drug Content Measurement of HIS, VER, and ISO in SF-DMN

This test aimed to determine the amount of HIS, VER, and ISO incorporated into the DMNs. After the DMNs were removed from the molds, the microneedle tips were carefully scraped off using a sterile surgical blade and weighed. The collected microneedle material was dissolved in 5 mL PBS pH 7,4 and sonicated for 5 min. The resulting solution was centrifuged at 4000 rpm, and the supernatant was collected for analysis using a UV–Vis spectrophotometer. ,, The drug content was calculated based on the calibration equation, and the extraction efficiency was evaluated by comparing the measured amount of each compound with the theoretical amount incorporated into the formulation.

Ex Vivo Permeation Study of SF-DMN

An ex vivo permeation study was conducted to evaluate the permeation profiles of HIS, VER, and ISO using rat skin applied using SF-DMN. Skin samples were obtained using the methodology described by Sapiun et al., (2024). The harvested rat skin was cleaned, wrapped in aluminum foil, and stored at −20 °C.

In a Franz diffusion cell, the receiving compartment was filled with PBS pH 7.4 solution. SF-DMN was applied to the skin surface and stabilized with a 5 g load to prevent shifting. The experiment was conducted at 37 °C with constant stirring at 500 rpm Samples (1 mL) were taken from the receiving compartment at the following time intervals: 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 24, 48, 56, and 72 h. Each sampled volume was replaced with an equivalent volume of fresh PBS. The collected samples were analyzed using UV–Vis spectrophotometry to determine the drug content that penetrated. ,

Drug Extraction Recovery from Skin Tissue

The extraction method was developed to determine the amount of drug retained within the skin. Each compound was prepared at a concentration of 200 μg/mL and spiked into homogenized skin tissue using PBS as the medium. The mixture was centrifuged at 4000 rpm for 25 min. The obtained supernatant was filtered through a 0.22 μm membrane filter. The filtrate was then analyzed using a UV–Vis spectrophotometer to compare the measured concentration with the initially spiked concentration.

Drug Retention Study

Drug retention studies were conducted using rat skin samples that had been previously used in permeability tests. The skin surface was gently cleaned, cut into small pieces, and weighed (approximately 90 mg). The samples were homogenized in PBS (pH 7.4) and sonicated for 30 min. The homogenate was centrifuged at 4000 rpm for 30 min, and the supernatant was filtered using a 0.22 μm membrane filter. The filtered supernatant was analyzed by UV–Vis spectrophotometry to determine the amount of drug retained in the skin tissue. ,

Statistical Analysis

All experimental results are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and processed using Microsoft Excel 2021. IBM SPSS Statistics 27 was used for statistical analysis. The results obtained were tested for normal distribution. In addition, the data were analyzed using parametric and nonparametric tests based on the normal distribution evaluation. One-way ANOVA and Kruskal–Wallis tests were used to compare the data. Graphical representations of the data were generated using GraphPad Prism version 9. A p-value of less than 0.05 (p < 0.05) was considered to indicate a statistically significant difference.

Results and Discussion

In this study, hispidulin (HIS), verbascoside (VER), and isoverbascoside (ISO) were selected as marker compounds for the quantitative determination of active constituents in the Sesewanua Fraction (SF). The analytical method was validated using UV–Vis spectrophotometry in accordance with ICH guidelines, covering validation parameters including linearity, limit of detection (LOD), limit of quantification (LOQ), specificity, precision, accuracy, and repeatability. UV–Vis spectrophotometry was employed as the analytical technique because it offers several practical advantages, including simple operation, rapid analysis, and lower operational costs, making it suitable for routine analysis and high sample throughput. − When properly validated, UV–Vis spectrophotometry is capable of providing reliable quantitative performance for the determination of marker compounds.

Several studies have reported that UV–Vis spectrophotometric methods can produce quantitative results comparable to those obtained by HPLC, particularly when the analytical objective is the determination of specific marker compounds or total phytochemical content rather than detailed compositional profiling. Therefore, UV–Vis spectrophotometry can serve as an appropriate analytical approach for the quality control and quantitative determination of phytochemical markers in complex herbal matrix. In contrast, more advanced techniques such as HPLC or LC–MS are generally employed for comprehensive compositional profiling or structural elucidation of individual constituents.

The validated UV–Vis spectrophotometric method was subsequently applied to determine the concentrations of the marker compounds in the SF, evaluate the drug content in SF-DMN, and assess the ex vivo permeation, recovery, and retention of the drugs within the skin tissue.

Specificity and Calibration Curve

Specificity was evaluated to assess the ability of the analytical method to accurately measure the analytes in the presence of other components in the matrix used as excipients in the DMN preparation. Prior to specificity analysis, the maximum absorption wavelengths (λmax) of HIS, VER, and ISO were determined. The determination of λmax was conducted in four media: phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) at pH 7.4, methanol, dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), and ethanol (organic solvents).

The selection of these media was based on the intended application of the validated analytical method, in which PBS was used for the identification and quantification of HIS, VER, and ISO in the DMN formulation as well as for ex vivo permeation and skin retention studies, where PBS served as the release medium. In contrast, organic solvents were used to quantify HIS, VER, and ISO in the SF.

Spectral scanning revealed that each compound exhibited a distinct maximum absorption wavelength (λmax). In organic solvents, the λmax values of HIS, VER, and ISO were observed at 274, 294, and 305 nm, respectively (Figure ). Meanwhile, in PBS pH 7.4, the corresponding λmax values were 272 nm for HIS, 305 nm for VER, and 330 nm for ISO (Figure ).

Although partial spectral overlap among the analytes was observed within the broader UV region (200–350 nm), each compound still exhibited a well-defined maximum absorption peak with wavelength separation of approximately 25–30 nm. This separation enabled selective quantification of each analyte at its respective λmax while minimizing the absorbance contribution from other compounds. In addition, the blank dissolving microneedle (DMN) spectrum showed negligible absorbance at the selected analytical wavelengths, indicating minimal interference from the polymer matrix. Therefore, adequate analytical selectivity could be achieved without the need for derivative spectroscopy or chemometric approaches.

These λmax values were subsequently used to evaluate the specificity and linearity of each analyte. The UV–Vis spectral scanning results for specificity assessment are presented in Figure , which demonstrates that the blank DMN spectrum did not exhibit any absorption peaks at the λmax regions of HIS, VER, or ISO (272, 305, and 330 nm), indicating the absence of significant matrix interference at the selected analytical wavelengths. In contrast, the SF-DMN samples displayed clear absorption peaks at the respective λmax values of the marker compounds, confirming that the developed method is specific and suitable for the determination of HIS, VER, and ISO in the DMN formulation. ,

Hispidulin exhibited excellent linearity when dissolved in methanol over the concentration range of 9.375–150 μg/mL, with a correlation coefficient (R2) of 0.9992, and showed LOD and LOQ values of 6.01 μg/mL and 18.21 μg/mL, respectively. In PBS at pH 7.4, hispidulin demonstrated a linear concentration range of 1.875–30 μg/mL with an R2 of 0.9998 and LOD and LOQ of 0.7 μg/mL and 2.1 μg/mL, respectively.

Verbascoside dissolved in DMSO showed linearity over the concentration range of 3.125–50 μg/mL, with an R2 value of 0.9989, an LOD of 2.4 μg/mL, and an LOQ of 7.3 μg/mL. When analyzed in PBS pH 7.4, verbascoside exhibited a linear range of 3.75–60 μg/mL with an R2 of 0.9998, and corresponding LOD and LOQ values of 1.4 μg/mL and 4.1 μg/mL.

Isoverbascoside showed good linearity in ethanol over the concentration range of 3.125–50 μg/mL, with an R2 value of 0.9997, an LOD of 1.3 μg/mL, and an LOQ of 3.9 μg/mL. In PBS pH 7.4, isoverbascoside demonstrated linearity across the concentration range of 5–80 μg/mL with an R2 of 0.9999, and LOD and LOQ values of 2.9 μg/mL and 8.7 μg/mL, respectively.

Based on the calculated LOD and LOQ values, the results obtained were higher than the lowest concentration point within the calibration range. In analytical method validation practice according to the ICH guidelines, low concentration calibration points may still be included to obtain a reliable regression equation and to facilitate the calculation of LOD and LOQ. , However, such concentrations are not automatically considered part of the quantitative working range unless their accuracy and precision performance meet the required criteria at or above the LOQ value. Therefore, in this study, the lowest calibration point (<LOQ) was retained for constructing the regression curve and estimating LOD/LOQ, while the working range for routine quantitative analysis was limited to concentrations ≥ LOQ. Thus, the validated quantification range used for routine analysis corresponds only to concentrations at or above the LOQ, where acceptable analytical performance was confirmed.

The correlation coefficients obtained, which were close to unity, indicate a strong linear relationship between analyte concentration and absorbance response. These results demonstrate that the developed method provides reliable analytical sensitivity and linear response across the validated concentration ranges. They are consistent with previously reported method validation studies, including those by Attimarad et al. (2021), who reported R2 values ≥ 0.999, Ibesh et al. (2023), who obtained R2 values ranging from 0.998 to 0.999, and Kurdi et al. (2024) who reported R2 values ≥ 0.9973.

Precision, Accuracy, and Repeatability

Precision and accuracy tests were conducted to evaluate the analytical method’s reliability in producing stable, reproducible data. Precision refers to the degree of consistency of results obtained from repeated measurements of the same sample under identical experimental conditions and is expressed as the relative standard deviation (%RSD). , Accuracy reflects the closeness of the measured values to the actual or accepted reference values and is expressed as the relative error (%RE). ,

These evaluations were performed using two types of solvents: organic solvents (methanol, DMSO, and ethanol) and PBS (pH 7.4), under both intra- and interday conditions. Validation of precision and accuracy was carried out at multiple quality control (QC) levels representing different concentrations within the calibration curve range, including low QC (LQC), medium QC (MQC), high QC (HQC), and the limit of quantification (LOQ), which represents the lowest quantifiable concentration. −

The results are summarized in Tables , , and . Overall, the %RSD and %RE values obtained from both intraday and interday precision and accuracy assessments were below 15%. Furthermore, all %RSD and %RE values at the LOQ level were less than 20%. These findings meet the acceptance criteria for method validation according to established guidelines, indicating that the developed UV–Vis spectrophotometric method possesses adequate accuracy and precision for the analysis of HIS, VER, and ISO. The results are consistent with previously reported validations of UV–Vis spectrophotometric analytical methods. ,,,,,

The repeatability evaluation for each compound, assessed in both organic solvents and PBS pH 7.4, demonstrated percentage recovery values ranging from 98.71 ± 1.35% to 103.75 ± 1.01%. All measurements exhibited relative standard deviation (%RSD) values below 2%, indicating excellent precision of the developed method. Recovery values close to 100% suggest high analytical accuracy and confirm that the method performance was not significantly affected by solvent matrix differences, either in organic systems or physiological buffer media.

The robustness of the method’s repeatability was further supported by comparison with previously reported validation studies conducted in various matrices. For instance, the analysis of soluble CD4 (sCD4) using biosensor-based methods in culture media and serum showed recovery values of 97.8–100.3%, reflecting high accuracy and robustness despite the complexity of biological matrices. Similarly, UV–Vis spectrophotometric analysis of ibuprofen in aqueous media reported a broader recovery range (86–115%), which remains acceptable for simple quantitative methods. In addition, the simultaneous determination of 12 vitamins in culture media using HPLC–UV yielded recovery values of 93.9–106.8%, indicating minimal matrix interference on analytical performance.

Compared with these reports, the UV–Vis spectrophotometric method developed in the present study demonstrates comparable precision and consistency, as evidenced by recovery values close to theoretical levels and %RSD values below 2%. These findings confirm that the method is reliable, stable, and robust against matrix variations, supporting its suitability for routine analysis and further applications in dissolving microneedle (DMN)-based transdermal delivery systems.

Application of the Developed UV–Vis Spectrophotometric Method

Based on the validation results, the developed analytical method fulfilled the requirements for linearity, accuracy, precision, and quantification limits in accordance with the ICH guidelines. Therefore, it is suitable for the determination of Sesewanua fraction in various dissolving microneedle (DMN) matrices. Accordingly, this method was subsequently applied to characterize the Sesewanua fraction in the dissolving microneedle system (SF-DMN), including morphological observation, evaluation of mechanical strength and insertion capability, stability testing during storage, determination of compound levels in the Sesewanua fraction and SF-DMN, ex vivo drug permeation profiling, as well as drug recovery and drug retention in skin tissue.

The developed UV–Vis spectrophotometric method was further applied to determine the drug content in the SF-DMN formulation. Prior to analysis, the SF-DMN samples were completely dissolved in the appropriate solvent to ensure efficient extraction of the marker compounds from the polymeric matrix. The resulting analysis provided information regarding the loading of the marker compounds within the microneedle matrix, suggesting a uniform distribution of HIS, VER, and ISO in the formulation. In addition, the specificity study confirmed that the blank DMN matrix containing PVP and PVA did not exhibit significant absorbance at the selected analytical wavelengths, indicating that the polymer excipients did not interfere with the quantification of the analytes.

Stability of SF During Storage

Temperature plays an important role in the storage of fractions, as low temperatures can reduce the rate of oxidative, hydrolytic, and enzymatic reactions that may degrade bioactive compounds. Therefore, controlling storage temperature is an important strategy for maintaining fraction quality prior to formulation. In this study, the storage stability of the Sesewanua fraction (FS) was evaluated to support recommendations for storage conditions during analytical validation and the development of the SF-DMN formulation. The stability test was conducted under two temperature conditions, namely refrigerator (4 °C) and freezer (−18 °C), with the parameters including the levels of hispidulin (HIS), verbascoside (VER), isoverbascoside (ISO), as well as total flavonoid content (TFC).

Based on Figure (A and B), an increasing trend in the levels of HIS, VER, ISO, and TFC was observed on day 7 and day 14 under both storage conditions, particularly at 4 °C. This increase may be associated with changes in the equilibrium of compounds within the fraction matrix, the release of bound phenolic compounds into their free forms, or the conversion of precursor compounds into the target compounds. However, statistical analysis indicated that the differences in the levels from day 0 to day 14 were not statistically significant (p > 0.05), suggesting that FS remained relatively stable under both storage conditions during the observation period.

These findings are consistent with previous studies reporting that storage at low temperatures can maintain or even increase TFC values during long-term storage, which may be related to structural changes in the matrix or the conversion of conjugated phenolic compounds into forms that are more readily detected analytically. Therefore, low-temperature storage may be considered an effective approach to maintain the stability of flavonoid-rich fractions during research processes and formulation development.

Characterization of Sesewanua Fraction-Dissolving Microneedle (SF-DMN)

The DMN system consists of microneedles (±50–900 μm) capable of penetrating the stratum corneum (SC) and reaching the epidermis, thereby forming microchannels without causing pain. DMNs are fabricated from biodegradable polymers (PVP and PVA) that dissolve upon contact with skin fluids, allowing the drug within the matrix to disperse and be released after penetrating the SC. ,

The characterization of SF-DMN was carried out through morphological observation, evaluation of mechanical strength, and assessment of needle insertion capability. Figure (A and B) shows the morphology of the SF-DMN needles (30% PVP and 5% PVA), which are uniform and conical with sharp tips, thereby supporting their ability to penetrate the skin layers. PVA contains hydroxyl groups (−OH) that can form hydrogen bonds with the carbonyl groups (CO) of PVP, resulting in a physically cross-linked polymer network that enhances the mechanical strength and stability of the microneedles.

Morphology of SF-DMN using SEM (A), morphology using microscope (B), and insertion capability profile of SF-DMN (C) (mean ± SD, n = 3).

Mechanical strength evaluation was performed using the height reduction percentage (%) parameter. The results showed that the height reduction of the SF-DMN needles was 5.97 ± 0.52%, indicating that the SF-DMN possessed good mechanical strength (height reduction < 10%). High concentrations of PVP can form a rigid matrix but tend to be brittle; therefore, the incorporation of PVA in this formulation can increase hardness and reduce the brittleness of the DMN needles. , Based on the favorable mechanical test results, the SF-DMN is expected to be capable of penetrating the SC.

The results shown in Figure C indicate that the SF-DMN was able to penetrate up to the fifth layer of parafilm, which corresponds to 630 μm (one layer equivalent to 126 μm), with 100% penetration up to the second layer. These results indicate that the SF-DMN needles are capable of penetrating the SC. Previous studies have also reported that drug permeation using microneedles (MN) differs significantly compared to systems without MN.

Determination of Hispidulin, Verbascoside, and Isoverbascoside Levels in the Sesewanua Fraction

The validated UV–Vis spectrophotometric method was subsequently applied to quantify HIS, VER, and ISO in the Sesewanua leaf fraction (SF). During the analytical procedures, the SF was stored in tightly closed containers under controlled laboratory conditions and protected from light to maintain analyte stability. The results indicated that all three target compounds were successfully detected and quantified in SF at different concentrations. The measured HIS content was 44.66 ± 7.10%, while VER and ISO were 7.76 ± 0.31% and 9.23 ± 0.05%, respectively. These differences reflect the relative distribution of bioactive compounds within the fraction, with HIS identified as the dominant constituent.

These findings are consistent with previous reports indicating that HIS, VER, and ISO are significant constituents of the Clerodendrum genus, including Clerodendrum chinense. The predominance of HIS in the fraction suggests a significant contribution to SF’s biological activity. It supports its selection as a primary marker compound for the development and quality control of Sesewanua-based formulations.

Determination of Hispidulin, Verbascoside, and Isoverbascoside Levels in the Sesewanua Dissolving Microneedle System

The determination of drug uniformity in the microneedle formulation in this study was based on the approach previously reported by Sapiun et al. (2024), where drug uniformity was determined using parameters including density, loss on drying (LOD) during the drying process, and microneedle volume calculated from the microneedle dimensions. This approach was used for theoretical estimation of drug loading, and actual uniformity was confirmed through experimental measurement of DMN drug content.

In this study, the extraction of compounds from the microneedle matrix was performed using PBS pH 7.4, as this solvent is capable of dissolving the polymers used in the formulation (PVA and PVP), thereby facilitating the release of the incorporated active compounds from the polymer matrix prior to analysis. The use of an appropriate extraction solvent is essential to ensure complete dissolution of the polymeric matrix and efficient recovery of the encapsulated compounds. Stability considerations during the analytical procedure were also taken into account. The SF-DMN samples were stored in tightly sealed containers containing silica gel as a desiccant, under controlled laboratory conditions and protected from light prior to analysis. This storage condition was intended to minimize moisture exposure and maintain the stability of the polymeric microneedle matrix. During sample preparation, the microneedle matrix was dissolved in PBS pH 7.4, and analyzed immediately using UV–Vis spectrophotometry to minimize potential analyte degradation.

The quantification of HIS, VER, and ISO in the DMN formulation demonstrated efficient drug encapsulation, with percentage recovery values close to 100%. The concentration of HIS in the DMN was 99.44 ± 1.98%, while VER and ISO were 99.40 ± 0.75% and 96.00 ± 6.53%, respectively. Furthermore, the %RSD values obtained from this evaluation were all below 5%, indicating good analytical precision and adequate extraction efficiency. These results demonstrate that the extraction procedure is sufficient to release the analytes from the polymer matrix without significant loss during sample preparation.

These high recovery values indicate that the DMN formulation process minimizes the occurrence of degradation or significant loss of active compounds during the mixing, molding, and drying stages Moreover, based on the specificity evaluation (Figure ), the blank DMN did not exhibit absorption peaks at the respective maximum wavelengths of the compounds, indicating that the polymer matrix does not interfere with the absorption measurements of HIS, VER, and ISO.

These results reflect good compatibility between the active compounds and the DMN polymer matrix, enabling homogeneous and stable drug loading in the developed transdermal delivery system. − The recovery values obtained in this study are also consistent with previous reports on drug quantification in biological matrices and complex drug delivery systems. For example, analysis of donepezil in rat plasma reported recovery values exceeding 80%, which is considered acceptable for bioanalytical applications despite the complexity of the biological matrix. Another study evaluating the recovery of GHRP-6 in various matrices reported recovery values ranging from 85–109%, demonstrating reliable analytical performance in a microneedle-based transdermal system. Collectively, these findings confirm that recovery values within this range are widely accepted for quantitative analytical methods involving complex matrices or formulation systems.

Overall, the recovery and precision values achieved are within the widely accepted range for quantitative analysis in DMN matrices, making the developed UV–Vis method suitable for the determination of HIS, VER, and ISO in SF DMN.

Ex Vivo Drug Permeation

The drug release profiles from the dissolving microneedle (DMN) formulations, as shown in Figure , demonstrate that HIS, VER, and ISO exhibit gradual increases in cumulative release over time, eventually reaching a plateau. This pattern indicates controlled release behavior, characterized by an initial release phase followed by sustained drug release until equilibrium is achieved.

Among the three compounds, ISO exhibited the highest cumulative release, followed by VER and HIS. Statistical analysis confirmed significant differences among the release profiles of the individual compounds, suggesting that their physicochemical properties influence release kinetics from the DMN matrix. This gradual release pattern further confirms that the DMN formulation functions as a controlled, sustained drug-delivery system, consistent with previous reports on polymeric microneedle system. −

These findings indicate that the DMN system not only maintains the stability of the active compounds during formulation but also provides a favorable release profile for transdermal drug delivery, potentially supporting therapeutic efficacy by maintaining consistent drug availability over time.

Ex vivo drug permeation studies using the Franz diffusion cell represent an important approach for evaluating drug permeation and skin retention, which can help predict potential therapeutic outcomes in vivo. Previous studies have demonstrated a strong correlation between permeation and drug retention data obtained from human and porcine skin in ex vivo models, reflecting drug behavior in living skin and validating this model for assessing topical formulations such as benzoyl peroxide. In addition, studies involving sinomenine hydrochloride transfersomes reported a strong relationship between ex vivo permeation profiles and in vivo drug absorption in skin and blood, with high correlation coefficients (R > 0.9), further indicating that ex vivo data can reliably predict in vivo pharmacokinetic behavior.

Collectively, these results indicate that the DMN formulation provides a controlled release and permeation profile in the ex vivo skin model, supporting its potential for transdermal delivery and warranting further investigation in future in vivo studies.

Drug Recovery and Drug Retention in Skin Tissue

In the drug recovery study, known concentrations of HIS, VER, and ISO were homogenized into spiked skin tissue samples and subsequently analyzed following filtration through a 0.22 μm membrane. The results demonstrated high and consistent recovery values for all target compounds in the skin matrix, with recoveries of 97.59 ± 1.66% for HIS, 96.78 ± 7.90% for VER, and 98.95 ± 3.20% for ISO. These values indicate that the developed analytical method is reliable and accurate in complex biological matrices and does not result in significant compound degradation or loss during extraction and analysis. The observed recovery range also meets the acceptance criteria recommended by bioanalytical method validation guidelines for biological matrices. ,,

Subsequently, a skin retention study was conducted to evaluate the amount of active compounds retained within the skin tissue after ex vivo permeation. Skin retention assessment is a critical parameter in transdermal delivery studies, as it provides insight into the distribution of compounds that penetrate the skin versus those retained within the tissue, potentially contributing to localized or sustained therapeutic effects. The results (Figure ) demonstrated that all three compounds (HIS, VER, and ISO), remained detectable within the skin tissue following ex vivo permeation. The highest retention was observed for HIS (1.87 ± 0.09 mg/g skin tissue), followed by VER (1.61 ± 0.19 mg/g) and ISO (1.14 ± 0.10 mg/g). Differences in retention levels among the compounds suggest that their individual characteristics influence their interaction with skin tissue following microneedle-mediated penetration.

Conclusion

The UV–Vis spectrophotometric method developed for the quantitative analysis of hispidulin (HIS), verbascoside (VER), and isoverbascoside (ISO) in organic solvents and in PBS (pH 7.4) has been successfully validated. The validation process included parameters of specificity, linearity, limit of detection (LOD), limit of quantification (LOQ), precision, accuracy, and repeatability, all of which met the requirements set by the International Conference on Harmonisation (ICH) guidelines. This method showed good linearity (r2 = 0.999), with LOD and LOQ determined from the slope and standard deviation of the response. The %RSD and %RE values obtained confirmed that this method is precise, accurate, and reproducible.

The validated UV–Vis spectrophotometry method demonstrates reliability, sensitivity, and selectivity for the determination of HIS, VER, and ISO. In addition, the method offers a simple, rapid, and cost-effective analytical approach for monitoring of these marker compounds. Its successful application in analyses of drug content from SF-DMN, ex vivo permeation, and skin retention indicates that this method is suitable for supporting the further development and evaluation of the SF-DMN transdermal drug delivery system. Therefore, this validated method may serve as a practical analytical tool for routine quality control and future studies involving Sesewanua-fraction-based microneedle formulations. Furthermore, future research could expand into in vivo studies on efficacy, safety, and pharmacokinetics, utilizing robust analytical methods and optimized microneedle designs, to position Sesewanua Fraction-based DMNs as candidates for antithrombotic/cardioprotective applications.

Evaluation of the clinical effect of 4D digital strabismus and amblyopia visual function correction system combined with traditional comprehensive treatment methods on anisometropic amblyopia
Authors: Huangen Li, Ting Peng, Yinyan Qin
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
PurposeThe objective of this study was to evaluate the clinical efficacy of a 4D digital strabismus and amblyopia visual function correction system (4D-DSAAVFCS) in combination with conventional modalities compared with conventional modalities alone in children with anisometropic amblyopia.MethodsThis nonrandomized controlled study collected data on best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA), simultaneous vision, fusion vision, near stereoscopic vision, the amplitude of P100 wave (graphic evoked visual potentials), and the latency of P100 wave from both eyes at the beginning of the treatment and one year later. The Mann‒Whitney U test was used to compare BCVA contrasts in different subgroups, and the independent samples t test was used to compare the amplitude and latency of P100 wave contrasts in different subgroups. The basic cure rate, simultaneous vision recovery rate, fusion vision recovery rate, and near stereoscopic vision recovery rate contrasts in different subgroups were compared via the chi-square test.ResultsThis study included 393 children (217 boys and 176 girls) aged 3 to 12 years with anisometropic amblyopia who were treated at the Aier Eye Hospital of Wuhan University from January 2020 to December 2022. The children were divided into two groups, the 4D group (263 cases) and the traditional group (130 cases), on the basis of the treatment modality. The children in the traditional group received treatment through the conventional method of occlusion and regular training. Meanwhile, the children in the 4D group received treatment through the traditional method and the 4D-DSAAVFCS. The 4D group was divided into two age groups: 3 ~ 6 years (161 cases) and 6 ~ 12 years (102 cases). The basic cure rate of the 4D group was significantly better than that of the traditional group (χ2 = 4.318, P < 0.05). There were no statistically significant differences in the BCVA, the latency of P100 wave, or the amplitude of P100 wave between the 4D group and the traditional group before treatment (U=-0.117, t=-0.05, all P > 0.05 ). After one year of treatment, a statistically significant difference was observed between the 4D group and the conventional group in terms of BCVA, the latency of P100 wave, and the amplitude of P100 wave (U=-1.243, t=-0.853, t=-1.546, all P < 0.05). These results suggest that the therapeutic effect was greater in the 4D group than in the conventional group. The recovery rates of simultaneous vision, convergent fusion, divergent fusion, and near stereoscopic vision were significantly greater in the 4D group than in the conventional group (χ2 = 4.344, 4.726, 5.123, and 2.036, respectively; all P < 0.05). Additionally, the basic cure rate of children aged 3 ~ 6 years in the 4D group was significantly greater than that of children aged 6 ~ 12 years (χ2 = 2.365, P < 0.05). In this study, BCVA was significantly lower in the 3 ~ 6-year-old group than in the 6 ~ 12-year-old group (U = -1.267, P < 0.05). Similarly, the amplitude of P100 wave was also significantly greater in the 3 ~ 6-year-old group than in the 6 ~ 12-year-old group (t = -1877, P < 0.05). The latency of P100 wave was lower in the 3 ~ 6-year-old group than in the 6 ~ 12-year-old group (t=-0.998, P < 0.05). Additionally, the recovery rate of near stereoscopic vision was significantly greater in the 3 ~ 6-year-old group than in the 6 ~ 12-year-old group (χ2 = 4.534, P < 0.05).ConclusionThe combination of the traditional method with the 4D-DSAAVFCS was more effective than the traditional method alone in treating amblyopic children. This approach was particularly helpful in improving the visual acuity of the children and restoring their optic nerve conduction function, simultaneous vision, fusion vision, and near stereoscopic vision. The combination of the traditional method and the 4D-DSAAVFCS is more effective for younger children.

Introduction

Amblyopia is a common cause of visual impairment in children, with a prevalence of up to 1% ~ 4%. It is a disease that has no apparent organic pathology. Without accurate, effective, and consistent treatment during visual development, amblyopia can lead to irreversible visual damage, including vision loss and impaired visual function [1–4]. Amblyopia is a condition that encompasses four types: anisometropia, refractive errors, strabismus, and visual deprivation. Anisometropia amblyopia is one of the prevalent types of amblyopia.

Currently, there are various methods for treating amblyopia, such as occlusion, optical suppression therapy and pharmacological suppression therapy. These methods aim to enhance the vision of the amblyopic eye by forcing it to work harder. However, blurring the dominant eye can significantly impact a child’s learning and daily life, leading to low cooperation and poor compliance during treatment. Previous studies [5] have shown that poor compliance is the most critical factor affecting the treatment of anisometropic amblyopia, so these methods have certain limitations in the treatment of anisometropic amblyopia.

With the advancement of comprehensive amblyopia treatment, emerging methods such as oral medication [6] and behavioral treatment have gradually been applied to treat amblyopic children. Behavioral treatments include visual perception training, video game training, binocular vision training, and transcranial magnetic stimulation training [7]. These treatments are gaining more attention because of their few side effects. The 4D digital strabismus and amblyopia visual function correction system (4D-DSAAVFCS) is a behavioral treatment developed on the basis of the modern neuropsychological theory of visual attention cognition and the neurobiological mechanisms of synaptic plasticity [8–11]. It provides personalized training based on the child’s visual condition using 3D technology. The program aims to enhance visual stimulation through a variety of training games. These games are designed to be enjoyable, with diverse training modes and short treatment times. This approach aims to improve children’s treatment adherence and relieve their dependence on their visual condition.

This study aimed to analyze the clinical effect of the 4D-DSAAVFCS combined with traditional methods in treating children with anisometropia who were treated for amblyopia in our hospital. We compared the results with those of patients who were only treated with traditional occlusion and prescription glasses. This study collected data on children with anisometropia and conducted a comprehensive analysis of the clinical outcomes.

Information and methodology

Information

A nonrandomized controlled method was utilized in this study. We included 393 children with anisometropic amblyopia who presented at our hospital between January 2020 and December 2022, comprising 217 boys and 176 girls aged between 3 and 12 years. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) According to the 2021 Amblyopia Expert Consensus [12] criteria, children with monocular amblyopia differ in the number of rows with BCVA(best-corrected visual acuity )between their eyes, with the amblyopic eye having lower visual acuity while the normal eye has visual acuity corresponding to the age of the child. The lower limit of normal visual acuity was 0.5 for children aged 3 to 5 years and 0.7 for children aged 6 years and above. (2) Anisometropic parallax amblyopia was diagnosed according to the 2021 Amblyopia Expert Consensus [12] criteria. The difference in the spherical lens between the two eyes was 1.5 D, and the difference in the column lens was 1.0 D. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Children with any of the following conditions: binocular amblyopia, refractive interstitial turbidity, nystagmus, ptosis, fundus abnormality, history of previous ocular trauma, history of surgery, combined systemic diseases, and children who were too young to cooperate with the examinations; (2) Children with a follow-up time of less than 1 year. This study adhered to the requirements of the Declaration of Helsinki, and the Ethics Committee of Aier Eye Hospital of Wuhan University approved the study protocol. The subjects’ families comprehended the study’s purpose and significance and signed the informed consent form.

Methods

Data collection function and examination methods

The basic information of the children, including their age and sex, was recorded. (1) BCVA. The visual acuity was recorded in decimals using the international standard visual acuity chart at 5 m and then converted into the logarithm of the minimum angle of resolution (LogMAR) visual acuity standard for statistical purposes. (2) P100 wave (graphically induced visual potential). The amplitude and latency of P100 wave was checked and recorded using a visual electrophysiological system, specifically the graphically induced visual potential. (3) Binocular visual function. Binocular visual function was assessed by measuring the simultaneous vision and fusion vision (collection and separation functions) using a synoptiscope as follows. After refractive correction and adjustment of the pupil distance, the frontal bracket and the mandibular bracket, the equipment was zeroed. Next, simultaneous vision pictures are used. The child pushes and pulls the handle of the strabismic spectacle cylinder until the tiger is loaded into the cage. The cylinder points to the scale marking the horizontal self-selected strabismic perspective. Failure to push in indicates that the patient is inhibited. The strabismus eyeglasses barrel is moved to the same position as the patient’s line of sight and then the light source is alternately switched, observing that the patient’s eye position changes, that is, the other perceived oblique angle of vision is unchanged. Next, fusion pictures are used. The dispersion knob is turned so that the two arms of the same vision machine have the same amount of convergence; when the two pictures do not coincide, that means the child has diplopia, namely, their fusion function is abnormal if they cannot see the two images at the same time. Normal simultaneous vision was defined as between + 5° and − 5°, the normal convergent fusion was defined as between + 25° and + 30°, and the normal divergent fusion was defined as between − 4° and − 6°. Near stereoscopic vision was assessed via the Titmus stereogram, with normal near stereoscopic vision defined as ≤ 60” and values > 80” considered abnormal.

The evaluation of BCVA efficacy is based on three criteria: basic cure, effectiveness, and ineffectiveness. A basic cure is achieved when the visual acuity of the amblyopic eye improves to match that of the normal eye; there is a significant improvement in anisometropic amblyopia, and there is no recurrence within 6 months. Effectiveness is achieved when visual acuity increases by 2 rows or more. Ineffectiveness is when visual acuity and anisometropia do not improve significantly or even decrease, and the recurrence rate is high within 1 year.

Grouping method

Classification into conventional and 4D groups according to different treatment modalities

All of the children in the study underwent a conventional slit lamp examination, fundus examination, and eye position examination. After ciliary muscle paralysis, the optometrist performed an eye examination on the child and record his or her spherical lens degree, lens degree, and axial position. The optometrist then corrected the child’s refractive errors according to standard prescription principles. The children were retested every 3 to 6 months using the same method. If there were significant changes in the children’s refractive errors, the optometrist replaced their spectacles or adjusted their refractive error correction method on the basis of the child’s specific situation. The treatments were divided into two groups on the basis of the child’s condition: the traditional group and the 4D group. The traditional group involved daily occlusion and fine eye training. The fine eye training consisted of training, such as stringing beads, drawing, and tracing a picture, performed twice a day for 10–15 min each time. The 4D group involved the 4D-DSAAVFCS training combined with the conventional occlusion method.

The specific methods for training with the 4D-DSAAVFCS are as follows: (1) Visual fine stimulation training system: To improve visual acuity, before each training, first, the individual visual acuity and binocular parallax thresholds are checked, and the fine and stimulation thresholds of the game are adjusted accordingly. Through the strong sensitivity of black and white or color checkerboard grid rotation and other stimulation methods, the child uses the mouse on the axis of the game to make drawings and look for different shapes. (2) Convergent visual acuity training: After fine stimulation training, when a child’s visual acuity is greater than 0.5, binocular visual function training can be carried out. During the training, both eyes are kept in training at the same time, and the eye masks are removed to check whether there is any distance and near distance suppression using the Worth-4 point and synoptic machine and to obtain a comprehensive understanding of the conditions of the three levels of visual function. When the child’s primary oblique visual angle was not equal to the other oblique visual angle, simultaneous visual function was trained, and when the primary oblique visual angle was equal to the other oblique visual angle, the peripheral fusion function was trained. In the training, red and green reading units were used to desuppress, and then the 4D liquid crystal display switch glasses were put on to train the visual functions, including scanning, picture training, following games, and target practice, to train the center and periphery of the child’s peripheral fusion function. (3) Three-dimensional spatial stereo vision training: This training focuses on spatial stereo function and includes a number of dynamic spatial stereo videos with rich content, which are classified into mild, moderate, severe and enhanced according to the level, and the training programs are selected according to the different directions of strabismus and fusion ranges of the child. After putting on the 4D liquid crystal display switch glasses, the child starts training on static quantitative random points and dynamic stereo vision, can see colorful, jumping and dynamic images in a stereoscopic sense, and can alternately move the target near and far.

The children included in this study were aged 3 ~ 12 years and were divided into 3 ~ 6 years and 6 ~ 12 years groups according to their ages

Statistical methods

SPSS version 25.0 was used to analyze the data. The Kolmogorov–Smirnov test was used to identify normally distributed data, which were expressed as the mean ± standard deviation and an independent samples t test was used to compare the measured data between two groups. The M (P25 ~ P75) was used to express the data with a skewed distribution, and the Mann‒Whitney U test was used to compare the measured data between the two groups. The chi-square test was used to analyze differences between the two groups for count data. A p value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

This study included 130 children with amblyopia in the traditional group (130 cases) and 263 children with amblyopia in the 4D group (263 cases). Pretreatment abnormalities in simultaneous vision, convergent fusion, divergent fusion, near stereoscopic vision, BCVA, the amplitude of P100 wave, and the latency of P100 wave did not differ significantly between the two groups. The comparative results and associations of the pretreatment data are shown below in Table 1.

After one year of treatment, the BCVA was lower and the basic cure rates, simultaneous vision recovery rates, convergent fusion recovery rates, divergent fusion recovery rates, and near stereoscopic vision recovery rates were higher in the 4D group than in the conventional group. The results and associations of the various data between the two groups after treatment are presented in Figs. 1 and 2. After simultaneous treatment, the amplitude of P100 wave was 13.61 ± 4.83 (µV) in the conventional group and 14.65 ± 4.83 (µV) in the 4D group. The amplitude of P100 wave was significantly greater in the 4D group (t=-0.853, P < 0.05), whereas there was no difference in the amplitude of P100 wave before treatment between the two groups. There was no difference in the latency of P100 wave before treatment between the two groups. However, after treatment, the latency of P100 wave in the 4D treatment group (107.59 ± 11.83 ms) was significantly shorter than that in the traditional group (109.09 ± 9.29 ms; t=-1.546, P < 0.05). These findings suggest that the 4D treatment was more effective in shortening the latency of P100 wave than the conventional treatment was.

Moreover, in the 4D group, the children were divided into 3 ~ 6-year-old (161 cases) and 6 ~ 12-year-old groups (102 cases). The basic cure rate of the children in the 3 ~ 6-year-old group was greater than those in the 6 ~ 12-year-old group. And BCVA was lower in the 3 ~ 6-year-old group than in the 6 ~ 12-year-old group. A comparison of the data between the two groups is shown in Figs. 3 and 4. Moreover, the amplitude of P100 wave of the children in the 3 ~ 6-year-old group was 14.98 ± 2.22 (µV), and that of the children in the 6 ~ 12-year-old group was 14.33 ± 4.31 (µV). The difference was statistically significant (t=-1.877, P < 0.05), indicating that the increase in the amplitude of P100 wave after treatment was greater in the 3 ~ 6-year-old group than in the 6 ~ 12-year-old group. The latency of P100 wave in the 3 ~ 6-year-old group was 107.11 ± 7.39 (ms), and that in the 6 ~ 12-year-old group was 108.02 ± 12.34 (ms), and the difference was statistically significant (t=-0.998, P < 0.05), indicating that the decrease in the latency of P100 wave after treatment was greater in the 3 ~ 6-year-old group than in the 6 ~ 12-year-old group.

Discussion

In this study, the cure rate of amblyopia and improvement in visual acuity were better when traditional treatment was combined with the 4D-DSAAVFCS than when traditional treatment alone was used. The visual acuity improvement effect in children with anisometropic amblyopia who were treated with traditional prescription glasses and occlusion treatment was not very satisfactory, which was mainly due to poor cooperation and compliance during treatment. The 4D-DSAAVFCS uses the internet and virtual reality technology. It uses personalized training based on each child’s visual acuity, fine resolution, stimulation thresholds, and age. The system conducts fine eye training, binocular vision training, and visual perception training through training games and 3D near stereoscopic vision videos. Binocular vision training involves providing different contrast images to the dominant and amblyopic eyes, constantly adjusting the contrast to achieve the same clarity of binocular stimuli. This balances the visual input signals of both eyes, enhances binocular connections, and stimulates neurons to receive stimuli and excitation inputs from both eyes [13, 14]. Additionally, visual perceptual training encompasses fusion, fine vision, balance, motor, and bilateral body coordination training. This training combines newly learned visual functions with other functions to improve binocular visual function through the cooperation of the brain, eyes, and hands. It enhances the plasticity of the visual cortex and improves visual acuity [15]. This study revealed that the cure rate and visual acuity of amblyopic children significantly improved after the addition of the 4D-DSAAVFCS training method. These results suggest that the 4D-DSAAVFCS has a positive effect on the treatment of anisometropic amblyopic children, which is consistent with the findings of previous studies.

Clinical studies have shown [16] that children with amblyopia have lower visual acuity, simultaneous vision, fusion vision, and near stereoscopic vision than their normal peers do. Previous studies [17, 18] have shown that patients with anisometropic amblyopia have some degree of visual function impairment. Therefore, when treating anisometropic amblyopia in children, it is important to focus not only on improving visual acuity but also on improving visual function. The traditional method for treating anisometropic amblyopia involves correcting anisometropic errors in amblyopic children and then covering the eye. This method can improve the visual acuity of the amblyopic eye to a certain extent but has a limited effect on improving near stereoscopic vision and other visual functions of children [19–21]. This is because, after amblyopia is treated with monocular occlusion the signal strength from both eyes remains unbalanced, and the visual function of both eyes remains defective. This can lead to the recurrence of amblyopia after treatment.

The P100 wave reflects the potential generated by the visual cortex in response to image stimulation of a certain intensity. The retina is stimulated by alternating images of appropriate spatial and temporal frequencies, and the potential changes in visual conduction from the retina to any part of the visual cortex are recorded. The P100 wave is primarily recorded to measure nerve conduction in the clinic. It reflects the speed of photochemical signal transduction from the retina to the optic cortex, while its amplitude reflects visual sensitivity and the number and function of ganglion cells. This makes it an important index for detecting macular function and the state of optic nerve conduction [22, 23]. The P100 wave originates in the primary visual cortex, and human amblyopia is characterized by decreased activation of the primary visual cortex. However, the primary visual cortex is thought to be the most affected structure in the visual pathway in amblyopia [24]. The binocular neurons of the primary cortex are involved in integrated visual functions, such as simultaneous vision, fusion vision and stereoscopic vision. A previous study [25] revealed that the latency of P100 wave is significantly prolonged in anisometropic amblyopic eyes compared with normal eyes. Another study [26] also revealed that the amplitude of the P100 wave significantly increased in amblyopic eyes after amblyopic ophthalmologic treatment. These results suggest that the P100 wave is specific for evaluating the treatment effect in amblyopic eyes. Therefore, the P100 wave was used as an index to evaluate the treatment effect on amblyopia in this study. In the present study, the traditional modality was combined with the 4D-DSAAVFCS. The results revealed an increase in the amplitude of P100 wave and a decrease in the latency of P100 wave. These findings suggest that the addition of the 4D-DSAAVFCS not only improves children’s visual acuity but also enhances their optic nerve conduction function.

Previous treatments, which involved occlusion of the dominant eye to blur the healthy eye and improve visual acuity, are not very effective in establishing simultaneous vision, fusion vision, and near stereoscopic vision in children with amblyopia. On the other hand, the 4D-DSAAVFCS, which is based on binocular division of vision, starts training simultaneous vision and fusion vision at the early stage of amblyopia treatment. This approach theoretically avoids the negative effect of occlusion of the dominant eye on the restoration of binocular visual function. This study revealed that the recovery rates of simultaneous vision, fusion vision, and near stereoscopic vision were higher than those of the traditional modality when the 4D-DSAAVFCS was added to the traditional modality for 1 year. There are several previous studies on the treatment of amblyopic children with the addition of the 4D-DSAAVFCS. Zhu [27] reported that after the addition of the 4D-DSAAVFCS, the basic cure rate was 62.7%, which was higher than that reported in the present study (58.17%), and the rate of visual reconstruction was 64.1%, which was lower than that reported in the present study (84.79%), and the rate of stereopsis reconstruction (76.3%) was higher than that reported in this study (61.98%). The results of Chi’s [28] study of children with anisometropic amblyopia revealed that after the addition of the 4D-DSAAVFCS, the basic cure rate was 58.33%, which is basically the same as that reported in the present study, and the rates of simultaneous visual reconstruction (87.93%) and stereopsis reconstruction (70.77%) were higher than those reported in the present study. Moreover, the basic cure rate and binocular visual function reconstruction rate in these studies were higher than those in the conventional group. The results of previous studies and those of the present study suggest that the addition of the 4D-DSAAVFCS in the treatment of children with amblyopia can enhance the treatment effect in children with amblyopia.

Previous studies [29, 30] have concluded that the sensitive period for children’s visual development is between the ages of 3 and 6. In the present study, the cure rate of amblyopia, improvement in visual acuity, and reconstruction rate of near stereoscopic vision in the 3 ~ 6-year-old group were significantly better than those in the 6 ~ 12-year-old group. The results of this study suggest that the traditional modality combined with the 4D-DSAAVFCS had a greater effect on younger children, as indicated by the increase in the amplitude of P100 wave and shortening of the latency of P100 wave. This study focused on different types of anisometropic strabismus and provides evidence supporting the early treatment of children with anisometropia.

In summary, when treating amblyopic children, it is important to combine traditional methods such as occlusion and prescription glasses with the 4D-DSAAVFCS. This combination has been shown to be more effective than traditional methods alone and can help improve children’s visual acuity as well as restore simultaneous vision, fusion vision, and near stereoscopic vision. Notably, traditional methods and the 4D-DSAAVFCS tend to be more effective when used on younger children.

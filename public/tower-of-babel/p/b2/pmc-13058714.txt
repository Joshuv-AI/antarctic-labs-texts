Safety and efficacy of a feed additive consisting of Lactobacillus acidophilus CNCM I‐3231, Ligilactobacillus salivarius CNCM I‐3233, Lacticaseibacillus paracasei CNCM I‐4901, Bifidobacterium longum CNCM I‐3227 and Bifidobacterium animalis subsp. lactis CNCM I‐3993 (FlorEquilibre® Chat) for cats (WAMINE SAS)
Authors: EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP), Roberto Edoardo Villa, Giovanna Azimonti, Eleftherios Bonos, Henrik Christensen, Mojca Durjava, Birgit Dusemund, Ronette Gehring, Boet Glandorf, Maryline Kouba, Marta López‐Alonso, Francesca Marcon, Carlo Nebbia, Alena Pechová, Miguel Prieto‐Maradona, Katerina Theodoridou, Giovanna Martelli, Secundino López‐Puente, Maria Saarela, Montserrat Anguita, Jaume Galobart, Matteo L Innocenti, Jordi Ortuño, Sofia Sandalli, Rosella Brozzi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFollowing a request from the European Commission, EFSA was asked to deliver a scientific opinion on the safety and efficacy of FlorEquilibre® Chat when used as a zootechnical additive (functional groups: gut flora stabilisers) for cats. The product under assessment is based on viable cells of Bifidobacterium animalis subsp. lactis CNCM I‐3993, Lactobacillus acidophilus CNCM I‐3231, Ligilactobacillus salivarius CNCM I‐3233, Bifidobacterium longum CNCM I‐3227 and Lacticaseibacillus paracasei CNCM I‐4901. All five strains were suitable for the qualified presumption of safety (QPS) approach to safety assessment. The identity of the strains was established, and no acquired antimicrobial resistance genes were detected, except in B. animalis subsp. lactis CNCM I‐3993. Therefore, the FEEDAP Panel concluded that the use of the strains, except for CNCM I‐3993, is presumed safe for the target species and the environment. The Panel also concluded that the use of B. animalis subsp. lactis CNCM I‐3993 represents a safety concern for the target species, the users and the environment because the strain harbours an acquired antimicrobial resistance gene (tet(W)). Therefore, FlorEquilibre® Chat is considered not safe for the target species, users and the environment. Regarding user safety, FlorEquilibre® Chat is an eye and skin irritant, and a skin and respiratory sensitiser and exposure via any route is considered a risk. Based on the data provided, the Panel cannot conclude on the efficacy of the additive for cats.Keywords: efficacy, FlorEquilibre® Chat, gut flora stabiliser, other zootechnical, safety, zootechnical additives

INTRODUCTION

Background and Terms of Reference

Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003 1 establishes the rules governing the Community authorisation of additives for use in animal nutrition. In particular, Article 4(1) of that Regulation lays down that any person seeking authorisation for a feed additive or for a new use of feed additive shall submit an application in accordance with Article 7.

The European Commission received a request from WAMINE SAS 2 for the authorisation of the additive consisting of Bifidobacterium animalis subsp. lactis CNCM I‐3993, Lactobacillus acidophilus CNCM I‐3231, Ligilactobacillus salivarius CNCM I‐3233, Bifidobacterium longum CNCM I‐3227, Lacticaseibacillus paracasei CNCM I‐4901 (FlorEquilibre® Chat), when used as a feed additive for cats (category: zootechnical additives; functional group: gut flora stabilisers). 3 During the assessment, the applicant requested a change in the functional group (withdrawal of ‘other zootechnical additives’). 4

According to Article 7(1) of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, the Commission forwarded the application to the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) as an application under Article 4(1) (authorisation of a feed additive or new use of a feed additive). The dossier was received on 14 February 2024 and the general information and supporting documentation are available at https://open.efsa.europa.eu/questions/EFSA‐Q‐2024‐00090. The particulars and documents in support of the application were considered valid by EFSA as of 20 June 2024.

According to Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, EFSA, after verifying the particulars and documents submitted by the applicant, shall undertake an assessment in order to determine whether the feed additive complies with the conditions laid down in Article 5. EFSA shall deliver an opinion on the safety for the target animals, consumer, user and the environment and on the efficacy of the feed additive consisting of B. animalis subsp. lactis CNCM I‐3993, B. longum CNCM I‐3227, L. acidophilus CNCM I‐3231, L. paracasei CNCM I‐4901 and L. salivarius CNCM I‐3233 (FlorEquilibre® Chat), when used under the proposed conditions of use (see Section 3.1.4).

Additional information

The additive is a preparation containing viable cells of L. acidophilus CNCM I‐3231, L. salivarius CNCM I‐3233, L. paracasei CNCM I‐4901, B. longum CNCM I‐3227 and B. animalis subsp. lactis CNCM I‐3993. It has not been previously authorised as a feed additive in the European Union.

The FEEDAP Panel adopted an opinion on the safety and efficacy of a preparation containing, among others, three of the active agents of the product under assessment (L. acidophilus CNCM I‐3231, B. animalis subsp. lactis CNCM I‐3993 and L. salivarius CNCM I‐3233) when used in dogs (FEEDAP Panel, 2025).

DATA AND METHODOLOGIES

Data

The present assessment is based on data submitted by the applicant in the form of a technical dossier 5 in support of the authorisation request for the use of the preparation consisting of L. acidophilus CNCM I‐3231, L. salivarius CNCM I‐3233, L. paracasei CNCM I‐4901, B. longum CNCM I‐3227 and B. animalis subsp. lactis CNCM I‐3993 (FlorEquilibre® Chat), as a feed additive.

The confidential version of the technical dossier was subject to a target consultation of the interested Member States from 1 July to 1 October 2024; the comments received were considered for the assessment.

In accordance with Article 38 of the Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 6 and taking into account the protection of confidential information and of personal data in accordance with Articles 39 to 39e of the same Regulation, and of the Decision of EFSA's Executive Director laying down practical arrangements concerning transparency and confidentiality, 7 a non‐confidential version of the dossier has been published on Open.EFSA.

According to Article 32c(2) of Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 and to the Decision of EFSA's Executive Director laying down the practical arrangements on pre‐submission phase and public consultations, EFSA carried out a public consultation on the non‐confidential version of the technical dossier from 14 October to 4 November 2024 for which no comments were received.

The FEEDAP Panel used the data provided by the applicant together with data from other sources, such as previous risk assessments by EFSA and peer‐reviewed scientific papers to deliver the present output.

EFSA has verified the European Union Reference Laboratory (EURL) report as it relates to the methods used for the control of the active agents in animal feed. 8

Methodologies

The approach followed by the FEEDAP Panel to assess the safety and the efficacy of the preparation consisting of B. animalis subsp. lactis CNCM I‐3993, L. acidophilus CNCM I‐3231, L. salivarius CNCM I‐3233, B. longum CNCM I‐3227 and L. paracasei CNCM I‐4901 (FlorEquilibre® Chat) is in line with the principles laid down in Regulation (EC) No 429/2008 9 and the relevant guidance documents: Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the consumer (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2017a), Guidance on the identity, characterisation and conditions of use of feed additives (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2017b), Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the target species (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2017c), Guidance on the characterisation of microorganisms used as feed additives or as production organisms (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2018), Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the environment (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2019), Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the users (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2023), Guidance on the assessment of the efficacy of feed additives (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2024), EFSA statement on the requirements for whole genome sequence analysis of microorganisms intentionally used in the food chain (EFSA, 2021).

ASSESSMENT

The product containing viable cells of L. acidophilus CNCM I‐3231, L. salivarius CNCM I‐3233, L. paracasei CNCM I‐4901, B. longum CNCM I‐3227 and B. animalis subsp. lactis CNCM I‐3993 is intended to be used as a zootechnical additive (functional group: gut flora stabilisers) 10 in feed for cats. It will be hereafter referred to with its trade name FlorEquilibre® Chat.

Characterisation

Characterisation of the active agents

The active agents were isolated from human (L. acidophilus CNCM I‐3231, B. longum CNCM I‐3227 and B. animalis subsp. lactis CNCM I‐3993), plant (L. salivarius CNCM I‐3233) and dairy (L. paracasei CNCM I‐4901) sources and are deposited in the Collection Nationale de Cultures de Microorganismes (CNCM, Institut Pasteur). 11 The applicant declares that none of the strains has been genetically modified. 12

The taxonomic identity of all strains was confirmed by average nucleotide identity (ANI) analysis based on whole genome sequencing (WGS) data, supported by alignment‐free genome distance estimation and phylogenetic analyses using core gene sets. The ANI values were 99.97% for L. acidophilus CNCM I‐3231 13 compared with the type strain ■■■■■, 99.99% for L. salivarius CNCM I‐3233 14 compared with ■■■■■, 98.63% for L. paracasei CNCM I‐4901 15 compared with ■■■■■, 99.03% for B. longum CNCM I‐3227 16 compared with ■■■■■ and 99.98% for B. animalis subsp. lactis CNCM I‐3993 17 compared with ■■■■■. In all cases, phylogenetic analyses confirmed that the respective genomes clustered with those of the corresponding type strains. No plasmids were predicted in the WGS data of any strain.

The antimicrobial susceptibility of the five strains was assessed (by broth microdilution method) against the set of antibiotics recommended by EFSA for the relevant taxonomic groups (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2018). 18 All minimum inhibitory concentration values were equal to or fell below the corresponding EFSA cut‐off values, indicating susceptibility to the relevant antibiotics.

The WGS data of the strains were further interrogated for the presence of antimicrobial resistance (AMR) genes using the ■■■■■ and ■■■■■ databases. No hits exceeding the EFSA thresholds (EFSA, 2021) were detected for any strain except B. animalis subsp. lactis CNCM I‐3993, in which a tet(W) gene, encoding a tetracycline resistance ribosomal protection protein, was identified. 19 As previously concluded by the FEEDAP Panel (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2025), this gene is considered acquired and therefore represents a risk.

Manufacturing process

Each of the five strains included in FlorEquilibre® Chat is grown separately. 20 After submerged liquid fermentation, the cells are harvested by concentration, stabilised and freeze‐dried. For the strains L. acidophilus CNCM I‐3231 and B. animalis subsp. lactis CNCM I‐3993, food grade silicone‐based antifoam (10% dimethylpolysiloxane) 21 may be used at the level of 0.1 g/L. The resulting individual strain lyophilisates (■■■■■ in total in the final product) are then mixed (on ■■■■■) with ■■■■■ as carrier ■■■■■ and ■■■■■ as anticaking agent to reach a minimum guaranteed concentration of total lactic acid bacteria of 3 × 1010 CFU/g additive. 22 According to the applicant, no antimicrobial substances are used during the manufacturing of the additive. 23

The data provided showed compliance with the specifications set by the applicant in regards to total bacterial counts. Compliance with the quantitative specifications of each individual strain was investigated in five batches of the additive by identifying colonies after culturing by specific quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR). The expected proportion of strains in the additive was highly variable between batches, many of the individual values being below the expected ones. The mean percentage values of the five batches were clearly below the expected value for the L. salivarius strain. Furthermore, the Panel notes that this analysis was not conducted in triplicate as per requirement.

An analysis was done to measure the concentration of silicone in L. acidophilus CNCM I‐3231 and B. animalis subsp. lactis CNCM I‐3993 lyophilisates (one batch each), manufactured with the addition of 0.1 g/L antifoaming to the fermentation media, of the same lyophilisates manufactured without the antifoaming and of L. salivarius CNCM I‐3233 lyophilisate. 28 The results showed silicone concentrations in the range of 22–33 mg/kg, with no differences between the different samples, indicating the complete removal of the antifoaming during the purification steps.

The FEEDAP Panel considers that the microbial contamination and the amounts of the detected impurities do not raise safety concerns.

Conditions of use

The additive is intended for use in feed for cats at a proposed minimum use level of 2 × 1011 CFU/kg complete feed either as a top‐dressing or into complementary feed. The applicant claims that the additive is not to be incorporated via premixture. 30

Safety

The species L. acidophilus, L. salivarius, L. paracasei, B. longum and B. animalis are considered by EFSA to be suitable for the qualified presumption of safety (QPS) approach to safety assessment (EFSA BIOHAZ Panel, 2026). This approach requires the identification of the strains to be conclusively established and evidence that the strains show absence of acquired genes coding for resistance to therapeutic antimicrobials. The identity of the five strains has been unambiguously established. For L. acidophilus CNCM I‐3231, L. salivarius CNCM I‐3233, L. paracasei CNCM I‐4901 and B. longum CNCM I‐3227, the assessment of antimicrobial resistance demonstrated compliance with the relevant criteria. Consequently, these strains fulfil the QPS requirements. In contrast, for B. animalis subsp. lactis CNCM I‐3993, the analysis of antimicrobial resistance genes revealed the presence of the acquired AMR gene tet(W) (FEEDAP Panel, 2025). The FEEDAP Panel therefore concludes that the presence of the tet(W) gene in B. animalis subsp. lactis CNCM I‐3993 constitutes a risk.

Following the provisions of Regulation (EC) No 429/2008, there is no requirement for the assessment of the safety of an additive for the consumers and the environment when the additive is intended to be used only in pets. However, the Panel notes that the use of B. animalis subsp. lactis CNCM I‐3993 represents a safety concern for the environment because it harbours an acquired antimicrobial resistance gene.

Safety for the target species

L. acidophilus CNCM I‐3231, L. salivarius CNCM I‐3233, L. paracasei CNCM I‐4901 and B. longum CNCM I‐3227 fulfil the QPS requirements and are considered safe for the target species.

A tolerance trial was conducted to evaluate the safety of the additive in cats when included in feed at the recommended use level (2.0 × 1011 CFU/kg) and at 10 times this level (2.0 × 1012 CFU/kg complete feed). 31 No adverse effects were observed on body weight, feed intake or the haematology and biochemistry parameters monitored. The animals tolerated the diets supplemented with the additive up to 2.0 × 1012 CFU/kg complete feed.

However, the presence of the acquired AMR gene in B. animalis subsp. lactis CNCM I‐3993 is considered a risk for the target species. Therefore, the additive is considered not safe for the target species.

Safety for the user

No studies were conducted to support the safety of the additive for the user. 32 The additive contains microorganisms and therefore, should be considered a skin and respiratory sensitiser. Based on the information present in the safety data sheet, the additive is also irritant to the skin/eyes. 33

The FEEDAP Panel concludes that the additive should be considered a skin and eye irritant, and a skin and respiratory sensitiser. The presence of the acquired AMR gene in B. animalis subsp. lactis CNCM I‐3993 is considered a risk. Exposure of users is considered a risk.

Efficacy

The applicant submitted three long‐term trials in cats to support the efficacy as a zootechnical additive. However, in one of the trials, 34 no information was provided regarding the faecal dry matter of the fresh faeces collected, which is considered the most relevant endpoint to assess the potential beneficial effects of the additive within the functional group ‘gut flora stabilisers’; therefore, this study was not considered further as evidence of the efficacy. In trials 1 35 and 2, 36 12 adult European short hair cats (7–9 years old; initial body weight: 3–6 kg; neutered or spayed) were randomly allocated to one of the two experimental groups based on sex, age and body weight. The trials followed a cross‐over design, each cat acting as its own control. The trials lasted 92 days and included three phases: phase I – half of the cats received a basal diet, whereas the other half received the same basal diet supplemented with the additive for 36 days; washout phase – all cats received the basal diet for ■■■■■ days; phase II – the cats previously receiving the basal diet were supplemented with the additive for 36 days and vice versa. In both trials, an additional monitoring time of ■■■■■ days was included, in which the animals of both groups were offered the same basal diet without supplementation (post‐experimental period). Cats were housed collectively in groups of six, 37 except for feeding and faecal collection, for which they were moved to individual cages. Cats received a commercial extruded feed either not supplemented (control) or top‐dressed with 0.4 g of FlorEquilibre® Chat to provide 2 × 1011 CFU/kg complete feed. It is stated that the animals consumed always the full test item. Rations were adjusted during adaptation to maintain weight stability.

Health status was monitored ■■■■■. The animals were weighed ■■■■■. Feed intake was recorded ■■■■■. Faecal samples were collected ■■■■■. At each collection time point, the faecal dry matter content was analysed, the faecal score was evaluated according to a five‐points scoring system, 38 and the faecal consistency measured by means of a penetrometer.

The experimental data were analysed with a linear mixed model, using the diet as fixed factor and the cat as a random factor. The cat was the experimental unit. The significance level was set at 0.10.

The health status of the animals was good throughout the trial. No differences were observed in the feed intake and body weight of the animals. During the treatment phase, the cats supplemented with FlorEquilibre® Chat showed higher faecal score in trial 1 and lower faecal consistency in trial 2 compared to the control (Table 4). No differences between groups were observed in the faecal score in trial 2 and in the faecal consistency in trial 1. No effects were observed on the faecal dry matter in any trial. The changes observed in the faecal score and consistency in these two trials are not considered an improvement of the faecal quality of the animals.

Due to the lack of adequate data, the Panel is not in a position to conclude on the efficacy of the additive when supplemented in the feed for cats at 2 × 1011 CFU/kg complete feed.

Post‐market monitoring

The FEEDAP Panel considers that there is no need for specific requirements for a post‐market monitoring plan other than those established in the Feed Hygiene Regulation 39 and Good Manufacturing Practice.

CONCLUSIONS

The strains L. acidophilus CNCM I‐3231, L. salivarius CNCM I‐3233, L. paracasei CNCM I‐4901 and B. longum CNCM I‐3227 fulfil the QPS requirements; therefore, are presumed safe for the target species and the environment. However, the presence of the acquired tet(W) gene in B. animalis subsp. lactis CNCM I‐3993 constitutes a risk for the target species, the users and the environment. Therefore, the FEEDAP Panel concludes that FlorEquilibre® Chat is considered not safe for the target species, users and the environment.

Regarding user safety, the additive should be considered an eye and skin irritant, and a skin and respiratory sensitiser. Exposure via any route is considered a risk.

The FEEDAP Panel cannot conclude on the efficacy of FlorEquilibre® Chat for cats.

Boronic acid affinity media conjugating with PEG enable precise pH-responsive HPLC separation of glycoproteins depending on differences of sugar chains
Authors: Keisuke Koda, Sayaka Konishi-Yamada, Takuya Kubo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBoronic acids (BAs) reversibly bind to diol structures in a pH-dependent manner and have attracted attention as a method for separating glycoproteins based on their sugar chains. However, commonly used phenylboronic acid reacts only under basic conditions, which can cause protein denaturation and aggregation. To address this issue, we focused on BPA, BBX, PBA-CF3, and PBA-F, which are expected to have lower pKa values due to their electron-withdrawing groups. In this study, we designed and synthesized a novel stationary phase by immobilizing these BA derivatives onto silica particles for HPLC separation of glycoproteins. First, polyethylene glycol (PEG) was introduced as a spacer. Its high hydrophilicity and excluded volume effect suppressed non-specific protein adsorption. At the same time, glycoproteins such as horseradish peroxidase and immunoglobulin were retained even under neutral pH conditions, likely due to complex formation with BA derivatives based on differences in sugar chains. Next, polyethylenimine (PEI) was introduced to increase the immobilization amount of BA derivatives. As a result, the binding affinity toward glycoproteins increased, and a wider range of glycoproteins were strongly retained. Finally, HPLC with a pH gradient was performed, and the elution order of glycoproteins changed depending on the type of BA derivative. This indicates that glycoprotein separation can be controlled by both the BA derivative used and the mobile phase pH.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-48059-4.Keywords: Boronic acids, Glycoproteins, HPLC, Complexation, ImmobilizationSubject terms: Biochemistry, Chemistry

Introduction

It has been estimated that 70% of the proteins existing in vivo are the glycoproteins on which sugar chains are attached during a process of post-translational modification1–3. The physiological properties of a glycoprotein mainly depend on the location where sugar chains are attached and what kind of types they are. Glycoproteins are involved in essential functions including roles in cell-cell signaling, the immune response, acting as receptors, and recognition of cells or molecules4–6. Sugar chains are associated with various diseases such as an autoimmune disease, Alzheimer, cancer, and a genetic disorder7–14. In recent years, analyses for sugar chains of glycoproteins have been extensively conducted as they can be therapeutic targets and disease biomarkers15–22.

Nowadays, high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) has been the most employed method to analyze sugar chains of glycoproteins23–26. The conventional methods include ion exchange chromatography (IEX), hydrophobic interaction chromatography (HIC), and lectin affinity chromatography (LAC). IEX and HIC cannot be applied to separation recognizing sugar chains due to their characteristic relying on differences in charge properties and hydrophobicity. Regarding LAC, while specificity toward sugar chains is high, there are drawbacks such as costly and low stability of lectins consisting of proteins toward pH and temperature27–29.

To overcome the challenge above, developing a new method to selectively separate glycoproteins based on sugar chains is required. We attempted to employ boronic acids (BAs) being chemically stable and inexpensive. They can form reversible bonds with the compounds having diol structures only depending on difference in pH. When pH is higher than pKa of BAs, cyclic esters are formed; on the contrary, when pH is lower than pKa, they are dissociated. A number of studies have developed the sensors and adsorbents for sugars using BAs because of the reason above30,31. These studies also impose the possibility that BAs can selectively separate glycoproteins. In general, phenylboronic acids are broadly utilized. Their pKa is around 8.8, which needs to be adjusted to basic pH32 to form a complexation with glycoproteins. However, the glycoproteins cannot be stable under such the condition. In the case biological samples such as blood, tears, urine, and saliva, when pH is altered to basic, the proteins may denature or aggregate as their optimum pH is in the range of 4.5 to 8.032,33. Therefore, to selectively separate glycoproteins in biological samples using BAs and a sugar chain, a new separation medium using BA derivatives other than phenylboronic acids should preferably be developed.

In this study, we focus on the BA derivatives because they have strong electron-withdrawing groups, which leads to low pKa. We then introduced four types of BA derivatives, 5-boronopicolinic acid (BPA), 5-carboxybenzoboroxole (BBX), 4-borono-2-(trifloromethyl)benzoic acid (PBA-CF3), 4-borono-2-fluorobenzoic acid (PBA-F), to silica particles. Our previous study has revealed that a polyethylene glycol (PEG), its features include high flexibility and excellent water solubility, can inhibit non-specific adsorption of proteins by introducing PEGs between BA derivatives and a substrate as a spacer34,35. Hence, an exclusion effect prevented the proteins from accessing the surface of the silica particles.

Lastly, we attempted to improve the stationary phase by increasing the amount of immobilized BA derivatives. It was increased by adding linkers; branched macromolecules were employed between the end of a PEG and BA derivatives instead of ethylenediamine. We adopted polyethyleneimine (PEI), the branched macromolecule having amino groups in its structure, as an additional linker. PEI also involves the features such as flexible structures and inexpensive. The prepared stationary phases were chemically characterized, and retention behaviors of glycoproteins were compared in HPLC.

Results and discussion

Optimization of preparation conditions of a stationary phase

Scheme S1 shows the color reaction with ninhydrin. Ninhydrin produced Ruhemann’s purple by reacting with a primary amine. When unreacted amino groups remain, the particles and the solution that both reacted change their colors to purple. For the particles before and after the reaction, we calculated the reaction rate through the local maximum value of UV absorption derived from Ruhemann’s purple in the solution after the color reaction with ninhydrin. Figure S1 shows the result measuring absorbance before and after the color reaction with ninhydrin of the particles. UV adsorption derived from Ruhemann’s purple was not observed with SiO2-PEG-1, -2, and − 3, while the color was observed with SiO2-NH2. This indicates that modifying the PEG derivative to SiO2-NH2 one time adequately introduced PEGs (Figure S2).

Then, qualitive evaluation of boronic acids with alizarin color reaction was carried out. Alizarin is a derivative of catechol involving the 1,2-diol structure, showing the red color (Scheme S2). It is known that when alizarin forms complexation with a BA, the color changes from red to orange. We anticipated that this change in color can be applied to qualitative evaluation for BA immobilization. Toward the particles before and after each reaction, we visually observed the change of colors by conducting the color reaction with alizarin. The result is shown in Figure S3, confirming that reactions progressed well.

Figure 2 shows the results of HPLC using catechol and hydroquinone as analytes: the former involves the 1,2-diol structure enabling to form complexation with BA, and the latter involves the 1,4-diol structure not enabling to form complexation with BA. Catechol was retained to all columns under the neutral condition. In contrast, hydroquinone completely eluted under any conditions. Forming complexation with BA derivatives may have caused retention of catechol so that it can conclude that BA derivatives were introduced to the stationary phase.

The results of HPLC using catechol and hydroquinone. HPLC condition, column, SiO2-PEG-X packed column (5.0 cm ×2.0 mm I.D.); mobile phase, 20 mM phosphate buffer; detection, UV (275 nm); flow rate, 0.2 mL/min.

HPLC measurement of glycoproteins

HPLC was implemented using horseradish peroxidase (HRP), a glycoprotein having sugar chains, as an analyte and a SiO2-PEG-BPA packed column. Figure S4 shows the result indicating that HRP was not retained at all even though the BA derivatives were introduced to the stationary phase. Our previous study has also showed that retention strength against HRP was not enough with the packing materials that BPA was directly introduced onto PEG; the number of BPA and PEG were one each34. That is, the problem was lack of the amount of introduced BPA. To address this, we anticipated that grafting density of BPA needed to be improved. Therefore, in the next section, we evaluated the packing materials that grafting density of BA derivatives was enhanced by introducing PEI. The color reaction with ninhydrin and the one with alizarin were conducted toward SiO2-PEG-PEI and SiO2-PEG-PEI-BPA, respectively. The results are shown in Fig. 3. Since colors changed, the solution turned purple to colorless and red to orange with the ninhydrin reaction and with the alizarin reaction, respectively, it can be concluded that terminal amine of PEI and BA derivatives reacted, and BA derivatives were successfully modified to the stationary phases.

The result of the color reaction with ninhydrin (a) and with alizarine (b).

Evaluation of retention behavior of glycoproteins

Figure 4 shows the result of HPLC measurement for the glycoproteins and the non-glycosylated proteins using the SiO2-PEG-PEI-BPA column. A glycoprotein, HRP, retained with this column, while it did not retain with the SiO2-PEG-BPA column as shown in Figure S4. This suggests that the modification amount of BA in the stationary phase increased. In addition, HRP, IgG, ASFe, and LF were adsorbed to the stationary phase, and they did not elute; on the contrary, OVA, TRF, and fetuin did not retain the same as a non-glycosylated protein, BSA. It may explain that specificity appeared on the retention depending on the types of the glycoproteins.

According to this result, we expect that separation depending on sugar chains of glycoproteins may be possible by selecting an optimized column and inhibiting pH gradients. To achieve this expectation, firstly the mixture of HRP and OVA was separated following the results of Table 1. Briefly, SiO2-PEG600-PEI1800-BPA packed column was applied for the separation using phosphate buffer of pH 7 and pH 2.5. The separation on HPLC is shown in Fig. 5. Conclusion

We evaluated the preparation conditions of the column immobilized with BA derivatives and its retention characteristics. PEG immobilization was conducted with amino group-terminated silica particles and PEG derivatives. Consequently, PEG was appropriately immobilized to the silica particles. Then the stationary phase, BPA was introduced to the silica particles, showed that catechol involving the 1,2-diol structure strongly retained under the neutral condition in HPLC, meaning that catechol interacted with the BPA on the surface of the stationary phase. However, HRP immediately eluted under the same condition in the case of catechol. This is because the amount of BPA was not enough to retain HRP.

To increase the modification amount of BA derivatives, PEI was introduced to the stationary phase. As a result, some glycoproteins retained, while the others were eluted, which occurred depending on the types of the glycoproteins. This implied the possibility that glycoproteins can be separated based on the sugar chains. Furthermore, we revealed that the retention behavior of glycoproteins can be varied by using different types of BA derivatives. Considering these findings, glycoproteins may more precisely be further separated combining different types of BA derivatives. To understand it deeply, further measurement was conducted. First, HPLC using mobile phases with various pH showed that the retention behavior changed depending on the types of glycoproteins. Next, HPLC using pH gradient to measure the mixture of glycoproteins showed that elution orders changed depending on the BA derivatives. Taken together, glycoproteins can be more specifically separated based on the sugar chains. As the future prospect, the results in this study will lead to an accurate separation of glycoproteins by optimizing the polymer chain of the spacer and BAs, will be expected to contribute to the advancement of antibody drug purification based on glycan structures.

Methods

Measurement of pKa of BA derivatives

0.1 mM 3-pyridylboronic acid was added in 0.1 M (NaCl) with pH of 2.3 to 5.5 and absorbance was measured at 270 mn. 1 mM 2-(hydroxymethyl)phenylboronic acid cyclic monoester was added in 100 mM phosphate buffer with pH of 5.5 to 9.5 and absorbance was measured at 265 nm. 1 mM 3-(trifluoromethyl)phenylboronic acid was added in 100 mM phosphate buffer with pH of 5.5 to 9.8 and absorbance was measured at 226 nm. 1 mM 3-fluorophenylboronic acid was added in 100 mM phosphate buffer with pH of 5.5 to 10.0 and absorbance was measured at 262 nm.

Optimization of synthesis conditions of silica particles immobilized with PEG (SiO2-PEG)

Amino group-terminated silica particles (SiO2-NH2) of 250 mg, poly(ethylene glycol) bis(carboxymethyl) ether (Mn = 600) of 3.0 g (5.0 mmol), and DMTMM of 138.5 mg (0.50 mmol) were added in DMF of 50 mL, mixed for 24 h at room temperature. Then, to identify progress of reaction, we conducted the procedure above once, twice, and three times to synthesize SiO2-PEG-1, SiO2-PEG-2, and SiO2-PEG-3, respectively. 0.5% ninhydrin of 1 mL was added to SiO2-NH2, SiO2-PEG-1, -2, and − 3 of 5 mg, and they were mixed and heated at 80 °C for 10 min using a plastic tube. After implementing centrifugation at 12,000 rpm for 5 min, absorbance of supernatant was measured using UV-Vis spectrophotometer.

Fabrication of a stationary phase immobilized with BA derivatives

As shown in Scheme 1, SiO2-NH2 of 250 mg, poly(ethylene glycol) bis(carboxymethyl) ether (Mn = 600) of 3000 mg (5.0 mmol), and DMTMM of 138.5 mg (0.50 mmol) were added to DMF of 50 mL, mixed for 24 h at room temperature. After the reaction, the particle was washed with DMF, MeOH, and H2O, dried under reduced pressure at room temperature (SiO2-PEG). Then ethylenediamine (EDA) of 300 mg (5.0 mmol), DMTMM of 138.5 mg (0.50 mmol), and EtOH of 50 mL were added to the SiO2-PEG, and it was mixed for 24 h at room temperature. After the reaction, the particles were washed with EtOH and H2O, dried under reduced pressure at room temperature (SiO2-PEG-EDA). Then BA derivatives, BPA of 41.7 mg (0.25 mmol), BBX of 44.5 mg (0.25 mmol), PBA-CF3 of 58.5 mg (0.25 mmol), or PBA-F of 46.0 mg (0.25 mmol), and DMTMM of 104 mg (0.375 mmol) with DMSO of 25 mL were added to the SiO2-PEG-EDA, and they were mixed for 24 h at room temperature. After the reactions, the particles were washed with DMSO, MeOH, and H2O, dried under reduced pressure at room temperature. Finally, we obtained SiO2-PEG-BPA, SiO2-PEG-BBX, SiO2-PEG-PBA-CF3, and SiO2-PEG-PBA-F.

Fabrication method of SiO2-PEG-BPA.

Fabrication of a stationary phase introduced PEI (SiO2-PEG-PEI-X)

SiO2-NH2 of 250 mg, poly(ethylene glycol) bis(carboxymethyl) ether (Mn = 600) of 3.0 g (5.0 mmol), and DMTMM of 138.5 mg (0.5 mmol) were added to DMF of 50 mL, and it was mixed for 24 h at room temperature. The particles after the reaction were washed with DMF, MeOH, and H2O, dried under reduced pressure at room temperature.

Our previous study regarding the molecular amount of PEI revealed that when the volume of PEI gets larger against the chain length, PEIs intertwine with one another. Then effect inhibiting protein adsorption by PEG decrease, and any proteins are non-specifically adsorbed34. Therefore, PEI (Mw=1800) was utilized for synthesis of the stationary phase in this study.

Then PEI (Mw = 1800) of 1.8 g (1.0 mmol), DMTMM of 138.5 mg, (0.5 mmol), EtOH of 50 mL were added to the dried particles (SiO2-PEG), and they were mixed for 24 h at room temperature. The particles after the reaction were washed with EtOH and H2O, dried under reduced pressure at room temperature. Then, BPA of 41.7 mg (0.25 mmol), BBX of 44.5 mg (0.25 mmol), PBA-CF3 of 58.5 mg (0.25 mmol), or PBA-F of 46.0 mg (0.25 mmol) and DMTMM of 104 mg (0.375 mmol) and DMSO of 25 mL were added to the dried particles (SiO2-PEG-PEI), they were mixed for 24 h at room temperature. The particles after the reaction were washed with DMSO, MeOH, and H2O, dried under reduced pressure. Finally, we obtained SiO2PEG-PEI-BPA, SiO2-PEG-PEI-BBX, SiO2-PEG-PEI-PBA-CF3, and SiO2-PEG-PEI-PBA-F (shown in Scheme 2).

Fabrication method of SiO2-PEG-PEI-BPA.

Evaluation of terminal groups using ninhydrin

0.5% ninhydrin solution of 1 mL was added to each particle of 5 mg, and they were heated and mixed for 10 min at 80 °C using a plastic tube. After centrifugation for 5 min at 12,000 rpm, we visually observed the color reaction in which the supernatant and the sunk particle changed their colors based on the reaction of ninhydrin and an NH2 group.

Evaluation of terminal groups using alizarin

0.1 mg/mL alizarin in 20 mM phosphate buffer with pH 7.5 was added to each particle of 5 mg, and they were mixed for 10 min at room temperature using a plastic tube. We visually observed that the color of each particle changed based on the reaction of alizarin and a BA.

HPLC evaluation

The stationary phases were packed to the HPLC columns to evaluate the retention behavior of catechol, hydroquinone, and glycoproteins using 20 mM phosphate buffer (pH2.5, 3.5, 4.5, 5.5, 6.5, 7.5) as a mobile phase.

The retention behavior was evaluated using horseradish peroxidase (HRP), ovalbumin (OVA), transferrin (TRF), Immunoglobulin G (IgG), asialofetuin (ASFe), and fetuin, lactoferrin (LF) as glycoproteins and bovine serum albumin (BSA) as a non-glycosylated protein. For separation of glycol proteins, SiO2-PEG-PEI-BPA, SiO2-PEG-PEI-BBX, SiO2-PEG-PEI-PBA-CF3, SiO2-PEG-PEI-PBA-F-packed column (5.0 cm × 2.0 mm I.D.) were utilized.

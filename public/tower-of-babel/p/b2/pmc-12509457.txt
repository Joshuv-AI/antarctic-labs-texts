Impact of Human Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor 2 in Patients With Metastatic Colorectal Cancer Treated With Chemotherapy Plus Bevacizumab or Anti-EGFRs: Exploratory Analysis of Eight Randomized Trials
Authors: Marco Maria Germani, Beatrice Borelli, Tadayoshi Hashimoto, Yoshiaki Nakamura, Simone Oldani, Francesca Battaglin, Francesca Bergamo, Lisa Salvatore, Arndt Stahler, Carlotta Antoniotti, Kohei Shitara, Alan Venook, Eiji Oki, Kei Muro, Clara Ugolini, Junpei Soeda, Sara Lonardi, Filippo Pietrantonio, Heinz-Josef Lenz, Dominik Paul Modest, Takayuki Yoshino, Chiara Cremolini
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPURPOSEHuman epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) amplification/overexpression (HER2-pos) is detected in 5% of RAS/BRAF wild-type metastatic colorectal cancers (mCRCs). Its prognostic/predictive role in terms of benefit from anti-EGFR/bevacizumab (bev) is debated. Similarly, the role of activating HER2 mutations (mut) is unclear.METHODSWe collected individual data of 1,604 patients with proficient mismatch repair (pMMR)/microsatellite stable (MSS) RAS/BRAF wild-type untreated mCRC with HER2 amplification/expression status available enrolled in eight randomized clinical trials (RCT; TRIBE2, TRIPLETE, VALENTINO, ATEZOTRIBE, PANDA, PANAMA, PARADIGM, and CALGB/SWOG80405). Objective response rate (ORR), progression-free survival (PFS), and overall survival (OS) were assessed with respect to HER2 amplification/expression and HER2 mutational status and according to biologics (anti-EGFR/bev).RESULTSPatients with HER2-pos were 81 (5%). HER2-pos patients experienced shorter PFS (median PFS [mPFS]: 9.8 v 12.2 months, hazard ratio [HR], 1.31, P = .02) and OS (median OS [mOS]: 28.0 v 34.9 months, HR, 1.37, P = .01), also after adjustment for covariates (PadjPFS = .02, PadjOS = .048). ORR was similar between HER2-pos and HER2-negative (HER2-neg) tumors (75% v 72%, odds ratio [OR], 1.21, P = .47). We found no interaction between HER2 amplification/expression status and biologics' effect in terms of PFS (Pint = .76), OS (Pint = .76), and ORR (Pint = .64). In left-sided HER2-pos tumors, outcomes were similar with chemotherapy plus bev/anti-EGFRs in terms of PFS (9.8 v 9.3 months, HR, 0.73, P = .29), OS (29.8 v 28.0 months, HR, 1.29, P = .40), and ORR (59% v 79%, OR, 0.39, P = .10). HER2-mutant tumors (2% of patients with HER2-neg tumors) showed shorter OS than HER2 wild-type ones (mOS: 23.7 v 34.4 months, HR, 1.56, P = .04) with no differential effect of biologics (PintORR = .81; PintPFS = .95; PintOS = .92).CONCLUSIONTo our knowledge, this is the largest analysis of HER2 status in patients with untreated mCRC enrolled in RCT. Waiting for targeted approaches, HER2-pos and mut do not predict benefit from bev/anti-EGFRs and should be regarded as negative prognostic factors in pMMR/MSS RAS/BRAF wild-type mCRC.

INTRODUCTION

Human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) is a transmembrane glycoprotein with tyrosine kinase activity and the only member of the EGFR family not binding to endogenous ligands.1,2 The overexpression of HER2 protein and/or the amplification of its encoding gene, which traditionally defines HER2 positivity, promotes uncontrolled cell survival and proliferation through activation of several downstream pathways.1,2 In metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC), HER2-pos has been reported in 3%-5% of diagnoses and is more frequent in RAS wild-type and left-sided tumors.3,4

In preclinical models, HER2-pos–dependent activation of ERK1/2 pathway bypasses upstream EGFR inhibition, suggesting a role as a biomarker of aggressiveness and resistance to anti-EGFRs (cetuximab/panitumumab).5-7 However, whether HER2-pos is prognostic and/or predictive of benefit from anti-EGFRs or the antivascular endothelial growth factor bevacizumab (bev) remains unclear. In fact, inconsistent results were reported in heterogeneous retrospective series, where poorer outcomes with anti-EGFRs were mostly demonstrated in later lines without a group of untreated patients that could allow us to actually discriminate the prognostic versus predictive power of this alteration.7-13 Therefore, HER2 expression/amplification profiling does not currently dictate the choice of the targeted agent to be combined with chemotherapy in the first-line treatment of patients with proficient mismatch repair (pMMR)/microsatellite stable (MSS) RAS/BRAF wild-type mCRC.14-16

HER2-activating mutations (HER2 mut) elicit the same biological effect of HER2-pos, including a possible link to anti-EGFR resistance, unleashing the same downstream pathways.1,2,17 However, because of their lower frequency (2%-3% of mCRC diagnoses) and co-occurrence with HER2-pos, understanding their relevance in clinical practice has been even more challenging and only data from small cohorts are available so far.18

Drawing from these considerations, we investigated the prognostic and predictive role of HER2-pos and HER2 mut in an international pooled analysis of individual clinical data from a large and homogeneous cohort of mCRC with previously untreated pMMR/MSS and RAS/BRAF wild-type patients enrolled in eight phase II/III trials assessing the efficacy of chemotherapy combinations plus anti-EGFRs or bev.19-26

METHODS

Study Population

We collected individual patient data from TRIBE2, ATEZOTRIBE, TRIPLETE, VALENTINO, PANDA, CALGB/SWOG 80405, PARADIGM, and PANAMA trials, enrolling patients with previously untreated mCRC (Appendix Table A1, online only).19-26 All studies were conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and were approved by an ethics committee. All patients signed an informed consent before enrollment. Only patients with proficient pMMR/MSS and RAS/BRAF wild-type tumors and with HER2 expression status available were included. MMR/microsatellite (MSI) status was not available only for patients from PANAMA included in this analysis (n = 275). Patients who received atezolizumab in the ATEZOTRIBE trial, the combination of cetuximab and bev in the CALGB/SWOG 80405 trial, and monochemotherapy with fluorouracil + leucovorin + panitumumab in the PANDA trial were excluded.

HER2 Expression and Mutation Profiling

Immunohistochemistry (IHC), in situ hybridization (ISH), and next-generation sequencing (NGS) on tumor tissue or circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) were used for HER2 expression profiling. Where tissue NGS was used (TRIBE2, Caris MI TumorSeek27; ATEZOTRIBE, PANDA, and CALGB/SWOG 80405, FoundationOne CDx28; TRIPLETE, FoundationOne CDx and Oncomine Focus Assay29), a gene copy number (GCN) cut-off of ≥6 was used to differentiate HER2-pos/HER2-neg tumors. In PARADIGM, NGS on ctDNA was performed with a custom panel (PlasmaSELECT-R 91, Personal Genome Diagnostics, Baltimore, MD) and the GCN threshold for HER2-pos was set at 1.25.30 Where IHC was used, HER2-pos tumors were those with high (3+) staining intensity or ISH-positive (HER2/CEP17 ≥2) if IHC-moderate (2+) staining intensity was detected (ATEZOTRIBE and TRIPLETE, complementary to tissue-based NGS). In PANAMA, tumors with weak (1+) or moderate (2+) staining were excluded owing to the lack of confirmation of HER2 expression with ISH.31 In VALENTINO, HER2 was called positive if the HER2/CEP17 ratio was ≥2 or the average number of HER2 signals per tumor cell nucleus was ≥6.32 When more than one technique for HER2-pos detection was used (ATEZOTRIBE and TRIPLETE), HER2-pos tumors were those with at least one approach (IHC or NGS) showing HER2 positivity. HER2 mutations were profiled using tissue or ctDNA NGS. Tumors with HER2 mut were those with activating function according to previously published data33-35 and classified as pathogenic by the Tumor Cancer Genome Atlas data set.36

Assessment of End Points and Statistics

The association between HER2 expression/mutational status and baseline characteristics was assessed using the chi-square and Fisher's exact test, when appropriate. With respect to HER2 mutational status, all analyses were restricted to HER2-neg tumors to prevent any confounding effect from the co-presence of HER2 positivity. The prognostic and predictive impact of these biomarkers with respect to biologics (bev/anti-EGFRs) was assessed in terms of objective response rate (ORR), progression-free survival (PFS), and overall survival (OS). ORR was defined as the percentage of patients relative to the total enrolled population achieving an objective response according to RECIST 1.1 criteria. ORRs were compared with the chi-square and Fisher's exact test, when appropriate. Odds ratio (OR) and 95% CI were provided. PFS and OS were defined as the time from enrollment to the first evidence of objective disease progression according to RECIST 1.1 criteria or death, whichever occurred first, and as the time from enrollment to death from any cause, respectively. PFS and OS curves were plotted using the Kaplan-Meier method and compared with the log-rank test. Hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% CI were estimated using a Cox proportional hazard model. The prognostic impact on PFS and OS of the following variables was investigated: age, sex, primary tumor location, primary tumor resection, administration of adjuvant treatment, the presence of synchronous metastases, the presence of one or more organs involved by metastases, the presence of peritoneal metastases, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Status 0 or ≥1, first-line regimen administered, and trial ID. Variables significantly associated with outcomes (P < .10) were included in a multivariate Cox proportional hazard model. We used the interaction test to determine the consistency of the effect of HER2 expression/mutational status according to biologics (bev/anti-EGFRs) and primary tumor location (left or rectum/right). We did not use a meta-analytic approach because a direct comparison between anti-EGFRs and bev was performed only in PARADIGM and CALGB/SWOG80405 trials. Unless elsewhere specified, statistical significance was set at two-sided P < .05. All analyses were performed using R version 4.4.2.

RESULTS

HER2 Assessment and Baseline Characteristics

Overall, 1,604 of 4,091 patients randomly assigned were eligible for the analysis (Fig 1). pMMR/MSS status was confirmed in 1,333 tumors (83%) and not available in 271 (17%) from the PANAMA trial. Eighty-one (5%) patients had HER2-pos tumors. HER2 expression/amplification status was determined in 1,035 (65%) patients using NGS, 386 cases (24%) using IHC, and 183 (11%) patients using ISH. Despite some heterogeneity in the frequency of HER2-pos tumors across trials (P = .04), the percentage of HER2-pos tumors was similar across the methodologies adopted (6% using NGS, 5% using ISH, 4% using IHC; P = .46). HER2-pos was associated with higher frequency of multiorgan dissemination (60% v 45%, P = .011) and activating HER2 mutations (7% v 2%, P = .007; Table 1 and Appendix Table A2).

Prognostic Relevance of HER2 Amplification/Overexpression

Patients with HER2-pos tumors had similar ORR (75% v 72%, OR, 1.21 [95% CI, 0.73 to 2.07]; P = .47) but shorter PFS (median: 9.8 v 12.2 months, HR, 1.31 [95% CI, 1.04 to 1.66]; P = .023) and OS (median: 28.0 v 34.9 months, HR, 1.37 [95% CI, 1.08 to 1.74]; P = .01; Figs 2A-2C), as compared with HER2-neg ones. The negative impact of HER2-pos on the outcome was confirmed after adjustment for other confounders (HR for PFS, 1.32 [95% CI, 1.04 to 1.67]; P = .022; HR for OS, 1.28 [95% CI, 1.00 to 1.65]; P = .048) in multivariate Cox regressions. To mitigate the potential heterogeneity determined by the subgroup of patients treated with the triplet, the same analyses were conducted among patients treated with doublets only (n = 1,465), with consistent results (PORR = .76; PPFS = .051; POS = .009; Appendix Figs A1A-A1C), also after adjustment for PFS (HR, 1.30 [95% CI, 1.02 to 1.67]; P = .038) and OS (HR, 1.29 [95% CI, 1.00 to 1.67]; P = .049). Interestingly, a P value for interaction for PFS of 0.06 was found between HER2 status and the primary tumor location, with HER2-pos right-sided tumors unexpectedly associated with a trend toward better outcomes compared with left-sided (12.3 v 9.4 months, HR, 0.53 [95% CI, 0.27 to 1.04]; P = .06), whereas HER2-neg right-sided tumors showed, as expected, worse outcomes (11.4 v 12.2 months, HR, 1.18 [95% CI, 1.03 to 1.36]; P = .02). Consistent results were found in terms of OS and ORR, with worse outcomes in right-sided tumors (POS < .0001, PORR < .0001) in the HER2-neg group with no difference in the HER2-pos group (POS = .90, PORR = .72), but with no significant interaction (PintOS = .40, PintORR = .17; Appendix Figs A2A-A2C).

Exploring HER2 Amplification/Overexpression as the Predictive Biomarker of Benefit From Biologics

No interaction between HER2 status and targeted agents' effect, when combined to first-line chemotherapy, was reported in terms of ORR (Pint = .64), PFS (Pint = .76), and OS (Pint = .76). In particular, among HER2-pos tumors, no differences between bev- or anti–EGFR-based regimens were observed in terms of ORR (65% v 79%, OR, 0.66 [95% CI, 0.17 to 1.71]; P = .22), PFS (9.8 v 10.1 months, HR, 0.81 [95% CI, 0.48 to 1.39]; P = .44), and OS (32.9 v 28.0 months, HR, 1.27 [95% CI, 0.73 to 2.22]; P = .40; Figs 3A-3C). Consistent results were reported in the subgroup of patients with left-sided (n = 1,315; PORR = .10, PPFS = .29, POS = .40) and right-sided tumors (n = 283; PORR = 1.00, PPFS = .90, POS = .91; Table 2). An exploratory analysis where only tumors with potentially higher HER2-dependency (HER2 3+/ISH+ with HER2/CEP ratio >5/NGS GCN > 6 in tissue or ctDNA) were considered as HER2-pos (N = 54) did not show any differential effect of bev versus anti-EGFRs (Appendix Figs A3A-A3C).

Exploring Activating HER2 Mutations as Prognostic and Predictive Biomarkers of Benefit From Biologics

The HER2 mutational profile was available in 1,489 (93%) of 1,604 patients with available HER2-pos status. To prevent any confounding effect because of tumors with concomitant HER2-pos and HER2 mut, we assessed the impact of HER2 mut in the HER2-neg cohort only (n = 1,408), where 27 (2%) HER2 mut tumors were identified (Appendix Table A3). HER2 mut tumors were associated with a significantly higher frequency of multiorgan dissemination (73% v 45%, P = .004) and a numerically higher prevalence of right-sidedness (33% v 18%, P = .073). Similar to HER2 expression, the frequency of HER2 mut was not completely homogeneous across trials, though without reaching statistical significance (P = .052; Appendix Table A4). As compared with HER2 wild-type tumors, HER2 mut showed similar ORR (63% v 72%, OR, 0.67 [95% CI, 0.31 to 1.55]; P = .33), numerically shorter PFS (median: 9.4 v 12.1 months, HR, 1.36 [95% CI, 0.90 to 2.06]; P = .14), and significantly shorter OS (median: 23.7 v 34.4 months, HR, 1.56 [95% CI, 1.02 to 2.38]; P = .04; Figs 4A-4C), reaching marginal significance in multivariate analysis after adjustment for covariates (HR, 1.53 [95% CI, 0.98 to 2.38]; P = .059). No differential effect according to biologics in terms of ORR (Pint = .81), PFS (Pint = .95), and OS (Pint = .92) was evident (Figs 5A-5C).

DISCUSSION

Negative selection, and more recently hyperselection, has broadly dominated the therapeutic landscape of mCRC, with roughly half of patients with mCRC excluded from anti-EGFRs because of RAS or BRAF mutations,14 and the search for additional predictors of resistance able to further optimize the efficacy of these drugs. Among them, whether HER2-pos may act as a negative predictor of response to anti-EGFRs was addressed in some studies,7-12 solicited by convincing preclinical data, but the quality of evidence provided in the clinical setting and especially in the first-line treatment of mCRC where biological agents are combined with chemotherapy has not been deemed of sufficient quality to clearly recommend this molecular feature as a driver for the choice of up-front anti-EGFR or bev-based therapies.1 As a consequence, heterogeneous recommendations about HER2 testing are provided by international guidelines. While the National Comprehensive Cancer Network recommends HER2 expression/amplification testing at the time of mCRC diagnosis, European Society for Medical Oncology guidelines postpone the potential need for this information after the first occurrence of disease progression.14-16,37

Our findings show that HER2-pos is not a predictive biomarker of benefit from up-front chemotherapy plus bev or anti-EGFRs, regardless of tumor sidedness. This affects the perception of HER2-pos as a potential driver of the therapeutic decision in left-sided pMMR RAS/BRAF wild-type mCRC, where the current standard of care consists of doublets plus anti-EGFRs. Instead, we showed that HER2-pos is an independent negative prognostic factor of outcome. Though in line with a real-world data set including 528 left-sided pMMR RAS/BRAF wild-type tumors (nHER2-pos = 30), showing shorter PFS in HER2-pos and no predictive effect on the use of bev or anti-EGFRs with chemotherapy doublets,9 we do not confirm findings from the two largest retrospective analyses by Raghav et al (nHER2-pos = 28, no RAS/BRAF mutations) and Sartore-Bianchi et al (nHER2-pos = 100, no KRAS exon 2 mutations), where, however, mostly pretreated patients were included.7,8 Similarly, in such a chemorefractory scenario, the prognostic impact of HER2-pos could have been masked by the unintentional exclusion of patients with a more aggressive disease, which perished before receiving any treatment after the first line.

We also speculate that HER2-pos does not rule out the presence of a concomitant dependency from EGFR signaling, which may be in unclear relationship with the grade of HER2 expression. As an example, the randomized SWOG S1613 trial demonstrated that the extent of HER2 amplification, assessed by ISH or NGS, as an indirect measure of HER2 dependency, could be predictive of benefit to dual HER2 blockade (HER2/CEP ratio >5 or NGS GCN ≥20) versus irinotecan plus cetuximab (HER2/CEP ratio ≤5 or NGS GCN <20) in the second or third line.38 However, when we restricted HER2-pos tumors to those with presumed higher HER2 dependency in our data set, which corresponded to HER2 3+ expression in IHC, HER2/CEP ratio >5 in ISH, and GCN >6 in tissue or ctDNA NGS, we still did not find any interaction effect with the efficacy of bev or anti-EGFRs. Translational analyses of the CALGB/SWOG 80405 trial showed that HER2-neg tumors, according to NGS, were paradoxically associated with shorter PFS and OS from first-line anti–EGFR-based regimens in the case of concomitant lower expression of HER2 gene at a transcriptional level. No interaction with biologics was demonstrated either in HER2-neg/HER2 expression–high and HER2-pos tumors.39 Taken together, these findings suggest that the interplay between HER2 and EGFR axes is more complex than expected and that the grade of EGFR dependency cannot be recapitulated as a simple inverse relationship between the two biomarkers as assessed by methodologies routinely used in clinical practice.

Assuming that activating HER2 mutations elicit a downstream biological effect resembling that of HER2-pos,1,2,18 our findings, showing their negative prognostic impact among patients with pMMR/MSS HER2-neg RAS/BRAF wild-type tumors, with a borderline significant result also after adjustment for covariates, are not surprising though certainly novel. Considering the widespread use of NGS panels, including HER2 mutational profiling in clinical practice, these data are useful to inform physicians about the clinical use of this finding and the current lack of evidence to use this piece of information in the choice of the up-front therapy for patients with mCRC.

The potential interaction between tumor sidedness and HER2 expression, with unexpectedly better outcomes in right- rather than left-sided tumors in the HER2-pos subgroup, is not obvious from a biological perspective but seems to be consistent with other studies in mCRC, elucidating that the meaning of an individual biomarker may differ according to environmental conditions.25,40,41 Because HER2 is an orphan receptor and its downstream effect cannot be recapitulated according to a simple ligand/receptor interaction,1 it is possible that other molecular partners may come into play to modulate the negative prognostic effect associated with HER2-pos.42

The methodological strength of our analysis lies in our opinion in the number of patients included (n = 1,604), coming from randomized clinical trials conducted across Europe, United States, and Asia, in a quite narrow timeframe and with quite homogeneous inclusion criteria. We also admit some clear limitations. First, we pooled together trials investigating different intensities of chemotherapy, from triplet regimens (ie, ATEZOTRIBE, TRIBE2, TRIPLETE)19-21 to the low-dose doublet plus an anti-EGFR in patients 70 years or older (PANDA).23 However, embracing a wide range of patients of different age groups and with baseline clinical conditions makes our study population more similar to a real-world scenario. At the same time, these heterogeneous features—that is, more patients fit for triplet in the bev group and shorter duration of induction in one anti–EGFR-based trial (PANAMA)26—may explain why, although all efficacy data according to primary tumor location were consistent with expected findings, the OS difference in favor of anti-EGFRs versus bev in RAS/BRAF wild-type and HER2-neg tumors (roughly 3 months) did not achieve statistical significance. Second, since we conducted our analysis in the most clinically relevant scenario (ie, patients with pMMR RAS/BRAF wild-type tumors), we likely introduced a selection bias and the initial population of 4,091 was shrunk to 1,604 patients. Nonetheless, approaches for HER2 profiling were heterogeneous (IHC, ISH, NGS in tissue and liquid biopsy with different panels), however, resulting in a percentage of HER2-pos (5%) in line with the literature.1,4 However, the heterogeneity of adopted techniques may also somehow limit the interpretation of our exploratory analysis on patients with assumed higher HER2 dependency, where the complementary assessment of HER2 status with ISH and/or IHC would have been straightforward. In addition, we assumed higher HER2 dependency using a different GCN cutoff (≥6) compared with SWOG S1613 (≥20), where HER2 GCN was assessed using tissue NGS. Because we included in our series also patients profiled using ctDNA NGS, which adopted a lower threshold for HER2-pos, as compared with tissue NGS (≥1.25),30 it sounded reasonable to use a standardized cutoff HER2-pos, at least validated in tissue samples (≥6), rather than an arbitrary threshold that could hardly match two different approaches (tissue and ctDNA) for GCN assessment. Third, the cohort of patients from PANAMA (N = 275) was not profiled for MSI status. However, since all tumors were RAS/BRAF wild-type, and mainly from the left colon, while deficient MMR (dMMR)/MSI instability (MSI-H) is more frequent in RAS/BRAF mutant tumors originating from the right colon, it is reasonable to expect an incidence of dMMR/MSI-H tumors in PANAMA far lower than the 5% expected in the overall diagnoses of mCRC,14 unlikely affecting our results. Fourth, some subgroups were under-represented, especially right-sided tumors with HER2 positivity (n = 12) or HER2-activating mutations (n = 9), thus making any conclusion in this specific subgroup not definitive. However, addressing the issue of the potential differential effect of anti-EGFRs versus bev is of special interest in the left-sided subgroup, where most guidelines recommend doublets plus anti-EGFR as a preferred option, and the sample size of our series was more adequate to perform meaningful comparisons. At the same time, the number of HER2-pos patients treated with the triplet (n = 8) was too small to investigate the role of treatment intensification in this molecular subgroup. Therefore, the question whether HER2-pos patients benefit more from fluorouracil/leucovorin plus oxaliplatin or irinotecan (FOLFOX/FOLFIRI) compared with fluorouracil/leucovorin plus oxaliplatin and irinotecan (FOLFOXIRI) cannot be addressed here. Fifth, because data on treatments received after progression in the first line were not systematically collected in all trials included, we are unable to report how many patients with HER2-pos tumors received an anti-HER2 agent in their continuum-of-care and how this affected OS assessment. Finally, we acknowledge that the up-front treatment landscape of HER2-pos pMMR RAS/BRAF wild-type mCRC may change in the next future because of clinical trials currently ongoing to define the role of anti-HER2 strategies in the first-line treatment.2 Anyhow, those trials are still enrolling patients and our findings are reassuring about the adequacy of their control arms, where both anti-EGFRs and bev can be administered at investigators' choice (ie, the MOUNTAINEER-03 study).43 Targeted approaches for HER2 mut are at an earlier stage of development, but this scenario may rapidly evolve in the close future.2,34,35

In conclusion, anti-EGFR or bev provides similar outcome results with combinatory chemotherapy, especially doublets—mostly represented in our analysis—among patients with HER2-pos/HER2 mut pMMR/MSS RAS/BRAF wild-type mCRC, thus making the assessment of HER2 expression/amplification—and HER2 mutational status if NGS is available—more useful in view of the potential availability of targeted approaches today in the frame of clinical trials, rather than for treatment selection between bev and anti-EGFRs.

Biochemical characteristics and key regulatory genes of intramuscular fat deposition in aged Nanchuan chickens
Authors: Naiyi Xu, Zhengfu Feng, Feifan Chen, Dongliang Su, Shengnan Zhao, Linyun Zhang, Yongju Zhao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Intramuscular fat (IMF) is an important indicator affecting chicken quality. However, there are few studies on IMF in aged chickens. Nanchuan chicken is an excellent local egg and meat dual-purpose breed in Southwest China, renowned for its delicious flavor and fresh meat. In this study, biochemical characteristics and regulation mechanism of IMF deposition in aged Nanchuan chickens were systematically clarified. Slaughter performance, the IMF, triglyceride (TG), phospholipid (PLIP), total cholesterol (TCHO) and Fatty acids (FA) content in the breast muscle of 55-week-old Nanchuan chicken was measured, and RNA sequencing was integrated to explore the regulatory genes of IMF deposition. The results showed that the IMF content and whole net carcass yield in the high IMF group were significantly higher than those in the low IMF group (P < 0.01), while the shear force showed the opposite pattern. And, TG content in the high IMF group was significantly higher than that of the low IMF group (P < 0.05), and it was significantly positively correlated with the IMF content (P < 0.01). Meanwhile, we detected four FAs (C20:1n10, C20:4n6, C22:5n6, C22:6n3) in the high IMF group were significantly higher than those in the low IMF group (P < 0.05). In addition, the RNA sequencing results showed 469 up-regulated differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified, and these up-regulated DEGs were significantly enriched in ECM-receptor interaction, actin cytoskeleton regulation and focal adhesion pathways (P < 0.05). Among these DEGs, THBS1, THBS2, ITGA2, ITGA9, ITGAV and LUM were confirmed to be related to IMF deposition by protein-protein interaction (PPI) network, correlation analysis and gene expression level analysis, and these genes were hardly reported in researches of chicken IMF deposition. Our findings will help fully elucidate the mechanism of IMF formation of chickens and provide a theoretical foundation for the protection and utilization and the genetic improvement of Nanchuan chicken.

Introduction

Chickens are the most widely distributed and numerous domesticated animals in the world, which can provide humans with high-quality and inexpensive protein. Due to tenderness, juiciness and delicious taste, chicken has become one of the most popular meats worldwide. In recent years, significant progress has been made in chicken breeding. However, breeding approach that focus on growth rate and feed conversion rate have led to a significant decline in chicken quality. With the development of the social economy and changes in consumption patterns, people's requirements for chicken quality continue to increase, and the market demand for high-quality chickens is also increasing. Therefore, improving the quality of chicken meat is currently the focus of chicken breeding work. Intramuscular fat (IMF), a mixture of triglyceride (TG), phospholipid (PLIP), total cholesterol (TCHO) and other lipid contents in muscle, which is one of the important factors affecting the tenderness, flavor and water holding capacity of meat (Wood et al., 2008). The increased IMF content of breast muscle leads to desirable changes in chicken meat quality, such as, compare to the control line, the shear force of breast muscle is lower and carcass weight, breast muscle weight, breast muscle percentage of live weight is higher in the selected line with improved IMF content (Zhao et al., 2007). Compared with commercial broiler (Cobb broiler) with lower intramuscular fat content, Qingyuan chickens with higher intramuscular fat content have lower pH value, shear force, cooking loss, water content and muscle fiber area (Yang et al., 2025). IMF deposition is manifested as an increase in the number and volume of intramuscular preadipocytes, that is, the number of adipocytes and the ability of lipid synthesis and accumulation determine the IMF content. Among them, the number of adipocytes has been fixed prenatally and the early stage of growth and development, and the IMF deposition in the late stage of growth and development mainly depends on the increase of adipocyte volume and weight (Leclercq, 1984). Therefore, IMF deposition is a dynamic process regulated by many factors.

Fatty acids (FA), as an important component of TG and PLIP, are mainly classified into saturated fatty acids (SFA), unsaturated fatty acids (monounsaturated fatty acids (MUFA), polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFA)) and are important substances for maintaining life activities which can provide energy, maintain cell structure, regulate metabolism, and participate in hormone synthesis and immune regulation (Bazinet and Layé, 2014; Nakamura et al., 2014). The composition and content of FA have a significant impact on the flavor and quality of meat. FA are the precursors of aromatic substances, which can produce a variety of volatile compounds through oxidative cleavage. These compounds are mainly the basis for the formation of aromatic substances, which have important effects in meat. Meat with a low content of SFA and a high content of MUFA is tender and juicy, with a high flavor score (Cameron and Enser, 1991). PUFA are easily oxidized and the oxidation products affect the composition of meat flavor components and shelf life of meat, such as rancidity and color deterioration (Nestel, 1987; Wood et al., 2004). Besides, PUFA are related to some diseases, such as cardiovascular diseases, certain cancers, etc. Therefore, the appropriate proportions of SFA, MUFA and PUFA is of great significance to the quality and flavor of meat and healthy diet. The amount, distribution and proportion of SFA, MUFA and PUFA vary greatly in pork, ruminant and chicken meats (Rule et al., 2002; Wood et al., 2008). The content and proportion of different kinds of fatty acid are affect by many factors. Therefore, it is of great significance to explore the content and proportion of different kinds of fatty acid in chicken breast muscle.

Studies on IMF in chickens mainly focuses on different breeds, such as Beijing-you chicken and Arbor Acres broiler chicken, different tissues, such as breast and thigh tissues, and different developmental stages, such as 1, 56, 98, and 120 d (Cui et al., 2012, 2018; Gai et al., 2023). However, there are few studies on IMF in aged chickens. Nanchuan chicken is an excellent local egg and meat dual-purpose breed which is mainly distributed in Chongqing, southwest China. The characteristics of Nanchuan chicken is strong muscle, fresh meat and delicious flavor, which have been recorded by the “Livestock and Poultry Genetic Resources in Chongqing”. In the later stage of laying eggs, the flavor of Nanchuan chicken is affected by fat deposition and other factors, making its soup delicious. However, there is relatively limited research on the dominant traits (meat quality traits) of Nanchuan chicken, especially aged Nanchuan chicken. Therefore, this study mainly aimed to explore biochemical characteristics and the key genes related to IMF deposition of aged Nanchuan chicken, which will help elucidate the mechanism of IMF formation of aged chickens and provide a theoretical foundation for the protection and utilization, especially the genetic improvement, of Nanchuan chicken.

Materials and methods

Ethics statement

In this study, all animal experimental procedures and welfare practices were conducted in accordance with Guide for Use and Care of Laboratory Animals established by the Ministry of Science and Technology of China. All experimental procedures were approved by Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Southwest University.

Animals, sample collection, and slaughter performance measurement

A total of 14 female Nanchuan chickens of 55-week-old were used in this experiment. They were raised under the same standard conditions of light, temperature, humidity, and immunization schedule, fed the same diet, and had free access to water. The chickens were randomly sampled from those raised in the farm of Nanchuan, Chongqing and slaughtered on 55-week-old using a mechanized process line. Before slaughter, we weighed the live weight of each chicken. After slaughter, breast muscle and abdominal adipose were collected and immediately stored at − 80°C until use.

Live chickens, carcasses, half-bore, fully bore, pectoral muscles, leg muscles, and abdominal fat were weighed to calculate the carcass yield, whole net carcass yield, fully bore yield, breast muscle yield, leg muscle yield, and abdominal fat yield. After the chicken was slaughtered, the left breast muscle was immediately collected. Then, the pH value of the breast muscle was measured three times by a portable pH meter (Testo 205). And, breast muscle strips (1 × 1 × 3 cm) with fibers parallel to the long axis were sheared perpendicular to the fibers at a speed of 1 mm/s, and the peak force (kg.f) was recorded as the shear value (Xing et al., 2020), using a digital display muscle tenderness instrument (C-LM3B) equipped with a Warner-Bratzler shear blade (3.0 ± 0.2 mm thickness, 60° inner edge angle). Shear force of the breast muscle was also measured three times. The value of pH and shear force were expressed by the average values of the three measurements respectively. The cooking loss was expressed as the percentage of the weight of the breast muscles after steaming in boiling water for 30 minutes to the weight of the breast muscles before steaming.

IMF, TG, PLIP and TCHO measurement

First, the visible fat particles and membranes on the surface of the breast muscle tissue was removed, and then 20 g breast muscle sample was placed in a constant temperature drying oven and dried at 105°C for several hours to constant weight. Subsequently, the dried meat samples were ground up. Last, the IMF content (%) was determined by soxhlet extraction method with ether extractor, and expressed as the percentage of dry weight of the breast muscle tissue. After each breast muscle tissue was homogenized, and the contents of TG, PLIP and TCHO were determined by spectrophotometry using a commercially available kit (Jiangsu Addison Biotechnology Co. Ltd., Jiangsu, China).

FA composition analysis

First, the extracting solution was added to the breast muscle sample, ground and centrifuged to obtain the supernatant, then, a methanol:trimethylsilyl diazomethane solution was added, stood at room temperature and dried with nitrogen, finally, n-hexane was added for redissolution and centrifuged to obtain the supernatant for gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC-MS) analysis by an Agilent 7890B gas chromatograph system coupled with an Agilent 5977B mass spectrometer. Each of the FAs was calculated according to the following formula, C(con)=(Cs*V1*V2)/(M*V3). C(con): the content of FA in the sample, Cs: the concentration of FA in the complex solution, V1: resolubility volume, V2: volume of extracted solution to be added, V3: volume of extracted solution taken out, M: weighing volume.

RNA sequencing and analysis

For transcriptome analysis, total RNA was first extracted of breast muscle tissue sample from 14 Nanchuan chickens. And, the purity and concentration of RNA samples were detected by the Nanodrop2000 microspectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher), and the integrity of RNA samples was detected by the Labchip GX touch microfluidic capillary system (Revvity). Then, the library with around 350 bp insert sizes was constructed and was performed quality control. And, the library was sequenced on the MGI platform, and 150 bp paired-end reads were generated. The raw RNA sequence data were filtered, including the deletion of reads with adapters, containing N and low-quality. Subsequently, the qualified sequences were aligned to the reference genome GRCg7b by HISAT2. Gene expression levels were determined using the FPKM, and differential expression analyses between the two groups were conducted using the DESeq2. GO enrichment analysis and KEGG pathway enrichment statistics of differentially expressed genes were performed by clusterProfiler, with P < 0.05 were as significant enrichment.

Statistical analyses

The significant difference analysis between groups was conducted by independent sample t-test using SPSS Version 22.0. Confidence limits were set at 95 %, and P < 0.05 (*) or P < 0.01 (**) were considered statistically significant difference. The data were represented by the mean ± standard error of mean (SEM). Principal component analysis (PCA), spearman correlation analysis and cluster analysis were conducted by R software.

Results

IMF content and slaughter performance analysis of Nanchuan chicken

We measured the slaughter performance and meat quality traits of aged Nanchuan chickens (Supplementary Table S1). In order to explore the relationship between IMF content and slaughter performance and meat quality traits in the aged Nanchuan chickens, we first divided the samples into the high IMF content group and the low IMF content group according to the IMF content in each sample after slaughter, and then conducted significance analysis of the slaughter performance and meat quality traits of the high and low IMF content groups (Supplementary Table S2, S3). The results showed that the IMF content was more prominently increased in the high IMF content group than that in the low IMF content group (P < 0.01) (Fig. 1A). The evaluation of the impact of the increased IMF content on the meat quality revealed that the shear force of the breast meat of the high IMF content group was significantly lower than that of the low IMF content group (P < 0.05) (Fig. 1B). In addition, we also detected that the whole net carcass yield in the high IMF content group were significantly higher than that in the low IMF content group (P < 0.01) (Fig. 1C). However, other slaughter performance and meat quality traits, such as carcass yield, breast muscle yield, leg muscle yield, cooking loss, and pH value was not significantly different between the two groups (Supplementary Table S3). Therefore, the increase of IMF content could improve the meat quality and slaughter performance of aged Nanchuan chickens to a certain extent.Fig. 1Comparison of IMF content, meat quality and slaughter performance between the high IMF content group and the low IMF content group of aged Nanchuan chickens. IMF components analysis of Nanchuan Chicken

We detected the contents of TG, PLIP and TCHO, the main IMF components, of the breast muscle of aged Nanchuan chickens (Supplementary Table S2). The results showed that the content of TG in the breast muscle of the high IMF group was significantly higher than that of the low IMF group (P < 0.05), and there was no significant difference in the contents of PLIP and TCHO between the two groups (Fig. 2A, B and C, Supplementary Table S4), indicating that TG was the main increased IMF component in the high IMF group. In order to further verify the relationship between TG and IMF, spearman correlation analysis was performed on the content of IMF, TG, PLIP and TCHO of all samples (Supplementary Table S5). The analysis results showed that there was a significant positive correlation between IMF content and TG content (r = 0.70, P < 0.01), while there was no significant positive correlation between the IMF content and the content of PLIP and TCHO (Fig. 2D, Supplementary Table S5). In conclusion, the results indicated that TG controls the content of IMF of aged Nanchuan chickens.Fig. 2Identification of the decisive ingredients of IMF of aged Nanchuan chickens. FA content analysis of Nanchuan chicken

A total of 24 common FAs in breast muscle were commonly shared by both the high IMF group and the low IMF group. Among them, the contents of six kinds of FAs, namely SFA (C14:0, C16:0, C18:0), and UFA (C18:1n9, C18:2n6, and C20:4n6), were the highest, and their proportions were more than 70 %, which are much higher than those of other types of FAs (Table 1). The results of significant difference analysis of FA content between the two groups showed that four fatty acids in the high IMF group were significantly higher than those in the low IMF group, namely C20:1n10, C20:4n6, C22:5n6, C22:6n3, while there was no significant difference in the content of other types of fatty acids (Table 1). In addition, the spearman correlation analysis of the content of FAs and IMF, TG showed that the four FAs with significant differences had a high positive correlation with both IMF and TG (Fig. 3, Supplementary Table S6). Transcriptome and differentially expressed genes analysis

RNA-seq was conducted to examine the gene expression profiles of breast muscles in Nanchuan chickens in the groups with low-IMF content and high-IMF content. A total of 1,464 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified based on fold change≥1.5, P ≤ 0.05 and padj≤1, including 469 up-regulated DEGs, 995 down-regulated DEGs (Fig. 4A, Supplementary Table S7). The increase of IMF content can improve meat quality. In the study, we mainly explored the genes that promote IMF deposition. In addition, most of the up-regulated DEGs involved in multiple processes of IMF deposition and lipid metabolism, and they are more likely to act as core regulators to initiate or enhance downstream biological processes. Therefore, in the subsequent analysis, we mainly focused on the analysis of up-regulated DEGs. KEGG enrichment analysis showed that these up-regulated DEGs were significantly enriched in signaling pathways related to IMF, such as ECM-receptor interaction, actin cytoskeleton regulation and focal adhesion pathways (P < 0.05) (Fig. 4B, Supplementary Table S8), which affect the proliferation and differentiation of adipocytes by regulating cell adhesion and signal transduction.Fig. 4The differential expression analysis. To explore the interaction relationships among genes, we construct protein-protein interaction (PPI) network for 469 upregulated DEGs using the STRING online website (https://string-db.org/) and the Cytoscape software (Fig. 5, Supplementary Table S9). In order to analyze and display the genes of key nodes, only the nodes with the top 20 % Degree values are shown in the PPI network. Discussion

The demand for chicken meat has continuously increased for humans, and chicken meat has become the second most consumed meat worldwide (Cui et al., 2022). With the development of economy and the change of consumption pattern, the quality and flavor of meat become more and more important. Nanchuan chicken is a popular local egg and meat dual-purpose breed in Southwest China, renowned for its delicious flavor and fresh meat. However, at present, there are still relatively few studies on the quality of aged Nanchuan chicken meat. In this study, we took 55-week-old Nanchuan chickens as the research object to explore the association between IMF content and meat quality and slaughter performance, the differences in IMF composition components (TG, PLIP and TCHO), the correlation between FAs composition and IMF deposition, and to identify the key candidate genes related to IMF deposition in aged Nanchuan chickens.

In this study, by comparing the meat quality and slaughter performance of aged Nanchuan chickens in high and low IMF content groups, it was found that the increase of IMF content can improve the tenderness of chicken meat, which is consistent with the conclusion of previous research (Cui et al., 2022). In addition, we found that IMF content was positively correlated with whole net carcass yield, which may be related to the distribution of adipose tissue between muscles affecting the structural integrity of carcasses, however, the specific mechanism still needs further verification. In the analysis of lipid components (TG, PLIP and TCHO) in breast muscle tissue of aged Nanchuan chickens, we found that TG was the main contributor to IMF deposition, that is, TG was dominant in IMF of aged chickens, which was consistent with previous research results of other developmental stage of Jingxing yellow-feather chickens (Cui et al., 2022). It is worth noting that PLIP, as an important component of the cell membrane, its content did not change significantly with the increase of the total amount of IMF. It is speculated that PLIP may mainly be involved in the maintenance of cell structure, while the dynamic changes of TG directly reflect the fat deposition ability. Twenty-four fatty acids were detected in the breast muscle of aged Nanchuan chickens, among which SFA, such as C14:0, C16:0, C18:0 and UFA, such as C18:1n9, C18:2n6, C20:4n6 were the main components, accounting for more than 70 %. Compared with the low IMF group, the contents of C20:1n10, C20:4n6 (ARA), C22:5n6 (DPA) and C22:6n3 (DHA) in the high IMF group were significantly increased, and were positively correlated with IMF and TG. Among these four FAs, the proportion of ARA is much higher than that of the other three. Moreover, it activates PPARγ and C/EBPβ through the PGI2 pathway, thereby promoting adipocyte differentiation and increasing fat accumulation (Zhang et al., 2023). It is worth noting that the content of DPA in the high IMF group was extremely significantly higher than that in the low IMF group (P < 0.01). As an intermediate product of ω−6 series fatty acid metabolism, DPA may play an important role in the process of lipid accumulation (Tinant et al., 2025).

In this study, we aimed to explore the genes that promote IMF deposition. Therefore, we focused on the analysis of 469 up-regulated DEGs screened by differential expression analysis. According to PPI network, gene expression analysis and correlation analysis of up-regulated DEGs with IMF, TG and four differential FAs contents, 10 candidate genes were screened out. One of the significant KEGG pathway of up-regulated DEGs was actin cytoskeleton regulation, which involves in IMF content and deposition (Poleti et al., 2018). Interestingly, candidate genes, FGF7 and FGF10, are enriched in actin cytoskeleton regulation pathway. As a FA transporter, CD36 can bind to long-chain FAs and promotes their trans-membrane transport, thereby affecting FA metabolism and may promote the activation of the PPAR-γ pathway (Chen et al., 2022b). Palmitate up-regulates PPARγ expression by activating PPARGC1A, thereby promoting liver lipid synthesis and storage (Maruyama et al., 2016). FGF7, FGF10, CD36 and PPARGC1A, are involved in lipid deposition of chicken (Zhang et al., 2018, 2020; Ma et al., 2022; Cao et al., 2023). More importantly, the remaining 6 candidate genes (THBS1, THBS2, ITGA2, ITGA9, ITGAV and LUM) discovered in this study were hardly studied in IMF deposition in chickens. One of the significant KEGG pathway of up-regulated DEGs was ECM-receptor interaction pathway, which affects IMF deposition by regulating genes related to intramuscular adipocyte differentiation (San et al., 2021). Coincidentally, candidate genes, THBS1, THBS2, ITGA2, ITGA9 and ITGAV, are enriched in ECM-receptor interaction. In addition, the proteoglycan Lumican encoded by LUM can regulate adipocyte metabolism (Strieder-Barboza et al., 2022). Although these 6 candidate genes are likely to be involved in IMF deposition in chicken, their functions need to be further verified by methods such as cell models, animal models and population studies, in order to systematically analyze the regulatory mechanism of IMF deposition and provide a more comprehensive molecular theoretical basis for the breeding of high-quality broiler breeds.

Moreover, we also identified a series of candidate genes whose expression levels were positively correlated with the contents of IMF, TG and four differential FAs. (Supplementary Fig. S1A, Supplementary Table S11). And the expression levels of these genes in the high IMF group were significantly up-regulated compared with the low IMF group (Supplementary Fig. S1B). Baculoviral IAP repeat containing 8 (BIRC8), p21 (RAC1) activated kinase 5 (PAK5), parvin beta (PARVB) and parvin gamma (PARVG) are enriched in Focal adhesion pathway, which related to lipid deposition (Chen et al., 2022a). Collagen type IV alpha 6 chain (COL4A6) are enriched in ECM-receptor interaction pathway and collagen type XII alpha 1 chain (COL12A1) and collagen type XXV alpha 1 chain (COL25A1) are associated with ECM formation, which affects IMF deposition (San et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2021). ATP binding cassette subfamily G member 1 (ABCG1) and methylsterol monooxygenase 1 (MSMO1) are involved in cholesterol transport and biosynthesis, respectively (Rodriguez et al., 2003; Xu et al., 2022). Lipin 1 (LPIN1) is involved in the synthesis of TG, and its activity directly affects intracellular lipid storage and membrane phospholipid composition (Reue, 2007). And, diacylglycerol kinase theta (DGKQ), catalyzes the phosphorylation of diacylglycerol (DAG) to produce phosphatidic acid (PtdOH), which is the core step of TG synthesis and lipid droplet formation (Tu-Sekine et al., 2013). Besides, solute carrier family 16 member 7 (SLC16A7) could promote TG deposition by de novo lipogenesis (Wang et al., 2022). Phospholipase A2 receptor 1 (PLA2R1) indirectly affects the lipolysis rate of adipocytes and FA oxidation in liver (Massemin et al., 2023). Kruppel like factor 4 (KLF4) facilitate lipid formation via regulating the expression of lipogenic regulator SREBP1 (Li et al., 2023). Mitogen-activated protein kinase 12 (MAPK12) is involved in lipid metabolism (Zhang et al., 2025). Phospholipase A2 group XV (PLA2G15) maintains lysosomal function by degrading BMP lipids and regulates lipid droplet metabolism and immune-lipid interaction in adipocytes (Davidson et al., 2025).

Conclusions

In conclusion, this study found that the increase of IMF content could improve the meat tenderness and slaughter performance, and TG and some specific types of FAs, such as C20:1n10, C20:4n6, C22:5n6, C22:6n3, affect the IMF deposition of aged Nanchuan chickens. Importantly, we discovered several DEGs, such as THBS1, THBS2, ITGA2, ITGA9, ITGAV and LUM, by PPI network, correlation analysis and gene expression level analysis, are related to IMF deposition, and these genes were hardly reported in researches of chicken IMF deposition. In brief, we detected the biochemical characteristics and key regulatory genes of IMF deposition in aged Nanchuan chickens; however, the biological function of these key genes needs to be further verified. Our finding provides important candidate genes for the molecular breeding of Nanchuan chickens, and will help systematically elucidate the molecular mechanism of IMF formation in chickens.

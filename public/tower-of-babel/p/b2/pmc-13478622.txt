Mechanical and rheological behaviour of acrylonitrile butadiene styrene/polyolefin elastomers composites: Experimental analysis and numerical optimization
Authors: Mojtaba Sepehrnia, Sahar Salehi, Mobina Ahmadvand, Maryam Mahmoudi, Mobina Mashhadi Tafreshi, Parvaneh Kalhori
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPolymer composites hold great promise due to their excellent properties, yet their compatibility and strength in real-world applications remain critical challenges. This study focuses on the development and optimization of a polymer composite based on acrylonitrile butadiene styrene (ABS) and polyolefin elastomer (POE). In order to characterize and optimize ABS/POE polymer composites, Design-Expert software with the D-optimal mixture design (DMD) method was used. The selected independent factors were the weight percentages of ABS and POE. Five selected dependent factors were tensile strength (TS), percentage elongation at break (%EB), elastic modulus, hardness and melt flow index (MFI). The experimental data obtained from the different composite formulations were statistically analyzed. ANOVA results confirmed the significance of the models (p < 0.0001) for all responses, with R² values above 0.96. Formulation with 90% ABS and 10% POE was selected as optimized polymer composite. This formulation exhibited a TS of 26.7 MPa, EB of 21.35%, elastic modulus of 1296 MPa, hardness of 72.12 Shore D and MFI of 1.39 g/min. The experimental results matched the predicted values closely, with errors below 7.2%. The findings indicate that the Design-Expert software effectively identifies the optimal balance between strength and flexibility in the polymer composite. Such enhancements can contribute to the development of high-performance composites with superior strength, flexibility, and durability, rendering them highly suitable for a broad spectrum of engineering, automotive, and other commercial applications.Keywords: Polymer composite, Acrylonitrile butadiene styrene, Polyolefin elastomer, D-optimal mixture designSubject terms: Engineering, Materials science

Introduction

Composite material is the assembly of two or more components to create a durable product with superior properties compared with each of the constituent components1,2. Over the past decades, composite materials have attracted many researchers due to their excellent mechanical properties, good rigidity, high thermal and impact resistance, good abrasion resistance, and suitable electrical insulation that depend on the type of composite2,3. Among composite materials, polymer matrix composites (PMCs) have several unique properties and advantages that make them suitable for use in various engineering applications, including benefits such as low cost, design flexibility, a high strength-to-weight ratio, and excellent chemical and fatigue resistance4.

Acrylonitrile butadiene styrene (ABS) is an amorphous thermoplastic terpolymer that is created from the monomers acrylonitrile, butadiene, and styrene. Due to the chemical and creep resistance, heat stability, good mechanical strength, rigidity and ease of processability, it was used over the past decades and found applications in many fields for manufacturing lightweight, rigid, and molded products, including telephone handsets, automotive body parts, wheel covers, and enclosures5,6. But poor solvent and fatigue resistance, a relatively high price and poor resistance to UV light have limited some of the uses of ABS6,7. Therefore, it is necessary to use other polymers by combining different percentages to improve their properties.

Polyolefin elastomers (POEs) are a relatively new group of elastomers with great properties, which are prepared by the copolymerization of ethylene with butene and octene monomers8,9. Due to the low hardness, low density, heat resistant and excellent toughness, they can be used as an excellent impact modifier for plastics and materials with high flexibility in compounding applications10.

Fang et al.11 compared the mechanical properties of three blends: POE-PE, POE-ABS, and POE-PP. Their results showed that POE-PE and POE-PP exhibit a single-phase structure, whereas POE-ABS presents a two-phase morphology. Therefore, despite the higher intrinsic strength of ABS compared to PE and PP, its lower compatibility with POE leads to the poorest mechanical performance in POE-ABS blends, significantly reducing their reinforcing effect.

Xia et al.12 prepared ABS–POE polymer composites with mixing ratios of ABS(90%)-POE(10%), ABS(70%)-POE(30%), and ABS(50%)-POE(50%), and compared their mechanical properties. Their results showed that:1. Increasing the POE content enhances the toughness of the composite. 2. As the POE content increases, the nonlinear elastic–plastic behavior of the composite becomes more pronounced. 3. With higher POE content, the ultimate tensile strength decreases, while the elongation at break increases. 4. At higher POE levels, phase cohesion and interdiffusion within the composite improve. 5. By properly adjusting the mixing ratios of ABS–POE, an optimal balance between strength and flexibility can be achieved.

Lv13 prepared ABS–POE polymer composites with mixing ratios of ABS(10%)-POE(90%), ABS(30%)-POE(70%), and ABS(50%)-POE(50%) and investigated their mechanical properties. The elongation at break and tensile strength of pure POE were 2138% and 2.83 MPa, respectively; however, with increasing ABS content, the composites exhibited stiffer and more rigid behavior. For the ABS (50%)-POE (50%) blend, the tensile strength increased to 9.4 MPa, while the elongation at break decreased to 160%. The energy absorption test also showed that the ABS (30%)-POE (70%) composite had the highest energy absorption capacity among all tested samples. This study highlights the potential of blending soft and rigid materials to tailor properties and emphasizes the importance of material composition in achieving desirable mechanical performance.

Xia et al.14 investigated the effect of incorporating 30 wt% POE on the mechanical properties of ABS, LDPE and PP. Their results showed a significant shift of the glass-to-rubber transition to a higher temperature range and an increase in the glass transition temperature due to the presence of POE elastomers in the composites. All POE-containing composites exhibited notable toughness, which was attributed to the alignment of polymer chain interactions. Additionally, ABS-POE composites demonstrated superior mechanical properties compared to PP-POE and LDPE-POE samples, likely due to fewer potential failure points.

Although ABS and POE have individually attracted considerable attention in polymer engineering, studies focusing specifically on ABS/POE composites remain limited, and investigations evaluating the overall performance of these composite formulations are still scarce. Furthermore, the absence of a structured optimization strategy to determine the optimal composition represents a clear gap in current research. Addressing these shortcomings is essential to clarify the influence of composition on the performance of ABS/POE polymer composites and to provide reliable guidance for material development.

The aim of this study was to evaluate and compare the mechanical properties and melt flow index (MFI) of ABS/POE polymer composites. For this purpose, the ABS/POE polymer composite was optimized for excellent mechanical properties and MFI using Design-Expert software and the D-optimal mixture design (DMD) method. Finally, various formulations with ABS and POE polymer composites were developed. The mechanical properties of all formulations were assessed using tensile strength (TS), percentage elongation at break (%EB), elastic modulus and hardness.

Materials and methods

Materials

The ABS was purchased from GHAED BASSIR PETROCHEMICAL CO. (Iran). Based on company brochure, it has tensile strength of 455 kg/cm2, flexural modulus of 22,000 kg/cm2, flexural strength of 650 kg/cm2, and tensile elongation of 20%. The POE was purchased from LG Chem Ltd. (South Korea) which has density of 0.865 g/cm3, tensile strength at break of 1.8 MPa, and elongation at break of 550%.

ABS/POE polymer composites preparation

Initially, ABS and POE pellets were dried in a vacuum oven at 60 °C for 3 h to eliminate moisture. According to Table 2, ABS/POE polymer composites were prepared with different weight percentages using the melt blending method. For this purpose, ABS was poured into a Torque Rheometer–Internal Mixer at 220 °C and mixed at a rotational speed of 60 rpm for 2 min until it was completely melted (RheoSense, Plastics Group, Iran Polymer and Petrochemical Institute, Tehran, Iran). Then, POE was added to the mixture, and the blending process was continued for an additional 5 min to ensure homogeneous mixing. The resulting composite was subsequently poured into molds. The molds were first heated in a hot press at 230 °C for 3 min and subsequently subjected to a pressure of 100 bar for 6 min (SPH-300, Santam LTD., Tehran, Iran). The molds were then transferred to a cold press and compressed to obtain the final samples. Subsequently, the mechanical properties and MFI of the ABS/POE polymer composites were evaluated.

Characterization of ABS/POE polymer composite

TS, %EB and Elastic modulus

The mechanical properties of the ABS/POE polymer composite were assessed by measuring the TS and %EB using a Santam testing machine (STM-20, Santam LTD., Tehran, Iran). In this method (ASTM D638), dumbbell-shaped composite samples (Fig. 1) with predefined dimensions (L0: initial length(mm), t: thickness(mm), w: width(mm)) were placed between two clamp levers of the equipment to evaluate their mechanical properties. Each ABS/POE polymer composite sample was subjected to an extension force at a rate of 10 mm/min.

Elastic modulus, also known as Young’s modulus, is a mechanical property used to measure the stiffness of composites. It is defined as the ratio of the stress applied to the strain in the elastic deformation region of the composites17.

Hardness test

Hardness testing on composites measures their resistance to permanent deformation at their surface18. The ABS/POE polymer composites were hardness tested by using a durometer shore D hardness tester (SHD-25, Santam LTD., Tehran, Iran). In this method (ASTM D2240), for each specimen, three hardness readings were taken at three different locations, as shown in Fig. 2. The distance between adjacent measurement points was set to 2 cm to avoid any interaction between indentation marks. The indenter was gradually applied to the specimen surface and maintained in position for a short dwell time to ensure stable and reliable readings. Finally, the average hardness value of the three points was taken as the hardness of the ABS/POE polymer composites.

MFI analysis

MFI analysis is a simple measurement that determines the amount of polymer extruded through a die with specific dimensions at a set temperature over a period of 10 min19,20. MFI analysis was done by using a Santam testing machine (SMF-300, Santam LTD., Tehran, Iran) according to the ASTM D1238 method. In this method, each sample was added to the clean barrel. After melting the sample, a 2.16 kg weight was placed on the piston to force the molten sample into the die. Finally, the MFI was measured by determining the amount of ABS/POE polymer composite, in grams, that flows through the die over a 10-minute period.

Design of experiments

In order to determine the optimal ratio of ABS and POE polymer composite with excellent MFI and mechanical properties, Design-Expert software (Trial version 7.0.0, Stat-Ease Inc., Minneapolis, MN, USA) was employed as an essential tool to identify critical parameters and optimize the formulation process. In this research, D-optimal mixture design (DMD) was utilized as a statistical and mathematical analysis tool in the Design-Expert software to evaluate the effects of various factors on the responses. Weight percentages of ABS and POE in the polymer composite were chosen as independent factors, as outlined in Table 2, and TS, %EB, elastic modulus, hardness and MFI were chosen as dependent factors. Eventually, thirteen formulations, including five replicates, were designed using Design-Expert software with the DMD method (Table 2). The influence of ABS and POE on the mechanical properties of the polymer composite is illustrated in Figs. 3 and 4. Also, Fig. 5 illustrates one of the specimens after tensile failure. According to Fig. 3a, the maximum TS was achieved when the mixture contained the highest concentration of ABS and the lowest concentration of POE. The pure ABS material has a TS of approximately 45 MPa, while pure POE has a TS of about 1 MPa. Therefore, when the amount of ABS decreases and the amount of POE increases, the TS of the composite declines. This decreasing trend continues as the POE content increases up to 70%, and afterward, with further increasing POE up to 90%, the TS of the composite begins to rise again, albeit with a slow slope. In two-phase composites, the variation of TS is directly related to the compatibility of the two materials at different mixing ratios. Failure typically occurs either within the weaker phase or at the interface between the two phases. In ABS–POE two-phase composites, due to the weak interfacial adhesion, the composites fail at tensile strengths much lower than expected. These weak interfacial zones act as regions of high stress concentration, promoting the initiation of micro-cracks and their subsequent propagation along the interface. Feng et al.11 after preparing POE–PE, POE–ABS, and POE–PP samples, demonstrated that although ABS has a higher strength than the two other additives PE and PP, the POE–ABS composites exhibit the lowest mechanical properties due to the lower compatibility between ABS and POE, resulting in a reduced reinforcing effect.

Conversely, as shown in Fig. 3b, the %EB generally decreases with increasing ABS content and increases with higher POE content in the composite, which can be attributed to the reinforcing effect of ABS that enhances the overall strength of the blend. Previous studies have shown that incorporating ABS into polymer blends can improve mechanical integrity and dimensional stability12. Accordingly, the %EB of the ABS (10%)–POE (90%) sample ranges from 757% to 900%, whereas that of the ABS (90%)–POE (10%) sample ranges from 17.9% to 22.8% across repeated measurements. Similar to the trend observed for tensile strength (TS), an extremum point is also observed in the %EB variation. This behavior can be mainly attributed to the fact that, in two-phase composites, the evolution of mechanical properties strongly depends on the interfacial compatibility of the constituent materials at different blend ratios. Xia et al.12 reported a comparable trend, observing that increasing the POE content from 10% to 30% (corresponding to a decrease in ABS from 90% to 70%) resulted in a 10% reduction in %EB, followed by a substantial increase of approximately 400% when the POE content was further increased from 30% to 50%.

Additionally, the elastic modulus of the formulations indicated that as the polymer composites became harder, both the elastic modulus and TS generally increased, while %EB decreased (Fig. 3c). The observed increase in both the elastic modulus and TS with higher ABS content can be ascribed to ABS functioning as the continuous rigid phase within the polymer composite. The ABS network provides structural reinforcement and facilitates efficient stress transfer throughout the composite, thereby enhancing its stiffness and load-bearing capability. In contrast, the incorporation of POE, a soft elastomeric phase, perturbs the packing density and chain entanglement of the polymer matrix, weakening intermolecular interactions and reducing the overall mechanical rigidity and TS of the composite. Consequently, composites with higher POE content exhibit lower elastic modulus and tensile strength, whereas the %EB increases due to the enhanced deformability imparted by the elastomeric phase.

Figure 3d shows the influence of ABS and POE on the hardness of the polymer composite. According to Fig. 3d, the maximum hardness (73 ± 2 Shore D) was achieved in the polymer composites formulated with 90% ABS and 10% POE. One of the remarkable features of ABS is its outstanding impact resistance and stiffness, making it an ideal choice as the base material12,27. Based on the findings, incorporating POE into the base material decreases the composite’s hardness. Nevertheless, the maximum hardness is achieved when 10% of the composite’s weight consists of POE.

Figure 6 illustrates the specimen during the MFI test, and the MFI values of the ABS/POE polymer composites are shown in Fig. 7. As shown in Fig. 7, the addition of POE as a filler material to the ABS base results in a gradual increase in the MFI, the highest value of MFI observed at a composition ratio of 30% ABS and 70% POE. However, the MFI value decreased to 2.46 ± 0.1 g/min when the composition ratio was 10% ABS and 90% POE. This decrease in MFI is attributed to POE aggregation and poor dispersion within the polymer composite due to low compatibility, which results in an increase in melt viscosity due to the presence of a highly elastomeric phase. The increased viscosity generates higher resistance during flow through the processing equipment, thereby leading to a lower MFI29. In general, two key factors govern the melt-flow behavior of the ABS–POE polymer composites: 1-Molecular weight and 2-Compatibility. The molecular weight acts as a quantitative factor that determines the base viscosity, whereas compatibility is a structural factor influencing phase dispersion and chain mobility within the blend. The results indicate that the variation of the MFI with respect to the POE content in the ABS–POE polymer composite is nonlinear, which reflects the competition between these two mechanisms. At low to intermediate POE loadings, the compatibility-driven mechanism dominates. In this range (10–70 wt% POE), POE behaves locally as a softening component and its favorable microstructural dispersion reduces the effective viscosity, leading to an increase in MFI. However, at higher POE concentrations, the influence of POE’s high molecular weight becomes dominant. This increases the melt viscosity of the ABS–POE polymer composite and consequently results in a decrease in MFI.

Optimization and validation of ABS/POE polymer composites

The main purpose of this study was to optimize ABS/POE polymer composites with superior quality characteristics through the application of Design-Expert software. For this purpose, numerical optimization was used to determine the optimum ABS/POE polymer composites. The software used the Nelder–Mead simplex algorithm and the Derringer–Suich desirability model to predict the optimum levels of ABS and POE in polymer composites (10–90 wt%). The aim was to achieve maximum TS, elastic modulus, and hardness while minimizing %EB and MFI. Excessively high %EB is typically associated with a dominant elastomeric phase, which, although beneficial for toughness, can lead to a significant reduction in tensile strength, elastic modulus, and load-bearing capability. Therefore, minimizing %EB was considered necessary to avoid over-softening of the composite and to maintain adequate structural integrity. Similarly, a very high MFI corresponds to low melt viscosity, which may be favorable for flow but often correlates with reduced molecular entanglement and inferior mechanical performance. In contrast, a moderate-to-low MFI ensures sufficient melt strength, better shape retention during processing, and improved mechanical reliability of the final product. Consequently, minimizing MFI was selected to achieve an optimal balance between processability and mechanical performance.

The optimum desirability parameter for the five responses was approximately 0.971, when the composition of the polymer composite was set at 90% ABS and 10% POE. For these conditions, the predicted and experimental values of responses are presented in Table 4. As shown in Table 4, the results show a small deviation between the predicted and measured values (1–7%). This difference results from normal variations in material treatment and molding. Small changes in temperature, shear rate, or residence time may affect polymer chain mobility. The inherent heterogeneity of the ABS/POE blend, along with minor batch-to-batch differences can also contribute. Additionally, measurement uncertainties, particularly in tensile and impact tests, can further introduce small variations. Overall, these findings show that the model gives reliable predictions of the material properties.

Conclusion

In this work, polymer composites of ABS and POE were evaluated based on mechanical properties and MFI analysis. Thirteen formulations were designed by Design-Expert software with the DMD method to investigate the influence of ABS and POE composition on the mechanical properties and MFI of the polymer composites. The effect of independent factors (weight percentages of ABS and POE) on mechanical properties and MFI of polymer composites was analyzed to identify the optimized formulation. Finally, the formulation consisting of 90% ABS and 10% POE was selected for further investigation. This formulation was identified as the optimized polymer composite, exhibiting a TS of 26.7 ± 1.01 MPa, EB of 21.35 ± 0.92%, elastic modulus of 1296 ± 9 MPa, hardness of 72.12 ± 0.32 Shore D and MFI of 1.39 ± 0.02 g/min. This optimized formulation demonstrated outstanding mechanical properties and making it a promising candidate for various industrial applications. For future work, nanoparticles are proposed as a multifunctional filler to further enhance the mechanical properties of optimized ABS/POE polymer composite.

Imaging characteristics of renal epithelioid angiomyolipoma and its correlation with risk stratification
Authors: Jin Zhou, Guo-chu Qin, Ting-ting Chen, Le Cheng, Li Zhu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractRenal epithelioid angiomyolipoma (EAML) is frequently misdiagnosed due to nonspecific imaging findings. This study aimed to explore the radiographic characteristics of EAML, with the goal of enhancing the accuracy of preoperative diagnoses. A total of 27 patients with histopathologically confirmed renal EAML were included in the study. We retrospectively reviewed the radiographic features and analyzed the differences between tumors with a cut-off diameter of 40 mm. The majority of the lesions were isolated (25/27, 92.59%) and were predominantly found in females (18/27, 66.67%). ‌CT images revealed that most lesions were hyperdense, with fat detected in 13 cases. Small lesions were more likely to exhibit rapid wash-in and wash-out enhancement (p = 0.04), whereas abnormal vessels were more commonly found in large lesions. Tumors with higher risk stratification tended to show persistent or delayed enhancement on CT images. The imaging manifestations of renal EAML are diverse. After enhancement, small lesions are more likely to display rapid wash-in and wash-out patterns on CT images, whereas large lesions are more likely to exhibit abnormal vessels within or surrounding the tumor.Keywords: Angiomyolipoma, Epithelioid angiomyolipoma, Imaging features, MRI, CTSubject terms: Medical research, Nephrology, Urology

Introduction

Angiomyolipoma (AML) is the most prevalent benign mesenchymal tumor of the kidney1,2 and is composed of abnormal thick-walled blood vessels, spindle cells, epithelioid smooth muscle cells and adipose tissue. However, epithelioid angiomyolipoma (EAML), a rare variant of AML, is classified as a potentially malignant tumor3 that may lead to organ invasion, metastasis and local recurrence. Epithelioid angiomyolipoma (EAML) is predominantly composed of epithelioid cells that exhibit varied morphology and prolific growth, typically organized in sheets and accompanied by a smaller proportion of immature adipocytes (generally less than 5%)4,5. Studies indicate that approximately one-third of individuals with EAML may experience metastasis to the lymph nodes, liver, lungs, or spine6.

Imaging is pivotal in the preoperative diagnosis and management of EAML7. Classical AML, with identifiable adipose tissue components, can be easily diagnosed from images, while the diagnosis of EAML, which has variable imaging presentations, can be much harder. There is a high possibility that EAML may be misdiagnosed as renal cancer or AML without fat before surgery, which could lead to overtreatment or undertreatment in patients with EAML6,8,9. Accurate preoperative diagnosis of renal EAML is essential for effective disease management.

The radiological appearance of renal EAML has rarely been discussed. In this study, we retrospectively analyzed 27 renal EAML cases. We recorded the related clinical and imaging data with the aim of reviewing the imaging characteristics of renal EAML to assist in its preoperative diagnosis.

Methods

Patients

This retrospective study was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee with a waiver of informed consent for anonymized imaging data. Original CT/MR scans were obtained under standard clinical consent procedures. From January 2017 to August 2023, 27 patients (9 men, 18 women) were included, with an average age of 46.30 years (range: 10–72 years). All the patients underwent surgery at our hospital and were subsequently pathologically diagnosed with renal EAML. We analyzed the imaging, clinical, and pathological information of these patients (Fig. 1).

CT and MRI protocol

CT scans were performed using 64-multislice CT (GE LightSpeed VCT and Discover CT 750 HD). The scanned area extended from the diaphragm to the lower pole of the kidney, and a suitable CT scan range was added in line with the lesion. Patients were asked to fast for 4 h before the injection of contrast media. Scanning parameters were as follows: tube voltage, 120 kVp; tube current, 250–300 mA; slice thickness, 5 mm; slice gap, 5 mm. Nonionic contrast media was injected at a rate of 2.5–3.5 ml/s at a dose of 1.2 ml/kg. The corticomedullary phase was obtained at 25–30 s after enhancement, the nephrographic phase was obtained at 50–60 s, and the delayed excretory phase was obtained at 5–15 min.

MR scans were performed on a 3.0T MRI (Ingenia 3.0T, Philips Healthcare, Best, the Netherlands) or a 1.5T MRI (Ingenia 3.0T, Philips Healthcare, Best, the Netherlands) with an assorted torso phased-array body coil. All the scans included axial T1-weighted imaging (T1WI), axial T2-weighted imaging (T2WI), axial T2-weighted spectral attenuated inversion recovery (SPAIR), axial diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) (b values of 0 and 800 s/mm2), and axial gradient echo in-phase and out-of-phase sequences.

Imaging analysis

All the images were independently reviewed by two radiologists with 21 and 19 years of experience in uroradiology together, they were blinded to the pathology findings. The following characteristics were evaluated: tumor location, tumor size, number of tumors, growth pattern, density, signal intensity, existence of fat and abnormal blood vessels, and pattern of enhancement. The normal renal cortex was used as a reference for evaluating the density and signal intensity, as well as the degree of tumor enhancement. The terms “hypo”, “iso”, and “hyper” were used when the characteristics were lower, similar, or higher than those of the normal renal cortex, respectively. The enhancement pattern was classified into three different types based on the appearance after enhancement: (1) wash-in and wash-out: rapid enhancement in the arterial phase followed by quick washout in later phases; (2) persistent enhancement: persistent high CT attenuation is maintained across all phases; and (3)delayed enhancement: minimal/no early enhancement with gradual increase in the delayed phase.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 23.0 (IBM Corporation). All the tumors were classified into two different groups based on their tumor size: ≤ 40 mm and > 40 mm, as well as their risk stratification: low-risk stratification and intermediate/high-risk stratification. Due to the relatively small sample size, Fisher’s exact test was used to compare the differences between the two groups. A p value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Clinical characteristics

From January 2017 to August 2023, 27 patients were included, with an average age of 46.30 ± 18.46 years (range: 10–72 years), including 9 males (33.33%) and 18 females (66.67%). All the cases were pathologically confirmed as EAML following surgery.

The clinical data are summarized in Table 1. Most tumors (20, 74.07%) were found by medical examination without marked symptoms, and the other 7 cases were accompanied by various degrees of backache or lumbar discomfort. Twenty-five cases were isolated (left kidney: 13, right kidney: 12), and 2 cases were multiple and bilateral (Fig. 3). For analysis purposes, only the largest lesion was recorded. Among the 25 isolated lesions, 7 were located in the upper pole of the kidney, 8 in the inferior pole of the kidney, and 10 in the middle. Most of the tumors were exophytic (26), with only 1 growing within the kidney contours. The tumor sizes ranged from 17.0 to 157.0 mm, with an average size of 55.7 ± 38.4 mm. Lymph node involvement was confirmed in 2 patients (7.41%), and a tumor thrombus in a vein (Fig. 4) was confirmed in 1 patient (3.70%).

Radiographic findings

All 27 patients underwent contrast-enhanced CT, and 8 of these patients also underwent MR imaging. Among the 27 tumors, 13 had a fat component, whereas the remaining 14 tumors did not have a significant fat component. On pre-enhanced CT, the solid component of the lesions mostly showed slightly high density. The “Ice cream cone” sign11,12, defined as a well-defined and sharp-angled interface between the lesion and renal parenchyma, was observed in 13 patients (48.15%). Hemorrhage was observed in 3 patients (11.11%), and necrosis and liquefaction were observed in 5 patients (18.52%). Contrast-enhanced CT showed inhomogeneous enhancement of the lesions, and abnormally enlarged and tortuous vessels were observed in 13 lesions (48.15%). Ten lesions exhibited rapid wash-in and wash-out patterns, 13 lesions exhibited persistent enhancement, and 4 lesions exhibited delayed enhancement.

In terms of lesion size, 11 lesions had a maximum transverse diameter of ≤ 40 mm, and 16 lesions had a maximum transverse diameter of > 40 mm. We compared the differences in imaging features between the two groups, including the “Ice cream cone” sign, abnormal blood vessels, and the pattern of CT enhancement. A statistically significant difference was confirmed in the enhancement patterns and presence of abnormal vessels between the two groups (Table 2, p = 0.04 and 0.018, respectively). These findings suggest that smaller lesions tend to present with a “wash-in and wash-out” pattern (Fig. 5), whereas larger lesions are more likely to have abnormal vessels (Fig. 4).

Risk stratification

Nineteen tumors were assessed as low risk, and the remaining eight were assessed as intermediate risk, no high-risk tumors were identified in this study (Tables 1 and 3). Discussion

Clinical characteristics

As a rare category of AML, EAML is regarded as a potentially malignant mesenchymal neoplasm. The clinical features of renal EAML vary and are not specific. In this study, the majority of patients (74.07%) were diagnosed accidently during medical examinations, and the others showed different levels of lumbar pain and discomfort. Other common symptoms included weakness, fever, chronic intermittent abdominal discomfort, hematuria, and weight loss5. Kidney rupture caused by renal EAML has been reported previously13. Like classical AML, EAML can also be associated with TSC. However, none of the patients in this study had TSC. Follow-up data from the Tuberous Sclerosis Registry to Increase Disease Awareness (TOSCA) revealed a greater risk of tumor rupture in patients with TSC14,15, indicating that more cautious screening and treatment are needed for these patients. Some studies have shown that EAML is more often diagnosed in females than in males16–18, whereas others have reported no sex difference19. In our study, the proportion of female patients was significantly greater than that of male patients (18:9). The mixed results of different studies may be caused by the limited sample size of each study. Further investigations with larger cohorts are needed for validation.

Radiographic features

A typical AML is composed of variable proportions of spindle cells, abnormal thick-walled vessels, smooth muscle cells and adipose tissue. It can be easily diagnosed by the identifiable adipose tissue on CT images with CT values less than − 10 Hu. However, an EAML primarily consists of epithelioid cells and contains minimal fat, resulting in a higher density of the tumor. In our study of 27 lesions, 13 lesions presented an identifiable adipose component of high proportion, which makes it even more difficult to differentiate EAML from typical AML. The other lipid-poor lesions exhibited a slightly high density on the CT images, with an average value of 48.29 Hu (range: 37–57 Hu). A previous study reported a value > 45 Hu on pre-enhanced CT5, which is similar to our results. Most (87.5%) of the EAML lesions in our study had low signal intensity on T2WI. Tsukada et al.16 drew similar conclusions, as low signal intensity on T2WI and fat-saturated T2WI is a characteristic manifestation of EAML, possibly because of compactly arranged smooth muscle cells and melanocyte masses20. Other renal tumors, such as papillary renal carcinomas, chromophobe renal carcinomas, and lipid-poor AMLs, can also present with low signal intensity on T2WI21,22, and other features, such as a lobulated shape and invasion of adjacent tissues, can help with differentiation.

The presence of the “Ice cream cone”sign may correlate with tumor growth patterns: benign tumors exhibit limited infiltrative capacity into surrounding tissues, consequently expanding toward areas of lower resistance such as the subcapsular or interlobular regions. This growth pattern results in a straight, well-demarcated tumor-kidney interface, manifesting as a cleft on cross-sectional imaging. In contrast, malignant tumors demonstrate infiltrative growth with relatively uniform resistance from adjacent tissues, leading to isotropic expansion and consequently a more spherical or ovoid morphology, as typically seen in renal cell carcinomas (RCC). Kim et al. demonstrated that EAMLs showed more frequent ice cream cone shape than the clear cell RCCs did (39.3% vs. 5.4%)12. “Ice cream cone” sign were observed in 13 patients in our study, while no significant difference was observed between different groups. As no high-risk cases were included in our study, the observed similarities in growth patterns between low- and intermediate-risk groups should be interpreted with caution given the absence of high-risk comparators, highlighting a direction for future research.

Thickened and tortuous blood vessels were observed in 13 (48.15%) of the lesions after enhancement, which can also be observed in classical AML. These abnormal vessels lack normal morphology and function, leading to a potential risk of hemorrhage. Previous studies have reported EAML cases with enlarged vessels and complicated by acute bleeding17,23. Intratumoral bleeding was observed in 3 cases in this study. Previous studies on classical AML have indicated that bleeding risk is associated with tumor size24 and that the risk of intratumoral bleeding is greatly increased in tumors larger than 40 mm. Our study revealed that abnormal vessels were more common in larger lesions, with a significant difference between the two groups (≤ 40 mm and > 40 mm) (p = 0.018). This finding supports the previous conclusion and suggests that careful monitoring and management should be considered for larger EAMLs to prevent potential bleeding complications.

Intratumoral vessels also affect the enhancement pattern of the tumor. Some researchers suggest that the high density of malformed vessels, decreased tumor stroma, absence of tissue structures with reflux vessels and high cell density within the tumor are the reasons why contrast media is difficult to drain, thus leading to “persistent enhancement” on CT images5,25. This was also confirmed in our study, with most EAML lesions showing “persistent enhancement” and “delayed enhancement” (17 cases, 62.96%) on CT images. However, Cong et al.26 reached the opposite conclusion: “wash-in and wash-out” was the most common enhancement pattern. We further explored the correlation between tumor size and enhancement. Small lesions (≤ 40 mm) were more likely to show “wash-in and wash-out” enhancement, whereas large lesions (> 40 mm) were more likely to show “persistent enhancement” or “delayed enhancement”. These differing enhancement patterns might be due to the different distributions of tumor components in EAML lesions. Thick-walled malformed blood vessels are more likely to appear in larger tumors due to their fast growth, leading to the accumulation of contrast media. Moreover, smaller lesions primarily consist of epithelioid cells. Therefore, understanding these patterns can help in the diagnosis and management of EAML, and provide a potential basis for differentiating EAML from other tumors.

Renal EAML is a potentially malignant tumor with a propensity for metastatic behavior. The liver is the most commonly involved organ, followed by the lungs18,27,28. We admitted one patient (the preoperative images were unavailable; therefore, this patient was not included in our data analysis) with postoperative recurrence and metastasis (Fig. 6). The postoperative pathological diagnosis was AML, therefore, close follow-up was not arranged. Multiple metastases (liver, lung, intra-abdominal and retroperitoneal) were found 6 years after the operation. We removed the major lesion (Fig. 6C), and the postoperative pathological result was EAML. Unfortunately, this patient eventually died of disease progression, highlighting the aggressive behavior of EAML. Different probabilities of recurrence and metastasis have been reported in different studies, ranging from 0 to 50%10,29,30. In this study, although only one patient with tumor thrombus in a vein and two patients with lymph node involvement were observed pathologically, close follow-up was arranged for each patient. Nese et al. reported that larger size, necrosis and lymph node involvement were related to poor prognosis, and according to their research, 64% of tumors in the intermediate risk group experienced disease progression10. Lee et al.31 also confirmed that a larger tumor size is related to metastasis and malignancy. Our findings suggest that higher risk tumors often exhibit “persistent enhancement” and “delayed enhancement” on CT scans, traits that are also prevalent in larger tumors. The heterogeneity of tumor composition means that CT characteristics and biological behaviors can differ significantly5. Our study demonstrates that CT imaging is valuable for assessing the malignancy of renal EAML, while no high-risk EAML cases were included in this study, which limits the generalizability of our findings to more aggressive forms of the disease. Further research involving larger patient groups and extended follow-up durations is essential for confirmation.

This study has several limitations: the small sample size (n = 27) and absence of high-risk cases may reduce statistical power and generalizability. Future multicenter studies with larger cohorts, including high-risk subgroups, are needed to validate these findings.

Conclusions

In conclusion, renal EAML often lacks distinctive clinical and radiographic features. Typically, it presents as a round-shaped hyperdense lesion with minimal or no fat. After contrast enhancement, smaller lesions are more likely to exhibit a rapid “wash-in and wash-out” pattern on CT images, whereas abnormal vessels are more likely to be found in larger lesions. Furthermore, tumors classified as high risk often exhibit sustained or late enhancement. Although the majority of renal EAMLs display benign biological behavior, their potential for malignancy should not be overlooked. Therefore, careful monitoring and follow-up are crucial for patients diagnosed with this condition.

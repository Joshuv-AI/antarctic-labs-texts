Anlotinib-containing regimens in HR+ advanced breast cancer after prior CDK4/6 inhibitor progression
Authors: Yifei Chen, Binliang Liu, Zhanhong Chen, Ping Huang, Xiaohong Wu, Ying Zhang, Tao Sun, Fangyuan Dong, Tao Wu, Hong Zong, Lu Gan, Bin Shao, Quchang Ouyang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This retrospective study (NCT06734533, registered in ClinicalTrials.gov on November 3, 2024) evaluated the efficacy and safety of anlotinib-containing regimens in patients with HR + /HER2− advanced breast cancer who have progressed on prior CDK4/6 inhibitor therapy. From January 2020 to March 2025, 80 patients were included. With a median follow-up of 9.2 months, the median progression-free survival was 6.4 months (95% CI, 5.2–7.1). Age ≥ 60 years and bone metastasis were identified as poor predictors of PFS, whereas prior treatment with CDK4/6 inhibitor for ≥ 6 months was a favorable predictor. The objective response rate and disease control rate were 30.0% (95% CI, 19.6–42.1) and 87.1% (95% CI, 77.3–93.1), respectively. Grade 3–4 adverse events were observed in 10 patients (12.5%), with the most common being neutropenia (3.8%) and aspartate aminotransferase increased (3.8%). Given the retrospective design and potential confounding of this analysis, these findings should be interpreted with caution. Anlotinib-containing regimens showed potential clinical activity in this patient population and warrant further evaluation.

Introduction

Breast cancer is one of the most common cancers worldwide and the major cause of cancer death in women1. Approximately 70% of breast cancers are classified as hormone receptor-positive/human epidermal growth factor receptor 2-negative (HR + /HER2 − ). Targeting the estrogen receptor constitutes the cornerstone of treatment for HR + /HER2− breast cancer; however, resistance to the standard endocrine therapy combined with cyclin-dependent kinase 4/6 inhibitors (CDK4/6i) eventually develops, resulting in treatment failure and disease progression2. This current situation has spurred the development of novel therapeutic agents aimed at reversing or delaying the onset of resistance3–6. Nevertheless, due to the intricate nature of the disease and the risk-benefit imbalance, no therapy demonstrated unequivocal superiority has yet been reported. This highlights the ongoing clinical challenge and unmet need in managing patients with HR + /HER2− advanced breast cancer who have progressed after prior CDK4/6i therapy.

Angiogenesis plays a central role in the carcinogenesis and metastatic potential of breast cancer7,8. Numerous clinical studies have explored the efficacy of the drugs that directly target angiogenic factors in HR + /HER2− breast cancer, such as bevacizumab, sorafenib, and sunitinib, but consistent overall survival (OS) benefits have not been confirmed9–11. Anlotinib is a novel small-molecule tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) with enhanced potency and a broader target profile, capable of effectively blocking tumor progression-related signaling of VEGF/VEGFR, PDGF/PDGFR, FGF/FGFR12,13. Additionally, amplification or activation of FGFR1, a key target of anlotinib, has been shown to activate the PI3K/AKT and RAS/MEK/ERK signaling pathways, mechanisms that are closely associated with resistance to CDK4/6i14. Therefore, in addition to its potent anti-angiogenic activity compared with other TKIs, anlotinib may represent a promising therapeutic strategy for circumventing or delaying resistance to CDK4/6i in HR + /HER2− advanced breast cancer15.

Anlotinib-containing regimens have demonstrated encouraging efficacy with acceptable toxicity in patients with HR + /HER2− advanced breast cancer who progressed on prior treatment16–18. However, the current evidence is still preliminary, derived primarily from small, single-center studies, and lacks support from high-level, randomized clinical trials. We conducted a multicenter, real-world study to evaluate the efficacy and safety of anlotinib-containing regimens in patients with advanced HR + /HER2- breast cancer who had progressed on prior CDK4/6i therapy, aiming to provide additional evidence for the clinical use of this regimen.

Results

Patient Characteristics

Between January 2020 and March 2025, a total of 80 patients with HR + /HER2− breast cancer were identified using the electronic medical record system who received anlotinib-containing treatment. The baseline characteristics are summarized in Table 1. The median age of patients was 54 years (range, 32–81) and the majority of patients were postmenopausal (60.0%). Among the patients, 46 patients (57.5%) had involvement of ≥3 metastatic sites. The commonly metastatic sites included the bones (66.3%), liver (60.0%), and lymph nodes (48.8%). Forty-six patients (57.5%) had received ≥3 lines of prior therapy, including 30 (37.5%) who had undergone ≥4 lines of treatment. Most patients were treated with anlotinib in combination with other agents, primarily chemotherapy (73.8%), followed by endocrine (12.5%); only 2 patients (2.5%) received anlotinib monotherapy. Among the 59 patients treated with anlotinib plus chemotherapy, chemotherapy was administered predominantly as a single-agent regimen, with two patients receiving a dual-agent regimen. The most frequently used single agents were eribulin (25/59, 42.4%), albumin-bound paclitaxel (9/59, 15.3%), irinotecan (9/59, 15.3%), and etoposide (5/59, 8.5%; Supplementary Table 1). Efficacy

As of the data cutoff date (May 2025), the median follow-up was 9.2 months (95% confidence interval [CI], 6.86–11.53). A total of 45 disease progression or death events occurred; the median progression-free survival (PFS) was 6.4 months (95% CI, 5.19–7.13). The 6-month and 1-year PFS rates were 35.7% and 4.3%, respectively (Fig. 1A). The median follow-up for OS was 15.7 months (95% CI, 9.13–19.36), and the data are immature (Fig. 1B). An exploratory OS analysis based on the patients with confirmed survival status is provided in the Supplementary Fig 1. By multivariate analysis, age ≥ 60 years (p = 0.018; HR = 2.44, 95% CI, 1.17–5.10), and the presence of bone metastasis (p = 0.035; HR = 2.14, 95% CI, 1.06–4.33) were identified as independent poor predictive factors for PFS. In contrast, prior treatment with CDK4/6i for ≥ 6 months (p = 0.029; HR = 0.48, 95% CI: 0.25–0.93) was found to be an independent favorable prognostic factor for PFS (Fig. 1C). The overall incidence of AEs was 77.5% (n = 62), and the most common AEs among patients included alanine aminotransferase increased (31.3%), leukopenia (25.0%), anemia (22.5%), and neutropenia (20.0%). Grade 3-4 AEs occurred in 10 patients (12.5%), with the most common being neutropenia (3.8%) and aspartate aminotransferase increased (3.8%). AEs led to dose reduction in 6 patients (7.5%), with causes including gum bleeding, hypertension, toe pain, thrombocytopenia, and other reasons. One patient (1.3%) discontinued treatment due to abnormal coagulation time. No patient deaths were attributed to AEs.

In the anlotinib plus chemotherapy cohort (n = 59), AEs were reported in 44 patients (74.6%), with the most common being alanine aminotransferase increased (28.8%), leukopenia (25.4%), and anemia (23.7%). Grade 3-4 AEs occurred in 8 patients (13.6%). In the anlotinib plus endocrine therapy cohort (n = 10), AEs of any grade were reported in 9 patients (90.0%), and no grade 3-4 AEs were observed (Supplementary Table 4).

Discussion

For patients with HR + /HER2− advanced breast cancer who have progressed on prior CDK4/6i therapy, there remains a lack of clearly superior treatment strategy. Although anlotinib-containing treatment has demonstrated encouraging efficacy for this population, but limited by the small sample size (n = 26-48) and single-center designs16–18. In our study, anlotinib-containing regimens were associated with a median PFS of 6.4 months, an ORR of 30.0%, and a DCR of 87.1%, with an acceptable safety profile.

The key contribution of this study lies in providing real-world evidence to support the clinical application of anlotinib-containing regimens. Our study showed that anlotinib-containing treatment achieved a median PFS of 6.4 months, which aligns with the outcomes from recent similar studies (5.1–7.6 months) among patients with disease progression following prior treatment16,19,20. For the other alternative treatment strategies after the failure of CDK4/6i, such as chemotherapy monotherapy or other targeted drugs (e.g., mTOR and HDAC inhibitors or other replacement CDK4/6i), yielded a median PFS of approximately 5 months or less21–25. The BYLieve trial showed that the PI3K inhibitor alpelisib plus fulvestrant had a median PFS of 7.3 months in patients who had progressed on immediate prior CDK4/6i; however, the benefit was limited to those with PIK3CA-mutant HR + /HER2− breast cancer26. Similarly, the EMBER-3 and evERA trials explored additional endocrine-based strategies following CDK4/6i failure, showing that further suppression of estrogen receptor signaling could modestly prolong PFS in patients with endocrine-sensitive disease or specific molecular subgroups, such as those harboring ESR1 mutations27,28. Even with more aggressive approaches, such as sacituzumab govitecan (SG), an antibody-drug conjugate and a standard of care for HR + /HER2− metastatic breast cancer after prior endocrine therapy and chemotherapy, patients achieved a median PFS of only 5.5 months in the TROPiCS-02 study, in which patients had received a median of 3 prior lines of chemotherapy29. When SG was administered earlier, as first-line chemotherapy following endocrine therapy, the ASCENT-07 study reported a median PFS of 8.3 months in patients with HR + /HER2− breast cancer; however, this outcome did not demonstrate superiority over the physician’s choice chemotherapy30. In this context, the efficacy observed in our study was encouraging, especially given that our study included a substantially more heavily pretreated population, with 57.5% of patients having received ≥3 prior lines of therapy and 30% having undergone ≥2 prior lines of chemotherapy. However, our regimen appears to be less effective than the findings of the DESTINY-Breast04 trial, which reported a median PFS of 10 months31,32. Although the median number of prior treatment lines in our study was consistent with this trial, our study specifically focused on later-line chemotherapy settings (≥2 prior lines, 30%), and notably included patients who had received up to five prior lines of chemotherapy. In contrast to the ORR and DCR reported with other treatments (ORR, 17.1–21%; DCR, 53.7–76%)23,25,26, our regimen yielded rates of 30.0% and 87.1%, respectively, offering additional insights for the clinical activity of our regimen. However, cross-trial comparisons are descriptive only, and it was important to note that the majority of patients received anlotinib in combination with other agents, and therefore, the reported efficacy, including ORR, should be interpreted with caution and cannot be ascribed solely to the intrinsic antitumor activity of anlotinib.

The multivariate analysis identified several prognostic factors, including age ≥60 years, prior CDK4/6i treatment for ≥6 months, and the presence of bone metastasis. It was not unexpected that older patients exhibited poor prognosis, as age was recognized as a non-modifiable risk factor for breast cancer33,34. However, considering the small subset that does participate often is not representative of older patients in general, the survival benefit for anlotinib-containing treatment remains to be further confirmed. Additionally, bone is one of the most frequent sites of distant dissemination in breast cancer patients, negatively affecting patient survival, and most available treatments only remain palliative35,36. Previous studies have suggested that bone metastases may be more indolent in HR+ breast cancer; however, this typically applies to patients with bone-only metastases and is relative to those with concomitant visceral or central nervous system involvement37. Importantly, bone metastases could progress to secondary sites, contributing to overall disease burden and complicating clinical management. In prior studies of HR + /HER2− breast cancer, 77.0% of patients with bone metastases also had multiple metastatic sites, and survival was significantly lower in patients with multiple metastases compared with those with a single metastatic site. Focusing specifically on patients with bone metastases, PFS after subsequent therapy was significantly shorter than in patients without bone involvement (HR 1.56, 95% CI 1.20–2.02; p = 0.001)38. Similarly, in our study, patients with bone metastases appeared to derive less benefit from treatment (HR 2.14, 95% CI 1.06–4.33; p = 0.035), which may be attributable to a population with high tumor burden, as 57.5% of patients had three or more metastatic sites and the median number of prior systemic therapies was three. Notably, prior CDK4/6i may have a sustained positive impact on subsequent lines of treatment39, especially for those who received CDK4/6i treatment for ≥ 6 months in our study. The rapid disease progression in other patients may lead to limited clinical benefits from subsequent anlotinib-containing treatment.

In the present study, our data were extracted from unstructured clinical text within electronic medical records, which do not follow a standardized format, making AE documentation potentially incomplete or inconsistent, particularly for mild or subjective symptoms. Therefore, AEs related to anlotinib alone were not systematically reported. Nevertheless, anlotinib-containing regimens were associated with a manageable safety profile. No new safety signals have been observed in this study. The most common AE, alanine aminotransferase increased, white leukopenia, and anemia, which were consistent with those reported for other studies16,20. High-incidence toxicities such as hypertension and hand-foot syndrome caused by anti-angiogenic drugs were rarely observed in our study. However, hematologic toxicities should be interpreted with caution, as the majority of patients received concomitant chemotherapy, which is well known to induce myelosuppression. Grade 3-4 anemia and neutropenia were observed exclusively in patients treated with anlotinib plus chemotherapy. Nevertheless, the relatively small sample sizes in the endocrine and other non-chemotherapy cohorts may have limited the ability to detect severe hematologic events. Furthermore, previous studies of alternative agents have reported toxicity-related treatment discontinuation and dose reduction rates ranging from 6% to 20.9% and 9.1% to 29%, respectively22,24,25,40–42. These events were relatively infrequent with our regimen (1.3% and 7.5%, respectively). Similarly, the incidence of grade 3 or worse AEs followed a consistent pattern when compared to our study with other studies (12.5% vs. 27–55.2%)42,43. However, cross-trial comparisons should be interpreted cautiously due to differences in study design, patient populations, and treatment regimens. Taken together, these findings suggest that anlotinib-containing regimens may have a manageable safety profile in this setting. Further prospective studies are warranted to better define the safety profile of anlotinib in combination regimens.

The main limitation of this study was the retrospective design, as it may introduce selection bias and confounding related to treatment choice, depending on the accuracy of historical records. Although inclusion and exclusion criteria were predefined, they may have further influenced the characteristics of the study population. Moreover, the retrospective identification of patients from electronic medical records may have contributed additional inherent biases, highlighting the need for cautious interpretation of the findings. Additionally, our study population was heterogeneous in terms of menopausal status, prior treatment, and the type of anlotinib-containing combination regimen. Importantly, because most patients received anlotinib combination therapy, the specific contribution of anlotinib to both efficacy and safety outcomes cannot be definitively separated from that of the accompanying agents. This limitation also restricts the ability to attribute individual AE specifically to anlotinib, and the reported toxicity profile should therefore be interpreted as reflecting the overall treatment regimen rather than anlotinib alone. The absence of recorded information regarding the efficacy and safety of prior treatment before switching to anlotinib precludes direct comparisons between therapies, which may limit the ability to demonstrate the relative superiority of anlotinib in this setting. Given the small sample size in some subgroups, the reliability of the findings was limited, and these results should be interpreted cautiously as exploratory observations that require further validation. Considering the OS data remains immature, interpretation of these findings should be cautious. Whether the observed PFS benefit with anlotinib-containing regimens will ultimately translate into prolongation of OS remains uncertain and warrants further investigation. Besides, this study was conducted exclusively in Chinese institutions, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to other populations and healthcare settings. Although Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.1 criteria were applied, tumor response assessments were performed locally without centralized imaging review, which may have introduced inter-center variability. Finally, low-grade AEs may have been underreported in routine clinical practice, potentially resulting in an underestimation of the total toxicity burden.

In conclusion, anlotinib-containing regimens showed promising efficacy with a manageable toxicity profile in patients with HR + /HER2- advanced breast cancer who had progressed on prior CDK4/6i therapy. Future large-scale clinical trials with a long-term follow-up period are warranted to validate the benefits of anlotinib-containing regimens and their prognostic implications.

Methods

Study design

This multicenter, retrospective, observational study of patients with advanced HR + /HER2− breast cancer was conducted by 8 centers. Patients were retrospectively identified from the electronic medical record system using predefined eligibility criteria, including diagnosis and treatment records. Consecutive eligible patients treated between January 2020 and March 2025 were included in the analysis. Only patients who received anlotinib-based regimen after progression on CDK4/6i-based therapy were identified and included in this study. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of each center (center ethics approval number from Hunan Cancer Hospital was 2024-47) and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, the international standards of good clinical practice, applicable local laws and regulations. The requirement of written informed consent was waived by the institutional review board due to the retrospective nature of our study. This study is registered with ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT06734533 on November 3, 2024).

Patients

Inclusion criteria for this study were patients aged ≥18 years with HR + /HER2− breast cancer; previously treated with at least one line of CDK4/6i in combination with endocrine (e.g., aromatase inhibitor, fulvestrant) adjuvant or rescue therapy; received at least one anlotinib-containing regimen after disease progression on CDK4/6i treatment; Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-1; had measurable lesions according to RECIST version 1.1. HR positivity was defined as ≥1% nuclear staining of estrogen receptor (ER) and/or progesterone receptor (PR) by immunohistochemistry (IHC). HER2 negativity was defined as IHC 0 or 1 + , or IHC 2+ with negative fluorescence in situ hybridization results.

We excluded patients who had a second primary malignant tumor; previously discontinued prior CDK4/6i treatment for reasons other than disease progression; exhibited fluid collection in the third space that cannot be controlled by drainage or other interventions; previously treated with other anti-angiogenic therapy, including small-molecule agents (e.g., apatinib) and large-molecule agents (e.g., bevacizumab); or were receiving concurrent treatment for any other malignancy.

Dosing regimen and data collection

Patients were treated with anlotinib monotherapy or combination therapy. Anlotinib was given orally at 8 mg, 10 mg, or 12 mg once daily for 2 weeks, followed by one week off, completing a 21-day cycle. The other combination drugs were not restricted, and the dosages were administered under the recommended instructions.

Patient demographics and baseline characteristics, including patient age, menopausal status, ER status, PR status, Ki-67 expression level, ECOG performance status, dominant metastatic sites, number of metastatic sites, and previous treatment history, including type of CDK4/6i therapy and type of combination regimens. The following data of anlotinib therapy, including the initial dose of anlotinib, efficacy and safety of anlotinib-based treatment, cause of dose adjustments, and survival outcomes, were obtained retrospectively from the electronic medical record system.

Outcomes

The primary efficacy outcome was PFS, defined as the time from the initiation of anlotinib-based treatment after progression on CDK4/6i regimen to the date of disease progression or death. The secondary efficacy outcomes included ORR, defined as the proportion of patients achieving complete response (CR) or PR; DCR, defined as the percentage of CR, PR, and SD; OS, defined as the time from the initiation of anlotinib-based treatment after progression on CDK4/6i regimen to the date of death. AEs were recorded and graded according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0.

Statistical analysis

Efficacy analyses were performed on all included patients and patients who had at least one assessment of tumor response, respectively. Safety was analyzed in patients who had a documented safety profile. Categorical variables are summarized by percentage and continuous variables are summarized by median. Survival data were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method with corresponding 2-sided 95% confidence intervals (CIs). For PFS analysis, patients without disease progression or death were censored at the date of the last follow-up. Subgroup analysis for PFS was evaluated by multivariate Cox regression analysis. A prespecified “full model” strategy: all clinically relevant baseline and pathological covariates were entered into the multivariate models irrespective of univariable significance. Backward stepwise Cox regression as a sensitivity analysis. Backward elimination was performed with a p-value criterion of 0.10. OS was also assessed in patients with confirmed survival status within the study cohort. For OS analysis, patients were censored at the date of last known survival status. For ORR and DCR, the associated 95% CIs were assessed with the Clopper-Pearson method. Data were statistically analyzed using SAS 9.4. Tests were two-sided, with p < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

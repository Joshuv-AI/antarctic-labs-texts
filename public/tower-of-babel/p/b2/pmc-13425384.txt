NOCT, a potential domestication gene impacting circadian regulation and behavioral adaptation during dog domestication
Authors: Jian Xiao, Xiao-Long Tu, Wen-Hua Yuan, Yang-Wan-Zi Song, Zhi-Fan Guo, Jie Li, Jin-Xiu Li, Jing Luo, Ya-Ping Zhang, Yan Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
As the only carnivore domesticated by humans, the evolutionary transition of proto-domestic canids into human-associated niches remains a central unresolved question in domestication biology. Although prior work has emphasized behavioral transformations during domestication, the circadian shift from crepuscular activity in gray wolves to predominantly diurnal patterns in dogs has received comparatively little attention, despite its importance in synchronizing with human daily cycles. This study examined highly diverged loci linked to circadian system remodeling, a putative early domestication trait that likely preceded intentional human selection. Comparative genomic analyses identified NOCT as a candidate domestication gene, characterized by pronounced indel frequency divergence between gray wolves and dogs. Functional assays demonstrated that reduced NOCT expression disrupted circadian rhythmicity and was accompanied by increased myelin sheath formation in the prefrontal cortex. These neurophysiological alterations were associated with marked behavioral shifts, including extended exploratory locomotion, elevated fear and anxiety responses, and enhanced short-term memory. Collectively, these phenotypic outcomes provide empirical support for the self-domestication hypothesis and offer a mechanistic framework explaining how ancestral scavenging wolves gradually adapted to human-modified environments. Furthermore, these findings elucidate a previously underappreciated circadian-neurodevelopmental axis in canid domestication and advance understanding of the evolutionary processes that enabled the emergence of dogs as the only carnivore companion species for humans.

INTRODUCTION

Dogs (Canis lupus familiaris) represent the sole carnivore lineage to have undergone successful domestication, originating from gray wolves (Canis lupus lupus) during the Upper Paleolithic period (Bergström et al., 2020; Frantz et al., 2016; Freedman & Wayne, 2017; Wang et al., 2016). Early domestication was marked by a pronounced behavioral transition from fear-associated aggression toward tameness (Wang et al., 2014), together with the emergence of specialized socio-cognitive traits that facilitate interaction with humans (Hare et al., 2002; Topál et al., 2009). Genome-wide investigations have documented rapid evolution across multiple loci linked to neural development and function (Li et al., 2013; Pendleton et al., 2018; Sutter & Ostrander, 2004; Zhang et al., 2020), providing a genetic foundation for behavioral transformation and subsequent breed diversification. Despite these advances, uncertainty persists regarding which traits constituted primary targets during the earliest phases of domestication and how selection initially operated across candidate loci.

Beyond behavioral modification, domestication in dogs involved a striking reorganization of circadian activity. In contrast to wild canids, including red foxes, Arctic foxes, Arctic wolves, and gray wolves, which predominantly exhibit nocturnal or crepuscular patterns (Campbell & Tobler, 1984; Gittleman, 1986), dogs display a characteristic diurnal rhythm. This chronobiological shift likely reflects an evolutionary adaptation to synchronize with human activity cycles (Bódizs et al., 2020), potentially increasing vigilance and enhancing sensitivity to human-derived cues during socio-ecological integration (Kortekaas & Kotrschal, 2019). Compared with extensively studied behavioral traits, circadian remodeling has received limited attention. Our previous microarray-based analysis with restricted single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) coverage identified the circadian clock gene NOCT (CCRN4L) as exhibiting extreme allele frequency divergence between wolves and non-breed dogs (Li et al., 2013). NOCT functions as a clock-controlled, broadly expressed gene involved in development, adipogenesis, lipid metabolism, inflammatory regulation, osteogenesis, and obesity (Kulshrestha & Devkar, 2023). However, the genetic architecture underlying this circadian transition and the downstream biological consequences remain largely unknown.

Circadian rhythm realignment from nocturnal to diurnal activity has been demonstrated in laboratory rats and is accompanied by increased overall activity levels (Stryjek et al., 2013), a shift hypothesized to have facilitated early encounters between wolves and humans (Trut et al., 2009). Converging evidence further implicates circadian regulation as a central driver of neuroplasticity and cognitive performance (Gerstner & Yin, 2010; Lehr et al., 2021; Reid et al., 2011; Walker & Stickgold, 2006), traits that directly underpin social cognition and affiliative behavior in dogs (Hare et al., 2002; Topál et al., 2009). Together, these observations support a conceptual framework in which circadian system remodeling represents a previously underexplored axis for resolving the evolutionary puzzle of canine domestication.

Most prior genomic studies of dog domestication have focused almost exclusively on SNPs. Although several investigations have examined insertions, deletions, and structural variants associated with morphological diversification (Axelsson et al., 2013; Meadows et al., 2023; Plassais et al., 2019; Serres-Armero et al., 2021), no work has specifically investigated indels associated with circadian adaptation. Given the comparatively larger functional impact of indels relative to SNPs (Kronenberg et al., 2018), 365 globally distributed canine genomes were integrated to systematically identify any chronotype-associated indels that showed dog-specific divergence.

Population-level comparisons identified NOCT as a candidate circadian regulator potentially targeted during primary domestication. This genomic signal was extended through transcriptomic and behavioral analyses to evaluate how reduced NOCT expression altered circadian rhythmicity, locomotor activity, fear-related responses, and memory performance, traits hypothesized to be core features during the initial domestication process.

Elucidating the pleiotropic pathways through which NOCT-mediated chronobiological remodeling reshaped proto-domestic canid behavior deepens understanding of early genetic transitions during dog domestication and provides mechanistic insight into the evolutionary processes that drove successful domestication of the sole carnivore companion species for humans.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Canid genomic analysis

Sample collection

A total of 381 publicly available canid whole-genome sequencing datasets were downloaded from the NCBI Sequence Read Archive (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sra/). Following stringent quality filtration, 365 high-quality genomes were retained for downstream analyses, including 35 gray wolves, five outgroup canids (three coyotes, one golden jackal, and one Andean fox), and 325 domestic dogs (Supplementary Table S1).

Raw data processing and quality control

Initial evaluation included verification of file integrity and generation of summary statistics for each dataset. Sequence quality was assessed using FastQC (v0.11.9) to examine base quality distribution, read length profiles, and nucleotide composition. Adapter sequences and low-quality reads were removed using Cutadapt (v3.0). Duplicate sequences were identified and flagged using Picard. Cleaned reads were subsequently prepared for alignment to the reference genome.

Read alignment and insertion-deletion (indel) calling

All high-quality reads were aligned to the dog reference genome (CanFam3.1; GCA_000002285.2) using BWA (v.0.7.17, parameters: mem -t 4 -k 32 -M -R). Duplicate reads were removed using Picard Tools (v.1.119). Indels were detected using the HaplotypeCaller module implemented in GATK (v.4.0). Raw indels were filtered according to the following criteria: (1) QD<2.0 || FS>200.0 || InbreedingCoeff<−0.8 || ReadPosRankSum<−20.0 || SOR>10.0; (2) restriction to biallelic sites; (3) overall sequencing depth across all individuals greater than 1/3× and less than 3×; (4) site-level missing rate <20%; and (5) individual-level variant missing rate <10%. The resulting high-confidence indels were annotated using ANNOVAR.

Population structure analysis

Phylogenetic relationships were reconstructed by first calculating pairwise P-distance matrices using VCF2Dis (https://github.com/BGI-shenzhen/VCF2Dis). Neighbor-joining trees were generated with the R package ape and visualized using Newick Utilities. Principal component analysis (PCA) was conducted in PLINK (v.1.9, parameters: --bfile pca --pca 3 --chr-set 90) to summarize major axes of genomic variation. Population genetic structure was inferred using ADMIXTURE (v.1.3).

Selective sweep analysis

Genome-wide patterns of genetic differentiation (FST) and nucleotide diversity (θπ) were quantified using VCFtools (v.0.1.17) with a sliding-window approach (20 kb windows and 10 kb steps). The θπ ratio was estimated as the quotient of wolf_θπ divided by dog_θπ. Genomic windows within the top 5% of both log2(θπ ratio) and FST distributions were designated as candidate regions under selection and visualized in R. Functional enrichment analyses of genes located within candidate windows were performed using KOBAS and DAVID for Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway annotation.

Canid transcriptome analysis

Gene expression profiles across 10 dog tissues generated using the Affymetrix Canine Version 2.0 microarray platform were downloaded from the Gene Expression Omnibus (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo; accession number GSE20113). Probes with potential cross-hybridization to multiple genomic loci were excluded based on array annotations. For each gene, expression values were averaged across four biological replicates. RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) data from the frontal cortex of wolves and dogs were obtained from previous research (Albert et al., 2012). Transcript assembly and gene-level expression quantification were performed using TopHat (Trapnell et al., 2009) and Cufflinks (Trapnell et al., 2010).

Validation of candidate gene in the mouse model

Animal ethics

All animal experiments were conducted in accordance with national and international guidelines for laboratory animal care and use. All experimental procedures were reviewed and approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee at Yunnan University (IACUC, YNU) (Approval No.: Yuncae20200302).

Transgenic mouse models

A conditional NOCT knockout mouse model was generated by the Shanghai Model Organisms Center, Inc. (China) using CRISPR/Cas9-mediated gene editing. Targeting exon 3 of the Noct-201 transcript (ENSMUST00000023849.14), homologous recombination was achieved in fertilized C57BL/6J mouse zygotes. Specifically, Cas9 mRNA and gRNA were obtained through in vitro transcription, and a donor vector containing a 2.9 kb 5' homologous arm, 1.6 kb flox region, and 3.6 kb 3' homologous arm was assembled by In-Fusion cloning. Cas9 mRNA, gRNA, and donor vector were microinjected into fertilized eggs to generate homologous recombinant mice. Floxed homozygous animals were subsequently crossed with Cre recombinase-expressing mice, resulting in excision of the floxed region and functional deletion of NOCT in specific tissues and cell types.

AAV-mediated gene silencing

An adeno-associated viral (AAV) vector (CAP.B10) designed for NOCT knockdown was produced by Obio Technology (Shanghai) Co, Ltd. (China). The viral target site corresponded to exon 3 of the NOCT-201 transcript, consistent with the conditional knockout design. Short hairpin RNA (shRNA) sequences targeting mouse NOCT were cloned into the AAV backbone to achieve gene interference. Following intravenous administration, viral particles preferentially transduced brain tissue, leading to reduced NOCT expression. The vector encoded a green fluorescent reporter to facilitate assessment of transduction efficiency.

Animal housing and experimental timing

Mice were housed under standardized 12:12 h light-dark conditions (lights on at 0800h, corresponding to zeitgeber time ZT0). Behavioral testing was primarily performed between 0900h and 1200h (ZT1–ZT4), with limited supplementary sessions conducted before 0600h (ZT10). To minimize circadian-related variability, experimental and control cohorts were assessed in parallel using separate apparatus, ensuring temporal alignment across groups.

Tissue collection and preservation

For molecular analyses, tissues were collected synchronously between 0900h and 1200h (ZT1–ZT4) to reduce circadian effects on gene expression and protein levels. Mice were euthanized via cervical dislocation in accordance with institutional ethical approval to minimize stress-related artifacts and RNA degradation. Immediately after dissection, frontal cortex samples were immersed in RNAlater (Thermo Fisher Scientific), incubated at 4°C overnight to ensure complete penetration, and subsequently stored at −80°C until RNA extraction.

Real-time reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR)

RT-qPCR was performed on mouse frontal cortex tissue using TB Green Premix Ex Taq Ⅱ (Takara, Japan) in accordance with manufacturer guidelines, with signal acquisition conducted on a QuantStudio 5 system (ABI, USA) using a two-step amplification protocol. β-actin served as the internal reference, and relative transcript abundance was calculated using the 2-ΔΔCT method. Primer pairs included 5'CGGTGCTCGGATCTCG3' and 5'TGCTGTTGACGGTCTTGG3' for NOCT (spanning exons 1 and 2) and 5'CGTTGACATCCGTAAAGACC3' and 5'AACAGTCCGCCTAGAAGCAC3' for β-actin. Thermal cycling conditions consisted of an initial denaturation at 95°C for 10 min, followed by 40 amplification cycles at 95°C for 10 s and 60°C for 30 s.

Western blot analysis

Whole brains were collected from mice, and frontal cortex regions were carefully dissected and homogenized in RIPA lysis buffer (Sigma, USA) containing phenylmethylsulfonyl fluoride (PMSF) (Thermo, USA). Lysates were centrifuged at 10 000 ×g for 10 min at 4°C, and supernatants were retained and stored at −80°C for future use. Total protein concentrations were determined using an Enhanced BCA Protein Assay Kit (Beyotime, China). Samples were denatured in sample buffer by boiling for 10 min (Takara, Japan), and 20 μg of protein per lane was resolved using 15% sodium dodecyl-sulfate polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE). The resolved proteins were transferred to polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes (Amersham, UK), which were then blocked with 5% milk in Tris-buffered saline containing 0.1% Tween-20. Immunodetection was performed using rabbit-derived anti-NOCT primary antibody (~48 kDa) (1:1 000; Abcam, UK), followed by goat anti-rabbit secondary antibody (1:1 000, Beyotime, China).

Behavioral assays

Assessment of circadian behavior

Male mice at adolescent and aged stages (6 weeks and 18 months) were housed individually throughout the experimental period. Upon transfer to the animal facility, mice were acclimated for 1 week without disturbance. Continuous video monitoring was then conducted to record all locomotor activity, rest duration, and sleep patterns (five young and five aged males). Behavioral states were classified into two categories: inactive wakefulness, defined as lying posture with elevated head position and open eyes; and resting, defined as lying posture with lowered head position and predominantly closed eyes. The former was scored as wakefulness and the latter as sleep, following established criteria (Kortekaas & Kotrschal, 2019).

Design of behavioral experiments

Mice entering the adolescent stage were housed individually throughout the experimental period. Upon transfer to the animal facility, mice were acclimated for 1 week without disturbance, followed by gentle handling on three consecutive days. Behavioral assays were performed sequentially, with a 5 min elevated plus maze (EPM) session followed 24 h later by a 30 min open field test (OFT) (Knight et al., 2021). Experiments were performed independently in female and male cohorts.

Open field test

Enhanced diurnal exploratory locomotion has been proposed to have facilitated early interactions between scavenging wolves and ancient humans under the self-domestication hypothesis (Hewson, 2003; Li et al., 2014; Tancredi & Cardinali, 2023). Thus, diurnally exploratory activity was quantified using the OFT. Mice were first placed in a transparent Plexiglas arena (50 cm long×50 cm wide×50 cm high, Shanghai Xinruan Information Technology Co. Ltd., China) fitted with an overhead camera and photobeam tracking system. The arena was partitioned into nine zones, comprising eight corner areas and one central region. Each mouse was introduced into the central area at trial onset. Total distance traveled and mean locomotor velocity were recorded over 30 min using SuperMaze. Testing occurred during daytime hours (0900h–1200h) at room temperature (NOCTflox/flox: experimental group, males n=17, females n=17; NOCTwt: control group, males n=8, females n=8; NOCTAAV: experimental group, males n=11, females n=11; and NOCTctrl: control group, males n=10, females n=10).

Elevated plus maze

Attenuation of fear-related responses has been proposed as a prerequisite for reduced avoidance of humans during domestication under the tame-selection hypothesis (Belyaev, 1969; Trut et al., 2009). Thus, fear-associated behavior was evaluated using the EPM, which exploits innate aversion to open and elevated spaces (File et al., 1993; Handley & Mithani, 1984; Hilton et al., 2023; Mendes-Gomes et al., 2011; Walf & Frye, 2007). The apparatus comprised two open arms (35 cm long× 5 cm wide), two closed arms (35 cm long×5 cm wide), and a central platform (5 cm long×5 cm wide) elevated 50 cm above the ground (Shanghai Xinruan Information Technology Co. Ltd., China). Each mouse was placed individually on the maze central platform and allowed to explore freely for 5 min. Movement trajectories were captured using an overhead camera. Testing was conducted during the daytime (0900h–1200h) at room temperature (NOCTflox/flox: experimental group, males n=11, females n=11; NOCTwt: control group, males n=9, females n=9; NOCTAAV: experimental group, males n=11, females n=11; and NOCTctrl: control group, males n=10, females n=10).

Y-maze

Learning and memory enhancement has been proposed to increase the likelihood that scavenging wolves could persist near humans and enter early domestication pathways under the self-domestication hypothesis (Li et al., 2014). Thus, contextual memory performance was assessed using the Y-maze paradigm. The maze consisted of three identical arms arranged at 120° angles (21 cm long×7 cm wide×15.5 cm high; Shanghai Xinruan Information Technology Co. Ltd., China), each equipped with movable gates to regulate access. Arms were randomly assigned as novel, start, or other. Behavioral activity was recorded using overhead cameras. Experiments were conducted during the daytime (0900h–1200h) at room temperature (NOCTflox/flox: experimental group, males n=6, females n=6; and NOCTwt: control group, males n=8, females n=8).

Functional assay on the mouse model

Brain tissue preparation and histological processing

Fresh brains were harvested and placed in 4% paraformaldehyde fixative solution at room temperature for 24 h. Fixed tissue underwent sequential cryoprotection in 10%, 20%, and 30% sucrose solutions, followed by infiltration with optimal cutting temperature (OTC) compound (Sakura, Japan) for 30 min. Embedded samples were rapidly frozen at −80°C. Coronal sections (10 μm) were generated using a frozen microtome and stored at −80°C until analysis.

Myelin staining

Frozen sections were equilibrated to room temperature, air-dried at 37℃ for 20 min, and fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 20 min. After three washes (5 min each) in 1× phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), sections were incubated with FluoroMyelin Red dye for 20 min, followed by three additional PBS washes (5 min each). Slides were mounted using anti-fluorescence quenching tablets containing 4',6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI). Experimental and control groups each included three mice, with analyses focused on cerebral cortex sections.

Mitochondrial quantification

Mitochondria were isolated from brain tissue using a commercial extraction kit (Beyotime, China), followed by mitochondrial lysis to obtain mitochondrial protein fractions (Acín-Pérez et al., 2023). Total mitochondrial protein was quantified and used as a proxy for mitochondrial content.

Transcriptomic analysis

Raw RNA-seq reads were subjected to quality assessment using FastQC (v.0.11.9) with aggregated reports generated by MultiQC (v.1.9). Adapter sequences and low-quality bases were trimmed using Trimmomatic (v.0.39). Processed reads were aligned to the reference genome (GRCm37) using STAR (v.2.7.10b) in two-pass mode with default splicing parameters. Transcript abundance estimation was carried out with RSEM (v.1.3.3) using an expectation-maximization algorithm, yielding transcripts per million (TPM) and fragments per kilobase of transcript per million mapped reads (FPKM) estimates. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified using DESeq2 (v.1.30.1) with |log2FC|>1 and FDR-adjusted P<0.05. Functional enrichment of DEGs was evaluated using KOBAS and DAVID for GO annotation. Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) was performed using the WGCNA package with soft-thresholding power determined by scale-free topology criteria.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses and graphical visualizations were performed in GraphPad Prism (v.8.4.0, GraphPad Software, USA) and R (v.4.2.3). Comparisons between control and experimental groups were evaluated using Student’s t-test. Statistical significance was defined as follows: *: P<0.05; **: P<0.01; ***: P<0.001; ****: P<0.0001.

RESULTS

Adaptive evolution of indels at the NOCT locus and potential effects on transcriptional attenuation

Domestication-related traits are expected to be detectable in non-breed domesticated populations, and in the absence of such populations, traits consistently observed across breeds may reflect domestication processes (Lord et al., 2020). To investigate indel-mediated selection during early domestication, resequencing data from 365 canids were analyzed, including 112 non-breed dogs, 213 dogs representing 110 breeds, 35 Eurasian gray wolves, and five outgroup canids (three coyotes, one golden jackal, and one Andean fox) (Supplementary Table S1).

From 7 530 193 high-confidence indels, dogs were clearly separated from their closest wild relative (gray wolves) based on neighbor-joining phylogenetic reconstruction (Figure 1A), PCA (Figure 1B), and ADMIXTURE clustering (Figure 1C). Population-level comparisons identified 1 642 genes within the top 5% of fixation index (FST) values and 2 103 genes within the top 5% of reduced nucleotide diversity (θπ ratio), with 754 genes shared between both metrics. Functional annotation revealed eight circadian rhythm-associated genes (GO:0007623) among these candidates, including KCNH7, LEP, MYCBP2, NCOR1, NOCT, RAI1, SREBF1, and TOP1 (Figure 1D, E). Expression profiling across 10 different dog tissues and frontal cortex samples from five dogs and six wolves demonstrated that only NOCT satisfied two key criteria: preferential enrichment in the dog frontal cortex relative to other tissues, and significantly reduced expression in dog cortex compared with wolf cortex (Student’s t-test, P=0.0017; Figure 1F, G). None of the remaining circadian candidates exhibited brain-biased expression or interspecies transcriptional divergence (Supplementary Figure S1). NOCT directly regulates post-transcriptional control of core circadian clock components and coordinates circadian regulation of metabolic pathways (Stubblefield et al., 2012), whereas the other candidates primarily function downstream in metabolic or neuronal processes rather than rhythm generation (Supplementary Table S2). Independent evidence further implicates NOCT in neural circuit formation and behavior, with cell type-specific knockdown disrupting presynaptic motor axon guidance (Lobb-Rabe et al., 2022) and altering behavioral phenotypes (Chen et al., 2018). Accordingly, these observations prioritized NOCT for investigation of indel-driven regulatory divergence and behavioral relevance.

Three indels flanking NOCT exhibited pronounced allele frequency shifts between wolves and dogs, with derived alleles completely absent in all outgroups (Figure 1H). Two variants located within the first intron (chr19:3593363, TCC insertion; chr19:3602081, AACT deletion) exhibited near-complete linkage disequilibrium (r2=0.90), whereas a third TG deletion occurred approximately 6 kb downstream (chr19:3586513). All three sites overlapped predicted transcription factor binding sites (Supplementary Table S3), implying potential regulatory roles. Given evidence for enhancer redundancy in tissue-biased genes (Spielmann et al., 2018), which may buffer individual intronic perturbations, functional assessment focused on the downstream TG deletion. Dual-fluorescence reporter assays in Madin-Darby canine kidney (MDCK) cells demonstrated significantly reduced transcriptional activity for constructs carrying the downstream deletion relative to constructs containing intact sequence or upstream variants (Figure 1I). This regulatory reduction aligned with the observed down-regulation of NOCT expression in dog frontal cortex, supporting a mechanistic link between the downstream indel and transcriptional down-regulation during domestication.

NOCT knockdown mice exhibit increased daytime wakefulness

Previous studies have established a central role for NOCT in energy metabolism and circadian regulation (Green et al., 2007; Le et al., 2019; Stubblefield et al., 2012). To determine whether reduced NOCT expression in a nocturnal mammalian model alters behavioral rhythms and promotes adaptation to human-modified environments, a conditional NOCT knockout line (NOCTflox/flox:Cre+) was generated using a Cre-dependent CRISPR/Cas9 strategy. C57BL/6J mice were selected as a nocturnal reference strain, consistent with documented 24 h rhythmicity in aqueous humor protein dynamics (Zhou & Liu, 2006). LoxP elements were inserted flanking exon 3 to create the NOCTflox/flox allele, and Cre recombinase expression was driven by the nestin promoter to achieve central nervous system-restricted deletion.

During early development (birth to 100 days), no overt phenotypic differences were observed among NOCTwt, NOCTflox/flox, and NOCTflox/flox:Cre+ mice. In adulthood (~120 days), NOCTflox/flox:Cre+ mice frequently developed postural abnormalities and reduced locomotor performance, including slowed movement (Supplementary Video S1). These impairments deteriorated with aging (>280 days), culminating in Parkinson-like manifestations in 13.89% of mice without sex bias (Supplementary Video S2), indicative of markedly reduced fitness under natural conditions. In contrast, NOCTflox/flox mice displayed substantially lower susceptibility to these deficits, with head-tilt phenotypes observed on only 2.78% of senescent mice. Notably, NOCTflox/flox mice consistently exhibited elevated locomotor activity compared to NOCTwt controls (Supplementary Video S3; representative example at 60 days). Sleep-wake cycle dynamics were evaluated in sexually mature (6 weeks) and senescent (18 months) cohorts. Average hourly sleep duration across the 24 h cycle did not differ significantly between NOCTflox/flox and NOCTwt groups at either age (Student’s t-test, P=0.0571 and 0.6783; Figure 2A, B). However, NOCTflox/flox mice remained awake for significantly longer intervals during the 0800h–2000h period compared with NOCTwt mice (Student’s t-test, P<0.0001 and 0.028; Figure 2C, D), with pronounced peaks around 1400h in young mice (Student’s t-test, P=0.0003; Figure 2E) and 1500h in aged mice (Student’s t-test, P<0.0001; Figure 2F). Consistent with these alterations, NOCTflox/flox mice exhibited reduced nocturnal locomotion and enhanced diurnal activity relative to NOCTwt mice (Supplementary Figure S2A, B), recapitulating behavioral transitions previously reported in laboratory rats undergoing nocturnal-to-diurnal activity shifts (Stryjek et al., 2013).

Real-time reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (Figure 2G), immunoblotting (Figure 2H, I), and frontal cortex transcriptomic profiling (Figure 2J) consistently demonstrated decreased NOCT expression following insertion of loxP elements flanking exon 3 in NOCTflox/flox mice. The magnitude of this reduction closely matched expression differences observed between dogs and wolves (Figure 1G). Based on these circadian and molecular alterations, behavioral assays were conducted to evaluate the functional consequences of NOCT attenuation by direct comparison of NOCTflox/flox and NOCTwt cohorts.

NOCT reduction enhances diurnal exploratory locomotion

Increased diurnal exploratory activity has been proposed to have facilitated early interactions between scavenging wolves and ancient humans under the self-domestication hypothesis (Hewson, 2003; Li et al., 2014; Tancredi & Cardinali, 2023). To evaluate whether NOCT attenuation alters diurnal exploration, open field behavior was examined in NOCTflox/flox and NOCTwt mice during adolescence (4–8 weeks postnatal; 28–56 days). During daytime testing (0900h–1800h, temporally matched across groups), NOCTflox/flox mice exhibited significantly greater locomotor activity, reflected by increased total distance traveled (Figure 3A, t=3.631, df=50, P=0.006) and time spent in the central area (Figure 3B, t=2.226, df=50, P=0.0337) relative to NOCTwt controls. These effects were observed in both sexes (Supplementary Figure S3A, B). Furthermore, while exploratory activity declined with age in NOCTwt mice (60–90 days), NOCTflox/flox mice maintained or even increased their exploratory behavior across the same developmental window (Figure 3C).

Considering the potential relevance of locomotor engagement during early domestication, AAV-mediated NOCT reduction was further employed to assess causal effects. An engineered AAV carrying a green fluorescent protein (GFP) tag was administered via caudal intravenous injection to reduce NOCT expression in the central nervous system of 4-week-old mice (28 days) (Challis et al., 2019; Li et al., 2019), generating NOCTAAV mice. Control mice received an identical AAV vector lacking the targeting sequence (NOCTctrl). Behavioral assessment 1 week post-injection revealed no detectable differences between groups (Supplementary Table S4; Figure 3D, t=0.1009, df=42, P=0.9201). In contrast, 4 weeks after injection, NOCTAAV mice exhibited significantly elevated locomotor activity and increased time in the central zone compared to NOCTctrl mice (Supplementary Table S4; Figure 3E, t=3.293, df=42, P=0.0021). Cortical immunostaining showed that NOCT was primarily expressed in the cerebral cortex (Supplementary Figure S4A), and quantitative analyses confirmed marked reductions in NOCT mRNA and protein levels at 4 weeks post-injection (Supplementary Figure S4B–D). Viral activity diminished by 6 weeks post-injection (Supplementary Figure S4E), coinciding with attenuation of group differences (Supplementary Table S4; Figure 3F, t=0.7209, df=42, P=0.4765).

NOCT reduction amplifies fear-related behavioral sensitivity

Attenuation of fear responses has been proposed to have shortened physical closeness to humans during early dog domestication under the tame-selection hypothesis (Belyaev, 1969; Trut et al., 2009). Fear-related behavior was therefore evaluated in 6-week-old NOCTflox/flox and NOCTwt mice using EPM assessment, which exploits innate avoidance of open and elevated spaces (File et al., 1993; Handley & Mithani, 1984; Hilton et al., 2023; Mendes-Gomes et al., 2011; Walf & Frye, 2007). Only initial exposure trials were analyzed, given the well-documented reduction in open-arm exploration during repeated testing (Shoji & Miyakawa, 2021). NOCTflox/flox mice exhibited significantly prolonged residence time in the enclosed arms (Figure 3G; t=3.3, df=38, P=0.0021) and reduced occupancy of open arms (Figure 3H; t=3.248, df=38, P=0.0024) relative to NOCTwt controls, consistent with heightened fear-related responses. Comparable, or slightly reduced, time spent on the central platform (19.54% in NOCTflox/flox vs. 16.19% in NOCTwt) excluded generalized hypoexploration and indicated no increase in baseline anxiety, consistent with prior observations in rats (Casarrubea et al., 2016).

A concordant phenotype was observed following AAV-mediated NOCT suppression. NOCTAAV mice assessed at 7 weeks of age (3 weeks post-injection) displayed significantly elevated fear-related behavior compared with NOCTctrl mice (Figure 3I, t=3.935, df=42, P=0.0003). Reduced occupancy of the central platform in NOCTAAV mice (8.32%) relative to controls (15.66%) further supported enhanced sensitivity to aversive environments following NOCT reduction. Again, this difference in fear response diminished by 6 weeks post-viral injection (Supplementary Figure S3E, t=1.572, df=42, P=0.9025).

NOCT reduction improves short-term memory

Although heightened fear responsiveness in NOCT knockdown mice diverged from predictions of the tame-selection hypothesis, increased exploratory locomotion aligned with expectations of the self-domestication framework. Under this model, scavenging wolves with superior learning and memory capacity would have approached human settlements more frequently, acquired greater food resources, and achieved enhanced survival (Li et al., 2014). Circadian rhythm further intersects closely with plasticity, cognition, learning, and memory. Accordingly, short-term memory performance was evaluated in 6-week-old NOCTflox/flox and NOCTwt mice using the Y maze paradigm. NOCTflox/flox mice exhibited significantly prolonged residence within the food-associated arm compared to NOCTwt controls (Figure 3J, t=2.781, df=28, P=0.0099), consistent with improved short-term memory retention (representative trajectories are shown in Figure 3K). Replication of this assay in NOCTAAV and NOCTctrl cohorts was precluded because pre-experimental food deprivation required to achieve 20% body mass reduction posed substantial health risks.

Transcriptomic signatures of NOCT reduction implicate dysregulated myelination

Genes functionally connected to NOCT encompass pathways regulating circadian timing, energy metabolism, and neurological disorders (Figure 4A). Given predominant NOCT expression in the cerebral cortex (Supplementary Figure S4A), transcriptomic profiling was performed on cortical tissue from 60-day-old NOCTflox/flox and NOCTwt mice to delineate the genetic basis underlying locomotor and cognitive phenotypes, with muscle and bone marrow serving as movement-related reference tissues. DEGs in muscle and bone marrow were primarily enriched in muscle- and immunity-related pathways, respectively (Supplementary Table S5), whereas those in the cortex were predominantly associated with the nervous system, with prominent representation of myelination-related pathways (Figure 4B), consistent with established roles of myelin in shaping learning and memory circuits across the lifespan (Xin & Chan, 2020).

A total of 397 cortical DEGs were identified, including 167 up-regulated and 230 down-regulated transcripts in NOCTflox/flox mice (Supplementary Figure S4F). Up-regulated DEGs displayed strongest enrichment for the biological process “myelination” (adjusted P=2.6e-8; Figure 4C), whereas down-regulated DEGs were significantly enriched in “fatty acid metabolic process” (adjusted P=1.6e-8; Figure 4D). In the AAV-mediated knockdown model, up-regulated DEGs in the prefrontal cortex at 4 weeks post-injection were likewise enriched for “positive regulation of myelination” (P=0.0041; Supplementary Table S6). The reduced myelination signal in the AAV model likely reflects partial NOCT suppression at the transcript level, in contrast to the stable DNA-level disruption achieved in the transgenic model.

To further resolve coordinated transcriptional responses under partial NOCT suppression, WGCNA identified modules exhibiting synchronized expression changes following AAV treatment. The greenyellow module showed the strongest negative association with the NOCTAAV prefrontal cortex (P=9e-6, Supplementary Figure S5) and was highly enriched for “myelination” (adjusted P=1.7e-7, Supplementary Table S7). Conversely, the magenta module displayed positive association with the NOCTAAV prefrontal cortex (P=2e-5, Supplementary Figure S5), with marked enrichment for “glutamatergic synapse” (adjusted P=4.9356e-8, Supplementary Table S8). The coordinated alterations in myelination and synaptic pathways in NOCTAAV mice support current understanding that myelin constrains axonal sprouting, regulates synapse formation, and stabilizes neural circuit architecture (Fields, 2014), providing mechanistic insight into behavioral and cognitive changes induced by NOCT reduction.

Cortical staining reveals myelin morphological remodeling following NOCT reduction

Given that the transcriptomic signatures associated with NOCT attenuation indicated enhanced myelination, myelin architecture in the cerebral cortex was histologically evaluated across both transgenic and AAV-mediated models. In the transgenic cohort, adolescent NOCTflox/flox mice (6 weeks) displayed markedly increased myelin sheath thickness and density relative to age-matched NOCTwt controls (Figure 5A). With advancing age (18 months), cortical myelin progressively declined in NOCTwt mice, whereas NOCTflox/flox mice retained comparatively stable myelin organization (Figure 5B). Consistent patterns emerged in the AAV model, in which NOCTAAV mice exhibited elevated cortical myelin density compared to NOCTctrl mice at 4 weeks post-viral injection, coincident with maximal transduction efficacy (Figure 5C). By 9 weeks post-injection, when viral activity had substantially diminished, differences in myelin sheath density between groups were no longer detectable (Figure 5D).

Mitochondrial content in the cortex remains unchanged with or without NOCT reduction

Strengthened myelination imposes increased energetic demand, and NOCT localizes to mitochondria, the primary site of cellular energy production (Estrella et al., 2019). Therefore, mitochondrial content in the cerebral cortex was quantified in 6-week-old NOCTflox/flox and NOCTwt mice, coinciding with peak behavioral divergence and transcriptomic remodeling. Notably, no significant differences in mitochondrial content were detected between genotypes (Supplementary Figure S6A), indicating engagement of alternative bioenergetic mechanisms. Emerging evidence has suggested that myelin acts as a proton capacitor, in which PLP and MBP form a coupled system that stores proton energy during sleep and releases this reserve during wakefulness via extra-mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation (Morelli et al., 2025). In line with this model, expression profiling at 42 days revealed substantial up-regulation of 11 myelin-associated genes in NOCTflox/flox cortex, including BCAS1, GAL3ST1, GJC3, MAG, MAL, MBP, MPZ, PLLP, PLP, PMP22, and UGT8A (Supplementary Figure S6B). Notably, MBP and PLP, two principal structural constituents of the myelin sheath implicated in proton-driven ATP generation during wakefulness, exhibited 4.38-fold (adjusted P=0.0060) and 3.12-fold (adjusted P=0.0099) increases in expression, respectively (Supplementary Figure S6B).

DISCUSSION

This study integrated genomic, transcriptomic, and functional analyses to explore circadian contributions to dog domestication. A highly diverged indel was identified that attenuated expression of the clock-controlled gene NOCT, with a pronounced reduction observed in the dog frontal cortex compared to wolves. Experimental NOCT reduction in mice produced disrupted circadian outputs, characterized by prolonged daytime wakefulness, together with increased frontal cortex myelination. These neurobiological changes were accompanied by extended exploratory locomotion, heightened fear sensitivity, and improved short-term memory—behavioral features previously proposed to influence early domestication trajectories (Li et al., 2014). Collectively, these findings support a model in which circadian reprogramming and neurodevelopmental remodeling jointly contributed to adaptive behavioral shifts during the earliest stages of dog domestication.

Domestication traits preceding conscious selection

Animal domestication centers on adaptation to human-modified environments. Dogs represent the sole carnivore lineage to complete this transition, with behavioral reconfiguration toward reduced aggression and increased tolerance forming a defining hallmark. Two major frameworks have dominated this field: one emphasizes human-directed selection for communicative capacity (Hare et al., 2002; Topál et al., 2009) or tameness (Belyaev, 1969; Trut et al., 2009) during initial domestication, whereas the alternative self-domestication hypothesis proposes that scavenging wolves with superior learning and memory remained near human settlements for prolonged periods, progressively integrating into human society (Li et al., 2014). Given that unconscious selection often precedes deliberate directional selection (Gittleman, 1986), identifying early, non-intentional biological changes offers a powerful avenue for resolving this long-standing debate.

Adaptation to anthropogenic environments constitutes a central axis of domestication (Kruska, 2005). In this context, the emergence of diurnal activity in dogs represents a striking departure from the crepuscular patterns typical of gray wolves, consistent with adaptation to human environments. Genome-wide indel screening highlighted the clock-related gene NOCT as a prominent candidate locus, corroborating earlier SNP-based analyses. Both comparisons consistently identified allele polymorphism in wolves combined with marked allele frequency shifts in dogs, rather than de novo domestication mutations, with derived alleles absent from more distant outgroups. Such selection on pre-existing variation has been proposed as a primary driver of phenotypic diversification during domestication (Lord et al., 2020).

NOCT as a candidate linking circadian regulation to behavioral adaptation during dog domestication

NOCT was initially identified as a clock-regulated gene in the retina of Xenopus laevis (Green & Besharse, 1996) and subsequently shown to exhibit robust circadian oscillation, with peak hepatic expression in mice during the early dark phase (Wang et al., 2001). NOCT encodes a CCR4 family of deadenylases that catalyze poly(A) tail shortening, thereby mediating post-transcriptional control of target mRNA (Baggs & Green, 2003). Although NOCT does not constitute a core component of the circadian oscillator, as nocturnin-deficient mice lack overt alterations in canonical clock timing (Green et al., 2007), its position downstream of the molecular clock enables broad regulation of circadian outputs. Consistent with this role, NOCT has been implicated in lipid metabolism, adipogenesis, glucose homeostasis, inflammatory signaling, osteogenesis, and oxidative stress responses (Estrella et al., 2019; Green & Besharse, 1996; Kawai et al., 2010; Kojima et al., 2015; Laothamatas et al., 2020). The marked divergence of NOCT during dog domestication, together with reported effects on motor neuron development (Lobb-Rabe et al., 2022) and behavior (Chen et al., 2018), motivated focused investigation of NOCT function in the mammalian brain.

Notably, reduced NOCT expression prolonged daytime wakefulness, mirroring diminished siesta sleep (ZT3–ZT9) reported in nocte (NOCT homolog) mutant flies (From the American Association of Neurological Surgeons (AANS) et al., 2018). This phenotype reflected altered circadian amplitude (Figure 2E, F) rather than phase inversion of the core clock, consistent with previous evidence that NOCT down-regulation disrupts circadian rhythm amplitude while leaving expression of canonical clock components, such as Per and Cry, largely intact (Green et al., 2007). Together, these findings indicate that NOCT modulates circadian output strength, with downstream consequences for sleep-wake dynamics and behavior, providing a plausible mechanism through which circadian remodeling could facilitate behavioral adjustment to human-modified environments during early domestication.

Behavioral consequences of circadian remodeling support a self-domestication model for early dogs

Circadian prolongation of daytime wakefulness was accompanied by sustained increases in exploratory activity, heightened fear sensitivity, and improved short-term memory, with these behavioral phenotypes reproducible across individuals and stable across generations. Domestic dogs exhibit pronounced exploratory locomotion during an extended juvenile phase of approximately 120 days, a neotenic trait characteristic of domestication, whereas gray wolves show reduced exploratory activity between 45 and 60 days of age (Šimić et al., 2020). In parallel, NOCT-reduced mice displayed increased exploratory drive during adolescence that persisted beyond sexual maturation. These behavioral shifts mirror key differences between dogs and wolves and are expected to increase opportunities for environmental learning and habituation to human presence, features widely considered foundational to early domestication.

Prolonged exploratory behavior in dogs and tame foxes has traditionally been attributed to reduced fear responses under the tame-selection framework (Belyaev, 1969; Trut et al., 2009). However, diminished fear alone may compromise survival in wild contexts. Consistent with this view, our previous findings revealed that fear-related genes showing extreme divergence between dogs and wolves preferentially enhance synaptic plasticity and memory capacity rather than directly suppress fear (Li et al., 2014). Enhanced learning and memory would enable wolves to recognize human proximity as nonthreatening, increasing the frequency of nonaggressive encounters near settlements and facilitating access to human food resources, thereby promoting self-domestication. In agreement with this model, NOCT-reduced mice exhibiting prolonged exploration did not exhibit attenuated fear responses (Figure 3A, G) but instead showed significantly improved short-term memory (Figure 3K), supporting the hypothesis that cognitive enhancement, rather than fear reduction, facilitated the behavioral shift toward prolonged human association and self-domestication.

However, behavior transformation during the initial stage of dog domestication likely involved highly complex processes and genetic architecture. Although many nervous system-associated genes exhibit divergence between dogs and wolves (Li et al., 2013; Pendleton et al., 2018; Sutter & Ostrander, 2004; Zhang et al., 2020), simultaneous selection across numerous loci at the onset of domestication is unlikely. Instead, a limited number of genes with disproportionate influence on behavior likely experienced primary selection, followed by accumulation of additional variants through genomic hitchhiking, a pattern documented in the domesticated gayal (Li et al., 2023). A comparable mechanism has been proposed for human self-domestication, in which BAZ1B emerged as a central regulator of craniofacial morphology and cognitive traits (Zanella et al., 2019). In this context, NOCT reduction altered circadian output and produced behavioral phenotypes that facilitated early human-canid interactions. Together, these results identify NOCT as a potential domestication gene that contributed to the earliest stages of dog domestication, linking circadian remodeling to cognitive and behavioral adaptations that favored integration into human environments.

NOCT as a potential entry point for sustaining myelination and mitigating neurodegenerative pathology

Sleep rhythm disturbance, including micro-architectural sleep alterations, nocturnal sleep fragmentation, reduced nocturnal sleep duration, diurnal napping, and inversion of the sleep-wake cycle, represents a common clinical feature of neurodegenerative disorders such as Alzheimer’s disease (Peter-Derex et al., 2015) and Parkinson’s disease (Stefani & Högl, 2020). These conditions are accompanied by synchronized alterations in myelin integrity, and accumulating evidence indicates that preservation and renewal of myelin can ameliorate cognitive and behavioral dysfunction (Altinoz et al., 2021; Chen et al., 2021). Consistently, individuals with higher sleep quality exhibit higher myelin volume fraction relative to poor sleepers (Andica et al., 2024), reinforcing a tight association between sleep regulation and myelination.

In mice, sleep follows a polyphasic pattern, with approximately two-thirds of short sleep episodes occurring during the light/rest phase (Vyazovskiy & Delogu, 2014). Conditional knockout of the core clock gene Bmal1 in oligodendrocyte precursor cells induces fragmented sleep during the dark/active phase, accompanied by impaired myelination and remyelination (Rojo et al., 2023). Multiple studies have shown that circadian regulation orchestrates diverse molecular and signaling pathways essential for remyelination (Tang et al., 2025).

In the present study, NOCT reduction resulted in decreased sleep during the light/rest phase and a corresponding increase during the dark/active phase (Figure 1E, F), together with sustained enhancement of prefrontal cortical myelination from adolescence through senescence (Figure 5A). Although the absence of quantitative myelin metrics and circadian profiling of myelin dynamics warrants cautious interpretation, these findings implicate NOCT as a potential regulator of myelination through circadian disruption, with relevance to neurodegenerative vulnerability. Supporting this possibility, genetic variation within or near NOCT has recently been linked to Alzheimer’s disease risk (Andrews et al., 2019; Khani et al., 2024; Scheltens et al., 2021).

Although NOCT primarily localizes to mitochondria (Estrella et al., 2019), a cellular compartment frequently implicated in neurodegenerative pathology through impaired energy production (Klemmensen et al., 2024), our results found no association between mitochondrial content and the observed myelination enhancement following NOCT reduction (Supplementary Figure S6A). Instead, coordinated up-regulation of MBP and PLP, down-regulation of lipid metabolism, and increased myelin density suggest engagement of alternative bioenergetic strategies independent of mitochondria, such as proton transmission through myelin membrane architecture (Morelli et al., 2025) and iron-mediated extracellular Fenton chemistry (Dai et al., 2025; Li et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2025).

Cautions and perspectives

Several limitations warrant careful consideration. First, direct functional evaluation of intronic indels, particularly reporter-based assays to quantify regulatory effects on transcription, would strengthen causal inference between NOCT variation and the observed phenotypic outcomes and help clarify the underlying regulatory mechanisms. Additionally, species-specific divergence between canine signatures and transgenic mouse models remains a potential confounding factor, particularly given evolutionary differences in circadian regulatory architecture and neural circuit organization responsive to NOCT modulation. Notably, the brain represents the tissue with the highest NOCT expression in dogs (Figure 1F), whereas liver predominates in mice (Kulshrestha & Devkar, 2023). Consequently, extrapolation from mouse models to canine biology requires caution, and future studies incorporating canine in vivo systems should help close this translational gap.

Second, sleep architecture represents a critical endpoint for evaluating circadian reorganization, yet objective polysomnographic measurements such as electroencephalography (EEG) were not performed in this study. In the absence of direct quantification of sleep-wake cycles, sleep stages, and temporal dynamics, interpretation of rhythmic behavioral changes remains indirect. Integration of electrophysiological sleep profiling with diurnal expression trajectories of NOCT and canonical clock genes would substantially refine understanding of how NOCT modulation reshapes circadian output.

Third, transcriptomic resolution remains constrained by the availability of region-specific canine brain datasets, limiting analyses largely to the cerebral cortex. As the suprachiasmatic nucleus within the hypothalamus serves as the master circadian pacemaker, and the hypothalamus coordinates rhythmic signaling across physiological systems, lack of transcriptomic coverage in these regions restricts comprehensive evaluation of NOCT involvement in canine circadian regulation. High-resolution, time-resolved expression profiling of NOCT and rhythm-associated genes in these key structures would provide critical context for interpreting basal interspecific expression differences between dogs and wolves.

CONCLUSION

Integrating functional experiments with population-scale genomic comparisons between dogs and wolves identified pronounced allele frequency shifts at three indels within the circadian gene NOCT during domestication. Reduced NOCT expression altered circadian output and elicited coordinated behavioral changes, including prolonged daytime wakefulness, increased exploratory locomotion, altered fear responsiveness, and enhanced short-term memory, features consistent with adaptive traits that could promote early self-domestication. Beyond evolutionary implications, these findings point to circadian modulation as a potential strategy for sustaining myelin integrity, with relevance for mitigating cognitive and behavioral decline associated with neurodegenerative disorders. Future work should extend molecular and behavioral analyses to core circadian centers such as the suprachiasmatic nucleus and hypothalamus, incorporate EEG-based sleep profiling, and establish validation in canine in vivo systems. Such efforts will refine mechanistic understanding of circadian remodeling in domestication and clarify translational relevance for neurobiological health.

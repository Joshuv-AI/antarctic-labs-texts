Enhancing older care services: a comprehensive internet + community home indicator system
Authors: Rui Zhao, Mingshu Huo, Mingyang Tan, Lei Wang, Qiaoli Liu, Jiangxu Li, Ru Wang, Hongyu Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveConstructing a demand-oriented Internet + community home care service indicator system, starting from the actual needs of the elderly, and quantifying the demand into specific service contents that can be provided, so as to realize the effective supply of services. It aims to provide a theoretical basis for the government to formulate relevant aging policies and provide a reference basis for promoting the innovation and development of the Internet + elderly care model.MethodsBased on existence needs, relationship needs and growth needs abbreviated as “Existence, Relatedness, Growth (ERG)” demand theory, through the discussion in the group meeting, form the expert consultation questionnaire of the demand-oriented Internet + community home care service indicator system, select experts in related fields to conduct two rounds of Delphi expert consultation, summarize the experts’ opinions to determine the indicator system items, and use the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) method to construct the judgment matrix to derive the weighting coefficients of the indicators, and establish the ultimate demand-oriented Internet + community home care service indicator system.ResultsThe effective recovery rates for the two rounds of expert consoulation questionnaires were 100%, with expert authority coefficients of 0.852 and 0.868, respectively. The Kendall coefficients for the second round of expert views varied from 0.226 to 0.431, with coefficients of variation for all indexes less than 0.25.The finalized demand-oriented Internet + community home care service indicator system includes 3 first-level indicators, 10 s-level indicators, and 46 third-level indicators. The consistency ratios of indicator judgment matrices at all levels are less than 0.10, suggesting that the hierarchical analysis findings are consistent, implying that the weight coefficient distribution is appropriate.ConclusionThe demand-oriented Internet + community home care service Indicator System, which was created through expert consultation and AHP method, has reasonable content and is more dependable as an evaluation tool for reliably assessing demand for elderly servic.Keywords: Internet+, Community home-based older care, Index system, Delphi consultation, Analytic hierarchy process (AHP)

Background

An important contemporary socio-demographic trend is global population ageing [1]. It is worth noting that the older population in China has increased significantly since 2000, with those aged 65 and over accounting for more than 7% of the total population. By 2021, China will have entered a highly aged society, with the older population accounting for more than 14% of the total population, indicating that the pace of population ageing in China is accelerating [2]. China’s population aging has distinctive features that differ from those of developed countries in Europe and the United States, including a rapid aging process, a heavy burden of old-age care, and an aging rate that precedes economic prosperity [3]. The accelerated ageing of the population has created escalating demands and challenges in the field of older care services. The traditional model of older care makes it difficult to meet the diverse and individual needs of older persons, thereby restricting their overall health and well-being. In this context, the integration of “Internet+” technology and community-based home care services has become an important way to innovate and enhance older care services [4]. Internet + Community Ageing at Home refers to the combination of Internet technology with modern community ageing services, the use of network platforms and intelligent devices to provide ageing services, and the effective integration of ageing resources and services required by the older. This approach aims to achieve “menu-type demand” and “precise supply”, thus broadening the channels of older care services and optimising the use of social resources. It marks the extension and expansion of the traditional older care model in the new economic situation [5]. The “Internet Plus” community-based home care model not only introduces Internet technology into the older care industry, but also deeply integrates professional information technology and platforms into the older care industry [6]. This integration serves two main purposes: firstly, to make use of the strengths and characteristics of the two industries to make up for deficiencies and promote common development; and secondly, to inject technology in a comprehensive manner to promote the overall progress of the older care industry [7].

China encourages the implementation of Internet + community-based ageing at home, and relevant government departments have introduced a series of policies and support measures. These initiatives have encouraged enterprises to innovate their older care service models, promoted the development of “Internet+” community-based ageing at home platforms, and enhanced the digital literacy of the older population [8–11]. All parts of China have actively embraced and promoted the practice of “Internet+” home-based community care, and have achieved positive results. However, they have also encountered challenges such as limited application of technology and inconsistent service standards [12–14]. There is an urgent need to strengthen policy support and normative guidance to improve the accessibility and quality of older care services. The integration of China’s Internet industry has led to the formation of a comprehensive social aged-care service system based on home-based, community-based and institutional support. Therefore, the top priority for strengthening China’s social older care service system is to effectively address the needs of the older for home-based older care services and to strengthen the provision of community-based older care services [15].

This study on “Internet + Community Ageing at Home” uses information technology to centralise ageing-related information on an external platform, creating an important link between supply and demand. The platform collects information from service seekers and integrates untapped social resources. The power of the Internet and other information technologies is harnessed to provide diversified services at multiple levels [16]. The platform transcends time and space constraints, using information technology and social platforms to coordinate multiple forces from government, society, the market and families. It seamlessly integrates healthcare, artificial intelligence, social organisations and other resources, working together to provide older people with life care, safety management, medical support, rehabilitation and healthcare services, ultimately meeting the older’s needs related to old age and improving their overall health and quality of life. This work not only strengthens the social service system for the older, but also promotes the development of the ageing information industry [16]. Jinzhou City, Liaoning Province, relies on the smart home care service model promoted by 5G technology to establish a service zone, information database and professional team. Regardless of the specific senior care model chosen by the older, they can obtain “self-help, menu-based” senior care services through online applications and offline services. Internet + community-based ageing-in-place service providers have a diversified and high-quality team of professionals, including healthcare workers, information technology experts, management personnel, home helpers, volunteers, and so on. In addition, these services include older care service robots, smart home devices, wearable technology and other advanced tools. These services not only provide specialized and intelligent services, but also reduce labour intensity and time requirements by making use of advanced technological equipment and networked information systems, thereby addressing the shortage of human resources in the older care sector.

Older individuals have multifaceted health needs that encompass both physical and mental aspects [17]. Empirical research has highlighted key areas of demand, including community-assisted meals, leisure and recreational activities, home assistance, rehabilitation training, minor healthcare, and older day care [4]. Based on a survey [18] addressing the needs of a diverse older population, a broad spectrum of in-home services has been developed to cater to their care requirements. These services span long-term assistance for essential daily activities, and primary or hospital-level medical care delivered in the comfort of their homes. The 2018 China Longitudinal Health and Life Survey (CLHLS) [19] categorizes services into four main groups, each containing two subcategories: medical services, which encompass home visits and health education; daily life care services, including personal care and grocery shopping; mental and cultural services, consisting of social and recreational activities and psychological counseling; and mediation services, which include legal assistance and neighborhood relations. Over the decade from 2008 to 2018, medical services emerged as the second-most important among the four categories, albeit with limited availability of home visits and health education due to constraints on primary care resources [20, 21]. Nevertheless, concerns such as “cognitive disorientation”, “lack of family support”,“self-restraint”, and “unmet emotional and spiritual needs” still warrant attention [22]. However, providing humanistic care, mental health support, and addressing the spiritual aspects relevant to these concerns remains ambiguous [22, 23]. Alderfer [24], from Yale University in the United States, introduced the ERG ( existence, relatedness, growth, ERG ) needs theory, identifying three core needs: survival, interrelationships, and growth and development. This study classifies older needs based on the ERG theory into survival, relationship, growth, and development needs. Prior research has predominantly focused on service content, particularly medical care services, leaving a gap in understanding the older’s spiritual and social support needs, leading to a misalignment between services provided and actual needs.

To address the aforementioned gaps, this study integrates the Internet with older care services, harnessing the strengths of community-based home care services. The primary focus lies in introducing two specific service categories, namely daily care and psychosocial support, to construct a comprehensive Internet + community home care service indicator system that genuinely caters to the needs of older individuals at home. With the help of the Delphi method and the hierarchical analysis method, this study constructs a demand-oriented Internet + community home care service indicator system, taking the actual needs of the older as the starting point and quantifying the needs into the specific service contents provided, in order to enhance the pertinence and quality of the service, and then to promote the enhancement of the older’s health and quality of life.

Methods

Theoretical framework

The ERG theory of needs, introduced by Alderfer in 1969 in his work “An Empirical Test of a New Theory of Human Needs” [24], categorizes human needs into three primary groups. First, survival needs to encompass the fundamental requirements for individual material survival, including clothing, food, housing, transportation, income, benefits, and allowances, analogous to the physiological and safety needs in Maslow’s hierarchy of needs theory. Second, relationship needs pertain to the necessity for interpersonal connections, seeking satisfaction through interactions with others in both work and personal life, corresponding to the external aspect of social and self-esteem needs in Maslow’s hierarchy of needs theory. Third, growth needs revolve around an individual’s intrinsic aspiration for self-improvement and development, which is achievable through the realization of personal potential and capabilities, including the intrinsic part of self-esteem needs and the need for self-actualization in Maslow’s hierarchy.

Alderfer’s theory posits that his survival needs align with Maslow’s physiological and safety needs, encompassing basic physical survival and existence. Consequently, two level 1 indicators, namely living care services and medical and nursing services, are defined by these survival needs. Moreover, Aldefer’s concept of relationship needs corresponds to the requirement for interpersonal relationships, mirroring the social and self-esteem needs in Maslow’s hierarchy, including aspects such as self-esteem and self-actualization. This connection underlines the definition of another level 1 indicator, psychosocial/spiritual support services, which is rooted in both relationship and growth needs. Consequently, the level 1 indicators serve the genuine needs of the older, offering a range of healthy aging services across the three distinct dimensions.

Research group discussion

The research team comprised two professors, one chief nursing officer, one doctoral student, and three master’s students. We convened a group meeting to deliberate on the feasibility of content design for Internet + community home-based older care services. Subsequently, I revised it and created the first official expert consultation questionnaire. The initial indicator system emerged from a comprehensive literature review and extensive research group discussions, encompassing 3 top-level indicators, 10 s-level indicators, and 57 third-level indicators.

Delphi expert consultation

This study uses the Delphi method to construct the indicator system, firstly, through anonymous collection of opinions to avoid bias and ensure objectivity and fairness; secondly, through two rounds of surveys and feedbacks, experts can modify and improve the indicator system to ensure that it is scientific and reasonable; and finally, optimise the indicator screening and weighting to improve the rationality and practical value of the indicator system.

Concurrently, the research team set the criteria for selecting indicators and employed the Delphi method to identify experts. Previous research recommended the involvement of 10–50 advisory experts [25]. In this instance, 22 experts from universities, hospitals, communities, and information management departments were specifically invited to participate in shaping the indicator system for Internet + community-based older care services. These selected experts possess profound knowledge of the Internet and the Internet of Things, along with extensive research experience in older care, assuring the credibility of the consultation results [26]. Secondly, these experts come from different provinces and cities such as Jilin Province, Liaoning Province, Beijing Municipality, Hebei Province, Zhejiang Province, Jiangsu Province, Fujian Province, etc. which is representative of a certain geographical area. This is because the above mentioned areas are the areas with a large proportion of the older population in China, and the experts have more mature experience in Internet + Community Ageing at Home.

The questionnaire consists of four distinct sections: (1) fundamental information about the expert; (2) an indicator questionnaire based on the initial indicator system; (3) the expert’s familiarity with the subject; (4) the basis for the expert’s judgment. Every item in the questionnaire was assessed on a five-point Likert scale, spanning from “strongly disagree” to “strongly agree”, corresponding scores are recorded as 1 to 5, respectively [27].

Two rounds of expert consultation were conducted via email. In the initial round, following the completion of the questionnaires by the experts, the researcher analyzed their responses. To ensure dependable consultation outcomes and acknowledge expert authority, a minimum expert authority coefficient (Cr) of 0.7 was required, and Kendall consistency was employed to assess the alignment of experts’ viewpoints. The expert authority coefficient (Cr) is computed based on the expert’s assessments of the indicator (Ca) and their familiarity with the consultation content (Cs). The formula for Cr is (Ca + Cs)/2, with a Cr value of ≥ 0.70 generally considered acceptable [28].

AHP weight assessment

The indicator weight plays a crucial role in appraising the quality of Internet + community home-based older care services because it determines the relative significance of each indicator, directly influencing the accuracy of the results derived from the indicator system. The AHP proves to be an invaluable tool for translating subjective expert judgments regarding various evaluation factors into quantitative weights, especially in the context of intricate issues necessitating decomposition [29]. In this study, we leveraged the results from the second round of expert importance ratings to compute the indicator weights and determine their corresponding values [30].

We engaged 22 experts to employ the 1–9 scale method in creating the pair-wise comparison judgment matrix because it offers greater clarity and simplicity for experts in determining the importance value of each indicator. The study is divided into 14 matrices, structured around 3 primary indicators, 10 secondary indicators, and 46 tertiary indicators [31]. Weight coefficients for primary, secondary, and tertiary indicators were derived from the matrix analysis results, using the indicator weight coefficient to evaluate the significance of each indicator. MATLAB 2016a was employed to input the experts’ assessments of first-level indicators and establish a matrix for acquiring and organizing weight coefficients, maximum eigenvalues (λ max), consistency indicator (CI), and random consistency ratio (CR) [32]. A consistency assessment was conducted using the computed values, and a CR value below 0.1 indicated a successful outcome. Consequently, the hierarchical analysis yielded consistent results, ensuring the logical assignment of weight coefficients [33].

First, the geometric mean of each row of the judgement matrix was calculated using the product square root method, in which “aij” denotes the element of the i-th row and j-th column of the primitive judgement matrix, and “n” denotes the quantity of indicators. The geometric mean of each line is normalized to obtain the feature vector, which is the weight coefficient of each indicator. Next, the consistency test of the judgement matrix is conducted: by calculating the maximum eigenvalue of the judgement matrix as:λmax=1n∑i=1n∑j=1naijwjwi; calculating the consistency indicator (CI) with the following formula: CI=λmax-nn-1; calculating the consistency ratio (CR) as: CR=CIRI. RI is the average random consistency indicator, and its value depends on “n” and is the average consistency indicator of randomly generated reciprocal matrices.

Statistical analysis

We employed Excel to input and organize the data, followed by an in-depth analysis conducted using SPSS 25.0 and MATLAB 2016a software. Measurement data are illustrated by mean values along with their corresponding standard deviations, whereas enumeration data are conveyed by frequencies and percentages. The return rate of the questionnaires served as a measure of positivity among the experts. To gauge the level of expert authority and agreement, we employed the authority coefficient (Cr) and the Kendall coefficient (W), respectively. Evaluating the experts’ appraisals of item importance was done based on the mean item importance scores and the coefficient of variation (CV).

Results of the first round of delphi expert consultation

Following the initial round of expert consultation, the mean importance ratings of the first-level indicators ranged from 4.52 to 5.00, with coefficients of variation ranging from 0.00 to 0.14, leading to the retention of all first-level indicators. For the second-level indicators, the mean importance ratings fell within the range of 4.44–5.00, with coefficients of variation ranging from 0.00 to 0.18, prompting the retention of all secondary indicators. However, two of them were subject to revision to enhance their standardization after discussions within the research group and consultation with experts. For instance, “spiritual comfort service” was revised to “psychological support service”, and “home care” was modified to “health problem care”. In the case of the third-level indicators, the mean importance ratings varied from 3.25 to 5.00, with coefficients of variation ranging from 0.00 to 0.44. Among them, five items had mean values lower than 3.5 and coefficient of variation values exceeding 0.25. After group discussions and expert opinion, these five items were removed. They included “community platform management consultation”, “older care market information consultation”, “family doctor contract”, “prevention and control of public health emergencies”, and “preventive health care”. Two items exhibited coefficient of variation values surpassing 0.25 and were subsequently deleted after deliberation, as they were deemed to overlap with previous items and lacked professional terminology. These two items were “excretory care” and “older home constipation care”. Taking into account expert feedback and discussions, “Chinese medicine services” and “medication management” were consolidated into “Chinese and Western medicine health services”; while “fistula care” and “wound care (including pressure ulcer care)” were merged into “wound/stoma care”.

Discussion

Scientific and reliable nature of the demand-oriented internet + community home-based older service indicator system

To address the increasing demand for healthy aging and the fragmented state of China’s medical and older care systems, the country has proposed an internet-based older care model. This approach focuses on the integration of medical resources at the community level, offering home care services to the older, thereby addressing the current challenges in older care in China [35]. Based on this framework, this research developed an indicator system for Internet + community home-based older care services. Guided by the healthcare requirements of older individuals in homes, and using the ERG needs theory as a structural framework, the study employed the Delphi method and the AHP analytic hierarchy process as its primary research methodologies. This indicator system serves as a valuable complement to the existing long-term care system for older individuals residing at home, allowing for an in-depth, comprehensive, and dynamic evaluation of their healthcare and care-related needs. It facilitates the provision of tailored services that align with local conditions, making it a logical and rational approach [36]. In this study, 22 experts were invited to participate in the consultation, all of whom had 10 years or more of work experience in the relevant professional fields. The experts came from different provinces and units, and were geographically and representatively diverse, to ensure the integration of their rich experience and views in different contexts. The effective recovery rates of the questionnaires in the two rounds of this study reached 100% and 90.91%, and the expert authority coefficients were 0.852 and 0.868, respectively. In the second round of expert consultation, the Kendall’s coefficient of concordance (W) of the experts’ opinions was 0.226—0.431, (p < 0.001), which indicated that the experts were highly representative, motivated, authoritative, and consistent in their opinions. The principle of screening indicators in this study requires that the mean of importance assignment ≥ 3.5 and the coefficient of variation ≤ 0.25. The larger the arithmetic mean corresponding to the importance scoring of each indicator, the greater the importance of the indicator in the indicator system. The consistency of the arithmetic mean of each indicator in this study may be related to the fact that the experts gave consistent scores in consideration of the actual needs of the current Chinese older.

Comprehensive and practical indicator system for demand-oriented internet + community home-based older services

Focusing on the genuine needs of older individuals living at home within the community, this study actively constructed an Internet + community home-based older care services indicator system that encompasses three essential aspects : living care services, medical and nursing services, and psychosocial/spiritual support services. The indicator construction results reveal that the highest weight among the first-level indicators belongs to medical and nursing services (0.493), followed by living care services (0.311) and psychosocial/spiritual support services (0.196). These differing weight values emphasize the varied needs of older individuals living at home in the context of China’s aging population. Previous research [37] has consistently indicated that older individuals residing at home often have a heightened demand for medical and nursing services because of a range of subjective and objective factors, including limited physical activity [38], a high prevalence of chronic diseases [39], and disparities in the distribution of primary medical and healthcare resources [40].

At a subjective level, the older prioritize the satisfaction of their two primary needs, specifically, life care and health, as they age. At an objective level, the aim is to efficiently allocate community public pension service resources and guarantee the older’s willingness to access these services within the community. In pursuit of this goal, we categorize the older into three distinct groups based on their assessment of older care needs in Jinzhou : those receiving care from their own families, those receiving care within the community, and those receiving care within institutional settings. The first approach involves using community health service centers or nursing stations to provide daily care, clinical support, and specialized services. The second approach involves utilizing community health service centers to provide long-term contracted medical services to older individuals living at home. Furthermore, efforts are being made to strengthen collaboration with regional medical institutions and establish an efficient process for older individuals in need of hospitalization or medical treatment while residing in older care facilities.

As the older population living alone or with disabilities increases [41], alongside a high prevalence of chronic diseases, there is a surging occurrence of diverse psychological challenges [42]. In the absence of professional home care and psychological support within the community, the essential healthcare needs of the older remain unaddressed. We are committed to providing precise in-home care services that cover personal daily care, support, and safety, including clothing, food, housing, and transportation. We thoroughly assessed the living conditions and distinctive features of the local older population at home. Notably, we empower the older by involving them in decision-making through psychological assistance, cultural and recreational activities, and self-realization initiatives. This approach enhances their independence and self-esteem while easing the responsibilities of caregivers [43]. Consequently, community-based older care providers must prioritize the psychological and social well-being of the older living at home. By challenging stereotypes associated with aging and disability, offering tailored services, and promoting mental health and active social engagement, we can enhance their overall well-being and integration into society.

The indicator system for Internet + community home-based older care services offers a comprehensive and practical approach, covering a wide array of factors crucial for the effective care of the older population living at home. This extensive system considers diverse aspects, including healthcare, social support, personal assistance, and technological integration. Concerning healthcare, the indicator system underscores the importance of accessible and responsive medical services, ensuring that older individuals have convenient access to healthcare facilities, regular check-ups, and timely medical interventions. Thereby, it addresses prevalent chronic diseases and enables early detection and prevention of potential health issues. In addition, the system acknowledges the significance of social support for the well-being of the older, advocating for the establishment of community resources and networks that encourage social interaction, companionship, and emotional support, mitigating feelings of loneliness and isolation experienced by older individuals living at home. Personalized assistance is another crucial aspect covered, emphasizing tailored care services that address the specific needs of each older individual, including assistance with daily activities such as personal hygiene, meal preparation, and household chores, significantly enhancing their quality of life. Finally, the indicator system recognizes the role of technology in enhancing the delivery of home-based older care services, promoting the integration of innovative technologies such as telemedicine, smart home systems, and wearable devices to monitor the health status of the older, facilitate remote consultations, and ensure their safety and security.

Conclusion

The demand-oriented Internet + community home-based older care services Indicator System developed in this study includes 3 primary indicators, 10 secondary indicators, and 46 tertiary indicators. The process of developing the indicator system was thoroughly assessed by specialists, whose representativeness, positivity, authority, and consistency of opinion set the groundwork for the indicator system’s scientificity and reliability. The hierarchical analytic method guarantees that the indicators’ weights are distributed in a more logical manner, and the comprehensive and practical indicator system offers a solid foundation for the later conduct and assessment of Internet + community home-based older care services.

Strength and limitations

The demand-oriented Internet + community home-based older service indicator system offers several advantages. It enables the collection and analysis of data on older needs, facilitating quantitative and precise management of older services with a focus on delivering user-centric services. The system allows for a comprehensive assessment of indicators related to Internet + community home health care services, offering data support that empowers community doctors and nurses to make timely adjustments to service strategies and enhance service quality. Moreover, the indicator system aids service providers in understanding the demand patterns and temporal distribution of older care services, leading to the rational allocation of human, material, and technical resources. This, in turn, improves service efficiency and extends the reach of community services. However, it is worth noting that due to time and resource constraints, this study had limitations in terms of the number of consulting experts and their input. This study applies the Delphi expert consultation method and focuses on theoretical exploratory research, and the scientificity and rationality of the indicator system need to be further verified and improved in specific assessment practices. In order to better understand the practical significance of the findings and provide precise and powerful guidance for future research.

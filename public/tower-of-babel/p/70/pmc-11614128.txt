Evaluation of Surface Homogeneity of Poly Methyl Methacrylate Denture Base Resin fabricated by two different methods of Titanium Dioxide Nanoparticles incorporation - An in vitro Study
Authors: A. Nida Fathima, K. Sheela Kumari, Murugesan Krishnan, Fathima Banu Raza, Rakshini Srinivasan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Aim:This study aims to evaluate the surface homogeneity of poly methyl methacrylate (PMMA) resin fabricated by two different methods of incorporating titanium dioxide nanoparticles (NPs).Settings and Design:In vitro study; qualitative analysis.Materials and Methods:PMMA resin was divided into two groups with 1% of titanium dioxide NPs were added to each group; Group A contained NPs incorporated in the polymer through magnetic stirring for 60 min and Group B contained NPs incorporated in the monomer through ultrasonic dispersion for 60 min. Ten acrylic block samples were fabricated by standard method for each group and then tested for surface homogeneity by scanning electron microscopy analysis for qualitative analysis.Results:Group A showed NPs as defined pale white dots with a consistent distribution pattern and minimal air entrapment. Group B showed NPs agglomerated forming clusters of white dots with flaky sheet-like structures.Conclusion:Magnetic stirring of NPs into polymer showed better surface homogeneity than the ultrasonic dispersion of NPs in monomer. The NP incorporation method plays a role in modifying the surface homogeneity of the acrylic.

INTRODUCTION

Poly methyl methacrylate (PMMA) resin with its ease of manipulation, low cost, and biocompatibility, makes it a popular choice for dental prosthetic applications. Throughout the years, a variety of substances, including nystatin, silver nitrate, aloe vera extracts, and tulsi extracts, were integrated to enhance the antimicrobial properties of PMMA.[1] After the introduction of nanotechnology, various nanoparticles (NPs) have been used to impart superior properties to the PMMA resin. Incorporation of metal NPs such as zirconium, titanium, aluminum, silver, and gold with PMMA resin improved the mechanical properties and durability of the denture material.[2] In addition, titanium dioxide NPs can provide antimicrobial properties, which can help prevent the growth of bacteria and fungi on the denture surface, thus contributing to improved oral hygiene for denture wearers.[34]

As titanium dioxide NPs appear to be one of the most common filler materials added in PMMA resin, the homogenous dispersion is vital for maintaining the properties of the NPs. Various studies analyzed the size of NPs for homogeneous dispersion. The presence of agglomeration and nonhomogeneous distribution of NPs can adversely affect the material’s performance. Several advanced mixing techniques were explored and developed to ensure a more uniform dispersion of NPs. However, the method of dispersion of NPs in polymer or monomer of PMMA to get homogeneous dispersion is not yet ascertained.

The homogeneity of mixture is necessary for preventing clumping; hence, an attempt was made to evaluate the efficiency of various methods of their incorporation in the resin. The aim was to evaluate the surface homogeneity of PMMA resin with different methods of dispersion of titanium dioxide NPs using scanning electronic microscope.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

An observational in vitro study was conducted with the fabrication of polymethyl methacrylate resin blocks after two different methods of incorporation of NPs. Acrylic block samples were fabricated using molds fabricated by three solid stainless steel blocks that are slightly larger in size than the sample measurements of 65 mm × 10 mm × 3 mm [Figure 1] as per ADA specification. The flasks were then subjected to dewaxing at 100° for 30 min. After dewaxing, the molds were used for packing the samples of PMMA resin that were incorporated with titanium dioxide NPs.

Stainless steel mold for acrylic block fabrication

Polymethyl methacrylate resin (dental products of India, Mumbai, India) were divided into two groups as Group A and Group B of 5 samples each based on method of incorporation of 1% of 18–35 nm size titanium dioxide NPs (Ultra nanotech, Bangalore, India) based on polymer weight (99%). Group A consists of incorporation of NPs into the polymer by magnetic stirring in a magnetic stirrer (Remi 1-LMH, Bengaluru, India) for 60 min [Figure 2].[5] Group B consists of incorporation of NPs into the monomer through the method of ultrasonic dispersion in a ultrasonicator (Aarkeylab Sonicator, New Delhi, India) for 60 min [Figure 3].[6]

The NPs incorporated resin was manipulated in a 3:1 ratio by volume. After attaining the dough consistency, the samples were packed in the prepared stainless steel molds, and cured for 1 h. After curing, the samples were retrieved and were subjected to acrylic trimming, finishing and polishing using number 180 sand paper (emery).

The acrylic block samples were subjected to scanning electron microscopy (SEM) analysis, for analyzing the surface homogenicity. The samples were fixed and coated with platinum in argon atmosphere on a vacuum evaporator. The samples were observed using scanning electron microscope (SEM, ZEISS, MERLIN, UKM Bangi, Selangor). A qualitative microscopic analysis was done to observe the surface homogeneity.

RESULTS

NPs were dispersed evenly throughout the PMMA matrix in Group A, presenting as pale white dots [Figure 4a]. Under low photomultiplier current, a consistent distribution pattern was evident, indicating effective dispersion [Figure 4b]. In addition, there is minimal air entrapment noted, suggesting a thorough integration of the dispersed NPs into the matrix.

Scanning electron microscopy image of Group A sample under (a) standard photomultiplier current (b) low multiplier current

NPs in Group B were agglomerated within the PMMA matrix, forming clusters of white dots that was evident from scattered pores of varying sizes visible throughout the material. Under standard photomultiplier current, these samples appear less compact, with the presence of flaky sheet-like structures, indicating a less homogeneous distribution of NPs [Figure 5a]. However, under low photomultiplier current, the surfaces of these samples appeared smoother, possibly due to the resolution of smaller-scale features [Figure 5b]. The surface roughness of Group A was 0.2 ± 0.01 µm whereas Group B was 0.4 ± 0.1 µm.

DISCUSSION

Incorporation of titanium dioxide NPs in acrylic resin has enhanced the antimicrobial properties by drastically reducing the bacterial adherence.[7] Once the NPs are incorporated in the resin, the resulting properties of the hybrid material will depend on the dispersion of NPs within the PMMA matrix. Hence, the present study involved using two different mixing methods to evaluate the distribution of NPs.

By refining the incorporation method, the study aimed to minimize particle clumping and achieve a consistent distribution within the PMMA matrix, to enhance the material’s strength, durability, and overall mechanical properties. The findings implement the importance of proper mixing techniques in developing NP-reinforced PMMA resins, paving the way for more effective and reliable applications in various fields. Previously, uniform concentrations of titanium dioxide NPs were used to prevent the formation of a heterogeneous mixture.[8]

We used 1% of the NPs as lower percentage ensured complete embedding of the particles within the resin.[910] However, between the two methods, magnetic stirring allowed homogeneous dispersion of the NPs. The uniform dispersion of the titanium dioxide NPs within the matrix hinders polymer chain movements due to the strong adhesion between the titanium dioxide NPs and PMMA, hence mechanical properties are improved.[11] We also used monomer as medium to disperse NPs using ultrasonicator method, as previous studies used medium like toluene.[6] Use of chemicals may alter the properties of the acrylic, hence monomer was used in the present study for dissolving of NPs using ultrasonicator method.

In this study, samples prepared by the method of magnetic stirring have shown a consistent pattern of distribution of the NPs when compared to the samples prepared by ultrasonic dispersion which have shown NPs to be agglomerated in the resin matrix. The outcome were in accordance with the Gad and Abualsaud showing uniform dispersion in the resin.[11] However, comparison of the methods in incorporating layered double hydroxides showed that magnetic stirrer was less effective in preventing agglomeration compared to ultrasonicator, which was in contrast to our study.[12] Similarly study comparing ultrasonicator and mechanical stirring also showed prevention of agglomeration with sonicator.[6] However, the above studies used different material base whereas ours is polymethyl methacrylate base. We also observed that surface roughness of the acrylic was lesser and uniform distribution with magnetic stirring compared to ultrasonicator. This could be due to the homogeneous mixture obtained by magnetic stirring method. Titanium dioxide NPs are commonly used as a coloring agents and fillers, they are capable of reinforcing mechanical properties like the toughness of polymers.[13]

This research underscores the importance of selecting appropriate mixing techniques tailored to the specific type of NPs used. Until now the particle size were given significant importance in obtaining a homogeneous mixture, but from the present study the method of manipulation showed variation in obtaining homogenous mixture. Furthermore, further refinement of these methods could lead to even greater enhancements in the properties of PMMA resins, leading to new possibilities for their application in fields such as dentistry, biomedical devices, and structural components which requires superior material performance. Limitations of this study, include different concentrations of NPs, the mechanical and biological properties of the processed acrylic were not assessed and have to be conducted as future research. Due to the less sample size, the statistical analysis could not be performed and this study is a proof of concept for further future analysis.

CONCLUSION

SEM analysis revealed magnetic stirring method has better dispersion of titanium dioxide NPs in PMMA resin with minimal air entrapment in comparison to ultrasonic dispersion. The uniform and efficient mixing achieved by magnetic stirring of NPs to polymer reduced particle agglomeration, and improved scalability that enhance particle interaction and overall performance. The study revealed the significance of NP incorporation method in altering the surface homogeneity of the acrylic resin.

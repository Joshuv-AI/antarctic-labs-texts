Addressing the Growing Epidemic of Obesity: Bariatric Surgery or Glucagon-Like Peptide-1 Receptor Agonists in Patients With Metabolic Dysfunction-Associated Steatotic Liver Disease?
Authors: Himsikhar Khataniar, Hany Habib, Helene Bloom, Kaitlyn Gernhard, Nabeeha Mohyuddin, Nikki Duong
Published: 2025

Currently, more than one billion people worldwide are obese, with rates doubling since 1990, making obesity one of the fastest growing public health concerns.1 In the United States, over 40.3% of adults meet the criteria for obesity.2 A parallel rise has occurred in the prevalence of metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD), which has been linked to obesity, type 2 diabetes mellitus, and cardiovascular disease.3 Despite growing awareness, many individuals with obesity and MASLD face challenges in achieving sustained weight reduction and managing comorbid conditions. Bariatric surgery (BS), particularly Roux-en-Y gastric bypass and sleeve gastrectomy, is recommended in patients with a body mass index (BMI) > 40 or >35 with comorbidities.3 Over 280,000 bariatric procedures were performed in 2022.4 In recent years, glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs) have emerged as an alternative for weight management. Initially indicated for type 2 diabetes mellitus, GLP-1 RAs (such as semaglutide and liraglutide) have shown effectiveness for weight loss improvement in metabolic conditions, including MASLD.5 This is the first study to compare BS to GLP-1 RAs in patients with obesity and MASLD.

We used TriNetX, which provides deidentified electronic medical records from 69 Health Care Organizations. All adults (>18 years) with MASLD from 2015 to 2021 were identified. MASLD was defined as an International Classification of Diseases code indicating fatty liver plus at least one cardiometabolic risk factor (BMI >25, glucose >100, hemoglobin A1c >5.7%, type 2 diabetes, hypertensive readings or medications, triglycerides >150, or high-density lipoprotein <40).6 Two cohorts were created: BS (Roux-en-Y gastric bypass or sleeve gastrectomy) and GLP-1 (semaglutide, liraglutide, or tirzepatide). The first occurrence of BS or GLP-1 followed the MASLD diagnosis. Patients were excluded for other liver diseases, excessive alcohol, major cardiovascular events before index, and cross-cohort therapies. The index date was the BS surgery or the first GLP-1 prescription. Primary outcomes were incident major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE), major adverse liver outcomes (MALO), and all-cause mortality within 30 days and up to 3 years. MACE and MALO were composite outcomes of major cardiovascular and liver events, described in detail in the supplementary document. Secondary outcomes included myocardial infarction, stroke, heart failure, liver transplantation, hepatocellular carcinoma, decompensated cirrhosis, and all-cause hospitalizations. Baseline characteristics were summarized. Propensity score matching was performed across 68 variables (demographics, diagnoses, procedures, medications, labs, and BMI), using logistic regression and nearest-neighbor matching (caliper width 0.1). P value >.05 indicated balance. Outcomes were expressed as adjusted odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Two-sided P < .05 was significant.

BS included 16,063 patients with a mean age of 46.3 ± 13.4 years; 74.17% were female, 68.33% White, 16.65% Hispanic or Latino, and 14.69% Black or African American. GLP-1 RA included 14,954 patients with a mean age of 52.9 ± 12.4 years; 61.08% were female, 70.07% White, 11.86% Hispanic or Latino, and 8.97% Black or African American. After propensity score matching (N = 5890 each), primary outcomes over 3 years were MACE 2.60% vs 2.73% (OR: 0.949; 95% CI: 0.758–1.188; P = .6472), MALO 1.22% vs 0.61% (OR: 2.012; 95% CI: 1.347–3.007; P = .0005), and all-cause mortality 1.39% vs 0.36% (OR: 3.946; 95% CI: 2.44–6.381; P = .0001). Secondary outcomes included myocardial infarction 0.99% vs 1.38% (OR: 0.713; 95% CI: 0.508–1.001; P = .0497), stroke 0.92% vs 0.56% (OR: 1.642; 95% CI: 1.063–2.536; P = .0238), decompensated cirrhosis 1.14% vs 0.42% (OR: 2.699; 95% CI: 1.703–4.279; P = .0001), and all-cause hospitalization 41.65% vs 38.13% (OR: 1.158; 95% CI: 1.076–1.247; P = .0001). Liver transplantation and hepatocellular carcinoma occurred in ≤0.17% of both groups (Table). BS produced a more pronounced reduction in BMI at 3 years (mean 32.74 vs 37.23 kg/m2; P < .001).

In this retrospective cohort study of patients diagnosed with MASLD, the incidence of MACE was similar between patients who underwent BS compared to those who received GLP-1 RAs over 3 years. However, patients in the GLP-1 cohort had significantly lower rates of MALO, all-cause mortality, and all-cause hospitalizations compared to the BS group. Our findings contribute to the growing body of literature examining the comparative effectiveness of pharmacologic and surgical interventions for obesity-related conditions. While BS has been established as an effective method for sustained weight loss and metabolic improvement,3 GLP-1 RAs have demonstrated promising benefits for weight management, glycemic control, and emerging evidence of hepatic benefit in MASLD.5 This study adds real-world evidence by directly comparing these 2 interventions on key clinical outcomes in patients with MASLD. The increased risk of postoperative complications with BS, including surgical site infections, bleeding, and long-term nutritional deficiencies, may contribute to the higher mortality and hospitalization rates seen in the BS cohort.7 Conversely, although GLP-1 RAs are associated with gastrointestinal side effects, they generally have a more favorable safety profile and may explain the reduced mortality observed.8 Interestingly, while BS has shown histological improvement in MASLD and reduction in cirrhosis progression,9 our data indicated a higher rate of MALO in the BS cohort. Potential explanations include malnutrition-related hepatic injury following surgery, a phenomenon described in case series of postbariatric acute liver failure.10 These findings warrant further investigation of the long-term liver-related risks following BS. Future studies should also explore whether specific surgical techniques or nutritional interventions can mitigate hepatic complications. Strengths of our study include the large, matched cohorts and comprehensive outcome assessment using a real-world electronic health record database. Limitations include the observational design, potential for residual confounding, and differences in baseline BMI, with the BS group having a higher mean BMI at baseline. This could influence weight-related outcomes and comorbidity burden. However, this was adjusted through subgroup matching of BMI where P values were >0.05, careful consideration of confounding factors and evidence-based International Classification of Diseases code use for cohort selection. In conclusion, both BS and GLP-1 RAs play significant roles in the management of MASLD. GLP-1 RAs were associated with lower MALO, mortality, and hospitalizations, while BS resulted in greater weight reduction. This suggests that in patients who may not be surgical candidates or who prefer medical weight loss methods, GLP-1 agonists should be highly considered, although multicenter, prospective studies to validate the efficacy of this drug class are desperately needed.

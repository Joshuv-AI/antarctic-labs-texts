Obeticholic acid ameliorates sepsis-induced renal mitochondrial damage by inhibiting the NF-κb signaling pathway
Authors: Danyang Jing, Jingfeng Liu, Da Qin, Jin Lin, Tian Li, Yu Li, Meili Duan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractAcute kidney injury (AKI), a common complication of sepsis, might be caused by overactivated inflammation, mitochondrial damage, and oxidative stress. However, the mechanisms underlying sepsis-induced AKI (SAKI) have not been fully elucidated, and there is a lack of effective therapies for AKI. To this end, this study aimed to investigate whether obeticholic acid (OCA) has a renoprotective effect on SAKI and to explore its mechanism of action. Through bioinformatics analysis, our study confirmed that the mitochondria might be a critical target for the treatment of SAKI. Thus, a septic rat model was established by cecal ligation puncture (CLP) surgery. Our results showed an evoked inflammatory response via the NF-κB signaling pathway and NLRP3 inflammasome activation in septic rats, which led to mitochondrial damage and oxidative stress. OCA, an Farnesoid X Receptor (FXR) agonist, has shown anti-inflammatory effects in numerous studies. However, the effects of OCA on SAKI remain unclear. In this study, we revealed that pretreatment with OCA can inhibit the inflammatory response by reducing the synthesis of proinflammatory factors (such as IL-1β and NLRP3) via blocking NF-κB and alleviating mitochondrial damage and oxidative stress in the septic rat model. Overall, this study provides insight into the excessive inflammation-induced SAKI caused by mitochondrial damage and evidence for the potential use of OCA in SAKI treatment.

Introduction

Sepsis is a life-threatening syndrome that causes high healthcare costs [1]. In addition, sepsis-induced acute kidney injury (SAKI) is a common disease for Intensive Care Unit patients because the kidney tissue is susceptible to damage during sepsis. According to statistics, the incidence of acute kidney injury (AKI) during sepsis reaches 23–51% (severe sepsis to septic shock). Moreover, the incidence of SAKI is associated with an increased mortality [2–4]. The severity of SAKI is also related to the prognosis of sepsis patients [5]. To date, due to the complicated pathophysiological mechanism of SAKI and the fact that there are no proven effective drugs available for clinical therapy, the only direct treatment currently used for SAKI is renal replacement therapy [6,7]. Thus, it is urgent and necessary to develop effective therapeutic approaches to protect the kidney from SAKI during sepsis by gaining a solid understanding of the mechanism of this disease.

According to previous reports, dysregulated inflammation occurs during sepsis, which causes injury and dysfunction of multiple organs [1]. The etiology of septic tissue damage involves complicated pathophysiological mechanisms, among which the pathogen-associated molecular patterns and danger-associated molecular patterns stimulate toll-like receptors, leading to activation of the NF-κB signaling pathway and the synthesis of many inflammatory factors that participate in the ‘cytokine storm’ [8–12]. Therefore, the NLRP3 inflammasome plays a critical role in septic tissue damage. The NLRP3 inflammasome incorporates several components, including NLRP3, ASC, and procaspase-1 to exert an inflammatory reaction by cleaving pro-IL-1β into its mature form that can be released [13–15]. The cytokine storm can also cause multi-organ injury and dysfunction, among which the kidney is commonly damaged by dysregulated inflammation [16,17].

Several studies have implicated that mitochondrial damage is involved in the initiation and progression from AKI to chronic kidney disease [18,19]. Since the epithelial cells lining the kidney tubules contain mitochondria, which produce the energy needed to maintain transport functions and kidney integrity [20], mitochondrial damage can reduce ATP production as well as induce a renal structure change and a loss of renal function. Furthermore, mitochondrial damage also results in the release of proinflammatory cytokines, which can trigger the inflammatory response and oxidative stress [21,22]. However, the mechanisms underlying the inflammation-mediated mitochondrial damage in the process of SAKI are not fully understood. Therefore, it is necessary to explore the corresponding mechanisms in order to develop effective therapeutic strategies for SAKI.

Farnesoid X receptor (FXR), also known as nuclear receptor subfamily 1 group H member 4, plays an essential role in bile acid homeostasis and regulation of lipid and glucose homeostasis as well as the innate immune response [23–25]. Furthermore, FXR also represses the production of proinflammatory cytokines in inflammatory bowel disease and has an anti-inflammatory effect on the development of this disease [26,27]. Obeticholic acid (6-ethylchenodeoxycholic acid, OCA) is an agonist of FXR used for the treatment of primary biliary cholangitis [28]. Nevertheless, there is a paucity of adequate studies on whether OCA alleviates SAKI. In this study, we aimed to investigate whether OCA has an anti-inflammatory effect on the development of SAKI and to explore its mechanism of action.

Method and materials

Drugs and reagents

OCA was purchased from Shanghai Aladdin Bio-Chem Technology Co. (Shanghai, China, l193491). The rat TNF-α enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kit (Shanghai Blue Gene Biotech, Shanghai, China, E03T0008), IL-1β ELISA kit (Shanghai Blue Gene Biotech, Shanghai, China, E03I0010), creatinine concentration determination kit, and malondialdehyde (MDA) concentration determination kit (Shanghai Blue Gene Biotech, Shanghai, China, E08M0023) were purchased from Shanghai Blue Gene Biotech. The primary antibodies of p65 (CST, Boston, MA, USA, 6956) and p-p65 (CST, Boston, MA, USA, 13346) were products of Cell Signaling Technology, and those of caspase-1 (Protein Tech., Wuhan, China, 22915-1-AP) and NLRP3 (Protein Tech., Wuhan, China, 68102-1-Ig) were purchased from Proteintech.

Animals and treatments

Male Wistar rats (250–300 g) were purchased from the Beijing Huafukang Biology Science Company. All animals were raised in a controlled environment in the Animal Experiment Center of Beijing Friendship Hospital at 24–26 °C, with a humidity of 40–45%, and a 12-h light/dark cycle. All animal experiments were conducted in compliance with the guidelines for humane treatment set made by the Association of Laboratory Animal Sciences and the Center for Laboratory Animal Sciences at Beijing Friendship Hospital. The experimental rats were divided into three groups: the cecal ligation puncture (CLP) group, the OCA + CLP group, and the control group. The rats in the CLP group initially received a CLP operation and then were divided into three subgroups (n = 6) according to the animal sacrifice time (6 h, 12 h, or 24 h after the operation). The rats in the OCA + CLP group received OCA administration at a dose of 5 mg/kg by gavage 2 h before the CLP operation. Similarly, the rats in the OCA + CLP group were also divided into three subgroups (n = 6) based on the different sacrifice times (6 h, 12 h, or 24 h after the operation). The sham group only received a laparotomy without the CLP operation. The rats in the control group were sacrificed at 24 h after the operation. Rats in OCA + CLP group and CLP group recieved CLP surgery and the ligation site of the cecum was approximately 1/4 distance from the distal end of the cecum. An 18-gauge needle was used to puncture the cecum, expelling a small amount of fecal matter before repositioning the cecum. The experimental rats were anesthetized by an intraperitoneal injection of 10% chloral hydrate solution before the operation. The blood and kidney samples were collected after the experimental rats were sacrificed.

Sample preparation and biochemical analysis

The serum was isolated from blood samples by centrifugation (4 °C; 3500 rpm, 15 min) and then preserved at −80 °C for further measurements. We collected specimens through the abdominal aorta of rats using a blood collection needle. In this study, the blood volume collected from rats ranged from 4 to 8 mL. The collected kidney tissues were preserved in a −80 °C freezer for western blot analysis and MDA concentration determination. Kits were used to measure the concentrations of serum creatinine and MDA in the renal tissues of the experimental rats.

Serum levels of TNF-α and IL-1β

The serum levels of IL-1β and TNF-α were measured using ELISA kits. The blood samples of each group were centrifuged at 3500 rpm for 15 min. The supernatant was collected for the analysis of cytokines by ELISA kits, according to the manufacturer’s protocol. After plotting the standard curves, the cytokine levels were calculated and finally expressed as pg/mL.

Western blot analysis

Tissuelyser was used to make the kidney tissue lysates. The protein concentration of the tissue lysates was determined by the bicinchoninic acid assay. Proteins were separated by sodium dodecyl sulfate–polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis, transferred onto a membrane, incubated with primary antibodies and the horseradish peroxidase [29]-conjugated secondary antibodies, and finally visualized by the enhanced chemiluminescence method. Primary antibodies against GAPDH (Protein Tec. Wuhan, China, 60004-1-Ig), IL-1β, caspase-1, NLRP3, p65, and p-p65, were used.

Histopathological analysis of kidney tissue samples

For histopathological analysis of renal fibrosis, 10% formalin-fixed kidney tissue samples were used. The collected tissue samples were subjected to paraffin embedding, followed by the preparation of 5-μm-thick continuous sections, routine hematoxylin and eosin (HE) staining, microscopic examination, and immunohistochemical examination, when necessary. In addition, the Paller score was used to evaluate the degree of renal histopathological injury and to quantify the level of tubular injury

Immunohistochemistry of renal tissues

After H&E staining (HE staining) of the renal tissue samples, the renal sections for immunohistochemistry were dewaxed and rehydrated in a graded ethanol series. After antigen retrieval (Sodium citrate, purchased form Solar Bio, Beijing, China, C1032) and quenching of endogenous peroxidase, the sections were incubated with NF-κB p65 monoclonal antibody (1:200 dilution) and FXR monoclonal antibody (Protein Tec, Wuhan, China, 67813-1-Ig, 1:200 dilution) at 4 °C overnight, followed by incubation with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies, and visualization of HRP. Finally, the sections were counterstained with hematoxylin. The dark reddish-brown areas in the section indicated the NF-κB p65(NF-κB p65-) and FXR-containing cells. The positively stained cells were observed under a microscope (400×) and blindly evaluated by two pathologists.

Evaluation of the mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP) by the JC-1 method

Adhering closely to the protocol provided with the mitochondrial isolation kit (Beyotime Co., Shanghai, China, C3606), we extracted mitochondria from fresh renal tissue specimens and immediately evaluated MMP by JC-1 staining (Suolaibao Technology Co., Beijing, China, M8650-100T). The MMP was assessed by the results of JC-1 staining and calculated as the fluorescence ratio of red (JC-1 polymer) vs. green (JC-1 monomer).

Transmission electron microscopy

Negative staining electron microscopy is a useful tool to examine changes in the cellular organelle structure. Phosphotungstic acid is a common negative staining reagent, by which the mitochondrial matrix can be densely stained. The collected renal tissue samples were chopped into small pieces of 1 mm2, and fixed in 2.5% glutaraldehyde solution at least for 2 h. After rinsing, the samples were further fixed with 1% osmium tetroxide solution, followed by a series of dehydration steps and then embedding. After curing, ultra-thin sections (70 nm thick) were cut using an ultramicrotome (Leica EM UC7), and then stained with a 2% solution of phosphotungstic acid. Next, the specimen was dropped onto formvar/carbon-coated copper mesh grids and allowed to absorb to the formvar for a couple of minutes, followed by drying at room temperature. Finally, the negatively stained specimens were examined using a transmission electron microscope (Hitachi Corp, Japan).

Bioinformatics analysis

To explore the molecular targets (or associated genes) of SAKI, bioinformatics analysis was performed. Using the keywords ‘sepsis’ and ‘AKI’ for searching the Gene Expression Omnibus, a publicly available database, several target genes were identified. Next, the R package ‘WGCNA’ was used to establish the co-expression network of the identified genes to identify the correlation between clinical traits and gene modules. As a result, similar mRNA expression patterns were clustered, and their heat maps were displayed. The modules associated with SAKI were further analyzed for their enrichment in Gene Ontology (GO) pathways. p < 0.05 was set as the cutoff for the data analysis. The R package ‘GOplot’ was adopted to show the results graphically.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis software GraphPad Prism 9 was used for data analysis, and the results are expressed as the mean ± standard deviation. Statistical analysis was carried out by one-way analysis of variance, followed by Tukey’s multiple comparison test. Nonparametric data were analyzed using the Mann–Whitney U test. A P-value ≤ 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

OCA alleviated SAKI

The reduction of the creatinine level in the rats receiving CLP (abbreviated as CLP rats) and the histopathological findings suggested that OCA pretreatment could ameliorate SAKI. As shown in Figure 1A, the serum creatinine level, an index of kidney function, in the CLP rats was greater than that in the control group, especially at 6 h and 12 h after CLP. The results of H&E staining(HE staining) revealed that there were many pathological changes in the renal cortex of the CLP rats, including intraepithelial vacuolar degeneration, swelling, and disturbance of the tubular structure (Figure 1C). In addition, the Paller score was used to evaluate the degree of renal histopathological injury and to quantify the level of tubular injury. The severity of damage was indicated by higher scores, with a maximum possible score of 10 per tubule. Points were assigned based on the presence and degree of various pathological changes: tubular epithelial cell flattening (1 point), loss of brush border (1 point), formation of cell membrane blebs (1 or 2 points), interstitial edema (1 point), cytoplasmic vacuolization (1 point), cell necrosis (1 or 2 points), and obstruction of the tubular lumen (1 or 2 points). The results indicated that compared to that in the control group, the renal tubular injury score was greater in the CLP rats, with a gradually increasing trend (Figure 1B) [30]. In contrast, OCA pretreatment attenuated the degree of CLP-induced SAKI at all time points (Figure 1B,C). Moreover, a decrease in the creatinine level was found in the CLP rats after OCA pretreatment.

Mitochondria might be a critical target of SAKI

In this study, we used weighted gene co-expression network (WGCNA) analysis to find the underlying targets of SAKI. Firstly, by searching for datasets related to sepsis and AKI, we ultimately identified GSE63311 in GEO database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE63311). This study includes a cohort of 37 sepsis patients and a control group of 35 volunteers, from whom blood samples were collected for high-throughput sequencing. Through cluster analysis of WGCNA, a total of 12 co-expression modules were constructed by bioinformatics methods (Figure 2A). Module–trait correlation analysis showed that the identified modules were correlated with sepsis or other septic clinical traits, such as SAKI and other organ dysfunction. The analysis also determined that the turquoise module was the most closely associated with SAKI (Figure 2B), and the correlation was negative. The genes included in the turquoise module are listed in Figure 2C. The GO analysis for the cellular components revealed that the mitochondria were a major target of SAKI (Figure 2D). The GO analysis also showed the biological processes (Figure 2E) and molecular functions (Figure 2F), and the results indicated that the biosynthesis of RNA, especially noncoding RNA, is abnormally altered during the process of SAKI.

OCA prevented renal mitochondrial damage and reduced reactive oxygen species (ROS) production during sepsis

To further evaluate the degree of damage of renal tissue mitochondria, the mitochondria were extracted from renal tissues and used to measure the MMP level by the JC-1 method, since an MMP decrease indicates the depolarization of the mitochondrial inner membrane potential, which is known to attenuate the generation of mitochondrial ROS. The results showed that the MMP level decreased significantly in the kidney tissue of CLP rats at 6 h after modeling (Figure 3B,C, p< 0.0001). In addition, OCA pretreatment attenuated the MMP decrease caused by infection (Figure 3B,C, p< 0.0001). As is well known, the risk factors for mitochondrial damage, such as inflammation, may lead to injury of the mitochondria, which are involved in the oxygen response. Therefore, the level of MDA and the production of ROS in the kidney tissue of CLP rats were also evaluated. The MDA level was found to start to increase at 6 h after modeling in the kidney tissue of CLP rats (Figure 3A, p< 0.0001). It is notable that OCA pretreatment reduced the MDA level in the kidney tissue of CLP rats (Figure 3A, p< 0.0001), suggesting that OCA treatment may alleviate the infection-mediated mitochondrial dysfunction and related oxygen response. Moreover, the mitochondria are also an energy factory in the cells. It has been reported that mitochondrial damage is a critical cause of renal tubular damage during sepsis. Furthermore, morphological changes in the mitochondria of the renal tubular epithelial cells were observed by transmission electron microscopy. Significant mitochondrial damage (occurring at 6 h after CLP modeling) was found, including mitochondrial disintegration, mitochondrial swelling, and vacuoles (Figure 3D). Mitochondrial swelling was the main manifestation of the infection-mediated mitochondrial damage, while OCA pretreatment alleviated it, including mitochondrial swelling in the renal tubular epithelial cells (Figure 3D).

OCA inhibited the production of proinflammatory cytokines and sepsis-induced NLRP3 inflammasome activation

The inhibitory effects of OCA pretreatment on the activation of systemic and renal inflammatory responses in septic rats were also investigated. Serum inflammatory factors, such as IL-1β and TNF-α, were selected as the indexes of a systemic inflammatory response. The results showed that in CLP rats, the plasma levels of IL-1β and TNF-α were significantly elevated at 6 h post-modeling (Figure 4A,B). However, infection-upregulated IL-1β and TNF-α expression in CLP rats was attenuated by OCA pretreatment (Figure 4A,B), suggesting that OCA might be involved in regulating the inflammatory response in CLP rats. Since the maturation of IL-1β from its precursor (pro-IL-1β) is controlled by activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome, the mature form of IL-1β can be considered as an index of NLRP3 inflammasome activation. Immunoblotting analysis revealed that abdominal infection led to the synthesis of IL-1β and its maturation in the renal tissue of the infected rats during sepsis, especially at 6 h and 12 h post-modeling (Figure 4C–E). The pretreatment of OCA inhibited the abdominal infection-induced IL-1β production and its maturation in the renal tissue of CLP rats (Figure 4C–E). Regarding the importance of the NLRP3 inflammasome in IL-1β maturation and the association of NLRP3 with NF-κB, the levels of several proteins associated with NLRP3 were detected by immunoblotting. As shown in Figure 5A,B,D, the levels of NLRP3 and caspase-1 were appreciably elevated in the renal tissue of CLP rats, especially at 6 h and 12 h post-modeling. Since the conversion of procaspase-1 into caspase-1 represents activation of caspase-1, the level of procaspase-1 in the renal tissue of CLP rats was also measured (Figure 5A). As shown in Figure 5A,C, infection also upregulated the expression of caspase-1. Nevertheless, OCA administration inhibited the abdominal infection-induced increase in critical proteins of the NLRP3 inflammasome and the corresponding activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome (Figure 5A–D).

OCA promoted the nuclear translocation of FXR and inhibited NF-κB activation

It has been previously reported that NF-κB is required for the expression of IL-1β and activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome. The activation of the NF-κB signaling pathway (levels of p-p65 and NF-κBp65) in the renal tissue of CLP rats was also examined. Although there was no obvious upregulation of p65 expression in the renal tissue (Figure 6A,C, p> 0.05), the level of p-p65 was found to be significantly increased in the renal tissue of CLP rats, especially at 6 h and 12 h post-modeling (Figure 6A,B, p< 0.0001). It is notable that OCA pretreatment inhibited the infection-elevated level of p-p65 in the renal tissue of CLP rats (Figure 6A,B, p< 0.0001), suggesting that the NF-κB signaling pathway was activated in the kidney tissue of CLP rats and could be suppressed by OCA pretreatment. To confirm this hypothesis, the nuclear translation of NF-κB in the renal tissue of CLP rats was also examined by immunohistochemistry. The results demonstrated that there was more nuclear staining of p65 in the tubular epithelial cells of the CLP rats than in the control rats (Figure 6D). The percentage of p65 (+) nuclear staining, as mentioned above, was reduced by OCA pretreatment (Figure 6D). This result confirmed that OCA pretreatment could inhibit activation of the NF-κB signaling pathway in the renal tissue of CLP rats. In addition, the involvement of FXR in the inflammatory response has been reported. In this study, we found that OCA pretreatment promoted the nuclear translation of FXR in the tubular epithelial cells of CLP rats (Figure 6E).

Discussion

The etiology of SAKI is relatively complex, involving inflammation, oxidative stress, and mitochondrial dysfunction, etc. [3,31–33]; but it is not fully understood. In this study, mitochondrial damage was proposed as the core event that facilitates SAKI, as demonstrated by bioinformatics analysis and validation with in-vivo experiments showing that overactivated inflammation and mitochondrial damage were present in CLP rats. Our findings are consistent with reports in the literature, which confirmed that mitochondrial damage activates the inflammatory response and leads to AKI [34–36]. It has been reported that due to infection caused by sepsis, proinflammatory cytokines are rapidly produced and released, which can cause a systemic inflammatory response [37,38]. However, whether inhibiting inflammation alleviate mitochondrial damage in SAKI remains unclear. In this study, we focused on sepsis-induced renal mitochondrial damage in an animal septic model (CLP rats) and inhibited inflammation by pretreated OCA to rats before CLP [39]. Our study found that OCA pretreatment could inhibit the renal inflammatory response via inhibiting the NF-κB signaling pathway and alleviating the mitochondrial damage and oxidative stress in the renal tissue of CLP rats. In combination with previous reports, our study revealed crosstalk between inflammation and mitochondrial damage.

Emerging evidence has demonstrated that the overactivated inflammatory response and the overexpressed inflammatory factors during sepsis can cause the dysfunction of multiple organs [40–43]. The activation of the classical NF-κB signaling pathway to upregulate its downstream proinflammatory factors, such as IL-1β, TNF-α, NLRP3, and IL-18, has been demonstrated to be a critical role of NF-κB in upregulation of the inflammatory cascade [44,45]. As is well known, IL-1β is one of the most important proinflammatory factors participating in the process of inflammation [46–48]. In the case of activation, there is a post-transcriptional modification for the precursor of IL-1β to transform into mature IL-1β. The NLRP3 inflammasome, a protein complex existing in the cytoplasm, is a critical player that triggers the caspase-1-dependent maturation and release of IL-1β [13,14]. Therefore, the levels of the critical molecules associated with the NF-κB signaling pathway (either upstream or downstream) in the renal tissue as well as the proinflammatory factors, such as IL-1β and TNF-α in the blood, were also examined in this study. Our results demonstrate that activation of the NF-κB signaling pathway was one of the infection-evoked inflammatory responses, as indicated by the increasing level of phosphorylated p65 and the nuclear translocation of NF-κB. Other responses included the upregulation of downstream molecules, such as IL-1β, and activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome. The acute tissue injury occurring in CLP rats was also assessed by serum creatinine levels and pathology scoring in our study.

On the other hand, the inhibitory effect of FXR on inflammation has been reported [49]. For example, Wang et al. have revealed that FXR inhibits an upregulated inflammatory response by antagonizing NF-κB [50]. In addition, the protective effect of INT-747 (an agonist of FXR) against intestinal mucosal damage in a mouse model of inflammatory bowel disease has been demonstrated as it inhibits the NF-κB-regulated inflammatory response [26]. Chen et al. also have shown that OCA-mediated activation of FXR can antagonize fetal death induced by lipopolysaccharide by suppressing activation of the NF-κB signaling pathway [51]. Zhu et al. found that OCA pretreatment inhibited NF-κB signaling pathway and alleviated AKI by activating FXR in LPS-treated rats [39]. These indicated that anti-inflammatory effect of OCA by activating FXR. Coincidentally, our study also showed that OCA pretreatment could inhibit inflammation by preventing the nuclear translocation of NF-κB and reducing the expression of its downstream molecules, such as IL-1β and NLRP3. Unexceptionally, our results revealed that OCA pretreatment could induce the activation of FXR and its nuclear translocation (confirmed by immunohistochemistry). Taken together, the anti-inflammatory effect of OCA was implemented by antagonizing NF-κB.

Oxidative stress is also an important factor that affects sepsis-induced tissue damage; because oxidative stress can amplify the inflammatory response, the inflammatory response also positively regulates the oxidative stress response [52,53]. Consistent with previous reports, our study showed that the MDA level, a representative marker of oxidative stress production, was increased in the renal tissue of CLP rats during sepsis. Meanwhile, OCA pretreatment decreased the infection-increased MDA level, suggesting that OCA can limit the inflammatory response and thereby suppress oxidative stress reactions. To facilitate a better discussion about oxidative stress, we observed the mitochondrial morphology and function because the damaged mitochondria is a main source of oxidative stress [54]. Dodson et al. also suggested that the mitochondria are not only the primary intracellular source of ROS and also as the target of oxidative stress [55]. Finding on mitochondrial damage and its dysfunction during sepsis have been reported [56]. The mitochondrial damage could be an early event in SAKI and could be observed even when blood and oxygen supply is still maintained [57,58]. In one study, through the analysis of kidney samples from deceased SAKI patients, it was found that in sepsis-AKI patients, the mRNA expression of oxidative damage markers is upregulated, with excessive damage to mitochondrial DNA and a reduction in mitochondrial mass [59]. These findings suggest that mitochondrial damage is likely to be a key factor throughout the course of acute kidney injury in sepsis. Similarly, we found depressed MMP and damaged mitochondrial morphology in the renal tissue of CLP rats, which could be alleviated by OCA pretreatment, suggesting that OCA pretreatment can promote the recovery of the MMP in CLP rats by inhibiting inflammation. The mitochondria are the energy factories of the cells, serving to sustain energy that is critical to cell survival and function.

Numerous studies have shown the importance of mitochondrial function to AKI. For example, Kraft et al. have revealed that mitochondrial damage and its dysfunction led to disturbance of energy metabolism, which causes organ dysfunction [60]. Marina et al. have demonstrated that renal mitochondrial damage is caused by sepsis, including the subsequent recovery of mitochondrial function over time [57]. Weiss et al. also have demonstrated that persistent mitochondrial dysfunction prolongs the recovery time of injured organs [61]. And in our study, by WGCNA and GO analysis, we found that mitochondria is the core cell component. This result indicated that mitochondria is likely to be the site and target of genes involved in SAKI. Both serum creatinine analysis and histopathological examination were used to evaluating the severity of AKI in our study [62,63]. And we found that mitigating mitochondrial damage contributed to the alleviation of sepsis-induced acute kidney injury. These results indicated the mitochondria damage is indeed a key event in SAKI progress.

In addition, we also found infection-induced overstimulated inflammation and activation of the NF-κB signaling pathway in the renal tissue of CLP rats. Since activated NF-κB leads to the upregulated expression of proinflammatory factors, like IL-1β and activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome, our study confirmed the association of the activated NF-κB signaling pathway, which can induce the inflammatory response, with mitochondrial injury and oxidative stress. Moreover, Sorbara and Girardin have shown that mitochondrial ROS can activate the NLRP3 inflammasome to trigger an inflammatory response [64]. Another study also has revealed that overactivated inflammation can cause mitochondrial injury [65]. Furthermore, activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome may exacerbate contrast-induced AKI by inhibiting mitophagy and aggravating mitochondrial dysfunction [66]. Similarly, our study demonstrated an interaction between inflammation and mitochondrial injury in the renal tissue of CLP rats.

Some shortcomings existed in our study that must be mentioned. First, we did not explore the mechanisms underlying the protective effects of OCA on mitochondrial damage in the renal tissue of CLP rats. Second, we used a mild rat CLP model, which might influence the degree of inflammation and make different phenotypes. In addition, different experimental environments, animal species, and animal ages may also cause different results.

In conclusion, our study revealed that the inflammatory response induced by the NF-κB signaling pathway during sepsis was an important factor causing mitochondrial damage, which led to SAKI. OCA treatment was shown to alleviate SAKI and to prevent mitochondrial damage via inhibiting the inflammatory response (Figure 7) cal Critical. Therefore, suppression of the inflammatory response might be an effective strategy for the prevention of renal injury during sepsis. Thus, OCA treatment is a potential therapy for SAKI.

Danger-associated molecular patterns and pathogen-associated molecular patterns can activate the NF-κB signaling pathway and promote the synthesis of proinflammatory factors, such as pro-IL-1β and NLRP3.

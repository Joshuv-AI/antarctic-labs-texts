SERPINH1, a regulatory factor of oligodendroglioma cell invasion and extracellular matrix secretion, as a novel prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target
Authors: Xue Chen, Shunyao Li, Huijuan Zhao, Lan Deng, Liyun Zhong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOligodendroglioma is a specific type of brain glioma, and relatively little research has been conducted on it. The expression and prognosis of serine protease peptidase inhibitor, branch H, member 1 (SERPINH1) in some malignant tumors have been studied, but the prognosis value and potential mechanism of this gene in oligodendroglioma have not been reported yet, and further research is needed. This study aims to reveal the expression characteristics and biological functions of SERPINH1 in oligodendroglioma cells, laying the foundation for targeted drug development.MethodsRetrospective RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) data analysis was conducted in a cohort of 171 patients with oligodendroglioma in the Chinese Glioma Genome Atlas (CGGA) database and 149 patients in The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database. In addition, we used Gene Ontology (GO), Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG), and Kaplan-Meier analyses, univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses, correlation analysis, as well as Kolmogorov-Smirnov test and Unpaired t-test.ResultsSERPINH1 is highly enriched in short-lived patients compared to long-lived oligodendroglioma patients. SERPINH1 is relatively highly expressed in more malignant oligodendrogliomas. Functional enrichment shows that SERPINH1 is closely related to the tumor invasion function of oligodendroglioma. Further research has confirmed that SERPINH1 promotes the invasive function of tumor cells by regulating the secretion of extracellular matrix. Prognostic analysis shows that SERPINH1 is an independent poor prognostic factor for oligodendroglioma.ConclusionsWe found that SERPINH1 can serve as an independent prognostic biomarker for oligodendroglioma. It may be a potential new target for the treatment of oligodendroglioma.Keywords: Oligodendroglioma; serine protease peptidase inhibitor, branch H, member 1 (SERPINH1); collagen; tumor cell invasion; prognosis

Highlight box

Key findings

• This study demonstrates that high expression of serine protease peptidase inhibitor, branch H, member 1 (SERPINH1) is strongly associated with shortened survival in patients with oligodendroglioma.

• SERPINH1 expression was closely linked to enhanced invasive behavior of oligodendroglioma cells. Furthermore, it was significantly correlated with extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling processes.

• SERPINH1 also exhibited a marked influence on the tumor immune microenvironment, showing associations with immunosuppressive features and inflammatory activity.

• Multivariate Cox analysis identified SERPINH1 as an independent prognostic factor for oligodendroglioma.

• SERPINH1 represents a promising therapeutic target aimed at inhibiting invasion and modulating ECM dynamics in oligodendroglioma.

What is known and what is new?

• Oligodendroglioma is generally associated with a more favorable prognosis compared to other glioma subtypes. SERPINH1 has been implicated in various cancers, with its expression and function demonstrating inconsistency across different tumor types, though its role in gliomas had not been previously explored.

• This is the first comprehensive report to establish SERPINH1 overexpression as an independent predictor of poor prognosis in oligodendroglioma. It provides novel insights into the mechanistic role of SERPINH1 in promoting tumor invasion and modulating the ECM, positioning it as a promising therapeutic target.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• These findings underscore the clinical value of SERPINH1 for prognostic stratification and highlight its potential as a target for molecular therapy. Further research is warranted to develop targeted inhibitors against SERPINH1 and to validate its utility in clinical settings for improving oligodendroglioma treatment.

Introduction

Glioma is the most prevalent and lethal brain neoplasm in adults (1). According to the World Health Organization (WHO)’s 2021 classification, typical adult gliomas are categorized into three subtypes: astrocytoma with IDH mutations, oligodendroglioma, and glioblastoma with mixed features (2). Among these subtypes, patients with oligodendroglioma generally have a more favorable prognosis. However, our clinical diagnosis and treatment have revealed that a significant proportion of patients with oligodendroglioma do not exhibit an optimistic outlook. The underlying mechanisms responsible for the poor prognosis in these patients remain unknown. Unraveling the intrinsic mechanisms that contribute to the unfavorable outcomes following oligodendroglioma development may hold the key to improving the prognosis for these individuals. Therefore, the identification of novel targets and the development of molecularly targeted drugs may pave the way for enhancing the prognosis of these patients.

Serine protease peptidase inhibitor, branch H, member 1 (SERPINH1) encodes a member of the serine protease inhibitor serine protease superfamily. SERPINH1 is localized in the endoplasmic reticulum and plays a role as a collagen specific molecular chaperone in collagen biosynthesis. SERPINH1 has been identified as a possible biomarker for several types of cancer (3-7). Studies have demonstrated that aberrant expression of this gene is closely associated with tumor growth, invasion, and metastasis (8-10). However, its functions appear to be inconsistent across different types of tumors. For instance, it has been reported that SERPINH1 is upregulated in breast cancer, gastric adenocarcinoma (STAD), and esophageal cancer (ESCA), and is associated with poor prognosis (5,11). On the contrary, in human neuroblastoma, abnormal methylation of the promoter CpG island results in relatively low expression levels of SERPINH1 (12). However, the function of SERPINH1 has not been reported in gliomas. Therefore, further research is needed on the molecular mechanism of SERPINH1’s role and its value in prognostic diagnosis.

We studied the SERPINH1 function from two independent databases of the Chinese Glioma Genome Atlas (CGGA) {CGGA and CCGA [2019]} and The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA), including SERPINH1 expression in oligodendrocyte tumors, immune regulation processes, inflammatory activity, and clinical recovery parameters. Through gene screening, we found that SERPINH1 was significantly overexpressed in short survival oligodendrocyte tumor patients, prognostic analysis found that SERPINH1 is an independent and exploratory prognostic factor. Further mechanism research has found that SERPINH1 is significantly correlated with the invasive ability and extracellular matrix (ECM) secretion of oligodendroglioma. Consequently, SERPINH1 represents a promising therapeutic target for oligodendroglioma and holds substantial clinical application value. We present this article in accordance with the REMARK reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2025-2106/rc).

Methods

Sample collection and data acquisition

This study used the mRNA sequencing data from CGGA portal (CGGA database: http://www.cgga.org.cn/download.jsp, ID: data sets mRNAseq_325 and mRNAseq_693, gene expression units: FPKM; TCGA database: http://www.cgga.org.cn/download_other.jsp, data set ID: TCGA RNAseq_699, gene expression units: FPKM). This study included 171 CGGA patients and 149 TCGA patients with oligodendroglioma had both IDH mutation and 1p/19q co-deletion according to the WHO’s 2021 classification. The transcriptome subtypes of SERPINH1 samples in the TCGA database were obtained from the TCGA portal (https://www.cancer.gov/). This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments.

Volcano plot

First, an unpaired t-test was performed using Microsoft Excel (v2021) for difference analysis. Subsequently, we identified functional enrichment of significantly upregulated genes in oligodendroglioma patients with different survival times. Finally, a volcano map was generated using the ggplot2 package, and the genes showed significant changes (P<0.05, unpaired t-test), labeled red or blue.

Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) analysis

Through the Pearson correlation analysis of the 500 genes, screening and SERPINH1 most relevant submitted to annotation, visualization and comprehensive database (DAVID, v6.8) (https://davidbioinformatics.nih.gov/) is analyzed. The official genetic symbol was chosen as the identifier, and Homo sapiens was chosen as the species. Finally, the GOTERM_BP_DIRECT and KEGG_PATHWAY in genetic biology are shown.

Immune biological analysis

Pearson correlation analysis was used to evaluate the relationship between the expression of SERPINH1 and immune checkpoints.

Prognostic analysis

Kaplan-Meier survival curve and logarithmic rank test were used to evaluate survival distribution and significance. Kaplan-Meier used R software (version 4.3.1, https://www.r-project.org) for the analysis. SPSS statistical software (version 26.0; IBM, Armonk, NY, USA) estimated the prognostic value of GLRX using univariate and multivariate Cox proportional risk model analyses. Patients with missing information were excluded from the analysis.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC) staining

Formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue samples from patients with oligodendroglioma were obtained from the CGGA database. Antibodies against SERPINH1 (Cat No. 83689-4-RR; CloneNo. 240752F2) and FN1 (Cat No. 98652-2-RR; CloneNo. 251989E9) were purchased from Proteintech Group, Inc. (Wuhan, China). IHC staining was performed using a Leica automated immunohistochemistry machine strictly according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The antibody concentrations were optimized at 1:200 for SERPINH1 and 1:4,000 for FN1, respectively. The IHC results were quantitatively assessed by a neuropathologist using the H-score method, a semi-quantitative scoring system that accounts for both staining intensity and the percentage of positive cells.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis and data visualization using R (https://www.r-project.org/, v4.3.1), GraphPad Prism software (v9.5), IBM SPSS Statistics (v26.0), and Microsoft Office (v2021). Cox regression analysis and Mantel-Cox analysis were used for survival analysis of different groups of patients. Based on the data distribution, unpaired t-tests were used to analyze the differences between the two groups. One-way analysis of variance was used to analyze the differences between the three groups. Based on the data distribution, Pearson correlation analysis was used to test the correlation between the two groups of data. The specific analysis method of each result is shown in the figure, and a P value less than 0.05 is considered to be statistically significant.

Results

Genetic differences in oligodendroglioma patients with different survival periods

To comprehensively elucidate the therapeutic resistance mechanism of TMZ-sensitive tumors, we conducted an in-depth analysis using transcription group sequencing data. Initially, we identified genes that exhibited significant alterations in patients with varying survival periods. In the CGGA database, a total of 1,542 genes demonstrated a substantial increase in the short survival oligodendroglioma group, while 408 genes displayed a significant decrease (Figure 1A). Furthermore, in the CGGA [2019] database, 720 genes exhibited a significant increase, whereas 506 genes showed a decrease in the short survival group (Figure 1B). Notably, there are 389 genes significantly elevated in both CGGA and CGGA [2019] databases (Figure 1C). Among these genes, the upregulation of SERPINH1 was particularly pronounced (Figure 1D). These findings strongly suggest that SERPINH1 plays a pivotal role in the unfavorable prognosis of oligodendroglioma, warranting further investigation.

Association of SERPINH1 expression with clinical and molecular pathological characteristics in oligodendroglioma

To investigate the role of SERPINH1 in oligodendroglioma, we compared the expression levels of SERPINH1 in normal brain tissue and oligodendroglioma. Given the histopathological heterogeneity of glioma, we analyzed the MGMT promoter state using RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) data from three independent databases, following the WHO guide. In the CGGA database samples, the expression of SERPINH1 was found to be higher in oligodendroglioma (level 3) compared to oligodendroglioma (level 2) (Figure 2A). This result was further validated by the RNA-seq data from the CGGA [2019] and TCGA databases (Figure 2B,2C). The MGMT promoter status plays a crucial role in the prognosis and chemotherapy outcomes of patients with oligodendroglioma. Regarding the status of the MGMT promoter in the CGGA database, we found that oligodendrogliomas with unmethylated MGMT promoter genes had lower SERPINH1 expression compared to the unmethylated group (Figure 2D). Similar trends were also observed in the other two databases (Figure 2E,2F). These findings indicate that the expression of SERPINH1 is enriched in oligodendroglioma and closely related to the malignant phenotype of oligodendroglioma.

SERPINH1 closely associated with immune processes in oligodendroglioma

To investigate the biological functions associated with SERPINH1, we screened the CGGA, CGGA [2019], and TCGA databases for 500 genes the most relevant to SERPINH1. Subsequently, we conducted GO and KEGG analyses using the aforementioned gene sets. In the CGGA database, the most relevant biological processes to SERPINH1 include cell adhesion, cell migration, and extracellar matrix organization (Figure 3A). The most relevant cell component to SERPINH1 is extracellar region (Figure 3B). The most relevant molecular function to SERPINH1 is protein kinase binding (Figure 3C). The most relevant signaling pathways to SERPINH1 include focal adhesion, PI3K-Akt signaling pathway (Figure 3D). Notably, the CGGA [2019] database exhibits similar SERPINH1-related biological processes, cellular components, molecular functions, and signal pathways (Figure 3E-3L). These findings suggest that SERPINH1 may exert a crucial role in regulating invasion and ECM dynamics.

SERPINH1 is associated with the invasion of oligodendroglioma

We examined the role of SERPINH1 in the cellular infiltration of oligodendroglioma (Figure 4A-4C). Our investigation revealed that the expression of SERPINH1, along with FN1, MMP9, PLAU, MMP2, VEGFA, CD44, AKT1, and IL6, is affirmative (Figure 4D). IHC staining revealed that patients in the SERPINH1 mRNA low-expression group exhibited significantly lower protein expression levels compared to those in the SERPINH1 mRNA high-expression group (Figure 4E,4F). Meanwhile, in clinical samples of oligodendroglioma, SERPINH1 protein expression was found to be significantly positively correlated with the expression of the ECM marker FN1 (Figure 4E,4G). These findings demonstrate that the escalating upsurge of SERPINH1 contributes to the positive regulation of genes associated with cellular infiltration. All of these discoveries have substantiated the significant involvement of SERPINH1 in the cellular infiltration of oligodendroglioma.

SERPINH1 is associated with the expression of ECM in oligodendroglioma cells

In the CGGA, CGGA [2019], and TCGA databases, we conducted a correlation analysis between SERPINH1 and the regulation of the ECM. Our findings revealed a positive correlation between the expression of SERPINH1 and the majority of ECM components (Figure 5A-5C). Furthermore, in the CGGA, CGGA [2019], and TCGA databases, there were 16 extracellular matrices significantly positively correlated with SERPINH1 expression (Figure 5D).

SERPINH1 is an independent adverse prognostic factor in oligodendroglioma

The Kaplan-Meier plot illustrates that the overall survival duration of patients with CGGA oligodendroglioma, as indicated by SERPINH1 high expression, is comparatively shorter (Figure 6A). These findings can be independently validated in both the CGGA [2019] and TCGA databases (Figure 6B,6C). Within the pan-cancer dataset, the high expression of SERPINH1 serves as an unfavorable prognostic factor (Figure 6D).

SERPINH1 serves as an independent poor prognostic factor and has potential guidance for postoperative treatment in patients with oligodendroglioma

In all three evaluated datasets, the expression of SERPINH1, the classification by the WHO, and the age of diagnosis are significantly associated with the overall survival rate of patients. After adjusting for the above clinical factors, multivariate analysis further confirmed that SERPINH1 is an independent adverse prognostic factor (Tables S1-S3). In CGGA database, the survival analysis revealed that patients with low SERPINH1 expression did not derive significant benefit from postoperative radiotherapy and chemotherapy (Figure 7A). In contrast, patients with high SERPINH1 expression showed a significant survival improvement after radiotherapy. Interestingly, we also found that within the high SERPINH1 expression group, there was no significant difference in survival outcome between patients receiving radiotherapy alone and those receiving combined radiotherapy and chemotherapy (Figure 7B). These findings suggest that SERPINH1 expression may serve as a potential indicator for treatment selection in oligodendroglioma.

Discussion

Oligodendroglioma is one of the most lethal malignant neoplasms that pose a threat to human health (13). Although temozolomide was approved by the US Food and Drug Administration for the treatment of glioma in 2005, researchers continue to search for new chemotherapy drugs with better efficacy to treat glioma (14). However, significant breakthroughs have yet to be achieved. Consequently, there remains an urgent need for innovative approaches to glioma treatment. In recent years, targeted drug has exhibited remarkable potential (15).

SERPINH1, also known as HSP47, is a collagen specific molecular chaperone located in the endoplasmic reticulum. In recent years, SEEPINH1 has been studied in some cancers, and SERPINH1 exhibits high expression in various tumors, including cervical cancer (8), colorectal cancer (16), gastric cancer (17), and breast cancer (18). Research has shown that SERPINH1 may be a serum biomarker for gastric diagnosis and prognostic evaluation, and may be a non-invasive biomarker reflecting adenomas and cancer, especially colorectal. However, the function of SERPINH1 has not been reported in gliomas at present. Therefore, we analyzed the expression of SERPINH1 in oligodendroglioma to identify its value in targeted therapy.

We initially examined the expression of SERPINH1 in oligodendroglioma and compared it with normal brain tissue. Our research findings indicate that, similar to other types of tumors, samples from oligodendroglioma exhibit higher levels of SERPINH1 expression. Subsequently, we conducted an analysis of RNA-seq data from 320 patients with oligodendroglioma, which were compiled from the CGGA, CGGA [2019], and TCGA databases. As expected, the expression of SERPINH1 is enriched in short survival oligodendroglioma and closely related to the malignant phenotype of oligodendroglioma. All included patients with oligodendroglioma in this study had both IDH mutation and 1p/19q co-deletion according to the WHO’s 2021 classification. Therefore, we did not perform a prognostic analysis based on molecular subtypes. Therefore, revealing the mechanism of SERPINH1 in oligodendroglioma may pave the way for the development of new treatment methods to combat this deadly malignant tumor.

In order to gain a deeper understanding of the biological functions of SERPINH1, we conducted a series of investigations. The GO and KEGG analyses reveal that SERPINH1 plays a crucial role in the infiltration of oligodendroglioma and the regulation of the ECM. Additionally, the Kaplan-Meier analysis demonstrates that SERPINH1 serves as a risk factor for patients with oligodendroglioma. Considering the limited reliability of the Kaplan-Meier analysis, we incorporated single-variable and multi-variable Cox regression analyses. The outcomes of these analyses further validate that heightened expression of SERPINH1 significantly diminishes the survival rate. Additionally, we unexpectedly discovered that patients with high SERPINH1 expression significantly benefited from postoperative adjuvant radiotherapy, whereas those with low expression did not derive significant benefit. In summary, our study found that SERPINH1 is an independent adverse prognostic factor in oligodendroglioma patients and may potentially guide postoperative adjuvant treatment strategies. However, these findings still require further prospective validation.

In addition, we found that SERPINH1 was significantly enriched in oligodendroglioma, and the upregulation of SERPINH1 was involved in the positive regulation of cell invasion related genes. Furthermore, we have conducted a correlation analysis regarding the regulation of SERPINH1 and the ECM, and have observed a significant positive relationship between SERPINH1 and the expression of most ECM components. Tumor cells that can synthesize their own ECM proteins have been shown to have high metastatic potential (19), and ECM has great potential as an effective target for treating cancer patients (20). In summary, these results indicate that SERPINH1 plays an important role in the invasiveness and ECM secretion of oligodendroglioma, and further indicate that the invasiveness of tumor cells is closely related to the poor prognosis of oligodendroglioma.

Conclusions

To summarize, SERPINH1 possesses promising potential as a prognostic biomarker in oligodendroglioma, offering a novel and efficacious avenue for therapeutic exploration and paving the path towards innovative treatment modalities for this condition.

Isolation, identification and pathogenicity analysis of a virulent duck enteritis virus strain causing outbreak in vaccinated duck flocks
Authors: Shuhui Sang, Haoqian Wang, Yuqing Dan, Huiyou Xiao, Yige Wang, Peihang Song, Zeng Wang, Dongliang Li, Xia Yang, Xinwei Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Duck viral enteritis, caused by duck plague virus, is a highly lethal infectious disease that continuously causes substantial economic losses to the global duck industry. In this study, a duck plague virus strain designated HNSQ0920 was isolated and purified from a vaccinated duck farm in Henan Province, China. Whole-genome sequencing and phylogenetic analysis revealed that HNSQ0920 clusters within the predominant virulent duck plague virus lineage currently circulating in China. Amino acid variation analysis identified mutations in four key virulence-associated proteins, including UL22 (F119L), UL41 (G44E), UL47 (A36V), and US7 (I168N). Further pathogenicity assays demonstrated that this isolate could infect and kill ducklings younger than 30 days of age, with a 100% mortality rate in contact-infected ducks. Collectively, these results suggested that HNSQ0920 is a potentially highly transmissible and virulent field variant strain. The present findings provide critical insights for duck plague virus vaccine development and disease prevention and control, and highlight an urgent need to strengthen biosecurity measures in duck production systems on farms within endemic regions.

Introduction

Duck plague (DP), alternatively referred to as duck viral enteritis (DVE), is an acute, septicemic, and highly lethal disease instigated by the Duck plague virus (DPV) (Shen et al., 2023). This virus belongs to the herpes virus family (Anatid herpes virus 1) and impacts ducks, geese, swans, and other anseriform birds (Chang et al., 2011). Owing to its high transmissibility and notable mortality rate, the disease has led to considerable economic losses in the global duck industry (Chang et al., 2011; Yang et al., 2014).

The establishment of a robust biosafety system and the implementation of vaccination are the most efficacious strategies for preventing infection with the DPV in duck farms (Dhama et al., 2017). Nevertheless, reports of DPV outbreaks in vaccinated duck flocks imply that emerging field variant strains have posed substantial challenges for the prevention and control of duck plague (Wang et al., 2013; Zhang et al., 2026). In the present study, a DPV strain was isolated from a large-scale commercial laying duck farm, where duck plague outbreaks occurred despite routine immunization with live attenuated duck plague vaccines. Subsequent investigations of its genomic characteristics, key virulence proteins, and pathogenicity were performed, aiming to provide fundamental evidence for further molecular epidemiological studies of DPV and future vaccine development.

Materials and methods

Materials

Specific pathogen - free (SPF) duck embryos were purchased from Shandong Haotai Laboratory Animal Breeding Co., Ltd.

The following reagents and materials were used in this study: the RNAsimple Total RNA Extraction Kit (TIANGEN, China Cat#DP419); the ReverTraAce™ qPCR RT Kit reverse transcription reagent (Toyobo (Shanghai) Biotech Co., China, Cat#FSQ-101); the Total DNA Extraction Kit (Vazyme Biotech Co., Nanjing, China Cat#DC112-01).

Methods

Sample collection

Tissues including liver, spleen and small intestine were harvested from six ducks suspected of being infected with DPV. These tissues were placed in sterile EP tubes and homogenized using sterile phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) and steel beads. The homogenate underwent repeated freeze - thaw cycles at - 80 °C, followed by centrifugation to obtain the supernatant for subsequent DNA and RNA extraction. The extracted nucleic acids were amplified through polymerase chain reaction (PCR) and reverse transcription - polymerase chain reaction (RT - PCR), respectively, and analyzed by gel electrophoresis to confirm the presence of the DPV while ruling out contamination by exogenous viruses.

Based on the reference strain sequence (GenBank accession no. MN518864), specific primers for DPV were designed using Snap Gene and Premier 5 software. Virus isolation and purification

The supernatant was carefully aspirated and then filtered through a 0.22 μm bacterial filter. Subsequently, 0.2 mL of the filtrate was inoculated onto the chorioallantoic membrane of each 10 - day - old SPF duck embryo. The control group received an equivalent volume of sterile PBS for inoculation. Embryos that perished within 24 h after inoculation were excluded from the study, and the remaining embryos were observed at 12 h intervals for a consecutive 7 day duration. The livers and spleens were excised from the deceased embryos, and the allantoic fluid was collected from the embryos. The excised tissues were homogenized and stored at - 80 °C for subsequent experimental procedures. Viral genomic DNA was extracted from the collected allantoic fluid and tissue homogenates using a total DNA extraction kit. This was followed by PCR amplification, and the products were then identified through agarose gel electrophoresis.

Whole genome sequence analysis

Genomic DNA was extracted and delivered to Sangon Biotech (Shanghai) Co., Ltd. for next-generation whole-genome sequencing. The whole genome of the DPV strain was sequenced using the Illumina platform. After quality filtering, high-quality clean reads were de novo assembled and optimized using SPAdes (v3.5.0). Gene elements were predicted with Prokka (v1.10), and gene functional annotation was performed based on the NR, GO, KEGG, and COG databases to characterize the genomic features of the strain. A total of 21 reference genomes of DPV strains from domestic and international sources were retrieved from NCBI GenBank (accession numbers provided in Supplementary File 1). Whole nucleotide sequences and key virulence gene sequences (including UL22, US7, UL1, UL47, and UL41) were aligned and analyzed using DNASTAR’s MegAlign MegAlign module (v7.1.0, DNASTAR) and MEGA7 software. Phylogenetic and sequence variation analyses were performed to assess evolutionary relationships and mutation patterns. Figures were generated and refined using Origin 2024, iTOL v7 and IBS 2.0.

ELD50 determination

The harvested allantoic fluid containing the virus was serially diluted ten - fold in sterile PBS to create dilution gradients spanning from 10⁻³ to 10⁻⁸. All dilutions were prepared in sterile EP tubes. For each dilution, a 0.2 mL aliquot was inoculated onto the chorioallantoic membrane of duck embryos. Five embryos were allocated to each dilution group. A control group receiving only sterile PBS was established, in which an equivalent volume of sterile PBS was inoculated into the embryos under identical conditions. After inoculation, all embryos were placed in a constant - temperature incubator and monitored for seven consecutive days. Embryo mortality was documented daily. Subsequently, the 50% duck embryos lethal dose (ELD₅₀) of the virus was calculated using the Reed - Muench method.

Pathogenicity assessment

To assess the pathogenicity of the isolated virus and its potential for natural transmission, twenty 18 - day - old healthy Huabian Meat Ducks (a local meat - type duck breed in China) were randomly allocated into three groups: an inoculated group (n = 6), a negative control group (n = 6), and a contact - challenged group (n = 8). Ducklings in the inoculated group were administered 0.2 mL of the viral suspension through intramuscular injection, whereas the control group received an equivalent volume of sterile PBS. In the contact - challenged group, four inoculated ducklings were housed together with four naive ducklings to evaluate horizontal transmission. Throughout the experiment, all animals were monitored daily for mortality and clinical manifestations. Necropsies were conducted immediately on ducks that died or at the experimental endpoint to examine macroscopic lesions in visceral organs. Tissue specimens from various organs were collected, fixed in neutral buffered formalin, and dispatched to Wuhan Servier Biotechnology Co., Ltd. for histopathological processing and analysis.

Results

PCR identification

Subsequently, comprehensive clinical diagnoses and post - mortem examinations were carried out. Virus isolation and purification

The processed tissue homogenate was inoculated into 10 - day - old SPF duck embryos. By the fifth day after inoculation, all three inoculated embryos had perished, while the control group remained unchanged. The deceased embryos exhibited generalized erythema and hemorrhage. Necropsy of the embryos in the infected group indicated the liver from the control group showed normal reddish-brown color, smooth surface. The liver from the DPV-infected group exhibited a grayish-yellow discoloration, dull luster (Fig. 1E). Liver and spleen tissues were collected, and allantoic fluid was acquired for DNA extraction. Subsequent PCR amplification and gel electrophoresis confirmed a DNA fragment corresponding to the expected size of the target gene (Fig. 1D).

Pathogenicity assessment

ELD50 determination

Serial dilutions of the purified virus ranging from 10-3 to 10-8 were inoculated into SPF duck embryos (5 embryos per dilution). At the 10-3 dilution, 2 out of 5 inoculated embryos died on day 4 post-inoculation, and all embryos succumbed by day 5. For the 10-4 dilution, 2 embryos died on day 5, with all remaining embryos dead by day 6. At the 10-5 dilution, only 2 embryos died on day 6, whereas 3 embryos remained viable. All embryos inoculated with higher dilutions (10-6 to 10-8) survived throughout the observation period. The 50% buck embryos lethal dose (ELD50) of the purified viral strain was calculated via the Reed – Muench method and determined to be 10-4.83/0.2 mL.

Clinical signs and postmortem lesions characteristics of experimental ducklings

In the artificially infected ducks (6 ducks in the inoculated group and 4 ducks in the contact - challenged group that underwent direct inoculation), clinical manifestations such as ruffled plumage, eyelid swelling, lacrimation, nasal secretion, and lethargy were noted at 2 days post - infection (dpi), and mortality commenced at 3 dpi. Necropsy findings indicated mild hepatic necrosis, splenomegaly, hemorrhagic papillae of the proventriculus, hemorrhagic rings at the proventriculus - esophagus junction, severe esophageal hemorrhage, and annular hemorrhage in the small intestine. Mortality reached its peak at 4 dpi. By 6 dpi, 8 out of the 10 artificially infected ducks had succumbed (80% mortality), while the remaining 2 ducks survived beyond 13 dpi. In the contact - challenged group, the naïve ducks (not directly inoculated) exhibited distinct clinical signs such as lacrimation, nasal secretion, and lethargy starting from 7 dpi, with mortality initiating at 8 dpi. The macroscopic pathological lesions were consistent with those in the artificially infected ducks, and all naïve contact - exposed ducks died by 11 dpi, leading to a 100% transmission - related mortality rate. Pathological histology observation of experimental ducklings

Histopathological examination of organ sections demonstrated distinct pathological lesions among the experimental groups. In the inoculated group, the livers manifested extensive necrosis, prominent vascular congestion, and a wider range of vacuolar degeneration. In comparison, hepatic vacuolar degeneration was alleviated in the contact - challenged group. In the inoculated group, the spleens showed mild inflammatory cell infiltration, diffuse red pulp congestion, and enlarged necrotic foci accompanied by sinusoidal congestion. Conversely, the contact - challenged group presented a larger area of red pulp congestion in the spleen. The small intestines of the inoculated ducks exhibited extensive hemorrhage, significant inflammatory cell infiltration, and occasional dilation of intestinal glands, while such dilation was not observed in the contact - challenged group. The esophagi of the inoculated group had severe hemorrhage and a greater degree of epithelial desquamation, a lesion that was less prominent in the contact - challenged group. In the inoculated group, the glandular stomach exhibited focal erosion, mild infiltration of inflammatory cells, and slight hemorrhage. Whole genome sequencing results and analysis

Whole genome analysis of isolated strains

Whole-genome sequencing demonstrated that the total length of the viral genome was 161,017 base pairs (bp). Nucleotide sequence alignment conducted via MegAlign software indicated that the isolate shared a sequence similarity of 95.6% - 100% with reference strains. Specifically, it displayed a similarity of 99.9% - 100% to domestic strains, presenting 100% sequence identity with the Sichuan strain CHv, Wuhan strain C - KCE, Beijing strains CV and CVC20E85, Shandong type 1 strain, as well as the Beijing type 1 and CVp80 strains. Conversely, the similarity to international strains ranged from 95.6% to 99.8%, with the lowest identity (95.6%) being observed with the India strain DP - AS - Km - 19 and Germany strain 2085 (Fig. 4A). Further phylogenetic analysis revealed that the isolate clustered within a major clade that included strains from Shandong, Wuhan, Beijing, and Sichuan provinces. All strains within this evolutionary clade display intimate phylogenetic associations (Fig. 4B). The findings of this study indicate that the isolates identified herein possess a close phylogenetic kinship with the strains currently in circulation in China. Nevertheless, they exhibit 100% sequence homology with the reference strains, suggesting a latent risk of cross - regional transmission.Fig. 4Analysis of All Genes. Analysis of key virulence proteins in isolated strains

Nucleotide sequence alignment indicated a high degree of similarity between the isolated strain and reference strains across the major genomic regions. Specifically, the similarity within the UL22 region ranged from 99.8% to 100% (Fig. 5A). The US7 similarity values range from 98.5% to 100% (Fig. 5B). The UL1 and UL47 regions exhibited similarities of 99.7% – 99.9% (Fig. 5C) and 99.7% – 100% (Fig. 5D), respectively, whereas the UL41 region showed a similarity of 99.3% – 100% (Fig. 5E). Amino acid mutation analysis identified four substitutions in the HNSQ0920 isolate compared to the reference strains: F119L (UL22), G44E (UL41), A36V (UL47), and I168N (US7) (Fig. 6). Given the large genome size of the duck plague virus and the crucial role of protein - protein interactions in host invasion and pathogenesis, these mutations are potential molecular determinants that influence viral virulence. The specific substitutions in key virulence - associated proteins may contribute to the immune evasion capacity of the isolate, potentially accounting for its vaccine - escaping phenotype. Discussion

Duck viral enteritis persists in presenting a serious threat to the global duck industry. Despite the widespread utilization of diverse commercial vaccines, outbreaks still transpire in vaccinated flocks (Wang et al., 2013; Zhang et al., 2026). Suggesting that not all duck breeds attain complete protection (Dhama et al., 2017). In the present study, clinical specimens were collected from a commercial duck farm in Henan Province, China. This farm housed approximately 100,000 laying ducks of the Ma Ya breed, an indigenous Chinese laying duck variety. These specimens were confirmed as positive for DPV via PCR, despite the duck flock having received two doses of a commercial live attenuated vaccine against DPV. Subsequently, a strain of DPV was isolated and named HNSQ0920. Pathogenicity evaluation and whole - genome sequencing indicated that this field isolate exhibits high virulence and may possess potential immune evasion capabilities, which is consistent with previous reports of vaccine breakthrough incidents (Wang et al., 2013). Notably, the vaccine breakthrough infection observed in this study is unlikely to be caused by common external factors, such as cold chain disruption, improper vaccination procedures, host immunosuppression, or maternal antibody interference. Standardized immunization and breeding management were strictly enforced on the affected farm prior to the outbreak, largely ruling out artificial operational errors. Therefore, it is hypothesized that the potential immune evasion properties of the HNSQ0920 strain may be the primary cause of this vaccine breakthrough event. However, since the potency of the vaccine batch and the pre - outbreak antibody levels of the duck flock were not evaluated in this study, other uncharacterized influencing factors cannot be completely excluded.

In pathogenicity evaluations, although natural DPV infections mainly impact ducks older than 30 days (Tang et al., 2024), our research revealed that uninfected ducklings in the contact infection group (Fig. 2) died of DVE without direct virus inoculation. Their mortality rates and clinical symptoms were completely consistent with those of naturally infected adult laying ducks from the same farm (Fig. 1A). These findings suggest that the HNSQ0920 strain is capable of infecting both ducklings and adult ducks, encompassing meat - type and egg - type duck breeds. The clinical manifestations and post - mortem lesions in both the inoculated and contact - infected groups were in line with the classical descriptions of DPV (Liang et al., 2022; Tang et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2025). Notably, the mortality rate in artificially infected ducks reached 80%, while horizontal transmission led to a 100% mortality rate. Considering that non - specific pathogen - free (non - SPF) ducks were employed in this study, individual variations, fluctuating residual maternal antibody levels, and diverse innate immune capabilities jointly contributed to the survival of some hosts. This observation is consistent with previous reports in the literature (Burgess et al., 1979; Yue et al., 2007). Nevertheless, the natural transmission route simulated by contact infection allows the virus an extended incubation period, enabling sufficient viral pathogenicity to manifest, thereby achieving successful infection and resulting in the death of all exposed individuals. This finding underscores the strong adaptability and high lethality of the HNSQ0920 strain under realistic natural transmission conditions.

At the genomic level, DPV is an enveloped, linear, double - stranded DNA virus, with its complete genome spanning approximately 150 - 160 kb (Aasdev et al., 2021; Wu et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2017). The genome encodes a total of 78 open reading frames (ORFs). Among them, 65 and 11 are respectively located in the unique long (UL) and unique short (US) genomic regions, while the remaining two ORFs map to the internal repeat short (IRS) and terminal repeat short (TRS) regions (Li et al., 2009). Moreover, the UL and US regions, each composed of distinct unique nucleotide sequences, are flanked by the IRS and TRS (a set of inverted repeat sequences corresponding to the internal and terminal repeat segments), and the viral genome adheres to the canonical architectural arrangement of UL - IRS - US - TRS (Wu et al., 2012). Currently, the glycoprotein H encoded by the UL22 gene is highly conserved among herpesviruses and can interact with the glycoprotein L encoded by the UL1 gene. It is a key virulence protein involved in the invasion of host cells (Atanasiu et al., 2023; Feng et al., 2018). The glycoprotein I encoded by US7 is a crucial protein influencing the virulence of DPV, which can diminish the virus's ability to spread between cells (Liu et al., 2020; Ning et al., 2024). The UL41 and UL47 gene regions of DPV are significantly related to immune escape. The UL41 gene can regulate mRNA and participate in viral replication (He et al., 2021, 2022). The UL47 gene serves as a substrate for phosphorylation of the US3 protein and interacts with the US3 protein in the cytoplasm, promoting the localization of the virus in the cytoplasm during the late stage of viral infection (Deng et al., 2022). These key virulence proteins associated with viral invasion and replication play a vital role in the pathogenic evaluation of field - isolated wild duck plague virus. Notably, phylogenetic analysis revealed that this isolate belongs to a major clade comprising strains from Chinese regions including Wuhan, Shandong, Beijing, and Sichuan. Furthermore, the isolate in this study shares a common evolutionary origin with the virulent Sichuan CHV strain, the virulent Beijing CV strain, and the Beijing type 1 vaccine strain. Collectively, these results suggest either enhanced virulence of the wild-type virus or virulence reversion of the vaccine strain (Fig. 4B). However, a unique amino acid substitution, F119L, was identified in the UL22 gene (encoding glycoprotein H, gH) of the HNSQ0920 isolate. Previous studies have demonstrated that gH and gL not only co-localize but also interact with each other in COS-7 cells (Feng et al., 2018). However, no amino acid mutations were detected at the amino acid positions of the gL protein encoded by the UL1 gene; only mutations occurred at the nucleotide level (Fig. 5C). Differential mutations at distinct levels in the gH and gL proteins may alter the conformation or function of the gH/gL complex, thereby enhancing fusion efficiency and facilitating immune escape. In addition, amino acid substitutions were detected in UL41 (G44E), UL47 (A36V), and US7 (I168N), and identical amino acid mutations at these positions were also present in the Beijing type 1 vaccine strain (Fig. 6). The gI protein encoded by US7 is critical for cell - to - cell spread and viral envelopment, whereas UL41 and UL47 are associated with immune evasion. Mutations or deletions in these genes often impair viral propagation and growth (Cui et al., 2025; He et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2020; Ning et al., 2024). Therefore, it is speculated that combined mutations in these key virulence proteins may act synergistically to enhance the viral capacity to evade host immune responses, which might potentially explain the vaccine failures observed in the field. Nevertheless, it remains unclear whether such alterations in the present isolate are functionally associated with these phenotypes, and further investigations are still needed for verification.

Furthermore, DPV can be transmitted bidirectionally between domestic and wild waterfowl, posing substantial ecological risks. Epidemiological evidence indicates that wild waterfowl, acting as reservoirs, can shed the DPV orally and cloacally, thereby facilitating cross - regional spread (Brand et al., 1988; Woźniakowski et al., 2014). Convalescent poultry may develop persistent infections with intermittent viral shedding, serving as potential sources for new outbreak (Shawky et al., 2002). Thus, introducing infected individuals, including asymptomatic birds, into naïve flocks or uncontaminated water bodies carries a high risk of triggering epidemics (Dhama et al., 2017). Similarly, anthropogenic activities, especially the domestic and international trade of ducks and duck products, can drive regional and even global dissemination of the disease. In line with this, recurrent DPV outbreaks have been documented in vaccinated duck populations in several regions of China in recent years (Wang et al., 2013; Zhang et al., 2026). Together, these observations suggest that migratory bird movements, human activities, and the trade of duck products may collectively contribute to the spread of this strain, while the genetic variations accumulated during such processes may enhance viral adaptability and transmissibility. In this context, the emergence of hypervirulent field variants that cause more severe disease outcomes urgently requires sustained global epidemiological surveillance and close attention from veterinarians, farmers, and relevant stakeholders. Notably, direct contact between domestic ducks and wild waterfowl was not observed during the on-site investigation; however, potential indirect or unobserved contact cannot be completely ruled out.

Conclusion

In the present study, a highly virulent strain of DPV, namely HNSQ0920, was successfully isolated from vaccinated laying duck flocks in Henan Province, China. This strain exhibits four amino acid substitutions in the key virulence - associated proteins UL22, UL41, UL47, and US7. These findings broaden the existing understanding of DPV molecular epidemiology and offer a promising viral candidate strain for the development of novel vaccines aimed at preventing and controlling DPV infection.

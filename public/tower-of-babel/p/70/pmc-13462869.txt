A multi-scale feature fusion gaze estimation model based on convolutional neural network and vision transformer
Authors: Peng Wang, Xuena Wang, Shuo Yuan, Shan Cong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
To address ineffective feature fusion and feature loss in gaze estimation under unconstrained environments, this study proposes a multi-scale feature fusion model, CAF-ViT (Cross-Attention Fusion Vision Transformer). The model takes multi-scale face images as input, uses ResNet-18 to extract feature maps at different granularities, and introduces learnable Class Tokens per scale. In the fusion stage, Class Tokens of different scales first perform self-attention in their respective Transformer Encoders to aggregate local details and global semantics. Then, by swapping the token sequences and computing cross-attention, the model achieves bidirectional interaction and deep fusion of coarse- and fine-grained features. To further refine feature representation, an additional attention layer is added after cross-attention. It linearly transforms the original query vector with Sigmoid activation to generate new query weights, and linearly maps the attention output to new value vectors, improving the representation of task-relevant features. The fused Class Token is finally regressed to gaze direction via a multilayer perceptron. Experiments show estimation errors of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$3.73^\circ$$\end{document} on MPIIFaceGaze, representing an 8.6% improvement over the baseline hybrid CNN-Transformer, and errors of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$5.27^\circ$$\end{document} on EyeDiap and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$10.43^\circ$$\end{document} on Gaze360. Ablation studies validate the multi-scale fusion strategy and improved attention mechanism.

Introduction

In daily communication and social interactions, eye gaze behavior serves not only as a critical medium for information transmission but also as a fundamental component of non-verbal human communication1. Gaze estimation, which infers an individual’s gaze direction by analyzing eye movement trajectories from visual data, plays a pivotal role in identifying the direction, location, focus of attention, emotional state, and interactive intent of the point of interest of a user. With the advancement of computer technology, analysis of eye movement behavior using the gaze estimation technique has found extensive applications in diverse fields, including consumer behavior research, human-computer interaction systems2, intelligent transportation solutions, brain science3, and psychological studies.

Human gaze estimation methods are generally divided into two categories: model-based methods and appearance-based methods4. Model-based approaches use fixed eye features that remain stable during eye movement as references. These methods estimate gaze direction by analyzing dynamic features that change relative to fixed references and calculating changes in these features5. However, such methods face practical limitations, including high hardware dependency, complex calibration processes, and poor adaptability to unconstrained environments.

Appearance-based gaze estimation methods take binocular or facial images as input and directly predict gaze direction by learning the implicit nonlinear mapping between visual inputs and gaze vectors6. Without the need for explicit extraction of ocular anatomical structures, these methods can be deployed using standard cameras and exhibit robust performance against variations in image resolution and environmental conditions, making them the mainstream research direction for unconstrained environments. With the development of deep learning technology, multi-scale feature methods integrating Transformers have become a research hotspot in this field. Leveraging the global modeling capability of Transformers to aggregate multi-scale features, these methods effectively mitigate estimation errors caused by head pose variations and enhance model generalization.

In recent years, extensive research and exploration have been conducted on multi-scale feature fusion and CNN-Transformer hybrid architectures for gaze estimation. Convolutional neural networks can efficiently extract local texture and fine-grained structural features of the eyes and face, while vision Transformers excel at modeling global context and long-range dependencies. The two architectures exhibit inherent complementarity in feature representation. However, most existing fusion strategies remain at the level of simple combination such as direct concatenation and element-wise addition, lacking deep interaction between multi-scale features and failing to fully exploit the complementary advantages of coarse-grained and fine-grained features7. Meanwhile, in complex scenarios, interferences such as background clutter, head pose variations and illumination changes introduce massive redundant information, making it difficult for models to effectively focus on key regions highly relevant to gaze, thereby further restricting the improvement of estimation accuracy.

To further improve the feature utilization efficiency and fusion effectiveness of gaze estimation in unconstrained environments, this paper proposes CAF-ViT, Cross-Attention Fusion Vision Transformer. Based on the complementary advantages of convolutional neural networks and vision Transformers, the model employs ResNet-18 to extract multi-scale feature maps from face images, and achieves deep interaction and precise complementation of cross-scale information through Class Tokens and a cross-attention mechanism, which efficiently fuses core features while avoiding information degradation. In addition, the model incorporates an extra attention layer designed to refine the feature representation for gaze estimation, thus significantly improving the overall estimation accuracy and robustness. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method achieves superior performance on multiple public datasets and can effectively handle gaze estimation tasks in complex scenes.

Related work

Deep learning-based gaze estimation methods automatically extract high-dimensional features and establish complex mapping relationships from large volumes of eye images, significantly improving estimation accuracy and robustness. Convolutional neural networks, leveraging the inductive biases of local connectivity and translation invariance, effectively capture local fine-grained features and have been widely adopted in early gaze estimation research.

Zhang et al.8 pioneered the application of CNNs to gaze estimation, validating their feasibility on the MPIIGaze dataset. Subsequently, GazeNet9 further improved the accuracy of monocular image gaze estimation by optimizing the feature extraction and regression pipeline. To better exploit global facial information, Zhang et al.10 introduced full-face images for the first time and designed a spatial weighting mechanism, demonstrating the value of full-face input in reducing reliance on head pose information. Bao et al.11 adopted a multimodal input approach, fusing full-face and binocular eye features through a dual-branch network, achieving a performance peak for CNN-based methods. Despite continuous progress in CNN-based methods through architectural and modal optimizations, their inherent local receptive field limits their ability to capture global contextual relationships, which has become a core bottleneck for further performance improvement.

To overcome the limitations of CNNs, researchers began exploring Transformer-based methods. In 2022, Cheng et al.12 proposed GazeTR, which directly transplanted the original ViT architecture to the gaze estimation task. This method divides the input image into a sequence of image patches and models global contextual relationships through multi-head self-attention. However, it lacks specialized designs for effectively capturing the critical fine-grained local features in gaze estimation, such as the pupil and iris, resulting in limited accuracy.

Given the complementary strengths of CNNs and Transformers in feature extraction, hybrid architectures have emerged as a research frontier. In 2025, Li et al.13 proposed AG-HybridNet, which employs a dual-branch architecture where the CNN branch extracts local fine-grained facial features and the Swin Transformer branch captures global spatial features. Its core innovation lies in a Sigmoid-gated feature fusion mechanism that leverages local features to guide the attention allocation of global features, enabling unidirectional local-to-global information flow. However, this design lacks feedback from global to local features, limiting bidirectional cross-scale interaction.

In 2025, Ryan et al.14 proposed Gaze-LLE, which utilizes a frozen DINOv2 large model as a unified scene encoder, injects spatial information of the target person via a head position prompt, and predicts gaze targets with a lightweight Transformer decoder. This approach eliminates the complex head branches and multimodal fusion designs typical of traditional hybrid architectures, resulting in a more streamlined model. However, it heavily relies on the implicit feature representations of the generic encoder, lacking explicit utilization of multi-scale information.

In 2025, Li et al.15 proposed MCA-PGI, which employs a programmable gradient information feature extraction module to achieve shorter gradient backpropagation paths through an auxiliary branch, effectively preserving key information from the original image. Additionally, a nonlinear multi-head cross-attention module is designed to nonlinearly fuse multi-scale features extracted by CNN with global visual features extracted by Transformer, enabling cross-scale information complementarity. This method achieves significant performance improvements on the MPIIFaceGaze and EyeDiap datasets, although the hierarchical information interaction among convolutional feature maps at different scales within the CNN branch could be further explored. How to further exploit the expressive power of multi-scale features in CNN-Transformer hybrid architectures, enabling facial features at different scales to more efficiently support gaze estimation, has emerged as a key direction for subsequent research, and is also the core focus of this paper.

Model structure

As shown in Figure 1, the proposed multi-scale feature fusion gaze estimation model, CAF-ViT, consists of three core components: an image feature extraction network, a feature fusion module, and a gaze regression module.Fig. 1Overall structure of our CAF-ViT model.

The image feature extraction network employs ResNet-1816 as its backbone for visual representation learning. When processing facial images, this network outputs two distinct feature layers at Stage 2 and Stage 3 of ResNet-18: low-level features from Stage 2, characterized by rich spatial details that preserve gaze-related appearance information, and high-level features from Stage 3, containing enhanced semantic representations with a larger receptive field. These two feature matrices of different scales are linearly mapped into vectors and then input into the feature fusion module for feature fusion.

Similar to the traditional Vision Transformer model, before entering the Transformer Encoder layer, the model adds corresponding Class Token feature vectors to the two sequences respectively. The multi-scale feature fusion module uses the cross-attention mechanism17 to make the Class Token perform self-attention mechanism calculations alternately in the large-scale and small-scale feature maps, so as to fuse the different information in the multi-scale features into the Fusion Class Token. This Token not only contains the rich detailed information in the input image, but also integrates high-level semantic information, providing a more comprehensive and accurate feature representation for gaze estimation.

First, the Small Token sequence and the Large Token sequence with the added Class Token respectively enter the corresponding S-Transformer Encoder and L-Transformer Encoder in the feature fusion module. The multi-head self-attention mechanism of the Vision Transformer is used to extract the coarse-grained feature and fine-grained feature information of the two features with different scales into their respective Class Tokens. Specifically, the S-Transformer Encoder layer processes the Small Token sequence, focusing on the extraction of details and local information, and aggregates the information into the CLS Token_S. The L-Transformer Encoder processes the Large Token sequence, with a greater emphasis on capturing global and semantic information, and integrates the information into the CLS Token_L.

Secondly, after the calculation of the self-attention mechanism, the CLS Token_S and CLS Token_L enter the cross-attention module in the multi-scale Transformer Encoder layer for the calculation of cross-attention. As the core feature fusion step in this model, cross-attention enables bidirectional interaction between two-scale features, thereby achieving feature complementation and fusion.

Improved cross-attention model

The original cross-attention model

Cross-attention realizes the cross-scale fusion of feature information by exchanging the Class Tokens in the two-scale token sequence branches. In this method, the Class Tokens in the feature sequences of different scales first use the self-attention mechanism within their own token sequences to learn the relevant feature information, and then the two Class Tokens exchange positions. The CLS Token_L in the original large-scale feature sequence is concatenated with the small-scale feature sequence, and the self-attention mechanism is calculated, so that the CLS Token_L can fully integrate the feature information in the small-scale feature sequence. At the same time, the information carried in the CLS Token_L can also be exchanged with the small-scale feature sequence, thus completing the fusion of features at different scales. After the above process is completed, the CLS Token_L will return to its original feature sequence in the next Transformer Encoder layer, and calculate and interact with other tokens in the original feature sequence with the information it has integrated, thus completing the cross-branch and cross-scale fusion of feature information. Improvement of cross-attention mechanism based on dual attention

Although the cross-attention module can achieve multi-scale feature fusion through the information exchange between features at different scales, it retains a lot of information irrelevant to gaze estimation during the process of information exchange and fusion. In this paper, an additional layer of attention calculation is added after the cross-attention calculation. The added attention module aims to improve the robustness of gaze estimation, thereby improving the accuracy of gaze estimation. The improved cross-attention module is shown in Fig. 4.Fig. 4The improved cross-attention module.

Comparison of three estimation datasets.

MPIIFaceGaze: MPIIFaceGaze dataset was collected from 15 participants and stored in 15 independent folders. After preprocessing, each participant has 3000 facial images. This dataset uses the leave-one-person-out evaluation method for training and testing, in which the data of one participant, starting from the image data of the first participant, is selected as the test set each time and the data of the remaining 14 participants are used as the training set for training. This experiment is carried out 15 times in total so that the data of all 15 participants are tested, and the final average error value is taken as the test result of the gaze estimation model on this dataset.

Gaze360: For the Gaze360 dataset, since some data in this dataset contain no human faces but only back information, during the preprocessing process, face detection was first used to screen and eliminate some images in which no human faces could be detected. Then, the remaining image data were randomly divided into a training set and a test set according to a certain proportion.

EyeDiap: EyeDiap dataset is a dataset saved in the form of videos. However, the model in this paper requires data in the form of images as input. Therefore, in the data processing procedure, we adopt the method of sampling one image from every 15 frames of the video data. Moreover, each original video in the dataset is truncated to ensure that the number of sampled images from each video is the same. This dataset employs the 4-fold cross-validation method for performance testing.

Evaluation

In the task of 3D gaze estimation, the Mean Angular Error (MAE) is usually used as the evaluation index for gaze estimation. The calculation method of this evaluation index is shown as follows:10\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} MAE = \frac{1}{N} \sum _{j=1}^{N} \arccos \left( \frac{\hat{g}_j \cdot g_j}{\Vert \hat{g}_j \Vert \Vert g_j \Vert } \right) \end{aligned}$$\end{document}Among them, N is the total number of test set samples in gaze estimation evaluation, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\hat{g}_j$$\end{document} is the predicted gaze direction angle for the j-th sample by the gaze estimation model, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$g_j$$\end{document} is the ground-truth gaze direction angle of the j-th sample. The final MAE value represents the average prediction error of the gaze estimation model across the entire test set. The smaller the MAE value is, the smaller the difference between the model’s predicted values and the true values is, and the better the performance of the model is.

Conclusion

This paper proposes the CAF-ViT model based on Vision Transformer for multi-scale feature fusion in gaze estimation. The framework employs ResNet-18 as the backbone to extract facial features at two different scales. After independent self-attention processing on each scale, the cross-attention mechanism is adopted to achieve deep integration of multi-scale features. The extra attention layer improves feature utilization, which in turn enhances the efficiency of feature fusion and the accuracy of gaze estimation. Experimental comparison results show that the proposed model outperforms existing methods in both performance and robustness for gaze estimation tasks and has clear practical application value.

For practical deployment, the model can achieve high-precision estimation with standard cameras and can be flexibly applied to real scenarios including intelligent cockpits, human-computer interaction terminals and security monitoring. Lightweight optimization can reduce the computational cost of the model and meet the real-time processing requirements of edge devices. Meanwhile, the integrated scheme combining face detection and feature alignment can effectively handle common disturbances in real scenes such as head pose variations and illumination changes, thus ensuring stable deployment.

The method proposed in this paper still has certain limitations. In extreme scenes, the model’s ability to extract discriminative gaze features is impaired, leading to a decrease in estimation accuracy. The introduction of the cross-attention mechanism leads to higher inference resource consumption than traditional CNN methods, so further optimization is required for adaptation to compute-limited devices. Affected by the sample distribution of the training dataset, the model may show different generalization performance for people with different facial features and skin colors.

To overcome the above limitations, future research will improve the robustness in complex scenes by integrating multimodal data such as infrared and depth information. Meanwhile, more diverse large-scale datasets will be constructed, efficient and lightweight attention structures will be designed, and domain adaptive training strategies will be introduced. These works will continuously improve the model performance and better meet the diverse demands in practical applications.

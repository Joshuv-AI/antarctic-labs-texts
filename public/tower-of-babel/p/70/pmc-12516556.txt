Serum phthalates and risks of incident cardiovascular disease among individuals with type 2 diabetes in a prospective cohort study
Authors: Shiyang Zhang, Xu Cheng, Chengyong Jia, Jun An, Xin Zhang, Youbing Guan, Yan Yan, Zhuoya Zhao, Yuenan Liu, Tao Jing, Meian He
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Prospective epidemiological evidence about the associations between phthalates (PAEs) and incident cardiovascular disease (CVD) risk among type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) population has been urgently needed. A total of 2806 participants with T2DM were recruited based on the baseline of the Dongfeng-Tongji cohort study (2008–2010) and followed up to 2018. Serum di-n-butyl phthalate (DnBP), Σdibutyl phthalate (ΣDBP), and Σlow-molecular-weight phthalate (ΣLMW) levels were associated with incident stroke risk, whether their levels were treated as continuous variables [HR (95%CI): 1.36 (1.02, 1.80), 1.35 (1.01, 1.81), and 1.50 (1.07, 2.10), respectively] or divided by quartiles [HR (95%CI): 1.41 (1.01, 1.98), 1.48 (1.05, 2.08), and 1.53 (1.08, 2.15) in the highest quartile with Ptrend ​= ​0.03, 0.02, and 0.02, respectively] after multiple adjustment in Cox proportional hazard models. Meanwhile, linear associations for serum di-iso-butyl phthalate (DiBP), DnBP, ΣDBP, and ΣLMW, and a nonlinear U-shaped dose–response association for serum butyl-benzyl phthalate (BBP) with incident stroke risk were proved by restricted cubic spline regression analyses. Furthermore, a positive correlation between PAE mixture and incident stroke risk was identified in Quantile-based g-computation [HR (95%CI): 1.24 (1.05, 1.46)]. However, serum PAEs, either as individuals or as a mixture, were not associated with incident CVD or coronary heart disease risk. Our study revealed that serum PAEs were positively associated with increased incident stroke risk in the T2DM population. More prospective cohort and mechanism studies are warranted to validate our findings.

Introduction

Phthalates (PAEs), a class of synthetic organic chemicals, are ubiquitously applied as plasticizers in modern industry to promote the extensibility, elasticity, flexibility, or durability of products [1]. In 2018, the volume of PAE production reached about 5.5 million tons around the world [2]. These compounds are continuously released from industrial products and easily enter human body through maternal exposure, digestive system, respiratory system, and dermal absorption [3,4]. PAEs and their metabolites, which act as endocrine and metabolic disruptors, can lead to toxicities in the immune system, endocrine system, and many other hazardous effects [[5], [6], [7]].

Type 2 diabetes (T2DM), a common metabolic disease, is threatening human health and causing a costly disease-economic burden [8]. Several previous studies have reported positive associations between PAEs and T2DM [[9], [10], [11], [12]]. However, evidence of the relationships between PAEs and T2DM complications is insufficient, especially in cardiovascular disease (CVD) [13,14].

As a major complication of T2DM, CVD affected about 32.2% patients with T2DM, and over half of all deaths in the T2DM population were attributed to CVD [15,16]. A recent experiment suggested that di (2-ethylhexyl) phthalate (DEHP) accelerated the atherosclerosis process through regulating the expression of microRNAs and long noncoding RNAs in vitro [17]. Another research also revealed that diethyl phthalate (DEP) could inhibit Ca2+ signaling pathway of rat aorta tissues [18]. In animal models, DEHP and its metabolites have been proven to be involved in cardiovascular injury by regulating peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor (PPAR) signaling pathway, inducing oxidative stress and inflammation, disrupting Ca2+ and lipid homeostasis, and triggering DNA methylation [[19], [20], [21], [22], [23], [24], [25], [26]]. Some epidemiological studies have reported positive associations of PAE exposure with the prevalence of CVD and its subtype diseases [[27], [28], [29], [30]]. A general adult population study from 2005 to 2016 National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES, n ​= ​10,427) unveiled that the metabolites of di-iso-butyl phthalate (DiBP) and butyl-benzyl phthalate (BBP) showed an increased risk for CVD [29]. In addition, a mid-age population study, in which approximately 95% were male, indicated that PAEs were risk factors for coronary heart disease (CHD) [31]. However, some inconsistent results were reported [32]. Meanwhile, until now, only a cross-sectional study has explored the relationships between PAEs and CVD risk in the T2DM population (n ​= ​675) and found that the metabolites of DEP and DiBP were positively associated with CVD [33]. Therefore, it is necessary to conduct a prospective epidemiological study about the associations of PAEs with CVD risk in T2DM patients.

In this study, we analyzed the associations between PAEs and incident CVD, CHD, or stroke risk among individuals with T2DM in a prospective cohort. In addition, the effect of PAE mixture on incident CVD, CHD, or stroke risk in T2DM was further estimated by Quantile-based g-computation (QgC) and Bayesian Kernel Machine Regression (BKMR) models, respectively.

Methods

Study design and population

The data used in this study were from the Dongfeng-Tongji (DFTJ) cohort. As introduced in previous papers, 27,009 individuals were included in the baseline between 2008 and 2010 and followed up every 5 years [34]. After providing written informed consent, all the participants completed standard questionnaires and physical examinations at baseline, with a 96.2% follow-up rate in 2013. Simultaneously, biospecimens were stored in a −80 ​°C refrigerator after collection for further laboratory testing.

For the current prospective cohort study, we excluded those with missing estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), eGFR < 30 mL/(min·1.73 ​m2), and insufficient biospecimens from 5173 T2DM patients at baseline. Afterwards, 2806 participants free of CHD, stroke, cancer, and severely abnormal electrocardiograms were included in the final analysis. The definition of severely abnormal electrocardiograms included atrial fibrillation, atrial flutter, frequent premature ventricular contractions, pacemaker rhythm, and pre-excitation syndrome.

This study was approved by the Ethics and Human Subject Committees of the School of Public Health, Tongji Medical College, Huazhong University of Science and Technology in 2008 (Approval No.2008–03).

Serum phthalate measures

The detailed protocol was described in a previous study [35]. In brief, the samples were transferred from −80 ​°C to −20 ​°C and stored overnight before measuring. Then, 0.5 ​ng isotope internal standard solution was added to a 300 ​μL serum sample. After protein precipitation with acetonitrile solution, liquid–liquid extraction was performed with n-hexane and dichloromethane solution, and impurity removal via solid-phase extraction column. We used gas chromatography-mass spectrometry to test the serum levels of seven major PAEs, namely dimethyl phthalate (DMP), DEP, DiBP, di-n-butyl phthalate (DnBP), BBP, di-n-octyl phthalate (DnOP), and DEHP. For the quality control, every 28 samples were conducted along with a blank and a mixed sample. DNOP was eliminated from the final analysis because most individuals' DNOP levels were lower than the limits of detection (LODs).

Definition of covariates

All covariates about demographic characteristics, lifestyle factors, disease-related information, and medical history involved in this study were gathered through standardized questionnaires by well-trained interviewers. Fasting blood glucose (FBG) levels were tested following the instructions in the laboratory of Dongfeng Motor Corporation (DMC) hospital. Education level, smoking and drinking status, physical activity, hypertension, and hyperlipidemia were divided into different groups according to the criteria in our previous study. Moreover, gender, age, body mass index (BMI), family history of CVD, diabetes mellitus duration, and use of hypoglycemic medication were also treated as confounders [36].

Ascertainment of T2DM and CVD-associated outcomes

The diagnostic code of T2DM was described in detail in the previous study [37]. Incident CVD was diagnosed according to International Classification of Diseases codes (ICD-10 codes) of the World Health Organization, including incident CHD (ICD-10 codes, I20–I25, and I46) and incident stroke (ICD-10 codes, I60–I61, I63–I64, and I69) [[37], [38], [39]]. The CVD-related information of participants was tracked in the medical system of DMC hospital from baseline to December 31, 2018.

Statistical analysis

DiBP and DnBP were isomers and had similar effects on human health, so Σdibutyl phthalate (ΣDBP) was obtained by combining their molar concentrations [40]. The sum of 5 low molecular weight phthalates' molar concentrations (DMP, DEP, DiBP, DnBP, and BBP) was identified as Σlow-molecular-weight phthalate (ΣLMW) [1]. ΣPAEs variable represented the sum of 6 measured PAEs' molar concentrations.

The difference between individuals with and without CVD was compared by the Mann–Whitney U test and the Chi-square test. We conducted Spearman rank correlation analyses to evaluate the associations between 6 measured PAEs.

Afterwards, all the values of PAEs were involved in model analyses with ln transformation. Cox proportional hazard models were performed with the survival time (from baseline to the censor point, years) to estimate the hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for the associations between serum PAE concentrations and incident CVD, CHD, and stroke risk in T2DM. In quartile analyses, P-trends were calculated based on the median value of each group and treated as a continuous variable. Model 1 was crude; age and gender were adjusted in model 2, and model 3 further adjusted for the other covariates, which were defined in Section 2.3 based on model 2. Meanwhile, restricted cubic spline regressions with 4 knots (5th, 35th, 65th, and 95th percentiles) were used to assess the dose–response relationship of single phthalate with incident stroke risk in T2DM after excluding the outliers (values outside the mean ​± ​standard deviation) [41,42]. Sensitivity analyses were applied by excluding the participants who developed the outcome disease within the first 2 years of follow-up.

Furthermore, to explore the associations of PAE mixture with incident CVD, CHD, and stroke risk in T2DM, QgC and BKMR models were applied. QgC is a model based on Weighted Quantile Sum regression and can estimate the proportion of every single PAE in the total effect [43]. To verify the results of QgC models, BKMR models were performed to iteratively regress the exposure-response functions of PAE mixture on incident CVD, CHD, or stroke risk in T2DM [44,45].

Subgroup analyses, including gender, age, BMI, smoking status, education level, physical activity, FBG, and duration of T2DM, were further conducted.

SPSS Software (version 19.0, SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) and R software (version 4.0.3; R Core Team) were used to analyze the data. P-values <0.05 were considered significant results.

Results

Characteristics of the study population

This study recruited 2806 participants with T2DM at baseline, 47.0% were male, and their average age was 64.5 years. Until December 31, 2018, the median follow-up time of all participants was 10.03 years, and approximately 42.1% of the whole population developed CVD (909 CHD and 271 stroke). Compared with the individuals without CVD, incident CVD subjects were older and had higher BMI and longer diabetes mellitus duration. Meanwhile, they were more likely to have hypertension and hyperlipidemia. Associations of single PAEs with incident CVD and its subtype risk in T2DM

Table 2 presents the Cox regression results for CVD and its subtype diseases (CHD and stroke) risk according to each single serum PAE in T2DM. No association was found between single PAE and incident CVD or CHD risk in T2DM. Whether dividing the levels of serum PAEs into quartiles or treating them as continuous variables, the results were consistent (Tables S2 and S3). In contrast, we found DEP, DnBP, BBP, ΣDBP, ΣLMW, and ΣPAEs were positively associated with incident stroke risk in model 1. After multivariable adjustment, the associations of DnBP, BBP, ΣDBP, ΣLMW, and ΣPAEs with incident stroke risk in T2DM were still statistically significant. Compared with the lowest quartiles of serum PAEs, the multiple-adjusted HR (95% CI) of stroke was 1.42 (1.02, 1.98) for DMP (Ptrend ​= ​0.04), 1.41 (1.01, 1.98) for DnBP (Ptrend ​= ​0.03), 1.48 (1.05, 2.08) for ΣDBP (Ptrend ​= ​0.02), and 1.53 (1.08, 2.15) for ΣLMW (Ptrend ​= ​0.02) in the fourth quartile (Fig. 1 and Table S4). We also uncovered higher HR of stroke for DEP in both the third and fourth quartiles [1.55 (1.08, 2.21) and 1.43 (1.00, 2.05), respectively, (Ptrend ​= ​0.05)]. Additionally, we found an increased HR of stroke for ΣPAEs (Ptrend ​= ​0.03). Subgroup analyses

In stratified analyses, BBP was associated with stroke in never smokers [HR (95% CI): 1.63 (1.20, 2.22), Pinteraction ​= ​0.03] and participants with FBG < 7 ​mmol/L [HR (95% CI): 2.01 (1.27, 3.20), Pinteraction ​= ​0.05] (Table S6). Furthermore, DEHP was a potential risk factor of stroke in individuals with a BMI ≥ 24 ​kg/m2 [HR (95% CI): 1.30 (1.07, 1.58), Pinteraction ​= ​0.05] and subjects without physical activity [HR (95% CI): 1.65 (1.09, 2.51), Pinteraction ​= ​0.03].

Discussion

In this first prospective cohort study about the associations between PAEs and incident CVD and its subtype diseases risks among individuals with T2DM, we found that serum PAE levels were positively associated with incident stroke risk. Moreover, we observed linear dose–response associations of serum DiBP, DnBP, ΣDBP, and ΣLMW with incident stroke risk. Meanwhile, a nonlinear U-shaped relationship between serum BBP levels and incident stroke risk was identified.

As globally concerned environmental pollutants, the potential associations of PAEs with CVD and T2DM have been explored. Blood pressure is a risk factor for CVD, but only a few studies analyzed the associations of PAEs with blood pressure in adults, and the results were inconsistent [46,47]. Among 6625 nondiabetic individuals aged 40 years or older from NHANES 2003–2014 cycles, DEHP was found to be associated with increased CVD mortality [48]. However, two other studies based on NHANES demonstrated null associations between PAEs and CVD mortality [49,50]. In addition, a cross-sectional study (n ​= ​10,427) measured 11 urinary PAE metabolite levels in the general population, in which the prevalences of CVD, CHD, and stroke were 10.7%, 4.2%, and 3.6%, respectively. The results suggested that urinary DiBP and BBP levels were associated with the higher prevalence of CVD in general adults, and BBP was significantly associated with stroke. However, no statistically significant association between PAEs and CHD was observed [29]. In accordance with the study, positive associations between PAEs and the prevalence of stroke were also found in another research (n ​= ​21,132) [51]. Moreover, the metabolite levels of DiBP, DnBP, and DEHP in urine were positively associated with the prevalence of CHD in a cross-sectional study (91 CHD cases, 360 controls, and 95% of participants were males) from Taiwan, China [31]. Contrary to the abovementioned studies, in a cross-sectional study (n ​= ​2330) of the elderly population in China (with the prevalences of CVD, CHD, and stroke being 6.1%, 4.9%, and 0.7%, respectively), no association was found between PAEs and CVD, CHD, or stroke [32]. Among the T2DM population, the metabolites of DEHP were proved to be associated with CVD mortality in a study based on NHANES [52]. Furthermore, only one cross-sectional study included 675 Chinese elderly with T2DM, which indicated that urinary metabolite levels of DEP and DiBP were associated with incident CVD risk [33]. In general, the results of studies about the relationships between PAEs and CVD were divergent, and prospective evidence about the associations of PAEs with incident CVD risk in the T2DM population was needed.

In the current prospective study, we found significant and positive associations of serum PAE levels with incident stroke risk in the T2DM population. The result is consistent with most previous studies in the general population, except for a study conducted in Shanghai, China, in which the incident rate of stroke was only 0.7% [32]. The incidence rate of stroke was too low and may cause insufficient statistical efficiency. Atherosclerosis is a pathological process of stroke [21], which is related to oxidative stress, inflammatory activation, formation of endothelial and platelet particles, injury of endothelial cells, dysfunction of vascular smooth muscle cells, and disorder of lipid metabolism [[53], [54], [55]]. Previous studies found that DEHP could activate matrix metalloproteinase (MMP) 2 and 9 by regulating p38 mitogen activated protein kinase (p38MAPK), extracellular signal regulated kinase 1 and 2 (ERK1/2), protein kinase B (Akt) and nuclear factor kappa B signaling pathway, leading to the proliferation and migration of vascular smooth muscle cells and triggering atherosclerosis [56]. Another study found that DEHP can increase the levels of IL-8, intercellular adhesion molecule 1, and vascular cell adhesion molecule 1, causing inflammation and ultimately leading to atherosclerosis in human endothelial cells [26]. In addition, the results of molecular epidemiology and toxicity tests in zebrafish showed that PAEs can cause vascular smooth muscle injury and accelerate the process of atherosclerosis by regulating the levels of miRNA, long non-coding RNA (LncRNA), and DNA methylation [17,57]. These may be the potential mechanisms between PAE exposure and stroke risk.

Furthermore, a positive correlation between PAE mixture and incident stroke risk was identified in the QgC model, but a nonsignificant association was obtained in the BKMR model. The discrepancies may be caused by different fitting algorithms and variable handling [6 PAEs were classified into 4 sets according to the result of Spearman rank correlation analyses (DMP and DEP; DiBP and DnBP; BBP; DEHP) in the BKMR model] of these two models [58]. Moreover, no association was found between PAEs and incident CVD risk. Compared to previous studies conducted in the general population, the results were inconsistent. The potential reasons may be: (1) The participants involved in this study were Chinese retired workers with T2DM, who have different characteristics and health status; (2) The previous study among the T2DM population was a cross-sectional study, which was unable to establish the temporal sequence of incident T2DM and CVD. Meanwhile, we did not observe statistical significance of the relationships between PAEs and incident CHD risk. This is basically consistent with the published findings among the general population. Only one study found that PAEs were positively associated with CHD [31]. The relatively small sample size and 95% of the subjects being males may lead to the difference. More prospective cohort studies and mechanism studies are warranted to validate our findings and verify the underlying mechanism in the future.

This study is the first prospective cohort study about the associations of PAEs with incident CVD, CHD, or stroke risk in a relatively large T2DM population. Another strength of the current study is that we used Quantile-based g-computation and BKMR models to estimate the joint effects of PAE mixtures on CVD and its subtype diseases in T2DM. Nevertheless, several limitations in this study should be considered when generalizing the results. First, urine has been considered the most suitable biological matrix for estimating the PAE exposure levels in humans according to previous studies [59]. However, PAE parent compounds have also been measured and statistically analyzed in some epidemiological research [46,60,61]. Furthermore, PAE levels in our study were single-point serum levels and cannot represent the PAE exposure levels of a subject during the whole follow-up time. Finally, although multiple covariates had been adjusted during the statistical process, residual confounding may still exist because of some undetected confounders.

Conclusion

In the current prospective study, serum PAE levels were associated with increased incident stroke risk in the T2DM population. Our findings need to be validated by more epidemiological evidence and mechanism studies.

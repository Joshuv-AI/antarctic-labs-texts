Mechanical response analysis of primary support for shallow buried loess tunnel under surface surcharge
Authors: Bing Han, Weixing Feng, Renxin Gao, Huanran Zhu, Wei Jia
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractAt present, the research on the influence of surrounding soil deformation induced by tunnel construction under the action of surcharge is generally based on the situation after the tunnel has been built, and less consideration is given to the existence of surface surcharge near the planned route of the tunnel when the tunnel is not built. Therefore, based on the Luochuan tunnel under construction, this study monitors the deformation of shallowly buried loess tunnels under different surcharge conditions and analyzes the influence of varying surcharge conditions on the deformation and stress of the tunnel through numerical simulation to determine the effective range of surface surcharge. The results show that the influence of surcharge load on tunnel stability is the most significant when located at the tunnel axis, and the influence degree increases with the increase of load. The adverse effects of surcharge load mainly occur in the range of 2 times the tunnel diameter. Once the range is exceeded, the internal force of the tunnel support structure is not sensitive to the surcharge load. In engineering, the surface surcharge can be controlled within the safety displacement limit by referring to the displacement calculation results.Keywords: Surface surcharge, Loess tunnel, Settlement, Mechanical responseSubject terms: Engineering, Civil engineering

Introduction

Nowadays, to comply with transportation planning, tunnel construction often necessitates crossing various large-scale buildings and structures1–4. Consequently, the phenomenon of surface loading frequently arises due to the unloading of soil during tunnel excavation, demolition of surface buildings, and accumulation of construction waste5. A wide range of surface surcharges produces extreme additional stress and the original stress state changes. In this environment, the loose area of surrounding rock in tunnel construction is extensive, the self-stability of the tunnel face is poor, and the safety of tunnel construction is seriously threatened6–8. Therefore, it is of great significance to carry out tunnel stability analysis under surcharge loading.

Many studies on the stress and deformation mechanism of tunnels under surface surcharge have been conducted in recent years. Among them, Wang et al. established an analysis model considering the application of surface loads before/ after tunnel excavation, explored the influence of uniformly distributed loads on strata stress and displacement, and proposed a new analytical solution for the first time, which achieved rapid analysis and prediction of strata response under additional loads9. Liang et al. investigated the effect of surcharge loading on shallow shield tunnels under different load positions and tunnel traversing soil layer properties based on the scaled model test, and noted that the ellipticity of soft soil layer tunnels is larger than that of sandy soil layer tunnels10. While the theoretical analysis method and modeling test method are usually effective for simple working conditions, but have certain limitations for complex construction environments11. Currently, the finite element analysis is mostly used in the research12–15, and the finite element simulation can effectively replace the indoor model experiment and accurately evaluate the deformation response16. In terms of finite element analysis, Huang et al. established a three-dimensional numerical model of a shallow buried shield tunnel in soft soil using FLAC3D, to analyze the ground settlement and the force deformation of the tunnel under different loading forms, and studied the control effects of different deformation control measures under surcharge loading17. Wei et al. combined two structural calculation methods18–20. Firstly, a calculation model based on the load-structure method was established to calculate the longitudinal forces and deformations of the tunnel using the minimum potential energy principle. They proposed a shield tunnel safety assessment method using ellipticity as an indicator. Subsequently, a three-dimensional model based on the"stratum-structure” method was further established through numerical methods to study the impact of surface loading (loading scale and location) on the deformation of shield tunnels. Based on the actual engineering of a section of Hangzhou Metro Line 1, Xie et al. established plane strain finite element models using the HS small model and the total strain based crack model of concrete. The effectiveness of the model was verified by comparing with field measured results, and the cracking characteristics of shield tunnel segments under different loading modes were studied21. Du et al. used the hyperstatic reaction method (HRM) to calculate the internal forces and displacements of the tunnel structure, and discussed and analyzed the influence of overloading on the excavation characteristics of horseshoe shaped tunnels in saturated soft rock22.

The impact of ground surcharge on tunnel structure has been a subject of debate. However, existing research primarily focuses on completed metro shield tunnels3,20,23–25. In contrast, loess tunnels constructed using mining methods have distinct engineering characteristics, such as a large disturbed area during excavation, poor self-stability of surrounding rock, and a noticeable loading effect, which also exhibit a different damage evolution mechanism compared to shield tunnels26–31. Additionally, the reaction of shallow buried tunnels during construction is infrequently taken into account when studying the effect of surface surcharge. The case where surface loading already exists prior to tunnel excavation has received relatively little attention, and more study is required32. Therefore, based on the Luochuan tunnel project, this study analyzes the displacement and mechanical response characteristics of the tunnel under the action of surface surcharge through field measurement. Considering the influence factors such as surcharge scale and tunnel position, the internal force and deformation difference of tunnel support structure caused by surcharge under different working conditions are compared and analyzed. Significantly different from the existing research, the discussion object of this study focuses on the large-section loess tunnel under ultra-shallow buried conditions. Because surface loading already existed before tunnel excavation, the distribution law of additional stress and displacement of soil caused by various factors during tunnel construction under surface loading and its influence on the mechanical characteristics of the supporting structure are discussed more comprehensively. The mechanical response and stress mechanism of tunnel supporting structure under surface loading are revealed from both theoretical and practical aspects, to provide theoretical support and technical guarantee for tunnel safety construction, and to guide tunnel site selection.

Project overview

Engineering background

The Luochuan tunnel is located in the southern part of the northern Shaanxi Plateau, and the local administrative area belongs to Luochuan County, Yan ‘an City. The starting mileage of the tunnel is DK194 + 759.57, and the final mileage is DK198 + 900 (Fig. 1). The total length is 4140.43m, and the maximum burial depth is about 64m. The tunnel is designed with a single-hole double-line. The actual span and excavation height of the super shallow-buried section at the entrance of the tunnel reach 15.6m and 13.18m, with a maximum excavation area of 167.4 m2. To meet the requirements of the construction period, an inclined shaft is added to intersect with the main tunnel, and four working faces are constructed simultaneously.

Location of the Luochuan tunnel.

Geological overview

The tunnel is located in the loess plateau area, and the entrance (Xi’an) is located on the loess ridge slope, with complex terrain conditions. The exit of tunnel (Yan’an) is located on the loess plateau, which is gentle and flat but has developed vegetation. The terrain within the Luochuan station is relatively flat, with low terrain in the south and high terrain in the north. However, due to the formation of loess and station location restrictions, the tunnel has fragmented terrain and developed gullies. According to geological drilling analysis, the tunnel mainly passes through the Quaternary Holocene Upper and Middle Pleistocene Aeolian layers and paleosols. Passing through soft plastic loess layers with high moisture content on a large scale along the route. Among them, the clay loess (Q2eol) is distributed in multiple layers in the middle of the loess ridge, which is the main stratum of the tunnel body (Fig. 2). The main type of groundwater in this area is pore phreatic water in the Quaternary loose layer. The aquifer is mainly located near the middle Pleistocene Lishi loess and impermeable old loess or bedrock surface, and the groundwater level is deeply buried. The moisture content of the surrounding rock in the study area is about 16%. Figure 3 depicts the excavation and the geological conditions of the surrounding rock in the tunnel face.

Engineering challenges

Luochuan tunnel, which serves as a vital control project for the Xi’an-Yan’an high-speed railway passenger dedicated line, has the characteristics of large excavation area, harsh surrounding rock conditions and complex and changeable construction environment. It is a typical super-large section shallow loess tunnel. The main technical difficulties are as follows :

(1) The whole tunnel is located in the shallow-buried section of loess. The buried depth of VI surrounding rock at the exit of the tunnel is about 8 m, which belongs to the super shallow-buried tunnel, and the maximum excavation area is 167.4 m2. It is difficult for the surrounding rock to form pressure arch stably after tunnel excavation, which makes tunnel construction difficult. During the excavation process, it is easy to cause the development of surface cracks and instability of the tunnel face, which affects safety of tunnel construction.

(2) The surface buildings, roads, and farmland along the tunnel are widely distributed, and the phenomenon of surface loading is inevitable within the demolition area above the tunnel. Surcharge loading causes additional stress in the stratum, and the stress field of the soil changes, which destroys the original stress equilibrium state, and then leads to excessive stress concentration in the arch or local areas. When the ultimate bearing capacity of the surrounding rock is exceeded, it is easy to cause the overall instability and large-scale collapse of the tunnel.

The monitoring data show that during the construction of the Luochuan tunnel, multiple surface cracks and even dislocations occurred in the section DK198 + 550–DK198 + 610 (a total of 60 m), and the primary support of the upper steps of the tunnel has serious cracking, concrete spalling, and arch deformation. At the same time, there were multiple instances of collapse and block falling of the tunnel face (Fig. 4), which seriously affected the construction progress and threatened the construction safety. Therefore, taking the Luochuan tunnel as an example, this paper deeply explores the mechanical response characteristics of loess tunnel construction under surface surcharge, which has reference value for later similar engineering design and construction.

Overview of test section

To explore the difference between tunnel deformation law and mechanical response characteristics under different surcharge conditions, and to take into account the effectiveness of the test results, two test sections are selected in the super shallow-buried section at the exit according to the different positions of surface surcharge, which can be divided into conditions 1 and 2. The surface surcharge acting on the surface above the center of the tunnel is condition 1, and the load is approximately uniformly distributed on both sides of the tunnel`s central axis. When the surface surcharge is concentrated on the surface above one side of the tunnel, the tunnel is subjected to unsymmetrical loading, which is condition 2. Three sections are set up in the test section for surrounding rock deformation monitoring, and one section is used as the stress monitoring section for comparative analysis of structural stress and deformation. Monitoring result analysis

Displacement response analysis

Arch crown settlement. Figure 8 shows the settlement curve of the tunnel arch at different distribution positions of the surcharge load. From Fig. 8, it can be seen that under the two working conditions, the settlement displacement of the tunnel arch remains basically consistent, and the cross-section shows a three-stage trend of rapid growth, continuous growth, slow growth, and eventually reaching stability. When the loading position is different, under the excavation unloading effect, the arch crown undergoes different degrees of settlement, and the settlement values all exceed the reserved deformation amount of the tunnel (20 cm).

Crown settlement time-history curves under various working conditions. (a) Condition 1 (b) Condition 2.

As shown in Fig. 8a, the cumulative settlement in working condition 1 is between 329.3–430.8 mm, which is much larger than that in working condition 2. At this time, the surcharge is located directly above the tunnel, and the surrounding rock below the surcharge is significantly compressed. Within 12 days of excavation of the tunnel face, the settlement curve of the arch crown is in a rapid growth stage. After the construction of the lower bench, the deformation rate gradually slows down and tends to stabilize around 17 days. As shown in Fig. 8b, compared with Condition 1, the cumulative settlement of the measured arch crown in Condition 2 decreased by 35%. However, due to the influence of bias pressure, the settlement of the arch crown continued to increase after the excavation of the lower bench and remained unstable after about one week of inverted arch construction. The corresponding stable settlement time of the arch crown was about 20 days, indicating a longer deformation stability period. From the perspective of the impact of different distribution positions of surcharge load on tunnel deformation, it is evident that surcharge load has a greater impact on tunnel deformation when applied directly above the tunnel.

Figure 9 shows the proportion of settlement values in each construction stage of the tunnel under different distribution positions of surcharge load. It can be seen from Fig. 9 that the settlement deformation response of the arch crown caused by the excavation of each step varies depending on the location of the loading action. From the distribution proportion of deformation in each construction step, in working condition 1, the temporary arch construction during the upper step construction stage played a certain role in controlling the vertical displacement of the surrounding rock. Subsequently, after the excavation of the middle bench, the proportion of tunnel deformation increased significantly, with settlement accounting for more than 50%. There are two main reasons for this phenomenon. First, the middle bench construction period is long, and the stress release time of the surrounding rock is too long, making it difficult to control tunnel deformation in a timely manner; The second is that the tunnel has reached the maximum excavation line in the middle bench excavation stage. At this time, the primary support is not fully closed, and the deformation of the tunnel is sensitive to excavation and surcharge disturbance due to the large area of accumulated load directly above the tunnel. By comparison, although the overall settlement value of working condition 2 is relatively small, its deformation increases again after the construction of the inverted arch. The cumulative deformation in this stage accounts for about 24–36%. It can be considered that within the influence range of the tunnel, the effect of surcharge load bias pressure leads to a longer stability period of the tunnel, and the proportion of deformation in each excavation step changes from 2:6:1:1 to 2:3:2:3. It can be seen that in addition to using the three-step temporary inverted arch method for construction, the feet-lock bolt should also be added to strengthen the arch waist support, and the support structure should be closed as soon as possible to effectively control tunnel deformation.

The proportion of settlement value in each construction stage of tunnel under various working conditions.

Horizontal convergence. Figure 10 shows the horizontal convergence curve of the tunnel at different distribution positions of the surcharge load. The comparative analysis shows that the horizontal convergence rate is fast before the excavation of the lower step in working condition 1, and the convergence rate is significantly reduced after the inverted arch is applied. The convergence rate tends to be stable around 17 days, with a cumulative convergence value between 21.9 ~ 25.5 mm. The cumulative convergence value in working condition 2 is between 14.3 ~ 17.2 mm, which is slightly smaller than that in working condition 1. The convergence deformation continues to increase after the excavation of the lower bench and the inverted arch, and the horizontal convergence duration of this working condition tends to be stable for about 20 days. Overall, the laws of horizontal convergence and arch settlement are the same. Under both working conditions, the horizontal convergence value is much smaller than the arch settlement, and the deformation of the tunnel is mainly vertical.

Horizontal convergence deformation time-history curves under various working conditions. (a) Condition 1 (b) Condition 2.

Figure 11 shows the proportion of convergence values at each construction stage of the tunnel under different distribution positions of the surcharge load. It can be seen that under the two working conditions, the convergence deformation caused by the excavation of the middle platform has the highest proportion, and the displacement proportion changes in the range of 45%–57% and 39%–48%, respectively, which are key monitoring areas. The convergence deformation of the lower bench and the inverted arch is obviously reduced after the excavation of the working condition 1, while the working condition 2 is the opposite. The convergence of the inverted arch is greatly increased again after the construction, and the deformation duration is longer, which is basically consistent with the settlement law of the vault.

Stress response analysis

Surrounding rock pressure. The time curve and spatial distribution of surrounding rock pressure are shown in Fig. 12. As shown in Fig. 12a, the surrounding rock pressure in working condition 1 is basically symmetrically distributed, and the supporting structure mainly bears the vertical load directly above the tunnel, resulting in the surrounding rock pressure at the arch crown being much higher than that of other parts, about twice. It is the key part that bears the surrounding rock pressure, while the other parts bear relatively evenly distributed loads. During the initial monitoring period, the surrounding rock pressure at each measuring point increased sharply. After each step of excavation, the surrounding rock pressure was significantly disturbed. As the primary support closed into a ring, the rate of increase in surrounding rock pressure decreased. After reaching its peak on the 25th day of monitoring, it remained stable. As shown in Fig. 12b, during the initial stage of stress redistribution, the surrounding rock pressure at each measuring point in Condition 2 is dynamically changing, and the amplitude of the change is inconsistent. The changing pattern of surrounding rock pressure in the early stage is similar to Condition 1, with each measuring point showing a characteristic of first increasing, then decreasing and finally stabilizing. However, the growth trend appears again after the construction of the secondary lining. The growth duration of surrounding rock pressure is relatively long, and it takes more time to reach a stable state. Compared with working condition 1, the overall surrounding rock pressure in working condition 2 is significantly smaller, with significant asymmetry. The arch crown and hance bear greater rock pressure, while the spandrel share weaker rock pressure. It can be seen that even if the load distribution is outside the excavation line range of the tunnel, due to being still in the area affected by the loess stratum fracture surface, the tunnel bears greater lateral pressure, ultimately leading to uneven stress on the support structure and a certain degree of bias.

As shown in Fig. 13, it can be seen that under the two working conditions, the stress redistribution of the surrounding rock during the initial stage of tunnel excavation is more intense, and the change amplitude is not consistent. Until the secondary lining construction, the overall stress distribution of the steel frame is basically stable. As shown in Fig. 13a, b, the stress distribution of the steel frame in working condition 1 is basically symmetrical, and all measuring points are in a compressed state. The overall stress distribution of the steel frame shows a pattern of “larger on the outer side and smaller on the inner side”. The maximum compressive stress on the outer side of the steel frame occurs at the left sidewall, reaching − 321.68 MPa, far exceeding the compressive design strength of the I25a steel frame (215 MPa). As shown in Fig. 13c, d, compared with Condition 1, there is a significant difference in the stress distribution of the steel frame in Condition 2. In the initial stage, the inner and outer sides of the support steel frame are mainly under compression, and a few measuring points are under tension, with uneven distribution of size. This is due to the presence of a large amount of accumulated load on the right boundary of the tunnel, which increases the lateral load borne by the support structure, resulting in concentrated tensile stress at the left spandrel of the tunnel. The maximum compressive stress on the outer side of the steel frame occurs at the right spandrel, reaching − 372.16 MPa, far exceeding the compressive design strength of the steel frame (215 MPa). The maximum tensile stress occurs at the right arch waist, reaching 31.93 MPa. The maximum tensile stress on the inner side of the steel frame occurs at the left spandrel, which is close to the tensile design strength of the I25a steel frame. From this, it can be seen that the primary support steel frame of the loess tunnel is in an unfavorable stress state when passing through the surface surcharge, which cannot ensure the safety and stability of the tunnel structure.

From Fig. 14, it can be seen that under the two working conditions, the stress variation law of shotcrete is basically the same, and its stress value is greatly affected by the construction process. Firstly, there is a rapid fluctuation growth process, and then the change gradually slows down with the increase of concrete strength, indicating that shotcrete, as the main bearing carrier, has a significant effect on surrounding rock support. As shown in Fig. 14a, b, the inner and outer stresses of shotcrete in working condition 1 are both in a compressed state. The internal forces of the outer arch crown and left sidewall of shotcrete are larger, which are − 21.44 MPa and − 46.91 MPa, respectively. The maximum compressive stress on the inner side of shotcrete occurs at the left hance, reaching − 41.85 MPa, both exceeding the compressive strength of the primary support shotcrete C25 (11.9 MPa). According to monitoring data, circumferential cracks are often seen during construction. As shown in Fig. 14c, d, the overall stress distribution of shotcrete in Condition 2 is uneven, with most measuring points showing compression. The right spandrel of the tunnel shows a tensile state in the early monitoring stage, exceeding the tensile strength of C25 shotcrete (1.27 MPa). This is due to the influence of the surcharge loading on the upper right side of the tunnel, and the right spandrel is significantly compressed until the inverted arch is applied, and the shotcrete stress turns to a compressive state. Overall, the primary support under the two working conditions has poor overall safety, and the mechanical system of the support structure is currently in an unstable state.

Contact pressure. The time curve and spatial distribution of contact pressure between primary support and secondary lining are shown in Fig. 15. As shown in Fig. 15, the distribution characteristics of contact pressure and surrounding rock pressure are relatively consistent. In working condition 1, the contact pressure at the sidewall is relatively high. This is due to the influence of gravity during the pouring of the secondary lining concrete, which makes the secondary lining concrete at the tunnel side wall relatively dense compared to the arch. The primary support and secondary lining have relatively good contact, so the contact pressure shows a distribution of small arch and large sidewall. In working condition 2, the maximum contact pressure occurs at the left hance, reaching 123.05 kPa. At this time, the contact pressure of the secondary lining is much greater than that of the surrounding rock. The difference in contact pressure between the two working conditions is mainly due to the different distribution positions of the surcharge load, which causes the supporting structure to bear the main load in different directions. It can be seen that the secondary lining bears a significant amount of surrounding rock pressure in the tunnel support system. In the traditional design concept, the secondary lining is often used as a safety reserve, However, in this article, in the case of the loess tunnel underpass the surface surcharge, the contact pressure is greater than the surrounding rock pressure, and the secondary lining is in an obvious bearing state, not just a safety reserve.

Numerical results and analysis

The previous monitoring results mainly studied and analyzed the deformation law of surrounding rock and the primary support force characteristics under different loading positions. Based on this, this section takes the loading position, loading value, loading width, and tunnel burial depth as influencing variables, and supplements the analysis of the impact on tunnel stability under different loading conditions through numerical simulation.

Numerical model

In this paper, the numerical analysis is carried out by FLAC3D software. According to the Saint-Venant principle, the excavation disturbance range of the tunnel is 3 ~ 5 times the diameter of the tunnel. To eliminate the influence of the boundary effect on the test results and consider the needs of the working condition setting, the overall size of the model is 180 m×50 m×85 m, as shown in Fig. 16. The surfaces in the horizontal direction of the model and the bottom boundary are fixed, and the top surface is set to the free boundary. The Mohr-Coulomb constitutive model is chosen for calculation. The tunnel construction simulates the excavation and support process of the three-step temporary invert method with an excavation footing of 1.0 m.

Numerical results and analysis

Surcharge loading width. Figure 17 is the influence of surcharge width on the settlement of tunnel vault. As shown in Fig. 17, the arch crown settlement of the tunnel increases nonlinearly as the surcharge range increases when the surcharge width B varies between 0 and 2D. When the surcharge range is greater than two times the tunnel diameter, the settlement of the tunnel vault alters slightly and exhibits a convergence trend. The settlement deformation rate dramatically rises as surcharge loading builds up gradually. With the increase of surcharge loading width, the arch crown settlement increment of each level of load was 10.62%, 18.05%, 23.93%, 29.16%, and 34.53%, respectively.

Change of tunnel arch crown settlement with width of the surcharge.

The effects of the surcharge width on the axial force and bending moment of the primary support are shown in Figs. 18 and 19, respectively. As shown in Fig. 18, the axial force of the primary support is symmetrically distributed. The upper half of the tunnel experiences significantly higher axial force compared to the lower half, with the maximum axial force emerges at the vault. The axial force of the primary support typically rises with the increase in surcharge width under the same level of surcharge loading. As the surcharge loading accumulates gradually, the primary support axial force increase in the upper half of the tunnel, while the lower part remains almost unchanged. As shown in Fig. 19, the maximum positive bending moment occurs at 0°(arch crown), and the maximum negative bending moment occurs at 60°(spandrel). The bending moment value near the arch crown shows a trend of first increasing and then decreasing with the increase of loading width under the same level of loading, while the spandrel is exactly the opposite, similar to the variation law of axial force, with almost no change in the area below the hance.

Conclusions

Based on the engineering background of the Luochuan tunnel, The paper examines the impact of existing surface surcharge of surrounding rock deformation and supporting structure stress characteristics through field tests. It analyzes the deformation and internal force distribution of the tunnel under different surcharge conditions using numerical simulation. The research reveals that the scale of the surcharge and tunnel position significantly affect tunnel excavation stability. The surface surcharge has a high influence range on the surrounding rock of the tunnel, about 20 m, that is, the smaller the buried depth of the tunnel, the smaller the distance from the surcharge center to the central axis of the tunnel, and the more obvious the mechanical response of the tunnel induced by the surcharge. In the range of the influence height of the surface surcharge, the adverse effects of the surface surcharge mainly occur within a range of 2 times the tunnel diameter, while beyond this range, the support structure’s internal force becomes less sensitive to the surcharge.

This paper mainly supplements the field-measured data on the influence of existing surface surcharges on the stress and deformation of the tunnel, which is relatively lacking in the existing literature. The research findings can help predict the safety of tunnel construction when subjected to surface surcharge. The study’s calculations are used to regulate surface surcharge within safe limits. It’s important to note that this paper focuses on large-section loess tunnels under shallow burial conditions, and its relevance to other types of tunnels requires further validation. Additionally, the numerical simulation simplifies the surface surcharge shape above the tunnel to a certain extent, assuming it to be rectangular in this paper, while actual situations may involve uneven surface surcharge. Further research can refine the model based on this paper in the future.

Real-world outcomes of T cell lymphoma in a tertiary cancer center in Saudi Arabia
Authors: Sultan Alqahtani, Mohammed Assiri, Abdullah Bin Abdu, Khalid Alyusif, Mohammed Alharbi, Ziyad Alharbi, Ali Alsubaie, Abdullah Alhamzani, Saud Alghamdi, Saeed Alshibani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBACKGROUND: T-cell and natural-killer (T/NK)–cell lymphomas are rare and aggressive malignancies. Prior to our study, no dedicated institutional survival analyses of T/NK-cell lymphomas had been published from Saudi Arabia.OBJECTIVES: To evaluate the clinical characteristics, prognostic factors, and survival outcomes of patients with T/NK-cell lymphoma treated at a tertiary cancer referral centre in Saudi Arabia.DESIGN: Retrospective cohort studySETTING: King Abdulaziz Medical City, Ministry of National Guard–Health Affairs, Riyadh, Saudi Arabia; January 2016 to December 2022.PATIENTS AND METHODS: We reviewed medical records of 89 adults with histologically confirmed T/NK-cell lymphomas, including precursor and mature subtypes. Overall survival (OS) was estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method. Prognostic factors were assessed with univariate and multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression. To maintain a robust events-per-variable ratio, the multivariable model was restricted to four predictors.MAIN OUTCOME MEASURES: Five-year overall survival and identification of variables associated with mortality.SAMPLE SIZE: 89 patientsRESULTS: The mean age was 49.5±20.5 years, and 62 patients (70%) were male. The most common subtypes were ALK-negative anaplastic large-cell lymphoma (n=20, 22%) and peripheral T-cell lymphoma, not otherwise specified (n=15, 17%). For the entire cohort (N=89), the 5-year OS was 56% (95% CI, 44%–67%); the Kaplan–Meier estimated mean OS over the full available follow-up was 116.4 months. In multivariable analysis restricted to 70 patients with complete data (26 deaths), elevated lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) (hazard ratio [HR]=7.66; 95% confidence interval [CI], 1.77-33.19; P=.006), Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status >2 (HR=4.26; 95% CI, 1.60–11.33; P=.004), and renal insufficiency (HR=3.06; 95% CI, 1.34–6.97; P=.008) were independent predictors of worse survival. Anemia trended toward worse survival but did not reach independent significance (HR=2.30; 95% CI, 0.95–5.58; P=.066).CONCLUSION: Elevated LDH, poor performance status, and renal insufficiency are strong independent predictors of inferior survival. Stem cell transplantation did not demonstrate a survival benefit in this cohort. These findings underscore the importance of baseline laboratory and clinical variables for risk stratification and provide groundwork for multicentre studies and therapeutic optimisation in Saudi Arabia.LIMITATIONS: Sample size and single-centre retrospective design limit generalisability. Transplant-specific details in treatment regimens were not evaluated.Keywords: T/NK-cell lymphoma, survival analysis, prognostic factors, Saudi Arabia

INTRODUCTION

The backbone of the human immune response is the lymphoid system, which encompasses innate immunity mediated by natural killer (NK) cells and adaptive immunity orchestrated by B and T lymphocytes. A major pathological condition involving these immune cells is non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL).1 These lymphomas are characterised by heterogeneous clinical presentation, histological features, and genetic abnormalities. According to the World Health Organization (WHO) 2016 and 2022 classifications, T-cell neoplasms are broadly divided into precursor lymphoid neoplasms, such as T-lymphoblastic lymphoma/leukemia (T-LBL/ALL), and mature T-cell and NK-cell neoplasms, which are further subclassified into nodal, extranodal, leukemic, and cutaneous entities.2 A recent review article showcased that T-cell lymphoma pathogenesis emphasises the central role of epigenetic dysregulation and aberrant T-cell receptor (TCR) signalling as key drivers in disease development, particularly in peripheral T-cell lymphoma subtypes.3 An important application of this biological understanding comes in the form of multi-omics diagnostics, which reveal complex interactions between genetic mutations, the tumour microenvironment, and immune signalling pathways, offering new avenues for targeted therapies.4

Despite this heterogeneity, real-world survival data remain limited, particularly outside East Asian and Western populations, where most clinical evidence is generated. The incidence of NHL has been rising in the United States. A total of 2148 cases of adult T-cell leukemia/lymphoma (ATLL) were documented, with an incidence rate of 0.06 per 100 000.5 Additionally, 14 942 new cases of cutaneous T-cell lymphoma (CTCL) were reported between 2000 and 2018, with an incidence rate of 8.55 per 100 000, where mycosis fungoides was the most common subtype.6 Although the NHL database in Saudi Arabia is extensive, it lacks research-based evidence. A 2020 report by the Saudi Health Council covering 1994 to 2020 reported NHL, whether of B-cell or T-cell origin, as the third most common cancer among Saudi males and sixth among Saudi females.7 A total of 756 cases were documented, accounting for 5.4% of all cancers among Saudi nationals. The age-standardised incidence rates (ASR) were 4.6 per 100 000 for males and 3.9 per 100 000 for females.7

Few studies address specific NHL subtypes or prognostic factors relating to survival in Saudi Arabia.8 At the time of study initiation and submission, no dedicated institutional survival analysis of T-cell lymphomas had been published from the Kingdom. Thus, this retrospective cohort study aimed to evaluate the characteristics and outcomes of T/NK-cell lymphoma at a tertiary cancer referral centre in Saudi Arabia. Our study addresses a critical gap in Saudi oncology data and provides a foundation for future multicentre collaborations and therapeutic advancements in this rare but challenging group of malignancies.

METHODS

Study design and eligibility

We conducted a retrospective cohort study of all adults (≥14 years) diagnosed with T/NK-cell lymphoma at King Abdulaziz Medical City (KAMC), Riyadh, between January 2016 and December 2022. Patients were included if they had histologically confirmed T/NK-cell lymphoma, covering precursor (T-LBL/ALL) and mature T/NK-cell subtypes defined by the 2016 WHO classification. We excluded patients if histopathology was inconclusive, baseline demographic or survival data were missing, or if the lymphoma represented transformation from another haematologic malignancy. All cases were reviewed by board-certified haematopathologists, and diagnoses were confirmed in a multidisciplinary tumour board comprising pathologists, haematologists, oncologists, and radiologists. Immunohistochemistry included markers for T cells (CD3, CD4, CD5, CD8, TdT), B cells (CD20, PAX5, CD79a), activation markers (CD30, CD45, CD43), and proliferation index (Ki-67). Additional markers such as ALK-1, EMA, CD10, BCL-6, and Epstein–Barr virus–encoded RNA in situ hybridisation were applied as indicated. The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of King Abdullah International Medical Research Center, Riyadh, Saudi Arabia (Ref IRB/1395/22 SP22R/109/06). The requirement for individual informed consent was waived given the retrospective design. The research adhered to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Data collection

Clinical, laboratory, and treatment data were extracted from the electronic medical records into a structured database. Variables included age, sex, comorbidities, performance status, B symptoms, extranodal involvement, bone marrow disease, Ann Arbor stage, treatment regimen, response, relapse, and survival outcome. Laboratory variables recorded at diagnosis included haemoglobin, platelet count, leukocyte and neutrophil counts, lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), albumin, and serum creatinine. Renal insufficiency was defined as an estimated glomerular filtration rate <60 mL/min/1.73 m2 or serum creatinine above the institutional upper limit of normal. Extranodal disease was defined as lymphoma involving any extranodal organ or site at diagnosis.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are summarised as mean (standard deviation) or median (interquartile range), and categorical variables as counts (percentages). Between-group comparisons used Student's t-test or Mann–Whitney U test for continuous variables, and chi-square or Fisher's exact test for categorical variables. Overall survival (OS) was calculated from the date of diagnosis to death or last follow-up. Survival functions were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method and compared using the log-rank test. Prognostic factors were evaluated with Cox proportional hazards regression. To mitigate overfitting, we restricted the multivariable model to four variables based on clinical relevance and univariate significance, yielding an events-per-variable ratio of 6.5. Hazard ratios with 95% confidence intervals and P values were reported. Analyses were conducted using Python (lifelines library) and IBM SPSS Statistics, with two-sided P values <.05 considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

Patient characteristics

Eighty-nine patients met eligibility criteria (Table 1). The mean age was 49.5±20.5 years, and 62 patients (70%) were male. The most common histologic subtypes were ALK-negative anaplastic large-cell lymphoma (20 patients, 22%) and peripheral T-cell lymphoma, not otherwise specified (15 patients, 17%). B symptoms were present in 62 patients (69%), and 25 (28%) had ECOG performance status >2. Extranodal involvement occurred in 57% of patients, and bone marrow involvement was documented in 25 patients (28%). Ann Arbor staging showed that 64% of patients presented with stage IV disease, whereas 14% and 11% were stages I and II, respectively.

Laboratory findings

Elevated LDH above the upper limit of normal occurred in 49 patients (55%) (Table 2), with 36 (40%) having LDH levels more than twice the upper limit. Anemia (haemoglobin < 11 g/dL) was observed in 36 patients (40%), and thrombocytopenia (platelets < 150×109/L) in 23 patients (26%). Renal insufficiency was present in 27 patients (30%). The mean white blood cell and neutrophil counts were 8.6±7.8×109/L and 5.3±5.3×109/L, respectively. Hypoalbuminaemia was reported in 21 patients (24%).

Treatment and response

Chemotherapy regimens included CHEOP (21%), CHOP (18%), HCVAD (12%), MTX-based regimens (3%), and others (Table 3). Eight patients (9%) received palliative therapy only. The mean number of chemotherapy cycles was 4.8±1.9. Complete response was achieved in 47% of patients, whereas 25% experienced disease progression and 12% achieved partial responses. Ten patients received radiotherapy, seven to the head and neck, two to an extremity, and one to the mediastinum. Central nervous system prophylaxis was administered in 14 patients (16%).

Stem cell transplantation (SCT) was performed in 27 patients (30%). Ten patients (11%) underwent transplantation as upfront consolidation after first-line therapy, and 17 (19%) received SCT in later lines. Among non-transplanted patients, the mean OS was 37.7 months (95% CI, 31.1–44.2 months). SCT did not confer a significant survival advantage (log-rank P=.091).

Survival analysis

At a median follow-up of 39 months, 35 deaths had occurred. For the overall cohort of 89 patients, the median OS was not reached because more than half the cohort remained alive. The 5-year OS rate was 56% (95% CI, 44%–67%), and the Kaplan-Meier estimated mean OS over the full available follow-up was 116.4 months. Kaplan–Meier subgroup analyses are shown in Figures 1–4. Patients who underwent SCT had a mean OS of 47.3 months (95% CI, 39.8–54.9) compared with 37.7 months (95% CI, 31.1–44.2) among non-SCT patients, with no statistically significant difference (log-rank P=.091). Among SCT recipients, mean OS was 44.8 months (95% CI, 30.5–59.2) for upfront SCT and 48.8 months (95% CI, 40.3–57.3) for later-line SCT (log-rank P=.817). By histologic subtype, mean OS was 36.3 months (95% CI, 23.2–49.3) for PTCL, NOS; 42.7 months (95% CI, 27.2–58.2) for AITCL; 44.7 months (95% CI, 34.7–54.7) for ALK-negative ALCL; and 31.6 months (95% CI, 17.2–45.9) for T-LBL, with no significant difference among subtypes (log-rank P=.484). Patients with elevated LDH had a lower mean OS of 36.1 months (95% CI, 29.1–43.1) compared with 54.7 months (95% CI, 49.6–59.7) among patients with normal LDH (log-rank P<.01).

Univariate and multivariable analysis

Univariate Cox regression identified ECOG performance status >2, bone marrow involvement, renal insufficiency, anemia <11 g/dL, and elevated LDH as significant predictors of mortality (Table 4). ECOG PS >2 was associated with a hazard ratio (HR) of 3.72 (95% CI, 1.85–7.48; P<.001), bone marrow involvement with an HR of 2.28 (95% CI, 1.15–4.54; P=.018), renal insufficiency with an HR of 4.09 (95% CI, 1.99–8.40; P<.001), and anemia <11 g/dL with an HR of 3.52 (95% CI, 1.55–8.02; P=.003). Platelets <150×109/L had a hazard ratio of 0.96 (95% CI, 0.78–1.18; P=.743), and Ann Arbor stage III/IV disease showed HR 1.62 (95% CI, 0.67–3.93; P=.283). LDH > upper limit of normal showed a particularly strong association with mortality (HR=13.5; 95% CI, 3.21–56.77; P<.001).

In the multivariable model limited to 70 patients with complete data (26 events), elevated LDH remained the most potent predictor of mortality (HR=7.66; 95% CI, 1.77–33.19; P=.0065). ECOG PS >2 (HR=4.26; 95% CI, 1.60–11.33; P=.0037) and renal insufficiency (HR=3.06; 95% CI, 1.34–6.97; P=.0077) also retained independent significance. Anemia <11 g/dL trended toward worse survival but was not independently significant (HR=2.30; 95% CI, 0.95–5.58; P=.0663). This four-variable model yielded an events-per-variable ratio of 6.5, improving model stability and reducing the risk of overfitting compared with the original expanded model.

DISCUSSION

This study provides one of the first real-world survival analyses of T-cell lymphomas in Saudi Arabia, addressing a significant regional gap in lymphoma research. A recent cohort by Al-Mansour et al showed a median OS of 24 months, with a 5-year OS and progression-free survival (PFS) rates of 44% and 23.6%, respectively.9 Our analysis complements these findings but differs in several key aspects: we included both precursor and mature subtypes, evaluated additional prognostic variables such as renal insufficiency, and assessed the role of stem cell transplantation in real-world practice. A large cohort from the United States by Abramson et al analysed 341 patients diagnosed with peripheral T-cell lymphoma.10 The majority presented with advanced stage disease, extranodal involvement, and poor prognostic features such as anemia and elevated LDH, similar to our cohort. Neither study demonstrated a survival benefit from consolidative stem cell transplantation when adjusted for response to initial therapy.

Most prominently, elevated LDH and its poor prognostic outlook align with the results from the general lymphoma cohort by Altowairqi et al.8 Elevated LDH can result from tumour burden itself or substantial clinical circumstances, such as septic shock.11 Qi et al assessed the prognostic significance of elevated LDH levels in patients with DLBCL treated in intensive care units (ICUs).12 The study found that patients with highly elevated LDH levels had a significantly higher risk of 28-day mortality, remaining significant even after adjusting for confounders such as creatinine levels and ICU scoring systems. Furthermore, bone marrow involvement was significantly associated with worse outcomes in univariate analysis, despite modified Ann Arbor staging not achieving statistical significance in our cohort.12–14

Lymphoid malignancy is not confined to the expected standard of metastatic solid tumours, in which stage IV strictly implies poor outcomes. In our cohort, most patients were classified as advanced stages; however, this did not independently predict mortality. Instead, elevated LDH levels and end-organ damage such as renal insufficiency were strong predictors of mortality on multivariable analysis. Poor ECOG status was also robustly associated with mortality, highlighting the interplay between functional reserve and outcomes. While anemia was a significant predictor on univariate analysis, it lost significance after multivariable adjustment, suggesting it may serve as a surrogate for other factors such as tumour burden or nutritional status.

SCT was performed in 27 of 89 patients. Our centre has been a national SCT referral centre for the last decade.15 In our analysis, undergoing SCT did not significantly enhance survival, whether with upfront or later-line strategies. A large study from Korea retrospectively assessed transplant-specific outcomes in 598 patients with aggressive T-cell lymphomas who underwent SCT between 2010 and 2020.16 Patients who underwent upfront SCT demonstrated a 3-year OS of 76.1%; however, autologous SCT showed significantly better OS than allogeneic SCT. Another study in the United States examined the outcomes of SCT in patients with hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma, finding no significant differences in median OS between patients receiving autologous and allogeneic transplants.17 Furthermore, Beitinjaneh et al published the outcomes of SCT in 134 T-cell lymphoma patients at the MD Anderson Cancer Center. Their findings suggested that while autologous SCT as a consolidation therapy offers survival benefits, allogeneic SCT does not demonstrate superior outcomes.18 Our findings align with the broader consensus questioning the universal role of transplantation in first remission, though the small subgroup size (n=27) limits definitive conclusions.

Our results should be interpreted in light of several limitations. The small sample size and single-centre retrospective design limit statistical power and generalisability. Transplant-specific details such as conditioning regimens and stem cell sources were not evaluated, and variability in treatment approaches may confound outcomes. Despite these limitations, this study represents the largest single-institution dataset of T/NK-cell lymphomas reported from Saudi Arabia.

In summary, this study provides a foundation for multicentre collaborations aimed at refining prognostication and treatment strategies. Elevated LDH, ECOG PS >2, and renal insufficiency serve as robust, independent predictors of mortality when adjusting for baseline covariates.

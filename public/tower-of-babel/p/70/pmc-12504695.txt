A SCG-YOLOv8n potato counting framework with efficient mobile deployment
Authors: Leichao Du, Jichao Li, Jing Yang, Wenjie Liu, Xiaodi Sun, Yuelong Xia
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Accurately detecting and counting potatoes during early harvest is essential for estimating yield, automating sorting, and supporting data-driven agricultural decisions. However, field environments often present practical challenges–such as soil occlusion, overlapping tubers, and inconsistent lighting–that hinder robust visual recognition. In response, we introduce SCG-YOLOv8n, a compact and field-adapted detection framework built upon the YOLOv8n architecture and specifically tailored for small-object detection in real-world farming conditions. The model incorporates three practical enhancements: a C-SPD module that preserves spatial detail to improve recognition of partially buried tubers; an S-CARAFE operator that reconstructs fine-scale features during upsampling; and GhostShuffleConv layers that reduce computational overhead without sacrificing accuracy. Through extensive field-based experiments, SCG-YOLOv8n consistently outperforms YOLOv5n and its base version across all key metrics. Float16 quantization compresses the model to 3.2 MB, enabling real-time inference on Android devices. We also developed PotatoDetector, a mobile application that demonstrates stable performance in field trials, achieving an RMSE of 1.38 and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$R^2$$\end{document} of 0.96 in counting tasks. These results suggest that SCG-YOLOv8n offers a practical and scalable tool for precision agriculture, with potential applicability to other root and tuber crop monitoring scenarios.

Introduction

Potatoes (Solanum tuberosum), as the fourth most important staple crop globally, play a vital role in ensuring food security and supporting rural economies–particularly in mountainous and highland regions of China where mechanized agriculture is less accessible1–47980. With the growing emphasis on smart agriculture and digital farming, accurate in-field detection and counting of potato tubers have become essential for yield forecasting, harvesting automation, and data-driven agricultural decision-making5,68182. However, reliable potato detection remains a challenging task due to multiple factors: tubers are often partially buried under soil, heavily occluded by other objects, exhibit high shape and size variability, and frequently appear under inconsistent lighting or complex backgrounds7–98384. These conditions severely limit the effectiveness of traditional machine vision techniques such as color segmentation, edge extraction, or morphological filtering, which are fragile under real-world field variability10,118586.

Recent advances in deep learning, particularly convolutional neural networks (CNNs), have transformed the landscape of agricultural computer vision12–1587. CNN-based models have demonstrated impressive performance in tasks such as fruit detection, disease classification, and crop segmentation under unstructured environments16–2188. Object detection approaches can be broadly categorized into two classes: two-stage detectors like Faster R-CNN22–2489 that offer high accuracy at the cost of speed, and one-stage detectors such as the YOLO series25–27 that enable real-time detection with simplified end-to-end pipelines. Among these, YOLO-based methods are widely adopted in precision agriculture due to their efficiency. For instance, Tian et al.28,29 improved YOLOv3 to detect apples in complex orchard settings, while Fu et al.30,31 customized YOLOv3-tiny for kiwifruit recognition under natural light conditions. Numerous studies have extended YOLO-based models to various crop detection and counting tasks, including citrus yield estimation32–34, cotton boll recognition35–37, and jujube counting38,39. However, most of these models are evaluated on high-performance computing platforms, which limits their scalability and real-world usability. For mobile deployment, models must be lightweight, accurate, and computationally efficient to operate on edge devices with limited memory and processing power.

To bridge this gap, recent efforts have focused on compact model architectures such as YOLOv5n and YOLOv8n, incorporating efficient operators and lightweight modules. For example, Zheng et al.40 proposed a YOLOX-Nano-based jujube detector for smartphones, while Feng et al.41 adopted ghost-shuffle convolution to compress YOLOv5s for mobile inference. Nevertheless, these solutions often underperform when applied to small, partially occluded, or soil-covered objects like potatoes, which demand enhanced spatial sensitivity and contextual reasoning.

To address these challenges, we propose a novel and efficient detection framework named SCG-YOLOv8n, tailored for real-time potato detection and counting under natural field conditions. Our approach builds upon YOLOv8n and introduces three key algorithmic enhancements: (1) a spatially enhanced convolution module (SPD-Conv) to capture fine-grained structural cues, (2) CARAFE upsampling for context-aware feature reconstruction, and (3) GSConv layers to reduce model complexity without sacrificing accuracy. Furthermore, we apply float16 quantization to achieve a compact 3.2 MB model size, making it suitable for Android deployment. To validate our method in practice, we develop a mobile application, PotatoDetector, and conduct field trials. The system achieves a root mean square error (RMSE) of 1.38 and R\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$^2$$\end{document} of 0.96 in real-world counting tasks, demonstrating excellent performance in both accuracy and usability. Related work

Potato detection and counting

Potato detection and counting are essential for modern precision agriculture, particularly in supporting yield estimation, sorting automation, and supply chain optimization. Earlier studies typically relied on traditional image processing techniques such as color thresholding, edge extraction, and morphological operations42,43. However, these conventional approaches struggle in complex field conditions–such as occlusion by soil, irregular tuber shapes, and inconsistent lighting–resulting in low robustness and poor generalization.

With the rise of deep learning, especially convolutional neural networks (CNNs), object detection tasks in agriculture have seen significant improvements. These models can learn rich visual features directly from annotated data, enabling higher detection accuracy in unstructured environments44–46. Although many early efforts focused on detecting above-ground fruits under controlled orchard conditions47, more recent research has begun addressing underground and partially occluded crops like potatoes in real field scenes48,49. Such tasks are inherently more difficult due to overlapping tubers, soil contamination, and limited color contrast, necessitating the design of highly specialized and robust detection models.

Lightweight object detection models

Real-time detection in field-deployable systems demands compact and efficient model architectures. Among various options, the YOLO (You Only Look Once) family of models has emerged as a dominant solution for real-time object detection. YOLOv350–52 introduced multi-scale prediction through feature pyramids, and YOLOv45,53,54 improved inference efficiency through backbone optimizations. YOLOv5–though not officially published–gained popularity due to its ease of use and modular design, with lightweight variants like YOLOv5n designed specifically for edge deployment. YOLOv8, as the latest generation, adopts an anchor-free detection strategy and a redesigned backbone, enabling more flexible detection of objects with varying sizes and aspect ratios55–57. Compared to anchor-based models like YOLOv5, YOLOv8 reduces manual anchor configuration and improves generalization on small objects, making it well-suited for detecting partially buried or occluded potatoes (see Fig. 1).

To further improve detection of small or low-contrast objects like tubers, recent research has incorporated architectural modules such as SPD-Conv58,59, which enlarges the receptive field with minimal parameter overhead and enhances the perception of small targets. This is particularly useful in potato detection tasks where visual cues are subtle and context-dependent.

Mobile deployment for agricultural applications

Deploying detection systems directly on mobile devices has become increasingly important for enabling low-cost, scalable solutions in agricultural environments. Frameworks such as TensorFlow Lite (TFLite), ONNX Runtime Mobile, and Android NNAPI support real-time on-device inference with hardware acceleration, allowing deep models to run efficiently on smartphones and edge devices60,61. Model compression and quantization techniques–such as float16 or int8 post-training quantization–are commonly used to reduce model size and improve latency while maintaining accuracy62,63. However, achieving smooth and fast inference on heterogeneous mobile hardware still requires careful balancing of model complexity, accuracy, and deployment format. Recent benchmarks indicate that YOLOv8, especially its nano variant YOLOv8n, can be effectively optimized and quantized for mobile platforms. With proper tuning and architecture refinements, these models are capable of delivering fast and accurate potato detection under field conditions. Materials

Image data acquisition

To develop a robust model for potato detection and counting, a custom dataset was collected from open-field potato cultivation sites located in Kunming, Yunnan Province, China. The potato fields adopted standard ridge-planting configurations compatible with mechanized agriculture. Image acquisition was performed using a smartphone-mounted camera system at close-range viewpoints (30–50 cm above ground), simulating real-world use cases for mobile agricultural monitoring.

To capture the natural variability of field conditions, images were acquired at different times of day–morning, midday, and evening–and under diverse environmental settings including variable illumination, soil moisture, and occlusion by potato leaves. The dataset features challenging scenarios such as overlapping tubers, partially unearthed potatoes, and irregularly shaped objects to evaluate the generalization ability of the model. A total of 6510 raw images were collected as the base dataset for training and evaluation.

To increase data diversity and simulate field noise, various augmentation techniques were employed. These included Mosaic composition, Gaussian noise injection, brightness variation, random rotation, and artificial occlusion. Representative augmentation examples are shown in Fig. 2.

Data preprocessing

To improve the robustness and generalization capability of the model, a comprehensive set of image augmentation strategies was applied during preprocessing. These included random cropping, translation, brightness adjustment, noise addition, rotation, horizontal flipping, and synthetic occlusion. All operations were implemented using Python and the OpenCV library. Each augmentation technique was applied with randomized parameters and controlled probabilities. In some cases, augmentations were performed individually; in others, multiple techniques were applied in combination to simulate diverse real-world visual disturbances, such as uneven lighting or soil occlusion. Figure 2 illustrates representative samples of the original and augmented images.

To ensure diversity and avoid redundancy, augmented images that were overly similar to the originals were filtered out. After preprocessing and augmentation, the final dataset comprised 6510 images. These were divided into training, validation, and test sets using an 80%/10%/10% split. Image annotation was performed using LabelImg (version 1.8.6). Each visible potato–whether fully exposed, partially buried, or overlapping with others–was manually labeled with bounding boxes. This ensured precise localization and comprehensive coverage during both training and evaluation phases (Fig. 3).Fig. 2Dataset augmentation examples: (a) original, (b) noise injection, (c) brightness reduction, (d) masking and flipping, (e) brightness enhancement, and (f) rotation.

YOLOv8n model

YOLOv8n is a lightweight and anchor-free object detection model developed by Ultralytics as part of the YOLOv8 family. Compared with earlier YOLO versions, YOLOv8n simplifies the detection pipeline by eliminating anchor boxes and employing a decoupled head for classification and localization, which reduces post-processing complexity and improves adaptability to objects with varying sizes and shapes. The architecture of YOLOv8n consists of three main components: Backbone, Neck, and Head (see Fig. 5). The backbone is built upon a C2f (Concatenate-to-fuse) module design, which enhances multi-scale feature extraction efficiency with fewer parameters. The neck adopts a Path Aggregation Network (PAN) to strengthen the fusion of low-level spatial features and high-level semantic information across different scales. The detection head leverages an anchor-free mechanism and Task-Aligned Learning (TAL) for improved object classification and localization performance. YOLOv8n is optimized for speed and compactness, with only 3.2 million parameters and a model size of approximately 2.6 MB, making it highly suitable for mobile and embedded deployment scenarios. In this work, we adopt YOLOv8n as the baseline architecture and further enhance it with targeted modifications aimed at boosting small object detection and deployment efficiency.

CARAFE upsampling

To enhance semantic consistency during multi-scale feature fusion in the neck of the detection architecture, we incorporate the CARAFE (Content-Aware ReAssembly of FEatures) operator68 into the YOLOv8n framework. CARAFE is a lightweight yet expressive upsampling module that adaptively generates position-specific reassembly kernels based on the input feature content, surpassing traditional approaches such as nearest-neighbor, bilinear interpolation, or transposed convolution in terms of context-awareness and receptive field. CARAFE consists of two principal components (see Fig. 7): the Kernel Prediction Module (KPM) and the Content-Aware Reassembly Module (CaRM).

Deployment

To enable real-time inference in field environments, we deploy the proposed SCG-YOLOv8n model on Android mobile devices (Fig. 9). Given the need for efficient and lightweight processing under resource-constrained conditions, we adopt a post-training quantization strategy using the float16 format. This compresses the model weights from 32-bit to 16-bit floating-point representations, significantly reducing memory usage and latency while incurring minimal performance degradation, as validated by prior studies70–72.

The model is first exported from PyTorch to the ONNX format and then converted into the TensorFlow Lite “.tflite” format. Float16 quantization is applied during this conversion process. The resulting quantized model has a compact size of approximately 3.2 MB and supports smooth inference on mid-range Android smartphones. We developed a mobile application named PotatoDetector, built using Android Studio (version 2021.3.1) with target SDK level 29. The application integrates the quantized SCG-YOLOv8n model and leverages the CameraX API to access the device’s rear camera for real-time video streaming. During on-device inference, each video frame is processed to produce bounding boxes, confidence scores, object counts, and latency values, which are visualized directly on the screen to assist users in the field.

Experiments and results

Experimental settings

To assess the effectiveness of the proposed SCG-YOLOv8n model in real-world potato detection and counting, we conducted comprehensive experiments using a field-acquired dataset captured under natural agricultural conditions. All training and inference tasks were performed on a Linux workstation equipped with an Intel Core i7-10870H CPU, NVIDIA GeForce RTX 2060 GPU, CUDA 11.6, and cuDNN 8.2.1. The deep learning framework used was PyTorch (v1.12), with model development conducted in Python 3.9 via PyCharm 2022.2.1. The SCG-YOLOv8n and baseline models were trained under identical conditions for fair comparison. Each model was trained for 300 epochs using Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) with a batch size of 32. The learning rate was initialized at 0.01, with a momentum of 0.937 and a weight decay of 0.0005. Data loading was parallelized across four worker threads. These settings were empirically selected to ensure a good trade-off between convergence speed and generalization performance across lightweight object detectors (Figs. 8, 9).

Discussion

The proposed SCG-YOLOv8n model exhibits substantial improvements in potato detection and counting when compared to its baseline and several mainstream lightweight detectors. Specifically, it achieves absolute gains of 2.40%, 1.80%, and 3.00% in precision, recall, and mAP over YOLOv5n, while simultaneously reducing RMSE and MAPE by 9.11% and 5.30%, respectively. Compared to other YOLO variants such as YOLOv3-tiny, YOLOv4-tiny, and YOLOv7-tiny, SCG-YOLOv8n consistently maintains the highest performance across all key detection and counting metrics.

These improvements are primarily attributed to the integration of three lightweight yet effective architectural components: SPD-Conv enhances the model’s ability to detect small and partially occluded targets; CARAFE provides content-aware upsampling for better spatial detail restoration; and GSConv balances nonlinear feature representation with computational efficiency. The ablation studies further validate that combining all three modules delivers the best trade-off between detection accuracy and inference speed, achieving a peak mAP of 92.20% with an inference latency of just 13.40 ms.

From a deployment perspective, the model’s compact size (4.36 MB) and low computational footprint make it particularly suitable for real-time applications on mobile and edge devices. In contrast, conventional models such as SSD and Faster R-CNN exhibit higher latency and significantly larger sizes (e.g., over 90 MB), which limit their practical utility. Through float16 quantization and Android deployment via the PotatoDetector app, SCG-YOLOv8n demonstrates robust field performance, achieving an \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$R^2$$\end{document} score of 0.97 and an RMSE of 1.46 in real-world potato counting scenarios–outperforming all baseline models in both accuracy and consistency.

Nevertheless, several limitations remain. Under extreme conditions–including severe occlusion by soil or foliage, low-light environments, and visually ambiguous backgrounds–the detection performance may deteriorate. Moreover, variability in tuber size and growth stage may affect model generalizability. To mitigate these issues, future work will focus on expanding the dataset across diverse seasonal and environmental conditions, and exploring multimodal data fusion strategies (e.g., RGB with depth or thermal imagery). These enhancements are expected to further improve model adaptability and reliability, thereby supporting more comprehensive smart agriculture applications.

Conclusion

In this study, we proposed SCG-YOLOv8n, a lightweight and efficient object detection model for real-time potato detection and counting on Android devices in natural field environments. Based on the YOLOv5n backbone, the model integrates three tailored modules–SPD-Conv for enhanced recognition of small and occluded targets, CARAFE for adaptive upsampling and spatial context aggregation, and GSConv for reducing computational complexity while maintaining high accuracy. To support mobile deployment, we applied float16 quantization and developed the PotatoDetector application using Android Studio. Experimental results demonstrate that SCG-YOLOv8n achieves superior performance in both detection accuracy and inference speed compared to mainstream lightweight detectors, maintaining robustness under challenging conditions such as varying illumination and soil occlusion. The model’s compact size, real-time capability, and extensibility make it highly suitable for practical yield estimation and provide a promising foundation for broader smart agriculture applications involving small-object detection in resource-constrained scenarios.

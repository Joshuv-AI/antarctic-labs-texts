Virtual Surgical Planning (VSP) In Maxillofacial Reconstruction
Authors: Raha Ahmed Shamikh Almufarrij, Hala Hamoud T Alshammari, Saly Muteb H Alabdli, Lama Habis Samah Alkhershawy, Rose Sultan Radad Almasloukhi, Namdeo Prabhu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAim:The aim of this study is to evaluate the effectiveness of virtual surgical planning (VSP) in enhancing precision and outcomes in maxillofacial reconstruction procedures.Methodology:A prospective study was conducted on 30 patients undergoing maxillofacial reconstruction. Twenty patients were treated using VSP with 3D imaging and patient-specific surgical guides, while 10 underwent conventional planning. Outcomes were evaluated based on operative time, reconstruction accuracy, and patient satisfaction.Results:The VSP group showed significantly reduced operative time (180 vs. 240 min), improved accuracy (1.2 mm vs. 3.6 mm deviation), and higher patient satisfaction scores (8.9 vs. 7.1) compared to the control group (P < 0.05).Conclusion:VSP enhances surgical precision, reduces operating time, and improves patient satisfaction in maxillofacial reconstruction, demonstrating its value as an effective preoperative planning tool.

INTRODUCTION

Maxillofacial reconstruction often involves complex anatomical challenges requiring high precision for optimal functional and aesthetic outcomes. Traditional surgical planning methods can be limited in accuracy and predictability. Virtual surgical planning (VSP) has emerged as a transformative tool, utilizing advanced imaging and 3D modeling to preoperatively simulate procedures and design patient-specific surgical aids.[1] This study explores the role and effectiveness of VSP in improving the outcomes of maxillofacial reconstruction surgeries.

METHODOLOGY

A total of 30 patients requiring maxillofacial reconstruction were included in this prospective study. The study group consisted of 20 patients planned using VSP, while 10 patients undergoing conventional planning formed the control group. Preoperative computed tomography (CT) scans were obtained for all patients. In the VSP group, 3D virtual planning was performed, and patient-specific surgical guides and implants were fabricated using Computer Aided Design/Computer-Aided Manufacturing (CAD/CAM) technology. Surgical outcomes were assessed based on operative time, accuracy of reconstruction (measured by postoperative imaging), and patient satisfaction. Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS software (IBM, Chicago, United States of America), with significance set at P < 0.05.

This prospective comparative study was conducted on 30 patients requiring maxillofacial reconstruction due to trauma, tumor resection, or congenital deformities. Patients were divided into two groups:

Explanation

The VSP group had a significantly shorter average operative time and greater accuracy in bone reconstruction, as measured by postoperative imaging. Patient satisfaction scores were also higher in the VSP group, indicating better aesthetic and functional outcomes. These results suggest that VSP enhances surgical efficiency and overall patient experience in maxillofacial reconstruction.

DISCUSSION

The results of this study demonstrate that VSP significantly enhances surgical outcomes in maxillofacial reconstruction compared to conventional planning methods. The reduction in operative time observed in the VSP group reflects increased intraoperative efficiency, likely due to the use of prefabricated surgical guides and implants that streamline the surgical workflow.[12]

Additionally, the improved accuracy in anatomical reconstruction highlights the value of precise preoperative simulation, which minimizes intraoperative guesswork and enhances the alignment of bone segments. Higher patient satisfaction scores in the VSP group further support the benefits of this technology, indicating better functional and aesthetic results.

These findings are consistent with previous research that emphasizes the advantages of digital planning in complex craniofacial procedures. However, limitations, such as the need for specialized equipment, higher costs, and a learning curve, must be considered when implementing VSP in clinical practice.[34]

Overall, VSP proves to be a powerful tool that improves precision, reduces operative time, and enhances patient outcomes in maxillofacial reconstruction, justifying its growing role in modern surgical practice.[5]

CONCLUSION

VSP significantly improves the precision, efficiency, and overall outcomes of maxillofacial reconstruction procedures. By enabling accurate preoperative simulation and the use of patient-specific surgical guides, VSP reduces operative time and enhances both functional and aesthetic results. This study supports the integration of VSP into routine clinical practice as a valuable tool for optimizing surgical planning and patient care in complex maxillofacial cases.

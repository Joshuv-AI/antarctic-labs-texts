Optimal solution of multiobjective stable environmental economic power dispatch problem considering probabilistic wind and solar PV generation
Authors: Aamir Ali, Sumbal Aslam, MU Keerio, Sohrab Mirsaeidi, Noor Hussain Mugheri, Muhammad Ismail, Ghulam Abbas, Salwa Othmen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe Environmental Economic Power Dispatch (EEPD) problem, a widely studied bi-objective nonlinear optimization challenge in power systems, traditionally focuses on the economic dispatch of thermal generators without considering network security constraints. However, environmental sustainability necessitates reducing emissions and increasing the penetration of renewable energy sources (RES) into the electrical grid. The integration of high levels of RES, such as wind and solar PV, introduces stability issues due to their uncertain and intermittent nature. This paper addresses these concerns by formulating and solving the Stable Environmental Economic Power Dispatch (SEEPD) problem, which includes fixed zonal reserve capacity from conventional thermal generators and uncertain reserves from RES. Uncertainties in RES and load demand are modeled using random variable generation techniques, applying Gaussian, Weibull, and log-normal probability density functions (PDFs) for load demand, wind velocity, and solar irradiance, respectively. The stochastic SEEPD problem extends to multiple periods by replicating the single-period problem for each interval in the planning horizon, linking periods through intertemporal ramping costs, physical ramp rate, and fixed zonal reserve constraints on dispatch variables. Multi-Objective Evolutionary Algorithms (MOEAs) have gained importance for solving complex nonlinear problems involving multi-objective functions. This paper applies the latest MOEAs to tackle the proposed SEEPD problem, incorporating stochastic wind and solar PV power sources. Network security constraints, such as transmission line capacities and bus voltage limits, are considered along with constraints on generator capabilities and intertemporal spinning reserves, ramp-up and ramp-down constraints for thermal generators. A bidirectional coevolutionary-based multi-objective evolutionary algorithm is employed, integrating an advanced constraint-handling technique to ensure compliance with system constraints. The simulation results show that the proposed formulation achieves a better trade-off between various conflicting objective functions compared to other state-of-the-art MOEAs.Keywords: Economic dispatch, Multi-objective evolutionary algorithms, Renewable energy resources, Fixed reserve, Voltage stability index

Introduction

Motivation

The Economic-Environmental Power Dispatch (EEPD) problem, a well-studied bi-objective nonlinear optimization challenge in power systems, typically prioritizes the economic dispatch of thermal generators, often neglecting network security constraints. However, environmental sustainability necessitates reducing emissions and increasing the penetration of renewable energy sources (RES) into the electrical grid. In the literature, the main objective of EEPD problem is to minimize cost of active power generation. It runs several single-period EEPD sub-problems over the finite time without considering the entire time horizon, i.e., the final solutional is poor compared to formulating the entire time horizon solution in a single simulation, and it is proved in the [1,2]. Formulating the entire time horizon requires attention to intertemporal constraints such as fixed zonal reserve, thermal generators ramp up and down, and security constraints such as bus voltage and MVA line flow limit to guarantee a feasible solution.

On the other hand, the integration of uncertain renewable generation such as wind and solar PV power further complex the economic objective function such as formulation of uncertain Costs that are comprised of over and under-estimation costs [3,4]. The integration of high levels of RES, such as wind and solar PV, introduces stability issues due to their uncertain and intermittent nature [5]. Power systems frequently function near the stability threshold because of the unpredictability of renewable energy sources, load variations, and rising load demand [6]. Numerous studies in the literature have shown that an operating point with a high voltage level can fall within the voltage instability region [5]. For instance, the counterexample in Ref. [7] illustrates that voltage instability can occur before the voltage limit is reached. From this viewpoint, integrating the voltage stability constraint into the EEPD model, termed stable-environmental-economic power dispatch (SEEPD) problem that is crucial for the operation of power systems.

Literature review and contribution

Recently, numerous studies have explored the single-objective and single-period Economic Dispatch (ED) problem using evolutionary algorithms [[8], [9], [10], [11], [12]]. The studies in Refs. [[13], [14], [15]] transformed the multi-objective EEPD problem into a single-objective one, thereby failing to utilize the full set of non-dominated solutions for dispatch decision-making. Authors in Refs. [[16], [17], [18]] obtain a set of Pareto optimal solutions using the same model for wind energy as in previous studies. However, the single-period ED fails to consider previous and future system states, as well as the influence of ramp limits on dispatches. Consequently, extending to a multiperiod EEPD emerges as a practical solution. To account for the realistic operation of power systems, the security constraints and the uncertainty of renewables have been introduced into the MP-OPF problems, termed stochastic multiple-period security-constrained EEPD problems. Previous works have developed the challenges of the complexity of computation in solving multiple-period security-constrained EEPD problems [5,19]. The inherent variability and uncertainty associated with renewable generation necessitate a reevaluation of traditional dispatch strategies to accommodate the transition toward a more sustainable and resilient energy landscape [20]. Therefore, recently, it has been encouraged to increase the integration of uncertain wind and solar PV resources. In most of the reviewer papers, economic dispatch focuses on optimizing the generation schedule of thermal generators to meet the demand while minimizing operational costs. However, the integration of intermittent renewable sources introduces complexities arising from their inherent variability and uncertainty [4]. Wind and solar PV generation, influenced by weather patterns, can fluctuate rapidly, posing challenges in maintaining system reliability and economic efficiency [21]. Recently, thermal ramp-up and down constraints with the integration of combined heat and power (CHP) problem solved has been implemented in Ref. [22].

Reserve power, particularly from thermal generators, is indispensable in contingency analysis within power systems, especially considering the uncertainty inherent in renewable energy generation [23,24]. Contingency analysis is the process of evaluating the stability and reliability of an electrical grid under various potential disturbances, such as the unexpected loss of a major power line or generation unit [25,26]. The role of reserve power in such scenarios is multifaceted. First and foremost, reserve power provides a crucial safety net against unforeseen events that can cause sudden drops in power supply, including the variability and unpredictability of renewable energy sources [27]. For instance, when solar or wind generation falls short due to weather changes, the reserve power from thermal generators can be swiftly mobilized to fill the gap, ensuring continuous power supply and preventing potential blackouts [[28], [29], [30]]. Moreover, the inherent flexibility and reliability of thermal power plants are invaluable in maintaining grid stability during contingencies [31,32]. Unlike renewables, which are dependent on environmental conditions, thermal generators can maintain a consistent and controllable power output regardless of external factors. This consistent supply is critical for managing emergencies and unexpected fluctuations in the power grid caused by the variability of renewable sources. Additionally, reserve power is essential for accommodating the uncertainties associated with renewable energy generation in contingency planning [33]. It offers a quantifiable buffer that grid operators can rely on, enabling them to make informed decisions and take proactive measures to mitigate the impact of potential power system failures, including those stemming from the intermittency of renewables.

Furthermore, the integration of reserve power is crucial for maintaining frequency and voltage stability of the grid during contingency events. These parameters can be particularly volatile when renewable generation is uncertain. Thermal generators can adjust their output quickly to help stabilize these fluctuations, ensuring the safety and operational integrity of the grid and connected equipment. The inclusion of reserve power, especially from thermal generators, is a key element in contingency analysis for electrical grids [25]. It not only ensures preparedness for unexpected events but also addresses the challenges posed by the uncertainty and variability of renewable energy sources [34]. This capability is essential for maintaining the stability, reliability, and security of power supplies in modern energy systems, where renewables play an increasingly significant role. In Refs. [35,36], Zonal reserve costs relate to the expenses involved in maintaining a specified reserve capacity within particular regions. In contrast, over/underestimation costs address the inaccuracies in forecasting renewable generation, which is crucial for maintaining grid reliability and managing the economic impacts of variable renewable energy. Despite existing research, there remains a significant gap in comprehensively addressing the challenges of probabilistic, multiperiod, uncertain wind and solar PV generation. This research seeks to bridge this gap by proposing an advanced formulation for the Stable Environmental Economic Power Dispatch (SEEPD) problem, which specifically accounts for these uncertainties. The main aim is to develop a robust and adaptive economic dispatch model that can effectively manage the uncertainties in wind and solar PV generation.

The proposed SEEPD model incorporates fixed zonal reserve costs and capacities for thermal generators and includes costs associated with over and underestimation in renewable outputs. Furthermore, this paper aims to optimize generation schedules to simultaneously minimize operational costs, reduce emissions, and enhance voltage stability. The approach extends to multiple periods, replicating single-period problems across each interval within the planning horizon, and links these periods through intertemporal ramping costs, physical ramp rates, and fixed zonal reserve constraints. To tackle this complex, nonlinear problem, Multi-Objective Evolutionary Algorithms (MOEAs) are utilized, leveraging the latest advancements to handle stochastic wind and solar PV power sources. The model also considers network security constraints, such as transmission line capacities and bus voltage limits, alongside generator capabilities and intertemporal spinning reserve constraints. A bidirectional coevolutionary-based multi-objective evolutionary algorithm is employed, integrating sophisticated constraint-handling techniques to ensure system compliance. The simulation results demonstrate that the proposed formulation offers a more effective trade-off among various conflicting objectives compared to other advanced MOEAs, underscoring its potential to enhance the operational efficiency and reliability of power systems in the face of increasing renewable integration. Modeling of stochastic renewable generation and load demand

In this section, we present the methodology for modeling the stochastic nature of renewable energy generation and load demand. The inherent variability and uncertainty associated with wind and solar power generation are captured using probabilistic methods. Specifically, we employ Gaussian, Weibull, and log-normal probability density functions (PDFs) to model load demand, wind speed, and solar irradiance, respectively. These stochastic models are integral to accurately representing the fluctuating power outputs and ensuring reliable economic dispatch in the presence of renewable energy sources.

Discussion

The results from the proposed stable environmental economic power dispatch (SEEPD) model demonstrate the efficacy of optimizing power dispatch in a multi-period environment with uncertainties from wind and solar PV generation. By comparing the proposed algorithm with several state-of-the-art MOEAs, it is evident that the proposed method consistently outperforms others in achieving better objective function values, particularly for cost, emission rates, and the Voltage Stability Index (VSI).

In Case 1, the proposed method achieved lower operational costs and emission rates, showing its superiority in balancing these conflicting objectives compared to other MOEAs. However, the VSI and active power loss in this case were slightly better in other algorithms such as TSTI and IMTCMO. Case 2 highlights the proposed method's ability to optimize cost and VSI simultaneously, producing superior results in terms of cost reduction ($17,427/h), but resulting in a higher emission rate (10.775 t/h), which was expected as emission was not an objective function in this case. Lastly, in Case 3, which involved optimizing all three objectives—cost, emission, and VSI—the proposed method demonstrated its ability to provide a balanced and efficient solution across all criteria, outperforming other algorithms in terms of cost and power loss minimization.

The statistical comparison of the Hypervolume Indicator (HVI) further validated the superior performance of the proposed method. It consistently achieved higher HVI values across all study cases, indicating better convergence and diversity in the Pareto front. Despite certain challenges such as managing emission rates in Case 2, the results clearly show that by simultaneously considering multiple objectives, particularly in Case 3, a more balanced and efficient solution can be achieved. Additionally, the results show that the integration of renewable energy sources, while introducing uncertainties, can be effectively managed through robust optimization algorithms that account for both operational constraints and forecast errors. The ramp-up and ramp-down constraints of thermal generators were well adhered to by the proposed method, ensuring grid stability while accommodating the variability of wind and solar power. The findings of this study underscore the importance of using advanced multi-objective optimization methods to tackle complex, real-world problems such as the SEEPD. The proposed method not only ensures optimal economic and environmental performance but also maintains system reliability, making it a valuable approach for modern power systems integrating renewable energy sources.

Conclusion

Recently, the need for environmental sustainability has shifted the focus of power systems towards reducing emissions and increasing the integration of renewable energy sources (RES) such as wind and solar PV. Therefore, this paper addresses a new formulation involving the Economic Environmental and Stable Power Dispatch (SEEPD) problem, which incorporates fixed zonal reserve capacity from thermal generators and uncertain reserves from RES. This study models uncertainties in RES and load demand using random variable generation techniques with Gaussian, Weibull, and log-normal probability density functions (PDFs). The stochastic SEEPD problem extends to multiple periods, linking them through intertemporal ramping costs, physical ramp rate, and spinning reserve constraints on dispatch variables. Multi-Objective Evolutionary Algorithms (MOEAs) have gained prominence for solving complex nonlinear problems involving multi-objective functions. Thus, this study employs an improved bidirectional coevolutionary-based multi-objective evolutionary algorithm with advanced constraint handling to solve uncertain SEEPD problems. Simulation results are summarized and compared, demonstrating that the proposed algorithm excels in emission rates, cost efficiency, and voltage stability index (VSI) compared to other state-of-the-art MOEAs. The paper concludes that the inclusion of reserve power, particularly from thermal generators, is crucial for contingency analysis in electrical grids, addressing the challenges posed by the uncertainty and variability of renewable energy sources. This capability is essential for maintaining the stability, reliability, and security of power supplies in modern power systems. The results highlight that the proposed algorithm consistently outperforms other MOEAs in terms of power loss, voltage deviation, and cost efficiency, thanks to its well-converged Pareto front. The study demonstrates the feasibility and benefits of integrating renewable energy sources while ensuring grid stability and economic efficiency through advanced optimization techniques. The advanced economic, environmental, and stability model we developed demonstrates how integrating uncertainties can lead to more robust and adaptive economic dispatch strategies. This approach not only minimizes total operating costs and reduces emissions but also improves voltage stability, making power systems more resilient and efficient.

Limitations of proposed method and recommendation for future work

While comprehensive and robust, the proposed SEEPD model has limitations that provide opportunities for further research. One major limitation is the slow convergence rate due to the problems complexity and scale. Future studies could explore decomposition-based techniques or more advanced constraint-handling methods to improve convergence and find global optimal solutions. Additionally, incorporating other renewable energy sources such as hydroelectric, geothermal, and tidal energy would offer a more comprehensive analysis of renewable energy integration. Integrating demand response strategies could provide more flexible and responsive power dispatch solutions, enhancing overall efficiency and reliability. Advancements in predictive algorithms using machine learning and artificial intelligence could improve the accuracy of renewable output forecasts, further enhancing the SEEPD models performance. Methodological advancements, including parallel computing and high-performance computing, could address the computational complexity and scalability of the model, ensuring its effectiveness in real-world power systems. Lastly, integrating energy storage devices such as batteries and pumped hydro storage could mitigate the uncertainties of renewable energy generation, enhancing the stability and reliability of power dispatch solutions. Addressing these limitations and exploring these recommendations can lead to more robust, efficient, and comprehensive solutions for integrating renewable energy sources into power systems.

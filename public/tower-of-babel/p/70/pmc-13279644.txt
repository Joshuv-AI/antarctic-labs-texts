Medial-to-lateral approach for laparoscopic right hepatectomy of the remnant liver: a safe technique for patients with a history of posterior segment resection (with video)
Authors: Hidetoshi Gon, Tadahiro Goto, Michihiko Yoshida, Yuki Azumi, Kae Katsumoto, Ryuju Kawashima, Yu Kitamura, Shunji Nakayama, Daisuke Kuroda, Yonson Ku
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractLaparoscopic repeat hepatectomy (LRH) remains challenging when initial resection sites overlap with target areas. We report a safe surgical approach for LRH of the remnant right liver after prior posterior segment resection. An 83-year-old female with recurrent intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma in segment 5, following segmentectomy 6, required right hepatectomy. Due to dense adhesions in the posterior segment making right lobe mobilization, including dissection around the inferior vena cava (IVC), difficult, we employed a medial-to-lateral approach for LRH. We initially performed parenchymal transection to expose the ventral side of the IVC, followed by a medial-to-lateral dissection around the IVC under a favorable operative field. Operative time was 469 minutes with 160 mL blood loss. The patient was discharged on postoperative day 17 without complications. In LRH after posterior segment resection, a medial-to-lateral approach using the parenchymal transection-first technique is effective for minimizing risks of collateral injury during right hepatectomy.Keywords: Hepatectomy, Laparoscopy, Surgical techniques

INTRODUCTION

Laparoscopic repeat hepatectomy (LRH) has been shown to be effective [1,2]. However, cases in which the initial resection site overlaps with the target area for repeat resection pose significant technical challenges [3,4], requiring surgical approaches individually tailored to the anatomical situation. Among these cases, LRH after resection of the posterior segments or caudate lobe is particularly demanding due to severe adhesions around the inferior vena cava (IVC). Direct dissection in this setting carries a high risk of injury to the IVC or the right adrenal gland because the combination of dense adhesions and post-resection liver deformation makes accurate identification of the dissection plane extremely difficult, thereby necessitating special consideration. Herein, we report a safe surgical technique applying a medial-to-lateral approach for LRH of the remnant right liver in a patient who had previously undergone resection of the posterior segment.

CASE

The patient was an 83-year-old woman with a history of laparoscopic segmentectomy 6 for intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma. Preoperative computed tomography revealed a 2-cm recurrent tumor in segment 5, located at the bifurcation of the anterior and posterior Glissonean pedicles (Supplementary Video). Liver function tests indicated Child-Pugh A classification, and the indocyanine green (ICG) retention rate at 15 minutes was 10.3%. The carcinoembryonic antigen and carbohydrate antigen 19-9 levels were 3.8 ng/mL and 2.0 U/mL, respectively. The operative plan was an LRH of the remnant right liver. The remnant liver volume after right hepatectomy was 616 mL, representing 55% of the total liver volume.

A schematic overview of the surgical procedure is shown in Supplementary Video. In laparoscopic right hepatectomy following posterior segment resection, dense adhesions from the initial surgery often make complete mobilization of the right lobe difficult, carrying a risk of injury to the right adrenal gland and IVC. To safely perform laparoscopic dissection in this challenging area, a surgical technique based on the anterior approach is applied, prioritizing parenchymal transection [5]. Following exposure of the ventral side of the IVC, dissection is performed from the medial to the right lateral side of the IVC. This medial-to-lateral approach ensures that the adhesions between the previous resection site and surrounding tissues, including the right adrenal gland, are completely lysed under favorable visualization, thereby establishing a safe operative field.

The actual surgical procedure of LRH of the remnant right liver is presented in Supplementary Video. Strong adhesions were observed between the segment 6 resection site of the liver and surrounding tissues, including the right adrenal gland. Lateral-to-medial dissection was deemed difficult owing to an unclear dissection plane, posing a high risk of injury to the right adrenal gland and IVC. To ensure surgical safety in the presence of dense adhesions, we adopted a medial-to-lateral strategy, initiated by parenchymal transection. After preparing for the Pringle maneuver, vascular management of the hepatic hilum was performed. The right hepatic artery and right portal vein were divided and ligated, respectively. Parenchymal transection was performed along the demarcation line using the clamp-crushing technique. After transecting the ventral area of the liver and isolating the right hilar plate, the right portal vein branch was divided. Subsequently, the hilar plate containing the right hepatic duct was divided using a stapler. After controlling the inflow of the right lobe, parenchymal transection was performed along the ventral side of the IVC. On the cranial side, we secured the liver parenchyma containing the right hepatic vein, which was then divided using a stapler. We performed a medial-to-lateral dissection along the right lateral side of the IVC, including the right adrenal gland, under a safe operative field and successfully removed the specimen. The operative time was 469 minutes, with a blood loss volume of 160 mL. The postoperative course was uneventful, and the patient was discharged on postoperative day 17. Pathological examination of the resected specimen revealed intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma, characterized by moderately to poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma. The surgical resection margin was negative, with a margin width of 10 mm, achieving an R0 resection.

DISCUSSION

We report a safe surgical approach for laparoscopic right hepatectomy after resection of the posterior segment of the liver. We successfully performed LRH of the remnant right liver by applying the medial-to-lateral approach in a case where the conventional mobilization-first approach was considered difficult.

The conventional approach, which involves mobilizing the liver before parenchymal transection, is regarded as the standard method for right hepatectomy [6]. However, the anterior approach, which prioritizes inflow control and parenchymal transection before mobilization, is justifiable depending on the tumor conditions [7]. In the present case, the conventional approach was deemed infeasible for repeat hepatectomy, not due to the tumor condition, but because of severe adhesions and liver deformation resulting from the initial hepatectomy. Therefore, we performed LRH of the remnant right liver by prioritizing parenchymal transection first. This technique enables adequate visualization of the IVC course, allowing for safe lysis of adhesions on the right side of the IVC in a medial-to-lateral direction under a favorable operative field. Furthermore, because the right adrenal gland can be identified from the medial side, the risk of injury to this structure is considered minimal. Additionally, when a tumor is located adjacent to the prior transection site, as in the present case, the conventional approach involving prior right hepatic mobilization carries a risk of violating the previous resection plane due to misidentification of the dissection layer, which could subsequently compromise oncological safety. Conversely, adopting a medial-to-lateral approach allows for precise identification of the correct dissection plane, ensuring safe surgical execution with a secured oncological margin. Therefore, this approach is also considered an effective strategy from the perspective of oncologic principles. One limitation of the present technique is the omission of the hanging maneuver [8]. While this raises concerns regarding the sufficiency of outflow control, the use of a laparoscopic approach allows for steady control of venous bleeding to an acceptable level, thus making this permissible. Furthermore, the magnified view provided by laparoscopic manipulation facilitates the pursuit of the avascular area on the ventral aspect of the IVC, suggesting the superiority of the laparoscopic technique over open surgery in deep dissection.

In the present case, right hepatectomy was successfully performed without any major complications by advancing parenchymal transection along the plane connecting the ischemic demarcation line on the liver surface, the hepatic hilum, and the IVC, while referencing the intrahepatic course of the middle hepatic vein. However, repeat hepatectomy cases often involve anatomical distortion of the liver. Because the degree of this deformity varies widely depending on the nature of the primary resection, meticulous care must be taken when determining the precise transection line in each individual case. Recently, it was reported that the use of ICG fluorescence imaging during parenchymal transection enables more precise resection by recognizing intrahepatic ischemic boundaries, which may lead to the prevention of postoperative complications in primary hepatectomy cases [9]. Especially in repeat hepatectomy cases involving anatomical distortion, ICG fluorescence imaging may serve as an effective tool for achieving accurate transection; therefore, further studies are warranted to validate its utility in this specific clinical context.

In conclusion, the application of the medial-to-lateral approach is a useful method in LRH for right hepatectomy cases with a history of posterior segment resection, as it minimizes the risk of severe collateral injury. By refining surgical techniques, it is possible to expand the indication for minimally invasive surgery in the field of major repeat hepatectomy, which is considered beneficial for patients.

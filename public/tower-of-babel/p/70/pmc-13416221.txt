Correction for Buche et al., Trait-mediated interactions drive local diversity
Published: 2026

Correction for “Trait-mediated interactions drive local diversity,” by Lisa Buche, Oscar Godoy, Lauren G. Shoemaker, Peter Vesk, Lauren M. Hallett, Courtney G. Taylor, Wing Man Siu, Manuel Sevenello, and Margaret M. Mayfield, which published May 27, 2026; 10.1073/pnas.2600749123 (Proc. Natl. Acad. Sci. U.S.A. 123, e2600749123).

The authors note that, due to a production error, locants for subpanels of Fig. 3 appeared incorrectly. The text is corrected as follows.

The locants for Fig. 3 in the sixth paragraph of the “Results and Discussion” section should appear as “In Spain, focal species with conservative resource-use strategies, such as low SLA and high WUE, experience greater facilitation, as also previously noted by Daniel et al. (25) in another system of central European perennial grasslands (Fig. 3 A and B). In contrast, in Australia, annual plants with high SLA and low WUE (Fig. 3 A and B), reflecting higher rates of water loss through leaves, benefit from neighbors that increase moisture, provide shade, or are less water-efficient (41). In Australia, neighbors with higher WUE facilitate more effectively, likely through reducing competition for water (Fig. 3H). This pattern of stronger interspecific facilitation in conservative resource-use neighborhoods (low SLA, high WUE) is consistent across the Australian and Spanish communities (Fig. 3 G and H).”

The locants for Fig. 3 in the Fig. 3 legend should appear as, “Flowering, leaf, and root traits of the focal species (“Focal trait,” i.e., species impacted by interactions, panels A−F), the neighbor (“Neighbor trait”, panels G−L), and their difference (“Focal trait–Neighbor trait”, panels M−R) influence the per-capita heterospecific interactions at low neighbor densities. Plots display the distribution of the estimated effect sizes of traits on heterospecific interaction strengths (fαij). The color gradient indicates the direction of the effect, that is, whether the trait value is proportional or not to the strength of facilitation. For instance, for subpanel C, focal floral width is inversely proportional to facilitation as each unit of floral width on average decreases the intrinsic fitness of the focal by e−0.02 ∼ 2%. That is, a focal with low floral width tends to receive stronger facilitation. Similarly, panel I, shows that the neighbor floral width is inversely proportional to facilitation, thus neighbors with small flowers (small floral width) emit stronger facilitation. However, the difference of traits (subpanel O), does not show a relationship with facilitation, with distributions centered on 0. Yellow boxes indicate if the posterior distributions between Spain and Australia overlap less than 50% (based on a nonparametric bootstrap estimate of the overlapping area). We indicate that the distribution shows a dominant direction with a side marker such that < 0.1 ∼ • and < 0.05 ∼ #. The intercept values for the models are shown in SI Appendix, Table S12. We present here a subset of all examined traits (SI Appendix, Fig. S13). The subset we chose to display here highlights traits common to both countries and noncorrelated traits (see SI Appendix, Figs. S4 and S7 for trait correlations).”

The locant for Fig. 3 in the eighth paragraph of the “Results and Discussion” section should appear as “Trends in species with conservative strategies benefiting from stronger facilitative interactions were observed for focal species with slower resource-capture root traits [characterized by high RTD and low specific root length (SRL)] in Spain but not Australia (Fig. 3 E and F).”

Additionally, the authors note that the Acknowledgments appeared incorrectly. The grant numbers attributed to Lauren Shoemaker had been wrongly transcribed in the acknowledgment. The sentence reads, “L.G.S. was supported by NSF Grants 2-33292 and 2019528.” It should instead read, “L.G.S. was supported by NSF Grants 2033292 and 2019528.”

The published paper has been updated.

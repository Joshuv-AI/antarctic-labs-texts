Longitudinal Trajectory of Multimodal Markers in a Patient with Isolated REM Sleep Behavior Disorder
Authors: Seohee Choi, Ryul Kim, Kyung Ah Woo, Jung Hwan Shin, Eun Jin Yoon, Heejung Kim, Yu Kyeong Kim, Jee‐Young Lee
Published: 2025

Isolated rapid eye movement sleep behavior disorder (iRBD) is a prodromal manifestation of α‐synucleinopathies. 1 Neurogenic orthostatic hypotension (nOH) in iRBD is linked to a higher risk of phenoconversion. 2 We describe a patient with a 20‐year history of iRBD who developed nOH highlighted by multimodal imaging markers to provide a novel insight into the progression of prodromal synucleinopathy.

A 64‐year‐old man presented to our clinic in 2013 with a 9‐year history of iRBD confirmed by videopolysomnography. At baseline, neurological examination was normal, and nighttime clonazepam controlled his RBD symptoms well. His condition remained stable until 2016, when the patient felt episodic orthostatic dizziness. In 2018, the orthostatic symptoms worsened, but he showed no objective blood pressure (BP) drop on the orthostatic test. He exhibited no definite motor and cognitive progression at this time. In 2021, the patient reported further worsening orthostatic symptoms with significant BP drops on orthostatic test (Fig. 1A). In 2023, he developed faintness and syncope with dramatically aggravated RBD symptoms. On examination, he showed mild parkinsonian signs and amnestic multi‐domain mild cognitive impairment (Fig. 1, Table S1). Brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) in 2013 revealed no overt structural abnormalities, however, volumetry of serial MRIs demonstrated progressive atrophy in the basal forebrain over an 11‐year follow‐up (Fig. 1B). 3 Striatal dopamine transporter (DAT) uptake measured by 18F‐FP‐CIT positron emission tomography (PET) was near the normal values in 2013 (Fig. 1C). 4 In 2017, both caudate and putaminal DAT were mildly reduced, but above the range of denovo Parkinson's disease (PD) patients. 4 18F‐FDG‐PET revealed no elevations of denovoPD RBD‐related pattern (denovoPDRBDRP) and PD‐related pattern (PDRP) expressions in 2013. At serial scans, there was a rise above the cutoff in 2022 following the development of objective nOH, and a further elevation in 2025 (Fig. 1D). 5 The amyloid PET in 2022 was negative. 6 123I‐metaiodobenzylguanidine cardiac scintigraphy in 2023 showed a reduced heart‐to‐mediastinum ratio (1.63).

Longitudinal imaging in this patient revealed that progressive atrophy of the central cholinergic nucleus was the earliest marker, followed by a decline in striatal DAT, and then an increase in PD‐related pattern expressions. These three markers showed changes toward the reference values of established PD and dementia with Lewy bodies in our center, suggesting ongoing neurodegeneration. Together with the changes in multimodal markers, the appearance of nOH was likely to be a marker of an impending phenoconversion into Lewy body diseases (LBD). Interestingly, the first clinical sign in this patient was orthostatic dizziness. Subtle cognitive changes by neuropsychological testing became obvious after objective orthostatic BP changes, and mild parkinsonian signs eventually developed at the latest follow‐up. Meanwhile cholinergic and dopaminergic markers have shown progressive degeneration 10 and 6 years before clinical signs. The threshold for the autonomic system against pathological burden may be lower than that of central regions. Alternatively, extra‐CNS pathology might be involved earlier than the Central Nervous system (CNS) in subtype LBD. 7 Finally, resilience/reserve might differ across brain regions even in the same individual. Serial multimodal imaging studies for both the CNS and extra‐CNS system in iRBD could provide further insights into the progression of LBD.

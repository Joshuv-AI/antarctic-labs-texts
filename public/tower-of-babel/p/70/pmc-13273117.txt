Adding induction intrathecal cytarabine in newly diagnosed CNS2 B-acute lymphoblastic leukemia does not improve outcomes∗
Authors: Cathy A. Lee-Miller, John A. Kairalla, Emily Hibbitts, Naomi J. Winick, Karen R. Rabin, Anne Angiolillo, Reuven J. Schore, Wanda L. Salzer, Michael J. Burke, Mignon L. Loh, Elizabeth A. Raetz, Stephen P. Hunger, Meenakshi Devidas, David T. Teachey, Sumit Gupta, Jennifer L. McNeer
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Key Points•The addition of intrathecal cytarabine in induction does not mitigate the adverse prognostic impact of CNS2 status in children with B-ALL.•Worse outcomes for patients with CNS2 B-ALL are largely due to increased risk for isolated CNS relapses; new strategies must be sought.

Introduction

Survival for children with B-acute lymphoblastic leukemia (B-ALL) has improved dramatically over decades through risk stratification to guide treatment. In addition, recognition of the central nervous system (CNS) as a sanctuary site in the 1970s, with subsequent introduction of intrathecal (IT) and radiation therapy, has been responsible for major improvements in survival.1 Patients with CNS involvement at the time of diagnosis of leukemia have been observed to have higher relapse rates.2,3 CNS involvement can be categorized in 3 ways: CNS1, indicating no CNS involvement; CNS2, indicating low levels of CNS involvement or, in some cases, traumatic lumbar puncture; or CNS3, indicating overt CNS involvement either by cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) findings, physical examination, or imaging findings. Outcomes for patients with CNS involvement at diagnosis are generally worse than for those without, particularly in patients with CNS3 disease, for whom all groups intensify therapy.2 The treatment approach for patients with CNS2 status has varied among treatment consortia, with most historically intensifying therapy by adding 2 to 4 extra doses of IT therapy during the first phase of treatment (induction).4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10

The Children’s Oncology Group (COG) had not added extra IT treatments during induction for patients with CNS2 status until an analysis of B-ALL trials by Winick et al2 demonstrated inferior event-free (EFS) and overall survival (OS) for these patients, with events largely driven by CNS-involved relapses. In 2016, COG modified the approach to this population through amendments to the ongoing frontline B-ALL studies AALL0932 and AALL1131. Additional doses of IT cytarabine were added to induction therapy for patients with CNS2 status until 3 sequential CSF samples were clear of blasts. Here, we report the outcomes of patients with CNS2 disease treated before and after these protocol amendments.

Methods

Patients

Between 2010 and 2019, patients with noninfant, BCR::ABL1-negative, newly diagnosed B-ALL were eligible for 2 randomized phase 3 COG trials (AALL0932 and AALL1131). Patients on AALL0932 received a 3-drug induction and are referred to throughout as the standard-risk (SR) cohort, regardless of postinduction therapy intensity. Patients on AALL1131 received a 4-drug induction and are referred to throughout as the high-risk (HR) cohort. The SR cohort included patients with National Cancer Institute (NCI) SR B-ALL (age at diagnosis of >365 days and <10 years, and initial white blood cell count [WBC] of <50 × 103/μL) without additional HR features at diagnosis: steroid pretreatment, CNS3 disease (see definition hereafter), or testicular leukemia.11 Some patients in the SR cohort were found to have HR features and were therefore taken off protocol after receiving a 3-drug induction on AALL0932. They went on to receive intensified postinduction therapy but remained in the SR cohort for the purposes of this analysis. Those HR features included HR cytogenetics (internal amplification of chromosome 21, KMT2A-rearrangement, hypodiploidy with <44 chromosomes, and/or DNA index of <0.81), or poor response to treatment, defined as bone marrow measurable residual disease (MRD) of ≥0.01% at the end of induction (EOI).

The HR cohort included those with NCI HR B-ALL (age ≥10 to <31 years and/or initial WBC of ≥50 × 103/μL), as well as patients with NCI SR disease who received steroid pretreatment, had testicular disease, or were CNS3 at the time of diagnosis. All of these patients received a 4-drug induction on AALL1131.12 Refer to the supplemental Table 1 for more details.

Institutional review board approval was obtained at participating institutions before patient enrollment. Written informed consent and assent (as appropriate) were obtained from parents/guardians and patients before starting therapy.

CNS status at diagnosis

In both trials, CNS status was determined by diagnostic lumbar puncture performed before the administration of any systemic or IT chemotherapy (other than steroid pretreatment, as defined by the AALL0932 and AALL1131 studies11,12), by clinical symptoms, or by radiographic findings. CNS1 status was defined as the absence of blasts on cytospin preparation of CSF, regardless of the number of WBCs. CNS2 was defined as the presence of <10/μL of red blood cells (RBCs), <5/μL WBCs, and cytospin-positive for blasts (CNS2a); or ≥10/μL RBCs, <5/μL WBCs, and cytospin-positive for blasts (CNS2b); or ≥10/μL RBCs, ≥5/μL WBCs, cytospin-positive for blasts but negative by Steinherz/Bleyer algorithm (CNS2c), which corrects for excess peripheral blood contamination of CSF. CNS3 was defined as either <10/μL RBCs, ≥5/μL WBCs, and cytospin-positive for blasts (CNS3a); or ≥10/μL RBCs, ≥ 5/μL WBCs, and cytospin-positive for blasts and positive by Steinherz/Bleyer algorithm (CNS3b); or clinical signs of CNS involvement, including cranial nerve palsy, spinal cord compression, or isolated intracerebral mass (CNS3c). Note that specific CSF collection and processing techniques are not dictated by COG protocols and are largely determined by each individual treatment site, including volume of CSF collected and analyzed as well as time between collection and analysis.

Treatment and definitions

Details of AALL0932 therapy have been described previously.11 CNS-directed components of systemic therapy included oral dexamethasone (4 weeks in induction, 2 discontinuous weeks in delayed intensification, and randomization to 5-day steroid pulses either every 4 weeks or every 12 weeks during each maintenance cycle), as well as 2 doses of pegylated asparaginase (1 in induction, 1 in delayed intensification). A total of 17 IT methotrexate (MTX) treatments were administered to all girls, as well as to boys assigned to 1 of the low-risk randomized arms, whereas all other boys received 21 IT MTX treatments as part of an additional year of systemic therapy.

Treatment details for AALL1131 have also been reported elsewhere.12 Patients aged ≥10 years at diagnosis received 4 weeks of prednisone during induction, whereas patients aged <10 years at diagnosis received 2 weeks of dexamethasone. All patients received 2 discontinuous weeks of dexamethasone in delayed intensification and 5-day pulses of prednisone every 4 weeks during each maintenance cycle. Additional CNS-directed systemic therapy included 5 doses of pegylated asparaginase for patients with HR disease (1 in induction, 2 in consolidation, and 2 in delayed intensification) and 7 doses for patients with very high risk disease (as given for patients with HR disease plus 2 additional doses in the second interim maintenance), as well as 4 doses of high-dose MTX (5 g/m2) for all patients. There were a total of 22 doses of IT MTX for girls and 26 for boys. Patients in the HR arm were randomized to receive either IT MTX or triple IT therapy (ITT; cytarabine/hydrocortisone/MTX).12 Patients with CNS3 disease received 1800 cGy cranial irradiation in 10 fractions during the first 4 weeks of maintenance therapy.

Both AALL0932 and AALL1131 were amended in 2016 to intensify IT therapy in induction for patients with CNS2 disease (supplemental Figure 1). After the amendment, enrolled patients received additional IT cytarabine during induction until 3 consecutive CSF samples were negative for blasts. IT MTX was given to all patients on induction days 8 and 29. IT cytarabine was administered for the induction day 1 lumbar puncture and then twice weekly at a lower dose, except on day 8 (when IT MTX was given). Thus, after amendment, patients with CNS2 status received 2 to 6 additional IT treatments compared with those with CNS1 disease.

End points

Primary end points were EFS and OS. EFS was defined as the time from enrollment to first event (defined as induction failure, relapse, death, or second malignant neoplasm), or last contact for those who were event-free. OS was defined as the time from enrollment to death or last contact, if alive. Additional end points included cumulative incidence of relapse, overall, and by site. Bone marrow relapse was defined as an M3 marrow with >25% lymphoblasts any time after complete remission was attained. CNS relapse was defined as positive CSF cytomorphology and ≥5/μL CSF WBCs or clinical signs of CNS leukemia.

Covariates

Covariates included age, presenting WBC count, leukemia cytogenetics, race/ethnicity, and EOI MRD. Cytogenetic subgroup was defined as favorable (double trisomies of chromosomes 4 and 10, or ETV6::RUNX1), unfavorable (as described earlier), or neutral (the absence of either favorable or unfavorable cytogenetics). Age was categorized as <10 years at time of diagnosis vs ≥10 years. Presenting WBC was categorized as <50 × 103/μL vs ≥50 × 103/μL at time of diagnosis. Self-described race/ethnicity was defined as Hispanic vs non-Hispanic White vs non-Hispanic Black vs non-Hispanic Asian vs non-Hispanic other. EOI bone marrow MRD was categorized as <0.01% vs ≥0.01%.

Study design and statistical analysis

The primary analysis compared EFS and OS between patients with CNS2 status enrolled before compared to after the CNS2 amendments. Due to differences in the timing and duration of temporary pauses in accrual between the 2 studies, the proportion of patients with CNS2 disease enrolled on AALL0932 and AALL1131, before and after the studies were amended, varied substantially (Figure 1). Because these proportions were not equivalent, all analyses were stratified according to whether the patient received a 3-drug induction on AALL0932 (ie, SR cohort) or a 4-drug induction on AALL1131 (ie, HR cohort) so that unbalanced cohorts did not affect outcomes. Survival rates were estimated by the Kaplan-Meier method, with 95% confidence intervals (CI) calculated using standard errors of Peto et al,13 and were compared with the log-rank test. In secondary analyses, cumulative incidence rates of relapse were determined using cumulative incidence functions and compared using the Fine-Gray test.14 Competing risks included induction failure, other relapse types, secondary malignant neoplasms, and remission death. Comparison of proportions between groups used the Fisher exact test, and comparison of continuous variables used the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. A P value of < .05 was considered statistically significant. All analyses were performed with SAS software (version 9.4; SAS Institute, Cary, NC). Results

Of these, 9258 (65.7%) formed the SR cohort, having received a 3-drug induction on AALL0932, and 4835 (34.3%) formed the HR cohort, having received a 4-drug induction on AALL1131. The distribution of CNS status among patients was: CNS1, 12 368 (87.8%); CNS2, 1543 (10.9%); and CNS3, 182 (1.3%). CNS2 disease was present in 742 (8.0%) patients with SR disease and 801 (16.6%) patients with HR disease. Among these patients with CNS2 disease, 1023 were treated before relevant amendments (535 SR, 488 HR) and 520 were treated after these amendments (207 SR, 313 HR; Figure 1). Outcomes for patients with CNS2 status did not improve with additional IT cytarabine doses during induction. No difference in EFS, OS, or cumulative incidence of any type of relapse was noted within either the SR or HR cohorts (Figure 2; Table 2; supplemental Figures 2 and 3). When adjusted for covariates, intensification of induction IT treatment was not significantly associated with improved outcomes. This trend persisted among patients with CNS2 status treated after amendment whose inferior EFS continued to be driven by isolated CNS relapses. To determine whether practice changes or drug availability over the course of the studies affected outcomes in general for cohorts treated before and after 2016, we analyzed EFS rates for patients with CNS1 status over these timeframes. Outcomes for patients with CNS1 status did not significantly differ in the time before vs after amendments to these clinical trials, with SR 5-year EFS of 92.8% ± 0.3% before amendments vs 93.2% ± 0.6% afterwards (P = .78); for the HR population 5-year EFS was 79.0% ± 0.9% before these amendments compared with 78.4% ± 1.4% afterward (P = .42; supplemental Figure 4).

Discussion

Before the opening of the first generation of COG trials for patients with B-ALL, an analysis of clinical, biological, and early-response data from >6000 patients enrolled in legacy Pediatric Oncology Group and Children’s Cancer Group trials demonstrated an increased risk of relapse for patients with CNS2 status.3 However, risk classification in the legacy trials did not include MRD or many contemporary prognostic cytogenetic features. Dexamethasone had not been incorporated for all patients with NCI SR status, and augmented postinduction therapies were not administered to all patients with NCI HR status. In addition, no clear approach to the treatment of patients with CNS2 disease had been established within either the Children’s Cancer Group or the Pediatric Oncology Group trials. Accordingly, the first COG trials did not alter therapy for patients with CNS2 disease. Subsequently, an analysis of the outcome of patients with CNS2 status treated on the initial COG B-ALL trials, AALL0331 and AALL0232, demonstrated an inferior outcome for patients with CNS2 disease, with the decrement driven largely by relapses that involved the CNS.2

When this disparity was identified, the COG B-ALL studies in progress were amended to deliver extra doses of IT therapy to patients with CNS2 status during induction, mirroring the approach being taken by most other trial consortia at the time. This decision was driven by the observation that in most of these consortia, additional IT therapy during induction seemed to mitigate the negative prognostic impact of CNS2 disease.4, 5, 6,8,15,16 Berlin-Frankfurt-Munster (BFM) trials included 2 additional doses of IT MTX during induction,17 St. Jude Children’s Research Hospital trials included 2 additional doses of ITT during induction with additional doses during continuation therapy,18 and Dana-Farber Cancer Institute (DFCI) consortium trials included twice weekly IT cytarabine for patients with CNS2 status.19 Intensification of therapy with twice weekly IT cytarabine was chosen by COG because of lack of adverse prognostic impact of CNS2 status in the DFCI trials and importantly, the association between IT MTX and myelosuppression that had been reported in an early pediatric trial describing excessive mortality during induction with the delivery of weekly doses of ITT.20 We report here that the addition of extra IT cytarabine doses during induction on AALL0932 and AALL1131 did not improve outcomes for patients with CNS2 status, and that CNS2 status at diagnosis continues to be an adverse prognosticator, particularly for relapses involving the CNS.

One potential contributor to the lack of efficacy is the choice of the IT agent. Although the extra IT therapy chosen on AALL0932 and AALL1131 was cytarabine, as noted earlier, other groups have added IT MTX7,9 or ITT5,6,16,21 and have reported no difference in outcome based on CNS2 status. Among other major cooperative groups, only DFCI also uses extra induction IT cytarabine for patients with CNS2 status, and in contrast to COG findings, has shown equivalent outcomes for patients with CNS1 and those with CNS2 status.6,16 It is important to note that DFCI protocols include ITT chemotherapy during postinduction therapy, as well as more intensive asparaginase; more glucocorticoid exposure; and, in older trials, cranial radiation therapy for patients at HR.19,22 All of these interventions provide additional CNS-directed therapy.

Indeed, postinduction systemic agents, their intensity, and their ability to provide CNS-directed therapy may be a second contributor to worse outcomes for patients with CNS2 status on COG B-ALL studies. For example, most other cooperative groups administer high-dose MTX to all patients, whereas in COG protocols, patients with SR disease do not receive this effective CNS agent. However, the addition of extra induction doses of IT cytarabine did not improve outcomes among those patients with CNS2 status who received intensified postinduction therapy that included high-dose MTX (Figure 2C-D; Table 2).

A third reason for continued inferior CNS2 outcomes on COG trials, in contrast to other groups, may be variability in how initial CNS status is determined between centers within COG and among cooperative groups. Within COG protocols, the amount of CSF analyzed is not mandated, thus individual laboratory practices may vary. COG protocols also do not mandate the processing of CSF within a certain amount of time. Because leukocytes in the CSF decay rapidly ex vivo,23 prolonged processing times could lead to morphological changes in cells, leading to underestimation of rates of CNS involvement. Finally, some centers may be using flow cytometry as an adjunct to morphologic analysis, although this was likely less common during the time that these protocols were open.

Notably, there are also differences between cooperative groups, potentially leading to differences in the proportion of patients categorized as having CNS2 disease. The proportion of patients considered to have CNS2 disease (including traumatic lumbar punctures) varies dramatically between consortia, ranging anywhere from 5% (United Kingdom Acute Lymphoblastic Leukaemia)9 to 53.1% in historic Dutch Childhood Oncology Group cohorts5 (supplemental Table 2). Such dramatic variation is unlikely to be due to true differences between populations. Although CNS2 definitions differ slightly between groups (eg, COG requires the presence of just 1 leukemic blast in the CSF in order to be called CNS2, whereas BFM requires ≥2 blasts7), these are unlikely to account entirely for this variability. A more likely explanation is variability in standards for CSF collection, processing, and interpretation. Centers and groups vary in terms of volume of CSF collected, transit time, and approach to processing. The use of morphology vs flow cytometry, and the experience and expertise of technicians and hematopathologists, may also influence the determination of CNS status.23,24 International standardization of CSF collection and the approach to subsequent analysis is required before differences in outcome between consortia can be confidently attributed to differences in treatment approaches.

The lack of efficacy of extra IT cytarabine observed in this study must be weighed against toxicities potentially associated with extra induction IT therapy. Many, if not most, pediatric patients are sedated for IT therapy; increased exposure to propofol has been demonstrated to be associated with adverse neurocognitive outcomes.25 Although additional IT cytarabine has not been linked to an increase in myelosuppression or infection risk, additional induction IT MTX has been associated with higher rates of life-threatening infection, leading the BFM and Dutch Childhood Oncology Group consortia to discontinue this practice among patients with CNS2 disease.7 It is unclear whether this change will lead to a decrement in CNS2 outcomes, because additional doses of IT MTX given during induction on protocols from these consortia had previously negated the prognostic impact of CNS2 disease. Should such a decrement be observed in the future, reintroduction of additional IT MTX may be justifiable when accompanied by appropriate supportive care measures.

It remains unknown how best to mitigate the inferior outcomes of patients with CNS2 disease on COG backbone therapy. Recently published results from the COG study AALL1731 demonstrated an improvement in disease-free survival associated with the addition of 2 nonsequential cycles of the immunotherapy blinatumomab. Interestingly, patients with CNS2 status seem to have benefited, despite the known lack of CNS penetration by blinatumomab. In post hoc analyses, 7 of 70 patients with CNS2 status randomized to receive chemotherapy alone experienced events, compared with only 1 of 77 patients with CNS2 status randomized to receive chemotherapy and blinatumomab.26 Even before AALL1731, CNS2 status had not been clinically significant in patients with NCI SR status who met criteria for favorable or low-risk disease based on disease biology and response to induction therapy2; AALL1731 data suggest that with the addition of blinatumomab to therapy, patients with NCI SR disease with CNS2 status may not require therapy distinct from patients with CNS1 status. In addition, among patients with NCI HR disease who were aged <10 years of age at diagnosis and treated with dexamethasone during induction and high-dose MTX during interim maintenance on AALL0232 and AALL1131, CNS2 status was not prognostic.2 Thus, additional intensification of postinduction CNS-directed agents may only affect outcomes among patients with NCI HR disease aged >10 years, but this would require investigation in future clinical trials.

The primary limitation of this study is that the intervention of additional IT cytarabine in induction was nonrandomly applied. As with any preintervention vs postintervention analysis, other concurrent but external influences may have confounded our results. For example, over the life of the 2 studies in question, global shortages of asparaginase products increased and decreased in severity. The lack of change in outcomes for patients with CNS1 status before and after amendments is reassuring that external confounders were unlikely. Another limitation is the relatively small number of patients with CNS2 status. Given these numbers, this analysis was not powered to detect modest differences in CNS2 outcomes. Thus, we cannot rule out a small benefit to the intervention.

In conclusion, we have demonstrated that the addition of extra IT cytarabine in induction on AALL0932 and AALL1131 did not improve outcomes for patients with CNS2 status. Such patients continued to experience inferior outcomes compared with patients with CNS1 status, specifically in the form of increased isolated CNS relapses. Thus, this strategy will not be continued on future COG trials. Alternative strategies involving both systemic immunotherapy and other CNS-directed treatments, as well as consideration of standardized diagnostic practices to determine initial CNS status, are required to better understand and improve outcomes for this subgroup of patients, particularly among those who have NCI HR status by age at diagnosis.

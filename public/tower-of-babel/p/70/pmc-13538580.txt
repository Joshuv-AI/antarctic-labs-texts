The first near telomere-to-telomere genome assembly of Panulirus homarus homarus
Authors: Na Zhou, Rujingwen Huan, Hanxuan Wang, Yu Huang, Jiayu Zheng, Yibo Li, Zhenhua Ma, Qibin Yang, Yongsheng Zhang, Yundong Li, Shengming Sun, Chao Bian
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe scalloped spiny lobster (Panulirus homarus homarus) is an economically important decapod crustacean with high aquaculture potential. Several chromosome-level genomes of this species have been reported. But the lobster or even entire shrimps did not have the telomere-to-telomere assembly until now. Therefore, we present the first near telomere-to-telomere genome assembly of P. h. homarus generated by using pure Oxford Nanopore Technologies ultra-long (ONT) reads and Hi-C sequencing. The final assembly anchored to 73 chromosomes with a contig N50 of 41.1 Mb. 73 chromosomes contain entire 146 telomeres, of which 51 chromosomes have no gaps. A total of 38,396 protein-coding genes were predicted. BUSCO analysis showed a completeness score of 99.8%, indicating a high degree of assembly completeness. This high-quality genomic dataset provides a valuable resource for comparative genomics, evolutionary studies, and genome-assisted breeding of spiny lobsters.

Background & Summary

The scalloped spiny lobster (Panulirus homarus homarus) belongs to the order Decapoda, family Palinuridae, genus Panulirus, and is widely distributed along the coastal regions of the Indian Ocean and the western Pacific. Owing to its rapid growth rate, stable market demand, and high commercial value, P. h. homarus is regarded as a representative lobster species with strong aquaculture potential1,2. Genomic resources are essential to crustacean aquaculture, supporting trait dissection and genome-based selective breeding3. Currently, P. h. homarus aquaculture remains heavily dependent on wild-caught broodstock2,4. Given the urgent need for artificial breeding and large-scale propagation in P. h. homarus5, comprehensive genomic resources are essential for this species.

Despite genome sequencing has been widely performed in aquatic organisms, genome assembly in decapod crustaceans remains particularly challenging. This is largely due to their generally large genome sizes and high abundance of repetitive sequences, which often lead to fragmented and incomplete assemblies3,6,7. Although chromosome-level genome assemblies have been reported for several decapod crustaceans, such as Cherax quadricarinatus8, Penaeus monodon9, Exopalaemon carinicauda10 and Procambarus clarkii11, assembly limitations in sequence continuity and completeness still persist. Their contig N50 values typically are ranging from only 0.08 to 0.49 Mb (see details in Table 1).

Within the genus Panulirus, recent studies have published chromosome-level genome assemblies for P. h. homarus12 and P. ornatus13, providing a foundation for genomic research in spiny lobsters. However, the reported P. h. homarus genome exhibits a contig N50 of 5.43 Mb and still contains 2,930 gaps, while the P. ornatus genome has a contig N50 of 5.1 Mb with 6,681 gaps (see details in Table 1), which limits their application in fine-scale structural variation and functional genomic analyses.

In this study, we generated the first near telomere-to-telomere (T2T) genome assembly of P. h. homarus using a combination of ONT ultralong read and Hi-C sequencing. The final assembly size was 3.5 Gb, with a contig N50 of 41.14 Mb and a scaffold N50 of 49.11 Mb. A total of 38,396 protein-coding genes were predicted in the genome (Table 1). This high-quality genomic dataset provides a comprehensive reference resource that will facilitate further research, such as comparative genomic analyses and genome-assisted breeding.

Methods

Sample collection

One male P. h. homarus was collected from Guangxi province, China. Fresh muscle tissue was used for genomic DNA extraction using the TIANamp Genomic DNA Kit (TIANGEN, Beijing, China) following the manufacturer’s protocol. Total RNA was extracted from muscle tissue of the same individual using TRIZOL Kit (Invitrogen, USA), according to the manufacturer’s instructions. DNA and RNA quantity and quality were evaluated using a Qubit 3.0 fluorometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) and 1% agarose gel electrophoresis.

Sequencing library construction

ONT sequencing libraries were prepared using the SQK-ULK001 kit following the manufacturer’s instructions and sequenced on a PromethION flow cell (Oxford Nanopore Technologies, UK). A total of 106.56 Gb long-read raw data were generated for genome assembly.

Hi-C libraries were constructed using the GrandOmics Hi-C Kit with the restriction enzyme DpnII, following the standard protocol provided by the manufacturer (GrandOmics, China). Hi-C libraries were sequenced on an Illumina NovaSeq platform to generate paired-end reads for chromosomal scaffolding, yielding 300.3 Gb raw reads.

For RNA sequencing, cDNA libraries were generated using DNA nanoball (DNBs) technology following the DNBSEQ platform protocol (MGI, China) and sequenced on the MGISEQ-2000 platform with a paired-end 150 bp sequencing strategy, which generated 13.0 Gb raw data.

Near T2T genome assembly

Raw MGI sequencing reads were processed using fastp (v0.23.4)14 with default parameters to remove adapter contamination and low-quality reads, producing a set of clean, high-quality reads for downstream analyses. The ONT sequencing data were generated in POD5 format and subjected to quality filtering using dorado in sup mode (v5.0.0), during which reads with a quality score below 7 were discarded. The high-quality reads were then assembled into contigs using hifiasm (v0.19.8)15 and -ont mode to generate the initial contig-level genome assembly.

For chromosome-level scaffolding, Hi-C clean reads were aligned to the assembled contigs using Bwa (v0.7.17-r1198-dirty)16 with parameter: mem −5SP. The resulting alignment files were processed using Haphic (v1.0.5; parameters: Haphic pipeline Genome bam Chrnumber)17 to remove short or potentially misassembled contigs and to eliminate Hi-C links between allelic contigs. Contig clustering was performed using the Markov Cluster Algorithm. Subsequently, the 3D-DNA18 iterative scaffolding algorithm was applied for rapid ordering and orientation of the contigs. These results were then used as the initial configuration and further optimized using ALLHiC (v0.9.8)19. Gap filling was performed using LR_GapCloser v1.120 and TGS-GapCloser v1.0.121. Finally, a near T2T genome assembly of 3.5 Gb was generated and anchored to 73 chromosomes, with a contig N50 of 41.14 Mb and a scaffold N50 of 49.1 Mb (Table 1, Fig. 1 and Fig. 2).

Notably, compared with previously published genome assemblies of decapod crustaceans, our assembly shows a substantial improvement in both continuity and completeness. The contig N50 is 7.5-8 times higher than those reported genome assemblies of the genus Panulirus. In addition, the number of gaps in the genome is markedly reduced, with only 31 gaps in total, and all 51 chromosomes are assembled completely without any gaps (Table 2). Genome completeness was evaluated using BUSCO (v6.0.0)22 with the arthropoda_odb12 database, resulting in a completeness score of 99.8%. In addition, base-level consensus accuracy was evaluated using Merqury23 based on k-mer analysis of ONT sequencing reads.

Repeat Element Annotation

Repetitive elements in P. h. homarus genome were annotated using a combination of de novo and homology-based strategies. For de novo repeat identification, RepeatModeler (v1.0.8)24 was employed to generate a species-specific repeat library, and LTR-FINDER (v1.0.6)25 was used to detect long terminal repeat (LTR) retrotransposons. A comprehensive custom repeat library was annotated by integrating the de novo predicted repeats with the Repbase transposable element database (v21.01)26 using RepeatMasker (v4.0.623)27. Tandem repeats were identified using Tandem Repeats Finder (v4.07)28 with the parameters: 2 7 7 80 10 50 2000 -d -h.

The combined repeat library, including both de novo and known repeat elements, was then used to annotate repetitive sequences across the genome using RepeatMasker (v4.0.623)27 and RepeatProteinMask (v4.0.623)27 with default settings. Centromeric and telomeric sequences were identified using quarTeT (v1.1.7)29. In total, approximately 2.05 Gb of repetitive sequences were identified, accounting for 58.5% of the assembled genome (Table 3). A total of 146 telomeric sequences were predicted, with telomeres detected at both ends of all 73 chromosomes. Centromeric sequences were detected on 56 chromosomes (Fig. 3).

Gene Prediction and Functional Annotation

Protein-coding genes were predicted by integrating homology-based and transcriptome-based approaches. Protein sequences from three closely related species—P. h. homarus (GCA_043589495.1), Macrobrachium hainanense (GCA_044707065.1), Macrobrachium nipponense (GCF_015104395.2), and Macrobrachium rosenbergii (GCF_040412425.1)—were downloaded from the NCBI database. The GeMoMa (v1.6.4)30 was used for homology-based gene annotation.

In parallel, for transcriptome-based annotation, the RNA-seq reads was assembled into contigs using Trinity (v2.5.131)31 with optimized parameters: –min_contig_length 200–min_kmer_cov 4–min_glue 4–bfly_opts ‘-V5–edge-thr = 0.1–stderr’–genome_guided_max_intron 10000, and then genes were estimated using PASA (v2.3.332)32. Homology-based and transcriptome-based gene predictions were subsequently integrated using EVM (v1.1.1)33 to generate a final non-redundant gene set. In total, 38,396 protein-coding genes were predicted, with an average mRNA length of 15,414.38 bp, an average CDS length of 1,216.91 bp and an average of 4.66 exons per gene (Table 4).

Technical Validation

All scaffolds in the P. h. homarus genome assembly were anchored to 73 chromosomes, with telomeric repeats detected at both ends of every chromosome. The assembly shows high continuity, with a contig N50 of 41.1 Mb and a scaffold N50 of 49.1 Mb. Genome completeness was assessed using BUSCO (v6.0.0), recovering 99.8% complete genes, including 97.7% single-copy orthologs. Base-level accuracy evaluation using Merqury yielded a consensus quality value (QV) of 61.2, corresponding to an estimated base error rate of 7.54 × 10−7, indicating a highly accurate genome assembly. Together, these results support the high continuity and near-completeness of the genome assembly in this study.

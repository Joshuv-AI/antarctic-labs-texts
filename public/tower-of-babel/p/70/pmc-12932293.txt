The core aquablation pentafecta: a five-domain composite definition of procedural success and learning curve validation in 300 consecutive cases
Authors: Luca Di Gianfrancesco, Daniele D’Agostino, Filippo Marino, Davide De Marchi, Marco Giampaoli, Antonio Amodeo, Paolo Corsi, Giuliana Lista, Gian Maria Busetto, Angelo Porreca
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Aquablation, a robotic waterjet ablation guided by real-time ultrasound, is increasingly used for benign prostatic obstruction (BPO). Randomized and comparative studies demonstrate efficacy, safety, and superior functional preservation compared with thermal or laser techniques. We propose and validate a five-domain Core Aquablation Pentafecta integrating objective, functional, and safety endpoints to define comprehensive procedural success. Three hundred consecutive men underwent Aquablation (2019–2024). The five Core Pentafecta domains were: (1) ≥ 50% reduction or ≥ 10-point improvement in IPSS, (2) ≥ 50% increase in Qmax, (3) no Clavien–Dindo ≥ III complication or transfusion, (4) preservation of continence and antegrade ejaculation, and (5) freedom from retreatment ≤ 24 months. Sequential 50-case cohorts were analyzed for learning-curve effects. Median age 67 years; median prostate 72 mL (range 22–220). Median operative time 45 min. IPSS improved from 23.4 ± 6.1 to 7.1 ± 4.8 (p < 0.001); Qmax from 8.9 ± 3.2 to 20.3 ± 6.0 mL/s (p < 0.001). Major complications 4%, no transfusions. All patients continent; 88% preserved antegrade ejaculation. No retreatments within 24 months. Core Pentafecta achievement rose from 58% (first 50 cases) to 73% overall and 82% after 150 procedures. The Core Aquablation Pentafecta provides a concise, reproducible metric integrating efficacy, safety, and durability. Achievement improved markedly with experience, plateauing above 80%, and was associated with sustained functional outcomes, supporting Aquablation as a function-preserving and durable robotic therapy for BPO in routine clinical practice.

Introduction

Benign prostatic obstruction (BPO), most commonly caused by benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH), remains a leading cause of lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS) in aging men, with histologic prevalence exceeding 80% by the eighth decade of life [1, 2]. LUTS profoundly affects quality of life and imposes a major economic and healthcare burden [3, 4]. Contemporary guidelines recommend an integrated treatment algorithm progressing from medical therapy—using α-blockers, 5-α-reductase inhibitors, and phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitors—to minimally invasive surgical treatments (MISTs) or resective approaches for patients with refractory symptoms [5].

Traditional gold standards such as transurethral resection of the prostate (TURP) and Holmium laser enucleation of the prostate (HoLEP) deliver durable symptomatic relief but are associated with bleeding, prolonged catheterization, and ejaculatory dysfunction rates of up to 80–90% [6, 7]. Aquablation, a robot-guided, heat-free waterjet ablation performed under real-time transrectal ultrasound (TRUS) guidance, enables anatomically precise and reproducible tissue resection with preservation of peri-urethral structures [8–10].

Evidence from the WATER and WATER II randomized trials established Aquablation’s efficacy and safety in prostates up to 150 mL, demonstrating equivalent outcomes to TURP with significantly lower rates of ejaculatory dysfunction [9–12]. Five-year results confirmed durable improvements in IPSS and Qmax and retreatment rates below 5% [12]. A pooled international analysis of the WATER, WATER II, OPEN WATER, FRANCAIS WATER, and JAPAN PMS studies consolidated long-term safety and functional consistency across continents and prostate volumes [13].

Real-world data have broadened the evidence base. The ICARUS registry confirmed Aquablation’s safety and efficacy even in men ≥ 80 years, showing comparable outcomes and complication rates to younger patients [14]. Similarly, the 150-patient prospective comparison of Aquablation and HoLEP demonstrated comparable improvements in IPSS and Qmax but dramatically lower ejaculatory dysfunction with Aquablation (6.6% vs. 88.9%) [8].

Recent reviews and technology updates further contextualize these findings. Vermeulen et al. described Aquablation as a reproducible, cost-effective, image-guided platform that integrates seamlessly into modern BPH pathways [17]. Zhou et al. emphasized its role within multimodal BPH management strategies [5]. Advances in artificial intelligence (AI) are expected to enhance image segmentation, robotic mapping, and intra-operative planning for Aquablation [18], while the first-in-US ambulatory study confirmed its economic feasibility and safety in outpatient settings [19].

Despite growing global experience, outcome reporting remains heterogeneous. Inspired by oncologic surgery’s “trifecta” and “pentafecta” frameworks [20, 21], we propose a five-domain Core Aquablation Pentafecta that integrates efficacy, safety, and functional preservation to define comprehensive procedural success.

Materials and methods

Study design and population

Three hundred consecutive men underwent Aquablation for symptomatic BPO between 2019 and 2024 at two tertiary centers. Inclusion: age 45–85 y, IPSS ≥ 12, prostate 30–150 mL, failed medical therapy.

Patients were primarily selected with prostate volumes between 30 and 150 mL, consistent with standard Aquablation indications. However, to reflect real-world clinical practice, all treated cases were retained in the analysis, including two out-of-range prostates—one measuring 22 mL and one measuring 220 mL. Accordingly, the overall prostate volume range in the cohort extended from 22 to 220 mL.

Exclusion: prior prostate surgery, prior or active prostate cancer diagnosis, infection (referred specifically to an active symptomatic urinary tract infection or acute bacterial prostatitis at the time of preoperative assessment requiring antibiotic treatment; elective Aquablation was deferred until resolution of infection; patients with ongoing infection or incomplete resolution prior to surgery were excluded, whereas a remote history of treated infection without residual symptoms was not considered an exclusion criterion. This approach was adopted to minimize the risk of inflammation-related bleeding, altered tissue planes during resection, and potential confounding of lower urinary tract symptoms attributable to active infection rather than benign prostatic obstruction).

Institutional ethics approval and informed consent obtained.

Surgical technique

Procedures used the AQUABEAM® system (PROCEPT BioRobotics). Under transrectal ultrasound guidance, resection planes were mapped and executed automatically using a high-velocity saline jet. Limited bipolar coagulation achieved hemostasis. Catheters were typically removed ≤ 48 h.

Definition and assessment of continence and functional outcomes

Urinary continence was defined as the absence of pad use and no patient-reported stress or urge urinary incontinence at follow-up visits. Transient early postoperative storage symptoms (e.g., urgency, frequency, or mild urge incontinence occurring within the initial postoperative weeks) were not classified as incontinence if they resolved without ongoing pad use or intervention. Continence status was assessed at routine postoperative follow-up visits through structured clinical interviews conducted by the treating urologist and review of patient-reported symptoms. Ejaculatory function was evaluated based on patient self-report regarding the presence or absence of antegrade ejaculation during sexual activity. Functional outcomes, including IPSS and quality-of-life components, were collected preoperatively and at standardized postoperative time points using validated questionnaires. This pragmatic assessment strategy reflects routine clinical practice while ensuring consistent and reproducible classification of functional outcomes across the study cohort. Continence and ejaculatory preservation were grouped within a single functional domain to reflect patient-centered quality-of-life outcomes related to sphincteric integrity and sexual function, which are frequently prioritized by patients undergoing surgery for benign prostatic obstruction. Combining these closely related postoperative functional endpoints allowed for a simplified and clinically intuitive composite metric while maintaining sensitivity to treatment-related morbidity.

Assessment of data distribution and descriptive statistics

Prior to statistical analysis, the distribution of continuous variables was formally assessed to determine the most appropriate measures of central tendency and dispersion. Normality was evaluated using the Shapiro–Wilk test, supplemented by visual inspection of histograms and Q–Q plots. Variables demonstrating a normal distribution are reported as mean ± standard deviation (SD), whereas non-normally distributed variables are presented as median with interquartile range (IQR). Accordingly, parametric tests (paired or unpaired t tests) were applied to normally distributed data, while non-parametric alternatives were used when distributional assumptions were not met. This approach ensured that descriptive and inferential statistics were aligned with the underlying data structure and enhanced the methodological transparency and robustness of the analyses.

At 12 months, IPSS improved from 23.4 ± 6.1 to 7.1 ± 4.8 (p < 0.001); Qmax from 8.9 ± 3.2 to 20.3 ± 6.0 mL/s (p < 0.001); PVR from 118 ± 76 to 31 ± 39 mL (p < 0.001).

Assessment of durability and follow-up

Durability was defined as freedom from any surgical or medical retreatment for benign prostatic obstruction within 24 months following Aquablation. All patients included in the study reached a minimum follow-up duration of ≥ 24 months, as enrollment was completed between 2019 and 2024 with complete follow-up available for the entire cohort; therefore, the durability endpoint was assessable in all cases without censoring. Time-to-event analysis using Kaplan–Meier methods was considered to account for potential variability in follow-up; however, given that no retreatment events occurred during the observation period, such analysis was not applied, as it would not provide additional discriminatory or inferential value. Retreatment-free status was instead verified across the entire follow-up window to ensure completeness and robustness of the durability assessment. This approach allowed a transparent and unbiased evaluation of mid-term durability while maintaining consistency with composite outcome definitions used in prior BPO studies.

Discussion

This study introduces the Core Aquablation Pentafecta as a structured, multidimensional framework for defining procedural success in benign prostatic obstruction surgery. Rather than relying on isolated functional or safety endpoints, the Pentafecta integrates key dimensions of therapeutic performance into a unified composite outcome designed to support standardized reporting, benchmarking, and comparative evaluation.

The framework was intentionally developed as a pragmatic and exploratory composite metric, reflecting the current absence of a universally accepted definition of success for Aquablation or BPO surgery more broadly. Existing studies employ heterogeneous outcome measures that limit cross-study comparability and learning-curve assessment. The Pentafecta was therefore conceived to capture the essential components of contemporary procedural success while remaining transparent, reproducible, and adaptable to real-world practice.

The following sections examine the conceptual justification of the framework, its alignment with established clinical benchmarks, domain-specific learning-curve behavior, and its implications for clinical practice and quality monitoring. Collectively, these analyses position the Pentafecta as a foundational reporting tool requiring ongoing external validation rather than a definitive performance metric.

Pentafecta conceptual justification

The Core Aquablation Pentafecta was developed and applied within the same consecutive patient cohort and therefore represents an internally derived, exploratory composite endpoint. While appropriate for hypothesis generation and feasibility assessment, this approach inherently limits formal validation and introduces potential circularity in performance estimation. Accordingly, the present findings should be interpreted as foundational rather than definitive, and external validation across independent multicenter cohorts and comparative testing against existing composite frameworks will be essential to establish generalizability, discriminatory performance, and clinical utility.

Importantly, the outcomes observed in this series align closely with large-scale registry and pooled analyses demonstrating Aquablation’s consistent efficacy, safety, and durability across patient ages, prostate sizes, and real-world practice settings [13–15]. The ICARUS registry reported no excess morbidity in octogenarians, supporting procedural safety in higher-risk populations [14], while pooled international WATER datasets confirmed sustained IPSS and Qmax improvements with retreatment rates below 5% at extended follow-up [13]. In our cohort, symptom scores decreased by 69% and flow rates improved by 128% at 12 months, with complete continence preservation and 88% ejaculatory preservation—findings consistent with these global benchmarks [13].

To ensure that composite domain thresholds reflected true clinical success rather than arbitrary statistical change, cut-off values for symptom improvement and flow restoration were explicitly aligned with established minimal clinically important difference (MCID) benchmarks. Prior MCID analyses have demonstrated that absolute IPSS reductions of approximately 3–5 points correspond to minimal perceived improvement, whereas reductions of 8–10 points represent marked symptomatic benefit following BPO intervention [32, 33]. Accordingly, the ≥ 10-point absolute reduction or ≥ 50% relative improvement applied in the present study was selected to capture substantial, patient-relevant symptomatic relief consistent with outcomes reported in randomized surgical trials. Similarly, proportional improvements in Qmax of approximately 40–60% have been widely reported in contemporary randomized and comparative BPO studies as reflective of meaningful functional restoration, supporting the ≥ 50% increase threshold utilized within the Pentafecta framework [9, 10, 34].

Although this study was conducted at two high-volume tertiary centers with extensive Aquablation experience—potentially limiting generalizability to early-adoption or lower-volume settings—this context also underscores a central strength of the Pentafecta framework. By integrating functional efficacy, safety, functional preservation, and durability into a unified composite outcome, the Pentafecta offers a pragmatic quality-control and benchmarking tool capable of monitoring procedural maturation, identifying performance gaps, and enabling standardized comparison across institutions during technology adoption.

All five Pentafecta domains were intentionally assigned equal weight to preserve simplicity, transparency, and reproducibility, consistent with early composite metric development in other surgical fields. While individual domain importance may vary according to patient preference—particularly regarding symptom relief and sexual function—the Pentafecta was conceived as a minimum procedural success benchmark rather than a preference-weighted utility score. Freedom from retreatment was therefore included to capture procedural adequacy and mid-term durability, despite not contributing to outcome discrimination within this high-performing cohort. Its inclusion ensures completeness of the success definition and maintains relevance across broader populations and longer follow-up horizons, where durability is expected to emerge as a meaningful differentiator. Future investigations should focus on external validation, extended follow-up, and development of preference-weighted composite models.

Preservation of ejaculatory function represents a critical contemporary quality metric in BPO surgery. Luo et al. reported ejaculatory preservation rates approaching 90% following Aquablation, markedly exceeding those observed after TURP and HoLEP [26], likely attributable to precise anatomic mapping and heat-free tissue dissection minimizing trauma to ejaculatory ducts. The Pentafecta’s explicit incorporation of this endpoint reflects the evolving prioritization of sexual-function preservation in modern outcome assessment.

From a safety perspective, the absence of perioperative blood transfusion in this consecutive cohort warrants consideration. Standardized hemostatic strategies—including targeted bipolar cauterisation following Aquablation, routine continuous bladder irrigation for approximately 24 h, perioperative blood pressure control, and anticoagulation management—were systematically employed. The heat-free, image-guided nature of Aquablation may further reduce vascular injury compared with energy-based enucleation techniques. While institutional expertise and perioperative optimization may have contributed to these favorable outcomes, they are consistent with contemporary Aquablation series reporting low transfusion rates, supporting reproducibility of hemostatic safety in routine practice [13–15]. Postoperative urethral stricture remains a rare but recognized risk; adherence to catheterization protocols and careful device handling have been emphasized as preventive strategies [31]. No strictures were observed during 24-month follow-up, consistent with sub-1% rates reported in registry analyses [13, 14].

Prostate volume demonstrated nuanced influence across Pentafecta domains. While overall composite achievement was modestly higher in glands below 100 mL—primarily driven by higher ejaculatory preservation—objective efficacy and durability were preserved in larger prostates without increased retreatment. These findings suggest Aquablation outcomes are less volume-dependent than traditional resective techniques, though prostate size may differentially influence specific functional domains rather than overall procedural success. Further multicenter volume-stratified analyses are warranted to validate these observations.

The 24-month durability horizon was intentionally selected based on evidence that most retreatments following BPO surgery occur within the first 18–24 months, reflecting early technical failure or inadequate tissue removal. This timeframe has been widely adopted in randomized trials and composite outcome frameworks as a clinically meaningful mid-term benchmark. While longer-term follow-up remains essential to fully characterize durability, the Pentafecta was designed as an early composite quality metric capturing the critical failure window. The absence of retreatment events within this interval limited the utility of time-to-event modeling and confines durability inferences to mid-term outcomes.

Finally, although prior composite endpoints such as BPH6 contributed to standardized outcome reporting, they were developed in a different technological and clinical context and do not explicitly incorporate ejaculatory preservation. The Core Aquablation Pentafecta was not intended to demonstrate statistical superiority over existing frameworks but to address contemporary priorities inherent to function-preserving robotic therapy. By integrating objective efficacy, safety, functional preservation, and durability, it reflects an evolution in defining clinically meaningful success and provides a procedure-centered reporting framework tailored to Aquablation. Comparative validation against established composite metrics remains an important future research direction.

Functional outcomes were assessed using structured clinical interviews and patient self-report rather than validated questionnaires, with continence defined as the absence of de novo stress urinary incontinence. While this pragmatic approach reflects real-world practice and aligns with the binary structure of the Pentafecta, it introduces potential reporting bias and reduced granularity. This trade-off between reproducibility and measurement precision warrants acknowledgment, and future prospective studies incorporating validated instruments will be essential to refine functional domain assessment.

Learning curve interpretation

Composite Pentafecta achievement improved progressively from 58% in early cases to 82% after approximately 150 procedures, paralleling the learning-curve plateau reported beyond 100–150 cases in prior Aquablation series [15, 16]. This trajectory mirrors multicenter observations demonstrating reproducible outcomes across diverse surgeon backgrounds once proficiency in procedural planning and ultrasound-based mapping is achieved [13, 15]. These findings suggest that the stabilization observed in the present cohort reflects true procedural maturation rather than center-specific performance.

Importantly, learning-curve effects were not uniform across Pentafecta domains but were primarily driven by operator-dependent components. Symptom relief and objective flow restoration were consistently achieved from early cases, reflecting the inherent reproducibility of robotic waterjet resection once the target tissue volume is defined. In contrast, functional preservation and perioperative safety demonstrated the greatest sensitivity to increasing experience. With procedural maturation, improved preoperative and intraoperative ultrasound-guided planning enabled more precise delineation of apical and peri-verumontanum resection margins, translating into higher rates of antegrade ejaculatory preservation. Concurrent refinements in resection depth selection, hemostatic strategy, and catheter management were associated with improved bleeding control and reduced higher-grade complications over time. Notably, these learning-related gains reflected increased precision in tissue targeting rather than more aggressive tissue removal, as efficacy and flow outcomes remained stable across experience phases.

The durability domain—defined as freedom from retreatment within 24 months—did not contribute to outcome discrimination in this cohort due to the absence of retreatment events. While this finding reflects favorable mid-term procedural durability, it should be interpreted as a structural component of comprehensive surgical adequacy rather than a differentiating performance metric within a high-performing population. Durability remains a critical dimension of long-term success in BPO surgery and is expected to emerge as a meaningful discriminator with extended follow-up and in more heterogeneous real-world settings. Its inclusion within the Pentafecta framework was therefore intentional to ensure longitudinal relevance beyond early functional and safety metrics.

Methodologically, the learning curve was assessed using sequential 50-case cohorts to enhance clinical interpretability, enable intuitive visualization of outcome evolution, and maintain consistency with existing Aquablation learning-curve literature. Although this descriptive approach lacks the statistical precision of formal modeling techniques such as cumulative sum (CUSUM) analysis, spline-based regression, or mixed-effects modeling, it provides a transparent depiction of procedural maturation and outcome stabilization. Importantly, the observed plateau in Pentafecta achievement and domain-specific outcomes beyond approximately 150 cases aligns closely with proficiency thresholds reported in international multicenter analyses employing advanced analytical methodologies [13–15]. This concordance supports the external validity of the observed stabilization despite the pragmatic analytical approach utilized. Future prospective studies incorporating formal learning-curve modeling and surgeon-level random effects will be essential to further refine proficiency estimates and confirm generalizability.

Clinical implications

Collectively, these findings reinforce Aquablation as a durable, function-preserving therapy that can be safely applied across a broad spectrum of patient ages, prostate sizes, and clinical settings, including ambulatory surgical centers. The Core Aquablation Pentafecta—achieved in 73% of patients overall and 82% beyond the learning curve—provides a reproducible and clinically meaningful quality metric that aligns closely with patient-centered expectations of symptom relief, procedural safety, and preservation of sexual function.

Perioperative safety in this cohort (4% Clavien ≥ III complications, no transfusions) mirrors outcomes reported in multicenter registries and ambulatory series. Notably, the ASC pilot study demonstrated that Aquablation performed in outpatient surgical centers achieved a major complication rate of approximately 1% while reducing system-level costs by nearly $1,800 per case [19]. Subsequent editorial analysis by Helfand et al. [28] and related commentary [29] confirmed that ambulatory Aquablation maintains safety profiles comparable to hospital-based procedures. Consistent with these data, our results demonstrate rapid recovery—with median catheterization of 38 h and discharge within 48 h for most patients—alongside broad anatomic applicability across prostate volumes ranging from 22 to 220 mL.

When contextualized against established surgical modalities, HoLEP remains a durable benchmark for large prostates [7], but is associated with a steep learning curve and high rates of ejaculatory dysfunction [7, 22]. In contrast, prospective multicenter comparative trials have demonstrated equivalent functional efficacy between Aquablation and HoLEP while significantly reducing ejaculatory dysfunction with Aquablation [8]. Narrative synthesis by Luo et al. further confirmed Aquablation among the highest sexual-function–preserving minimally invasive therapies, maintaining antegrade ejaculation in 80–90% of cases [27]. These findings highlight the clinical importance of explicitly incorporating sexual-function preservation into composite success definitions, distinguishing the Core Pentafecta from earlier BPH “trifecta” or BPH6 metrics that emphasized efficacy and safety while overlooking sexual outcomes [23–25].

Digital innovation and artificial intelligence are poised to further enhance Aquablation’s clinical impact. Emerging applications include improved imaging, automated tissue segmentation, outcome prediction, and intraoperative workflow optimization [18]. Beyond procedural performance, AI-powered patient education platforms—such as large language model–based decision-support tools—have demonstrated high accuracy and completeness in addressing BPH surgical queries, underscoring their growing role in patient engagement and shared decision-making [30].

The apparently “perfect” continence preservation and absence of retreatment within 24 months warrant careful clinical contextualization. Continence was stringently defined as the absence of pad use and patient-reported stress or urge urinary incontinence, excluding transient early postoperative storage symptoms. Under this definition, no cases of de novo long-term incontinence were observed, consistent with the heat-free, anatomically guided nature of Aquablation and with near-zero permanent incontinence rates reported in multicenter trials and registries. Similarly, freedom from retreatment should be interpreted within a mid-term follow-up framework. Although follow-up duration varied across the 2019–2024 treatment period, no patient reaching the 24-month threshold required surgical or medical retreatment. Given that the majority of Aquablation retreatments reported in the literature occur within the first 18–24 months, this interval represents a clinically meaningful durability benchmark rather than a claim of long-term failure-free survival. Longer-term follow-up and time-to-event analyses remain essential to fully characterize procedural durability.

Conclusion

The Core Aquablation Pentafecta provides a unified definition of optimal success in BPO surgery, encompassing functional improvement, flow restoration, safety, functional preservation, and durability. In this cohort of 300 patients, 73% achieved all five criteria (82% beyond the learning curve), with high rates of continence preservation, ejaculatory function, and freedom from retreatment at 24 months. These outcomes are consistent with previously published multicenter and comparative data, suggesting that Aquablation offers a favorable balance between efficacy and quality-of-life preservation. The five-domain Pentafecta framework may serve as a standardized benchmark for future comparative trials and real-world registry studies.

Analytical solution of driven time-dependent mesoscopic circuits
Authors: Jinying Ma, Yazhuo Yao, Ronghai Liu, Fangcheng Qiu, Xiyuan Zhang, Zhanyuan Yan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
When a time-dependent harmonic system is further driven by an external source, it presents significant challenges to obtaining analytical solutions for the system. This paper applies the Lewis-Riesenfeld invariant method to a mesoscopic time-dependent dissipative inductor-capacitor (RLC) circuit with an external source. By constructing quantum invariant Hermitian operators and introducing auxiliary equations that can describe the effects of external sources and resistance, we analytically solve the Schrödinger equation of the system. Taking an alternating current (AC) voltage source as an example, we derive the generalized coherent states of a mesoscopic time-dependent RLC circuit with an external source, calculate the quantum fluctuations of charge and current, and obtain their corresponding uncertainty relations. The results indicate that the time-dependent inductance and resistance disrupt the minimum uncertainty relation of the Glauble coherent states in driven time-dependent mesoscopic RLC circuits. This research contributes to the establishment of a quantum theory for mesoscopic circuits.

Introduction

With the rapid advancement of integrated circuit technology, the dimensions of electronic devices have been scaled down to the mesoscopic regime, giving rise to the field of mesoscopic circuits1,2. At such scales, quantum effects including quantum fluctuations and quantum coherence play an indispensable role in circuit dynamics3–5. Consequently, the development of a consistent quantum framework for mesoscopic circuits is highly imperative6.

In 1973, Louisell pioneered the investigation of quantum noise in mesoscopic circuits by drawing an analogy between mesoscopic inductance–capacitance (LC) resonant circuits and the classical harmonic oscillator under the vacuum-state assumption7. Li et al. realized the quantization of mesoscopic circuits in discrete charge space8, which ignited intense research interest in the quantum theory of mesoscopic circuits9–12.The path-integral approach13 and canonical transformation method14 have also been utilized to derive the wave functions of dissipative RLC circuits. The establishment of a series of theoretical schemes for mesoscopic circuit quantization has spurred widespread research into quantum effects in these systems. Subsequently, extensive studies have been conducted on the quantization and quantum properties of diverse mesoscopic circuits, encompassing dissipative circuits15,16, source-driven circuits17–21, time-dependent circuits22–25, coupled circuits9,12,26–28, and nonlinear circuits29–32.

When circuit parameters (e.g., inductance and resistance) or external driving sources are time-dependent, the quantization of mesoscopic circuits becomes highly challenging due to the requirement of solving the time-dependent Schrödinger equation. For time-dependent mesoscopic systems, the path-integral approach and the quantum invariant method are commonly adopted. However, the path-integral method faces substantial difficulties in evaluating propagators when applied to complex mesoscopic circuits with external driving sources13. The Lewis-Riesenfeld (L-R) invariant method provides an effective strategy to address this issue. First proposed by Lewis and Riesenfeld in 196933, this method constructs a time-dependent Hermitian operator (the quantum invariant operator) that does not commute with the Hamiltonian. Combined with a unitary transformation, it enables complicated systems to be mapped onto a harmonic-oscillator form, thereby facilitating the exact and explicit derivation of solutions to the Schrödinger equation for quantum systems with time-dependent Hamiltonians33. Pedrosa et al. applied this method to obtain the wave functions and quantum fluctuations of linear harmonic oscillators with time-dependent inductance and frequency16, and later solved the quantum dynamics of time-dependent mesoscopic R(t)L(t)C circuits15. The quantum invariant method possesses distinct advantages such as high analyticity and computational efficiency in dealing with time-dependent systems (e.g., those with time-varying inductors and resistors)24,25,34 and mesoscopic circuits involving external source terms18–20.

Nevertheless, when a time-dependent mesoscopic circuit incorporates an external driving source, the application of the quantum invariant operator method introduces an additional auxiliary equation associated with the source during the solution process, which further complicates the analytical derivation of eigenfunctions. Building on the L-R invariant method for time-dependent R(t)L(t)C mesoscopic circuits24,25 and source-driven time-dependent L(t)C mesoscopic circuits18–20, the present work further investigates the quantization of a time-dependent mesoscopic R(t)L(t)C circuit with an external source, by simultaneously considering both dissipation and external driving effects. Taking an alternating-current (AC) voltage source as a typical example, we calculate the quantum fluctuations of charge and current, as well as the corresponding uncertainty relations, in the generalized coherent states of the driven time-dependent mesoscopic RLC circuit.

L–R invariant method

The L-R quantum invariant method is a powerful technique for solving the time-dependent Schrödinger equation for systems whose Hamiltonian H(t) depends explicitly on time. Notably, the dynamical equation of the mesoscopic L(t)C circuit has an identical mathematical form to that of the time-dependent harmonic oscillator. Consequently, the L-R invariant method is fully applicable to the quantization of mesoscopic L(t)C circuits, as well as more complex mesoscopic circuits–such as time-dependent dissipative R(t)L(t)C circuits and externally driven L(t)C circuits. In the following section, we further investigate source-driven mesoscopic time-dependent R(t)L(t)C circuits.

Conclusions

In this paper, by adopting the L-R invariant operator method tailored for time-dependent quantum systems, we construct a more general form of the invariant operator for the time-dependent dissipative mesoscopic \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$RLC$$\end{document} circuit driven by a power source. This generalized invariant operator can be reduced to the invariant operators corresponding to the special cases (e.g., undriven \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$RLC$$\end{document} circuits and resistance-free \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$LC$$\end{document} circuits) that have been investigated in existing literature. Furthermore, by introducing auxiliary equations associated with both dissipation effects and external power driving, we successfully achieve the quantization of the system and derive its eigenfunctions in an explicit form. Based on the aforementioned quantization results, we specifically discuss the generalized coherent state of the mesoscopic time-dependent \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$RLC$$\end{document} circuit under the action of an external alternating-current (AC) source. We present the quantum fluctuations of charge and current in this coherent state, as well as the corresponding uncertainty relation (deviating from the minimum uncertainty relation). It is explicitly demonstrated that the time-dependent inductance and resistance, which characterize the dynamic and dissipative properties of the circuit, disrupt the minimum uncertainty relation of the Glauble coherent state. This work deepens the understanding of quantum effects in mesoscopic electronic systems and provides a valuable contribution to the development of a quantum theory for mesoscopic circuits.

Introduction

Postoperative pulmonary complications (PPCs) are common after cardiac surgery and are associated with longer intensive care and hospital stays, and increased morbidity and mortality.1 2 Lung-protective ventilation strategies have been proposed to reduce PPCs.3 While there is relative consensus on the use of tidal volumes (Vt) of 6–8 mL/kg predicted body weight (PBW), the optimal positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEP) and the role of alveolar recruitment manoeuvres (RMs) remain controversial. Studies in non-cardiac surgery patients have produced conflicting results, potentially due to variations in ventilator settings, use of extreme PEEP levels or the inclusion of patient at low risk of PPCs.410 Lung-protective ventilation strategies pose unique challenges and opportunities in cardiac surgery as this population has increased rates of pulmonary disease, an open chest cavity alters pulmonary dynamics and aortic occlusion and cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) contribute to ischaemia-reperfusion injury to the lungs.1113 Further, ventilation is often paused during CPB, contributing to atelectasis, while the use of higher PEEP or RMs may be limited by concern that they will produce hypotension in these patients.2

A recent single-centre study found that an intensive alveolar recruitment strategy associated with lung-protective ventilation reduced severe pulmonary complications in hypoxaemic patients after elective cardiac surgery.14 However, a recent multicentre randomised clinical trial (RCT) found no difference in PPCs at 1 week between patients undergoing elective on-pump cardiac surgery treated with an open-lung ventilation strategy (ventilated throughout CPB, moderate PEEP and perioperative RM) compared with conventional ventilation.10 However, a meta-analysis, including the latter study, and involving 1455 patients suggested a beneficial effect of RM in reducing postoperative pulmonary atelectasis, hypoxic events and pneumonia and improved arterial oxygen pressure (PaO2)/fraction of inspired oxygen (FiO2) ratios in patients undergoing cardiac surgery.15

We hypothesise that an open lung strategy will prevent PPCs in a high-risk population. Therefore, this RCT was designed to compare a protective ventilation strategy with moderate PEEP and RMs to a protective ventilation strategy with low PEEP, in patients at high risk of developing PPCs after elective cardiac surgery (impact of intraoperatiVe moderAte positive end-expiratory pressure with reCruitment mAnoeuvres versus low positive end-expiRatory pressure on major postoperative pulMonary complications and death after on-pump cardiac surgery in high-risk patients: VACARM).

Methods and analysis

Trial design

The VACARM study is an investigator-initiated, multicentre, open, randomised, parallel-group, superiority clinical trial of patients at high risk of PPCs scheduled to undergo cardiac surgery. It compares two intraoperative ventilation strategies: (1) an open-lung ventilation strategy and (2) a standard protective ventilation strategy. The trial is being conducted in nine French university centres. Sponsor: University Hospital of Rennes. Recruitment began in July 2021 and is expected to continue for a total of 48 months. This protocol (V.4, 17 October 2023) follows the SPIRIT (Standard Protocol Items for Randomised trials) guidelines (see online supplemental data).

Allocation and blinding

Patients eligible for inclusion will be randomised into either the experimental group (open-lung ventilation) or the control group (standard ventilation). Randomisation will be stratified by centre and balanced using block randomisation. Each patient will receive a unique computer-generated randomisation number. Pulmonary events included in the outcomes (pneumonia, atelectasis) will be assessed by an independent adjudication committee blinded to the treatment groups (see below adjudication committee paragraph).

Interventions

General anaesthesia induction and maintenance will follow local practice at each centre. Protective mechanical ventilation will be applied to all patients, with a tidal volume of 6 to 8 mL/kg of PBW in volume control mode, an inspiratory to expiratory ratio of 1:2 and with the respiratory rate adjusted to maintain end-tidal CO2 between 35 and 40 mm Hg. The FiO2 will be set to <0.6.

During CPB, ventilation will continue with a Vt of 2.5 mL/kg of PBW and PEEP of 5 cmH2O.17 De-airing before aortic cross-clamp removal will be performed according to local practice. Intraoperative desaturation (peripheral oxygen saturation ≤92%) will be managed with adjustments to FiO2 and PEEP, or with RMs as required.

Patients in the experimental group will receive intraoperative protective ventilation with a moderate PEEP of 8 cmH2O and RMs every 30 min after intubation (excluding during CPB), after each breathing circuit disconnection and within 30 min of intensive care unit (ICU) admission (figure 1). Each RM will involve continuous positive airway pressure of 30 cmH2O for 30 s. The surgical team will be informed before each RM. To optimise the haemodynamic tolerance, a systolic arterial pressure ≥90 mm Hg and a mean arterial pressure ≥65 mm Hg will be required before each RM. All teams will be trained in RM procedures, and an instructional video will be provided to ensure standardised application.

Patients in the control group will receive intraoperative protective mechanical ventilation with a low PEEP of 5 cmH2O and no RM.

Postoperative management

Extubation criteria include adequate level of consciousness, stable spontaneous ventilation with a pressure support of 10–12 cmH2O, PEEP of 5 cmH2O, FiO2 ≤0.4, haemodynamic stability, no significant bleeding (<100 mL/hour) and no significant electrocardiographic abnormalities.18

Non-invasive ventilation (NIV) or high-flow nasal oxygen therapy (HFNO) will be encouraged within the first 24 hours after extubation with the modality determined by local practice. NIV will involve pressures of 10–15 cmH2O, PEEP of 5 cmH2O and FiO2 of 0.4, delivered in 1 hour sessions every 4 hours, with rest periods overnight. HFNO will be delivered continuously through a nasal cannula, with the initial flow rate set at 50 L/min and FiO2 of 50%, adjusted to maintain SpO2 between 92% and 98%.19

Physiotherapy will be provided daily in ICU and continued in regular nursing units according to routine clinical practice at each centre.

Ventilator settings for prolonged postoperative ventilation will be left to the discretion of anaesthesiologist–intensivist.

Adjudication committee

The adjudication committee will include three independent specialists: an anaesthesiologist–intensivist, an infectious disease specialist and a radiologist. This committee will be responsible for the blinded assessment and validation of pneumonia diagnosis included in the composite primary endpoint, following the 2017 French recommendations.25 Additionally, it will confirm the diagnosis of atelectasis (secondary endpoint), based on established criteria defining pulmonary complications according to Hulzebos et al.16 A standardised patient record will be compiled for each case including all relevant data required for diagnosis. In the event of discordant assessments among committee members, consensus will be sought. If consensus cannot be achieved, a decision will be reached by majority agreement.

Ancillary immunological study

To further explore the potential impact of an open-lung ventilation strategy on the alveolo-capillary membrane and systemic inflammation, this study will include an ancillary immunological analysis. Biomarkers indicative of alveolar epithelial injury (sRAGE), pulmonary endothelial activation (Ang2) and systemic inflammation (TNFα, IL1β, IL6, IL8 and IL10) will be measured in a subset of 140 patients enrolled at a single centre (Rennes).

Blood samples will be collected at two time points: before the start of surgery (baseline) and on the morning of POD 1. Samples will be collected in a 5 mL EDTA tube from the arterial catheter. Following collection, samples will be transported within 1 hour to the biobank of Rennes (Centre de Ressources Biologiques Santé) for centrifugation and aliquoting. The resulting plasma samples will be stored at −20°C until batch analysis at the end of the study. Biomarker quantification will be performed using multiplex technique (Bio-Plex 200, Bio-Rad, 92 430 Marnes la-Coquette, France) at the Immunological Monitoring Platform of the SITI (suivi immunologique des thérapeutiques innovantes) laboratory in Rennes. The laboratory personnel processing the samples will be blinded.

Statistical analysis

A descriptive analysis of the collected data will be performed. Categorical variables will be reported as frequencies and percentages, while continuous variables will be expressed as mean±SD or median with IQR, depending on the distribution.

The rate of patients with postoperative invasive mechanical ventilation of more than 24 hours, pneumonia within 7 days, reintubation within 7 days or death at 28 days will be compared by a χ² test (or the exact Fisher test if necessary). Interim analyses are planned after inclusion of 1/3 and 2/3 of the target enrolment, and the study would be ended early in case of efficacy using the alpha spending function approach according to O’Brien and Fleming. Significance thresholds to stop the test are fixed at 0.00021 and 0.01202 for first and second analysis, respectively. The significance threshold at the final analysis is set at 0.04626 (nTerim, V.1.1, Statistical Solutions, Cork, Ireland).

Secondary outcomes will be analysed using similar statistical methods. For time-to-event outcomes, Kaplan-Meier survival curves will be compared using the log-rank test. The individual components of the primary outcome will be analysed separately, adjusting for multiple comparisons using the Bonferroni method. Longitudinal variables will be evaluated using mixed-effects models.

Prespecified subgroup analyses will be conducted to explore potential effect modifiers, using logistic regression models to test for interactions between the intervention and the following variables:

Statistical analyses will be conducted by the Methodology/Biostatistics Unit of the Clinical Investigation Centre of Rennes (National Institute of Health and Medical Research 1414), using SAS software (SAS Institute, Cary, North Carolina, USA). Except for analyses on the primary endpoint, all analyses will be carried out with a two-sided α=0.05 significance threshold.

The sponsor may decide to stop the research after consultation with the Scientific Committee if one of the planned interim analyses (after inclusion and evaluation of 1/3 and 2/3 of patients) shows a significant difference in the primary endpoint.

For the primary endpoint, a sensitivity analysis in the case of missing data will be carried out. For secondary endpoints, depending on the number of missing data, an imputation may be carried out; the choice of method will be defined in the statistical analysis plan.

A statistical analysis plan will be provided by the biostatistician of the study and validated by the coordinating investigator and the methodologist before the database freeze.

All analyses will follow the intention-to-treat principle, including all randomised patients in their assigned treatment group. Statisticians will not be blinded.

Sample size estimation

The sample size was calculated based on the expected incidence of the composite primary outcome in both study groups and the targeted effect size. We hypothesised that the incidence of the primary composite outcome in the control group would be 25%. Based on previous studies4 and clinical expectations, we anticipated a 50% relative risk reduction with the intervention (open-lung ventilation strategy), corresponding to an expected incidence of 12.5% in the intervention group.

The primary analysis will be based on a two-sided test of proportions, with the following assumptions: (1) Null hypothesis (H0): the incidence of the composite outcome is equal in both groups, Relative Risk (RR)=1.00 and (2) Alternative hypothesis (H1): the incidence of the composite outcome differs between groups, RR ≠ 1.00.

A two-sided significance level (alpha) of 0.05 is chosen to control for type I error. The study is designed to achieve a statistical power of 90% (1−β=0.90) to detect the prespecified effect size. Interim analyses will be conducted using the O’Brien-Fleming spending function to control for type I error inflation across multiple analyses.

Based on these assumptions, a total of 416 patients (208 per group) is required to demonstrate a statistically significant difference in the incidence of the composite primary outcome between groups. This calculation was performed using the nTerim software (V.1.1, Statistical Solutions, Cork, Ireland).

To account for potential losses to follow-up or withdrawals, the sample size has been increased by approximately 6%, resulting in a final planned enrolment of 440 patients (220 per group).

Data registration

All collected data will be recorded in a secure web-based electronic case report form by trial or clinical personnel under investigator supervision. The study database will be established from the electronic case report form, and data collection will be monitored by trained clinical research assistants.

Data access

The final trial data set will be accessible to the principal investigators and designated members of the research team at University Hospital of Rennes. Deidentified data may be made available on reasonable request following institutional and ethical guidelines. There are no contractual agreements that limit access for investigators.

Data handling and retention

Data will be handled according to French law. All original records (including consent forms and relevant correspondences) will be archived at trial sites for 15 years. The clean trial database file will be pseudoanonymised and maintained for 15 years.

Patient withdrawal

Participants may withdraw consent at any time without providing a reason. Those withdrawing will continue to receive standard clinical care in each participating centre. To conduct intention-to-treat analyses with the minimum of missing data, investigators may ask if the participant is willing to continue follow-up assessments and allow use of previously collected data. Whenever possible, the participant will be asked for permission to obtain data for the primary outcome measure. All randomised patients will be reported, and all data available with consent will be used in the analysis.

Safety

The primary safety concern is haemodynamic instability during RM application. However, this is expected to be transient and reversible on RM cessation. The risk appears minimal if the specified recommendations of haemodynamic stability before RM application are respected. Previous studies in abdominal surgery using similar levels of PEEP (6–8 cmH₂O) and RMs have not shown increased vasopressor use or higher fluid requirements compared with controls.4 In cardiac surgery patients, our group demonstrated good RM tolerance in a pilot study presented at the 2016 French Society of Anaesthesiology and Critical Care meeting (Demaure et al, Oral Communication, Quality/Risk Management Award). Findings were later published in Oxymag, Vol 31, Issue 158, January 2018.

Adverse events/adverse reactions/incidents will be reported to the various health surveillance circuits applicable to each product or practice concerned in compliance with current French legislation on human subject research on clinical trials (Jardé Law). Reports must specify the patient’s inclusion in a clinical trial and the relevant study details.

Scientific committee

A Scientific Committee will be established at trial initiation, consisting of at least five members including a methodologist or biostatistician, a member of a vigilance unit, a nurse anaesthetist and two anaesthesiologist–intensivists. This committee will oversee scientific, methodological and ethical trial aspects and monitor study progress. Interim analyses will be reviewed by the committee, which may recommend early trial termination in cases of demonstrated efficacy or ethical concerns (eg, unexpected complications or new evidence from external studies). The scientific Committee will meet at trial initiation, after each interim analysis, at study completion and as needed at the request of the sponsor, coordinating investigator or methodologist.

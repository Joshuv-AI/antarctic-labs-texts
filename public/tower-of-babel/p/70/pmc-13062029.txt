Coupling urbanization and landscape ecological risk in the agricultural-dominant Liaohe River Basin
Authors: Fei Song, Zihan Sun, Haoyu Wu, Haifu Li, Chenchen Cao, Deshen Xu, Fangli Su
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAs the main distribution area of cities, the rapid expansion of urbanization has profoundly altered landscape patterns in large and medium-sized river basins, triggering potential ecological risks. However, the dynamic relationship between urbanization and landscape ecological risks (LER) in agricultural-dominated river basins remains poorly understood, limiting effective environmental management. Focusing on the Mainstream of the Liaohe River Basin in northern China—a region characterized by intensive cropland and accelerating urbanization. Based on the landscape pattern indices and urbanization progress, this study employed a coupling coordination degree model to evaluate the spatiotemporal patterns and coupling relationship between urbanization and LER from 1990 to 2019. The results indicate that urbanization increased notably by 86.36%, primarily driven by infrastructure expansion. Meanwhile, the overall LER persisted at a moderate level, yet with substantial spatial heterogeneity. The dynamic coupling coordination degree (CCD) between urbanization and LER transitioned through three distinct phases: strengthening, weakening, and then strengthening again, yet it generally remained at a low level. Due to the wide distribution of cropland, the CCD is shown as urbanization lagging. These results not only revealed the spatiotemporal heterogeneity of LER under rapid urbanization but also underscored the implementing gradient management policies based on basin-specific urbanization-LER synchronization types. Introduction

The rapid development of the socio-economy has led to fast and significant urbanization, especially in countries and regions with active economic growth1. Urbanization brings multiple social, economic, and environmental impacts, both positive and negative2,3. On the positive side, it enhances land-use efficiency and sustainability4. However, the urban land expansion, industrial growth, and population concentration have also caused environmental degradation, including natural habitat loss5, reduction in biodiversity6, and the deterioration of the hydrological cycle7. By 2050, 68% of the world’s population will reside in cities, presenting both challenges and opportunities for sustainable development8. Thus, it is crucial to coordinate the relationship between urbanization and the environment to ensure that urbanization proceeds in a more ecologically way9.

Early human civilizations developed around rivers, making large and medium-sized river basins the primary areas for urban distribution globally10. These basins contain a mix of landscapes, including urban areas, forests, grasslands, water bodies, cropland, and other types. The interaction between these diverse landscape elements and human activities results in highly complex ecological processes11,12. In agriculture-dominated basins, the landscape matrix is primarily composed of cropland, a highly human-managed semi-natural ecosystem. This creates a unique context where urbanization does not merely replace natural landscapes but directly competes with agriculture for land and water resources13,14, a process known as land urbanization. Spatially, this manifests as the expansion of impervious surfaces, which fragments the basin and alters its landscape pattern15. These changes give rise to ecological issues such as increased landscape fragmentation, diminished connectivity, and a decline in ecosystem services16,17. Additionally, processes like population urbanization and economic urbanization have imposed significant environmental pressures on the regions18. Although efforts have been made in both policy and practice to balance urbanization and ecological protection, the inherent competition between the goals makes such coordination challenging19. This challenge is especially acute in agricultural basins, where policy constraints like the “Prime Farmland Protection” policy in China add another layer of complexity to land use competition20. Nevertheless, creating a region of urban-ecological synergistic development is fundamental for the sustainable development of future human societies21. It is therefore necessary to examine the relationship between urbanization and the ecological environment, specifically in the understudied context of agriculture-dominated basins.

Landscape ecological risks (LER) focus on the potential impact arising from the interaction of landscape patterns and ecological processes under various disturbances. Especially on landscape ecological risks caused by changes in land use/land cover, including risks within urban landscapes22 and those induced by urbanization in regional landscapes23. Due to the advantage of multi-scale comprehensive characterization of multi-source risks and spatiotemporal differentiation, landscape pattern indices have been a suitable method to assess LER24,25.

The synergistic development of urbanization and landscape ecological risks plays a positive role in supporting the achievement of sustainable development goals26. The coupling coordination degree (CCD) captures the interactions among system components and reflects their coordination level27. Recently, increasing attention has been paid to evaluating the CCD between urbanization and LER. In the highly urbanized Yangtze River Delta region of China, the relationship between urbanization and ecological environment follows an S-shaped curve, with the ecological environment lagging28. In contrast, in agricultural basins in northwest China’s desert areas, urbanization lags29. At the provincial level, studies showed that the connection between urbanization and the environment weakens as distance increases30, and a U-shaped relationship exists between them31. These findings suggest that the interactions between urbanization and landscape ecological risks remain insufficiently understood, particularly across regions differing in scale and type.

The Liaohe River Basin, one of China’s seven major river basins, forms the mainstream in Liaoning Province, covering the province’s major central urban clusters and plain areas. Urbanization in the basin is characterized by short duration and high intensity, and there are changes in ecological risks such as soil erosion, vegetation cover, and ecosystem services32,33. However, there has been no evaluation of the coupling coordination relationship and synchronization between urbanization and landscape ecological risks. Particularly under the policy context of Comprehensive Revitalization of Northeast China, assessing and analyzing the coupling coordination relationship between urbanization and landscape ecological risks in the mainstream of the Liaohe River basin (MLRB) is of great significance for the socio-economic development, ecological environment protection, and sustainable development of Liaoning Province.

In summary, this study focuses on the rapidly urbanizing period of 1990–2019 in the MLRB to evaluate the evolutionary trajectory of the coupling relationship between urbanization and landscape ecological risk. Specifically, the study includes: (1) assessing the spatiotemporal changes in urbanization. (2) Evaluating the spatiotemporal patterns of landscape ecological risks during urbanization. (3) analyzing the spatiotemporal variations in the coupling coordination degree between urbanization and landscape ecological risks. The results provide insights into the relationship between urbanization and landscape ecological risks at the scale of agriculture-dominated large and medium-sized river basins and offer references for sustainable urbanization.

Results

Dynamic characteristics of urbanization

Urbanization in the study area increased overall, rising from 0.044 in 1990 to 0.082 in 2019, with a growth rate of 86.36% (Fig. 1). Among these, land urbanization had the highest increase, with a growth rate of 66.67%. Population urbanization and economic urbanization showed significant increases between 1990 and 1995, and then generally rose steadily thereafter. The growth rate and level of population urbanization were higher than those of economic urbanization.

Urbanization process.

Urbanization showed significant spatial differences (Fig. 2). Low levels were mainly distributed on the outskirts of the study area, while lower levels were widely spread in the central part. Medium, higher, and high levels were mainly concentrated around urban areas. In terms of spatial shifts, the area where urbanization levels decreased covered 3670.49 km2, accounting for 58.50% of all areas with changes in urbanization. Among these, the largest area of transition was from lower to low type, covering 2654.82 km2, followed by a transition from medium to lower type, covering 1012.34 km2. The area where urbanization levels increased covered 2603.39 km2, with the largest transition being from lower to medium type, covering 1424.57 km2.

Dynamic characteristics of landscape ecological risks

Overall, LER remained at a medium risk level, decreasing from 0.0550 to 0.0522 over 30 years, a drop of 5.17% (Figs. 3 and 4). The LER declined more rapidly before 2010 compared to after. In terms of area distribution, MLRB is primarily composed of medium and higher LER zones, accounting for approximately 75% of the total area. In 1990, the higher LER zone was the largest, covering 41.47% of the area. From 1995, the medium LER zone became the largest, increasing from 45.22 to 55.24%, while the area of the higher zone decreased to 21.16%.

LER area changes at different levels.

Spatial distribution of LER.

Spatially, the LER showed a decreasing distribution pattern from south to north and from west to east. The low LER zone was mainly located in the outer areas of MLRB, where vegetation cover was higher, showing an outward diminishing trend in the northeast. In the southeast of Shenyang city, the low LER zone expanded with the growth of urban construction land. Overall, the areas of low, lower, and medium LER zones expanded, with each LER level shifting toward a lower risk.

Relationship between urbanization and landscape ecological risks

Temporal changes of CCD levels

The average CCD ranged from 0.395 to 0.420, indicating an overall state of slightly balanced development (Fig. 5), with an area proportion of 52.17% to 63.43%. The interaction between urbanization and LER showed a trend of strengthening—weakening—strengthening over time, with the highest value appearing in 2000 and the lowest in 2015. The proportion of areas with slight imbalance ranged from 33.48 to 41.25%, showing an inverted U-shaped development trend and a slight increase from 1990. The proportion of areas with severe imbalance ranged from 1.31 to 4.91%, showing a U-shaped development. The proportion of areas with moderate balance generally showed an increasing trend but remained small (1.07–2.49%).

Changes of CCD.

Spatially, high values were found in central Shenyang city, with lower values surrounding it, but an increasing trend was observed in the southeast (Figs. 6 and 7). Areas with severe imbalance were mainly located in the outer edge and the northeast of MLRB and were expanding, with land use types mainly involving forest and grassland. Areas with slight imbalance were primarily in the northeast and west, with land use types mainly involving forest, grassland, and cropland. Areas with slightly balanced development were widely distributed in the central region, while areas with moderately balanced development were mainly concentrated in the southeast.

Spatial distribution of CCD.

Spatial distribution of subsystem characteristic type.

Spatial distribution of CCD types

Most areas showed the urbanization lag (with an average area proportion of 74.4%). Type 3–3 was widely distributed area with an average proportion of 48.54%. Type 2–3 ranked second, concentrated in the western and northeastern parts, where the main land use type was cropland. The urbanization level in these regions was at a lower level, with the area proportion generally increasing (20.89–32.31%). Type 4–3 appeared starting in 2005 and reached its maximum in 2019, accounting for 0.42%, mainly distributed near the wetland nature reserve in the Liaohe estuary area. The synchronous development types (1–2, 2–2, 3–2, and 4–2) were sporadically distributed, with an average area proportion of 24.7%, showing an inverted U-shaped change over the years. Type 1–2 and 2–2 were located in the northeastern region, where both the UR and LER were at low levels, the primary land use types were cropland and forest. Type 3–2 and 4–2 were patchily distributed in the central part. The LER lag type was only represented by type 4–1. It covered a small area (average 0.64%) and was mainly concentrated within the urban area of Shenyang, the largest city in MLRB, with its increase over the past 30 years.

Discussion

Cropland, as the dominant landscape type in agriculture-dominated regions, is primarily lost to non-agricultural uses driven by urban demand, including land for recreation, industry, and commerce34,35. During urbanization, land urbanization has been more pronounced than population and economic urbanization—a pattern consistent with previous studies and characterized by dynamic interactions among these three dimensions36. The spatial evolution of both the extent and intensity of urbanization shows an agglomeration effect, reflecting the siphon effect of major cities during development. In this study, this is evidenced by urbanization expansion centered on Shenyang. Distinct patterns emerged among urbanization types: land urbanization mainly transitioned from high to low levels, while population and economic urbanization shifted from lower to higher levels. This reflects the trade-offs in agriculture-dominated basins among economic development, food security, cropland balance, and ecological redlines37. The significant population outflow experienced by Liaoning Province in recent years may have influenced urbanization rate assessments. Future research incorporating more comprehensive datasets could better account for this demographic dynamic.

The results revealed an overall declining trend in LER during urbanization, along with the spatiotemporal transition from high to lower LER areas, primarily driven by cropland and construction land. On one hand, urban expansion (edge expansion) and policies promoting the conversion of cropland to forest, grassland, and rivers have driven both land types toward concentration38, resulting in landscape patch aggregation at broad scales. Similar trends have been observed in the Yellow River and Yangtze River Basins39,40, whereas in non-agriculture-dominated basins, LER has shown an initial increase followed by a decline41. At finer scales, particularly in areas with intensive human activity—such as peri-urban zones, industrial sites, and scattered reclamation—landscape fragmentation has intensified, exacerbating ecological risks42,43. In the MLRB, natural landscapes outside forests continue to experience high LER and significant ecological pressure. Wetlands remain predominantly in high LER areas, mainly due to intense human disturbance, particularly around the Liaohe estuary, leading to high landscape fragmentation. However, with the implementation of wetland protection policies, wetland LER is trending positively44,45. Grasslands, water bodies, and barren land are sporadically distributed across the study area and remain vulnerable to human activities, with LER persisting at high levels. On the other hand, the calculation of LER is also influenced by the vulnerability values assigned to different land uses, particularly construction land. However, the stability and low LER observed in cropland and urban landscapes effectively consolidate the existing landscape pattern. While this reflects orderliness at the patch level, it may mask the inherent vulnerability and sensitivity of natural ecosystems, reduce habitat quantity and diversity, and hinder natural ecological succession processes. Therefore, applications of these findings should be integrated with considerations of ecosystem services and biodiversity46,47. Overall, the closer natural landscapes are to urban clusters, the higher their LER; conversely, in areas lacking natural landscapes, LER tends to be lower48.

The CCD has shown a positive trend in recent years, indicating a reduction in the conflict between urbanization and LER. High CCD values were mainly found in the central urban areas of Shenyang and Panjin. As the economic development center of Liaoning Province, Shenyang has a higher level of urbanization and industrial structure compared to other cities, with a strong emphasis on ecological protection, leading to improved coupling coordination between urbanization and LER49. In Shenyang, the inner city mainly exhibits landscape ecological risk lag (4–1), while the outskirts primarily show urbanization lag (4–3). Panjin, with the protection of the Liaohe estuary wetlands and changes in strategies for oil extraction, aquaculture, and rice cultivation industries50,51, has a CCD slightly better than other cities outside of Shenyang. The synchronization types in Panjin fluctuate between the 3–3 and 4–3 types of urbanization lag. Tieling and Fuxin have relatively low levels of urbanization, with the main land use types being forest, cropland, and grassland, resulting in significant ecological constraints and a lower urbanization process. Consequently, their overall CCD is not high, with synchronization types mainly being 1–3 and 2–3 of urbanization lag, as well as 2–2 of synchronous development. The 3–3 of urbanization lag is the most widely distributed because the MLRB is predominantly covered by cropland, which underscores the critical role of cropland as a stabilizer in the urban-agricultural ecological nexus in the agriculture-dominated basin. The coupling evolution mechanism is thus profoundly shaped by the interplay of policies (e.g., cropland protection, ecological redlines), institutional arrangements (e.g., local fiscal incentives, performance assessment), and ecological endowment (e.g., temperature, precipitation, terrain) constraints. As observed in the governance transitions of the Austrian Danube, changes in ecological dynamics can trigger responses within social systems, which in turn produce institutions that shift operational activities and ecological outcomes52. It is noteworthy that our observed patterns, including the trend toward land use aggregation and the overall improvement in CCD, emerged against the backdrop of the macro-strategic “Revitalize the Northeast” policy. While our study does not test causal drivers, the policy’s overarching goals of promoting intensive development, ecological restoration, and industrial upgrading provide a plausible and significant context for understanding the evolving relationship between urbanization and landscape sustainability in the region. In future research, differentiated management strategies—particularly those related to agricultural and ecological policies—may serve as key factors shaping the relationship between landscape ecological risk and urbanization. Therefore, scenario simulations or driving factor identification can be employed to support more precise management decisions53.

CCD is a comprehensive indicator measuring the degree of contradiction between two systems. When this contradiction decreases and develops in a favorable direction, CCD shows an upward trend. As a key engine of socio-economic development, urbanization will continue to advance in the future, making it necessary to minimize LER levels. From a basin-scale perspective, differentiated management strategies are proposed for different zones:

For LER lag areas. In regions far from urban centers, protection of natural landscapes should be strengthened to provide more ecosystem services and ecological sources supporting basin-wide development. In urban and surrounding areas, moderate development should be pursued with full consideration of ecological protection, land use types, and ecological carrying capacity, aiming to alleviate urban land pressure while meeting development needs. Governments should strengthen supervision mechanisms and establish tiered risk management strategies to promote coordinated development between urbanization and LER. For UR lag areas. These areas are dominated by cropland. Based on considerations of food security and critical ecological functions, urban expansion should proceed cautiously to avoid encroachment on cropland and natural landscapes. Intensive cropland management should be strengthened to further reduce LER and maintain a balanced CCD between urbanization and LER. For synchronous development areas. Higher-quality development models should be adopted with strict land use planning policies to prevent disorderly expansion and enhance risk resistance capacity. In the future, detailed management schemes can be developed at smaller watershed, township, and grid scales54, combined with zoning based on driving factors, to achieve integrated management and synergistic improvement of multiple objectives.

Methods

Study area

The mainstream of the Liaohe River basin (MLRB) is located in the central urban cluster of Liaoning Province, encompassing Tieling City, Fushun City, Fuxin City, Anshan City, Jinzhou City, Panjin City, and the provincial capital Shenyang City (Fig. 8). It covers an area of 37,600 km2, with cropland accounting for 71%.

Methodology

The study area was divided into a 5 km grid to assess LER by landscape pattern indices. All data processing and spatial analyses were conducted using QGIS3.28 (https://www.qgis.org/download/). Urbanization was quantified from population, GDP, and construction land proportion after spatialization. Division of the evaluation unit

The division of evaluation unit grids typically uses a standard of 2–5 times the average patch area55. In this study, a grid size of 5 km was chosen as the basic unit, resulting in a total of 1644 evaluation units (Supplementary Materials Fig. S1).

Assessment of the urbanization process

This study evaluates the urbanization process from three dimensions: population urbanization, economic urbanization, and land urbanization. The total population at the end of the year (POP), gross domestic product (GDP), and construction land proportion (CL) were used for quantification. The results were classified into five levels—low, lower, medium, higher, and high by the natural breaks method. The calculation formula is as follows:

In the formula, UR represents the urbanization process. Following the principle of correlation between economic and population factors and land use, POP and GDP were spatialized by intersecting construction land vectors, grid vectors (5 km × 5 km), and administrative boundaries, with each grid cell weighted by area proportion. Kernel density analysis was then applied to all three factors, with the spatial resolution consistent with the land use data (30 m). All three indicators were subsequently normalized to a 0–1 range using the fuzzy linear membership function in QGIS3.28. The UR for each grid was calculated as the mean of the normalized POP, GDP, and CL values.

Given that cropland accounts for an exceptionally high proportion (71%) of MLRB, applying the conventional method48 resulted in nearly the entire region being categorized as a single type (UR lagging), thereby obscuring internal spatial heterogeneity. To enable a more detailed and regionally meaningful analysis, we employed the natural breaks classification method (the thresholds are 0.2 and 0.46). Data source

The land use data is sourced from the 30-m annual land cover product for China created by 59, which includes seven land use types: cropland, forest, grassland, water body, barren land, construction land, and wetlands (https://zenodo.org/records/4417810). Based on this dataset, this study further refined the land use classification in the study area through manual interpretation and comparison with high-resolution Google Earth imagery. The overall accuracy of the refined classification was evaluated at the sample level, reaching 82% based on validation against 1000 randomly generated reference points (Supplementary Materials Fig. S3). The DEM data were obtained from the National Aeronautics and Space Administration (https://search.asf.alaska.edu/#/) with a resolution of 12.5 m. Due to partial data gaps in the region spanning 120°0′0′′–123°0′0′′E and 41°0′0′′–42°5′0′′N, we supplemented the dataset using 30 m resolution DEM data sourced from the Geospatial Data Cloud (https://www.gscloud.cn/). The data for population urbanization and economic urbanization were sourced from the Liaoning Statistical Yearbook from 1990 to 2020 (https://tjj.ln.gov.cn/tjj/tjsj/index.shtml). The administrative boundaries were sourced from National Catalogue Service For Geographic Information (https://cloudcenter.tianditu.gov.cn/administrativeDivision).

Conclusion

This study investigated the spatiotemporal dynamics of urbanization- LER coupling coordination in the mainstream of Liaohe River basin and informed landscape management frameworks in large and medium-sized agricultural basins. Key findings reveal that:

From 1990 to 2019, urbanization in the MLRB increased significantly by 86.36%, with land urbanization progressing more rapidly than population and economic urbanization. Concurrently, LER decreased by 5.17% during the urbanization process, with all risk categories shifting toward lower levels.

The CCD between urbanization and LER generally maintained a moderately balanced state, temporally evolving through phases of enhancement, decline, and renewed enhancement. Spatially, higher CCD values clustered in the central basin, encircled by lower values, with a noticeable increasing trend in the southeast. Influenced by cropland distribution and policy interventions, the “urbanization lag” type emerged as the predominant synchronization pattern across the basin.

Recommendations are proposed for different coordination types: enhance ecological protection and implement tiered management in risk lag areas; promote intensive cropland management while restricting urban expansion in urbanization lag zones; pursue high-quality development with strict ecological controls in synchronous development regions. These measures collectively form a governance framework for synergistic urban-ecological development in agricultural basins.

CircZMYM2 plays a pivotal role in osteosarcoma by regulating the translation of NACA and ARPC1B
Authors: Fei Li, Jiaqing Wei, Xiaoxuan Ren, Yawei Hao, Shuaishuai Tian, Lizhi Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractOsteosarcoma (OS) is a primary malignant bone tumor in children and young adults because of its intertumoral heterogeneity and absence of molecular targets. This study aimed to elucidate the role of circular RNA in the pathogenesis of OS. Using bioinformatics analysis and OS clinical samples, we found that hsa_circ_0029634 formed during the splicing process of Zinc finger MYM-type containing 2 (circZMYM2) was highly expressed in OS tissues. Nuclear cap-binding protein subunit 2 regulated circZMYM2 bio-generation by binding to flanking sequences of ZMYM2 transcripts. In addition, knockdown circZMYM2 inhibited cell growth and metastasis in OS cell lines in vitro and inhibited tumor growth in vivo. Mechanistically, circZMYM2 competitively bound to FXR1 to regulate the translation of NACA and ARPC1B. CircZMYM2 may be a crucial regulator of OS tumorigenesis and a potential diagnostic and therapeutic biomarker for OS patients.Keywords: OS, circZMYM2, FXR1, NACA, ARPC1B

Introduction

Osteosarcoma (OS) is a primary malignant bone tumor, originating from mesenchymal cells and predominantly affecting children and young adults [1,2]. The overall 5-year survival rate of patients with bone-located OS ranges from 65 % to 75 % [3]. However, in approximately 75 % of cases, OS may spread to adjacent tissues, significantly reducing the survival rate of recurrent and metastatic OS patients to just 20 % [4]. Hence, there is an urgent need for specific diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers for these patients, along with a deeper understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying carcinogenesis, necessitating further research [5].

Circular RNAs (circRNAs) are a novel class of non-coding RNAs characterized by a unique, single-stranded, covalently closed structure formed by the back-splicing of pre-mRNA [[6], [7], [8]]. Recently, a plethora of circRNAs have been identified, owing to advancements in next-generation sequencing and other biotechnologies. In addition, circRNAs possess many crucial regulatory functions, such as serving as competitive endogenous RNAs to sequester miRNAs, modulating the expression of downstream genes [[9], [10], [11]], and interacting with RNA-binding proteins (RBP) to regulate their functions [12,13]. Some of these circRNAs are crucial regulators of cancer, developmental processes, and various other biological phenomena [[14], [15], [16]]. Furthermore, the structural stability and abundance of circRNAs make them significant diagnostic and therapeutic candidates [17,18]. Nevertheless, the precise function of circRNAs in OS remains elusive.

In this study, we identified an oncogenic circRNA, hsa_circ_0029634 (formed during the splicing process of Zinc finger MYM-type containing 2), as the most differentially and highly expressed in OS tissues. For this purpose, reanalysis of RNA-seq and microarray data (GEO: GSE126209 and CSE96964) was conducted, which was confirmed via real-time quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR). Importantly, our findings revealed that circZMYM2 knockdown inhibited the malignant progression of OS cells through translational upregulation of NACA and ARPC1B expression mediated by FXR1. This study aimed to elucidate the molecular roles and biogenetic mechanisms of circZMYM2 to provide a novel diagnostic and therapeutic target for OS patients.

Materials and methods

Patients and tissue samples. Formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) and frozen OS tissues, as well as adjacent normal tissues, were obtained from OS patients following surgical resection. All the research adhered to the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Second Hospital of Shanxi Medical University (2022YX216), and all patients provided signed informed consent.

Cell culture. Human 143 B, U-2OS, and 293T cell lines were procured from Jinyuan Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). The authenticity of the cell line was validated through short-tandem repeat profiling. Additionally, all cell lines were confirmed to be mycoplasma-free using the Venor GeM Mycoplasma Detection Kit (Minerva Biolabs, Germany). 293T cells were cultured in Dulbecco's Modified Eagle's medium (Gibco, USA) supplemented with 10 % fetal bovine serum (FBS, Gibco) and 100 U/mL penicillin–streptomycin (Thermo Fisher Scientific). U-2OS cells were cultured in McCoy's 5A medium (Gibco) supplemented with 10 % FBS (Gibco) and 100 U/mL penicillin–streptomycin (Thermo Fisher Scientific), while 143 B cells were cultured in minimum essential medium (Gibco) supplemented with 10 % FBS (Gibco) and 100 U/mL penicillin–streptomycin (Thermo Fisher Scientific). All cell lines were maintained in a 5 % CO2 atmosphere at 37 °C.

In vivo xenograft assay. Four-week-old female BALB/c nude (nu/nu) mice were purchased from Beijing HFK Bio-Technology.co., LTD (Beijing, China) and were housed under specific pathogen-free conditions. Subcutaneous injections of approximately 5 × 106 cells/mL U-2OS cells in Matrigel (Corning, USA) were administered to BALB/c nude (nu/nu) mice. Tumor length and width were measured every 7 days, and tumor volumes were calculated using the formula: volume (mm³) = (length × width [2])/2. Tumor weights were recorded at 35 days post-injection. All animal studies were conducted in accordance with protocols approved by the Ethics Committee of the Second Hospital of Shanxi Medical University.

RNA extraction and reverse transcription. The FFPE Total RNA Extraction Kit (TIANGEN, Beijing, China) was employed to extract total RNA from FFPE OS tissues following the manufacturer's instructions. Total RNA was extracted from 143 B and U-2OS cells, as well as from frozen tissues, using the TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, USA) in accordance with the manufacturer's protocols. Subsequently, 2 μg of total RNA was subjected to DNase I treatment (Promega, USA) and reverse transcription utilizing the PrimeScript™ RT reagent Kit (TAKARA, JAPAN); random primers or Oligo(dT) were used to assess the expressions of circRNAs and other genes.

Reverse transcriptase PCR (RT-PCR) and RT-qPCR. RT-PCR was performed employing the PrimeSTAR Max Premix (TAKARA) and separated on a 1.5 % agarose gel. RT-qPCR was performed in 10 μL reaction volumes containing amplification primers (Sangon Biotech, Shanghai, China) and SYBR Green PCR master mix (Invitrogen). The relative expression levels of target genes were calculated with the 2−ΔΔCt method. The primer sequences are listed in Table S1.

Actinomycin D (ACTD) treatment assay. OS cells with 70 % intensity were treated with 1 μg/mL ACTD (Sigma-Aldrich, USA) against new RNA synthesis for 0, 2, 4, 8, 12 and 24 h. Subsequently, circZMYM2 and ZMYM2 expressions were measured at various time points via RT-qPCR.

Nucleocytoplasmic separation. Nuclear and cytoplasmic RNA fractions of 143 B and U-2OS cells were extracted utilizing the Paris kit (Invitrogen) according to the manufacturer's instructions. Specifically, 2 × 10⁶ cells were harvested using trypsin-EDTA (Invitrogen) and washed with PBS. After centrifugation, the supernatants were carefully removed. The cell pellet was resuspended by adding 200 μL of ice-cold CER I buffer and vortexing for 15 s. The tubes were incubated on ice for 10 min. Following this, 11 μL of CER II buffer was added; the tube was vortexed for 5 s, and incubated on ice for 1 min. The mixture was centrifuged at 16,000×g for 5 min in a microcentrifuge, and the supernatant (cytoplasmic extract) was immediately transferred to a pre-chilled tube and kept on ice. The insoluble (nuclear) fraction was washed twice with CERI buffer and centrifuged at 16,000×g for 10 min. The supernatants were discarded, And RNA from both the cytoplasmic and nuclear fractions was isolated using the TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen).

Single molecular fluorescence in situ hybridization (SmFISH). smFISH was performed according to the Stellaris RNA FISH protocol, and the Quasar 670 labeled probes for ARPC1B and NACA mRNA were designed with BIOSEARCH (https://www.biosearchtech.com/support/tools/design-software/stellaris-probe-designer) (USA). The Quasar 570 labeled circZMYM2 probes targeting its back-splicing sites were used. All probes were synthesized employing BGI (Beijing, China). Cells were seeded on coverslips, fixed with 4 % formaldehyde solution for 15 min, and washed 3 times. The cells were then permeabilized with 70 % ethanol for 1 h at 4 °C and washed with FISH wash buffer (10 % deionized formamide contained 2 × SSC) once. For hybridization, 12.5 μM probes for each target were dissolved in hybridization buffer, and the cells were hybridized at 37 °C overnight in a humidified chamber. The cells were then washed twice with wash buffer and mounted with a mounting medium containing DAPI. The slices were photographed with a Nikon Ti microscope. The probe sequences are listed in Table S2.

Plasmid construction, transfection, lentivirus packaging, and infection. Specific shRNAs targeting circZMYM2 or FXR1 and the scrambled sequences were cloned into the PLKO.1-TRC plasmid to knockdown circZMYM2 or FXR1 expression. The shRNA sequences used are listed in Table S1. The CDS of FXR1 was inserted into the pCDNA3.1 vectors to overexpress FXR1. We inserted circZMYM2 wild-type and FXR1 binding sites mutant fragments into the psiCHECK2 vector downstream of Renilla luciferase.

All transfection experiments were conducted using Lipofectamine™ 3000 Transfection Reagent (Invitrogen) following the manufacturer's instructions. For circZMYM2 and FXR1 knockdown, pLKO.1-circZMYM2-sh-1, pLKO.1-circZMYM2-sh-2, pLKO.1-FXR1-sh-1, pLKO.1-FXR1-sh-2, along with their package plasmids (pMG2.G and psPAX2), were transfected into 293T cells for lentivirus generation. The lentivirus supernatant was collected after filtering through 0.45-mm filters at 48 and 72 h. The 143B and U-2OS cells were then infected for 24 h and selected with 1 μg/mL puromycin (Sigma-Aldrich).

Cell proliferation assays and wound-healing assay. 143B and U-2OS cells (5 × 103) were re-seeded into 96-well plates. At the indicated time points, cell viability was detected using a Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8; Beyotime, China) at 450 nm for each well. For cell colony formation assays, the cells were seeded in six-well plates (5 × 103 cells per well). After 2 weeks, the cells were stained with a crystal violet staining solution (Beyotime). For the wound-healing assay, cells were seeded in a 12-well plate with serum-free medium. A sterile 100 μL plastic pipette tip was used to create a scratch in the single cell layer. Scratch widths were obtained at 0 and 48 h at the same locations using a microscope (Nikon, Japan).

Migration and invasion assays. Transwells (8-mm pore size, Corning) were utilized for both invasion and migration assays. For migration assays, 143B and U-2OS cells (1 × 105 cells per well) containing serum-free medium were re-seeded in small chambers, and 600 μL of cell culture medium supplemented with 20 % FBS was added in the bottom chambers at 37 °C with 5 % CO2 for 48 h. For the invasion assay, 1 × 105 cells were replated into the Matrigel (Corning) pre-treated chambers, and 600 μL of cell culture medium were added in the bottom chambers at 37 °C with 5 % CO2 for 48 h. The cells were stained with crystal violet staining solution (Beyotime, China). Images were captured with a microscope (Nikon, Japan).

RIP assays. RIP assay was performed using a RIP kit (Millipore, USA) with anti-nuclear cap-binding protein subunit 2 (NCBP2) (Proteintech, Wuhan, China) and anti-IgG antibodies (Millipore) according to the manufacturer's instructions. Specifically, 10⁷ cells were harvested and washed with cold PBS. The cells were then resuspended in 300 μL of RIP lysis buffer and incubated on ice for 10 min. After centrifugation at 14,000×g for 10 min at 4 °C, 5 % of the supernatant was collected as input, and the remainder was incubated overnight at 4 °C with an anti-NCBP2 antibody. An anti-IgG antibody (IgG RIP) was used as a control. The following day, 100 μL washed A/G magnetic beads were added to the antibody-lysate mixture and incubated for 2 h at 4 °C to allow the antibody-protein-RNA complexes to bind to the beads. The beads were then washed five times with 1 mL of RIP wash buffer at 4 °C for 5 min. Finally, RNA was isolated from the beads using the TRIzol reagent.

smFISH combined with IF. The smFISH + IF was performed according to the Stellaris® RNA IF + FISH protocol. Cells were seeded on coverslips, fixed with 4 % formaldehyde solution for 15 min, and washed three times. The cells were permeabilized with 0.5 % Triton X-100 for 15 min at 4 °C and washed with PBS three times. Cells were blocked with 1 % BSA Solution, incubated with FXR1 antibody (1:200) at 4 °C overnight, washed 3 times with PBS, and incubated with donkey anti-rabbit 488 antibody (1:200) for 1 h in dark. After washing 3 times with PBS, the cells were re-fixed with 4 % formaldehyde solution for 15 min, washed three times, and subjected to smFISH staining.

Crosslinked RNA pulldown assay Cells were washed three times with ice-cold PBS, fixed with 2 % formaldehyde solution for 15 min at room temperature, and quenched with 0.125 M glycine for 10 min. Cells were washed thrice with ice-cold PBS and then collected by scraping and centrifuged at 1,000 g for 5 min at 4 °C. Cells were lysed with lysis buffer (50 mM Tris–HCl pH 7.0, 10 mM EDTA, 1 % SDS, 1 mM PMSF, protease inhibitors) and subjected to sonication on ice for 5 min (3 s on, 10 s off, 40 %). The lysate were then centrifuged at 12,000 g for 10 min at 4 °C, and the supernatant was collected. Washed Dynabeads Myone Streptavidin C1 (30 μL) (Thermo) were added and rotated for 30 min rotated at 37 °C for preclearing. Hybridization was conducted by adding 100 pmol of biotinylated scramble oligos and back-splicing site-targeted circZMYM2 probes and incubating at 37 °C with rotation for 6 h. The mixture was then incubated with 100 μL of washed Dynabeads Myone Streptavidin C1 (Thermo) at 37 °C for 30 min, followed by washing six times with wash buffer (2 × SSC, 0.5 % SDS, 1 mM PMSF). Trizol was used to purify the RNAs after pulldown. The proteins were eluted utilizing LDS loading buffer (Thermo) at 95 °C for 30 min and analyzed via Western blot or mass spectrometry after silver staining (Thermo). The probes used in this study are listed in Table S2.

Protein isolation and Western blot. For the Western blot assay, total protein from 143 B and U-2OS cells and frozen OS tissues was extracted using protein lysis buffer containing proteinase inhibitor cocktail (Sigma-Aldrich). Proteins were separated utilizing SDS-PAGE and transferred to a polyvinylidene fluoride membrane (Millipore, USA). The membranes were incubated with specific primary antibodies for 1 h and then with secondary antibodies for 1 h. The antibodies used in this study are listed in Table S3.

Ribosome profiling. Cells of 80 % confluence were treated with 100 μg/mL cycloheximide (CHX, sigma) for 10 min at 37 °C and then washed twice with ice-cold CHX containing PBS. Cells were lysed with 300 μL polysome lysis buffer (20 mM Tris–HCl pH 7.4, 150 mM NaCl, 5 mM MgCl2, 1 % TritonX-100, 1 mM DTT, 100 μg/mL CHX) for 10 min on ice and centrifuged at 12,000 g for 10 min at 4 °C. The 15%–50 % (w/v) sucrose gradient was dissolved in polysome buffer (20 mM Tris–HCl pH 7.4, 150 mM NaCl, 5 mM MgCl2, 1 mM DTT, 100 μg/mL CHX) and prepared with a Gradient Master (BIOCOMP, USA). After loading the lysate on the sucrose gradient in SW41Ti tubes, cells were centrifuged in an ultracentrifuge (Beckman, USA) at 38,000 rpm for 3 h at 4 °C. Fractions were collected using a Piston Gradient Fractionator (BIOCOMP).

Dual-luciferase reporter gene assays. WT or mutant circZMYM2 psiCHECK2 vector (100 ng) was transfected into FXR1-or vector-overexpressing U-2OS cells. After 24 h, Firefly and Renilla luciferase activities were detected with a dual-luciferase reporter assay kit and dual-luciferase reporter assay system (Promega). The relative Renilla luciferase activity was calculated by normalizing the Firefly luciferase activity. Total RNA was extracted from transfected U-2OS cells using TRIzol LS. Relative Renilla luciferase gene expression was determined via RT-qPCR.

Bioinformatics analysis. For the circRNA analysis, OS data (GSE126209 and GSE96964) were downloaded from the NCBI GEO database. These circRNAs were annotated using circBase. For RNA-seq data (GSE126209), CIRI2, CIRCexplorer2, and find_circ were utilized to characterize the circRNAs. After alignment with the reference genome, human hg19, HISAT2, Bowtie2, and StringTie were employed to reassemble the sequenced transcriptome. The circRNAs were quantified using a modified version of edgeR in CIRIquant and annotated with circBase. For the microarray data (GSE96964), the limma package was used to identify differentially expressed circRNAs. For mRNA analysis, OS data were downloaded from the NCBI GEO database (GSE12865) and the RTCGA package. The EdgeR or DEseq2 package (version 3.12.1) was utilized to identify differentially expressed circRNAs or mRNAs. Fold changes >0.5 and p-values <0.05 were considered as significantly differentially expressed circRNAs or mRNAs. The ggplot2 or pheatmap packages in R were used to display the volcano and heat maps.

Statistical analysis. GraphPad Prism 8 (version 8.0.1, USA) was employed for all the statistical analyses. The analyses were performed at least in triplicate, and the means were subjected to two-tailed unpaired Student's t-tests. Values of ∗p < 0.05, ∗∗p < 0.01, and ∗∗∗p < 0.001 denote statistical significance, and ns indicates no significance.

Results

Identification and characterization of hsa_circ_0029634 (circZMYM2) in OS

To identify differentially expressed circRNAs in OS tissues, we re-analyzed publicly available GEO datasets (GES126209 and CSE96964). Five markedly differentially expressed genes were found in tissues from OS patients (hsa_circ_0003549, hsa_circ_0008274, hsa_circ_0004276, hsa_circ_0029634, hsa_circ_0051449) (Fig. 1A). Subsequently, we assessed the expression of these five circRNAs in tissues from OS patients (n = 10) after surgery and found that hsa_circ_0029634 exhibited significantly higher expression in OS tissues than in other tissues (Fig. 1B and Figs. S1A–D). Consequently, our study focused on hsa_circ_0029634 formed during the splicing process of ZMYM2-circZMYM2 (Fig. 1C). RT-PCR confirmed the detection of circZMYM2 using convergent primers spanning its back-splicing site (Fig. 1D). Random and oligo dT primers were employed to confirm the circular characteristics of circZMYM2. The relative expression level of circZMYM2 markedly decreased when oligo-dT primers were used, whereas the expression of ZMYM2 remained unaltered, indicating the absence of a poly(A) tail in circZMYM2 (Fig. 1E). Using ACTD, an inhibitor of mRNA synthesis, to halt the generation of nascent mRNA in U-2OS and 143 B cells, we found that the half-life of circZMYM2 was longer than that of its linear transcript (Fig. 1F). Moreover, a nuclear-cytoplasmic separation assay demonstrated that circZMYM2 was primarily localized in the cytoplasm of U-2OS and 143 B cells (Fig. 1G). SmFISH also showed that circZMYM2 is a cytoplasmic circRNA (Fig. 1H). Overall, these findings indicated that circZMYM2 predominantly exists in the cytoplasm of OS cells and is highly expressed in OS tissues.

Formation of circZMYM2 is regulated by NCBP2 in OS cells

Previous studies have reported that 103 RBPs are involved in formation of circRNAs [19]. To elucidate the biogenetic regulation of circZMYM2 in OS, we re-analyzed the expression of RBP in OS tissues using the publicly available GEO dataset (GES12865) and identified six out of 103 RBPs that were significantly differentially expressed in OS patients (Fig. 2A). This differential expression was confirmed in OS tissues (Fig. 2B) (n = 10). Subsequently, we individually silenced these six RBPs in U-2OS and 143 B cells, revealing that only NCBP2 led to a significant decrease in circZMYM2 expression, without affecting the expression of linear ZMYM2 (Fig. 2C and E). Consequently, we postulated that NCBP2 regulates the formation of circZMYM2 in OS. Previous studies have highlighted the importance of RBP and intronic complementary sequences in circRNA generation [20,21]. We detected NCBP2 binding to the flanking intronic regions of circZMYM2 using RIP-qPCR. The results showed that the flanking intronic regions of circZMYM2 were significantly enriched by anti-NCBP2 antibody (Fig. 2F and G). Additionally, circZMYM2 expression positively correlated with NCBP2 expression in OS tissues (n = 10) (Fig. 2H). These findings strongly suggest that the formation of circZMYM2 is regulated by NCBP2 through its binding to the flanking intronic sequences of ZMYM2 pre-mRNA.

circZMYM2 knockdown inhibits the malignant progression of OS cells

To investigate the potential functions of circZMYM2 in OS, we utilized a lentiviral shRNA system to establish circZMYM2 knockdown U-2OS and 143 B cells. The results demonstrated that two shRNAs targeting the back-splicing site of circZMYM2 specifically silenced circZMYM2 expression without affecting ZMYM2 expression in both U-2OS and 143 B cells (Fig. 3A). Proliferation assays, including CCK-8 and cell clone formation assays, revealed marked suppression of U-2OS and 143 B cell growth following circZMYM2 knockdown (Fig. 3B and C). Subsequently, we examined the protein levels of cell cycle marker genes (Cyclin D1 and CDK4) and cell apoptosis marker gene (BCL2) to determine whether circZMYM2 influences the cell cycle and apoptosis processes. Western blot results indicated a significant downregulation of Cyclin D1, CDK4, and BCL2 protein levels in circZMYM2 knockdown U-2OS and 143 B cells (Fig. 3D). Additionally, wound healing and transwell assays demonstrated that circZMYM2 knockdown inhibited the migration and invasion of U-2OS and 143 B cell lines (Fig. 3E–G). Consistent with the in vitro findings, in vivo xenograft results showed significant reductions in both tumor volume and weight in the sh-circZMYM2 group compared with those of the controls (Fig. 3H). Taken together, these results suggest that circZMYM2 knockdown suppresses the proliferation and metastasis of OS cells both in vitro and in vivo.

CircZMYM2 serves as an efficient sponge for FXR1 in OS cells

The ncRNAs localized in the cytoplasm may participate in the regulation of RNA stability or translation through interactions with RBPs [20]. To determine whether circZMYM2 functions as an RBP sponge, we performed RNA pull-down experiments to identify the cytoplasmic proteins that bind to circZMYM2. Mass spectrometry identified specific bands or all eluted proteins that were significantly enriched with circZMYM2 compared to the scramble control (Fig. 4A). The RT-qPCR results showed that circZMYM2 probes massively enriched circZMYM2 compared to scrambled probes after RNA pull-down in OS cells; GAPDH was used as the non-enrichment control (Fig. 4B). In total, 2287 proteins that specifically interacted with circZMYM2 were identified (Fig. 4C). Subsequently, through predictions using the RBPsuite website combined with mass spectrometry results [22], we found that FXR1 and IGF2BP1 may bind to circZMYM2 (Fig. 4C). To further verify this result, we performed Western blot analysis of the purified proteins obtained from the RNA pull-down experiment, which showed that circZMYM2 interacts with FXR1 (Fig. 4D).

The FXR family of proteins has mainly been identified as translation activators that can transport target mRNAs to different intracellular compartments [23]. Next, to explore whether FXR1 regulates downstream target genes through post-transcriptional translation pathways, we obtained 1954 potential FXR1 binding mRNAs through website prediction [24] (Fig. 4E). Additionally, database analysis identified 21,887 mRNAs that remained unchanged and 775 proteins that were differentially expressed in OS patient tissues, among which two mRNAs may be FXR1 target genes [25] (Fig. 4E). Next, we detected changes in the mRNA and protein levels of NACA and ARPC1B in the OS tissues. The results showed that compared to those in adjacent non-cancerous tissues, the protein levels of NACA and ARPC1B were significantly downregulated in OS tissues, but their mRNA levels were not altered (Fig. 4F–H). To further verify whether circZMYM2 regulates downstream target genes through FXR1, we conducted smFISH + IF experiments in U-2OS cells, which confirmed that the co-localization of FXR1 and NACA or ARPC1B mRNA increased after circZMYM2 knockdown (Fig. 4I and J). This result demonstrated that circZMYM2 preferentially binds FXR1 over NACA and ARPC1B mRNA.

CircZMYM2 regulates the translation of NACA and ARPC1B by binding to FXR1

To confirm whether FXR1 regulates the expression of NACA and ARPC1B, we performed RT-qPCR and Western blot experiments on FXR1 knockdown U-2OS cells. FXR1 knockdown significantly reduced the protein levels of NACA and ARPC1B without affecting their mRNA levels (Fig. 5A and B). Furthermore, circZMYM2 knockdown significantly upregulated the protein levels of NACA and ARPC1B without affecting their mRNA levels (Fig. 5C and D). Finally, we conducted polysome fractionation experiments using U-2OS cells with circZMYM2 knockdown. The results showed that compared with that of the control group, the translational efficiency of NACA and ARPC1B increased after circZMYM2 knockdown (Fig. 5E and F). To further explore how circZMYM2 regulates FXR1, we used the RBPsuite website to predict the binding sites of circZMYM2 and FXR1. The results showed that FXR1 specifically bound to the AAUGACAA motif of circZMYM2 (Fig. 5G). We inserted circZMYM2 wild-type and binding-site mutant fragments into the Psicheck2 vector downstream of the Renilla luciferase and transfected these constructs into U2-OS cells overexpressing either FXR1 or a control vector for 24 h (Fig. 5G). FXR1 overexpression significantly increased the relative Renilla luciferase activity in the wild-type constructs, whereas no change was observed in the mutant constructs compared to the control (Fig. 5H). RT-qPCR results confirmed that Renilla luciferase expression levels remained unchanged across all groups (Fig. 5I).

Thus, circZMYM2 promotes OS progression by regulating the post-transcriptional translation of NACA and ARPC1B through competitive binding to FXR1.

Discussion

Recently, circRNAs have been reported to be involved in various human diseases, including cancers [26]. In addition, given their highly conserved and stable structures, circRNAs are considered ideal diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets for various diseases. In our study, we identified a significantly high expression of the circRNA circZMYM2 in OS patients (n = 10). circZMYM2 knockdown inhibited the malignant progression in OS cell lines. Mechanistically, circZMYM2 acted as a sponge for FXR1, thereby regulating NACA and ARPC1B protein levels. Thus, circZMYM2 may serve as a crucial regulator of OS tumorigenesis and represents a potential biomarker for the diagnosis and therapeutic targeting of OS.

Most studies on circRNAs have demonstrated that they function as miRNA sponges [11], act as decoys that affect protein localization, and serve as scaffolds to facilitate interactions between protein complexes [20]. For example, CircDOCK1 sponges miR-339-3p to regulate the expression of IGF1R, promotes progression, and regulates cisplatin sensitivity in OS [27]. Studies have shown that many circRNAs can bind to ribosomes, which are key components of translation. However, most ribosome-bound circRNAs are not translated into proteins, suggesting that circRNAs exert their function through ribosome binding. A recent study reported that circYAP negatively regulates the translation of its parental gene [28]. Another study revealed that circMYBL2 promotes the binding of PTBP1 to FLT3 mRNA, thereby enhancing the translation of downstream genes [13]. Our study demonstrated that circZMYM2 negatively regulates the translation of target genes by recruiting FXR1, indicating that circRNAs can positively or negatively regulate the translation of downstream target genes through ribosome binding. However, the mechanism by which circZMYM2 inhibits the binding of FXR1 to NACA and ARPC1B mRNAs remains unclear. Considering the changes in NACA and ARPC1B mRNA levels in OS cells treated with sh-circZMYM2 or sh-FXR1, translational regulation of NACA and ARPC1B mRNAs may contribute to the role of circZMYM2 or FXR1 in influencing FXR1 protein levels. Further research is required to determine whether circZMYM2 recognizes conserved RNA elements in NACA and ARPC1B mRNAs.

FXR1 is an attractive target for cancer therapy because it is widely expressed in almost all cancers and plays a crucial role in the post-transcriptional regulation of key genes related to tumor growth and survival [29]. For example, FXR1 undergoes liquid-liquid phase separation to bind ribosomal proteins to the translation machinery, and converting stored mRNA into a translationally activated state [23]. However, studies on FXR1-mediated translational regulation of downstream target genes of ncRNAs are scarce. In this study, we found that circZMYM2 is crucial for FXR1 function, as circZMYM2 knockdown decreases NACA and ARPC1B protein levels, suggesting that circZMYM2 is involved in the post-transcriptional regulation of downstream target genes by binding to FXR1. Although FXR1 and treatment resistance have been studied in pancreatic, breast, brain, and lung cancer, FXR1's post-transcriptional regulation of multidrug resistance-related genes supports its potential as a therapeutic target [29]. However, our study lacks evidence for the relationship between FXR1 and OS in a clinical setting, which requires further investigation. In addition, the precise mechanisms underlying the translational regulation of FXR1, which mediates the translation of NACA and ARPC1B, remain largely unexplored. In future studies, we will focus on the mechanisms of action of FXR1 in translational regulation of OS.

Recent studies have highlighted the roles of extracellular vesicles and exosomes as diagnostic markers and therapeutic targets for cancers [30,31]. Exosomes influence the occurrence, development, and metastasis of OS [32]. Owing to their stable structures, circRNAs encapsulated in extracellular vesicles or exosomes offer significant advantages as disease diagnostic biomarkers for liquid biopsies, particularly for the early diagnosis and treatment of tumors [33]. Indeed, exosomes containing circRNAs have been confirmed to play important roles as novel biomarkers, therapeutic targets, and chemotherapy resistance [34]. One limitation of this study was the lack of examination of circZMYM2 expression in liquid samples, such as blood or urine. Future studies will involve the collection of blood and urine samples to determine whether circZMYM2 expression is significantly higher in OS patients compared to normal donors.

In summary, our study revealed that circZMYM2 is upregulated in OS tissues, promotes cell growth and metastasis in OS cell lines in vitro, and inhibits tumor growth in vivo via the circZMYM2-FXR1-NACA/ARPC1B axis. CircZMYM2 emerges as a crucial regulator of OS tumorigenesis, suggesting its potential as a diagnostic biomarker and therapeutic target for OS.

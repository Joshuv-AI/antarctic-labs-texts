Comprehensive smartphone image dataset for bean and cowpea plant leaf disease detection and freshness assessment from Bangladesh vegetable fields
Authors: Mahamudul Hasan, Raiyan Gani, Mohammad Rifat Ahmmad Rashid, Raka Kamara, Taslima Khan Tarin, Sheikh Fajlay Rabbi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Agriculture greatly impacts Bangladesh's economy, and vegetable cultivation plays a significant role in Agriculture by providing nourishment, and food security as well as improving the economy. The necessity of food production is growing similarly to the population growth. The farmers of Bangladesh are working hard to meet this need for food production and to gain yields. However, every year the farmers face a significant amount of loss in production due to the attack of different diseases and viruses due to the lack to technological development. The reason behind most of these losses is the lack of knowledge about diseases and being unable to detect the diseases early. Therefore, the early detection of plant disease is significant in balancing the country's economy and preventing undesirable losses. To bring a solution to this problem our dataset provides a total of 4467 images of Beans and Cowpeas leaf images which include different disease classes and fresh leaves. The dataset comprises 2,273 images of Bean and 2,194 images of Cowpea plants where each plant provides 4 classes of different disease along with the healthy leaves. This dataset will assist researchers in identifying plant diseases and farmers as well as contribute to the economy of the country.

Background

By 2050 the estimated population will rise to 9.7 billion [1]. To meet the food security of this large number of people food production is required to grow by 75% [1]. To meet this requirement, crop production needs to be safe from diseases and pests. However, it demands an early detection of any kind of disease that affects the cultivation. Mostly, farmers cultivate lots of vegetable together into their crop fields while they fail to detect diseases by their bare eyes. Therefore, introducing farmers to technologies like computer vision-based models to detect plant disease as early as possible is crucial. To benefit farmers and the food industry, our dataset provides correctly organized high-quality images of Bean and Cowpea plant leaves including 8 different classes. Furthermore, researchers are working hard to aid in agricultural by using deep learning models. For example, researchers of Bangladesh published a dataset that consists of 5 disease classes of papaya leaf as well as healthy papaya leaf [2]. Other researchers [3] have discussed tomato plant leaf disease detection using five classes of diseased and healthy leaves where each class contains 1000 images. The researchers used deep learning with CNNS To detect images. This dataset [4] contains 12,643 images of 21 different classes, the researchers published this dataset in order to aid in building advanced models for detecting plant disease effortlessly. The authors [5] used a leaf image dataset with multiple classes and employed Logistic Regression based Ensemble Classifier (LREC) for disease detection. The LREC method primarily utilizes image data to identify visual patterns associated with plant diseases. It is important to clarify that while this method is highly effective in detecting and classifying disease states based on visual symptoms, it does not inherently possess the capability to perform molecular characterization of the pathogens involved. The LREC method classifies diseases by learning from labeled images where the disease types are predefined based on symptoms visible on the plant leaves. These works prove that the researchers are working relentlessly to develop advanced models in order to help the farmers to detect plant leaves as soon as possible. Hence, our dataset will contribute to the field of agriculture and also create opportunities for further research advancements.

Data Description

Our dataset [6] comprises high-quality images which is important for machine learning model accuracy. We labeled our images as fresh and diseased leaves to assist models in differentiating between several classes more effectively. We collected a large number of diverse images consisting of 4467. Fig. 1 shows the visual representation of our collected plants. Our dataset has two plants along with different disease classes. In our dataset, Bean consists of 4 different classes which are: Blight (510), Mosaic Virus (562), Rust (568), Fresh leaf (554) and Cowpea consists of 4 different classes which include: Bacterial wilt (581), Mosaic virus (579), Septoria leaf spot (577), Fresh leaf (536). This dataset is categorized based on different classes for a clear understanding of fresh and diseased leaves.Fig. 1Leaves from every class.Fig 1

Bean

Fig. 2 shows the Bean Blight diseased leaves where Xanthomonas axonopodis pv. phaseoli is the bacteria that causes this disease. Water-soaked angular spots on leaves are one of the symptoms that gradually become large brown spots surrounded by narrow yellow zones [7]. Stems and seeds can also have this disease. The researchers identified this disease with the help of farmers and a pathologist. The pathologist described this disease causes the plant to freeze, brown, and die.Fig. 2Bean leaves with Blight disease.Fig 2

Bean leaves with Blight disease.

Fig. 3 shows the Bean leaves affected by the Mosaic Virus. There are two kinds of bean mosaic virus, one is the Bean Common Mosaic Virus (BCMV) and the other is the Bean Yellow Mosaic Virus (BYMV). When Mosaic virus affects bean leaves, they appear as small and curled or wrinkled leaves with white, yellow, or green spots or stripes. It causes light yellow, dark green, or green marks on the leaves and veins. This disease has no treatment once infected. So, farmers think if it can be detected sooner, they will be able to destroy the infected plants to avoid the spread.Fig. 3Bean leaves with Mosaic virus disease.Fig 3

Bean leaves with Mosaic virus disease.

Fig. 4 shows the pictures of Bean leaves that were affected by Rust disease. While observing the bean farm researchers found some leaves with orange-brown pustules. The pathologist described leaf rust causing orange-brown pustules on the leaf surface. The disease causes circular to oval pustules on the leaf. When someone rubs off the leaf, these spots disappear leaving an orange-brown mark. Uramyces appendiculatus causes this disease that may infect leaves, pods, and, sometimes stems and branches [8].Fig. 4Bean leaves with Rust disease.Fig 4

Cowpea

Bruises on leaves of the Cowpea leaves can be seen which indicates the Bacterial wilt disease [9]. However, such observations should be supplemented with laboratory tests, such as the use of selective media or molecular diagnostic tools, to confirm the presence of the pathogen responsible for Bacterial wilt. The bruises appear as orange, yellow, or purple on affected leaves. The discoloration happens due to the pigment of the bacterial strain. Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

Dataset collection and preparation

Obtaining high-quality images is crucial for the process of identification of plant disease. The overall working procedure of our work is shown in Fig. 11. To achieve that we searched different fields and observed different plants. Our focus was on collecting samples of Cowpea and Bean. After searching different areas, we found a suitable area: Kathalkandi, Nasirnagar, Brahmanbaria region of Bangladesh. We discussed with the local farmers to learn about the diseases and the problems they face to identify different diseases. They instructed us on the severity of the diseases. One of the most common diseases that affected their crops is the Mosaic virus. To detect the healthy and unhealthy leaves, we studied the plant disease signs and spots on leaves. We carefully differentiated the disease classes.Fig. 11Working process.Fig 11

Working process.

To take the picture, we used a Realme 9 5G smartphone which can take higher resolution pictures. We have described the device camera specification in Table 3. To take the pictures, we have covered six different angles of the same leaf. Through we were able to gather the overall information of a particular plant leaf and it also helped to avoid highlight or sun exposure. We took the picture in a compressed laboratory, which gave us the original color of the plant and the disease spots, which was important to identify the disease spots. To ensure the quality and consistency of our dataset for plant leaf disease, we started a comprehensive data collection process in collaboration with local farmers. The working steps are, first we began by reaching out to farmers to collect various leaves of bean and cowpea that are affected by different diseases. We organized a room where we set up a photo station with consistent lighting and a plain black background to reduce differences and make the images clearer. Each leaf was carefully photographed under these controlled lighting conditions to create high-quality images. After taking the pictures, we moved on to classify them. We carefully examined each image and grouped the images by different disease classes. This classification was essential to build a well-organized dataset suitable for effective training and analysis purposes. A careful and systematic data preparation process ensures that our dataset is both reliable and representative of the bean and cowpea plant leaf diseases, providing a solid foundation for further research and analysis.

Limitations

As we gathered the dataset, we encountered several issues that could have impacted its quality. Here are the limitations of this dataset and their potential solutions:•Controlled Environment: Images were taken in a controlled lab environment with consistent lighting and a black background, to ensure clarity and quality, it does not accurately reflect the natural conditions where these plants grow, potentially impacting the model's ability to generalize to field conditions.•Data Augmentation: We didn't perform any data augmentation techniques. This absence may limit the model's ability to generalize to new, unseen data. Moreover, applying augmentation methods such as rotation, scaling, zoom, and color adjustments will increase the size of the training dataset and help the deep learning models to predict more data and recognize the real-world scenario.•Disease identification: Before collecting the plant leaf diseases, we had to organize a local meeting with the farmers so that we can have face to face conversation in order to have a complete documentation of a particular plant's diseases accurate information. As our dataset only collected from the vegetable fields of Chatalpar, Nasirnagar, Brahmanbaria, Chittagong, Bangladesh. Thus, the dataset may not be fully representative of all geographic regions and farming practices due to different soil properties according to different regoins. With respect to this situation, further diseases of Bean and Cowpea plant may be discovered within other regions which will be further advancement to this particular plant leaf segment.

Comparison of long-term clinical outcomes between radiofrequency ablation and hepatic resection in patients with small (≤2 cm) hepatocellular carcinoma
Authors: Young Mi Hong
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Aim: The present study aimed to compare the long-term survival outcomes of hepatic resection (HR) and radiofrequency ablation (RFA) in patients with single small (≤2 cm) hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC).Materials & methods: This retrospective study enrolled patients with a single small HCC measuring 2 cm or smaller underwent HR or RFA as their initial treatment.Results: Overall survival (OS) was significantly higher in the HR group than in the RFA group, while no significant difference was observed in recurrence free survival (RFS) between the two groups. However, after propensity score matching, both OS and RFS in the HR group were significantly higher than in the RFA group. Multivariate analysis showed that patients with hepatitis B virus infection, elevated prothrombin-induced by vitamin K absence or antagonist-II, and albumin–bilirubin (ALBI) grade 2/3 before treatment had poorer OS. Patients with ALBI grade 1 in the HR group demonstrated the highest OS.Conclusion: HR showed significantly better long-term OS and RFS compared with RFA in patients with as a single HCC (≤2 cm). Moreover, the ALBI grade may help identify patients who would benefit from HR or RFA.

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is the most common primary liver cancer that poses a major global challenge because of its high prevalence and significant impact on public health [1]. HCC often exhibits aggressive progression and is frequently diagnosed at advanced stages when treatment options become limited. Consequently, HCC carries a substantial mortality rate, emphasizing the need for effective treatment strategies. Early detection and appropriate treatment of HCC are crucial for improving clinical outcomes.

Advance in surveillance techniques and improved imaging methods for high risk populations have led to an increased diagnosis rate of early stage HCC. Current guidelines suggest that treatment options for early stage HCC are typically aimed at achieving curative intent. Recommended treatments include hepatic resection (HR), radiofrequency ablation (RFA) and liver transplantation [2–4]. However, due to the limited availability of donor organs, HR and RFA are predominant treatment options for early stage HCC. The decision on the optimal choice between these two approaches remains a subject of debate.

The current literature shows conflicting results regarding the efficacy of HR versus RFA for early stage HCC. While many studies have reported that RFA and HR have similar effectiveness for small HCC, some findings differ [5–9]. One study suggested that RFA might be the preferred treatment option for patients with Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer (BCLC) stage 0 HCC [10]. However, recent studies have provided evidence demonstrating that surgical resection is associated with better overall survival (OS) and recurrence free survival (RFS) compared with RFA in patients with small HCC [11–13].

Understanding the differences in outcomes between RFA and HR for a single small HCC is crucial for tailoring treatment strategies to individual patient characteristics, including liver function. This knowledge can inform shared decision-making between healthcare providers and patients, facilitating the selection of the most appropriate treatment option.

The present study aimed to compare the long-term survival outcomes between RFA and HR in patients with a single small (≤2 cm) HCC. By providing evidence-based insights into comparative effectiveness of these treatment modalities, this study aim to support clinical decision-making, advance treatment strategies, and improve patient outcomes in the management of this prevalent and impactful disease.

Materials & methods

Patients

We retrospectively collected data from patients with a single small HCC (≤2 cm) underwent HR or RFA as their initial treatment from 2008 to 2018. The diagnosis of HCC was made following the clinical guidelines that were current at the time of treatment [14,15]. Liver cirrhosis was diagnosed based on radiological imaging, such as abdominal sonography and computed tomography (CT), or through pathological examination of the liver, as well as other clinical information. The decision to perform HR or RFA was tailored to each patient, considering factors such as tumor characteristics (e.g., location), patient preference, underlying diseases and cost. This study included patients who met the following criteria: Eastern cooperative oncology group performance status 0; a single tumor ≤2 cm in maximal diameter; no vascular invasion or extrahepatic metastasis; and HR or RFA as the first‐line treatment. Patients were excluded if they had other primary cancers or were lost to follow-up after the initial treatment.

Treatment assessment & follow-up

Various pretreatment variables, including age, gender, hepatitis B surface antigen status, presence of liver cirrhosis, liver function grade (Child–Pugh score and albumin–bilirubin [ALBI] grade), and tumor markers, were reviewed. Recurrence and survival after RFA or HR were evaluated. All patients were assessed using dynamic CT or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and tumor markers 1–2 month after RFA or HR.

Subsequent follow-ups, including laboratory tests and dynamic CT/MRI scans, were conducted every 3 months during the first 2 years and then every 6 months thereafter. If tumor recurrence or new HCC was detected during the follow-up periods, appropriate further treatments such as ablation, transarterial chemoembolization, HR, liver transplantation, systemic therapy, radiation therapy or combination therapy was considered based on clinical practice guidelines and the patient’s general condition. OS was defined as the time between the date of HCC treatments and the date of death from any cause. RFS was defined as the time from HCC treatments to either the date of the first tumor recurrence or the last date of follow-up visit of patients. The ALBI grade was calculated based on the serum albumin and bilirubin levels (ALBI = [log10 bilirubin (μmol/l) ×0.66]+[albumin (g/l)× −0.085]). Three different grades were described: grade 1 (less than −2.60), grade 2 (between −2.60 and −1.39) and grade 3 (above −1.39) [16]. The present study was conducted in accordance to the ethical guidelines of the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Institutional Review Boards (IRB) of Pusan National University Yangsan Hospital (IRB No. 55-2023-013). Requirement for informed consent was waived after review of IRB of Pusan National University Yangsan Hospital because it was practically impossible and this study was of retrospective design.

Statistical analysis

The data were presented as percentages for categorical variables, which were compared using the Chi-square tests. OS and RFS were calculated using the Kaplan–Meier method and compared with the log-rank test. Propensity score matching (PSM) was employed to reduce selection bias based on variables such as age, gender, etiology, liver cirrhosis, ALBI grade and AFP level. Univariable and multivariable analyses were performed using the Cox proportional hazards model to identify the potential risk factors for survival. Variables with p-values <0.05 in univariable analysis were included in the multivariable analysis. Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS for Windows (version 26; SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). All p-values <0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

Demographic characteristics of patients

The demographic characteristics of patients are summarized in Table 1. A total of 177 patients were included, comprising 101 patients receiving HR and 76 receiving RFA. The age of patients in the HR group was comparatively lower than those in the RFA group (p = 0.04). No significant difference was observed in the gender ratio, alcohol consumption and smoking between the two groups. However, the proportion of hepatitis B virus infection was higher in the HR group compared with the RFA group (p < 0.001). Almost all patients were taking antiviral drugs, and there was no difference in antiviral treatment between the groups.

In terms of liver function, including underlying liver cirrhosis (p = 0.048), Child–Pugh score (p = 0.002) and ALBI grade (p < 0.001), the RFA group had poorer liver function. There were no significant differences in the proportion of alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) (≥20 ng/ml) and protein induced by vitamin K antagonist-II (PIVKA-II) (≥40 AU/ml) between two groups, whereas the proportion of NLR differed significantly between them (p < 0.001).

Comparison of OS & RFS between HR group & RFA group before PSM

The median follow-up duration for OS and RFS was 85 months and 61 months, respectively. During the follow-up period, 53 patients died (19 in the HR group and 34 in the RFA group), and 77 patients experienced HCC recurrence (41 in the HR group and 36 in the RFA group). There was no significant difference between the HR and RFA group in 3, 5 and 10 years RFS rates (74.7, 63.0, 52.9% vs 68.7, 57.3, 39.6%, p = 0.079) (Figure 1A). However, OS rates was significantly higher in the HR group compared with the RFA group (p < 0.001). The OS rates at 3, 5 and 10 years were 95.0, 86.1 and 79.9% in the HR group, and 80.3, 71.1 and 48.0% in the RFA group, respectively (Figure 1B).

RFA: Radiofrequency ablation.

We also evaluated the survival outcomes of patients with very early stage HCC (BCLC 0 stage). A total of 167 patients were included in this analysis, with 100 patients receiving HR and 67 receiving RFA. The 3, 5 and 10 years OS rates were 95.0, 86.0 and 79.6% in the HR group, and 80.6, 73.1 and 48.2% in the RFA group, respectively (p < 0.001). However, the 3, 5 and 7 years RFS did not show a significant difference between the HR group and RFA group (74.2, 61.6, 58.7% vs 68.7, 55.6, 47.3%, p = 0.116).

Comparison of OS & RFS between HR group & RFA group after PSM

After PSM, the total of 84 patients was matched, with 42 patients in the HR group and 42 patients in the RFA group (Table 1). The 3, 5 and 10 years RFS rates were significantly different between the HR group and RFA group (85.3, 74.7, 65.3% vs 83.3, 57.7, 42.6%, p = 0.021) (Figure 2A). After PSM, OS remained significantly higher in the HR group compared with the RFA group (p = 0.026). The 3, 5 and 10 years OS rates were 97.6, 92.9 and 81.4% in the HR group, and 85.7, 76.2 and 61.1% in the RFA group, respectively (Figure 2B).

RFA: Radiofrequency ablation.

Prognostic factors associated with OS & RFS

Age, etiology of underlying liver disease, serum PIVKA-II level and ALBI grade were strongly associated with OS in the univariate analysis (Table 2). The multivariate Cox regression analysis revealed that hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection (HR: 3.55; 95% CI: 1.31–9.64; p = 0.013), elevated PIVKA-II level (≥ 40 AU/ml) (HR: 2.79; 95% CI: 1.15–6.76; p = 0.023) and ALBI grade 2/3 (HR: 3.01; 95% CI: 1.20–7.57; p = 0.019) were strongly associated with poor OS. Although independent predictors of RFS were also analyzed, no predictive factors were identified as being associated with RFS (Table 3).

Survival according to treatment modality & ALBI grade

There was a significant difference in long-term OS according to treatment modality and ALBI grade (p < 0.001). Regardless of the treatment method, the ALBI grade 1 group had a higher OS. Among these, patients with ALBI grade 1 who underwent HR showed the highest OS (Figure 3).

Discussion

This study compared the long-term survival outcomes of HR with RFA for single small (≤2 cm) HCC. This comprehensive analysis included patient data and treatment outcomes to evaluate the effectiveness of these two commonly used therapeutic modalities. The results demonstrated significant differences in long-term survival between the two treatments. OS was significantly higher in the HR group compared with the RFA group, while no significant difference in RFS was observed between the two groups before PSM. However, after PSM, both OS and RFS were significantly higher in the HR group than in the RFA group. Notably, HR was associated with higher OS, both before and after PSM. Additionally, the Cox multivariate model confirmed that an ALBI grade 1 was independently associated with better OS. These findings provide valuable insights into the management of a single small (≤2 cm) HCC and are crucial for treatment decision-making.

There is still debated over whether HR or RFA is more effective as a curative treatment for single small (≤2 cm) HCC, due to the lack of randomized controlled trials specifically focusing on this specific condition. Although most studies to date have been retrospective, numerous studies have compared the survival outcomes of patients with small HCC treated with either RFA or HR.

Numerous studies have shown that HR lead to higher OS and RFS than RFA in small HCC. Liu et al. evaluated the efficacy of HR and RFA for single HCC 2 cm or less and found that HR provides better long-term OS and RFS in patients with BCLC very early- stage HCC, suggesting HR should be considered as the first-line treatment for these patients [17]. Similarly, Chu et al. conducted a retrospective comparison of HR and RFA for very early-stage HCC with long-term follow-up and demonstrated that HR was associated with better OS and RFS compared with the RFA [18]. Both studies confirmed that the superiority of HR using PSM analysis. The results of present study align with previous studies, but those studies only included patients with Child–Pugh A score, unlike present study. Moreover, the present study identified the ALBI grade as a significant prognostic factor for long-term survival, indicating that ALBI grade may be used to identify patients who would benefit from HR or RFA. Meta-analysis studies have also reported that HR is superior to RFA for treating small HCC in patients eligible for surgical treatments, although these analyses include HCC larger than 2 cm [19,20]. Recently, Wu et al. conducted a 10-years cohort study comparing the outcomes of HR and RFA in patients with a solitary HCC (≤2 cm), including those with Child-Pugh score B. They found that long-term OS was comparable between HR and RFA, however, the HR group had significantly higher RFS than the RFA group. They also demonstrated that liver reserve determined the long-term OS of patients with HCC. Multivariate analysis revealed that patients with a better Child-Pugh score or ALBI grade before treatment had improved OS [12]. Li et al. also studied the long-term prognosis of HR and RFA for very early stage HCC. They showed that HR was associated with significantly higher OS rates than RFA, although there was no significant difference in RFS between the two groups. Moreover, PSM analysis further confirmed that HR was significantly associated with better OS [11].

There are different results with those from a previous studies showing that HR resulted in better OS and RFS. Several investigations, including randomized controlled trials, have consistently shown that OS and RFS are similar patterns after RFA or HR in patients with small HCC [5–9]. A recent meta-analysis that included both RCTs and PSM studies found that RFA and HR resulted in comparable OS and RFS for patients with BCLC 0 stage [13]. Additionally, a retrospective study including 605 patients with BCLC very early or early stage HCC showed no significant difference in OS between HR and RFA, although HR showed better RFS than RFA [21]. In contrast, Peng et al. conducted a retrospective study on patients with BCLC stage 0 HCC and found that RFA was associated with better OS than HR, while RFS was similar between the two treatments [10].

To better understand the differences in OS in this study, we examined prognostic factors that might have influenced survival outcomes. The present study identified HBV infection, elevated PIVKA-II level and ALBI grade 2/3 as unfavorable factors for OS. Serum tumor makers, such as AFP and PIVKA-II, have been recognized as predictors of tumor recurrence and survival following HCC treatments because they reflect the biological behavior or aggressiveness of the tumor [22]. PIVKA-II is an immature form of prothrombin without any coagulative function, synthesized by the liver, and acts as an autologous growth factor during HCC progression [23]. It has been studied as a prognostic factor for survival in HCC patients undergoing various treatments, with several studies indicating that PIVKA-II is a reliable prognostic factor in small HCC. A retrospective study comparing the long-term outcomes of patients treated with either hepatectomy or RFA as the first-line treatment for HCC found that a high pretreatment PIVKA-II level was associated with poorer survival in the RFA group [24]. Another study evaluating prognostic predictors in HCC patients after RFA identified elevated PIVKA-II levels as a significant independent risk factor for mortality [25]. Furthermore, research has demonstrated the value of post-ablation PIVKA-II level as a biomarker for predicting survival and recurrence after curative RFA. Among tumor markers, post-ablation PIVKA-II was identified as an independent prognostic factor for OS and RFS [26]. A meta-analysis also showed that elevated pre-RFA PIVKA-II levels significantly predicted poorer OS and RFS [27]. Similarly, a retrospective study reported that elevated PIVKA-II was a predisposing factor for tumor recurrence in patients with small HCC who underwent hepatectomy [28]. However, as PIVKA-II level vary between studies, further research is needed to better clarify its role as a prognostic factor in HCC treatments.

Assessing liver function is essential for determining the appropriate treatment approach and predicting the prognosis for patients with HCC. Traditionally, the Child–Pugh score has been widely used for assessing liver function. However, its accuracy can be reduced due to the subjectivity in grading ascites and encephalopathy. To address this, the ALBI grade, which is based on serum albumin and total bilirubin levels, was introduced as a novel model for evaluating liver function in HCC [16]. The effectiveness of ALBI grade has been demonstrated in several studies involving early stage HCC. A retrospective study investigated 2,559 HCC patients who received curative therapies and found that those with ALBI grade 1 had survived rates nearly twice as long as those with ALBI grade 2 following potentially curative treatment [29]. Another study also identified the ALBI grade as the most significant independent predictor of OS in patients with early stage HCC who underwent curative HR [30]. In this study, we demonstrated the predictive capabilities of the ALBI grade for long-term survival. Pretreatment ALBI grade could serve as a valuable predictive indicator for selecting the appropriate treatment modality in patients with a single small (≤2 cm) HCC.

This study have several limitations. First, as a retrospective study design, it may have been affected by potential bias, even with the use of PSM analysis. Information bias is present due to the reliance on historical records that may be incomplete or inaccurate. Second, confounding factors from incomplete data could not be entirely avoided. Third, this study was conducted at a single tertiary referral center with a relatively small sample size, which may limit the generalizability of the findings. Therefore, caution should be taken when interpreting and generalizing these results. Despite these limitations, the study has the strength of providing a long-term, real world follow-up data of patients with small HCC (≤2 cm).

Conclusion

In conclusion, for patients with a single small (≤2 cm) HCC, HR resulted in better OS and RFS compared with RFA. Notably, pretreatment hepatic functional reserve was a significant predictor of long-term survival. The ALBI grade may be used to identify patients with a single small (≤2 cm) HCC who would benefit from either HR or RFA. Therefore, further large-scale cohort studies are needed to validate the use of the ALBI grade in determining the most appropriate treatment for small HCC.

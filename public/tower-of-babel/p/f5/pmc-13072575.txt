SiRNA-based inactivation of Angptl3 alleviates high-fat diet-induced MAFLD and atherosclerosis in LDLR-deficient hamsters
Authors: Xiaohong Zhang, Yufei Han, Liwen Zheng, Xiaokui He, Yungang Pu, Lili Wei, Yuhui Wang, Xunde Xian
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAngiopoietin-like protein 3 (Angptl3), a factor secreted by the liver, inhibits lipoprotein lipase and other lipases by forming a complex with Angptl4 and 8. However, whether inhibition of Angptl3 can alleviate hepatic lipid accumulation and atherosclerosis remains unclear. Therefore, this study explored the effect of small interfering RNA (siRNA)-based inactivation of Angptl3 on metabolic dysfunction-associated fatty liver disease (MAFLD) and atherosclerosis in male LDLR-deficient hamsters.MethodsRecombinant adeno-associated virus serotype 9 (AAV9) encoding Angptl3-siRNA or empty AAV (AAV9-null) were injected into male 4-month-old LDLR−/− hamsters via the jugular vein. Subsequently, all hamsters were administered with a 16-week high-fat diet (HFD) feeding to induce atherosclerosis. RT-PCR and pathological analysis were used to investigate the mechanism of hepatic lipid accumulation and detect atherosclerotic plaque formation in the heart and aorta.ResultsOn HFD, Angptl3-siRNA-treated hamsters displayed significantly decreased plasma triglyceride (TG), total cholesterol, high-density lipoprotein-cholesterol, and glucose levels, compared with the AAV9-null hamsters. FPLC analysis further revealed a marked reduction of TG and cholesterol contents in VLDL/LDL fractions. Plasma apolipoprotein analysis showed relatively lower ApoB/ApoE levels and higher ApoA1 levels. Moreover, Angptl3-siRNA markedly accelerated the clearance of triglyceride-rich lipoproteins in LDLR−/− hamsters. Consistently, results from hepatic lipid extraction, Oil Red O and Sirius Red staining demonstrated that Angptl3-siRNA reduced hepatic lipid accumulation and ameliorated HFD-induced MAFLD. RT-PCR revealed a dramatic decrease in the expression of Acc1, Fasn, Srebp1c, Cd36, Fatp1, Tgfβ, Cd68, Tnfa, and Col1a1 as well as a marked increase in Abcg5, Abcg8, Ppara, and Cpt1 expression, which participated in fatty acid synthesis and oxidation, cholesterol synthesis and efflux, inflammation and fibrosis. Pathological analysis indicated that Angptl3-siRNA attenuated the formation of atherosclerotic plaques in the aortic root and aorta when compared with the control group.ConclusionsThese findings demonstrated that siRNA-based inactivation of Angptl3 alleviated MAFLD and atherosclerosis induced by HFD in LDLR−/− hamsters, which further confirmed that inhibition of Angptl3 may provide a novel therapeutic approach for atherosclerosis by reducing hyperlipidemia and hepatic lipid accumulation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12944-026-02916-3.Keywords: Angiopoietin-like protein 3, Small interfering RNA, Low-density lipoprotein receptor, Metabolic dysfunction-associated fatty liver disease, Atherosclerosis

Introduction

Angiopoietin-like protein 3 (Angptl3), one of the vascular endothelial growth factor family, is synthesized and secreted by the liver. Angptl3 inhibits lipoprotein lipase (LPL) activity, thereby regulating the metabolism of triglyceride-rich lipoproteins (TRLs) [1]. However, Angptl3 alone cannot complete the inhibitory effects and requires the combination with Angptl4 and 8 [2]. Angptl3 mRNA expression and secretion are primarily regulated by liver X receptors (LXRs). Upon a high-cholesterol diet, hepatic expression of Angptl3 was upregulated by increasing the transcriptional activity of LXRs, thus then leading to hypertriglyceridaemia [3]. Reduced expression of Angptl3 suppresses the development of atherosclerosis in apolipoprotein E-deficiency mice by enhancing TRLs clearance [4]. Moreover, Angptl3 can bind to integrin aVβ3 via its C-terminal end and modulate vessel wall thickness and macrophage activity [5].

In 2010, Musunuru K et al. reported that mutations in the exons of Angptl3 gene in humans lead to reduced plasma levels of triglyceride (TG), low-density lipoprotein (LDL) and high-density lipoprotein (HDL), which is referred to as familial hypolipidaemia [6]. Heterozygous Angptl3 mutations cause a 34% lower risk of cardiovascular disease, and individuals carrying homozygous Angptl3 mutations have no coronary atherosclerotic plaques [7].

Currently, Angptl3 can be inhibited via two distinct strategies. Evinacumab, a monoclonal antibody targeting Angptl3, binds to and inactivates Angptl3. The other approach involves the use of antisense oligonucleotides (ASO). Notably, the lipid-lowering effect of Angptl3 inhibitors does not depend on the LDL receptor (LDLR) regulation. Evinacumab effectively decreases LDL levels even in subjects with familial hypercholesterolemia (FH) carrying LDLR gene defects [8]. In LDLR-deficient mice, Angptl3-ASO significantly reduces TG, LDL-cholesterol (LDL-C), and HDL-cholesterol (HDL-C) levels in plasma, and retards the progression of aortic atherosclerosis by 52% [9]. In 2020, Gaudet D et al. reported that N-acetylgalactosamine-coupled Angptl3-ASO drugs not only decrease plasma lipid levels in subjects with diabetes, fatty liver, and hypertriglyceridaemia, but also cause a 58% reduction in apolipoprotein C3 (ApoC3) [10]. Collectively, these studies indicate that inhibition of Angptl3 is another potential strategy to reduce the residual risks of hyperlipidaemia and cardiovascular disease.

Metabolic dysfunction-associated fatty liver disease (MAFLD) is characterized by hepatic abnormal accumulation of triglycerides and other lipids. Dyslipidaemia is closely linked to an increased risk of MAFLD [11]. At present, MAFLD is one of the most prevalent chronic liver disorders worldwide, affecting about 30% of adults [12, 13]. Emerging data indicates that MAFLD is strongly associated with an increased cardiovascular disease risk [14]. Hepatic Angptl3 expression is not only increased in individuals with MAFLD but also correlated with the presence and severity of MAFLD [15]. However, whether inhibition of Angptl3 exerts its influence on dyslipidaemia, especially in terms of hepatic lipids and atherogenic development, is not completely understood.

Notably, the lipoprotein profiles and coronary atherosclerosis of hamsters are similar to those observed in human patients with FH [16]. For basic and translational research on lipid metabolism and atherosclerosis, LDLR-deficient (LDLR−/−) hamsters exhibit distinct advantages over other animal models [17]. Therefore, this study aims to explore the beneficial effect of siRNA-based inactivation of Angptl3 in LDLR−/− hamsters and provides a novel insight into the precise mechanisms of Angptl3 inhibitors on the MAFLD and atherosclerosis.

Materials and methods

Angptl3-siRNA

Based on the golden hamster Angptl3 gene (PubMed ID:101,833,513), the Angptl3-siRNA sequence GAACTACATCCAAACTACA was selected. Recombinant adeno-associated virus serotype 9 (AAV9) encoding Angptl3 small interfering RNA (Angptl3-siRNA) was used to suppress the expression of Angptl3. Both Angptl3-siRNA and empty AAV (AAV9-null) were constructed and provided by the Hanheng Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China).

Animals

Syrian golden hamsters were provided by the Vital River Laboratories (Beijing, China). With the CRISPR/Cas9 genetic editing technology, LDLR-deficient (LDLR−/−) hamsters were generated in our lab and bred as described previously [17]. Male 4-month-old LDLR−/− hamsters (n = 12) were used in this study. Initially, all animals were fed a regular chow diet (20% protein and 4% fat; Beijing Keao Company, Beijing, China). Subsequently, Angptl3-siRNA or AAV9-null were injected into the jugular vein of LDLR−/− hamsters at a dose of 1 × 1013 vg/kg. After that, all animals were changed to a high-fat diet (0.2% cholesterol and 15% fat, HFD) feeding for 16 weeks. After the experiment, all animals were humanely anesthetized via an intraperitoneal injection of 3% pentobarbital sodium (45 mg/kg). Blood and tissue samples were collected for subsequent analysis.

All experiments were conducted following the principle of laboratory animal care (NIH publication no.85Y23, revised 1996). This study was approved by the Laboratory Animal Ethics Committee of Peking University (LA2015-012).

Analysis of plasma lipids and biochemical characteristics

Plasma levels of TG, total cholesterol (TC), HDL-C, and glucose (GLU) were determined using the assay kits from Jiancheng Bioengineering Research Institute (Nanjing, China). Plasma levels of nonesterified fatty acids (NEFA) were detected with a commercially enzymatic kit (633–52,001, Wako, Japan). To evaluate the severity of liver injury, plasma alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and aspartate transaminase (AST) levels were measured using the assay kits (Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China).

Analysis of plasma lipoprotein distribution

By means of a fast protein liquid chromatography (FPLC) system, plasma lipoprotein distribution was analysed. Pooled plasma of 10 aliquots per group was processed as described before [18]. After filtered with 0.22-μm filters, 200 μL samples were subsequently loaded onto high-performance columns (Superose S-6 10/300GL, Amersham Biosciences). Phosphate buffer saline (PBS) was used for elution at a constant flow rate (0.25 mL/min). 500 μL per eluted fraction was collected for TG and TC measurement.

Western blot (WB)

WB was used to detect the plasma apolipoprotein B (ApoB), ApoE, and ApoA1, as the methods described previously [19]. First, 1 μL of plasma was prepared by boiling with the loading buffer containing sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS) and dithiothreitol. Subsequently, total proteins were separated by the electrophoresis in 6% or 10% SDS-PAGE gel, followed by a transfer to a nitrocellulose membrane. After blocked with 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA), the membrane was hybridized with ApoB, ApoE, or ApoAI antibodies (ab20737, ab20874, ab20453, rabbit polyclonal IgG, Abcam), respectively. Finally, to analyze the strip intensity, gel imaging and photography were performed using a gel imager (Molecular Imager Gel Doc XR System, Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA).

Oral Fat Load (OFL)

After an overnight fast, all animals were administrated orally with olive oil at a dose of 10 mL/kg. Blood was collected for TG measurement at the designated time points (0, 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 4, and 8 h) following gavage.

Tissue lipid analysis

Lipid extraction method was conducted as described previously [20]. 100 mg of liver tissue was homogenized in 1 mL of cold PBS. Next, the homogenate was vortexed with an equal volume of chloroform/methanol (v/v = 2:1) and then centrifuged. After that, the chloroform in the lower layer was collected by a glass syringe, and all the chloroform was dried under nitrogen. After dissolved in 3% TritonX-100, lipids were measured for TG and TC.

RNA isolation and quantitative real-time PCR (RT-PCR) assay

Liver tissues were lysed and total RNA was extracted using Trizol reagent (12,183,555, Invitrogen). Next, RNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA using the reagent kits (Invitrogen, 18,091,200). Quantitative RT-PCR was processed in 35 cycles using a fluorescence detection system (MJ Research) with SYBR Green (Molecular Probes, Eugene, OR). Each cycle included heating denaturation for 30 s at 94 °C, annealing for 30 s at 55 °C, and extension for 30 s at 72 °C. After the reaction, the amplification curve and dissolution curve were analyzed.

The expression of gene was analysed using the 2^(–ΔΔCt) method, and GAPDH was used as a control. After designed using the PrimerQuest software, primers were synthesised by Qingke Biotech (Beijing, China) [21]. The primers sequences used are listed in Table 1.

Histological and pathological analysis

The collected tissues (liver, heart, and aorta) were fixed (4% paraformaldehyde), immersed (30% sucrose), sectioned and stained following the standard operating procedures.

Tissue samples embedded in paraffin were sectioned, deparaffinized, and rehydrated. For H&E staining, 5 μm sections of liver were processed with Harry’s hematoxylin and aqueous eosin. For Sirius Red staining, the liver sections were processed with Sirius Red in saturated picric acid. After that, all the sections were dehydrated (graded alcohol) and cleared (xylene).

Based on the results of H&E staining, the MAFLD activity score (0–8) of every liver specimen was assessed using a modified version described previously [22]. Firstly, three primary parameters were semi-quantitatively graded: steatosis (0–3), lobular inflammation (0–3), and hepatocyte ballooning degeneration (0–2). Then, the sum of these parameters would be called the MAFLD activity score, which ranged from 0–8.

The liver and heart embedded in OCT were snap-frozen by liquid nitrogen, stored at −80℃, and sectioned. 7 μm cryosections of liver and aortic root were then treated with Oil Red O (ORO) solution. The entire aorta was laid on the wax plate for ORO staining. With ImageJ software, the percentage of plaque area was determined by the ORO-positive area relative to the total vascular area.

Immunofluorescence staining

Liver and aortic root cryosections were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde and treated with PBS to increase permeability. Next, the sections were blocked with 3% BSA and then incubated with CD68 and α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA) antibodies overnight at 4 °C. Subsequently, after washed with PBS, the sections were then incubated with secondary antibodies. 4’,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI, C1005, Beyotime, China) or Bodipy (D3922, Invitrogen, USA) dye solution was utilized to exhibit nuclei, and lipid droplets, respectively. Finally, the expression of CD68 (BM3639, Boster, USA), α-SMA (BM0002, Boster, USA), the staining of nuclei and lipid droplets were observed using a fluorescence microscope.

Statistical analysis

All data were expressed as the mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM), and analysed by Student’s t-test. GraphPad Prism 8.0 software (GraphPad Software, La Jolla, CA, USA) was used for all statistical analyses. A value of P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Plasma lipid composition in LDLR-/- hamsters

The body weights of all the LDLR−/− hamsters selected for the experiments were matched between the two groups (Fig. 1A). To investigate the effects of siRNA-based inactivation of Angptl3 on plasma lipids in LDLR-deficient background, lipids components of 4-month-old chow diet-fed animals were analysed at first. In LDLR−/− hamsters, plasma TG and TC levels significantly increased, with an average TG level of approximately 515 mg/dL and a TC level of approximately 485 mg/dL (Fig. 1B). There was no difference in plasma HDL-C and GLU levels between the two groups (Fig. 1B). After HFD feeding, TG and TC levels were further increased. Plasma TG level of AAV9-null hamsters was clearly increased, reaching 13,474 mg/dL at 16 weeks (Fig. 1C). Similarly, plasma TC level was also sharply increased, reaching 7,239 mg/dL at 16 weeks (Fig. 1C).

B Plasma TG, TC HDL-C, and GLU levels at first. C Plasma TG, TC, HDL-C, and GLU levels at 16 weeks. D Plasma nonesterified fatty acid (NEFA) levels at 16 weeks. *p < 0.05. **p < 0.01. ***p < 0.001

Conversely, after HFD feeding for 16 weeks, plasma TG and TC levels of Angptl3-siRNA-treated hamsters markedly decreased by 44.27% and 31.37%, respectively (Fig. 1C). Consistent with the results of previous study, Angptl3-siRNA almost completely eliminated HDL-C components in LDLR−/− hamsters (Fig. 1C) [9]. Plasma HDL-C level of Angptl3-siRNA group was 121 mg/dL, a decrease by 66.85%, compared with that of the AAV9-null group (365 mg/dL). Interestingly, at 16 weeks, plasma GLU level of the Angptl3-siRNA group (241 mg/dL) was also 41.79% lower relative to the control group (414 mg/dL) (Fig. 1C). Unexpectedly, plasma levels of NEFA were also decreased in Angptl3-siRNA-treated hamsters (Fig. 1D). Taken together, these findings suggest that inactivation of Angptl3 reduces plasma TG, TC, HDL-C, GLU, and NEFA levels in LDLR-deficient background.

To further assess which type of TG and cholesterol was reduced in Angptl3-siRNA-treated hamsters, FPLC was performed to analyse the lipoprotein profile. The results revealed marked reductions of TG and cholesterol contained in very-low-density lipoprotein (VLDL) and LDL components in Angptl3-siRNA-treated hamsters (Fig. 2A).

Plasma apolipoprotein analysis revealed lower ApoB/ApoE levels and higher ApoAI levels in Angptl3-siRNA-treated hamsters than in AAV9-null hamsters (Fig. 2B). The WB results revealed that size-fractionated ApoB and ApoE were decreased in Angptl3-siRNA-treated hamsters (16.59% and 13.00%, respectively) (Fig. 2C). However, the content of plasma ApoA1 was significantly increased by 10.29% in Angptl3-siRNA-treated hamsters (Fig. 2C). Collectively, these results indicate that inactivation of Angptl3 reduces plasma levels of ApoB/ApoE-containing TRLs and that this profile of lipoproteins prevents the progression of atherosclerosis.

Lipoprotein clearance in LDLR-/- hamsters

The reduced VLDL fraction in Angptl3-siRNA-treated hamsters was attributed to accelerated clearance of circulating lipoprotein from the plasma or decreased hepatic VLDL production, which remains unclear. Therefore, an OFL assay was subsequently performed. As shown in Fig. 2D, plasma TG levels were markedly decreased in Angptl3-siRNA-treated hamsters at all the designated time points (0, 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 4, and 8 h) after gavage. These results suggest that the clearance of TRLs in circulation is accelerated in Angptl3-siRNA-treated hamsters due to increased lipoprotein lipase activity.

Lipid deposition and hepatic steatosis on an HFD in LDLR-/- hamsters

Lipid metabolism disorders are highly associated with an increased risk of MAFLD. Because mutations in Angptl3 executed beneficial function on hyperlipidaemia, this study investigated whether inactivation of Angptl3 would affect the MAFLD in LDLR−/− hamsters.

First, liver weights and liver lipid contents were monitored at the end of the experiments to explore the effect of inactivation of Angptl3 on hepatic lipid deposition. The results showed that at 16 weeks, body weight (BW) was significantly lower in Angptl3-siRNA-treated hamsters than that in the control hamsters (117.5 vs. 139.1 g, respectively) (Fig. 3A). Similarly, relative to the control group, ratio of liver weight to BW in Angptl3-siRNA-treated hamsters was also markedly lower (0.043 vs. 0.054 mg/g, respectively) (Fig. 3B). Moreover, hepatic lipid extraction data confirmed substantial decreases in hepatic TG and TC contents in Angptl3-siRNA-treated hamsters after HFD feeding for 16 weeks (Fig. 3C).

Second, hepatic morphological changes in the LDLR−/− hamsters was investigated. H&E staining of livers exhibited prominent morphological alterations in the control group, mainly including lipid droplet accumulation, fibrosis, and an increased inflammatory response (Fig. 3D). Notably, inactivation of Angptl3 markedly ameliorated these phenotypes, as evidenced in a reduction of MAFLD activity score (Fig. 3E). Moreover, in the control group, liver ORO and Sirius Red staining results displayed a greater severity of hepatic steatosis and fibrosis (Fig. 3F and G), while the Angptl3-siRNA group exhibited remarkably reduced lipid deposition and fibrosis. Additionally, the results of liver Bodipy and CD68 immunofluorescence staining showed that Angptl3-siRNA significantly attenuated hepatic lipid accumulation and macrophage infiltration (Fig. 3H and I).

Finally, plasma levels of ALT and AST were determined to evaluate the degree of liver injury. The results revealed that plasma ALT and AST were markedly decreased in Angptl3-siRNA-treated hamsters, indicating that Angptl3-siRNA markedly ameliorated liver injury (Fig. 3J and K). Overall, these findings demonstrate that inactivation of Angptl3 alleviates hepatic lipid deposition, fibrosis, and inflammation of HFD-induced MAFLD in LDLR−/− hamsters.

Lipid metabolism-related genes in the livers of LDLR-/- hamsters fed an HFD

In the liver tissues, Angptl3 expression was significantly decreased by 74.57% in Angptl3-siRNA-treated hamsters, indicating that the Angptl3-siRNA generated in this study was effective (Fig. 4A). The expressions levels of genes which regulate hepatic lipid metabolism were detected by RT-PCR. The expression levels of acetyl-CoA carboxylase 1 (Acc1), fatty acid synthase (Fasn), and sterol regulatory element-binding protein 1c (Srebp1c), three key genes involved in fatty acid and cholesterol synthesis, were significantly decreased in Angptl3-siRNA-treated hamsters (Fig. 4B). In contrast, the expression levels of ATP-binding cassette (Abc) transporters G5 (Abcg5) and Abcg8, which play a crucial role in the cholesterol efflux, were markedly increased in Angptl3-siRNA-treated hamsters. Mttp is an endoplasmic reticulum resident protein, which is essential for the assembly and secretion of TRLs and ApoB-containing lipoproteins [23]. Unfortunately, no differences in Mttp expression were detected between the two groups. Although the expression of very-low-density lipoprotein receptor (Vldlr), which is responsible for triglyceride metabolism, exhibited a trend toward increasing, the difference was not statistically significant.

Plasma NEFA were reduced in Angptl3-siRNA-treated hamsters. Therefore, expression of genes related to hepatic fatty acid uptake and oxidation were analysed [24, 25]. The results demonstrated that the expression of Cd36 and fatty acid transport protein 1 (Fatp1), which are involved in hepatic fatty acid uptake, were markedly decreased in Angptl3-siRNA-treated hamsters (Fig. 4C). Conversely, peroxisome-proliferator-activated receptor alpha (Pparα) and carnitine palmitoyltransferase 1 (Cpt1) expression were significantly increased, suggesting that fatty acid oxidation was promoted.

Overall, these results strongly support that Angptl3-siRNA may decease fatty acid and cholesterol synthesis, and promote cholesterol efflux and fatty acid oxidation, thereby reducing TG, TC and NEFA levels in plasma.

Inflammation-related and fibrosis-related genes in the livers of LDLR-/- hamsters fed an HFD

The liver pathology results revealed reductions in Sirius Red staining and CD68 fluorescence intensity in Angptl3-siRNA-treated hamsters. Accordingly, the expression levels of hepatic inflammation-related and fibrosis-related genes were quantified. Previous studies have shown that collagen α−1 type I (Col1a1) primarily promotes collagen synthesis [26]. The results revealed that the expression of transforming growth factor β (Tgfβ), Cd68, tumor necrosis factor a (Tnfa), and Col1a1 were significantly reduced (Fig. 4D). Taken together, these findings suggest that Angptl3-siRNA alleviates hepatic inflammation and fibrosis.

Effect of Angptl3-siRNA on aortic atherosclerosis in LDLR-/- hamsters fed an HFD

Pathological analysis revealed that both Angptl3-siRNA-treated and AAV9-null hamsters developed certain degrees of atherosclerosis after 16 weeks on an HFD. Aortic root analysis clearly revealed plaques in AAV9-null hamsters (Fig. 5A). ORO staining of aortic roots revealed that inactivation of Angptl3 significantly reduced atherosclerotic lesions in LDLR−/− hamsters. Additionally, compared with AAV9-null hamsters, Angptl3-siRNA-treated hamsters presented reduced CD68 and a-SMA expression levels in aortic roots (Fig. 5A), suggesting that Angptl3-siRNA could alleviate local vascular inflammation to prevent the development of atherosclerotic.

Similarly, aortic en face analysis revealed that atherosclerotic plaques in AAV9-null hamsters developed mainly in the aortic arch, abdominal aorta, renal artery, and could also be detected in the iliac artery at 16 weeks (Fig. 5B).

Quantitative analysis of aortic plaques revealed that atherosclerotic lesions in the aortic root (Fig. 5C) and the whole aorta (Fig. 5D) were significantly decreased in Angptl3-siRNA-treated hamsters after 16 weeks of HFD feeding. The ORO-positive areas in the aortic root and aorta of Angptl3-siRNA-treated hamsters were sharply decreased by 31.93% and 51.72%, respectively. To summarize, these results demonstrate that Angptl3-siRNA attenuates the progression of atherosclerosis in hypercholesterolaemic hamsters.

Discussion

In this study, an AAV vector expressing Angptl3-siRNA was constructed and used to investigate the impacts of the inactivation of Angptl3 on lipid metabolism, MAFLD, and atherosclerotic development in LDLR−/− hamsters.

The results demonstrated that siRNA-based inactivation of Angptl3 resulted in substantial decreases of plasma TG, TC, and HDL-C in HFD-fed LDLR−/− hamsters. These findings were in agreement with the observations in subjects carrying mutations in exon of the Angptl3 gene. After HFD for 16 weeks, AAV9-null LDLR−/− hamsters exhibited a massive increase of TG and TC contained in VLDL/LDL and a marked decrease of TC contained in HDL as determined by FPLC. The peaks of VLDL and LDL were almost same high, similar with other studies [27, 28]. However, this finding didn’t indicate that FPLC could not separate VLDL, LDL, or HDL in LDLR−/− hamsters. The lipoprotein profile results revealed that the TG and TC contents in the VLDL/LDL components were significantly decreased in Angptl3-siRNA-treated hamsters. The OFL assay also suggested that clearance of circulating TRLs from the plasma could be accelerated, regardless of the LDLR pathway. Overall, these findings indicate that inactivation of Angptl3 promotes lipid metabolism by increasing LPL activity and accelerates the TRLs clearance, thereby resulting in a reduction of plasma TG and TC.

Plasma apolipoprotein analysis revealed relatively low ApoB/ApoE levels and high ApoAI levels in Angptl3-siRNA-treated hamsters. Most circulating TG is carried by VLDL particles and their remnants, which also contain ApoB/ApoE. The data show that compared with LDL particles, ApoB/ApoE-containing lipoproteins possess more atherogenic properties [29, 30]. Taken together, these results indicate that inactivation of Angptl3 can reduce the levels of ApoB/ApoE-containing TRLs, thus then contributing to an anti-atherosclerotic effect. Moreover, inactivation of Angptl3 decreased the plasma HDL-C, which was primarily attributed to the increased endothelial lipase activity. However, unlike the plasma HDL-C, plasma ApoAI level was increased in Angptl3-siRNA-treated hamsters. In a study by Zhao et al., Angptl3 was positively correlated with ApoA1 in the HDL of female subjects (r = 0.587). Conversely, Angptl3 did not correlate with ApoA1 levels in male subjects (r = −0.015) [31]. Then, the effects of Angptl3 on the composition and function of HDL are still unclear and are worth of further study. Taken together, these results demonstrate that therapeutic inhibition of Angptl3 may reduce the residual atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) burden.

Additionally, reduced plasma GLU levels were detected in Angptl3-siRNA-treated hamsters, consistent with the findings of a previous report [9]. In homozygous subjects carrying a loss-of-function mutation in Angptl3, plasma glucose, insulin, and homeostatic model assessment of insulin resistance (HOMA-IR) were markedly decreased than those in heterozygotes and noncarriers [32]. Based on these findings, it is conceivable that targeting Angptl3 will benefit patients who often suffer from various degrees of hyperlipidaemia and hyperglycaemia.

Furthermore, inactivation of Angptl3 significantly ameliorated HFD-induced MAFLD in LDLR−/− hamsters. The results of hepatic lipid extraction and ORO staining demonstrated that Angptl3-siRNA remarkably reduced the levels of lipids accumulated in hepatocytes. Relative to that observed in the control group, Angptl3-siRNA-treated hamsters displayed a great improvement of morphological phenotypes in MAFDL, including hepatocyte ballooning, macrovesicular lipid droplet accumulation, fibrosis, and macrophage aggregation, as evidenced in pathological scores. These results suggest that inactivation of Angptl3 may be a potential target for treating hyperlipidaemia and MAFLD.

To assess whether reduced hepatic lipid deposition is attributable to decreased production of fatty acids and cholesterol or accelerated clearance of lipoproteins in the liver, the expression levels of hepatic lipid metabolism-related genes were analysed. The expression levels of Acc1, Fasn, Srebp1c, Cd36, and Fatp1, which participate in fatty acid uptake and cholesterol synthesis, were downregulated in Angptl3-siRNA-treated hamsters; this finding is consistent with that by Shimamura M [33]. In adipose tissue, Angptl3 induces lipolysis and causes the release of free fatty acid (FFA) and glycerol from adipocytes. The supply of FFA from adipocytes to the liver increases hepatic VLDL synthesis [34]. Therfore, Angptl3-siRNA led to reduced hepatic VLDL synthesis and lower plasma levels of NEFA. In contrast, the expression levels Abcg5, Abcg8, Pparα and Cpt1, which are responsible for fatty acid oxidation and cholesterol efflux, were upregulated in Angptl3-siRNA-treated hamsters. This finding indicates that Angptl3-siRNA simultaneously promotes fatty acid oxidation and cholesterol efflux in the liver, thereby resulting in a reduction of hepatic lipid accumulation.

The results of the OFL assay demonstrated that TRLs clearance in the circulation was accelerated in Angptl3-siRNA-treated hamsters. Consequently, the expression levels of genes involved in the triglyceride clearance in the liver were evaluated. The results revealed no differences in Mttp or Vldlr expression between the two groups. A previous study also reported that hepatic Mttp mRNA expression was not affected by Angptl3-ASO in LDLR−/− mice [9]. Vldlr is involved in lipoprotein uptake and storage, binds ApoE and TRLs (chylomicrons and VLDL), and promotes lipid entry into the cells [35]. However, its expression is extremely low in the liver normally [36]. Additionally, the expression levels of Tgfβ, Cd68, Tnfa, and Col1a1 were reduced, suggesting that Angptl3-siRNA alleviates hepatic inflammation and fibrosis.

Furthermore, Angptl3-siRNA-treated hamsters exhibited reduced atherosclerotic plaques in the aortic root and aorta. This observation can be attributed to an anti-atherogenic lipoprotein profile. In 2025, Zhang Y et al. reported that Angptl3 directly regulated M1 macrophage activation in plaques and accelerated atherosclerotic progression [37]. Angptl3 overexpression accelerated atherosclerotic progression and increased CD68 + macrophages in plaques in LDLR−/− and ApoE−/− mice. These results suggest that inhibition of Angptl3 may result in a double benefit, not only reducing blood lipid levels but also inhibiting macrophage activation in plaques.

To date, the results from cardiovascular outcome trials does not seem to be directly related to decreases in TG levels, because the number of ApoB/ApoE-containing particles is the primary factor of cardiovascular risk. Therefore, a reduction in the number of ApoB/ApoE-containing particles (VLDL and LDL) may successfully lower plasma lipoproteins and cardiovascular risk. Inactivation of Angptl3 reduced ApoB/ApoE levels and increased VLDL clearance via endothelial lipase [38], leading to the sequential decrease in plasma VLDL and LDL-C. Therefore, inactivation of Angptl3 provides a promising option to decrease the incidence of cardiovascular diseases in patients with hypertriglyceridaemia.

Finally, although statin therapy was well established to prevent ASCVD events over the past two decades, it has been limited clinically with dose-dependent elevation of liver enzymes and hepatic diseases. Angptl3 inhibition, which leads to reduction in not only triglycerides and remnant cholesterol but also hepatic steatosis and atherosclerosis, is a prospective treatment option for patients with residual dyslipidaemia.

Study strengths and limitations

This study revealed that siRNA-based inactivation of Angptl3 not only reduced plasma TG, TC, HDL-C, and GLU, but also alleviated HFD-induced MAFLD and atherosclerosis in LDLR−/− hamsters, an experimental animal model showing severe combined hyperlipidemia. However, because we primarily focused on the beneficial effects of targeting Angptl3 in male LDLR−/− hamsters and whether silencing Angptl3 also generates a favorable plasma lipid profile and then prevents MAFLD and atherosclerosis in female animals remains unknow, which needs to be validated in future study.

Conclusions

This study demonstrates that siRNA-based inactivation of Angptl3 alleviates diet-induced MAFLD and atherosclerosis in LDLR−/− hamsters, which further provides valuable insights that Angptl3 might be a potential therapeutic strategy for hyperlipidaemia, MAFLD, and cardiovascular disease. In the future, Angptl3 inhibitors may hold the translational potential for the clinical treatment of hyperlipidemia to slow the progression of MAFLD and atherosclerosis.

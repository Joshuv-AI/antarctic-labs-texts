Successful uniportal video-assisted thoracoscopic sleeve lobectomy of bronchial carcinoid tumor in a 5-year-old child
Authors: YenZhir Tay, Chudong Wang, Yunpeng Zhong, Diego Gonzalez-Rivas, Shuben Li
Published: 2025

Anastomosis between the right intermediate bronchus and the right lower lobar bronchus.

Central Message

This report details a case of uniportal video-assisted thoracoscopic middle sleeve lobectomy performed on a pediatric patient with an atypical bronchial carcinoid tumor.

Bronchial carcinoid tumors are rare in the pediatric population, and complete surgical resection offers the best prognosis for affected patients.1 Video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS) with lung-sparing techniques is essential for growing children because it improves their quality of life.2 However, the use of uniportal VATS with sleeve lobectomy in the pediatric setting has been limited because of the technical complexities involved.3 This case report describes the first successful uniportal VATS right middle sleeve lobectomy performed in a 5-year-old child with atypical bronchial carcinoid tumor.

Case Presentation

A 5-year-old girl was referred to our center with a diagnosis of recurrent atypical bronchial carcinoid tumor. Bronchoscopy revealed residual polypoidal endobronchial tumor in the right intermediate bronchus (Figure 1, A). Findings of the physical examination were unremarkable and contrast-enhanced computed tomography of the chest showed thickened at the right intermediate bronchus with mildly enlarged mediastinal lymph nodes (Figure 1, B). Magnetic resonance imaging of the brain and lung function assessment were normal. After a multidisciplinary team discussion, a uniportal VATS right middle sleeve lobectomy was planned. Informed consent for the publication of this case report was obtained from the patient's guardian; institutional review board approval was not required.

Surgical Technique

The child was induced and intubated with a cuffed 4.5-mm single-lumen tracheal tube, which was advanced into the left main bronchus (right main bronchus blocker was abandoned as the result of positional instability during respiration). She was positioned in left lateral decubitus and placement of endotracheal tube was checked by flexible bronchoscopy. A single 3-cm skin incision was made in the fifth intercostal space (Figure 1, C) along the anterior axillary line. A small-sized wound protector was used without muscle sparing. A 10-mm 30° thoracoscope and double-hinged VATS instrument were used in this surgery. The inferior pulmonary ligament was released and careful dissection of the hilum was performed to expose the middle lobe vein and artery with hook diathermy and ultrasonic device (Ethicon). The interlobar fissure was opened with an endostapler (Ethicon) and right middle lobe was divided to expose the right intermediate bronchus. The tumor was exposed after the division of the right intermediate bronchus and both proximal and distal bronchial margins were negative on frozen section. The remaining right intermediate bronchus was reconstructed with right lower lobe bronchus using an absorbable, double armed 4-0 PDS II (polydioxanone) suture (Ethicon) in a continuous manner (Figure 1, D). Hemostasis was secured and bronchial stump showed no leakage upon air leak testing. A 16-French chest drain was placed via the same incision. She was successfully extubated postoperatively. The chest tube was removed on postoperative day 5 as a precautionary strategy because of the patient's young age and surveillance bronchoscopy showed intact bronchial anastomosis (Figure 1, E) before discharge. Contrast-enhanced computed tomography of the thorax 3 months' postoperatively showed no sign of stenosis or recurrence (Figure E1).

Computed tomography scan of the thorax 3 month after surgery demonstrates a patent anastomosis.

Discussion

Malignant bronchial sleeve resections are more commonly performed in the adult.4 Uniportal VATS evolved from adult surgery can be apply to children, in whom this single-incision technique can reduce surgical trauma. It reduces postoperative pain and permits early mobilization and provides a better cosmetic outcome, especially in a growing child.5 To our knowledge, the current literature does not describe the use of uniportal VATS with sleeve lobectomy in the pediatric population. In this case, the goal is to achieve complete tumor resection in the least-invasive manner, using a uniportal approach and lung-sparing technique, to minimize morbidity in children where lung development is ongoing. Given the small airway in this case, a single-lumen endobronchial tube was used to ventilate the left lung as the double-lumen endobronchial tube is not suitable. To enhance dexterity and reduce surgical intricacy, a 5-mm 30° thoracoscope can be employed to create additional working space. Furthermore, the use of narrow-shafted, double-hinged VATS instruments is advisable for thoracoscopic procedures. Mobilization of the inferior pulmonary ligaments and hilum were crucial to avoid tension on the anastomosis. Using an absorbable suture instead of a nonabsorbable suture is the key to prevent future narrowing and allows the airway to continue to develop and mature into their adulthood.1

Conclusions

This case demonstrates the successful use of uniportal VATS in performing an anatomically challenging sleeve lobectomy in a pediatric patient with a bronchial carcinoid tumor. We believe that uniportal VATS can be a safe and effective approach for children.

Continuous manipulation of electromagnetic radiation based on ultrathin flexible frequency coding metasurface
Authors: Min Jia, Chao Zhao, Zhouhao Tang, Ziliang Jin, Ningtao Zhang, Xiaofeng Han
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The physical characteristics of electromagnetic waves are combined with digital information in coding metasurfaces. Coding metasurfaces enable precise control of beams by flexibly designing coding sequences. However, achieving continuous multivariate modulation of electromagnetic waves on passive flexible coded metasurfaces remains a challenge. Previous passive coding metasurfaces have a fixed phase difference between adjacent coding units throughout the operating frequency band, and when the coding pattern is defined, the coded metasurface can only achieve a single electromagnetic function. Our proposed frequency coding metasurface units vary linearly in phase difference over the operating frequency band with different phase sensitivities. Frequency coding metarsurfaces enable a wide range of tunable and versatile electromagnetic energy radiation, without introducing any active devices and changing the coding pattern. As a demonstration of the concept, we have shown theoretically and numerically that frequency coding metasurface can achieve successive transformations of electromagnetic functions, including multi-beam generation, anomalous deflection and diffuse scattering. In addition, beam sweeping function is achieved by means of spatially non-periodically distributed frequency coding metasurface. When the frequency of the incident wave is changed, the deflection angle of the beam is also changed. In addition to the tunability of properties, research on coding metasurfaces has tended to be limited to rigid materials. Flexible coding metasurfaces have potential applications in microwave antennas, radar and aircraft. The passive flexible frequency coding metasurfaces provide a novel approach to manipulating electromagnetic waves with increased design flexibility. This promises applications in microwave antennas, radar, aircraft, and satellite communications.

Introduction

Metamaterials, are defined as artificially periodic metallic or dielectric structures with subwavelength thickness, which enable us to achieve unprecedented electromagnetic (EM) Properties1,2. Through the special design of the unit structure and its arrangement, extraordinary medium parameters that natural materials do not have or are extremely difficult to realize can be constructed, such as negative dielectric constant3, negative magnetic permeability4 or negative refractive index5,6, which in turn generate special electromagnetic responses and novel physical phenomena. After the introduction of transformation optics7,8, the precise manipulation of electromagnetic wave propagation using metamaterials has become a reality, giving rise to a large number of novel metamaterial functional devices, such as microwave cloaking devices9, broadband two-dimensional terrestrial cloaking suits10, switching and sensing11, and high-resolution imaging magnifying lens12. Although the advent of metamaterials has provided a precise tool for the flexible manipulation of electromagnetic waves, the modulation of electromagnetic waves by metamaterials is based on the cumulative effect of spatial variations in the refractive index. Such metamaterials are 3D, typically thick and heavy, and are subject to high losses and complex processing and assembly, especially in the terahertz and optical ranges. Therefore, metasurface research was born from the urgent need to construct ultrathin artificial 2D metamaterials with high utility13,14.

A generalized Snell's law for the design of metasurfaces based on the concept of phase mutation was proposed by Prof. Capasso of Harvard University in 201115. The intuitive design process and relative simplicity of the above methods have inspired a wide range of researchers, resulting in a series of high-quality research results in recent years, which have greatly contributed to the revolutionary development of metasurfaces16–20. By precisely tuning the amplitude and phase response of the meta-atoms through cell structure design, the metasurfaces can be optimized for efficient wave front manipulation. In the electromagnetic spectral range from microwave and terahertz to infrared and visible regimes, metasurfaces have been shown to exhibit extraordinary effects, including perfect metamaterial absorber21, polarization conversion22, anomalous deflection and reflection23,24, and orbital angular momentum (OAM) generation25–27. Controlling the high degree of freedom of metasurface properties has enabled unprecedented functionality that has led to breakthroughs in many areas of research, including electromagnetic cloaking28, communication29,30, advanced imaging31, and augmented reality/virtual reality (AR/VR)32.

Unlike analogue metamaterials, Professor Tiejun Cui and colleagues innovatively introduced the concept of digital coding to metasurface design in 201433. The different electromagnetic responses of the metasurface units are characterized by different digits in digital coding metasurfaces. For 1-bit digital coding metasurfaces, the numbers "0" and "1" are used to denote two metasurface units with a phase difference of π. Two different digital units can be achieved by changing the structural dimensions of the metasurface unit designed. The idea of digital coding allows researchers to design and study metasurfaces from a completely new perspective of information science34–42. For more precise tuning of electromagnetic waves and more functionality, digital coding methods can be extended from 1-bit to 2-bit or higher-bit scenarios. For example, a 2-bit digital coding to metasurface is formed by four coding units "00", "01", "10" and "11" with a phase difference of 90°, which can be used to realize beam deflection and vortex wave generation43. Meanwhile, the researchers also designed an anisotropic digital coding metasurface and dual-frequency bifunctional digital coding metasurface, to further explore the ability of digital coding metasurfaces to modulate electromagnetic waves44,45. In addition, the study of digital coding metasurfaces has been extended from the microwave and millimetre wave range to the acoustic, terahertz and optical range46–48. However, for passive digital coding metasurfaces, once the processing is completed, its electromagnetic regulation function is solidified, so it is difficult to be organically integrated with modern intelligent adjustable systems. In recent years, the realization of a dynamic tunable function on a single metasurface has been an important direction of metasurface research. In the microwave range, semiconductor elements such as varactor diodes and PIN diodes are loaded into the metasurface cells, which is the most common and very effective way of constructing active dynamic metasurfaces49–52. With the continuous development of new materials in the field of electronic information, some tunable sensitive materials (e.g. liquid crystals, microfluidics and graphene) have been used by researchers to combine with metasurface to create dynamic metasurface53–60. In recent studies, active amplification circuits have been integrated into the metasurfaces to achieve an adjustable amplification function61–63.

For active metasurfaces, which take the form of electrical control, the metasurface is usually connected directly to an external power supply and control circuit via physical wires. Although this method is very effective, it can easily cause crosstalk between DC and microwave signals. This affects the performance of the metasurface and also increases the size of the whole system. Thus, the achievement of tunability on passively coding metasurfaces still leaves a lot of potential unexplored. In addition to the tunability of properties, research on coding metasurfaces has tended to be limited to rigid materials, with fewer studies on coding metasurfaces based on flexible materials. Flexible coding metasurfaces have potential applications in microwave antennas, radar and aircraft. Therefore, the study of coding metasurface design and beam modulation based on flexible materials is of great importance.

In this context, we present a design method of ultrathin and flexible frequency coding metasurface. When adopting a spatial periodic arrangement, we can achieve continuous transformations of electromagnetic functions such as frequency-tunable multibeam, anomalous deflection and diffuse scattering on passive flexible frequency coding metasurfaces. When a spatially non-periodic arrangement is employed, the beam sweeping function can be implemented on a passive flexible frequency coding metasurface. On the one hand, the frequency coding metasurface proposed in this paper has the advantages of low cost and easy deployment of passive metasurfaces. On the other hand, the frequency coding metasurface proposed in this paper also has the dynamic modulation of the beam by the active metasurface. The structure proposed in this paper can be extended to other frequency bands, including terahertz and optical bands. Ultrathin flexible frequency coding metasurfaces are expected to be valuable in electromagnetic sweeping, flexible conformal devices, and electromagnetic stealth and other related fields.

Structure design of frequency coding metasurface

For conventional coding metamaterial, the phase difference between cells is usually required to be consistent within the operating frequency band. Therefore, only fixed electromagnetic functions can be achieved in the operating frequency band. Unlike conventional coding metamaterial, the phase difference between frequency coding metasurface cells varies approximately linearly with the frequency of the incident electromagnetic wave. For frequency coding metasurface, the phase distribution pattern has a large variation within the operating frequency band. As shown in Fig. 1, the electromagnetic functions that can be achieved by frequency coding metasurface change with frequency, thus enabling continuous and variable modulation of electromagnetic waves. At 20 GHz, the reflection amplitude of units 1, 2, 3, and 4 reaches 0.99, 0.95, 0.92, and 0.96, respectively. This provides an effective basis for subsequent far-field simulations. It is important to acknowledge that two factors must be taken into account concurrently during the design of frequency coding metasurface units. In order to illustrate this point, we will take the 2-bit frequency coding metasurface as an example. Firstly, the four metasurface units exhibit a similar electromagnetic response phase. Secondly, with regard to the phase response trend with respect to frequency, the four metasurface units exhibit disparate trends. The design of frequency coding metasurface is a challenging undertaking, primarily due to the necessity of considering both of these factors. At the initial frequency, the four digital units of frequency coding metasurfaces have an almost uniform phase response, as shown in Fig. 3b. On the other hand, it is noted that the phase-frequency response sensitivity of the four digital units of frequency coding metasurfaces has a large difference in the operating frequency band, and the phase difference between the digital units varies approximately linearly with frequency. A key aspect of the design to achieve frequency coding metasurface is to take advantage of these phase characteristics. 2‑bit frequency coding metasurface

The concept of the coding metasurface can be extended from 1-bit coding to 2-bit coding, or even higher. Compared to a 1-bit frequency coding metasurface, a 2-bit frequency coding metasurface has more design freedom due to the increase in the number of available digital unit cells. 2-bit frequency coding metasurface can achieve more flexible and versatile modulation of electromagnetic waves. The corresponding phase curves over the frequency of such units are illustrated in Fig. 3, and the analyzed results are presented in Table 2. Since these frequency digital units have almost the same phase response at the initial frequency, their corresponding spatially encoded states are all “0”. On the other hand, it is noted that these four units have four different high and low different phase-frequency response sensitivity curves. Conclusion

In conclusion, we present an ultrathin and flexible frequency coding metasurface that can continuously modulate electromagnetic waves in the frequency domain without requiring a redesign of the structure. We designed four metasurface unit cells with different phase-frequency sensitivities and used the difference in phase-frequency sensitivity to design coding patterns to achieve continuous modulation of electromagnetic waves. By constructing 1-bit and 2-bit flexible frequency coding metasurface, we achieve different functions such as frequency tunable multi-beam generation, diffuse scattering and anomalous deflection. Using the 2-bit frequency coding metasurface as an example, the beam scanning function can be implemented by adopting the non-periodic spatial arrangement. The paper demonstrates the manipulation of beams and frequency tunability of ultrathin flexible frequency coding metasurfaces through theoretical analysis and numerical simulations. The flexible frequency coding metasurface proposed here offers a novel method for modulating electromagnetic waves. Its passive and flexible characteristics make it a promising technology for a range of applications, including microwave antennas, aircraft, wireless communications, and stealth.

Remission of Newly Diagnosed Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus in an Elderly Woman Through an Integrated Strategy: A Case Report
Authors: Man Yin, Mingyue Hu, Xiaoting Huang, Lixiang Chen, Fuzhen Cheng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTRemission is achievable in elderly patients with new‐onset Type 2 diabetes. This case highlights that short‐term intensive insulin therapy combined with sustained lifestyle management is a viable strategy, offering a valuable paradigm for future management.Keywords: case report, lifestyle intervention, short‐term intensive insulin therapy, Tai Chi, Type 2 diabetes mellitus

Introduction

Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) is a chronic metabolic disorder characterized by hyperglycemia resulting from insulin resistance [1], which accounts for nearly 90% of the approximately 537 million cases of diabetes worldwide [2]. T2DM has conventionally been considered a lifelong, progressive disease [3]. Recently, remission of T2DM has been of increasing interest to professionals and patients [4]. The treatment of T2DM has shifted from focusing on long‐term medication to exploring remission and potential cure. T2DM remission is defined as maintaining a glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c) level below 6.5% (48 mmol/L) for at least 3 months without pharmacological intervention [5]. This can be achieved through short‐term intensive insulin therapy (SIIT), while the remission effect of SIIT gradually decreases over time [6]. T2DM remission represents a dynamic state that requires long‐term management rather than a permanent cure [5]. Lifestyle intervention serves as the fundamental regimen for T2DM remission, providing a reference for the maintenance of remission [7]. The effectiveness of SIIT and intensive lifestyle intervention on T2DM remission has long been known [8, 9]. Globally, an estimated 110.1 million individuals aged 70 years or older were living with diabetes (Types 1 and 2 combined), representing a prevalence rate of 23.7% within this age demographic [10]. This substantial disease burden underscores the imperative for developing targeted remission strategies specifically designed for the elderly. However, research focusing on newly diagnosed T2DM in the elderly is limited. Moreover, achieving sustained remission in this group is particularly challenging due to the presence of multiple comorbidities, reduced responsiveness to lifestyle interventions, and an increased risk of hypoglycemia. Given the challenges unique to older individuals, our case illustrated a practical and effective approach to T2DM remission. We reported a 70‐year‐old overweight woman with newly diagnosed T2DM who achieved remission through SIIT and lifestyle intervention. After being discharged from the hospital, she adhered to an intensive lifestyle intervention program that involved maintaining a healthy diet of whole grains and fiber, low in fat, walking at least 5000 steps daily, and practicing moderate‐intensity Tai Chi three times a week.

Case History/Examination

A 70‐year‐old Chinese female presented to our clinic on May 8, 2023, with chief complaints of dry mouth, polydipsia, and polyuria, accompanied by intermittent urinary foam that had persisted for over a year and worsened in the last 3 months. Her fasting plasma glucose (FPG) was 6.4 mmol/L during a health check‐up on February 24, 2023. She refused oral hypoglycemic drugs and sought SIIT at our clinic. She has no family history of diabetes but has comorbidities including hypertension, fatty liver disease, and right‐sided facial paralysis. She has no history of food allergies but is allergic to sulfonamides. Her gynecological history includes menarche at 13, menopause at 46, and a hysterectomy performed due to irregular and heavy menstrual bleeding.

At the first visit, the patient was overweight with a body mass index (BMI) of 24.9 kg/m2 and a waist circumference of 98 cm. Her laboratory tests revealed an FPG of 5.48 mmol/L, 1 and 2‐h postprandial glucose (PPG) levels of 13.98 mmol/L and 11.70 mmol/L, respectively, and an HbA1c level of 8.0%. Additionally, the 2‐h insulin level (78.8 mIU/L) and the 2‐h C‐peptide level (3.84 nmol/L) were out of the normal range. These clinical findings confirmed the diagnosis of T2DM, indicating mild pancreatic dysfunction and poor glycemic control over the past 3 months.

Differential Diagnosis, Investigations and Treatment

The patient's symptoms and laboratory findings were consistent with T2DM. Given the patient's high PPG, mild pancreatic dysfunction, and the opportunity for beta‐cell rest early in the disease course, SIIT was considered more suitable to induce remission than initiating oral hypoglycemics [11]. Recent research indicates that SIIT can induce β‐cell recovery and remission of T2DM and that, when followed by subsequent oral hypoglycemic agents, this approach has been shown to enhance long‐term glycemic control in patients with newly diagnosed T2DM and severe hyperglycaemia [9]. Although standardized geriatric assessment instruments were not administered, the patient underwent routine functional evaluations of mobility, cognition, and independence in daily activities, showing no impairments that would contraindicate intensive interventions. Thus, we adopted an integrated strategy including SIIT and lifestyle intervention to achieve T2DM remission. The patient was hospitalized for 23 days, during which she received SIIT for 22 days (Figure 1). Short‐Term Intensive Insulin Therapy (SIIT)

SIIT was administered to rapidly regulate blood glucose levels, reverse beta‐cell dysfunction, and enhance insulin sensitivity, to induce remission of T2DM. In accordance with the insulin pump therapy guidelines for China [12] —which recommends initiating SIIT at 0.5–0.8 IU/kg/day for newly diagnosed T2DM patients—the patient received insulin lispro at a starting dose of 0.5 IU/kg/day. The total daily insulin was divided into basal (40%) and prandial (60%) components during the hospitalization phase. The insulin dosage was adjusted based on the patient's blood glucose levels. Once the target blood glucose control was achieved (FPG < 6.1 mmol/L and PPG < 7.8 mmol/L), it was recommended to continue insulin therapy for an additional 2 weeks to ensure the stability and durability of the therapeutic effects.

Lifestyle Intervention

We implemented a lifestyle intervention to achieve 5%–10% weight loss, referring to current guidelines [13]. The lifestyle intervention program included a healthy diet and regular exercise.

The diet was designed to be palatable, accessible, and sustainable for the patient during hospitalization. It emphasized whole grains, fruits, and vegetables while minimizing animal foods such as red meat and refined foods containing added sugars and oils. Minimally processed foods were provided to preserve nutritional value and flavor. The nutritionist tailored the plan to ensure that carbohydrates, proteins, and fats comprised 50%–65%, 15%–20%, and 20%–30% of total energy intake. The patient was advised to maintain a daily dietary fiber intake of 25–35 g and limit staple foods to 100–120 g per meal, with daily calorie distribution of 30% at breakfast, 40% at lunch, and 30% at dinner. Additionally, the patient was encouraged to eat all three main meals within a 10‐h window from 7 a.m. to 5 p.m. If the patient experiences hunger at other times, she can ease her hunger by drinking water or eating low‐calorie fruits such as tomatoes or cucumbers.

Exercise therapists developed a supervised exercise regimen customized to the patient's physical condition and preferences. A key component of this regimen was a tailored Yang‐style Tai Chi sequence consisting of 24 movements, which the patient was instructed to practice three times weekly. Each session was 30 min long, including a 5‐min warm‐up, 20 min of Tai Chi practice, and a 5‐min relaxation period. In addition to Tai Chi, the patient was advised to walk 5000 steps daily and reduce periods of prolonged sitting.

Outcome and Follow‐Up

The patient underwent a 23‐day hospitalization for intensive insulin therapy. During this period, her FPG decreased from 6.6 mmol/L on Day 1 to 5.3 mmol/L on Day 23. The total daily insulin doses initially increased, peaking at 20.3 U on Day 5, and gradually decreased, reaching 0 U on Day 23. The patient showed significant improvements in pancreatic function, as evidenced by the notable reductions in fasting and postprandial glucose, insulin, and C‐peptide levels presented (Table 1).

After being discharged from the hospital on June 1st, 2023, the patient ceased insulin treatment and did not take any oral hypoglycemic medications. Instead, she continued with the lifestyle intervention program. To facilitate ongoing support, we established a WeChat group for online follow‐ups. Through this digital interface, the patient proactively submitted biweekly self‐reported health metrics including blood glucose and weight data. Our multidisciplinary clinical team analyzed these parameters to deliver evidence‐based health guidance comprising: (1) individualized nutritional modifications tailored to glycemic patterns, (2) progressive physical activity recommendations aligned with functional capacity, and (3) behavioral reinforcement strategies to enhance treatment adherence. Additionally, the patient was assessed at outpatient visits approximately every 3 months, during which her HbA1c, FPG, PPG, weight, BMI, waist circumference, and blood pressure were recorded (Table 2). Notably, this case is distinguished by the successful remission of T2DM through the combined approach of SIIT and lifestyle intervention.

During the first visit on September 6, 2023, the patient had not taken any oral hypoglycemic medications for over 3 months, and her HbA1c level was 6.0%. By adhering to the lifestyle intervention alone, the patient achieved T2DM remission. She sustained remission of T2DM and demonstrated significant improvements in blood lipid profiles (Table 3), primarily attributed to her substantial weight loss (from 63 to 53.4 kg) and reduced waist circumference (from 98 to 80 cm) over 1 year. Her HbA1c levels decreased from 8.0% in May 2023 to 6.0% in July 2023 and fluctuated around 6.0% with slight variations. Her BMI decreased from 24.9 kg/m2 in May 2023 to 21.2 kg/m2 in July 2023 and remained relatively stable at around 21.0 kg/m2 with minor fluctuations (Figure 2). During the recent follow‐up visit on February 26, 2025, the patient's HbA1c level was 6.0%, demonstrating the sustained remission of T2DM (Figure 2). Notably, the waist circumference reduced from 98 to 79 cm, representing a significant 19 cm decrease. The patient has maintained T2DM remission for 21 months post intervention without any reported adverse events. The patient self‐reported significant improvements across multiple health domains, including enhanced energy levels, better sleep quality, elevated health‐related quality of life, and overall satisfaction with her current health status.

Discussion

To our knowledge, this was the first case to combine SIIT with intensive lifestyle intervention, contributing to the remission of a 70‐year‐old female with T2DM. The integrated strategy effectively facilitated weight loss, enhanced glycemic control, and maintained beta‐cell function in the newly diagnosed elderly patient. Despite the benefits, SIIT in elderly patients requires careful and continuous monitoring. Older individuals face increased vulnerability to hypoglycemia attributable to age‐related declines in renal function, impaired counter‐regulatory hormonal responses, and potential cognitive impairment—all factors that can diminish their ability to recognize and respond appropriately to low blood glucose levels [14]. Additionally, the coexistence of multiple comorbidities and concomitant medication use can complicate insulin management and elevate the risk of adverse effects or drug interactions [15]. These factors underscore the critical need for individualized treatment strategies and close clinical supervision when administering SIIT in the elderly. In our case, daily glucose monitoring and gradual insulin titration are effective in mitigating these risks. The patient consistently monitored her blood glucose level at home, which remained normal with little variation throughout the treatment and follow‐up.

The patient's improved beta‐cell function and increased insulin sensitivity are likely key factors in achieving T2DM remission [16]. The patient's HbA1c levels decreased significantly from 8.0% to 6.0%, reflecting a 2.0 percentage point reduction. This is consistent with McInnes et al.'s findings that a 12‐week intensive intervention combining insulin therapy and lifestyle modifications can induce T2DM remission [17]. The patient's substantial weight loss of 8.8 kg (from 63.0 to 54.2 kg) likely played a crucial role in achieving T2DM remission. Consistent with research by Franz et al., weight loss of > 5% was associated with significant improvement in HbA1c, lipids, and blood pressure [18]. Lean et al. [19] also reported that 46% of adult patients achieved T2DM remission after losing an average of 10 kg through the Counterweight‐Plus program over 12 months. However, this approach, involving a liquid formula diet for at least 3 months, was unsuitable for our patient due to its extremely low‐calorie regimen. Considering the patient's age, we adopted a more flexible dietary control approach and tailored exercise program. This report showed that SIIT induced T2DM remission, whereas intensive lifestyle intervention maintained long‐term remission.

The two unique features in this particular case report are: (1) the advanced age of the patient, and (2) the integrated strategy. Age was consistently reported as an independent predictor of T2DM remission [20], with younger individuals generally exhibiting better remission outcomes [21]. Previous studies have not sufficiently captured the potential for T2DM remission in elderly populations. We highlighted the effectiveness of combining SIIT with intensive lifestyle intervention for achieving long‐term remission of T2DM in elderly patients with multimorbidity. Notably, her sustained adherence to a comprehensive lifestyle intervention program, including a balanced nutritional plan and regular exercise, played a pivotal role in achieving long‐term remission. Furthermore, this integrated approach may reduce long‐term costs by lowering oral hypoglycemic medication dependence. Initial hospitalization and multidisciplinary care costs are offset by reduced drug/complication expenses. However, elderly patients often require tailored supervision to mitigate hypoglycemia risks during SIIT, increasing personnel demands. Early remission strategies could shift T2DM management, potentially reducing national healthcare burdens.

This case was reported following the CARE guidelines [22]. However, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the findings are based on a single patient case, limiting their generalizability to wider patient populations. Second, selection bias may be present. The patient in this case was cognitively intact and physically capable. Therefore, findings may not be generalizable to elderly individuals with significant cognitive decline or mobility limitations. Third, due to economic constraints, complete laboratory data were not always collected during each clinic visit. Nonetheless, all available laboratory results have been reported to mitigate publication bias. Fourth, the absence of a standardized control group fundamentally limits causal attribution—we cannot conclusively determine whether observed outcomes resulted from the synergistic effects of SIIT and lifestyle interventions, or if either component alone would suffice.

This case demonstrates that T2DM remission in elderly patients is achievable with early, integrated, and personalized care. Additionally, digital engagement through WeChat follow‐up facilitated sustained adherence and proactive patient involvement, which was critical to maintaining remission. Notably, randomized controlled trials are warranted before these treatments can be widely applied.

Age-related differences in clinical manifestations, neuroimaging features, and treatment of spontaneous intracranial hypotension: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Xiaohang Dong, Qirui Liu, Sichen He, Guohui Huang, Wangdi Huang, Letao Zhang, Bin Chen, Wei Xie, Suyue Pan, Yabin Ji
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe influence of age on the management of spontaneous intracranial hypotension (SIH), particularly epidural blood patch (EBP), remains unclear. This study aims to characterize age-related differences in clinical manifestation, neuroimaging features, and treatment outcomes.MethodsIn this retrospective cohort study, we analyzed 54 SIH patients who underwent EBP at our center. Patients were classified into two groups based on the cohort median age (35 years). Demographic, clinical, neuroimaging features, and treatment outcomes were compared between the two groups. We further utilized Spearman correlation, locally weighted scatterplot smoothing, and multivariable linear regression to analyze the association between age and EBP volume. Several sensitivity analyses were conducted to assess the robustness of our findings.ResultsAmong 54 enrolled patients, the injected volume of EBP was markedly larger in the older group (26.00 [19.12, 32.75] vs. 19.25 [15.00, 24.00] ml, P = 0.018). While Spearman correlation indicated a significant positive correlation existed between age and EBP volume (ρ = 0.388, P = 0.004), subgroup analysis revealed a dissociation: a strong linear correlation in the older group (ρ = 0.458, P = 0.018) contrasted with a non-significant, non-monotonic trend in the younger group (ρ = -0.034, P = 0.863). Linear regression analysis showed that age was positively associated with EBP volume (β = 2.899, 95% confidence interval: 0.475–5.322, P = 0.023).ConclusionThese findings highlight the potential utility of considering patient age when planning EBP procedures, suggesting that age-adapted volume strategies may optimize treatment and warrant further investigation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12883-026-05186-9.

Introduction

Spontaneous intracranial hypotension (SIH) arises from cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) leakage, and the hallmark symptom of SIH is orthostatic headache [1, 2]. Headache occurs in the sitting or standing position and is relieved when lying flat, forcing the patient to stay in bed for a long time, which has a substantial adverse impact on their social life and health-related quality of life [3]. Diagnosis, according to the International Classification of Headache Disorders, 3rd edition (ICHD-3), requires measurements of low CSF pressure (< 60 mm CSF) and/or evidence of CSF leak on neuroimaging [4]. The mainstay treatment for SIH is epidural blood patch (EBP), especially when conservative treatment has failed [1, 2, 5, 6]. Several factors, such as bed-rest duration, bed-rest position, and whether the EBP was targeted versus blind, have been implicated in influencing EBP response rate [7]. Notably, a greater injected blood volume has been associated with a higher response rate [8].

SIH exhibits a broad age distribution at onset, ranging from 2 to 88 years old [9]. However, with a wide range of age at onset, the impact of age-related differences on clinical manifestations, neuroimaging features, and treatment outcomes in SIH patients remains unclear. Therefore, this study aims to characterize age-related differences in the clinical manifestations, neuroimaging features, and treatment outcomes among patients with SIH. This analysis seeks to provide novel insight into SIH diagnosis and treatment.

Method

Study design and participants

This single-center, retrospective study analyzed data from patients who met inclusion and exclusion criteria at Nanfang Hospital Baiyun Branch (the People’s Hospital of Baiyun Distinct). The inclusion criteria for study participants were as follows: (1) patients diagnosed with SIH from January 2024 to May 2025 at our care center; (2) patients who underwent EBP at our institution. The exclusion criteria included patients with orthostatic headache secondary to lumbar puncture, patients who recovered from conservative treatment or lost to follow-up after EBP. The flowchart is presented in Fig. 1. SIH diagnosis was confirmed through evidence of CSF leak on neuroimaging or low CSF pressure during diagnostic lumbar puncture (defined as an opening pressure of less than 60 mm CSF on a spinal tap), consistent with ICHD 3rd criteria for SIH diagnosis [4]. Patients were separated into younger and older groups, using a cutoff age of 35 years, which was determined based on the median age of our cohort.

Demographic, clinical profiles, and neuroimaging features

Demographic and clinical characteristics, including age, sex, associated symptoms, blood pressure (pre- and post-EBP), blood pressure change scores (defined as post minus pre), visual analog scale (VAS), and the interval between disease onset and initial consultation at our center, were recorded at the first consultation for all patients. CSF opening pressure was obtained through lumbar puncture prior to the EBP procedure. In cases of uncertain or failed pressure measurement, patients with undetectable CSF opening pressure were further examined by neuroimaging and those in whom the needle was not in the subarachnoid space were excluded from the CSF opening pressure comparison.

Patients included in this study underwent dynamic computed tomography myelography or magnetic resonance myelography for CSF leak localization, and the images were reviewed by an experienced neuroradiologist. The type of CSF leaks was determined by classification system proposed by Farb [10]. Additionally, typical features of SIH, including dural enhancement and dural collections, were also collected.

EBP techniques

EBP was performed by an experienced neurologist in patients who had failed conservative treatment such as bed rest. The volume of EBP was ascertained by injecting autologous venous blood until the patient experienced severe pain at the puncture site (defined as a VAS score greater than 8). EBPs were performed under sterile conditions with patients placed in a left lateral recumbent position. Following local anesthesia with 2% lidocaine injected into the skin and soft tissue, an epidural needle was advanced into the intended entry site. Aspiration confirmed no blood or CSF return. The correct position was confirmed by the absence of blood or CSF upon aspiration, and it was subsequently verified under C-arm fluoroscopy. Autologous venous blood was drawn from the patient by a trained nurse and slowly injected into the epidural space. After the procedure, the injected blood volume and the patients’ vital signs were documented immediately. Patients after EBP were required to remain in bed rest for at least 6 h and received continuous routine fluid support and symptomatic treatment.

Treatment response

A good response was defined as being able to perform activities for more than 8 h per day with no or only mild headache (VAS 0–3) after EBP, persisting for at least 3 months. On the contrary, a poor response was defined as incomplete symptomatic relief of symptoms, worsening of symptoms after the initial EBP, or the requirement of additional EBP. Patients’ responses were assessed by professional nurses via telephone visits. Additionally, follow-up radiological response, which was graded using a 4-point scale (complete resolution, partial resolution, no change and worsening), was also obtained in the subset of patients to assess the resolution of SIH.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were reported as median and interquartile range [IQR] for non-normal distributions and as mean ± standard deviation (SD) for normal distributions. The assumption of normality was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Missing data were handled by complete-case analysis. Categorical variables were presented as frequencies (percentages, %). Categorical group differences were assessed using the Pearson Chi-squared test or Fisher’s exact test as appropriate, while continuous variables were compared using the independent Student’s t-test for normally distributed data and the Mann-Whitney U test for non-normally distributed data.

To explore the relationship between age and EBP volume, we first utilized a locally weighted scatterplot smoothing (LOWESS) curve to visualize the overall trend. The association was then quantified using Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient (ρ) for the entire cohort and for each age subgroup (≤ 35 years and > 35 years). Multivariable linear regression analysis was subsequently performed to quantify the association between age and EBP volume, adjusting for sex, baseline blood pressure, and injection location.

Several pre-specified sensitivity analyses were performed to enhance the robustness of our study: (1) All comparisons were repeated using the cutoff of 37.5 years, which was the reported median onset age of SIH patients in China [11]. (2) To evaluate potential bias introduced by unsuccessful lumbar puncture, we compared baseline characteristics between patients with valid CSF opening pressure and those who failed to obtain a measurable pressure. (3) To account for loss to follow-up, we performed an intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis on treatment efficacy. Those who lost to follow-up were considered to have a ‘poor response’, and the response rate was recalculated.

All statistical analyses were performed with R 4.5.0 and GraphPad Prism 7.0, considering a two-sided P < 0.05 as statistically significant.

Result

Treatment and outcomes

Among 54 patients who received EBP at our center and had complete follow-up data, 40 of them (74.1%) achieved a good response. Nevertheless, no significant difference was observed between the two groups. Compared with younger patients, older patients exhibited higher EBP volume (26.00 [19.12, 32.75] vs. 19.25 [15.00, 24.00] ml, P = 0.018) (Table 2). Follow-up radiological responses were summarized in Supplementary Table 1. Of the 41 patients with available imaging, complete resolution occurred in 25 (61.0%), partial resolution in 12 (29.3%), no change in 3 (7.3%), and worsening in 1 (2.4%), respectively. Additionally, as detailed in Supplementary Fig. 1, the injected EBP volume varied significantly in different regions (P = 0.033).

Association between EBP volume and age

We then investigated the relationship between the EBP volume and age as a continuous variable. A significant positive correlation was observed between the EBP volume and age (ρ = 0.388, P = 0.004). However, as shown in Fig. 2, the curve showed a non-linear relationship across the entire age spectrum. To statistically validate this observed pattern, Spearman correlation analyses were conducted for the two subgroups, which confirmed an age-dependent dissociation. In patients younger than 35 years, no significant monotonic correlation was observed, but a slight U-shaped trend was noted (ρ = -0.034, P = 0.863). In contrast, age remained strongly correlated with EBP volume within the older group (ρ = 0.458, P = 0.018). Multivariable linear regression analysis, as presented in Table 3, showed that age remained an independent determinant of EBP volume after adjustment for sex, baseline blood pressure and injection location (β = 2.899, 95% confidence interval: 0.475–5.322, P = 0.023).

Sensitivity analysis

In the sensitivity analysis, we first separated our cohort into two groups based on the median onset age of SIH patients in China (37.5 years) [11]. Older group exhibited significantly higher SBP after EBP procedure (113.00 [108.00, 122.00] vs. 105.00 [101.75, 112.25] mmHg, P = 0.013) and EBP volume (28.50 [21.12, 33.38] vs. 18.75 [15.00, 24.00] ml, P = 0.001) (Table S2). We then compared baseline characteristics between patients with valid CSF opening pressure and those who failed to obtain a measurable pressure. Baseline characteristics were comparable between the two groups, other than VAS score (3.00 [2.00, 3.50] vs. 2.00 [0.00, 2.00], P = 0.025) (Table S3), indicating patients with higher VAS scores more prone to unsuccessful measurement. Furthermore, to address potential bias introduced by loss to follow-up, we conducted an ITT analysis. As detailed in Table S4, when all patients lost to follow-up (n = 4) were imputed as having a poor response, the difference in response rates between the younger (≤ 35 years) and older (> 35 years) groups remained non-significant (19/29[65.5%] vs. 21/29 [72.4%], P = 0.777), which was consistent with our main analysis.

Discussion

In this analysis of SIH patients, age-associated differences in clinical manifestations, neuroimaging findings, and treatment outcomes of EBP were identified. Our major findings are as follows: (1) Aside from elevated SBP and DBP after the EBP procedure in older patients, no significant differences were observed in CSF opening pressure or clinical characteristics between age groups. (2) Older patients tended to require a larger volume of blood during EBP. (3) The relationship between age and the EBP volume was not linear. While a significant positive correlation was observed in the overall cohort (ρ = 0.388, P = 0.004), LOWESS curve visualization and subgroup analysis uncovered a distinct, age-dependent pattern: a non-monotonic, slightly U-shaped trend in patients younger than 35 years (ρ = -0.034, P = 0.863) contrasted with a strong linear correlation in those older than 35 years (ρ = 0.458, P = 0.018). Age remained an independent determinant for EBP volume after adjusting for sex, baseline blood pressure, and injection location (β = 2.899, 95% confidence interval: 0.475–5.322, P = 0.023).

EBP exerts its therapeutic effect through two complex mechanisms staggered in time [12]. Initially, injection of autologous blood into the epidural space increases both epidural and CSF pressures, thereby normalizing cerebral blood flow, which is considered to be the dominant contributor to the immediate effect of the EBP [12, 13]. Subsequently, after injection of the autologous blood, blood clot formation is considered to ultimately seal the dural tear and facilitate restoring normal CSF pressure, underlining the long-term therapeutic efficacy in achieving sustained symptom resolution [14–16]. Notably, in our analysis, we found that older SIH patients received significantly larger EBP volume, and this association remained robust after adjustment for several potential confounders. Based on the EBP mechanism, this age-related discrepancy may be attributed to reduced dural elasticity and changes in connective tissue. With advancing age, the body’s connective tissues, including the spinal dura mater, experience a decline in flexibility [17]. Consequently, higher EBP volume was required in the older patients, intending to achieve sufficient pressure and ensure a successful sealing of CSF leaks.

The analysis of the relationship between the EBP volume and age indicated a dose-response relationship. We observed a significant positive correlation between age and EBP volume across the entire cohort. However, the association was driven predominantly by a strong linear correlation in patients older than 35 years, whereas a non-monotonic, slightly U-shaped pattern was observed among the younger patients. This supports the hypothesis of an age-dependent shift in the underlying etiology, which affects treatment needs. In older patients (> 35 years), the progressive, linear increase in EBP volume aligns with age-related degenerative changes. Conversely, the complex, non-linear pattern in younger patients (≤ 35 years) is likely to reflect etiological heterogeneity. These insights suggest that age should be considered in the EBP treatment strategy.

Our study identified that SBP and DBP after the EBP procedure were significantly higher in older patients. However, the change in SBP and DBP showed no difference between the groups, reflecting baseline differences rather than a greater cardiovascular response attributable to a larger volume of EBP injection. Nevertheless, as established by previous studies, cerebral perfusion pressure (CPP), defined as mean arterial pressure (MAP) minus intracranial pressure (ICP), is the net pressure gradient that drives oxygen delivery to cerebral tissue [18]. Thus, it is theoretically plausible that a continuously higher MAP could possibly be an adaptive response to maintain CPP in the face of chronically low ICP, although the CSF opening pressure in our study was within the normal range and comparable across groups. However, it is important to note that lumbar puncture, as a snapshot of CSF pressure, cannot reflect the intracranial pressure in the upright position, nor can it provide any information regarding the CSF dynamics [19]. Additionally, several studies have demonstrated that normal CSF pressure is quite common in SIH patients [20–23]. Therefore, future studies should integrate dynamic assessment of MAP, ICP, and CPP to fully understand the hemodynamic profile in SIH patients.

In this study, our findings offer preliminary insights for clinical practice in SIH management. Age, an easily assessable clinical parameter, may serve as a simple indicator for initially estimating the expected EBP volume range. This means that for older SIH patients, clinicians might consider employing a relatively higher EBP volume to avoid potential treatment failure.

However, we recognize several limitations in this study. First, our study is a single-center retrospective study with a limited sample, which may compromise statistical power. Second, considering that SIH is a relatively rare disease, with the average annual incidence rate for all ages being 3.7 per 100,000 population [24], future studies should expand the sample size through multi-center analysis and adopt a prospective design to develop a better knowledge of the role of age in the clinical practice of SIH.

Conclusion

This study identified several age-related nuances in SIH management. Key findings include the potential need for larger EBP volumes in older patients, a non-linear relationship between age and volume that suggests different underlying etiologies by age group, and associated hemodynamic differences. Thus, incorporating age into clinical assessment may help anticipate treatment needs and tailor management. However, given the single-center, retrospective design and limited sample, further studies are warranted to confirm whether an age-adapted EBP volume strategy improves clinical outcomes.

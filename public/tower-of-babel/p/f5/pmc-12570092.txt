Neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy in HPV-associated vulvar and vaginal squamous cell carcinoma with PD-L1 CPS < 1:Dual pCR evidence
Authors: Enyu Tang, Jia Zeng, Yangchun Sun
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundWhile surgery and radiotherapy remain primary treatments for HPV-associated vulvar/vaginal squamous cell carcinoma (SCC), the significant complications and impaired quality of life underscore the need for alternative approaches. We report two cases achieving pathological complete response (pCR) with neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy.CasesTwo biopsy-confirmed HPV-associated vulvar/vaginal SCC cases with inguinal lymph node metastases (both PD-L1 CPS < 1) received three cycles of neoadjuvant camrelizumab plus nab-paclitaxel/cisplatin. Subsequent imaging demonstrated marked tumor regression, permitting urethra-preserving resection. Histopathological analysis confirmed pCR, with no recurrence at 6-month follow-up.ConclusionNeoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy may be an effective treatment for HPV-associated vulvar/vaginal SCC, potentially independent of PD-L1 CPS status, while improving post-treatment quality of life compared to conventional therapies.

Introduction

Vulvar and vaginal cancers are rare gynecologic malignancies (3 %-6% of female reproductive cancers), predominantly HPV-associated squamous cell carcinomas (SCCs). Early-stage vulvar SCC (VSCC) is primarily managed surgically, whereas advanced cases require multimodal therapy with significant treatment-related morbidity and suboptimal outcomes (Abu-Rustum et al., 2024). Radiotherapy remains the cornerstone for vaginal squamous cell cancer (VaSC), especially in advanced stages, while surgery is reserved for early cases (Vaginal Cancer, 2025). The two-year recurrence rate for vulvovaginal cancer patients with locally advanced disease is approximately 12 %-37 %. However, for those with recurrent or metastatic disease, the five-year survival rate is only about 20 % (Nogueira-Rodrigues et al., 2025). Recent advances in immunotherapy show promise, as demonstrated by a study evaluating neoadjuvant camrelizumab plus chemotherapy for locally advanced cervical cancer (NACI study, NCT04516616), where patients achieved 98 % response rates with manageable adverse effects (Li et al., 2024). Camrelizumab, an immune checkpoint inhibitor, exerts its antitumor effects by blocking the engagement of programmed cell death protein-1 (PD-1) with its ligands, thereby activating T-cell function and enhancing the body's immune response against tumors (Cohen et al., 2020). Sharing pathological features with CSCC, vulvar/vaginal SCCs may show similar immunotherapy responses, supported by preliminary efficacy evidenced in pembrolizumab/nivolumab (Chehade et al., 2025). We present two cases of vulvar/vaginal SCCs successfully treated with neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy, suggesting its potential as a novel therapeutic strategy to improve outcomes while preserving quality of life.

Case 1

A 61-year-old woman presented with a 3-year history of intermittent perineal bleeding. Physical examination revealed a 4 × 3 × 3 cm exophytic mass at the urethral-vaginal junction, extending superiorly to the urethral meatus and 1 cm into the vaginal introitus, with a palpable 3 × 2 × 1 cm right inguinal lymph node. MRI and PET/CT confirmed the presence of a 3.5 × 2.7 × 2.0 cm locally advanced VaSC with bilateral inguinal lymph node metastases, measuring up to 1.4 cm in maximum diameter (FIGO stage IIIC) (Fig. 1A). Tumor testing showed HPV positivity and PD-L1 combined positive score (CPS) < 1. After declining standard chemoradiotherapy, she received 3 cycles of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy (nab-paclitaxel + cisplatin + camrelizumab), achieving remarkable tumor regression to 0.7 × 0.6 cm and nodal reduction to 0.9 cm (Fig. 1B-C). She subsequently underwent radical vulvectomy with bilateral inguinal lymphadenectomy and meatal reconstruction. Final pathology demonstrated vulvar focal residual invasive SCC and complete nodal response, both with prominent treatment effects. Case 2

A 60-year-old woman presented with nonprovoked vaginal bleeding and perineal pain. Gynecological examination revealed nodular tumors involving the upper two-thirds of the posterior vaginal wall, measuring 3.5 × 3 cm. The lower third showed multifocal areas of roughness, most prominent at the 4o'clock position on the lateral vaginal wall. Imaging revealed a 3.4 × 1.6 × 2.5 cm ill-defined mass in the upper posterior vaginal wall invading the fornix, with pelvic/inguinal lymphadenopathy (FIGO stage III) (Fig. 2A). Biopsy confirmed HPV-associated moderately differentiated SCC (CPS < 1). She received 3 cycles of nab-paclitaxel/cisplatin/camrelizumab. Post-treatment MRI showed that the soft tissue mass in the posterior wall of the upper vagina had significantly reduced in size but became indistinct in margin (Fig. 2B). Subsequent radical hysterectomy, vaginectomy and lymphadenectomy showed only residual VAIN1 with marked treatment-related changes and no nodal involvement. Discussion

The management of VSCC and VaSC faces challenges, including treatment-related morbidity and limited evidence for novel therapies due to their rarity. Notably, malignancies with shared histology but different primary sites may exhibit similar treatment responses. Firstly, we report two cases of HPV-associated VSCC/VaSC treated with neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy, a regimen validated in cervical cancer. Both patients achieved pathological complete response. Notably, Case 1 achieved urethral preservation, and both patients avoided adjuvant radiotherapy, significantly improving postoperative quality of life compared to conventional upfront surgical approaches. Furthermore, a pivotal endometrial cancer immunotherapy trial showed pembrolizumab conferred PFS benefit independent of PD-L1 expression, which was not a stratification factor (Eskander et al., 2025). Therefore, unlike prior NACI protocols requiring CPS ≥ 1 with induction chemotherapy, both CPS < 1 patients in this study achieved clinical benefit without induction, consistent with the known immune-hot nature of HPV-driven carcinomas (Jenkins et al., 2025). This clinical observation provides novel evidence for expanding the potential beneficiary population of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy, warranting further validation through large-scale studies. Study limitations include small sample size and short follow-up. Nonetheless, these preliminary data highlight the potential of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy in VSCC/VaSC, possibly extendable to other HPV-related squamous carcinomas. Further investigations should explore optimal immunotherapy combinations and broader applications.

Conclusion

These cases highlight the potential of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy in HPV-associated VSCC/VaSC, achieving pathological complete responses while preserving organ function and avoiding adjuvant radiotherapy. Clinical benefit was observed irrespective of PD-L1 status, suggesting broader applicability for HPV-driven cancers. Though limited by small sample size, these findings support further investigation into chemoimmunotherapy for rare HPV-related squamous carcinomas, balancing efficacy and quality of life. Larger studies are needed to optimize regimens and confirm long-term outcomes.

The Prevalence of Toxoplasma gondii in Saudi Arabia (1994–2023): A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis
Authors: Hassan N. Moafa, Abdullah H. Altemani, Ali Alaklabi, Khalid Y. Ghailan, Alkhansa Alshabi, Majid Ahmed Darraj, Hammad Ali Fadlalmola
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Toxoplasmosis is one of the most common parasites affecting humans in diverse populations caused by T. gondii. This study aims to systematically review and analyze the prevalence of T. gondii infection among various population categories in Saudi Arabia. Our search was done in five databases: PubMed, Scopus, Cochrane Library, Embase, and Web of Science to find the relevant studies from inception to November 2023. The pooled prevalence of toxoplasmosis among the total population living in Saudi Arabia was estimated using a random-effect meta-analysis approach, and Comprehensive Meta-Analysis software was utilized for this analysis. Our study included 30 case–control and retrospective studies published from 1994 to 2023 involving 20,699 patients from different regions in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia. Various cities were included, such as Al-Ahsa, Najran, Riyadh, Arar, Mecca, al Khobar, Mushait, Tabuk, jazan, Hail City, Almadinah Almunawwarah, AL-Ahsa, and Abha; 27 reported outcomes related to IgG seroprevalence, revealing an overall toxoplasmosis prevalence of 27.5% in Saudi Arabia. Fifteen studies that measured IgM seroprevalence found an overall toxoplasmosis prevalence of 2.2%. Specifically for pregnant women, IgG seroprevalence was 28%. Among different age categories, the highest toxoplasmosis prevalence was observed in the group aged 31–45 years, reaching 32.5%, while the lowest prevalence was in the 10–20 years category at 19.3%. Regarding gravidity, the grand multi-gravida group exhibited the highest prevalence at 32.9%, with an upper limit of 47.8%. Furthermore, individuals who consumed freshwater demonstrated a higher incidence than those drinking bottled water, with respective prevalence rates of 33.5% and 29.4%. In conclusion, the prevalence of toxoplasmosis in Saudi Arabia is lower than the global average, with significant variations across different age groups, water sources, and dietary habits. Targeted educational programs and public health interventions are essential to raise awareness and reduce the risk of T. gondii infection. Future research should focus on improving study quality and exploring the broader implications of toxoplasmosis on public health in Saudi Arabia.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s44197-024-00314-5.

Introduction

Toxoplasma gondii is one of the most common parasites affecting humans, causing toxoplasmosis [1, 2]. This infectious disease is widespread globally and can infect humans and other warm-blooded animals [3]. Approximately one-third of the global human population is seropositive for T. gondii [4]. The prevalence of toxoplasmosis tends to be higher in regions with hot, humid climates and lower altitudes, which favor the survival of oocysts. The parasite undergoes different cellular transformations throughout its life cycle, including sporozoites, bradyzoites, and tachyzoites [5]. The parasite's life cycle comprises two main stages: a sexual stage in cats and an asexual stage that manifests in all warm-blooded animals, including humans [6].

Toxoplasmosis can be transmitted through three main pathways: The initial path involves the transmission of the parasite through food, where a person becomes infected by consuming raw or undercooked meat or using water contaminated with T. gondii cysts [7, 8]. The second pathway is the accidental ingestion of oocysts from cat feces or contaminated soil [9]. The third pathway is congenital transmission from an infected mother to her unborn child during pregnancy [10]. This disease is particularly significant due to its association with abortion and congenital disorders in intermediate hosts [11]. Toxoplasma can be transmitted from a pregnant woman to her fetus in approximately 40% of cases when the woman acquires T. gondii infection during pregnancy, leading to abortion or fetal abnormalities such as blindness [12].

The spread of infection has multiple routes: regarding the routes of infection, one of the significant risk factors for toxoplasmosis comprises deficiencies in environmental sanitation, the presence of domestic cats and rodents, consumption of contaminated food, and contact with contaminated soil [13].

The prevalence rates vary widely among individuals from different countries, ranging from 10 to 80%. Also, prevalence rates may fluctuate within a specific country or among various groups in the same region [14]. Although the disease is highly prevalent, those infected with T. gondii with a healthy immune system may suffer from mild symptoms such as muscle aches, headaches, and fever. However, the infection can be more severe in people with compromised immune systems, leading to potentially serious complications [15].

Identifying the prevalence of toxoplasmosis in Saudi Arabia may shed light on the level of awareness and knowledge about the disease among the different categories of the population. The obtained information may be crucial for designing educational programs for specific people and spreading awareness about the disease. The study could identify particular risk factors contributing to the transmission of T. gondii in the Saudi Arabian context, in addition to reducing the transmission of toxoplasmosis among the population. By considering the regional differences in lifestyle, we can find out the reason for the prevalence of toxoplasmosis in a specific region more than in the other areas.

As we need to understand the current epidemiological landscape of toxoplasmosis in Saudi Arabia, we aim to investigate the prevalence of toxoplasmosis in different regions and different daily habits among the population in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia.

Materials and Methods

Study Design

This systematic review and meta-analysis were conducted according to the “Cochrane Handbook”. The study adhered strictly to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines [16, 17].

Literature Search and Eligibility Criteria

We used the following search strategy in PubMed, Scopus, Cochrane Library, Embase, and Web of Science from inception to November 2023: ((“Saudi Arabia” OR Saudi OR KSA OR “Kingdom of Saudi Arabia”) AND (“Toxoplasmosis” OR “Toxoplasma” OR “Toxoplasma gondii” OR “T. gondii” OR Toxoplasmas OR gondius)). We included any paper with the following criteria: (1) Population: Saudi Arabian population, (2) Exposure: T. gondii, (3) Outcome: Prevalence of T. gondii in Saudi Arabia in general and categorized to subgroups according to the occurrence in pregnant women, gender, type of drinking water, type of the food consumed, socioeconomic status, residence, gravidity, and gestational period. (4) Study design: Observational studies. We excluded any studies written in any other language except English. Additionally, we excluded reviews, all types of letters, and case reports.

Study Selection and Data Extraction

Two authors extracted the data from the included studies, with a third author revising any differences or disagreements between them. After removing duplicates, the authors screened the titles and abstracts, ensuring each met the inclusion criteria. The other articles that did not meet the criteria were excluded. After carefully examining the complete texts of the articles, we employed a specific data extraction to collect the essential information from the selected studies. The extracted data encompassed the following key elements: Study ID, study design, city, residency, age, females, diagnostic test, educational status, the prevalence of Toxoplasmosis (IgG), prevalence of Toxoplasmosis (IgM), total participants number, and conclusion.

Risk of Bias Assessment

We determined the quality level of our included studies according to the National Institutes of Health’s (NIH) tool [18], as all the included studies were cohorts and cross-sectional studies, and the NIH tool is designed to assess the quality of observational studies. This tool involves questions about the risk of various biases and confounding factors. The reviewers’ assessments were categorized as “good,” “fair,” or “poor” based on the scores obtained from this evaluation.

Data Synthesis

After collecting the prevalence of T. gondii in Saudi Arabia, we pooled it as a proportion with 95% CI s; the random effect model was applied. We investigated the statistical heterogeneity between studies using the I2 statistics chi-squared test, with p < 0.1 considered heterogeneous and I2 ≥ 50% suggestive of high heterogeneity. We conducted statistical analyses using Comprehensive Meta-Analysis Software (CMA).

Results

Study Selection

A total number of 759 studies was found after searching five different databases. We excluded 83 duplicates. When we did titles and abstracts screening of the 595 studies after removing duplicates, we found that 531 must be excluded as they are unrelated to our objective. Sixty-four studies underwent full-text screening, and 30 were included; 27 [19–45] were considered for analysis, and 3 [46–48] were included as narrative evidence. Moreover, ten studies with 12,961 population measured the outcome with IgM. They found that the prevalence of toxoplasmosis in pregnant women in Saudi Arabia is 2.7%, ranging from 1.6 to 3.7%. The results for IgG and IgM were heterogeneous, as the p-value is less than 0.0001. Prevalence of T. gondii in Saudi Arabia in pregnant women (IgM)

Prevalence of T. gondii in Saudi Arabia Related to Abnormal Pregnancy Outcomes

We included eight studies with 960 populations that measured this outcome with IgG seroprevalence, and the results showed a 28.5% prevalence related to previous abortion as abnormal pregnancy. Two studies with 700 population measured this outcome with IgM and found that the prevalence related to previous abortion as abnormal pregnancy as a cause of T. gondii in women in Saudi Arabia was 4.9%, ranging from 3.1% as the lower limit and 6.6% as the upper limit. The results for IgG and IgM were heterogeneous as the p-value is less than 0.05 in the IgM group and 0.0001 in the IgG group. Supplementary Figs. 2 and 3, respectively.

Prevalence of T. gondii in Saudi Arabia According to Age Categories

Regarding IgG seroprevalence, seven studies with 355 populations ranging from 10 to 20 years showed a prevalence of 19.3%, while 10 studies with 2622 populations ranging from 21 to 30 years showed a prevalence of 23.4%. Finally, the last age category included 12 studies with a 2868 population that ranged from 31 to 45 years, with a prevalence of 32.5%. The results of IgG seroprevalence according to age categories were heterogeneous as the value is less than 0.0001. Supplementary Fig. 4.

For IgM seroprevalence, two studies with a 70 population ranging from 10 to 20 years showed a prevalence of 8.1% with an upper limit of 17.7%, while 5 studies with a 1710 population that ranged in age from 21 to 30 years showed a prevalence of 2.9%. Finally, the last age category included five studies with a 2047 population that ranged from 31 to 45 years with a prevalence of 1.7%. The results of IgM according to age categories were heterogeneous as the value is less than 0.0001. Supplementary Fig. 5.

Prevalence of T. gondii in Saudi Arabia According to Gender

Twenty-six studies with 15,153 population included females with IgG seroprevalence and showed a prevalence of 28.5%. In comparison, two studies with a 167 population included males with IgG seroprevalence and showed a result of 29.5%, and the upper limit of males was 71.1%. The results were heterogeneous, as the p-value was less than 0.0001. Supplementary Fig. 6.

Prevalence of T. gondii in Saudi Arabia Stratified by Gestational Period

Patients in the first trimester were included in nine studies with a 1731 population that had a prevalence of 30.8% of toxoplasmosis based on IgG seroprevalence. Another nine studies with 1301 population mentioned the second trimester, with a prevalence of 32.3%, and 7 studies with 1134 population mentioned the third trimester, with a prevalence of 32.4%. The results were heterogeneous, as the p-value was less than 0.0001. Supplementary Fig. 7.

Regarding IgM seroprevalence, patients in the first trimester were included in four studies with a 515 population that had a prevalence of 5.1% of toxoplasmosis. In contrast, another four studies with a 575 population mentioned the second trimester with a prevalence of 6.2%, and four studies with a 689 population mentioned the third trimester with a prevalence of 6.4%. The results were heterogeneous, as the p-value was less than 0.001. Supplementary Fig. 8.

Prevalence of T. gondii in Saudi Arabia According to Gravidity

This outcome was divided into three subgroups concerning IgG seroprevalence: the first group was primigravida, which included six studies with a 495 population with a prevalence of 23% and an upper limit of 33.7%. The second group was multi-gravida, which included seven studies with a 1284 population with a prevalence of 29.3% with an upper limit of 38.8%, and the third group was grand multi-gravida, which was mentioned in seven studies with a 1087 population with a prevalence of 32.9% with an upper limit of 47.8%. The results were heterogeneous, as the p-value was less than 0.001. Supplementary Fig. 9.

Concerning IgM seroprevalence, the first group was primigravida, which included two studies with an 182 population with a prevalence of 3.5% and an upper limit of 7.5%. The second group with a 491 population was multi-gravida, which included two studies with a prevalence of 2.2% with an upper limit of 3.5%, and the third group was grand multi-gravida, which was mentioned in two studies with a 468 population with a prevalence of 1.1% with an upper limit of 2%. Supplementary Fig. 10.

Prevalence of T. gondii in Saudi Arabia According to Residence

Based on the IgG seroprevalence group, the results were divided into two main categories, and seven studies were included in each category; rural residences with an 814 population have a higher prevalence than urban residences with a 1646 population, with 29.5% and 25.3%, respectively. The results were heterogeneous, as the p-value was less than 0.0001. Supplementary Fig. 11.

Prevalence of T. gondii in Saudi Arabia Stratified by Socioeconomic Status

The outcome was divided into five subgroups concerning IgG seroprevalence: the first group was governmental employees, which included two studies with a 113 population with a prevalence of 44.2% and an upper limit of 53.4%. The second group was illiterate or primary level, which included seven studies with a 663 population with a prevalence of 39% with an upper limit of 48.6%. The third group was nurses and homemakers, mentioned in four studies with a 1205 population with a prevalence of 33.3%. The fourth group was secondary students, who were included in five studies with a 974 population with a prevalence of 29.9%. The last group was university students, who were mentioned in five studies with a 746 population with a prevalence of 18.3%. The results were heterogeneous, as the p-value was less than 0.0001. Supplementary Fig. 12.

Regarding IgM seroprevalence, the first group was the illiterate or primary level, which included one study with 20 populations with a prevalence of 4.7% and an upper limit of 13.9%. The next group was nurses and housewives, mentioned in two studies with a 671 population with a prevalence of 3.8%. The last group was university students included in one study with 117 population with a prevalence of 2.6%. The results were heterogeneous, as the p-value was less than 0.001. Supplementary Fig. 13.

Prevalence of T. gondii in Saudi Arabia According to Type of Drinking Water

Based on IgG seroprevalence, two studies with a 226 population found that the prevalence of toxoplasmosis in people drinking bottled water is 29.4%. In comparison, two studies with a 374 population mentioned that the prevalence of toxoplasmosis in people drinking fresh water is 33.5%. The results were heterogeneous, as the p-value was less than 0.0001. Supplementary Fig. 14.

Prevalence of T. gondii in Saudi Arabia According to Type of Food Consumption

The IgG seroprevalence group mentioned the outcome in eight different studies. It categorized the types of food as undercooked meat, evaluated in eight studies with a 966 population, and unwashed fruit or vegetables, which was mentioned in 2 studies with a 589 population. The prevalence of toxoplasmosis in the group that consumed undercooked meat is 34.9%, with a lower limit of 20.1% and upper limit of 50%, and the prevalence of toxoplasmosis in the group that consumed unwashed fruit or vegetables is 36.5%, with an upper limit of 69.7%. The results were heterogeneous, as the p-value was less than 0.0001. Supplementary Fig. 15.

Region Prevalence of T. gondii in Saudi Arabia

Based on IgG, the eastern region was mentioned in three studies with a 1229 population, the northern region in 7 studies with a 7983 population, the southern region in 8 studies with a 1792 population, and the western region in 8 studies with a 6776 population. Discussion

The prevalence of toxoplasmosis in Saudi Arabia, as indicated by our meta-analysis, is lower than the global average. Specifically, the overall IgG seroprevalence is 27.5%, while the IgM seroprevalence is 2.2%. These findings are consistent with previous studies conducted in the region, which reported similar prevalence rates among women of reproductive age. Our analysis revealed that the highest prevalence of toxoplasmosis was observed in individuals aged 31–45 years, with a seroprevalence of 32.5%. This age group may be more exposed to risk factors associated with T. gondii infection, such as increased contact with soil and consumption of undercooked meat. Conversely, the lowest prevalence was found in the 10–20 age group, at 19.3%. This could be attributed to differences in lifestyle and dietary habits among younger individuals.

Toxoplasmosis is emerging as a significant global health concern, affecting 30–50% of the human population worldwide. From a clinical standpoint, the parasite’s extended presence in most infected individuals’ tissues is commonly considered asymptomatic [49]. Our overall prevalence in Saudi Arabia is almost similar to a previous meta-analysis that was made in Saudi Arabia for women of reproductive age, which found an overall prevalence of 27.8 [50].

Pregnant women in Saudi Arabia exhibited an IgG seroprevalence of 28%, which is comparable to the prevalence reported in other Middle Eastern countries. A study in Syria that included females of childbearing age found that the IgG prevalence was 26.7% [51]. Another study in Malaysia from 2008 to 2018 found that the prevalence in pregnant women was 42.5% [52]. Our study results showed that rural residences have a prevalence of 29.5%, which is relatively low compared to other rural communities, as a previous study in rural areas found that seroprevalence was equal to 79.45% [53]. Another survey of the rural population of Southern Rio Grande do Sul found that the prevalence of IgG toxoplasmosis was 53.2% [54]. The risk of congenital toxoplasmosis remains a significant concern, as T. gondii infection during pregnancy can lead to severe fetal outcomes, including abortion and congenital abnormalities.

Our study also highlighted the impact of water sources on toxoplasmosis prevalence. Individuals consuming freshwater had a higher prevalence (33.5%) than those drinking bottled water (29.4%). This suggests that waterborne transmission of T. gondii oocysts may be a critical factor in the spread of the infection. Additionally, rural residents showed a higher prevalence (29.5%) than urban residents (25.3%), likely due to increased exposure to contaminated soil and closer contact with domestic animals.

The IgM seroprevalence, indicative of acute toxoplasmosis infection, was 2.2% across 15 studies, which is lower than in other Arab countries. For instance, a study in Egypt reported an IgM toxoplasmosis prevalence of 9.78%. A previous meta-analysis of the Eastern Mediterranean region documented the highest prevalence of acute toxoplasmosis infection at 2.5% [55]. The global pooled prevalence of IgM Toxoplasma gondii positivity was 1.9%, as reported by Bigna et al., who included a worldwide population of pregnant women in their study [56]. Our findings for pregnant women showed an IgM prevalence of 2.7%. A meta-analysis by Rostami et al. in 2020, which included 311 studies with 320 datasets from 1,148,677 pregnant women across 91 countries, estimated the global prevalence of latent toxoplasmosis in pregnant women to be 33.8%. In our study, the prevalence of latent toxoplasmosis in pregnant women in Saudi Arabia was 28.7% [57].

Our research is highly relevant to clinical practice, offering valuable perspectives on the prevalence of T. gondii infection in Saudi Arabia. Moreover, it has the potential to illuminate the occurrence of toxoplasmosis in particular provinces when compared to the overall population, in addition to showing the categories of the population at higher risk of infection than others and giving them a chance for preventive measures and actions to prevent the occurrence instead of curing the disease. Understanding the prevalence rates and patterns can guide healthcare professionals in considering toxoplasmosis as a potential cause of symptoms and encourage appropriate diagnostic testing. Identification of the prevalence of toxoplasmosis is crucial for assessing the risk to vulnerable populations, such as pregnant women and individuals with compromised immune systems, providing them with early intervention to reduce morbidity. The study’s findings can be incorporated into continuing medical education programs for healthcare professionals, ensuring they stay updated on the current prevalence rates and clinical implications of toxoplasmosis in the Saudi Arabian context.

The sensitivity and specificity of laboratory tests to diagnose T. gondii infection can significantly influence the reported prevalence rates. Different studies included in our meta-analysis utilized various diagnostic methods, such as enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), polymerase chain reaction (PCR), and indirect fluorescent antibody test (IFAT). Each method has its sensitivity and specificity, which can affect the detection rates of IgG and IgM antibodies. ELISA is commonly used due to its high sensitivity and ease of use, but it may produce false-positive results in some cases. On the other hand, PCR is highly specific and can detect the presence of T. gondii DNA, but it is more expensive and technically demanding. IFAT is also used for its high specificity but requires specialized equipment and expertise. The variability in diagnostic methods across studies can lead to differences in reported prevalence rates. For instance, studies using highly sensitive tests like PCR may report higher prevalence rates than those using less sensitive methods. This variability underscores the need for standardization in diagnostic approaches to ensure comparability of results across different studies.

Our study possesses several strengths, including a more significant number of included studies and populations compared to previous meta-analyses conducted in different regions of Saudi Arabia. The aggregation of data from diverse studies enhances the generalizability of our findings, offering a more representative estimate of prevalence applicable to broader populations. We meticulously designed various subgroups to comprehensively address all aspects related to the prevalence of toxoplasmosis in Saudi Arabia. Additionally, our study addresses a scientific gap, paving the way for future research directions and establishing significant priorities for clinical professionals. However, our study also has its limitations. Many of the included studies were of low quality. Not all outcomes were uniformly covered across all articles, with variations in reporting between studies.

Furthermore, a notable limitation is the absence of information on IgG avidity in most studies, hindering our ability to accurately determine infection status and understand the maturation of the immune response and infection dynamics over time. The designs of the included studies also precluded the availability of sufficient data for conducting a robust risk factor analysis. Lastly, the seroprevalence data, drawn from limited participant samples in cross-sectional studies, may not adequately reflect national seroprevalence rates.

Conclusion

The overall IgG prevalence of toxoplasmosis in Saudi Arabia appears to be lower than the global prevalence. Notably, individuals aged 31–45 exhibited a higher prevalence than other age groups. Our findings suggest that consuming bottled water is associated with a lower incidence of toxoplasmosis than drinking fresh water. Residence in rural areas may contribute to a higher prevalence, possibly due to lifestyle factors and increased contact with soil containing oocysts from infected cats. Moreover, individuals consuming undercooked meat or unwashed fruits and vegetables were more prevalent than the general population. Higher gravidity in females was also associated with an elevated prevalence of toxoplasmosis.

These results emphasize the importance of targeted educational programs nationwide to enhance understanding and awareness of the disease. Public health interventions should promote safe food handling practices, improve water sanitation, and reduce exposure to contaminated soil. Additionally, healthcare providers should be vigilant in screening and educating high-risk groups, such as pregnant women and rural residents, to prevent and manage toxoplasmosis effectively.

Future studies should aim for higher quality and reduced heterogeneity in data. Investigating the broader consequences of toxoplasmosis on the Saudi Arabian population is crucial for a comprehensive understanding of the disease’s impact. Further research should also explore the effectiveness of various intervention strategies in reducing the prevalence and transmission of T. gondii.

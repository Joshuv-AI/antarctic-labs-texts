Clinical outcomes following transcatheter aortic valve replacement in patients with concordant versus discordant aortic stenosis☆
Authors: Prabhjot Hundal, Zaid Abood, Viviana Zlochiver, David Boulware, Mohamed Osman, Renuka Jain, Suhail Allaqaband, Tanvir Bajwa, A Jamil Tajik, M Fuad Jan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractStudy objectiveThis study aimed to evaluate clinical outcomes of patients with symptomatic discordant (aortic stenosis) AS following transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR).DesignThis is a retrospective cohort study.SettingPatients who underwent a TAVR for severe symptomatic native AS with preserved left ventricular ejection fraction ≥50% from 2012 to 2022 at our institution were included.ParticipantsPatients were divided into two cohorts, concordant AS (met all AS criteria) and discordant AS (< 3 criteria).InterventionsTAVR.Main outcomes measuresThe primary endpoint was all cause mortality and secondary outcomes were hospital readmissions for myocardial infarction (MI) and stroke.ResultsThis study evaluated 2021 patients; the median age was 82.6 (77.0–87.2) years, and 53% were female. There was no difference in long-term mortality between discordant or concordant AS (HR: 0.89 [95% CI: 0.77–1.02]; P = 0.09). However, the discordant group had a higher 1-year mortality rate than the concordant group (14.5% vs 10.1%, respectively; P = 0.0054) and comparable mortality rates at 5 years (52.0% vs 50.5%, respectively; P = 0.18). The long-term readmission rate for MI was higher for the discordant group (HR: 0.47 [95% CI: 0.27–0.80]; P = 0.005), and there was no difference in readmission for stroke or transient ischemic attack (HR: 1.33 [95% CI: 0.92–1.94]; P = 0.133).ConclusionsPatients with symptomatic severe AS with discordant echocardiographic findings may have similar survival outcomes than those with concordant AS after undergoing TAVR.Keywords: Concordant aortic stenosis, Discordant aortic stenosis, TAVR

Introduction

Transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) has become an established and widely adopted therapeutic option for the treatment of severe symptomatic aortic stenosis (AS), with randomized clinical trials demonstrating comparable outcomes to surgical aortic valve replacement (SAVR) in appropriately selected patients across varying surgical risk profiles. [1]. The pivotal change to incorporate TAVR stems from an abundance of evidence, including results from the PARTNER 1, 2, and 3 trials that demonstrated the safety and efficacy of TAVR in treating severe AS in varying patient risk groups [2], [3], [4]. The benefits of TAVR continued to be observed as the 5-year mortality rates of TAVR in patients with low, intermediate, or high surgery risk remain comparable to surgical aortic valve replacement [5], [6], [7]. Although the trial designs included severe symptomatic AS patients, they did not investigate the topography and various hemodynamic phenotypes of severe AS and subsequent outcomes concerning TAVR.

The traditional diagnosis of severe AS is met when all three echocardiographic parameters are fulfilled: aortic jet peak velocity ≥ 4.0 m/s, mean transvalvular pressure gradient ≥40 mmHg, and aortic valve area (AVA) ≤ 1.0 cm2 [8]. However, in clinical practice, many patients with severe AS exhibit conflicting echocardiographic parameters when all three diagnostic criteria are not simultaneously met [9]. Such patients are referred to as demonstrating discordant AS compared to those patients where all three criteria are met (concordant severe AS). This complexity and heterogeneity of severe AS is also supported by the AHA/ACC guidelines that categorize severe AS into subgroups based on the hemodynamic profile of the aortic valve [1]. However, there is a lack of contemporary data describing the AS phenotypes and outcomes in discordant AS patients who undergo TAVR.

This study aimed to identify and compare clinical and survival outcomes in patients with discordant versus concordant AS who underwent TAVR.

Methods

Patient selection

From January 2012 through August 2022, patients who underwent a TAVR procedure at our institution were retrospectively identified. The decision for these patients to undergo TAVR instead of SAVR was made through a heart team approach led by our interventional cardiologist, cardiovascular surgeons, and general cardiologists in our cardiovascular valve clinics. The patients with clinically significant severe AS and no prior AV procedure were included in this study. This cohort was screened to meet LVEF ≥50%. The cohort was then divided into two groups; patients meeting all three echocardiographic criteria for severe AS (AVA ≤ 1 cm2, AV peak velocity ≥ 4 m/s, and AV mean gradient ≥40 mmHg) were classified as the concordant AS group. In contrast, those who met at least one, but not all three criteria were labeled as discordant AS.

Outcomes

The primary outcome was all cause mortality through a 10-year study period. The secondary outcomes included hospital readmission for MI (defined by myocardial ischemia observed through a rise in cardiac biomarkers), readmissions for stroke/transient ischemic attack (TIA) (defined by the interruption of blood to the brain diagnosed by a neurologist), and echocardiogram hemodynamics. A comprehensive echocardiogram was obtained for both cohorts at the 1-year and 5-year follow-up. Device success was defined according to the 2011 Valve Academic Research Consortium (VARC-1) criteria. Adverse events were defined according to the standardized definitions from the Society of Thoracic Surgeons/American College of Cardiology Transcatheter Valve Therapy (STS/ACC TVT) registry v2.1. Aortic valve data was extracted from the pre-procedural computed tomography angiography, which was performed in most patients as part of routine TAVR planning. When unavailable, the most recent echocardiogram was used for native aortic valve data collection. This study was approved by the Advocate Health Wake Forest University School of Medicine Institutional Review Board. Due to the retrospective nature of this study, a waiver of consent and HIPAA was obtained.

Statistical analysis

Differences between groups were assessed with either Chi-square tests or Fisher's exact tests for categorical variables, and Wilcoxon Rank Sum tests for numerical variables. Time-to-event analyses were conducted using Kaplan-Meier curves and log-rank tests. Temporal outcome differences were reported by evaluating clinically relevant time points at 1- and 5-year post-TAVR alongside 10-year Kaplan-Meier estimates. All tests were deemed to be statistically significant at the 0.05 level. SAS version 9.4 was used for all analyses. Continuous variables are presented as median and interquartile range, and categorical variables as frequencies and percentages. Missing data were minimal for primary outcomes and key covariates, and no imputation was performed. Analyses were conducted using available data and the proportional hazards assumption was assessed for all Cox regression models and was not violated. Multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression analyses were performed adjusting for age, sex, and Society of thoracic Surgeons (STS) risk score.

Results

Patient characteristics

A total of 2021 patients were evaluated in this study (Supplemental Fig. 1). The median age of the entire cohort was 82.6 years (77.0–87.2), 53% were female, and there were no significant differences in age or gender distribution between the groups. However, the discordant group presented with a higher burden of comorbidities compared to the concordant group, including higher rates of permanent pacemaker (12% vs. 7.8%, P = 0.002), prior percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) or coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) (41.2% vs. 33.9%, P = 0.0007), chronic lung disease (13.2 vs 10%, P = 0.022), atrial fibrillation (36.7% vs. 25.4%, P < 0.001), and severe proximal left anterior descending artery (LAD) stenosis (>70%) (20.2% vs. 14%, P = 0.009) (Table 1).

Pre-procedure evaluation

Table 2 represents the pre-procedural evaluation between the concordant and discordant groups. Bicuspid AV was more frequently observed in the concordant group (4.6% vs. 1.9%, P = 0.007). The Society of Thoracic Surgeons Risk scores were higher in the discordant group than concordant (3.8 vs 3.5, P = 0.004). At baseline, the left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) was lower in the discordant group (62% vs 64%, P < 0.0001). The AV calcium score was significantly lower in the discordant group than in the concordant group (1814 v 2628, P < 0.0001). In the total population, the AV calcium score was reviewed based on the echocardiographic criteria of AS (Fig. 1). The patients with a mean AV gradient ≥40 mmHg had a significantly higher calcium score than those with a lower gradient (2602 vs 1759, P ≤0.001). Similarly, patients with an AV peak velocity ≥ 4 m/s had higher calcium scores than those who did not (2475 vs. 1606, P = 0.001). In contrast, there was no statistically significant difference in calcium scores between groups based on the AVA (P = 0.977).

Discordant echocardiography profiles

The discordant group included patients identified as having clinically severe AS without meeting all three echocardiographic criteria. This group showed significant variations in the AS related echocardiogram parameters.

As demonstrated in Fig. 2, 558 (58%) of the discordant patients met one of the three criteria for severe AS. The AVA was the criteria most frequently met (94.2%). Additionally, the largest subgroup of discordant patients were those that only met the AVA criteria (54.5%) without fulfilling the AV gradient or AV peak velocity. The least met criterion in the discordant cohort was the AV mean gradient (6.8%), irrespective of whether they met the other two criteria. 56 patients (5.8%) had an AVA ≥ 1 cm2. Only two patients (0.2%) in this cohort met the AV gradient without meeting the other two.

Procedure information

The self-expanding valves were the most used valves for the procedure (Medtronic, 86.5%). Device implantation success rate was similar between the two cohorts (Table 2). Only three patients (0.1%) needed to be converted into a surgical aortic valve replacement, which did not significantly differ between the two cohorts.

Pre/post-procedural assessment

The pre-procedural and immediate post-procedure hemodynamic comparison is shown in Fig. 3. Prior to TAVR, the discordant group had lower AV peak velocity (3.9 m/s vs 4.5 m/s, P < 0.0001), lower AV mean gradient (34 mmHg vs 47 mmHg, P < 0.0001), and a higher AVA (0.8 cm2 vs 0.7 cm2, P < 0.0001) than the concordant group. Immediately following TAVR, the discordant group continued to have a lower AV peak velocity (1.9 m/s vs 2.1 m/s, P < 0.0001), lower AV mean gradient (7 mmHg vs 8 mmHg, P < 0.0001), and a higher AVA (2.3 cm2 vs 2.2 cm2, P = 0.009) compared to the concordant group. Both groups saw statistically significant improvement in AVA, AV mean gradient, and AV peak velocity post procedurally (P < 0.0001).

Adverse events

The in-hospital adverse events were recorded and presented in Table 3. The discordant group had lower rates of in-hospital post-procedural perforation (0.1% vs 0.8%, P = 0.040). They also displayed lower rates of major vascular complications (0.1 vs 1.1, P = 0.004). The rates of atrial fibrillation (2.7%), ischemic stroke (2.7%), and pacemaker/ICD implantation (13.2%) were comparable between the concordant and discordant groups.

Outcomes

The median follow up for the cohort was 3.7 years (95% CI: 3.5–4.0). For the primary outcomes, there was no statistically significant difference in survival between the concordant and discordant groups through 10 years (Graphical Abstract). The 1-year mortality rate was higher for the discordant AS group compared with the concordant AS group (14.5% vs 10.1%; P = 0.0042). There was no statistically significant difference in the 5-year mortality rate between the two cohorts (Table 3). The discordant group showed higher hospital admission rates for MI (P = 0.005), as shown in Fig. 4. However, there was no statistically significant difference in stroke or TIA hospitalization (Fig. 5). An adjusted Cox proportional hazards regression analysis on the primary and secondary outcomes was performed and showed results consistent with the unadjusted analysis.

Discussion

This study evaluated clinical outcomes following TAVR in symptomatic severe AS patients with concordant versus discordant echocardiographic profiles in a large, real-world single-center cohort with preserved LVEF. First, patients with discordant AS experienced higher early (1-year) mortality, but showed similar long-term survival compared with those with concordant AS. Second, discordant AS patients had higher long-term readmission rates for myocardial infarction (MI), without difference in cerebrovascular events. Lastly, discordant AS was characterized by substantial heterogeneity in echocardiographic parameters, with the AVA being the most frequently met criterion, whereas the AV mean gradient was least frequently met.

The higher 1-year mortality observed in the discordant AS group is likely multifactorial and reflects the significant differences in baseline patient characteristics rather than valve specific factors. Patients with discordant AS in our cohort had a higher burden of cardiovascular comorbidities, including atrial fibrillation, prior coronary revascularization, conduction abnormalities requiring permanent pacemaker implantation, and higher STS risk scores. These findings may suggest that discordant AS represents a population with more advanced systemic and vascular disease which may predispose them to early adverse outcomes despite successful valve intervention. Importantly, procedural characteristics such as valve type and device success rates were similar between groups and suggest that differences in outcomes are unlikely to be driven by procedural factors. The absence of a long-term survival difference indicates that once patients survive the early post-procedural period, the hemodynamic benefits of TAVR may mitigate these baseline differences, and outcomes may converge over time.

When evaluating the long-term readmission rate for MI in the discordant AS group, it is likely that this is driven by a higher burden of underlying coronary and vascular disease rather than differences in valvular hemodynamics. The discordant AS group had significantly greater prevalence of prior PCI, CABG, and higher rates of proximal LAD disease. Despite this, rates of stroke and TIA were similar between groups, indicating that cerebrovascular risk may be more dependent on other clinical factors.

These findings should be interpreted in the context of existing literature evaluating discordant AS. The safety and utility of TAVR have not been well described in discordant AS [10], [11], [12], [13]. Much of existing literature has focused on specific hemodynamic subtypes, such as paradoxical low-flow, low-gradient AS or has included heterogenous populations with reduced ejection fraction, which limits direct comparisons. In a meta-analysis by Ullah et al., outcomes across various AS subtypes were evaluated, though the populations included patients with mixed flow states and LVEF [14]. In contrast, the present study specifically examines patients with preserved ejection fraction and defines discordance based on standard echocardiographic criteria aligned with current ACC/AHA guidelines. [1]. Importantly, our approach reflects real-world classification or practice, where symptomatic AS patients may be grouped into the discordant category without strict hemodynamic subclassification. Our observations extend prior work by demonstrating that, despite higher early mortality, long-term survival following TAVR is comparable between discordant and concordant AS in a large, real-world cohort. These results highlight both the variability of discordant AS and showcase the effectiveness of TAVR across varying hemodynamic presentations.

Within the discordant cohort, the AVA was the most frequently met criterion for severe AS, whereas transvalvular gradients and peak velocities were less commonly elevated. This pattern reflects the fundamental differences in how these parameters are derived and their dependence on flow conditions. Both peak velocity and mean gradient are directly influenced by transvalvular flow, and in patients with reduced flow, despite preserved ejection fraction, these measurements may underestimate the severity of the AS. Derived from the simplified Bernoulli's equation, the AV mean gradient is calculated by using the AV peak velocity, and various flow states may lead to inaccurate readings [15]. In contrast, the AVA, derived from the continuity equation utilizing the left ventricular outflow tract (LVOT) diameter, LVOT velocity time integral (VTI), and the AV peak velocity, provides a more flow independent assessment, although it remains subject to measurement variability through operator experience [15], [16]. Some examples of low-flow states in preserved LVEF patients are seen in advanced diastolic dysfunction, severe LV wall thickening (LV hypertrophy), impaired LV filling, and reduced longitudinal systolic function.

These factors likely explain the observed heterogeneity within the discordant AS cohort, where patients frequently meet AVA criteria but fail to meet gradient or velocity thresholds. This is consistent with prior reports demonstrating that discordant echocardiographic profiles are common in clinical practice. Pibarot et al. has reported that up to 40% of patients with AS exhibit discordant findings, which can create diagnostic and therapeutic challenges associated with this population [17]. This complex interplay between flow dynamics, measurement variability, and disease physiology highlights the importance of integrating multiple echocardiographic parameters when evaluating AS severity.

We observed that patients with concordant AS had higher AV calcium scores, which correlated with higher AV mean gradients and peak velocities. However, long-term clinical outcomes were similar between groups, suggesting that calcium burden alone may not fully account for differences in outcomes following TAVR, despite the AV calcium being a strong independent predictor of adverse events in AS [2], [18]. Interestingly, concordant AS patients demonstrated higher rates of procedural vascular complications, whereas discordant AS patients had a greater burden of baseline vascular disease. The discrepancy may reflect differences in procedural factors, vascular anatomy, or selection bias inherent to a retrospective study cohort.

At follow-up, both concordant and discordant AS groups showed sustained benefit in their echocardiographic hemodynamics following TAVR. While modest differences in AV mean gradient were observed at 5 years, these were small and of uncertain clinical significance as the long-term mortality rates remained comparable. These observations should be interpreted cautiously given the limited number of patients with available echocardiographic follow-up data at that time point. Overall, these findings suggest that the hemodynamic benefits of TAVR are preserved over time across both concordant and discordant AS phenotypes, even with significant baseline differences in echocardiographic profiles.

There are several limitations to this study. First, this is a single-center, retrospective analysis and is therefore subject to inherent selection bias and unmeasured confounding. Second, the study population consisted predominantly of elderly patients with low to intermediate surgical risk, which may limit generalizability to broader populations. Third, discordant AS was defined as an umbrella category of patients with symptomatic severe AS with preserved ejection fraction that did not meet all three echocardiographic criteria. This group was not further stratified by flow status utilizing the stroke volume index (paradoxical low-flow, low-gradient AS versus normal-flow, low-gradient AS). Fourth, echocardiographic measurements are operator-dependent and subject to variability, particularly in parameters such as LVOT diameter that influence AVA calculation. Finally, follow-up echocardiographic data were not available for all patients and were obtained as part of routine clinical care. These limitations highlight the need for prospective studies with standardized imaging protocols and consistent hemodynamic subclassification.

Conclusions

In this large, real-world cohort, patients with discordant severe symptomatic AS and preserved ejection fraction demonstrated similar long-term survival following TAVR compared with those with concordant AS, despite higher early mortality and increased rates of MI. In the discordant AS cohort, the AVA was the most readily met criterion among patients, with many failing to meet the AV mean gradient threshold. These findings highlight the broad clinical heterogeneity of discordant AS and suggest that TAVR provides effective and durable outcomes across varying echocardiographic profiles.

OCT-based morphological prognostic factors for structural improvement after anti-VEGF therapy in wet age-related macular degeneration
Authors: Tengbo Rao, Jiarui Yang, Huijin Chen, Xuemin Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTo investigate the relationship between baseline optical coherence tomography (OCT) features of wet age-related macular degeneration (wAMD) and prognosis, and evaluate anti-VEGF treatment effects under varying baseline OCT conditions.MethodsThis retrospective study included patients diagnosed with wAMD who underwent monthly anti-VEGF therapy for 1–3 months. Baseline, one-month, and three-month OCTs recorded morphological features. Treatment effectiveness was defined as central retinal thickness (CRT) reduction ≥ 25%, or absence of subretinal fluid (SRF) and intraretinal fluids (IRF) post-treatment.ResultsData from 251 eyes were analyzed. After one injection, effective responders had smaller baseline lesion sizes (11.01 ± 9.93 vs. 12.53 ± 8.80, P = 0.046), lower PED height (124.10 ± 232.55 vs. 216.06 ± 248.66, P < 0.001), and larger baseline IRF (86.52 ± 154.74 vs. 31.26 ± 81.14, P = 0.002) compared to non-responders. After three injections, effective responders continued to show larger baseline IRF (65.94 ± 135.20 vs. 20.28 ± 61.93, P = 0.015). Multivariate analysis indicated greater CRT reductions at one and three months were associated with larger baseline CRT (P < 0.001), larger baseline IRF (P < 0.001 and P = 0.012), and absence of retinal hemorrhage (P = 0.003).ConclusionsBaseline OCT features significantly influence treatment responsiveness in wAMD. Tailoring anti-VEGF regimens based on these features may improve outcomes.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12886-026-04690-3.Keywords: OCT, Prognostic factors, Anti-VEGF, Wet age-related macular degeneration

Introduction

Age-related macular degeneration (AMD) is a major cause of irreversible vision loss in individuals over the age of 50 [1]. AMD can be categorized into wet age-related macular degeneration (wAMD) and dry age-related macular degeneration, depending on the presence or absence of neovascularization. Over the last decade, intravitreal anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) medications have significantly improved outcomes in eyes with wAMD [2, 3]. However, predicting individual short-term structural and functional responses to anti-VEGF injections based on current clinical guidelines remains challenging due to wide variations in treatment response among patients [4]. Inappropriate treatment can have serious consequences for patients, and thus, choosing an appropriate examination and treatment strategy is critical for cost-effective patient care [5].

Optical coherence tomography (OCT) is an important diagnostic tool for detecting wAMD progression [6]. OCT images reveal the anatomical structure of the fundus and allow for the observation of structural changes in the retina. OCT plays a vital role in interpreting the prognosis of wAMD and evaluating treatment effects on patients. Best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) is currently used as an indicator for functional improvement in patients with wAMD, while certain baseline OCT changes, such as central retinal thickness (CRT), retinal exudation extent, and the integrity of retinal layering, are recognized as indicators for morphological improvements. These factors are frequently used to evaluate treatment responsiveness [7].

However, there is a lack of studies on how baseline status influences morphological improvement [8]. Patients with wAMD often present with multiple types of morphological changes on OCT before initiating treatment. Previous studies have suggested that the main factors affecting the prognosis of wAMD patients include BCVA [9], age [10, 11], and the size of retinal lesion [12]. Furthermore, macular structural improvement in wAMD patients may be related to a variety of baseline morphological indicators, which can be divided into the following categories: vitreous limiting membrane-related changes, retinal thickness, retinal fluids or edema, and external retinal structure [13]. However, such studies have not reached a consensus, and clinical evidence is needed to guide the clinical treatment plan for patients.

In this study, we aimed to find baseline morphologic features that impact the effectiveness of anti-VEGF injections. The findings of this study provide a clinical data foundation for anti-VEGF injections in AMD patients, offering structural insights into the drug responsiveness of different types of AMD. Additionally, it lays the groundwork for the development of new therapeutic agents in the future. Consequently, this will result in improved prognoses for patients with wAMD and avert rapid loss of vision.

Methods

Participants

This study passed the ethical review of the Peking University Third Hospital Medical Science Research Ethics Committee, and was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Helsinki Declaration. This study included patients diagnosed with wAMD between January 2020 and June 2022. All patients received intravitreal injections of anti-VEGF agents for wAMD treatment at Peking University Third Hospital.

Inclusion criteria were as follow: (1) Patients who were first diagnosed with “wet age-related macular degeneration” at Peking University Third Hospital from January 2020 to June 2022, and had not received any previous treatment for wAMD prior to this; (2) All wAMD patients were treated with anti-VEGF intravitreal injection; (3) The anti-VEGF applied by the patient was same during the treatment, including Conbercept, Aflibercept, and Ranibizumab.

Exclusion criteria were as follow: (1) Diagnosed with polypoidal choroidal vasculopathy (PCV) or type 3 macular neovascularization (MNV); (2) Patients with previous intravitreal injection of anti-VEGF agents Agent history; (3) History of other previous eye diseases, including glaucoma, pathological myopia, epiretinal membrane, etc. The diagnosis of wAMD, PCV, and type 3 MNV in all patients depended on OCT and fundus fluorescein angiography (FFA) [14].

Data acquisition

In this study, AMD patients receiving anti-VEGF injections underwent Heidelberg Spectralis SD-OCT (Heidelberg Engineering, Heidelberg, Germany) examinations before the initiation of treatment, one month after the first injection, and one month after completing three injections. The OCT scans selected were foveal-centered and we retrieved the following features from OCT images: lesion size, CRT (defined as the distance between the internal limiting membrane and the putative retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) at the fovea), pigment epithelial detachment (PED), degree of PED, the discontinuities of the ellipsoid zone (EZ) and the external limiting membrane (ELM), intraretinal fluid (IRF) and the size, subretinal fluid (SRF), retinal hemorrhage (RH) and the presence of posterior vitreous detachment (PVD). The measurements of IRF and PED were based on the largest lesion’s length and height on the OCT images (Fig. 1). Retinal hemorrhage needs to be confirmed by fundus photography and FFA examination. Definition of response to anti-VEGF therapies

The criteria for assessing morphological improvement was derived from previous studies [15]. The primary outcomes include the change of CRT (△CRT = CRT (baseline) - CRT (after treatment)) and the rate of CRT changes (△CRT%=△CRT/ CRT (baseline)). The therapeutic efficacy was graded as effective or invalid. Effective was defined as △CRT% ≥ 25%, or with no SRF and IRF after treatment, while invalid was defined as △CRT%<25% and with the existence of SRF or IRF after treatment.

Statistical analyses

Statistical analyses were conducted using the SPSS software (version 26.0; IBM). Categorical data were presented as frequency counts, and the values of all continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation. Various statistical methods were applied to compare differences in patient characteristics. Pearson’s Chi-squared test was utilized to compare discontinuous variables. The Shapiro-Wilk test was performed to assess the normality of continuous data. Student’s t-test was used to compare data that conformed to normality, whereas the Mann–Whitney U test was used to compare data that did not conform to normality. Associations between the response to anti-VEGF agents and possible features were evaluated using multiple linear regression and logistic regression analysis. P value < 0.05 was regarded as statistically significant.

Multiple linear regression of factors affecting the change of CRT value

Multiple linear regression models were used to include factors that were significantly associated with △CRT after anti-VEGF therapies. Table 2 presents the results of the multiple linear regressions of △CRT and △CRT% after one or three rounds of anti-VEGF injections. Patients who had higher baseline CRT (P < 0.001, β = 0.395 and P = 0.003, β = 0.031) and larger IRF size (P < 0.001, β = 0.342 and P = 0.008, β = 0.041) were more likely to have higher △CRT and △CRT% after one round of injection. The existence of discontinuity of the ellipsoid zone (Table 2, P = 0.020, β = 14.877) was more likely to have higher △CRT% after one round of injection. However, patients who had retinal hemorrhage were more likely to have less △CRT and △CRT% (Table 2, P = 0.002, β=-81.028 and P = 0.001, β=-15.705).

Discussion

This study primarily analyzed the OCT structural characteristics of AMD patients before treatment and the morphological changes in the retina after anti-VEGF injections to explore differences in treatment responsiveness among patients with varying OCT features. We found that changes in CRT one month and three months after anti-VEGF injections were primarily associated with baseline CRT levels, the size of IRF, and the presence of retinal hemorrhage before treatment. Regarding treatment efficacy, patients with smaller baseline PED, larger IRF, and the absence of retinal hemorrhage demonstrated better treatment responsiveness.

Anti-VEGF drugs achieve therapeutic effects for AMD by effectively binding to VEGF-A, VEGF-B or placental growth factor thereby inhibiting VEGF activation [16]. However, there are currently differences in the responsiveness to anti-VEGF treatment among AMD patients. From our regression results, the baseline factors associated with better treatment responses can be categorized into several aspects, including indicators reflecting the severity of AMD exudation, the severity of the lesion, and retinal functional status.

IRF, SRF are pathological features where fluid characteristics can be observed, potentially reflecting the extent of exudation in AMD. In our study, the presence of IRF as well as the presence of SRF lead to greater △CRT. These indicators reflect the activity of AMD and have also been found in previous studies to potentially play a role in preventing the progression of AMD to geographic atrophy [17]. Our study also found that patients with a larger baseline PED exhibited less morphological improvement after treatment, which is consistent with previous research findings [18]. This suggests that intraretinal fluid is more readily absorbed after treatment, while sub-RPE exudates are often more difficult to resolve in the short term.

Hemorrhage is an indicator of the severity of AMD and is also the primary factor we identified for poor responsiveness to anti-VEGF treatment. Iron ions in subretinal hemorrhage may have toxic effects on the RPE and photoreceptors, thereby impacting the morphological and functional prognosis of AMD patients [19]. Anti-VEGF drugs can, to some extent, exert their therapeutic effect by stabilizing the tight junctions of RPE and improving the structure of neovascularization [20]. However, some studies indicate that hemorrhage can still occur even during anti-VEGF treatment. This suggests that other mechanisms may be involved in the bleeding process [21]. Therefore, for AMD patients with baseline hemorrhage, anti-VEGF treatment alone may not be sufficient to alleviate their symptoms.

The integrity of the EZ and ELM may reflect the functional status of the retina. Our study found that the discontinuity EZ or ELM at baseline will gain greater △CRT after treatments. In past studies, however, it was found that absence or interruption of EZ and ELM tended to be associated with worse visual prognosis. A study showed that disruption in the outer retinal layers at baseline, particularly ELM, appears to predict poor photoreceptor restoration and worse final BCVA [22]. Another study similarly showed that initial disruption of EZ and ELM can be useful predictors of worse visual outcomes after anti-VEGF treatment in wAMD patients [23]. Morphological improvement may not be fully consistent with functional improvement. Disruption of the EZ and ELM often means that photoreceptors are destroyed and difficult to recover, so even if retinal exudation is relieved, it is difficult to restore its function [24]. This may indicate that the current anti-VEGF treatments can better relieve retinal exudation, but have limited effect on retinal function recovery.

Our study may reveal that the effectiveness of anti-VEGF on morphological improvement can be evaluated much earlier. Past studies recommended assessing the efficacy of treatment 3 months after initiation of anti-VEGF therapy [25, 26]. However, in these studies, no observations were made at time points earlier than 3 months after the initiation of treatments. In our study, we found that significant improvement in the retinal structure could be observed after just one anti-VEGF injection. Additionally, the factors influencing treatment outcomes after three months were found to be almost identical to those at one month. Therefore, we suggest that morphological improvement after 1-month treatment is also a reliable predictor of long-term patient responsiveness to anti-VEGF.

Our study has some limitations waiting to be solved in further studies. First, since previous studies have suggested that type III MNV and PCV may involve different pathogenic mechanisms compared with wAMD, this study only included patients with type I and type II MNV [27, 28]. The OCT findings in these two subtypes may be influenced by distinct prognostic factors. For instance, prior research has indicated that the prognosis of type III MNV may be associated with subretinal hyperreflective material [29]. This study focused solely on prognostic analyses based on manually measured OCT features. Although manual measurements are generally reliable, several AI-enhanced automatic segmentation algorithms are now available that can quantify three-dimensional retinal fluid parameters—such as PED volume—which may offer more precise measurements in future research [30]. In addition, several newer imaging modalities may enable deeper assessment of retinal biomarkers. For example, OCTA has demonstrated superior sensitivity compared with OCT for detecting early neovascularization, suggesting that OCTA-derived parameters could be incorporated into future analytical frameworks [31, 32].

Conclusion

In summary, we found that patients with smaller PED, larger IRF size, and no retinal hemorrhage had better morphological prognosis. For patients with retinal hemorrhage, anti-VEGF treatment alone may be insufficient. The factors influencing prognosis at one month post-treatment were largely consistent with those at three months, suggesting that AMD patients’ prognosis could be assessed earlier in the treatment process for more timely intervention.

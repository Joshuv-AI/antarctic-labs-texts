Enhancing IoT-based healthcare security with grey filter bayesian CNN and optimization algorithms
Authors: A Kalaivani, P Kavitha, G Dhivya, D Aruna, T Thulasi, Xu Yong, Choon Kit Chan, Pushpa Rani
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe integration of Internet of Things (IoT) devices in healthcare systems has significantly enhanced patient monitoring and intervention capabilities, offering real-time data for improved decision-making. However, the widespread deployment of IoT devices has introduced critical security vulnerabilities, making healthcare systems vulnerable to cyberattacks that compromise patient safety and data privacy. This paper aims to address the security challenges in IoT-enabled healthcare by developing an advanced anomaly detection system that integrates the Grey Filter Bayesian Convolutional Neural Network (GFB-CNN), the Crow Search Algorithm (CSA), and the Binary Grey Wolf Optimizer (BGWO). The GFB-CNN is employed to filter out noise and uncertainties in the data, thereby improving the accuracy of anomaly detection. Meanwhile, CSA and BGWO optimize feature selection and tackle class imbalance, thereby enhancing computational efficiency. An experimental evaluation of the CIC IoT dataset demonstrates that the proposed method significantly outperforms existing security solutions, providing a robust and real-time anomaly detection framework. The results confirm that integrating GFB-CNN, CSA, and BGWO offers a promising approach for enhancing security in IoT-based healthcare systems, thereby ensuring better protection against cyber threats. This study contributes to the advancement of secure IoT healthcare solutions, paving the way for more reliable and scalable health system access in future healthcare applications.Keywords: Internet of things, Grey filter bayesian convolutional neural network, Anomaly detection, Wireless sensor network, Crow search algorithm, And binary grey wolf optimizerSubject terms: Engineering, Health care, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Real-time data, networking, and automation from the IoT are changing various industries1. The IoT enhances healthcare by enabling remote diagnosis, continuous monitoring, and prompt intervention2. Wearable sensors, smart medical gadgets, and networked healthcare systems collect vast amounts of patient data. These technologies provide personalized treatment strategies and deliver patient health insights3. Real-time data interchange enhances patient outcomes, improves healthcare operations, reduces costs, and increases efficiency4.

However, the growth of healthcare IoT increases security threats. Attackable connected devices are widely utilized in low-memory, low-processing contexts5. Data breaches, unauthorized access, and other cyber risks may endanger healthcare data on these devices6. Because health data is sensitive and healthcare operations are vital, security breaches can have a major impact. Medical misdiagnosis, treatment delays, and even death can result from these breaches. One of the significant drawbacks of IoT-based healthcare systems is their susceptibility to security threats. Traditional security solutions often fail to address IoT concerns, which can lead to this vulnerability7,8. Traditional security methods struggle to cope with the dynamic and diverse IoT networks. Because various network’ devices have different capabilities, data formats, and communication protocols9. Data from the IoT is often loud, imprecise, and uneven, making it harder to discover security vulnerabilities10.

In healthcare, real-time monitoring, early diagnosis, and individualized therapy have changed patient care due to the fast integration of IoT devices. However, increased device adoption has created security weaknesses and new attack surfaces in healthcare networks11. Cyberattacks, including data breaches, unauthorized access, and intentional interference, may compromise patient data, disrupt medical services, and potentially put patient lives at risk in IoT-based healthcare systems. IoT networks are routinely deployed in varied locations with different security standards, making it challenging to maintain consistent protection. Security mechanisms now rely on perimeter defense and basic anomaly detection, which fail to handle the real-time IoT threats’ complexity and evolution. IoT devices create massive volumes of data, which delays threat detection and increases attack exposure. Many current solutions fail to process this data. These difficulties need real-time, scalable, and resilient IoT-based healthcare security solutions. IoT-enabled healthcare systems face unique security challenges; therefore, this research proposes a novel anomaly detection framework that integrates advanced methods, including the GFB-CNN, CSA, and GWO.

This paper proposes a novel approach to enhancing IoT-based healthcare security by integrating GFB-CNN with advanced optimization algorithms, such as BGWO and CSA, to address these challenges. The GFB-CNN framework is designed to improve anomaly detection by filtering out noise and managing uncertainty in IoT data, thereby increasing the accuracy and reliability of security systems. The combination of CSA and BGWO yields optimized feature selections while also addressing class imbalance issues prevalent in IoT datasets. These optimization techniques ensure that the model operates efficiently, even in resource-constrained environments, delivering robust and instantaneous security solutions that safeguard patient information and ensure the accuracy of medical care.

Despite the significant progress made in IoT security and anomaly detection, several key challenges remain unaddressed in existing literature. Many approaches rely on traditional ML models that are susceptible to noise and uncertainty, which diminishes their effectiveness in real-world applications. Additionally, many studies fail to adequately address the problem of class imbalance, which leads to suboptimal detection performance, particularly in IoT networks with skewed distributions of attack-to-normal traffic. Furthermore, optimization techniques used for feature selection in IoT anomaly detection are often limited by their computational inefficiency, restricting their applicability in real-time security applications34. While some methods have integrated optimization algorithms, they do not fully leverage advanced DL architectures or combine them with effective noise reduction techniques to achieve better accuracy and robustness. The proposed method addresses these gaps by integrating the GFB-CNN, which handles data noise and uncertainty, enhancing detection accuracy.

Proposed methodology

This suggestion encompasses two domains: cyber spaces linked to anomaly detection and physical medical facilities for patients35. The suggested cyber-physical system is depicted in Fig. 1, which illustrates how cyberspaces utilize physical inputs to measure performance using technology.

The data generated by sensors provides summaries of a patient’s health, including blood pressure, sugar levels, heart rate, and other vital details. Additionally, each patient has a unique ID for future communications, ensuring send-less network capabilities36. Three categories of users occupy physical space: patients, physicians, and healthcare facilities. We must run three technology modules in cyberspace. Medical data collection and analysis are covered in Module 1. To obtain the final findings, Module 2 assists in processing the medical data within specific limits. The primary innovation is the optimization of BGF-CNN variables20 through the use of the CSA and BGWO approaches37, which aids in classifying medical information and subsequently enhances its security through improved IoT techniques.

It is divided into two areas: cyberspace and physical space. BGF-CNN is used to classify medical data in physical space, and the CSA and BGWO techniques are then used to optimize the results. The proposed GFB-CNN incorporates three innovations: (1) Grey Filtering embedded within CNN layers to denoise encrypted IoT healthcare traffic, ensuring cleaner feature representations compared to conventional pre-processing; (2) Bayesian priors within the convolutional structure to enhance robustness against uncertain and adversarial network conditions, a feature absent in current CNN-based IoT security frameworks; and (3) a dual metaheuristic optimization scheme where the CSA tunes key hyperparameters (learning rate, convolutional kernel size, dropout rate) while the BGWO performs adaptive feature selection, reducing computational load and improving detection accuracy.

The Grey Filter is utilized to address the challenge of noisy and uncertain data, a common issue in IoT networks. By applying this filter, the model effectively smooths out irrelevant noise and sharpens the focus on meaningful patterns in the data, enhancing the accuracy of anomaly detection. The CSO is employed for feature selection, optimizing the input space by selecting the most relevant features for detecting anomalies. This reduces dimensionality, speeds up computation, and guarantees that the model concentrates only on essential data, leading to improved classification performance, especially in imbalanced datasets. The GWO is applied for parameter optimization, fine-tuning the model’s hyperparameters, such as learning rate and decision boundaries.

BGWO algorithm

Traditional GWO is a metaheuristic method with a bio-inspired design. Grey wolves’ hunting habits and leadership style are imitated by GWO38. Grey wolves belong to the Canidae family, which has a rigid social hierarchy. Their swarm intelligence-based hunting approach has earned them widespread recognition. As pack hunters or social predators, grey wolves hunt prey (targets) in packs of 5–12 wolves. Beta (β), Alpha (α), omega (ω), and delta (δ) are leaders within packs, and cluster leaders are alpha (dominant) wolves. All choices regarding hunting, sleeping spots, waking hours, punishment, and other matters are made by the Alpha, which the entire group then follows. The next level of wolves is the beta pack, which supports the alpha’s directives across the group and serves as an advisor to the alpha. This suggests that β assists α in making decisions and performing other activities while controlling other wolves at a lower level39. As the group’s subleaders, beta wolves are most likely to become wolves if one of the α wolves dies or becomes extremely ancient. The hierarchy that follows is . They must bow to both and ; together, they give instructions to the following lower-level wolves (also known as ω wolves). This level includes elders, guardians, scouts, hunters, and sentinels. When hunting and supplying food for the group, hunters help α and β. Sentinels ensure the group’s safety and security. Scouts check the boundaries of search spaces and notify the group if any hazards are encountered40. Caregivers take care of weak wolves. Lastly, elder wolves that were once α or β are now experts.

Omega wolves are those that are found at the lowest rung of the dominance system. They take the blame. They are always behind more experienced wolves. Food is allowed for the omega wolves to eat last. Omega wolves may seem insignificant, but when they are eliminated or dropped from groups, clusters face internal conflicts. The reason for this is that ω expelled all the wolves’ anger and aggression. This maintains the hierarchy and satisfies the entire group. In certain situations, ω is also the group’s babysitter. The exploration and exploitation stages are critical to improving performance in any population-based strategy. The primary issue with BGWO is that, during the process, all seeking agent sites are rationalized based on α, β, and δ values, as agents cannot individually explore search spaces. This updating process typically results in early convergence. Additionally, the optimization procedure results in relaxed convergence, as it provides limited local search capabilities in the latter stages43. It balances local and global searches by combining BGWO with CSA, which uses Fx, i for regulating positional updates and allows agents to modify step movements in the direction of new agents. These factors are important for attaining global optimal values, and higher values of Fx, i result in global searches, whereas lower values lead to local searches. During initial testing, the model achieved a convergence rate of approximately 85% within the first 100 iterations when applied to the CICIoT2023 dataset, indicating its ability to quickly adapt to typical IoT traffic. However, in real-time scenarios, several challenges such as high data velocity, noisy inputs, and evolving attack patterns can affect this convergence rate. For instance, when exposed to real-time data streams, the convergence time may increase by up to 25% due to fluctuating data quality and more complex, multi-vector attacks.

Result and discussion

CICIoT2023 dataset

The CICIoT2023 dataset is utilized in the current study, leveraging the publicly accessible IoT attack dataset, CICIoT2023. The dataset enables implementable security analytics for real-time IoT operations. The authors utilized a topology of 105 IoT devices to conduct 33 distinct attacks. The seven categories into which these assaults are divided are DoS, DDoS, Recon, Brute Force, Web-based, Spoofing, and Mirai. The collection comprises 169 files, available in both PCAP and CSV file formats. The CSV files are PCAP-processed files that produce 46 characteristics, representing the various types of assault. There is an uneven distribution of recorded samples across categories, with Web-Based and Brute-Force having significantly lower representation, a typical indicator of an imbalanced dataset. For the evaluation of the ML models to be credible, the dataset is pre-processed and balanced. To enhance the training durations and prediction performance of ML models for both binary and multi-class dataset formats, more data characteristics are eliminated. This research conducted the Wilcoxon signed-rank test and the Friedman test across all comparative models and datasets to evaluate whether the observed gains in accuracy, false-positive reduction, and energy efficiency are statistically significant. The Wilcoxon test results yielded p-values < 0.05 for all pairwise comparisons between GFB-CNN and the baselines, indicating significant performance differences. The Friedman test further confirmed that GFB-CNN ranks first among all evaluated models with a critical difference (CD) value exceeding the Bonferroni-Dunn threshold.

The GFB-CNN was trained with a learning rate of 0.001, a batch size of 32, and 50 epochs in total. The convolutional filters were set to 64 with a filter size of 3 × 3, using the ReLU activation function. For the CSA, a population size of 50 was used, with a maximum iteration count of 100, and a step size of 0.2. The exploration rate was set to 0.5, with a decay factor of 0.95. The BGWO was configured with a population size of 30, a maximum generation limit of 50, and a mutation probability of 0.1. The cognitive, social, and exploration parameters (C1, C2, C3) for the BGWO were set to 2.0, 2.0, and 0.5, respectively, to guide the optimization process effectively.

The proposed GFB-CNN performance is analyzed based on the metric classification accuracies, intersection over union (IoU), specificities, and sensitivities, and comparisons with the latest state-of-the-art methods from reputable sources, such as WbAES12, AMI Lab Architecture13, SVM with RSA14, PSL-RH with ECC15, ACO-DDTHA16, Blockchain-based IoT security20, LSSOA21, and ML-based cyberattack detection frameworks22. Class imbalance is a well-known challenge in anomaly detection tasks, particularly when dealing with datasets like the CICIoT2023, where normal traffic significantly outnumbers attack instances. This imbalance can lead to biased models that tend to predict the majority class (normal traffic) while underperforming in detecting rare but critical anomalies (attacks). In this research, we address this issue through a combination of techniques. First, the CSA is employed for feature selection to reduce the impact of irrelevant features that could contribute to bias in the model. Second, the GFB-CNN incorporates noise reduction mechanisms, improving the model’s ability to generalize in the presence of class imbalance. Third, the BGWO optimizes the model’s hyperparameters, enhancing its sensitivity to minority classes.

Data imbalance is a common challenge in many real-world ML applications, particularly in the field of anomaly detection for IoT-based systems. In these systems, normal network traffic often far exceeds the volume of attack or anomaly data, creating an imbalanced dataset. This imbalance can significantly impair the performance of traditional ML models, as they tend to be biased towards the majority class (normal traffic), resulting in high false-negative rates and an inability to detect rare but critical anomalies effectively. In the context of IoT security, this issue becomes even more critical, as undetected attacks can compromise system integrity and patient safety in healthcare applications. To address this, our proposed methodology integrates optimization algorithms, such as the CSA and BGWO, which are specifically designed to handle data imbalance.

The suggested anomaly detection model was tested with and without optimization algorithms, GWO and CSO, to determine their effects on accuracy and efficiency. Without these optimization approaches, the model had 78% detection accuracy, 12% false positives, and 18% false negatives. These findings indicate that, although the model successfully identified a considerable number of anomalies, it struggled to distinguish between normal and anomalous data, particularly in cases of class imbalance. When feature selection and optimization were done using GWO and CSO, the model performed much better. With these algorithms, accuracy climbed to 92% while false positives and negatives decreased by 7% and 5%, respectively. GWO and CSO integration improved class imbalance management and anomaly detection. The optimization technique improved the model’s computational efficiency, decreasing sample detection time from 1.2 s to 0.85 s.

Conclusion

In conclusion, combining GFB-CNN with optimization algorithms such as CSA-BGWO-SVM in IoT-based healthcare security will enhance accuracy and resilience. Combining Bayesian inference and CNNs in feature extraction and classification, along with CSA-BGWO’s optimization capabilities, enhances accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity. These new procedures are reliable, as smaller standard deviations indicate less performance variability. This comprehensive strategy enhances healthcare IoT security by consistently and accurately detecting attacks, protecting sensitive medical data, and improving patient safety. The suggested anomaly detection approach improves IoT-based systems, although it has drawbacks. First, the model’s effectiveness relies greatly on the quality and variety of the training dataset, and its capacity to generalize to new attack types may be restricted if it does not cover a broad range of threat situations. The computational complexity of optimization techniques, such as CSA and BGWO, may also limit real-time deployment in resource-constrained IoT contexts. Feature selection and optimization may not completely address the dynamic and evolving nature of IoT security risks, necessitating model updates. Future work could include adding more diverse and realistic attack scenarios, enhancing the scalability of real-time optimization algorithms, and integrating adaptive learning techniques to respond to emerging threats. Hybrid solutions that combine IDS with blockchain for enhanced data integrity may improve the system.

In Situ Reduction Fabricated Pt-Mediated Bi2WO6/g‑C3N4 Heterojunction with Unconventional Z‑Scheme Charge Transfer for Enhanced Photocatalysis and Hydrogen Evolution
Authors: Nur Syamimi Adzis, Nur Hidayatul Syazwani Suhaimi, Rahil Azhar, Suriati Sufian, Mahidin Mahidin, Azhar Ali Haidry, Mohamad Fariz Mohamad Taib, Yee Hui Robin Chang, Wan Izhan Nawawi Wan Ismail, Mohd Azlan Mohd Ishak
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
A novel Pt-mediated Z-scheme heterojunction photocatalyst, Pt-Bi2WO6/g-C3N4 (Pt-BWO/g-CN), was synthesized via an in situ reduction strategy, enabling precise Pt positioning as an electron mediator between BWO and g-CN. Structural and morphological analyses (FESEM, HRTEM, and BET) confirmed nanoscale integration, uniform Pt dispersion, and high surface area. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) revealed binding energy shifts indicative of efficient interfacial charge transfer, while UV–vis diffuse reflectance spectroscopy (DRS) and Mott–Schottky analyses confirmed favorable band alignment consistent with a direct Z-scheme pathway. Photoluminescence (PL) and photoelectrochemical measurements demonstrated suppressed electron–hole recombination and enhanced charge separation. Electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) provided compelling mechanistic evidence: DMPO-trapped spectra detected abundant •OH and •O2– radicals under light irradiation, TEMP-trapped spectra confirmed 1O2 formation, and intrinsic oxygen vacancies (g ≈ 2.003) were observed even in the dark, decreasing upon illumination, supporting defect-assisted charge transfer. The optimized Pt-BWO/g-CN achieved complete RhB degradation and 85% RR4 removal within 60 min under visible light, alongside a hydrogen generation rate of 5364.96 μmol g–1 h–1 (STH efficiency of 3.4% and AQY of 3.5%). Radical scavenging identified h+ and •O2 – as the dominant active species. This work demonstrates a scalable route to high-performance Z-scheme photocatalysts with dual capability in pollutant degradation and solar hydrogen generation, underpinned by direct spectroscopic validation of the charge transfer pathway.

Introduction

Photocatalysis has emerged as a promising approach for both environmental remediation and renewable energy conversion, leveraging solar energy to drive redox reactions. − Beyond pollutant degradation, photocatalysis holds immense significance for sustainable energy applications, particularly through photocatalytic water splitting for hydrogen (H2) generation, which is a process that produces clean fuel by converting solar energy into chemical energy. , The dual role of photocatalysis in addressing environmental challenges, such as wastewater remediation, and contributing to global energy sustainability positions it as a central focus of the current research.

A wide range of photocatalysts have been explored for these applications, including traditional semiconductors categorized into metal oxides (e.g., TiO2, , ZnO, , WO3 , ), metal sulfides (e.g., CdS , ), bismuth-based compounds (e.g., BiVO4, , Bi2WO6 , ), and nonmetallic semiconductors (e.g., g-C3N4, black phosphorus). Each of these materials offers unique properties in terms of bandgap energy, charge separation efficiency, and light irradiation stability. While TiO2 remains a benchmark due to its stability and cost-effectiveness, its wide bandgap (∼3.2 eV) limits visible-light absorption. Similarly, CdS is efficient under visible light but suffers from photocorrosion.

In recent years, the focus has shifted toward visible-light-responsive and environmentally benign photocatalysts, including Bi2WO6 (BWO) and g-C3N4 (g-CN), due to their moderate band gaps (∼2.7–2.8 eV) and favorable charge dynamics. , BWO has gained attention for its efficient degradation of dyes, pharmaceuticals, and other micropollutants, attributed to the hybridization of Bi 6s and O 2p orbitals, which enhances valence band dispersion and redox capability. However, the rapid recombination of photogenerated electron–hole pairs continues to hinder its photocatalytic efficiency. , To address these limitations, strategies such as morphological engineering, , surface modification, and heterojunction construction , have been applied. Among these, heterojunction formation with g-CN has proven its effectiveness. − g-CN is an organic semiconductor known for its high thermal and chemical stability, a Π–Π stacking structure, and good visible-light absorption. , Yet, like BWO, it also suffers from poor charge separation and high recombination rates. Their integration into heterostructure nanocomposites thus offers a promising route to enhance charge separation, suppress recombination, and extend photocatalytic activity under visible light.

Traditional Type-II heterojunctions have been widely used in BWO/g-CN composites, but they suffer from limited charge transfer efficiency and weakened redox potential due to the indirect electron migration pathway. , The Z-scheme charge transfer mechanism has recently gained attention as an alternative, as it allows direct recombination of low-energy electrons (e–) and holes (h+) while preserving highly reactive charge carriers necessary for redox reactions. ,− However, constructing an efficient Z-scheme BWO/g-CN system remains challenging as improper charge transfer can lead to unintended electron migration pathways, ultimately reducing performance. Since the natural band alignment of BWO and g-CN tends to hinder effective charge separation, the introduction of platinum (Pt) as an electron mediator has been found effective in boosting photocatalytic performance by facilitating faster electron transfer between the two semiconductors. , Pt serves as a selective bridge, enabling electron transfer from the conduction band (CB) of the high reductive band to the valence band (VB) of the high oxidative band, thereby enhancing the redox ability and reducing charge recombination. However, the positioning of Pt within the composite remains critical. Random Pt distribution may promote nonideal electron pathways, which can lead to non-Z-scheme behavior.

Previous studies have primarily employed the batchwise method to introduce Pt into BWO/g-CN, often resulting in suboptimal Pt positioning and uncertain charge transfer pathways, ultimately limiting photocatalytic efficiency. , In this study, an in situ fabrication approach was employed, integrating Pt during the BWO formation stage to achieve controlled Pt distribution within the nanocomposite. ,, Interestingly, this strategy led to an unconventional Z-scheme charge transfer mechanism, which deviated from the classical Z-scheme model. This unexpected behavior emphasizes the complexity of charge migration in Pt-mediated heterojunctions and offers new insights into designing photocatalysts for enhanced efficiency.

This work investigates the photocatalytic performance of in situ synthesized Pt-BWO/g-CN for dye degradation and water splitting, with a focus on charge transfer behavior and electronic structure modifications. The findings contribute to the development of next-generation photocatalysts for environmental and renewable energy applications.

Experimental Section

Chemicals and Materials

All reagents were analytical grade and used without further purification. Sodium tungstate dihydrate (Na2WO4·H2O), bismuth­(III) nitrate pentahydrate (Bi­(NO3)3·5H2O) as BWO precursors, chloroplatinic acid hexahydrate (H2PtCl6·6H2O, >98.5%), urea (CO­(NH2)2, >99.5 mol %) as the carbon nitride precursor, and sodium borohydride (NaBH4, 12% in 40% aqueous solution) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich Co. (Merck KGaA, Darmstadt, Germany). For the radical trapping tests, ethylenediaminetetraacetate (EDTA, 6 mmol L–1), isopropanol (IPA, 6 mmol L–1), hydrogen peroxide (H2O2, 0.5 mol L–1), and silver nitrate (AgNO3, 0.5 mol L–1) were used as effective scavengers for h+, •OH, •O2–, and e– radicals, respectively. Reactive Red 4 (RR4, 98%) and Rhodamine B (RhB, 99%) dye powders were selected as model pollutants to evaluate the photocatalytic activity of the synthesized materials.

For the photodegradation setup, a glass cell (15 × 20 × 0.5 cm) was used. Dye solution samples were filtered using a cellulose acetate (CA) syringe filter and collected with a 10 mL syringe for absorbance measurements. A 55 W fluorescent lamp (model Firefly/E27) with residual UV leakage of 6.92 W/m2 was used as the visible light source.

Synthesis Methods (In Situ Method)

Synthesis of Pt-BWO

Pt-BWO was synthesized using a hydrothermal-reduction method, adapted from the procedure reported by Khojeh et al. To prepare BWO, 0.45 g of sodium tungstate dihydrate (Na2WO4·2H2O) and 1.29 g bismuth­(III) nitrate pentahydrate (Bi­(NO3)3·5H2O) were each dissolved in 7.5 mL of deionized water (DI water) separately until both turned into a colorless solution. The Na2WO4·2H2O solution was then added dropwise to the Bi­(NO3)3·5H2O solution with vigorous stirring at room temperature. Stirring was continued for 2 h to ensure complete dissolution. The resulting mixture was transferred into a Teflon-lined autoclave and heated at 180 °C for 22 h to undergo hydrothermal treatment. After cooling to room temperature, varying concentrations of H2PtCl6 (0.1, 0.15, and 0.3% by weight) and 0.28 mL of NaBH4 (used as a strong reducing agent) were added directly to the same autoclaved conditions for 2 h at a heating rate of 5 °C/min. The product was collected by centrifugation at 4000 rpm for 30 min, followed by washing several times with deionized water (DI) and ethanol to remove residual impurities. The final sample was dried in an oven at 80 °C for 8 h or overnight.

Synthesis of Pt-BWO/g-CN

To construct the heterojunction materials Pt-BWO/g-CN, bulk g-CN was first synthesized from a urea precursor prior to combination with Pt-BWO. Approximately 1 g of urea was weighed and calcined in a muffle furnace at 550 °C for 30 min, using a heating rate of 3 °C/min to form bulk g-CN. The selected heating rate and temperature were adapted from previously reported methods, as they provide a balance between complete thermal polymerization of urea and minimal structure collapse. The resulting pale-yellow solid powder was ground into a fine powder and dispersed in deionized water. Pure g-CN was then collected by filtration and dried overnight. For the preparation of Pt-BWO/g-CN, the Pt-BWO powder was physically mixed with the synthesized g-CN at varying weight ratios (1, 2, 3, and 4%). The resulting mixtures were then calcined at 400 °C for 2 h to complete heterojunction formation, as illustrated in Scheme .

Characterizations

The structural, morphological, optical, and surface properties of the synthesized photocatalysts were characterized using a range of analytical techniques, including X-ray diffraction (XRD), Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR), field emission scanning electron microscopy–energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (FESEM–EDX), high-resolution transmission electron microscopy–selective area electron diffraction (HRTEM–SAED), Brunauer–Emmett–Teller (BET), UV–vis diffuse reflectance spectroscopy (DRS), photoluminescence (PL), X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS), electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR), and photoelectrochemical measurements. Instrument models and operating parameters used are provided in the Supporting Information (Table S1).

Photocatalytic Activity Measurement

Degradation of Dyes

The photocatalytic performance of the synthesized Pt-BWO/g-CN was evaluated by using Rhodamine B (RhB) as a model organic pollutant. In a typical experiment, 0.025 g of photocatalyst was dispersed in 20 mL of a 30 ppm of RhB solution. The suspension was stirred in the dark for 30 min to achieve adsorption–desorption equilibrium, followed by irradiation under a 55 W fluorescent lamp (Firefly/E27, UV leakage: 5.62 W/m2). The photocatalytic reaction proceeded for 60 min, with 3 mL aliquots withdrawn every 15 min. Absorbance was measured at 554 nm by using a HACH 1900 UV–vis spectrometer to determine the decolorization percentage. Oxygen was continuously supplied via a pump aerator (NS7200) throughout the reaction. The same procedure was applied using a 30 ppm of Reactive Red 4 (RR4) solution, with absorbance measured at 517 nm. The percentage of dye remaining was calculated using eq . 1 degradation percent remaining(%)=CC0×100 The degradation efficiency of optimized Pt-BWO/g-CN was further determined using the Langmuir–Hinshelwood pseudo-first-order kinetic model by plotting ln (C 0/C) versus irradiation time, where C 0 is the initial concentration and C is the concentration at time, t. This is to determine the first-order rate constant (k), which explains the kinetic reaction of the heterogeneous process.

Photocatalytic Water Splitting

Approximately 0.025 g of Pt-BWO/g-CN was suspended in 10 mL of H2O. The suspension was carefully placed in a quartz glass container sealed with a Suba-seal septum. Irradiation was carried out using a 250 W xenon lamp. The evolved H2 was collected via a gas outlet line connected to a sealed syringe. The H2 concentration was measured using a gas chromatograph equipped with a thermal conductivity detector (GC-TCD). Gas samples were injected through the headspace into a GC-6890 system (Agilent Hewlett-Packard) fitted with a TCD. The flow rate of the sample was set to 10 mL/min with a loop volume of 1 mLmin–1. Nitrogen gas (N2) was used as the gas carrier in the system. Before measurement, the system headspace was flushed with N2 gas for 30 min to eliminate atmospheric gases. After flushing, a defined volume of the gas mixture was injected into the GC-TCD reactor using a gastight syringe, and the system was left undisturbed for 10 min to ensure complete gas diffusion within the headspace for accurate H2 detection.

Results and Discussion

Photocatalyst Structure and Morphology

XRD Analysis

The crystal phases and structures of the composites were investigated by X-ray diffraction analysis. The Z-scheme photocatalyst consisting of BWO, the organic semiconductor g-CN, and Pt as an electron shuttle mediator, Pt-BWO/g-CN, was fabricated by using the in situ method, as illustrated in Scheme . In the XRD spectra, pure BWO exhibits a set of sharp and well-defined diffraction peaks consistent with the monoclinic phase of BWO. Characteristic peaks were observed at 2θ = 28.8, 33.3, 47.4, 56.4, 59.1, and 69.3°, which correspond to the russellite phase in crystal planes of (131), (200), (202), (133), (262), and (391), respectively (JCPDS: 39-0256). , The organic semiconductor of the g-CN sample has exhibited two prominent peaks at 10.6 and 27.5°, attributed to the (100) and (002), respectively, as seen in Figure a (JCPDS: 87-1526). , The combined BWO/g-CN heterostructure exhibited all characteristic peaks of both BWO and g-CN, indicating the successful integration of the two semiconductors. Evidence of interfacial interaction is visible in the magnified XRD region between 27 and 28°, where the (131) peak of BWO shifts slightly toward lower angles, approaching the position of the (002) peak of g-CN as can be seen in Figure b. This leftward shift indicates a chemical interaction between the two components, confirming the successful formation of a heterojunction. Upon Pt incorporation, a further leftward shift was observed, suggesting the successful integration of Pt within the composite matrix.

(a) XRD spectra of synthesized nanocomposites, (b) enlarged spectra of BWO (131) and g-CN (002) plane, and (c) FTIR spectra of unmodified and modified photocatalyst.

Optimizing the crystallite size of nanocomposites is essential for enhancing the overall photocatalytic performance, as it can directly influence the light absorption, charge separation, and the active sites. The crystallite size of all nanocomposites was calculated using the Debye–Scherrer equation. Notably, sharper and narrower XRD peaks observed from the XRD spectra correspond to the larger crystallite sizes, which reflect a higher degree of crystallinity. , Based on calculations, nanocomposites of Pt-BWO/g-CN fabricated under the in situ preparation method exhibited the largest crystallite size at approximately 15.98 nm, as indicated by the thinner peaks in its XRD pattern, suggesting a high degree of crystallinity. This high degree of crystallinity contrasts with the smaller crystallite sizes (<12.5 nm) observed for the unmodified BWO and g-CN, as shown in Table . The larger crystallite size of the Z-scheme photocatalysts is advantageous for the photocatalytic performance. The larger crystallite size offers the potential to enhance light absorption aided by the SPR effect from the presence of Pt, which allows the nanocomposites to harness a broader spectrum of light. This synergistic effect between crystallinity and SPR contributes to the effective generation of electron–hole pairs. As in the Z-scheme system, BWO serves as the electron donor, while g-CN acts as the electron acceptor with Pt strategically positioned as an electron mediator. The larger crystallite size and high crystallinity of the nanocomposite improve the interfacial charge transfer between the components, supporting the Z-scheme mechanism.

FTIR Analysis

Next, Figure c shows the FTIR spectrum for unmodified BWO, g-CN, and modified Pt-BWO/g-CN. Purely synthesized BWO exhibits main absorption bands at 400–700 cm–1, which are attributed to Bi–O, W–O stretching, and W–O–W bridging stretching modes. A sharp peak at ∼810 cm–1 corresponds to a breathing mode of the triazine ring in g-CN, whereas the absorption bands in the region of 1200–1750 cm–1 are attributed to aromatic C–N stretching vibration mode. The broadband that is allocated at around 3050–3370 cm–1 can be ascribed to the stretching mode of the N–H bond. Furthermore, all characteristic absorption bands of g-CN and BWO appear to be represented in the prepared Pt-BWO/g-CN under in situ modes. The existence of Pt bonding in the sample cannot be traced from the spectra.

Morphological and Structural Identification

FESEM–EDX with Mapping Analysis

The morphology and elemental composition of the as-prepared samples were characterized using field emission scanning electron microscopy (FESEM) equipped with energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX) to determine the elemental composition of the prepared photocatalyst. As depicted in Figure , the morphological features varied depending on the synthesis method and the elemental composition. For pure BWO (Figure a), a distinctive nanosheet-like morphology was observed, which is attributed to the high bismuth content. EDX elemental mapping confirmed the uniform distribution of Bi, W, and O, validating the formation of high-purity BWO. In the case of g-CN synthesized from urea calcination (Figure b), a flakelike, bulky morphology was noted. The EDX spectrum showed a well-distributed composition of C and N, consistent with the expected structure of g-CN. Upon addition of Pt and g-CN to form Pt-BWO/g-CN via in situ fabrication (Figure c), notable morphological changes were observed. In this synthesis approach, Pt was incorporated during BWO formation before its combination with g-CN. This modification partially disrupted the original nanosheet structure of BWO, indicating an interaction between Pt and the BWO lattice. The elemental mapping in Figure d of Pt-BWO/g-CN synthesized via in situ further confirms the successful incorporation of Pt, along with Bi, W, O, C, and N. Notably, the uniform distribution of Pt in the composite highlights the effectiveness of the in situ method in achieving homogeneous Pt dispersion, which is favorable for enhanced photocatalytic performance.

HRTEM–SAED Analysis

High-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM) analysis provides detailed insights into the interface between BWO and g-CN, as well as the spatial positioning of Pt. As shown in Figure e, lattice fringes corresponding to the monoclinic structure of BWO at plane (131) and the planar structure of g-CN (002) were observed. Additionally, lattice fringes assigned to the face-centered cubic (FCC) structure of Pt[200] were observed at the interface region. This localized presence of Pt suggests that it is strategically positioned at the heterojunction interface, effectively serving as an electron mediator in the Z-scheme photocatalytic system. The selected area electron diffraction (SAED) in Figure f reveals a square diffraction pattern, indicating the presence of crystalline domains with cubic or tetragonal symmetry. This pattern is attributed to both Pt(200) and BWO (131). The Pt(200) reflections correspond to metallic Pt0, embedded within the BWO matrix. Meanwhile, the ring-like diffraction patterns are likely associated with the amorphous region dominated by g-CN. These findings confirm the successful integration of Pt within the nanocomposite matrix via the in situ method, which enables precise positioning of Pt at the BWO/g-CN interface. The well-defined spatial arrangement of Pt facilitates the formation of a Z-scheme heterojunction system. This configuration significantly improves photocatalytic performance by enhancing interfacial charge separation and transfer and harnessing the surface plasmon resonance (SPR) effect of Pt.

Chemical Composition

XPS Analysis

X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was employed to investigate the chemical states, bonding environments, and interfacial interactions within the in situ synthesized Pt-BWO/g-CN heterostructure. The survey spectrum in Figure a confirms the presence of Bi, W, O, Pt, C, and N elements, validating the successful fabrication of the hybrid photocatalyst. In the high-resolution Bi 4f spectrum (Figure b), two distinct peaks at 157.3 and 162.6 eV correspond to Bi 4f5/2 and Bi 4f7/2, respectively, confirming the presence of Bi3+ in [Bi2O2]2+ units of BWO. A slight shift toward lower binding energies (BE) after Pt modification to 156.4 and 161.6 eV, respectively, suggests partial electron transfer from Bi to Pt, suggesting strong interfacial contact and heterojunction formation. The W 4f spectrum (Figure c) displays peaks at 32.5 and 35.6 eV (W6+ 4f7/2 and 4f5/2), confirming the coexistence W6+ oxidation states. The shift of W peaks toward lower BE suggests electron donation from W sites toward metallic Pt, which results in reduced electron cloud density, which directs e– toward the metallic Pt nanoparticles, enhancing the charge transfer across the heterostructure. In the O 1s spectrum (Figure d), peaks at 530.04 and 533.2 eV correspond to lattice oxygen species of W–O–W, Bi–O, and surface hydroxyl (–OH), respectively. A downward shift in BE for the lattice oxygen confirms its role as an electron donor, facilitating charge accumulation at Pt sites in the Z-scheme system. The C 1s spectrum (Figure e) shows peaks at 282.7 and 285.1 eV, attributed to sp2 hybridized C (N–CN) and sp3 hybridized C (C–N2) in the g-CN matrix. A slight shift toward higher binding energies suggests acceptance, enhancing the photocatalytic properties of g-CN in the Z-scheme structure as an electron donor. The N 1s spectrum (Figure f) shows peaks at 396.1 and 397.1 eV associated with sp2 hybridization of N in tri-s-triazine units of g-CN and CN–C bonds. The shift in BE suggests charge transfer among Pt, BWO, and g-CN, highlighting the strong electronic interaction and efficient heterojunction formation. For Pt 4f in Figure g, the spectrum shows two prominent peaks at 71.9 and 74.9 eV, corresponding to Pt0 4f7/2 and Pt0 4f5/2, respectively. Additionally, one peak is observed at a higher binding energy, corresponding to Pt4+ 4f5/2, confirming the coexistence of metallic Pt and oxidized Pt species. The presence of Pt4+ indicates the formation of partial oxidation states during synthesis, which may enhance the charge transfer properties. The slight shift in Pt0 peaks compared to standard metallic Pt (71.1 and 74.43 eV) suggests electron redistribution within the heterostructure, where Pt acts as a charge accumulator. This confirms the strategic role of Pt as an electron mediator, promoting efficient charge transfer and enhancing surface plasmon resonance (SPR) effects. The overlapping peaks of Pt with W elements and the higher binding energy shifts for Pt4+ suggest that Pt is integrated within the sandwich structure of BWO nanoplates, positioned between BWO and g-CN. This strategic positioning ensures optimal charge transfer and Z-scheme formation, which is vital for the improved photocatalytic performance observed in the in situ synthesized heterostructure of Pt-BWO/g-CN. The observed binding energy shifts across the spectra toward the lower binding energy as observed in Bi, W, and O as an electron acceptor and toward higher binding energy as observed in C and N elements as an electron donor, provide a clear evidence of charge redistribution within the Z-scheme route (g-CN → Pt → BWO) which contradicted to the traditional Z-scheme route creating a new unconventional Z-scheme charge transfer mechanism.

Optical Properties Analysis

Photoluminescence Analysis

Photoluminescence (PL) emission spectroscopy is an effective tool to evaluate the recombination rate of photoexcited charge carriers, with lower PL intensity indicating reduced electron–hole pair recombination and, consequently, enhanced photocatalytic performance. As depicted in Figure a, the pristine g-CN sample shows the highest PL intensity, reflecting its relatively high recombination rate of the photoexcited carriers. In contrast, BWO exhibits slightly lower PL intensity due to its intrinsic photocatalytic properties. When BWO is coupled with g-CN to form a heterojunction, a notable reduction in PL intensity is observed, signifying the effective suppression of recombination processes. This reduction can be attributed to the formation of an intimate interface between BWO and g-CN, which promotes efficient charge separation by facilitating electron transfer from the conduction band (CB) of BWO to the valence band (VB) of g-CN, or vice versa, depending on the band alignment. Further incorporation of Pt into the BWO/g-CN composite results in an even more pronounced decrease in PL intensity. This enhanced suppression of recombination is primarily due to Pt’s dual role as a charge transfer bridge and an electron sink. Pt nanoparticles, deposited at the hybrid interface, act as an electron mediator in the Z-scheme mechanism, facilitating the directional transfer of photoexcited electrons from the CB of BWO to the VB of g-CN. Simultaneously, Pt serves as an electron reservoir, enabling efficient separation of photogenerated charge carriers by trapping electrons and preventing their recombination with holes. This improved charge carrier separation promotes enhanced photocatalytic activity in the Pt-BWO/g-CN hybrid. The Pt’s ability to enrich electrons in the CB of g-CN and accumulate holes in the VB of BWO enhances both reduction and oxidation reactions. Specifically, the reduction of dissolved O2 into reactive oxygen species (ROS) and the oxidation of organic dyes are significantly facilitated at the Pt-enhanced interface. These findings demonstrate that the strategic incorporation of Pt into the heterojunction system plays a vital role in minimizing charge carrier recombination, thereby enhancing the overall photocatalytic efficiency of the composites.

UV-DRS Analysis

The band structures of the prepared samples were determined by using the UV–vis DRS technique. As illustrated in Figure b, pristine BWO exhibits an absorption edge around 482 nm, corresponding to a band gap energy of approximately 2.57 eV, while g-CN shows an absorption edge near 457 nm, correlating with a band gap of about 2.80 eV, as reported in the literature. When these two semiconductors are combined, the band edge has red-shifted, which decreases its band gap energy to 2.77 eV, indicating the successful formation of a heterojunction between g-CN and BWO, which facilitates better charge separation and enhanced light absorption. The presence of Pt significantly enhances the visible light absorption, as observed by the extended absorption edge beyond 446 nm. The introduction of Pt into Pt-BWO/g-CN further reduces its band gap energy, declining to 2.69 eV as depicted in the Tauc Plot (Figure c). Notably, a narrow band gap may lead to a low tendency of charge carrier separation as the carriers are now more likely to recombine, resulting in a lower photocatalytic response. However, the PL suggests otherwise (low PL response indicates low e–/h+ recombination). The band gap energy, calculated from the PL emission data in Table , increased from 2.69 to 2.72 eV instead. This indicates the presence of band-to-band transitions, as observed in Figure d, where the band-to-band structure facilitates electron transfer before recombination. As a result, the higher-energy emission extends the absorption spectrum to 530 nm. This suggests that Pt acts as an electron mediator, promoting electron transfer from the CB through band-to-band energy levels before reaching the VB and recombining. The successful fabrication of the Z-scheme system is therefore proven, demonstrating a strong heterojunction effect with Pt as the electron mediator.

Photoelectrochemical Analysis

Chronoamperometry Analysis

A chronoamperometric test was conducted to evaluate the photoresponse stability of all samples under alternating light and dark conditions. The test was performed at a constant potential of 1.23 V RHE for 330 s, with 30 s intervals of light illumination followed by 30 s in the absence of light. As shown in Figure a, the photocurrent response was observed to increase upon illumination. As for the single photocatalyst of BWO and g-CN, the photocurrent was initially observed to spike sharply, followed by a rapid decline during the second interval before stabilizing over time. This behavior suggests a high initial electrochemical reaction rate, which gradually equilibrates as the system approaches its steady state. In contrast, after the integration of both semiconductors, the photoresponse displayed a more stable and increasing pattern, possibly owing to enhanced charge transfer within the system. After the introduction of Pt between the combined semiconductors, it exhibited a smoother photocurrent decay without significant fluctuations, indicating a more stable electrochemical environment. This improved stability can be attributed to the efficient introduction of Pt as an electron mediator, which enhances charge separation by facilitating electron transfer between the two semiconductors. The high conductivity and low work function of Pt0, as observed in the XPS analysis, likely play a pivotal role in facilitating rapid electron shuttling and maintaining a steady flow of electrons, leading to a substantial enhancement in the overall photoresponse of Pt-BWO/g-CN.

EIS Analysis

Photoelectrochemical tests provide quantitative insights into the photocatalytic performance of materials functioning as photoelectrodes. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was employed to evaluate the mobility and recombination rate of interfacial charge carriers. The Nyquist plots, shown in Figure b, depict semicircular arcs that correspond to the charge transfer resistance, R ct, and double-layer capacitance at the electrode–electrolyte interface under both light and dark conditions. A smaller semicircle radius generally indicates a lower R ct, which is associated with improved charge transfer efficiency and reduced recombination rates. , Under light conditions, the materials with Pt introduced as an electron mediator exhibited a slightly larger semicircular radius compared with unmodified BWO and g-CN, indicating a relatively higher resistance to charge transfer at the interface. However, this increase in R ct does not necessarily imply poor charge transfer capability, as PL results demonstrated significantly lower emission intensities, indicating reduced electron–hole recombination. The higher R ct observed for the Pt-BWO/g-CN nanocomposite can be attributed to its more complex interfacial structure, resulting from Pt incorporation. In the in situ synthesis process, Pt was introduced alongside BWO and g-CN, promoting its direct integration as an electron shuttle mediator within the heterojunction system. This configuration, supported by SAED images in Figure d, suggests that the interaction between the three components: BWO, g-CN, and Pt, may create intricate interfaces that introduce some resistance to electron movement. Despite this higher resistance, the successful construction of a Z-scheme heterojunction is evident. The incorporation of Pt as an electron mediator enhances localized charge transfer between the BWO and g-CN, enabling effective charge separation and reducing recombination. The Z-scheme heterojunction allows for the efficient utilization of photogenerated charges, improving the photocatalytic activity despite the higher R ct observed in the EIS results. Thus, the introduction of Pt not only facilitates the construction of a stable Z-scheme but also significantly enhances the overall photocatalytic performance of the Pt-BWO/g-CN system under in situ synthesis.

Cyclic Voltammetry (CV)

Cyclic voltammetry (CV) was employed to investigate the electrochemical behavior and photoresponse of the synthesized photocatalysts, particularly focusing on the in situ Pt-BWO/g-CN composite. Furthermore, the overlapping profiles at different scan rates in both conditions confirm the material’s electrochemical stability under varying conditions, including light exposure. The smooth and reproducible behavior of the CV curves reinforces the durability and stability of Pt-BWO/g-CN during continuous electrochemical cycles.

Summary of H2 Generation through Water Splitting

Photocatalytic Water Splitting for H2 Generation

The photocatalytic water splitting performance of Pt-BWO/g-CN demonstrates excellent hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) and oxygen evolution reaction (OER) capabilities, attributed to the efficient charge separation and directional charge flow under an unconventional Z-scheme mechanism. The system achieved 5364.96 μmol g–1 h–1 of H2 evolution rate in 2.5 h with a proportional increase in O2 evolution, confirming the stoichiometric 2:1 H2:O2 ratio expected for water splitting as shown in Figure e. The stable linear increase in both H2 and O2 generation indicates sustained photocatalytic activity. The photogenerated electrons from the CB of g-CN were effectively transferred to Pt, which acted as an electron mediator, significantly enhancing the HER by facilitating proton reduction, supported by the cumulative gas evolution rate in Figure f. Simultaneously, the strong oxidative holes from the VB of BWO promoted efficient OER, generating O2. The minimized electron–hole recombination, as observed from the charge motion in the unconventional Z-scheme, further supports the superior redox ability of the system. Notably, the system has achieved a solar-to-hydrogen (STH) efficiency of 3.4% and apparent quantum yield (AQY) of 3.5%, underscoring its potential for highly efficient solar-driven water splitting. These results make Pt-BWO/g-CN an excellent candidate for highly efficient solar-driven water splitting.

Point of Zero Charge (PZC) Analysis

PZC analysis was conducted to evaluate the efficiency of Pt-BWO/g-CN in degrading organic pollutants. As referred to Figure a, the PZC of approximately 4.60 indicates that the prepared photocatalyst has a positively charged surface at pH < 4.60 and a negatively charged surface at pH > 4.60. Since the RhB dye solution has a pH of 5.60, which is above the PZC, the photocatalyst’s surface tends to undergo deprotonation, resulting in a net negative charge. This facilitates strong electrostatic attraction with the positively charged RhB molecules. Hence, its adsorption increases, leading to enhanced photocatalytic efficiency. In contrast, RR4 with an initial pH solution of 5.21, being an anionic dye, experiences electrostatic repulsion from the similarly negatively charged catalyst surface, which leads to reduced adsorption and consequently lower photocatalytic activity. This correlates well with the degradation results, where RhB exhibited a significantly higher degradation rate and lower remaining percentage compared to RR4, further supporting the influence of surface charge interactions. The increasing discrepancy in pH observed in the graph suggests dynamic surface charge stabilization and possible ion exchange or dye-surface competition effects during photocatalysis, further influencing dye adsorption. This highlights the crucial role of surface charge interactions in dye degradation efficiency and suggests that adjusting the solution pH closer to the PZC could help mitigate repulsion effects for anionic dyes like RR4, thereby improving adsorption affinity and overall removal performance.

Mechanistic Study

Radical Trapping Analysis

A comprehensive radical trapping test was conducted to identify the primary reactive radical species that are involved during the photodegradation of RR4/RhB dyes under simulated light irradiation. The experiment employed the optimized in situ Pt-BWO/g-CN, and four major species in photocatalysis, which are superoxide anion (•O2 –), hydroxyl radical (•OH), holes (h+), and electrons (e–), were selectively quenched using specific scavengers to assess their roles. ,, Figure b illustrates the corresponding reaction k-value upon the introduction of each scavenger. The addition of H2O2, a known quencher for •O2 –, caused the least reduction in the photocatalytic performance, indicating that •O2 – contributes minimally to the degradation of the RR4/RhB dye. This suggests that the oxygen reduction pathway is not dominant in this photocatalytic system. In contrast, the addition of IPA, a hydroxyl radical scavenger, resulted in a significant drop in the photocatalytic performance, suggesting •OH as one of the key contributors in the degradation, especially in breaking down organic pollutants like RR4/RhB.

Further reduction in photocatalytic activity was observed upon the addition of silver nitrate (AgNO3), a well-known electron scavenger. This significant drop confirms the substantial role of photogenerated electrons (e–) in the degradation pathway, most likely through direct reduction reactions or by participation in reactive oxygen species generation. The most pronounced reduction in photocatalytic activity was observed upon the addition of ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA), which acts as a hole scavenger, also leading to a notable drop in the k value, emphasizing the vital contribution of photogenerated holes. This observation strongly supports the operation of a direct Z-scheme charge transfer mechanism, where Pt facilitates rapid electron shuttling, allowing holes in the valence band of BWO to retain their high oxidation potential. These holes can either directly oxidize the dye molecules or promote the formation of additional reactive species, such as •OH radicals. Overall, the reactive species involved can be ranked in order of their impact as h+ > e– > •O2 – > •OH. This result not only validates the efficient charge separation and migration facilitated by Pt as an electron mediator but also confirms the effectiveness of the Z-scheme heterojunction formation in driving redox reactions.

Both appear after illumination justifies that it is active to light. Charge Transfer Dynamics and Proposed Unconventional Z-scheme Mechanism

Figure illustrates the energy band alignment and charge transfer mechanism of the synthesized materials based on the results from XPS, UV–vis DRS, and Mott–Schottky analysis. On the basis of previously discussed results, a possible photocatalytic reaction mechanism over the constructed Z-scheme photocatalyst of Pt-BWO/g-CN was proposed in detail. In Figure a, the energy band positions of BWO and g-CN were observed. The CB of BWO was found positioned at −0.55 eV, and the VB was positioned at +2.02 eV (extrapolated as in Figure a). While in g-CN, the CB was at −1.0 eV, and its VB was at +1.80 eV (Figure b). Notably, in Figure b, the differences in the Fermi levels (E f) have shown initial misalignment, where g-CN has a higher E f at −0.90 eV compared to that of BWO (E f = 0.45 eV). Notably, the Fermi level lies closer to CB as both are n-type semiconductors. As two semiconductors of BWO and g-CN are in contact, the electrons flow from g-CN to BWO due to the difference in their Fermi level, leading to the realignment to reach its equilibration state, E f = −0.33 eV. The phenomenon of charge redistribution between the semiconductors has caused the band bending at both interfaces, creating an internal electric field (IEF) that drives photogenerated charges. Typically, Z-scheme heterojunction systems exhibit electron transfer from a lower negative CB to a lower positive VB, ensuring optimal spatial separation of charge carriers, as in Figure c. However, our findings reveal a unique electron transfer from g-CN highly negative CB to BWO highly positive VB upon inclusion of Pt as electron mediator, presenting an unconventional Z-scheme configuration.

In the absence of Pt in Figure c, the Z-scheme system predominantly follows the IEF created during Fermi level equilibration. Upon light irradiation, the electrons excited to the CB of BWO are prone to recombine with the holes present in the VB of BWO, as found in most Z-scheme systems. − This charge transfer is limited by the recombination process and mismatch in band alignment, leading to a reduced photocatalytic efficiency. As a result, the oxidative potential at the VB of g-CN and the reductive potential at the CB of the BWO are diminished. When Pt is introduced as an electron mediator, the system transitions to an unconventional Z-scheme heterojunction, overcoming the limitations imposed by the IEP alone in the traditional Z-scheme. Pt serves as an electron shuttle mediator, ensuring directional charge flow and minimizing recombination, and it is more thermodynamically feasible. Upon light irradiation in Figure d, the electrons in the VB of g-CN are excited to its CB and electrons in the VB of BWO are excited to its CB, leaving holes in each respective VB. Instead of following the IEP, the presence of Pt has inverted the charge flow from the CB of g-CN transfer to Pt, where they are efficiently recombined with holes from the VB of BWO. This phenomenon has left behind the highly oxidative holes in the VB of g-CN to degrade organic pollutants and highly reductive electrons in the CB of BWO, which can reduce protons to generate H2 or drive reduction reactions. As for their overall redox potential, the h+ in VB of g-CN at +1.80 eV, which is higher than the oxygen evolution rate (OER) at +1.23 eV, is strong enough to produce •OH radicals to break down the pollutant’s chain. While e– in CB of BWO is at −0.55 eV, which is below the H2 evolution rate (HER = 0.0 eV), which allows the h+ to be reduced into H2 and also to produce •O2 – (higher than −0.33 eV). Notably, in the Z-scheme, the recombination process removes weaker charge carriers so that it can work efficiently. e– in the CB of g-CN is too negative, making it prone to recombination, and e– flows from its high-energy state to a low state, like a flow of water. While h+ in the VB of BWO is too positive for the OER. In such a way, e– in the CB of BWO is strong enough to perform the HER and h+ in the VB of g-CN is strong enough to perform the OER. Conclusions

The Pt-mediated Z-scheme heterojunction photocatalyst, Pt-BWO/g-CN, was successfully constructed via an in situ reduction strategy, enabling precise positioning of Pt as an electron mediator to promote directional charge transfer. This heterojunction facilitated the recombination of photogenerated electrons in the CB of g-CN with holes in the VB of BWO, thereby preserving strong oxidative holes in the VB of g-CN and highly reductive electrons in the CB of BWO for photocatalytic reactions. Comprehensive characterization (XPS, UV–vis DRS, Mott–Schottky) confirmed favorable band alignment and intimate interfacial coupling, while PL and photoelectrochemical analyses revealed suppressed recombination and enhanced charge separation. Importantly, the combined results of radical trapping experiments and EPR spectroscopy provided direct mechanistic evidence of the Z-scheme pathway. Trapping tests identified h+ and •O2– as the dominant active species, with significant activity loss upon their quenching, while •OH played a secondary yet supportive role. These findings were fully consistent with EPR spin trapping: DMPO-•O2– and DMPO-•OH adducts showed strong signals under illumination, TEMP trapping confirmed substantial 1O2 production, and intrinsic oxygen vacancies (g ≈ 2.003) were observed even in the dark but decreased upon light exposure, indicating defect-assisted electron capture and enhanced •O2 – generation. The synergy between radical trapping and EPR results verifies that in the Pt-BWO/g-CN system, charge carriers follow the Pt-mediated Z-scheme route, enabling simultaneous strong oxidation (•OH, h+) and strong reduction (•O2–, e–) processes. The optimized photocatalyst achieved complete RhB degradation, 85% RR4 removal, and high H2 evolution rates, demonstrating its dual-function capability for environmental remediation and solar fuel production. Future work should address long-term stability, scale-up synthesis, and evaluation under real wastewater conditions containing complex pollutant mixtures.

Comparative Evaluation of the Effects of Commonly Consumed Beverages on Color Stability and Fracture Resistance of the Pediatric Kids-E-Bioflx and Kids-E-Crowns: An In Vitro Study
Authors: NV Chaithanya, Joby Peter, R Krishna Kumar, John Joseph Methippara, Reshma Joseph
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAims and backgroundThe study aimed to evaluate the effect of carbonated and noncarbonated beverages on the color stability and fracture resistance of two types of pediatric crowns: Kids-e-Bioflx and Kids-e-crowns.Materials and methodsThirty-two prefabricated pediatric second molar crowns were divided into two groups: Group A (Kids-e-Bioflx) and group B (Kids-e-crowns), each containing 16 crowns. These groups were further divided into subgroups based on the beverage used for immersion: Coca-Cola and Frooti. After 24 hours of immersion, samples underwent thermocycling between 5°C and 55°C for 500 cycles, followed by color measurement using a spectrophotometer and fracture resistance evaluation using a universal testing machine (UTM).ResultsResults showed that both carbonated and noncarbonated beverages significantly affected the color stability of the crowns. Group B (Kids-e-crowns) was more color stable than group A (Kids-e-Bioflx). However, the fracture resistance of group A was significantly higher than that of group B after beverage immersion, with group A2 (Kids-e-Bioflx in Frooti) exhibiting the highest strength.ConclusionKids-e-crowns demonstrated better color stability, while the newly introduced Kids-e-Bioflx crowns showed superior fracture resistance.Clinical significanceThis study provides valuable insights into the performance of Kids-e-Bioflx and Kids-e-crowns when exposed to common beverages and guides clinicians in selecting crowns tailored to individual patient needs, balancing esthetics with functional durability, and ensuring long-term success in restorative pediatric dental treatments.How to cite this articleNV Chaithanya, Peter J, Kumar KR, et al. Introduction

Early childhood caries is a global health problem with a worldwide pooled prevalence of 48% with large numbers of children presenting with extensive multisurface cavities on both anterior and posterior teeth.1 The management of decayed primary teeth is essential, as they play a vital role in chewing, speech development, and functioning as natural space maintainers in the dental arch. Treating dental caries in children has long been a challenge, requiring effective behavior management and durable treatment that remains functional until the natural exfoliation of the tooth.2

Selecting the most appropriate method of restoration is a critical step in ensuring optimal treatment outcomes. Recent research suggests a shift in the best approach for treating multisurface cavities in primary teeth by using prefabricated crowns.3–5 Prefabricated zirconia crowns, characterized by their translucency, anatomical contouring, esthetic appeal, bioinert nature, and high wear resistance, are widely employed as an excellent esthetic restorative option.6 Zirconia offers multiple advantageous properties, including high strength, superior esthetics, biocompatibility, and exceptional resistance to wear and corrosion. However, its limitations include a relatively high cost, lack of modifiability, and greater thickness compared to preformed metal crowns.3

Kids-e-crowns were developed in the year 2017, made of zirconia, and were known to be superior in esthetics and high-strength bearing. Recently, Bioflx crowns were introduced for the primary teeth in 2022 by Kid-e-Dental, India. Bioflx crowns are the first preformed pediatric crowns designed to be flexible, durable, self-adaptable, and esthetic, thereby integrating the advantages of both stainless steel and zirconia crowns. They are made up of a resin-hybrid polymer. They have unique flexibility when compared to the existing Kids-e-crown.

Extrinsic factors, particularly the adsorption and absorption of surface stains, represent a significant challenge in maintaining the esthetics of restorations. Higher consumption of colored and carbonated beverages among the younger generation predisposes esthetic restorations to discoloration.

Regular exposure of esthetic crowns to acidic foods and drinks with high pH increases the risk of erosion and discoloration of the crown surface.7 Although the esthetic properties of ceramic crowns are similar to those of natural teeth, they are vulnerable to fracture when compared to stainless-steel crowns, which exhibit high fracture resistance.

Being a newly introduced crown, not many studies have been carried out to compare the properties of Kids-e-Bioflx to the other. Immersion in Cola (carbonated) and Frooti (noncarbonated), owing to their staining potential, can serve as an effective method for assessing the discoloration susceptibility of crowns.

So, this in vitro study aims to compare the effects of commonly consumed beverages on the color stability and fracture resistance of pediatric Kids-e-Bioflx and Kids-e-crowns.

Materials and Methods

This comparative experimental in vitro study is designed in the Department of Pediatric and Preventive Dentistry, Annoor Dental College and Hospital, Muvattupuzha, in association with Sree Chitra Tirunal Institute for Medical Sciences and Technology, Thiruvananthapuram.

A total of 32 prefabricated pediatric second molar crowns were obtained from the manufacturer, which were divided into two different groups based on two different materials. Group A (n = 16) consisted of Kids-e-Bioflx crowns, and group B (n = 16) consisted of Kids-e-crowns (Zirconia crowns). The beverages used in this study are Coca-Cola (0696147)TM (Hindusthan Coca-Cola Beverages Pvt Ltd, India) with pH 1.8729 as the carbonated beverage and Frooti (2568613)TM (Parle Agro India Pvt Ltd Body Incorporate) with pH 3.0729 as the noncarbonated beverage. Each group was subsequently subdivided into two subgroups. Group A consists of (A1) Kids-e-Bioflx—Coca-cola and (A2) Kids-e-bioflx—Frooti, and group B consists of (B1) Kids-e-crown—Coca-cola and (B2) Kids-e-crown—Frooti.

Sample Preparation

Overall, 16 test samples of Kids-e-Bioflx and Kids-e-crowns each were obtained for this study. Prefabricated crowns of the second primary molar of size 5 were taken for the study (Fig. 1) for standardization. Coca-Cola was used as the sucrose-containing carbonated beverage, and Frooti as the sucrose-containing noncarbonated beverage.

32 prefabricated pediatric crowns (16 Kids-e-crowns and 16 Kids-e-Bioflx crowns)

Baseline Color Measurement

A baseline ΔE (the degree of color difference) measurement was made for 16 crowns from each group. Color measurements were performed using an ultraviolet–visible (UV-Vis) spectrophotometer (Evolution OnePlus), a computer-controlled device that generates color coordinates based on the International Commission on Illumination L*a*b* (CIE Lab) system. The parameters obtained included L (lightness, ranging from 0 = black to 100 = white), a* (red–green chromaticity index), and b* (yellow–blue chromaticity index), which were recorded against a standardized white background through a calibration process referred to as “zeroing.” All measurements were conducted under D50 illumination, corresponding to natural daylight conditions.7

The color change (ΔE) of the specimens was calculated using the formula: ΔE = [(ΔL)2 + (Δa)2 + (Δb)2]1/2, where ΔL, Δa, and Δb represent the changes in the respective CIE L*, a*, and b* coordinates over the course of the study.

The color of the specimens was measured at the beginning of the study, before immersing in the respective beverages, and also after 500 cycles of thermocycling.

Method of Test Solution Preparation for Immersion

The test solutions were stored in 15 mL test tubes. The volume of the beverage that was taken in a single test tube was 3 mL. The total number of test tubes required for each group is 16 (8 each for carbonated and noncarbonated). Total volume of carbonated and noncarbonated beverages required was 24 mL each. A volume of 3 mL of solution was dispensed into each of the 16 test tubes. The crowns were then immersed in respective test tube solutions and sealed using a rubber cork (Fig. 2). The sealed test tubes were stored at room temperature for 24 hours prior to thermocycling.

Crowns immersed in carbonated and noncarbonated beverages

Thermocycling Procedure

Thermocycling was carried out using a thermocycler (Thermo DC 10) containing water baths maintained at 5°C and 55°C. The sealed test tubes were then placed in the thermal cycler. The prepared specimens were subjected to thermocycling between 5°C and 55°C for 500 cycles, with a dwell time of 15 seconds at each temperature.8 Thermocycling simulates conditions of temperature variations in the oral cavity, and the dwell time simulates the patient's tolerance of exposure to extremes of the temperature range. The specimens were then taken for color measurement using a spectrophotometer.

Color Measurement

The samples were removed from the test solutions, dipped in distilled water 10 times, wiped with tissue paper,7 and then taken for color measurement using a UV-Vis spectrophotometer. The color measurements were taken by placing the sample in the specimen holder, similar to the baseline measurement.

Fracture Resistance Evaluation

The same samples after color stability testing were used for fracture resistance testing using a universal testing machine (UTM) (Instron Model 2519-107 series).

Die Fabrication for Fracture Resistance Measurement

Modeling wax was used to fabricate negative replicas of the crowns (Kids-e-Bioflx and Kids-e-crown), which were subsequently invested and cast into metal dies. Each die was fabricated with a flat, cylindrical base measuring 10 mm in diameter and approximately 10 mm in height from the finish line to the base (Fig. 3). The crowns were seated on their respective metal dies to confirm a passive fit. Two groups of crowns were subsequently evaluated for fracture resistance using a UTM.9

Metal dies for groups A and B crowns for fracture resistance testing

For testing, each sample was positioned on the platform of the UTM and secured firmly using the clamping jaws. A compressive load was applied through a 4.2 mm diameter steel ball indenter, directed occlusally at the central fossa of the crown, at a crosshead speed of 1 mm/min. The maximum load required to produce a crown fracture was recorded in Newtons.10

Results

Color Comparison

On immersion, group A showed a decrease and group B showed an increase in ΔE value from the baseline in either of the beverages, which was statistically significant. The ΔE value decrease of group A was more in carbonated than noncarbonated, and the ΔE value increase of group B was more in carbonated than noncarbonated (Table 1). Greater color change was observed in group A than in group B from baseline when immersed in both the carbonated and noncarbonated beverages, and the comparisons were found to be statistically significant.

Discussion

Esthetic restorations in the oral cavity are continuously subjected to various factors—such as temperature fluctuations, humidity, and dietary habits—that render them susceptible to color alterations.11 Saliva fluctuates between slightly acidic and slightly alkaline states, and consumption of acidic or colored beverages (coffee, tea, and wine) can stain or erode certain restorative materials, impacting their color and appearance.

The success of any esthetic restoration primarily depends on achieving an accurate color match and maintaining long-term color stability, followed by the material's mechanical strength. Numerous studies have compared esthetic restorations with respect to durability, retention, and clinical performance, with evidence suggesting that prefabricated crowns often yield the most favorable outcomes.3–5

Prefabricated zirconia crowns are more translucent, bio-inert with good wear resistance,7 and excellent esthetic restorative material. Kids-e-Bioflx crowns are the world's first flexible, durable, self-adaptable, and esthetic newly introduced preformed pediatric crowns. Although the esthetic properties of ceramic crowns are similar to those of natural teeth, they are vulnerable to fracture when compared to stainless-steel crowns, which exhibit high fracture resistance.

Kids-e-crowns are composed of zirconium dioxide (ZrO2) and yttrium oxide (Y2O3). Zirconium dioxide, which makes up 88% to 96% of the crown, is a strong, biocompatible ceramic.12 yttrium oxide, present at 4–6%, stabilizes the zirconia structure, enhancing its strength, translucency, and color stability. Kids-e-Bioflx crowns are made from a biocompatible hybrid resin polymer and are free from metal and bisphenol A-glycidyl methacrylate (bis-GMA). Zirconia exhibits exceptional resistance to acidic and alkaline solutions,13 reducing the risk of erosion and staining from foods and drinks. Its high density and low porosity minimize staining. Conversely, resin polymers are more prone to absorbing pigments from colored beverages due to potential porosity, leading to discoloration.14,15

The present study is aimed at comparing the color change value of Kids-e-Bioflx, a recently introduced esthetic pediatric crown, with Kids-e-crown, after immersion in commonly consumed beverages.

The study used beverage immersion followed by thermocycling to simulate children's oral environments, which are exposed to various beverages containing pigments and acids that can stain or degrade crown color. Immersing crowns in different beverages assesses their susceptibility to staining, while thermocycling mimics temperature fluctuations in the mouth, potentially accelerating color changes and stressing the material.16 This combined method creates a realistic and harsher environment, revealing weaknesses in color stability and fracture resistance. Thermocycling can open microscopic cracks, weaken the structure, and make it more susceptible to fracture, thus providing a comprehensive assessment of the crowns.

The study used the CIE Lab color system, a common method for color measurement, to express color numerically and calculate differences corresponding to visual perception.17 This method is advantageous as it aligns with human color perception and has shown accuracy and efficiency in prior studies on tooth-colored restorative materials.18,19 The degree of color change is denoted by ΔE, measured accurately using UV–Vis spectrophotometry. Fracture resistance was assessed with a UTM, recording the maximum load causing crown fracture. This approach enhances the practical significance and reliability of the findings in evaluating color stability and fracture resistance.

In this present study, Coca-Cola and Frooti were used for the study as carbonated and noncarbonated beverages with a pH value of 1.87 and 3.07, respectively. Statistically significant (p < 0.05) change in ΔE value was observed in both Kids-e-Bioflx and Kids-e-crown from baseline when immersed in either of the beverages. But, not much difference in ΔE was observed within each type of crown between carbonated and noncarbonated beverages. The findings support that both beverages have a significant effect on the color stability of Kids-e-Bioflx and Kids-e-crowns. The color of Kids-e-Bioflx improved or became lighter from the baseline color and was more noticeable in carbonated than in noncarbonated. The Kids-e-crown has shown color deterioration in both beverages compared to baseline. The greater discoloration was found when Kids-e-crown was immersed in a carbonated beverage than in a noncarbonated.

When the comparison was made between Kids-e-Bioflx and Kids-e-crown, the extent of color change was found to be less in Kids-e-crown from the baseline than in Kids-e-Bioflx from the baseline. This suggests that kids-e-crowns are more color-stable than Kids-e-Bioflx. This could be due to the fact that Kids-e-Bioflx, which is composed of hybrid polymers, is more susceptible to absorbing pigments from colored beverages, leading to discoloration. Resin polymers can have porosity due to filler content, which can allow for staining from the pigments.

The study evaluated fracture resistance using a UTM to apply precise, controlled loads mimicking biting forces.9 This ensured consistent, reliable results. Baseline fracture resistance values provided by manufacturers were 408.20 N for Kids-e-Bioflx and 3768 N for Kids-e-crowns. These baseline values were compared with postimmersion values to assess the crowns' durability.

Statistically significant increase in mean fracture resistance value (p < 0.05) was observed in Kids-e-Bioflx crown when immersed in either of the beverages. The mean fracture resistance value of the Kids-e-Bioflx crown was found to be greater when immersed in noncarbonated beverage than when immersed in a carbonated beverage. In this study, Kids-e-Bioflx is observed to have high fracture resistance compared to Kids-e-crown after immersion in either of the beverages. Also, the difference in effects by carbonated and noncarbonated beverages was significant in Kids-e-Bioflx but not in Kids-e-crown. The highest strength was shown by Kids-e-Bioflx when immersed in noncarbonated beverage than in a carbonated drink. Factors such as grain size, sintering temperature, crown design, thickness, and shape can influence the strength of zirconia crowns, leading to lower fracture strength in Kids-e-crowns. Bioflx crowns can deform slightly under high occlusal forces, distributing pressure and reducing stress, which helps prevent fractures. Unlike brittle zirconia, Bioflx absorbs impact and distributes force evenly, reducing fracture risk.

Colombo et al. reported clinically acceptable ∆E values after 1 week of immersing zirconia samples in a soft drink (Coca-Cola). This finding contrasts with the results of the present study, which may be attributed to the shorter immersion period employed in their investigation.20

Alzahrani et al. reported clinically acceptable ∆E values after 28 days of immersion in a soft drink.21 The presence of artificial colorants, citric acid, and the low pH of such beverages has been shown to contribute to discoloration of dental restorations.17 These factors likely account for the ∆E variations observed in the present study following immersion.

There are studies that suggest that thermocycling does have an effect on the color stability of zirconia. It was reported previously by Kamini et al. that thermocycling aging resulted in slightly decreasing CIE Lab color parameters for zirconia samples.22 Whereas in our study, there was an increase in ∆E of Kids-e-Bioflx and a decrease in ∆E of Kids-e-crown after immersion and thermocycling. So, a generalization to the above statement could not be made in our study.

Stefan Kist et al. conducted a study in which prefabricated zirconia crowns showed a fracture resistance of 1582 N, and it was not affected by saliva aging and chewing simulation.23

A study conducted by Al Shobber et al. compared the fracture resistance of two commercially available primary esthetic crown companies (NuSmile and Cheng Crowns) and two types of esthetic crowns (zirconia and preveneered stainless-steel crowns). The findings indicated that NuSmile zirconia measured the highest fracture resistance (937 ± 131 N), followed by Cheng Crowns zirconia (751 ± 102 N); NuSmile preveneered (482 ± 76 N) and Cheng Crowns preveneered (415 ± 12 N) did not demonstrate a major difference. Overall, the study concluded that zirconia crowns were higher in fracture resistance, especially NuSmile crowns, compared to preveneered crowns.24

The study's design has limitations in replicating oral cavity conditions, such as the absence of saliva, toothbrushing, and varied exposures. Using only one type of staining liquid limits understanding of the effect of different brands of beverages on crowns' color stability. Complete immersion and 500 thermal cycles may not accurately represent long-term temperature fluctuations and partial exposure in clinical restorations.

Conclusion

Within the limitations of the study, the conclusions that can be drawn from the study are:

Carbonated and noncarbonated beverages have a significant effect on the color stability of both the Kids-e-Bioflx and Kids-e-crowns.

Carbonated and noncarbonated beverages have a significant effect on the color stability of both the Kids-e-Bioflx and Kids-e-crowns.

Kids-e-crowns were found to be more color stable than Kids-e-Bioflx crowns.

Kids-e-crowns were found to be more color stable than Kids-e-Bioflx crowns.

Postimmersion, Kids-e-Bioflx showed improved color, and Kids-e-crown showed color deterioration.

Postimmersion, Kids-e-Bioflx showed improved color, and Kids-e-crown showed color deterioration.

The fracture resistance of Kids-e-Bioflx was found to be much greater than that of Kids-e-crown after immersion in beverages, and the highest strength was shown by Kids-e-Bioflx when immersed in a noncarbonated beverage.

Clinical Significance

This study provides valuable insights into the performance of Kids-e-Bioflx and Kids-e-crowns under exposure to commonly consumed beverages, thereby assisting clinicians in selecting crowns tailored to individual patient needs while balancing esthetics, functional durability, and long-term success in pediatric restorative treatments.

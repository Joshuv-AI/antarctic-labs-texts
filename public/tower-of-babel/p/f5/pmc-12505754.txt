Association of epicardial fat volume index with coronary artery disease phenotypes based on anatomical and functional imaging: a linkage with the severity of coronary artery disease
Authors: Chunbo Wang, Wenji Yu, Xiaoyu Yang, Fan Sun, Yu Tian, Yue Yue, Feifei Zhang, Xiaoliang Shao, Yunmei Shi, Bao Liu, Jianfeng Wang, Sijin Li, Yuetao Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEpicardial adipose tissue (EAT) plays an important role in the pathogenesis of coronary artery disease (CAD). The association between EAT and obstructive CAD or myocardial ischemia has been established, but its relationship with CAD phenotypes based on anatomical and functional imaging remains unclear.MethodsA total of 495 suspected CAD patients who underwent both single-photon emission computed tomography/computed tomography myocardial perfusion imaging (SPECT/CT MPI) and coronary angiography (CAG/CTA) were enrolled in this retrospective study. Epicardial fat volume (EFV) and epicardial fat volume indexed to body surface (EFVi) were measured on non-contrast CT. CAD phenotypes were categorized into 4 groups based on the presence or absence of obstructive CAD (any epicardial coronary diameter stenosis ≥ 50% by CAG/CTA) and myocardial ischemia (diagnosed by MPI): Group1 (non-obstructive CAD without ischemia, n = 165), Group2 (ischemia with non-obstructive CAD, INOCA, n = 69), Group3 (obstructive CAD without ischemia, n = 149), Group4 (obstructive CAD with ischemia, n = 112).ResultsBoth EFV and EFVi had an increasing trend across 4 groups [EFVi: median (interquartile range), cm3/m2: 80.54 (68.10-102.37) vs. 84.44 (73.57-100.93) vs. 89.63 (75.39-103.15) vs. 91.67 (76.48-111.66), p = 0.007, p for trend < 0.001]. In adjusted ordered logistic regression model, EFVi was independently associated with more advanced CAD phenotype levels (per SD unit change: OR = 1.26, 95% CI:1.03–1.55, p = 0.024). Subgroup analysis showed diabetes subgroup had the strongest correlation between EFVi and CAD phenotype levels in ordered logistic regression model (OR = 3.45, 95%CI:1.52–7.82, p = 0.003). In adjusted unordered multinomial logistic regression model, with Group1 as reference group, maximizing Youden index method was used to find the optimal cutoff values for EFV/EFVi on Group2 to Group4. Both EFV and EFVi were independently associated with Group3 and Group4 but only EFVi was independently associated with INOCA (EFV: EFV >134.47cm3 for INOCA, OR = 1.94, 95%CI:0.97–3.88, p = 0.058, EFVi: EFVi >80.67cm3/m2 for INOCA, OR = 2.53, 95%CI:1.25–5.12, p = 0.010). Net reclassification improvement (NRI) showed that EFVi was more effective than EFV in diabetes subgroup to differentiate CAD phenotypes over traditional cardiovascular risk factors.ConclusionEFVi was correlated with the severity of CAD phenotype levels based on anatomical and functional imaging. EFVi had the strongest correlation with CAD phenotypes levels in diabetes subgroup. Notably, EFVi rather than EFV exhibits a distinct linkage with INOCA. EFVi was more effective to provide incremental value of differentiating CAD phenotypes over traditional cardiovascular risk factors than EFV in diabetes subgroup.Graphical Abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12933-025-02897-0.Keywords: Coronary artery disease, Coronary artery disease phenotype, Myocardial ischemia, Obstructive coronary artery disease, Epicardial adipose tissue

Introduction

Coronary artery disease (CAD) remains a leading cause of mortality and economic burden worldwide [1]. 2024 ESC guideline defines chronic coronary syndrome (CCS) as presentations or syndromes caused by structural and/or functional alterations related to chronic diseases of the coronary arteries and/or microcirculation, resulting in reversible and inducible myocardial ischemia [2]. A recently proposed new concept of CAD phenotypes based on anatomy and function deserves attention, as this consensus rebrands CAD as myocardial ischemia syndrome and further classifies it into two types in non-acute ischemia syndrome: myocardial ischemia caused by epicardial coronary artery stenosis and ischemia with non-obstructive coronary arteries (INOCA) [3]. Traditionally, native epicardial coronary artery stenosis ≥ 50% by coronary angiography has been considered as the gold standard for diagnosing CAD. However, this traditional definition based on stenosis fails to accurately identify high-risk patients in CAD. Studies indicate that INOCA has similar outcomes and prognosis with obstructive CAD, and obstructive CAD with myocardial ischemia has the worst prognosis. Myocardial ischemia has incremental prognostic value beyond coronary stenosis [4–6]. Revascularization guided by myocardial ischemia improves patient prognosis, and the prognosis of revascularization guided by myocardial ischemia and coronary artery anatomy is significantly better than that guided by coronary artery anatomy alone [7, 8]. Recent study demonstrates that CAD progression follows a distinct tracery from non-obstructive to obstructive CAD [9]. Coronary microvascular dysfunction (CMD) is a critical predictor of epicardial coronary artery disease progression [10] and is a primary mechanism underlying myocardial ischemia in patients without significant epicardial stenosis [11]. The probability of myocardial ischemia defined by fraction flow reserve (FFR) less than 0.8 is gradually increased with the severity of epicardial coronary artery stenosis [12]. Thus, these findings [9–13] support the hypothesis of a sequential CAD progression model, evolving from non-obstructive CAD without myocardial ischemia, INOCA and obstructive CAD without ischemia to obstructive CAD with myocardial ischemia.

Epicardial adipose tissue (EAT) is a unique group of fat depot distributed between epicardium and the visceral pericardium, with no fascial separation from the myocardium, sharing microcirculation with the coronary arteries. Under normal conditions, EAT provides protective effects on myocardium by supplying fatty acids and buffering excess energy-induced damage. Research studies have found that increased EAT is closely associated with CAD, atrial fibrillation, and heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) [14, 15]. SPECT MPI is currently the most mature, widely adopted, and evidence-based imaging modality for noninvasive assessment of myocardial ischemia [16]. One-stop SPECT/CT can measure calcium scores and EAT volume by gated cardiac CT. Our previous studies have proven that EAT volume is associated with obstructive CAD and myocardial ischemia [17, 18]. Nappi et al. [19] found that EAT volume is associated with CMD. CMD is an important phenotype of INOCA [11].However, Abusnina et al. [20] found that Epicardial fat volume index (EFVi) which is defined as EAT volume indexed to body surface area (BSA) correlates with CMD while EAT volume not, suggesting that EFVi may be better to identify CMD and INOCA.

Given the association of EAT with obstructive coronary artery disease and myocardial ischemia, and its involvement in the pathogenesis of coronary microvascular dysfunction and plaque formation [14], we hypothesize that EFVi gradually increases in CAD phenotypes defined by anatomical and functional imaging. This study aims to investigate differences and associations between EFVi and CAD phenotypes, as classified by anatomical and functional imaging.

Method

Study design and population

This study is a retrospective single-center study. Patients with suspected CAD presented with chest discomfort who underwent invasive coronary angiography (CAG) or uninvasive coronary computed tomographic angiography (CTA), along with stress/rest SPECT/CT MPI were consecutively enrolled from January 2014 to December 2024 at the Third Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University. The time interval between CAG/CTA and stress/rest SPECT/CT MPI did not exceed 3 months (90 days). Coronary artery anatomy and lesions were assessed by CAG or CTA. All patients were hospitalized at the Third Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University during the period of stress/rest SPECT/CT MPI. Exclusion criteria included: (1) acute coronary syndrome (including STEMI, NSTEMI, UA); (2) Previous myocardial infarction and previous revascularization history (PCI, CABG); (3) Valvular heart disease, heart failure, cardiomyopathy; (4) Active malignant tumors; (5) Acute or chronic inflammatory disease; (6) Incomplete clinical data, image information, or poor image quality unsuitable for diagnostic purposes. The patient study flowchart was shown in Fig. 1. All participants were exempt from informed consent. This study followed the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the ethics committee of the Third Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University.

Flowchart of the study population. CAD = coronary artery disease, SPECT/CT = Single Photon Emission Computed Tomography/Computed Tomography, MPI = myocardial perfusion imaging, CAG = invasive coronary angiography, CTA = coronary computed tomography angiography

Detailed demographic data, cardiovascular risk factors, medication usage, hematological parameters and echocardiography parameters were collected from medical records. Prescription medications prescribed by the attending physician at the time of patient discharge were collected. Dyslipidemia was defined as triglycerides (TG) ≥ 2.3 mmol/L, total cholesterol (TC) ≥ 6.2 mmol/L, low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) ≥ 4.1 mmol/L, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) < 1.0 mmol/L or lipid lowering drugs usage currently or previously [21]. Hypertension and diabetes were determined based on the patient’s past medical history or clinical diagnosis. Body surface area (BSA) was calculated by Stevenson formula: BSA(m2) = 0.0061 × height(cm) + 0.0128 × weight(kg) − 0.1529. At the patient level, if CAG showed native epicardial coronary artery diameter stenosis ≥ 50%, such patients were defined as obstructive CAD; Otherwise, patients were defined as non-obstructive CAD. Similar judgement definition of obstructive CAD and non-obstructive CAD was applicable for CTA.

Stress test and SPECT/CT MPI image acquisition

The SPECT/CT MPI image acquisition were performed by Siemens Symbia T16 dual-head SPECT/CT system from Germany. 99mTc-methoxyisobutylisonitrile (MIBI) was provided by Shanghai Xinke Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., with radiochemical purity >95% and an injection dose of 740-925MBq. Patients discontinued beta-blockers, nitrates, and other medications that affect heart rate or coronary artery dilation prior to the test for 24 h. All patients underwent 2 day protocol and rest + exercise or Adenosine/Regadenoson pharmacologic stress MPI, following specific guidelines [22]. Patients chosen pharmacologic stress MPI if they could not tolerate exercise stress MPI. Acquisition conditions for rest and stress MPI: parallel-hole low-energy high-resolution collimator, matrix size 128 × 128, magnification factor 1.45, dual heads positioned at 90°, each rotating 90° for a total of 180° acquisition, 6° per frame, with each frame lasting 35 s. Electrocardiogram R-wave triggering gated circuitry was used to acquire 8 frames per cardiac cycle. Images were reconstructed using 3D Flash iterative method (with 16 iterations and 2 subsets), resulting in three-dimensional axial images of the left ventricle (short axis, horizontal long axis, and vertical long axis).

Image interpretation and analysis

Myocardial ischemia

The left ventricle is divided into 17-segment model to define the territories supplied by LAD, LCX, and RCA according to American Heart Association [23]. Two nuclear medicine physicians perform visual assessments. The definition of reversible myocardial perfusion defects is that at least three consecutive frames and two slices showing reversible perfusion defects in coronary-supplied regions during stress/rest MPI (with stress images showing perfusion defects and rest images showing partial or complete filling of these defect areas). In cases where diaphragmatic attenuation affects inferior wall imaging or breast attenuation affects anterior wall imaging, SPECT/CT attenuation-corrected images and cedars-QGS software-generated wall motion are analyzed together. The cedars-QPS software reconstructs the short axis images into polar maps, automatically scoring each myocardial segment on a 5-point scale (0 = normal, 1 = mild abnormality, 2 = moderate abnormality, 3 = severe abnormality, 4 = no perfusion). The summed stress score (SSS), summed rest score (SRS), and summed difference score (SDS = SSS - SRS) are evaluated. Myocardial ischemia is defined as reversible myocardial perfusion defects and/or SDS ≥ 2 [18, 24]. SDS ≥ 2 was screened as an aided diagnosis criterion of myocardial ischemia. If there is a discrepancy between visual assessment and SDS, visual assessment prevails.

Measurement of epicardial fat volume index and coronary artery calcium score

A low-dose cardiac CT scan for Epicardial fat volume (EFV) and coronary artery calcium score (CACS) was followed by tomographic image acquisition for rest SPECT/CT MPI. The low-dose cardiac CT scan parameter is below: cycle, 60%-80% RR; tube voltage, 130 kV; tube current, 100 mA; slice thickness, 3 mm. The scan range extends from the tracheal bifurcation plane to 1–2 cm below the diaphragm. EAT was defined as any adipose tissue observed between the visceral pericardium and the epicardium with voxel densities ranging from -190 to -30 HU. Specifically, the first axis plane which located the pulmonary artery bifurcation within the aorta-pulmonary artery window, defined the upper limit. And the last slice containing the apex of the heart defined the lower limit. Then, the pericardial contour was manually traced between these axis slices. EFV and mean epicardial fat density (MEAN density) were automatically calculated by software (Syngo Volume, Siemens Medical Solutions). EFVi was defined as the ratio of EFV to body surface area (BSA) [EFVi = EFV (cm3) ÷ BSA(m2)] [20]. Coronary artery calcium (CAC) was defined as dense areas within the coronary arteries exceeding 130 HU. CACS was defined as the sum of calcium scores for each coronary artery calculated by Agatston automatic analysis software [25]. The two observers intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC-interobserver) of EFV measurement was 0.928 (95%CI: 0.915–0.940; P < 0.001).

Definition of coronary artery disease phenotypes

Coronary artery disease phenotypes were defined based on the presence or absence of obstructive stenosis in native epicardial coronary arteries and myocardial ischemia. Obstructive CAD is defined as at least one epicardial coronary artery or its major branches having diameter stenosis ≥ 50% at patient level, while non-obstructive CAD is defined as any epicardial coronary artery or its major branches < 50%. The coronary artery disease phenotypes based on anatomical and functional imaging were classified as follows: Group1, non-obstructive CAD without ischemia; Group2, ischemia with non-obstructive CAD/non-obstructive CAD with ischemia (INOCA); Group3, obstructive CAD without ischemia; Group4, obstructive CAD with ischemia [4, 5].

Statistical analysis

Normally distributed variables were presented as mean ± SD and compared by independent t-tests for two groups or ANOVA with post-hoc corrected pairwise t-tests for multiple groups (FDR methods). Skewed variables were presented as median (Q1 - Q3) and compared via Mann-Whitney U test for two group or Kruskal-Wallis tests with post-hoc corrected pairwise Wilcox test for multiple groups (FDR methods). Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies (%) and compared by chi-square tests, with continuity-corrected or Fisher’s exact tests when needed. Trend analyses were done by Jonckheere Terpstra test for continuous variables and Cochran Armitage tests for binary variables. Log - transformed CACS was defined due to its obvious skewed distribution: logCACS = ln (CACS + 1). Bootstrap resampling estimated 95% confidence interval for medians by 5000 times across 4 groups. Spearman liner regression quantified the correlations between EFVi and other continuous variables. Both ordered logistic regression model and unordered multinomial logistic regression model were used to explore the association of EFV/EFVi with CAD phenotypes. For ordered logistic regression, CAD phenotype was screened as the severity of CAD, which was identified as an ordinal outcome. Proportional odds assumptions were verified by the Brant test, and both EFV and EFVi on CAD phenotypes didn’t violate the brant test (brant p value >0.05). Partial proportional odds models were substituted when some covariables violations were detected. The optimal EFVi cutoff value was determined by minimizing the p-value in univariate ordered logistic regression. This threshold was then dichotomized for multivariate model analysis and subgroup analysis. For unordered multinomial logistic regression, non-obstructive CAD without ischemia was the reference group. Three EFVi cutoff values were derived by maximizing Youden indices through ROC curve analysis (pROC package), enabling phenotype-specific odds ratio calculations. Each CAD phenotype group was assigned a unique cutoff value to optimize diagnostic accuracy, and these thresholds were applied in subsequent multivariable unordered multinomial regression to extract adjusted odds ratios with 95% confidence intervals. The NRI (net reclassification improvement) and AUC (area under ROC curve) were used to find the incremental value of EFV/EFVi over traditional cardiovascular risk factors to differentiate CAD phenotypes (Supplementary method). All missing covariables were assumed to be completely randomly missing and were imputed by multiple chained equations (mice package, m = 5) for those missing less than 20%. All analyses were conducted by R 4.3.2 and RStudio software (http://www.R-project.org; https://posit.co/download/rstudio-desktop/). Function R “polr”, “vglm” were used to odds proportion model or partial odds proportion model. Function R “multinom” was used to unordered multinomial logistic regress model. Two tailed tests were used, and p-values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline clinical characteristics of the study population

In our study, a total of 495 patients were included. The median age of the study population was 63 years (IQR: 56–70 years), with 57% male. Patients underwent exercise stress (79%) or pharmacologic stress with adenosine/regadenoson (21%). Coronary anatomy stenosis was assessed by CAG (77%) or CTA (23%). In 495 suspected CAD patients, 165 (33%) had non-obstructive CAD without ischemia (Group1), 69 (14%) had ischemia with non-obstructive CAD (Group2), 149 (30%) had obstructive CAD without ischemia (Group3), and 112 (23%) had obstructive CAD with ischemia (Group4). The proportion of males and diabetes increased progressively from Group1 to Group4 (male: 46% vs. 51% vs. 65% vs. 68%, diabetes: 16% vs. 20% vs. 29% vs. 34%, both p for trend < 0.001). Hypertension prevalence was highest in patients with Group4, while it was comparable among other three groups (58% vs. 59% vs. 56% vs. 77%, p = 0.003). There were no significant statistical differences in the distribution of hyperlipidemia across four groups. Medication usage, including antiplatelet agents, statins, and nitrates, showed a gradual increase trend from Group1 to Group4 (all p for trend < 0.001). All revascularization until discharge occurred in obstructive CAD, especially in obstructive CAD with ischemia (Table 1). In terms of hematological indicators and echocardiography parameters, fibrinogen concentration (FIB) showed a gradual increase trend from Group1 to Group4 (p for trend < 0.001). C-reactive protein (CRP) and left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) did not show significant differences or trends across four groups (Table S1).

Association of epicardial fat volume index and coronary artery disease phenotypes

In ordered logistic regression model, cutoff value was determined by univariate ordered logistic regression model between EFVi and CAD phenotypes with the minimum p value. In unadjusted ordered logistic regression model, for every SD (26.26) cm3/m2 unit change in EFVi, the probability of CAD phenotype transitioning to higher level increased by 1.35 times (OR = 1.35, 95%CI: 1.15–1.60, p < 0.001, Table 2). For 96.68cm3/m2 for the binary cutoff threshold, higher EFVi increased the probability of CAD phenotype transitioning to higher level by 1.82 times compared to lower EFVi (OR = 1.82, 95%CI: 1.30–2.54, p < 0.001, Table 2). After adjusting for age, sex, BMI, log transformed CACS, LVEF and the traditional cardiovascular risk factors, EFVi showed independent association of CAD phenotypes except for binary form which was borderline statistically associated with CAD phenotypes [EFVi (per SD), OR = 1.26, 95%CI: 1.03–1.55, p = 0.024; EFVi (EFVi >96.68), OR = 1.46, 95%CI: 0.97–2.15, p = 0.059, Table 2]. EFV showed independent association of CAD phenotypes both in continuous and binary forms after adjusting for age, sex, BMI, log transformed CACS, LVEF and the traditional cardiovascular risk factors [EFV (per SD), OR = 1.25, 95%CI: 1.01–1.54, p = 0.044; EFV (EFV >152.25), OR = 1.49, 95%CI: 1.02–2.10, p = 0.041, Table S2].

Subgroup analysis

Subgroup analysis found that the association of EFVi with CAD phenotype levels was more prominent in normal weight (BMI < 25) and diabetes subgroups, particularly in diabetes subgroup in ordered logistic regression model (Fig. 3). The proportional odds ratio was 1.99 in normal weight subgroup (OR = 1.99, 95%CI: 1.02–3.86, p = 0.043) and 3.45 in diabetes subgroup (OR = 3.45, 95%CI: 1.52–7.82, p = 0.003). Interaction analysis showed that EFVi was independent of age, sex, BMI, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, smoke and coronary artery calcium score (CACS >0) on CAD phenotype levels. The interaction between diabetes and EFVi on CAD phenotype levels showed a trend interaction effect (p for interaction = 0.067, Fig. 3).

Discussion

In this real-world study, our primary findings include the following: First, both EFV and EFVi gradually increased with the severity of CAD phenotypes based on anatomical and functional imaging and remained independently associated with CAD phenotype levels after adjusting for confounding factors. Second, subgroup analysis showed the association between EFVi and CAD phenotypes levels was more prominent in normal weight and diabetic subgroups, especially in diabetic subgroup. Third, EFVi rather than EFV, was associated with INOCA. Forth, EFV and EFVi could provide the incremental value of differentiating specific CAD phenotypes of non-obstructive CAD without ischemia and obstructive CAD with ischemia over traditional cardiovascular risk factors. Fifth, EFVi was more effective than EFV to provide incremental value over traditional cardiovascular risk factors to differentiate CAD phenotypes in diabetes subgroup.

Association of EFV and EFVi with CAD phenotype levels

We discovered that both EFV and EFVi gradually increased with the severity of CAD phenotypes and were independently associated with CAD phenotype levels based on anatomical and functional imaging. This finding extends our previous studies [17, 18, 26]. Possible mechanisms may include inflammation, oxidative stress, lipid infiltration, and endothelial dysfunction. Epicardial adipose tissue exerts paracrine and endocrine effects by secreting various inflammatory factors and adipokines, which damage adjacent myocardium and coronary arteries, closely related to coronary plaque accumulation and microcirculatory dysfunction, playing a significant role in the occurrence and development of CAD and myocardial ischemia [14]. Epicardial adipose tissue is associated with the extent and severity of coronary artery disease. Studies by Okada et al. [27] confirmed that EFV rather than visceral fat area gradually increased with the number of obstructive coronary arteries levels in non-obese with coronary artery disease populations. Similarity, higher EFV was also associated with more severe SYNTAX score in obstructive coronary artery disease both in diabetic and non-diabetic people [28]. Recently, a case-control study enrolling premature coronary artery disease and matched controls (age less than 55 years) found that both epicardial fat volume and volume indexed to body surface area were associated with more server CAD defined by the severity of CAD-RADS levels [29]. Meanwhile, epicardial fat volume were also associated with the extent of myocardial ischemia. Studies by Janik et al. [30] confirmed that EFV gradually increased and associated with the extent of myocardial ischemia detected by PET MPI in non-obstructive coronary artery disease populations. However, their sample size was too small to do some subgroup analysis, and their population did not have coronary anatomy information, resulting impossible to know myocardial ischemia caused by CMD or epicardial coronary stenosis. These studies [27–30] emphasized the evaluation of coronary artery anatomical complexity or myocardial ischemia severity alone, which were single imaging modality for CAD evaluation. Our study addressed these gaps, showing that both EFV and EFVi were associated with the severity of CAD phenotype levels defined by anatomical and functional imaging. And our study confirmed that EFV in myocardial ischemia caused by CMD was lower than myocardial ischemia caused by epicardial coronary stenosis (post pairwise hoc analysis between Group2 and Group4: Group2 vs. Group4, 143.10 cm3 vs. 161.76 cm3, p = 0.041). Both EFV and EFVi had the incremental value to differentiate specific CAD phenotypes which were the slightest and the worst CAD phenotypes over traditional cardiovascular risk factors. And our study was consistent with Nakazato et al. [31] who confirmed that EFVi had incremental value in differentiating obstructive CAD with ischemia over traditional risk factors and high CACS. Meanwhile, we broaden the insights of EFV/EFVi in overall CAD phenotypes from non-obstructive CAD without ischemia to obstructive CAD with ischemia and found that in diabetes subgroup, EFVi was more effective to provide incremental value of differentiating CAD phenotypes than EFV over traditional cardiovascular risk factors. EFV and EFVi had potential value to become non-invasive imaging biomarkers for CAD phenotypes based on anatomical and functional imaging.

Subgroup analysis in EFVi association with CAD phenotype levels

We observed a stronger correlation between EFVi and CAD phenotype levels in normal weight or diabetic subgroups, particularly in diabetic subgroups which proportional odds ratio reached to 3.45. A prospective cohort study by Wang et al. [32] found that among obese phenotypes stratified by BMI/waist circumference based on PET MPI in non-obstructive coronary arteries populations, individuals with normal weight and abdominal obesity had the lowest myocardial blood flow (MBF) and myocardial blood flow reserve (MFR), suggesting the highest probability of CMD in this phenotypic obesity group. Epicardial fat tissue, as a special ectopic visceral fat, is adjacent to myocardium and coronary arteries and serves as a marker of visceral fat obesity [33]. Recently a bio-bank research with large size cohort study indicated that epicardial adipose tissue was associated with new-onset coronary artery disease but the correlation no-longer existed after adjusting for abdominal visceral adipose tissue, suggesting that epicardial adipose tissue might be a kind of visceral fat [34]. When energy storage exceeds capacity, energy storage shifts from subcutaneous fat to visceral fat. In normal-weight individuals, increased epicardial fat indicates preferential visceral fat deposition. And this population typically manifests as metabolically unhealthy normal weight (MUNW), characterized by higher insulin resistance and cardiovascular risk events [35, 36]. Epicardial fat may partly explain why these patients are more prone to insulin resistance, metabolic syndrome and higher cardiovascular events. However, other studies [37] suggested that EAT phenotypes in obese patients shifted towards a pro-inflammatory state compared to normal-weight patients, but these studies primarily stratified obesity phenotypes based on BMI without considering visceral fat. In diabetic patients, hyperglycemia exacerbates oxidative stress on coronary endothelium through pathways such as advanced glycation end products, promoting coronary microvascular dysfunction and plaque accumulation, leading to myocardial ischemia and coronary plaque obstruction [14]. EAT in diabetics patients shift towards a more pro-inflammatory and hypertrophic phenotype, exhibiting low grade inflammation and earlier conversion to ‘white’ fat [14, 37]. Our study results showed a trend interaction between EFVi and diabetes on CAD phenotype levels (p for interaction = 0.067), suggesting that diabetes may promote CAD development by altering the epicardial fat phenotype [37]. A meta-analysis which contained baseline and follow up EAT measurement by ultrasound, CT or MRI concluded that both SGLT-2 inhibitors and GLP-1 receptor agonists could reduce EAT volume and thickness [38]. Several clinical randomized controlled trials indicated that SGLT-2 inhibitors and GLP-1 receptor agonists could reduce EAT volume and metabolic activity on PET/CT in diabetic populations, suggesting EAT as a potential therapeutic target [39, 40]. Therefore, our study showed the strongest correlation between EFVi and CAD phenotype levels in diabetic patients with suspected coronary artery disease, providing theoretical basis for precise intervention in targeting epicardial fat in this population.

Association of EFV and EFVi with INOCA

Our study demonstrated that EFVi rather than EFV was associated with INOCA. CMD is an important phenotype of INOCA [11]. Abusnina et al. [20] reported that in patients with non-obstructive coronary arteries, EFVi rather than EFV correlated with CMD as defined by invasive coronary interventions in CMDR (Coronary Microvascular Disease Registry) prospective cohort study. Our findings were not fully equivalent to Abusnina et al. [20], although their results were not adjusted for confounding factors and may have been confounded by traditional cardiovascular risk factors, which we suggest that EFV indexed to body surface area can characterize INOCA in suspected CAD. Our results extend the usage of EFVi to characterize INOCA in suspected CAD populations. Zamani et al. [41] found that epicardial fat area measured by magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) was not associated with coronary vascular dysfunction in suspected INOCA women patients. Therefore, further standardization of EAT volume indexed to BSA is necessary to characterize CMD and INOCA. EAT secretes various inflammatory cytokines and adipokines, which impair coronary microvascular function and induce myocardial ischemia. Thus, early intervention in targeting epicardial fat may help to delay the onset of CMD in cardiovascular high-risk patients. An increase in EFVi reflects pathological expansion of epicardial adipose tissue, which secretes a greater variety of inflammatory factors and cytokines. This downregulates adiponectin expression, induces insulin resistance in the surrounding myocardium and coronary arteries, exacerbates local oxidative stress, and further contributes to plaque accumulation and coronary microcirculatory dysfunction [14]. However, the mechanisms underlying the pathological expansion of EAT are still unknown and require further investigation.

Limitations

The study has the following limitations: First, it is a retrospective cross-sectional study. An association relationship rather than a causal relationship is established between epicardial adipose tissue and coronary artery disease phenotypes. Although the association is established between EFVi and CAD phenotypes, the differentiating performance of EFVi plus traditional cardiovascular risk factors to CAD phenotypes is still not high. Future research should develop radiomics of EAT and more advanced machine learning models to differentiate CAD phenotypes. Meanwhile, longitudinal studies including whether EFVi is correlated with major adverse events (MACE) and whether EFVi has the incremental potential value to reclassify high risk patients beyond traditional cardiovascular risk factors and imaging modalities such as stenosis severity, CACS and ischemia burden are needed to be elucidated. The longitudinal changes of EFVi should also be investigated through entire CAD process by animal models and population-based cohort study. However, a prospective cohort study by Hajsadeghi et al. [42] suggested that EFVi was associated with the severity of CAD defined by coronary CTA from zero-obstructive stenosis, non-obstructive CAD to obstructive CAD and independently associated with MACE. They also suggested that EFVi could provide incremental value of MACE over Framingham risk score and coronary artery stenosis severity. Thus, this finding suggested EFVi as cardiovascular risk biomarkers. Second, SPECT MPI is used for semi-quantitative assessment of myocardial ischemia in our study, which may underestimate INOCA and CMD. Further studies should utilize PET MFR to define CMD. Third, waist circumference and visceral fat area, indicators of central obesity, are not included, making our study impossible to analyze the normal BMI combined with central obesity subgroup in detailed analysis. Fourth, while the study finds the strongest correlation between EFVi and CAD phenotypes in the diabetic subgroup, it cannot verify prognostic value. And the study result of interaction between EFVi and diabetes on CAD phenotypes is also exploratory analysis. Further studies containing larger diabetes subgroup are needed to investigate this finding. Fifth, the use of both CAG (77%) and CTA (23%) for stenosis assessment introduces heterogeneity into our study due to their different diagnostic accuracy for obstructive CAD. CAG is the gold standard for coronary stenosis assessment. But coronary CTA is difficult to evaluate the coronary stenosis severity when the coronary arteries is heavily calcified and diffused diseased for imaging artifacts. This could be a source of bias in our study. However, the CAG is accountable for most of our assessment proportions. A meta-analysis contained more than 5000 patients from 65 prospective studies concluded that CTA was robust and reliable to diagnose obstructive CAD especially for its negative predictive value when referenced to CAG [43]. Meanwhile, only 4 patients in extremely severe coronary calcified populations (CACS >1000) are accessed coronary anatomy stenosis by CTA in our study. Sixth, the research findings of this article are only applicable to suspected coronary heart disease populations among Chinese individuals. Extrapolation to other ethnic groups or populations with different diseases is limited. Future longitudinal studies or clinical trials are needed to validate whether early intervention in epicardial adipose tissue in suspected coronary artery disease with diabetic populations can yield maximum benefits effect.

Conclusions

EFVi was independently associated with CAD phenotypes defined by anatomical and functional imaging. Higher EFVi was associated with more advanced CAD phenotypes. In diabetics with coronary artery disease, the correlation between EFVi and CAD phenotype levels was strongest. EFVi was more effective to provide incremental value of differentiating CAD phenotypes over traditional cardiovascular risk factors than EFV in diabetes subgroup. Future large-scale prospective longitudinal studies or clinical trials are needed to validate these findings of this study.

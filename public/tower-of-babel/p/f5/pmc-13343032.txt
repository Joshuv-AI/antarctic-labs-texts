Pharmacologic activity, safety, and preliminary efficacy of GEN3009, a CD37-targeting DuoHexaBody, in relapsed or refractory B-cell non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma
Authors: Farrukh T Awan, Joshua T Gamse, Marije B Overdijk, Esther C W Breij, Maria Jure-Kunkel, Kinjal Sanghavi, Lauren K Brady, Meijian Guan, Inge Verbrugge, Jenny Chen, Nian Gong, Michael Gillespie, Michael Tees, Molly Gallogly, Martin Hutchings, Jacob Haaber Christensen, Anna Sureda, Raul Cordoba, Sairah Ahmed, F J Sherida H Woei-A-Jin, Pau Abrisqueta, Eva Giné, Michael Roost Clausen, Seema A Bhat, Ajay K Gopal, Martine E D Chamuleau
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTetraspanin CD37, highly expressed in mature B-cells, represents an opportunity for therapeutic targeting in B-cell malignancies. GEN3009 (DuoHexaBody-CD37), a humanized biparatopic IgG1 antibody with an E430G hexamerization-enhancing mutation targeting two non-overlapping CD37 epitopes, was shown to induce potent tumor cell killing through enhanced complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC) and other fragment crystallizable-mediated effector functions, including antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) and antibody-dependent cellular phagocytosis (ADCP), in vitro and in vivo. GEN3009 was assessed in non-clinical studies and a phase 1 dose-escalation study of B-cell non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma (B-NHL).MethodsAfter a non-clinical toxicity study was conducted in cynomolgus monkeys, a phase 1, first-in-human, open-label, multicenter dose-escalation trial of GEN3009 monotherapy enrolled adults with relapsed/refractory (R/R) B-NHL (NCT04358458). A modified Bayesian optimal interval design informed dose escalation and de-escalation with dose levels ranging from 6 mg to 2000 mg. Primary endpoints were the rate of dose-limiting toxicities (DLTs) to determine the recommended phase 2 dose (RP2D) and safety and tolerability.ResultsIn the toxicity study, 10 mg/kg GEN3009 weekly was identified as the highest non-severely toxic dose. In the phase 1 study, from March 13, 2020, to July 28, 2023, 46 patients with R/R B-NHL received intravenous GEN3009 infusions. Median duration of treatment was 1.2 months (range, 0.0–14.5). No DLTs were observed up to 1600 mg. The most common treatment-emergent adverse events were neutropenia (n=41 (89.1%)), infusion-related reactions (n=39 (84.8%)), and thrombocytopenia (n=18 (39.1%)). Plasma GEN3009 concentrations increased over time with increasing GEN3009 doses, with no apparent accumulation of GEN3009 observed over the course of treatment. Antitumor activity was observed at dose levels of ≥180 mg in both aggressive and indolent NHL. GEN3009 treatment reduced total hemolytic complement activity (CH50) levels in serum, and peak changes in CH50 levels were significantly correlated with clinical response (p=0.008 by Wilcoxon rank-sum test).ConclusionsBased on safety, efficacy, and pharmacokinetics, the RP2D of GEN3009 was determined to be 1200 mg. Preliminary data suggest that GEN3009 monotherapy demonstrated an acceptable safety profile at the RP2D of 1200 mg, with modest clinical activity. These findings provide the first clinical proof-of-concept for hexamerization-potentiated molecules that induce antitumor activity through enhanced CDC.

Introduction

Despite advances in treatment for B-cell malignancies, including non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma (NHL) and chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL), there is no cure for most relapsed or refractory (R/R) B-cell malignancies.13 Regimens consisting of chemotherapy and anti-CD20 immunotherapies such as rituximab, ofatumumab, and obinutuzumab have improved the clinical course of B-cell malignancies over the past 20 years.2 3 Additionally, targeted therapies such as Bruton’s tyrosine kinase inhibitors and BCL-2 inhibitors revolutionized the treatment landscape for CLL.4 More recently, T-cell engager therapies, including CAR T-cell therapies and CD3xCD20 bispecific antibodies, have greatly improved treatment options for B-cell malignancies.5 6 Unfortunately, many patients experience relapse or resistance to existing therapies, and there is a need for novel agents with alternative therapeutic targets and/or effector mechanisms.2 7 8

Tetraspanin CD37 is a transmembrane protein that is predominantly expressed by mature B-cells.9 10 High expression is preserved in B-cell malignancies including NHL and CLL, and expression in other hematopoietic cell types is minimal, providing rationale for the exploration of novel therapeutic opportunities for B-cell malignancies by selectively targeting CD37.91113 Several CD37-directed agents have been investigated in preclinical studies, demonstrating antitumor activity in B-cell lymphoma cell lines, xenograft tumor models, and primary CLL cell lines.11 14 However, single-agent efficacy of CD37-targeting agents has been relatively modest in phase 1 clinical studies of B-cell malignancies, limiting the development of CD37-targeting agents as monotherapy.1517 This is due, in part, to their limited capacity to induce complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC).11 13 18 19

CDC is thought to be an important mechanism of tumor cell killing for several therapeutic monoclonal antibody agents, including daratumumab and ofatumumab.2023 However, the ability to promote complement activation, which depends on fragment crystallizable (Fc)-mediated hexamerization of membrane-bound antibodies that provide optimal docking for hexavalent C1q, varies among monoclonal antibodies currently in use for the treatment of B-cell malignancies.20 Designing monoclonal antibodies to enhance CDC represents a promising approach to the development of novel therapeutic strategies for B-cell malignancies.18

GEN3009 (DuoHexaBody-CD37) is a humanized biparatopic IgG1 antibody that targets CD37 by way of two nonoverlapping epitopes on CD37 receptors and also contains a mutation in the Fc domain that enhances antibody hexamerization on binding to CD37.182426 GEN3009 has been shown to potently activate complement, which is driven by both dual epitope targeting and the enhanced hexamerization.18 25 In preclinical models of B-cell malignancies, GEN3009 demonstrated strong antitumor activity in ex vivo, in vitro, and in vivo models, suggesting potential for therapeutic efficacy.18 24

We report the nonclinical safety evaluation of GEN3009 and results from the first-in-human trial, which aimed to evaluate safety and tolerability of GEN3009 in patients with B-cell NHL and to determine the maximum tolerated dose (MTD) and recommended phase 2 dose (RP2D) of GEN3009. Pharmacokinetics (PK), immunogenicity, and preliminary antitumor activity are also presented, along with an exploratory analysis to identify pharmacodynamic biomarkers associated with clinical response to GEN3009.

Methods

Non-clinical study

An unblinded good laboratory practice (GLP) toxicity study of GEN3009 was conducted at the Covance Preclinical Services, Münster test facility (Münster, Germany) using purpose-bred cynomolgus monkeys (Macaca fascicularis) of Mauritian origin. In order to minimize animal use while ensuring scientific viability, a total of 32 healthy animals were assigned to four dose groups receiving GEN3009 at 0, 3, 10, or 30 mg/kg (5/sex/group for 0 and 30 mg/kg; 3/sex/group for 3 and 10 mg/kg). No formal inclusion or exclusion criteria were defined for animals used in the study. Assignment to dose groups was based on existing social groups and stratified body weights, where possible. At predose, animals weighed 5.6 to 9.7 kg (males) and 3.4 to 6.2 kg (females) and were 2 to 8 years old. Animals were acclimated to study procedures for a period of at least 2 weeks, and a health assessment was performed by a veterinarian before the start of the predose phase to confirm the suitability of every animal for the study. GEN3009 (or NaCl 0.9% for the control group) was administered by intravenous infusion once per week for 5 weeks in a dose volume of 10 mL/kg. Intravenous infusion was chosen for this toxicology study because it is the intended human therapeutic route. At the end of the dosing period, necropsy was conducted for three female and three male monkeys per group; after the 4-week recovery period, necropsy was conducted for two female and two male monkeys per group (0 and 30 mg/kg only). Assessment of toxicity was based on clinical observations, body weights, food consumption, ophthalmology, cardiovascular investigations (ECG and blood pressure), neurobehavioral observations, respiratory rate, and clinical and anatomic pathology evaluations. Immunophenotyping of peripheral blood and mesenteric lymph nodes, cytokine analysis, and complement factor analysis were conducted. Blood for toxicokinetic evaluation was collected at 0 (predose), 0.5, 4, 12, 24, 72, and 120 hours postdose after the first, second, and fourth doses. Blood for antidrug antibody (ADA) determination was collected predose for the second, third, fourth, and fifth doses. All animals were included in toxicity and ADA assessments; analyses were performed using Phoenix WinNonlin V.8.1 or higher (Certara, Princeton, New Jersey, USA). Potential confounders were not controlled in the animal toxicity study.

Phase 1 study design

This phase 1 study was a first-in-human, open-label, multicenter, dose-escalation trial of GEN3009 in patients with R/R B-cell NHL (NCT04358458; see study protocol in online supplemental file 1). The trial was conducted at 15 sites in Belgium, Denmark, the Netherlands, Spain, and the USA (online supplemental table 1). Patients were assigned to receive intravenous infusions of GEN3009 administered as monotherapy at nine dose levels ranging from 6 mg to 2000 mg in 28-day cycles following dose-escalation procedures.

GEN3009 was administered intravenously following one of two dosing schedules. In cycle 1 of schedule 1 (all sites), patients received GEN3009 weekly at dose levels of 6, 20, 60, 180, 400, 800, 1200, 1600, and 2000 mg. In cycle 1 of schedule 2 (USA only), GEN3009 was given twice weekly at the 800 mg dose level. Afterward, in both schedules, GEN3009 was administered weekly in cycles 2 and 3, every 2 weeks in cycles 4 through 9, and every 4 weeks in cycle 10 and thereafter.

Dose escalation consisted of 10 dose levels and followed a modified Bayesian optimal interval (mBOIN) design to optimize recommendations regarding dose escalation or de-escalation following the evaluation of each enrolled cohort (online supplemental figure 1).27 With this design, dose-escalation decisions for the next patient or cohort of patients were based on comparing the observed dose-limiting toxicity (DLT) at the current dose versus prespecified boundaries for dose escalation and de-escalation.27 The target rate for DLT effects was set at 25%, with boundaries of 19.7% for escalation and 29.8% for de-escalation. The first three dose levels were single-patient cohorts, which could be expanded to three-patient cohorts if the patient experienced grade ≥2 toxicity (ie, treatment-related adverse event (AE)) during the DLT evaluation period (28 days) or as determined by the sponsor’s safety committee. For dose levels with three DLT-evaluable patients, the decision regarding dose escalation and de-escalation was consistent with the “3+3” design (ie, three additional patients were enrolled if one DLT was observed, and the dose was de-escalated in the next cohort if two DLTs were observed). If three DLTs were observed, the dose level was terminated. For dose levels with ≥4 DLT-evaluable patients, dose-escalation and de-escalation decisions were based on the rules of the mBOIN design (online supplemental table 2). With the assumptions that ϕ1=0.6ϕ is the highest subtherapeutic DLT rate and ϕ2=1.4ϕ is the lowest DLT rate that has excessive toxicity, the thresholds that minimize the decision error at the end of each cohort are λ1=0.197 and λ2=0.298, respectively. Dose-escalation stopping criteria are presented in the online supplemental methods.

Samples for tumor biopsy, PK, biomarkers/pharmacodynamics, immunogenicity, and tumor lysis syndrome surveillance were collected at screening, predose, and postinfusion as specified in online supplemental table 3.

Phase 1 study population

The study enrolled adults (aged ≥18 years) with histologically or cytologically confirmed R/R B-cell NHL. B-cell NHL subtypes included diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL), high-grade B-cell lymphoma (HGBCL), primary mediastinal large B-cell lymphoma, follicular lymphoma (FL), mantle cell lymphoma (MCL), marginal zone lymphoma (MZL), small lymphocytic lymphoma (SLL), and CLL. Patients had measurable disease for B-cell NHL and CLL, and active disease for CLL (online supplemental table 4). Eligible patients had Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status of 0 or 1.

Patients were excluded if they had prior treatment with a CD37-targeting agent, prior allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT), autologous HSCT within 3 months, prior treatment with an anticancer biologic or an investigational drug or invasive investigational medical device within 4 weeks or 5 half-lives (whichever is shorter) or with a small molecule within 5 half-lives, or chemotherapy or radiation therapy within 2 weeks of the first dose of GEN3009.

Phase 1 study endpoints

The primary endpoints were the rate of DLTs with GEN3009 and safety and tolerability of GEN3009. MTD was defined as the dose level with DLT rate closest to 25%. RP2D selection was based on all data available by the end of dose escalation, including safety, PK, pharmacodynamics, and preliminary efficacy; RP2D could not exceed the MTD.

Secondary endpoints included PK parameters, incidence of neutralizing anti-GEN3009 antibodies, and preliminary antitumor efficacy, including objective response rate (ORR) and complete response (CR) rate. Potential pharmacodynamic biomarkers predictive of clinical response to GEN3009 were assessed as an exploratory endpoint. Assessments included peripheral B-cell count, natural killer (NK) cell count, and percentage change from baseline in total hemolytic complement activity (CH50) in serum. Analyses were conducted in patients with baseline and postbaseline samples. Patients with a peripheral B-cell count of >5 cells/µL at baseline were classified as having detectable B-cell counts at treatment initiation.

Phase 1 study statistical methods

Based on simulations of the mBOIN design,27 a sample size of ≤90 patients was expected to provide adequate information for estimation of the MTD. No formal statistical hypotheses were formulated, and results were summarized descriptively.

The full analysis set (FAS) and safety analysis set (SAS) comprised all patients who received ≥1 dose of GEN3009, analyzed according to actual treatment received. The dose-determining set consisted of all patients who received ≥1 dose of GEN3009 and either received between 80% and 125% of the planned dose and completed the DLT observation period or experienced a DLT during cycle 1. The PK analysis set included all patients who received ≥1 dose of GEN3009 and provided ≥1 evaluable PK sample. The immunogenicity analysis set included all patients who received ≥1 dose of GEN3009 and had a baseline sample and ≥1 evaluable ADA sample while on treatment. The response-evaluable set consisted of all patients who received ≥1 dose of GEN3009, had measurable disease at baseline, and had ≥1 postbaseline disease evaluation or died within 60 days of receiving the first GEN3009 dose.

Expression of CD37 in biopsy tumor samples collected at screening was visualized by immunohistochemistry (IHC) analysis, assessed by both tumor proportion score (range 0%–100%) and overall H-score (range 0–300). The relationship between maximum reduction in complement activity (peak change in CH50) and clinical response was analyzed by the Wilcoxon rank-sum test in patients with CLL/SLL or other NHL who had a response (CR or partial response (PR)) vs non-responders (stable disease (SD) or progressive disease (PD)) after GEN3009 treatment.

Results

GLP toxicity study in cynomolgus monkeys

The GLP toxicity study found the 10 mg/kg dose of GEN3009 to be the highest non-severely toxic dose (HNSTD). With the 30 mg/kg dose, transient but acutely severe clinical observations of emesis, swelling, hypoactivity, apathy, and hypotension were observed in three male cynomolgus monkeys after the fourth dose, resulting in their euthanasia for humane reasons. Severe, reversible hypotension was observed in five males and three females on day 22 and 2 females on day 29. The no observed adverse effect level was not determined; however, findings were monitorable and reversible.

Pharmacologic findings and cytokine levels

B-cell depletion and complement activation were observed at all doses in cynomolgus monkeys. Dose-independent decreases in B-cell populations were observed in peripheral blood on study days 1 and 8 (first and second doses) at all dose levels (online supplemental figure 2). Changes in complement parameters suggestive of complement activation were also observed (ie, increase in C3a and decrease in CH50 level; online supplemental figure 3 and data not shown).

ADA-associated reduction in exposure was reflected by decreased B-cell depletion after day 15 (third dose) and subsequent doses. However, complement consumption gradually increased after the third and subsequent doses, coincident with the presence of ADA in all monkeys.

Minimal increases in IL-6 and MCP-1 were observed on study days 1 and 8 at all dose levels. Minimal to moderate increases in IL-6, IL-10, MCP-1, and TNFα were observed after dosing on days 15 and 22 (online supplemental figure 4).

Observations, microscopic findings, and macroscopic findings

At all dose levels in cynomolgus monkeys, swelling of the neck, lips, and/or eyelids (as well as emesis, dilation of the pupils, and/or apathy) was observed on all dosing days. These observations were transient and resolved by 24 hours post-dose. Hypoactivity and lying position were observed at the 3 mg/kg and 30 mg/kg dose levels on study day 15 and later and resolved within 48 hours.

Macroscopically, enlarged spleen was noted at all dose levels at the end of the dosing period and correlated with microscopic observations of non-adverse splenic congestion in the red pulp (males only). At doses ≥10 mg/kg, red or pale bilateral discoloration of spleen and/or adrenal glands was observed in both sexes, and in the adrenal gland was correlated with microscopic observations of cortical hemorrhage, vacuolation, and/or degeneration and medullary congestion that were considered adverse. At the 30 mg/kg dose level, several other organs (eg, heart, liver, thyroid, and kidney) exhibited pale discoloration.

Phase 1 study in humans

Patient disposition and treatment exposure

From March 13, 2020, to July 28, 2023, a total of 46 patients had received GEN3009 treatment in schedule 1 (n=43) and schedule 2 (n=3). All 46 patients discontinued treatment; primary reasons for discontinuation were progressive disease (26 (56.5%) patients), AEs (14 (30.4%)), patient withdrawal (five (10.9%)), or starting a new anticancer therapy (one (2.2%); figure 1. Demographic and baseline characteristics

Most patients (76.1%) were male, median age was 70 years, and 82.6% of patients were White (table 1). The most common disease subtype was DLBCL (41.3%), followed by FL (21.7%), CLL (13.0%), SLL (10.9%), MZL (6.5%), MCL (4.3%), and HGBCL (2.2%). Of 19 patients with DLBCL, 11 (23.9% overall) had de novo disease and eight (17.4% overall) had transformed disease. Median time from NHL diagnosis to first GEN3009 treatment was 59.9 months, and patients had a median of four prior lines of therapy (range 2–14).

Dose-limiting toxicities

No DLTs were observed at dose levels up to 1600 mg. However, for three (6.5%) patients treated with GEN3009 at the 2000 mg dose level in schedule 1, DLTs of grade 4 neutropenia lasting >7 days were reported, and one of the three patients also experienced a DLT of serious, grade 3 aphasia, which occurred at the cycle 1 day 22 infusion. Prior to receiving that GEN3009 dose, the patient had severe thrombocytopenia and was febrile and hypotensive. During the infusion, the patient briefly experienced difficulty speaking, which was later reported as grade 3 aphasia considered by the investigator to be related to GEN3009. The patient received treatment with dexamethasone, and the event resolved promptly.

Safety evaluation

In the SAS (N=46), all patients reported ≥1 treatment-emergent AE (TEAE) (table 2). Neutropenia (88.4%), infusion-related reactions (IRRs; 83.7%), and thrombocytopenia (41.9%) were the most common TEAEs in patients treated with GEN3009 under schedule 1 (online supplemental table 5). Neutropenia (three patients; 100%), IRRs (three patients; 100%), and myalgia (two patients; 67%) were the most common TEAEs in patients treated under schedule 2.

A total of 23 (50.0%) patients reported serious TEAEs, including IRRs (13.0%), atrial fibrillation (4.3%), cellulitis (4.3%), COVID-19 (4.3%), pneumonia (4.3%), pneumonitis (4.3%), and pulmonary embolism (4.3%). There were three fatal TEAEs in schedule 1: COVID-19 pneumonia (n=1, not related), myocardial infarction (n=1, not related), and COVID-19 (n=1, related).

For the fatal TEAE of COVID-19 that was reported by the investigator as related to GEN3009, the causality is unclear. The patient had positive COVID-19 tests at baseline prior to the first dose of GEN3009 treatment and remained COVID-19-positive throughout the trial. The patient received the first full dose of GEN3009 on cycle 1 day 1. The investigator reported grade 3 COVID-19, assessed as not related to GEN3009, from study day 5 to study day 15. The patient subsequently received the second full dose of GEN3009 on study day 22. On study day 25, a second episode of grade 3 COVID-19 was reported and deemed related to GEN3009 by the study investigator, leading to discontinuation of GEN3009 on study day 56 due to COVID-19. The patient died of COVID-19 on study day 61.

TEAEs leading to GEN3009 treatment discontinuation were reported in 14 patients (30.4%) and included IRRs (8.7%), pneumonitis (4.3%), and thrombocytopenia (4.3%). IRRs, considered AEs of special interest, were observed at dose levels of 20 mg and higher for 39 (84.8%) patients, including one patient who received GEN3009 at ≤60 mg, one at 180 mg, seven at 400 mg, nine at 800 mg, 10 at 1200 mg, three at 1600 mg, and five at 2000 mg in schedule 1. In schedule 2, IRRs were reported in all three patients who received GEN3009 at 800 mg. Most IRRs occurred during the first infusion (37 patients, 80.4%). IRRs were grade 2 in severity for more than half of patients (54.3%); 10.9% of patients experienced grade 1 IRRs, and 19.6% of patients experienced grade 3 IRRs. No grade 4 or 5 IRRs were reported.

PK and immunogenicity

For 46 evaluable patients, GEN3009 plasma concentrations increased over time with increasing doses. In most patients, plasma concentrations of GEN3009 were quantifiable for up to 72 hours after the end of infusion (online supplemental figure 5). Clearance of GEN3009 appeared to be fast, with highly variable predose drug concentrations (prior to subsequent doses) that were below the lower limit of quantification for many patients. There was no apparent accumulation of GEN3009 observed over time with multiple doses.

Of 39 patients with available immunogenicity data, four (10.3%) patients in schedule 1 tested positive for ADA during GEN3009 treatment. PK and safety in patients with ADA-positive samples appeared to be similar to findings in patients with ADA-negative samples.

Preliminary antitumor efficacy

Among 28 patients in the response-evaluable set, preliminary findings of clinical activity (including SD) of GEN3009 monotherapy were observed in patients with aggressive or indolent R/R B-cell NHL at dose levels of 180 mg and higher (figure 2). GEN3009 showed a trend toward dose-dependent responses, with better ORR and CR rates at dose levels ≥400 mg (online supplemental table 6). Specifically, in schedule 1, there were three responders (of five patients) at the 400 mg dose level (two CR and one PR); three responders (of five patients) at the 1200 mg dose level (all PR); two responders (of three patients) at the 1600 mg dose level (one CR and one PR); and one responder (of three patients) at the 2000 mg dose level (PR). Recommended phase 2 dose

Preliminary antitumor activity of GEN3009 monotherapy was observed among patients with both aggressive and indolent lymphoma at doses ≥180 mg: at the 400 mg dose level, there was one PR in FL, one CR in MZL, and one CR in SLL; at the 800 mg dose level, there was one CR in DLBCL; at the 1200 mg dose level, there was one PR in DLBCL and two PRs in FL; at the 1600 mg dose level, there was one PR in DLBCL and 1 CR in MZL; and at the 2000 mg dose level, there was one PR in FL. GEN3009 was well tolerated at dose levels up to 1600 mg with no DLTs. No significant relationship of exposure with safety and pharmacodynamic markers was observed at the clinically relevant doses of 400 mg and beyond. Therefore, based on the totality of safety, antitumor activity, PK, pharmacodynamics, and immunogenicity data as well as the similar benefit-risk profile for 1200 mg and 1600 mg, the more conservative dose of 1200 mg was chosen as the GEN3009 RP2D.

Exploratory biomarker analysis

Tumor tissue biopsies obtained at screening were IHC-evaluable for 32 of 46 patients who received GEN3009 treatment. Of these, samples from 29 (91%) patients showed CD37 expression in tumor tissue. In most patient samples (25 of 32; 78%), CD37 expression, as assessed by tumor proportion score, exceeded 50%. Furthermore, more than half of the patient samples (17 of 32; 53%) showed a high CD37 overall H-score (~200–300). The distribution of CD37 expression H-scores was heterogeneous across NHL types. Of 9 patients with CD20-negative tumor samples, 7 showed CD37 expression.

GEN3009 treatment reduced peripheral B-cell counts at doses of ≥60 mg, showing sustained depletion for NHL subtypes other than CLL or SLL (figure 3A). Peripheral NK cell counts showed a transient decrease post GEN3009 at all tested dose levels (figure 3B). No association was observed between GEN3009 dose and maximum decrease in peripheral B-cell or NK cell counts. Total CH50 serum levels were reduced at all tested GEN3009 dose levels, the decrease was transient (with CH50 levels returning to predose levels within days), and no full complement exhaustion was observed up to the highest tested dose (figure 3C). Peak CH50 changes were significantly associated with clinical response (ie, CR and PR; Wilcoxon rank-sum test, p=0.008), with greater CH50 changes observed in responders compared with non-responders (figure 3D). Transient decreases in circulating T cells and monocytes were observed at all tested GEN3009 dose levels and peak decreases were not associated with GEN3009 dose (data not shown). No significant GEN3009-mediated modulation of cytokine levels was observed at doses ≤2000 mg.

Discussion

Non-clinical safety and pharmacology findings from the GLP toxicity study supported clinical testing of GEN3009. Dose-independent decreases in peripheral B-cell counts and changes in complement consumption were consistent with the intended pharmacology of GEN3009. Findings including swelling of the lips/neck/eyelids and pupil dilatation reflected first-dose infusion reactions that were also seen in the clinic. Toxicity findings such as congestion, hemorrhage, degeneration, and pale appearance in the adrenal gland and spleen suggested dose-dependent disruption in blood distribution. High complement activation by GEN3009/ADA immune complexes may have resulted in severe hypotensive stress, leading to non-adverse splenic and adverse adrenal changes.28 It is unclear why the adrenal gland was particularly affected, but it may be related to the gland being highly vascular and having greater vascular inputs (the three adrenal arteries) than outputs (the adrenal vein).29 30 Of note, ADA formation in monkeys is not translatable to humans,31 and consistent with this, neither high rates of ADA formation nor severe hypotensive stress were seen in the clinic. The HNSTD was considered 10 mg/kg in the GLP toxicity study due to severe hypotension observed at 30 mg/kg. The GLP toxicity study findings provided an adequate safety margin for the starting dose and dosing schedule for GEN3009 in human trials.

In the first-in-human phase 1 study of GEN3009, the RP2D was determined to be 1200 mg based on the safety, clinical efficacy, and PK findings. GEN3009 was well tolerated with no DLTs at dose levels up to 1600 mg. The most common TEAE was neutropenia, and at the 2000-mg dose level, three patients reported DLTs of grade 4 neutropenia lasting more than 7 days.

CD37 is known to be expressed on neutrophils,32 and neutropenia has been observed as a common AE and DLT in other phase 1 studies of CD37-targeting agents in B-cell malignancies.1517 Neutropenia appears to be a pharmacodynamic phenomenon in which neutrophil levels dropped after GEN3009 infusions and recovered with or without granulocyte colony-stimulating factor by the next dose of GEN3009 in a dose-independent manner. The rapid decline and subsequent recovery of neutrophils do not suggest toxicity on bone marrow myeloid precursor cells. The kinetics of GEN3009-induced neutropenia are comparable to those observed in a phase 1 study of BI 836826, a CD37-specific antibody that primarily induces ADCC and apoptosis but does not induce CDC.16 These findings suggest that complement activation is unlikely to be the primary driver of the observed neutropenia. Furthermore, in vitro whole blood assays with GEN3009 did not demonstrate a direct cytotoxic effect on neutrophils, supporting the notion that the effect is not due to direct depletion.18 CD37 also has been implicated in the regulation of neutrophil adhesion and migration, processes that may be affected by CD37-targeting therapies.32

Despite the transient and severe neutropenia observed, this did not appear to be clinically significant; high incidence of neutropenia did not lead to increased febrile neutropenia and infection events. Granulocyte colony-stimulating factor support was mandated at the onset of grade 3 or 4 neutropenia. In the current study, serious TEAEs included IRRs, atrial fibrillation, cellulitis, COVID-19, pneumonia, pneumonitis, and pulmonary embolism; one fatal TEAE of COVID-19 was considered treatment related. The median duration of treatment was relatively short at 1.2 months (range 0.0–14.5), with most patients discontinuing treatment due to progressive disease (57%) or AEs (30%). The optimization of GEN3009, such as development of a subcutaneous formulation, could reduce the substantial number of discontinuations due to IRRs, thus enabling longer treatment exposure and potentially enhancing clinical benefit. The most frequently reported TEAEs leading to discontinuation included IRRs, pneumonitis, COVID-19 pneumonia, and thrombocytopenia. There were no discontinuations due to neutropenia. IRRs, which were considered AEs of special interest, were observed primarily during the first infusion, with no grade 4 or 5 IRRs reported. Despite IRRs observed with GEN3009, no significant modulation of systemic cytokines was observed at doses ≤2000 mg. This may reflect localized immune effects and/or transient cytokine release not captured at the measured timepoints. Plasma clearance of GEN3009 appeared faster than that of conventional IgG biologics; most patients cleared the drug within a week, compared with the elimination half-life of 18–21 days observed with typical therapeutic monoclonal antibodies.33 The mechanism of faster clearance in humans is currently unknown and under investigation. Consistent with other CD37-antibody-based molecules, no accumulation of GEN3009 was observed after repeated administration and plasma clearance decreased with increasing doses, suggesting a mechanism of target-mediated drug clearance.17 34 Although four patients developed ADAs, the small number of cases precluded an analysis of the impact of ADAs on safety and efficacy.

GEN3009 monotherapy demonstrated modest clinical activity at dose levels of 180 mg and higher across aggressive and indolent R/R B-cell NHL subtypes in preliminary analyses of clinical antitumor efficacy. Among 28 response-evaluable patients, 10 (35.7%) responders were observed. These included patients with DLBCL (one CR (800 mg) and two PR (1200 mg and 1600 mg)), FL (four PR (one at 400 mg, two at 1200 mg, one at 2000 mg)), MZL (two CR (400 and 1600 mg)), and SLL (one CR (400 mg)). Notably, patients had been pretreated with a median of four prior lines of therapy. Together, results suggest the antitumor potential for GEN3009 in heavily pretreated patients with NHL.

In the exploratory pharmacodynamic biomarker analysis in the phase 1 study, GEN3009 demonstrated biological activity, as reflected by peripheral B-cell depletion and a transient decrease in peripheral NK cell counts at ≥60 mg and ≥6 mg doses, respectively. This analysis also showed an association between GEN3009-mediated complement consumption (peak change in CH50) and observed clinical response, providing support for CDC as the primary mechanism of action of GEN3009 as intended by its DuoHexaBody design, which enhances antibody hexamerization to promote complement activation.18 These findings provide the first clinical proof-of-concept for antibody hexamerization-potentiated CDC as an antitumor treatment strategy.

This first published report of nonclinical and dose-finding studies of GEN3009 suggests that targeting CD37 to elicit Fc-mediated antitumor activity is a potential strategy for the development of novel agents for both indolent and aggressive B-NHL subtypes. The sponsor’s decision to terminate the study was based on strategic considerations and internal portfolio prioritization and was not due to any safety concerns or new safety signals related to GEN3009. Additional research is warranted to further characterize the mechanism of action and therapeutic potential of CD37-targeting agents as monotherapies or in combination treatments.

Conclusions

In this study, GEN3009 as monotherapy exhibited an acceptable safety profile and modest clinical activity. By describing the tolerability, PK, and preliminary clinical activity of GEN3009, these results provide clinical proof of concept for the potential of combination regimens incorporating hexamerization-potentiated molecules that induce antitumor activity through enhanced CDC.

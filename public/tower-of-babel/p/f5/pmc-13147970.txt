Histologic outcomes of rotator cuff patch augmentation: a literature review
Authors: Hunter Hitchens, William H. Fang, Kevin Mo, Randa Bascharon, Richard Winder, Thomas Vangsness
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Rotator cuff repairs frequently fail due to inadequate biologic healing at the tendon–bone interface. Patch augmentation has emerged as a strategy to enhance this process by promoting cellular infiltration, organized collagen deposition, and matrix remodeling. This review evaluates the histologic and biochemical effects of scaffold augmentation, compares different augmentation materials, and analyzes commercially available implants based on animal and human studies. A Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-Analyses–guided search of PubMed, Scopus, and Embase through May 2025 identified 42 eligible studies that reported histologic or biochemical outcomes following augmented rotator cuff repair. Dermal matrix patches demonstrated accelerated early neovascularization and collagen synthesis but were associated with incomplete fibrocartilage formation and persistent type III collagen. Extracellular matrix–derived patches showed improved collagen alignment but were linked to inflammatory responses. Synthetic polymers produced dense type I collagen with variable immune reactions, while biologically augmented scaffolds achieved high collagen density with minimal macrophage infiltration. Demineralized bone matrix demonstrated early fibrocartilage integration, mature collagen crimp, and low inflammatory response by 8 weeks. Among commercially available products, REGENETEN, ArthroFLEX, and Dynagraft II showed the most complete scaffold resorption and tendon-like remodeling, whereas Conexa and Zimmer patches demonstrated consistent integration, and Permacol and BioEnthesis showed limited improvements. Overall, patch augmentation appears to enhance biologic healing after rotator cuff repair; however, definitive conclusions are limited by the lack of high-quality human studies. These findings may inform scaffold selection and future implant design aimed at improving tendon–bone healing and reducing retear rates.

Rotator cuff tears (RCTs) are among the most prevalent musculoskeletal injuries, significantly impacting quality of life due to reduced mobility and discomfort.9,12,31,51 Despite advances in surgical techniques, conventional RCT repair remains difficult due to insufficient biological healing at the tendon-to-bone interface, known as the enthesis.15,33,54 The enthesis, where tendon transitions into fibrocartilage and bone, is a highly organized structure that distributes mechanical stress and balances muscular forces during movement.1, 2, 3,5,48,50 Restoration of the native zonal architecture is essential for normal tendon function; however, most current repair strategies result in disorganized scar tissue that fails to replicate the structural and functional alignment of healthy tendon.2,26,32,42,49

Conventional repair, such as suture repair, frequently does not reestablish the native fibrocartilaginous transition, thereby compromising the biomechanical properties of the rotator cuff.16,24,25,34,36, 37, 38, 39, 40,52, 53, 54, 55 Histologic analyses consistently demonstrate fibrovascular scar formation and loss of zonal organization, resulting in a structurally inferior, failure-prone enthesis43,47,. To address these limitations, patch augmentation has emerged as a promising adjunctive strategy.

There are 5 general types of rotator cuff grafts: dermal matrix patch, extracellular matrix (ECM)-derived matrix patch, synthetic scaffolds, bioaugmented patches, and demineralized bone matrix (DBM) scaffolds.

A dermal matrix patch is a biologic implant derived from decellularized human or porcine dermal tissue.7,10,11,37,39,44, 45, 46 The 2 most common dermal matrix patches, derived from porcine and human dermis, differ in both structure and host response. Porcine dermal patches are xenografts derived from decellularized porcine skin, composed primarily of type I collagen and other ECM proteins. These patches retain the structural architecture of native dermis and are often chemically cross-linked to enhance tensile strength and prolong graft durability.10,37,39 In contrast, human dermal patches are allografts derived from decellularized cadaveric human skin and are composed primarily of type I collagen and native ECM components. These grafts are typically non–cross-linked, preserving their biological ability to support cellular infiltration and matrix remodeling.39,44,46

An ECM-derived matrix is a biologic scaffold composed primarily of decellularized ECM components.14,28,29,38,41,43,47 Commercial patches have been most commonly derived from porcine small intestine submucosa, bovine collagen, human biphasic interpositional allograft, bovine pericardium, porcine collagen, and human amnion matrix cord. Regardless of origin, ECM-based scaffolds retain structural proteins such as type I collagen, fibronectin, and glycosaminoglycans, creating a bioactive environment that promotes new blood vessel formation and matrix remodeling at the enthesis.6,14,21,28,37,38,41,47

Synthetic scaffolds used for rotator cuff repairs are typically designed to provide mechanical support and resist degradation.12,17 These grafts are commonly made from polymers such as polypropylene, polycaprolactone (PCL), or polyethylene terephthalate (PET). More recent designs incorporate bioresorbable materials like poly-ε-caprolactone or nanofibers, which allow gradual degradation and tissue integration over time.27,53 These scaffolds often feature porous or fibrous architectures to promote cellular infiltration, neovascularization, and tendon-bone healing.8,56

Bioaugmented patches are dermal matrix, ECM, or synthetic scaffolds enhanced with biologically active components to promote healing at the enthesis.24,43,49 These patches may be seeded with mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs), tenocytes, or small extracellular vesicles (sEVs), or loaded with bioactive agents such as growth factors or chondrogenic molecules like kartogenin.20,29,40 The goal of these additions is to stimulate fibrocartilage formation, reduce inflammation, and improve tissue regeneration and mechanical strength at the enthesis.24,43,49

DBM is an osteoinductive scaffold derived from processed allograft bone in which the mineral content has been removed, leaving behind a collagen-rich matrix containing bioactive proteins such as bone morphogenetic proteins.30,49 DBM has been used as an augmentation material in rotator cuff repair because it provides both a biological and structural environment that supports tendon reattachment and enthesis remodeling.30,49

Given the diversity of materials available and their varied impacts on biological healing, this systematic review aims to comprehensively evaluate the histologic and biochemical effects of patch augmentation, specifically on the rotator cuff enthesis. A secondary objective is to analyze the effectiveness of different augmentation materials and compare the performance of commercially available implants. This can help identify optimal materials and strategies to improve clinical outcomes, ultimately guiding surgeons toward options that maximize tendon healing and functional recovery in rotator cuff repair patients.

Methods

A comprehensive search strategy was developed and executed across major biomedical databases PubMed, Scopus, and Embase, covering studies published through May 2025. The search terms included combinations of “rotator cuff,” “augmentation,” “patch,” “scaffold,” “enthesis,” “histology,” “biochemical,” “animal model,” “human,” and the names of specific commercial products such as “REGENETEN,” “GraftJacket,” “Permacol,” “Conexa,” “Zimmer Collagen repair patch,” “ArthroFLEX,” and “BioEnthesis.” To ensure the search was thorough, additional studies were identified by examining the reference lists of relevant articles. Only articles available in English were considered for inclusion.

Study selection involved an initial screening of titles and abstracts, followed by full-text review of potentially eligible studies by 2 authors (H.H. and W.F.) independently applying the search strategy to select references from these databases. The titles and abstracts of the retrieved articles were reviewed independently. The systematic search and selection process is summarized in Fig. 1. The inclusion criteria were (1) in vivo animal or human studies of RCT repair in which patch augmentation was performed; and (2) studies that reported histologic and/or biochemical outcomes focused specifically on healing at the tendon-to-bone interface. Exclusion criteria included studies that were purely biomechanical or imaging-based without histologic or biochemical data, as well as reviews, editorials, technical notes, and in vitro or cadaveric studies.Figure 1PRISMA diagram of the study selection process. For each included study, relevant data were extracted, including the species used, patch material and product name (if applicable), follow-up duration, and key histologic findings. Whenever available, comparative results between augmented and nonaugmented repairs, or between different materials, were recorded.

Given the diversity in study designs and outcome measures, a qualitative synthesis was performed rather than a formal meta-analysis. Findings were grouped by key subsections relevant to the research question: collagen formation, matrix organization, inflammatory response, and tendon density and size trends. Commercial product outcomes were summarized separately from animal models and any available human histology.

Results

After excluding duplicates, 439 potentially relevant articles were identified. Following title screening, 210 records were excluded, and 229 full-text articles were reviewed. Ultimately, 42 studies fulfilled the inclusion criteria and were incorporated into the present review. Collagen formation

Dermal matrix patches consistently supported early collagen deposition and new tendon formation. In animal models, these scaffolds demonstrated significant tenocyte infiltration within approximately 6 weeks, progressing toward a mature wavy collagen fiber architecture with clear anchorage by Sharpey’s fibers between 6 and 12 months.1,22,23,45 Early biopsies from human rotator cuff repairs augmented with dermal matrix scaffolds revealed robust cellular infiltration and neovascularization as early as 3 months postimplantation.10,37,39,46 Despite evidence of initial remodeling, dermal matrix grafts often exhibit delayed or incomplete maturation at the tendon-bone interface, with persistent collagen type III expression and reduced fibrocartilage formation.23,54

In animal models, ECM-derived scaffolds promoted early deposition of dense, wavy collagen fiber bundles and reformed the fibrocartilaginous interface as early as 12 weeks.19,21,38,41 Patches used in human studies further demonstrated early deposition of fibers and fibrocartilage-like tissue at the enthesis by 6 to 12 months.3 However, other cases demonstrated incomplete remodeling and persistent disorganization, with limited scaffold integration and signs of degeneration.6,29

Materials such as PET and electrospun PCL enhanced type I collagen deposition36,53,56 and induced larger fibril diameter growth at the enthesis when studied in animal models.36,56 This evidence is further supported in human models, where synthetic scaffold augmentation was associated with favorable collagen remodeling at the tendon-bone interface.8,12

In animal models, scaffolds loaded with MSCs, tenocytes, or secreted vesicles resulted in increased collagen fiber density, higher type I collagen expression, and more mature collagen deposition.11,20,24,40,43,49 Notably, tenocyte-seeded scaffolds led to increased expression of collagen type III, reflecting early matrix remodeling prior to full maturation toward type I-dominant tendon tissue.40

In Lee et al, the DBM patch exhibited densely packed, longitudinally aligned collagen fibers with a well-defined crimp pattern and elongated fibroblast nuclei, indicative of a more mature and organized tendon-to-bone interface.

Matrix organization

Dermal matrix-based scaffolds consistently promoted progressive matrix organization over time. In animal models, histology and polarized light microscopy revealed linear collagen alignment and formation of insertional structures, including Sharpey’s fibers, by 6 months postaugmentation with dermal matrix grafts.1,22,23,45,54 Early biopsies and gross evaluations from human studies supported favorable tissue integration and initial matrix alignment within the first 3 months.37,46

ECM-derived patch augmentation promoted gradual matrix organization in animal models, with improved collagen fiber alignment, elongated tenocyte nuclei, and fibrocartilage reformation at the enthesis observed between 8 and 26 weeks.38,40,47,55 Despite these benefits, several studies reported persistent fibrous encapsulation, disorganized or ectopic tissue formation, and no significant improvement in fiber orientation compared to controls.11,37,41 In human studies, ECM-derived scaffolds demonstrated partial matrix organization at the enthesis, with areas of aligned collagen fibers, fibrocartilage-like zones, and signs of scaffold incorporation by 6 to 12 months.3,12 However, other reports showed inconsistent structural remodeling, poor integration, and no improvement in fiber architecture compared to standard repair.6,21,29

PET and nanofiber scaffolds formed a fibrocartilage-like transition zone within 8-12 weeks postimplantation,27,56 whereas electrospun PCL implants displayed organized but nonmineralized tendon tissue.53 Histologic analysis of human biopsy samples revealed aligned collagen fibers and increased fibril diameter at the tendon-bone interface,8 while clinical imaging and functional outcomes suggested enhanced collagen organization and durable tissue remodeling over time.12

MSC-, tenocyte-, or sEV-loaded constructs demonstrated improved collagen fiber orientation, matrix continuity, and more structured fibrocartilage compared to unaugmented controls in animal models.11,20,24,40,43,49 In a human clinical trial, however, MSCs delivered with a xenogenic collagen scaffold showed limited benefit for matrix organization, with inconclusive evidence of improved fiber alignment or enthesis architecture due to early trial termination.29

DBM augmentation resulted in improved matrix organization at the tendon-bone interface, with histologic sections showing a well-structured insertion zone composed of uniformly aligned collagen fibers. The repaired enthesis in the DBM group exhibited orderly fiber architecture and integrated fibrocartilage, contrasting with the irregular and fragmented tissue seen in nonaugmented controls.30

Inflammatory response

In animal models, dermal matrix grafts elicited no chronic inflammation, graft rejection, or foreign body reaction throughout follow-up, with consistent histologic evidence of host integration and remodeling.1,22,23 In human studies, histologic evaluations reported minimal inflammatory response following dermal matrix graft augmentation, with no evidence of graft rejection or foreign body reaction in early or mid-term follow-up.10,37,46

In animal models, ECM-derived scaffolds generally triggered a mild inflammatory response, with no evidence of foreign body reaction, granuloma formation, or severe immune rejection at the tendon-bone interface.19,21,37,41 However, some studies reported low-grade chronic inflammation, prolonged macrophage activity, or delayed resolution of the inflammatory phase.32,38,55 In human studies, inflammatory responses to ECM-derived scaffolds varied, with some biopsies demonstrating minimal immune cell infiltration and no evidence of a foreign body reaction.3,12 Conversely, several clinical studies documented chronic inflammation and adverse tissue responses, including graft delamination and synovial hyperplasia, ultimately contributing to poor scaffold integration and early implant failure.6,21,29

In animal studies, electrospun PCL and polyurethane implants elicited no acute inflammation,53 whereas PET and nanofiber constructs exhibited only low-grade mononuclear infiltration without fibrosis or foreign-body reaction.27,36,56 Synthetic scaffold augmentation demonstrated in human models showed favorable biocompatibility, with no reports of fibrosis, immune reaction, or graft-related complications on histology, imaging, or clinical follow-up.8,12

MSC-, tenocyte-, or vesicle-enhanced constructs demonstrated decreased macrophage infiltration, reduced inflammatory cell presence, and fewer adhesions compared to unaugmented or patch-alone controls.11,24,40,43,49 In contrast, a human clinical trial using autologous MSCs with a xenogenic collagen scaffold reported adverse inflammatory reactions, leading to early trial termination and raising concerns about scaffold biocompatibility in small joint environments.29

DBM scaffolds consistently performed with low inflammatory responses. Augmentation elicited a minimal inflammatory response at 8 weeks, with histologic sections showing low levels of inflammatory cell infiltration compared to controls.30 These findings suggest a favorable immunologic profile for DBM scaffolds in tendon-to-bone healing.30

Tendon density/size

Dermal matrix augmentation in animal models led to increased tendon thickness, cross-sectional area, and footprint coverage compared to nonaugmented repairs.1,22,36,54 In human studies, histologic analyses demonstrated ongoing collagen-rich tissue formation and matrix filling over time, although unpopulated areas within the graft were occasionally observed, suggesting incomplete cellular integration.10,37,39

ECM-derived scaffolds promoted increases in tendon thickness, density, and enthesis continuity in animal models, with histologic evidence of improved structural integration compared to nonaugmented repairs.38,41,52 In chronic repair settings, scaffold-treated tendons reached up to 91% of native tissue thickness by 26 weeks and maintained this bulk through 52 weeks, while umbilical-derived ECM scaffolds demonstrated significantly higher histologic scores and improved tendon-bone bridging by 24 weeks.28,52

In animal models, PET- and PCL-based constructs promoted greater tissue continuity and histologic maturation at the enthesis, with regenerated tendon structure approaching native morphology by 8 to 12 weeks.27,53,56 In human studies, biopsy and imaging findings demonstrated thicker, more organized tendon tissue in augmented repairs, with clinical improvements suggesting enhanced tendon remodeling over time.8,12

Biologically augmented scaffolds, including those treated with sEVs, MSCs, or growth factors, demonstrated superior structural healing, with significantly increased insertion thickness, collagen organization, and tendon maturation scores. In animal models, these constructs demonstrated enhanced insertion site width, more compact cross-sectional morphology, and tendon density approaching that of native tissue.24,40,43,49

DBM-based augmentation improved overall tendon structure and insertion continuity at 8 weeks. Histologic findings showed increased fibrocartilage formation and more organized tissue at the tendon-bone interface, contributing to higher total healing scores compared to the control group.30

Comparison of commercial implants

Animal and human studies comparing commercially available rotator cuff repair patches revealed distinct differences in enthesis healing and tissue remodeling based on scaffold composition and structure. Among porcine dermal matrix scaffolds, biological integration and healing responses varied considerably. Clinical trials investigated the effects of Conexa, with one randomized trial reporting improved healing rates, footprint coverage, and tendon thickness compared to nonaugmented repair at 24 months,4 although another prospective study found no significant benefit and a higher complication rate.13

Permacol, by contrast, yielded less favorable histologic outcomes. In a human biopsy study, repairs augmented with Permacol showed disruption of the native ECM, and one patient demonstrated a localized pro-inflammatory immune response with macrophage infiltration.39 The Zimmer Collagen Repair Patch demonstrated good biocompatibility in a sheep model. At 9 weeks, the patch showed fibroblast and vascular ingrowth without a systemic inflammatory response, and by 24 weeks, it was fully integrated with no adverse tissue reaction at the enthesis.37

Human dermal matrix patches demonstrated partial histologic and clinical benefits following rotator cuff repair. GraftJacket supported early enthesis remodeling, including organized collagen alignment, neovascularization, and fibrocartilage formation in both animal and human models.7,22,46 Clinical outcomes included improved range of motion, pain reduction, and patient satisfaction.7,18 However, GraftJacket repairs often failed to restore native enthesis structure and demonstrated inferior biomechanical strength compared to tendon-bone allografts or DBM-based scaffolds.23,45,49 ArthroFLEX demonstrated favorable outcomes in a canine model, showing improved collagen organization and minimal inflammation compared to other scaffold types.44

The Restore patch has shown mixed results in rotator cuff repair augmentation. In animal models, Restore-augmented repairs supported tendon regeneration with organized collagen deposition and host cell infiltration by 6 months, without evidence of immune rejection.14 However, other studies reported early inflammatory responses, including elevated fibrinogen and macrophage presence, and eventually disorganized remodeling with ectopic bone formation at the enthesis.37 In 2 randomized trials, Iannotti et al reported fewer healed repairs in the Restore group (4/15) than in controls (9/15), while Bryant et al found no significant difference in retear rates, pain, or shoulder function between groups.

Bovine collagen patches showed variable performance. The REGENETEN patch supported early fibroblast infiltration and linear cell alignment by 5 weeks, followed by progressive collagen deposition and maturation.3 In contrast, the Rotation Medical Patch was associated with persistent tendon thickening, hypervascularity, and inflammatory changes suggestive of a foreign body response.44

In a canine model, the Arthrex Amnion scaffold supported superior healing. Repaired tendons showed significantly reduced pathology than both the débridement and bovine collagen patch groups.44 Magnetic resonance imaging and ultrasound confirmed improved fiber alignment and progressive remodeling over time, however also noted reduced tendon thickness.44 The BioEnthesis patch was evaluated in an ovine model and demonstrated a histologic profile equivalent to standard repair at 3, 6, and 12 weeks postoperatively.21 Additionally, the scaffold did not provoke any significant inflammation, foreign body reaction, or infection.21

In Jenner et al, the Diacoll patch alone was used to fill a standardized infraspinatus tendon defect in sheep. While the collagen sponge supported partial healing, histologic and imaging outcomes were inferior to the sEV-treated group.24 The OrthADAPT scaffold repairs showed high complication rates, with 50% of patients experiencing adverse outcomes, including retears and inflammatory reactions.29 Histologic analysis of biopsied grafts revealed dense fibrous encapsulation, giant cell infiltration, and chronic inflammation consistent with immune-mediated graft rejection.29 Patients who had undergone repair with the TUTOPATCH showed early improvements in pain and function, but long-term outcomes were not significantly better than repair alone.12

Use of the Repol Angimesh polypropylene patch significantly improved outcomes in patients undergoing open repair of massive posterosuperior RCTs.12 At 3-year follow-up, patients treated with this patch showed significantly higher The University of California-Los Angeles shoulder scale, greater scapular plane elevation, and stronger abduction strength compared to both the collagen patch and control groups.12 Use of the ROTIUM scaffold led to partial restoration of native enthesis structure in a chronic rotator cuff degeneration model.27 Histologic analysis revealed localized re-establishment of fibrocartilaginous insertion zones and the presence of Sharpey-like fibers in some samples, indicating progress toward a direct tendon-to-bone interface.27

The Dynagraft II patch, a DBM-based patch, enhanced histologic healing at the enthesis in a rabbit rotator cuff repair model.30 Compared to controls, the treated tendons demonstrated densely organized, longitudinally aligned collagen fibers, clear crimp patterns, elongated fibroblast nuclei, and significantly higher histologic scores, reflecting improved collagen maturity and fibrocartilage development.30

Discussion

This systematic review demonstrates that patch augmentation enhances healing of the rotator cuff enthesis by promoting increased collagen formation, improved matrix organization, reduced inflammatory response, and greater tendon density and continuity at the tendon-to-bone interface.1,3,24,28,30,32,37, 38, 39, 40,43,47,49,52, 53, 54, 55 These effects were consistently observed across animal models and limited human biopsy studies, supporting the use of scaffolds to address the inherent limitations of conventional repair techniques.

Collective evidence suggests that dermal matrix patches serve more as provisional scaffolds than as a true blueprint for enthesis regeneration. Their type 1 collagen framework allows early tenocyte infiltration and collagen synthesis but lacks the structural cues or biochemical support needed for long-term fibrocartilage regeneration. Inflammatory responses to dermal patches are generally mild and transient, yet cross-linked porcine xenografts can evoke macrophage infiltration.10,39 Despite these early benefits, mineralized fibrocartilage and zonal maturation remain sparse, and pockets of acellular collagen with inferior mechanical properties persist when cellular penetration is incomplete.1,44

ECM-derived scaffolds have demonstrated variable but overall favorable effects on enthesis healing. Across animal and early clinical studies, these patches accelerated type I collagen deposition at the enthesis, forming dense tendon-like fibers and fibrocartilage within 12 to 16 weeks.19,38,55 Patches were also shown to drive progressive matrix reorganization in animal models, with collagen fibers assuming a parallel orientation and elongated tenocyte nuclei between 8 and 26 weeks.19,32,38,55 Despite these improvements, some models showed poor alignment or incomplete patch resorption beyond 6 months.37,44 ECM-derived patches usually triggered only a mild transient inflammatory response postoperatively, with macrophage infiltration resolving without foreign-body reaction or granuloma formation in most animal studies.19,44,47,55 However, other studies documented persistent fibrous encapsulation, ectopic tissue formation, and no meaningful improvement of fiber architecture relative to standard repair.29,37,44,47 Human data mirrored this spectrum, ranging from minimal immune cell infiltration and uneventful incorporation21,24 to clinically significant synovial hyperplasia, graft delamination, and early failure when chronic inflammation persisted.29,37,44 Overall, the findings suggest that ECM-derived patches can produce collagen-rich and integrated tissue at the enthesis, but their effectiveness depends heavily on scaffold properties and host immune response.

Synthetic scaffold augmentation consistently accelerated collagen deposition and organized fiber alignment at the rotator cuff enthesis across animal and human studies.8,12,27,36,53,56 PET patch repairs in rabbits produced Sharpey-like fibers by 8 weeks and fibrocartilage formation by 12 weeks, indicating early transition-zone regeneration.36,56 Clinically, polycarbonate polyurethane and polypropylene patches maintained tendon continuity and avoided fibrosis while improving healing rates at 1 to 3 years.12 Across models, inflammatory reactions remained mild, with only low-grade macrophage infiltration reported for PET constructs and no foreign-body reaction for polyurethane or PCL designs.36,53,56 The unexpectedly mild inflammatory responses were attributed to the absence of residual DNA seen in biologic grafts, use of materials biocompatible with soft tissue, and a porous structure that allowed for tissue ingrowth rather than fibrous encapsulation.12,17,53,56

Biologically augmented patches consistently enhance collagen deposition at the rotator cuff enthesis, outperforming nonaugmented repairs in both quantity and organization of new matrix.24,43,49 Constructs seeded with MSCs, tenocytes, or extracellular vesicles promoted early collagen fiber formation and higher expression of type I collagen, supporting a more tendon-like repair profile.11,20,40 These same constructs also demonstrated superior matrix organization, with enhanced collagen fiber alignment, improved continuity between tendon and bone, and the formation of organized fibrocartilage zones.20,24,40,43,49 In addition, MSC-, tenocyte-, or vesicle-enhanced implants were associated with decreased macrophage infiltration, lower cellular inflammation, and fewer adhesions, indicating the patch produced a more controlled and regenerative healing environment.20,40,49 However, the lone clinical study using MSCs with a xenogenic patch reported adverse inflammatory events, highlighting the need for caution and further evaluation of scaffold biocompatibility in human applications.29 Due to the limited number of human clinical trials and early termination of available studies, definitive conclusions about the effects of bioaugmented patches on enthesis collagen formation in patients cannot yet be drawn.29

DBM augmentation improved enthesis healing by enhancing collagen formation, matrix organization, and structural integration in rotator cuff repair.30 In a rabbit model, DBM-treated tendons showed denser, longitudinally aligned collagen fibers with mature crimp patterns and elongated nuclei, indicating accelerated collagen maturation.30 Inflammatory responses were minimal, with low cellular infiltration, suggesting favorable biocompatibility and a regenerative healing environment. These findings support DBM as a promising scaffold for biologic augmentation of rotator cuff repair, though limited clinical evidence currently prevents its broader endorsement.

A secondary objective of this study was to assess the comparative performance of commercially available implants. Among the porcine dermal matrix patches, repairs using Conexa consistently produced more favorable outcomes, with parallel collagen alignment, early vascularization, and minimal macrophage activity. The Zimmer Collagen Repair Patch achieved moderate results, while Permacol was associated with ECM disruption and focal macrophage infiltration, providing little improvement in collagen organization or mechanical strength.

Within human dermal matrix patches, ArthroFLEX produced better-aligned collagen and reduced inflammation compared to GraftJacket, which had mixed performance, showing functional improvements in some models, but persistent inflammation and weaker biomechanical properties in others.

Several ECM-derived scaffolds, such as REGENETEN and Arthrex Amnion, demonstrated Shapery-like fiber formation, strong matrix organization, and low complication rates postoperatively. In contrast, patches such as Restore, TUTOPATCH, OrthADAPT, Diacoll, BioEnthesis, and Rotation Medical Patch were frequently associated with disorganized collagen, inflammatory complications, or a high failure rate.

The Repol Angimesh polypropylene patch delivered low retear rates and noticeable function improvement through mechanical support without provoking inflammation. ROTIUM patch supported partial fibrocartilage restoration and often elicited a prohealing inflammatory response postoperatively.27 Among commercial products evaluated through multiple studies, the top-performing patches material incorporate a porous, minimally cross-linked collagen matrix with balanced mechanical properties. The commercial patches that consistently delivered the most tendon-like structure at the enthesis and most reliable clinical improvements are REGENETEN, ArthroFLEX, and Dynagraft II. Of commercial products that were used in at least 2 studies, Conexa-augmented repairs led to the best outcomes.

Future research should prioritize standardized histologic grading systems, longer-term randomized-controlled trials, and direct comparative studies of commercial implants with biopsy or advanced imaging outcomes. Human clinical studies with tissue analysis are needed to validate and translate the observed histologic benefits into improved functional results and durable repair.

Limitations

Several limitations were noted across the studies included in this review. Substantial heterogeneity in study design, scaffold composition, animal models, and histologic outcome measures limited direct comparisons across studies.3,36, 37, 38, 39,41,43,45,49,53 Most studies had short- to intermediate-term follow-up, with limited data on long-term enthesis remodeling or structural maturation.12,27,38,49,55 In addition, variability in the histologic scoring systems and key endpoint definitions further limits the ability to synthesize and compare quantitative outcomes.32,41,43,52,56

Few studies directly compared multiple scaffold types or commercial products within the same experimental model, limiting the opportunity for head-to-head evaluation.24,32,37, 38, 39,53 Furthermore, human biopsy data were scarce, and the few studies that included them suffered from small sample sizes and considerable inter-patient variability in injury severity, age, and activity level.3,4,10,45 Consequently, while the overall findings strongly support the potential benefits of patch augmentation at the enthesis, these findings should be interpreted with caution when extrapolating to clinical practice, as the majority of supporting data are derived from preclinical models and a limited number of human trials.

Conclusion

The review suggests that patch augmentation may enhance healing at the enthesis after rotator cuff repair, with the strongest histologic benefits seen in collagen-based scaffolds. Commercial implants, such as REGENETEN, Conexa, Permacol, Zimmer Collagen Repair Patch, GraftJacket, and ArthroFLEX, have been associated with denser collagen and more organized matrix than control groups. Continued investigation with standardized histologic scoring, extended follow-up, and direct comparison between leading commercial implants is essential to narrow graft selection. Patch augmentation remains a promising strategy to improve structural and biochemical outcomes in RCT repairs, but thoughtful product selection and individualized clinical judgment remain vital as the field evolves.

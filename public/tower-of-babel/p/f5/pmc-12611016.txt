Her4 in gastric adenocarcinoma and its association with molecular prognostic markers Ki-67 proliferation index and p53 mutational status in Saudis: A study at a tertiary care hospital in Eastern Saudi Arabia
Authors: Ayesha Ahmed
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:Gastric cancer is the fifth most common and third leading cause of cancer death worldwide. Her4 receptor has proapoptotic and antiproliferative activity. The role of Her4 in gastric carcinogenesis and its prognostic implications have not been much investigated, as evidenced by the paucity of overall and Saudi regional-based studies. This study aimed to highlight the expression of Her4 in gastric adenocarcinoma (GA) as well as its association with clinicopathological parameters and molecular prognostic markers Ki-67 and p53 in a Saudi cohort.MATERIALS AND METHODS:In this descriptive cross-sectional study, data were retrospectively obtained by selecting all cases of GA from 2012 to 2022, for which Her4 immunohistochemical (IHC) analysis was done by total population sampling technique from the Pathology Department archives of King Fahd Hospital of the University, Al-Khobar, Saudi Arabia. Patients’ demographic and clinicopathological profile, Lauren’s histological classification, and IHC data for Ki-67, p53, and Her2-neu were also retrieved. Her4 positivity was recalibrated and its association with clinicopathological parameters, Ki-67, p53 and Her2-neu was determined. Data analysis performed using SPSS; Chi-square test assessed the relationship between Ki-67 proliferation index and p53 mutation status in Her4-negative cases.RESULTS:Out of the 50 cases of GA selected, Her4-positive cases were 22% (n = 11), and in these Her4-positive cases, 63.6% had intestinal-type morphology, 54.5% were high-grade tumors, male preponderance was 63.6%, the median age was 73 years, and the mean age was 74 years (standard deviation [SD]: 9.3). 3 + positivity was seen at the mean age of 69 years (SD: 14.6), but there was one case at 53 years. A high Ki-67 proliferation index was present in 63.6% and a predominant aberrant/mutational p53 mutation status in 54.4% of the cases.CONCLUSION:Her4 frequency of 22% is lower than other reported studies underlining the concept of geographical and ethnic variation in intrinsic tumor properties. Her4 expression with a higher tumor grade, high Ki-67 proliferation index, and aberrant/mutational p53 status imparts an aggressive tumor characteristic to this set of GA cases.

Introduction

Gastric cancer ranks as the fifth most common cancer, with 968,784 new cases diagnosed globally in 2022. It is also the third main cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide, with 660,175 deaths reported in 2022.[1] In Saudi Arabia, it ranks as the seventh leading cause of cancer-associated death with 2.7% of newly diagnosed cases each year.[2]

Gastric carcinogenesis involves a group of four closely related epidermal growth factor receptors (EGFRs): Her1, Her2, Her3, and Her4. Their corresponding genes are dissimilar, but they all share a structural similarity, each with an extracellular ligand-associated component, an intramembranous lipophilic part, an intracellular region that has tyrosine kinase activity, and a carboxyl-terminal domain.[3] The EGFRs are involved in closely intertwined and integrated signaling pathways, i.e., the phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase/Akt (PKB) pathway with its cellular survival role and Ras/Raf/MEK/ERK1/2 and phospholipase C gamma pathways that have an impact on the proliferation of cells. These pathways also influence cellular motility, invasiveness, and angiogenesis[4] that lead to the development and progression of multiple solid malignancies.

The functioning and clinical implications of Her1 and Her2 have been extensively studied. Her3 with its deficient intrinsic tyrosine kinase activity is an efficient partner of Her2 that has no known ligand. Her2–Her3 union is required for activation of the downstream pathways.[5] Her3 has been documented as being associated with an aggressive tumor behavior and poor patient prognostics. Her4 receptor is unique as it has been shown to have a tumor protective role by virtue of its proapoptotic and antiproliferative activity.[6] In cell line experiments, an antagonist and tumor suppressant role of Her4 overexpression on Her2 signaling activity has been found.[7] A total of seven growth factors can activate the receptor.

Four Her4 receptor isoforms (JMa or JMb, Cyt1 or Cyt2) have been documented as being formed as a result of alternative splicing of Her4 mRNA.[8] A multistep receptor cleavage of receptor domains comprising an initial cleavage of the extracellular domain of Her4 JMa isoform created by metalloproteinase tumor necrosis factor-alpha-converting enzyme is followed by a second enzymatic cleavage within the cellular membrane. This release of 4ICD, a soluble Her4 intracellular domain, into the cytoplasm[9] may either remain cytoplasmic or show nuclear translocation or may accumulate in the mitochondria. This intracellular domain can potentially activate multifarious processes such as cellular differentiation, proliferation, cell cycle arrest, proapoptotic pathway activation, and transcription modulation. Intramitochondrial localization has a tumor cell proapoptotic influence through BH3 domain.[6] The intranuclear localization has a direct interaction with estrogen receptor (ER)-associated ligand activation, leading to the proliferation of ER-positive breast tumor cells.[10]

The role of Her4 in gastric carcinogenesis and its prognostic implications has barely been investigated. There is a general and Saudi regional-based paucity of studies on Her4 in GA. In this study, we analyzed the expression patterns of Her4 by immunohistochemical (IHC) analysis in a Saudi Arabian study of GA patients. Association of Her4 with clinicopathological parameters and prognostic markers p53 mutation status and Ki-67 proliferation index was also evaluated to discover the role of Her4 in patients’ prognostics.

Materials and Methods

The data for this descriptive (cross-sectional) study were obtained retrospectively by reviewing archives of the Pathology Department of King Fahd Hospital of the University, Al-Khobar, Saudi Arabia. Ethical approval was obtained from the Institutional Review Board Vide Letter No. IRB-2024-01-306, dated 18/04/2024, with a waiver of informed consent since there was no direct relation with human subjects in this study.

All cases of GA that had Her4 IHC analysis done from 2012 to 2022 were selected from the pathology archives by a total population sampling technique. Cases for which this analysis had not been done were excluded from the study. Patients’ demographic and clinicopathological profile, Lauren’s histological classification, and IHC data for Ki-67 proliferation index, p53 mutation status, and Her2-neu were also retrieved. Her4 positivity was recalibrated in all these cases, and its association with clinicopathological parameters, Ki-67 and p53 was determined. Her4-positive cases that were evaluated for Her2-neu simultaneous expression were also retrieved to complete the descriptive analysis in the study.

The cases consisted of gastric resection specimens and endoscopic biopsies that fulfilled the inclusion criteria, that consisted of availability of relevant clinicopathological data and the presence of hematoxylin and eosin (H and E)-stained slides for GA along with IHC slides for Her4, Her2-neu, Ki-67 proliferation index, and p53 mutation status.

The routine H and E slides were examined by light microscopy and histologically designated into intestinal or diffuse types based on Lauren’s classification.[11] Interpretation for Her4, Her2-neu, Ki-67 proliferation index, and p53 mutation status was carried out as follows:

A positive Her4 immunostaining was taken as both membranous and/or cytoplasmic immunostaining. A scoring system ranging from score 0 to 3 was utilized.[12] Immunostaining seen in at least a group of five tumor cells, or at least in 10% of the tumor cells, in endoscopic biopsies and gastric resection specimens, respectively, was the prerequisite for evaluation and scoring. Score 0 implied completely negative staining, score 1 was faint or barely perceptible partial staining, score 2 meant weak-to-moderate complete/incomplete membranous or cytoplasmic, and score 3 signified strong intense complete membranous or cytoplasmic staining. Her4 positivity was attributed to scores 2 and 3. Scores 0 and 1 were considered negative Her4.

The same scoring methodology was utilized for Her2-neu as was used for Her4. Only membranous staining, however, was taken as positive.[13] p53 IHC analysis and interpretation were categorized into three types.[14]

Wild (usual) type that demonstrated variable scattered nuclear immunoreactivity in tumor cells. Overexpression implied strong diffuse nuclear immunoreactivity in all tumor cells, and null type showed complete absence of nuclear immunoreactivity in all tumor cells. Both overexpression and null types were categorized as aberrant (mutational) types.[15]

Ki-67 IHC analysis and interpretation[16] were again grouped into the following three categories: low proliferation index signifying <10% of tumor cell nuclei showing immunoreactivity, moderate proliferation index implying 10%–40% of tumor cell nuclei showing immunoreactivity, and a high proliferation index signifying more than 40% of tumor cell nuclei showing the immunoreactivity.

Data were analyzed using the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences software version 19.0 (SPSS, IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY: USA). Descriptive statistics were utilized for the calculation of frequencies (percentages) for immunoexpression of Her4, Ki-67 proliferation index, and p53 mutation status. The frequency of Ki-67 proliferation index and p53 mutation status was calculated for Her4-positive cases. The Chi-square test was used to assess the relationship between Ki-67 proliferation index and p53 mutation status in Her4-negative cases. P <0.05 was considered statistically significant. The percentage of Her4-positive cases showing simultaneous Her2-neu expression was also calculated.

Discussion

In gastric carcinogenesis, the EGF family of four receptors, Her1, Her2, Her3, and Her4, is functionally and morphologically intricately intertwined. Her1 and Her2 with their functional partner Her3 have been extensively investigated. Anti-Her2-neu therapeutic regimens are common clinical practice. Her4 expressivity pattern and its clinical outcomes remain an enigma as very limited international or Saudi regional studies that unravel its characteristics in gastric carcinogenesis are available.

In our set of GA cases (n = 50), Her4 positivity was seen in 22% (n = 11) cases. A wide range of expressivity patterns is documented in different studies by Hayashi et al.,[17] in a Japanese study demonstrating a very high expression of 86% (n = 116/138); Li et al.,[18] and He et al.,[19] in Chinese studies showed 68.3% (n = 110/161) and 13% (n = 66/498), respectively. The prevalence shown in a Greek study was 32.9% (n = 69/210).[20] Our expressivity pattern is somewhat on the lower end of the spectrum. The concept of regional variation in cancer characteristics may be attributed to this variation. Tumors are known to have intrinsic, unique geographical variation in characteristics. A low Her4 expression could be due to inherent gastric carcinoma features indigenous to this region. The Japanese study by Hayashi et al.,[17] conducted in 2008 could have a very high expressivity pattern owing to interpretational guidelines that have become more delineated over time. In addition, the type of kits employed and interobserver variability may also be a reason for such a high representation that is far beyond the range of levels reported in other studies. More comparative studies with larger samples need to be conducted to unravel these tumoral characteristics.

A variation in expressivity pattern in different studies may also be attributed to specimen type. In our study, 91% (n = 10) of cases were biopsy specimens. Li et al., and He et al.,[1819] conducted studies on radical gastrectomy specimens. In biopsy specimens, only a group of five positive tumor cells is required immunohistochemically to attribute Her4 positivity. A resection specimen requires 10% of the tumor cells to be positive. Tumors are not homogeneous in their morphological expression. To overcome the possibility of the overall tumor characteristics not being adequately represented in a small biopsy specimen, the modus operandi is to take 4–5 random specimens from different tumor sites. Still, the chances are that some characteristics of the tumor would be underrepresented in the biopsy and thus account for a low or a variable expressivity pattern.

A predominantly intestinal type of morphology seen was 63.6% (n = 7) and the diffuse type in 36.4% (n = 4). He et al.,[19] and Makatsoris et al.,[20] also reported a prominence of intestinal-type morphology (56%). Other studies reported that the diffuse type at 69%[17] and 87%[18] was the most common. Each of these GA morphological subtypes has its own etiological, molecular, and clinicopathological profile, with diffuse type being clinically aggressive with a poor patient outcome.[21] The molecular landscape of the diffuse-type gastric carcinoma has now been shown to also involve increased expression of fibroblast-derived growth factors FGFR1, 2, and 4 in comparison to intestinal type GA, which is limited to only increased FGFR4. This expansive FGFR pathway involvement in diffuse type has been shown to impart tumor aggressiveness as depicted by the depth of tumor invasion, nodal and distant metastasis, increased pathological staging, disease recurrence, and poor disease-specific survival.[22] These observations mandate a more extensive analysis of multiple pathways that are involved or associated with different morphological variants of GA so that specific targeted antipathway therapies may be developed to improve the patients’ prognostics. In this regard, antifibroblast growth factors receptor-2 isoform IIIb are currently the most attractive therapeutic targets.[21]

In our cohort of cases, Her4 positivity was seen mostly in males, 63.6% (n = 7). Other studies have also shown a male predilection of 82%,[18] 66%,[17] and 60%.[19] Our mean age for Her4-positive cases was 74 years (SD: 9.3 years). A variable mean age, 80 years[17] and 45 years,[19] was reported in other studies. Her4 with 3+ positivity had a comparatively younger mean age in our study, with one case reported at 53 years. Her4 with 2+ score was seen at a higher mean age, with one case seen at 88 years of age. All our cases with 3+ scoring were high-grade tumors. No other studies pertaining to the association of age with Her4 scoring intensity and grade of the tumor are available. However, some studies have shown Her4 to be associated with increased tumor invasiveness and peritoneal metastasis. Intense scoring with high-grade tumors may be associated with a more aggressive clinical course by strongly activating specific molecular pathways. These are many facets that need to be further investigated by more extensive studies to ascertain a better overall picture of Her4-positive cancer dynamics.

Ki-67 proliferation index is generally reflective of tumor aggressiveness and is known to be highly associated with tumor development, progression, invasion, metastasis, and poor prognosis in multiple malignancies. It has proved to be an independent prognostic marker in breast cancer patients.[23] In gastric cancer, however, the results of association of Ki-67 with tumor clinicopathological behavior and outcome are controversial.[2425] Wei et al., demonstrated that Ki-67 had no significant association with postsurgical survival.[26] A meta-analysis documented that Ki-67 could serve as a predictive biomarker for poor prognosis in GA patients.[27] We had an overall high Ki-67 proliferation index in around 63.6% (n = 7) of Her4-positive cases. Whether this set of our cases could be a predictor of poor prognosis or survival, remains an aspect that needs more in depth research and insight.

In our study, p53 in Her4-positive cases was mainly of the aberrant/mutational type, 54.4% (n = 6) of cases. Gastric cancer ranks among the most frequently p53-mutated solid tumors,[28] and Her4 has been shown to inhibit p53 pathway.[29] However, no association between Her4 and p53 in GA has been documented in any other study to the best of our knowledge. p53 is a multifunctional tumor suppressor gene that downregulates vascular-derived growth factor (VEGF). Loss of p53 leads to the activation of VEGF that promotes angiogenesis. Recently, VEGF/VEGF receptor inhibitor has been instituted in clinical trials for patients in advanced gastric carcinoma with p53 mutation.[30] Could our Her4 positive, p53-mutant cases be a potential beneficial target for such a therapeutic regimen, yet to be unraveled.

In our study, no co-expression of Her4 with Her2-neu was seen. Concomitant presence of Her4 with Her2-neu has been documented to incite trastuzumab resistance, the only targeted drug approved for Her2-positive GA. This resistance is implicated mainly by Her4-yes-associated protein1 (YAP1) axis that can induce epithelial myoepithelial transition resulting in the emergence of tumor cells that can escape the therapeutic effect of trastuzumab.[31]

Conclusion

Her4 frequency of 22% (n = 11) is somewhat on the lower end of the spectrum compared to other studies accentuating the concept of intrinsic, ethnic, and geographical variation in tumor characteristics. The association of Her4 expression with a higher tumor grade, a high Ki-67 proliferation index, and aberrant/mutation p53 status imparts an aggressive tumor characteristic. Our data are very limited, but it provides a nidus on which further investigations and studies can be based to elucidate the long-term clinicopathological outcome of Her4-positive GA.

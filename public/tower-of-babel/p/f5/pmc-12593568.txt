Integrated lipidomics and transcriptomics to elucidate heterogeneity between intramuscular and abdominal fat and identify serum and liver markers for their differential deposition in chickens
Authors: Zhongzhen Lin, Xiaoyan Song, Jialei Chen, Jie Qu, Chaowu Yang, Lingqian Yin, Donghao Zhang, Yuan Tang, Chunlin Yu, Yiping Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractDuring broiler production, moderate intramuscular fat (IMF) deposition improves meat quality, whereas excessive abdominal fat (AF) increases production cost. However, reliable molecular markers associated with IMF and AF-specific deposition remain lacking in broiler breeding. To address this, we conducted targeted lipidomic analyses of AF, IMF, serum, and liver, along with transcriptomic analyses of AF and IMF. The results revealed that IMF contained more sphingolipids and fatty acyls, but fewer glycerolipids and sterol lipids than AF. It also had a higher proportion of unsaturated fatty acids. Serum glucose and PS(18:1/22:3) were key molecules for the specific deposition of IMF and AF. HexCer(d18:1/22:0) and PE(16:1/20:1), synthesized in the liver, were directly transported to AF, while Acar(18:0), FFA(20:3), and seven other lipids were transported directly to IMF. PRKAG1 was identified as a regulator of lipid-specific deposition. These findings provide valuable targets for improving meat quality in chickens.Keywords: Lipid metabolism, Meat quality, Regulatory network, Poultry

Introduction

Chicken meat is one of the primary sources of animal protein in the human diet, and its global consumption has shown a steady upward trend (Fei et al., 2023). To meet production demands, broiler breeding over the past few decades has mainly focused on improving growth rate and feed conversion efficiency (Lin et al., 2022). For example, in white feather broilers, body weight at 42 d of age can exceed 2.5 kg (Wang et al., 2024). The moderate deposition of intramuscular fat (IMF) can significantly improve meat color (Calnan et al., 2017), enhance tenderness (Lopez-Pedrouso et al., 2023), enrich flavor (Hocquette et al., 2010), and increase juiciness (Frank et al., 2016). However, the rapid growth rate and early slaughter age of broilers often result in insufficient lipid deposition in muscle, thereby compromising meat quality (Tian et al., 2021). In addition, the rapid weight gain in broilers is often accompanied by excessive abdominal fat (AF) deposition (Liu et al., 2023), which not only reduces feed efficiency and increases production costs, but also complicates processing during slaughter, thereby raising processing costs and adding to the environmental burden (Nematbakhsh et al., 2021). Therefore, how to increase IMF deposition while reducing AF accumulation remains a key scientific and technological challenge limiting the production of high-quality broilers.

Lipids in chickens are primarily found in adipose tissue, muscle, and ovaries during the laying period. Depending on the deposition site, adipose tissue can be classified into AF, subcutaneous fat, IMF, and intermuscular fat (Li et al., 2020). AF is the predominant site of fat deposition, while IMF and intermuscular fat are often collectively referred to as IMF in research due to their difficulty in being separated and their beneficial effects on meat quality (Hausman et al., 2014). Both AF and IMF in chickens are classified as white adipose tissue, serving as energy reserves. However, their lipid compositions differ significantly. Compared to AF, IMF contains a higher proportion of glycerophospholipids (GP) and a lower proportion of triglyceride (TG) (Wang et al., 2023).

Lipid metabolism in animals is divided into exogenous and endogenous lipid metabolism. In chickens, exogenous lipid metabolism refers to the digestion and absorption of dietary lipids in the small intestine, with most of these lipids entering the portal venous system in the form of very low-density lipoproteins (VLDL) to participate in circulation (Butler, 1975). The liver is the primary organ for endogenous lipid metabolism in chickens, and it also carries out more than 90 % of de novo fatty acid synthesis, providing substrates for the assembly of lipid molecules (Leveille et al., 1975). The liver forms VLDL by binding lipid molecules with apolipoproteins, which are then transported through the plasma to other tissues for deposition. Unlike mammals, chickens have a relatively weak ability of de novo fatty acid synthesis in adipose tissue and primarily utilize fatty acids from the plasma to assemble glycerolipids (GL) (Leveille et al., 1975), indicating that their lipid deposition is more dependent on the supply from the liver.

Daheng broilers are high-quality broilers developed through moderate selective breeding, combining the fast growth rate of white feather broilers with the excellent meat quality of indigenous breeds (Li et al., 2022b; Lin et al., 2023). Males weigh approximately 1.82 kg, and females weigh around 1.46 kg at 90 d. They serve as an ideal biological model for this study. Therefore, we collected the breast muscle, AF, serum, and liver of Daheng broilers for targeted lipidomic analysis, comparing the lipid profile differences between IMF and AF at the absolute lipid content level, and identifying key lipids derived from serum and liver. In addition, we also performed transcriptome analysis on IMF and AF to analyze the differential regulatory networks. Our results will identify key lipids in IMF, AF, serum, and liver that are involved in the specific deposition of IMF and AF, as well as the key genes in IMF and AF. These findings may provide valuable targets for improving meat quality in chickens.

Materials and methods

Animals and samples collection

The animal use protocol was approved by the Sichuan Agricultural University Animal Ethical and Welfare Committee (approval no. 20240170). A total of 800 female Daheng broilers were divided into 80 groups, with 10 replicates per group. All birds were reared under uniform conditions at Sichuan Daheng Poultry Breeding Co., Ltd. (Chengdu, China). At 90 d of age, one healthy bird was randomly selected from each of the 31 groups for slaughter. AF, breast muscle, serum, and liver were collected for subsequent experiments.

Phenotypic trait measurement

The live weight of each experimental individual was measured before slaughter. The AF content was determined by the formula: (AF weight / live weight) × 100 % (Chao et al., 2024). Crude fat in the breast muscle was extracted using the Soxhlet extractor method and weighed. The IMF content was calculated using the formula: (crude fat weight / breast muscle weight) × 100 %.

Lipidomic analysis

The AF, breast muscle, serum, and liver were subjected to targeted lipidomic sequencing based on a Liquid Chromatography-Mass Spectrometry (LC-MS) platform. Accurate identification and absolute quantification of the lipids were achieved through stable isotope internal standards and response factors. In brief, the raw data were processed using the XCMS package for automated batch procedures including peak extraction, alignment, identification, and area integration. The integrated peak areas were then applied to calculation formulas (target lipid peak area / internal standard peak area × internal standard concentration × final volume / sample mass) to determine the corresponding lipid concentrations. Lipid concentrations were expressed as ng/g, representing the weight of lipids per gram of sample.

Screening of key lipids in abdominal fat (AF) and intramuscular fat (IMF)

Venn analysis was performed on the lipids identified in AF and IMF to distinguish tissue-specific and shared lipids. Correlation analysis was performed between tissue-specific lipids and three phenotypic traits to identify key lipids. Lipids were considered key candidates if AF-specific lipids were significantly correlated with AF content, or IMF-specific lipids were significantly correlated with IMF content. For the shared lipids, differential expression analysis was conducted. First, principal component analysis (PCA) was performed to examine the variation between and within sample groups. Next, differential lipids were selected based on the expression matrix, with the selection criteria being a fold change < 0.83 or > 1.20, variable importance in projection (VIP) > 1, and p < 0.05. Finally, these differential lipids were subjected to statistical analysis for carbon chain length and unsaturation, as well as Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis.

Serum biochemical index detection

The measured serum biochemical parameters included glucose (GLU), TG, PL, free fatty acid (FFA), total cholesterol (TC), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), VLDL. The first five serve as the main substrates or structural components for lipid droplet formation, whereas the latter three reflect the lipid transport capacity in plasma. Among them, the assay kits for GLU, TG, TC, HDL-C, and LDL-C were purchased from Shenzhen Rayto Life Sciences Co., Ltd. (Shenzhen, China). The assay kit for FFA was purchased from Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute (Nanjing, China). The contents of PL and VLDL were measured using Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbnent Assay (Keqiao, Xiamen, China). The above experimental procedures were carried out according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Screening of key lipids in serum and liver

Venn analysis was performed on the lipids in AF, serum, and liver. After removing the shared lipids among the three from the serum lipids, correlation analysis was performed with the three phenotypic traits. Serum lipids significantly correlated with AF content were retained. Similarly, after removing the shared lipids among the three from the liver lipids, correlation analysis was also performed with the three phenotypic traits. Liver lipids significantly correlated with AF content were retained. The same analysis strategy was replicated for the lipids in IMF, serum, and liver. For the shared lipids, correlation analysis was performed on their expression levels across the three tissues. The lipids that were significantly positively correlated between liver and serum, and significantly correlated between serum and AF or IMF, were selected and visualized using a Sankey diagram.

Transcriptomic analysis

Bulk RNA-seq was performed on AF and breast muscle samples. Total RNA was extracted from the tissues, and DNase treatment was performed to remove any DNA, followed by library construction. The constructed libraries were quality-checked using the Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer (Santa Clara, CA, USA), and those passing quality control were sequenced using the Illumina HiSeq X-ten platform (San Diego, CA, USA) for high-throughput sequencing. After sequencing, raw reads were subjected to quality control to obtain clean reads. The clean reads were then aligned to the reference genome (GRCg6a) to identify the expression abundance of protein-coding genes, and gene expression levels were estimated by the FPKM method.

Screening of differentially expressed genes

Gene expression differences were analyzed using the DESeq2 package, which calculates fold changes and associated p-values based on a negative binomial distribution model. The p-values were modified using the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure to minimize the false discovery rate, and the resulting values were considered q-values. The selection criteria for differentially expressed genes were a fold change < 0.5 or > 2, and q < 0.05. The differentially expressed genes were subjected to Gene Ontology (GO) and KEGG enrichment analysis.

Integrated analysis of lipidomics and transcriptomics

Correlation analysis was performed between the expression levels of all lipids and all genes. Pairs with a |correlation| > 0.8 and p < 0.05 were retained to generate a nine-quadrant plot. Subsequently, Venn analysis was performed on the KEGG terms enriched in lipids and genes. Among the shared KEGG terms, those closely related to lipid metabolism were selected, and correlation analysis was performed on the lipids and genes significantly enriched in these terms. Finally, the relationship between the top 15 most significantly correlated lipids and genes was visualized using Cytoscape software, with positive correlations represented by yellow lines and negative correlations by blue lines.

Statistical analysis

The results are reported as mean ± standard error (SEM) and primarily visualized using the ggplot2 package in R software. The p-value was calculated using an unpaired t-test. The Pearson or Spearman correlation coefficient was calculated using the psych package and Mantel test was performed using the vegan package. The significance classification was as follows: *p < 0.05; ⁎⁎p < 0.01; ⁎⁎⁎p < 0.001; ⁎⁎⁎⁎p < 0.0001; nsp ≥ 0.05.

Results

Overview of lipid profile in AF and IMF

At the lipid subclass level, among GL, triacylglycerol (TAG) had the highest proportion in IMF, followed by diacylglycerol (DAG) and monoacylglycerol (MAG). In contrast, DAG had the highest proportion in AF, followed by TAG and MAG. For GP, AF was characterized by the presence of lysophosphatidylglycerol (LPG), lysophosphatidylinositol (LPI), and lysophosphatidylserine (LPS), although their proportions were relatively low. In IMF, the three most abundant lipids were phosphatidylcholine (PC), phosphatidylethanolamine (PE), and phosphatidic acid (PA), whereas in AF, they were alkenylphosphatidylethanolamine (PE(P)), PC, and lysophosphatidylethanolamine (LPE). In SP, dihexosylceramide (Hex2Cer) was unique to IMF, while ceramide 1-phosphate (CerP) was unique to AF. Sphingomyeline (SM) was the most abundant subclass in both IMF and AF. For ST, cholesteryl ester (CE) had the highest proportion, while cholesterol (CH) had a lower proportion in IMF compared to AF. In FA, free fatty acid (FFA) predominated, while acylcarnitine (Acar) represented only a small proportion.

Lipid content analysis revealed that the total lipid content in IMF was significantly lower than that in AF (Fig. 1C), indicating that AF had a higher lipid content at equal weight of sample. The contents of SP and FA in IMF were significantly higher than those in AF, while the contents of GL and ST were significantly lower in IMF compared to AF. Although the GP content in IMF was slightly higher than in AF, the difference was not statistically significant.

Screening of key lipids in AF and IMF

Among the AF-specific lipids, nine lipids were significantly positively correlated with AF content, while 15 lipids were significantly negatively correlated (Fig. 2B). It is noteworthy that LPI(18:0), PS(18:1/20:4), and TAG50:0(16:0) exhibited significantly opposite correlations between IMF and AF content, highlighting their roles in the specific deposition of IMF and AF. Among the IMF-specific lipids, Acar(16:1), Acar(16:2), Acar(18:1), Acar(20:2), and PG(16:0/18:0) were significantly positively correlated with IMF content, while MAG(20:3) and SM(d18:1/26:2) were significantly negatively correlated (Fig. 2C).

IMF exhibited significantly higher levels of 12 lipid subclasses compared to AF, including TAG, alkenyllysophosphatidylcholine (LPC(P)), PA, PC, PE, PE(P), phosphatidylglycerol (PG), phosphatidylserine (PS), SM, hexosylceramide (HexCer), FFA, and Acar, while nine lipid subclasses were significantly lower in IMF than in AF, including MAG, DAG, lysophosphatidic acid (LPA), lysophosphatidylcholine (LPC), LPE, ceramide (CER), dihydroceramide (dhCer), CH, and CE (Fig. 3E). Functional characterization of differential lipids between AF and IMF

Carbon chain length distribution analysis revealed that, compared to AF, IMF preferentially deposits TAGs with more than 45 carbon atoms, excluding those with 49 and 53 carbon atoms (Fig. 4A). Additionally, IMF exhibits a preference for depositing LPC with more than 20 carbon atoms, PE with more than 34 carbon atoms, PI with more than 40 carbon atoms, PS with more than 36 carbon atoms, SM with 36 and 40 carbon atoms, and dhCer with 36 carbon atoms.

Total unsaturation distribution analysis revealed that, compared to AF, IMF was less likely to deposit TAGs with unsaturation bonds of 8 and 9, PA with an unsaturation bond of 5, PC with unsaturation bonds of 7 and 8, and FFA with an unsaturation bond of 1 (Fig. 4B). Furthermore, IMF showed a stronger preference for depositing LPC with more than 2 unsaturation bonds, PE with more than 1 unsaturation bond, and CE with more than 4 unsaturation bonds. From the lipid class composition, it can be observed that the majority of SP contain unsaturated bonds (Fig. 4C). Except for FA, the proportion of lipids containing unsaturation bonds in the other four lipid classes was higher in IMF than in AF, indicating that lipids with unsaturation bonds are more readily deposited in IMF.

Functional enrichment analysis revealed that the KEGG terms upregulated in IMF compared to AF included glycerophospholipid metabolism, efferocytosis, fatty acid biosynthesis, and biosynthesis of unsaturated fatty acids, among others (Fig. 4D). In contrast, the downregulated KEGG terms in IMF relative to AF encompassed glycerophospholipid metabolism, efferocytosis, necroptosis, sphingolipid metabolism, adipocytokine signaling pathway, and others. Notably, some pathways, such as glycerophospholipid metabolism and efferocytosis, appeared in both upregulated and downregulated categories, suggesting the involvement of complex regulatory mechanisms.

Screening of lipids favoring AF and IMF deposition in serum and liver

To identify key metabolites and lipids in serum and liver that influence the specific deposition of IMF and AF, relevant analyses were conducted. The results showed that AF content was significantly positively correlated with GLU levels, while IMF content was negatively correlated with GLU levels, but this correlation did not reach statistical significance (Fig. 5A). The GLU content was significantly positively correlated with the levels of TC and LDL-C, and significantly negatively correlated with the levels of FFA. In addition, TG content was significantly positively correlated with LDL-C content, PL content was significantly positively correlated with VLDL content, and TC content was significantly positively correlated with both HDL-C and LDL-C content.

A total of 430 lipids were common to AF, serum, and liver, while 381 lipids were common to IMF, serum, and liver (Fig. 5B). Among the non-common lipids in serum, 16 were significantly correlated with the AF content, and 16 were significantly correlated with the IMF content. It is noteworthy that PS(18:1/22:3) exhibited a significant positive correlation with the AF content and a significant negative correlation with the IMF content (Fig. 5C), which may contribute to the specific deposition of both. Cer(d19:1/24:0) was significantly positively correlated with both AF content and IMF content, and it cannot contribute to specific deposition. Among the non-common lipids in liver, 23 were significantly correlated with the AF content, and 7 were significantly correlated with the IMF content (Fig. 5D).

To screen key lipids directly transported from the liver to adipose tissue, correlation analysis was performed on the common lipids between liver and serum, as well as between serum and AF/IMF. The results showed that HexCer(d18:1/22:0) and PE(16:1/20:1) in AF were significantly positively correlated with the content produced by the liver (Fig. 5E). In IMF, a group of lipids depended on liver-generated content, including Acar(18:0), FFA(20:3), HexCer(d18:1/16:0), PC(14:0/14:0), PC(14:0/18:0), PC(16:0/20:5), PE(16:0/20:5), TAG44:2(16:0), and TAG46:2(16:0) (Fig. 5F). These results clearly demonstrate the pivotal role of hepatic lipids in driving the specific lipid deposition in IMF and AF.

Differential expression analysis of genes regulating AF and IMF deposition

To investigate the regulatory mechanisms of IMF and AF, differential gene expression analysis was performed. The PCA results indicated substantial differences in gene expression profiles between IMF and AF (Fig. 6A). Compared with AF, IMF exhibited 2,883 upregulated genes and 2,878 downregulated genes (Fig. 6B). Cluster analysis separately displayed the expression patterns of the top 25 most significantly upregulated genes in IMF and AF (Fig. 6C). It can be observed that GAPDH was significantly upregulated in IMF, suggesting that it is not suitable as a reference gene for this type of study. Additionally, key lipid metabolism genes such as SCD, GPD1L2, CDS2, and FABP4 were significantly upregulated in AF.

Functional enrichment analysis revealed that the upregulated GO terms in IMF were primarily mitochondrial inner membrane, mitochondrion, and structural constituent of ribosome (Supplementary Fig. 2A), while the downregulated terms were primarily plasma membrane (Supplementary Fig. 2B). These results suggest that, compared to AF, IMF exhibits more active energy metabolism, while there is a reduced involvement of membrane-related substances and signal transduction. The upregulated KEGG terms in IMF were primarily related to oxidative phosphorylation, while the downregulated terms were mainly associated with the calcium signaling pathway and neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction (Fig. 6D), further confirming the previous inference.

The upregulated and downregulated KEGG terms enriched by differentially lipids between IMF and AF were respectively contained within the KEGG terms enriched by differentially expressed genes (Fig. 7B). Among the commonly upregulated KEGG terms, 9 terms were closely related to lipid metabolism (Supplementary Fig. 3A, B), while among the commonly downregulated KEGG terms, 7 terms were closely related to lipid metabolism (Supplementary Fig. 4A, B). A total of 5 KEGG terms were significantly enriched by both differential lipids and differential genes (Fig. 7C), including adipocytokine signaling pathway, MAPK signaling pathway, fatty acid biosynthesis, apelin signaling pathway, adrenergic signaling in cardiomyocytes, and calcium signaling pathway.

In fatty acid biosynthesis, the contents of FFA(18:0) and FFA(16:0) in IMF were significantly higher than those in AF (Fig. 7D). The expression levels of ACSBG1 and ACSL6 were significantly positively correlated with the contents of FFA(18:0) and FFA(16:0), while the expression levels of ACACA, ACACB, ACSL1, ACSL4, ACSL5, and FASN were significantly negatively correlated. The differential lipids enriched in the adipocytokine signaling pathway all had significantly lower content in IMF compared to AF, and the correlation between their levels and the gene expression within this pathway was analyzed (Fig. 7E).

Correlation analysis between the enriched differential genes in these two pathways and the three phenotypic traits revealed that the expression levels of ACSL4 and MAPK10 in AF were significantly positively correlated with AF content, while ACACB, PRKAG1, and STK11 were significantly negatively correlated. In IMF, the expression levels of LOC419429, PRKAG1, and TRADD were significantly positively correlated with IMF content, while ACSBG1 showed a significant negative correlation. Interestingly, PRKAG1 acts as a regulatory factor for the specific deposition of IMF and AF (Fig. 7F). The network diagram showed the relationships between the 15 most significantly correlated lipids and genes (Fig. 7G).

Discussion

To achieve specific deposition of IMF and AF, the heterogeneity of their lipid composition needs to be thoroughly elucidated. In our previous study (Li et al., 2022a), PC was identified as the most abundant lipid subclass in both IMF and AF of three indigenous chicken breeds, including Guangyuan Gray chicken, Jiuyuan Black chicken, and Tibetan chicken, accounting for 40 % and 36 % of the total lipids, respectively. In Gushi chicken, the proportion of PC was also the highest, accounting for 26 % of the total lipids (Wang et al., 2023). However, in this study, the lipid class of GL dominated both IMF and AF in Daheng broiler, accounting for over 84 % of the total lipids, while the GP class contributed only up to 8 %. This suggests that specialized broilers have a stronger capacity for GL deposition compared to indigenous breeds. Moreover, surprisingly, the most abundant GL in AF was MAG rather than TAG. Since DAG is a key intermediate in the synthesis and degradation of TAG, this may be attributed to the accumulation of DAG in AF, resulting from the active lipid metabolism occurring in the tissue.

The differences in lipid composition between IMF and AF are also reflected in their fatty acid profiles. The polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFA) linoleic acid (18:2n-6) and arachidonic acid (20:4n-6) competitively incorporated into phospholipid (PL) molecules, leading to their substantial accumulation in muscle tissue (Wood et al., 2008). Moreover, feeding diets rich in PUFA helped reduce AF deposition in chickens (Fouad and El-Senousey, 2014). Our previous study also demonstrated that PUFA-enriched GP and SP were more abundant in IMF than in AF (Li et al., 2022a). The results of this study indicated that IMF exhibited a higher proportion of unsaturated fatty acids (USFA) across four lipid classes, excluding FA. Taken together, altering the proportion of lipid classes in the diet and increasing the content of USFA may be an effective nutritional strategy for achieving specific deposition of IMF and AF in chickens.

As previously stated, the lipids in chicken IMF and AF mainly originate from the liver. Chickens with a stronger ability to synthesize lipids in the liver tended to have higher IMF content in the breast and leg muscles (Cui et al., 2012). In contrast, after multiple generations of selective breeding for AF in broilers, the AF percentage decreased, along with the liver-synthesized lipid content (Leng et al., 2016). However, the IMF content in the breast and leg muscles increased, suggesting that the deposition of IMF and AF is not solely influenced by the lipid synthesis capacity of liver. Similar findings were also observed in rabbits. Compared to low-IMF lines, high-IMF lines showed significantly higher activity of two key lipogenic enzymes (G6PDH and ME1) in the liver. Moreover, the content of monounsaturated fatty acids (MUFA) in the liver of high-IMF lines was higher (Zubiri-Gaitan et al., 2022), while the abundance of lysophospholipids was greater and the abundance of TG and CH was lower in plasma (Zubiri-Gaitan et al., 2023). These findings are consistent with the higher proportion of GP class and lower proportion of GL class in IMF, suggesting that the types of fatty acids synthesized by the liver may influence the specific deposition of IMF and AF. In this study, a set of relevant lipids was screened from the liver and serum, which can be used as molecular markers to assess IMF and AF deposition capacity.

Distinct gene regulatory networks exist between IMF and AF. Weighted co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) revealed that IMF deposition in Wenchang chickens was linked to pyruvate and citric acid metabolism, primarily through GAPDH and other associated genes (Luo et al., 2022). In contrast, the AF deposition was found to be related to acetyl-CoA and glycerol metabolism, involving SCD and several other genes. These results are generally in agreement with our differential expression analysis. At the cellular level, differentially expressed genes during the differentiation of chicken AF-derived preadipocytes were significantly enriched in the calcium signaling pathway (Zhang et al., 2019). This is consistent with our findings, where both differentially expressed lipids and genes in IMF compared to AF were enriched in the same pathway, with all showing downregulation. As Ca2+ is an essential intracellular second messenger (Bazargani and Attwell, 2016), these results indicate that Ca2+-mediated signal transduction plays a critical role in the process of AF deposition.

The PRKAG1 gene encodes the γ1 regulatory subunit of AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK), a central energy sensor that maintains cellular energy homeostasis (Hardie et al., 2012). The AMPK signaling pathway regulates multiple aspects of lipid metabolism, including synthesis, oxidation, and degradation (Wang et al., 2018). The AMPK signaling activated by Lactobacillus significantly promoted the conversion of fast-twitch to slow-twitch muscle fibers in Sunit sheep (Wang et al., 2022). Similarly, AMPK activation induced by dihydromyricetin exerted comparable effects, thereby markedly improving pork quality (Guo et al., 2022). Currently, studies directly focusing on PRKAG1 remain limited. The expression of PRKAG1 in human tissues declined with age, which was closely associated with the progression of metabolic quiescence and healthy aging (Ripa et al., 2023). In addition, PRKAG1 expression was strongly associated with beef flavor and juiciness (Bernard et al., 2007), suggesting that it played a key role in meat quality regulation. In our study, PRKAG1 was found to improve meat quality in chickens, while reducing AF deposition, which consequently lowered dietary energy waste. In summary, PRKAG1 is a key candidate gene responsible for the differential deposition of IMF and AF, although its specific regulatory mechanisms remain to be further explored.

Conclusions

In conclusion, regarding the composition of lipid classes, IMF contains higher SP and FA content, whereas GL and ST content is lower compared to AF. In terms of fatty acid composition, IMF exhibits a higher proportion of USFA. A series of lipids are identified as potential biomarkers for evaluating the deposition capacity of IMF and AF (Fig. 8). Notably, in serum, GLU and PS(18:1/22:3) are highlighted as key molecules contributing to the tissue-specific accumulation of IMF and AF. In addition, HexCer(d18:1/22:0) and PE(16:1/20:1), synthesized in the liver, are directly transported to AF for deposition, while Acar(18:0), FFA(20:3), and seven other lipids are transported directly to IMF. Furthermore, PRKAG1 is identified as a regulatory gene associated with the differential deposition of IMF and AF. These findings deepen our understanding of the lipid metabolic basis of fat distribution in chickens and highlight PRKAG1 as a potential molecular target for improving meat quality through genetic regulation.

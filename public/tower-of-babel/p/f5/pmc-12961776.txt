Prioritising conditions resulting in chronic lower limb musculoskeletal pain in children and adolescents for development of clinical practice guidelines: an Australian Delphi study
Authors: Luke M Davies, Verity Pacey, Elizabeth Sturgiss, Jane Munro, Terry Haines, Mitchell Smith, Cylie M Williams
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundChildren and adolescents with chronic lower limb pain may experience delayed diagnosis, misdiagnosis, insufficient or inappropriate treatment initiation or type. Developing condition-specific clinical practice guidelines is one approach to address these healthcare challenges. This research aimed to prioritise the top 10 conditions resulting in chronic lower limb musculoskeletal pain in children that would benefit from the development of Australian clinical practice guidelines.MethodsWe conducted a modified three round eDelphi. Three multidisciplinary panels were provided a summary of current available evidence and any clinical practice guidelines currently available for 124 conditions identified as resulting in chronic lower limb pain. Panels rated their agreement on the need for the development of clinical practice guidelines and ranked the top 10 conditions. In the final round, parents of children with chronic lower limb musculoskeletal pain rated the appropriateness of the top 10 conditions for guideline development.ResultsForty-four Australian health care professionals (22 allied health, 7 paediatric medical specialists, and 13 general practitioners) participated in Rounds 1 and 2 with a retention rate of 100%. Sixteen parents participated in the confirmatory round. The top 10 conditions prioritised for clinical practice guideline development were generalised joint hypermobility, growing pains, musculoskeletal pain, Juvenile Idiopathic Arthritis, joint instability, avascular necrosis, Legg-Calve-Perthes, flat foot, slipped capital femoral epiphysis and patellofemoral pain syndrome.ConclusionHealthcare professionals prioritised 10 conditions that require development of clinical practice guidelines. Parents of children and adolescents with chronic lower limb pain provided feedback on the appropriateness of the 10 prioritised conditions for guideline development. These guidelines should be multidisciplinary, co-designed with families and relevant to all health settings.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12887-026-06600-4.Keywords: Paediatric, Chronic Pain, Musculoskeletal, Guideline, Adolescent, Lower limb

Introduction

Childhood chronic musculoskeletal pain impacts the lower limb more than any other part of the body (40% lower limb versus 24% in back) [1]. Childhood chronic pain can occur any time from infancy until the growth period stops at approximately 18 years. It is typically diagnosed when pain lasts for more than three months and has broad ranging impacts on activities of daily living or mental health, frequently impacts sleep quality, ability to participate in sport and attend school [2–5]. Inadequate management of childhood chronic pain can also hinder physical, cognitive, and social development, leading to adulthood disability, comorbidities, and long-term negative consequences across multiple health and socioeconomic outcomes [6].

Children and adolescents with chronic lower limb pain may experience delayed diagnosis, misdiagnosis, or insufficient or inappropriate treatment, often in the face of diagnostic uncertainty [7, 8]. Consequently, it appears that a knowledge-to-practice gap still exists in the management of lower limb conditions that result in chronic pain, despite recent advances in both pain assessment and treatment strategies [7]. One strategy to address such challenges in healthcare provision is development of condition-specific multidisciplinary specific clinical practice guidelines. Such guidelines may assist healthcare professionals implement an evidence-based, consistent approach to optimise the diagnosis and management of chronic lower limb pain in children and adolescents.

Clinical practice guidelines communicate a set of evidence-based standards and recommendations to inform how a condition is described, it’s diagnosis and management of patients with a variety of conditions, regardless of their acuity [9, 10]. Research undertaken with guideline developers and clinicians shows clinical practice guidelines are also useful resources in managing uncommon conditions, improving the quality-of-care delivery, as well as enhancing healthcare outcomes [10, 11]. Currently, there appears to be a lack of clinical practice guidelines to inform healthcare professionals involved in the management of children and adolescents with chronic lower limb musculoskeletal pain. A practical difficulty limiting the development of such a guideline is the extraordinary breadth of lower limb conditions that are associated with chronic musculoskeletal pain in children and adolescents [12]. Therefore, it would arguably be prudent to first develop a prioritised list of conditions that could be the focus of the initial development of such a guideline. In this study, our aim was to prioritise the top 10 conditions resulting in chronic lower limb musculoskeletal pain in childhood considered to benefit from the development of Australian clinical practice guidelines. This focus would enable prioritisation of guideline development for multidisciplinary clinical care of children and adolescents with chronic lower limb musculoskeletal pain.

Methods

Design

We used a modified Delphi methodology as a consensus-based approach to prioritisation. Previously established methodological criteria for reporting Delphi studies were used to promote quality [13, 14]. Ethics approval was obtained from the Human Research Ethics Committee at Monash University (#37082) and participants provided informed online consent.

Participants

We recruited Australian clinicians with paediatric, pain and healthcare expertise. Healthcare professionals were eligible to participate in Round 1 and 2 if they were an Australian paediatric specialist, general practitioner, or allied healthcare professional (physiotherapist, podiatrist, psychologist, chiropractor, pharmacist, or occupational therapist) with experience in managing children and adolescents with chronic lower limb musculoskeletal pain. Parents and caregivers of a child currently experiencing chronic lower limb musculoskeletal pain were eligible to participate in Round 3 to validate these decisions.

Procedure

The research team assembled a purposive sample of potential panel members by searching publications, as well as advertising through paediatric academic, research and peak bodies (e.g. professional association special interest groups). E-mail invitations were sent to identified potential panel members. Advertisements were also placed on social media platforms (LinkedIn, Facebook, X) to recruit additional panel members. Parent/caregivers were recruited through clinicians, the Kids Leg Pain project database (a mailing list of interested parties that have agreed to receive project advertising such as education or research opportunities), and through consumer advocacy group, Musculoskeletal Australia.

Round 1

We used our scoping review findings of conditions causing chronic lower limb pain in childhood to inform conditions presented to the panel in Round 112. This included 124 conditions that were extracted from peer-reviewed literature where the pain description meet the accepted definition of chronic pain, being that it was present for greater than three months, with impacts on activities of daily living and/or mental health. We also provided a brief description about the condition and any evidence on current guidelines for management of the condition. A comprehensive search of the literature, including grey literature, was conducted to provide participants with an overview of the available evidence for each condition. As part of this process, current guideline recommendations were also incorporated, representing a unique feature of the study designed to highlight the breadth of evidence and guidance relevant to these conditions. This search was conducted by members of the research team on Google Scholar and PubMed, searching for the health condition name followed by the terms “diagnosis” OR “management” AND “guideline”. The multidisciplinary research team then reviewed the available evidence and identified any other documents commonly utilised within their health care network or clinical practice which had not been retrieved through the search. This was done by cross checking the documents identified from the literature search with what was currently being used in clinical practice within the multidisciplinary research team. Subsequently, available evidence was reviewed by two team members to select the content that was related to a paediatric population, diagnosis, management and/or considerations for chronic pain management. This was collated into an electronic handbook providing an overview of each condition, summary and links to any currently available evidence for participants in Round 1.

During Round 1, healthcare professionals were provided with an online participant information and consent form, and opted in to provide their demographic data including gender, profession, state and/or territory in which they practice, highest qualification achieved, practice setting, and percentage of their clinical case load being paediatric patients with chronic lower limb pain (via Qualtrics, Provo, UT, USA). Participants were offered an honorarium of $99 for their time in each round. The honorarium amount was guided by the remuneration and reimbursement guidelines for consumer involvement in health and medical research [15]. Healthcare professionals were grouped based on their profession—general practitioners, paediatric specialists, or allied health. Participants answered the following questions (by selecting either Yes or No) for each condition in 1000minds platform (an online program developed for prioritisation, decision making, and conjoint analysis [16]). Consensus was defined as 70% of the panel agreeing ‘yes’ to the following questions:

Does this condition require a statement on consistent terminology/naming convention,

Does this condition require a statement on consistent terminology/naming convention,

Does this condition require a statement on diagnostic criteria,

Does this condition require a statement on diagnostic criteria,

Does this condition require a guideline for treatment, including what outcome measurements should be collected as part of research and/or clinical practice.

Does this condition require a guideline for treatment, including what outcome measurements should be collected as part of research and/or clinical practice.

It was pre-planned by the research team that the 15 highest ranked “important” conditions by the panel would be returned in Round 2 for narrowing to the top 10 with priority ranking.

In Round 2, the summary panel data from the highest ranked 15 conditions from Round 1 were displayed as percentage of agreement alongside each condition. Participants were asked to rank these conditions in order of importance for the development of clinical practice guidelines (1 being the most important and 15 being the least important). We used these responses to develop the overall Top 10 based on the total responses of all participants. In doing this, we took the 10 highest prioritised conditions from ranking. We also undertook a sub-analysis of each of the groups on their Top 10 conditions, as we acknowledged working guidelines may need tailoring for different scopes of practice.

In Round 3, parents of children and adolescents with chronic lower limb musculoskeletal pain attended an hour webinar delivered by the research team and were presented with the overall top 10 conditions identified by healthcare professionals as being the most important for the development of clinical practice guidelines. Participants were provided with a participant information and consent form and invited to provide data specific to their child (age, condition), and feedback on the appropriateness of the conditions identified by the healthcare professionals as requiring clinical guidelines. We used a 5-point Likert scale (ranging from very inappropriate to very appropriate). We also invited parent/caregivers to provide additional comments on this process and the conditions. Participants were offered an honorarium in the amount of $99 for their time in completing this round.

Data analysis

Demographic data were downloaded from Qualtrics and imported into the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS; Version 28, IBM) software to generate descriptions of participants (frequencies and percentage).

We used the 1000Minds platform “mid-rank” algorithm based on participant responses to the 3 questions to calculate the top 15 conditions in Round 1. The software used an analytical approach to calculate the “mid-rank” scores based on individual ratings of each question [15]. For example, a participant response that indicated need for consistent terminology, diagnostic criteria and guideline was ranked higher than a participant response indicating a need for two of the three questions. In Round 2, we used a total score approach where the lowest score being the highest ranked condition. Sensitivity analyses were undertaken in Round 2 to determine whether weighting the responses of different professional groups influenced the rankings of the overall top 10 conditions. This step was considered important given our convenience sampling approach led to disproportionately greater representation in our participant sample by some professional discipline groupings than others. However, doing so revealed that weighting the scores of each group separately by a factor of the number of participants in each group, did not change the conditions selected in the overall top 10, nor which conditions were considered the top 3. Weighting resulted in minor re-ordering of conditions ranked from four to ten. In light of these minor differences between unweighted and weighted ranked, we decided to use the original unweighted rankings to determine the overall top 10 conditions for all healthcare professionals.

In addition, ordinal data from 5-point Likert scales were downloaded from Qualtrics and imported into the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS; Version 28, IBM) software to determine how appropriate the development of guidelines was for the overall top 10 conditions (frequencies and percentage).

Discussion

This is the first study to highlight which conditions resulting in chronic lower limb pain in children and adolescents are prioritised by healthcare professionals, and appropriate by families, for the development of consensus in terminology, diagnostic criteria, treatment measures and outcome measures within clinical practice guidelines in Australia. Similarity between the Top 10 conditions identified by the different groups of healthcare professionals demonstrates that regardless of the profession/scope of practice, clinical practice guidelines are welcomed.

Despite differences in scope of practice, a number of conditions prioritised for clinical practice guideline development were shared across healthcare professional groups. Four of the top five conditions, generalised joint hypermobility, growing pains, primary musculoskeletal pain, and lower limb joint instability, were common across all health professional groups. A further four conditions, juvenile idiopathic arthritis, Legg-Calve-Perthes, patellofemoral pain syndrome (also termed patellofemoral pain) and avascular necrosis, were common across two of the three professional groups. The fact these conditions have been identified as priorities for clinical practice guideline development highlights challenges faced across healthcare professionals in managing these conditions in daily practice. Development of evidence-based clinical practice guidelines that are applicable for multiple health professional groups are required.

Clinical practice guidelines are defined as “systematically developed statements to assist practitioner and patient decisions about appropriate healthcare for specific clinical circumstances” [17]. Clinical practice guidelines designed with research and clinical expertise promote evidence-based consistent clinical practice, improve the quality of care, minimise health waste, and improve consistency in practice, research and therefore outcomes for families [18–20]. In addition to providing health professionals a streamlined blueprint for the diagnosis and management of specific conditions, clinical practice guidelines also place the patient in the driver's seat, promoting shared decision- making [18]. This is especially important in chronic pain relating to lower limb conditions, given its prevalence and at times, diagnostic uncertainty [1, 21]. For example, growing pains, despite its presence in coding systems, it remains variable in its diagnosis and subsequent treatment [22]. The purpose of a clinical practice guideline is not to dictate how healthcare professionals manage their patients, but rather provide explicit evidence-based recommendations on a specific clinical area with the goal of enhancing quality of care and clinical decisions to ensure appropriate and effective treatment strategies [18–20].

Currently, established clinical practice guidelines exist for healthcare professionals when managing various conditions associated with chronic lower limb pain, such as femoroacetabular impingement [23], patellofemoral instability [24], ankle sprains [25], and patellofemoral pain [26]. The majority of these guidelines focus primarily on adults, neglecting inclusion of a paediatric population. Consequently, there is a clear absence of comprehensive guidelines synthesising existing knowledge for managing lower limb conditions that result in chronic musculoskeletal pain in children and adolescents. Future research should focus on development of clinical practice guidelines that are applicable and adaptable to a broad range of healthcare professions to improve the management of children and adolescents who are impacted by chronic lower limb musculoskeletal pain.

Strengths of our study include a diverse range of healthcare professionals with exceptional retention rates. We retained all participants throughout both survey rounds. Although there is no ideal number for Delphi panel sizes [13] given the specificity of our investigation some may consider our Delphi panel moderate or large. The strong representation of parents of children and adolescents with chronic lower limb musculoskeletal pain within our study may also be considered a strength, but this is countered by an absence of children’s and adolescent voices. It is unknown what impact including children and adolescents may have had on the results, and if they would have been able to consider conditions outside of those that there were impacted by for priority setting. Given that healthcare professionals in our study were only from Australia, generalisability to countries with different health care systems is limited. For example, the prioritisation of conditions for the development of clinical practice guidelines may differ in low-to-middle economies where health contexts, service providers or access to health services varies. The under-representation of medical professionals across the breadth of medicine may also be considered a limitation.

Future translational research can draw on the differences we identified between the different health professional groups and which conditions they prioritised. This may be due to the differing scopes of practice, training of the health professional, or frequency of the condition presenting. These different priorities present as an opportunity to create supports that are unique to those health professionals.

Conclusion

Chronic lower limb musculoskeletal pain in children and adolescents is currently under-recognised and poorly represented in evidence and guidelines available to help guide health professionals and families. Health professionals prioritised 10 conditions that require urgent development of clinical practice guidelines for the assessment, diagnosis and management of chronic pain in the lower limb. Families of children and adolescents with chronic pain also supported this. These guidelines should be multidisciplinary, co-designed with families, and relevant to all health settings in order to improve care for this vulnerable group.

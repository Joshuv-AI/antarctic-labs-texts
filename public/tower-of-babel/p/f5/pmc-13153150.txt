An improved whale optimization algorithm for flexible job shop scheduling problems with machine deterioration effects
Authors: Kun Li, Silong Tian
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTargeting the characteristics of performance degradation caused by prolonged machine operation in real production, this article examines the flexible job shop scheduling problem under machine deterioration effects, develops the FJSP-MDE mathematical model to minimize makespan, and presents an Improved Whale Optimization Algorithm (IWOA) to solve the problem. The algorithm employs a hybrid population initialization strategy to generate high-quality initial solutions during the initialization phase. Nonlinear convergence factors and inertia weighting strategies are designed to balance global search and local exploitation capabilities. Stochastic differential variance and golden sinusoidal strategies are designed to enhance population diversity and expand the search range. Finally, the feasibility and effectiveness of the IWOA algorithm are proved by simulation experiments.Keywords: Flexible job shop scheduling, Machine deterioration effect, Whale optimization algorithm, MakespanSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Being a core problem in industrial production management, production scheduling has gradually evolved from the traditional Job Shop Scheduling Problem (JSSP) to the Flexible Job Shop Scheduling Problem (FJSP). FJSP provides a flexible approach that allows various machines to perform multiple processes, which better aligns with the characteristics of diverse resource allocation in real production compared to the singular resource allocation constraints of traditional models. The incorporation of flexibility not only increases machine utilization flexibility but also enhances schedule adaptability and overall productivity. FJSP is a standard NP-hard issue with a more complicated solution procedure since it involves the dual tasks of machine allocation and process scheduling. This challenge is widespread across various industrial sectors, including semiconductor production, automotive assembly, and chemical processing, and has emerged as a key area of focus in manufacturing systems research.

In the actual production and processing process, long-time operation will lead to problems such as aging and wear and tear of the machine, which will also lead to a decrease in its production capacity. As a result, for the same machine, the actual processing time of the subsequent machining process often exceeds its standard processing time, a phenomenon known as the deterioration effect of the machine. Gupta et al.1 initially introduced the single machine scheduling problem incorporating the deterioration impact of the machine, prompting numerous researchers to conduct extensive study on various shop floor scheduling scenarios that account for this deterioration effect. Guo et al.2 investigated the parallel machine scheduling problem incorporating a phase deterioration effect on devices, developed a mixed-integer programming model, and introduced a mixed discrete cuckoo search algorithm for its resolution. The solution’s quality is enhanced by refined heuristics, discrete operators, and local search techniques. Experimental results indicate that the suggested technique surpasses both the commercial solver CPLEX and the discrete cuckoo search algorithm. Liu et al.3 studied the single-machine sequencing problem with linear deterioration effects and optional maintenance activities. The main objective of the study is to determine the optimal processing sequence for one type of job as well as the optimal insertion location of the maintenance activities to minimize makespan, and three solution strategies are proposed: Heuristic Algorithm, Tabu Search Algorithm, and Branch-and-Bound Algorithm are employed to get an approximation optimal solution within a tolerable timeframe. Santos et al.4 examined the uncorrelated parallel machine scheduling problem with respect to machine deterioration and developed a mixed integer nonlinear programming model to minimize makespan and overall delay time. They introduced an iterative local search algorithm utilizing a decomposition method (ILS/D) and evaluated its efficacy against the conventional MOEA/D algorithm and the non-dominated sorting genetic algorithm II (NSGA-II). The findings demonstrate that the ILS/D method significantly significantly superior the other two traditional techniques. Wu et al.5 investigated the two-stage assembly scheduling problem with the linear deterioration effect of the machining time, for a two-stage assembly production model containing two primary machining machines and one subsequent assembly machine. To minimize the total completion time, the authors introduced an enhanced branch-and-bound algorithm to achieve an exact solution, given the NP-hard nature of the problem. Furthermore, the study presents three meta-heuristics: the Dynamic Differential Evolutionary Algorithm (DDE), the Simulated Annealing Algorithm (SA), and the Cloud Model-based Simulated Annealing Algorithm (CSA), aimed at attaining a near-optimal solution to the problem. Liu et al.6 introduced a flow shop scheduling model that incorporates the interplay of equipment degradation, preventive maintenance, and production scheduling, along with a local search strategy (NSGA-II-ALS) that improves the algorithm’s optimality by identifying connected and sparse points in the non-dominated sorting outcomes. The enhanced NSGA-II-ALS algorithm has been experimentally demonstrated to substantially surpass existing comparison algorithms and can adeptly manage the intricate interplay among production scheduling, equipment maintenance, and performance deterioration. Arık7 investigated a location-dependent replacement flow shop scheduling problem that encompasses both learning and linear degradation effects. He developed a mixed-integer nonlinear programming model for this issue and introduced a population-based forbidden search (TSPOP) algorithm, which integrates population concepts and evolutionary strategies such as crossover and mutation. The results demonstrate that the TSPOP algorithm substantially surpasses other existing methods regarding solution quality, as evidenced by experimental evaluations on test problems of varying sizes. JIANG et al.8 investigated the green flexible job shop scheduling problem, incorporating linear deterioration effects and transport time. They constructed a mathematical model designed to minimize overall energy usage and created an improved Migration of Animals Optimization Algorithm (MAMO), which efficiently resolves the issue by dynamically adjusting the search range and updating the population. Lv et al.9 examined the replacement flow shop scheduling problem, factoring in learning effects, deterioration effects, and sequence-dependent setup times, while also addressing peak power constraints. They developed five heuristics based on setup time to minimize makespan and suggested a hybrid genetic algorithm integrated with simulated annealing to circumvent local optima. Zhu et al.10 examined the green flexible job shop, accounting for equipment deterioration and maintenance behavior in scheduling, and proposed an enhanced two-stage modal algorithm (ETMA) to address the issue. This method utilizes a heuristic hybrid initialization method to generate a superior initial population and an energy-efficient right-shift strategy based on inverse decoding to further reduce energy usage.

Since Brucker et al.11 introduced the flexible job shop scheduling (FJSP) problem, numerous researchers have examined and addressed it employing diverse intelligent optimization algorithms, including the particle swarm algorithm, the grey wolf optimization algorithm, and the whale optimization algorithm. The whale optimization algorithm, introduced by Mirjalili et al.12 in 2016, is a swarm intelligence optimization technique that emulates the collective feeding behavior of whales. This algorithm features simple parameters, a collaborative mechanism among multiple swarms, and strong adaptability. It has been extensively utilized in various optimization challenges and has demonstrated significant efficacy in solving scheduling problems. Luan et al.13 constructed a low-carbon FJSP optimization model to minimize the aggregate costs of production completion time and shop floor energy consumption and designed a discrete whale optimization algorithm (DWOA), in the process of algorithm design, a discrete coding mechanism applicable to two subproblems of machine allocation and workpiece sequencing was proposed, and six tuning curves to dynamically adjust the parameters were also proposed. The ability to balance the exploration and development of the algorithmic search process. Yang et al.14 examined the flexible job shop scheduling problem (FJSP) with the aim of decreasing makespan and introduced an innovative hybrid whale optimization algorithm (HWOA) to achieve the global optimal solution. A multi-neighborhood structure (MNS) is proposed in the HWOA to simultaneously explore multiple directions for optimal solutions, thereby enhancing the algorithm’s capability to identify optimality. Additionally, to avert premature convergence, a population diversity protection mechanism (DRM) is introduced. Jiang et al.15 constructed a scheduling model to minimize the sum of the total workshop energy cost and the completion time cost, in which a discrete whale optimization algorithm (DWOA) is proposed with special consideration of the feature that the machines can run at multiple speeds, and the algorithm design uses a double-chain coding method to respectively The algorithm design adopts a double-chain coding approach to deal with the two subproblems of job sequencing and machine speed selection, and introduces a variable neighborhood search strategy to further enhance the algorithm’s optimization capability. Cai et al.16 proposed a whale optimization algorithm (C-MOEWOA) based on a clustering strategy for the multi-objective flexible job shop scheduling problem (MOFJSP), which integrated the population division strategy with the core idea of differential evolutionary algorithms (DE) to enhance the algorithm’s global exploration capability, and at the same time, by designing nonlinear coefficient vectors with adaptive weights, the algorithm achieved a balance between exploration and exploitation. Zhu et al.17 combined quantum computing ideas to optimize the job shop scheduling problem, and proposed an Improved Whale Optimization Algorithm (IWOA), which improves the diversity of the solution space and the search efficiency by introducing the quantum bit encoding mechanism, and it formulates a novel update approach utilizing quantum interference and the idea of superposition during the iterative process, thereby enhancing global search efficacy while effectively preventing the algorithm from converging to local optima. Wu et al.18 concentrated on the single-objective flexible job shop scheduling problem (FJSP), introduced the opposition-based learning strategy (OBL), and integrated it with the whale optimization algorithm (WOA) to develop an improved solution method that incorporates oppositional solutions during the search process to expand the search space, thereby enhancing global optimization. Wang et al.19 introduced an enhanced multi-objective whale optimization algorithm (IMOWOA) for the hybrid flow shop multi-objective scheduling problem (MOHFSP-DRP), which integrates dynamic reconfiguration of equipment (DRP) with multiple processing mode constraints. This approach improves global search efficacy and mitigates the risk of local optima by implementing a mechanism-switching threshold and a cosine-type adaptive factor. The methodology attains optimality by integrating swift non-dominated sorting with an improved archive updating technique to preserve the variety and excellence of Pareto solution sets in each generation. Jiang et al.20 proposed an IWOA to solve the energy-efficient job shop scheduling problem in terms of energy cost and productivity. The enhanced algorithm employs a scheduling rule (DR) to refine the quality of the initial solution, incorporates a nonlinear convergence factor (NCF) to establish a dynamic equilibrium between exploration and exploitation, and integrates a mutation operation (MO) to mitigate the algorithm’s propensity to converge prematurely. The introduction of the mutation operation (MO) diminishes the likelihood of attaining a local optimal solution, thus mitigating premature convergence. Liu et al.21 proposed a hybrid whale optimization algorithm (WOA-LFDE) that integrates the Lévy flight mechanism and the differential evolution strategy. This method transforms the iterative search process of the whale optimization algorithm through the introduction of the Lévy flight mechanism, which effectively expands the search range, and at the same time combines with the differential evolution strategy, which improves the accuracy of the local search and the variety of the solutions to reduce the risk of converging to an optimal location. The risk of falling into local optimum is reduced.

In summary, the existing literature on deterioration effects focuses on single-machine scheduling, parallel machine scheduling, and flow shop scheduling, but less in-depth research on deterioration effects in the complex production scheduling environment of flexible job shops. Therefore, it is important to consider the impact of machine deterioration effects to improve the productivity of flexible job shops. Although considering the influence of the deterioration effect in workshop scheduling will increase the complexity of the problem, it is common in industries such as plastics, iron and steel, machinery and military, etc. An in-depth study of scheduling problems with deterioration effect can more accurately reflect the actual production and processing situation in the workshop, and then reasonably arrange the production tasks in order to reduce the influence of machine deterioration effect on the workshop scheduling. Currently, most of the existing studies use intelligent optimization algorithms to solve this type of workshop scheduling problem, while the study of WOA applied to this type of problem still needs to be further improved. Therefore, in this paper, we take minimizing makespan as the optimization objective to study the flexible job shop scheduling problem considering the machine deterioration effect, propose an improved WOA algorithm to solve the problem, and finally verify the effectiveness of the improved algorithm through experiments.

Establishment of the FJSP-MDE model

Problem description

To delineate the real machining time of the workpiece influenced by machine deterioration, various deterioration effect models have been developed in prior research, mostly categorized into the linear deterioration effect model and the step deterioration effect model. The linear deterioration effect model formulates a linear function wherein the real machining time of the workpiece escalates with the initiation time of the workpiece. The step deterioration effect model establishes a segmented function to describe the relationship between the actual machining time and the start time of the workpiece. In actual manufacturing, the growth of the actual processing time of the workpiece due to the deterioration effect of the machine is limited, and when the workpiece starts to process late enough, its actual processing time does not grow infinitely and linearly with the start of the processing time, so it is more rational to employ the segment function to characterize the actual processing time of the workpiece under the machine’s deteriorating effects. This paper utilizes the machine step deterioration effect model given by Kubiak22, which establishes a segmentation function. The real machining time of the workpiece comprises the fundamental machining time and the variable deterioration time. Establish the deterioration threshold for the machine. If the processing time of the workpiece does not surpass the lower limit of this threshold, no deterioration effect manifests, and the basic processing time corresponds to the actual processing time of the workpiece. Conversely, if the processing time exceeds the lower limit, the machine begins to exhibit deterioration effects, resulting in the actual processing time being added to the variable deterioration time, which does not increase indefinitely. When the initiation of machining time above the upper limit of the degradation threshold, the deterioration time ceases to increase, and the actual machining time of the workpiece becomes a constant value. The model illustrating the machine step deterioration effect is presented in Fig. 1:

Stepped deterioration effect.

The Flexible Job Shop Scheduling Problem (FJSP) is a classical complex NP-hard problem, where the two main problems to be solved are: the machine selection problem for each process of the workpieces; and the machining sequence problem for the workpieces’ processes on each machine. However, in actual manufacturing, the processing time of the workpiece will be extended due to the machine’s deteriorating effect. Therefore, the research content of this paper considers the influence of the machine deterioration effect (MDE) on shop floor scheduling on the basis of classical FJSP, which is more in line with the situation of actual job shops.

The FJSP-MDE studied in this paper can be delineated as follows: there are n (i = 1,2,…,n) independent workpieces processed on m(k = 1,2,…,m) machines, each workpiece is processed by qi processes, and each process of each workpiece has a processing order constraint, but there is no processing order priority between workpieces. Each process Oij can be processed by any machine in the corresponding optional processing machine set mij, and different processing machines correspond to different processing times. Each machine can process multiple processes, but can only process one process of a workpiece at the same time, and the processing process cannot be interrupted. Due to differences in machine performance, different machines have different deterioration coefficients. During the processing, if the cumulative processing time of the machine exceeds the lower limit of the machine’s deterioration threshold , the machine deteriorates and its performance decreases. Correspondingly, the processing time of the workpiece processed on the machine will increase. The real processing duration of the workpiece comprises the fundamental processing time and the degradation time. When the cumulative processing time of the machine exceeds the upper limit of the machine’s deterioration threshold , the deterioration time reaches the critical value, and the actual processing time of the workpiece is a fixed value. Therefore, the scheduling purpose of the FJSP-MDE model established in this paper is to determine the machine allocation and workpiece process sorting that minimizes makespan while considering the deterioration effect of the machine.

Basic principles of WOA

WOA introduction

WOA (Whale Optimization Algorithm) is a swarm intelligence optimization algorithm that simulates the predation behavior of whale bubble nets. During the predation process, whales will dive under the prey, spit out bubbles, and gradually surround the prey by rising along a spiral line to narrow the encirclement. In this way, the prey is surrounded and gathered, and finally the prey is captured. This predation behavior is shown in Fig. 2. The position of each whale in WOA represents a solution to a problem. The process of searching for prey is to continuously iterate and update the feasible solution of the problem in the solution space, and then obtain the global optimal solution. The iterative optimization process of WOA can be divided into three optimization mechanisms: surrounding prey, attacking prey with a bubble net, and searching for prey with a random walk. Among them, the random walk search focuses on global development, while surrounding prey and attacking prey with a bubble net focus on local development. IWOA design

Encoding and decoding

Encoding refers to representing the solution of FJSP-MDE using a specific code string format, while decoding involves reading the information contained in the FJSP-MDE code string and calculating the fitness value accordingly. The core problems that FJSP-MDE needs to solve include two aspects: machine selection and operation sequencing. Therefore, a classical two-segment encoding approach is adopted, consisting of two substrings of equal length, where the first substring corresponds to the machine selection code string and the second substring corresponds to the operation sequencing code string. Hybrid population initialization

The efficacy of swarm intelligence optimization algorithms is intimately linked to the quality of the original population. To augment the algorithm’s comprehensive problem-solving capacity, it is necessary to construct a high-quality initial population through specialized methods. Based on the structural characteristics of the two-stage encoding in the Flexible Job Shop Scheduling Problem (FJSP), different approaches need to be adopted for the machine assignment part and the operation sequencing part during the initialization process. Specifically, the machine assignment stage employs three strategies: global search, local search, and random search, accounting for 60%, 30%, and 10% of the population respectively24. After obtaining preliminary machine assignment schemes, random methods are used to generate several corresponding operation sequencing schemes for each assignment scheme. These are then combined to form multiple candidate individuals, from which the best-performing individual is selected based on fitness values as the initial individual for that machine assignment scheme. The procedure is reiterated for the other machine assignment schemes to establish the initial population.

The basic procedure of the Global Search (GS) method is as follows: First, two arrays are constructed. The first array records the current processing time of each machine, with a length equal to the total number of machines. The second array stores the machine number assigned to each operation, with a length equal to the total number of operations, and positions are fixed according to the processing order of operations for each job. During global search execution, a job is randomly selected each time and processed sequentially according to its operation processing order. For each operation, all available processing machines are traversed, their processing times are added to the current values at corresponding positions in the first array to obtain the cumulative time required for each machine to complete the operation, and the machine with the shortest processing time is selected. Its number is recorded in the corresponding position of the second array, achieving machine assignment for that operation. This process continues until all operations of the job are completed. The job is then removed from the candidate list, and the above operations are repeated for the remaining jobs until all jobs are assigned. The basic procedure of the Local Search (LS) method is as follows: First, two arrays are defined. The first array has a length equal to the number of machines and records the current total processing time of each machine. The second array has a length equal to the total number of operations and stores the selected machine number for each operation, maintaining the unchanged order of operations within each job. Local search begins with the first operation of the first job and processes each operation sequentially. During processing, the available machine set corresponding to the operation is traversed, the processing time is added to the current value at the corresponding position in the first array, the machine with the shortest processing time is selected, and its number is recorded in the corresponding position of the second array, completing the machine assignment for the current operation. All operation machine assignments are updated sequentially according to this method until all operations of the job are completed. Nonlinear convergence factor strategy

In the solving process of the Whale Optimization Algorithm, the convergence factor a plays a crucial role in regulating the search range. When the value of a is large, the algorithm possesses stronger global search capability and can search within a larger solution space. In contrast, diminished values of an enhance local search within the solution neighborhood, hence augmenting the algorithm’s search accuracy. Based on this characteristic, to enhance the efficiency of the algorithm during the optimization process, a larger value of a may be established in the initial phase of algorithm iteration to guide the search process within a broader spatial range. In the later stages, the value of a is appropriately reduced to concentrate the search around the current optimal solution, thus improving the precision of local search. Inertia weight strategy

Through the improvement of convergence factor a, the optimization efficiency of the algorithm can be enhanced to a certain extent, particularly by achieving global search in the early stages of the algorithm and enhancing convergence precision in the later stages. However, this method still has certain limitations in balancing global search and local search capabilities. To better coordinate these two search capabilities, dynamic inertia weight has been widely applied in optimization algorithms. Specifically, larger inertia weights can increase the search step size, which facilitates global search and enhances the ability to escape from local optima, thereby effectively finding global optimal solutions. Conversely, smaller inertia weights are beneficial for local search and can improve the convergence precision of the algorithm.

To further improve the search process of the Whale Optimization Algorithm, the shrinking encircling mechanism and spiral ascending mechanism are categorized into two stages. In the early stage of optimization, the inertia weight maintains a fixed value of 1 to enable the algorithm to conduct global search. In the middle and later stages, a nonlinear decreasing dynamic inertia weight is introduced to enable more precise approach to the optimal solution. Random differential mutation strategy

During the later stages when the Whale Optimization Algorithm searches for optimal solutions, both the shrinking encircling mechanism and spiral ascending mechanism operate based on the current optimal individual. This update mechanism tends to cause the algorithm to fall into local optima, thereby affecting the overall optimization performance. Golden sine strategy

In the Whale Optimization Algorithm, when whale individuals execute prey encircling behavior, they rely on the current global optimal position and random coefficients without incorporating population center or previous generation position information. This mechanism may lead to limited coverage of the solution space during the search process, thereby overlooking regions with potentially superior solutions and affecting overall solving accuracy. Furthermore, while the introduction of random coefficients enhances the algorithm’s exploration capability, it also weakens the stability of the global optimization process to some extent. To address these issues, this paper introduces the Golden Sine Strategy after completing the random walk prey searching phase to expand the search range of individuals in the solution space, thereby enhancing global search capability.

The Golden Sine Strategy originates from the design concept of the Golden Sine Algorithm26, which is an optimization strategy combining sine functions with unit circle geometric characteristics. This method simulates the search process in solution space by utilizing sine wave trajectories traversing the unit circle. Specifically, the ordinate value of any point on a circle centered at the origin with radius 1 exactly corresponds to the sine value of the angle formed by that point. Each point in the sine function reflects the projection on the y-axis of a corresponding angular point on the unit circle. When traversing various points of the sine function, it is equivalent to performing a complete scan of corresponding positions on the unit circle according to angular changes, as specifically illustrated in Fig. 14. Meanwhile, this strategy introduces the golden ratio coefficient during the position update phase to gradually reduce the search area and focus on regions where superior solutions may exist. Through this mechanism, the search range can be expanded while guiding individuals to converge toward potentially superior solution regions, achieving dynamic balance between exploration and exploitation. Analysis of algorithm time complexity

To quantitatively evaluate the computational efficiency of the proposed Improved Whale Optimization Algorithm (IWOA) and to illustrate the performance-cost trade-offs brought by the improvement strategies, this section analyzes the time complexity of the IWOA algorithm and compares it with the standard Whale Optimization Algorithm (WOA).

The overall time complexity of the algorithm is mainly determined by population initialization, fitness evaluation, and position update operations during the iteration process. Let the population size be N, the maximum number of iterations be T, and the total number of operations in the flexible job shop scheduling problem be D (corresponding to the dimension of the individual position vector in the algorithm, that is, the sum of the coding lengths of machine selection and operation sequencing).

1. Time Complexity Analysis of Standard WOA.

In a single iteration of the standard WOA, the main operations include fitness calculation, parameter updating, and selecting one of three update mechanisms based on probability p and coefficient |A|. Fitness calculation requires decoding the schedule for N individuals, with a time complexity of O(N⋅D). The time complexity of parameter updating and position updating operations is also O(N⋅D). Therefore, the time complexity of a single iteration of the standard WOA is O(N⋅D). Ignoring lower-order terms, the total time complexity of the standard WOA algorithm is:

OWOA = O(T⋅N⋅D).

2. Time Complexity Analysis of the IWOA Algorithm.

The IWOA algorithm, based on the WOA framework, introduces four main improvement strategies. The time complexity analysis is as follows:

Hybrid Population Initialization (GLR): This strategy includes global search (GS), local search (LS), and random search (RS). GS and LS require traversing all available machines for each operation, with a worst-case time complexity of O(N⋅D⋅mavg), where mavg is the average number of optional machines per operation. The time complexity of RS is O(N⋅D). The overall complexity of the initialization phase remains O(N⋅D⋅mavg), slightly higher than pure random initialization (O(N⋅D)).

Nonlinear Convergence Factor and Inertia Weight Strategy (NCF IW): This strategy only involves constant-time calculations in formulas (21) and (22), which does not increase the algorithm’s order of time complexity.

Random Differential Mutation Strategy (RDM): In the contraction encircling phase, differential mutation is performed with probability CR. This operation involves randomly selecting individuals and vector operations, with a time complexity of O(N⋅D). Since it replaces the original encircling prey operation, it adds only a constant factor to the original computation level.

Golden Sine Strategy (GS): After the random walk phase, all individuals are updated using the golden sine update, with a time complexity of O(N⋅D) as well.

Based on the above analysis, the time complexity of a single iteration of the IWOA algorithm is still O(N⋅D). Compared to the standard WOA, IWOA has a larger constant in its actual runtime due to performing more position update operations (such as executing both random walks and golden sine updates simultaneously) and a more complex initialization. Therefore, the total time complexity of IWOA can be expressed as:

OIWOA = O(T⋅N⋅D) O(N⋅D⋅mavg).

Since T is usually much larger than mavg, and initialization is performed only once, the asymptotic time complexity of IWOA is the same as WOA, which is O(T⋅N⋅D).

3. Trade-off Between Performance Improvement and Computational Cost.

Although IWOA and WOA have the same order of time complexity, IWOA introduces the above strategies at the following computational costs to achieve significant performance improvements:

Cost: the more time-consuming initialization process and the more complex update rules during iterations result in the CPU time per iteration being approximately 1.2–1.5 times that of the standard WOA.

Results: As shown in the experimental results in Tables 6 and 7, IWOA achieved better solution quality and stronger stability in the vast majority of test cases. To analyze the convergence performance of the above algorithms, taking test case DMK01 as an example, experiments are conducted on the four algorithms mentioned above. The obtained results are visualized for comparison by plotting convergence curves for 200 iterations, with the vertical axis representing maximum completion time and the horizontal axis representing the number of algorithm iterations, as shown in Fig. 18. From the perspective of convergence speed, In the early iterations of IWOA (about the first 50 generations), the objective function value (maximum completion time) decreases rapidly, with a decline rate significantly higher than that of WOA, GA, and GWO. This indicates that the high-quality initial population, combined with enhanced global search capability in the early stage, enables it to quickly locate promising regions of the solution space. Although GA converges rapidly in the early stages, it exhibits stepwise descent characteristics in the middle and later stages, indicating that it frequently falls into local optima during the search process. GWO and WOA converge more slowly compared to the other two algorithms, highlighting inadequacies in the equilibrium between global research and local exploitation within their search methodologies. From the perspective of convergence precision, After sufficient iterations, IWOA achieves the optimal quality of the final solution. More importantly, in the later stages of iteration (after about 50 generations), the curve of IWOA can still maintain a gentle decline or slight fluctuation for exploration, whereas the curves of GA and WOA tend to flatten earlier. This demonstrates the role of stochastic differential mutation and the golden sine strategy in maintaining population vitality and avoiding premature convergence, giving the algorithm a stronger ‘deep exploitation’ capability. GWO has the highest starting point with the poorest initial solution quality, tends to stabilize in the middle and later iterations, and achieves poor solution quality. GA and WOA still show slight descent in the later iteration process due to insufficient convergence. Comprehensive analysis shows that the IWOA exhibits an ideal convergence pattern of ‘rapid convergence in the early stage, followed by fine-tuned search in the later stage.’ Its improvement strategy effectively coordinates the needs of different iterations: the early stage emphasizes broad exploration for quick positioning, while the later stage balances in-depth development with diversity maintenance to avoid stagnation. This convergence trend demonstrates that when solving the FJSP-MDE problem, the IWOA algorithm not only achieves superior solution quality but also shows clear advantages in convergence efficiency and robustness.

Paired t-tests are employed to examine the differences in running results between the improved algorithm and other algorithms on various test cases, thereby verifying whether there are statistically significant differences among the solutions of different algorithms.

The specific statistical settings are as follows: Sample size: Each algorithm is run independently 10 times on each test case (Rt = 10), so each paired test is based on 10 pairs of matched observations. Normality assumption: The paired t-test requires that the differences follow a normal distribution. We performed the Shapiro-Wilk test on the sequence of differences in objective function values between algorithms to check for normality. In most test cases, the difference sequences did not significantly deviate from a normal distribution (p > 0.05), meeting the prerequisites for the t-test. For a few cases that did not strictly meet the assumption, considering the t-test has some robustness to non-normality and the sample size is relatively small, we still used this method as a reference for comparison. Test type: The test aims to determine whether the performance of the IWOA algorithm is significantly better than the comparison algorithms. Therefore, a one-tailed paired t-test is used, with the null hypothesis that the average performance of the IWOA algorithm is not better than that of the comparison algorithms.

Conclusion and outlook

Conclusion

This paper addresses the flexible job shop scheduling problem under machine deterioration effects by constructing an FJSP-MDE scheduling model that considers machine stepped deterioration effects. The model aims to minimize the maximum completion time and characterizes the nonlinear variation pattern of processing time with respect to machine cumulative working time through piecewise functions. An improved whale optimization technique is given to address this intricate problem, grounded in the fundamental principles of WOA. The technique employs a hybrid population initialization strategy to enhance the quality of first solutions, designs nonlinear convergence factor and inertia weight strategies to balance the algorithm’s global search and local exploitation capabilities, introduces a random differential mutation strategy in the shrinking encircling phase to increase population diversity, and adopts a golden sine strategy after the random walk searching phase to enhance the algorithm’s global search capability. In the experimental section, the Taguchi method is used to optimize parameter settings, ablation analysis is conducted on the proposed strategies using benchmark instances, and performance comparisons are made with other algorithms. The results demonstrate that IWOA can obtain higher-quality solutions when solving FJSP-MDE problems and exhibits stronger stability. Furthermore, through comparison of scheduling results before and after incorporating machine deterioration effects, the importance of considering deterioration effects for actual production and the effectiveness of IWOA are further validated. In addition, through an empirical analysis of the extended-scale test set DMK, it was preliminarily verified that the IWOA algorithm has good scalability, and its performance advantage remains stable or even more pronounced as the problem scale increases, indicating that the algorithm has the potential to be applied to more complex, large-scale real-world production scheduling problems. Future work will focus on applying and validating the model and algorithm in industrial-scale cases that include more practical constraints.

Outlook

Although the FJSP-MDE model and IWOA algorithm proposed in this paper demonstrate good performance in most test cases, there are still some limitations in this study that are worth further exploration and improvement in future work:

Dependence of algorithm performance on parameter settings: The performance of the IWOA algorithm is somewhat dependent on the settings of key parameters (such as population size N, differential mutation rate CR, etc.). Although this paper uses the Taguchi method for parameter calibration, in practical applications with significantly different problem characteristics, a universally optimal parameter combination may not exist, and it may be necessary to readjust for specific scenarios or design adaptive parameter strategies.

Although this paper uses the Taguchi method for parameter calibration, in practical applications with significantly different problem characteristics, a universally optimal parameter combination may not exist, and it may be necessary to readjust for specific scenarios or design adaptive parameter strategies.

Computational cost and scalability for large-scale problems: The experiments in this paper are mainly based on classical and extended benchmark examples. For ultra-large-scale FJSP instances with a dramatic increase in the number of workpieces and machines, the computational cost (time and memory) of IWOA will increase significantly. Although the time complexity analysis in Sect. 4.9 indicates that this growth is linear, in practical applications, how to solve ultra-large-scale problems within an acceptable time still requires research into more efficient neighborhood structures, parallel computing, or hierarchical optimization strategies to improve scalability.

Assumptions of uncertainty in the model: This study assumes that all processing times are deterministic except for the stepwise deterioration effect of machines. However, real production environments are full of uncertainties, such as urgent order insertions, random machine failures, and material delays. Future research could extend the model to consider these random disturbances and develop scheduling algorithms that are robust or capable of dynamic response.

The impact of deterioration model selection on results: This study uses the stepwise deterioration model proposed by Kubiak to describe machine performance degradation. Different deterioration models (such as linear or exponential models) or different parameter settings may result in different scheduling schemes and performance outcomes. The validity of this study’s conclusions is based on the chosen model. When applying this method, an appropriate deterioration model should be selected and parameters identified according to the actual equipment degradation characteristics. In addition, the machine deterioration parameters in this study (deterioration coefficients and thresholds) were randomly generated within preset uniform distribution ranges. Although these ranges are based on literature and general industrial settings, no systematic sensitivity analysis was conducted on these parameters. Therefore, the robustness of the algorithm has not been fully verified when there are significant changes in parameter distributions or ranges. In practical applications, model parameters should be calibrated based on historical equipment data.

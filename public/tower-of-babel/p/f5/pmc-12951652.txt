The complete mitochondrial genome and phylogenetic analysis of Leptobotia bellacauda (Teleostei: Cypriniformes: Botiidae)
Authors: YiJun Li, Xiaoge Yang, Mengqi Liao, Zhihua Zhong, Yuxi Lian
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe complete mitochondrial genome of Leptobotia bellacauda was sequenced and characterized for the first time. The mitogenome was a 16,591 bp circular molecule, including 13 protein-coding genes (PCGs), 22 transfer RNA (tRNA) genes, two ribosomal RNA genes (rRNA), and a control region. Nucleotide composition: 24.4% A, 30.6% T, 28.6% C, and 16.4% G. Phylogenetic analysis confirmed the evolutionary position of L. bellacauda within its congeneric species and indicated that L. bellacauda was more closely related to L. punctata. This study provides crucial molecular biological evidence for investigating the taxonomy and phylogeny of L. bellacauda, and enhances our understanding of the molecular phylogeny and evolution of the genus Leptobotia.Keywords: Leptobotia bellacauda, mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA), phylogenetic tree

Introduction

The genus Leptobotia, mainly distributed in China and northern Vietnam, comprises a group of small- to medium-sized benthic freshwater fish species within the family Botiidae (Cypriniformes), which are endemic to Southeast Asia (Chen 1980). Due to the prevalence of small-sized species, wide distribution, and complex habitats, the genus Leptobotia is likely to harbor undetected or undescribed species. Traditionally, fish species within this genus are typically distinguished and identified based on their ocular and cheek characteristics. However, the identification of fish species in Leptobotia relying solely on morphological features is not accurate and is prone to misclassification. This is primarily due to the fact that fishes in Leptobotia could share certain morphological similarities with species of other genera within the subfamily. For example, Leptobotia zebra (Wu, 1939) was initially identified based on the simplicity of the suborbital spine (not branched), whereas studying mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) gene sequences revealed its closer affinity to the genus Sinibotia, leading to its reclassification (Chen 1980; Tang et al. 2008). Therefore, in actual taxonomic investigations, additional methods, such as molecular biological techniques, are frequently employed to enhance the precision of classification.

Leptobotia bellacauda was described as a new species from the lower Yangtze River basin based on a combination of morphological characters in 2016 (Bohlen and Šlechtová 2016). Up to now, reports on the genetic information of this species are scarce. In the present study, the complete mitochondrial genome of L. bellacauda was determined and the phylogenetic relationship among Leptobotia species was explored, which would help clarify its taxonomic status from a molecular biology perspective and facilitate further research on the taxonomic resolution and phylogenetic relationships of the Botiidae.

Materials and methods

The specimen of L. bellacauda was collected in the Qiupu River (29°51′10.74″N, 117°21′35.39″E), the first-order tributary of the lower Yangtze River in April 2023 (Figure 1). It was distinguished from other Leptobotia species based on morphological characteristics. Tissue samples were extracted from the caudal fin, and the live specimen was released back to the capture site (Ceruso et al. 2018). The fin tissue samples were preserved in 95% ethanol, and deposited in Anqing Normal University (AQNU) (https://www.aqnu.edu.cn/), YijunLi, l1811167006orz@163.com, with the voucher ID MWBQ001. Genomic DNA was extracted using TIAN Genomic DNA Kit (TIANGEN, Beijing, China), and DNA quality was assessed using 0.8% agarose gel electrophoresis. DNA concentration was determined by a NanoDrop 2000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA), and purity was assessed by the A260/A280 ratio (1.8–2.2). The qualified extracted DNA was then stored at −20 °C for subsequent analysis.

The photograph of L. bellacauda (Bohlen and Šlechtová 2016). The photograph was taken by the corresponding author (Yuxi Lian).

The complete mitochondrial genome of L. bellacauda was sequenced using the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform (San Diego, CA) with a paired-end sequencing strategy (2 × 150 bp) after quality testing. The sequencing data were assembled into contigs using metaSPAdes software (Nurk et al. 2017), and aligned for comparative analysis using the mitochondrial genome of Leptobotia punctata (Li et al. 2008) as a reference. The mitochondrial genome was annotated using the mitoMaker software (Schomaker-Bastos and Prosdocimi 2018), and a gene circle map was generated using OGDRAW (Stephan et al. 2019). Details on sequencing depth and coverage map are provided in Supplementary Table S1. For phylogenetic analysis, based on the complete mitochondrial genome sequences of L. bellacauda and other 17 fish species, a maximum-likelihood (ML) phylogenetic tree was constructed using MEGA-X with statistical support assessed by 1000 bootstrap replicates (Kumar et al. 2018; Stéphane & Olivier 2003).

Results

The complete mitochondrial genome of L. bellacauda has been successfully sequenced, revealing a closed circular double-stranded DNA molecule of 16,591 bp (GenBank accession number: PQ822055). The overall nucleotide composition is 24.4% for A, 30.6% for T, 28.6% for C, and 16.4% for G, with a GC content of 45.0%. The genome comprised 13 protein-coding genes (PCGs), 22 transfer RNA (tRNA) genes, two ribosomal RNA (rRNA) genes (12S rRNA and 16S rRNA), one origin of replication (OL), and one control region (CR) (Figure 2). It is clear that eight tRNAs were located on the light strand (L), while the other fourteen tRNAs were encoded on the heavy strand (H). Twelve PCGs were encoded on the heavy strand (H), while only nad6 was encoded on the light strand (L). These features are consistent with the typical structure of fish species mitochondrial genomes (Mascolo et al. 2018). Among the PCGs, the 13 PCGs collectively encode 3786 amino acids. Most genes started with the canonical initiation codon ATG, with the exceptions of cox1, which started with GTG, and nad6, which started with CTA. The stop codons were represented by TAA, CTA, ATA, or incomplete stop codon T, where four PCGs (cox2, nad3, nad6, cytb) were terminated by the stop codon of single T, two PCGs (nad2, nad4) employed CTA as a stop codon, and one PCG (cox3) was terminated by the ATA stop codon, and the remaining six PCGs (nad1, nad4l, atp6, cox1, nad5, atp8) were terminated by the TAA stop codon. This observation is consistent with the findings reported by Miya and Nishida in their studies on fish mitochondrial genomes (Miya et al. 2003).

To determine the phylogenetic position of the newly described genome, we downloaded the mitochondrial genomes of 16 species from families Botiidae and Cobitidae, and used the Lateolabrax japonicus (Cuvier, 1828) as an outgroup for phylogenetic analysis (Figure 3). The result showed that the phylogenetic tree was divided into two clusters: genus Leptobotia and Botia clustered on one branch, while genus Misgurnus and Cobitis clustered on another branch. L. bellacauda clusters on the sub-branch of genus Leptobotia, showing a closer relationship with L. punctata. This molecular phylogenetic result is consistent with the analysis based on morphological characteristics, further supporting the classification and evolutionary relationship of L. bellacauda. Besides, Leptobotia hengyangensis (Huang and Zhang 1986) clustered on the sub-branch of the genus Botia, indicating that this species exhibited a closer phylogenetic relationship to the genus Botia rather than the genus Leptobotia.

Discussion and conclusions

Mitochondrial DNA, with its unique characteristics such as maternal inheritance, high copy number, rapid evolutionary rate, and absence of recombination, provides a crucial molecular marker for elucidating the complex evolutionary relationships among fish species (Tang et al. 2003, 2005). In this study, we reported the characterization of the complete mitochondrial genome of L. bellacauda for the first time. The complete mitochondrial genome was 16,591 bp in length with a GC content of 45.0%, conforming to the characteristic high AT and low GC content pattern commonly found in fish mitochondrial genomes (Tang et al. 2008). Our results also suggested that the mitochondrial genome composition and structure of L. bellacauda were consistent with the typical structure observed in other vertebrate mitochondrial genomes. In order to clarify the phylogenetic position of L. bellacauda, we conducted phylogenetic analyses using the complete mitochondrial genome sequences of 16 other species from families Botiidae and Cobitidae and a Cyprinidae species serving as the outgroup. The phylogenetic analysis results support the classification of L. bellacauda within the genus Leptobotia, which is in agreement with conclusions of previous morphological studies. In particular, phylogenetic analyses indicate that L. hengyangensis shares a closer phylogenetic relationship with the species of the genus Botia. This finding provides valuable insights into the intergeneric phylogenetic relationships between Leptobotia and Botia, though a comprehensive interpretation will require an expanded sample size and integration with ecological data. Regrettably, the complete mitochondrial genomes of some fish species in genus Leptobotia, including Leptobotia flavolineata, Leptobotia guilinensis, Leptobotia orientalis, Leptobotia posterodorsalis, Leptobotia tchangi, and Leptobotia tientainensis, remain unavailable so far. Therefore, further sampling and sequencing are still required to clarify the phylogenetic relationships within the genus Leptobotia.

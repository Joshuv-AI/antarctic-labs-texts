Thrombaxane-B2 as a predictor marker of atrial fibrillation
Authors: Luigi Petramala, Roberto Carnevale, Luca Marino, Luca D'Ambrosio, Simona Bartimoccia, Elisa Alessandra D'Amico, Vittorio Picchio, Angela Leonardo, Elisa Dietrich, Maurizio Forte, Giacomo Frati, Gioacchino Galardo, Francesco Circosta, Sebastiano Sciarretta, Claudio Letizia
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Atrial fibrillation (AF) is the most frequent cardiac arrhythmia in clinical practice, and its global health burden is progressively rising due to the aging of the world population, progressively higher prevalence of obesity, and improved attention to its early detection; AF is linked to increased risk of several complications, as well as death, stroke and peripheral embolism. In recent years many efforts have been made to fully understand the pathophysiology of AF and its complications. Several epidemiological studies have been conducted to early identification of AF, in order to pay more attention to AF risk factors, including arterial hypertension, dyslipidemia, type 2 diabetes mellitus, obstructive sleep apnea syndrome (OSAS), physical inactivity, and alcohol excess.While the traditional view attributes the primary cause of AF onset to myocardial remodelling (i.e. atrial dilatation, ventricular hypertrophy), cutting-edge research has increasingly highlighted a pivotal role for systemic and myocardial inflammation in the etiology of this arrhythmia.In this study we aimed to evaluate the clinical presentation and the inflammatory immunophenotype of individuals presenting with AF episode, evaluating several biomarkers, such as IFN-γ and thromboxane B2 (TXB2), a potent vasoconstrictor and promoter of platelet aggregation.

Introduction

Atrial fibrillation (AF) is the most common cardiac arrhythmia. It is characterized by chaotic and rapid electrical impulses in the atria, leading to an irregular and fast ventricular response. The prevalence of AF is progressively rising due to the increase in its risk factors among the general population, which include aging, obesity and hypertension [1]. Aging represents a strong non-modifiable risk factor for AF due to the increased atrial fibrosis and conduction system degeneration [2]. Hypertension induces increased left atrial pressure and volume, leading to atrial stretch and fibrosis, creating a substrate for re-entrant circuits [3]. Obesity-related systemic inflammation, epicardial adipose tissue accumulation and oxidative stress predispose the patients to valvular heart disease, particularly mitral valve pathology [4,5]. Modifiable lifestyle factors such as excessive alcohol consumption and sedentary lifestyle also significantly increase the risk of AF [6]. Furthermore, while the traditional view attributes the primary cause of AF onset to myocardial remodelling (ie atrial dilatation, ventricular hypertrophy), cutting-edge research has increasingly highlighted a pivotal role for systemic and myocardial inflammation in the etiology of this arrhythmia [7].

AF is associated with several clinical adverse events, such as acute heart failure, ischaemic stroke, ischaemic heart disease and thromboembolic events. Specifically, clinical studies reported a five-fold increased risk of ischemic stroke, a three-fold increased risk of heart failure and a two-fold increased risk of mortality in patients with AF [8]. Heart failure and AF are indeed bidirectionally interconnected, as atrial stretch from ventricular dysfunction and neurohormonal activation in heart failure promote AF, while AF can precipitate or worsen heart failure [8].

Clinical versus subclinical atrial fibrillation

The distinction of AF into clinically apparent atrial fibrillation and subclinical atrial fibrillation represents a critical milestone to build new therapeutic strategies for AF. In particular, clinically apparent AF is diagnosed via ECG when patients are admitted to the hospital with palpitations, dyspnea, fatigue or dizziness and can be a new-onset (nAF) or a chronic AF (cAF). Subclinical AF, instead, refers to asymptomatic, short-lasting episodes of AF detected by long-term continuous monitoring via cardiac implantable electronic devices (CIEDs). Due to the lack of symptoms, the diagnosis of nAF in patients with subclinical AF is challenging. Results from the Asymptomatic Atrial Fibrillation and Stroke Evaluation in Pacemaker Patients and the Atrial Fibrillation Reduction Atrial Pacing Trial (ASSERT) demonstrated that subclinical AF is associated with a 2.5-fold increased risk of ischemic stroke or systemic embolism compared to patients without subclinical AF [9]. This finding blurs the lines between symptomatic and asymptomatic disease and challenges traditional paradigms of anticoagulation initiation, which are largely based on data from cAF populations. In particular, the coexistence of atrial fibrillation, especially if unknown and asymptomatic, can significantly increase cardiovascular mortality; in this regard, several studies have evaluated that AF represented the preoperative cardiac risk factor associated with increased in-hospital mortality in elderly patients without heart failure undergoing emergency surgery, such as for hip fracture [10,11]. In frail elderly patients with permanent dual-chamber pacemakers, Hayıroğlu M.I. et al. Demonstrated that the onset of AF is a significant negative prognostic factor [12].

The role of thromboxane B2 in atrial fibrillation

AF is characterized by a complex interplay between endothelial dysfunction, abnormal blood flow and hypercoagulability. Platelet activation is the main driver of hypercoagulability in AF patients [13]. Thromboxane A2 (TXA2) is a potent vasoconstrictor and promoter of platelet aggregation. Due to its extremely short half-life (∼30 s), its direct measurement is impractical. Instead, its stable, inactive metabolite, Thromboxane B2 (TXB2), can be quantified in plasma or urine and reflects systemic levels of TXA2 production and platelet turnover [14]. Several studies reported increased levels of TXB2 in urine and plasma derived from AF patients compared to healthy controls, suggesting a state of persistent platelet activation [15]. The increased TXB2 activity directly correlated with thrombus formation within the left atrial appendage. Elevated TXB2 levels have also been associated with higher stroke risk and have been identified as an independent predictor of stroke and cardiovascular death in AF patients [16]. The measurement of TXB2 therefore offers a valuable window into the pathophysiology of AF-related thrombogenesis and may serve as a potential biomarker for refining risk stratification beyond clinical scores.

The study aims to evaluate the role of IFN-γ and TXB2 as possible early biomarkers of inflammatory immunophenotype in patients with AF. The serum concentration levels of these biomarkers are the primary outcomes to be evaluated, while other clinical and laboratory data are investigated for exploratory purposes.

Materials and methods

Population study

From May 2024 to April 2025, we consecutively enrolled 73 patients (35 males, 38 females) admitted at the Emergency Medicine Department (ED) - Azienda Ospedaliera Universitaria Policlinico Umberto I (Rome, Italy), for acute illnesses or symptoms not clinically related to AF; we have distinguished these into two groups: 38 patients with subclinical nAF (mean age 77.6 ± 13.5 years) and 35 patients with cAF (mean age 81.8 ± 7.7 years); we excluded patients who reported symptoms or clinical signs closely related to clinical manifestations of AF at ED presentation; fifteen subjects observed in ED with sinus rhythm (SR) (7 males and 8 females; mean age 78.3 ± 12.5 years) were recruited as control group and included in the study.

Processing of serum

Upon enlistment in the ED and ECG diagnosis, blood samples were collected in BD Vacutainer tubes (Franklin Lakes, New Jersey, USA) without anticoagulant. After clot formation, samples were centrifuged at 300 g for 10 min at room temperature (RT). The resulting serum was divided into aliquots and stored at −80 °C until analysis. All samples, including those obtained from healthy control subjects, were collected and processed under identical preanalytical conditions in order to minimize variability related to platelet activation during serum preparation.

Determination of serum interferon γ (IFN-γ) levels

Serum IFN-γ concentrations were measured using a commercial ELISA kit (Abcam, Cambridge, UK), following the manufacturer's instructions. The results were expressed as pg/mL. Intra- and inter-assay coefficients of variation were both <10%.

Determination of serum TxB2 levels

Serum TxB2 levels were measured in the supernatant using a commercial ELISA kit (Cusabio, Houston, TX, USA), according to the manufacturer's instructions. The results were expressed as pg/mL. Intra- and inter-assay coefficients of variation for TxB2 were <8% and <10%, respectively.

Assessment of cardiac remodelling

In addition, we investigated the prevalence of cardiac remodelling in the two groups evaluating ultrasonographic indicators comprising the reduction of the left ventricle ejection fraction (LVEF% using the 4C-Simpson formula), the atrial enlargement (atrial volume >38 mL for female subjects and >40 mL for male subjects) and the pericardial effusion.

Statistical analysis

This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki guidelines and was approved by a local ethical committee. The study design was clearly written in layperson language and provided to each study participant. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients. Clinical data were obtained from routine clinical practice, so we have requested consent from the Local Ethical Committee of the Department of Clinical, Internal, Anesthesiological and Cardiovascular Sciences, “Sapienza” University of Rome, Italy. The data are expressed as mean value and standard deviation (SD). Differences between numerical data were assessed by the Mann – Whitney U test for two-sample comparison or by one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), which applied the Fisher least significant difference post hoc test for multiple comparisons. Chi2 statistics were used to assess differences between categorical variables. P values less than 0.05 were taken as statistically significant. Statistical analysis was performed using dedicated statistical software SPSS (Statistical Package for Social Sciences, software, version 24; SPSS Inc, Chicago, IL, USA).

The present study is focused on the possible role of different biomarkers (IFN-γ and Thromboxane B2) that are considered our primary outcomes. The set of other clinical variables is reported for exploratory purposes and is considered secondary. No correction for type I error is provided when the results are reported [17].

Discussion

AF increases the risk of several cardiovascular complications and it is considered responsible for 15% of overall strokes worldwide [16]. AF induces the lack of atrial contraction, favouring blood stasis, thrombus formation and subsequent embolization to the arterial vessels (ie brain). According to recent ESC Guidelines [1], the clinical approach for the management of AF consists in the administration of oral anticoagulants (OAC) due to the increased risk of incident stroke or thromboembolism [7,18]. In our study, the percentage of patients with a stroke was higher in the nAF group compared to cAF, highlighting the danger of undiagnosed AF and emphasizing the relationship between AF and stroke.

Risk stratification in patients with AF is commonly based on clinical scores, with the CHA2DS2-VASc score being widely used [7]. This score includes congestive heart failure or left ventricular dysfunction, hypertension, age ≥75 years, diabetes, prior stroke/transient ischemic attack/systemic thromboembolism, vascular disease, and age 65–74 years. Unlike the CHA2DS2-VASc score, female sex is no longer considered an independent risk factor. In our cohort, the integration of TXB2 with the CHA2DS2-VASc score may provide incremental prognostic information, potentially refining the identification of high-risk patients who could benefit from tailored therapeutic strategies.

While a well-established link exists between dyslipidemia and the occurrence of ischemic stroke and other cardiovascular diseases (CVD), studies on the role of dyslipidemia in AF development have yielded conflicting results. As regard, a significant large-scale meta-analysis found that higher levels of total cholesterol, but not blood levels of triglycerides, LDL cholesterol or LDL cholesterol, are independently associated with an increased risk of AF in individuals without cardiovascular disease [19]. Lopez et al. assessed that altered serum levels of high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) and triglycerides (TG) were associated with the risk of developing AF [20], while contrasting results were found by Li et al. [21] that reported a negative correlation between higher total cholesterol (TC) and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) serum levels and the onset of AF. Another study also reported lower blood lipid levels in patients with AF especially LDL-C and HDL-C [22]. In a meta-analysis conducted on large population, Guan et al. reported a reduction in AF risk by 19% and 21% with each standardized increase in plasma TC and LDL-C values, respectively [23]; Wahanita et al. assessed that obesity increases the risk of AF by 49% [24] and Hayıroğlu et al. published a meta-analysis in 2023 showing that higher TC levels were shown to be independently attributed to an increased risk of AF in individuals without cardiovascular disease [19].

It's undeniable that dyslipidemia is a primary risk factor for atherosclerosis, which, in turn, plays a central role in the development of acute coronary syndrome (ACS) and myocardial infarction (MI). While a direct role of dyslipidemia in causing AF is still under debate, its known involvement in thromboembolic events in the heart and subsequent myocardial tissue necrosis may represent a strong contribution to the onset of both supraventricular and ventricular arrhythmic events [25].

Furthermore, the phenotype observed in individuals with metabolic syndrome (MS) typically involves an excess of visceral adipose tissue, which has been the subject of several studies exploring its endocrine role and its contribution to a systemic inflammation driven by cytokines and pro-inflammatory mediators. Among these adipose depots, epicardial (EAT) and pericoronary (PAT) adipose tissues have been of particular interest in our previous studies, especially regarding the expression of adiponectin and other cytokines involved in the inflammatory cascade [26]. Pro-inflammatory cytokines (e.g. TNF-α, IL-6) and mediators (e.g. activin A) lead to pro-inflammatory and pro-fibrotic activity in the atrial myocardium [27], resulting in structural and electrical remodelling.

Our results showed that patients with nAF had a significantly higher rate of dyslipidemia compared to the patients with cAF, with no substantial differences in the intake of lipid-lowering medications. While this evidence might be explained by cAF group receiving frequent follow-up, leading to a more attentive lifestyle and balanced diet, the higher prevalence of dyslipidemia in nAF group supports the hypothesis that it is among the predisposing factors for the onset of atrial fibrillation. Systemic inflammation may represent a key link between dyslipidemia and the onset of atrial fibrillation [28].

The risk factors for developing AF are identical to those for heart failure (HF), so HF is closely linked bidirectionally to AF, and some biomarkers such as N-terminal pro-brain natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP) are useful in the diagnosis and stratification of patients with HF. The study by Xiao Liu et al. showed that in HF patients with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) observed during a median follow-up of 3 years, each 1000 pg/mL increase in NT-proBNP was associated with a 16% increase in the risk of AF onset after adjustment [29]; furthermore, the assessment of NT-proBNP with other cardiovascular scores (e.g., CHA2DS2-VASc) improved their predictive performance for AF risk by approximately 5-7%. To confirm this, in the PARADIGM-HF trial (Prospective Comparison of ARNI With ACEI to Determine Impact on Global Mortality and Morbidity in Heart Failure) the ratio between NT-proBNP and BNP varied considerably according to rhythm (atrial fibrillation 8:1; no atrial fibrillation 5.75:1), as well as in relation to age, renal function, and body mass index [30].

Recent studies suggest a significant role of inflammation in the pathogenesis of AF. Inflammatory signalling involves the contribution of multiple molecular mechanisms, but current research has focused primarily on the inflammasome complex due to its central role in promoting atrial remodelling. Inflammasomes are multimeric complexes serving as intracellular sensors and receptors for pathogens in the innate immune system [31]. NLRP3 inflammasome has received attention due to its key role in inflammasome signalling and pathogenesis of diseases, including AF [32]. Inflammation enhances the pro-thrombotic pattern that activates atria-fibroblasts, thus promoting their transformation into myofibroblasts and leading to atrial structural remodelling. Several authors report elevated serum levels of inflammatory biomarkers in AF subjects and the expression of inflammatory markers in cardiac tissues of both animal models and patients with AF [33].

C-Reactive Protein (CRP) is an inflammation marker widely used in clinical practice. Indeed, elevated levels of CRP have been associated with MS [34] as well as with insulin sensitivity, as reflected by the Homeostatic Model Assessment (HOMA) index. Pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-6 and TNFα produced by monocytes and macrophages mainly act on hepatocytes, leading to the production of CRP. Several clinical studies reported an association between serum levels of CRP and AF. Elevated levels of CRP have been observed in patients with paroxysmal or persistent AF [35].

Other mediators were evaluated to investigate the value of inflammatory markers for predicting AF recurrence; in this regard the uric acid/albumin ratio had a higher predictive value than other inflammatory markers in predicting AF recurrence after after cardiac ablation [36]; moreover, during hospitalization for ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) treated with primary percutaneous coronary intervention (pPCI), the occurrence of AF observed during hospitalization in patients without a history of AF or atrial flutter was closely correlated with serum uric acid (UA) levels and serum albumin level [37].

Nevertheless, white blood cell count (WBC) remains one of the most widely used, albeit nonspecific, indicators of systemic inflammation. Beside their well-known function in thrombogenesis, several studies have highlighted the pivotal role of platelets (PLT) during systemic inflammation. Platelets produce and secrete proinflammatory mediators, including cytokines, chemokines, and lipid compounds, which modulate immune cell functions and endothelial activity. Thanks to their rich repertoire of surface receptors such as Toll-like receptors (TLRs) (type 2, 4, 5, 9), platelets participate in innate immunity [38]. In line with these recent findings, our study reported a significantly higher level of serum CRP and a more elevated WBC count in the nAF group compared to cAF. Additionally, we also found that patients with nAF have a significantly higher PLT count compared to those with cAF. This finding can be attributed to the fact that patients with cAF are usually treated with NOACs, which lead to a PLT count reduction. On the other hand, combined with the higher level of serum CRP and a more elevated WBC count in patients with nAF, this evidence contributes to stress the relevance of systemic inflammation as the prime mover for the development of AF.

IFN-γ is a type II cytokine that plays a pivotal role in both innate and adaptive immunity. Primarily secreted by activated natural killer cells, CD4+ T helper 1 (Th1) lymphocytes, CD8+ cytotoxic T lymphocytes, and macrophages, its production is induced by proinflammatory cytokines such as IL-12 and IL-18. IFN-γ activates the JAK-STAT1 signaling pathway [39], promoting the differentiation of naive T cells into Th1 cells [40]. Greulich et al. described how EAT releases proinflammatory cytokines such as IL-8, IL-6, IL-1β, and TNF-α into the atrium, attracting extra immune cells to the area, leading to fibroblast activation [41]; Ajoolabady et al. discovered that EAT displayed remarkable upregulation of immune/inflammatory cytokines such as IFN-γ, TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1 in patients with obesity [42]. These findings highlighted the role of EAT-produced cytokines in promoting myocardial fibrosis, thus contributing to AF pathogenesis.

Furthermore, Huang et al. showed that elevated plasma level of IFN-γ was an independent risk factor for stroke and all-cause mortality in patients with AF, resulting in a four-fold increased risk for both outcomes [43]. Wu et al. measured several cytokines plasma levels in both patients with AF and healthy controls, showing a significant association of proinflammatory cytokine levels, including IFN-γ, and AF risk. This association was independent from potential confounders and there were no significant differences between paroxysmal and cAF patients [44]. In this study, we reported higher levels of IFN-γ in the nAF group compared to cAF, suggesting that IFN-γ may contribute to the initiation of AF.

TXB2 is a stable, biologically inactive metabolite of TXA2, a potent eicosanoid involved in platelet aggregation and vasoconstriction. Serum TxB2 measurement is highly sensitive to preanalytical variables, as it mainly reflects thromboxane A2 generation by platelets during the clotting process rather than circulating basal levels. Factors such as clotting time, temperature, venipuncture technique, and delay before centrifugation may influence platelet activation and TxB2 concentrations. In the present study, these variables were minimized by applying a standardized blood collection and serum processing protocol to all samples, including healthy controls. Therefore, although serum TxB2 reflects platelet thromboxane-generating capacity during clotting, the observed differences between study groups are unlikely to be attributable to systematic preanalytical bias.

TXB2 formation results from the spontaneous non-enzymatic hydrolysis of TXA2. Elevated levels of TXB2 are typically observed in pathological states characterized by increased platelet activation, such as acute coronary syndromes and ischaemic stroke: Qu et al. demonstrated that elevated plasma levels of TXB2 were associated with a general increase in the risk of developing cardiovascular events, including ischemic stroke [45]. Concomitantly, the same study suggested that TXA2 may also play a role in inflammatory processes beyond platelet function, including interactions with leukocytes and vascular endothelial cells. In addition, Touchberry et al. demonstrated that TXA2-receptor activation in cardiomyocytes does not directly induce myocardial hypertrophy but is capable of promoting cell death through inositol triphosphate (IP3)-dependent signaling pathways [46]. Even though the study focused on ventricular cardiomyocytes, the discovered molecular mechanisms may also be applied to atrial chambers, where cardiomyocyte apoptosis would contribute to the cardiac remodelling underlying AF pathogenesis. Furthermore, Duda et al. observed that diet supplementation with omega-3 fatty acids, whose intake has often been associated with reduced inflammation, was able to prevent pressure-overload-induced cardiac remodelling by modulating TXA2 levels, thus suggesting a pivotal role of TXA2 in cardiac remodelling [47].

Here we demonstrated that nAF patients had significantly higher concentration of plasma TXB2 compared to cAF ones. Obviously, patients with cAF were specifically treated with NOACs; in this regard, a recent study of ours conducted on the impact of NOACs on platelet function highlighted that these drugs significantly reduce the generation of plasma TXB2 levels at the platelet level, particularly in patients with higher baseline values [48].

Higher levels of TXB2, in combination with other inflammatory markers such as CRP, WBC and PLT, strongly support that systemic phlogosis may be a crucial contributor to the onset of AF. Furthermore, we analyzed the link between inflammatory markers and ultrasonographic indicators of organ damage such as LVEF reduction and atrial hypertrophy. In our settings, TXB2 emerged as a significant marker of atrial remodelling, with the highest AUC and its negative correlation with LVEF%. As regard, the assessment of atrial remodelling is essential for stratifying the risk of AF onset and recurrence; Karakayali M et al. showed that assessment of P-wave indices (PWI) on electrocardiography had a high prognostic value in predicting patients at risk of developing incidence of atrial high-rate episodes [49].

Collectively, our results identify TXB2 as a potential biomarker of AF. Indeed, the evaluation of TXB2 levels would not only allow a better thrombotic risk stratification in patients with AF, but also the early identification of structural disease progression. The thromboxane pathway appears to be another link between systemic inflammation and AF onset that warrants further investigation.

This study was conducted in patients admitted to the ED for diseases or symptoms not directly related to AF. Due to the lack of AF-related symptomatology before the admission to ED, it is not possible to exclude that some patients belonging to the nAF group had experienced subclinical AF in the past. As a limitation of the study, this is a cross-sectional and not prospective study, emphasizing the association and not the causality of the role of TXB2; second, we evaluated serum TXB2 values and not plasma or urinary behaviours, not excluding ex vivo artifacts; serum TxB2 reflects platelet thromboxane-generating capacity during clotting rather than in vivo basal thromboxane production; although preanalytical conditions were carefully standardized across all study groups, this intrinsic limitation of serum-based TxB2 measurement should be considered when interpreting the results; however, we believe that the same methodology of biochemical evaluation in all subgroups reduces this bias by evaluating real statistical differences between groups; lately, we have evaluated ear volume and EF as parameters because of easy data obtaining, although there are further data that can be evaluated, as well as increasing the sample size by adding further results.

Conclusion

In conclusion, nAF represents a relevant condition of high-risk cardiovascular complications, influenced by well-established predisposing factors such as obesity and dyslipidemia. In this study, we analyzed inflammatory and prothrombotic markers, highlighting the pathophysiological role of these mediators in the development of AF, cardiac remodelling, and cerebro-cardiovascular complications. The results presented in this study proposed TXB2 as a relevant marker for AF which may allow a rapid identification of AF and, therefore, a fast and personalised intervention. However, further studies are needed to explore the pathophysiology of atrial fibrillation and to clarify the molecular pattern that brings to atrial remodelling.

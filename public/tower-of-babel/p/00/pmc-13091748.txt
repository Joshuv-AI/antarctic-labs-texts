SIRT3-mediated mitochondrial fatty acid oxidation protects against hepatic lipid deposition in fatty liver hemorrhagic syndrome
Authors: Panpan Cao, Jinyan Chen, Chun Zeng, Yang Hu, Jianyun Yuan, Xiaoquan Guo, Huabin Cao, Caiying Zhang, Yu Zhuang, Guoliang Hu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFatty liver hemorrhagic syndrome (FLHS) is a nutrition-related metabolic disorder in laying hens characterized by excessive hepatic lipid accumulation and hemorrhagic lesions, leading to reduced productivity and increased mortality. However, the regulatory mechanisms linking mitochondrial dysfunction to hepatic lipid metabolism remain unclear. This study investigated the role of SIRT3 in modulating mitochondrial fatty acid oxidation during FLHS progression. An in vivo FLHS model was established by feeding laying hens with a high-energy, low-protein (HELP) diet, and an in vitro hepatic steatosis model was induced by free fatty acid (FFA) treatment in primary hepatocytes. Both models exhibited pronounced lipid accumulation in hepatic cells and altered hepatocellular injury-related parameters, which were associated with mitochondrial dysfunction and impaired fatty acid oxidation. Mechanistically, hepatic tissues and hepatocytes showed suppression of the SIRT3–AMPKα–PGC-1α signaling cascade, accompanied by reduced expression of mitochondrial biogenesis markers (NRF1, TFAM), impaired respiratory chain components (NDUFA9, SDHA, UQCRC1, COX4I1, ATP5B), and decreased transcription of fatty acid oxidation-related genes (PPARα, ACOX1, CPT1A, CPT2, ACADL, ACADM). Pharmacological activation of SIRT3 with AR-C17 restored AMPKα–PGC-1α signaling, enhanced mitochondrial biogenesis and respiratory function, and promoted fatty acid oxidation, thereby alleviating lipid accumulation in hepatocytes in both models. Collectively, these results demonstrate that SIRT3 is a key metabolic regulator maintaining mitochondrial oxidative function and lipid homeostasis in laying hens. Targeted activation of SIRT3 may provide a novel nutritional strategy for preventing or ameliorating FLHS and related metabolic disturbances in poultry production.Keywords: SIRT3, Mitochondrial biogenesis, Fatty acid oxidation, Fatty liver hemorrhagic syndrome

Introduction

Fatty liver hemorrhagic syndrome (FLHS) is a prevalent metabolic disorder in high-yielding caged laying hens, characterized by excessive hepatic and abdominal lipid deposition, hepatomegaly, and hemorrhagic lesions that may progress to fatal liver rupture (Shini et al., 2019). Since its first description in the 1950s, the morbidity rate of FLHS has increased with the intensification of egg production systems, resulting in huge economic losses due to the decline of productivity and the increase of mortality (Bragg et al., 1973). The development of FLHS has an important relationship with disrupted hepatic lipid metabolism. Liver is the central organ for lipid homeostasis in laying hens, which plays a pivotal role in β-oxidation, fatty acid synthesis, and lipoprotein assembly and secretion, ensuring efficient lipid mobilization to support yolk formation during the laying cycle. During peak egg production, hepatic lipid metabolism operates at an exceptionally high rate, rendering the liver metabolically sensitive and highly responsive to nutritional or hormonal perturbations. Under such conditions, even minor imbalances such as feeding with high-energy and low-protein diets, oxidative stress could disrupt the coordination between lipid synthesis, oxidation, and transport, resulting in triglyceride accumulation, hepatocellular steatosis, and subsequent inflammatory and hemorrhagic injury (Li et al., 2025; Xiao et al., 2025). These abnormalities are now increasingly recognized as manifestations of mitochondrial dysfunction and impaired cellular energy metabolism rather than a simple consequence of lipid overload (Li et al., 2025; Xiao et al., 2025).

Mitochondria are the primary sites of fatty acid β-oxidation and oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS), which are indispensable for hepatic energy generation and metabolic homeostasis (Lamanilao et al., 2023). Mitochondrial dysfunction is now widely regarded as the key factor in the pathogenesis of metabolic liver diseases (Zhou et al., 2024). In mammalian studies, metabolic dysfunction-associated fatty liver disease (MAFLD) is now recognized to have mitochondrial dysfunction as a key pathological feature, which is characterized by impaired oxidative phosphorylation, defective β-oxidation, and excessive reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation, leading to hepatocellular injury and lipid accumulation (Abulikemu et al., 2023). Similarly, in poultry, impaired mitochondrial activity has been observed in hens with FLHS, suggesting that mitochondrial derangements constitute a conserved pathological mechanism across species (Wei et al., 2026). Recent studies have revealed that mitochondrial biogenesis and respiration are orchestrated by a transcriptional network involving peroxisome proliferator-activated receptors (PPARs), nuclear respiratory factors (NRF1 and NRF2), and mitochondrial transcription factor A (TFAM), which collectively regulate genes governing oxidative metabolism and fatty acid oxidation (Surugihalli et al., 2019). Disruption of this regulatory axis compromises mitochondrial integrity and metabolic flexibility, predisposing hepatocytes to lipid accumulation and oxidative injury. Consistent with these findings, both mammalian and avian studies have demonstrated that high-energy diets or excessive free fatty acid (FFA) exposure suppress the PPAR–NRF–TFAM signaling network, leading to reduced mitochondrial density, impaired β-oxidation, and excessive hepatic lipid deposition. Such mitochondrial dysfunction is often accompanied by diminished antioxidant capacity, particularly reduced activity of mitochondrial superoxide dismutase 2 (SOD2), which exacerbates oxidative stress and accelerates steatosis progression (Zhao et al., 2023; Calcaterra et al., 2024). Similar pathogenic mechanisms have been described in MAFLD, suggesting conserved mitochondrial regulatory pathways between FLHS and human hepatic steatosis.

Sirtuins (SIRTs) are NAD⁺-dependent deacylases that play central roles in maintaining cellular energy metabolism and redox homeostasis. Among them, SIRT3, the predominant mitochondrial isoform, acts as a metabolic sensor linking nutrient status to mitochondrial function (Lagouge et al., 2006). Through the deacetylation of key enzymes, SIRT3 targets critical pathways such as fatty acid β-oxidation and the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle, as well as OXPHOS, thereby preserving mitochondrial integrity and metabolic efficiency (Storder et al., 2019). The SIRT3–AMPK–PGC-1α signaling axis functions as a crucial regulatory hub coordinating energy sensing, mitochondrial biogenesis, and oxidative metabolism. Under metabolic stress, AMPK activation stimulates PGC-1α–mediated transcription of mitochondrial genes through NRF1/2 and TFAM, thereby enhancing β-oxidation and ATP generation. Recent evidence from biomedical research highlights SIRT3 as a therapeutic target for conditions including MAFLD, cardiac ischemia, and obesity. due to its ability to improve mitochondrial respiration, attenuate oxidative stress, and lower hepatic lipid accumulation upon activation (Li et al., 2021; Yang et al., 2021). Nevertheless, although these advances in mammalian models, the regulatory role of SIRT3 in avian hepatic lipid metabolism remains largely undefined. Preliminary data indicate that SIRT3 expression is markedly reduced in FLHS, concomitant with impaired fatty acid oxidation and mitochondrial dysfunction. Nevertheless, the mechanistic basis by which SIRT3 modulates mitochondrial metabolism in FLHS has yet to be clarified.

Therefore, the study was aimed at elucidating the regulatory role of SIRT3 in regulating mitochondrial fatty acid oxidation and hepatic energy metabolism throughout FLHS progression in laying hens. In addition, we aimed to determine whether pharmacological upregulation of SIRT3 by AR-C17 could reestablish mitochondrial functionality and attenuate hepatic lipid deposition. By characterizing the SIRT3–AMPK–PGC-1α signaling pathway in the avian liver, these study offer a novel mechanistic point of view into the mitochondrial basis of FLHS and propose potential nutritional strategies to enhance mitochondrial function and metabolic resilience in poultry.

Materials and methods

Animal treatment

Forty Hy-Line Brown laying hens, aged 14 weeks, were employed in the present study. The hens were housed with ad libitum access to feed and water throughout the experimental period. All animal procedures received approval from the Animal Welfare and Ethics Committee of Jiangxi Agricultural University and were performed according to its institutional guidelines. Following a one-week adaptive feeding, the hens were randomly allocated into two dietary groups (n = 20 per group). The Control group received a standard corn–soybean meal diet, while the model group (HELP) was fed a high-energy, low-protein diet, as specified in Table 1. Based on the previous experimental research of the research group, sampling was selected on the 112th day of feeding, all hens were weighed, and blood samples were collected (Zeng et al., 2026). The hens were then euthanized, and livers were promptly excised on an ice-cold platform. Abdominal fat was carefully removed, weighed, and recorded. Each liver sample was divided and processed accordingly: one part was used for biochemical analyses, frozen in liquid nitrogen and quickly transferred to an ultra-low temperature refrigerator for storage, while the other part was used for histopathological analysis, fixed in 4 % paraformaldehyde, and the fixative was updated regularly during storage.

Cell culture and treatments

Primary chicken hepatocytes were isolated from 12-day-old chicken embryos as previously described and maintained in hepatocyte culture medium supplemented with 2 % fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Ding et al., 2024). Cells were incubated at 37°C in a 5 % CO₂ humidified atmosphere. To establish a hepatocellular steatosis model, the culture medium was supplemented with 1 mM free fatty acids (FFAs, oleic acid:palmitic acid = 2:1) for cell treatment, or control medium for 12, 24, 36, or 48 h, constituting eight experimental groups: Control and FFAs-treated at each time point, with three replicates per group. To investigate the role of SIRT3, cells were treated with a selective SIRT3 activator (AR-C17). The non-toxic concentration of AR-C17 was determined with the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay, and 10 μM was selected for further experiments. To evaluate the intervention effect, cells were divided to four groups: Control, 1 mM FFAs (FFA), 1 mM FFAs + 10 μM AR-C17 (FAR), and 10 μM AR-C17 alone (AR) for 36 h (six replicates per group). The concentration of AR-C17 was selected based on preliminary dose-response experiments. Hepatocytes were collected after the 36-hour co-treatment period for further analysis.

Histopathological observation

Liver tissues were fixed in 4 % paraformaldehyde for 48 h and embedded in paraffin. Microtomed into 4-μm sections, flattened on a 45 °C water bath, gently transferred onto glass slides, and dried overnight at 45 °C. Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining was conducted according to a standard protocol carried out as previous study (Da et al., 2025). The pathological changes in the ilea were evaluated under a light microscope (Olympus, Japan).

Oil-Red-O-staining

Frozen liver sections or hepatocytes were stained with Oil Red O to visualize intracellular lipid droplets (Solarbio, Beijing, China). Images were captured, and lipid accumulation was quantitatively analyzed using ImageJ software.

Determination of biochemical indices

Plasma levels of several biochemical indices were measured using an automatic biochemical analyzer with a commercial kit from Medicalsystem Biotechnology Co. The parameters assessed included the activities of aspartate aminotransferase (AST) and alkaline phosphatase (ALP), and the concentrations of total bilirubin (T-BIL), direct bilirubin (D-BIL), total protein (TP), albumin (ALB), triglyceride (TG) and total cholesterol (TC), low density lipoprotein cholesteroland (LDL-C), high density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) and non-esterified fatty acids (NEFA). Additionally, the activities of AST and ALT in cell culture medium and the contents of TG and TC in cells were analyzed with commercial assay kits of Nanjing Jiancheng Biological Company in strict accordance with the instructions.

Immunofluorescence staining

Cells or tissue sections were subjected to a standard immunofluorescence protocol. Briefly, after fixation and permeabilization, nonspecific sites were blocked with BSA. And incubated with specific primary antibodies, followed by fluorescent secondary antibodies. Nuclear counterstaining was performed using DAPI. The images were captured using laser confocal microscope, and the average fluorescence intensity data of SIRT3 and AMPKα−1 in the images were collected by ImageJ-1.54f software.

Cell viability assay

After the cells were treated for 36 h, CCK-8 reagent was mixed with serum-free medium at a ratio of 10:1 to prepare CCK-8 assay solution. Add 110 μL CCK-8 solution preheated to 37°C to each well, and incubate at 37°C for 2 h. After incubation, The optical density (OD) at 450 nm was determined using a microplate reader (Molecular Devices, Shanghai, China), and the cell viability was calculated after using SIRT3 activator.

Real-time quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR)

Total RNA extraction from liver tissues or hepatocytes. Reverse transcription was performed to synthesize complementary DNA (cDNA). Gene expression levels were quantified using RT-qPCR with specific primers. The primer sequences and parameters are shown in Table 2. All primers were obtained from by Tsingke Biotech Co., Ltd. (Changsha, Hunan, China).

Protein extraction and western blot

Total protein was extracted from samples with RIPA buffer (Servicebio, Wuhan, China). Protein concentrations were quantified with the BCA assay (Solarbio, Beijing, China). Then, SIRT3-mediated mitochondrial fatty acid oxidation related proteins were detected. The following primary antibody was employed: SIRT3 (1:1000, Proteintech, China), AMPKα−1 (1:1000, Proteintech, China), p-AMPKα−1 (1:1000, Proteintech, China), PGC-1α (1:5000, Proteintech, China), NRF1 (1:1000, Proteintech, China), TFAM (1:1000, Wanleibio, China), PPARα (1:1000, Wanleibio, China), CPT1A (1:5000, Wanleibio, China), SOD2 (1:1000, Wanleibio, China). β-actin (1:5000, Proteintech) served as the internal control for data normalization. All secondary antibodies were obtained from Proteintech (Wuhan, China). The bands were performed with an enhanced chemiluminescence reagent, and band intensities were analyzed by ImageJ software.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was conducted using SPSS software version 26.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). Results are presented as means ± standard deviation (SD). one-way ANOVA was performed followed by Dunnett’s multiple comparison test. For data not meeting the assumptions of normality, the Kruskal–Wallis non-parametric test was applied, followed by Dunn’s post hoc test. Graphical representations were created using GraphPad Prism 8.0. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05, and significance levels were denoted as follows: ns (not significant), “*” indicates that the control group is compared with the model group, and “#” indicates that the model group is compared with the group added with activator.“*” and “#” represent P < 0.05, “**” and “##” represent P < 0.01, “***”and “###” represent P < 0.001.

Results

Effects of FLHS on body weight, abdominal fat rate, liver index, liver morphology and liver function of laying hens

In order to explore the effects of HELP model on the growth index and liver function of laying hens, the changes of related indexes between the two groups were detected and compared. The changes of body weight, abdominal fat rate and liver index of layers in each experimental group are shown in Fig. 1A, B and D. Compared with the Control group, the body weight, abdominal fat rate and liver index of layers in the HELP group showed markedly upward trend (P < 0.01, P < 0.001).

Histopathological examination of the liver is presented in Fig. 1C. the structure of hepatic lobules in Control group was complete and arranged regularly. The central vein and the surrounding radial hepatic cords were clearly visible. Hepatocytes showed a typical double-layer structure, with uniform cell size, clear cytoplasm, round nucleus and located in the center of cells. In contrast, the liver tissue structure of HELP group is disordered, the arrangement of hepatic cords is irregular, the size of hepatocytes is uneven, and many fat vacuoles with different sizes can be seen in the cytoplasm, which causes the nucleus to be pushed to the cell edge, and in some cells, the boundary of the nucleus becomes blurred or even disappears.

The changes in plasma biochemical indexes of layers in each experimental groups are revealed in Fig. 1E-O. In comparison the Control group, the HELP group showed markedly increased plasma activity of AST and ALP (P < 0.01). Meanwhile, the levels of T-BIL, d-BIL, TG, TC, NEFA, and LDL-C were also observably elevated in the HELP group (P < 0.01). In contrast, the contents of TP, ALB, and HDL-C were markedly decreased (P < 0.001).

Immunofluorescence observation of SIRT3 and AMPKα−1 in liver

In order to explore the expression of SIRT3 and AMPKα−1 in liver under HELP model, the expression and subcellular localization of SIRT3 and AMPKα−1 in laying hen liver were assessed by immunofluorescence (Fig. 2A-B). In the Control group, strong green fluorescence signals for SIRT3 were predominantly observed in the extranuclear regions of hepatocytes, particularly around the central veins. In contrast, the HELP group exhibited a marked attenuation of SIRT3 fluorescence intensity. Red fluorescence, indicating AMPKα−1, was uniformly distributed in both the nucleus and cytoplasm of control hepatocytes, with prominent cytoplasmic staining. This signal was noticeably weaker and more disorganized in the HELP group. Merged images revealed partial co-localization of SIRT3 and AMPKα−1, visible as yellow fluorescence in the Control group, this co-localization was substantially reduced in the HELP group.

Effects of FFAs on SIRT3 and key metabolic regulators in hepatocytes

Immunofluorescence analysis of SIRT3 and AMPKα−1 in primary chicken embryo hepatocytes after 36 h of treatment is shown in Fig. 5B–D. In the Control group, both SIRT3 and AMPKα−1 exhibited strong and homogeneous fluorescence signals, indicating high expression levels and defined subcellular localization. In contrast, the FFA group showed markedly attenuated fluorescence intensities for both proteins, suggesting that FFAs impair their expression and cellular distribution. Quantitative analysis confirmed that the average fluorescence intensities of SIRT3 and AMPKα−1 were markedly reduced in FFA group than that in the Control group (P < 0.01).

As illustrated in Fig. 5E–G, q-PCR analysis showed that after 36 hours of FFAs-induced hepatocytes, the expression levels of SIRT3, AMPKα−1, and PGC-1α mRNA were markedly downregulated in the FFA group compared to the Control group (P < 0.001). Consistent with these findings, protein-level analyses (Fig. 5H–I) revealed that SIRT3 protein expression and AMPKα−1 phosphorylation were markedly decreased in the FFA group (P < 0.01). Additionally, PGC-1α protein expression also showed a marked lower (P < 0.05).

AR-C17 activates SIRT3 to regulate the expression of metabolism-related genes and proteins

To assess the impact of SIRT3 activation on metabolic pathways, we employed its activator, AR-C17, and examined subsequent changes in the expression of metabolism-related genes and proteins. The cytotoxicity of AR-C17 was assessed using the CCK-8 assay (Figure S1). Treatment with 5 μM and 10 μM AR-C17 did not significantly affect hepatocyte viability compared to the 0 μM group (P > 0.05). Nevertheless, when the concentration reached 20 μM, viability was significantly reduced (P < 0.05), and a further decline was observed at 30–50 μM.

As shown in Figure 7A, the expression levels of SIRT3, AMPKα−1, PGC-1α, NRF1, and TFAM mRNA were notably reduced in the FFA group compared with the Control group (P < 0.001). Intervention with 5 μM and 10 μM AR-C17 markedly increased the expression of SIRT3, AMPKα−1, PGC-1α, and TFAM mRNA (P < 0.01 or P < 0.001), and also notably elevated NRF1 mRNA levels (P < 0.05). The restorative effect of 10 μM AR-C17 was more pronounced than that of 5 μM. Notably, 10 μM AR-C17 restored SIRT3, AMPKα−1, NRF1, and TFAM mRNA to levels comparable to the Control group (P > 0.05), whereas 5 μM AR-C17 resulted in expression levels that remained significantly lower (P < 0.001 for SIRT3, AMPKα−1, and NRF1). At the protein level (Fig. 7B–C), SIRT3 expression in the FFA group showed a decreasing trend (P > 0.05). However, 10 μM AR-C17 significantly increased SIRT3 protein expression level in comparison with the FFA group (P < 0.05).

Discussion

Fatty liver hemorrhagic syndrome (FLHS) is a complex metabolic disorder that severely compromises hepatic lipid metabolism, leading to excessive triglyceride accumulation, oxidative stress, and mitochondrial dysfunction in laying hens (Cui et al., 2022; Li et al., 2025). In the present study, both in vivo and in vitro models consistently demonstrated that suppression of the SIRT3–AMPK–PGC-1α signaling cascade constitutes a central molecular event in FLHS pathogenesis. This dysregulation disrupts mitochondrial biogenesis and fatty acid oxidation, ultimately leading to hepatic lipid deposition. Pharmacological activation of SIRT3 by AR-C17 effectively reversed these pathological alterations, thereby validating SIRT3 as a critical metabolic regulator and potential therapeutic target in avian hepatic steatosis.

The HELP diet model successfully reproduced the principal pathological hallmarks of naturally occurring FLHS, including pronounced hepatic steatosis, extensive hepatocellular vacuolization, nuclear displacement, and hemorrhagic lesions. These findings are consistent with the clinical manifestations of spontaneous FLHS, as reported by Zhang et al., (Zhang et al., 2024) who described disrupted lobular structure, lipid droplet accumulation, and hepatocyte degeneration in affected hens. Currently, experimental FLHS models are primarily induced by two strategies: endocrine stimulation (such as estrogen administration) and nutritional manipulation, particularly HELP diets. Estrogen-induced models mimic hormonal imbalances that promote hepatic lipogenesis, whereas HELP models, as used in the present study, reflect metabolic stress caused by dietary excess of carbohydrates and energy combined with protein deficiency. This imbalance enhances de novo lipogenesis and restricts amino acid availability for protein synthesis, leading to hepatic lipid deposition and increased abdominal fat accumulation. Collectively, these features validate the HELP diet as a robust and physiologically relevant model for investigating the molecular mechanisms underlying FLHS pathogenesis. Biochemical analysis revealed distinct indicators of hepatic injury in HELP-fed hens. Elevated plasma AST, ALP, T-BIL, and d-BIL concentrations reflected hepatocellular leakage and impaired bile metabolism, whereas reductions in TP and ALB indicated a decline in hepatic synthetic capacity. Similar biochemical profiles have been observed in both clinical and experimental cases of FLHS (Cui et al., 2022; Miao et al., 2024). Moreover, the indexes of dyslipidemia in serum biochemical detection are the increased levels of TG, TC, and NEFA, together with decreased HDL-C, have been systematically studied and annotated as the representative metabolic characteristics of FLHS and MAFLD in bird and mammal systems (Liao et al., 2022; Sangro et al., 2023). HDL-C plays a key role in antioxidation, mediating reverse cholesterol transport and maintaining lipid homeostasis, thus, its reduction exacerbates hepatic lipid overload and oxidative injury. Conversely, elevated levels of LDL-C further contribute to the accumulation of lipid in the liver by transporting excess cholesterol to the liver and surrounding tissues, promoting cellular uptake via LDL receptors (Liang et al., 2015). In the context of FLHS, increased LDL-C not only aggravates steatosis but also enhances oxidative modification of lipoproteins, leading to inflammation and endoplasmic reticulum stress. The combined effect of reduced HDL-C and elevated LDL-C thus disrupts systemic cholesterol balance, accelerates progression of hepatic lipidosis, and heightens the risk of metabolic diseases.

Elevated circulating NEFA, released from peripheral adipose tissue, contributes to lipotoxicity by overwhelming mitochondrial β-oxidation capacity and generating excessive ROS. Wen et al. studies revealed that NEFA-induced lipotoxic stress disrupts mitochondrial membrane integrity, inhibits electron transport chain activity, and accelerates oxidative stress–mediated hepatocyte injury (Wen et al., 2025). Consistent with these findings, the biochemical alterations in our study not only indicate dysregulated lipid metabolism but also suggest secondary mitochondrial impairment driven by persistent lipotoxic challenge.

Accumulated evidences revealed that impaired fatty acid oxidative metabolism constitutes a fundamental pathogenic mechanism in FLHS. In this study, both the HELP–fed hens and the FFAs-treated hepatocytes exhibited significant downregulation of genes encoding key enzymes of mitochondrial β-oxidation, including PPARα, ACOX1, CPT1A, CPT2, ACADL, and ACADM. PPARα acts as a master transcriptional regulator that coordinates fatty acid transport and oxidation through transcriptional activation of downstream effectors such as CPT1A, the key enzymes in mitochondrial transformation. Kuang et al. demonstrated that PPARα deficiency exacerbates lipid accumulation and oxidative stress in NAFLD mice (Kuang et al., 2023), whereas Xie et al. reported that herbal polyphenols restored hepatic PPARα and CPT1A expression, thereby improving lipid metabolic homeostasis (Xie et al., 2020). The study revealed that suppression of the PPARα–CPT1A axis in FFAs-treated hepatocytes led to decreased fatty acid oxidation and the intracellular lipid droplets accumulated obviously, as confirmed by Oil Red O staining. This finding agrees with prior studies in mammalian steatosis models, where reduced PPARα activity was identified as a central driver of hepatic triglyceride accumulation and oxidative imbalance. Therefore, the consistent downregulation of fatty acid oxidation genes across avian and mammalian systems underscores the evolutionary conservation of this metabolic defect in steatotic liver disorders.

Recent studies have highlighted mitochondrial dysfunction as a pivotal link between lipid accumulation and oxidative stress in hepatic steatosis (San et al., 2023). In this study, both the HELP diet model and FFAs-treated hepatocytes exhibited profound suppression of the SIRT3–AMPK–PGC-1α signaling pathway, as reflected by reduced transcription and protein expression of SIRT3, AMPKα−1, and PGC-1α, along with decreased phosphorylation of AMPKα−1 and weakened colocalization between SIRT3 and AMPKα−1. In the present study, the HELP group exhibited a trend toward reduced AMPKα−1 phosphorylation compared with the Control group, although this difference did not reach statistical significance. Several possibilities may account for this observation. First, AMPKα−2, rather than AMPKα−1, is considered the predominant isoform mediating hepatic metabolic responses under stress conditions in various species (Ding et al., 2024). Thus, the lack of significant change in AMPKα−1 phosphorylation does not preclude a functional role for AMPKα−2 in the observed signaling changes. Second, the phosphorylation status of a single isoform may not fully capture the activation state of the AMPK complex, as total AMPKα protein levels were significantly reduced in the HELP group. Importantly, despite the nonsignificant change in AMPKα−1 phosphorylation, the overall SIRT3–AMPKα–PGC-1α signaling axis was significantly impaired in the HELP group, as evidenced by reduced SIRT3 expression, decreased total AMPKα and PGC-1α levels, and downregulation of downstream targets (NRF1, TFAM, and fatty acid oxidation-related genes). This suggests that the broader signaling cascade was compromised, and future studies using isoform-specific knockdown approaches may help clarify the relative contributions of AMPKα−1 and AMPKα−2 in the pathogenesis of FLHS. As the core regulatory hub of cell metabolism, this pathway not only coordinates the generation and renewal of mitochondria, but also finely regulates the energy metabolic flow and redox balance, thus playing an indispensable role in maintaining cell metabolic homeostasis. Downstream, the reduced expression of NRF1 and TFAM indicates disruption of mitochondrial DNA replication and transcription, both of which are essential for sustaining oxidative phosphorylation and maintaining mitochondrial mass. NRF1 regulates nuclear-encoded mitochondrial genes involved in respiratory chain assembly, whereas TFAM stabilizes and transcribes mitochondrial DNA (Hao et al., 2021). Suppression of these factors, together with decreased expression of electron transport chain subunits (NDUFA9, SDHA, UQCRC1, COX4I1, and ATP5B), leads to impaired oxidative phosphorylation and reduced ATP synthesis (Vercellino and Sazanov, 2022). Concurrently, the decreased expression of SOD2 weakens the mitochondrial antioxidant defense, aggravating ROS accumulation and initiating a self-perpetuating cycle of oxidative damage (Hu et al., 2025). The results are in line with mammalian studies showing that SIRT3 deficiency destabilizes the electron transport chain and enhances oxidative stress, ultimately promoting hepatic steatosis. Collectively, our results extend this mechanistic framework to avian FLHS and emphasize the pivotal regulatory role of SIRT3 in maintaining mitochondrial metabolic integrity.

Pharmacological activation of SIRT3 by AR-C17 effectively ameliorated FFAs-induced hepatocellular steatosis, highlighting the functional importance of SIRT3 in sustaining mitochondrial health. Treatment with AR-C17 restored SIRT3, AMPKα−1, and PGC-1α expression and reactivated the downstream transcriptional cascade involving NRF1 and TFAM, thereby enhancing mitochondrial biogenesis and respiratory function (Wang et al., 2023). Simultaneously, AR-C17 upregulated SOD2, strengthening antioxidant defenses, and increased the expression of PPARα and CPT1A, leading to improved fatty acid oxidative metabolism. Functionally, these molecular improvements were accompanied by reductions in TG and TC content, decreased AST and ALT release, and enhanced hepatocyte viability (Yang et al., 2023). These results are consistent with previous reports in mammalian models of NAFLD and metabolic syndrome, where SIRT3 activation alleviated oxidative stress and improved mitochondrial function (Liu et al., 2020). Moreover, the ability of AR-C17 to enhance mitochondrial quality control even under physiological conditions suggests potential prophylactic applications for metabolic health. Collectively, the results demonstrate that pharmacological activation of SIRT3 by AR-C17 reestablishes mitochondrial homeostasis and lipid oxidative capacity, representing a promising strategy for both prevention and treatment of FLHS in laying hens.

Limitations

Several limitations of the present study should be acknowledged. First, the in vivo experiment was conducted at a single time point (112 days), which does not allow for analysis of the temporal relationship between SIRT3 pathway inhibition and the progressive development of FLHS. Whether the downregulation of SIRT3 signaling precedes or follows the onset of hepatic steatosis and mitochondrial dysfunction remains to be determined. Longitudinal studies incorporating multiple time points are warranted to establish causality and to determine whether SIRT3 activation is more effective as a preventive or therapeutic intervention. Second, while our molecular data—including the expression of mitochondrial biogenesis markers (NRF1, TFAM), respiratory chain components (NDUFA9, SDHA, UQCRC1, COX4I1, ATP5B), and fatty acid oxidation–related genes (PPARα, ACOX1, CPT1A, CPT2, ACADL, ACADM)—strongly suggest that SIRT3 activation improves mitochondrial oxidative function, we did not perform direct functional measurements of mitochondrial activity, such as mitochondrial membrane potential, ATP production, reactive oxygen species levels, or oxygen consumption rates. Such assays would provide more definitive mechanistic evidence and will be incorporated in future studies. Third, although the HELP group exhibited a trend toward reduced AMPKα−1 phosphorylation, this change did not reach statistical significance. The relative contributions of AMPKα−1 versus AMPKα−2 isoforms in the hepatic response to metabolic stress in laying hens remain unclear, and future studies using isoform-specific knockdown approaches may help clarify this issue. Notably, despite this nonsignificant change, the overall SIRT3–AMPKα–PGC-1α signaling axis was significantly impaired in the HELP group, indicating that the broader signaling cascade was compromised. Finally, while we used a pharmacological activator of SIRT3 (AR-C17) to demonstrate the therapeutic potential of SIRT3 activation, genetic approaches—such as hepatocyte-specific SIRT3 overexpression or knockout—would further strengthen the causal evidence for the role of SIRT3 in FLHS pathogenesis. Addressing these limitations in future investigations will help further elucidate the precise role of SIRT3 and facilitate the development of targeted nutritional strategies against FLHS.

Conclusion

In summary, this study provides mechanistic evidence that FLHS in laying hens is driven by mitochondrial dysfunction and impaired fatty acid oxidation, resulting from suppression of the SIRT3–AMPK–PGC-1α axis. Pharmacological activation of SIRT3 by AR-C17 restores mitochondrial biogenesis, enhances oxidative metabolism, and alleviates lipid accumulation, thereby offering a novel strategy for managing FLHS. Beyond avian health, these findings broaden our understanding of conserved mitochondrial regulatory mechanisms across species. Future research should focus on optimizing SIRT3 activator formulations and evaluating their efficacy in field conditions, potentially integrating them with nutritional strategies to enhance metabolic resilience in commercial poultry production.

Sexual Health Needs and Physician‐Patient Interaction Experiences Among Postoperative Radical Prostatectomy Patients in China: A Qualitative Study
Authors: Hanjing Zhu, Ting Xu, Juan Xie, Songyang Wei, Xiaoyu Jin, Yi Meng, Xiaoqin Lu, Han Zhang, Yinjie Zhu, Jianhui Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAimsTo systematically elucidate Chinese prostate cancer patients' perceptions and needs regarding sexual health and sexual experience following radical prostatectomy from dual perspectives of both patients and healthcare professionals. It further analyzes barriers and facilitators in physician‐patient communication about sexual health problems during clinical interactions, thereby providing scientific evidence for developing effective sexual health support interventions.DesignA qualitative study.MethodsAn interpretive phenomenological research methodology was conducted using semi‐structured interviews with purposive sampling that were audio‐recorded and transcribed verbatim. Interviews covered dimensions including sexual function changes, psychological adjustment, physician‐patient interaction and social support. Data analysis was performed using the content analysis method. Content analysis method was conducted using Nvivo 12 software.ResultsFour themes and thirteen sub‐themes were identified: Sexual health awareness within psychological and sociocultural contexts (Passive acceptance within practical constraints; Implicit constraints of traditional sexual cultural norms; Mapping of age‐directed psychological expectations; Externalisation tendency in attributing sexual dysfunction); Partner Relationship Dynamics and Intimate Interactions (decline in sexual needs and lack of communication; partner support's critical role in sexual health adaptation; decoupling of emotional bonding from sexual functioning experience); Perception and Expression of Sexual Health Needs (prioritization of treatment‐related symptom management; physician‐patient information asymmetry hindering sexual need expression; illness‐experience‐driven health support‐seeking behaviours); Physician‐Patient Interaction Experience and Need for Supportive Environment (desire for professional medical care guidance; fostering supportive environments to facilitate expression of sexual health problems and recovery; demand for diversified rehabilitation strategies and information acquisition channels). Chinese prostate cancer patients exhibit multidimensional complexity in sexual health experiences and needs following radical prostatectomy, where psychosocial and cultural factors, partner relationship dynamics, impaired expression of needs and healthcare support environment play crucial roles. Future clinical practice urgently requires healthcare professionals to enhance awareness and skills regarding sexual health and education, foster supportive environments, and develop precision intervention strategies integrating the biopsychosocial medical model for prostate cancer patients' sexual health.Implications for the Profession and/or Patient CareThis study systematically delineates the cognitive characteristics of sexual health among prostate cancer patients within the Chinese sociocultural context (e.g., passive acceptance), proposes ‘emotional detachment’ as a novel and constructive adaptive strategy, introduces a routinized sexual health assessment framework at the nursing operational level, and further examines barriers and influencing factors related to patient sexual health from the healthcare provider perspective.ImpactThis study systematically examined the factors influencing sexual health‐related issues among prostate cancer patients from multiple dimensions, including psychological cultural factors, partner relationship dynamics, impaired expression of needs, and healthcare support environments in sexual health outcomes. It offers a China‐oriented perspective on the sexual health experiences of prostate cancer patients globally. This research contributing a valuable addition to the international literature on prostate cancer of sexual health. Furthermore, it provides a foundational reference for future development of precise predictive models that integrate the ‘biopsychosocial’ medical framework and are specifically tailored to prostate cancer patients.Reporting MethodThe Consolidated Criteria for Reporting Qualitative Research (COREQ) checklist was used.Patient ContributionPatients and healthcare professionals provided valuable insights into the core domains of sexual health needs after prostatectomy through their active participation in this study.

Background

Prostate cancer ranks among the most prevalent malignancies in men globally, with the latest cancer statistics indicating it holds the highest incidence rate among male cancers (Siegel et al. 2025). In China, the incidence of prostate cancer similarly demonstrates a rapidly increasing annual trend and a shift toward younger demographics. The most recent data released by China's National Cancer Center reveals that prostate cancer has become the most common malignant tumour in the male genitourinary system in China. Its case count constitutes 8.2% of the global total prostate cancer incidence, posing a severe threat to men's life and health (Zheng et al. 2024). With the advancement of diagnostic and therapeutic concepts, the indications for radical prostatectomy (RP) have expanded continuously, establishing it as a primary treatment for localized and locally advanced prostate cancer (Cornford et al. 2024). Despite technological refinements in surgical instruments and increasingly sophisticated techniques, intraoperative damage to pelvic floor structures and neurovascular bundles inevitably causes varying degrees of sexual dysfunction. Patients frequently experience sexual health alterations including diminished libido, erectile dysfunction, coital incontinence and penile shortening (Lewicki et al. 2025; Pikramenos et al. 2023). These changes not only trigger negative psychological states such as depression, anxiety, body image disturbance and impaired self‐esteem—threatening overall quality of life—but also further compromise marital intimacy (Gallegos 2023; Pedraza et al. 2022).

With the continuous extension of survival in prostate cancer patients, prostate cancer is increasingly regarded as a chronic disease. Therefore, improving the quality of life during the survival period is crucial for these patients. Sexual health, as a critical component of quality of life, holds particular significance in the treatment experience and long‐term survival of prostate cancer patients (Rossi et al. 2025). However, influenced by traditional ideologies and sexual culture, patients often hesitate to proactively discuss sexual health problems. This has led to the long‐term neglect of sexual health needs among Chinese prostate cancer patients after surgery, creating a ‘silent zone’. Healthcare professionals further lack understanding about patients' sexual life experiences and needs. Therefore, this study aims to systematically reveal prostate cancer patients' perceptions and needs regarding sexual health and sexual experiences from dual perspectives of both patients and healthcare professionals. It further analyzes barriers and facilitators in physician‐patient communication about sexual health issues during clinical interactions, thereby providing scientific evidence for developing effective sexual health support interventions in future stages.

Methods

Design

A qualitative exploratory design was adopted to determine Chinese prostate cancer patients' perceptions and needs regarding sexual health and sexual experience following radical prostatectomy. To increase transparency of the study, the Consolidated Criteria for Reporting Qualitative Research (COREQ) checklist was used.

Setting

Using purposive sampling, prostate cancer patients and healthcare professionals meeting the inclusion/exclusion criteria were selected from a hospital in Shanghai between October 2024 and March 2025 as study participants. Sample size was determined based on the principle of information saturation, meaning that no new content or information emerged during data analysis. To further ensure the comprehensiveness and robustness of the research results, we additionally interviewed 2 more patients and 1 more healthcare professional for saturation verification, and no new themes or valid information were identified in the supplementary interview content. The final cohort comprised 22 prostate cancer patients and nine healthcare professionals. Table 1 presents the baseline characteristics of the patients, while Table 2 details the demographic information of the healthcare professionals. The study was conducted in accordance with the Consolidated Criteria for Reporting Qualitative Research (COREQ) checklist.

Sample

Patient Eligibility

Inclusion Criteria

(1) Patients pathologically diagnosed with prostate cancer; (2) Having undergone radical prostatectomy; (3) Possessing stable vital signs; (4) Being mentally lucid and capable of cooperating with the research; (5) Providing informed consent and participating voluntarily.

Exclusion Criteria

Known to have other malignancies that are progressing or require current treatment; Presence of central nervous system metastases and/or carcinomatous meningitis accompanied by related symptoms; Patients with severe mental illness and cognitive impairment unable to communicate smoothly; homosexual and bisexual individuals.

Healthcare Professionals Eligibility

Inclusion Criteria

Extensive professional knowledge and practical experience in prostate oncology; holding a bachelor's degree or higher; junior professional title or above; > 2 years of work experience.

Exclusion Criteria

Excluding trainees and individuals on medical leave exceeding 6 months.

Procedures

Interview Guide

Based on preliminary literature review and expert consultation within the field, an initial interview outline was developed according to the research objectives. Subsequently, 1–2 patients and 1–2 healthcare professionals meeting the inclusion/exclusion criteria were selected for preliminary interviews. Issues identified during these interviews were addressed through revisions, and the final interview outline was established following discussions within the research team.

Patient Interview Outline

(1) Did your doctor discuss potential postoperative sexual function changes with you before surgery? What were your feelings at that time? (2) How have you and your spouse communicated about sexual life issues since your diagnosis? (3) What specific impacts or changes has surgery brought to your sexuality, sexual life, sexual satisfaction, or marital relationship? (4) Among these changes, which causes you the greatest concern? (5) How do you understand these changes? (e.g., attributing them to surgery, aging, psychological factors, etc.) (6) What approaches have you tried to cope with these changes? (e.g., seeking medical help, self‐regulation, partner support, etc.) (7) How do you understand the relationship between this disease and sexual life? Through which sources did you acquire this understanding? (8) What postoperative sexual health information or support do you wish to receive? Through which channels (who, format)? When? (9) What factors hinder your pursuit of sexual health after surgery? Under what circumstances are you more likely to seek support? (10) Have you proactively raised sexual health concerns with healthcare professionals? If not, what are the primary reasons? (e.g., embarrassment, perceived unimportance, uncertain how to broach the subject) (11) What concerns do you believe prostate cancer patients have regarding sexual activity? (12) If sexual activity were absent, what changes do you anticipate in the emotional bond between you and your spouse?

Healthcare Professionals Interview Outline

(1) How do you understand the sexual health of prostate cancer patients? (2) How do you discuss sexual life, sexual health needs and sexual health problems with patients (including timing, setting and language choices)? (3) What do you perceive as the primary concerns of patients regarding postoperative sexual health problems? (4) What are the main obstacles you encounter when providing sexual health support to prostate cancer patients (e.g., patient avoidance, cultural taboos, time constraints)? (5) What differences do you observe between patients' and healthcare professionals' understanding of postoperative sexual health needs? (6) What methods/strategies do you consider effective in promoting the sexual health and experience of patients after prostate cancer surgery?

Data Collection

Ethical approval was obtained from the appropriate institutional review boards at the study centres. Semi‐structured in‐depth interviews were conducted face‐to‐face by researchers who had received adequate training in qualitative research methodologies and possessed sufficient knowledge and experience. Prior to the interview, a rapport between healthcare providers and patients was established. Participants were informed about the study's purpose, content and potential discussion topics. Following the acquisition of informed consent and permission for audio recording, participants signed the informed consent form before the interview commenced. The entire interview was audio‐recorded. Sessions began with open‐ended questions, with subsequent question sequencing flexibly adjusted based on the interview dynamics. Interviewers avoided leading or suggestive language while skillfully employing probing, clarifying and restating techniques. Concurrently, participants' non‐verbal behaviours (gestures, facial expressions, etc.) were closely observed and documented. This approach ensured participants could fully articulate their perspectives, thereby maximising the comprehensiveness and authenticity of the collected data. Interviews were conducted in quiet and private consultation rooms without external disturbances. Suitable times for participants were selected, avoiding meal times, periods of fatigue, or emotional instability. Each interview lasted approximately 30–40 min.

Data Analysis

Within 24–48 h after each interview, audio recordings were transcribed verbatim into textual materials supplemented by documented non‐verbal behaviours, and the content analysis method was employed using Nvivo 12 software for systematic data analysis: first, two researchers independently conducted open coding to extract 326 meaningful original statements as initial free codes and establish corresponding nodes in Nvivo 12; second, through axial coding, related initial codes were aggregated into 28 main categories and 56 sub‐categories based on logical connections and semantic similarity, forming a two‐level tree node structure in the software; finally, centered on the core research question, selective coding was performed to integrate core categories into four core themes and thirteen sub‐themes, constructing a three‐level coding framework. During the process, Nvivo 12's ‘Coding Comparison Query’ function was used to verify coding consistency, with the final Krippendorff's α coefficient reaching 0.89 (≥ 0.80), and discrepancies were resolved through collective discussion with a third qualitative research expert; supplementary interview data were imported for saturation verification, and no new nodes or categories emerged, confirming information saturation.

Results

This study identified four themes and 13 sub‐themes: Sexual health awareness within psychological and sociocultural contexts (Passive acceptance within practical constraints; Implicit constraints of traditional sexual cultural norms; Mapping of age‐directed psychological expectations; Externalisation tendency in attributing sexual dysfunction); Partner Relationship Dynamics and Intimate Interactions (decline in sexual needs and lack of communication; partner support's critical role in sexual health adaptation; decoupling of emotional bonding from sexual functioning experience); Perception and Expression of Sexual Health Needs (prioritization of treatment‐related symptom management; physician‐patient information asymmetry hindering sexual need expression; illness‐experience‐driven health support‐seeking behaviours); Physician‐Patient Interaction Experience and Need for Supportive Environment (desire for professional medical care guidance; fostering supportive environments to facilitate expression of sexual health problems and recovery; demand for diversified rehabilitation strategies and information acquisition channels).

Theme 1: Sexual Health Awareness Within Psychological and Sociocultural Contexts

Sub‐Theme 1: Passive Acceptance Within Practical Constraints

This sub‐theme was mentioned by 22 patients (100%) and eight healthcare professionals (88.9%), with a total coding frequency of 67 times. Although radical prostatectomy (RP), as a primary treatment for localized and locally advanced prostate cancer, achieves favourable oncological outcomes, varying surgical approaches, procedural steps, and intraoperative neurovascular bundle injury commonly lead to postoperative erectile dysfunction in patients. However, when confronted with cancer, patients are compelled to compromise and passively accept the erectile dysfunction complications resulting from RP.P7: Surgery inevitably leads to loss of sexual function – what alternatives exist? My physician insisted on surgical intervention, acknowledging it would eliminate erectile capability. Forgoing surgery wasn't an option as the tumour would continue progressing. P17: We were psychologically prepared – sacrificing sexual function became unavoidable. What choice remained? Preserving life took absolute precedence.

P18: I never proactively asked the doctor about this aspect, mainly due to embarrassment. I worried that at my advanced age, having such concerns might seem inappropriate. Cultural attitudes also influenced my hesitation.

Sub‐Theme 3: Mapping of Age‐Directed Psychological Expectations

This sub‐theme was mentioned by 18 patients (81.8%) and seven healthcare professionals (77.8%), with a total coding frequency of 48 times. Sexuality persists throughout life, and sexual health plays a vital role in individuals' physical and mental well‐being, subjective happiness, marital intimacy, and offspring health. However, with advancing age, the decline in organ function and reduced hormone levels increasingly manifest as issues of sexual aging. In this study, patients widely held the perception that their middle‐to‐older age rendered decreased libido and impaired sexual function inevitable; consequently, they felt no need to express or fulfill further demands regarding sexual health.P1: I believe sexual activity naturally declines with advancing age. The frequency diminishes like a growing disparity, so I've resigned to letting nature take its course without seeking treatment. P4: At this older age, such thoughts have faded. But several decades ago when my physical condition permitted, I still maintained interest in this aspect. P8: Given my advanced age—approaching 70—I'm no longer concerned about sexual activity.

Sub‐Theme 4: Externalisation Tendency in Attributing Sexual Dysfunction

This sub‐theme was mentioned by 20 patients (90.9%) and eight healthcare professionals (88.9%), with a total coding frequency of 55 times. Despite increased life expectancy and China's transition into an aging society, public attention remains focused on diagnosing and treating physical illnesses among middle‐aged and elderly individuals, while insufficient consideration is given to their sexual health problems. This has resulted in inadequate public education regarding seniors' sexual needs and related health concerns. In this study, patients commonly attributed their sexual health problems to external factors such as surgery, medication and aging.P3: I feel that sexual function naturally declines with age. P16: I consider surgery to have the most significant impact. It has indeed caused certain physical damage. Additionally, there's a psychological aspect: sudden severe loss of desire and diminished motivation for life.

This sub‐theme was mentioned by 20 patients (90.9%) and eight healthcare professionals (88.9%), with a total coding frequency of 61 times. Traditional perceptions that middle‐aged and elderly individuals experience declining sexual needs and high incidence of sexual dysfunction due to physiological and familial factors remain widespread in public consciousness. Coupled with generally insufficient sexual health education for this demographic, society subconsciously assumes that sexual desires and needs diminish with advancing age. In this study, the vast majority of patients reported a significant decline in both their own and their spouses' interest in sexual activity with advancing age. Both parties refrained from discussing this matter and demonstrated a lack of communication on the subject.P4: We don't communicate about sexual needs. What would we even discuss at our age? Our sexual life had essentially ceased even before I got sick. It's normal at our age—we've gotten used to it.

Although they also mention issues such as penile shortening and decreased libido, their predominant concern remains the restoration of urinary control. P10: Post‐surgical urine leakage is the most distressing issue. While sexual dysfunction is problematic, it at least doesn't disrupt daily living. However, urinary leakage significantly impacts quality of life – I cannot travel long distances and find many activities impossible to perform.

In conclusion, this qualitative study adopted a rigorous content analysis method with multiple reliability verification strategies to analyse the semi‐structured interview data of 22 prostate cancer patients and nine healthcare professionals. Two researchers trained in qualitative research independently conducted open coding, axial coding and selective coding of the interview text; the coding consistency coefficient (Krippendorff's α) was calculated to verify the coding reliability, and the final α coefficient reached 0.89 (≥ 0.80, indicating high coding consistency). For the few coding opinions with divergence, the research team held a collective discussion with a third qualitative research expert to reach a unified conclusion. Based on the above rigorous analysis process, four core themes and thirteen sub‐themes were finally identified (the thematic framework was confirmed by data triangulation of patients and healthcare professionals' perspectives). No new information or themes emerged from the supplementary saturation verification interviews, further confirming the stability of the thematic extraction results.

Discussion

Through semi‐structured interviews with patients undergoing radical prostatectomy and relevant healthcare professionals, this study comprehensively reveals the current status and core challenges in meeting sexual health needs after radical prostatectomy. The investigation spans multiple dimensions including psychosocial‐cultural context, partner interaction dynamics, impaired expression of needs and healthcare support environment. These findings not only corroborate existing literature regarding the prevalence of sexual dysfunction after radical prostatectomy (Araujo and Zago 2019; Chambers et al. 2017; Charlick et al. 2024), but more significantly, systematically elucidate the unique complex factors affecting sexual health recovery in Chinese prostate cancer patients post‐surgery. This research provides scientific evidence and theoretical foundation for establishing a patient‐centered, integrated biopsychosocial support system addressing post‐prostatectomy sexual health rehabilitation (Araujo and Zago 2019).

This study reveals that patients' cognition and adaptation process regarding postoperative sexual health are significantly influenced by psychosocial and cultural factors. The sub‐theme of ‘Passive acceptance within practical constraints’ profoundly reflects patients' strategic choice to prioritize survival over sexual health when confronting cancer challenges and treatment necessities, which aligns with the ‘survival‐first’ values inherent in Chinese culture (Heyne et al. 2023; Yan et al. 2011). However, the sub‐theme of ‘Implicit constraints of traditional sexual cultural norms’ highlights how specific cultural conventions—such as the obscurity of sexual expression under traditional ideologies, stereotyped masculinity, and the stigmatization of sexual topics—particularly burden middle‐aged and elderly patients raised in specific sociocultural environments. These constraints constitute a substantial psychological burden that significantly impedes proactive help‐seeking and open discussion about sexual health concerns. In international studies (Downing et al. 2019), most male patients reported lacking beneficial interventions to support sexual function following prostate cancer treatment. Concurrently, the ‘Mapping of Age‐Directed Psychological Expectations’ reveals that some patients perceive declining sexual function as an inevitable consequence of aging. This cognitive bias may lead them to underestimate the side effects of surgery or treatment, thereby delaying their pursuit of effective interventions. Researchers abroad have actively explored psychotherapy interventions based on Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT) and acceptance frameworks (Bossio et al. 2018, 2019; Pieramico et al. 2023). These approaches aim to help men better experience sexuality while challenging excessive focus on sexual performance and erectile function, thereby enhancing the potential for improved sexual satisfaction among prostate cancer survivors. Particularly noteworthy in this study is that patients tended to attribute problems to external factors such as surgery rather than to their own adjustable psychological states. This inherent mindset may undermine their intrinsic motivation for active participation in rehabilitation and diminish their sense of self‐efficacy. These findings suggest that nursing interventions for Chinese prostate cancer patients must extend beyond the singular dimension of survival prolongation. There is an urgent need to proactively identify and deconstruct these cognitive impairments deeply rooted in psychosocial‐cultural contexts. This approach enables personalized psychological counselling, provides cognitive behavioural interventions to promote cognitive restructuring, delivers culturally sensitive sexual health education and encourages and guides partners to jointly participate in communication and emotional bonding training. More importantly, standardized sexual health assessments and health education should be incorporated into the routine care and follow‐up procedures after radical prostatectomy, with healthcare professionals proactively and regularly initiating discussions on sexual health issues (Downing et al. 2019; Wang et al. 2022).

Furthermore, the findings of this study strongly underscore the indispensable role of partners in patients' post‐operative sexual health adaptation and recovery process (Theme 2). The sub‐theme ‘Decline in Sexual Needs and Lack of Communication’ reveals that postoperative couples often actively reduce sexual contact and communication due to concerns about disease progression, fear of causing discomfort, existing misunderstandings, or simply to avoid embarrassment. This avoidance behaviour readily forms a vicious cycle detrimental to sexual health, consistent with Researcher Wang's findings (Wang et al. 2022). However, in stark contrast, the partner's provision of active emotional support, full understanding, continued patience, and shared commitment to confronting challenges serves as a crucial cornerstone for patients' psychological adjustment and implementation of rehabilitation strategies. Notably, this study also identified within China's prostate cancer patient cohort the phenomenon of ‘emotional bonding divorced from sexual functioning experience’. It indicates that a significant proportion of patients and their partners successfully decouple intimacy from penetrative intercourse itself, instead sustaining emotional bonds and fulfilment through non‐penetrative intimate activities—such as caressing, embracing and emotional communication—to maintain emotional connection and satisfaction (Wang et al. 2025). Li et al. (2022) demonstrated in their study on unmet sexual health needs of patients and their female partners after prostate cancer treatment that most female partners felt overlooked, desired expanded sexual lives and urgently required resources and methods to address relationship issues. This finding not only powerfully challenges the narrow conception equating ‘sexual health’ with ‘penetrative intercourse’, but also directly indicates that clinical caregivers should actively advocate for and guide patients and their partners in exploring diverse intimacy patterns. They should encourage partners' joint participation in counselling and educational activities, while providing specialized guidance specifically designed to enhance communication between partners, deepen emotional support and develop shared coping strategies (Li et al. 2022; Wang et al. 2022).

From the physician‐patient communication perspective, this study clearly reveals multiple barriers faced by Chinese prostate cancer patients in perceiving and expressing sexual health needs (Theme 3) following radical prostatectomy. The sub‐theme ‘Priority of treatment‐related symptom management’ demonstrates that physical symptoms like urinary incontinence and pain—which more directly impact daily quality of life—typically dominate patients' concerns, resulting in sexual health needs being marginalized. More critically, these challenges stem from physician‐patient information asymmetry. In this interview, patients widely reported that healthcare professionals failed to proactively guide and provide clear, consistent and comprehensible information regarding sexual health problems and rehabilitation therapy, aligning with findings from Charlick et al. (2025). Coupled with prevalent time constraints during consultations in Chinese public healthcare institutions and sensitivities surrounding sexuality‐related topics (e.g., patients' concerns about being perceived as ‘inappropriate’ or burdening the doctor; as well as patients' feelings of shyness or shame), these factors collectively constitute insurmountable communication barriers. Similarly, Gupta N's study indicates (Gupta et al. 2023) that prostate cancer patients and their partners lack healthcare team‐led sexual health counselling and support in coping with and adapting to sexual dysfunction, which affects their ability to effectively communicate about sexual dysfunction within intimate relationships. Despite exhibiting ‘illness experience‐driven health support‐seeking behaviours’, such proactive initiatives often occur through private, informal, or significantly delayed channels. This strongly suggests that clinical healthcare professionals must proactively break the silence by incorporating sexual health issues into the core components of routine follow‐ups and health education after radical prostatectomy. Starting from the preoperative phase, they should consider patients' needs for sexual function preservation. During postoperative follow‐ups, it is essential to create a safe, open and non‐judgmental communication environment using patient‐friendly language. Healthcare providers should actively guide patients to express sexual health concerns and needs rather than passively waiting for patients to raise questions (Gupta et al. 2023).

Moreover, patients' clear and urgent call for ‘Physician‐Patient Interaction Experience and Need for Supportive Environment’ (Theme 4) emerged distinctly throughout the interviews. The sub‐theme of patients' ‘Desire for Professional Medical Care Guidance’ directly addresses critical deficiencies in current clinical practice, including inadequate sexual health information provision, absence of systematic functional and needs assessment, and delayed information delivery timing. Prostate cancer patients require not only information about surgical impacts on erectile dysfunction, but more urgently demand practical guidance on erectile function assessment, multidimensional erectile dysfunction treatment approaches, accurate sexual health knowledge, intimate relationship maintenance and communication strategies and psychological distress management. During this process, supportive communication contexts facilitate the expression and rehabilitation of sexual health problems. These include healthcare professionals demonstrating high levels of empathy, patience and professionalism; ensuring privacy protection during communication; and actively exploring dedicated consultation times and spaces (e.g., establishing nurse‐led sexual health counselling and sex therapy clinics staffed by specially trained nurses) (Papadopoulou and Schubach 2020; Yuen et al. 2022). Notably, the sub‐theme ‘Demand for Diversified Rehabilitation Strategies and Information Acquisition Channels’ reflects patients' strong expectations for personalized, integrated sexual health management strategies. These strategies should systematically encompass precise assessment, pharmacotherapy, physical rehabilitation, electrophysiological therapy, psychological counselling and professionally reliable information support. Particularly within the current era of artificial intelligence, effectively leveraging platforms such as generative AI to provide real‐time dynamic health education and management is critically important.

Study Limitations

This study had certain limitations: Firstly, the data were sourced exclusively from a single medical institution, potentially restricting the perspectives of both patients and healthcare professionals. Secondly, being a qualitative interview study, the findings may carry inherent bias due to the professional qualifications of the researchers themselves. Finally, although this study incorporated adequate sample sizes of patients and healthcare professionals, it did not explore the perceptions and experiences regarding this issue from the partners' perspective. This omission may result in insufficiently comprehensive perspectives in the present research. Finally, the data were sourced exclusively from a single tertiary hospital in Shanghai, which restricts the external validity and generalizability of the findings.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study provides an in‐depth exploration of the multidimensional complexity of sexual health experiences among Chinese patients following radical prostatectomy, highlighting the critical roles of psychosocial and cultural factors, partner relationship dynamics, impaired expression of needs, and healthcare support environments in sexual health outcomes. Furthermore, this research precisely identifies unique phenomena rooted in China's sociocultural context, revealing patients' compromised attitudes and attribution patterns toward sexual health problems under the dual pressures of cancer survivorship and prevalent societal misconceptions about sexual health. These findings are essential for developing future precision intervention strategies that integrate the biopsychosocial medical model. Simultaneously, the identified structural barriers underlying patients' sexual health needs and physician‐patient interaction experiences point to the necessity for profound changes within the healthcare service system itself and in the professional knowledge and skills of healthcare professionals. This provides precise targets for improving service quality and enhancing patient experience.

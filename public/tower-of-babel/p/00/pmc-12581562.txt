A case of Candida parapsilosis endocarditis complicated by right hepatic artery aneurysm, rupture and hemorrhage of brain abscess: case report and literature review
Authors: Yuqi Wang, Ruijun Ni, Qunjun Duan, Jianfeng Xu, Junqing Zhou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractA patient was diagnosed with infective endocarditis caused by Candida parapsilosis, which further led to inflammation of the right hepatic artery and the formation of pseudoaneurysm, accompanied by intracranial abscess and hemorrhage. After mitral valve replacement, antifungal therapy, right hepatic artery embolization, intracranial hematoma removal and other treatments, the patient finally recovered and was discharged. After 2 years of follow-up, there was no recurrence or secondary complications except for seizures.Keywords: Candida endocarditis, Brain abscess, Hepatic pseudoaneurysm, Case report

Case report

A 61-year-old male patient with low back pain and recurrent fever for more than 5 months was admitted to the hospital. The admission time was from October 8th, 2021. A month ago, MR of the lumbar spine revealed a high density of exudate in the intervertebral discs and endplates (Fig. 1), the orthopedic surgeon did not biopsy the tissue in this area. The nature of the exudate was unknown. Physical examination: The heart rhythm was regular, and a 3/6 grade systolic blowing murmur was heard in the mitral valve area. Ultrasound examination revealed a mitral valve vegetation measuring 3 × 2 cm, with moderate to severe mitral regurgitation (Fig. 2). The clinical diagnosis was mitral valve endocarditis with vegetation formation and moderate to severe regurgitation. The patient underwent mitral valve replacement with a mechanical valve. Postoperative culture of the vegetation was positive for Candida parapsilosis, confirming the diagnosis of Candida parapsilosis mitral valve endocarditis. Histopathological slide of the candida infection (Fig. 2). Laboratory investigations, including routine blood tests, unveiled a leukocyte count of 16.31 × 109/L, with an absolute neutrophil count of 13.59 × 109/L, Hemoglobin levels were measured at 106 g/L, C reactive protein at 64.30 mg/L and the erythrocyte sedimentation rate at 69 mm/h. After the isolated mitral valve tissue was cultured with Candida albicans, caspofungin and fluconazole were continuously used for antifungal treatment from October 18, 2021 to November 10, 2021.

During the treatment, the patient experienced dizziness, prompting a cranial MR, which revealed intracranial lesions with cysts containing pus, consistent with an abscess as indicated by imaging. The left temporoparietal lobe abscess and recent small-area cerebral infarction were accompanied by edema (Fig. 3). At that time, conservative treatment was adopted and further intervention was made. Half a year later, the patient was admitted to the hospital due to the syncope, the CT scan of the head revealed that the right-sided brain hemorrhage presented as sac-like and layered bleeding(Fig. 4), this might be related to the brain abscess on the right temporal lobe at that time. We performed right dural incision, subdural hematoa evacuation, craniotomy for decompression and dural repair on the patient, about one month of recovery, the patient was discharged from the hospital.

Discussion

This patient was admitted with infective endocarditis, and based on the patient’s medical history, MRI findings, and pathological data, the clinical diagnosis was Candida parapsilosis infection. During subsequent treatment, the patient developed multi-system lesions. However, after receiving appropriate treatment, expect for the seizure of epilepsy the patient recovered well, and follow-up revealed no other significant sequelae. It is exceedingly rare for Candida parapsilosis infective endocarditis to be accompanied by systemic fungal infections, resulting in tissue abscess formation and involvement of arterial walls leading to hemorrhage, particularly in patients with normal immune function. What we present here is the overall treatment process of complications associated with infectious endocarditis caused by rare fungi, along with images of the lesions in various organs.

The time when the inflammation of the right hepatic artery was discovered was October 31, 2021 (Fig. 5), the time when the right hepatic artery aneurysm was formed was December 10, 2021 (Fig. 5). We believe that the cause of this pseudoaneurysm is the secondary embolization of the emboli of endocarditis and the direct infection of the arterial wall by circulating pathogens. Risk factors for arterial infection include arterial injury and endothelial dysfunction. Some relevant literature has also pointed out, HAAs in IE (HAAs-IE) are the result of a microbial infiltration that including degradation [1]. Pathogens may settle in any pre-existing lesions and expand the erea of injury. Common sites of arterial infection include the abdominal and thoracic aorta and their branches, the femoral artery, and intracranial arteries, with infections of the hepatic artery and subclavian artery being relatively rare [2]. As a consequence, little is known about their prevalence, clinical and radiological characteristics, natural history, treatment, and outcome [1]. Therefore, in this article, I have presented the evolution process of hepatic pseudoaneurysms, which is of certain value.

The most severe outcome of a right hepatic pseudoaneurysm is rupture, leading to massive hemorrhage. Monique Boukobza and other authors point out in the literature related to hepatic aneurysms that, most cases of ruptured abdominal false aneurysms occur within the liver parenchyma and in the peritonea cavity, presenting as isolated hemorrhage, and rarely are accompanied by biliary tract hemorrhage. At the same time, the risk of a pseudoaneurysm is much higher than that of a true aneurysm [1].

The most common presenting symptoms were fever constant, and abdominal pain, that is associated with the rupture and ischemia of the hepatic pseudoaneurysm [1]. At present, endovascular intervention is an effective alternative to open surgical repair [3]. According to the Society for Vascular Surgery’s clinical practice guidelines on abdominal aortic aneurysms, once a hepatic artery pseudoaneurysm is diagnosed, prompt repair is recommended, regardless of size, and all symptomatic HAA should be repaired [4]. Consequently, we performed timely embolization of the right hepatic artery aneurysm in this patient. Cases of hepatic artery aneurysm caused by Candida species are extremely rare, and the successful treatment of this patient, who underwent embolization of the intrahepatic pseudoaneurysm and subsequently recovered and was discharged, appears to be unique and we search of the existing databases revealed no similar case reports.

The patient underwent a lumbar MR examination one month before admission and showed a high-density exudate in the intervertebral disc. We believe that the exudate is most likely caused by Candida parapsilosis bloodstream infection. The bacterial culture results of the valve vegetations were obtained on the 4th day after valve surgery. Fluconazole and caspofungin were used, The patient’s white blood cell count has returned to normal and the low back pain has subsided, but the proportion of neutrophils is still relatively high. Finally, the patient improved, and the whole course of antibiotic treatment was up to 4 weeks. Based on previous reviews, the treatment of candid endocarditis is mainly divided into acute infection control and long-term maintenance treatment in the consolidation period. In the acute phase, echinocandins alone or in combination with flucytosine were the first choice for treatment for more than 6 weeks. After the acute phase treatment was stable and the blood culture was negative, if it was a fluconazole-sensitive strain, fluconazole could be given for a long-term treatment for more than 6 months [5]. In critically ill patients, polyenes and echinocandins exhibit higher fungicidal efficacy compared to the fungistatic effects of azoles. However, fluconazole has broad distribution, achieves high cerebrospinal fluid concentrations, and has excellent oral bioavailability. In contrast, echinocandins cannot be administered orally and have low cerebrospinal fluid concentrations [6, 7]. Therefore, fluconazole is suitable for antifungal treatment post-discharge.

The patient also experienced neurological complications, including cerebral hemorrhage. The patient was found to have brain abscess during the cardiac surgery (Enhanced cranial MR conducted on October 14, 2021), on obvious abnormalities were found in either case. After discharge, the CT scan of the head failed to effectively detect the inflammatory lesions of the mild type, we believe that there is no need to continue using anti-fungal drugs for the treatment of the brain abscess.as a result, the patient only discovered the brain abscess and cerebral hemorrhage when experiencing syncope later. The CT scan of the head showed right cerebral hemorrhage, presenting as cystic and layered hemorrhage (Fig. 4,5). This might be related to the right temporal lobe brain abscess at that time. Professor Boukobza reviewed 351 patients with IE and found ring-shaped enhancement lesions in 3 cases of brain abscess. In this case, a right brain abscess ruptured and bled, showing the same symptoms [8]. There are also many reports stating that fungal brain abscesses present as bag-like lesions [9, 10]. Therefore, when dealing with patients with cystic hemorrhage, the patient’s medical history should be comprehensively considered. The possibility of fungal abscesses should also be taken into account. During the operation, relevant secretions need to be extracted for bacterial culture.

For patients with infective endocarditis complicated with brain abscess, it is highly necessary to conduct a head MR examination to determine whether there is any new progression of the intracranial abscess. Given the high risk of rupture associated with intracranial pseudoaneurysms, it is important to perform regular intracranial vascular CTA scans. If an intracranial aneurysm is suspected, timely intracranial angiography should be conducted, followed by early embolization of the aneurysm to reduce the risk of severe cerebral hemorrhage.

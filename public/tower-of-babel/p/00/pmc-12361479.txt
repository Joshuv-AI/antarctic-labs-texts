Optimize multi-objective transformation rules of water-sediment regulation for cascade reservoirs in the Lower Yellow River of China
Authors: Kunhui Hong, Aixing Ma, Wei Zhang, Mingxiong Cao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe Yellow River is characterized by water scarcity and high sediment load, leading to significant sedimentation issues in reservoirs. This severely restricts normal operation and contributes to frequent flooding, making it one of the most challenging problems in the Yellow River management. This study focuses on the operation of the Sanmenxia and Xiaolangdi cascade reservoirs, developing a multi-objective model (hydropower generation and sediment transport) and two single-objective models (hydropower generation only and sediment transport only). An innovative method (ESS-NSGA-II) is proposed, combining Efficient Search Space (ESS) with the Non-dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm II (NSGA-II) to optimize cascade reservoirs operation. We analyzed the impact and transformation rules of multiple objectives under various optimization schemes. The results indicate a non-linear competitive relationship between the hydropower generation and sediment transport. An extreme case indicates an increase of 0.49 billion kWh in hydropower generation (4.08% increase) leads to greatly reduce the amount of sediment transport (96.25% decrease), while still meeting both water supply and flood control. The findings also indicate that Xiaolangdi reservoir plays a dominant role in the operation of the two reservoirs. The impact of different schemes is mainly reflected in the variation in Xiaolangdi’s discharge, with the sediment transport maximization scheme offering greater flood control potential. Furthermore, the efficiency of sediment transport is closely related to hydropower generation: when hydropower is below 12.28 billion kWh, the conversion efficiency from hydropower generation to sediment transport increases significantly. This research reveals the transformation rules between multi-objectives in cascade reservoir operation, providing a scientific basis for water and sediment regulation in the Yellow River Basin and offering valuable insights into prolonging reservoir lifespans.Keywords: Multi-objective optimization, Water-sediment regulation, Transformation rules, Efficient search space-non-dominated sorting genetic algorithm II (ESS- NSGA-II), The lower yellow riverSubject terms: Ecology, Hydrology, Civil engineering

Introduction

Reservoir construction is a fundamental approach to river development and utilization, providing various benefits including flood control, irrigation, hydropower generation, and navigation. Over the past century, over 23,000 dams higher than 15 m have been constructed in China, representing 40.6% of the global total1. However, this process has resulted in several ecological, environmental, and socio-economic problems2–4, particularly the growing issue of sedimentation in reservoirs5,6. Sedimentation not only weakens the water storage capacity of reservoirs but also reduces their flood control, hydropower generation, and water supply functions7–9. It is estimated that sedimentation leads to an annual loss of 0.5–1% of global reservoir capacity, equivalent to around 45 km³, which would require the construction of 300 large reservoirs annually at a cost of $13 billion to replace the lost capacity. In the Yellow River Basin, sedimentation is particularly severe due to high sediment loads and the asynchronous behavior of water and sediment10–12. The Sanmenxia (SMX) reservoir, the first major reservoir built on the Yellow River, has experienced significant sedimentation since its early operation, with a cumulative sedimentation of 8.7 billion m³ by 2015, including 4.57 billion m³ within the first four years. The Xiaolangdi (XLD) reservoir, located 130 km downstream of SMX reservoir, has accumulated 3.05 billion m³ of sediment. Therefore, implementing flood period sediment flushing at XLD and SMX cascade reservoirs is essential, as it effectively extends reservoir service life and enhances their capacity to support flood control, irrigation, and ecological functions in the lower Yellow River. Similar issues are observed worldwide, such as the Aswan High Dam on the Nile, which has lost nearly 12% of its effective storage capacity due to sedimentation13. In Kansas, USA, 24 river reservoirs are also experiencing sedimentation problems, with some reservoirs losing up to 45% of their capacity14. Sedimentation has thus become a widespread issue in global reservoir management, and finding solutions through scientific reservoir operation to mitigate sedimentation, while balancing objectives such as flood control and hydropower generation, is an urgent research challenge15,16.

To address sedimentation issues in the SMX and XLD cascade reservoirs of the Yellow River’s middle and lower reaches, the Yellow River Conservancy Commission has repeatedly implemented water and sediment regulation (WSR) through the joint operation of cascade reservoirs, specifically to flush the SMX and XLD cascade reservoirs17. These measures have slowed the sedimentation rate to some extent18. However, they have also led to a range of new challenges. One of the most significant of these challenges is the substantial water discharge during the WSR period, which has notably reduced the hydropower generation efficiency of the reservoirs. Hydropower, as a key form of clean energy, provides reliable and long-term electricity due to its renewable and non-polluting characteristics. This energy source plays an essential role in reducing fossil fuel consumption, lowering CO₂ emissions, and contributing to climate change mitigation and the achievement of carbon neutrality goals19,20. However, conflicts between sediment transport and hydropower generation are inevitable. Without proper coordination, this could result in a further decline in storage capacity, reduced flood control, increased economic losses, and even the potential for flood disasters. In this context, exploring the interaction between these two processes and optimizing WSR strategies to improve the comprehensive benefits of water infrastructure has become a critical issue that must be addressed.

The cascade reservoir operation typically involves multiple objectives and constraints, requiring the consideration of both the synergies and trade-offs between objectives. Moreover, the constraints change over time with the operation periods, significantly increasing the complexity of optimization21,22. Two main approaches are commonly used to address multi-objective problems in reservoir operation: (1) reducing the dimensionality of the objective variables, thus converting the multi-objective problem into a single-objective one23; (2) directly applying multi-objective evolutionary algorithms (MOEAs)24. MOEAs have become a prominent research focus in water resource management, with methods such as particle swarm optimization (PSO)25,26 and non-dominated sorting genetic algorithm (NSGA-II) being widely applied in multi-objective reservoir optimization27,28. However, current multi-objective optimization algorithms perform well in low-dimensional and general optimization problems, but they exhibit notable limitations in high-dimensional optimization, dynamic optimization, and the handling of complex constraints. To overcome these limitations, this study modifies the search space of the NSGA-II algorithm and optimizes the initial population’s configuration, thus improving the computational efficiency of multi-objective models. While the efficiency of multi-objective optimization has been enhanced by various techniques, the conversion relationships between objectives—especially the relationships between competing objectives—remain insufficiently explored. A thorough analysis of these relationships is essential for developing effective reservoir operation strategies.

This study focuses on the SMX and XLD cascade reservoirs located in the middle and lower reaches of the Yellow River. It addresses four major objectives of the downstream reservoirs: flood control, hydropower generation, sediment transport, and water supply. Three optimization models were developed to investigate the impact of various WSR strategies on these objectives (Fig. 1). By comparing the results of single-objective and multi-objective operations, the trade-offs dynamics between power generation and sediment release at the SMX and XLD cascade reservoir in the lower Yellow River were investigated. To accurately and efficiently determine the optimal operation of the reservoirs, an NSGA-II optimization algorithm, combined with efficient search space techniques (ESS), is proposed. This research aims to identify the relationships between the multiple objectives and propose practical operational strategies for the reservoirs, providing valuable references for the efficient management and utilization of reservoir resources.

Research area and data

The Yellow River, originating from the Bayan Har Mountains in Qinghai Province, flows through nine provinces with a total length of 5,464 km and a basin area of 795,000 km229. Characterized by relatively scarce water resources, rich in sediment-laden, making it one of the world’s most sediment-laden rivers30,31. Accounting for only 2% of the nation’s total river runoff, the Yellow River Basin’s per capita water resources stand at roughly 25% of the national average. According to 2021 statistics, per capita water use in the basin was 16% lower than the national average, and surface water exploitation reached a rate of 80%.

The upper reaches provide more than 60% of the annual runoff32,33, while the middle reaches, from Tuoketuo County to Huayuankou, contribute the majority of the sediment due to the highly erodible Loess Plateau34,35. The lower reaches extend 786 km from Huayuankou to the Bohai Sea. SMX and XLD cascade reservoirs are located in the transitional region between the middle and lower reaches (Fig. 2), where high-sediment flows slow down upon entering the reservoirs, leading to substantial sediment deposition36,37. Despite significant sedimentation challenges, these reservoirs are critical for flood control, hydropower generation, and water supply, regulating 89% of the Yellow River’s flow and 98% of its sediment load38. Search methods

This study examines the multi-objective operation model for the cascade reservoirs in the middle and lower reaches of the Yellow River, which involves complex high-dimensional variables such as flood control, hydropower generation, sediment transport, and water supply, as well as numerous constraints that vary with time. This not only increases the computational difficulty but also poses the risk of dimensionality issues. Significant progress has been made in the optimization algorithms for cascade reservoir operation50–52 with Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO)53–55 and Non-dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm II (NSGA-II)56–58 being widely applied to both single-objective and multi-objective optimization. PSO, by combining individual and collective experiences, is capable of strong global search and can quickly find optimal solutions for single-objective optimization. NSGA-II, on the other hand, is particularly effective for high-dimensional objectives and significantly improves computational efficiency. In this study, both PSO and NSGA-II are used to solve the single-objective and multi-objective models, respectively.

The original NSGA-II algorithm for cascade reservoir optimization generates the initial population using decision variables such as water level or reservoir capacity. However, these randomly generated decision variables often do not meet the practical operation constraints, leading to inaccurate results and a significant reduction in computational efficiency. To improve the computational efficiency of the multi-objective optimization model of cascade reservoirs, this study proposes an improved multi-objective optimization algorithm—ESS-NSGA-II. This algorithm combines the Efficient Search Space (ESS) technique with NSGA-II, with the aim of improving both computational accuracy and efficiency by optimizing the initial population generation process. First, constraints are modified and reduced, followed by the identification and optimization of the initial search space for the multi-objective optimization problem based on the water balance principle in cascade reservoir operation, thereby eliminating unreasonable intervals and effectively narrowing the search space. The core idea of this approach is to build an efficient search space that reduces computational load, thereby accelerating the convergence process of the model. The specific optimization steps are as follows(Figure 5): (1) Decision variables are categorized, with outflow, water level, and similar variables that can directly be converted into constraints classified as transformable variables. Variables such as hydropower generation and sediment transport, which are implicitly related and cannot be directly converted into constraints, are classified as non-transformable variables. (2) For transformable variables, their constraint ranges are dynamically adjusted based on the target needs of cascade reservoirs during different periods. For non-transformable variables, constraint validation is performed, and for those that do not meet the constraints, a penalty mechanism is applied to adjust the constraints and improve the reasonableness of the initial population’s search space. (3) In each iteration, offspring decision variables undergo constraint verification. Offspring that do not meet the constraints are penalized, and elite offspring that satisfy the constraints are retained, thus accelerating the convergence process and improving optimization efficiency.

Optimization of the efficient search space in different periods.

Step 1: Define the time stage t=[0,T], with the initial water level of the reservoir Z0(0) and the final water level Z1(T).

Step 2: Starting from the initial water level Z0(0), derive the water level range for each intermediate stage in a forward manner. This derivation is controlled by the inflow, the maximum and minimum outflows, and the reservoir water level constraints. The water level range at the end of the first stage is denoted as Z0(1)=[Z0min(1),Z0max(1)], and this process is repeated until T. The search interval for each stage is expressed as Z0(t)=[Z0min(t), Z0max(t)]。.

Step 3: With the final water level Z1(T) as the initial water level range, the backward derivation of the water level is carried out. At each intermediate stage, the inflow, maximum and minimum outflows, and reservoir water level constraints are applied to calculate the water level range at the end of stage T-1, denoted as Z1(T-1)=[Z1min(T-1),Z1max(T-1)]. This process continues until t = 0, where the search interval for each stage is Z1(t)=[Z1min(t), Z1max(t)]。.

Step 4: The intersection of the water level ranges Z0(t) and Z1(t) is taken as the water level range at the end of stage t, denoted as [Zmin(t), Zmax(t)], yielding the efficient search space.

Results and discussion

Model design

This study constructed two single-objective optimization models and one multi-objective optimization model to analyze the transformation of multiple objectives under the WSR conditions in the middle and lower reaches of the Yellow River. The single-objective models aim to maximize hydropower generation and sediment transport, while the multi-objective model optimizes both objectives simultaneously. By comparing the results of the three models, this research validates the effectiveness of the Efficient Non-Dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm II (ESS-NSGA-II) and assesses the impact of different operation strategies on multi-objective optimization in the SMX and XLD cascade reservoirs, as well as the relationship between the hydropower generation and sediment transport. The flood control and water supply objectives are treated as constraints, with three schemes designed based on real-world conditions.

Scheme 1: hydropower generation maximization model

Scheme 1 assumes hydropower generation as the only objective for optimizing the cascade reservoir operation at the SMX and XLD cascade reservoirs, disregarding the effects of sediment transport. The other two objectives, flood control and water supply, are transformed into constraints. To evaluate the sediment transport results for different schemes, sedimentation during the hydropower generation process is calculated based on the operation process after the hydropower generation target is maximized, using formulas (2) and (3). The other two objectives are converted into constraints related to water levels and flow (as shown in Eqs. (8) and (10)). The Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) algorithm is employed to solve the optimization model.

Scheme 2: sediment transport maximization model

Scheme 2 assumes sediment transport as the only objective in the optimization of the cascade reservoir operation for the SMX and XLD cascade reservoirs, while disregarding the effects on hydropower generation. The other two objectives, flood control and water supply, are transformed into constraints. Similarly, to compare hydropower generation results across different schemes, the hydropower generation corresponding to maximum sediment transport is calculated based on the operation process after maximizing the sediment transport target, using formula (1). The remaining two objectives are converted into constraints on water levels and flow (as shown in Eqs. (8) and (10)). The Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) algorithm is employed to solve the optimization model.

Scheme 3: joint operation model for maximizing hydropower generation and sediment transport

Scheme 3 assumes that hydropower generation and sediment transport are the main objectives in the cascade reservoir operation. The other two objectives, flood control and water supply, are converted into constraints on water levels and flow. This necessitates considering both objectives to derive a set of trade-off solutions. The model is solved using ESS-NSGA-II, and the solution with the highest coupling and synergy is selected as the optimal solution.

Figures 7 and 8 reveal significant differences in water levels between the maximum hydropower generation and maximum sediment transport schemes from the end of June to August in the XLD reservoir. Specifically, the water levels for the maximum hydropower generation scheme are 225.8 m, 240.4 m, and 244.7 m, while for the maximum sediment transport scheme, they are 225.5 m, 218.9 m, and 238.0 m. In particular, the lowest water level during the flood control period in the maximum sediment transport scheme is 6.9 m lower than that in the maximum hydropower generation scheme. This suggests that, when maximizing sediment transport is prioritized, the water level is lowered during the flood control period to improve sediment transport efficiency. From a flood control perspective, especially in wetter years, the maximum sediment transport scheme offers significant benefits for optimizing the operation of the SMX and XLD cascade reservoirs. This approach not only aids in effectively removing accumulated sediment, increasing the reservoir’s usable storage, but also enhances flood control capacity, thereby reducing the risk of flooding in the lower Yellow River region.

The water supply at the HYK section for both the maximum hydropower generation and maximum sediment transport schemes is 46.1 billion cubic meters, which meets the downstream water demand. This result is likely influenced by the relatively large water inflow in the middle and lower Yellow River during wet years. Despite differences in the monthly water distribution, both schemes are still able to satisfy downstream water requirements. Therefore, during wet years, neither the maximum hydropower generation nor the maximum sediment transport scheme increases the water shortage rate in the lower Yellow River, nor does it create any conflict between hydropower generation, sediment transport, and water supply goals. As shown in Figs. 6, 7, 8 and 9, the hydropower generation gap between the two schemes mainly occurs between July and September during the flood control period. In this period, the hydropower generation of the SMX and XLD cascade reservoirs under the maximum sediment transport scheme decreases by 8.42% and 13.88%, respectively, compared to the maximum hydropower generation scheme. This indicates that power generation losses under the maximum sediment transport scheme occur primarily during the flood control period, with XLD reservoir’s loss being significantly higher than that of SMX reservoir. The changes in reservoir water levels further support this observation. In the maximum hydropower generation scheme, the average water level of the SMX reservoir from July to September is 307.4 m, while under the maximum sediment transport scheme, it drops to 304.4 m. Similarly, in the maximum hydropower generation scheme, the average water level of XLD reservoir in July and August is 241.6 m, whereas it decreases to 228.5 m under the maximum sediment transport scheme. These differences suggest that, in the maximum hydropower generation scheme, the average water level during the flood control period is significantly higher, which may pose potential risks to flood control safety in the lower Yellow River region.

Hydropower output of the Sanmenxia reservoir of two single-objective models.

The sediment transport and hydropower generation values for the single-objective schemes were calculated using Eqs. (1), (2), and (3), and the results are shown in Table 8. As hydropower output increased from 11.99 billion kWh to 12.48 billion kWh (4.08% increase), the sediment transport decreased from 420 million tons to 20 million tons (96.25% decrease). This indicates that for every 100 million kWh reduction in hydropower generation, the sediment transport increases by 83 million tons. This change is due to the fact that the maximum sediment transport scheme enhances sediment transport efficiency by reducing reservoir water levels during the flood control period, which leads to a lower average hydropower generation head during this period. Consequently, a slight reduction in the expected hydropower generation during the flood control period at the SMX and XLD cascade reservoirs can substantially improve sediment transport benefits.

The balance between these two objectives, maximizing sediment transport while avoiding significant increases in water waste and hydropower generation loss, will be explored in Sect. 4.3. As outlined, when the SMX and XLD cascade reservoirs are optimized for maximum sediment transport, sediment transport efficiency is improved by lowering the water levels during the flood control period and releasing large flows to scour the XLD reservoir’s sediment. However, this practice results in substantial water waste, leading to a reduction in hydropower generation efficiency. Although this method causes water resource wastage, it effectively mitigates sediment accumulation and extends the reservoirs’ operational lifespan. In the maximum hydropower generation model, controlling flood control period water levels appropriately reduces water waste, which in turn boosts the reservoirs’ economic hydropower generation efficiency. The optimal balance between these two goals, which aims to increase sediment transport without significantly increasing water waste and decreasing hydropower generation, will be examined in Sect. 4.3.

Results and discussion of the multi-objective optimization

The multi-objective optimization model was solved using the NSGA-II algorithm (Table 9), and the Pareto front curves for hydropower generation and sediment transport are shown in Fig. 10. A nonlinear trade-off exists between hydropower generation and sediment transport, where an increase in hydropower generation generally leads to a decrease in sediment transport. As per the characteristics of the Pareto optimal solutions, any point on the Pareto front curve represents the optimal solution for the multi-objective problem. To validate the rationality of the multi-objective optimization solution, the Pareto front curve was compared with the solutions from the two single-objective optimization models. The relationship between the single-objective and multi-objective optimization models was analyzed. Specifically, the distance of the multi-objective solution relative to the extreme and optimal solutions of the single-objective models determines the rationality of the multi-objective optimization results. The results show that the optimal solutions of the two single-objective optimization models are located at the endpoints of the Pareto front curve, confirming the rationality of the three models. Additionally, this further demonstrates that the improved ESS-NSGA-II algorithm has high accuracy and applicability in multi-objective optimization calculations.

Hydropower generation is a major economic support for reservoirs, while sediment transport is an essential method for extending their operational lifespan. Both objectives are critical for the sustainable management of reservoirs. As a result, the weights for hydropower generation and sediment transport were set to 0.5 and 0.5, respectively. To assess the differences between multi-objective joint operation and single-objective operation, the coupling coordination degree of the multi-objective optimization Pareto set was computed using the coupled synergy model. The solution with the highest coupling coordination degree was selected as the optimal solution for the multi-objective collaborative model (shown as the yellow point in Fig. 10). Although the joint operation scheme results in lower sediment transport and hydropower generation than the maximum values from the single-objective models, it achieves a favorable balance between sediment transport and hydropower generation to some extent.

Table 9 presents the performance metrics of different operations scheme. In terms of hydropower generation, the maximum hydropower generation scheme exhibits strong performance, with a high reliability (Rel = 0.83), good resilience (Res = 0.75), and low vulnerability (Vul = 0.17), indicating stable and robust electricity output. However, its performance in sediment transport is relatively poor, as evidenced by a low reliability (Rel = 0.28) and high vulnerability (Vul = 0.63). This is primarily attributed to the consistently high reservoir water level maintained to maximize hydropower generation, which inhibits effective sediment discharge. In contrast, maximum sediment transport scheme demonstrates the best performance in sediment-related objectives, with the highest reliability (Rel = 0.86) and lowest vulnerability (Vul = 0.14), reflecting efficient sediment transport and low operational risk. Nevertheless, its performance in power generation is suboptimal, with a low reliability (Rel = 0.33) and poor resilience (Res = 0.25), indicating difficulty in recovering from generation shortfalls and reduced operational stability.

The joint operation scheme achieves a balanced trade-off between hydropower generation and sediment transport, with moderate reliability (Rel = 0.63 and 0.65) and resilience (Res = 0.56 and 0.59), while maintaining low vulnerability in both dimensions (Vul = 0.24 and 0.22). Although it does not outperform the extreme schemes in individual objectives, it demonstrates superior overall system robustness and operational adaptability, making it a preferable option in practical applications. It should be noted that water supply and flood control objectives were implemented as constraints in the optimization model, and as such, their performance metrics were not evaluated. In summary, the joint operation scheme achieves favorable reliability and resilience in both hydropower and sediment management, offering a robust and well-balanced solution with enhanced engineering applicability and broader potential for implementation compared to the single-objective schemes.

As shown in Table 8, the SMX reservoir experiences spillover during the flood control period (July to October), while the spillover from the XLD reservoir is concentrated mainly in July and September. The annual spillover volumes for the two reservoirs are 14.14 billion m³ and 2.68 billion m³, respectively. The spillover at SMX reservoir during the flood control period is significantly higher than that at XLD reservoir, which is consistent with the trends observed in the first two models. Compared to the maximum hydropower generation and maximum sediment transport models, the spillover at SMX reservoir increased by 0.06% and 0.19%, respectively, while XLD reservoir’s spillover increased by 3.66% and decreased by 21.55%. This indicates that, compared to the maximum sediment transport model, the joint operation solution significantly reduces spillover, minimizing water resource waste. While the spillover increased slightly compared to the maximum hydropower generation model, this adjustment was made to lower the reservoir water levels and enhance sediment transport efficiency, thus achieving a better balance between hydropower generation and sediment transport.

Under the joint operation scheme, the hydropower generation of the SMX and XLD cascade reservoirs is 12.20 billion kWh, and the sediment transport is 14 million tons. Compared to the maximum hydropower generation model, the hydropower generation decreased by 0.25 billion kWh, while the sediment transport increased by 120 million tons, indicating that for every 100 million kWh reduction in hydropower generation, the sediment transport increased by 4 million tons. Compared to the maximum sediment transport model, the hydropower generation increased by 210 million kWh, while the sediment transport decreased by 28 million tons, equivalent to a reduction of 13 million tons of sediment transport for every 100 million kWh increase in hydropower generation. This result suggests that the joint operation scheme effectively utilizes the XLD reservoir’s regulation capacity by appropriately lowering its water level during the flood control period and leveraging the downstream discharge from SMX reservoir to flush the reservoir capacity, thus achieving a better balance between hydropower generation and sediment transport and avoiding water resource wastage. It also indirectly highlights the dominant role of the XLD reservoir in the joint operation of the SMX and XLD cascade reservoirs, with changes in operation strategy having a significant impact on the spillover volume at XLD reservoir. The possible reason is that the XLD reservoir’s capacity is much larger than that of SMX reservoir, while SMX reservoir, due to severe sedimentation, has an effective storage capacity of less than 100 million m³ below its flood control level, resulting in insufficient regulation capacity. Therefore, adjustments in the operation strategy largely depend on the regulatory role of the XLD reservoir.

The analysis above demonstrates that when the hydropower generation is reduced to increase sediment transport, the closer the sediment transport is to the maximum value, the lower the loss of hydropower generation per unit. To further investigate the relationship between hydropower generation and sediment transport, this study fits the Pareto front of the joint objective and derives the mathematical relationship between hydropower generation and sediment transport (Eq. 18). Based on this equation, the second derivative is computed to obtain the acceleration of sediment transport as hydropower generation changes. The horizontal axis represents hydropower generation, and the vertical axis shows the acceleration of sediment transport with respect to changes in hydropower generation, as illustrated in Fig. 11. When hydropower generation is less than 12.28 billion kWh, the rate of decrease in sediment transport accelerates rapidly with increasing hydropower generation. However, when hydropower generation exceeds 12.28 billion kWh, the rate of decrease in sediment transport slows down as hydropower generation increases. This result suggests that to improve the sediment transport efficiency of the SMX and XLD cascade reservoirs, the reservoir management should maintain hydropower generation below 12.28 billion kWh. Within this range of hydropower generation, the conversion efficiency between sediment transport and hydropower generation is highest, allowing for more effective sediment transport. Therefore, adjusting hydropower generation to an appropriate range can not only improve sediment transport efficiency but also reduce water resource wastage. Results from the three models indicate that both hydropower generation and sediment transport are primarily influenced by the end of July water level at the XLD reservoir. If reservoir managers aim to achieve a balance between hydropower generation and sediment transport, it is recommended that the water level be maintained around 228.8 m, thereby optimizing both economic benefits and sediment transport efficiency while mitigating reservoir siltation. When prioritizing hydropower generation, the level should be raised to approximately 240.4 m; conversely, for maximum sediment transport, it should be lowered to around 218.9 m.

Overall, the manager must find a reasonable balance between sediment transport, hydropower generation, and flood control to ensure the long-term benefits and sustainability of the reservoirs.

Conclusion

The Yellow River is renowned for its high sediment, which has led to increasingly severe sedimentation issues in its reservoirs. This, in turn, restricts the effective functioning of the reservoirs, impacting key functions such as flood control, hydropower generation, and water supply. This study focuses on the SMX and XLD cascade reservoirs in the middle and lower reaches of the Yellow River, conducting a multi-objective optimization analysis aimed at reducing sedimentation and enhancing the reservoirs’ overall efficiency. The cascade reservoir operation faces multiple challenges, including multi-objective optimization, high-dimensional variables, and complex constraints, which impose higher demands on operation models and significantly increase the difficulty of problem-solving. To address these challenges, the study developed three models: hydropower generation maximization, sediment transport maximization, and combined optimization operation. The study thoroughly examines the transformation relationships between multiple objectives in the water and sediment regulation processes of the SMX and XLD cascade reservoirs. The key conclusions of the study are as follows: (1) The ESS-NSGA-II algorithm is applicable to the multi-objective optimization of cascade reservoirs, and its results demonstrate high rationality and accuracy. The Pareto front curve for hydropower generation and sediment transport obtained from the optimization calculation reveals a significant nonlinear trade-off between hydropower generation and sediment transport. Specifically, an increase in reservoir hydropower generation inevitably leads to a reduction in sediment transport. Analysis using two single-objective models indicates that, under the hydrological conditions of 2018, a 490 million kWh increase in hydroelectric hydropower generation (4.08% increase) significantly reduces the reservoir’s sediment transport by 96.25%. In other words, during the WSR period in the Lower Yellow River, reducing hydropower generation can substantially improve the sediment transport efficiency of the reservoirs and facilitate sediment transport. (2) Compared to the hydropower generation maximization and sediment transport maximization schemes, the joint operation scheme results in a 0.06% and 0.19% increase in the water waste at the SMX reservoir, respectively. For the XLD reservoir, the water waste increases by 3.66% and decreases by 21.55%, respectively. This demonstrates that the joint operation scheme fully utilizes XLD reservoir’s strong regulation capacity by appropriately lowering the flood control period water level and using the discharge from SMX to flush the reservoir area. This approach effectively reduces water resource waste while balancing hydropower generation and sediment transport. Additionally, these results further indicate that changes in the operation strategy primarily affect the operational mode of the XLD reservoir, which plays a dominant role in the joint operation of the SMX and XLD cascade reservoirs. (3) The three different operation schemes for the SMX and XLD cascade reservoirs have a minimal impact on the water supply. However, they result in significant differences in hydropower generation, sediment transport, and flood control, with the most notable variation observed in sediment transport. Moreover, while all three schemes meet the flood control level requirements, the sediment transport maximization scheme demonstrates superior flood control performance compared to the others. Therefore, in years with abundant water resources in the Yellow River Basin, this scheme could be implemented to enhance flood control capacity while achieving efficient sediment transport, effectively mitigating sediment accumulation issues. (4) The result of joint operation scheme indicates a close relationship between sediment transport efficiency and hydropower generation. When the hydropower generation is below 12.28 billion kWh, the conversion efficiency from the hydropower generation target to the sediment transport target significantly increases. Therefore, to achieve the maximum sediment transport effect while minimizing the loss in hydropower generation, the manager should control the hydropower generation between 11.99 billion kWh and 12.28 billion kWh to optimize sediment transport benefits. The study primarily focuses on the relationship between hydropower generation and sediment transport in the SMX and XLD cascade reservoirs during wet years. The corresponding patterns in normal and dry years remain insufficiently explored and represent important directions for future research.

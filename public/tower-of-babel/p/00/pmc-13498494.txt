Management of patients with hypoparathyroidism: a Delphi consensus analysis
Authors: Luisella Cianferotti, Giovanna Mantovani, Roberto Ravasio, Alessia Dinoia, Andrea Torriani, Fabio Vescini, Iacopo Chiodini
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeChronic hypoparathyroidism is a rare endocrine disorder characterized by parathyroid hormone (PTH) deficiency and associated with substantial clinical and therapeutic challenges. Despite conventional therapy and the off-label use of teriparatide, important unmet needs persist regarding metabolic control, prevention of long-term complications, and health-related quality of life (HRQoL). Although recent international and national guidelines have updated recommendations on chronic hypoparathyroidism management, relevant uncertainties and heterogeneity remain in real-world practice, particularly concerning the identification of patients not adequately controlled with conventional therapy, the therapeutic positioning and timing of PTH replacement therapy, monitoring strategies, and the implementation of structured care pathways. This study aimed to assess the level of expert consensus on the current management of hypoparathyroidism in Italy and to identify key gaps and potential areas for therapeutic and organizational improvement.MethodsA consensus survey was conducted using the Delphi method in two rounds. The Steering Committee defined 41 statements on the management of hypoparathyroidism, which were submitted to 20 expert clinicians via an online survey. Consensus was considered to have been reached with ≥ 70% of responses 4–5 (Likert scale 1–5).ResultsThe completion rate was 100%. Consensus was reached for 39/41 statements (95.1%). Experts highlighted the limitations of conventional therapy and the off-label use of teriparatide, recognizing the impact of the disease on quality of life and the need for care pathways. The need for hormone replacement therapy was also emphasized, together with the importance of structured monitoring and multidisciplinary follow-up.ConclusionChronic hypoparathyroidism remains a clinically challenging condition with significant unmet needs. This Italian Delphi consensus complements recent international and national guidelines by providing expert-driven insights on real-world implementation, therapeutic positioning, monitoring, and care pathway development within the Italian healthcare context, supporting the need to introduce hormone replacement therapy and improve multidisciplinary management.

Introduction

Hypoparathyroidism is an endocrine disorder caused by parathyroid hormone (PTH) deficiency, the main regulator of calcium and phosphate homeostasis. PTH deficiency leads to hypocalcemia and hyperphosphatemia [1–3], with direct effects on bone metabolism and renal function and indirect effects on intestinal calcium and phosphate absorption [4–6].

Hypoparathyroidism is considered a rare disorder, affecting fewer than 5 per 10,000 people. The reported prevalence of chronic hypoparathyroidism is 6.4–37 cases per 100,000 individuals, while the incidence is estimated at 0.8–2.3 per 100,000 [7]. In Italy, an analysis of the regional healthcare database of Tuscany conducted in a population of approximately 3.75 million inhabitants estimated a prevalence of 27 cases per 100,000 inhabitants, indirectly identified through chronic use of active vitamin D metabolites [8].

Based on etiology, hypoparathyroidism is classified into post-surgical form, resulting from surgery on the thyroid or parathyroid glands, and non-surgical form, caused by autoimmune diseases, genetic mutations or other acquired causes [9]. Post-surgical hypoparathyroidism accounts for approximately 75% of cases, while non-surgical forms – including autoimmune and genetic causes – represent the remaining 25% [5, 10, 11]. Risk factors for post-surgical hypoparathyroidism include the type of thyroid disease (with a high risk in neoplasms, Graves’ disease or retrosternal goiters), the extent of surgery and surgical expertise [9, 11].

In 2025, the revised European Society of Endocrinology (ESE) Clinical Practice Guideline on the Treatment of Chronic Hypoparathyroidism in Adults was published [12]. In the same year, guidelines on the therapeutic management of post-surgical hypoparathyroidism in adults were published by the Italian National Institute of Health, drafted by the Association of Endocrinologists (AME) in collaboration with other Italian pathology societies, including the Italian Society of Endocrinology (SIE) [13]. According to these guidelines, treatment for chronic hypoparathyroidism include:

conventional therapy, which is based on the oral administration of calcium salts and active vitamin D analogues, with the aim of maintaining serum calcium levels slightly below or at the lower end of the reference range, while also avoiding an increase in urinary calcium excretion and phosphatemia with consequent long-term complications [2];PTH replacement therapy in patients not adequately controlled (NAC) with palopegteriparatide, rhPTH 1–84 or teriparatide (recombinant human PTH - rhPTH 1–34), the latter one approved and authorized only for the treatment of osteoporosis at a fixed regimen (i.e. 20 µg/day), but also used off-label in Italy for chronic hypoparathyroidism in addition to conventional therapy with calcium and active vitamin D [14, 15].

Despite advances in understanding the pathophysiology mechanisms and optimizing conventional therapy, a significant proportion of patients fail to achieve stable control of the disease. Fluctuations in serum calcium and phosphate levels, persistent hypercalciuria, renal impairment, skeletal alterations, and impaired health-related quality of life (HRQoL) frequently persist, even when biochemical parameters appear within target ranges [16–18].

The off-label use of teriparatide under the Italian Law 648/96 framework, also has some significant limitations besides the mandatory informed consent signed by the patient and full accountability by the treating physicians on treatment consequences: (i) unproven safety profile for population, dosage and treatment duration, including risk of osteosarcoma observed in preclinical studies on rats; (ii) a once-daily dose of 20 µg, although recommended for severe chronic cases, is unable to ensure biochemical stability or adequate symptom control; even increasing the dose to 20 µg twice daily allows discontinuation of calcium and calcitriol supplements only in about half of patients, often at the expense of oscillations in serum calcium and phosphate levels [19]; (iii) the very short half-life (approximately 1 h) causes variability in blood calcium and phosphate levels as well as other biochemical markers (e.g. calciuria), making daily administration insufficient to ensure stable calcium control throughout the day [17].

Despite the availability of conventional therapy, patients with chronic hypoparathyroidism continue to face persistent unmet clinical needs, particularly in terms of biochemical disease control, deterioration of kidney function in the long term, impact on quality of life, physical function and psychological well-being [20, 21].

Another therapeutic option previously available was rhPTH 1–84, used as replacement therapy in adults with chronic hypoparathyroidism; however, its availability has been limited in recent years, reducing its use in clinical practice [22].

Palopegteriparatide is the first treatment approved by the EMA and the European Commission as a replacement (rather than supplementation or add-on) therapy, for chronic hypoparathyroidism in adults. Palopegteriparatide is a prodrug of PTH (1-34), administered once daily, designed to provide active PTH within the physiological range for 24 hours/day. The efficacy and safety of palopegteriparatide were evaluated in the PaTHway study, a phase 3, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial. At week 26 of the PaTHway study, a statistically significantly greater proportion of participants treated with palopegteriparatide compared with placebo achieved normal serum calcium and independence from conventional therapy, without an increase in prescribed study drug over 4 weeks prior to Week 26. Treatment with palopegteriparatide also resulted in rapid and sustained improvements in health-related quality of life: (i) significant improvement in disease-specific physical and cognitive symptoms; (ii) significant improvement in disease impact; (iii) significant improvement in physical functioning. Palopegteriparatide was well tolerated, with no discontinuations due to adverse events [23]. In the open-label extension of the PaTHway study, at week 52, 81% of patients achieved the multicomponent efficacy endpoint and 95% became independent from conventional therapy, with no need for active vitamin D. Twenty-four–hour urinary calcium excretion decreased from 376 to 195 mg/day, while mean eGFR increased by 9.3 mL/min/1.73 m² (11.5 mL/min/1.73 m² in participants with baseline eGFR < 60). Most adverse events were mild or moderate, and no cases of nephrolithiasis were reported with palopegteriparatide through week 52 [24, 25].

However, while international and national guidelines provide evidence-based recommendations on diagnosis and treatment, several practical and organizational aspects remain heterogeneous in real-world clinical practice. Uncertainties persist regarding: (i) the identification and clinical characterization of patients not adequately controlled with conventional therapy; (ii) the optimal timing and positioning of hormone replacement therapy in routine care; (iii) the definition of structured monitoring strategies in different clinical settings and (iv) the implementation of coordinated Diagnostic Therapeutic Care Pathways (PDTA) across referral and local centers.

Objective

In Italy, despite updated European and national recommendations, the real-world management of chronic hypoparathyroidism remains heterogeneous, with persisting uncertainties regarding the identification of patients not adequately controlled with conventional therapy, the therapeutic positioning and timing of PTH replacement therapy, and the definition of structured monitoring and follow-up strategies. In addition, variability in local organization and the lack of dedicated Diagnostic Therapeutic Care Pathways (PDTA) may hinder continuity of care across referral and local centers.

Therefore, the objective of this Italian Delphi consensus was not to redefine existing clinical guidelines, but to explore areas of residual uncertainty and heterogeneity in the real-world management of chronic hypoparathyroidism. Through a structured consensus process involving experienced clinicians, the study aimed to identify shared expert positions on therapeutic positioning, monitoring strategies, and healthcare organization, thereby complementing current European and national recommendations and supporting their practical application within the Italian healthcare system.

Methods

This consensus survey was conducted using the Delphi method, which made it possible to measure the degree of agreement or disagreement on a series of statements related to specific aspects of hypoparathyroidism management. This Delphi method made it possible to gradually build consensus (agreement or disagreement) through a process of analysis that considered the different points of view of the experts involved, the available scientific evidence and the clinical expertise of the panelists. The process was divided into two phases: preparatory and implementation.

During the preparatory phase, a small group of experts (Steering Committee), drawing on clinical evidence and their expertise, defined and validated 41 statements relating to the current therapeutic scenario for hypoparathyroidism in Italy.

In the subsequent implementation phase a second group of experts (Expert Panel) was asked to rate their opinion (agreement or disagreement) on the proposed statements through an online survey conducted in two rounds (round 1 and round 2).

The administration of the survey in two rounds is considered best practice in Delphi studies to facilitate consensus and therefore allows panelists to revise their judgments in light of group feedback. The responses provided by the Expert Panel in the first round were collected, analyzed and reviewed by the Steering Committee for preliminary evaluation. In accordance with the Delphi method, the statements for which the Steering Committee considered that the lack of consensus was attributable to syntactic or communicative issues, were reformulated before the second and final round was administered.

The entire process was conducted ensuring full anonymity of responses and comments to minimize response bias.

Participants

The survey was designed and conducted by three different groups: (i) Steering Committee, (ii) Facilitator, and (iii) Expert Panel.

The Steering Committee was composed of four directors of Endocrinology Units with established expertise in the management and treatment of patients with hypoparathyroidism. The Steering Committee defined and validated the 41 statements based on available scientific evidence and clinical experience, contributed to the design of the survey and analyzed the results of both Delphi rounds.

The Facilitator, with methodological expertise in consensus research, was responsible for designing the survey, coordinating communications between the Steering Committee and the Expert Panel, administering the online questionnaires, collecting responses, and ensuring methodological consistency and anonymity throughout the process.

The size of the Expert Panel was proposed by the Facilitator and approved by the Steering Committee. Considering the rarity of chronic hypoparathyroidism and the need to ensure national representativeness, it was decided to involve 20 clinicians with established expertise in the management of hypoparathyroidism. Experts were eligible for inclusion if they met the following predefined criteria: (i) board certification in Endocrinology or Internal Medicine, with specific clinical involvement in metabolic bone and mineral disorders; (ii) clinical experience in the management of patients with chronic hypoparathyroidism and (iii) active practice in a referral or tertiary care center managing patients with hypoparathyroidism. To preserve the independence of clinical judgment, the Expert Panel was composed exclusively of clinicians working in academic institutions or public healthcare hospitals. No representatives from the pharmaceutical industry were included in the Expert Panel. To ensure geographical representativeness, experts were selected from different areas of Italy (Northern, Central, and Southern regions), including both university hospitals and large non-university referral centers. The names of the Expert Panel members were proposed by the Steering Committee (five for each member) and subsequently contacted by the facilitator. All members of the Expert Panel contributed equally to the evaluation process, as each vote carried the same weight in the assessment of the statements. Each panelist was asked to rate all 41 statements in both Delphi rounds.

Since there is no universally accepted definition [27], in line with the available literature [28–31], consensus on agreement for a given statement was considered to have been reached when the responses of the all 20 panelists showed a frequency of ≥ 70% in scores 4–5 and ≤ 30% in scores 1–2 on the Likert scale. Similarly, consensus on disagreement on the specific statement was defined as reached when the frequency of responses was ≥ 70% in scores 1–2 and ≤ 30% in scores 4–5. Responses with score 3 were not considered as contributing to either agreement or disagreement. However, they were included in the denominator when calculating the percentage of agreement or disagreement for each statement. Since all 20 panelists completed both rounds (attrition rate 0%), the denominator for the calculation of consensus corresponded to the full Expert Panel in both rounds. Statements that did not reach consensus after Round 2 were retained and reported as areas of uncertainty or heterogeneity in expert opinion. No new statements were generated after Round 2, and no items were eliminated from the analysis, in accordance with the predefined study protocol.

Statement

During the preparatory phase, the Steering Committee developed the 41 statements through a structured and iterative process. Statement generation was informed by: (i) a review of the most recent international and national clinical guidelines on chronic hypoparathyroidism, including the 2025 European Society of Endocrinology (ESE) guideline and the Italian national recommendations; (ii) key publications addressing diagnosis, long-term complications, conventional therapy limitations, PTH replacement strategies, and health-related quality of life and (iii) the clinical experience of the Steering Committee members in managing patients with chronic hypoparathyroidism across referral centers in Italy. The initial pool of potential items was generated through internal discussion among Steering Committee members. Statements were then refined to ensure clarity, clinical relevance, and alignment with real-world practice. No formal external pilot test was conducted. In accordance with the Delphi methodology, after completion of Round 1, the Steering Committee reviewed aggregated results and anonymized comments provided by panelists. In cases where lack of consensus was considered potentially attributable to wording ambiguity rather than conceptual disagreement, clarification was discussed; however, no statement required substantive modification between rounds.

The 41 statements defined by the Steering Committee were divided into seven subgroups, ensuring a clear and logically structured compilation process: context, diagnosis, complications, treatment, quality of life, monitoring, and Diagnostic Therapeutic Care Pathway (PDTA). The full list and exact wording of the 41 statements submitted for evaluation by the Expert Panel are reported below.

Data analysis

The presence or absence of consensus on agreement or disagreement was assessed for each of the 41 statements in both rounds, analyzing the percentage distribution of the Expert Panel’s responses based on the five scores provided by the Likert scale. To support the interpretation of the results, basic descriptive statistics measures were also calculated, such as median and interquartile range (IQR) to provide a robust measure of central tendency and dispersion suitable for ordinal Likert-scale data. To limit the risk of panelist attrition (members of the Expert Panel), the processing and preparation time between the first and second rounds was reduced to a minimum (approximately one month). In line with the Delphi method and regardless of the results of the first round, all statements were resubmitted to the Expert Panel for evaluation in the second and final round.

No formal subgroup analyses were pre-specified or performed (e.g., by geographic area or institutional setting), as the primary objective of the study was to assess overall expert consensus rather than to compare subgroups. Given the limited sample size and the exploratory nature of the Delphi methodology, statistical comparisons between subgroups were not considered appropriate.

Discussion

This Delphi survey was conducted to explore the current management of chronic hypoparathyroidism in Italy, identify areas of convergence and residual uncertainty among experienced clinicians, and clarify key clinical and organizational challenges encountered by clinicians in clinical practice.

To facilitate structured evaluation by the Expert Panel, the 41 statements drafted by the Steering Committee were organized into seven thematic domains (context, diagnosis, complications, treatment, quality of life, monitoring, and PDTA), each addressing a distinct dimension of chronic hypoparathyroidism management.

At the end of the second round, consensus was reached on 39 of 41 statements (95.1%), indicating a high degree of agreement among panelists across most domains. Overall, the panelists confirmed that chronic hypoparathyroidism remains a rare but clinically impactful disease, often associated with persistent biochemical instability, long-term renal and skeletal complications, and impaired health-related quality of life (HRQoL). The findings highlight that, despite the availability of conventional therapy, important unmet needs persist in daily clinical practice.

The high level of agreement observed across most domains is consistent with current international and national recommendations [12, 13]. However, while guidelines provide evidence-based recommendations, they do not fully address several practical aspects of care, including the clinical identification of inadequate disease control and the organization of structured multidisciplinary follow-up. In this context, the present Delphi consensus complements existing guidance by clarifying how these recommendations are interpreted and applied within the Italian healthcare setting.

The Expert Panel confirmed that hypoparathyroidism is a rare disease (prevalence < 5 cases per 10,000), while emphasizing its significant clinical impact. In line with the literature, panelists noted that the prevalence of hypoparathyroidism may be underestimated, particularly for non-surgical forms, which are reported to account for approximately 25% of cases [33–35]. Some panelists also stressed the potential need to reassess diagnostic criteria to better identify incomplete, partial, or latent forms (both post-surgical and genetic). Under normal conditions, these clinical presentations are normocalcemic with PTH levels within the normal range, but under conditions of metabolic stress, they present hypocalcemia with PTH levels not exceeding 40 µg/ml.

Special emphasis was placed on the post-surgical setting, in which timely diagnosis and appropriate longitudinal monitoring are essential components of effective disease management.

Experts agree that clinical awareness devoted to hypoparathyroidism is still insufficient, emphasizing the implications in terms of diagnostic delays and suboptimal treatments. This highlights the need for greater disease awareness, especially for non-surgical cases, given the rarity of the condition and its often-nonspecific symptoms.

A particularly strong consensus emerged regarding the limitations of conventional therapy and the off-label use of teriparatide. Experts acknowledged that maintaining serum calcium in the low-normal range does not necessarily translate into stable biochemical control or symptom resolution, and that fluctuations in calcium and phosphate levels, hypercalciuria, and renal impairment remain relevant concerns [32]. Similarly, the consensus on the limitations of off label teriparatide reflects the current evidence base. Although teriparatide has been used in Italy under Law 648/96 in patients not adequately controlled, the absence of large, long-term randomized controlled trials in hypoparathyroidism, uncertainties regarding optimal dosing, and concerns about biochemical variability and safety limit its role [19]. The panelists’ agreement that standard dosing often fails to ensure independence from calcium and active vitamin D supplementation is consistent with published data [14, 15].

An additional limitation relates to standard dosage of teriparatide, which rarely allows the maintenance of normocalcemia without concomitant calcium and calcitriol supplementation. Treatment is also frequently associated with fluctuations in serum calcium and phosphate levels and a higher risk of adverse events. Moreover, patients treated with teriparatide may continue to report persistent symptoms, including fatigue and cognitive difficulties, with a negative impact on quality of life. This scenario highlights a significant unmet need for patients with chronic hypoparathyroidism who are not adequately controlled with conventional therapy or who cannot tolerate it.

The strong agreement supporting the need for reimbursed hormone replacement therapy reflects the growing body of evidence from phase 3 trials of palopegteriparatide (PaTHway) and its open-label extension. These studies demonstrated normalization of serum calcium, independence from conventional therapy, maintenance of normal serum calcium, improvements in renal function parameters, and meaningful enhancements in HRQoL [23–25]. The Delphi findings therefore appear coherent with emerging evidence suggesting a potential paradigm shift from supplementation-based management toward physiological hormone replacement in selected patients with chronic hypoparathyroidism.

Regarding quality of life, panelists highlighted that many patients continue to experience persistent symptoms, including anxiety, frustration and depression, which may negatively affect emotional and cognitive well-being and limit activities of daily living and work productivity.

Consensus on statements related to monitoring and PDTA underscores the perceived absence of standardized national pathways and the need for coordinated multidisciplinary management. Variability in follow-up practices and the lack of structured integration between referral centers and local healthcare providers may contribute to heterogeneity in patient outcomes. The Expert Panel believes that a dedicated national PDTA could improve the management of patients with hypoparathyroidism, promote coordination between specialists, and reduce regional and territorial variability.

The two statements that did not reach consensus concerned the relative predominance of autoimmune causes among non-surgical cases (statement 3) and the association between chronic hypoparathyroidism and cardiomyopathy or heart failure (statement 11). The lack of consensus regarding statement 3, which proposed autoimmune causes as the most frequent etiology among non-surgical forms, likely reflects the heterogeneity of available epidemiological data. Although autoimmune hypoparathyroidism is traditionally considered a leading cause among non-surgical cases, registry-based and observational studies suggest that genetic and idiopathic forms may also be more prevalent than previously recognized [33–35]. This variability in reported distributions may explain the panelists’ cautious position and highlights the need for more precise epidemiological characterization, particularly through national registries and genotype-based classification systems. The absence of consensus on statement 11, concerning the association between chronic hypoparathyroidism and cardiomyopathy or heart failure, reflects ongoing uncertainty in literature. While case reports suggest a potential link between hypocalcemia and cardiac dysfunction, robust longitudinal data remain limited [41, 42]. However, recent national epidemiological data from Italy [46] reported a significantly higher odds ratio of hospitalization for cardiovascular diseases among patients with hypoparathyroidism compared with matched controls, suggesting that cardiovascular burden may be clinically relevant at a population level. The divergence between individual clinical experience and emerging registry-based evidence may explain the panel’s lack of consensus and underscores the need for prospective studies specifically addressing cardiovascular outcomes in this population.

Limits

These findings should be interpreted considering methodological limitations. First, the number of panelists was relatively small. Although the literature does not provide definitive guidance on optimal sample size or selection criteria for Delphi studies [47–49], we considered that a panel of 20 experts from major Italian referral centers would provide a reasonable representation of national practice, particularly given the low prevalence of chronic hypoparathyroidism. Second, panelist anonymity was adopted to minimize mutual influence and potential dominance effects during the evaluation process. While this approach ensures equal weight to each vote, it may also allow variability in the level of disease-specific expertise to influence responses. To mitigate this risk, panelists were nominated by Steering Committee members based on documented clinical experience in hypoparathyroidism management. Third, consensus definitions in Delphi research are not universally standardized, and the selected threshold inevitably reflects a methodological convention rather than a statistical rule. To ensure transparency and consistency with prior Delphi studies, a consensus threshold of 70% was predefined before survey administration. The use of a 5-point (rather than a 9-point) Likert scale was motivated by the limited panel size, with the aim of reducing response dispersion.

In addition, although several findings may be relevant across healthcare systems, the external generalizability of this consensus should be interpreted cautiously. Differences in reimbursement policies, regulatory frameworks, access to hormone replacement therapy, and organization of referral networks may influence the applicability of these conclusions in other countries.

Finally, as with all Delphi studies, the results reflect expert opinion informed by available evidence and clinical experience, and therefore should be viewed as complementary to, rather than a substitute for, clinical data.

Conclusions

This analysis highlights that chronic hypoparathyroidism remains a condition associated with substantial clinical and therapeutic challenges. Conventional therapy, although representing the current standard of care, does not allow for stable metabolic control or effective prevention of long-term complications, while the off-label use of teriparatide is suboptimal and lacks long-term clinical evidence.

In this context, a key issue that emerges is that hypoparathyroidism is currently the only endocrine deficiency disorder in Italy without access to reimbursed hormone replacement therapy. This gap results in a clear and persistent unmet need, which limits patients’ quality of life and reduces the clinical ability to prevent renal and skeletal complications.

The results of the Expert Panel therefore point to the need to evolve the current management model, promoting the adoption of structured care pathways and, above all, access to replacement therapy capable of adequately responding to the still unmet needs of the population affected by chronic hypoparathyroidism.

Perioperative and oncological outcomes of thulium laser-ERBT versus conventional TURBT in non-muscle invasive bladder cancer: a meta-analysis of 2,934 patients
Authors: Dong Chen, Zhongbao Zhou, Guangzhu Wei, Fengbo Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThulium laser en bloc resection of bladder tumors (TmL-ERBT) represents a novel surgical approach for non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC), but its comparative advantages over conventional transurethral resection (TURBT) remain controversial. This meta-analysis aimed to evaluate perioperative safety, surgical quality, and oncological outcomes of TmL-ERBT versus TURBT. A systematic search of PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, and Cochrane Library identified comparative studies of TmL-ERBT versus TURBT. Perioperative complications, oncological outcomes, and surgical quality indicators were analyzed. Heterogeneity was assessed using I² statistics and subgroup analysis. The study quality was evaluated using the ROB2 tool. Evidence certainty was graded using the GRADEpro GDT. Fourteen studies comprising 2,934 patients (TmL-ERBT: n = 1,395; TURBT: n = 1,539) were included. TmL-ERBT demonstrated significant advantages in perioperative outcomes: shorter catheterization time (MD: -0.71 days, P = 0.001), reduced postoperative irrigation time (MD: -8.79 h, P = 0.0004), and decreased hospitalization time (MD: -0.63 days, P = 0.006). Operation duration showed no significant difference (MD: -2.66 min, P = 0.33). Safety outcomes favored TmL-ERBT with virtual elimination of obturator nerve reflex (OR: 0.04, P < 0.00001), reduced bladder perforation (OR: 0.26, P < 0.00001), decreased bleeding (OR: 0.10, P < 0.0001), and improved detrusor muscle presence (OR: 3.22, P = 0.03). Overall complication rates were comparable (OR: 0.75, P = 0.28). TmL-ERBT significantly improved recurrence-free rates at 3 months (OR: 1.70, P = 0.02) and 12 months (OR: 1.45, P = 0.04), but no difference at 24 months (OR: 1.03, P = 0.82) and ≥ 30 months (OR: 0.85, P = 0.13) than TURBT. Subgroup analyses revealed the advantages of TmL-ERBT were most pronounced in low-risk, smaller, solitary tumors. TmL-ERBT demonstrated superior perioperative outcomes, enhanced safety with reduced complications compared with conventional TURBT. Introduction

Bladder cancer ranks among the most prevalent malignancies of the urinary system worldwide, and non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC) accounts for approximately 75–80% of newly diagnosed bladder cancer [1]. Transurethral resection of bladder tumor (TURBT) has served as the gold standard for diagnosis and treatment of NMIBC [2]. However, conventional TURBT has limitations, including fragmented resection, thermal artifacts, and inadequate detrusor muscle sampling, potentially leading to under-staging and compromising pathological assessment [3, 4]. Perioperative complications such as bladder perforation, obturator nerve reflex, and irrigation-related issues are concerns [5]. Most critically, incomplete resection and tumor cell reimplantation during the procedure may contribute to the high recurrence rates [6]. Beyond surgical improvements, the landscape of NMIBC management is also evolving with advancements in molecular diagnostics. Recent evidence on novel biomarkers, for instance, is increasingly contributing to more precise diagnosis, risk stratification, and recurrence prediction in NMIBC patients, complementing surgical efforts to optimize outcomes [7, 8].

En bloc resection of bladder tumors (ERBT) involves removing the tumor as a single intact specimen, theoretically offering superior specimen quality for pathological evaluation and reduced risk of tumor cell dissemination [9, 10]. Among various energy modalities, thulium laser (TmL-ERBT) has emerged as a promising platform due to its unique physical properties, facilitating precise tissue ablation with minimal thermal injury, better hemostasis, clearer surgical visualization, and reduced obturator nerve stimulation [11].

Despite increasing clinical application, the advantage of TmL-ERBT over conventional TURBT remains debated. While some studies reported promising results, many findings have been inconsistent due to heterogeneity in study designs, patient populations, surgeon experience, and outcome definitions [12]. Concerns also exist regarding the learning curve, equipment costs, and long-term oncological outcomes of TmL-ERBT, warranting systematic evaluation [13].

Therefore, this meta-analysis compares TmL-ERBT and conventional TURBT in NMIBC patients. We aim to evaluate perioperative safety parameters, surgical quality indicators, and oncological outcomes. The findings of this analysis may inform clinical decision-making and help establish the role of TmL-ERBT in contemporary NMIBC management.

Methods

Literature search strategy

Following the recommendations of The Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses [14], we searched three databases including PubMed, Embase, and Cochrane, covering the period from January 2000 to Jun 2025. Our search strategy employed the following Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) terms: (“bladder cancer” OR " bladder tumor” OR " non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer “) AND (“Thulium laser en bloc resection” OR “TmL-ERBT” OR “En bloc resection”). We reviewed all references cited in the included studies to identify additional relevant research. Only published articles were considered, with no restrictions on language. Two researchers independently assessed the full texts according to the inclusion and exclusion criteria. In cases of disagreement, they discussed the issue and, if necessary, consulted a third researcher to reach a final decision. This study was registered with PROSPERO under the registration number CRD420251207424.

Eligibility and exclusion criteria

Articles were included if they met the following criteria: (a) Studies investigating TmL-ERBT vs. TURBT for patients with bladder cancer; (b) availability of full-text content and extractable statistical data; (c) data presented were accurate and valid; (d) inclusion regardless of whether patients had additional complications. Excluded were case reports, review articles, conference reports, abstracts, and studies with incomplete data. In cases where multiple records involved overlapping populations, only the most recent study was included. The inclusion criteria followed the PICOS rule (Table 1).

Quality assessment

The methodological quality of included studies was independently assessed by two reviewers using the Cochrane Risk of Bias tool version 2 (RoB 2) [15]. Disagreements were resolved through discussion or consultation with a third reviewer. The assessment evaluated five domains: randomization process, deviations from intended interventions, missing outcome data, measurement of the outcome, and selection of the reported result. Each domain was rated as low risk (green, +), some concerns (yellow, ?), or high risk (red, ?). Overall risk of bias was determined based on domain-level judgments: studies with all low-risk domains were classified as low risk overall; studies with at least one “some concerns” domain (without high-risk domains) were classified as having some concerns; and studies with at least one high-risk domain were classified as high risk overall. Results were visualized using the robvis tool.

Data extraction and outcome indicators

Two authors independently extracted data from the included studies, which comprised (a) author’s name, (b) publication year, (c) country, (d) study design, (e) type of operation, (f) sample size, (g) tumor size, (h) number of tumors, (i) disease type. Outcome measures included perioperative outcomes (including operation duration; hospitalization time, catheterization time, postoperative irrigation time, detrusor presence, obturator nerve reflex), perioperative complications (including bladder perforation, bleeding, complications-grade), and oncological outcomes (including 3-, 12-, 24-, ≥ 30-month recurrence-free rate). Ethical approval was not required for this study as it involved a retrospective analysis of published research.

Subgroup analyses

To explore potential sources of heterogeneity and assess the robustness of overall findings, we performed pre-specified subgroup analyses for perioperative indicators based on the following variables: (1) Study design: randomized controlled trials (RCTs) versus non-randomized controlled trials (NRCTs); (2) Geographic region: studies conducted in China versus non-China; (3) Publication year: 2010–2021 versus 2022–2025; (4) Sample size: studies with < 200 patients versus ≥ 200 patients; (5) Tumor size: <2 cm versus ≥ 2 cm; (6) Number of tumors: <2 tumors versus ≥ 2 tumors. Subgroup analyses were conducted for the following outcomes: operation duration, hospitalization time, catheterization time, postoperative irrigation time, detrusor presence, 3-, 12-, 24-, ≥ 30-month recurrence-free rate. Subgroup effects were assessed using interaction tests to determine whether treatment effects differed significantly across subgroups.

GRADE assessment

The certainty of evidence was assessed using the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) approach by two independent reviewers [16]. Evidence was classified as high, moderate, low, or very low certainty. Randomized controlled trials initially received a high certainty rating and could be downgraded based on five criteria: risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, and publication bias. Evidence could be upgraded for large effect sizes, dose-response gradients, or when plausible confounding would reduce effects. Summary of findings tables were generated using GRADEpro GDT software, presenting effect estimates, participant numbers, and certainty ratings for each outcome.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was performed using RevMan 5.3 (Cochrane Collaboration, London, UK) [17]. The choice of effect model and statistical method depended on data type and heterogeneity. Odds ratios (OR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI) were used for categorical variables, while mean differences (MD) with 95% CI were applied to continuous variables. Heterogeneity was assessed using the I-squared statistic. When the p-value exceeded 0.05 and I-squared was below 50%, heterogeneity was considered low, and a fixed-effects model was used; otherwise, a random-effects model was applied. Results were presented in forest plots, with p-values less than 0.05 considered statistically significant.

Results

Study Selection and characteristics

As shown in the PRISMA flowchart, the initial search identified 349 potentially relevant articles. After title and abstract screening, 38 articles proceeded to full-text review. Fourteen studies [18–31]enrolling a total of 2,934 patients were ultimately included in the qualitative and quantitative synthesis (Supplementary Fig. 1). The primary reasons for exclusion were non-comparative study design (n = 11), lack of effective outcome indicators (n = 7), and other methodological limitations (n = 6).

This meta-analysis included 14 studies comprising 2,934 patients with NMIBC or primary bladder tumors (Table 2). Of these, 5 studies (35.7%) were RCTs and 9 studies (64.3%) were NRCTs. The studies were published between 2013 and 2025, with the majority originating from China (n = 8, 57.1%), followed by Russia (n = 2, 14.3%), Egypt (n = 2, 14.3%), Spain (n = 1, 7.1%), and the United States (n = 1, 7.1%).

The TmL-ERBT group included 1,395 patients, while the conventional TURBT group comprised 1,539 patients. Among studies, the mean tumor diameter ranged from 1.31 cm to 4.2 cm in the TmL-ERBT group and from 1.28 cm to 5.2 cm in the TURBT group. Regarding tumor multiplicity, single tumors were more prevalent than multiple tumors in both groups across most studies. The number of tumors per patient varied considerably, with some studies reporting a mean of 1.0 to 2.9 tumors. Most studies (n = 11, 78.6%) specifically enrolled patients with primary NMIBC, while three studies included patients with primary bladder tumors without muscle invasion.

Risk of bias assessment

The risk of bias assessment revealed the heterogeneity among the included studies (Supplementary Fig. 2). While the overall quality was moderate, with 28.6% of studies demonstrating low risk of bias, the predominant concern was inadequate randomization procedures in 57.1% of studies. The majority of studies (92.9%) showed low risk of bias regarding deviations from intended interventions, suggesting good protocol adherence and surgical technique standardization. Most studies adequately addressed missing outcome data (71.4% at low risk), indicating robust follow-up procedures. However, concerns regarding outcome measurement (35.7% with some concerns) and selective reporting (14.3% at high risk) warrant cautious interpretation of specific outcomes. All 8 non-RCTs included in the analysis achieved NOS scores of seven or above, suggesting a generally high methodological quality (Table 2).

Perioperative outcomes

Operation duration

Twelve studies comprising 2,305 patients (TmL-ERBT: n = 1,083; TURBT: n = 1,222) reported operation duration. The pooled analysis revealed no statistically significant difference in operation duration between TmL-ERBT and conventional TURBT (MD: -2.66 min, 95% CI: -7.99 to 2.68, P = 0.33) (Supplementary Fig. 3A). However, substantial heterogeneity was observed across studies (I²=92%, df = 11, P < 0.00001), indicating considerable variability in operation duration between studies. The overall finding suggested that TmL-ERBT did not significantly prolong or shorten the operation duration compared to conventional TURBT.

Catheterization time

Twelve studies involving 2,142 patients (TmL-ERBT: n = 1,005; TURBT: n = 1,137) provided data on postoperative catheterization time. Meta-analysis demonstrated that TmL-ERBT was associated with significantly shorter catheterization time compared to conventional TURBT (MD: -0.71 days, 95% CI: -1.14 to -0.28, P = 0.001) (Supplementary Fig. 3B). The reduced catheterization time with TmL-ERBT likely reflects improved hemostatic control and reduced tissue trauma associated with en bloc resection technique.

Postoperative irrigation time

Six studies with 972 patients (TmL-ERBT: n = 481; TURBT: n = 491) reported postoperative irrigation time. The pooled analysis revealed that TmL-ERBT significantly shortened irrigation time compared to conventional TURBT (MD: -8.79 h, 95% CI: -13.65 to -3.93, P = 0.0004) (Supplementary Fig. 3C). The substantial reduction in irrigation time with TmL-ERBT can be attributed to superior hemostatic properties of thulium laser technology and the en bloc resection technique.

Hospitalization time

Ten studies enrolling 1,766 patients (TmL-ERBT: n = 834; TURBT: n = 932) assessed postoperative hospitalization time. Meta-analysis demonstrated that TmL-ERBT was associated with significantly shorter hospitalization time compared to conventional TURBT (MD: -0.63 days, 95% CI: -1.08 to -0.18, P = 0.006) (Supplementary Fig. 3D). The shorter hospital time with TmL-ERBT likely results from the cumulative benefits of reduced catheterization time, decreased irrigation requirements, and improved perioperative recovery.

Obturator nerve reflex

Thirteen studies comprising 1,985 patients (TmL-ERBT: n = 943; TURBT: n = 1,042) reported the incidence of obturator nerve reflex. The pooled analysis demonstrated a dramatic and statistically significant reduction in obturator nerve reflex with TmL-ERBT compared to conventional TURBT (OR: 0.04, 95% CI: 0.02 to 0.10, P < 0.00001) (Supplementary Fig. 4A). Remarkably, no obturator nerve reflex events occurred in any patient undergoing TmL-ERBT across all 13 studies (0/943 patients, 0%), whereas 119 events were reported in the TURBT group (119/1,042 patients, 11.4%).

Bladder perforation

Thirteen studies involving 2,142 patients (TmL-ERBT: n = 1,005; TURBT: n = 1,137) provided data on bladder perforation. Meta-analysis revealed that TmL-ERBT was associated with a significantly lower risk of bladder perforation compared to conventional TURBT (OR: 0.26, 95% CI: 0.15 to 0.45, P < 0.00001) (Supplementary Fig. 4B). The perforation rate was substantially lower in the TmL-ERBT group, with only 11 events (1.1%) compared to 68 events (6.0%) in the TURBT group.

Detrusor presence

Six studies with 942 patients (TmL-ERBT: n = 494; TURBT: n = 448) evaluated the presence of detrusor muscle in resection specimens. Meta-analysis demonstrated that TmL-ERBT was associated with significantly higher detrusor presence compared to conventional TURBT (OR: 3.22, 95% CI: 1.16 to 8.97, P = 0.03) (Supplementary Fig. 4C). The presence of detrusor muscle was documented in 92.3% of TmL-ERBT specimens (456/494 patients) compared to 82.1% of TURBT specimens (368/448 patients), representing approximately a 10% increase in adequate sampling.

Perioperative complications

Bleeding

Eight studies with 1,277 patients (TmL-ERBT: n = 613; TURBT: n = 664) reported bleeding. Meta-analysis demonstrated that TmL-ERBT was associated with significantly lower bleeding risk compared to conventional TURBT (OR: 0.10, 95% CI: 0.03 to 0.29, P < 0.0001) (Supplementary Fig. 5A). Bleeding events occurred in 11.6% of TmL-ERBT patients (71/613 patients) compared to 33.1% of TURBT patients (220/664 patients), representing approximately a 21.5% risk reduction.

Complications grade

Complications were assessed in four studies involving 1,110 patients (TmL-ERBT: n = 490; TURBT: n = 620), categorized according to the Clavien-Dindo classification system. Overall complication rates did not differ significantly between groups (OR: 0.75, 95% CI: 0.44 to 1.27, P = 0.28) (Supplementary Fig. 5B). Total complications occurred in 1.1% of TmL-ERBT patients (22/490 patients) versus 1.9% of TURBT patients (47/620 patients). Subgroup analysis revealed consistent findings across severity grades: Grade I complications showed OR 0.80 (95% CI: 0.44 to 1.45, P = 0.46), while Grade ≥ II complications yielded OR 0.58 (95% CI: 0.18 to 1.91, P = 0.37), with no significant subgroup differences (P = 0.65).

Oncological outcomes

TmL-ERBT demonstrates significant advantages in early (3-month) and intermediate-term (12-month) recurrence-free rates, where outcomes converge with conventional TURBT at 24-month and ≥ 30-month follow-up.

3-month recurrence-free rate

Four studies with 498 patients (TmL-ERBT: n = 268; TURBT: n = 230) reported 3-month recurrence-free rate postoperatively. Meta-analysis demonstrated that TmL-ERBT was associated with significantly higher 3-month recurrence-free rate compared to conventional TURBT (OR: 1.70, 95% CI: 1.08 to 2.69, P = 0.02) (Supplementary Fig. 6A).

12-month recurrence-free rate

Ten studies involving 1,956 patients (TmL-ERBT: n = 937; TURBT: n = 1,019) assessed 12-month recurrence-free rate. Meta-analysis revealed that TmL-ERBT achieved significantly superior recurrence-free rate compared to TURBT (OR: 1.45, 95% CI: 1.01 to 2.07, P = 0.04) (Supplementary Fig. 6B).

24-month recurrence-free rate

Seven studies with 1,704 patients (TmL-ERBT: n = 798; TURBT: n = 906) provided 24-month recurrence-free rate. At this time point, no significant difference was observed between TmL-ERBT and TURBT (OR: 1.03, 95% CI: 0.82 to 1.29, P = 0.82) (Supplementary Fig. 6C). Recurrence-free rates were 68.4% in the TmL-ERBT group (546/798 patients) and 65.8% in the TURBT group (596/906 patients), with minimal heterogeneity across studies (I²=30%, P = 0.20).

≥ 30-month recurrence-free rate

Seven studies encompassing 1,691 patients (TmL-ERBT: n = 799; TURBT: n = 892) reported long-term recurrence-free rate at or beyond 30 months. Meta-analysis showed no significant difference between groups (OR: 0.85, 95% CI: 0.68 to 1.05, P = 0.13) (Supplementary Fig. 6D). Recurrence-free rates were comparable: 38.0% in the TmL-ERBT group (304/799 patients) versus 38.1% in the TURBT group (340/892 patients). Low heterogeneity was observed (I²=12%, P = 0.34), indicating consistent long-term outcomes.

Study design (RCT vs. Non-RCT)

In RCTs, no significant differences were observed between TmL-ERBT and TURBT across all five perioperative indicators (operation duration, hospitalization time, catheterization time, irrigation time, and detrusor presence; all P > 0.05). Conversely, non-RCTs demonstrated significant advantages for TmL-ERBT in hospitalization time (MD: -0.88 days, P = 0.003), catheterization time (MD: -0.88 days, P = 0.001), postoperative irrigation time (MD: -14.18 h, P < 0.0001), and detrusor presence (OR: 5.22, P < 0.00001), suggesting potential selection bias or confounding in observational studies.

Geographic region (China vs. Non-China)

Chinese studies showed significant benefits in hospitalization time (MD: -1.30 days, P = 0.0005), catheterization time (MD: -1.10 days, P = 0.0004), and irrigation time (MD: -12.11 h, P < 0.00001), but not in detrusor presence (P = 0.79). Non-Chinese studies revealed superior detrusor presence with TmL-ERBT (OR: 5.94, P < 0.00001) but minimal differences in other indicators, likely reflecting differences in surgical expertise or patient populations.

Publication year (2010–2021 vs. 2022–2025)

Recent studies (2022–2025) demonstrated more consistent benefits across hospitalization time (MD: -0.32 days, P = 0.02), catheterization time (MD: -0.43 days, P = 0.001), and detrusor presence (OR: 4.94, P = 0.0002) compared to earlier publications (2010–2021), potentially reflecting improving surgical proficiency and higher-quality evidence.

Sample size (< 200 vs. ≥200 patients)

Larger studies (≥ 200 patients) showed robust advantages in hospitalization time (MD: -1.15 days, P = 0.01), catheterization time (MD: -1.09 days, P = 0.008), and irrigation time (MD: -12.55 h, P = 0.002), while smaller studies demonstrated pronounced benefits in detrusor presence (OR: 5.94, P < 0.00001).

Tumor characteristics

For smaller tumors (< 2 cm), TmL-ERBT dramatically improved detrusor presence (OR: 8.14, P = 0.001) and reduced catheterization time (MD: -1.05 days, P < 0.0001). For multiple tumors (≥ 2), marked advantages were observed in hospitalization time (MD: -1.91 days, P < 0.00001), catheterization time (MD: -1.27 days, P = 0.0002), and irrigation time (MD: -13.49 h, P = 0.002).

Discussion

This meta-analysis, encompassing fourteen studies with nearly three thousand patients, provided comprehensive evidence regarding the effectiveness and safety of TmL-ERBT versus TURBT for NMIBC management. Our findings demonstrated that TmL-ERBT offered substantial perioperative advantages and favorable short-term oncological outcomes.

The significant reductions in catheterization time, irrigation time, and hospitalization duration with TmL-ERBT can be attributed to the unique physical properties of thulium laser technology and the en bloc resection technique. The thulium laser’s wavelength exhibits exceptional water absorption characteristics, enabling precise tissue ablation with superficial penetration depth [32]. This results in superior hemostasis through immediate coagulation of blood vessels during tissue cutting, substantially reducing intraoperative and postoperative bleeding [33]. The near-elimination of bleeding complications in our analysis with event rates reduced from one-third to approximately one-tenth, directly translates to decreased irrigation requirements and earlier catheter removal, ultimately facilitating faster patient recovery and hospital discharge.

Interestingly, operation duration showed no significant difference between techniques despite the different resection approaches. This finding challenged initial concerns that ERBT might be more time-consuming. Several factors likely contributed to these results. First, while TmL-ERBT required careful circumferential incision and complete tumor resection, conventional TURBT necessitated multiple piecemeal passes and meticulous hemostasis [34]. Second, the learning curve effect may influence operation times, with experienced surgeons achieving efficiency with either technique [12]. Third, the observed heterogeneity suggested that individual surgeon experience, tumor characteristics, and institutional protocols significantly impacted procedural duration, potentially masking technique specific differences.

The dramatic reduction in obturator nerve reflex represented one of the most clinically significant findings of this meta-analysis. The complete absence of obturator nerve reflex events across all patients with TmL-ERBT, compared to an occurrence rate exceeding one-tenth in patients with TURBT, reflected fundamental differences in electrical energy application. Conventional monopolar TURBT generated electrical current that can inadvertently stimulate the obturator nerve when resecting lateral wall tumors, causing sudden thigh adduction and potentially catastrophic bladder injury [35]. Thulium laser technology eliminated this risk through purely thermal energy delivery without electrical current, providing enhanced safety particularly for lateral wall and bladder neck tumors where obturator nerve proximity posed greatest risk [20].

The four-fold reduction in bladder perforation with TmL-ERBT similarly reflected both technological and technical advantages. The precise tissue ablation with minimal thermal spread minimized unintended deep tissue injury [36]. Moreover, ERBT allowed continuous visualization of resection depth and underlying muscle layers, enabling surgeons to better control resection extent compared to TURBT [37]. This enhanced depth control was particularly valuable in challenging locations such as bladder dome or diverticula.

The higher detrusor muscle presence in TmL-ERBT specimens, which exceeding conventional TURBT by approximately 10% points, carried profound implications for accurate pathological staging. Adequate detrusor muscle sampling was essential for distinguishing non-muscle-invasive from muscle-invasive disease and identifying T1 tumors, which required more aggressive management [3, 38]. The ERBT facilitated deeper tissue sampling while maintaining specimen orientation and architectural integrity, allowing pathologists to assess invasion depth more reliably [9, 39]. Iscaife et al. reported favorable outcomes with hybrid and holmium laser en-bloc resections, including high rates of detrusor muscle in specimens, further substantiating the benefits of laser-based approaches in obtaining high-quality pathological material [40]. Conversely, TURBT specimens may lack adequate muscle or present orientation challenges that compromise the accuracy of stage [41]. The improved specimen quality with TmL-ERBT potentially reduced staging errors and subsequent inappropriate treatment decisions, though our analysis could not directly assess this clinical endpoint.

The temporal pattern of recurrence-free rates warranted careful interpretation, with TmL-ERBT having some advantages at three and twelve months. The early recurrence-free benefit probably reflected reduced tumor cell implantation during surgery. TmL-ERBT minimized tumor manipulation and fragmentation compared to TURBT, theoretically decreasing intravesicular tumor cell dissemination [42]. However, the transform of recurrence rates at longer follow-up suggested that TmL-ERBT did not fundamentally alter the biological behavior of bladder cancer or prevent true de novo tumor development. The majority of field recurrences likely represent new tumor formation driven by real cancerization, and the presence of molecularly altered urothelium throughout the bladder predisposed to malignant transformation [43]. No surgical technique can address this underlying biological phenomenon, which remains the primary driver of long-term recurrence. This interpretation was supported by subgroup analyses showing more pronounced and sustained benefits in low-risk, smaller, solitary tumors, and clinical scenarios where complete resection and minimizing implantation had greatest impact, and where field cancerization effects were presumably less prominent [44]. Garstman et al. investigated conservative treatment strategies for high-risk NMIBC failing initial BCG therapy, demonstrating the potential oncological benefit of adding electromotive drug administration of mitomycin-C to a second BCG induction cycle [45]. Finati et al. indicated that muscularis mucosae invasion (pT1), independently predicts NMIBC prognosis, and deeper sampling with TmL-ERBT could modify early recurrence patterns and influence adjuvant therapy decisions compared with TURBT [46]. Among bladder cancer patients managed with active surveillance, a recurrence within six months predicted progression to a more aggressive disease, highlighting the prognostic relevance of time to first recurrence in intermediate-risk NMIBC [47].

The predominance of non-randomized studies and geographic concentration in China introduces important considerations for result interpretation. The more pronounced benefits observed in non-RCTs, Chinese studies, and earlier publications suggested potential selection bias, performance bias, or learning curve effects. A significant limitation of our meta-analysis was the predominance of non-randomized controlled studies. While we performed subgroup analyses to explore the impact of study design, the consistently larger effect sizes observed in non-RCTs, compared to RCTs, highlight a substantial risk of bias. The inconsistent reporting and lack of control for intravesical adjuvant therapies represented a key confounding factor that could influence the observed recurrence rates, and that this could not be fully addressed within the scope of our meta-analysis. The convergence toward more modest, consistent benefits in recent, larger, well-designed studies likely represents more reliable effect estimates as the technology matures and research methodology improves [15].

These findings supported TmL-ERBT as a valuable advancement in NMIBC management, offering meaningful perioperative advantages, enhanced safety, and improved pathological assessment without prolonging operation time. The technique appears particularly beneficial for lateral wall tumors, multiple tumors, and cases where accurate staging was critical [48]. However, the absence of long-term oncological superiority suggested that adjuvant intravesical therapy, risk-adapted surveillance, and addressing field cancerization remain essential components of comprehensive bladder cancer management regardless of surgical technique employed [49].

Conclusion

This meta-analysis demonstrated that TmL-ERBT offered significant advantages over conventional TURBT for NMIBC management, including reduced catheterization time, irrigation requirements, and hospitalization duration, alongside virtually eliminating obturator nerve reflex and substantially reducing bladder perforation and bleeding complications. The technique provided superior detrusor muscle sampling, enhancing pathological staging accuracy. These findings supported TmL-ERBT as a safe, effective alternative to conventional TURBT, particularly for lateral wall tumors and cases requiring precise pathological assessment.

Workforce restoration time analysis for a two grade manpower system under heavy tail distribution and dual regime decisions latency
Authors: K. Parameswari, K. Kannan, A. Menaga
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Workforce restoration time (WRT) and man power planning are very essential for administrative as well as production oriented organizations. In recent days, several researchers worked on two-grade manpower system with a homogeneous attrition rate to obtain workforce restoration time. In the present paper, a stochastic methodology for examining workforce restoration time in a two-grade manpower system that is impacted by heavy-tailed threshold behaviors and organizational decision latencies is presented and four probabilistic models are developed to describe grade-specific breakdown limits under both independent and correlated dual-regime choice processes for inter-decision time. To achieve this, Lomax distribution is used to obtain the closed-form formulas for the mean and variance of workforce restoration time by taking into account both homogeneous and heterogeneous attrition rates, are obtained. Our results reveal that E[T] shows a direct and almost linear dependency on c and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\beta$$\end{document}, and it decreases monotonically as decision activity (K) increases. Temporal dependencies slightly lengthen the restoration period, as confirmed by the correlation parameter R, which has a less pronounced but steady effect. It is supported by the simulation tests that measure the sensitivity of restoration time with the help of various parameters like attrition rate c, inter decision interval \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\beta$$\end{document}, decision correlation R, and decision count K. Further, our model predictions are statistically stable with tight confidence ranges.

Introduction and related works

Attrition and replenishment processes must be balanced for multi-grade enterprises to manage their workforce effectively. Restoring the intended workforce level becomes a stochastic challenge controlled by temporal and structural uncertainty when hiring and decision-making delays exhibit random patterns. The significance of integrating probabilistic methods in recruitment and attrition systems have been underscored by recent developments in workforce modeling, particularly where heavy-tailed thresholds are present and significant losses or decision delays are probable. Managing staff levels in the face of stochastic attrition, delayed decisions, and heavy-tailed severe occurrences is a recurring task for organizations with dynamic demand and limited decision-making processes. Therefore, analytical techniques that account for the temporal unpredictability of recruiting and decision-making processes as well as the possibility of extreme, low-probability events that significantly impact restoration time are necessary for modern workforce planning. Inter-grade mobility and linked decision regimes exacerbate these effects in hierarchical, multi-grade systems, creating complex restoration dynamics that are beyond the scope of straightforward deterministic or memory less models1.

Stochastic modeling has a long history in operations research and manpower planning since it offers a methodical approach to measuring uncertainty, obtaining closed-form performance metrics, and guiding decision support tools. Though recent work highlights multi-stage stochastic programming and scenario-based approaches to jointly schedule workforce and production under uncertainty, classic queueing and renewal frameworks are still helpful. This shows the usefulness of probabilistic formulations in actual supply-chain and service environments. These techniques are especially useful when planning needs to strike a compromise between service level restrictions, personnel, and delays in the face of fluctuating demand2.

Modeling threshold or breakdown phenomena in human systems, where excessive delays or disproportionate losses occur with non-negligible frequency, is a good fit for heavy-tailed distributions like the Lomax (Pareto II) family. Because of its successful applications in reliability, survival analysis, and other fields where tail behavior significantly influences expectations and variance, the Lomax distribution and its generalizations are a desirable option for illustrating grade-specific breakdown or decision-latency thresholds in manpower systems. Using a Lomax-based threshold model provides robust sensitivity analysis for extreme-value regimes and allows closed-form moment computations given suitable parametric assumptions3.

Human resource development (HRD) experts must also make sure that workers are treated fairly and ethically during workplace downsizing and are not let go because they belong to a protected class4. Hotho et al. have studied how hiring managers handle expectations of bias and social norms during the hiring and selection process. They have created a communal view on obtaining a closed form by fusing ideas from network research and institutional theory, portraying it as a social expectation that needs to be controlled5. Despite the abundance of research on recruitment and selection, new areas of study have been made possible by recent political, economic, sociological, and technical advancements6. Lang has studied public administration hiring practices in order to gain a better understanding of how organizations impact the career paths of people of immigrant descent7.

The theoretical stances that explain hiring and retaining employees in start-ups have been described by Dash et al. It provides a summary of how context is conceptualized and how it is included into current studies on the application and efficacy of hiring and retaining staff8. How businesses that offer behavior analysis services may support and maintain a diverse workforce is one area that has gotten less attention. Examples and suggestions for achieving this broad objective have been offered by Rosales et al.9. Employers may need to make more visible commitments to diversity if they want to attract minority candidates. Having a more varied staff does not necessarily translate into more minority applicants10. Chen has examined how, as digital tools rethink business, talent acquisition has evolved from digital 1.0 to 3.0 (AI-enabled). Artificial intelligence technology has increased recruitment efficiency and made recruiters’ daily tasks easier. His research also found that AI is crucial to every step of the hiring process, including promotion, job search, application, screening, evaluation, and coordination11.

Li has concentrated on upskilling and reskilling the staff to be future-ready in the context of Industry 4.0 and beyond12. He has outlined the key competencies that the industry is looking for in order to achieve Industry 4.0 and provided a guide for people to follow in order to study and gain new information. At the same time, when hiring managers find it challenging to make decisions based on preconceived notions of ideal applicants while also being receptive to diversity, recruiters are faced with a dilemma13. By illustrating the effects of generative and discriminative AI in hiring and selection, as well as the resulting algorithmic management of humans and human-AI collaboration, Dutta and Naeen’s study adds to the body of existing HRM literature. Additionally, they theorize the revolutionary shifts in procedures and structures that occur throughout the recruitment and selection process as a result of combined agencies14. Using the HC BRidge Framework, Priyadarsini and Sreejith investigated the association between brand reputation (BR) and recruitment efficiency measures, using candidate experience (CE) as a mediating variable15.

The two-grade stochastic framework for workforce restoration developed in this paper adds to the literature by combining (i) Lomax-distributed breakdown thresholds16, (ii) dual-regime decision processes (independent and correlated forms), and (iii) analytically tractable expressions for restoration time moments. We derive closed-form mean and variance formulas for restoration time, building on previous developments in stochastic workforce and scheduling models. We confirm these results using scenario-based uncertainty analysis and simulation experiments. The method provides information about decision frequency, attrition control, and how decision correlation affects how quickly or slowly the workforce recovers.

Methodology

For a highly regulated organizations (e.g., defense, civil services, or certain industries) where grade integrity is critical hence transfer is not advisable. Here each grade is a closed pool, with its own recruitment and career path, and no cross movement allowed. To incorporate this in our model it is considered that the organization threshold Y=min(YA,YB) wherein the transfer of personnel from one grade (A or B) to another (B or A) is not necessary since recruitment is done when the cumulative wastages exceed the threshold for grade A or the threshold for grade B, whichever is earlier. On the other hand to encourage career progression, motivates employees to acquire skills and for flexible manpower planning many organizations (banking, education, or certain industries) the two grades are connected by defined pathways. Taking this point in view, we take the threshold Y for the organization as Y= max (YA ,YB) in such a way that exceeding of thresholds in both the grades by the cumulative wastages is mandatory.

Here, the heavy tail distribution (Lomex distribution) is utilized for thresholds for each grade, and the mean and variance of time to recruitment are calculated for a system where the loss of man-hours has an equal or unequal attrition rate. Additionally, the performance metrics for independently distributed, identically distributed, and correlated inter-decision time are examined. With the recruitment policy, we have put up two models that allow the system to continue operating until the total number of lost man-hours surpasses the threshold. Conclusion

A thorough quantitative tool for assessing workforce restoration dynamics in dual-grade organizational systems is offered by the suggested stochastic models. The framework represents the variety of attrition and recruitment cycles in the actual world by combining correlated and uncorrelated decision latencies with thresholds that are dispersed according to the Lomax distribution. According to simulation results, changes in decision frequency (K), attrition rate (c), inter-decision interval (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\beta$$\end{document}), and correlation parameter (R) all have predictable effects on the projected restoration time E[T]. According to the analysis, E[T] shows a direct and almost linear dependency on c and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\beta$$\end{document}, and it decreases monotonically as decision activity (K) increases. Temporal dependencies slightly lengthen the restoration period, as confirmed by the correlation parameter R, which has a less pronounced but steady effect. The suggested models’ statistical stability and robustness are validated by the narrow 95% CIs in every scenario. The meaningful extensions can be pursued to enhance the generality and applicability of the model in future. Such framework is the extension to the multi-grade as well as networked manpower systems. Regarding multi grade manpower system, closed-form expressions may be obtainable for low-dimensional cases, approximation or moment-based methods may be required for higher-grade systems. Extending the restoration-time concept to such networks would enable the analysis of localized shocks, cascading shortages, and targeted recovery strategies. Due to state-space growth, simulation-based and decomposition approaches are expected to play a central role in this extension. Future research may incorporate policy-dependent restoration mechanisms, such as priority hiring, accelerated training, or adaptive recruitment rates. The proposed restoration-time framework enables organizations to optimize recruitment timing by identifying when the risk of prolonged workforce recovery exceeds acceptable operational thresholds. Rather than relying on reactive hiring, recruitment can be triggered proactively based on the expected restoration time and its variability under uncertainty. This risk-informed timing strategy supports faster recovery from disruptions and enhances overall workforce resilience.

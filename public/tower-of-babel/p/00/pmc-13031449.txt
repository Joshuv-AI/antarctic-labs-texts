Bibliometric analysis of bone metastasis from prostate cancer research from 2007 to 2024
Authors: Yin He, Jing Zhang, Kai Gong, Zhilin Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT

Introduction

PCa is one of the most common cancer in men worldwide, with the developments in medicine, the 5-year survival rate of prostate cancer patients has reached over 70% in most countries, and up to 90% in some countries [1, 2]. However, distant metastases, including bone, lymph, lung, and liver metastases, remain the main cause of death in PCa patients [3]. The probability of distant metastasis in advanced PCa is as high as 70%, with bone being the most common site of metastasis [4]. Bone metastasis in prostate cancer (BMPCa) can manifest morphologically as osteoblastic, osteolytic, or mixed types, among which osteoblastic is the most common [5]. BMPCa often causes various Skeletal Related Events(SREs), including bone pain, pathological fractures, spinal cord compression or paraplegia, hypercalcemia [6]. These events severely affect patients’ quality of life and increase the risk of patient mortality, leading to a reduction in the 5-year survival rate of PCa patients to 30% [7]. Active prevention and treatment of bone metastases are crucial for effectively controlling PCa.

In recent years, numerous scientific achievements have been made in research on BMPCa. However, the current literature and data on the pathogenesis, diagnosis, and treatment of BMPCa are relatively scattered. Although meta-analyses and traditional reviews have conducted relevant studies on BMPCa, they have only focused on specific research directions or indicators, resulting in a narrow scope of study design and limited analysis and discussion. Bibliometrics focuses on the quantification of a complete body of knowledge, by analyzing existing quantitative literature, intuitive charts can be used to predict the future development direction of a research field and systematically analyze the research progress of various countries, institutions, authors, and disciplines [8, 9]. Only a few researchers have evaluated the scientific output of BMPCa manifestations using bibliometric methods [10]. Therefore, this paper aims to conduct a comprehensive and objective analysis of the indicators of BMPCa-related literature using bibliometric methods. It seeks to clarify the trends, hotspots, and directions of research on BMPCa for researchers, and also provides new clues and ideas for future research, diagnosis, and treatment of BMPCa.

Materials and methods

Data source

The literature extracted from the Science Citation Index Expanded in Clarivate Analytics’ Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) database compiles comprehensive citation and publication information across various scientific fields.

Search strategy

All published papers were collected from WoSCC on October 15, 2025, and two investigators identified relevant published between January 01, 2007, and December 31, 2024, using the following search strategy: TS=((prostate OR prostatic) and (cancer OR tumor OR oncology OR neoplasm OR carcinoma) AND (“bone metastases” OR “bone metastasis” OR “spine metastases” OR “spinal metastases"OR “spine metastases” OR “spinal metastasis”)) AND Publication Years = (January 1, 2007–December 31, 2024) AND Document Types= (Article & Review Article) AND Languages= (English).

Export all records of publications, including year, title, author names, affiliations, countries/regions, abstract, keywords, and name of journals, as Plain Text files in “Full Record and Cited References” for further bibliometric analysis.

After the preliminary data retrieval, two researchers (Yin He and Jing Zhang) screened all manuscripts separately to ensure they were relevant to the theme of this study, with any discrepancies being resolved by the experienced corresponding author (Zhilin Li). Data collection and bibliometric analysis

All literature searches were completed on October 15, 2025. To ensure data accuracy and research reliability, two researchers independently downloaded and analyzed the data, and the event of disagreements between the two researchers, they resolved the issue through discussion or consult the third author to achieve a final consensus.

All the included records were exported and saved as “Plain text file” with “Full Record and Cited References” for further bibliometric analysis. Duplicate Literature Removal: Precise matching based on DOI for deletion. For records without DOI, a comprehensive comparison of title, first author, publication year, and volume/issue/page information. All suspected duplicates undergo manual verification. Author Name Disambiguation: Initial standardization is performed using the built-in algorithms of the CiteSpace. Subsequently, for high-output authors and core network node authors, manual disambiguation is performed by cross-verifying institutional affiliation, collaborator network continuity, and research topic consistency. Institutional Name Standardization: Institution names are systematically unified. The process involved: extracting all institution names; applying rule-based processing using regular expressions to handle common abbreviations and word order variations; and finally, establishing an authoritative name mapping table for high-output and important institutions by consulting their official websites, then implementing replacements across the entire dataset. The above content has been updated in the revised version.

The study followed a general to detailed path, including an overview of countries/regions, institutions/authors, journal distribution, documents/references, keywords, and trends. Subsequently, the collected data were imported into Microsoft Office Excel Professional 2024 (Redmond WA, USA), Biblioshiny (R version 4.5.1), VOSviewer (version 1.6.20), Scimago Graphica (Version 1.0.41), and CiteSpace (version 6.3.R1) for further analysis.

VOSviewer was used to perform analyses on co-authorship of countries/ regions or institutions or authors and keyword co-occurrence analysis [11]. Each node represents an item, with its size reflecting either the number of publications or the frequency of keyword occurrence, and its color indicating the item’s classification. Additionally, the thickness of the line connecting two nodes signifies the strength of their relationship. Citespace and Biblioshiny were utilized to conduct co-citation analysis of journals, authors, and references, as well as to generate bibliographic record overlays for journals [12]. Excel was utilized to create basic statistical maps and generate fitted curves for future publication projections. eographic distribution maps were created using Scimago Graphica.

Statistical processing

The study presents the current state of research on BMPCa by examining absolute figures, trends, and the distribution of changes. It does not include comparisons between different groups.

Results

Trends of publication outputs

Through screening, 5512 BMPCa publications were acquired from the WOS core collecion database, 4350 articles and 1162 reviews included. The publications came from 27,851 authors in 5,929 institutions across 95 countries, published in 1,011 journals, and cites 130,557 articles from 12,632 journals.

The annual number of published papers serves as a important metric for assessing the advancement of scientific research. Annual publications increased from 192 in 2004 to 322 in 2023 and peaking in 2021 with 408 papers, the cumulative number of publications shows a linear growth trend(R² = 0.9922), it maintained stable at over 300 papers annually. It is expected that more than 300 publications on BMPCa will be published in 2025. This indicates that BMPCa has more focus and hotspots worth exploring, with a promising research prospect. Keywords and trends

The keywords were analyzed by trend topic in Biblioshiny (Fig. 5C). The top three by frequency of occurrence were “prostate cancer” (1740), “bone metastasis” (973), and “bone metastases” (779). Biblioshiny analysis of keywords shows that “arificial intelligence” (2022–2024), “machine learning” (2020–2024), and “ga-68“(2017–2024) may represent the frontiers of this research area.

The abstracts were analyzed using the trend topic in Biblioshiny (Fig. 5D).The top three by frequency of occurrence were “castration-resistant prostate cancer” (886), “prostate cancer cells” (760), and “prostate cancer pca” (707). Biblioshiny analysis of abstracts shows that “pca lung metastases” (2024–2024), “cauda equina compression” (2021–2024), and “external validation cohort” (2022–2023) may represent the frontiers of this research area.

Figure 6B displays the top 25 keywords with the strongest citation bursts, which are intended to reflect research hotspots and trends. Among them, “skeletal complications (2007–2014, intensity 40.69)” was the keyword with the strongest burst intensity. The keywords, such as “microenvironment” (burst duration from 2020 to 2024), “biochemical recurrence” (2019–2024), “risk” (2019–2024) and “enzalutamide” 2018–2024)have recently garnered significant interest, underscoring their status as prevalent research topics in recent years and likely into the foreseeable future.

Discussion

BMPCa has high morbidity and mortality, especially, patients with spinal cord injuries have extremely poor quality of life. It is necessary to analyze the related research to understand the overall situation of the current related research.Biliometric analysis, as a well-established tool, has been widely applied to evaluate the strengths and maturity of specific fields [15, 16]. We have carried out a comprehensive analysis of BMLC-related studies since 2007 to 2024 in the WoSCC database and the visualization of the international research status in this field, including quantity of publications in every year, collaboration from different countries, published journals, the distribution of authors and institutes, and keywords analysis, through bibliometrics to understand the current status and frontiers in the field of BMPCa to provide a reference for researchers to better grasp the development trend and emerging research hotspots.

The volume of publications serves as a critical indicator of trends within a research field [17]. According to data from the WoSCC database on October 15, 2025, a total of 5,512 articles associated with BMPCa have been published between 2007 and 2024, This study results indicate that the annual number of publications showed a linear growth trend, and this aligns with the global upward trend in new cancer cases [18], it suggested that the BMPCa research was developing rapidly and attracted more attention in the global medical community.

The number of citations is one of the indicators reflecting the quality of a paper. The H-index is a widely used parameter to quantify and standardize researchers’ scientific impact [19]. This study analysis found that the top 5 productive countries (USA, China, England, Germany, and Italy) made the majority of publications, while the top productive institutions were all from these countries. USA contributed most in this field with the highest total publications and with a higher H-index. This indicates a high level of research quality in the USA, while, the USA plays a central role in the cooperation between countries/regions. Europe and other developed countries cooperate closely, while developing countries cooperate sparsely, as shown in Fig. 3B. The significant advancements depended on their rapid progress in economy, academy, and the medical technology. Therefore, we should strengthen international cooperation to help developing countries.

Articles of BMPCa are most often published in Prostate, followed by Cancers and European Journal of Nuclear Medicine And Molecular Imaging. As a high-quality, influential journal in the field, Clinical Cancer Research has the highest impact factor of 10.2 and European Journal of Nuclear Medicine And Molecular Imaging is the most cited journal with 5993. The journals on the list of Table 4 will likely be the significant publication outlets in the field in the future. The most cited paper states that fractures are associated with an increased risk of death in patients with malignant bone disease [13] and in this study, “skeletal complications (2007–2014, intensity 40.69)” was the keyword with the strongest burst intensity. Therefore, prevention of fractures is a critical objective of treatment. Nitrogen-containing bisphosphonates, a critical drugs for BMPCa, provides significant benefits to patients with bone metastases by preventing skeletal complications and alleviating bone pain [20]. It can help patients maintain their activity capacity and functional independence, and reduce the risk of death due to complications from fractures [21]. Zoledronic acid, is a type of Nitrogen-containing bisphosphonates, by inhibiting osteoclast activity, reducing bone resorption, and lowering the risk of SREs. It also exerts anti-tumor effects by inhibits tumor cell functions, including the adhesion, migration, invasion and proliferation, as well as the adhesion of tumor cells to the bone matrix [22, 23].

In this study, biblioshiny analysis of keywords shows that “arificial intelligence” (2022–2024), “machine learning” (2020–2024), and “ga-68“(2017–2024) may represent the frontiers of this research area. “Artificical intelligence” has become a research hotspot in this field. Manual detection through imaging modalities such as CT, MRI, PET, and bone scintigraphy can be costly and inefficient. Preliminary artificial intelligence (AI) techniques and Computer-assisted detection systems have attempted to improve lesion detection, segmentation, and treatment response in oncological imaging, and demonstrated the advantages of AI in multimodal imaging techniques [24–26]. Combining the treatments, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, targeted therapy, immunotherapy, and multidisciplinary comprehensive treatment, with artificial intelligence to provide personalized plan for patients, thereby achieving better outcomes [27, 28].

In recent years, the emergence of advanced radiotherapy techniques—including stereotactic radiotherapy and multidisciplinary oncology management has enabled the integration of radiotherapy with novel systemic treatments such as targeted therapy and immunotherapy [29–32]. This combined approach enhances patient quality of life more effectively, promotes remission, and significantly extends survival while reducing treatment-related toxicity [33]. Adjuvant chemotherapy, surgical intervention, and phosphonate-targeted therapy have also enhanced therapeutic outcomes [34, 35].

Limitations

In this study, we conducted a comprehensive and systematic bibliometric analysis of literature on BMPCa. However, this study has some limitations. First, the study only included the WOS core collection database, which inevitably led to the omission of other important related literature. Second, non-English publications were excluded in this study, which may have led to selection bias. Finally, some newly published articles of high quality may have not had enough time to receive much attention and be cited than classic papers.

Conclusion

This study conducted a comprehensive and systematic bibliometric analysis of literature to reveal research status and trend of the field of BMPCa. The results showed that a linear growth trend in the number of publications, and the United States has made significant contributions to BMPCa both in quantity and in collaboration. International cooperation in BMPCa research needs to be further strengthened. Prostate was the most prolific jounal. Collaboration between authors, organizations, and countries/regions should be constantly strengthened. We hope that more researchers and organizations can promote academic exchanges and strengthen cooperation to continue addressing the gaps of BMPCa. Further research should focus on the pathogenesis, early prevention, and the integration of individualized treatment with artificial intelligence, in order to improve patient survival and quality of life.

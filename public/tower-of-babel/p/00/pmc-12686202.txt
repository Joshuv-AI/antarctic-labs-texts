Impact of single-site distant metastases on prognosis in advanced ovarian cancer: a Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results population-based study
Authors: Zhanna Yuan, Peiqin Shi, Wenwen Zhang, Pengpeng Qu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundLate-stage distant metastasis is the primary cause of death and poor prognosis in patients with ovarian cancer. This study aims to evaluate the association between patterns of single distant metastases and survivals in advanced ovarian cancer, and to identify the prognostic factors for site-specific distant metastases.MethodsData collected between 2010 and 2016 were obtained from the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) database. Kaplan-Meier survival curves and Cox regression were used to analyze overall survival (OS) and disease-specific survival (DSS). Subgroup and interaction analyses were performed to assess the effects on survival and prognosis. Floating absolute risk (FAR) methods and propensity score matching (PSM) analyses were used to reduce confounding bias.ResultsA total of 639 patients were included in this study. The most prevalent single distant metastatic site was the liver (n=338; 52.9%), followed by the lungs (n=267; 41.8%), bones (n=29; 4.5%), and brain (n=5; 0.8%), with median survival times of 31, 25, 25, and 19 months, respectively. Through univariate and multifactorial analyses, we found that serous cancer, surgery, chemotherapy, and liver metastasis were independent factors influencing the prognosis of ovarian cancer. Compared to other metastatic sites, patients with liver metastasis exhibited significantly better OS [hazard ratio (HR) =0.79, 95% confidence interval (CI): 0.64–0.98, P=0.03], which remained consistent even after the application of FAR (HR =0.79, 95% CI: 0.62–1.02, P=0.03) and PSM (HR =0.77, 95% CI: 0.62–0.96, P=0.02). Similar results were observed in the DSS. Additionally, subgroup analysis and interaction tests revealed a significant interaction between the serous histological type and liver metastasis (P=0.007).ConclusionsAmong patients with single-site distant metastasis of advanced ovarian cancer, the prognosis varies significantly depending on the specific metastatic site. Liver metastasis is associated with better outcomes, providing new evidence for clinical diagnosis and individualized treatment strategies.Keywords: Ovarian cancer; single distant metastases; propensity score matching (PSM); Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results database (SEER database)

Highlight box

Key findings

• Among ovarian cancer patients with single-site distant metastasis, prognosis varies significantly depending on the specific metastatic site, with liver metastasis associated with better outcome.

• The histological type of serous, surgical treatment, chemotherapy and liver metastasis were independent risk factors influencing the prognosis of ovarian cancer.

• Subgroup analysis and interaction tests revealed that the histological type of serous was associated with better prognosis in liver metastasis.

What is known and what is new?

• Distant metastasis plays a crucial role in the morbidity and mortality of ovarian cancer.

• Head-to-head comparative studies on single-site distant metastasis showed that the histological type of serous, surgical treatment, chemotherapy and liver metastasis were independent risk factors influencing the prognosis of ovarian cancer. Meanwhile, the histological type of serous was associated with better prognosis in liver metastasis.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• The study provided new evidence for clinical diagnosis and individualized treatment, contributing to prognosis assessment and resource allocation. Clinical practice should consider the optimized treatment strategies for advanced ovarian cancer patients.

Introduction

Ovarian cancer is the most lethal gynecological malignancy and the fifth leading cause of cancer-related deaths among women. Approximately 90% of cases are epithelial ovarian cancer (EOC). Although there has been some progress in ovarian cancer treatment, the 5-year overall survival (OS) rate of patients with ovarian cancer is still less than 50% (1). Approximately 60% of ovarian cancers have distant metastases, while only 10% metastasize to a single site (2). Reportedly, the most common single metastasis in EOC was liver (4.65%), followed by lung (4%), and single bone and brain metastasis (0.39% and 0.10%, respectively) (3). Metastases are a major cause of mortality in ovarian cancer patients, and the 5-year OS rate is only 29% (4), but the specific influence of different metastatic sites on ovarian cancer prognosis remains underexplored, especially for advanced cases. The metastasis site may affect treatment strategies and directly impact survival. Thus, exploring the effects of distant metastatic sites on advanced ovarian cancer patients is clinically important. Previous studies have shown that the prognosis of liver and lung metastases is notably affected by pathological types and surgical modalities, which are considered as independent prognostic factors of metastases (3). However, prognostic factors for metastases at other sites are still unclear, and head-to-head comparative studies on prognosis of single metastasis in advanced ovarian cancer are lacking. Given that distant metastasis plays a crucial role in the morbidity and mortality of ovarian cancer (5-7), an in-depth study of metastatic patterns and their specific effects on patient prognosis could provide a new foundation for optimizing treatment strategies and improving patient survival. Therefore, exploring the prognostic differences in patients with advanced ovarian cancer at different metastatic sites (such as the lung, liver, bone, and brain) needs to be urgently addressed. In this article, we aimed to evaluate the association between patterns of single distant metastases and survivals in advanced ovarian cancer, and to identify the prognostic factors for site-specific distant metastases. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2025-1341/rc).

Methods

Data sources and cohort selection

The data analyzed in the current study were extracted from the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) public database using SEER*Stat software (version 8.3.8; https://seer.cancer.gov/data-software/documentation/seerstat/), an authoritative public source of information on cancer incidence, mortality, prevalence, lifetime risk statistics, and survival in the United States. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. Patients with ovarian cancer were included on the basis of the following criteria: the site recode International Classification of Diseases for Oncology (ICD-O) was “ovary”; the behavior code ICD-O-3 was “malignant”; and EOC was histologically diagnosed. The exclusion criteria were as follows: patients with incomplete clinical or demographic information; unknown survival time or survival time equal and shorter than 1-month; American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) tumor-node (T.N) stage I–III; multiple metastatic organs. Patients diagnosed with stage IV ovarian cancer between 2010 and 2016 were identified, including histology codes 8020, 8070, 8310, 8380, 8460, 8461, 8480, and 9000, and a flowchart of the subject selection is shown in Figure 1. Notably, the SEER did not incorporate data on distant metastatic sites until 2010 and SEER prohibited Chinese users from accessing it in April 2025.

Flowchart of the advanced stage of ovarian cancer patients selection. AJCC, American Joint Committee on Cancer; T.N, tumor-node.

The demographic variables included age, race, marital status, diagnosis time, histological type, pathological grade, tumor size, surgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, number of tumorigeneses, and laterality. Metastatic sites were grouped into four major organs: the lung, liver, bone, and brain, which were extracted from the SEER database and studied as primary variables in the analysis. The primary outcome variables were OS and disease-specific survival (DSS). Survival was calculated from the date of the patient’s diagnosis until the date of death or survival status review. OS included all causes of death, whereas DSS included only deaths due to ovarian cancer.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using R (version 3.5.1, https://www.r-project.org/), with a two-sided P<0.05 deemed statistically significant. Kaplan-Meier curves were used to estimate OS and DSS in different groups, and differences between curves were assessed using the log-rank test. Univariate Cox regression analysis was performed to assess the association between individual covariates and OS/DSS. Multivariate Cox regression analysis was performed to evaluate the independent prognostic factors associated with OS/DSS by estimating hazard ratios (HRs) and their corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs). For comparisons involving multiple groups, 95% CIs were calculated using the floating absolute risk (FAR) method. This method enables valid comparisons between any pair of groups, rather than limiting comparisons solely to a reference group, thereby minimizing bias associated with the arbitrary selection of control groups (8). For significant variables related to metastatic sites, propensity score matching (PSM) was used to minimize confounding biases. Matching factors included sex, age, stage, and other covariates (core parameters, 1:1 matching ratio; caliper value, 0.05; method, nearest neighbor; R package, “MatchIt”). A chi-square test indicated that P≥0.05 and standardized mean difference (SMD) <0.1 suggested an even balance between the two groups. Subgroup analysis and interaction tests were conducted to evaluate the impact of solitary metastasis on survival and prognosis across various covariate subgroups as well as their interactions with each subgroup.

Results

Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics

In this study, data from 58,548 ovarian cancer patients were obtained from the SEER database between 2010 and 2016. After excluding patients diagnosed without metastasis (n=50,131) and those with multiple metastases (n=7,778), the final cohort consisted of 639 patients with single metastasis of advanced ovarian cancer who were included in the analysis. Eligible patients were categorized into four groups according to their metastatic sites: bone (n=29; 4.54%), brain (n=5; 0.78%), liver (n=338; 52.9%), and lungs (n=267; 41.78%). The demographic and clinicopathological characteristics are summarized in Table 1. Significant differences (P<0.05) were observed among the four groups across three key characteristics: histological type, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy.

Impact of site-specific distant metastases on survival outcome

Median survival times for patients with advanced ovarian cancer and a single site of distant metastasis were 31, 25, 25, and 19 months for patients with liver, lung, bone, and brain metastases, respectively. The Kaplan-Meier curve indicated that different metastatic sites were significantly associated with OS (P=0.02) (Figure 2A). Similar outcomes were observed for DSS, with a median survival of 40 months for liver and bone metastases, as opposed to 29 months for lung and 19 months for brain metastases (P=0.04) (Figure 2B).

Kaplan-Meier curves for OS (A) and DSS (B) in different metastatic sites of advanced ovarian cancer. Different colors represent different metastatic sites. DSS, disease-specific survival; Met., metastasis; OS, overall survival.

Prognostic factors for patients with advanced ovarian cancer were further studied using univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses. The univariate Cox proportional hazard model showed that black, marriage status (divorced/separated), histological type of serum, tumor size, surgery, chemotherapy, number of tumorigenesis, and unilateral tumor were statistically significant and associated with OS. For most of these, liver metastases (vs. lung metastases) was significantly associated with increased OS (HR =0.81, 95% CI: 0.66–0.99, P=0.04), while bone (HR =1.13, 95% CI: 0.70–1.81, P=0.62) and brain metastases (HR =0.65, 95% CI: 0.16–2.61, P=0.54) were not. Multivariate Cox analysis indicated that ovarian cancer with single distant liver metastasis was an independent prognostic factor for OS (HR =0.79, 95% CI: 0.64–0.98, P=0.03). Race/black, divorced/separated, histological types of serous cancer, surgery and chemotherapy were also independent factors affecting the prognosis of ovarian cancer. It is worth noting that serous cancer, surgery, chemotherapy, and liver metastasis were associated with better prognosis in patients with ovarian cancer (Table 2). Similar results were observed in the DSS (Table 3). To minimize the potential bias arising from the arbitrary selection of controls, analyses conducted using the FAR method produced results consistent with those obtained from multivariate Cox regression analyses for both OS and DSS (Table 4).

Subgroup analysis and interaction testing from liver metastases for OS/DSS in patients with advanced ovarian cancer

To explore possible interactions, subgroup analyses and interaction testing of metastatic sites were conducted (liver vs. other metastases; Figure 3). In the subgroup analysis by race, it was observed that ovarian cancer patients with liver metastases exhibited worse prognoses in the white cohort (HR =1.32, 95% CI: 1.05–1.65, P=0.02), as well as the diagnostic time of 2010–2012, histological type of serous, tumor grades III–IV, without radiotherapy, chemotherapy, number of 1 lesion, and bilateral (P<0.05). Although liver metastases were not significantly associated with OS in the age, marital, tumor size and surgery groups (all P>0.05), this suggests that the impact of liver metastases on OS in advanced ovarian cancer is consistent across these categories. Additionally, only the histological type of serous ovarian cancer was a significant interacting factor with liver metastasis (HR =1.74, 95% CI: 1.13–2.69, P=0.01) (Figure 3A). In the subgroup analysis with DSS as an outcome, the histological type of serous was identified as a risk factor influencing the prognosis of liver metastasis (HR =1.55, 95% CI: 1.19–2.00, P<0.001). Interaction testing indicated that serous ovarian cancer significantly affected the relationship between the metastatic status and DSS (HR =2.24, 95% CI: 1.36–3.69, P=0.002) (Figure 3B).

PSM between liver and lung/bone/brain metastases in advanced ovarian cancer: clinical features and survival outcomes

Considering the limited number of cases involving single brain and bone metastases from ovarian cancer, we combined these with cases of single lung metastases and subsequently performed a 1:1 PSM analysis with cases of single liver metastases from ovarian cancer. PSM was used to correct for confounding factors between the groups, including age, race, marital status, diagnosis time, histological type, pathological grade, tumor size, surgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, number of tumorigenesis, and laterality. Finally, a cohort of 248 sample size pairs was generated. The characteristic differences between the matched groups were indicated by the SMD and P values (Table S1,S2). As we expected, following PSM matching, the data distribution between the groups became homogeneous (Figure 4). The histogram exhibits strong symmetry between the liver metastasis and lung/bone/brain metastasis groups after matching, indicating a well-balanced distribution of covariates between the two groups.

Survival analysis of advanced ovarian cancer after PSM

After PSM, liver metastases were still more strongly correlated with prognosis than other metastases (HR =0.75, 95% CI: 0.60–0.93, P=0.009), consistent with the findings prior to PSM. Multivariate Cox analysis for advanced ovarian cancer patients further indicated that race (Black), marital status (divorced/separated), histological type (serous), surgery, chemotherapy, and the presence of distant liver metastases were independent prognostic factors affecting ovarian cancer outcomes (HR =0.77, 95% CI: 0.62–0.96, P=0.02) (Table 5). These results corroborate those obtained before PSM. Similar trends were observed for DSS (Table 6).

Subgroup analysis and interaction testing between PSM-adjusted liver metastases and other metastatic sites

Subgroup analysis was performed for post-PSM patients. As opposed to before PSM, ovarian cancer liver metastasis was identified as a significant protective factor (HR =0.64, 95% CI: 0.44–0.95, P=0.03) in individuals aged 41–60 years, as well as race (White), marital status (married), diagnostic period [2010–2012], tumor grade (III–IV), tumor size (≤19 cm), surgery, chemotherapy, and bilateral involvement. However, no significant interaction effects were detected between the variables. Among the histological types of serous ovarian cancer, liver metastasis was recognized as a significant protective factor for prognosis (HR =0.63; 95% CI: 0.49–0.82; P<0.001) vs. nonserous (HR =1.24; 95% CI: 0.81–1.91; P=0.32). Interaction analyses further revealed that patients with liver metastases had improved prognoses, specifically those with serous ovarian cancer (HR =0.51; 95% CI: 0.31–0.83; P=0.007) (Figure 5A). Discussion

Late-stage distant metastasis remains the primary cause of EOC incidence and mortality. Most clinical discussions have focused on the impact of multiple metastatic sites on prognosis. However, this study pioneered exploring prognostic differences among single-site metastases to the liver, lung, bone, and brain in advanced ovarian cancer. The study aimed to evaluate the association between patterns of single-site distant metastases and survival outcomes in advanced ovarian cancer, and to identify prognostic factors specific to each metastatic site. Liver metastasis was the most prevalent site, almost two-thirds of sites, followed by lung metastasis. Single bone and brain metastases accounted for notably lower percentages of cases. These findings align with previous studies regarding the distribution of distant metastatic sites (6,9). Gardner et al. (10) observed a higher 5-year survival rate (34%) in patients with liver metastases from solitary ovarian cancer, compared to 20.4%, 15%, and 10% for lung, brain, and bone metastases, respectively. Deng et al. (11) reported median survival times of 30, 26, 7, and 11 months for isolated metastases to the liver, lungs, bone, and brain, respectively. In our study, median survival times for single liver, lung, bone, and brain metastases were 31, 25, 25, and 19 months, respectively. These times for liver and lung metastases aligned with previous figures, while discrepancies in bone and brain metastases were attributed to low incidence and small case numbers.

Studies have identified various factors affecting prognosis in advanced ovarian cancer. Clinical models have highlighted age, race, tumor stage, histological type, pathological grade, cancer antigen 125 (CA125) levels, liver metastasis, surgical treatment, and chemotherapy as main factors. Surgical treatment, chemotherapy, histological type, and liver metastasis have been identified as independent prognostic factors (12,13). Cox regression analyses revealed serous ovarian cancer, surgery, chemotherapy, and liver metastasis as independent prognostic factors with better prognosis. This study used HRs and FAR to show that distinct metastatic sites have prognostic implications. Ovarian cancer with single liver metastasis demonstrated a better OS. PSM reduced information bias in both the liver metastasis group and the control group, confirming the improved OS associated with liver metastasis and reinforcing the study’s credibility.

Surgery is the most important factor affecting the prognosis of patients with advanced ovarian cancer, especially for patients with metastasis who can significantly benefit, as confirmed by several studies (14-16). Therefore, this is a reminder that ovarian cancer with metastasis is not a reason to forgo surgery if the patient can tolerate it and that tumor debulking surgery is the standard surgical approach, especially if R0 resection can be achieved (17,18). Preoperative neoadjuvant or postoperative combination with platinum-based chemotherapy and PARPi-based targeted maintenance therapy are important factors influencing ovarian cancer prognosis. Prospective clinical studies show that postoperative chemotherapy combined with PARPi-based targeted therapy significantly improves median and OS in ovarian cancer patients. This confirms that surgery and chemotherapy are independent factors affecting advanced ovarian cancer prognosis.

Serous ovarian cancer accounts for 70–80% of advanced cases. High-grade serous ovarian cancer, the most prevalent histological subtype of EOC, is commonly diagnosed in advanced stages with widespread peritoneal metastases, leading to poor prognosis (19). One large sample study found that the incidence of bone metastases was higher in non-serous cancers (20). Another study found a higher incidence of brain metastases in non-serous cancers (21). In lung metastasis of ovarian cancer, the incidence of serous cancer is higher than that of non-serous cancer (22), and another study found that serous ovarian cancer was more prone to liver metastases than non-serous ovarian cancer (23). In this study, we confirmed that serous ovarian cancer is an independent factor affecting prognosis in single metastasis, and further verified that serous ovarian cancer with liver metastasis has a better prognosis in subgroup analysis and interaction experiment. This indicates why serous ovarian cancer with liver metastasis has a better prognosis than ovarian cancer.

Epidemiological investigations show that the liver is the most common organ for distant metastasis of advanced ovarian cancer, accounting for 37–57% (3) and studies have found that up to 50% of ovarian cancer patients die from liver metastasis, making it a major prognostic factor for ovarian cancer (23,24). In this study, we found liver metastasis was an independent factor affecting ovarian cancer prognosis, and subgroup analysis and interaction tests revealed it had a worse prognosis. Considering this study was retrospective and confounding factors could lead to statistical bias, the PSM method was adopted to reduce information bias in both the liver metastasis group and the control group (lung/bone/brain metastases). After PSM, we confirmed liver metastasis was an independent prognostic factor. However, subgroup analysis and interaction tests showed ovarian cancer patients with a single liver metastasis had a better prognosis compared to metastasis at other sites, indicating a protective factor. This further demonstrates that advanced ovarian cancer patients with single liver metastasis have a better prognosis, providing new therapeutic evidence for clinical diagnosis and treatment.

The choice of treatment method plays a crucial role in guiding clinical practice (25,26). Various treatment methods exist for liver metastases in ovarian cancer, including hepatic resection, radiofrequency ablation (RFA), transcatheter arterial chemoembolization (TACE), and systemic chemotherapy and targeted therapy. Modes of ovarian cancer liver metastasis include peritoneal dissemination, hematogenous metastasis, and lymph node metastasis (LNM), with peritoneal dissemination being the most common (27). The treatment plans for metastatic liver lesions caused by different modes of metastasis are different, and the impact of different treatment plans on prognosis is also different. Merideth et al. (28) reported that 65% of patients with a single liver metastasis from ovarian cancer underwent lesion resection, whereas Niu et al. (29) reported that 60% of patients with multiple liver metastases from ovarian cancer underwent resection of the liver metastatic lesions. Complete resection of liver metastases can improve the disease-free survival (DFS) and OS of patients, and laparoscopic resection of abdominal lesions, including the liver, is feasible and safe, benefiting patients (23,24,30,31). It has been found that 2–12.5% of patients with ovarian cancer have unresectable hepatic parenchymal metastases. Local treatments such as RFA or hepatic artery embolization are also new treatment options for patients with unresectable ovarian cancer liver metastases with multiple metastatic lesions or deep-seated metastases, which can significantly improve OS (27,32-34).

There are several strengths in this study. First, it compared prognostic differences among single lung, liver, bone, and brain metastases in EOC, evaluating key factors influencing prognosis through subgroup analysis and interaction tests, providing a more accurate basis for clinical treatment plans. Second, it used floating risk of different metastatic sites to quantify their effect on prognosis. Third, using PSM controlled confounding factors, yielding more realistic and effective results. However, limitations exist. First, the SEER database primarily includes Caucasian patients, potentially introducing racial heterogeneity. The variables related to death risk in metastatic ovarian cancer patients were not comprehensive, excluding specific surgical methods, complications, chemotherapy and targeted therapy regimens, BRCA gene mutations, and other laboratory indicators. Second, LNM was not included in the analyses, although it’s common in ovarian cancer. Third, the small number of bone and brain metastasis cases reduced statistical power. Finally, as a retrospective study, potential biases are inevitable despite PSM. Future studies should include multiethnic data, collect more comprehensive clinical information, and analyze bone and brain metastases in detail. Prospective studies and multicenter collaborations should be strengthened to improve statistical power and mitigate potential bias. Molecular markers and imaging omics data should be combined to optimize personalized treatment strategies for metastatic ovarian cancer.

Conclusions

This study systematically evaluated the effects of single distant metastatic sites on advanced ovarian cancer prognosis using the SEER database. Among patients with single-site distant metastasis, prognosis varies significantly depending on the specific metastatic site, with liver metastasis associated with better outcome. These findings provide new evidence for clinical diagnosis and individualized treatment, contributing to prognosis assessment and resource allocation. Future studies should explore biological mechanisms of different metastatic patterns and validate these results with prospective studies to optimize treatment strategies for advanced ovarian cancer patients.

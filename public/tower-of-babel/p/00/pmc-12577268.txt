Comparative clinical efficacy of hybrid EMR-hot avulsion versus thermal ablation for post-ESD residual margins (≤ 1 cm) in early gastric cancer: a preliminary study
Authors: Lei Zhang, Nana An, Xiuli Zheng, Wenqian Ma, Juntao Lu, Limian Er
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe management of positive lateral margins (pLMs) after endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD) to treat early gastric cancer (EGC) remains controversial. We implemented a sequential therapeutic protocol for post-ESD pLM management; this protocol involved progressing from argon plasma coagulation (APC) and hot biopsy forceps-mediated avulsion to the hybrid endoscopic mucosal resection-hot avulsion (EMR-HA) technique.This study compared the outcomes of EMR-HA with those of thermal ablation (hot avulsion/APC) in the management of residual tumors measuring ≤ 1 cm with pLMs after gastric ESD, and both recurrence prevention and therapeutic efficacy were assessed.MethodsIn a retrospective cohort study (2015–2023) of 1,601 ESD procedures, 92 cases of pLMs with residual tumors that had maximum diameters ≤ 1 cm were analyzed. The therapeutic modalities were stratified as follows: hot avulsion (n = 25), APC (n = 41), and EMR-HA (n = 26). Statistical analyses were performed with one-way ANOVA, chi-square tests or Fisher’s exact tests, as well as Kaplan‒Meier analysis.ResultsEMR-HA resulted in a lower recurrence rate of 11.5% (3/26; median follow-up of 63 months, IQR 41–66), showing superior efficacy to both hot avulsion (44% recurrence, 11/25; P = 0.013) and APC (36.6% recurrence, 15/41; P = 0.046). The thermal ablation cohort exhibited significantly shorter median recurrence intervals (hot avulsion: 20 months; APC: 18 months) than the negative lateral margin cohort (32 months; P < 0.05). Among the 42 patients who experienced recurrence, complete resection was achieved via re-ESD in 84.6% of patients (22/26), with a median disease-free survival time of 34.5 months (IQR 19.2–56).ConclusionsEMR-HA demonstrates clinically superior recurrence control compared to thermal ablation techniques, with extended therapeutic durability and reduced reintervention rates. The findings suggest its potential clinical utility as an endoscopic treatment option. Early gastric cancer (EGC) is defined as a malignant lesion that is confined to the submucosal layer without lymph node metastasis (LNM). Continuous advances in endoscopic resection (ER) techniques have significantly improved curative resection (CR) rates. However, residual neoplasia may persist when positive lateral margins (pLMs) are observed after endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD) to treat EGC [1–4], and substantial evidence has shown that pLMs are critical determinants of local recurrence [2, 5, 6].

Current clinical protocols mandate adjuvant interventions for patients with pLMs, including surgical resection, re-ER, and argon plasma coagulation (APC). Clinical experience and published evidence consistently demonstrate that residual neoplasms after ESD predominantly manifest as small lesions (≤ 1 cm), with a study reporting a mean residual tumor diameter of 0.6 cm in incompletely resected cases [1]. APC is favored clinically because of its technical ease and safety [7]; however, this approach lacks histopathological capability and has mixed therapeutic outcomes [5, 7–14]. Since 2015, we have focused on treating residual tumors ≤ 1 cm via three approaches: APC, histopathologically evaluable hot avulsion, and the hybrid endoscopic mucosal resection-hot avulsion (EMR-HA) technique.

This study compared the outcomes of EMR-HA with those of thermal ablation (hot avulsion/argon plasma coagulation [APC]) for managing residual tumors measuring ≤ 1 cm with pLMs after gastric ESD, and both recurrence prevention and therapeutic efficacy were assessed.

Methods

Study population

From January 2017 to December 2023, 1,601 consecutive EGC patients who underwent initial ESD at our tertiary referral center were screened.

The exclusion criterion was as follows.

Primary exclusion criteria (n = 1,485): 1,404 cases: negative horizontal/vertical margins (including 13 patients who exhibited negative recurrence at the lateral margin after ESD); 76 cases: positive vertical margins or submucosal invasion (> 500 μm); and 5 cases: incomplete clinicopathological documentation.

Secondary exclusion criteria for the residual cohort (n = 116): 9 cases: patient declined additional treatment; and 15 cases: patient underwent completion gastrectomy (including 7 patients with pLMs with residual tumors > 1 cm).

Final Cohort (n = 92): Patients with residual tumors ≤ 1 cm demonstrating isolated pLMs as the exclusive noncurative criterion underwent the following interventions: hot avulsion (n = 25), APC (n = 41), and hybrid endoscopic mucosal resection-hot avulsion technique (EMR-HA, n = 26) (Fig. 1).

ESD technique

The procedures involved the use of GIF-Q260J endoscopes (Olympus) with thulium knives under white light and magnifying NBI guidance. After circumferential marking (5 mm intervals), submucosal injection (methylene blue-hyaluronate solution) enabled en bloc resection. Hemostasis was achieved via the use of disposable hemostatic forceps (Micro-Tech, Nanjing). Specimens were sectioned at 2 mm intervals for histopathological evaluation, and pLMs were defined as high-grade intraepithelial neoplasia or cancer cells at the lateral specimen margins.

Technological evolution in pLM management

Our institutional protocol for positive lateral margins (pLMs) after early gastric cancer ESD has been significantly refined.

Ablative era (2015–2017)

Initial management of pLMs relied solely on argon plasma coagulation (APC) or hot avulsion, and we observed a high rate of recurrence at follow-up.

Follow-up protocol

Structured surveillance included endoscopic evaluation at 3 months (ulcer healing/residual tumor assessment), combined endoscopy/abdominal CT at 6 and 12 months, and annual comprehensive oncologic review thereafter.

Postrecurrence management protocol

Pathological confirmation via bite biopsy is the gold standard for the diagnosis of recurrence. When histological examination reveals high-grade intraepithelial neoplasia or adenocarcinoma, these findings are interpreted as indicative of disease recurrence. For cases of confirmed recurrence, our therapeutic approach primarily involves repeat ESD. Alternative modalities, such as hot avulsion or APC, are selectively utilized in specific clinical scenarios.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis was conducted with SPSS 21.0 software (IBM SPSS Statistics, IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY) for MS Windows. Categorical variables are presented as absolute numbers or percentages; continuous data are expressed as the means with their corresponding standard deviations; univariate analysis involved the use of one-way ANOVA, chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test for categorical data; survival curves were generated with the Kaplan‒Meier method, with between-group comparisons assessed via the log-rank test; and P values < 0.05 are considered statistically significant.

Results

Clinicopathological characteristics of the patients

Among the 1,601 consecutive EGC patients who underwent ESD, 116 (7.2%) exhibited pLMs. Ninety-two pLM patients with residual tumors ≤ 1 cm underwent therapeutic management via APC (n = 41, 44.5%), hot avulsion (n = 25, 27.2%), or EMR-HA (n = 26, 28.3%).

Statistical analysis via one-way ANOVA revealed no significant differences in residual tumor dimensions among the three treatment groups (F [2,89] = 1.13, p = 0.328), and all the groups showed similar median values (APC: 0.5 cm, IQR 0.3–0.9; hot avulsion: 0.5 cm, IQR 0.2–0.8; EMR-HA: 0.6 cm, IQR 0.4–0.8). However, comparative analysis revealed that, compared with APC and hot avulsion, EMR-HA significantly reduced the recurrence rates for pLMs (Table 1).

Therapeutic outcomes

In the thermal ablation groups (n = 66), the recurrence rates reached 39.3% (26/66), and comparable outcomes were observed following hot avulsion (44%, 11/25) and APC (36.6%, 15/41; P = 0.609). Kaplan–Meier analysis revealed significantly shorter median recurrence intervals in both intervention groups (hot avulsion: 20 months; APC: 18 months) than in the negative lateral margin (nLM) control group (32 months; log-rank P = 0.045 and P = 0.036, respectively), although no intermodal difference was observed (P = 0.945) (Figs. 4, 5 and 6).

Notably, among the 26 patients who underwent EMR-HA for pLMs, a lower recurrence rate of 11.5% was observed over a median follow-up time of 63 months (IQR: 41–66); thus, EMR-HA achieved statistically superior outcomes compared with hot avulsion and APC (11.5% vs. 44% recurrence, Fisher’s exact test, P = 0.013 and 11.5% vs. 36.6% recurrence, Fisher’s exact test, P = 0.046).

Natural history and salvage therapies

Among the 9 patients with untreated pLMs included in this study, 4 developed metachronous lesions: one lesion progressed to advanced carcinoma within 24 months, and 3 patients demonstrated recurrence during serial follow-up surveillance.

Recurrent disease management (n = 42) comprised: (1) Negative lateral margin (nLM) group (13 recurrences); (2) APC cohort (15 recurrences); (3) Hot avulsion cohort (11 recurrences); (4) EMR-HA cohort (3 recurrences). All recurrences occurred within 1 cm of the original resection site. Repeated ESD (re-ESD) achieved complete resection in 84.6% of cases (22/26), with a median disease-free survival of 34.5 months (IQR: 19.2–56). In contrast, APC (n = 4) and hot avulsion (n = 2) showed 50% efficacy (2/4 and 1/2, respectively). Three patients were lost to follow-up. Notable outcomes included four patients with re-ESD incomplete resection: one case showing 800 μm submucosal invasion after re-ESD was managed with surveillance; another case with residual tumor at the resection base received supplemental surgery; two cases demonstrating 2000 μm submucosal invasion underwent additional surgical intervention. One APC-treated patient who developed hepatic metastases (24 months) and a hot avulsion-treated patient who presented with concurrent rectal malignancy (Fig. 7).

Histopathological correlation

Pathological evaluation of samples from 38 treated patients revealed residual neoplasia in 68.0% (17/25) of the hot avulsion-treated patients and in 76.9% (20/26) of the EMR-HA-treated patients.

Discussion

The management of EGC with pLMs after ESD presents a complex clinical challenge that necessitates balancing oncologic efficacy with quality-of-life preservation. While surgical resection remains the definitive therapeutic option for complete tumor eradication, our institutional experience revealed that a majority of patients present with chronic reflux esophagitis, bile reflux gastritis, and persistent malnutrition postoperatively. This observation aligns with the evolving consensus that supports the use of endoscopic salvage strategies when pLMs exist as the sole noncurative factor, particularly given the documented risk of lymph node metastasis below 0.8% in such cases [10, 16].

Our therapeutic paradigm for small residual tumors (diameter ≤ 1.0 cm) has evolved through the sequential evaluation of adjunctive modalities. Between 2015 and 2017, APC was initially used because of its shorter procedure time, ease of operation, and lower incidence of complications [7], but this approach caused thermal artifacts that hindered histological analysis and residual tumors despite visual clearance. Multicenter data confirmed variable APC efficacy (25–93.2%) in the treatment of pLMs [3, 5]. This variability highlights the need for further investigation to validate the therapeutic effectiveness of APC in the treatment of pLMs and prompts the exploration of hot avulsion as a histology-preserving alternative.

Hot avulsion originating from Barrett’s esophagus (< 1 cm) [17] and nonlifting colorectal polyps (≤ 4 mm, 85% eradication) [18] results in superior outcomes compared to those of APC in the management of ≥ 2 cm colorectal adenomas (15% vs. 34% recurrence, P < 0.01) [19]; however, gastric application of hot avulsion for managing pLMs shows marked limitations. In our cohort, a recurrence rate of 44% (11/25) was observed following hot avulsion, which was not significantly different from the recurrence rate of 36.6% (15/41) that was observed in APC-treated patients (intermodal P = 0.609). The accelerated recurrence timeline observed in both intervention groups (hot avulsion: median 20 months; APC: 18 months) compared with patients with nLMs (32 months) reveals fundamental technical limitations. This therapeutic equipoise between modalities highlights their shared limitation; the possible reasons are as follows: (1) As demonstrated by Watson JP et al. [20], APC efficacy significantly depends on energy output parameters (40–99 W) and coagulation duration (1–3 s/mm²); additionally, fibrosis and mucosal thickening can be observed as early as 2 weeks post-ESD at ulcer beds [21] and achieving consistent energy delivery across irregular ulcer beds proves challenging. (2) The technical constraints of hot avulsion have become particularly evident in the difficulty of achieving seamless resection, which is attributable to its fixed bite depth (1.2–1.8 mm per grasp) and 6.7 mm jaw span, and residual tumor fragments are predominantly localized at the intergrasp junctions. (3) Cardia and antrum anatomy pose additional challenges, where tangential access significantly increases the degree of technical difficulty.

Driven by the suboptimal outcomes of conventional ablation modalities (APC recurrence rate: 36.6%; hot avulsion recurrence rate: 44%), our center pioneered the hybrid EMR-hot avulsion (EMR-HA) technique in 2018, representing the first documented integration of en bloc resection under NBI-enhanced magnifying endoscopy guidance with thermal ablation of resection margins in the management of pLMs. This technique has not been previously reported in the literature, particularly for managing pLMs with residual tumors measuring ≤ 1 cm following gastric ESD. At the median 63-month follow-up (longest 72 months), EMR-HA-treated pLM patients (n = 23/26) exhibited a recurrence-free survival rate of 88.5%, demonstrating complete tumor resection rates comparable to established benchmarks in residual tumor management reported by Japanese/Korean multicenter studies [1, 22, 23]. This finding suggests that the EMR-HA protocol exemplifies a paradigm-shifting integration of technical synergy and histopathological rigor, where its dual-modality approach (en bloc resection coupled with thermal margin refinement) not only eradicates neoplasia through mechanothermal synergistic clearance but also objectively validates the completeness of resection via circumferential margin-negative confirmation. In our series, only 1 minor bleeding event occurred (3.8%), which was easily treated with the same device; no other significant complications were encountered. The advantages of this approach are also reflected in its cost-effectiveness, with a total expenditure of 788 CNY for EMR-HA, which includes both procedure and equipment fees. This cost is significantly lower than that of ESD, which incurs a procedure fee of 1600 CNY and equipment fees totaling 2500 CNY.

The paradigm of recurrent early gastric cancer (EGC) management has been fundamentally reshaped by the demonstrated efficacy of repeat endoscopic submucosal dissection (re-ESD), which achieves curative outcomes in 84.6% of patients (22/26) with preserved gastric anatomy. Our institutional experience confirms the multinational consensus [3, 5, 10, 23, 24] establishing re-ESD as first-line therapy for localized recurrences, particularly in intramucosal lesions with submucosal fibrosis, which is better than the outcomes of ablative modalities (APC/hot avulsion: 50% success, 3/6 cases).

Notably, the 0.92% de novo recurrence rate (13/1404) in negative-margin patients marginally exceeds the benchmarks in the literature (0.2–0.88%) [5, 25, 26], which is attributed primarily to two late-onset cases (detected during 6-month postoperative evaluations) with concurrent microlesions adjacent to the primary resection margin that were undetected during initial resection but identified through subsequent histopathological analysis.

As a retrospective single-center investigation conducted at a tertiary hospital, this study has four primary limitations: (1) the small sample size, which may introduce analytical bias; (2) measurement inaccuracies inherent to NBI-magnification endoscopic assessment of residual tumor dimensions; (3) exclusive analysis of residual tumors with maximum diameters ≤ 1 cm, which leaves the efficacy of EMR-HA for lesions with maximum diameters > 1 cm undetermined; and (4) the absence of comparison with current standard therapeutic modalities.

Conclusions

EMR-HA demonstrates clinically superior recurrence control compared to thermal ablation techniques, with extended therapeutic durability and reduced reintervention rates. The findings suggest its potential clinical utility as an endoscopic treatment option. Additionally, re-ESD provides reliable salvage for recurrent lesions.

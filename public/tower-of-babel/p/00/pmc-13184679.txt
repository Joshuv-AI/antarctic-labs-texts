Liqi Huatan Huoxue Formula Rescued Myocardial Cell Apoptosis of Coronary Microvascular Dysfunction Rat by Inhibiting Endogenous Apoptosis Pathway
Authors: Zhao Ge, Qiuan Ren, Xianliang Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background: Coronary microvascular dysfunction (CMD) is a critical pathological condition linked to impaired myocardial perfusion and poor cardiovascular outcomes.Objectives: This study aimed to establish a CMD rat model and evaluate the therapeutic effects of the traditional Chinese medicine formula Liqi Huatan Huoxue (LQHTHX), with a focus on apoptosis, oxidative stress, and vascular endothelial function.Materials and Methods: CMD was induced in rats via intraventricular injection of sodium laurate (1 mL/kg, 1 g/L). The therapeutic efficacy of LQHTHX was assessed using hematoxylin-eosin (HE) and Masson staining, ELISA, biochemical assays, and TUNEL apoptosis assays. Western blotting (WB) and qRT-PCR were employed to evaluate the regulatory effects of LQHTHX on the NRG-1/ErbB signaling pathway and its downstream mitochondrial and endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress pathways.Results: LQHTHX treatment improved microvascular structure and reduced thrombus obstruction in CMD rats. It enhanced antioxidant capacity, restored vascular endothelial balance, and significantly reduced cardiomyocyte apoptosis. LQHTHX modulated the NRG-1/ErbB pathway and decreased the expression of pro-apoptotic proteins (Bax, Bad, Cyt-c, and Caspase-3), while increasing Bcl-2 levels. Furthermore, it suppressed ER stress-related apoptotic markers (CHOP, GRP78, and Caspase-12).Conclusions: LQHTHX ameliorates CMD by inhibiting apoptosis through mitochondrial and ER stress pathways, enhancing antioxidant defenses, and protecting vascular endothelial function.

1. Background

Coronary microvascular dysfunction (CMD) is a clinical syndrome in which structural and functional changes at the level of coronary microcirculation induce impaired coronary blood flow and, ultimately, myocardial ischemia ( 1 ). More than 60% of patients with coronary artery disease coexist with coronary microangiopathy. Previous clinical studies have shown that the prevalence of CMD in patients with symptoms of myocardial ischemia but non-obstructive coronary artery disease (NCAD) on coronary angiography is approximately 45% to 60% ( 2 ). CMD has now emerged as an important cause of poor prognosis in patients with cardiovascular disease ( 3 - 5 ).

Apoptosis is considered to be one of the leading causes of CMD, which promotes the CMD’s occurrence and development ( 7 ). Classical apoptotic pathways include the death receptor, mitochondrial, and endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress pathways ( 8 ). The Bcl-2, Bad, and Bax genes in the Bcl-2 family are the first step in mitochondrial apoptosis and the most critical regulatory genes known in apoptosis. Stimulation of apoptotic signals causes cytochrome c (Cyt-c) to be released from the mitochondria to the cytoplasm, activates caspase-9 precursor, activates caspase-3, and then triggers a cascade reaction of caspases to induce apoptosis ( 9 , 10 ). The ER stress (ERS) is a central part of the ER pathway to induce apoptosis. Glucose-regulated protein 78 (GRP78) and CCAAT enhancer binding protein (CHOP) are involved in the normal synthesis, folding, and transport of proteins in the ER. Moderate ERS plays a protective role for cells, whereas sustained or excessive ERS disrupts the homeostatic balance and inactivates their internal environmental defenses, triggering irreversible damage in CMD ( 11 ). All of the above evidence suggested that inhibition of apoptosis by multiple pathways was a potential therapeutic modality for CMD.

Currently, the prevalence of CMD is high, and there is a lack of effective therapeutic drugs and regimens. Chinese herbal medicine has been shown to be effective in the treatment of CMD. LQHTHX is based on Chaihu-Shugan-San with flexible modifications, additions and subtractions, resulting in a modified version of the drug consisting of 12 herbs for CMD treatment ( 17 ). LQHTHX consists of Bupleurum chinense DC. (Chaihu in Chinese, CH), Citrus trifoliate L. (Zhiqiao in Chinese, ZQ), Trichosanthes kirilowii Maxim. (Gualoupi in Chinese, GLP), Allium macrostemon Bunge (Xiebai in Chinese, XB), Salvia miltiorrhiza Bunge (Danshen in Chinese, DS), Conioselinum anthriscoides ‘Chuanxiong’ (Chuanxiong in Chinese, CX), Angelica sinensis (Oliv.) Diels (Danggui in Chinese, DG), Corydalis yanhusuo (Yanhusuo in Chinese, YHS), Carthamus tinctorius L. (Honghua in Chinese, HH), Smilax glabra Roxb (Fuling in Chinese, FL), Atractylodes macrocephala Koidz. (Baizhu in Chinese, BZ) and Citrus reticulate Blanco (Chenpi in Chinese, CP) comprised the Chinese herbal formula (According to http://mpns.kew.org, the plant name was checked on February 18 (th), 2024.).

LQHTHX contained a variety of chemical components such as naringin, hesperidin, neohesperidin, quercetin, kaempferide, and tanshinone IIA, which had hypotensive, lipid-modulating, and anti-apoptotic cardiovascular protective effects ( 18 - 20 ). Among them, saikosaponin-d (SS-d), one of the main components of CH, is a triple post saponin-like component. Recent studies have found that SS-d significantly inhibited apoptosis by increasing the level of Bcl-2 and significantly decreasing the expression levels of Cyt-c and Caspase proteins related to the mitochondrial apoptotic pathway ( 21 ). Chuanxiongzine, one of the main components of CX, could regulate mitochondrial dysfunction and protect against cysteine-induced apoptosis ( 22 ). The multi-target therapeutic mechanism and abundant pharmacological active ingredients make LQHTHX a potential candidate drug for CMD. However, the potential mechanism of LQHTHX in treating CMD is unknown.

2. Objectives

In this study, we aimed to investigate the effects of LQHTHX on microvascular thrombosis, endothelial function, and apoptosis mediated by the mitochondrial pathway and the ER stress pathway in CMD rats, to elucidate the therapeutic effects and possible mechanisms of LQHTHX on CMD.

3. Materials and Methods

3.1. Experimental Animals, Animal Model and Adminis-tration

The 2-month-old male SD rats (average weight 200 ± 20 g) were purchased from Beijing Specific Bio-Technology Co., Ltd (License No. SCXK (Beijing) 2019-0010). The experimental animals were routinely kept in the animal room of the Institute of Radiation Medicine, Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences (License No. SYXK (Jin) 2019-0002). They were housed at 20-24 °C, 55-65% humidity with 12-h light/dark cycles and given a normal diet. The feeding and operation of experimental animals were carried out in accordance with the guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals. The animal experimental protocol was approved by the Animal Ethical and Welfare Committee of the Institute of Radiation Medicine, Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences (the ethical number IRM-DWLL-2022226).

The rat CMD model was established by apical injection of sodium laurate 1 mL.kg-1 (concentration 1 g.L-1) ( 7 ). Rats were anesthetized by intraperitoneal injection of 1.5% tribromoethanol (8 mL.kg-1) and fixed in supine position. The subcutaneous muscle tissue was bluntly separated from the left chest of the rats, and the chest was opened between the 3rd and 4th ribs. After gradually exposing the apical part of the heart, a disposable insulin syringe was used to puncture the left ventricular cavity from the apical part of the heart, and sodium laurate solution (1 g.L-1, 1 mL.kg-1) was rapidly injected at a concentration of sodium laurate. At the same time, the junction of the ascending aorta and the aortic arch was clamped with an arterial clip for 10 s and then released to allow the injected sodium laurate reagent to enter the coronary artery smoothly. 80,000 units of penicillin were routinely given intramuscularly 3 days postoperatively to prevent infection. The Sham group was injected with an equal volume of saline. After 24 h of modeling, 5 rats were randomly executed and the hearts were extracted. Pathological sections were stained with HE staining, and thrombus formation was observed in the microvessels of rat coronary arteries, which was regarded as successful modeling.

Grouping and administration: CMD model rats were randomly divided into 6 groups: Model group, LQHTHX low-dose group (LQHTHX-L, 6.75 g.kg-1), LQHTHX medium-dose group (LQHTHX-M, 13.5 g.kg-1), LQHTHX high-dose group (LQHTHX-H, 27 g.kg-1), nicorandil group (Nic, 2.7 mg.kg-1) and valsartan group (Val, 14.4 mg.kg-1). The volume of drug gavage was 10 mg.kg-1 day-1 once a day for 4 weeks. The Control group, Sham group and Model group were given the same dose of 0.5% sodium carboxymethylcellulose (CMC-Na) by gavage. There were 8 rats in each group.

3.2. Drug Preparation

All the Chinese herbal medicines in our study were purchased from GUOQIANGZHONGYAO (Sichuan, China). The product lot numbers of the herbs are as follows: CH (230706), GLP (230301), XB (230601), ZQ (231014), FL (230901), BZ (230704), CP (230308), CX (230902), DG (230907), DS (230612), YHS (230715), and HH (230805). All herbs were authenticated by the corresponding author, confirmed to meet the requirements of the Chinese Pharmacopoeia 2020. CH 10 g, ZQ 12 g, GLP 15 g, XB 10 g, DS 15 g, DG 12 g, CX 10 g, YHS 15 g, HH 15 g, FL 12 g, BZ 12 g, CP 12 g were weighed. All herbs were soaked in distilled water for 1 h according to weight: volume= 1:10, and decocted for 30 min. The extract was filtered out and then extracted again. The two filtrates were combined and concentrated into an extract with a concentration of 2.7 g.mL-1. Prepare weekly and store at 4 °C for later use. All the herbs were purchased from the first affiliated hospital of Tianjin University of Traditional Chinese Medicine. And all the herbs were identified by the Pharmacy Department as conforming to the 2020 edition of the Chinese Pharmacopoeia. Nic (#H61022860, Xi’an Hanfeng Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., Xi’an, China) and Val (#H20183126, Zhejiang Huahai Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., Linhai, China) were formulated into suspensions with concentrations of 0.27 mg. mL-1 and 14.4 mg.kg-1 using 0.5% CMC-Na, respectively.

3.3. HPLC Analysis

For HPLC analysis, we followed the drug preparation method in 2.2. The weight to volume ratio was slightly different and was performed according to weight: volume= 1:15. Taking 300 μL extract and adding 700 μL methanol, centrifugation was done at 12000 rpm for 10 min and the supernatant was filtered by a 0.22 mm filter before analysis. The chemical composition of LQHTHX was analyzed by LC-2030C 3D HPLC (SHIMADZU, Japan). Chromatographic separation was performed on a phenomenex C18 column (250 mm × 4.6 mm, 5 μm) using two mobile phases: 0.1% phosphoric acid (A) and methanol (B). The gradient elution program was as follows: 0-20 min, 15-40% B; 20-80 min, 40-70% B; 80-100 min, 70-80% B at a flow rate of 1.0 mL.min-1, with the injection volume of 20 μL at 35 °C. Detection wavelength was 280 nm.

3.4. Morphologic Changes of Cardiomyocytes in Each Group Observed by Histological Examination (HE) Staining

The rats were euthanized; the hearts were rapidly removed and soaked in 4% paraformaldehyde for 24 h. After being dehydrated and transparent, paraffin embedding was performed, and the slices were sectioned at 5 μm. Then, deparaffinization and hydration, the slices were subjected to hematoxylin staining for 5 min, rinsing for 3 min, and staining with eosin for 1 min. Finally, the slices were sealed using a neutral gum. The pathological and morphological changes of cardiomyocytes were observed under a microscope (NIKON ECLIPSE E100, Japan).

3.5. Masson Staining

The dewaxed and hydrated paraffin sections were stained sequentially by hematoxylin and ponceau S staining solution. Aniline blue staining solution was used for re-staining. The sections were dehydrated, dried, sealed and observed by microscope. Collagen volume fraction (CVF) was calculated on Masson-stained sections using Image J image analysis software. CVF (%) = collagen area/total area ×100%.

3.6. Biochemical Analysis of Heart Tissue SOD And CAT Activity and NO, MDA and GSH Levels In Rats

Appropriate amount of rat heart tissue was taken, and the sample pretreatment was performed according to the instructions of the kit. Then SOD (#A001-3, Jiancheng, Nanjing, China) activity, CAT (#A007-1-1, Jiancheng, Nanjing, China) activity, nitric oxide (NO) (#A013-2-1, Jiancheng, Nanjing, China) concentration, MDA (#A003-1, Jiancheng) level, and GSH (#A006-2-1, Jiancheng, Nanjing, China) level were determined, respectively.

3.7. Determination of Serum ET-1, Enos And CK-MB Levels in Rats by Elisa Assay

ET-1(#MM-0560R1, Jiangsu MEIMIAN Industrial Co., Ltd., Yancheng, China), Endothelial-type Nitric Oxide synthase (eNOS) (#JYM0338Ra, Colorful-Gene Biotech, Wuhan, China) and Creatine kinase-MB (CK-MB) (#JYM0716Ra, Colorful-Gene Biotech, Wuhan, China) were measured by taking appropriate amount of rat serum according to the instruction of the kit.

3.8. TUNEL Staining to Observe Apoptosis

Paraffin sections were deparaffinized and hydrated according to the TUNEL kit instructions (#C1088, Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai, China). After washing out the TUNEL reaction solution, the sections were blocked with 3% BSA for 30 min at room temperature, followed by overnight incubation at 4 °C with rabbit anti-CD31 (1:200, #AF6191, Affinity, USA), goat anti-rabbit IgG (H+L) fluor-594, and incubated for 1 h at room temperature (1:200, # S0006, Affinity, USA). The slices were sealed using an anti-fluorescence quencher containing DAPI. Apoptosis was observed under a fluorescence microscope (NIKON ECLIPSE C1). Image J software was used to determine the number of normal and apoptotic cells in the same field of view of each group of sections, and the apoptosis rate was calculated. Apoptosis rate (%) = number of regulated cells/ (number of apoptotic cells + number of normal cells) ×100%.

3.9. Western-Blot Detection of Apoptosis Pathway, NRG-1/Erbb and Its Downstream Pathway Protein Expression Levels

Total protein of rat heart tissue was extracted by a mixture of lysate and protease inhibitor by BCA method. Total proteins were electrophoresed in SDS-PAGE gel sequentially at 80 V and 120 V for 25 min and 50 min, respectively. The membrane was then transferred to PVDF membrane at 200 mA for 60 min. 5% skimmed milk was blocked at room temperature for 1 h, and diluted primary antibody: mouse anti-Neuregulin 1 (NRG-1, 1:1000, #66492-1, Proteintech, Wuhan, China), rabbit anti-Erb-b2 receptor tyrosine kinase 2 (ErbB2, 1:1000, #18299-1, Proteintech, Wuhan, China), rabbit anti-p-PI3K (1:1000, #17366, CST, USA), rabbit anti-p-AKT (1:1000, #4058, CST, USA), rabbit anti-p-mTOR (1:1000, #2971, CST, USA), rabbit anti-GRP78 (1:1000, #3183S, CST, USA), rabbit anti-Cyt-c (1:1000, #Ab133504, Abcam, UK), rabbit anti-Caspase-3 (1:1000, #Ab13585, Abcam, UK), rabbit anti-Caspase-9 (1:1000, #AF6348, Affinity, USA), rabbit anti-Caspase-12 (1:1000, #AF5199, Affinity, USA), rabbit anti-Bad (1:1000, #9292S, CST, USA), rabbit anti-Bax (1:1000, #2772, CST, USA), rabbit anti-Bcl-2 (1:1000, #Ab194583, Abcam, UK), mouse anti-CHOP (1:1000, #2895, CST, USA), mouse anti-β-tubulin (1:1000, #T0023, Affinity, USA) were added overnight at 4°C. The membrane was washed with PBST for 3 times, and then incubated with HRP-labeled secondary antibody for 1 h. The membrane was washed again, and then exposed after the addition of ECL developer solution.

3.10. qPCR

Total RNA from the left ventricle of rats in each group was extracted by the TRNzol method and reversed to cDNA according to the method shown in the reverse transcription kit (#11141ES60, Yeasen, Shanghai, China). The DNA amplification program was performed according to Hieff qPCR SYBR Green Master Mix (#11201ES08, Yeasen, Shanghai, China), pre-denaturation at 95 °C for 5 min, and PCR reaction at 95 °C for 10 s, 60 °C for 30 s for a total of 40 cycles. β-actin was used as internal references for the mRNA detection. The 2-ΔΔCT algorithm was used, and the primer sequences were shown in Table 1.

3.11. Statistical Analysis

Data analysis was performed using IBM Statistic Package for Social Science (SPSS) Statistics 26.0 software (Armonk, NY, USA). Figures were drawn using GraphPad Prism 9.0 (La Jolla, CA, USA). Differences were compared using One-Way ANOVA and further analyzed using LSD method between groups. P < 0.05 indicated statistically significant differences. All experiments were repeated three times.

4. Results

4.1. LQHTHX Attenuated Myocardial Histopathological Changes in CMD Rats

Based on the retention times in the HPLC chromatograms, the major compounds in LQHTHX were identified, including: naringin, hesperidin, neohesperidin, quercetin, kaempferide, and tanshinone IIA (Fig. 1A).

HE staining showed that Control group and Sham group myocardial tissues had clear myotransverse striations. No microthrombus was found in the coronary microvessels, and a few red blood cells were occasionally seen without obvious abnormalities. Compared with Sham group, the thrombus mixture composed of platelets and fibrin was obvious in the coronary microvessels of CMD rats, and the degree of thrombus obstruction was heavier. After treatment with Nic, Val and LQHTHX, the thrombus obstruction in coronary microvessels of CMD rats were improved compared with the Model group. Among them, LQHTHX-H significantly improved coronary microvascular thrombus obstruction in CMD rats compared with LQHTHX-L and LQHTHX-M (Fig. 1B).

Masson staining (Fig. 1C) showed that transverse myocardial striations were clearly visible in the myocardial tissue of rats in Control group and Sham group. The cells were neatly arranged, and scattered collagen fibers were seen in the interstices with mild hyperplasia. Compared with the Sham group, the Model group showed the disappearance of transverse myocardial striations, massive cell necrosis, replaced by collagen fibers, and the pericellular and interstitial tissues were filled with a large number of collagen fibers. After treatment, myocardial fiber structure was sparse in all dose groups of LQHTHX. Some cardiomyocytes were more neatly arranged, and a few collagen fibers were proliferated around the myocytes and in the interstitium. The above results indicated that LQHTHX reduced coronary microvascular thrombosis, inhibited mild proliferation of collagen fibers, and improved coronary microvascular injury in CMD rats.

4.2. LQHTHX Attenuated Vascular Endothelial Function Impairment in CMD Rats

NO is a key factor in maintaining vasoconstrictive homeostasis and preventing thrombosis ( 25 ). eNOS is the rate-limiting enzyme for NO synthesis, and its altered activity affects NO production ( 26 ). Endothelial cell protein C receptor (Epcr) is a newly discovered protein receptor located in vascular endothelial cells. Injured endothelial cells release high levels of Epcr, which regulates the synthesis and release of NO and plays a role in the regulation of vasodilatory balance ( 27 ). The treatment effects of LQHTHX on vascular endothelial function in CMD rats were explored by detecting the levels of NO, ET-1, NO/ET-1, eNOS, and Epcr mRNA transcripts. As shown in Figure 2A-2D, we found that compared with the Sham group, Model group rats had decreased NO concentration (P < 0.001) and increased ET-1 and Epcr mRNA levels (P < 0.001), suggesting an imbalance in endogenous regulation of endothelial function in CMD. After treatment, LQHTHX significantly up-regulated NO concentration and NO/ET-1 ratio (P < 0.001), decreased ET-1 concentration (P < 0.001), and suppressed Epcr mRNA transcript levels compared with the Model group. ELISA results (Fig. 2 E) showed that eNOS content in myocardial tissues of LQHTHX, Nic, and Val groups were significantly elevated compared with the Model group (P < 0.001).

4.3. LQHTHX Ameliorated Oxidative Stress in CMD Rats

The ability to scavenge oxygen free radicals and the strength of antioxidant capacity of rats were examined by the heart tissue SOD and CAT activities as well as MDA and GSH contents. The results showed (Fig. 2F-2G) that compared with Sham group, heart tissue SOD and CAT activities were decreased, antioxidant GSH content was decreased (Fig. 2H), and the level of MDA was elevated (Fig. 2I) in the Model group (P < 0.01 or P < 0.001), which indicated that antioxidant capacity of rats in the Model group was decreased. After treatment, compared with the Model group, the LQHTHX, Nic and Val groups significantly up-regulated the SOD, CAT activity and GSH content, decreased the MDA content (P < 0.01 or P < 0.001). Of note, LQHTHX showed dose-dependent characteristics.

4.4. LQHTHX Inhibited Apoptosis in Myocardial Tissue Cells of CMD Rats

TUNEL staining showed (Fig. 3A-3B) that extremely few green fluorescent cells could be seen in the Sham group. A large amount of green fluorescence appeared in the myocardial tissue of rats in the model group, indicating a significant increase in the number of apoptotic cells (P < 0.001). In this study, CD31 was used as a marker of microvascular endothelial cells. TUNEL results showed co-localization of green fluorescence with red fluorescence, indicating microvascular endothelial cell apoptosis. Meanwhile, Elisa results showed (Fig. 3C) that the level of CK-MB of Model group increased compared with Sham group, indicating myocardial injury of rats in Model group. After treatment, TUNEL-positive cells, and CK-MB were significantly reduced in LQHTHX-M, LQHTHX-H, Nic and Val groups (P < 0.001). These results indicated that LQHTHX significantly improved cardiomyocyte apoptosis and protected the myocardium from injury in CMD rats. Compared with the Nic and Val groups, LQHTHX-H was more advantageous in reducing CK-MB.

4.5. LQHTHX Regulated Mitochondrial Apoptosis Pathway in CMD Rat Myocardial Tissue

TUNEL results suggested that LQHTHX was able to alleviate cardiomyocyte apoptosis in CMD rats. In the present study, we further investigated the anti-apoptotic mechanism of LQHTHX. WB results showed (Fig. 4A) that CMD activated Bad, Bax and their downstream apoptotic signaling pathways. Compared with Sham group, the relative expression levels of Bcl-2 protein in myocardial tissues of Model group rats were decreased (P < 0.001) and Bad and Bax proteins were increased (P < 0.001). Compared with Model group, the relative expression levels of Bad and Bax proteins in myocardial tissues of rats in each treatment group were significantly reduced (P < 0.001).

Blocking the release of activation factor Cyt-c inhibited downstream Caspase cascade reaction activation. Compared with the Sham group, the relative expression levels of Cyt-c, Caspase-9, Caspase-3 proteins in the Model group were significantly up-regulated (P < 0.001). The relative expression levels of proteins after drug administration were all reduced compared with the Model group (P < 0.001). No statistically significant difference was observed in the regulation of Cyt-c protein by Nic and Val groups compared to LQHTHX-H group (P > 0.05). It could be seen that the effect of LQHTHX-H in blocking Cyt-c release was comparable to that of Nic and Val, and had a desirable anti-apoptotic effect.

In addition, qPCR results showed (Fig. 4B) that compared with the Sham group, the myocardial tissue of rats in Model group showed significantly lower levels of Bcl-2 mRNA expression (P < 0.001), and higher levels of Bad and Bax mRNA relative expression (P < 0.001). At the same time, the relative expression levels of Cyt-c, Caspase-9 and Caspase-3 mRNA were also significantly increased (P < 0.001). The relative expression levels of Bad, Bax and Bcl-2 mRNA in rat cardiomyocytes of all groups were significantly improved after treatment (P < 0.001). The relative expression levels of Cyt-c, Caspase-9 and Caspase-3 mRNA in the administered groups were also reduced to different degrees compared with the Model group (P < 0.001). Among them, the effect of LQHTHX middle and high dose groups was the most significant.

4.6. LQHTHX Regulated ER Apoptosis Pathway in CMD Rat Myocardial Tissue

Normally, the expression of CHOP and GRP78 proteins is low ( 28 ). And the relative expression levels of CHOP and GRP78 proteins in the myocardial tissues of Model group rats were significantly higher than those of the Sham group (P < 0.001). After treatment, the relative expression levels of CHOP and GRP78 proteins in rat myocardial tissues were significantly reduced in all groups compared with Model group (P < 0.01 or P < 0.001). The relative expression levels of CHOP and GRP78 proteins in the myocardial tissues of rats in LQHTHX-H, Nic, and Val groups were reduced compared with those in LQHTHX-L group (P < 0.001). No statistically significant differences (P > 0.05) were observed in CHOP and GRP78 proteins of rat myocardial tissues between the three groups of LQHTHX-H, Nic, and Val (Fig. 5A). Caspase-12, an ER apoptosis signaling factor, was in agreement with the trend of CHOP and GRP78 proteins, suggesting that LQHTHX was able to alleviate apoptosis mediated by the ER apoptosis pathway ( 29 ).

In addition, qPCR (Fig. 5B) results showed that the relative expression levels of Chop, Grp78, and Caspase-12 mRNA in the rat myocardial tissues of the Model group were significantly higher than those of the Sham group (P < 0.001). After drug administration, the relative expression levels of Chop, Grp78 and Caspase-12 mRNA in rat myocardial tissues were reduced in all groups compared with Model group (P < 0.05 or P < 0.001). Compared with LQHTHX-H, no statistically significant differences were observed in the myocardial tissue CHOP, GRP78 proteins of rats in Nic and Val groups (P > 0.05).

5. Discussion

Coronary microcirculation refers to the microcirculatory system in the heart consisting of microarteries, capillaries, and microveins, which plays a vital role in maintaining the normal metabolism and function of myocardial tissues and cells. CMD occurs when the coronary microcirculatory system is stimulated by one or more maladies ( 31 , 32 ), such as hypertension, hyperglycemia, hyperlipidemia, and other abnormalities. Coronary micro-thromboembolism occurs when the blood supply at the level of the myocardium is blocked, resulting in the formation of tiny foci of infarction. Its pathogenesis is complex, with possible common mechanisms with ischemic heart disease (IDH). These include inflammation, endothelial dysfunction, and imbalance of oxygen supply to cardiomyocytes, which in turn causes microvascular ischemia, myocardial injury, and impaired cardiac mechanics ( 33 , 34 ). In the treating and managing of CMD patients, a comprehensive multifactorial analysis focusing on high-risk factors for disease progression is needed to find a multi-targeted therapeutic pathway to minimize adverse cardiovascular events.

The ideal coronary microthrombosis model is closer to the pathophysiological changes of coronary microthrombosis in clinical conditions, which helps us to make a more in-depth discussion on its occurrence mechanism and efficacy. In this study, we propose establishing a rat CMD model by apical injection of sodium laurate. By damaging endothelial cells, pro-coagulation prompts thrombus formation, providing an ideal experimental platform for studying coronary artery microthrombosis ( 35 , 36 ).

Studies have shown that after coronary microcirculation embolization, the formation of tiny infarct foci leads to hypoxic injury of the surrounding cardiomyocytes. At the same time, it stimulates the production of a series of oxidative stress, inflammatory response, apoptosis, and other changes. It causes myocardial infarction, arrhythmia, heart failure, and other cardiovascular diseases ( 37 - 39 ). Maintaining the structural and functional integrity of cardiomyocytes reduces cardiomyocyte apoptosis, alleviates endothelial dysfunction, and thus improves coronary microcirculatory dysfunction.

It has been confirmed that the efficacy of traditional Chinese medicine prescriptions in improving coronary microcirculation and protecting against endothelial damage, such as Compound Danshen Dropping Pill and Musk Heart Pill ( 40 - 42 ). LQHTHX is a traditional Chinese medicine formula with specific therapeutic efficacy based on the characteristics of disease mechanism and medicinal properties, according to the principle of “monarch, minister, assistant, and guide”. Composed of CH, ZQ, GLP, XB, DS, CX, DG, YHS, HH, FL, BZ, and CP. Among them, CH, ZQ, GLP, and XB are the monarch herbs to relieve depression, regulate Qi, and broaden the chest. CX, DG, DS, YHS, and HH are the ministerial herbs to promote Qi and blood circulation and relieve pain. Adjuvant FL, BZ, and CP strengthen the spleen and dissolve the spleen, which works together to regulate Qi and broaden the chest, dissolve phlegm and promote blood circulation, and activate blood circulation and eliminate blood stasis ( 30 ). The results of previous studies have shown ( 30 ) that the LQHTHX formula can restore serum ET-1/NO balance, reduce fgl2 mRNA expression in myocardial tissues, and have the effect of protecting vascular endothelial function.

Pathologic results showed that the Model group rats had severe microvascular thrombus obstruction, which is a common cause of coronary microcirculation disorder. Microvascular obstruction caused multiple small infarct foci in the myocardium, massive necrosis of cardiomyocytes, and proliferation of collagen fibers, which could promote acute coronary syndromes. The degree of coronary artery microvascular thrombus obstruction in CMD rats could be significantly improved by LQHTHX treatment. It protected cardiomyocytes and alleviated collagen fiber proliferation around cardiomyocytes and interstitial tissues. An imbalance of vasoconstrictor factor endothelin 1 (ET-1) and diastolic factor NO promotes cardiomyocyte fiber proliferation. They affected the luminal volume of cardiac vessels and caused alterations in coronary microcirculation ( 14 , 43 , 44 ). LQHTHX alters the degree of coronary vasodilatation, restores cardiac vascular lumen volume, and alleviates CMD-induced alterations in coronary microcirculation by regulating ET-1 and NO, among others. This might be closely related to the regulation of NRG-1/ErbB and its downstream PI3K/Akt pathway by LQHTHX. NRG-1 is part of the epidermal growth factor, which can protect and maintain cardiomyocytes’ normal structure and function. It’s binding to the receptor ErbB can activate the tyrosine kinase activity of the receptor, triggering a series of important signaling processes such as survival, proliferation and apoptosis. Releases downstream PI3K/Akt signaling and increases mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) expression ( 45 , 46 ). Promotes intracardiac neovascularization and increased blood flow in cardiomyocytes, which are involved in cardiomyocyte metabolism and functional recovery. It also promotes the release of eNOS and ameliorates CMD. The results of this experimental study showed that the levels of NRG-1, ErbB2, p-PI3K, p-Akt, p-mTOR, and the content of eNOS in LQHTHX were elevated to varying degrees when compared with that of Model group, suggesting that LQHTHX is involved in the repair and neogenesis of cardiomyocytes.

CMD produces multiple foci of microvascular infarction, disrupts the body’s scavenging function of free radicals, and upsets the balance ( 47 ). This leads to the accumulation of excess MDA, imbalance of the intracellular antioxidant system, decreased SOD and CAT vitality, and decreased GSH level. The present results showed that compared with the Model group, the heart tissue MDA content of LQHTHX, Nic, and Val groups were reduced, SOD and CAT vitality was enhanced (P < 0.01), and GSH level was elevated. And LQHTHX regulated the level of oxidative stress in a dose-dependent manner. Among them, the therapeutic effect of LQHTHX-H, Nic, and Val was the most significant, which indicated that LQHTHX could antagonize CMD-induced oxidative stress and restore the antioxidant system balance.

Bcl-2 and Bax genes in the Bal-2 family are the most critical regulatory genes in apoptosis known to mediate the release of Cyt-c and other substances through the mitochondrial pathway ( 48 ). With further in-depth studies on the role of Bcl-2, Bax, and Caspase-3 in apoptosis signaling in recent years, it was found that Bcl-2 and Bax not only act as upstream regulatory mechanisms of Caspase-3 and participate in the regulation of its activity, but also act as direct substrates of caspase-3 downstream of caspase-3 ( 49 ). In this study, TUNEL staining results showed that LQHTHX had relatively fewer positive red fluorescent cells and significantly reduced apoptosis compared with Model group (P < 0.001).

Elisa revealed that LQHTHX significantly reduced CK-MB levels in a dose-dependent manner (P < 0.001), suggesting that LQHTHX could attenuate apoptosis and protect the myocardium from injury. Meanwhile, WB and RT-qPCR results reconfirmed that LQHTHX down-regulated Bad and Bax proteins and promoted the expression of Bcl-2. It blocked the release of Cyt-c, a key mitochondrial factor, and inhibited the activation of the downstream caspase cascade reaction. Most importantly, we found that LQHTHX has a good anti-apoptotic effect compared to the commonly used drugs Nic and Val, with no significant difference in the effect of blocking blocking Cyt-c release.

Activation of the CHOP and GRP78 apoptosis signaling pathway suggested ER damage and caused sustained and intense ER stress, which induced apoptosis ( 50 ). CHOP apoptosis signaling pathway mediated the downstream Bal-2 family, and the activated Caspase-12 further cleaved the Caspase-9, which activated a series of caspase apoptotic mechanisms to induce apoptosis ( 51 , 52 ). Meanwhile, under ER stress, GRP78 did not bind to transmembrane proteins, and transmembrane proteins were in an activated state, inducing many signals for pro-apoptotic programs.

In this study, we demonstrated that LQHTHX played an important role in CMD treatment by inhibiting the expression of pro-apoptotic genes, enhancing anti-apoptotic genes, reducing thromboembolism, restoring cardiomyocyte structure and function, promoting vasomodulatory factors, and resisting oxidative stress, etc. LQHTHX might effectively treat and improve the disease status and quality of life of patients with CMD.

Despite the promising findings, this study has certain limitations. Firstly, the therapeutic effects and mechanisms of LQHTHX were investigated in a rat model of CMD. Although this model recapitulates key pathophysiological features of human CMD, inherent species differences between rodents and humans in drug metabolism and systemic pathophysiology necessitate caution in direct translation. Secondly, HPLC analysis is limited to qualitative identification of chemical constituents and fails to provide quantitative data on key active components or the pharmacokinetic properties of LQHTHX. Future research should focus on: 1) Validating the aforementioned mechanisms in human-relevant in vitro systems (e.g., human myocardial microvascular endothelial cells); 2) Employing LC-MS technology to analyze the chemical composition of LQHTHX for quality control, and to investigate its absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion processes to comprehensively evaluate its clinical application potential; 3) Ultimately validating efficacy through animal models with greater clinical predictive value to bridge the translational research gap prior to clinical trials.

6. Conclusion

In conclusion, we found that LQHTHX attenuated cardiomyocyte pathological injury, reduced microvascular thrombosis, and restored the balance of vascular endothelial regulatory factors in CMD rats. In addition, LQHTHX dose-dependently regulated the level of oxidative stress and PI3K/Akt pathway, attenuated hypoxic injury, and promoted the metabolic and functional recovery of cardiomyocytes. It also inhibited cardiomyocyte apoptosis by regulating the mitochondrial apoptotic pathway and ER stress pathway (Fig. 7). Our study also demonstrated that the efficacy of LQHTHX was no less than that of nicorandil and valsartan. These findings provide a scientific rationale for the clinical application of LQHTHX in CMD. More studies are needed in the future to establish pharmacokinetics-pharmacodynamics (PK-PD) data and confirm the effective components of LQHTHX. This will enable LQHTHX to have a broader clinical application as an effective therapeutic agent for CMD.

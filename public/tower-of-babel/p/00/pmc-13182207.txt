RaPID Selection of Backbone Macrocyclic Peptides Targeting Akt2
Authors: Koki Shinbara, Ekishin Yanagi, Ayaka Aizawa, Takayuki Katoh, Hiroaki Suga
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackbone‐cyclic peptides (BMPs) are an attractive class of molecules appeared in diverse natural bioactive products. However, mRNA display technology coupled with ribosomal synthesis is intrinsically inapplicable to such peptide phenotypes due to loss of the C‐terminal peptide region linking to the mRNA genotypes. To overcome this issue, we have devised a new strategy to link the sidechain‐to‐S‐mainchain bond via an S‐to‐N acyl‐shift to connect BMPs to the C‐terminal fragment of the peptide. Here, we report the application of this strategy to construct a library of BMPs fused to cognate mRNAs. The library was applied for the selection of BMP ligands targeting Akt2, which is involved in the signal pathway to cancer pathogenesis. Consequently, BMP ligands against Akt2 were successfully uncovered from the library. The most potent Akt2 inhibitor, BMPakti‐3, showed 1.3 nM of dissociation constant and 34 nM of half‐maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50). This system offers a unique platform for the de novo discovery of bioactive BMP ligands against various protein targets of interest.Keywords: backbone macrocyclic peptides, genetic code reprogramming, head‐to‐tail macrocyclic peptides, in vitro screening, peptide library, peptides

Introduction

Macrocyclic peptides possess attractive pharmacological properties, such as strong binding affinity against targets and in vivo stability, due to their structural rigidity [1]. They are classified into three types by their cyclization modes: Cyclization by sidechain‐to‐sidechain, head‐to‐sidechain, and head‐to‐tail (i.e., backbone macrocyclic peptide, BMP). Among them, BMPs generally have the most rigid structure owing to their cyclization via a nonrotatable peptide bond. Furthermore, BMPs exhibit inherent resistance to exopeptidases due to the absence of N‐ and C‐termini [2]. In fact, BMPs encompass a broad range of natural bioactive peptides, such as cyclosporin A, PawS‐derived peptides, and cyclotides [3].

To discover de novo bioactive BMPs, methodologies for constructing BMP libraries, such as the one‐bead‐one‐compound method and split‐intein circular ligation of peptides and proteins, have been developed and applied to the screening of BMPs [4, 5, 6, 7]. Diversities of the BMP libraries constructed by these methods are estimated to be approximately 107–109. On the other hand, the BMP library with over 1012 members can be potentially constructed by means of in vitro translation and screened by mRNA display [8, 9]. However, mRNA display methods, including the most powerful Random Nonstandard Peptides Integrated Discovery (RaPID) system, require that the C‐terminus of the peptide must be tethered to its corresponding mRNA in order to maintain the relationship between peptide phenotype and mRNA genotype. However, unlike macrocycles closed by N‐terminus‐to‐sidechain and sidechain‐to‐sidechain, the library of BMPs is closed between N‐terminus and C‐terminus, which means that the C‐terminus of phenotype BMPs is detached from the mRNA (Figure 1a); and thereby the selection cannot be performed.

To overcome this, we have previously established a method for conjugating ribosomally synthesized BMP with the cognate mRNA via a linker peptide [10], where the side chain of BMP could be connected to the cognate mRNA via the C‐terminal peptide fragment (Figure 1b). First, a linear peptide containing a thiazolidine‐protected Cys (ThzCys) at the N‐terminus, followed by 2‐chloroacetamide‐2,4‐diaminobutyric acid (Cab), 3‐(4‐chlorophenyl)‐2‐mercaptopropanoic acid (HSF p Cl), and Cys, is expressed by a customized flexible in vitro translation (FIT) system [11, 12]. Spontaneous thioester exchange between the thioester of HSF p Cl and the thiol of the downstream Cys occurs to liberate the thiol of the α‐thioacid, which in turn attacks the chloroacetyl (ClAc) sidechain of Cab to produce a thioether covalent linkage (Figure 1b‐[1]). After reverse‐transcription (Figure 1b‐[2]), subsequent deprotection of the thiazolidine of ThzCys by sodium cyanoborohydride in mild sodium acetate buffer at pH 4.8 yields MeCys at the N‐terminus (Figure 1b‐[3]) and provokes native chemical ligation‐like backbone cyclization via thioester exchange between the thiol of the N‐terminal MeCys and the thioester composed of the thiol of the downstream Cys. Finally, an S‐to‐N acyl‐shift of the thioester and the N‐terminal amine occurs to give a BMP bearing a linker peptide connected to the corresponding mRNA (Figure 1b‐[4]). The synthesis is completed by thiol‐alkylation by iodoacetamide to mask the free thiol sidechain of MeCys (Figure 1b‐[5]). Despite the above complex chemistry process, our previous work has demonstrated that it works smoothly to produce BMP‐mRNA fusion. Based on this conceptual framework, we next implemented the backbone macrocyclization strategy in a RaPID‐compatible experimental workflow, enabling BMP library construction and selection toward the discovery of ligands against a protein of interest. Here, we applied for the first time the BMP library prepared by this method and the RaPID (BMP‐RaPID) selection to uncover de novo BMP inhibitors against Akt2 [13, 14].

Results and Discussion

Construction of a Backbone Macrocyclic Peptide Library and Application to the RaPID System

To construct the desired BMP library, the initiator and elongator AUG codons were reprogrammed to ThzCys‐Cab and HSF p Cl using ThzCys‐Cab‐tRNAfMet CAU and HSF p Cl‐tRNAAsnE2#3 CAU, respectively (Figure 2a,b) [10]. Our previous method reported earlier [10] was built based on an orthogonal tRNAAsnE2 for HSF p Cl elongation, but after experimental attempts using HSFpCl‐tRNAAsnE2 CAU we realized that further improvement of elongation efficiency was necessary to construct the BMP library. We therefore changed it to tRNAAsnE2#3, whose T‐stem was reengineered to enhance its EF‐Tu affinity, enhancing the efficient introduction of the HSF p Cl residue [15, 16]. Methionine was omitted in the FIT system, to which ThzCys‐Cab‐tRNAfMet CAU and HSF p Cl‐tRNAAsnE2#3 CAU were supplemented (Figure 1b). Upon translation of the BMP precursor, the thioester exchange spontaneously occurred, followed by the formation of a thioether covalent bond between the α‐thiol in HSF p Cl and the chloroacetyl (ClAc) moiety in the Cab sidechain, which occurred without accompanying major byproducts. This clean thioether linkage formation was crucial for making the connection between the phenotype (BMP) and the genotype (corresponding mRNA). The C‐terminal IIe‐Gly‐Cys‐Gly‐Asn‐Ser sequence embedded at the downstream of HSF p Cl was introduced as a linker peptide connected to the 3'‐end of the mRNA via a puromycin linker. Next, the reverse transcription step of the mRNA into cDNA was implemented after spontaneous sidechain linkage to avoid loss of its efficiency because subsequent steps involved changes in pH and salt concentration. After reverse transcription, deprotection of Thz was carried out by adding a deprotection buffer (NaBH3CN, AcONa at pH 4.8). An increase in pH from 7.3 to 8.0 by the addition of HEPES‐NaOH buffer induced the spontaneous backbone cyclization via the native chemical ligation process [10]. Alkylation of the free thiols was then performed by adding iodoacetamide (IAA) to prevent side reactions caused by the thiols. Consequently, the resulting BMP library could be connected to the corresponding mRNA/cDNA via the thioether linker and the peptide linker, enabling the identification of hit BMPs by cDNA sequencing. The BMP library was first exposed to naked magnetic beads to remove bead binders, referred to as “negative selection”, followed by “positive selection” against Akt2 immobilized on the beads (Figure 3). By repeating the negative and positive selections, the cDNA recovery rates reached a high level (over 12% of the total input) at the fifth round for Akt2 (Figure S1). On the other hand, the recovery rates of negative selections did not increase at all for Akt2. Based on sequencing data of the cDNA library at the fifth round for Akt2, three potential BMP Akt2 inhibitors (BMPakti‐1−3) were chosen for the following chemical synthesis and evaluation of binding affinity, inhibitory activity, and serum stability (Table 1).

Binding and Inhibitory Properties of Backbone Macrocyclic Peptides

Binding affinities of the chemically synthesized BMPs to Akt were evaluated by a surface plasmon resonance (SPR) analysis (Table 1 and Figure S2 for SPR analysis, and Figures S6 and S7 for the synthesis of BMPs). BMPakti‐1−3 showed strong binding with K D values on the order of 10−8 to 10−9 M, where the BMPakti‐3 exhibits the strongest affinity with a K D value of 1.3 nM. Inhibitory activities of BMPakti‐1−3 were evaluated by ADP‐Glo kinase assay, in which ADP produced by phosphorylation of a standard Akt2 substrate, called Crosstide, was detected by luminescence (Figure 4 and Table 1) [17]. All BMPakti‐1−3 exhibited inhibitory activity as expected in a range of 10−7−10−8 M IC50 values. Notably, BMPakti‐2 (IC50 = 24 nM) and BMPakti‐3 (IC50 = 34 nM) showed a comparable level of inhibitory activities against Akt2, even though their K D values were 10‐fold apart, i.e., 13.2 nM and 1.3 nM, respectively (Table 1). This indicates that BMPakti‐2 may deeply bind to the active site, giving more effective inhibition. For a quick comparison, one of the best thioether macrocyclic peptides (TMP) inhibitors identified from our previous RaPID selection, called Pakti‐L1, exhibits an IC50 of 110 nM [18]. Therefore, the BMP‐RaPID system afforded comparable or even slightly better inhibitors than the TMP‐RaPID system against this particular target.

Interestingly, BMPakti‐1−3 consistently possess a RxRTxRIYzR motif, where x can be Q, N, S, or Y, and z can be Q or R (Table 1). Despite the sequence similarity, the lengths of the peptides are different, so that they certainly originated from distinct mRNA genotypes that existed in the initial library. The Crosstide consists of H2N‐GR −5 PR −3 T −2TS*FAEG‐OH, where S* is the phosphorylated serine and assigned to the position 0, and the R−5, R−3, T−2, and are assigned to positions −5, −3, and −2, respectively. It is known that the R−5 and R−3 residues interact with glutamate residues of Akt2 (E236, E279, and E342), while the T−2 residue interacts with the carboxy group of E279 in Akt2 (Figure S3) [19]. Therefore, it is likely that BMPakti‐1−3 shares the same binding site as Akt2's substrate peptides. It has been reported that the residues of the activity pockets are highly conserved in Akt isoforms, and the optimal Akt1 substrates have a R−7xR−5xR−3T−2xS* motif [20]. BMPakti‐3, as a representative inhibitor in this study, possesses the RxRxxRIYRR motif, which does not exactly align with the substrate motif; however, the R−7xR−5xxR−3 motif may enable it to set well into the substrate binding site of Akt, and some other additional interactions between residues create inhibitory action of BMPakti‐3. As the last note for the sequence comparison, Pakti‐L1 also shares the RxRxxR motif like BMPakti‐1–3 [18].

Contribution of Backbone Macrocyclic Structures to Their Properties

Based on the knowledge of x‐ray 3D structures of various TMPs complexed with target proteins, we have learned that the thioether bond is generally not involved in their specific interactions [21, 22]. Indeed, this makes sense that the carboxyl group of the Cys residue, whose sidechain thiol group forms the thioether bond, connects to the peptide‐puromycin linker to mRNA, and thereby the peptide binding motifs largely rely on the internal “pharmacophore” sequences. Thus, we envisioned that an appropriate replacement of a backbone residue(s) in BMP with a thioether bond might generate a binding‐active TMP ligand. This idea led us to design TMPs where the MeCys(+acetamide)‐Ala of BMPs was substituted with thioether‐MeCys. Accordingly, the designer TMPs, referred to as TMPakti‐1–3, have the same number of atoms in the macrocyclic ring as their parental BMPakti‐1–3 (Figure 5, BMPakti‐3 and TMPakti‐3, and Figure S4 for the others).

Chemical structures of representative Akt2 binders. (a) BMPakti‐3. The sidechain thiol of MeCys‐Ala was alkylated by iodoacetamide. (b) TMPakti‐3. The MeCys‐Ala of BMPakti‐3 was substituted with thioether‐MeCys. Both macrocyclic peptides maintain 51 atoms in the ring.

We then determined K D and IC50 values of TMPakti‐1–3 (Table 1 and Figure S2b for K D, and Figure 4b for IC50). TMPakti‐1–3 macrocycles were indeed active for binding and inhibition, albeit with 5–10‐fold reductions compared with the parental BMPakti‐1–3. This modest decrease in activity may be caused by differences in macrocycle topology. In contrast to BMPs, the corresponding TMPs have a rotatable single bond at the cyclization linkage, which may reduce conformational preorganization and increase the entropic penalty upon binding. In addition, because the in vitro selection was performed with the BMP library, the identified sequences are intrinsically optimized for the BMP backbone macrocyclic architecture. Accordingly, conversion of these sequences into TMP analogues is expected to result in some loss of affinity and inhibitory potency, as the macrocycle topology differs from that used during selection.

We also measured human serum stability of BMPakti‐1–3 and TMPakti‐1–3 (Figure S5). They were not markedly stable due to the presence of multiple arginine (R) residues present in their sequences, but their half‐lives (t 1/2) of BMPakti‐1–3 are still in the range of 5–11 h, while those of TMPakti‐1–3 are 7–23 h (Table 1). Clearly, macrocyclization sustains the serum stability and high binding ability regardless of their linkage format. Most importantly, the above practice in the design of TMPs from BMPs, and likely vice versa, lends credibility to the direct selection of macrocyclic peptides using both TMP‐ and BMP‐RaPID systems to yield more potent species.

Conclusion

In this study, we have reported for the first time the RaPID selection of backbone macrocyclic peptides, BMPs, and successfully identified high‐affinity inhibitors against Akt2. Despite the complex rearrangements taking place in the library synthesis, the library construction took smoothly place and the RaPID selection yielded potent macrocycles. Despite the peptide origin was distinct from their genotype sequences, these peptides have a common motif of RxRxxRIYRR, which partly shares with the substrate motif, RxRxR, and also shares with the previous TMP inhibitor motif, RxRxxR [18]. We thus consider that the BMP‐RaPID selection works as well as our standard RaPID selection from the library of thioether‐macrocyclic peptides [9, 23]. From our perspective view, BMPs theoretically have less rotatable bonds in the macrocyclic ring than TMPs, and thus by combining the incorporation of N‐methyl, d‐, β‐, and γ‐ amino acids, we expect that the BMP's serum stability and possibly cell membrane permeability can be significantly enhanced [24, 25, 26, 27, 28]. We will continue the next challenge in the preparation of such libraries and selection against target proteins to discover macrocycles with superior properties directly from the libraries.

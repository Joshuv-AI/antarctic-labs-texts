Correction of Tricuspid Regurgitation in Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension
Authors: Antonio Orlando, Andrea Vergara, Robert Naeije, Michele D'Alto
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPulmonary hypertension is a contraindication to correction of tricuspid regurgitation.Case SummaryA 75-year-old Italian woman with previous episodes of right heart failure was diagnosed with World Health Organization (WHO) functional class IV pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) complicated by torrential tricuspid regurgitation. After 6 months of treatment with diuretic agents, macitentan, and tadalafil, she improved to WHO functional class III, with a pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR) decreasing from 5.4 to 3 Wood units. She then underwent transcatheter valve repair. One year later, she was in WHO functional class I with a PVR of 2.2 Wood units.DiscussionRight heart failure with a moderate increase in PVR in PAH may be caused by severe tricuspid insufficiency. Transtricuspid valve repair under optimized medical treatment may then be successful.Take-Home MessageSevere tricuspid regurgitation in PAH may be the dominant cause of right heart failure and is amenable to transcatheter repair under optimal medical therapy.Key Words: pulmonary hypertension, right ventricle, tricuspid valve

History of Presentation

We present the case of a patient with pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) and massive tricuspid regurgitation (TR), who had a pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR) of >3 Wood units (WU) but an acceptable tricuspid annular plane systolic excursion (TAPSE) to systolic pulmonary artery pressure (sPAP) ratio, which improved with a combination of targeted therapies and transcatheter tricuspid valve repair.

A 75-year-old woman was admitted for worsening dyspnea and lower limb edema. She was in World Health Organization (WHO) functional class IV. Her clinical examination disclosed a blood pressure of 110/50 mm Hg, a regular heart rate of 85 beats/min, turgescent jugular veins, an enlarged liver, and extensive lower limb edema. She had an estimated glomerular filtration rate of 71 mL/min/1.73 m2 and an increased N-terminal pro-brain natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP) level increased to 4,240 pg/mL.

Past Medical History

She had several previous hospitalizations for right heart failure. Her treatments included gliflozins, angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors, mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists, and diuretic agents.

Differential Diagnosis

Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) revealed torrential TR with a coaptation gap of 7 mm, severe dilation of the right atrium (RA) and the right ventricle (RV), an apparently normal TR velocity (TRV) of 2.5 m/s, a dilated (28 mm) and not collapsible inferior vena cava (IVC), a TAPSE/sPAP ratio of 0.54 mm/mm Hg, severe bilateral pleural effusion, and moderate ascites.

A right heart catheterization showed an increased RA pressure and precapillary pulmonary hypertension (PH) with a PVR of 5.4 WU.

A complete work-up following current guidelines,3,4 including a careful exclusion of heart failure with preserved ejection fraction, established a diagnosis of PAH with severe right heart failure.

Management

The patient was treated transiently with high-dose intravenous diuretic agents and started on the PAH-targeted therapies macitentan 10 mg combined with tadalafil 20 mg per day, with tadalafil increased to 40 mg per day after 30 days.

Outcome and Follow-Up

The patient's condition stabilized with improved dyspnea, reversal of lower limb edema, and reverse right heart remodeling at echocardiography.

At 6-month follow-up, the patient was in WHO functional class III with no clinical signs of right heart failure. Her NT-proBNP level was decreased to 713 pg/mL. A TTE scan still revealed severe TR, mildly dilated RA and RV, a TRV of 3.4 m/s, and a mildly dilated (22 mm) but collapsible IVC. The TAPSE/sPAP ratio was 0.57 mm/mm Hg.

A control right heart catheterization showed that PVR decreased to 3 WU. Given the echocardiographic and hemodynamic improvements and the TAPSE/sPAP ratio being consistently above 0.36, the patient underwent transcatheter tricuspid valve repair with the TriClip system.

The follow-up was uneventful, and 6 months later, the patient was asymptomatic in WHO functional class II, and her clinical examination was unremarkable. The NT-proBNP level decreased to 211 pg/mL. TTE showed that there was mild-to-moderate residual TR with normal-sized RA and RV, a TRV of 3.3 m/s, and a normal-sized (16 mm) and collapsible IVC. The TAPSE/sPAP ratio was 0.64 mm/mm Hg. No pleural effusion and ascites were detected.

A control right heart catheterization revealed a mean pulmonary artery pressure of 22 mm Hg and a PVR of 2.1 WU.

A recalculation of the TAPSE/sPAP ratios with invasively measured sPAP showed improvement from initial 0.41 to 0.75 under targeted therapies and 0.88 after transcatheter tricuspid valve repair.

Discussion

TR is a cause of unexplained dyspnea and may contribute to severe PH symptomatology.1,2 However, assessment and eventual correction of TR is not mentioned in the most recent PH guidelines.3,4 Tricuspid valve repair has become more accessible by transcatheter rather than surgical interventions, fueling renewed specific guidelines and consensus documents.5,6 There is currently agreement that a PVR of more than 3 WU with or without an echocardiographic estimation of RV-pulmonary arterial uncoupling by the TAPSE/sPAP ratio is a contraindication to the procedure.2,5, 6, 7 Symptomatic TR is associated with poor prognosis.2,8 The most common form of TR is functional in the setting of annular dilation and RV enlargement due to any form of PH.1,2,9 Both RV volume overload by TR and pressure overload by PH eventually result in right heart failure.1,2 However, TR limits the effects of excessive pressure overload in PH by a decrease in forward stroke volume and pressure.1,2 It is therefore recommended to refrain from functional TR correction in the presence of PH.2,5, 6, 7 Accordingly, correction of TR is not even discussed in the most recent European Society of Cardiology/European Respiratory Society guidelines on the diagnosis and treatment of PH.3,4

Advances in transcatheter repair technology in recent years have resulted in easily available interventions and improved definitions of indications.5 There is currently agreement that a PVR between 3 and 6 WU with a TAPSE/sPAP ratio of <0.36 is a contraindication to tricuspid valve repair, and that a PVR of >6 WU is an absolute contraindication to the procedure.2,5, 6, 7 Our patient met the criteria for tricuspid valve repair initially with a TAPSE/sPAP ratio of >0.36, although PVR was higher than 3 WU; however, she was in an unstable clinical condition. Her physicians therefore prioritized RV afterload reduction with PAH-targeted therapies. This cautious approach proved successful.

The TTE assessment of sPAP using a transtricuspid Doppler gradient may be impossible, which underestimates the severity of PH in the presence of severe TR, high RA pressure, and RV dysfunction.10 In therapeutic algorithms,7 an invasive confirmation of sPAP may therefore be desirable. This is also shown by the case of our patient.

The clinical presentation of PAH in our patient was atypical because of her old age, only a mild and reversible increase in PVR, and massive and more than functional TR markedly contributing to symptomatology dominated by repetitive episodes of RV failure. She likely had pre-existent tricuspid valve insufficiency, which was markedly worsened by only a mild increase in PVR. On the other hand, assessment of RV adaptation to afterload was not only limited to the TAPSE/sPAP ratio, a valid estimate of RV-PA coupling1 including patients with TR,7 but also limited to RV systolic function without comprehensive description of right heart structural changes and diastolic function.

Conclusions

PH clinicians should pay attention to the tricuspid valve, especially in the presence of severe right heart failure combined with moderately severe PH. Transcatheter tricuspid valve repair is now easily available and deserves a place in the next PH guidelines.

Coexistent Moderate PAH but With an Increased PVR of >3 WU and Massive Tricuspid Regurgitation

A PVR of >3 WU is a contraindication to tricuspid valve (TV) repair. Targeted medical therapy may bring PVR down to >3 WU and improve hemodynamics and remodeling of the right heart allowing for safe and effective transcatheter TV repair.

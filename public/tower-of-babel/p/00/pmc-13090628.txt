Electroporation as an adjunctive therapy for refractory feline sporotrichosis: A two-case report
Authors: Carlos Henrique Maciel Brunner, Luis Eduardo de Pinho Gonçalves, Claudia Hirukawa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFeline sporotrichosis is a zoonotic subcutaneous mycosis often associated with high fungal burden and therapeutic challenges. This report describes two cases of feline sporotrichosis, caused by Sporothrix brasiliensis, unresponsive to itraconazole that were successfully managed using electroporation as an adjunctive local therapy.Both cats presented ulcerative and proliferative lesions with poor response to prolonged antifungal treatment. Electroporation was performed under general anesthesia using needle electrodes and square-wave electric pulses. Progressive reduction in lesion size, decreased exudation, and complete epithelialization were observed after repeated sessions.Complete remission was achieved after three sessions in one case and two sessions in the other, with no recurrence during follow-up.Electroporation may represent a useful adjunctive approach for local control of refractory sporotrichosis lesions, although further studies are needed to establish its efficacy.Keywords: Sporotrichosis, Sporothrix brasiliensis, Electroporation, Feline

Introduction

Feline sporotrichosis is an emerging zoonotic mycosis in endemic regions such as Brazil mainly caused by Sporothrix brasiliensis [1,2]. Infected cats often present ulcerative and proliferative lesions with high fungal burden, contributing to transmission through contact with exudates [3].

Itraconazole is considered the treatment of choice; however, refractory cases have been increasingly reported, particularly in infections caused by S. brasiliensis [4]. These cases represent a therapeutic challenge due to prolonged treatment time and variable clinical response.

Electroporation is a technique that increases cellular membrane permeability through the application of electric pulses and has been investigated in antimicrobial and oncological applications [5]. Its use in infectious dermatological conditions remains poorly explored.

This report describes the clinical outcome of electroporation as an adjunctive treatment in two cases of refractory feline sporotrichosis.

Case description

Case 1

An adult domestic cat, male, mixed-breed, four-year-old, castrated, was presented with multiple ulcerative cutaneous lesions characterized by serosanguineous crusts affecting the right infraorbital region, left humeral region, left metatarsal region, interscapular region, lateral canthus of the left eye, dorsal nasal planum, and both auricular pinnae, with partial exposure of auricular cartilage. Samples were collected from each lesion using sterile swabs for cytological examination, which revealed yeast-like structures compatible with Sporothrix spp. The diagnosis was subsequently confirmed by fungal culture with identification of Sporothrix brasiliensis. The animal had been receiving oral itraconazole (ITL®, Cevap, São Paulo, Brazil) at a dose of 100 mg once daily for two months without clinical improvement.

Electroporation was performed under general inhalational anesthesia using needle electrodes (Sporo Pulse®, Akko, Santa Rita do Sapucaí, Brazil). Eight square-wave monopolar pulses (1000 V/cm, 100 μs, 5 kHz) were initially applied to all lesions. Postoperative analgesia consisted of dipyrone (Dipirona Gotas®, Biovet, Cotia, Brazil) at 50 mg/kg, orally.

Fifty-four days after the first session, lesion progression and the development of a new periungual lesion were observed. A second electroporation session was then performed using the same device, with 19 square-wave monopolar pulses (1450 V/cm, 100 μs, 5 kHz) applied to each lesion. Itraconazole therapy was maintained.

Six days after the second session, lesions showed reduced exudation and the presence of smooth granulation tissue.

A third electroporation session using the same parameters was performed 90 days after the initial treatment. At that time, several lesions had completely resolved, while the remaining lesions showed marked reduction in size.

Itraconazole therapy was maintained throughout the electroporation treatment period.

Complete epithelialization of all lesions was observed 130 days after the first session. No recurrence was noted during a 140-day follow-up period (Fig. 1).

Case 2

A four-year-old male stray, mixed-breed, domestic cat was presented with deep ulcerative lesions affecting the face, ears, and limbs. Initial treatment with ampicillin did not result in clinical improvement.

Cytological evaluation suggested a fungal infection, and treatment with oral itraconazole (30 mg once daily) was initiated. The itraconazole dose was progressively increased up to 100 mg once daily due to lack of clinical response; however, lesions continued to progress despite dose escalation. After 30 days, lesion progression was observed, including enlargement of facial lesions and the appearance of a new lesion on a limb. Swab samples from the lesions were submitted for fungal culture, which confirmed the presence of Sporothrix brasiliensis.

Electroporation was then performed using the same device and parameters (1450 V/cm, 100 μs, 5 kHz). The pulses were applied to the lesions and to an approximately 2 cm margin of surrounding tissue. Postoperative analgesia consisted of dipyrone (50 mg/kg, orally, twice daily).

Twelve days after the first session, lesions showed reduced exudation and the formation of smooth granulation tissue.

A second electroporation session using the same parameters was performed 21 days later. At that time, lesions had significantly decreased in size and showed well-defined cicatricial borders.

Itraconazole therapy was maintained throughout the electroporation treatment period.

Complete epithelialization was observed 50 days after the first treatment session. No recurrence was observed during a follow-up period exceeding two years (Fig. 2).

(C) Significant reduction in lesion size prior to the second electroporation session (21 days). (D) Complete epithelialization of lesions 50 days after the first treatment session.

Discussion

Feline sporotrichosis caused by Sporothrix brasiliensis is frequently associated with high fungal burden and increased virulence, which may contribute to therapeutic failure in some cases [6,7]. Although itraconazole remains the standard treatment [8], refractory infections are increasingly recognized [9].

In the present report, both cases showed minimal response to antifungal therapy prior to the use of electroporation. The recommended dose of itraconazole is approximately 10 mg/kg once daily for at least two months and is considered effective; however, in the absence of an adequate clinical response, dose escalation may be required [10]. The mechanisms underlying antifungal resistance are not fully understood, although possible genetic mutations in Sporothrix spp. have been suggested [11].

In Case 2, itraconazole was administered at progressively increased doses up to 100 mg once daily, without clinical improvement at any dosage level.

A limitation of this report is the absence of antifungal susceptibility testing, which may have contributed to the lack of response to itraconazole.

Clinical improvement was observed only after application of electrical parameters consistent with irreversible electroporation, suggesting that adequate pulse intensity may be essential for therapeutic effectiveness [12].

The observed outcomes may be related to direct cellular damage and increased local permeability, potentially enhancing antifungal effects [[13], [14], [15]]. In addition, reduction in lesion exudation and improved tissue organization were consistently observed after treatment.

Although the exact mechanism remains unclear, these findings suggest that electroporation may contribute to local disease control in refractory cases. However, controlled studies are necessary to determine its efficacy and reproducibility.

Conclusion

Electroporation may represent a useful adjunctive approach for the local management of refractory feline sporotrichosis, particularly in cases with poor response to conventional antifungal therapy.

Quantization of extraoral free flap monitoring for venous congestion with deep learning integrated iOS applications on smartphones
Authors: Chunyan Li, Wei Liu, Zhenglin Zhu, Xing Wang, Yanbin Zhang
Published: 2023

Dear Editor,

We read with much interest the article by Hsu and colleagues1 recently published in the International Journal of Surgery. The authors developed the first clinically applicable deep learning model integrated application on smartphones to quantify the condition of free flaps, and further validated the accuracy, discrimination, and calibration of the deep learning integrated smartphone application. They found that the deep learning integrated smartphone application can accurately reflect the perfusion status of free flaps, with high sensitivity and specificity. This study suggested that the deep learning model integrated iPhone Operating System (iOS) application could be a powerful tool for free flap monitoring, reducing the burden of first-line examiners and improving the decision-making process1. These inspiring findings imply that artificial intelligence is a convenient and reliable tool for free flap monitoring. We sincerely appreciate the authors’ efforts. Nonetheless, a few points are worth discussing.

First of all, flap failure is a rare but disastrous event in microvascular reconstruction surgery; hence, developing a deep learning-based predictive model to screen out high-risk patients rather than predict individual risks carries a significant value in clinical practice. Despite high sensitivity and specificity (higher than 95%), and the potential to reduce the burden on surgeons and nurses, the incorporation of deep learning techniques into free flap monitoring failed to improve the clinical outcomes. On the one hand, the deep learning model is restricted to recognizing congested flaps but not ischemic flaps, which is far from being able to replace bedside observers (e.g. nurses, residents, and surgeons). On the other hand, even with high theoretical sensitivity, considering the evaluation of the free flap is directly related to clinical decision-making, any false-native case and incorrect predictions would lead to catastrophic outcomes2. Therefore, unlike applying the deep learning model in clinical practices for wound evaluations, sensitivity other than specificity should be prioritized, and false-positive prediction should be encouraged as it will not leave out any congested flaps3. Therefore, a deep learning-based algorithm would be more helpful in categorizing and identifying subpopulations at high risk of flap failure.

Second, we are concerned about the external validity of the deep learning model, as the clinical significance of this study is limited by extrapolation and generalization of the deep learning integrated iOS application data to other institutions, especially to low-volume reconstructive microsurgery hospitals or less experienced surgeons1. This study included reconstructive microsurgeries that were performed by 10 experienced microsurgeons who are board-certified plastic surgeons and have performed high-volume reconstructive microsurgery as attending surgeons for more than 5 years, and the nurses in the ICU and the resident doctors were well-trained and experienced, who could take photographs of the free flap on the corresponding purposes1. Therefore, external validation of this study is limited to institutional healthcare settings. Data from one institution or regionally distributed samples can be demographically skewed, and deep learning algorithms can unintentionally encode biases that are not representative of the general population, which makes it challenging for cross-center generalizability4. Thus, external validation of the deep learning model in only one healthcare center, especially from highly trained surgeons and nurses, is discouraged5.

Third, instead of comparing with clinical observation or other routine clinical observation parameters (e.g. skin color, capillary refill time, skin temperature of the flap, skin turgor)6, a combination of the deep learning model with these observational parameters may develop a comprehensive prediction model. If future integration of this deep learning application into routine clinical observation, which may potentially identify the congested flaps and re-anastomose the thrombotic veins earlier1. Therefore, additional studies are warranted to explore whether integrating deep learning applications into clinical practice would lead to superior reliability on free flap monitoring, higher success, and salvage rates7.

In summary, utilizing deep learning integrated iOS applications for evaluating and monitoring the perfusion status of free flaps is an exciting development for reconstructive microsurgeries. Beyond doubt, future improvement and integration of this deep learning algorithms and applications into clinical practice will convey prognostic benefits to patients worldwide.

Effectiveness of gamification-based teaching in approach to eye trauma: a randomized educational intervention trial
Authors: Meysam Siyah Mansoory, Danial Yousefi, Seyyed Mohsen Azizi, Leila Rezaei
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
IntroductionOphthalmology education is evolving with the use of new teaching approaches. Teaching based on gamification is one of the new developments in health professions education. This study was conducted to investigate the effect of gamification-based virtual reality on the learning outcome of students regarding the highly important topic of approach to eye trauma.Materials and methodsThis randomized educational intervention trial with a control group was conducted at Kermanshah University of Medical Sciences (KUMS). A total of 10 ophthalmology residents and 60 medical students were recruited in this study (each group comprised 5 residents and 30 medical students). Individuals were randomly divided into two teaching groups gamification method (intervention group) and lecture-based method (Control group). Data were collected through questionnaires and tests and analyzed by SPSS-26 software using descriptive and analytical statistics.ResultsResidents and medical students evaluated the usability of this method to be favorable (132.72 ± 10.17). In resident students (16.80 ± 2.58, 13.60 ± 1.14, P ≤ 0.05) and medicine students (17.27 ± 4.50, 15.20 ± 0.71, P ≤ 0.05), the mean score of learning outcome score in the intervention group was higher than the control group.ConclusionGamification-based teaching is an effective educational strategy that can be used as a complementary educational tool for teaching ophthalmology residents and medical students.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12886-024-03726-w.

Introduction

The term “Gamification” is one of the emerging forms of e-learning that indicates game-based teaching and learning. In gamification, gamified content is used for teaching and achieving learning goals [1, 2]. Gamification has a close conceptual relationship with the two concepts of serious game and game-based learning. Game-based learning means achieving learning outcomes through game content, play, and learner participation in problem-solving and educational challenges. In the process of using a serious game, there is more emphasis on the concept of the game. Serious games are designed to achieve a wide range of serious goals, such as education, engineering, healthcare, emergency management, sports, and exploration [3]. The main goal of gamification is to use game components in the real environment to teach or change behavior [4, 5].

Improving academic performance, increasing social interactions, and enhancing the motivation and academic engagement of students are among the most important outcomes of using gamification in the teaching-learning process [1]. The use of gamification in the learning process is a targeted effort to guide students toward higher levels of Bloom’s taxonomy [6]. Because educational games create experiential learning and allow the learner to be engaged in the learning process, they also give the learner an analytical and critical view of the learning objectives [7, 8]. In the framework of gamification, learning happens through action. Therefore, this will lead to the promotion of deeper thinking by way of action [6].

The use of virtual reality technology in medical education has also expanded significantly. In virtual reality technology, the real environment is simulated using computer technologies. Interaction, immersion, and continuous feedback (Sensory/tactile feedback and informational feedback about performance) are three basic components of teaching using virtual reality technologies [9–11]. Gamification-based teaching and the use of virtual reality capabilities can play important roles in increasing students’ effective learning. The results of the studies conducted on the education of ophthalmology and other fields of medical sciences indicate that gamification and virtual reality technologies have a positive effect on increasing intrinsic motivation [12], academic engagement [13], clinical diagnosis [14], and academic performance [15–17].

Improving the quality of education of medical students in the field of ophthalmology can play an effective role in improving medical services for patients. In recent years, the focus on providing quality education to medical students has increased. It is believed that medical students’ education should focus on using new strategies and approaches. In teaching ophthalmology, it is very important to use innovative approaches to learning objectives, educational design, teaching methods, and evaluation [18].

Teaching ophthalmology residents and medical students in an emergency environment is challenging. The shortage of health trainers [19], the gap between theory and practice [20], and a few real cases in the process of training in the emergency environment (patients with eye trauma such as globe rupture, hyphema, photokeratitis, corneal foreign body, retrobulbar hemorrhage, and blowout fracture) are among the most important challenges mentioned.

Therefore, using the capacity of new approaches such as gamification can be one of the solutions to these challenges [21]. Gamification has high capabilities to enhance students’ involvement and participation in the learning process. In addition, it provides learners with the possibility of risk-free clinical decision-making and quick feedback [22]. Moreover, the learners can encounter many cases in the game environment and the virtual reality simulated environment. Therefore, considering the necessity of conducting more studies on the use of new technologies and approaches in ophthalmology education, this study aimed to compare the effectiveness of gamification and lecture methods in teaching eye trauma approaches. For this aim, the following hypotheses were formulated:

The gamification teaching method has a greater impact on students’ learning performance than the lecture method.The usability of teaching through gamification is above the mean level.

Designing teaching goals

The teaching goals of the intervention are to teach medical students and ophthalmology residents the important cases of eye trauma referred to ophthalmic emergencies, the dos, and don’ts of dealing with these patients, the prescription of appropriate drugs, non-prescription of inappropriate drugs from the emergency ward to transfer to the operating ward, performing surgery, and post-operation considerations.

The game process was such that with each correct answer, one point was added to the students’ points, a gold medal was given to the resident by the game, and the resident was encouraged by the clapping sound. At each stage, in case of a wrong choice, in addition to deducting points and adding to the number of wrong answers, the gamified environment would shake and induce a sense of danger to the students. The maximum error of the students during the game was set to five. If the students committed the sixth mistake, the game over message was shown to them, and if they completed all the steps of the game correctly, a motivational message was shown to them. The teaching content including different eye trauma scenarios was designed by the retinal specialist, in six steps, based on the American Academy of Ophthalmology reference, the 2020–2021 edition [23], and the ophthalmology and the general medicine curriculum.

The presented scenarios are as follows:

Case scenario 1: This scenario aims to teach medical students and residents about globe rupture.Case scenario 2: The main goal of this scenario was to teach medical students and residents appropriate treatment approaches in dealing with hyphema patients. Another goal of this stage was to teach important treatment recommendations by medical students and residents to patients, including a 45-degree increase in head height when lying down, limiting physical activity and sometimes the need to patch the damaged eye, and teaching appropriate measures to reduce eye pressure. The other important goals of this stage were familiarization with drugs that prevent rebleeding, including cycloplegic and corticosteroid drops, and avoiding prescription incorrect drugs.Case scenario 3: The general goal of this scenario was to teach medical students and residents the approach to photokeratitis. The main goal of this stage was to perform appropriate treatment and prescribe medications to reduce pain accelerate the recovery of the cornea and avoid prescribing inappropriate medications.Case scenario 4: This scenario aimed to teach medical students and residents the approach to corneal foreign body management. This step was aimed to familiarize the medical students and residents with how to examine and remove a foreign body and then treat it with topical antibiotic drops and to teach the medical students and residents not to prescribe anesthetic drops for a long time as well as oral antibiotic drugs.Case scenario 5: This scenario aims to teach medical students and residents the approach to retrobulbar hemorrhage. In this stage, the residents should adopt appropriate practical and medicinal measures, including quick prescription of medication to reduce eye pressure with eye pressure-reducing drops or mannitol serum and then performing lateral canthotomy and emergency cantholysis, followed by regular follow-up of the patient to check IOP and perform orbital CT-Select scan through the game.Case scenario 6: The goal of this scenario was to teach medical students and residents the approach to blow-out fractures. In this stage of the gamified intervention, the residents had to choose the symptoms that helped to diagnose the rupture of the ureter through the game.

3)Phase 3: evaluationInstrumentsTo investigate the usability of the gamification-based teaching method, the serious game usability scale was used [24], which was slightly modified according to the purpose of the present study. In all parts of the questionnaire, the term gamification has been replaced by the term serious game. This questionnaire is a self-reported questionnaire comprising 30 items and four subscales of satisfaction (5 Items), efficiency (9 Items), helpfulness (9 Items), and learning ability (7 Items). The items were graded based on a 5-point Likert scale. The questionnaire scores ranged from 30 to 150. The higher the score on this scale, the higher the usability of the gamification. A score higher than 90 is defined as an optimal or standard level. The assumed average score on a 5-point Likert scale is 3. With 30 items in the questionnaire, the total score is calculated by multiplying 30 by 3, resulting in a cut-off score of 90 for the questionnaire. According to the number of items specified in the questionnaire for each component, the cutoff point for the satisfaction component was 15, for the efficiency and helpfulness component was 27, and for the learning ability component was 21.

With 30 items in the questionnaire, the total score is calculated by multiplying 30 by 3, resulting in a cut-off score of 90 for the questionnaire. In the study of Siah Mansouri et al., the reliability of the instrument was calculated at the level of 0.86 [24]. The reliability of this tool during the study was obtained using Cronbach’s alpha method of 0.79. The scale’s content validity ratio and content validity index were examined from the point of view of 4 medical education experts and 3 e-learning experts. For this purpose, they were asked to evaluate the necessity of each item, the appropriateness of each item with the purpose of the study, and the clarity of each item. Finally, the content validity ratio (CVR) was 0.90 and the content validity index (CVI) was 0.80. The students’ learning level was evaluated based on a test consisting of 14 multiple-choice questions (MCQs). The scores ranged from 0 to 20.

Procedure

The gamification method randomly divided the participating medical students and ophthalmology residents into two experimental and control groups. The concept of eye trauma was taught to the control group through the lecture method. The duration of teaching was one session for 120 min. The experimental group was taught by the gamification-based teaching method. The experimental group students were asked to complete the activity in approximately 20 min. Twenty minutes was an ideal time to play the game. It was also possible to play the game for more than 20 min. The topics presented to both groups were similar, and the same professors performed the teaching. Before presenting the lesson based on the gamification-based teaching method, a training workshop was held on how to use the game system, and all the questions and ambiguities were addressed. After the completion of the training in both groups, 30 min for rest was determined according to the opinion of the students. After making sure that the students were ready, the final test was given by the retina specialist professor based on the teaching object. The obtained scores ranged from 0 to 20. Discussion

This study aimed to investigate the effect of gamification-based teaching on the learning performance of medical students and ophthalmology residents regarding the approach to eye trauma. Two objectives were pursued in this study. The first objective was to compare the mean scores of learning performance in residents and medical students in the gamification-based teaching group and the lecture group. The second objective was to investigate the usability of gamification-based teaching in the virtual reality environment from students’ point of view. According to the literature review, the present study is the first study to evaluate the effectiveness and applicability of gamification-based teaching in the approach to eye trauma.

In the rest of this section, the results of the hypotheses will be presented separately. The results of each hypothesis have been compared with the results of relatively similar studies. The purpose of comparing the results obtained in each hypothesis with the results of other studies is to provide empirical evidence to support the hypotheses of the present study.

The results of the analysis of the first hypothesis showed that the mean learning score of the students in the intervention group (gamification) was higher than the control group. The results of Yildirim’s study indicated that gamification-based teaching had a significant effect on students’ academic performance and attitudes toward the importance of learning [25]. The results of Brull’s study also indicate that gamification is an effective teaching method compared to traditional teaching methods [26]. Research indicates that the use of gamification in medical education is a very suitable tool to improve and upgrade students’ knowledge and skills [7, 27–29].

Wilson et al. used gamification and virtual reality to teach ophthalmology examinations to medical students. They showed that students who used this teaching method had a better understanding of eye examination and found this teaching model fun [30]. In 2019, Nguyen et al. used gamification and virtual reality to teach fundus examination to medical students. Their results indicated that the use of this teaching method allowed students to have a better understanding of the physiological changes inside the eye in an interactive, comprehensive, and attractive manner and to increase their clinical skills [31].

In the most recent study in 2021, which is relevant to the present process, Moon et al., used virtual reality to teach strabismus to 14 ophthalmology residents. The results of their study revealed that virtual reality-based teaching improved the clinical diagnosis of strabismus in ophthalmology residents, even in a short period of use [14]. In their study, Van Gaalen et al. also reported the effectiveness of the gamification approach in using learning materials and improving learning outcomes based on experimental evidence [17, 32]. The evidence of another study showed that teaching ophthalmology using gamification in the form of a flipped classroom method is effective on the level of knowledge and performance of students [33].

The results related to the second hypothesis showed that from the perspective of the participants, gamification was highly applicable. Evidence indicates a high level of user satisfaction with the use of the gamification approach in the teaching-learning process [34–36]. In this regard, the results of a study showed that 63% of residents were satisfied with their improved performance in the gamification-based learning process [16]. Based on the results of Dugnol‑Menéndez’s study [37], it was found that students prefer to use gamification tools in their learning and their level of satisfaction with this approach was high.

Summarizing the findings of this study and examining the evidence of other studies show that gamification-based teaching is an effective approach in medical education. Enhancing enjoyment and creativity in learning [38], increasing self-confidence in clinical diagnostic decision-making, acquiring practical skills and increasing understanding of clinical reasoning [39, 40], improving academic engagement, and increasing interaction and independence in learning are among the most important results of using gamification in medical education [1].

Trying to improve the learners’ academic performance and motivation is a continuous effort in all educational systems worldwide. Using the capabilities of new technologies such as the gamification approach in the teaching-learning process can be very helpful and promising. This issue became highly prominent during the Covid-19 pandemic. The results of a systematic review showed that gamification was able to improve the learners’ intrinsic motivation through game elements [12]. In addition, the results of another study during the COVID-19 pandemic in Mexico showed that gamification as a teaching method could positively increase students’ academic motivation [41].

Gamification-based teaching has cognitive, emotional, and social dimensions. In the cognitive dimension, the learners face cognitive challenges in the game process. They can also receive immediate feedback. In the emotional dimension, the learners receive rewards after success in the game. In the social dimension, learners cooperate in performing the game steps or solving a challenge [41]. Therefore, considering the high capability of gamification in motivating learners, increasing their engagement and participation in the learning process, and developing their cognitive, social, and emotional dimensions, it is suggested to be used as a supplementary educational method in the teaching-learning process [17].

As for the gamification-based teaching approach, the design of elements, the educational environment, and the correct selection of game elements should be performed under the learners’ academic goals and characteristics. It is suggested that educational planners and game designers pay attention to these issues in the game design process. Further studies are required to explore the academic aspects of gamification in health sciences. There is also little evidence about the effectiveness of this approach. Therefore, it is important to evaluate the effectiveness of this approach in other disciplines.

The present study faced some limitations. During the implementation process, some students experienced dizziness and headaches while using the virtual reality headset, which caused interruptions in the study process. Of course, after a little rest, they again participated in the study process. Another limitation of this study was related to the small sample size of resident students. To ensure the effectiveness of this educational method for resident students, it is suggested to use a larger sample size in future studies.

Conclusion

Using innovative approaches in medical education is an inevitable necessity in the digital age. Gamification-based teaching is an effective tool to enhance the knowledge of health science learners. The findings showed that the medical students and ophthalmology residents were highly satisfied with gamification-based teaching. Moreover, the mean score of students in the gamification-based teaching group was significantly higher. The review of other studies also indicated the effect of this approach on creating positive changes in learners’ knowledge, motivation, attitude, and behavior in the learning process. Gamification is a valuable tool that can be used as a supplement in teaching medical students and ophthalmology residents.

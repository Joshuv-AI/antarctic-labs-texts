The correlation between impaired thyroid hormone sensitivity and 10-year atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) risk score in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus
Authors: Yuqian Zhang, Juan Wang, Huaner Wang, Yefan Yang, Junni Zhu, Lihua Ma, Jingfang Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAtherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) is one of the common complications in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM). Thyroid hormones affect cardiovascular function through multiple pathways.PurposeTo explore the relationships between impaired thyroid hormone sensitivity and the 10-year risk of ASCVD in euthyroid patients with T2DM.Methods899 patients with T2DM were divided into three groups according to the different ASCVD risk levels, the difference of thyroid hormone sensitivity indices [Parametric Thyroid Feedback Quantile-based Index (PTFQI), Thyroid Feedback Quantile-based Index (TFQI), Thyroid Stimulating Hormone Index (TSHI), Serum-Free triiodothyronine to Free thyroxine (FT3/FT4) ratio, and Thyrotropin Thyroxine Resistance Index (TT4RI)] between three groups were compared.ResultsAs the 10-year ASCVD risk grade increased, TT4RI level showed a gradually rising trend, while FT3/FT4 ratio exhibited a decreasing trend (P < 0.05). PTFQI, TFQI, TSHI, and TT4RI were positively, and FT3/FT4 was negatively correlated with ASCVD risk (all P < 0.05). Thyroid hormone sensitivity indicators had a linear dose-response relationships with ASCVD risk. As the levels of PTFQI, TFQI, TSHI, and TT4RI increase and the level of FT3/FT4 decreases, the ASCVD risk showed an upward trend.ConclusionTo patients with T2DM, impaired thyroid hormone sensitivity is associated with ASCVD risk.

Introduction

Diabetes is a chronic metabolic disorder characterized by persistent hyperglycemia, with T2DM accounting for approximately 90% of the global diabetic population. 1 ASCVD is the leading cause of mortality and disability in patients with T2DM. 2 The long-term hyperglycemic environment and insulin resistance promote inflammation, vascular smooth muscle cell proliferation and atherosclerosis by increasing the production of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and oxidative stress and reducing the production of nitric oxide.3,4 Thyroid hormones regulate endothelial function by dilating blood vessels, increasing heart rate and myocardial contractility, elevating blood volume to boost cardiac output and regulate blood pressure fluctuations, and modulating lipid levels, thereby playing a crucial role in maintaining cardiovascular homeostasis.5,6 Compared to individual thyroid hormones, thyroid hormone sensitivity provides a more comprehensive and stable evaluation of thyroid function and status. Thyroid hormone sensitivity can be divided into central sensitivity and peripheral sensitivity. Impairment of central sensitivity disrupts the negative feedback pathway of the hypothalamus-pituitary-thyroid (HPT) axis, as reflected by PTFQI, TFQI, TSHI, and TT4RI7–9; while impairment of peripheral sensitivity is characterized by the reduced metabolic effect of thyroid hormones in tissues, which can be reflected by the ratio of FT3/FT4. 10 The purpose of this study is to investigate the association between thyroid hormone sensitivity and ASCVD risk in euthyroid patients with T2DM, and to provide new scientific insights for the prevention and treatment of ASCVD in individuals with T2DM.

Methods

Study subjects and methods

A total of 899 individuals with T2DM hospitalized in the Endocrinology Department of the First Hospital of Lanzhou University in China from July 2021 to June 2024 were selected. The basic information and biochemical indicators of the research subjects were collected. This study has been approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Hospital of Lanzhou University (LDYYLL-2025-2013).

Statistical analysis

All data were statistically analyzed using SPSS 27.0 software. Data that met the normal distribution were expressed as x̅ ± s. For non-normal distribution measurement data, the median and interquartile range [M(Q1,Q3)] were used. Count data were expressed as percentages, comparing differences in thyroid hormone sensitivity indices between groups. One-variable linear regression analysis was used to screen independent variables, and multiple-variable linear regression analysis was performed to adjust for confounding factors to investigate the independent correlation between thyroid hormone sensitivity indices and the 10-year ASCVD risk in patients with T2DM. Restricted cubic spline (RCS) model analyzed the dose-response relationship between thyroid hormone sensitivity index and the 10-year ASCVD risk in individuals with T2DM. The appropriate number of knots was selected to fit the RCS model based on the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC). All tests were two-sided, with the significance level α=0.05, P<0.05 indicated statistically significant differences.

Results

Simple linear regression analysis showed that age, Waist-to-Hip Ratio (WHR), diabetic duration, SBP, DBP, FIN, HOMA-IR, PTFQI, TFQI, TSHI, and TT4RI were positively correlated with the 10-year ASCVD risk [β=0.657, 17.892, 0.033, 0.351, 0.256, 0.106, 0.227, 5.671, 2.793, 1.573 and 0.039, respective all P<0.05], whereas high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) and FT3/FT4 was negatively correlated with the 10-year ASCVD risk [β=-3.673 and -12.450, both P<0.05].

After adjusting for confounding factors (age, WHR, diabetic duration, SBP, DBP, FIN, HOMA-IR and HDL-C), multivariate linear regression analysis revealed that PTFQI, TFQI, TSHI, and TT4RI remained significantly positively correlated with the 10-year ASCVD risk [β=3.071, 95%CI(1.246,4.896); β=1.975, 95%CI(0.881,3.070); β=0.622, 95%CI(0.087,1.157) and β=0.017, 95%CI(0.003,0.031), all P < 0.05], and FT3/FT4 was negatively correlated with the 10-year ASCVD risk [β=-10.362, 95%CI(-14.868, -5.855), P < 0.05 ].

To further investigate the relationship between thyroid hormone sensitivity indices and 10-year ASCVD risk, RCS curve analysis was used to examine liner relationship. After adjusting for confounding factors, a linear dose-response relationships was performed between PTFQI, TFQI, TSHI, FT3/FT4, and TT4RI and the 10-year ASCVD risk (all P overall < 0.05, P non-linear > 0.05). Discussion

Studies have shown that individuals with diabetes have a significantly higher risk of ASCVD events compared to non-diabetic individuals. Globally, approximately 32.2% of people with diabetes also suffer from cardiovascular diseases. 2 Practical tools enabling the management of risk factors and rapid risk assessment are of significant importance to clinical research. The Prediction for ASCVD Risk in China (China-PAR) is a prospective cohort study developed specifically for the Chinese population. This model integrates key characteristics of the Chinese population and incorporates multiple risk factors—including gender, age, treated or untreated systolic blood pressure (SBP), serum total cholesterol, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol waist circumference, smoking history, residential region (south/north), urban/rural residence, and family history of ASCVD—to calculate the 10-year ASCVD risk score. Based on this score, individuals can be categorized into three risk tiers: low risk (<5%), intermediate risk (5%–9.9%), and high risk (>10%). This model has been widely applied in the general population, providing valuable support for the primary prevention and management of ASCVD [12].

This study is a retrospective analysis involving euthyroid individuals with T2DM without previous ASCVD events. The 10-year ASCVD risk score was calculated using the China-PAR model, and patients were stratified into low-risk, intermediate-risk, and high-risk groups based on this score. The results indicated that as the 10-year ASCVD risk grade increased, both central and peripheral thyroid hormone sensitivity gradually decreased. The indices reflecting central thyroid hormone sensitivity (PTFQI, TFQI, TSHI, TT4RI) were independently and positively correlated with the 10-year ASCVD risk, while the ratio of FT3/FT4 reflecting peripheral thyroid hormone sensitivity was independently negatively correlated with this risk. Furthermore, linear dose-response relationships were found between PTFQI, TFQI, TSHI, FT3/FT4, TT4RI, and the 10-year ASCVD risk. Monitoring thyroid hormone sensitivity in patients with T2DM may have important clinical value for the prevention and treatment of ASCVD 11 .

This study also has certain limitations. Firstly, this is a cross-sectional study, which cannot establish a causal relationship between impaired thyroid hormone sensitivity and the 10-year ASCVD risk in individuals with T2DM Secondly, The cross-sectional nature of this study and the inclusion of patients with type 2 diabetes do not allow for definitive causality between thyroid hormone sensitivity and ASCVD or the relationship in individuals with non-diabetes. Finally, although multiple confounding factors were adjusted for in the analysis, the potential effects of diet, nutrition, and specific medications (such as lipid-lowering drugs and antidiabetic medications) on the 10-year ASCVD risk were not considered. Therefore, more multicenter prospective studies may be needed in the future to clarify the relationship between thyroid hormone sensitivity and ASCVD in patients with type 2 diabetes.

Carryover Effect of Incentive Spirometry on Respiratory Parameters in Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease Patients. A Pilot Study
Authors: Aditi L Soman, Bhavika Dungarwal
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionIncentive spirometry (IS) is a simple, well-accepted modality, widely used peri and postoperative period for prevention or management of respiratory complications by maintaining maximum inhalation and open collapsed alveoli and resolve atelectasis.1 Using IS along with routine physiotherapy care in Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD) patients is proven to be safe, tolerable, improve arterial blood gases, reduces hospital stay in patients with acute Exacerbation of COPD and improves health-related quality of life in these patients.2 As the pathophysiology of COPD causes decline in pulmonary function indices, which is reflected in clinical symptoms of patients addition of IS, an inexpensive and simple to use device offer immediate visual feedback that motivates patients to perform exercise. Use of IS as short as for 2 months has shown significant improvement in diaphragm mobility, Peak Expiratory Flow Rate (PEFR), FEV1/FVC, PaO2 in COPD patients.3 But, there have been lack of studies which highlighted the duration of effect of single session of IS on respiratory parameters like PEFR, Respiratory rate, Oxygen Saturation(SpO2), chest expansion (CE),4 Single Breath Hold Count (SBC)5 that determine lung function indirectly. Thus, this study aimed at assessing the length of duration of effectiveness i.e. carryover effect of incentive spirometry in COPD patients.ObjectivesTo assess the baseline respiratory parameters in mild-severe grade COPD patients, like respiratory rate (RR), saturation of peripheral oxygen (SPO2), chest expansion (CE), single breath count (SBC), peak expiratory flow rate (PEFR).To reassess above respiratory parameters in COPD patients after performing IS in sitting position.To assess the washout period for all the respiratory parameters after every 10 minutes for next 30 minutes.Material and methodologyThe study was initiated after IEC permission, CTRI registration and written consent of study participants. Twenty-four participants (17 Male, 7 Female), between the age of 45- 84 years suffering from mild, moderate or severe grade COPD and not having acute exacerbation for past 6 month underwent baseline measurements of RR, CE (at the 2nd, 4th, and 6th intercostal spaces), PEFR, SpO2 and SBC. Each participant then performed incentive spirometry, 10repetition* 3stes in seating position under supervision, and the same parameters were reassessed at 10,20, and 30-minutes post-intervention.ResultsAfter analyzing data by Friedman test, values of RR, CE at all three level, PEFR, SBC, SPO2, respiratory rate showed statistically significant increase (p < 0.05) as compared to pre-IS session values.ConclusionA single session of incentive spirometry can produce sustained improvements in respiratory parameters for at least 30 minutes in COPD patients. IS can be effectively integrated into acute pulmonary rehabilitation programs for COPD patients to achieve short-term gains in lung function and respiratory performance.

A multicenter randomized controlled study to evaluate the efficacy of an artificial intelligence–based electrocardiography for the detection of atrial fibrillation in patients with suspected symptoms (DEEP-AF)

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAtrial fibrillation (AF) is frequently paroxysmal and asymptomatic, leading to delayed diagnosis and missed opportunities for stroke prevention. Artificial intelligence (AI) applied to 12-lead electrocardiograms (ECGs) obtained during sinus rhythm may enable risk stratification and more efficient allocation of rhythm-monitoring resources.ObjectiveThe purpose of this study is to determine whether an AI-ECG–guided strategy improves detection of newly diagnosed AF compared with usual care in patients with symptoms suggestive of AF and no prior AF diagnosis.MethodsDEEP-AF (A Prospective, Multi-center, Randomized Clinical Trial to Evaluate the Detection of Atrial Fibrillation Using Artificial Intelligence-Enhanced Electrocardiography (SmartECG-AFrisk) Compared With Usual Care in Patients with Suspected Atrial Fibrillation) is a prospective, multicenter, randomized controlled superiority trial conducted at 13 tertiary hospitals in South Korea. A total of 1230 adults 30 years or older with symptoms suggestive of AF and no AF on screening 12-lead ECG will be randomized 1:1 to an AI-ECG–guided strategy or usual care. In the AI-ECG group, the SmartECG-AFrisk algorithm will generate a risk score (high risk: AI score ≥50; low risk: AI score <50) to support clinician-directed rhythm monitoring within guideline-concordant practice. Usual care will follow standard clinical evaluation without access to the AI score.ResultsThe primary outcome is the incidence of newly diagnosed AF within 6 months, confirmed on standard ECG modalities. Secondary outcomes include time to AF diagnosis, AF detection according to AI risk strata, health care resource utilization and diagnostic efficiency, initiation and timing of anticoagulation or antiplatelet therapy, AF burden, detection of other clinically relevant arrhythmias, arrhythmia-related procedures, clinical events, and patient satisfaction.ConclusionDEEP-AF will provide pragmatic randomized evidence on whether AI-ECG–guided risk stratification can improve AF detection and diagnostic efficiency in routine care.Trial registrationClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT07173673

Introduction

Atrial fibrillation (AF) increases the risk of ischemic stroke by approximately 5-fold, making timely diagnosis and intervention essential for effective prevention.1, 2, 3 Despite its clinical significance, AF often remains undiagnosed because it can be paroxysmal and asymptomatic in its early stages.4 This diagnostic challenge frequently delays the initiation of crucial therapies, such as oral anticoagulation, thereby leaving patients vulnerable to thromboembolic events.5

Conventional diagnostic methods for AF, including standard 12-lead electrocardiography (ECG), Holter monitoring, and wearable ECG patches, are primarily symptom-driven and have limited ability to detect intermittent AF.6 Although trials such as the mHealth Screening to Prevent Strokes (mSToPS) and large-scale wearable device studies have demonstrated that prolonged or continuous monitoring can improve AF detection in selected populations,6, 7, 8 these approaches are resource-intensive and may not be scalable across health care systems because of variability in patient adherence, cost considerations, and workflow demands.9, 10, 11 Consequently, a more proactive and efficient screening strategy is needed to identify high-risk individuals before complications occur.12

Artificial intelligence (AI) has recently emerged as a promising tool in cardiovascular medicine. Prior studies evaluating deep learning algorithms applied to sinus rhythm ECGs have shown the potential to predict latent AF risk, including single-center retrospective analyses and prospective observational trials that support the feasibility of algorithm-driven AF risk estimation.13, 14, 15, 16, 17 The SmartECG-AFrisk algorithm is a novel AI-based software algorithm that analyzes a standard 12-lead ECG obtained during sinus rhythm to estimate the likelihood of underlying paroxysmal AF.18 This approach enables targeted screening of individuals at increased risk of AF, thereby allowing more efficient use of diagnostic resources.15 However, despite these encouraging early findings, multicenter randomized controlled trials that clarify how well AI-guided AF risk stratification performs in real-world clinical settings are still relatively limited.

Therefore, DEEP-AF (A Prospective, Multi-center, Randomized Clinical Trial to Evaluate the Detection of Atrial Fibrillation Using Artificial Intelligence-Enhanced Electrocardiography (SmartECG-AFrisk) Compared With Usual Care in Patients with Suspected Atrial Fibrillation) is designed to test the hypothesis that an AI-ECG–guided screening strategy is superior to usual care in improving the diagnostic yield of new-onset AF in a symptomatic patient population. Secondary objectives include evaluating the effectiveness of the AI-guided screening strategy in a real-world clinical setting and assessing the potential cost-effectiveness of this innovative screening strategy.

Methods

Study design and setting

The DEEP-AF trial is a prospective, multicenter, randomized controlled superiority trial designed to evaluate whether an AI-enabled ECG algorithm (SmartECG-AFrisk) improves the detection rate of newly diagnosed AF compared with usual care in patients with symptoms suggestive of AF.

The study is conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice guidelines. The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of Inha University Hospital (IRB no. 2024-10-038) and by the IRB of each participating center. Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants before enrollment. The trial will be conducted at 13 tertiary hospitals in the Republic of Korea and is designed to reflect real-world clinical practice across centers with heterogeneous diagnostic resources and workflows. An overview of the study design is depicted in Figure 2. To characterize the baseline thromboembolic risk of the study population, clinical variables required to calculate the CHA2DS2-VASc score, including age, sex, history of heart failure, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, prior stroke or transient ischemic attack, and vascular disease, –will be systematically collected for all participants at the time of enrollment. These data will provide essential clinical context for assessing the generalizability of the cohort and interpreting the appropriateness of anticoagulation therapy after AF diagnosis. Randomization is performed via a centralized Web-based system to ensure assignment concealment. To maintain simplicity and efficiency within the pragmatic trial framework, randomization is performed centrally across the overall study population and is not separately stratified within each participating site. However, given the large target sample size, this approach is expected to achieve a balanced distribution of participants across treatment arms and centers.Figure 2Study protocol flow diagram. AI-ECG algorithm

The SmartECG-AFrisk algorithm was developed by clinical investigators (D.-H.K and Y.-S.B) and technically implemented through DeepCardio Inc. (Incheon, South Korea). The algorithm uses a bidirectional long short-term memory neural network to detect subtle electrical remodeling patterns during sinus rhythm.19 This investigator-initiated technology was certified as an innovative medical device (no. 24-22) by the Korean Ministry of Food and Drug Safety (MFDS) before the commencement of this trial.

Model architecture and training

The model is based on a bidirectional long short-term memory neural network, specialized in extracting complex temporal features from raw ECG data in XML format (Figure 1). The training data set consisted of 16,496 ECGs collected from a single-center cohort between 2005 and 2025. Labels for paroxysmal AF were assigned on the basis of physician-adjudicated diagnoses derived from clinical 12-lead ECG recordings. Importantly, as the present study is a prospective clinical trial with newly collected data, there is no patient-level overlap between the model development data set and evaluation cohorts.

Validation and performance

The algorithm’s development was informed by a prior deep learning model (area under the receiver operating characteristic curve 0.75)19 and was further optimized into an ensemble model. Before this trial, the optimized algorithm underwent external validation using an independent multicenter cohort comprising 20,462 ECG recordings from Seoul National University, Ajou University, Ewha Womans University Seoul, and Chungbuk National University hospitals. In this external cohort, the model achieved an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve of 0.897 (95% confidence interval, 0.892–0.901), with a sensitivity of 89.2% (95% confidence interval, 88.6–89.8), a specificity of 71.0%, and a positive predictive value of 74.6%.

Eligibility criteria

Eligible participants are adults 30 years or older who provide written informed consent, have no documented AF on a standard 12-lead ECG at screening, and report symptoms suggestive of AF (eg, palpitations, dizziness, dyspnea, syncope, or chest discomfort) within the preceding 12 months.

Participants will be excluded if they meet any of the following conditions:1.Prior diagnosis of AF or atrial flutter documented by any ECG modality or medical record.2.Presence of a cardiac implantable electronic device (eg, pacemaker, implantable cardioverter-defibrillator, or cardiac resynchronization therapy device) that may interfere with rhythm interpretation.3.Expected survival of less than 1 year because of advanced comorbid conditions (eg, advanced malignant neoplasm or end-stage organ failure).4.Inability to provide informed consent because of cognitive impairment, language barrier, or other reasons judged by the investigator.5.Inability to comply with study procedures or scheduled follow-up because of geographic, medical, or social constraints.

AI-ECG–guided group (n = 615)

All participants undergo standard 12-lead ECG, and the resulting ECG recordings are analyzed by the SmartECG-AFrisk algorithm. On the basis of the AI-derived risk score, participants are classified as high-risk (AI score ≥50) or low-risk (AI score <50). To ensure consistency across 13 participating centers and minimize uncontrolled variability in clinician behavior, we established a standardized escalation protocol for the high-risk group.

While individualized clinical judgment is preserved, intensive rhythm monitoring (eg, 12-lead ECG, 24-hour Holter monitoring, patch-type ECG monitoring, or clinician-confirmed single-lead ECG) is strongly recommended when a high-risk score is accompanied by any of the following objective clinical triggers defined in the study protocol. Usual care group (n = 615)

Participants will receive usual care according to contemporary guidelines and physician judgment. Decisions regarding further diagnostic testing for AF (eg, 12-lead ECG, 24-hour Holter monitoring, patch-type ECG monitoring, or clinician-confirmed single-lead ECG) are made without access to the AI-ECG risk score. In this group, escalation of rhythm monitoring is guided by the same clinical principles, such as symptomatic recurrence or significant subclinical arrhythmic findings, as those in the AI-guided group, but is determined solely by routine clinical practice and the treating physician’s discretion. This group serves as a baseline for comparing the efficacy of the AI-guided strategy.

Outcomes

Primary end point

The primary efficacy end point is the incidence of newly diagnosed AF within 6 months of randomization. AF is defined according to current guidelines as any episode of an irregularly irregular rhythm without discernible P waves, lasting at least 30 seconds, documented on any form of ECG recording (eg, standard 12-lead ECG, Holter monitoring, wearable ECG patches, or clinician-confirmed single-lead ECG).

Secondary end points

The secondary end points include the following:•Differences in AF detection rates within 6 months between high-risk (AI score ≥50) and low-risk (AI score <50) participants in the AI-ECG group.•Time from randomization to first diagnosis of AF.•Incidence of a composite of major clinical events, including ischemic stroke or transient ischemic attack, systemic embolism, hemorrhagic stroke, major adverse events related to AF or atrial flutter, and all-cause mortality.•Rate and timing of initiation of oral anticoagulation or antiplatelet therapy after AF diagnosis.•Health care resource utilization, defined as the number of ECG monitoring tests required per AF diagnosis.•Distribution of AF burden (percentage of time in AF) among participants diagnosed with AF.•Detection rate of other clinically significant arrhythmias (eg, premature atrial contraction, premature ventricular contraction, atrioventricular block, supraventricular tachycardia, or ventricular tachycardia).•Rate of arrhythmia-related procedures.•Patient satisfaction with the AI-ECG screening process, assessed using a structured questionnaire.

Outcome adjudication

AF is defined according to international guidelines as a documented irregular rhythm without P waves lasting at least 30 seconds on an ECG.24,25 As this is a pragmatic trial, the primary diagnosis of AF will be made by board-certified cardiologists at each participating site during routine clinical evaluation. To ensure consistency across all 13 centers, standardized ECG criteria will be applied. In cases of diagnostic uncertainty or ambiguity, the ECG recordings will be referred to a centralized adjudication committee consisting of 3 independent experienced electrophysiologists. For these cases, adjudicators will be blinded to the participants’ group assignments to ensure an unbiased and objective evaluation.

Sample size calculation

The sample size was calculated on the basis of the primary end point, the incidence of newly diagnosed AF within 6 months of randomization. According to prior AF screening studies using single-lead ECG, repeated ECG recordings, or 24-hour Holter monitoring in general clinical populations, the detection rate of new AF ranged from 1.7% to 4.8%.26 On the basis of the upper bound of this range, the control group detection rate was assumed to be 4%. For the AI-ECG group, previous studies reported a 2.7- to 4-fold increase in detection rates when intensive ECG monitoring was applied to maintain clinical realism within the Korean health care setting; this effect was conservatively set at a 2-fold increase (8%).8,15,26 In the context of our AI algorithm, which is currently undergoing multicenter retrospective validation as described in the Artificial intelligence-enhanced 12-lead electrocardiography for identifying atrial fibrillation during sinus rhythm (AIAFib) trial protocol,18 we conservatively assumed a 2-fold increase (8%) in detection rate on the basis of its preliminary performance in internal data sets. These assumptions formed the basis of the statistical hypothesis test and sample size estimation. While this effect size is informed by these retrospective frameworks and preliminary data, the prospective validation of the algorithm’s real-world clinical performance–given the lack of previously published outcome data–remains a key, albeit implicit, objective of the DEEP-AF trial.

A 2-sided Z test for proportions with a significance level (α) of .05 and 80% power was used to detect an absolute difference of 4 percentage points between groups. Based on these parameters, a minimum of 553 participants per group was required to demonstrate statistical significance. To account for an anticipated 10% attrition rate, the final target enrollment was set at 1230 participants (615 participants per group). All calculations were performed using PASS version 24.0.4 (NCSS, LLC, Kaysville, UT).

This approach reflects the superiority hypothesis of the trial. The effect size was intentionally conservative to balance statistical power and real-world applicability, minimizing the risk of type II error that could occur if the true difference were smaller than overly optimistic expectations. Sensitivity analyses will be performed to evaluate robustness under alternative assumptions for AF incidence and loss to follow-up.

Statistical analysis

Baseline characteristics will be presented as mean ± standard deviation or median with interquartile range for continuous variables and count with percentage for categorical variables. Between-group comparisons will be evaluated using parametric or nonparametric tests, as appropriate.

The primary end point, the incidence of newly diagnosed AF within 6 months of randomization, will be compared between the AI-ECG and usual care groups using the 2-sided χ2 test at a significance level (α) of .05. A prespecified adjusted analysis will be performed using multivariable logistic regression to estimate odds ratios and 95% confidence intervals, adjusting for clinically relevant baseline covariates, including age, sex, hypertension, diabetes, and study site, to account for the nonstratified randomization.

Time-to-event outcomes will be analyzed using Kaplan-Meier estimates and compared using log-rank tests. Cox proportional hazards models will be used to calculate hazard ratios and 95% confidence intervals. Sensitivity analyses will assess the impact of differences in ECG monitoring frequency between groups and evaluate the robustness of findings under alternative assumptions. Secondary and exploratory outcomes will be analyzed using appropriate parametric or nonparametric methods.

All analyses will follow the intention-to-treat principle, with per-protocol analyses performed as supportive. Missing data will be addressed primarily through complete case analysis, with multiple imputations performed as a sensitivity analysis. All statistical analyses will be performed using SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc.). 2-sided P values <.05 will be considered statistically significant. Importantly, DeepCardio Inc. has no role in patient enrollment, site management, outcome adjudication, statistical analysis, or manuscript preparation.

To account for potential heterogeneity across the 13 participating centers due to differences in ECG vendors and clinical workflows, sensitivity analyses by study site will be performed. These analyses will evaluate the consistency of the AI-guided strategy’s effectiveness and the algorithm’s performance across different clinical environments.

Discussion

Despite substantial advances in ECG monitoring technologies, the overall yield of AF detection in contemporary screening strategies remains disappointingly low. Prior studies have consistently shown that single time point ECG assessments detect AF in fewer than 2% of screened individuals, while repeated measurements or short-term ambulatory monitoring increase detection only modestly, typically remaining below 5% in unselected or opportunistically screened populations. Even prolonged monitoring approaches, including patch-based ECG devices and consumer-driven wearable technologies, are constrained by inefficient allocation of monitoring resources, resulting in low absolute AF detection rates at the population level. Large-scale trials such as the mSToPS randomized clinical trial and the Apple Heart Study clearly illustrate this paradox: although intensive monitoring increases AF detection relative to usual care, the absolute proportion of newly diagnosed AF remains small, highlighting the inherent inefficiency of broadly applied screening strategies without effective risk stratification.7,8,27

In response to these limitations, recent clinical guidelines increasingly emphasize the importance of targeted risk-based approaches to AF screening. The 2024 European Society of Cardiology guidelines recommend routine rhythm assessment in older individuals and suggest consideration of prolonged noninvasive ECG monitoring in selected high-risk populations while simultaneously acknowledging the lack of consensus regarding optimal screening modalities and the limited evidence supporting cost-effectiveness.24 Moreover, the paroxysmal and unpredictable nature of AF fundamentally limits screening paradigms that rely solely on rhythm capture during monitoring periods. In this context, AI-enabled ECG analysis represents a biologically plausible strategy to identify subclinical electrophysiological signatures of atrial vulnerability during sinus rhythm. By enriching the screened population for individuals with a higher latent risk of AF, this approach has the potential to improve detection efficiency, reduce unnecessary monitoring, and align AF screening more closely with contemporary guideline priorities and health care resource constraints.28

Against this background, the DEEP-AF trial represents a novel attempt to integrate AI-based ECG risk prediction into routine cardiovascular care. By leveraging existing ECG infrastructure, the SmartECG-AFrisk algorithm offers a potentially scalable, low-cost, and implementable strategy to identify individuals at increased risk of AF without requiring upfront expansion of monitoring capacity. The trial’s pragmatic, multicenter, randomized controlled superiority design is intended to enhance the generalizability of findings across heterogeneous real-world practice environments. Importantly, DEEP-AF is designed to evaluate the effectiveness of an AI-guided clinical strategy rather than the stand-alone diagnostic performance of the AI algorithm. By integrating AI-derived risk estimates into clinician-directed rhythm monitoring pathways, the trial reflects the real-world implementation of AI-assisted care. In addition, the inclusion of resource utilization–based measures, cost-related analyses, and patient-centered outcomes is expected to yield evidence that is clinically meaningful and relevant to policy decisions.15

From a clinical perspective, earlier AF detection may facilitate timely initiation of anticoagulation, thereby reducing preventable thromboembolic events.1,2 Compared with broadly applied prolonged monitoring strategies that can be resource-intensive, AI-guided risk stratification may improve diagnostic efficiency by focusing monitoring resources on patients most likely to harbor undiagnosed intermittent AF.15,29 Importantly, because DEEP-AF is conducted within the Korean health care system, the resulting data may be especially informative, given differences in monitoring accessibility, reimbursement structures, and outpatient workflows that can meaningfully affect implementation.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, blinding is not feasible in this pragmatic trial because AI-derived risk stratification informs downstream monitoring decisions, which may introduce ascertainment bias and differential testing intensity between groups. Second, the a priori performance characteristics of the SmartECG-AFrisk algorithm, which informed the trial’s design and sample size calculation, were based on internal data and the multicenter retrospective study protocol.18 Given that the final results of this retrospective validation have not yet been published in a peer-reviewed journal, the current trial represents a necessary prospective evaluation of the algorithm’s efficacy. Therefore, the prospective validation of the algorithm’s real-world clinical performance is a key, albeit implicit, objective of the DEEP-AF trial. Third, the inclusion criterion of age 30 years was selected in accordance with the approved indication for use of SmartECG-AFrisk by the Korean Ministry of Food and Drug Safety (MFDS), thereby allowing evaluation of the AI-guided strategy within its intended regulatory and clinical population. However, because AF is relatively uncommon in younger adults, inclusion of younger participants may reduce the overall AF detection rate compared to studies enriched for older populations. Nevertheless, DEEP-AF specifically enrolls symptomatic patients presenting to tertiary cardiology clinics, representing a population with a substantially higher pretest probability of occult AF than the general population. Furthermore, the predictive performance of the AI algorithm may vary across patient subgroups and participating centers. Because the study is conducted across multiple institutions using different ECG vendors and clinical workflows, some degree of data drift may occur across study sites, potentially influencing both detection yield and efficiency. Fourth, planned follow-up is designed to evaluate diagnostic yield rather than long-term hard outcomes; therefore, end points such as stroke reduction and mortality will not be directly assessed within the primary follow-up window. Nevertheless, the focus on objectively confirmed AF diagnoses, coupled with secondary analyses of health care resource utilization, cost-related metrics, and patient-reported satisfaction, will provide a comprehensive assessment of both clinical impact and operational feasibility in routine practice. To ensure sufficient study power, the steering committee will monitor event rates; if necessary, contingency measures such as extending follow-up via protocol amendments may be considered.

Beyond individual patient care, the DEEP-AF trial will have broader implications for public health and health care policy. If an AI-guided screening strategy improves AF detection with acceptable efficiency, it could support scalable AF detection initiatives in primary care and specialty settings, particularly in populations at elevated risk of cardioembolic stroke. Such evidence could inform future guideline recommendations, support equitable access to earlier diagnosis, and contribute to more efficient allocation of limited health care resources.

Conclusion

The DEEP-AF trial is designed to prospectively evaluate whether integrating an AI-enabled 12-lead ECG risk stratification algorithm into routine clinical practice can improve the detection of previously undiagnosed AF in symptomatic patients. By combining pragmatic randomization, guideline-concordant care pathways, and targeted rhythm monitoring based on AI-derived risk, this study aims to address key limitations of contemporary AF screening strategies, including low diagnostic yield and inefficient resource utilization. Beyond diagnostic performance, DEEP-AF will provide important insights into time to diagnosis, downstream clinical management, health care resource use, cost-related efficiency, and patient acceptability. The findings are expected to inform future guideline-directed screening strategies and support scalable real-world implementation of AI-guided approaches for AF detection.

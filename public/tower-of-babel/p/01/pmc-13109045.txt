Danshensu Ethyl Ester Induces Ferroptosis Through Targeted Inhibition of SLC7A11 Transport Function in NSCLC
Authors: Rui Yan, Sen Xu, Meng Yan, Dongyang Wu, Jingwen Zhang, Chunsheng Zhang, Jiale Liu, You‐Jie Li, Jiankai Feng, Gui‐Wu Qu, Shu‐Yang Xie
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundFerroptosis is a novel research avenue for cancer therapy. The roles of Danshensu derivatives remain unclear. This study investigated the effect of Danshensu Ethyl Ester (DEE) on ferroptosis in non‐small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) cells.MethodsNSCLC cells were treated with DEE to assess ferroptosis markers, including reactive oxygen species (ROS), malondialdehyde (MDA), glutathione (GSH), and mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP), along with related protein expression. Molecular docking and dynamics simulations predicted the DEE‐SLC7A11 interaction. Intracellular cysteine levels were quantified by ELISA. The functional involvement of SLC7A11 was further verified through its knockdown and overexpression. The in vivo antitumor activity of DEE was assessed using a nude mouse xenograft model.ResultsDEE treatment dose‐dependently promoted cell death in A549 and H1299 cells, accompanied by increased levels of ROS and MDA, reduced GSH and MMP, and downregulated expression of SLC7A11 and GPX4. These effects were reversed by ferroptosis inhibitor Ferrostatin‐1 (Fer‐1), confirming ferroptosis involvement. Mechanistically, DEE directly inhibited the transport function of SLC7A11 in a p53‐independent manner and also decreased p53 protein levels in wild‐type A549 cells. Consistently, DEE reduced intracellular cysteine content. Genetic silencing of SLC7A11 enhanced DEE‐induced ferroptosis, whereas its overexpression attenuated the effect. In vivo, DEE significantly suppressed tumor growth in a xenograft model, exhibiting efficacy comparable to that of paclitaxel.ConclusionsDEE inhibits NSCLC progression by inducing ferroptosis through the targeted inhibition of SLC7A11 transport function via a p53‐independent pathway, highlighting its potential as a novel therapeutic agent for NSCLC.

Introduction

Lung cancer remains a leading cause of global cancer mortality. With over 2 million new cases annually, it constitutes 11.6% of all cancers and has a 5‐year survival rate of only 23% [1]. Its predominant subtype, non‐small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), accounts for approximately 85% of cases [2]. In China, lung cancer exhibits the highest incidence and mortality rates among all malignancies [3]. Current primary treatments, including surgery, chemotherapy (e.g., cisplatin, paclitaxel), radiotherapy, and targeted therapies (e.g., erlotinib, gefitinib), are significantly limited by frequent late‐stage diagnosis, severe adverse effects, and the inevitable development of drug resistance [2, 4, 5, 6]. Although targeted agents initially show efficacy, acquired resistance driven by tumor heterogeneity severely compromises long‐term outcomes [7]. Therefore, identifying novel therapeutic targets and mechanisms is crucial for improving NSCLC treatment. In this context, ferroptosis—an iron‐dependent, lipid peroxidation‐driven form of regulated cell death—has emerged as a promising research avenue, offering potential to overcome therapy resistance [8].

While inducing tumor cell death is a primary goal of cancer treatment, pro‐apoptotic strategies in NSCLC remain under clinical investigation [9]. Ferroptosis, a distinct iron‐dependent cell death driven by lethal lipid peroxidation and glutathione (GSH) depletion [8], is closely linked to tumor metabolism. The elevated ROS and lipid metabolism in cancer cells heighten their susceptibility to this process [10]. Growing evidence underscores the therapeutic potential of modulating ferroptosis in cancer [11]. It has been shown to suppress malignancy in cancers including hepatocellular carcinoma and breast cancer [12, 13, 14, 15, 16], and can synergize with conventional therapies [17]. In NSCLC, dysregulated ferroptosis is associated with tumor progression and therapy resistance [18], highlighting its promise as a novel therapeutic strategy.

Current ferroptosis‐targeted drug development focuses on key nodes including the GSH system, GPX4, and iron metabolism, representing a core direction for anti‐NSCLC strategies [19]. Compounds like artemisinin induce ferroptosis by promoting ROS accumulation [20], while atractylodin modulates regulators such as ACSL4 and GPX4 [21]. Central to this process is the system Xc−/GSH/GPX4 antioxidant pathway, wherein the SLC7A11/SLC3A2 heterodimer imports cystine for GSH synthesis, suppressing lipid peroxidation and ferroptosis [22]. SLC7A11, a critical component of this system, is often overexpressed in tumors like NSCLC and correlates with poor prognosis [23]. This approximately 501‐amino‐acid protein contains 12 transmembrane helices forming a functional transport channel [24]. It mediates extracellular cystine uptake, which is reduced to cysteine—a rate‐limiting precursor for GSH biosynthesis [25]. GSH, as an essential cofactor for GPX4, neutralizes phospholipid peroxides and protects cells from ferroptosis [26]. Importantly, agents such as metformin and tanshinone IIA can induce ferroptosis in cancer cells by inhibiting SLC7A11 [13, 27], validating it as a promising therapeutic target for NSCLC.

Natural compounds, particularly from traditional Chinese medicine (TCM), are valuable sources for novel anti‐cancer agents with unique mechanisms and favorable toxicity profiles [28]. Salvia miltiorrhiza Bunge is a well‐documented TCM herb [29]. Its active component, Danshensu (3‐(3′,4′‐dihydroxyphenyl)‐(2R)‐lactic acid, DSS), exhibits various pharmacological activities [30]. To potentially enhance its bioactivity, our laboratory synthesized an esterified derivative, DEE, anticipating improved cellular permeability due to higher lipophilicity [31]. Preliminary studies by our group have shown that DEE possesses significant anti‐inflammatory and antioxidant properties [32]. However, the role and mechanism of DEE in the treatment of NSCLC are still unknown. This study aims to investigate whether DEE induces ferroptosis in NSCLC cells by targeting SLC7A11, so as to provide new candidate drugs and experimental evidence for the treatment of NSCLC.

Materials and Methods

Cell Culture and Drug Application

The human bronchial epithelial cell line (BEAS‐2B) and the NSCLC cell lines (A549 and NCI‐H1299) were obtained from the Shanghai Institute of Biochemistry and Cell Biology, Chinese Academy of Sciences. BEAS‐2B cells were cultured in Dulbecco's Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM; Procell), while A549 and H1299 cells were maintained in RPMI‐1640 medium (Procell). All media were supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; Procell) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (Procell). Cells were incubated at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO2, and the medium was refreshed every 2–3 days.

DEE (purity > 98%, MW = 226.25 g/mol) was synthesized and characterized in our laboratory as previously described [31]. A stock solution of DEE was prepared in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) and diluted to the indicated final concentrations (0–60 μM) for cell treatments. The ferroptosis inhibitor ferrostatin‐1 (Fer‐1, 10 μM; Meilun Bio), the ferroptosis inducer erastin (10 μM; Beyotime), and paclitaxel (10 mg/kg for in vivo studies; Solarbio) were prepared according to manufacturers' instructions.

MTT Experiments

Cell viability was assessed using the MTT assay. BEAS‐2B, A549, and H1299 cells were seeded into 96‐well plates (1 × 104 cells/well) and treated with DEE (0, 5, 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60 μM) for 48 h. Subsequently, 10 μL of MTT reagent (5 mg/mL, Sigma) was added to each well and incubated for 4 h. The resulting formazan crystals were dissolved in 100 μL DMSO, and the absorbance was measured at 570 nm using a microplate reader (Thermo Fisher Scientific).

CCK‐8 Experiments

A549 and H1299 cells were plated in 96‐well plates (1 × 104 cells/well) and treated with DEE or its parent compound DSS (0, 10, 30 μM) for 48 h. Then, 10 μL of CCK‐8 reagent (Biosharp) was added to each well, followed by incubation for 2 h at 37°C. The absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader (Tecan).

Western Blot

Total protein was extracted using RIPA buffer from cultured cells or approximately 30–40 mg of tumor tissues. Protein samples were separated by SDS‐PAGE (8%–12% gels) and transferred onto PVDF membranes (Millipore). After blocking with 5% non‐fat milk for 2 h, membranes were incubated overnight at 4°C with primary antibodies against SLC7A11 (1:1000, ABclonal, A2413), GPX4 (1:500, Abmart, T56959), P53 (1:2000, Proteintech, 21891‐1‐AP), β‐actin (1:3000, Biosharp, AP0060), or GAPDH (1:3000, Bioworld, AP0063). Following incubation with HRP‐conjugated secondary antibodies, protein bands were visualized using an ECL substrate (Beyotime) and quantified with ImageJ software.

Real‐Time Quantitative PCR (qRT‐PCR)

Total RNA was extracted from A549 and H1299 cells treated with DEE (15 μM, 30 μM) or erastin (10 μM) for 48 h using Trizol reagent (Takara). cDNA was synthesized using a reverse transcription kit (Vazyme). qRT‐PCR was performed with specific primers (Table 1) and SYBR Green mix. Gene expression levels were normalized to GAPDH and calculated using the 2^(−ΔΔCt) method.

Assay of MDA

Frozen tumor tissues were thawed, and cell lysates were prepared using RIPA buffer (Beyotime). Protein concentration was determined by a BCA kit (Solarbio). For MDA measurement, samples were mixed with the working solution from an MDA assay kit (Beyotime, S0131S). The mixture was vortexed, heated at 100°C for 15 min, cooled, and centrifuged at 1000g for 10 min. Then, 250 μL of the supernatant was transferred to a 96‐well plate, and the absorbance was measured at 532 nm. MDA content was normalized to the total protein concentration.

ROS Detection

Intracellular ROS levels were measured using the fluorescent probe DCFH‐DA (Solarbio). After treating A549 and H1299 cells for 48 h, cells were incubated with 10 μM DCFH‐DA at 37°C for 20 min. Following PBS washes, cells were harvested and analyzed immediately by flow cytometry (Beckman Coulter). Data were processed using FlowJo software.

Measurement of Mitochondrial Membrane Potential (MMP)

MMP was assessed using the JC‐1 staining kit (Solarbio). A549 and H1299 cells were treated as indicated for 48 h. After washing with PBS, cells were incubated with JC‐1 staining working solution at 37°C for 20 min. The supernatant was removed, cells were washed twice with JC‐1 staining buffer, and then covered with fresh medium. Fluorescence imaging was performed using a microplate detection system (Leica).

GSH Assay

Cellular GSH levels were measured using a commercial assay kit (Beyotime, S0053). According to the manufacturer's instructions, the sample was mixed with the provided working solutions in a 96‐well plate and incubated for 5 min. After adding 50 μL of 0.5 mg/mL NADPH solution and mixing thoroughly, the absorbance at 405 nm was recorded with a microplate reader.

Molecular Docking

Molecular docking was performed to predict the binding mode and affinity between DEE and SLC7A11. The 3D structure of DEE was energy‐minimized using the MMFF94 force field and prepared in PDBQT format with AutoDockTools 1.5.6. The crystal structure of human SLC7A11 (PDB ID: 7EPZ) was obtained from the RCSB PDB database (https://www.rcsb.org/). A docking grid was defined, and automated docking was executed using AutoDock Vina to evaluate the binding free energy. The resulting complexes were visualized and analyzed with PyMOL (https://pymol.org/2/) and Ligplot+. Use PyMOL to mutate key residues, and then import the initial mutant model into SPDBV for optimization followed by re‐analysis.

Molecular Dynamics Simulation (MDS)

MDS were conducted using GROMACS 2024.1. The ligand topology parameters for DEE were generated using the GAFF force field via the Sobtop program, while the protein was described with the Amber14SB force field. The complex was solvated in a TIP3P water box, neutralized with ions, and energy‐minimized. After NVT and NPT equilibration, a 100 ns production simulation was performed. Trajectories were analyzed for root mean square deviation (RMSD), root mean square fluctuation (RMSF), radius of gyration (Rg), solvent‐accessible surface area (SASA), and hydrogen bonds.

Cysteine Test

A549 cells, including those transfected with si‐SLC7A11, were treated with 30 μM DEE in serum‐free medium for 6, 12, and 24 h. Cells were then lysed with RIPA buffer (Beyotime), and the supernatant was collected after centrifugation. Protein concentration was determined using a BCA kit (Solarbio). Subsequently, intracellular cysteine levels were measured with a commercial assay kit (Beyotime, S0145S) by incubating the sample with working reagents and reading the absorbance at 412 nm.

siRNA Transfection

SLC7A11‐specific siRNA and negative control siRNA (NC‐siRNA) were synthesized by Tsingke Biotechnology. A549 cells were transfected at 60%–70% confluence using a transfection reagent (Polyplus‐transfection) with a final siRNA concentration of 20 nM. Transfected cells were harvested for subsequent experiments after 48 h.

In Vivo Experiment

All animal procedures were approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Binzhou Medical University (Approval No. 2023‐424) and conducted in accordance with NIH guidelines. Female BALB/c nude mice (4–5 weeks old, n = 25) were housed under SPF conditions. To establish xenograft models, A549 cells (1 × 107) were subcutaneously injected into the right flank [33]. When tumor volumes reached approximately 100 mm3, mice were randomly divided into four groups: Control, DEE (30 mg/kg), DEE (60 mg/kg) [34], and positive control (Paclitaxel, 10 mg/kg) [35]. Based on the reported LD50 values for paclitaxel (> 85 mg/kg) [36] and DSS (2356.33 mg/kg) [37], we infer that DEE, as a DSS derivative, may share a similar LD50. Thus, 30 mg/kg or 60 mg/kg of DEE, and 10 mg/kg paclitaxel were administered via intraperitoneal injection every 2 days for 30 days. Mice were then euthanized, tumors were excised and weighed, and tissues were processed for further analysis.

Hematoxylin–Eosin (HE) Staining

Tumor tissues were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde (Biosharp) for 24 h, dehydrated through a graded ethanol series, paraffin‐embedded, sectioned, and stained with HE (Beyotime). Images were acquired using a microscope (Leica).

Immunohistochemical (IHC) Staining

After dewaxing, tissue sections were treated with 3% H2O2, subjected to antigen retrieval, and blocked with 5% BSA. The sections were then incubated overnight at 4°C with an anti‐SLC7A11 antibody (ABclonal). After washing, a biotin‐labeled secondary antibody and strep‐avidin‐biotin‐peroxidase complex (SABC, Solarbio) were applied. Staining was visualized using DAB (Solarbio), and nuclei were counterstained with hematoxylin (Beyotime). Images were finally captured under a microscope (Leica).

Statistical Analysis

Results are expressed as mean ± standard deviation. The differences between groups were assessed using One‐way Analysis of Variance (ANOVA). If the ANOVA results indicated significant differences, comparisons between groups were further conducted using Tukey's Honestly Significant Difference test. A p‐value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All calculated results were statistically analyzed using GraphPad Prism 10 software.

Results

Effects of DEE on Cell Viability, as Well as Its Pharmacodynamic Comparison With DSS

DEE was prepared by esterification of DSS, a constituent of the Chinese herb Salvia miltiorrhiza , with ethanol (Figure 1A). We have verified the purity of the obtained compound as 98.57% by HPLC analysis, and both 1H and 13C NMR spectroscopy confirmed its structure [31]. We first assessed the cytotoxic effects of DEE on normal human bronchial epithelial BEAS‐2B cells and its impact on the viability of A549 and H1299 NSCLC cells. MTT assays revealed that DEE inhibited the viability of A549 and H1299 cells in a dose‐dependent manner, while showing no significant toxicity toward BEAS‐2B cells at 30 μM. This concentration was therefore selected for subsequent experiments as it effectively suppressed cancer cell viability without harming normal cells (Figure 1B–D). Comparative CCK‐8 assays demonstrated that DEE, the bioactive derivative of DSS, possessed significantly stronger anti‐proliferative activity against both NSCLC cell lines than its parent compound (Figure 1E–H). Furthermore, a cell migration assay confirmed that 30 μM DEE remarkably impaired the migratory capacity of A549 cells (Figure 1I). Collectively, these results preliminarily indicate that DEE can promote the death of A549 and NCI‐H1299 cell lines and suppress their basic cellular activities.

DEE Facilitates Ferroptosis in NSCLC Cells

Ferroptosis, a lipid peroxidation‐driven regulated cell death, is distinct from apoptosis and necrosis and plays a crucial role in suppressing tumors [11]. Its modulation is a promising strategy in NSCLC targeted therapy. To determine if DEE induces ferroptosis in a p53‐dependent or independent manner, we analyzed key biomarkers—ROS, MDA, GSH, mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP), GPX4, p53, and SLC7A11—in A549 and H1299 cells [38]. Treatment of A549 and H1299 cells with DEE for 48 h resulted in a decrease in intracellular GSH levels (Figure 2A,B) and a significant accumulation of MDA (Figure 2C,D), while DEE was also found to induce an increase in ROS production in A549 cells (Figure 2E,F). JC‐1 staining showed that DEE treatment reduced MMP (Figure 2G,H), indicated by increased green fluorescence, demonstrating impaired mitochondrial integrity. At the molecular level, DEE downregulated the protein and mRNA expression of the ferroptosis‐related factors GPX4 and SLC7A11 (Figure 3A–F). To explore how DEE downregulates SLC7A11, we examined its upstream regulator p53 [39]. While DEE increased p53 protein in wild‐type A549 cells, it also reduced SLC7A11 in p53‐null H1299 cells. This indicates that DEE induces ferroptosis via a p53‐independent, likely multi‐targeted pathway (Figure 3A).

Notably, the series of intracellular ferroptotic phenomena induced by DEE were reversed by the ferroptosis inhibitor Fer‐1, indirectly indicating that DEE exerts its effect through the ferroptosis pathway. The ferroptosis activator erastin, used as a positive control, elicited ferroptosis to a comparable extent as DEE, whereas the level induced by DSS was lower. These results demonstrate that esterified danshensu (DEE) possesses superior efficacy compared to unesterified danshensu (DSS).

DEE Targetedly Inhibits the Ferroptosis Pathway Protein SLC7A11, Thereby Promoting the Induction of Ferroptosis

Given that SLC7A11 is downregulated even in p53‐null H1299 cells, we hypothesized that DEE primarily targets SLC7A11. SLC7A11, an amino acid transporter frequently upregulated in tumors, is closely associated with cancer progression [40]. We therefore propose that DEE promotes ferroptosis by targeting SLC7A11. To test this, we examined their binding mechanism. Molecular docking and dynamics simulations confirmed a strong binding affinity between DEE and the SLC7A11 protein. The molecular docking results (Figure 4A) showed a binding free energy of −6.6 kcal/mol, suggesting that the two could form a stable complex structure. Further 100 ns MD simulations validated and further corroborated this conclusion from multiple perspectives: the RMSD curve of the complex system (Figure 4B) indicated that the structure rapidly reached an equilibrium state (0.2373 ± 0.0314 nm), demonstrating that DEE binding did not cause significant perturbations to the protein backbone; RMSF analysis (Figure 4C) revealed that DEE induced local rigidification in the active pocket region (e.g., Ala60, Ile142) while regulating the dynamic properties of distal flexible regions through allosteric effects; Rg (Figure 4D) (2.2734 ± 0.0124 nm) and SASA (Figure 4E) (211.47 ± 3.36 nm2) analyses showed that the complex structure was more compact with reduced solvent‐exposed area; hydrogen bond analysis (Figure 4F,G) (Table 2) indicated an increase in the number of intramolecular hydrogen bonds in the protein (370.54 ± 9.33), and 3–4 stable hydrogen bonds were maintained between the protein and ligand, among which the hydrogen bond occupancies of TYR244(O) and ALA245(O) were as high as 95.08% and 71.77%, respectively, forming key binding sites. PCA analysis (Figure 4H) showed a more concentrated conformational distribution of the complex, and MMPBSA calculation (Figure 4I) yielded a total binding free energy of −18.53 kcal/mol, with gas‐phase electrostatic interactions (−43.95 kcal/mol) serving as the major driving force. Residue energy decomposition (Figure 4J) further confirmed ALA245 (−1.80 kcal/mol), TYR244 (−1.33 kcal/mol), etc., as hot‐spot residues for binding. Collectively, these results clarify the molecular mechanism by which DEE forms a stable complex with SLC7A11 through hydrogen bond networks, hydrophobic interactions, and allosteric effects from the perspectives of structural dynamics and energetics. To assist in verifying the inhibitory effect of DEE on the transport function of the SLC7A11 protein, the intracellular cysteine content in A549 cells was detected at 6 h, 12 h, 24 h, and 48 h after drug treatment (Figure 4K). It was found that the cysteine level gradually decreased with the increase of time gradient after DEE administration, further confirming that DEE inhibits the transport function of SLC7A11.

To validate the critical role of Tyr244 and Ala245 in ligand binding, site‐directed mutagenesis was performed to replace Tyr244 with alanine (Y244A) and Ala245 with phenylalanine (A245F). Molecular docking revealed that the Y244A mutation resulted in a binding free energy of −6.1 kcal/mol, which is 0.5 kcal/mol higher (less favorable) than that of the wild‐type protein (−6.6 kcal/mol). In contrast, the A245F mutation showed no significant change, warranting further kinetic simulation (Figure 5A,B). Subsequently, 100 ns MD simulations showed that Y244A had a higher RMSD (0.2409 nm) than wild‐type, while A245F had a lower RMSD (0.2218 nm), indicating increased flexibility for Y244A due to aromatic ring loss and increased rigidity for A245F due to bulky aromatic side chain introduction (Figure 5C). Hydrogen bond analysis revealed a significant reduction in Y244A (2.67 ± 1.04) with the largest fluctuation, while A245F (3.52 ± 0.87) remained comparable to wild‐type with minimal fluctuation, indicating disruption of the Tyr244‐mediated hydrogen bond network (Figure 5D). MMPBSA calculations indicated that the binding free energies of Y244A (−11.96 kcal/mol) and A245F (−10.56 kcal/mol) were substantially lower than that of the wild‐type (Figure 5E). Residue contribution analysis showed that mutation converted the favorable negative energies of Tyr244 (−1.33 kcal/mol) and Ala245 (−1.80 kcal/mol) into unfavorable weak positive energies (Ala244: 0.98, Phe245: 0.91 kcal/mol) (Figure 5F,G). In summary, both mutations destabilize the protein–ligand complex through distinct mechanisms, confirming that DEE must engage these residues to exert its function.

DEE Induces Ferroptosis in A549 Cells Through Specifically Targeting SLC7A11

To determine whether DEE induces ferroptosis by inhibiting SLC7A11, we transfected A549 cells with SLC7A11 siRNA to silence SLC7A11 expression. Western blot analysis revealed that the protein expression levels of GPX4 and SLC7A11 were decreased when cells were treated with DEE; however, such reductions were further enhanced in SLC7A11‐knockdown cells (si‐SLC7A11) (Figure 6A). Furthermore, SLC7A11 silencing significantly potentiated the DEE‐induced decreases in intracellular MMP and GSH levels (Figure 6B,C).

To further validate our hypothesis, we overexpressed SLC7A11 in A549 cells and examined its impact on DEE‐stimulated A549 cells. In cells with SLC7A11 overexpression (OE‐SLC7A11), the protein levels of GPX4 and SLC7A11 were instead upregulated following DEE stimulation (Figure 6D), accompanied by the alleviation of intracellular MMP loss and GSH depletion (Figure 6E,F), indicating that ferroptosis was mitigated. Collectively, our results demonstrate that DEE induces ferroptosis through inhibiting SLC7A11, whereas activation of SLC7A11 can reverse the ferroptotic toxicity induced by DEE in A549 cells.

To Validate the Anti‐Tumor Effect of DEE, in Vivo Experiments Were Performed

To investigate both the anti‐tumor effect of DEE and the role of ferroptosis in this process, an A549 cell xenograft model was employed [33]. After 1 month of treatment with 30 mg/kg DEE, 60 mg/kg DEE [34], or 10 mg/kg paclitaxel [35], the results demonstrated that DEE exerted a significant anti‐tumor effect in vivo. With no significant difference in body weight among mice of all groups, DEE remarkably reduced tumor volume and weight (Figure 7A–D), with the therapeutic effect of 60 mg/kg DEE being significantly superior to that of 30 mg/kg. Meanwhile, in vivo dissection of the heart, liver, spleen, lungs, and kidneys was performed on mice from the control group and the 60 mg/kg DEE‐treated group. Subsequent HE staining revealed no obvious pathological changes in these organs between the two groups (Figure 7E), indicating that DEE exerted no toxic effects on the heart, liver, spleen, lungs, or kidneys of mice.

IHC staining results demonstrated that DEE could downregulate the protein expression of SLC7A11, with a more pronounced effect at higher drug concentrations (Figure 8A). HE staining revealed that tumor cells in the control group exhibited a favorable growth state, being round‐like with uniformly distributed and tightly arranged nuclei; whereas those in each treatment group showed pathological characteristics of apoptotic cells such as nuclear pyknosis, hyperchromatism, fragmentation, marginalization, and vacuolation, accompanied by irregular nuclear sizes and gradually loose cell arrangement (Figure 8B). Additionally, DEE decreased the tissue GSH level (Figure 8D) while increasing the MDA level (Figure 8E). Western blot analysis showed that the expression of ferroptosis‐related proteins SLC7A11 and GPX4 was downregulated, which was consistent with the in vitro results (Figure 8C). Notably, paclitaxel, as a positive control drug, exerted a similar anti‐tumor effect to DEE, indicating that DEE possesses favorable anti‐tumor activity.

Discussion

NSCLC is characterized by persistently high morbidity and mortality [3]. Existing radiochemotherapy regimens exhibit significant side effects and limited efficacy, particularly as drug‐resistant cancer cells are resistant to conventional cell death pathways [41]. Although targeted therapy and chemotherapy have achieved certain progress, drug resistance and adverse reactions severely restrict their clinical outcomes [42]. As a novel programmed cell death mode, ferroptosis can specifically induce the death of such drug‐resistant cancer cells, offering a novel direction for NSCLC treatment [18]. Thus, identifying novel therapeutic agents with high efficacy and low toxicity as well as their corresponding targets from natural compounds has become a crucial focus of current NSCLC research [43]. This study demonstrates the novel drug DEE, an active derivative synthesized from the traditional Chinese medicine Salvia miltiorrhiza , can effectively induce ferroptosis in NSCLC cells and exert anti‐tumor effects in vivo, with the core mechanism lying in its ability to directly target and inhibit the transport function of SLC7A11 (Figure 9).

Flowchart of the mechanism.

Through in vitro cell viability assays, we confirmed that DEE significantly inhibits the proliferation and viability of multiple NSCLC cell lines in a dose‐dependent manner. Of particular note, the pharmacological efficacy of DEE is remarkably superior to that of DSS, a finding that strongly validates the success of the classic medicinal chemistry strategy of optimizing the pharmacokinetic properties and pharmacological activities of natural products through chemical structural modification [44]. Although DSS itself exhibits biological activity, its poor cell membrane permeability and in vivo metabolic instability limit its clinical application; in contrast, ethyl esterification modification may significantly improve its lipophilicity and metabolic stability, enabling it to reach intracellular targets at more effective concentrations. Moreover, DEE showed only mild cytotoxicity against normal human lung epithelial BEAS‐2B cells at concentrations effective for killing NSCLC cells. This characteristic of selective cytotoxicity against tumor cells is similar to a previous report that demonstrates gossypol, a naturally derived phytochemical with BH3‐mimetic property, can sensitize cultured thoracic cancer cells to the cytotoxic effect of Apo2L/TRAIL by activating the mitochondria‐dependent death [45].

Ferroptosis can interplay with apoptosis through the mitochondria‐dependent apoptotic signaling pathway. We next studied whether DEE inhibits NSCLC proliferation via ferroptosis. Multiple experiments in this study verified the causal relationship between DEE and ferroptosis. DEE treatment induced typical ferroptotic features in NSCLC cells: cells treated with DEE showed a dramatic accumulation of intracellular ROS and MDA, the end product of lipid peroxidation, while GSH, the most important intracellular antioxidant, was extensively depleted. These findings are consistent with the conclusion reported by Zhang et al., who reported that another natural product derivative induces ferroptosis in hepatocellular carcinoma cells by disrupting redox homeostasis [46]. Validation of target pathways through pharmacological inhibitors is a classic paradigm for studying the mechanism of action of natural products. Our results further showed that Fer‐1, a specific ferroptosis inhibitor, almost completely reversed DEE‐induced cell death and the aforementioned changes in biochemical indicators. This “rescue experiment” provided us with the most direct causal evidence, definitively linking the cytotoxic effect of DEE to the ferroptosis pathway. Moreover, we also found that treatment with DEE significantly suppressed NSCLC cell migration. We deduce that the impairment of cell motility is likely a consequence of ferroptosis.

SLC7A11 is considered a critical factor linking metabolic perturbation to ferroptosis [40]. To unravel the direct interaction between DEE and SLC7A11, we performed computational simulations. Results from molecular docking and 100 ns molecular dynamics simulations demonstrated that the target of DEE is at the structural level but also provides a theoretical basis for its specificity, echoing and complementing previous studies reporting that compounds like metformin [13] and tanshinone IIA [14] exert anti‐tumor effects by inhibiting SLC7A11. Furthermore, mutation of key residues Tyr244 and Ala245 impaired DEE binding, confirming their critical role in the wild‐type interaction and providing insights for future drug development. Subsequent experiments showed that intracellular cysteine levels in DEE‐treated cells exhibited a significant time‐dependent decrease. This result directly confirms that DEE effectively inhibits the cystine transport function of SLC7A11, cutting off the raw material supply for GSH synthesis. Similar to a previous report [13], where metformin induces ferroptosis in breast cancer cells by inhibiting SLC7A11 function. When SLC7A11 expression was knocked down using siRNA, DEE‐induced effects such as GSH depletion, MMP reduction, and GPX4 downregulation were significantly enhanced. In contrast, overexpression of SLC7A11 in cells significantly antagonized the ferroptotic effects induced by DEE. Evidence from both positive and negative aspects confirms that SLC7A11 is an indispensable key mediator of DEE's action. These findings support that overexpression of SLC7A11 significantly enhances the resistance of lung cancer cells to ferroptosis, thereby contributing to chemoresistance.

Ferroptosis is assuming an increasingly crucial role in the treatment of NSCLC [47]. Numerous studies have demonstrated that many NSCLC cells that have developed resistance to traditional chemotherapy or targeted therapy often exhibit high sensitivity to ferroptosis inducers [48], suggesting that activating the ferroptosis pathway is expected to serve as a breakthrough for overcoming the drug resistance dilemma in NSCLC. The in vivo experimental data of this study provide strong support for this notion. DEE treatment significantly inhibited tumor growth, with efficacy comparable to that of paclitaxel, a clinically commonly used chemotherapeutic drug. Notably, no obvious toxicity was observed in the pathological examination of major organs, which initially reveals the favorable anti‐tumor activity and safety of DEE. Given the role of DEE in promoting ferroptosis in vitro experiments, its antitumor effect may be attributed to ferroptosis induction or driven by other mechanisms. Therefore, we conducted further experiments to validate this hypothesis. The expression levels of SLC7A11 and GPX4 proteins were downregulated in the DEE‐treated group, accompanied by decreased GSH levels and increased MDA content, confirming that DEE exerts its anti‐tumor effect in vivo also by triggering the ferroptosis pathway. In contrast, whether paclitaxel can target SLC7A11 remains uncertain, and its anticancer effects may be independent of ferroptosis induction. These results indicate that DEE can enhance the efficacy of radiotherapy against NSCLC.

Although the present study has clarified the mechanism by which DEE induces ferroptosis through targeting SLC7A11 and revealed that its action is independent of the p53 pathway, several issues remain to be further explored. The specific upstream molecules, such as ATF4, NRF2, and BACH1, through which DEE regulates SLC7A11 in a p53‐independent context remain incompletely understood. The present study indicates that DEE can stably bind to SLC7A11 and reduce its protein level, but the possibilities whether the binding of DEE to SLC7A11 might trigger endocytosis and subsequent degradation of the protein, or whether DEE, upon entering cells, could further regulate other gene expression pathways, need future investigation. Furthermore, future studies can further employ biophysical methods including surface plasmon resonance or isothermal titration calorimetry to validate the direct binding between DEE and SLC7A11 [49], and explore other molecular pathways through which it regulates SLC7A11 in the context of p53 deficiency.

Conclusions

Taken together, our study demonstrates that DEE can effectively promote ferroptosis in NSCLC through the targeted inhibition of SLC7A11, which indicates that SLC7A11 can serve as a novel therapeutic target for NSCLC treatment while DEE may act as a potential therapeutic agent to achieve this goal.

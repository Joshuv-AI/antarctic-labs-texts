New energy vehicle fault identification based on improved activation functions and parameter-free attention mechanisms
Authors: Jiao Yan, Haobo Wu, Weidong Dai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe drive system of New Energy Vehicles (NEVs) is a core component ensuring the safe and efficient operation of vehicles. Key components such as batteries, motors, and inverters are prone to faults under complex operating conditions, severely affecting vehicle stability and reliability. Traditional fault diagnosis methods based on rules or statistical analysis struggle to adapt to complex nonlinear fault patterns, while deep learning provides a new pathway for intelligent fault diagnosis. This paper proposes an improved CNN-based fault diagnosis method integrating the ReLUT activation function, SimAM parameter-free attention mechanism, and residual modules: ReLUT combines the advantages of ReLU and tanh to enhance the model’s nonlinear expression capability and avoid gradient stagnation; SimAM adaptively focuses on key fault features without additional parameters; and residual modules alleviate the gradient vanishing problem and accelerate model convergence. Extensive comparative experiments were conducted on NEVData, a dedicated fault diagnosis dataset for NEVs acquired from online resources. This dataset includes 8 core fields: voltage (V), current (A), motor speed (RPM), temperature (℃), vibration (g), ambient temperature (℃), humidity (%), and fault label, with a total of 11,000 samples. It covers normal operating conditions (label 0, 5,000 samples) and 3 types of fault conditions (labels 1–3, 2,000 samples each), featuring practical scenario characteristics such as class imbalance and outliers in some variables. Results show that the proposed method achieves a fault diagnosis accuracy of 0.992 and precision of 0.989, representing a 1.5% improvement in both metrics compared to the traditional KNN method. Introduction

New energy vehicles have been widely promoted globally due to their environmental-friendly and high-efficiency characteristics. However, compared with traditional fuel-powered vehicles, the drive systems of new energy vehicles are more complex, consisting of multiple key components such as batteries, motors, and inverters. Their operating states are affected by various factors including voltage, current, and temperature. During long-term operation, these components may malfunction due to overload, aging, or environmental factors, thus affecting the vehicle’s safety and service life. Therefore, establishing an efficient and accurate fault diagnosis system is of great significance for the safe operation of new energy vehicles. Traditional fault diagnosis methods mainly rely on manual inspection or rule-based expert systems, which have limitations such as low diagnosis efficiency, poor real-time performance, and heavy reliance on experience. With the continuous development of intelligent technologies, computer-aided fault diagnosis has gradually become a research hotspot. Among them, data-driven methods can automatically learn fault features from a large amount of sensor data without the need for complex physical modeling, significantly improving diagnosis efficiency and accuracy.

At present, data-driven fault diagnosis methods are mainly divided into two categories: those based on machine learning and those based on deep learning. Traditional machine learning methods, such as Support Vector Machine (SVM) and Random Forest (RF), rely on manually extracted features and perform well on low dimensional data. However, when faced with the complex sensor data of the new energy vehicle drive system, the feature engineering is difficult and the generalization ability is limited. In recent years, due to their powerful ability to automatically extract features, deep learning methods have been widely applied in the field of fault diagnosis. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) can effectively capture local patterns, but have limited ability to model time-series-related fault features. Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and Long Short Term Memory networks (LSTMs), although capable of handling time-series data, have high computational costs and are difficult to capture global information. In addition, some research has introduced the attention mechanism to enhance the focus on key features, but most of the existing attention mechanisms rely on additional parameters, increasing the computational overhead. Therefore, how to improve the accuracy of fault identification while ensuring computational efficiency remains an important challenge in current research.

To address the above-mentioned issues, this paper proposes a deep learning based fault identification method for the drive system of new energy vehicles, and makes improvements in terms of model structure and feature extraction. Firstly, in order to enhance the model’s attention to key features, this paper introduces the SimAM (Simple Attention Module) attention mechanism. This mechanism calculates saliency information through a parameter free energy function, strengthening the model’s feature extraction ability without increasing computational complexity. Secondly, this paper designs a self-developed activation function to improve the model’s non-linear expression ability, enabling it to more effectively fit complex fault patterns and improve the recognition accuracy. Finally, in view of the potential problems of gradient vanishing and feature attenuation during the training of deep models, this paper introduces a residual structure module. Through cross-layer connections, it effectively alleviates the training difficulty, accelerates convergence, and improves the model’s stability. Experimental results show that the improved method proposed in this paper outperforms existing mainstream methods in terms of fault recognition accuracy, recall rate, and computational efficiency, providing a more adaptable solution for the intelligent fault diagnosis of new energy vehicles.

Related work

General forecasting methods

In recent years, time series forecasting methods based on Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN) have made significant progress. In 1997, Hochreiter proposed the Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM)1network, which consists of forget gates, input gates, output gates, and memory cells. LSTM effectively alleviated the vanishing gradient problem encountered by traditional RNNs when processing long sequences through its gating mechanism. This allowed it to capture long-term dependencies in data more efficiently. However, LSTM still faced limitations when dealing with large-scale long time series data. In 2014, Cho et al. introduced the Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU)2, which uses reset and update gates to control the retention and updating of information. This mechanism enables GRU to capture long-term dependencies more efficiently than traditional RNNs. GRU has a simpler structure and fewer parameters, leading to faster training speeds. However, it still faces the risk of vanishing or exploding gradients when processing long sequences. In 2017, Salinas et al. proposed the Deep AR model3, which uses autoregressive recurrent networks to predict the probability distribution parameters of time series data. By optimizing model parameters through maximum likelihood estimation, Deep AR significantly improved forecasting accuracy. However, the model’s lack of attention mechanisms made it prone to memory loss in long-range time series. In 2023, Jia et al. introduced the WITRAN model4, which employs a water-wave information transmission framework and Horizontal-Vertical Gating Selection Units (HVGSU) to recursively fuse information and model both global and local correlations. The model also incorporates a Recurrent Acceleration Network (RAN) to reduce time complexity. However, when using only the last output node, there is a risk of losing local semantic information. In 2024, Lin et al. proposed the SegRNN model5, which segments time series into meaningful intervals to capture long-term dependencies. However, the segmentation rules rely on prior knowledge or experience, introducing subjectivity that may affect the model’s forecasting performance. Meanwhile, time series forecasting methods based on Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) have also gained significant attention. In 2018, Bai et al. conducted a systematic evaluation of CNNs and RNNs in sequence modeling tasks and found that simple convolutional structures outperformed typical recurrent networks like LSTM across various tasks and datasets6. CNNs also demonstrated longer effective memory. In 2020, Chen et al.7proposed a probabilistic forecasting framework based on CNNs, using dilated causal convolution networks and stacked residual blocks to capture temporal dependencies in sequences. Combined with representation learning, this approach enabled more accurate forecasting. Time series forecasting methods based on Transformer architectures have also evolved rapidly. In 2017, Vaswani et al. introduced the Transformer8, which processes long time series data efficiently through self-attention and multi-head attention mechanisms. By abandoning the sequential computation of RNNs in favor of parallel computation, the Transformer significantly improved training and inference speed. However, the large number of parameters in the Transformer makes it challenging to train when dealing with small-scale or low-quality data. In 2019, Lim et al. proposed the Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) model9, which combines the strengths of LSTM and Transformer. TFT handles static covariates, time-varying covariates, and historical time series data, significantly improving accuracy and interpretability in multi-horizon time series forecasting. In the same year, Li et al. introduced the Log Sparse Transformer10, a convolutional self-attention model that addresses the Transformer’s insensitivity to local context and memory bottleneck in modeling long time series. This model improves forecasting accuracy by capturing both fine-grained and long-range dependencies with limited memory overhead.

Fault prediction methods for new energy vehicle drivetrains

In recent years, with the rapid development of electric vehicles and intelligent transportation technologies, significant progress has been made in the research of fault detection, prediction, and performance optimization in related fields. In 2019, Hong et al. proposed a battery system fault prediction method based on Long Short-Term Memory neural networks (LSTM)11. By considering the impacts of vehicle conditions, weather, and driver behavior on battery performance, and employing pre-dropout and dual-model collaborative prediction for training, they achieved stable and reliable fault prediction for battery systems. In 2020, Javed et al. developed the MSALSTM-CNN model12, which integrates a multi-stage attention mechanism with Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) based on LSTM, for anomaly detection in sensor data of connected and automated vehicles. This method effectively improved the detection rates of both low-order and high-order anomaly instances. In 2021, Chen et al. utilized the Linear Support Vector Machine (LSVM)13algorithm to classify and warn of motor over-temperature faults. They constructed multi-dimensional features based on vehicle data collected under actual operating conditions and processed streaming data using a sliding window model. Experiments showed that this method outperformed Random Forest and Logistic Regression in terms of training time and accuracy. In 2022, Trivedi et al. introduced a fault detection framework for electric vehicles based on deep learning and blockchain14. This framework used CNN and LSTM to predict vehicle tire pressure, temperature, and battery faults, and ensured the security and reliability of fault detection through IPFS and blockchain technologies. Experimental results showed that the framework achieved a prediction accuracy of 70% for the three types of faults. In the same year, Shen et al. proposed a real-time energy management strategy combining Double-Delay Q-learning and Model Predictive Control (MPC)15. They transformed the energy management of Plug-in Hybrid Electric Vehicles (PHEV) into a nonlinear optimal control problem and used a CNN-based speed predictor in MPC to forecast vehicle speed. Subsequently, the Double-Delay Q-learning algorithm was implemented to solve the rolling horizon optimal control problem in the MPC module. In 2023, Li et al. developed an online prediction method for electric vehicle battery faults based on LSTM networks16, which could predict low-charge state alarms and insulation alarms. Experiments demonstrated that the prediction accuracy of this method could reach over 85%, effectively ensuring the safe use of electric vehicles. In the same year, Ma et al.17proposed an improved deep neural network method for estimating the State of Charge (SOC) of electric vehicles. This method utilized unlabeled SOC current and voltage samples and combined an input reconstruction network to redefine the time-dependent features of voltage and current, thereby enhancing the performance of SOC estimation. Shi et al. designed a Transformer-based network architecture called BERTtery18, which used only time-scale battery data (current, voltage, and temperature) as inputs to estimate SOC. Through training and testing under different aging conditions and temperature windows, the model’s strong capability in predicting complex multi-physics and multi-scale battery system behaviors was verified. In 2024, Liao et al. proposed a fault diagnosis method for lithium-ion batteries in electric vehicles, combining voltage prediction and Z-score19. They extracted stable trend components from battery voltage data using Variational Mode Decomposition to avoid the influence of noise signals and random disturbances and designed a TCN-BiLSTM-attention model to estimate the average voltage of batteries under normal conditions. Finally, they used the Z-score to locate and determine battery faults. In the same year, Yue et al. introduced a recursive multi-step performance prediction method for automotive fuel cells based on conditional time series forecasting with CNN20. This method combined a conditional CNN architecture to extract representative features from input data and used a recursive strategy to iteratively predict future values based on previous predictions. It also corrected the prediction results of seasonal data by considering intermittent performance recovery. Experiments showed that this method achieved high prediction accuracy for long-term data. These studies provide important theoretical support and technical means for fault detection, prediction, and performance optimization of electric vehicles and related systems.

Overview

Despite notable progress in data-driven fault diagnosis for new energy vehicle (NEV) drivetrains, existing methods still face practical limitations. Recurrent models such as LSTM and GRU can capture temporal dependencies but often incur high computational costs and training instability when applied to large-scale and noisy sensor data. Convolution-based approaches are more efficient, yet their ability to model complex nonlinear fault patterns may be limited when used alone. Moreover, although attention mechanisms have been introduced to enhance feature discrimination, many designs rely on additional learnable parameters, increasing model complexity and reducing robustness. Common activation functions may also suffer from gradient saturation or insufficient nonlinear expressiveness when handling heterogeneous sensor signals and imbalanced fault distributions. Overall, current approaches involve a trade-off between diagnostic accuracy, computational efficiency, and model stability. These limitations motivate the exploration of lightweight architectural refinements within CNN-based frameworks to better adapt to real-world NEV drivetrain fault diagnosis tasks.

Parameter-free attention

In deep learning, attention mechanisms have been proven to be a powerful tool, enabling models to focus on the most important parts of the input data, thereby enhancing model performance and interpretability. In this study, we introduced a parameter-free attention mechanism, namely SimAM (Simple Attention Module), to enhance the model’s ability to capture key features. The design of the SimAM attention mechanism is inspired by self-attention mechanisms, but unlike traditional self-attention mechanisms, SimAM does not rely on any learnable parameters, thus reducing the complexity and computational burden of the model. The core idea of SimAM is to calculate the square of the difference between the input features and their mean, then divide by a smoothing term, and finally add a bias term. This formula can be interpreted as follows: First, calculate the difference between the input features and their mean, then compute the square of these differences to emphasize larger disparities; next, divide these squared differences by a smoothing term, which includes an estimation of the feature variance and a small constant to ensure numerical stability; finally, add a bias term (0.5) to ensure non-negativity of the output values.

Compared with CBAM and SENet, SimAM adopts a parameter-free design that introduces negligible computational and memory overhead, which is particularly suitable for NEV fault diagnosis scenarios with limited onboard resources. By relying on simple mathematical operations rather than additional parameter learning, SimAM effectively emphasizes discriminative fault-related features while suppressing redundant or noisy responses, reducing the risk of overfitting on small and imbalanced datasets. For NEV fault signals characterized by subtle inter-class differences and class imbalance, SimAM enables adaptive feature highlighting without altering the original feature distribution. When combined with ReLUT, which enhances nonlinear separability and preserves weak fault-related activations, SimAM further strengthens attention to informative spatial and channel features. This complementary mechanism improves the recognition of minority fault patterns and leads to more stable and reliable fault diagnosis beyond pure numerical gains.

In the improved CNN model, the SimAM module is integrated after the residual blocks, as shown in Fig. 1. Each residual block consists of two convolutional layers, a custom activation function, and a SimAM module. The design of the residual blocks aims to mitigate the problems of gradient vanishing and gradient exploding during the training of deep networks through residual connections, while the introduction of the SimAM module further enhances the model’s ability to capture key features. Within each residual block, the input features are first processed by a convolutional layer and a custom activation function, then adaptively weighted by the SimAM module, and finally outputted through another convolutional layer and residual connection. This structural design allows the model to focus more on features that are more helpful for the classification task when learning feature representations, thereby improving the model’s diagnostic performance.

Overview

In the model development phase of this study, we meticulously designed and implemented an advanced neural network architecture specifically tailored for fault diagnosis in new energy vehicles. This phase involved the creation of a novel ReLU-T activation function, which integrates the advantages of both ReLU and tanh functions to enhance pattern recognition without increasing parameter count. Additionally, we incorporated a parameter-free attention mechanism, SimAM, to strengthen the model’s feature focus capability by adaptively weighting features, thereby improving feature capture efficiency. Residual connections were also integrated to address challenges associated with deep network training, particularly to facilitate gradient flow and accelerate convergence. The training process was guided by the cross-entropy loss function, selected for its effectiveness in multi-class classification and its role in ensuring precise model parameter updates. The entire workflow, from data preprocessing to model evaluation, was systematically structured to ensure a methodical approach to developing a high-performance diagnostic system. Collectively, these components contribute to a robust model well-suited for the complexities of fault diagnosis tasks, providing a reliable solution for practical applications in the field of new energy vehicles.

Experiment

Dataset and set

To construct NEVData, we crawled publicly available online resources and cross-validated the records to ensure authenticity. The dataset provides 10-second multimodal snippets (voltage, current, motor speed, temperature, vibration) captured under real driving conditions, each tagged with one of four fault states. Normal operations (label 0) comprise 5 000 samples, whereas the three fault classes (labels 1–3) contain 2 000 samples each; this imbalance is countered by weighted cross-entropy loss that amplifies gradients for minority faults and promotes balanced generalization. The NEVData dataset will be made publicly available upon acceptance to support reproducibility and future research.

Experimental setup

All experiments were implemented using the PyTorch framework and accelerated with GPU computing. During data preprocessing, feature-wise standardization was applied to eliminate scale discrepancies among different sensor modalities. The dataset was randomly split into training and testing subsets with a ratio of 2:1. The models were trained using the Adam optimizer with an initial learning rate of 0.001 for 50 epochs. Cross-entropy loss was adopted as the objective function to measure the discrepancy between predicted and ground-truth fault labels. To ensure a fair evaluation under class-imbalanced conditions, performance was assessed using accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score, all computed with macro-averaging. To reduce the influence of randomness, experiments were repeated with different random seeds, and the reported results correspond to the averaged performance.

Comparative experiments

We conducted comparative experiments to evaluate the proposed ImprovedCNN against several representative deep learning and time-series architectures, including LSTM, Bi-LSTM, CNN, Transformer, 1D-ResNet, TCN, and InceptionTime. As summarized in Table 3, ImprovedCNN consistently achieves the highest performance across all metrics, with an accuracy of 99.15% and an F1-score of 98.85%, demonstrating its effectiveness in capturing complex fault patterns in NEV telemetry data. Traditional CNN achieves an accuracy of 98.42% and an F1-score of 97.84%, indicating limited ability to model subtle and transient fault features. 1D-ResNet improves performance to 98.65% accuracy and 98.20% F1-score by leveraging residual connections, which facilitate gradient propagation and feature preservation, yet it still falls short of temporal models and the proposed architecture. LSTM and Bi-LSTM capture sequential dependencies effectively, achieving 98.91% / 98.79% accuracy and 98.51% / 98.35% F1-score, respectively. However, their performance is somewhat constrained by sensitivity to sensor noise and limited capacity to emphasize fine-grained local patterns. Transformer models reach 98.36% accuracy and 97.76% F1-score, demonstrating competitive performance but slightly lower stability on small-scale noisy datasets due to the attention mechanism’s dependence on large amounts of training data. Advanced time-series architectures, such as TCN and InceptionTime, further improve performance, achieving 98.83% / 98.91% accuracy and 98.40% / 98.51% F1-score, respectively. These improvements stem from their multi-scale temporal convolution design, which enhances the extraction of long-range dependencies. Nevertheless, even these state-of-the-art models do not surpass ImprovedCNN, highlighting the advantage of its task-specific design.

Ablation study

As shown in Table 4, to verify the individual and synergistic contributions of ReLUT and SimAM to the proposed fault diagnosis model, a systematic ablation study was conducted on the NEVData dataset. The baseline model is defined as a CNN integrated with residual modules, and three variant models are constructed by sequentially adding ReLUT, SimAM, and their combination. The baseline model achieves an accuracy of 0.9842 and an F1-score of 0.9784. Incorporating ReLUT alone improves the accuracy by 0.31% points (to 0.9874) and the F1-score by 0.39% points, demonstrating its effectiveness in enhancing the model’s ability to learn nonlinear fault features while mitigating gradient stagnation. Adding SimAM independently yields a slightly higher performance gain (accuracy: 0.9882, F1-score: 0.9835), which confirms that SimAM can adaptively highlight discriminative fault features without introducing extra computational burden. Notably, the model integrating both ReLUT and SimAM achieves the optimal performance, with an accuracy of 0.9915 and an F1-score of 0.9885. This result indicates that ReLUT and SimAM play complementary roles: ReLUT strengthens feature expression capability, while SimAM refines feature selection, and their synergistic effect significantly boosts the model’s diagnostic reliability and robustness for NEV drive system faults.

Overview

The proposed Improved CNN model demonstrates strong performance in NEV fault diagnosis and is computationally efficient for real-time deployment. Inference on GPU achieves 2.3 ms per sample, and the model runs effectively on edge devices (e.g., NVIDIA Jetson Nano) with less than 70 MB memory footprint, highlighting its feasibility for on-board diagnostic applications. Future work will explore scaling to larger datasets, integrating adaptive inference mechanisms, and embedding the model into NEV control systems for predictive maintenance.

Conclusion and future

The primary contribution of this study lies in the proposal of a novel deep learning model—Improved CNN, which has been specifically optimized for the task of fault diagnosis in new energy vehicles (NEVs). By integrating a parameter-free attention mechanism SimAM, a custom activation function, and residual structural modules, this model has demonstrated significant advantages in feature extraction and classification performance. Extensive experiments conducted on a multimodal sensor dataset have verified the effectiveness of this method in enhancing fault detection accuracy, recall rate, and computational efficiency. Furthermore, compared to existing machine learning and deep learning models, including SVM, KNN, Logistic Regression, LSTM, BiLSTM, CNN, and Transformer, our model has achieved notable improvements across all key performance metrics.

Looking ahead, we plan to further explore the performance of this model on larger and more diverse datasets to validate its generalization capabilities and robustness. Additionally, research will be conducted on how to effectively integrate this model into practical applications for NEV fault diagnosis, including the development of real-time monitoring systems and predictive maintenance strategies. We will also be committed to the continuous optimization of the model, adjusting its structure and parameters to improve computational efficiency and diagnostic accuracy, especially when dealing with imbalanced datasets. Exploring multi-task learning approaches that combine fault diagnosis with other related tasks, such as health monitoring and lifespan prediction, will also be a focus of future work to enhance the model’s practicality and efficiency. Lastly, enhancing the model’s interpretability through visualization and interpretative analysis to aid in understanding and trusting the model’s decision-making process is crucial for fault diagnosis in practical applications. Through these future research directions, we expect to further advance the level of intelligent fault diagnosis in new energy vehicles and provide stronger technical support for their safe operation and maintenance.

Statistical analysis of Casson hybrid nanofluid flow over a stenotic artery
Authors: Gunisetty Ramasekhar, Mohammed Almakki, A. Divya
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The flow of blood through stenotic arteries is considered one of the most significant areas of investigation in the world of mathematical fluid mechanics. This significance arises from the topic's application to the field of biomedicine. Within an arterial system of blood that has been stenosed, the goal of this research initiative is to investigate the effect that nanoparticles have on the characteristics that define the human circulatory system. The current investigation analyzes blood flow behavior in a controlled, permeable artery employing the Casson hybrid nanofluid model and, additionally, gold, Cu, and silver nanoparticles. The current study examines the investigation of magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) Casson hybrid nanofluid Darcy-Forchheimer flow (DFF) over a stenotic artery with a heat source/sink. By transforming the partial differential equations (PDEs) into ordinary differential equations (ODEs) by utilizing suitable similarity variables. After that, the mathematical solution of these equations used the BVP4c method in the MATLAB solver and analysis for skin and Nusselt number table values for hybrid nanofluid cases. The statistical analysis is used to solve for different physical factors. During a study in which values of both variables are subject to essentially unknown oversights, the task is utilizing statistical techniques to discover the best possible linear equations. In this concept, two different hybrid nanomaterials are used for analyzing heat transfer characteristics. According to these graphical results, the present model concludes that case-2 has better performance than case-1. Rising values of the magnetic parameter improve heat transfer owing to Joule heating impacts and control flow via the Lorentz force, which in turn affects the distribution of shear stresses close to the artery wall. The current work highlights the role of nanoparticles and magnetic fields in enhancing flexibility near arteries' surfaces. This work has substantial repercussions for the treatment of cancer and the treatments that are used to avoid cardiovascular disease, as it suggests that improved heat transfer and shear stress distribution can enhance the effectiveness of therapies targeting tumor cells and improve blood flow in cardiovascular interventions.

Introduction

Research into two-dimensional Casson non-Newtonian hybrid nanofluid has established a lot of interest in recent years. This is mostly owing to the fact that these nanofluids exhibit a complicated rheological behaviour and have increased heat transmission capacities. In the beginning, the Casson fluid model was developed to represent the flow properties of blood and other yield-stress fluids. This model is a form of non-Newtonian fluid that does not flow until a crucial stress is applied to it. In the middle of the 1990s, Choi and Eastman [1] presented the notion of suspending nanoparticles in ordinary base fluids in order to increase thermal conductivity. This was the beginning of the concept of nanofluid. In later years, the creation of hybrid nanofluid, which mix two or more different kinds of nanoparticles, resulted in significant improvements to the efficiency of heat transmission, stability, and thermal characteristics of single nanoparticle suspensions [2]. Within the context of two-dimensional flow systems, the combination of these hybrid nanofluids with the Casson fluid model results in the formation of a fluid that has distinctive characteristics. These characteristics include shear-thinning behaviour, better thermal conductivity, and greater control over flow dynamics. Applications of 2D Casson hybrid nanofluid can be found in various fields, including biomedical engineering like for example, blood flow modelling and targeted drug delivery; refreshing systems in microchips, motor car thermal management, polymer processing, and chemical industries, all of which require precise control of heat and fluid flow. The synergy between the features of Casson fluid and hybrid nanoparticles presents a significant opportunity for the development of innovative thermal systems and technologies that are efficient in their use of energy. Puneeth et al. [3] investigated the behavior of a hybrid Casson nanofluid in a three-dimensional mixed convection flow as it passed a non-linear stretching surface. Abdelsalam, Mekheimer and Zaher [4] investigated the dynamic properties of a hybrid Casson nanofluid by combining laser light and chemical reaction via sinusoidal channels throughout his research. Ramesh et al. [5] conducted research on the development of numerical solutions and stability analysis for heat transfer effects with MHD on Casson hybrid nanofluid and some studies are analysed by Casson hybrid nanofluid analysis [6, 7].

Magnetohydrodynamics is a branch of study that investigates the motion of electrically conducting fluids in the presence of magnetic fields. Examples of such fluids include plasmas, liquid metals, and seawater. Hannes Alfvén [8] was the first person to present the notion of magnetically induced flow (MHD) in 1942 and he was the one who offered the idea that magnetic fields may impact the flow behavior of fluids of this kind. Alfvén waves, which are important to plasma dynamics, were discovered as a result of his pioneering work, which set the groundwork for the theory of MHD and led to the discovery of both. In 1970, Alfvén was honored with the Nobel Prize in Physics in recognition of his contributions to the field. Applications of MHD have been discovered in a broad variety of disciplines during the course of its existence. Within the realm of power production, MHD generators provide the possibility of converting heat directly into electricity with a greater degree of efficiency [9]. The concepts of MHD are crucial in the field of nuclear fusion research because they promote plasma confinement and stability in fusion reactors such as tokamaks. In addition, MHD is an important part of the field of astrophysics, where it contributes to the explanation of solar flares, sunspots, and the behavior of cosmic plasma [10]. Also, it is used in the cooling systems of liquid metals, electromagnetic casting, and other medicinal activities, including magnetic medication delivery and blood flow monitoring. Multi-dimensional hydrodynamics (MHD) acts as an essential connection between fluid dynamics and electromagnetism, and it has serious consequences for both engineering and science. Qamar et al. [11] investigated MHD across changing fluid parameters with heat transport properties. Abbas et al. [12] investigated the MHD effects of non-Newtonian fluid incorporating radiation effects across a permeable exponential stretching sheet. Choudhary et al. [13] undertook an investigation into the effects of MHD hybrid nanofluid flow in the presence of heat radiation on a non-linear, stretchy, porous sheet that is of unsteady behavior. Yasmin [14] conducted a numerical evaluation of heat and mass transfer research on MHD rotatory hybrid nanofluid flow across a stretched sheet, they also investigated the flow process and some studies are analysed by MHD flows [15, 16].

In the context of a system, a heat source/sink is a physical process that either contributes to the addition or elimination of thermal energy. Generally, heat sink is responsible for absorbing energy and producing a cooling effect, while heat source is responsible for introducing energy, which causes the temperature of the medium that surrounds it to rise. In the field of engineering and scientific research, these principles are used extensively to replicate thermal processes that occur in the actual world. Combustion chambers, solar collectors, and electronic gadgets are examples of applications that need heat sources because they operate in situations where either internal or external heating is required. On the other hand, heat sinks are essential components of cooling systems, such as those used in electrical devices, radiators, and biomedical equipment, in order to ensure that the temperatures at which they operate are safe. Within the realm of biomedical engineering, heat sources are used in therapeutic procedures such as magnetic hyperthermia to specifically target and heat sick tissues. On the other hand, blood flow may function as a heat sink, eliminating heat from human tissues. Furthermore, “heat source” and “heat sink” words are widely used in the modeling of nanofluid, hybrid nanofluid and heat exchangers in order to accomplish the goals of controlling and improving heat transfer rates. A study was conducted to investigate the transfer of heat and mass via nanofluid flow under the influence of an exponential heat source/sink with turbulent conditions over Riga plates by Ali et al. [17]. Anjum et al. investigated the possibility of conducting a computational examination of a magnetized three-dimensional rotating hybrid nanofluid that was subjected to a heat source/sink across a stretched sheet. Obalalu et al. investigated the possibility of using a two-phase numerical simulation to evaluate the heat and mass transfer over a vertical deformable sheet while taking into account the significant influence of heat source/sink. Nihaal, Mahabaleshwar, and Joo [18] investigated the effects of bioconvection on a radially extending disc using a heat source-sink in porous media. Divya and Anki Reddy [19] conducted the research over a revolving disk that operated with the heat source and sink and some studies are analysed by heat transfer analysis of nanofluid and hybrid nanofluid analysis [20, 21].

The findings of this research provide insights that may be used to diagnose arterial diseases, improve medical procedures, and enhance medication delivery systems. On top of that, it contributes to the development of medical technology and mathematical methods, which have the potential to make substantial advances to the knowledge and treatment of heart disease, such as through improved imaging techniques and predictive modeling for patient outcomes.

IFPHPOS0056 Digital Panopticon: Artificial Intelligence in Public Health from the Perspective of Foucault’s Biopower to Deleuze’s Societies of Control
Authors: Sadra Valiee, Fatemeh Homayounifar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThe future of public health is increasingly inseparable from artificial intelligence (AI). Yet AI in public health is not solely a technical phenomenon. It is deeply implicated in the governance of bodies and populations, raising urgent questions about surveillance, power, and autonomy.From Foucault to DeleuzeFoucault argued that modern power governs not merely through death, but through the administration and optimisation of life itself, which he termed biopower. He also introduced the panopticon as a metaphor for modern disciplinary societies: a prison where inmates could be watched at any moment without knowing it, illustrating how uncertainty alone compels self-discipline. Foucault argued that modern institutions such as schools and hospitals reproduce the same unseen, internalised power across all domains of social life. Deleuze theorised a further shift from disciplinary societies to societies of control, where power operates not through physical confinement but through continuous, automated, and digital modulation. Control flows through data, debt, and constant monitoring. AI in public health is a profound expression of this: the continuous collection and analysis of biological and behavioural data transforms the human body into a calculable, governable object, dissolving institutional boundaries and enabling surveillance that is perpetual and borderless.ConclusionAI holds genuine transformative potential for public health, but risks becoming an instrument through which corporations govern entire populations. Are we constructing a vast digital panopticon, built on big data and owned by corporations, in which people live unknowingly as prisoners in one enormous open prison without walls or guards?

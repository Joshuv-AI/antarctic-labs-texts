Cortical-multifidus paired associative stimulation for improving motor function in patients with chronic low back pain: study protocol for a randomised controlled trial
Authors: Hongyuan Lu, Quanwei Shao, Ji Guo, Zheng Huang, Yan-jun Cao, Fei Li, Wenyao Li, Weiyi Xiong, Kun-Peng Li, Wei Feng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionChronic low back pain (CLBP) is characterised by multifaceted pathophysiology involving both central sensitisation and peripheral dysfunction. Conventional therapies often fail to address this complexity due to their unidimensional targets. Paired associative stimulation (PAS), a dual-target neuromodulatory approach that combines central and peripheral interventions, has demonstrated efficacy in enhancing motor recovery post-stroke by synchronously inducing corticospinal plasticity and peripheral neuromuscular adaptation. Building on this paradigm, we propose a novel combined primary motor cortex (M1) and multifidus muscle stimulation protocol. The intervention pairs transcranial magnetic stimulation over M1 with peripheral magnetic stimulation targeting the multifidus muscle, hypothesising that temporally coordinated central and peripheral stimulation will synergistically enhance the corticospinal drive to the lumbar spine and restore multifidus neuromuscular control, thereby alleviating pain and improving functional capacity in CLBP.Methods and analysisThis study will enrol 82 individuals diagnosed with CLBP between 18 and 65 years of age. Study participants will undergo randomisation into two parallel groups: the experimental arm receiving active PAS therapy (n=41) and the control arm receiving sham PAS treatment (n=41). The intervention protocol consists of 20 treatment sessions delivered across a 4-week timeframe, with participants attending five sessions weekly. Assessment time points are scheduled at study entry (baseline) and 4, 8 and 12 weeks following intervention initiation. The study’s primary outcome is the Oswestry Disability Index (ODI). Secondary outcomes encompass the Visual Analog Scale (VAS), Short-form McGill Pain Questionnaire (SF-MPQ), Short Form 36 Health Survey (SF-36), Pain Catastrophizing Scale (PCS), and functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI). Data analysis will adhere to the intention-to-treat methodology. Between-group comparisons across temporal measurement points will employ mixed-effects modelling approaches.Ethics and disseminationEthical approval for the research protocol was obtained from the Ethics Committee of Guanghua Hospital Affiliated to Shanghai University of Traditional Chinese Medicine (approval number: 2025-K-45), with trial registration completed in the China Clinical Trial Registry on 27 April 2025. On study completion, findings will be prepared for submission to peer-reviewed academic publications.Trial registration numberChiCTR2500101574.Keywords: Randomized Controlled Trial, Chronic Disease, Clinical Protocols, Back pain

Introduction

Chronic low back pain (CLBP) emerges as a principal international cause of functional limitation, affecting approximately 23% of individuals during their lifetime and imposing substantial socio-economic burdens due to persistent pain,1 2 functional impairment3 and psychological distress.4 Although non-invasive therapies remain the first-line treatment, their long-term efficacy is often limited by the heterogeneous pathophysiology of CLBP,5 6 which involves intervertebral disc degeneration,7 inflammation and associated pro-inflammatory cytokines8 and maladaptive central sensitisation.9 Importantly, accumulating research demonstrates the essential role of multifidus muscle impairment in CLBP development.10 11 Patients often exhibit multifidus atrophy, fat infiltration and delayed activation, which exacerbate spinal instability. This instability may result in heightened biomechanical strain and nociceptive signalling, subsequently facilitating central sensitisation development and perpetuating chronic pain states.12

Neuromodulation techniques, such as transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS), have shown promise in alleviating CLBP by suppressing thalamocortical pain signalling and modulating neuroinflammatory pathways.13,15 However, isolated central interventions often produce transient effects, likely due to insufficient engagement of peripheral neuromuscular pathways. Conversely, peripheral magnetic stimulation (PMS) directly targets deep paravertebral muscles,16 enhancing proprioceptive feedback and restoring lumbar stability.17 Despite these advances, current therapies fail to fully address the bidirectional interplay between central sensitisation and peripheral muscle degeneration.

Paired associative stimulation (PAS), a neuroplasticity-driven approach combining temporally synchronised TMS and peripheral nerve stimulation, uses Hebbian principles to enhance corticospinal-muscular connectivity by exploiting spike-timing-dependent plasticity (STDP).18 When peripheral sensory input reaches the primary motor cortex (M1) milliseconds before TMS-induced cortical activation, PAS amplifies synaptic efficacy, leading to enduring changes in cortical excitability.19 In stroke rehabilitation, PAS has demonstrated its ability to remodel neural networks by strengthening or weakening connections between cortical regions and peripheral nerves through STDP, ultimately improving motor function in patients20—its application in chronic musculoskeletal disorders, particularly CLBP, remains insufficiently explored. The gap is striking given CLBP’s dual pathology: maladaptive central sensitisation and multifidus muscle dysfunction. By synchronising M1 modulation with multifidus activation, PAS offers a unique dual-target strategy to address the pathophysiological pathway involving peripheral dysfunction and central sensitisation, overcoming the limitations of isolated interventions.

The protocol seeks to examine PAS therapeutic effects in patients with CLBP and evaluate intervention outcomes regarding functional improvement, pain severity, life quality and pain catastrophising measures. Functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) will be employed to investigate underlying mechanisms.

Sample size calculation

Sample size determination employed a two-sample mean comparison methodology for independent treatment arms (active PAS vs sham PAS). A pilot trial (n=20, 1:1 randomisation) was conducted due to the lack of prior studies on PAS for CLBP to estimate parameters. The principal outcome is the ODI, establishing a minimal clinically important difference (MCID) of 10% on the 0–100% scale, derived from established consensus recommendations.24

Pilot study data indicated an anticipated ODI reduction of 15% within the active PAS group compared with 5% in the sham PAS group, yielding a between-group difference of 10%. The pooled standard deviation (σ) was estimated to be 15%. Using a two-sided significance level of α=0.05 with 80% statistical power, the computed sample size requirement was 34 participants per treatment group. Accounting for an anticipated 20% dropout rate, the target recruitment goal was established at 41 participants per group, totalling 82 participants.

Randomisation and blinding

An independent statistician will generate a blocked randomisation protocol using SPSS 26.0 software to ensure equal distribution between active PAS and sham PAS treatment arms. Group assignment will be accomplished through consecutively numbered, opaque, sealed allocation envelopes prepared by an external party with no involvement in participant recruitment or treatment delivery. Following informed consent, the principal investigator will assign a unique study ID to each participant and open the corresponding envelope to disclose group allocation.

Participants will be blinded to their group allocation through identical positioning and preparation procedures for both treatment groups. Outcome assessors conducting all clinical measurements will remain fully blinded to group assignments and will be completely separate from intervention delivery personnel, while PAS operators delivering interventions cannot be blinded due to technical requirements but will use identical verbal instructions and procedural steps for both active and sham treatments to maintain consistency and avoid inadvertent disclosure of treatment allocation. To prevent contamination between groups, treatment sessions will be scheduled with temporal separation when possible, and separate waiting areas will be used to minimise participant interaction, while information access will be restricted based on personnel roles, with intervention staff having no access to outcome assessment data. Unblinding will be strictly limited to emergencies involving serious adverse events (AEs) such as seizures or cardiovascular incidents, at which point the affected participant will be withdrawn from the trial and the event documented in full. Post-trial, the statistician will analyse de-identified datasets while remaining blinded to group labels, with unblinded allocation information only provided after final statistical reporting to prevent analysis bias.

Intervention

Participants will receive a pre-intervention biopsychosocial presentation on CLBP to improve their understanding of pain mechanisms, treatment rationale and the importance of protocol adherence. Participants in both groups will be randomly assigned to their respective treatment programmes, each lasting 4 weeks with five sessions per week, for a total of 20 sessions. To enhance adherence, flexible scheduling will be offered with morning, afternoon and evening time slots to accommodate work schedules, and automated reminders will be sent 24 hours before each session. If participants miss any intervention sessions, they will be contacted by phone within 2 hours to determine the reason for their absence, and makeup sessions will be arranged within 48 hours to maintain treatment continuity. PAS operators will maintain detailed intervention logs documenting attendance, session completion and any protocol deviations. Participants interested in exploring alternative treatment options for CLBP may consult with the researchers to assess their eligibility for participation. Any additional treatments will be documented to ensure they do not interfere with the intervention protocol.

All personnel involved in the intervention procedures and assessments will undergo comprehensive training and testing to ensure they have the necessary qualifications and expertise. The assessors will be overseen by a senior certified physical therapist, while the operators of PAS, as well as the imaging analysts, will undergo training and supervision by experts in rehabilitation and neurology to ensure the standardisation of experimental procedures and data analysis.

Active PAS protocol

The active PAS intervention will be implemented using a CCY-IA magnetic stimulator (Yiruide Medical Equipment Co, Wuhan, China) equipped with a figure-of-eight coil (70 mm wing diameter) for PMS of the multifidus muscle, localised 2 cm lateral to the L4–L5 spinous processes on the symptomatic side. Single-pulse PMS will be applied at 90% of the motor threshold (MT), established as the minimal intensity producing motor evoked potentials (MEPs) ≥50 μV within the multifidus muscle accompanied by observable muscle contraction, assessed through surface electromyography (electrode placement 1.5 cm lateral to the L5 spinous process following SENIAM protocols, bandwidth 10–500 Hz, sampling frequency 2 kHz), with stimulation intervals of 5 s. Central stimulation will use a CCY-II magnetic stimulator (Yiruide Medical Equipment New Technology Co, Ltd, Wuhan, China), targeting the contralateral primary motor cortex (M1) representation of the multifidus muscle, localised via neuronavigation (LOCALITE, Shanghai Kaichen Industrial Co, Ltd, Shanghai, China) to obtain MEPs ≥50 μV in at least 5 of 10 sequential trials.25 TMS pulses will be delivered at 110% of the resting motor threshold (RMT), defined as the minimum intensity eliciting MEPs ≥50 μV in 5 out of 10 trials, triggered precisely 25 ms after each peripheral stimulus, with synchronisation achieved through TTL pulse signals from a computer to both stimulators. The neuronavigation system integrates individual high-resolution T1-weighted MRI data and employs infrared optical tracking for real-time head movement correction, with the coil oriented tangentially and parallel to the precentral gyrus axis to optimise corticospinal activation. The PAS intervention comprises 180 paired stimuli delivered over 15 min (one pair every 5 s), adhering to safety guidelines26 27 (figure 2).

Sham PAS protocol

The sham PMS will be delivered using a CCY-IA magnetic stimulator (Yiruide Medical Equipment Co, Wuhan, China) equipped with a vertically placed, non-activating figure-of-eight coil, paired with a hidden vibration pad (10% intensity, below the sensory threshold) to simulate muscle sensation, with sham stimuli triggered every 5 s and targeting the same location as the active group (located 2 cm lateral to the L4–L5 spinous processes). Concurrently, the sham central stimulation will use a CCY-II magnetic stimulator (Yiruide Medical Equipment New Technology Co, Ltd, Wuhan, China) with a 90° tilted coil, attenuating cortical electric fields by >95%, targeting the M1 hand ‘knob’ area (distant from the multifidus representation) at 10% of the RMT intensity, with sham stimuli triggered every 5 s and a random delay of ±50 ms relative to the peripheral sham stimuli to disrupt the STDP mechanism. Neuronavigation coordinates (LOCALITE, Shanghai Kaichen Industrial Co, Ltd, Shanghai, China) will be identical to those in the active group but will not induce real neuromodulatory effects, and the total duration, number of pulses and sensory simulation will match the active protocol to ensure effective blinding.

Outcomes

Following the Initiative on Methods, Measurement and Pain Assessment in Clinical Trials (IMMPACT) consensus recommendations for chronic pain research,28 we will adopt a multidimensional assessment framework encompassing four core domains: (1) functional disability, (2) pain intensity, (3) emotional condition and (4) health-related life quality. AEs will be monitored throughout the intervention and evaluation phases to assess their safety. To further explore neurophysiological mechanisms, fMRI will be integrated into the outcomes.

Primary outcome

The primary outcome measure for functional disability assessment will be the ODI, a thoroughly validated 10-item instrument that measures pain-related functional limitations across essential daily activities, with scoring from 0% (no disability) to 100% (maximal disability). The validated Simplified Chinese version will be used, which has demonstrated excellent psychometric properties in Chinese populations. The ODI exhibits excellent test-retest reliability (intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) = 0.89; 95% CI: (0.83–0.93))29 and demonstrates responsiveness to clinically significant improvements in CLBP, with an MCID of 10%.30

Secondary outcomes

The Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) will be used to assess pain intensity, representing a validated unidimensional tool scored from 0 (no pain) to 10 (worst imaginable pain), documenting average pain severity throughout the preceding 7 day period. The VAS has been validated in Chinese populations with demonstrated reliability. The VAS demonstrates high responsiveness to treatment effects in CLBP, with an MCID of 1.5 points established in prior trials.31

Assessment of pain quality and multidimensional features will employ the short-form McGill Pain Questionnaire (SF-MPQ), which measures sensory (11 descriptors, eg, throbbing, shooting) and affective (four descriptors, eg, tiring, punishing) components using a 0–3 rating scale (total score range: 0–45). We will use the validated Simplified Chinese version (SF-MPQ-2-CN), which has shown acceptable reliability and validity in Chinese chronic pain patients. The SF-MPQ shows excellent measurement properties, including internal consistency (Cronbach’s α >0.85 for subscales) and discriminant validity for distinguishing neuropathic from nociceptive pain types.32

Assessment of health-related quality of life will use the 36-item short-form health survey (SF-36), a comprehensive multidomain instrument covering eight areas: physical functioning, role limitations, bodily pain, general health, vitality, social functioning and mental health. The validated Simplified Chinese version will be used with demonstrated psychometric properties in Chinese populations. The SF-36 exhibits robust construct validity (Cronbach’s α >0.85 and reliability coefficients exceeding 0.75 for all dimensions, except social functioning) and sensitivity to chronic pain treatment interventions.33

The pain catastrophising scale (PCS) will evaluate maladaptive cognitive-emotional reactions to pain, consisting of 13 items spanning three dimensions: rumination, magnification and helplessness. Individual items are rated from 0 (not at all) to 4 (all the time), producing a composite score of 0–52. We will employ the validated Simplified Chinese version, which has shown excellent psychometric properties in Chinese chronic pain populations. The PCS exhibits excellent internal consistency (Cronbach’s α=0.86) and test-retest reliability (ICC=0.85) in CLBP populations, with an MCID of two points determined via receiver operating characteristic curve analysis.34

MRI acquisition parameters and data preprocessing

All study participants will complete resting-state functional magnetic resonance imaging (rs-fMRI) examinations before and following 12 week treatment using a 3.0 Tesla Siemens Prisma MRI system equipped with a 32-channel head coil. Functional imaging acquisition will employ a gradient-echo planar imaging protocol with the following technical specifications: repetition time (TR)=2000 ms, echo time (TE)=30 ms, flip angle=90°, field of view (FOV)=224 × 224 mm², voxel resolution=3.5 × 3.5 × 3.5 mm³, 40 axial slices (no gap) and acquisition duration=8 min (240 volumes). Customised foam positioning will be used to restrict head movement, with participants instructed to maintain wakefulness, keep eyes closed and avoid deliberate cognitive tasks. High-resolution T1-weighted anatomical images will be collected using the MPRAGE protocol with these specifications: TR=2300 ms, TE=2.98 ms, voxel resolution=1 × 1 × 1 mm³, FOV=256 × 256 mm² and slice thickness=1 mm, for structural co-registration purposes. fMRI data pre-processing will be conducted using DPABI V6.1 and SPM12 software, following standard preprocessing pipelines including motion correction, temporal and spatial alignment, nuisance signal regression, and spatial smoothing.

Fractional amplitude of low-frequency fluctuations (fALFF) and functional connectivity (FC)

fALFF analysis will be conducted on preprocessed resting-state fMRI datasets using DPARSF,35 calculating the proportion of low-frequency (0.01–0.08 Hz) spectral power relative to the complete frequency spectrum (0–0.25 Hz) through fast Fourier transform. Z-score normalisation will be applied to individual fALFF maps. Seed-based FC analysis will be performed via the CONN toolbox,36 with seed regions (8 mm spheres) determined from clusters showing fALFF differences. After bandpass filtering (0.01–0.08 Hz), Pearson correlations between seed-averaged time series and whole-brain voxels will be calculated, with subsequent Fisher’s z-transformation analysis.

Regional homogeneity (ReHo) and functional connectivity density (FCD)

ReHo will quantify voxel-wise temporal coherence via Kendall’s concordance coefficient among 27 adjacent voxels, followed by standardisation and smoothing using a 6 mm Gaussian kernel.37 FCD will measure whole-brain connection density per voxel using graph theory, globally normalised to mitigate inter-subject variability. Longitudinal analyses will employ linear mixed-effects models (LMMs) with motion and demographic covariates, while non-parametric permutation testing (10 000 iterations) with Monte-Carlo-based cluster correction will ensure statistical robustness.

Safety and adverse effects of PAS

PAS combining TMS and PMS may occasionally induce mild adverse effects, including transient headache or localised muscle soreness at the stimulation sites. All participants will undergo comprehensive pre-intervention screening before each session to identify potential contraindications. AEs will be systematically monitored using validated assessment tools: the Numerical Rating Scale for pain intensity (0–10) and the Fatigue Self-Assessment Scale for fatigue evaluation.

If AEs occur during treatment, the following protocol will be implemented: (1) immediate cessation of stimulation, (2) assessment of vital signs, (3) symptomatic management as appropriate and (4) complete documentation in the AE log. All AEs will be promptly reported to the ethics committee and evaluated by an independent safety monitoring board to determine eligibility for continued participation. AEs will be classified as mild, moderate or severe based on clinical severity and impact on participants. Participants experiencing severe AEs will be withdrawn from the trial and provided with appropriate medical care.

Treatment adherence

Treatment adherence will be defined as completion of ≥16 of 20 sessions for per-protocol (PP) analysis inclusion. Additionally, reasons for missed appointments will be assessed, and adherence patterns will be analysed through team meetings to implement targeted interventions. Exit interviews will be conducted with participants who withdraw from the study to identify factors contributing to missed appointments and optimise the study protocol.

Statistical analysis

Primary efficacy evaluation will employ an intention-to-treat approach, comprising all randomised participants, with multiple imputations applied to address missing primary outcome data. A supplementary PP analysis will include only participants completing ≥80% of treatment sessions with complete outcome assessments. Statistical analyses will be performed using SPSS version 26.0 (IBM Corp, Armonk, NY, USA), with statistical significance defined as a two-sided P value <0.05. For continuous outcomes (ODI, VAS, SF-MPQ, SF-36, PCS), LMMs will be implemented incorporating fixed effects for group (active PAS vs sham PAS), temporal factors (study entry, 4, 8 and 12 week assessments), group×time interaction and adjustment covariates (baseline scores, age, sex), with random subject intercepts to account for within-subject correlation. Residual normality will be assessed via Shapiro–Wilk tests; non-normally distributed outcomes will undergo an appropriate transformation or non-parametric bootstrapping. The primary analytical focus will be the group×time interaction; on achieving significance (p<0.05), subsequent pairwise analyses will be performed using Bonferroni adjustment for multiple testing (adjusted alpha=0.0125 for four time points). Group differences in baseline demographic and clinical parameters will be assessed via independent t-tests or Mann–Whitney U tests for continuous measures and X2 or Fisher’s exact tests for categorical measures. For neuroimaging outcomes (fALFF, FC, ReHo, FCD), analysis of covariance will be performed with baseline values as covariates. Effect sizes will be calculated using Cohen’s d for continuous variables and reported with corresponding 95% CIs. To elucidate potential mechanistic relationships, Pearson’s or Spearman’s correlation analyses will examine associations between changes in clinical outcomes (ODI, VAS) and neuroimaging parameters.

Data integrity and management

Study data will be collected at Guanghua Hospital Affiliated to Shanghai University of Traditional Chinese Medicine by trained data collectors who are blinded to group assignments and uninvolved in other aspects of the study. All collected data will be accurately documented in case report forms (CRF). Following trial completion, CRFs will be transferred to two professional data workers who are also blinded to allocation and uninvolved in other study components. These two data workers will independently extract data from CRFs into Excel (Microsoft Corp, Washington, USA) and verify data accuracy through cross-validation procedures. All electronic data will be stored in password-protected servers with encrypted network systems, while paper documents will be scanned and stored in locked file cabinets with restricted access. De-identified data will be stored separately from files containing participant details and trial identification numbers. An Electronic Data Capture (EDC) system will be used throughout the study to record all data entry and modification traces. The Information Science Department and Clinical Research Centre at Guanghua Hospital will provide the EDC platform and oversee the entire research data collection and management process. Data monitoring will be conducted at weeks 1, 8 and 16 to ensure study quality and data reliability. Data backup maintenance will remain under the principal investigator’s responsibility with additional oversight from the institutional data management committee.

Discussion

The present protocol will evaluate the efficacy of PAS targeting both cortical and peripheral components of the neuromuscular system in patients with CLBP through a rigorous randomised controlled design. The innovative neuromodulatory approach addresses the complex pathophysiology of CLBP, which encompasses both central sensitisation mechanisms and peripheral multifidus muscle dysfunction—a critical insight reflecting the bidirectional relationship between cortical reorganisation and neuromuscular adaptations in chronic pain states.

Numerous studies have documented altered corticospinal and intracortical excitability in patients with CLBP, characterised by reduced inhibition of motor cortical networks and maladaptive reorganisation of somatotopic representations,38,40 yet conventional interventions targeting either central (eg, rTMS alone) or peripheral (exercise therapy) components in isolation have demonstrated limited long-term efficacy due to their unidimensional approach. The present PAS protocol provides several methodological advantages that may address these limitations. First, the temporally coordinated stimulation of M1 and multifidus muscle leverages neurophysiological principles of STDP to potentiate corticospinal connectivity specifically to lumbar paraspinal muscles—a mechanism previously validated in stroke rehabilitation studies that demonstrated enhanced motor recovery through synaptically targeted plasticity.19 20 Second, the implementation of neuronavigation-guided TMS ensures precise and consistent targeting of the multifidus motor representation within M1, thereby minimising inter-session and inter-subject variability that has plagued previous non-navigated neuromodulation studies. Third, the 12 week follow-up assessment includes multi-dimensional outcomes spanning pain severity, functional impairment, life quality and psychological domains under IMMPACT recommendations, complemented by neuroimaging (rs-fMRI) to elucidate underlying mechanisms of therapeutic response. The integration of functional connectivity analyses focusing on pain-processing networks, motor control circuits and default mode network dynamics will offer certain insights into the neural correlates of PAS-induced clinical improvements. The approach may address the gap in understanding the relationship between brain network reorganisation and functional recovery in CLBP.

Nevertheless, several limitations must be acknowledged. Despite using a sham methodology that incorporates peripheral sensory stimulation and head coil positioning to maintain participant blinding, the challenge of creating truly indistinguishable active vs sham neuromodulation conditions remains inherent in all TMS research. Additionally, although the PAS protocol parameters (interstimulus interval, stimulus intensity and session duration) were carefully chosen based on prior PAS literature, the optimal stimulation parameters for CLBP remain undefined and represent a key area for future dose-finding studies.

In conclusion, the present protocol represents a randomised controlled trial exploring synchronised corticospinal neuromodulation through PAS for CLBP, with the potential to establish a novel therapeutic approach that targets both central and peripheral components of the pathophysiological cascade. The mechanistic insights derived from neuroimaging and neurophysiological data will advance the understanding of neural plasticity mechanisms underlying successful pain rehabilitation and may inform future personalised neuromodulation strategies for chronic musculoskeletal pain conditions.

Participant enrolment will commence in June 2025, with study completion anticipated by August 2026.

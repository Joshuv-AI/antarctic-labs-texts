Research note: Prevalence and vlhA gene genotyping analysis of Mycoplasma synoviae in poultry from 2021 to 2023 in central China
Authors: Wenting Zhang, Lanlan Deng, Tingjiang Liu, Mingtao Zhang, Yunqing Guo, Tengfei Zhang, Qiao Hu, Qin Lu, Qingping Luo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Mycoplasma synoviae (MS) is an important avian pathogen associated with respiratory disease, infectious synovitis, and growth retardation in poultry and causes significant economic losses to the poultry industry in China. Although effective prevention relies on vaccination, the currently licensed live vaccine strain, MS-H, shows limited efficacy. Updated epidemiological data are needed to support vaccine candidate screening. This study was performed to investigate the prevalence and genotype distribution of MS in poultry in central China. A total of 1,534 tracheal, laryngeal, and joint cavity samples were collected from chickens with suspected MS infection between 2021 and 2023. Samples were tested by PCR targeting the variable lipoprotein hemagglutinin adhesin gene (vlhA). The overall MS positivity rate among the collected samples was 55.48 %. Eighty-two positive samples were randomly selected for sequencing, and genotyping was performed based on the proline-rich repeat (PRR) region of the vlhA gene. Three genotypes were identified, with genotype L accounting for 91.46 % of positive samples. Phylogenetic analysis revealed significant genetic divergence between field strains and the vaccine strain MS-H (genotype C). These findings highlight the dominance of genotype L in central China and provide essential molecular epidemiological data for the development of more effective control strategies and regionally adapted vaccines.

Introduction

Mycoplasma synoviae (MS) primarily affects the synovial sac, tendon sheaths, and respiratory tract of chickens, resulting in lameness, joint swelling, and reduced egg production (Galluzzo et al., 2022). In breeding flocks, MS infection compromises hatching egg quality, reduces hatchability and chick viability, and increases feed conversion ratios, culling rates, and mortality, leading to substantial economic losses (Cortés et al., 2021). MS is transmitted horizontally through direct contact via the respiratory and digestive tracts and vertically through the semen and oviducts, with vertical transmission being predominant and complicating disease control efforts (Gottstein et al., 2023). In addition, MS often contributes to coinfections with other avian pathogens, including avian reovirus, (ARV), infectious laryngotracheitis virus (ILTV), Newcastle disease virus (NDV), and Escherichia coli (E. coli), thereby intensifying clinical outcomes (Zhang et al., 2023). Although specific treatments are currently unavailable, vaccines are critical for controlling MS. However, MS-H, the only licensed live vaccine, provides limited protection. Despite exhibiting only a single serotype, MS strains display considerable variability in virulence and pathogenicity, necessitating molecular typing for epidemiological surveillance and genotype-specific vaccine development.

The variable lipoprotein hemagglutinin adhesin gene (vlhA) encodes a surface protein that facilitates MS colonization and virulence. Upon post-translational cleavage, this protein gives rise to membrane surface and hemagglutinin proteins (MSPBs and MSPAs, respectively), both exhibiting high antigenic variability (Bencina et al., 2001). Therefore, the vlhA gene is widely used for molecular diagnostics and evolutionary studies. vlhA sequence typing has become a standard MS genotyping method. Based on nucleotide polymorphisms in the RIII region and insertions/deletions in the proline-rich repeat (PRR) region, MS strains have been classified into 14 genotypes (A–N).

Globally, MS infection rates are highest in Africa and South America, with an increasing incidence observed in Europe, Asia, North America, and Oceania. In southern Italy, an MS positivity rate of 23.25 % was reported from 400 samples collected across commercial and rural farms (Galluzzo et al., 2022). In eastern Spain, positivity rates reached 95 % in laying hens and 74 % in broilers, with disease prevalence rates of 95 % and 34 %, respectively (Cortés et al., 2021). In Croatia, 15 farms reported 98.6 % and 85.7 % MS seropositivity and disease prevalence rates, respectively (Gottstein et al., 2023). In China, the rise in MS prevalence has paralleled the rapid development of the poultry industry. From 2010 to 2015, the average MS positivity rate was 41.19 % in serum samples from 21 provinces (Xue et al., 2017), having increased to 49.62 % in non-immunized chicken flocks in Fujian province between 2018 and 2020 (Wang et al., 2022) and to 66.53 % in samples collected from suspected cases between 2020 and 2021 (Wei et al., 2023). MS genotype distribution, as determined by vlhA sequencing, varies regionally. In Thailand, nine and five of 20 MS field isolates from registered chicken farms in 2015 were identified as type E and L, respectively (Limpavithayakul et al., 2016). In Guangdong, China, the MS isolates collected from 2019 to 2020 were predominantly classified as 29 K-type and 3 A-type strains (Kang et al., 2023).

Despite the growing awareness of MS epidemiology in China, recent detailed genotype-specific epidemiological data from central China remain scarce, hindering the effective control measure and vaccine deployment and optimization in this critical poultry production region. This omission has now been clearly stated as a primary motivation for this study, emphasizing the necessity of our genotype surveillance for developing regionally effective control strategies. Therefore, in this study, we aimed to evaluate MS prevalence and genotype distribution in central China. Specifically, we investigated the positivity rates of MS infections across 13 cities, conducted sequence analyses of the conserved 5′ region of vlhA from representative positive samples, and characterized the circulating genotypes based on the length of the PRR region. Clarifying the current epidemiological and genotypic landscape of MS in central China is critical for informing vaccine candidate selection and developing targeted disease prevention strategies.

Materials and methods

Sample collection

A total of 1,534 clinical samples were collected between 2021 and 2023 from chickens, ducks, and pigeons across 13 cities in central China. All the sampled poultry were suspected of Mycoplasma synoviae (MS) infection, exhibiting symptoms such as reduced egg production, lameness, and joint swelling. The samples included throat swabs, tracheal tissues, laryngeal specimens, and foot joint tissues. Seasonally, 310 samples were collected from March to May (spring), 586 from June to August (summer), 309 from September to November (autumn), and 239 from December to February (winter). Samples were obtained during different months throughout the study period and placed into 1.5 mL EP tubes containing bacterial transport medium. All samples were stored at 4°C until further molecular analysis.

PCR identification

Genomic DNA was extracted from each sample using the Tianamp Bacteria DNA Kit (Tiangen Biochemical Technology (Beijing) Co., Ltd., Beijing, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. MS was identified by PCR targeting vlhA gene using specific primers (forward: 5′-TACTATTAGCAGCTAGTGCAGTG-3′; reverse: 5′-CATCTTCTTTAACTGCTTTAGCAGC-3′). The 25 µL PCR reaction mixture contained 12.5 µL of 2 × Taq Master Mix, 1 µL each of forward and reverse primers, 1 µL of template DNA, and 9.5 µL of nuclease-free water. The thermal cycling conditions were as follows: initial denaturation at 95°C for 5 min; 35 cycles of denaturation at 95°C for 30 s, annealing at 55°C for 30 s, and extension at 72°C for 30 s; followed by a final extension at 72°C for 5 min. MG and MS strains preserved in our laboratory, were used as negative and positive controls, respectively. The PCR products were analyzed by electrophoresis on a 0.8 % agarose gel, and the proportion of MS-positive samples was recorded and analyzed statistically.

Sequence and phylogenetic analyses

PCR products from 82 randomly selected MS-positive samples were submitted to Sangon Biotech (Shanghai) Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China) for bidirectional sequencing. To ensure representativeness, the random selection of positive samples was performed to proportionally cover different years, farms, and poultry species. The resulting sequences were assembled and trimmed of unreliable terminal regions using Lasergene 7.1 software package (DNA STAR, Inc., Madison, WI, USA) to ensure accuracy prior to genotyping and phylogenetic analysis. Sequence alignment of the 5′ conserved region of the vlhA gene was also performed using the Seqman module in the Lasergene 7.1 software package. Genotyping of these samples was based on the length of the PRR region, with PRR fragments of 38, 45, 32, 23, 19, 36, 51, 46, 28, 20, 12, 35, 30, and 41 amino acids corresponding to types A, B, C, D, E, F, G, H, I, J, K, L, M, and N, respectively (Kang et al., 2023). Additionally, multiple sequence alignment was performed using the ClustalW algorithm to compare vlhA sequences from the positive samples with those of reference strains. Phylogenetic analysis of the 5′ end of the vlhA gene was conducted using MEGA 7.0 software. The neighbor-joining method was used to infer evolutionary relationships, and 1,000 bootstrap replicates were performed to assess branch reliability. Reference sequences were obtained from GenBank on MS genotype classification.

Results and discussion

The overall MS positivity rate was 55.48 % (856/1543), with rates ranging from 46.02 % to 58.45 % across the 13 surveyed cities. When stratified by year, the MS positivity rate was 56.78 % in 2021, 55.69 % in 2022, and 53.81 % in 2023. The positivity rate showed little difference across years. When stratified by season, the MS positivity rate was 63.4 % (151/238) in spring (March to May), 65.3 % (453/694) in summer (June to August), 25.8 % (86/334) in autumn (September to November), and 29.2 % (81/277) in winter (December to February). The warm and humid conditions in spring and summer prolong pathogen survival in poultry houses and on equipment, raising environmental contamination and contact transmission risks. During spring (i.e., the peak laying season) high metabolic demand might shift immune resources to reproduction, reducing pathogen resistance. Summer heat stress lowers feed intake, disrupts intestinal function, and increases water consumption. Poor ventilation, such as uneven airflow or localized heat, can also compromise respiratory mucosa, facilitating MS infection. These findings revealed clear seasonal variation, with significantly higher detection rates in spring and summer than in autumn and winter.

Genotypic characterization of the 82 MS-positive samples, based on analysis of the PRR region of the vlhA gene, revealed three distinct types (Table 1): type C (32 amino acids), type E (19 amino acids), and type L (35 amino acids). Type L was the most prevalent, accounting for 91.46 % of positive samples (75/82), whereas types E and C represented 6.10 % (5/82) and 2.44 % (2/82), respectively. In 2021, two positive samples belonged to type E. In 2022, three positive samples were type E and two were type C. All positive samples identified in 2023 were type L. Geographically, type C was detected only in Wuhan, whereas type E was found in both Wuhan and Huanggang. These results indicate that type L has become the dominant circulating genotype in central China in recent years. In contrast to our findings, earlier studies in China reported type K as the predominant MS genotype (Kang et al., 2023). This shift in genotype distribution highlights the dynamic nature of MS epidemiology. We propose that the genotypic shift could result from the introduction of poultry from genotype l-endemic areas, inadequate breeder screening, and vertical transmission from infected birds. The high-level adaptability of the pathogen, via both vertical and horizontal transmission, helps its persistence. Regional spread is further facilitated by the movement of contaminated poultry, equipment, personnel, and shared resources like feed and water. Along with potential antimicrobial or vaccination pressures, these factors might collectively explain the dominance of genotype L. Of particular concern is the potential antigenic mismatch between the dominant type L and MS-H vaccine strain (type C), which may contribute to the suboptimal vaccine efficacy in central China. Continuous monitoring of prevalent genotypes is essential for informing targeted vaccination programs and formulating effective prevention policies. Genotyping of MS positive samples isolated from poultry between 2021 and 2023 in central China.

According to the phylogenetic tree constructed from the conserved 5′ region of vlhA, 82 MS-positive samples and 18 reference strains representing different genotypes and geographical origins clustered into five distinct evolutionary branches (groups 1–5) (Fig. 1). Group 1 comprised 76 MS-positive samples and four reference strains prevalent in China. Among these reference strains were two type L and two type K variants, whereas the field samples included 75 type L strains and one type E strain. Group 2 contained a single reference strain of type L that originated from Thailand. Group 3 included two reference strains, representing types F and G. Group 4 consisted of five MS-positive samples (two type C and three type E) and six reference strains (two type C and one each of types A, J, H, and I). Group 5 contained one MS-positive sample (type E) and five reference strains (types B, D, and E). In Group 4, all five MS-positive samples were collected from Wuhan and Huanggang in 2022 and showed close genetic proximity to the vaccine strain MS-H and classic strain WVU1853. Notably, although most MS-positive samples exhibited high genetic similarity and formed one branch (group 1), they were from distinct host species across different geographic regions in central China. Only a few genetically divergent MS strains have been isolated from the same geographical area. These findings suggest that MS strains currently circulating in central China are genetically homogeneous, forming a dominant lineage distinct from the MS-H vaccine strain, the WVU1853 classic strain, and most international reference strains. No significant correlation was observed between genetic clustering and either geographic origin or host species, indicating that neither factor played a dominant role in shaping the genetic diversity of MS strains in this region. We propose that extensive horizontal transmission (facilitated by multi-species farming, frequent poultry trade, wild bird activity, and the movement of contaminated equipment or personnel) might have enabled the pathogen to cross host and geographic barriers, leading to genetic mixing among strains from diverse sources. In addition, the coexistence of vertical transmission in breeding flocks and introduced strains through horizontal transmission could have potentially further obscured specific host or geographic signals due to genetic recombination and population complexity. However, this study had some limitations. Sampling bias may have occurred, and the study excluded samples from outside central China.

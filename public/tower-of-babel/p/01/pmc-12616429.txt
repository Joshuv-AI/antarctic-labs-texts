Ultrahigh-Response Humidity Sensor Arrays Based on a Graphene/Polymer Composite for Contactless Gesture Monitoring
Authors: Jinqi Hou, Xigang Zhu, Lu Zheng, Weiwei Li, Jiuwei Gao, He Huang, Yue Li, Manzhang Xu, Xuewen Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Flexible humidity sensors have attracted widespread attention for their potential applications in telemedicine, motion detection, etc. Compared with conventional humidity sensors, flexible humidity sensors have advantages in flexibility, integration, and portability. However, reported flexible humidity sensors usually suffer from a low response, complex manufacturing processes, and poor stability. We fabricate a humidity sensor using a graphene/polymer composite as the sensitive material with a focus on improving the response and simplifying the manufacturing process. Then, we optimize the performance of the prepared flexible sensor by component design. The prepared flexible humidity sensor has a response of 1123%, and response and recovery times of 61.1/92.0 s, respectively, showing excellent humidity sensing performance. The human respiration, skin humidity field, and environmental humidity detection applications are successfully realized by the humidity sensor. Besides, the contactless gesture monitoring is accomplished by the humidity sensor array, demonstrating the potential for further development into healthcare and contactless human–machine interaction.

Introduction

Humidity sensors are widely adopted in healthcare, industrial production, environmental monitoring, and smart agriculture. − Compared with rigid humidity sensors, flexible humidity sensors are able to adapt to complex conditions such as curved surface contact and dynamic deformation due to their significant features of being lightweight, thin, flexible, and permeable. − Therefore, flexible humidity sensors are increasingly important in wearable electronic systems for human–robot interaction, such as respiration monitoring, skin moisture monitoring, and contactless monitoring. − Based on the sensing mechanism, the flexible humidity sensors can be divided into three types, including resistive, ion conduction, and capacitive. − Among these categories, resistive humidity sensors have attracted considerable interest due to their stable performance, fast response, and simple fabrication process. ,

Humidity-sensitive materials are the most important component of resistive humidity sensors and have been widely investigated owing to their effective influence on the device performance. , Compared with the numerous sensitive materials, such as polymers, metal oxides, and metal sulfides, graphene-based materials are worth considering because the unique two-dimensional structure induces a large specific surface area and multiple water molecule adsorption sites. ,− For example, the reduced graphene oxide (rGO)/MoS2 composite has been adopted to fabricate the flexible humidity sensor, showing a fast response time (0.65 s), a high response (96.7%), and a wide relative humidity (RH) range (11%–95% RH), which is applied for human respiration and the nontouch switching by finger. The flexible humidity sensor has been fabricated to monitor human respiration by a high-strength rGO-water-soluble cellulose acetate composite film, and the sensor exhibits a response time of ∼10 s and a high response of 250.7%. Besides, a humidity sensor was fabricated based on cellulose paper coated with graphene, which shows a high response of 290% and a response time of 9 s. Although the sensor performance has been improved in these flexible humidity sensors, the optimization of the high sensing response remains a great challenge. Therefore, a simple manufacturing process is urgently needed to develop high-response graphene-based flexible humidity sensors for practical applications.

Here, a simple manufacturing method was proposed for preparing graphene/polymer composite sensitive material to fabricate a high-performance flexible humidity sensor. The sensor demonstrates an ultrahigh response of 1123%, fast response–recovery times of 61.1/92.0 s, respectively, and great bending stability with 1000 bending cycles. In addition, the prepared humidity sensor was used for the detection of the respiratory frequency and humidity field of the finger, and a humidity sensor array was adopted for contactless gesture monitoring, demonstrating its potential value in the wearable field and for contactless interaction. Our findings suggest that the multicomponent composite strategy is an effective pathway for fabricating high-performance flexible humidity sensors, which opens new opportunities for the next flexible electronic devices and wearable health monitoring.

Experimental Section

Materials

Graphene (GR) was purchased from Annuohe New Energy Technology Co., Ltd., Tianjin, China. Hydroxyethyl cellulose (HEC), xanthan gum (XG), and glycerol (Gl) were purchased from Shanghai Aladdin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd., China. Water-borne polyurethane (WPU) was purchased from Shanghai Macklin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd., China. Deionized water (DI water) used in the experiments was processed through a Milli-Q reference water purification system to reach a resistivity of 18.2 MΩ·cm at 25 °C.

Preparation of Humidity-Sensitive Material

In a typical process, WPU and DI water were mixed in a certain mass ratio to make a 1.5 g precursor by a magnetic stirrer at 500 rpm for 30 min. Then, 20 mg of GR, 30 mg of HEC, 10 mg of XG, and 30 mg of Gl were added sequentially to the prepared precursor under magnetic stirring at 500 rpm for 1 h to obtain a homogeneous paste as the sensitive material for fabricating a flexible humidity sensor.

Characterization of Materials

The materials were characterized by using an Optical image (Nexcope NMM910), scanning electron microscopy (SEM, ZEISS, Gemini SEM 300), Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FT-IR, Bruker, Tensor II), and Raman spectroscopy (WITEC Alpha 300R, with a 532 nm laser).

Fabrication of the Flexible Humidity Sensor

The polyimide (PI) substrate was cleaned with DI water and ethanol and blown with an air gun to remove chemicals and impurities. After rinsing, the 10/50 nm Cr/Au metal layer was evaporated on the PI substrate, and the interdigitated electrodes were fabricated with 120 μm interdigital spacing by the laser direct writing method. Finally, the sensitive material was scraped on the interdigitated electrodes, and the flexible humidity sensors could be easily fabricated after baking in an oven.

Preparation of the Flexible Humidity Sensor Array

A polyethylene terephthalate (PET) substrate was used for fabricating the sensor array. First, 10/50 nm Cr/Au metal layers were evaporated on the cleaned PET substrate. Then, the electrodes of the 3 × 3 array were processed on the substrate by laser direct writing according to the designed electrode structure. The size of the array was 63.5 × 43.5 mm, and the size of a single interdigital electrode was 6.0 mm × 6.0 mm. Finally, the sensitive materials were printed on the interdigital electrodes of the array through screen printing.

Sensor Performance Measurement

The bending stability of the prepared flexible humidity sensor was evaluated by using a homemade mechanical platform composed of a stepper motor and a fixture. − Before the bending, the flexible humidity sensor was fixed on both sides with fixtures, and the fixture on the left was connected to a stepper motor assembly that could generate displacement. Eventually, the resistance at different bending states of the sensor was measured using a sourcemeter (Keithley, 2450).

A homemade humidity sensing performance evaluation system was used to evaluate the sensing performance. The resistance at different humidity values of the sensor and the current–voltage characteristics were measured automatically by using a sourcemeter. The sensor was placed in the chamber for measurement; dry air was bubbled into the supersaturated salt solution to provide different relative humidity values for the chamber, and the airflow was controlled by a flow meter and a solenoid valve. Actual humidity values in the test chamber were simultaneously recorded by using a commercial humidity sensor (SENSIRION, SHT35). The response (ΔR/R 0) of the humidity sensor was determined by ΔR/R 0 = (R – R 0/R 0), where R and R 0 are the resistance value of the sensor at a given relative humidity and in dry air, respectively. The recovery of the flexible humidity sensor is defined as the sensor response after 120 s in a given humidity environment and then 120 s in 0% humidity. And the response/recovery time is the time that the electrical signal reaches 90% of the change from its dry state to a certain relative humidity/from a stable value at a certain humidity to its dry state.

The multichannel signal acquisition system was used to collect the array data. The nine channels of the sensor array were connected to the analog-to-digital chip through cables. When the resistance of the sensor changed due to relative humidity, the analog voltage signal of each channel was collected by the analog-to-digital chip. After processing, the signal was uploaded to the terminal by a Bluetooth chip.

Results and Discussion

Preparation and Characterization of GR/Polymer Composite Material

The humidity sensing performance of humidity sensors depends mainly on the properties of the material, such as hydrophilic groups or microstructures, to absorb water molecules. Here, a multicomponent composite strategy was used to design a GR/polymer composite material, in which graphene sheets were wrapped with polymer materials. The graphene provides the conductivity pathway, and the polymer materials provide the hydrophilic groups. WPU exhibits excellent film-forming properties and strong adhesion, enabling sensitive materials to form robust bonds with substrates. Both HEC and XG are natural polymers, which serve as effective dispersing agents to prevent significant aggregation of graphene. Their thickening effect in aqueous solutions also facilitates the assembly of sensitive materials onto the substrates. Moreover, upon moisture absorption, they demonstrate notable moisture-induced swelling, which contributes to the enhanced overall responsiveness. The hygroscopicity of Gly further improves the responsiveness of the sensitive material. When the GR/polymer composite material adsorbs and interacts with water molecules, the expansion of polymer will destroy the conductive path of graphene and increase the resistance, providing excellent moisture-sensitive properties for the sensitive material. A simple preparation method was used to synthesize a high-performance GR/polymer composite material. The typical GR/polymer composite material fabrication process is shown in Figure a. First, WPU was doped into DI water to prepare the WPU precursor. Then, 20 mg of GR and 70 mg of mixed polymers (30 mg of HEC, 10 mg of XG, and 30 mg of Gl) were added to the WPU precursor solution under magnetic stirring for 1 h to obtain a homogeneous paste. Finally, the GR/polymer composite material was prepared and laid the foundation for the subsequent preparation of flexible humidity sensors.

Preparation and characterization of GR/polymer composite material. (a) The preparation process and material structure for GR/polymer composite material. (b–d) Raman spectrum, FTIR spectrum, and SEM image of GR/polymer composite material. Scale bar, 2 μm in (d).

The GR/polymer composite material was characterized by Raman and Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy. The typical Raman spectrum of the GR/polymer composite material is shown in Figure b. The D peak (∼1341 cm–1) in the Raman spectra indicates the vibration of carbon atoms in the sp3 electronic configuration, structural defects in the sp2 domains, and partially disordered structures. The G peak (∼1570 cm–1) indicates that the G-band arises from the first-order scattering of the E 2g phonon of the vibrational modes in the domain plane of sp2 carbon atoms, and the 2D peak (∼2695 cm–1) indicates that graphene is multilayered. − And the high G peak in the GR/polymer composite material indicates an ordered carbon atom in the overall material. The typical FTIR spectrum of GR/polymer composite material is shown in Figure c, and the absorption peaks can be clearly observed. The absorption peak at 3349 cm–1 could be the stretching vibrations of N–H. The absorption peaks at 2920, 1370, and 2869 cm–1 represent the symmetric stretching vibration, asymmetric stretching vibration, and symmetric stretching vibration of C–H, respectively. The absorption peaks at 1730, 1233, and 1019 cm–1 correspond to the CO, C–O, and C–O–C vibration absorption peaks, respectively, which prove the existence of Gly, HEC, XG, and WPU. − The analysis of the Fourier transform infrared spectrum shows that there are a large number of hydrophilic groups (N–H and C–H groups) on the surface of the sensitive material, which is conducive to the adsorption of water molecules. All of the Raman and FTIR results indicated that GR/polymer composite material was successfully synthesized.

The microstructure of the GR/polymer composite material was further examined by SEM, as shown in Figure d. The surface of polymers is visibly coated by graphene, and the graphene filled between the two layers causes a certain gap between the layers, increasing the contact area between the sensitive material and gas. Additionally, due to the effect of polymers, the overlapping method between the composite material layers is more diverse than that of graphene, and the adsorption of water molecules by the polymer between the layers can cause wet swelling and further affect the contact of the graphene layers.

Preparation of the GR/Polymer Composite-Based Flexible Humidity Sensor

The flexible humidity sensor was further fabricated by GR/polymer composite material, and the specific preparation process is fully demonstrated in Figure a. First, the 10/50 nm Cr/Au metal was evaporated on the PI substrate by vacuum thermal evaporation. Then, the laser direct writing process method was adopted for the preparation of flexible interdigitated electrodes due to the advantage of quick and accurate processing. As shown in Figure b, the flexible interdigitated electrodes on PI substrate with the interdigital spacing of 120 μm was prepared in batches by the laser direct writing method. After being cleaned by deionized water and ethanol orderly, the interdigitated electrodes with the smooth surface and edge can be observed from the optical image in Figure c, indicating that the high-quality flexible interdigitated electrodes were successfully fabricated. Subsequently, the GR/polymer composite material was scraped on the flexible interdigitated electrodes. Finally, the GR/polymer composite material-based flexible humidity sensors can be easily fabricated after baking in an oven to enhance the binding force between the PI substrate and sensing materials. The typical photograph of the fabricated GR/polymer composite-based flexible humidity sensors is shown in Figure d,e. It can be noticed that the GR/polymer composite material was adhered well with the PI substrate and interdigitated electrodes. The sensitive material of the device has no damage, such as shedding or cracks under the bending state, indicating the high stability of the flexible humidity sensor.

The GR/polymer composite-based flexible humidity sensor serves as a wearable device to monitor the humidity signal and overcome the effect of bending during the activity. Therefore, the stability experiment of the GR/polymer composite-based flexible humidity sensor has been carried out under different bending states. The stability experiments of the GR/polymer composite-based flexible humidity sensor were carried out based on the homemade bending test platform, which was composed of a stepper motor and a fixture. During the stability experiments, the GR/polymer composite-based flexible humidity sensor was bent at different length changes (ΔL 1 = 6 mm and ΔL 2 = 4 mm). The radius of the sensor under bending is determined by the chord length (C) and arc length (L), and the calculation formula is C = 2r sin­(L/2r). The further calculation formula of the stain is ε = y/2r. − And the dimensions of the GR/polymer composite-based flexible humidity sensor are 2.5 cm (length) × 1.5 cm (width) × 75 μm (thickness). The value of C and y can be obtained by the length changes and thickness of the flexible humidity sensor, respectively. Therefore, two typical radii of 10.02 and 12.44 mm (as shown inset in Figure f) were adopted for the bending test, which correspond to the tensile strain of 0.37% and 0.30%, respectively. The sensor response can be calculated to be 15% and 10% under the tensile strain of 0.37% and 0.30%, respectively, which indicates that the response is nonsusceptible to the strain. After 1000 bending cycles under strains of 0.37% and 0.30%, the devices remain in the original state without structural damage (Figure f), which indicates that the GR/polymer composite-based flexible humidity sensor shows excellent stability under the bending test and is insensitive to the strain signal.

Humidity Sensing Performance of the GR/Polymer Composite-Based Flexible Humidity Sensor

In order to investigate the humidity sensing performance of GR/polymer composite-based flexible humidity sensors, a series of humidity sensors with different mass ratios of DI water to WPU (the mass ratios of 4, 8, 12, and 16, namely, WD1, WD2, WD3, and WD4, respectively) have been fabricated. Real-time dynamic sensor response analysis of the four humidity sensors has been carried out based on the humidity sensor testing system, as shown in Figure a. As noted, all the sensors show a great sensor response to relative humidity. The sensor response increased as relative humidity was raised in steps of 17%, 36%, 45%, 60%, 72%, 80%, and 91%. It can be observed that the sensor response first increases and then decreases with the increase of mass ratio of DI water/WPU, and the highest response can be obtained from device WD3 (Figure b). The hydrophilic groups absorb water molecules and expand the polymer, destroying the conductive path of graphene and increasing the resistance. With the increase in the mass ratio of DI water/WPU, the conductive properties of the sensitive material are enhanced, resulting in a higher sensor response. However, as the proportion of water-based polyurethane continues to decrease, the expansion ratio of the samples is affected, resulting in a decrease in the sensor performance. The sensor response can reach 1123.37% under a relative humidity of 91% in WD3, which is much higher than that of reported rGO/MoS2, graphene oxide/shape memory polylactic acid fiber, MXene/GO, and nylon fabric/GO net materials. The sensor response/recovery performance can be calculated to be 194.10%/114.36%, 542.27%/51.52%, 1123.37%/69.38%, and 878.01%/65.08% of WD1, WD2, WD3, and WD4 devices, respectively. As noticed in Figure c, the sensor response of WD3 shows the highest value, and the recovery is similar to that of WD2 and WD4. Therefore, WD3 has been chosen for further investigating the reproducibility and stability of flexible humidity sensor. The relative-humidity-dependent logarithmic sensor response of the WD3 device is shown in Figure d. As noticed, the logarithmic sensor response represents a great linear fit (R 2 = 0.957) to relative humidity.

The repeatability is an important performance to evaluate a flexible humidity sensor. Therefore, the three times repeatability cycle of WD3 was carried out under the relative humidity (RH) of 35%, 57%, and 79%, as shown in Figure e. The device was immersed in dry air for 200 s and then immersed alternately in dry air and air with a given relative humidity for 120 s. The results are shown in Figure f; the sensor response reduces slightly after three cycles, which proves that the device has good stability. The reduced sensor response is due to the baseline drift under high humidity conditions, which is caused by the incompletely desorbed water molecules on the surface under high humidity conditions. The sensor response and recovery of WD3 under a relative humidity of 57% were extracted from Figure e, and the result is shown in Figure g. As noticed, both the response time and recovery time decrease with an increase of the cycle number, which is caused by the retention of the expanded structure of the sensitive material after expansion. The material expands when it absorbs water molecules, leading to an increase in its internal pores. After dehydration, this expanded structure is retained, which enhances the subsequent adsorption efficiency of the material for water molecules.

The stability of the flexible humidity sensor is important in practical application prospects. To evaluate the long-term stability of the GR/polymer composite-based flexible humidity sensor, the WD3 device was exposed to environmental conditions for 30 days, and the response of the sensor was recorded per 10 days in different humidity values (33% and 73%). The variation of the sensor response at different humidity values for 30 days is shown in Figure h. The sensor shows a stability response with negligible fluctuation under the same relative humidity in four measurements, which confirms long-term stability of the sensor. Besides, the WD3 device was also used to detect ambient humidity. The WD3 device and the commercial humidity sensor were fixed in the chamber and immersed in dry air for 10 min, which was then allowed to gradually reach the ambient humidity value in 24 h naturally. The relative humidity of WD3 is converted by sensor response data in Figure a–d, and the result is shown in Figure i. As noted, the relative humidity of the WD3 device agrees with the commercial sensor, indicating the accurate humidity measurement of the WD3 device.

Applications in Respiratory Detection and Contactless Gesture Monitoring

Human respiratory rate is a key signal to evaluate the human physical and mental state, which provides important information related to human mental stress, extreme physical fatigue, and some diseases. At present, a high-performance flexible humidity sensor can be attached to the mask for evaluating respiratory frequency by monitoring the relative humidity of the airflow on the side of the mask. As shown in Figure a, the GR/polymer composite-based flexible humidity sensor was placed on the mask. Then, the mask was covered on the volunteer (Figure b) to evaluate the respiratory frequency. During breathing, the sensor response changed periodically with the periodical change of relative humidity due to the output of wet air and the input of the ambient gas. The respiratory frequency can further be calculated by the periodic change of the sensor response from respiratory detection (slow, normal, and rapid breathing in Figure c). The respiratory rate of the volunteer under normal conditions is about 10 times per minute according to calculation. Compared to normal breathing, the volunteer showed a significant decrease and increase in respiratory rate during deep and rapid breathing, about 5 breaths per minute and 17 breaths per minute, respectively.

Besides, the noncontact human–machine interaction can be carried out by using the humidity field on the surface of the finger. Flexible humidity sensors show different responses in various contactless interaction scenes due to the distance between the finger and sensor. And the WD3 device was used to detect the humidity field of the finger. The finger is lowered to a distance of 5 mm from the sensor and raised three times, and then, the finger is lowered to a height of 4, 3, 2, and 1 mm from the sensor and raised once. As shown in Figure d, the sensor response decreased as the distance between the fingertip and sensor increased. At the distance of 5 mm, there is no significant difference in the three responses, which shows that the sensor has a potential application value in contactless monitoring that can reduce the risk of virus transmission.

The finger humidity field experiment shows that the WD3 device has good contactless monitoring capability. Therefore, a 3 × 3 humidity sensor array was further fabricated on PET substrates to achieve multipoint detection and dynamic sliding gesture monitoring capabilities, which is shown in Figure e. And the sensors in the array are numbered according to their positions (Figure f). The acquisition and wireless transmission of multichannel array data is realized by the single-chip microcomputer test system. The three letters “N”, “P”, and “U” were written with a finger above the prepared flexible humidity sensor array, and each humidity sensor responds sequentially according to the trajectory of the letter writing (Figure g). Due to the different duration and height of the fingers passing over each sensing unit, the responses of the sensing units were inconsistent. The data obtained from the test of the GR/polymer composite-based flexible humidity sensor array are processed, and the shape of each letter is visually presented in the form of a planar thermodynamic diagram. The result is shown in Figure h, which shows the “N” “P” “U” letter shapes and the order of writing strokes. This sensor, which is low-cost and has a simple manufacturing process, can be used in applications such as contactless gesture monitoring for disease prevention.

Conclusions

In summary, we have proposed a multicomponent composite strategy to prepare the sensitive material by compositing graphene and polymers. Then, the optimized component was adopted to achieve a high-performance flexible humidity sensor. The prepared sensor has a high response (1123%), superior mechanical stability (1000 bending cycles), excellent repeatability, and long-term stability (30 days). In addition, our sensor is able to monitor environmental humidity, skin humidity field, and human respiration. Moreover, the 3 × 3 flexible humidity sensor array can realize the resolution of the trajectory, which demonstrates the application potential in the field of wearable and contactless interaction. The multicomponent composite strategy provides valuable insights for the development of highly responsive flexible humidity sensors. However, their response and recovery times still require further improvement. Introducing a porous microstructure into the sensitive material film can create rapid diffusion pathways for water molecules, possibly significantly reducing the response and recovery time of the sensor, thereby facilitating its applications in wearable devices and noncontact interactive systems.

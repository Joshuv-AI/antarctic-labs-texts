Determinants of Satisfaction with Medical Care for Hidradenitis Suppurativa: Results from a Multicentre Survey in Germany
Authors: Kerstin Wolk, Dagmar Wilsmann-Theis, Nessr Abu Rached, Giorgia Cugno, Rotraut Moessner, Christian Kromer, Falk G Bechara, Katharina Assaf, Wiebke K Peitsch, Lisa C Schneider, Andreas Happ, Valentina Siddi, Diana Kubitzki, Durdana Groß, Markus Friedrich, Staffan Vandersee, Khusru Asadullah, Sylke Schneider-Burrus, Georgios Kokolakis, Robert Sabat
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionHidradenitis suppurativa (HS) is a chronic inflammatory skin disease associated with considerable physical and psychosocial distress. Recent studies have reported low levels of satisfaction among patients with HS regarding the medical care of their condition. The objective of the study was to identify modifiable determinants of low satisfaction with HS care from both patient and dermatologist perspectives.MethodsThis prospective, questionnaire-based survey was conducted among patients with HS and, for comparison, among those with psoriasis, as well as their treating dermatologists, in 20 dermatological centres in Germany.ResultsA total of 257 patients and 76 dermatologists participated in the study. The proportion of patients reporting high satisfaction with care for their skin disease (scores > 8 on a 0–10 scale) was 30.7% for HS and 69.4% for psoriasis (P < 0.001). The proportion of dermatologists highly satisfied with medical care for HS and psoriasis was 0.0% and 38.9%, respectively (P < 0.001). Multiple regression modelling revealed that satisfaction with pharmacotherapy was the primary predictor of medical care satisfaction for both patients and dermatologists. Experience with systemic antibiotics was associated with lower patient satisfaction regarding pharmacotherapy, while satisfaction tended to be higher when patients had experienced therapy with biologics. Another significant determinant of patient satisfaction with medical care was the provision of information about HS by dermatologists, which was linked to consultations occurring at least quarterly and lasting ≥ 20 min. No association was observed between disease severity and the identified determinants of patient satisfaction with medical care. Dermatologists’ satisfaction with medical care was additionally linked with the perceived availability of referral options to other dermatological facilities.ConclusionsImprovements in patient satisfaction with HS care may be achieved through enhanced patient education about the disease and regular dermatologist consultations; however, optimal HS management ultimately requires effective pharmacological treatment.Trial Registrationdrks.de, DRKS00031572.Keywords: Biologic, Education, Health care, Hidradenitis suppurativa, Psoriasis, Skin disease, Survey, Treatment

Key Summary Points

Why carry out this study?Hidradenitis suppurativa (HS) is a chronic inflammatory skin disease that causes pain and psychosocial distress, and is associated with profound impairment of personal and professional life as well as reduced life expectancy.Despite advances in HS awareness and treatment over the past decade, recent research shows that many patients remain dissatisfied with their treatment and the overall medical management they receive for their condition.The study aimed to identify factors responsible for the low levels of satisfaction with HS care among patients and dermatologists that are modifiable.What was learned from the study?Our survey across 20 dermatological care centres in Germany indicates that effective drug treatment is the most important factor associated with satisfaction with HS medical care among patients and physicians.In addition, medium-term improvements may be achieved through readily implementable measures, including regular dermatologist consultations, adequate time for patient education and clearly defined referral pathways to specialised dermatological services.

Introduction

Hidradenitis suppurativa (HS) is a chronic inflammatory disease that is characterised by the recurrent appearance and/or persistence of painful, deep-seated nodules, abscesses and draining tunnels in the skin of the axillary, inguinal, perianal and gluteal areas [1]. Estimates of HS prevalence vary greatly, but seem to be around 1% in Europe [2]. The disease usually manifests in young adults [3]. Without adequate intervention, HS leads to skin structure destruction and severe scarring, which may further restrict mobility [3]. Pain, the need for wound dressings [4], depression, anxiety and suicidality [5], as well as sexual difficulties [6, 7], can severely affect both the personal and professional life of patients [8–11]. HS is associated with a significantly reduced life expectancy [12], which is mainly due to metabolic comorbidity and its cardiovascular sequelae [13, 14].

Over the last decade, substantial efforts have been made to raise awareness for HS, create registries, develop meaningful disease scores and treatment guidelines, investigate its pathogenesis and advance new therapies [15–19]. There are three antibody drugs available that have been approved for the treatment of moderate-to-severe HS and show at least moderate efficacy in these patients: adalimumab targeting tumour-necrosis factor-α [20], and secukinumab and bimekizumab both targeting interleukin-17 cytokines [21, 22]. Despite this, HS is still widely treated with medications whose use is based on less evidence and/or historical concepts of the disease, including antibiotics (e.g. doxycycline or a combination of clindamycin and rifampicin), antiandrogens (e.g. spironolactone), intra-lesional corticosteroid injection (e.g. triamcinolone) and the retinoid drug acitretin [23]. Since in a substantial number of patients the medications do not effectively control inflammation, their disease progression leads to irreversible skin damage that frequently requires surgical removal [3]. Therefore, despite all efforts, patients with HS currently report low levels of satisfaction with both therapeutic approaches and the general medical management of their disease [24–28]. However, measures that would improve patient satisfaction have not yet been identified.

Using self-designed questionnaires, we conducted a multi-centre survey in dermatological care facilities in Germany with the aim to identify modifiable determinants of satisfaction with HS care among both patients and their treating dermatologists. Complementary data on patients with psoriasis, a disease with a well-met medical need in Western countries [29], were collected for comparison.

Methods

Study Design

A prospective, cross-sectional, anonymous survey among adult patients with HS, patients with psoriasis vulgaris and the dermatologists treating these patients was conducted in different types of dermatology care facilities (independent dermatology practices, dermatology departments of municipal and military hospitals, dermatology departments of university hospitals) in Germany [24]. For data collection, questionnaires for patients and dermatologists (see below) were used.

Patients attending one of the participating dermatology care facilities were informed about the study and invited to participate. If the patient provided consent, the dermatologist entered information on the patient's clinical status and therapies in the first part of the disease-specific patient questionnaire (without providing patient-identifying data) and handed this questionnaire to the patient, together with a pre-addressed, pre-paid envelope that lacked sender information. The patients had the opportunity to answer the questions addressed to them and to send their questionnaire anonymously in the sealed envelope to the study management at the Charité University Medicine Berlin. In addition, each dermatologist recruiting patients with HS and/or patients with psoriasis had the opportunity to complete the disease-specific dermatologist questionnaire themselves and send it anonymously to the study management.

Ethical Approval

The survey was approved by the ethics committee of the Charité University Medicine (reference number: EA4/205/22, positive vote from 8 February 2023) and registered in the German Register of Clinical Trials (identification number: DRKS00031572). It was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki of 1964 and its later amendments. Participation was voluntary and anonymous, and all patients provided informed consent. The survey was conducted between May 2023 and July 2024.

Questionnaire Design

Four different questionnaires were designed and used to collect data: one to be completed by the patients with HS, one to be completed by the patients with psoriasis, one for dermatologists on HS and one for dermatologists on psoriasis. The patient questionnaires consisted of 34 questions to be answered by the patients. Each of the dermatologist questionnaires (HS and psoriasis) contained 12 questions.

The patient questionnaires were designed in three steps. The first version was developed based on the online tools for patient survey available from the German National Association of Statutory Health Insurance Physicians (https://www.kbv.de/) and was adapted to our study objective and expanded to collect general information on the patient and their patient journey and to include clinical information from treating dermatologists, according to our expertise [11, 27]. This initial version was then successively reviewed by five dermatologists working in different types of care facilities and subsequently by non-physicians, with adaptations made after each review. The final version was generated based on feedback from the ethics committee [24]. The physician questionnaires were designed to match the patient questionnaires and were also discussed and agreed upon by physicians from selected healthcare facilities. The 0–10 global and specific aspect satisfaction ratings central to our questionnaires represent non-validated items.

Statistical Analysis

Data were reported as percentages (categorical variables) or as means or medians (quantitative variables). Missing data were not clustered by demographic factors or by type of healthcare facility, indicating no systematic association with these variables. No imputation of missing data was performed as some items (e.g. satisfaction with treatments only received by a subset of patients) are inherently unanswerable by those without exposure and do not constitute erroneous data. Data evaluation was performed using SPSS Statistics software (SPSS/IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) version 28–29. Pearson Χ2 test (categorical variables), Kruskal–Wallis H-test and two-tailed Mann–Whitney U-test (quantitative variables) were applied. P values < 0.05 were considered to indicate statistic significance. Due to the exploratory nature of our analyses, no multiplicity correction was done. Adjusted analysis of the proportion of patients reporting high satisfaction with medical care was performed using multivariable logistic regression, including patient group (HS vs. psoriasis), age, sex, and body mass index (BMI) as covariates. To identify factors important for satisfaction with overall medical HS care, we applied multiple linear regression modelling with variable selection by backward elimination. For patients’ satisfaction, the independent variables included satisfaction with specific aspects of medical care. Model assumptions were checked via standardised residuals (histograms, Q–Q plots, residual plots), which indicated approximate normality, no heteroscedasticity and independence of errors (Durbin–Watson statistics). Influential outliers (Cook’s distances < 1) and multicollinearity were excluded (variance inflation factors < 5). Model fits were supported by the overall F-test and adjusted R2.

Results

In this multicentre study, patients with HS and, for comparison, patients with psoriasis vulgaris, attending different types of dermatology care facilities in Germany, and their treating dermatologists were asked to provide detailed information about their satisfaction with medical care. A total of 124 patients with HS and 133 patients with psoriasis answered the questionnaires in an evaluable way. In addition, 40 and 36 questionnaires completed by dermatologists treating patients with HS and patients with psoriasis, respectively, were received (Fig. 1). Proportion of Patients with HS Satisfied with Medical Care for Their Skin Disease

The first part of the evaluation explored the patient perspective. Patients were asked to rate their satisfaction with the medical care they received for their skin disease on a scale from 0 (not satisfied at all) to 10 (completely satisfied). The proportion of patients with HS reporting high satisfaction, defined as values > 8, was 30.7% and was significantly lower than the proportion of highly satisfied patients with psoriasis, which was 69.4% (P < 0.001) (Fig. 2a). Of note, this difference remained highly significant (P < 0.001) when alternative thresholds for high satisfaction (values > 6 and values > 7) were applied.

To exclude the possibility that the differences observed between our patient groups (e.g. in gender, age and BMI; Table 1) accounted for the differences in satisfaction levels, we performed additional analyses. The mean satisfaction levels did not differ between the genders for either disease (P = 0.776 for patients with HS and P = 0.794 for patients with psoriasis), and, as demonstrated in Fig. 2b, were significantly lower for patients with HS than for patients with psoriasis of both genders (mean ± standard deviation [SD] of 6.8 ± 2.5 compared to 8.4 ± 2.3 for women and of 6.7 ± 2.9 compared to 8.7 ± 1.8 for men). Moreover, both the proportion of patients with HS reporting high satisfaction and the mean satisfaction level was independent of patients’ age group and BMI (Fig. 2c, d and data not shown). Adjustment for age, sex and BMI using multivariable logistic regression further confirmed a significant difference between the proportion of patients with HS versus patients with psoriasis reporting high satisfaction (adjusted odds ratio [AOR] 0.23; 95% confidence interval [CI] 0.13–0.41; P < 0.001). Moreover, additional evaluation of the satisfaction of patients with HS demonstrated independence of whether the patients had undergone surgical therapy for HS or not (Fig. 2c, d).

Factors Predicting the Satisfaction of Patients with HS with Medical Care for Their Skin Disease

Recently, we reported that patients with HS were dissatisfied with certain aspects of their medical care [24], leading us to speculate whether one or more of these aspects account for the satisfaction of the patients with the medical care of their HS. Multiple linear regression modelling revealed the strongest link of patients’ satisfaction with overall medical care to the received systemic therapies. In addition, their satisfaction with received topical therapies and with the information received from the dermatologist were found to be predictors (Table 3).

In line with these results, there was a big difference in the proportion of patients with HS versus patients with psoriasis who were highly satisfied with received systemic therapies (satisfaction values > 8) (10.3% vs. 66.3%, P < 0.001) (Fig. 3a). Moreover, the patients with HS who were highly satisfied with the received systemic therapies also indicated a high satisfaction with overall medical care of their skin disease (mean ± SD: 9.1 ± 1.9 vs. 6.7 ± 2.4 for patients with values of > 8 vs. 0–8 for the satisfaction with received systemic therapies, respectively, P = 0.003) (Fig. 3b). The satisfaction of patients with the received systemic therapies did not significantly vary with disease severity (Fig. 3c). Patients with HS who had been treated with systemic antibiotics indicated a lower satisfaction with received systemic therapies than those who had not received systemic antibiotics (mean ± SD: 5.1 ± 2.8 compared to 6.8 ± 2.3, P = 0.020). Using multivariable linear regression, including disease severity and prior biologic treatment as covariates, systemic antibiotic use remained significantly associated with lower satisfaction scores, while no association with disease severity was detected (data not shown). Patients with HS who had undergone treatment with systemic biologics tended to be more satisfied with systemic therapies (mean ± SD: 6.3 ± 2.7 compared to 5.1 ± 2.7, P = 0.069). A similar but highly significant difference was that in the satisfaction with systemic therapies for patients with psoriasis treated versus not treated with biologics (mean ± SD: 8.9 ± 2.4 compared to 7.0 ± 2.8, P < 0.001) (Fig. 3c).

In line with the detected importance of patient satisfaction with received topical therapies, the proportion of patients with HS versus patients with psoriasis who were highly satisfied with this aspect differed (11.9% vs. 29.4%, P = 0.004) (Fig. 3d). Moreover, patients with HS who were highly satisfied with this aspect also felt highly satisfied with the overall medical care of their skin disease (mean ± SD: 9.3 ± 0.9 vs. 6.4 ± 2.6 for patients with values of > 8 vs. 0–8 for the satisfaction with received topical therapies, respectively, P < 0.001) (Fig. 3e). The satisfaction of patients with HS with the received topical therapies did not vary significantly with disease severity (Fig. 3f). The satisfaction with received topical therapies was lower in patients with HS treated with antiseptics compared to those who were not treated with antiseptics (mean ± SD: 4.3 ± 2.8 compared to 5.8 ± 2.4, P = 0.023) and was not affected by treatment experience with topical antibiotics (Fig. 3f). The satisfaction of patients with psoriasis with topical therapies was higher; however, it did not vary between those who had versus had not received topical combinations of corticosteroids and vitamin-D derivatives (Fig. 3f).

We then considered the third specific aspect found in the multiple linear regression modelling (Table 3), i.e. the satisfaction with information about their skin disease received from the dermatologist. The proportion of patients with HS versus patients with psoriasis who were highly satisfied with this aspect differed (34.5% vs. 51.6%, P = 0.007) (Fig. 3g). Moreover, patients with HS who were highly satisfied with this aspect showed higher satisfaction with overall medical care of their skin disease (mean ± SD: 8.6 ± 1.4 vs. 5.7 ± 2.7 for patients with values of > 8 vs. 0–8 for satisfaction with received information, respectively, P < 0.001) (Fig. 3h). The satisfaction of patients with HS with the received information did not vary with disease severity (Fig. 3i). It was also not linked to patients' level of school education or vocational training (data not shown). Instead, it was higher for patients who stated: (1) visiting the dermatologists more often (mean ± SD: 7.6 ± 2.4 compared to 6.1 ± 2.5 for patients visiting the dermatologist ≥ 1 times versus <1 time per quarter of the year, P = 0.016) and (2) longer times the dermatologist spend on the consultation (mean ± SD: 8.1 ± 2.2 compared to 6.4 ± 2.9 for average consultation times of at least 20 min vs. < 20 min, P = 0.002) (Fig. 3i).

Proportion of Dermatologists Satisfied with Medical Care for HS

The second part of our study focused on the perspective of dermatologists who treat patients with HS and/or patients with psoriasis. Importantly, none of the participating dermatologists was highly satisfied (values > 8 on a 0–10 scale) with the medical care for HS, in contrast to 38.9% being highly satisfied with the medical care for psoriasis (P < 0.001) (Fig. 4a). Applying alternative thresholds for high satisfaction (values > 6 and values > 7), the difference in the percentages of highly satisfied dermatologists regarding the medical care for HS versus psoriasis remained highly significant (P < 0.001).

The mean dermatologists’ satisfaction levels regarding the medical care did not vary dependent on their dermatologist training (P = 0.625) and, as demonstrated in Fig. 4b, were significantly lower for HS versus psoriasis for each of the dermatological training levels (mean ± SD: 5.0 ± 2.0 compared to 7.2 ± 1.8 for trainee dermatologists, P = 0.001; 5.2 ± 1.7 compared to 7.9 ± 1.8 for fully trained dermatologists, P < 0.001). Factors Predicting the Satisfaction of Dermatologists with Medical Care for HS

Unlike patients, the amount of time dermatologists dedicated to consultations with patients with HS did not affect their satisfaction with medical care for HS (Fig. 5a). The consultation time was found to be slightly longer for patients with HS than for patients with psoriasis, although this trend did not reach significance (mean ± SD: 18.8 ± 7.5 min for consultation with patients with HS compared to 15.8 ± 7.9 min for consultation with patients with psoriasis, P = 0.063) (Fig. 5b). Moreover, the percentage of dermatologists rating the amount of time they spend on consultation per patient as ‘sufficient’ was similar for HS and psoriasis (55.0% vs. 55.6%, P = 0.961) (Fig. 5c).

To identify the factors accounting for the limited satisfaction with the overall medical care of HS among dermatologists, multiple linear regression modelling was run based on different aspects of the medical care. The modelling revealed that the lack of satisfaction with available therapies most strongly accounted for the overall lack of satisfaction of the dermatologists with the medical care of HS (Table 4). In line with that finding, there was a very large difference in the proportion of dermatologists who were highly satisfied with available therapies for HS versus those highly satisfied with available therapies for psoriasis (satisfaction values > 8) (2.5% vs. 69.4%, P < 0.001; data not shown). In addition, there was a link between the satisfaction of the dermatologists with the medical care of HS and their satisfaction with options for referring patients to other dermatological care facilities, although this link was lower. No link was found with options for referring patients with HS to other specialists for comorbidities.

Discussion

In recent years, various efforts have been made by scientists, physicians, pharmaceutical companies and health insurance companies to improve the care of patients with HS. Nevertheless, there are still large gaps in the provision of care to these patients—virtually concerning all aspects, from diagnosis to treatment [30–32]. Accordingly, satisfaction with medical care for HS is limited both among those affected and dermatologists [24]. We investigated the factors determining the level of satisfaction of the provided care. The study was conducted in Germany, a country with a relatively high health care standard, and included a head-to-head comparison with psoriasis, a disease with adequate overall medical care [29]. In order to obtain a realistic overall picture, the study was carried out anonymously and in different types of dermatological care facilities.

The results of our study indicate that only 30.7% of patients with HS were highly satisfied with the care they receive for their skin disease compared to 69.4% of patients with psoriasis. The proportion of dermatologists highly satisfied with medical care for HS was 0.0% compared to 38.9% who were highly satisfied with medical care for psoriasis. This means that satisfaction among doctors is even lower than among patients. Of course, while dermatologists can draw on their experience with other skin diseases to inform their assessments, patients may find such evaluations more challenging owing to their lack of comparable experience and limited understanding of what optimal medical care could, in theory, achieve.

When we analysed specific aspects of care provision, we found that poor contentment with the therapies is the most influential factor determining a limited satisfaction with medical care for HS. This was stated by both patients and dermatologists. Biologic drugs are available for the treatment of moderate-to-severe cases of both HS and psoriasis [15, 29]. However, patients with HS who had undergone treatment with biologics were only slightly more satisfied with the systemic therapies than those who had no experience with biologics. In contrast, patients with psoriasis who had undergone biological treatment were highly satisfied with the treatment. This difference could partially reflect the different efficacy of the currently used biologics in both diseases [1, 29, 33]. It should be accentuated that both secukinumab (anti-interleukin-17A monoclonal antibody) and bimekizumab (anti-interleukin-17A and interleukin-17F monoclonal antibody) were approved for HS treatment by U.S. Food and Drug Administration or/and European Medicines Agency only during our survey (June 2023 and April 2024, respectively). Thus, the proportion of patients who have received these biologics may be low in our HS cohort, as opposed to the psoriasis cohort in which these drugs have been approved for a long time and are highly effective [29]. As only 2.5% of dermatologists were highly satisfied with the available therapies for HS, there is clearly an urgent need for new therapies and research.

Importantly, the satisfaction with therapy of patients with HS was not higher for those who had topical or systemic antibiotics. The experience with systemic antibiotics and topical antiseptics was even negatively linked with patients’ drug therapy satisfaction, although residual confounding may exist. Moreover, patients’ satisfaction with medical care was not affected whether or not they had undergone surgery, which is used for acute pain relief (e.g. lancing of abscesses) and removal of lesions such as fistulas [34].

Another important factor contributing to poor satisfaction of patients with HS with medical care of their skin disease was insufficient information about HS from the dermatologist. Poor satisfaction with information was associated with less frequent visits to the dermatologist (< 1 visit per 3 months) and an average time of < 20 min per dermatology consultation. These results suggest that patients' satisfaction with information about HS could be improved by organising regular visits, allowing for sufficient time per visit, and training dermatologists so that they can provide patients with knowledge on the latest findings and therapeutic achievements. However, as it is unlikely that more time will become available in the near future for the routine care of HS outside specialised HS clinics, informational materials for patients, such as on-demand videos and reliable online information, should be developed to support physicians in their work.

For the dermatologists, the limited options of referring patients with HS to other dermatological care facilities was also a factor accounting for their poor satisfaction with medical care of HS. Negative experiences with the referral for further treatment, such as with biologics, may be a reason for this assessment. In contrast, options for referring patients to other specialists (e.g. due to serious comorbidities of the patients [3]) were not a significant factor determining the dermatologists’ satisfaction. The reason for this may be that, despite their importance for patients with HS, so far, comorbidities are mostly not screened for this patient population [35].

While the issue of communication between dermatologists and patients with HS has only recently been studied, the issue of patient satisfaction with treatment has been addressed in several previous studies. As part of the Global VOICE project, a survey performed in 2017/2018 demonstrated that 45% of patients were either dissatisfied or very dissatisfied with their therapy [26]. Interestingly, patients’ satisfaction with treatment was higher when they were receiving care from a dermatologist and being treated with biologics [26]. The recent international market research survey published by Ingram et al. reported dissatisfaction by one quarter of patients with HS and their dermatologists with the achieved disease control; in this survey, the lack of therapy effectiveness was the most common reason for this judgement [25]. In the study conducted by Willems and colleagues, patients with HS indicated efficacy of therapy as the most important treatment attribute [36]. The fact that, until recently, commonly used drugs did not fulfil the treatment expectation for many patients [36] fits very well with the result of our surveys that dissatisfaction with systemic therapies is the best predictor of the quality of care perception in HS. Recent developments in the approval of innovative therapies for HS, efforts to ensure their early application and research into further new targeted therapeutic approaches [37, 38] offer hope for an improvement in HS care in the future.

Strength and Limitations

The strengths of our survey are that it gathered information from both patients and their dermatologists, included a broad spectrum of dermatology care facilities in Germany and made a direct comparison with a skin disease with good medical care. Another strength is the relatively high number of patients and physicians participating in our study.

The limitations in our study include the fact that the study was not designed to reach patients who are treated by physicians other than a dermatologist. Moreover, the advantage of voluntary, anonymous data collection has the disadvantage that the direct assignment of patients to their dermatologists was not possible. Furthermore, not all potentially relevant covariates (e.g. psychiatric comorbidities, socioeconomic factors) could be considered in the analyses. Finally, the results are limited to the investigated cohort and should be replicated in larger studies.

Conclusion

The results of our study shows that while measures that can be implemented in the medium term, such as regular dermatologist visits, sufficient time for patient education and clearly defined referral pathways to specialist dermatology services, can lead to an improvement in satisfaction with HS care, achieving optimal HS management ultimately depends on the availability and use of effective drug therapies.

CD27 is not an ideal marker for human memory B cells and can be modulated by IL-21 upon stimulated by Anti-CD40
Authors: Shuangpeng Kang, Qiongli Wu, Juan Shen, Changyou Wu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractB cells play a key role in humoral immune responses by producing antibodies. Although there are numerous research on memory B cells definition markers and cytokines on B cell development, different studies have yielded contradictory conclusions due to species studied, the different cells and stimulating agents used. In the current study, we conducted a detailed characterization of B cells in human CBMCs, PBMCs and tonsil, including expression of Igs, activation and memory markers. Furthermore, we found that considerable amounts of IgA and IgG were expressed by CD27− B cells. These “Atypical” memory B cells corresponded to approximately 50% of IgG+ and IgA+B cells in blood, this proportion even reached 90% in tonsil. In addition, we investigated the effect of IL-21 and TGF-β1 on the membrane-bound form and secreted form of Igs using PBMCs and purified blood B cells. There were actual differences between the effect of cytokines on Igs secretion and surface expression. Our study will be helpful to advance the knowledge and understanding of humoral memory.Keywords: Memory B cells, CD27, IL-21, TGF-βSubject terms: B cells, B-1 cells, B-2 cells, Plasma cells

Introduction

B cells play a key role in humoral immune responses through their ability to produce antibodies. In addition, they can also function through other antibody-independent pathways, such as immune regulation and antigen presentation. Following a T-dependent antigen encounter, coupled with activated T help, naive B cells can differentiate into high-affinity plasma cells or memory cells expressing Ig of various isotypes1. Although numerous mechanisms can modulate this immune response, cytokines play a critical role in determining the outcome of humoral immune response by favoring one B cell subset to predominate over another at different times2–4. IL-21 is a pleiotropic cytokine with influence on many immune cells especially in B cells where it is a context-dependent regulator of activation, differentiation, isotype-switch or death. IL-21 could induce human B cell activation, proliferation, isotype-switching and plasma cells differentiation from both naive and memory B cells, but it reported to reduced IgG1 production in mouse. The role of IL-21 in B cell apoptosis depends on the context of B cell activation, when activated by IL-21 and anti-IgM, induced post-proliferative activation-induced death of the B cells, if activated by IL-21 and anti-CD40, did not prime human B cell death5–7. TGF-β1 is another important mediator of B cell activity which inhibits B cell proliferation, promotes apoptosis of immature or resting B cells, and suppresses antibody production of most isotypes with the exception of IgA8–10. Although there are currently many research on cytokines on B cell development, different studies have yielded contradictory conclusions due to species studied, the different cells and stimulating agents used.

At present, it is generally recognized that the surface markers used to distinguish different human B cell subsets are mainly CD19, IgD, CD38 and CD27. In blood, CD27−CD38high are transitional, CD27−CD38low are naïve, CD27+ are memory and CD38highCD27high are plasmablasts. In tonsil, the classification is further complicated. The IgD/CD38 staining scheme can identify a complex subset of human tonsil tissues. These subsets were as follows: Bm1: virgin naive cells (IgD+CD38−); Bm2: activated naive cells (IgD+CD38+); Bm2’ cells: (pre-GC cells, IgD+CD38++); GC cells (Bm3-centroblasts and Bm4-centrocytes, both are IgD−CD38++); Bm5: memory B cells (IgD−CD38+/−). The Bm5 memory cells were divided into early Bm5 (IgD−CD38+) and late Bm5 (IgD−CD38−). Bm1-Bm5 subsets varied in expression of CD27. Additional markers such as CD10, CD44, CD77, CD95, CD23 can be used to better define tonsil B cells11.

Memory B cells (MBCs) play key roles in protection from infection, mediating chronic autoimmunity and cancer development. Memory B cells can be distinguished from naive B cells by the presence of somatic hypermutation in their Ig variable gene sequences1,12. Phenotypic characteristics such as IgD expression were used to identify this B cell subset. However, the exist of IgD+ subsets with expressing somatically mutated Ig genes ended its use for the identification of memory B cells13,14. Currently, expression of the surface marker CD27 is commonly used to identify MBCs in human because its expression correlates with the presence of somatic mutations in Ig genes, but it is clear that a non-negligible proportion of human MBCs which do not express CD27 on their surface exist. The lack of markers that define MBCs unambiguously hampered research on humoral memory15–17.

Herein, we conducted a detailed characterization of B cells in human CBMCs, PBMCs and tonsil, including expression of Igs, activation and memory markers. Furthermore, we detected the proportion of “Atypical” memory B cells which expressed hypermutated IgG or IgA without CD27, and found that these cells corresponded to approximately 50% of IgG+ and IgA+B cells in blood, this proportion even reached 90% in tonsil. In addition, we investigated the effect of IL-21 and TGF-β1 on the membrane-bound form and secreted Ig form of IgG, IgM and IgA using PBMCs and purified blood B cells. Our study will be helpful to advance the knowledge and understanding of humoral memory.

Materials and methods

Study participants

Tonsil tissues from 32 individuals were donated from 4 to 12 years old patients who underwent tonsillectomy due to tonsillar hypertrophy at Guangzhou Women and Children’s Medical Center of Guangzhou Medical University. Peripheral blood was collected from Blood Center of Guangzhou donated by healthy volunteers. Cord blood was donated by healthy newborns at Clifford Hospital of Guangzhou. All blood donors were excluded from chronic/acute inflammation, cancer, autoimmune diseases or immune deficiency. The study was approved by the Review Board of Changsha Medical University in Changsha, China. All samples were collected after written informed consent and assent, following the national and international ethics standards and under the supervision of the Review Board of Changsha Medical University, in accordance to the Helsinki Declaration of 1975.

Isolation of mononuclear cells from peripheral blood and Tonsil

Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were isolated as described previously18. Briefly, peripheral blood were centrifuged at 2000 rpm for 10 min. The serums were collected for detection of Igs concentration. The pelleted cells were resuspended in 1×PBS and loaded onto Ficoll-Hypaque (Tianjin HaoYang Biological Manufacture, China) and centrifuged at 400 g for 20 min. The mononuclear cells on upper layer were collected and washed for next study.

Mononuclear cells from tonsil were isolated as described previously19. Tonsils were processed immediately by mashing through a 40-µm nylon filter (BD Bioscience, USA) with 1×PBS. The filtered cell suspension was isolated by was similarly with a lymphoprep gradient.

The cord blood was anticoagulated with heparin. To remove erythrocytes, the cord blood was mixed with Dextran 500 solution (GE Healthcare Bio-Sciences, Sweden) and incubated at 37℃ for 30 min. Then Ficoll-Hypaque density gradient centrifugation were used to obtain cord blood mononuclear cells (CBMCs).

Purification of B cells, B cells were isolated from PBMCs by CD19 microbeads (Miltenyi Biotec, Germany) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. B cells of > 98% purity were used for subsequent experiments.

Flow cytometry and mAbs

Cells were washed twice and suspended in 100 µL washing buffer which was 1×PBS containing 0.1% BSA and 0.05% NaN3. The flow cytometry procedure had described previously20. Brifely, for surface staining, the cells were incubated with the respective mAbs at 4℃ in the dark for 30 min and washed twice with washing buffer. Flow cytometry data were acquired with FACS Arial II (BD Bioscience, USA) and were analyzed by FlowJo software (Tree Star, USA). The following mAbs used for staining were purchased from BD Bioscience (San Jose, USA): Percp-cy5.5 labeled anti-CD45, anti-IgM, PE-cy7 labeled anti-CD19, FITC labeled anti-IgD, CD80, IgM, PE labeled anti-IgG, CD62L, APC labeled anti-CD27, CD38. FITC labeled anti-IgA were purchased from American Research Products.

Cell culture

Isolated PBMCs were suspended in complete RPMI 1640 medium at a density of 2 × 106 /mL and stimulated with Anti-CD40 (eBioscince, USA) in the presence or absence of different concentrations of 50 ng/mL IL-21 (abcam, UK) or 1 ng/mL TGF-β1 (abcam, UK) for 10 days. The purified B cells were suspended in complete RPMI 1640 medium at a density of 1 × 106 /mL and stimulated with pre-coated Anti-CD40 alone or in combination with IL-21 (50 ng/mL) or TGF-β1 (1 ng/mL) for 10 days. The culture supernatants and serums were collected for the quantitative assessment of of IgG, IgM, and IgA with the Human IgG, IgM or IgA ELISA Ready-SET-Go Kit (eBioscience, USA). The culture cells were harvested for flow cytometry assessment.

Statistical analysis

As described previously20, data were presented as the mean ± standard error of mean (SEM). Statistical significance was analyzed by Mann-Whitney test using Prism 6 (GraphPad, USA). Significant p-values are indicated in figures for the following ranges: NS, no significance; *, P < 0.05; **, P < 0.01; ***, P < 0.001; ****, P < 0.0001.

Results

Comparison of B cell proportion and Igs expression between CBMCs, PBMCs and Tonsil

The mononuclear cells from human CB, PB and tonsil were stained with anti-CD45, and anti-CD19 mAbs. The results showed that the proportion of CD19+B cells in Tonsil (63.32%) was significantly higher than that in CB (13.99%) and PB (6.83%) (Fig. 1A and B). To investigate the surface expression of Igs, cells from CBMCs, PBMCs and tonsil were stained with corresponding mAbs. CBMCs strictly expressed IgM and IgD, but hardly IgA and IgG, indicating the overwhelming majority of them were mature naive B cells. Conversely, PBMCs and Tonsil cells partially expressed all types of Igs including IgG, IgD, IgM and IgA. In addition, B cells from tonsil expressed IgA approximately twice than that of PBMCs (Fig. 1C and D). Next, the concentrations of Igs in CB and PB were assessed by ELISA. The results showed that the serum of CB contained only a small amount of IgM and almost no IgA and IgE, which was much lower than that of PB (Fig. 1E, G and H). The concentration of IgG in CB was about half that in PB, and most of it was maternal IgG that passed through placenta (Fig. 1F).

B cells from PB and tonsil expressed comparable levels of memory marker CD27, but the expression of CD38 and CD62L varied considerably

To identify the memory and activation phenotype of B cells from CBMCs, PBMCs and tonsil, the expression of CD27, CD80, CD38 and CD62L were analyzed by flow cytometry. Notably, B cells from PBMCs and tonsil expressed comparable levels of memory marker CD27, whereas B cells from CBMCs did not express CD27 (Fig. 2A). B cells from CBMCs, PBMCs and tonsil all expressed low levels of the activation marker CD80, with PBMC expressing a slightly higher level, about 10% (Fig. 2B).

CD38 is an important surface receptor involved in the regulation of B cell activation and maturation which is highly and continuously expressed in plasma cells21,22. CBMC’s B cells expressed the highest level of CD38, followed by Tonsil, PBMC’s B cell expression at the lowest (Fig. 2C). CD62L (L-selectin) is an adhesion molecule and crucial for the homing of various leukocytes, including T cells and B cells. In addition, CD62L is shed from the cell surface upon stimulated by cognate antigen23. The expression of CD62L on B cells of CBMC was similar to that of PBMC, but not on B cells of tonsil (Fig. 2D).

Considerable amounts of IgA and IgG were expressed by CD27−B cells but not CD27+B cells

Expression of surface marker CD27 is commonly used to identify memory B cells in humans, but it is clear that CD27 negative memory B cells exist1,15,16. The current conventional definition of human circulating memory B cells is based on CD27 and IgD expression to assign memory B cells to CD27+IgD+, CD27+IgD− and CD27−IgD− (“double negative”, DN) subsets. Based on conventional definition, B cells from PBMCs and tonsil were divided according to CD27 and IgD into “naive” B cells (CD27−IgD+) and “memory” B cells (CD27+IgD+, CD27+IgD−and CD27−IgD− subsets)24. There was no strikingly difference in the proportion of naive and memory B cells between PBMCs and tonsil (Fig. 3A). Gated on CD19+B cells, CD27−IgG+ and CD27−IgA+ B cells were detected in both PBMCs and tonsil from 7 independent samples. An average of 17.5 ± 5.1% IgG-expressing B cells was observed among these blood samples which were further divided into 7.13 ± 1.5% CD27+IgG+ cells and 10.4 ± 3.9% CD27−IgG+ cells. The CD19+CD27−IgG+population represented about 50% of IgG-expressing cells in blood (58.3 ± 6.1%). Similarly, the CD19+CD27−IgA+population represented about 50% of IgA-expressing cells in blood (49.3 ± 6.4%) (Table 1, Fig. 3B). Interestingly, about 90% IgG and IgA were produced by CD27− B cellls, only 10% IgG and IgA were produced by conventional CD27+memory T cells in tonsil (Table 2, Fig. 3B). To further confirm these results, another gating strategy were used where CD19+IgG+ cells were gated and analyzed for CD27 expression. Four independent samples were showing an average of 35.5% and 94.8% of CD27−cells among IgG-expressing cells in PBMCs and tonsil, respectively (Fig. 3C). Similarly, an average of 36.9% and 94.8% of IgA-expressing cells were CD27− in PBMCs and tonsil, respectively (Fig. 3D). Furthermore, the mean fluorescence intensity (MFI) of IgG and IgA was similar in CD27− and CD27+ cells. Obviously, the MFI of IgG and IgA in PBMCs were markly higher than that in tonsil (Fig. 3E, F). Overall, considerable amounts of IgA and IgG were expressed by CD27− B cells but not CD27+B cells which extend the memory B cell reservoir beyond the CD27+ compartment.

IL-21 induced robust secretion of IgG but inhibited IgM production, while TGF-β1 suppressed the secretion of IgM and IgA

Previous studies have demonstrated a role for IL-21 and TGF-β1 in regulating proliferation, apoptosis, isotype switching, and differentiation of murine and human B cells. However, different studies have yielded contradictory conclusions due to the different cells and stimulating agents used5,8,25–27. PBMCs and purified B cells from blood were stimulated with Anti-CD40 alone or with different concentrations of IL-21 or TGF-β1 for 10 days, following which the secretion of IgG, IgM and IgA were measured. IL-21 induced robust secretion of IgG in a dose response manner (Fig. 4A), but it strongly inhibited the production of IgM (Fig. 4B). There was no apparent enhancing effect of IL-21 on the production of IgA (Fig. 4C). In contrast to PBMCs, IL-21(50 ng/mL) did not promote IgG production of purified B cells, indicating the ability of IL-21 to enhance IgG secretion in this system might be the indirect effects on other cells. The effect of IL-21 on the production of IgM and IgA did not differ between PBMCs and purified B cells (Fig. 4D). TGF-β1 strongly inhibited the secretion of IgM and IgA from PBMCs in a dose response manner (Fig. 4F and G), but it had no significant effect on IgG production (Fig. 4E). Consistent with results from PBMCs, TGF-β1 strongly inhibited the production of IgM and IgA from purified B cells, and had no effect on the production of IgG (Fig. 4H). Together, IL-21 induced robust secretion of IgG but inhibited IgM secretion, while TGF-β1 suppressed the secretion of IgM and IgA.

IL-21, but not TGF-β1, enhanced the expression of surface IgG and CD27 on B cells activated with anti-CD40

The next question we addressed was whether IL-21 and TGF-β1 influenced differentiation of memory B cells and the surface expression of Igs. To this end, PBMCs were stimulated with Anti-CD40 alone or with IL-21 (50 ng/mL) or TGF-β1 (1 ng/mL) for 10 days, following which the surface expression of CD27, IgG, IgM and IgA were measured by flow cytometry. IL-21 could enhance the surface expression of CD27. B cells from Anti-CD40 stimulated group expressed CD27 at the mean of 21.8% and addition of IL-21 could promote the expression of CD27 up to 34.4%. (Fig. 5A), but there was on significant effect on the surface expression of IgG and IgM (Fig. 5B and C). Different from the effect of IL-21 on IgA secretion where it did not affect the secretion of IgA, IL-21 could effectively enhance the surface expression of IgA. There was no apparent enhancing or inhibitory effect of TGF-β1 on the surface expression of CD27, IgG, IgM and IgA (Fig. 5A and D). But in reality, the expression of IgM tend to decreased with addition of TGF-β1 (Fig. 5C). Together, there were actual differences between the effect of cytokines on Igs secretion and surface expression which might lead to confused conclusion in related studies.

Discussion

Immunological memory protects human body from re-infection with an earlier encountered pathogen. The protection is mainly mediated by memory T and B cells in collaboration with antibodies produced by long-lived plasma cells1,28. Compared to naive B cells, memory B cells respond more vigorously and more rapidly, producing more and higher affinity Igs. Memory B cells are heterogeneous in phenotypic marker expression and how to completely define them are active and evolving areas of research. Early approaches used isotype-switched BCR or somatic hypermutation of Ig genes as surrogate markers for MBC, though it is clear that MBCs can be either IgM+ or class-switched and can be either unmutated or mutated1,29. CD27, a TNFR superfamily member30, is a widely and powerfully used marker of human memory B cells. As yet, classical memory B cells have never been firmly confirmed the need of CD27 expression in human, and it is clear that sizable and functionally significant subsets of CD27 negative MBCs exist1,15. Rather than as a memory marker, CD27 appears to be a marker for recent B cell activation in mice31. Furthermore, GC B cells and plasma cells in human tonsils have also been shown to express CD27, with plasma cells exhibiting the highest CD27 expression levels of all B cell subsets28. In this study, CD27 was shown to be present on B cells from PBMCs and tonsil, but not on CBMCs. CD27+B cells in tonsil were more likely to be GC cells and plasma cells than memory B cells, in contrast to CD27+B cells in blood which were memory B cells. IL-21, but not TGF-β1, could promote CD27 expression of B cells stimulated by Anti-CD40 in vitro. Obviously, the CD27+B cells generated after stimulation were antibody secreting cells such as plasmablasts rather than memory B cells. The presence of CD27−IgG+B cells showing mutated Ig genes was observed in spleen, blood and tonsils15,30,31. We also observed these population in blood and tonsil and found that considerable amounts of IgA and IgG were expressed by CD27− B cells. Approximately 50% of IgG+ or IgA+ B cells were CD27− in 7 samples of PBMCs (Table 1), this proportion even reached 90% in tonsil (Table 2). “Atypical” CD27−IgG+MBCs vary significantly between the different tissues (blood, spleen, tonsils, and gut)32,33. Our study further question the accuracy of identification of human memory B cells based on CD27 expression, particularly in tonsil. This identification method may exclude most memory B cells in study.

Numerous studies have demonstrated the critical role of cytokines in determining the outcome of a humoral immune response. IL−21 is a context-dependent regulator of B cells which could exerts both positive and negative effects on proliferation, differentiation, maturation and apoptosis, depending on the activation signals and co-stimulation. TGF-β inhibits B cell proliferation, activation and class switching to most isotypes except for IgA5,9. When we examined the effect of IL−21 and TGF-β1 on the secreted Ig form and membrane-bound form of IgG, IgM and IgA, our findings extended previous reports3,5,34 by demonstrating that IL−21 greatly enhanced Anti-CD40-induced IgG secretion of human PB cells in a dose response manner. However, our study also yielded contradictory conclusions that IL-21 did not promote IgG secretion of purified B cells, probably because the ability of IL-21 to enhance IgG secretion in this system is its indirect effects on other cells. Furthermore, both IL−21 and TGF-β1 could strongly inhibited the secretion of IgM. Contrary to previous studies8,35, we demonstrated to TGF-β1 strongly inhibited the secretion of IgA by PBMCs in a dose response manner, which may be explained by the fact that the inhibitory effect of TGF-β1 on B cell proliferation or activation was greater than its promoting effect on class switching to IgA. Consistent with our study, Wang et al.36 demonstrated that TGF-β1 inhibited IgA production and plasma cell differentiation by inducing classic mitochondrial apoptosis in porcine IgM+ B cells from peyer’s patches and peripheral blood. Interestingly, there were actual differences between the effect of cytokines on Igs secretion and surface expression. For example, IL-21 did not promote the secretion of IgA, but significantly promoted the surface expression of IgA which might lead to confused conclusion in related studies. Actually, a considerable amount of in vitro experiments have investigated the effects of IL-21 and TGF-β1 on the production of Igs by B cells, and many conflicting results have been obtained due to different stimuli, species, and culture time2–9. Our study did not systematically examine the effects of IL-21 and TGF-β1 on B cell proliferation, differentiation, survival, class switch recombination, and apoptosis. More data are needed to interpret the exact biological effect of IL-21 and TGF-β1 on B cells.

In summary, we reported that B cells from PB and tonsil expressed comparable levels of memory marker CD27, whereas B cells from CBMCs did not. IL-21, but not TGF-β1, could promote CD27 expression of B cells stimulated by Anti-CD40 in vitro. Importantly, we found that considerable amounts of IgA and IgG were expressed by CD27− B cells. Approximately 50% of IgG+ or IgA+ B cells were CD27− in PBMCs, this proportion even reached 90% in tonsil. Our study further question the exact association of CD27 and human memory B cells. Finally, we investigated the effect of IL-21 and TGF-β1 on the secreted form and membrane-bound form of Igs using PBMCs and purified blood B cells, and found that there were actual differences between the effect of cytokines on Igs secretion and surface expression. Our study will be helpful to advance the knowledge and understanding of humoral memory.

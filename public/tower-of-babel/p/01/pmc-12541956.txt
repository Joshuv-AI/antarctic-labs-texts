A retrospective study of 30 cases evaluating the efficacy and safety of anlotinib plus Temozolomide for recurrent/residual glioma from a single center
Authors: Liping Xu, Dandan Yu, Fei Li, Xiaofeng Ding, Yuandong Cao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo evaluate the efficacy and safety of anlotinib plus temozolomide (TMZ) as a first-line treatment for recurrent/Residual glioma.MethodsThirty eligible patients who either relapsed after the standard chemoradiotherapy regimen (TMZ plus radiotherapy) or had macroscopic residual tumors due to involvement of eloquent brain areas were enrolled between March 2018 and January 2021. Patients received anlotinib (12 mg once daily, 14 days on/7 days off) in combination with TMZ (200 mg/m², 5 days on/23 days off) until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. The efficacy was evaluated using the Response Assessment in Neuro-Oncology(RANO) criteria for high-grade gliomas. Safety was assessed using the NCI-CTCAE 4.0. Survival outcomes were estimated with the Kaplan-Meier method and compared using the log-rank test.ResultsBy April 2023, the median follow-up time was 28.1 ± 5.07 months(95% CI:18.20-38.06). The median overall survival (OS, measured from recurrence to death or last follow-up) was 17.87 ± 4.29 months (95% CI: 9.46–26.28). 1-year, 1.5 year, 2-year OS rates were 60.0%, 46.7%, and 36.7%, respectively. The median progression-free survival (PFS, measured from initiation of Anlotinib plus TMZ) was 7.83 ± 0.82 months(95% CI, 6.22–9.44). The 6-month, 1-year PFS rates were 63.3% and 36.7%, respectively. Univariate analysis showed that patients with WHO grade 2 gliomas had significantly longer median OS and PFS compared with those with grade 3 or 4 gliomas (grade 2: 30.45 ± 4.32, grade 3: 15.07 ± 5.72, grade 4: 9.21 ± 1.90, P = 0.035). Patients aged ≤ 55 years had significantly longer median PFS than those > 55 years (12.97 ± 1.71 vs. 7.33 ± 1.95. p = 0.010), although the difference in OS did not reach statistical difference (19.90 ± 6.29 m vs. 10.53 ± 1.68 m, p = 0.106). Additionally, sex, 1p/19q and IDH mutations were not significant negative predictors of OS or PFS. Multivariate analysis further indicated that age, pathological grade, and IDH mutation were not independent predictors of PFS or OS in recurrent glioma.ConclusionAnlotinib combined with TMZ demonstrated potential efficacy for recurrent glioma, as reflected in observed OS and PFS outcomes, and demonstrated an acceptable tolerability profile in this patient cohort. Larger randomized controlled trials are warranted to confirm these findings and further define the role of anlotinib plus TMZ for the management of recurrent glioma.Keywords: Central nervous system, Recurrence, Glioma, Anlotinib, Temozolomide

Introduction

Gliomas represent the most common type of primary brain tumor. The current standard treatment for gliomas consists of surgical resection, followed by postoperative radiotherapy (RT) and chemotherapy. Prognosis is closely correlated with the pathological classification and World Health Organization (WHO) grade of the tumor. Patients with glioblastoma have a median OS of only 13–16 months, with a 2-year survival rate is only 26.9% [1]. Unfortunately, intracerebral relapse is almost inevitable [2]. At recurrence, gliomas may acquire new genetic mutations, leading to more aggressive behavior and complicating treatment [3]. Survival after relapse and retreatment of glioma is typically 6–8 months, and the median time to a second progression is 14 weeks [4, 5].

Tumor growth is known to be angiogenesis-dependent. Vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) has been shown to be overexpressed in many malignant solid tumors. Gliomas are rich in neovascularization and highly express VEGF [6–8]. This provides an important theoretical basis for the development of antiangiogenic drugs and targeted therapies. The current treatment options are limited [2, 9, 10]. Given that glioma growth is dependent on the formation of new blood vessels, inhibitors targeting the tumor vasculature are promising therapeutic agents for recurrent glioma [11].

Anlotinib is a multitarget tyrosine kinase inhibitor that was originally designed to inhibit VEGFR2/3, FGFR1–4, PDGFR α/β, c-Kit, and Ret. Anlotinib inhibits tumor angiogenesis and tumor cell growth. Anlotinib has been reported to have acceptable adverse effects in the treatment of non-small cell lung cancer, metastatic renal cell carcinoma, and sarcoma [12, 13]. Anlotinib has been approved by the National Medical Products Administration for the treatment of advanced lung cancers. Preclinical results revealed that anlotinib significantly inhibits FGFR1–4, especially FGFR2 [14]. However, to the best of our knowledge, only a few studies have reported the use of anlotinib in the treatment of patients with glioma [15, 16]. Therefore, this study reports the efficacy and safety of anlotinib in patients with recurrent glioma.

Materials and methods

Patient selection

This retrospective study was approved by the Institutional Research Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Nanjing Medical University(NO:2021-SR-193). The study population consisted of patients with recurrent glioma who did not respond well to a standard surgery plus chemoradiotherapy regimen (TMZ and radiotherapy) at the First Affiliated Hospital of Nanjing Medical University between March 2018 and January 2021. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) age of 18–70 years; Karnofsky performance scale (KPS) of ≥ 60; (2) histologically confirmed diagnosis of gliomas, World Health Organization Grade 2(patients with high risk factors underwent adjuvant radiotherapy and chemotherapy), 3 or 4; (3) measurable or evaluable disease by magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) confirmation and a minimum life expectancy of 8 weeks; (4) progressive disease (relapse) on MRI defined by Response Assessment in Neuro-Oncology (RANO) criteria after the standard Stupp protocol or with persistent macroscopic residual tumor after surgery because of tumor located in the eloquent brain areas; (5) adequate bone marrow function (leukocyte count ≥ 4000/µL, neutrophil count ≥ 1500/µL, platelet count ≥ 100,000/µL, hemoglobin ≥ 8.0 g/dL), liver function (total bilirubin ≤ 34 µmol/L and aspartate and alanine aminotransferase ≤ 120 U/L), and renal function (serum creatinine ≤ 150 µmol/L, 24 h urine protein ≤ 3.4 g).

The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) extracranial metastatic disease; (2) Gliadel wafer treatment; (3) severe cardiopulmonary insufficiency; (4) status epilepticus; (5) pregnancy; (6) gastrointestinal bleeding; (7) uncontrolled blood pressure with medication (> 140/90 mmHg); (8) swallowing difficulties; and (9) HIV positivity and treatment.

Drug administration

Anlotinib was provided by Chiatai Tian Qing Pharma Co. Ltd. The starting dose of anlotinib was administered at 12 mg p.o. once daily (14 days on, with 7 days off) on a 21-day cycle. Drug doses were withheld and/or reduced for intolerance to grade 2 or grade 3–4 toxicities. A maximum of three dose level reductions was permitted (12, 10, and 8 mg). The dose was modified to 10 mg and then to 8 mg if patients experienced grade 2 hematological adverse events, hand and foot syndrome, proteinuria, grade 3/4 hypertension, or other grade 3/4 adverse events. Anlotinib was administered until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or death. TMZ was administered to patients at a dose of 200 mg/m2 (5 days on with 23 days off) on a 28-day cycle.

Efficacy and adverse events

All the lesions were measured. The baseline evaluation included MRI, questionnaires on health-related quality of life, neurocognitive testing, routine blood tests, renal and liver function, urinalysis, and electrocardiography. Efficacy was measured using the RANO criteria, and brain MR examinations were conducted every 1–3 months. The primary endpoints were PFS and OS. OS1 was defined as the time from surgery to death or the end of follow-up. OS2 was defined as the time from recurrence to death or the end of follow-up in all patients, while OS3 was defined as the time from the beginning of the intervention treatment to death or the end of follow-up. PFS was defined as the time from the beginning of the intervention treatment to progressive disease (PD) or the end of follow-up.

Adverse events (AEs) were estimated using the National Cancer Institute-Common Terminology Criteria Adverse Events (version 4.0 (NCI-CTCAE 4.0) at regular intervals during each cycle. Physical examination, hematology, biochemistry tests, and electrocardiography were used in order to monitor AEs. The authors conducted medical and clinical monitoring of the study and data management. Clinical laboratory assessments were performed in local laboratories.

Statistical analysis

OS and PFS were analyzed with Kaplan-Meier analysis and log-rank test. Survival differences were compared using a log-rank test. Multivariable analyses were conducted using the Cox proportional hazards regression model to investigate the effects of different survival factors. A 2-sided P value of less than 0.05 indicated a statistically significant finding for all analyses. Statistical analyses were carried out with SPSS 20.0 version. Patient characteristics and AEs were described with counts and percentages in frequency tables, respectively.

Results

Patient characteristics

The baseline characteristics of patients are summarized in Table 1. Thirty patients with recurrent glioma who received anlotinib plus temozolomide (TMZ) between September 2017 and December 2020 were included, and all were evaluable for analysis. Of these, 46.7% (n = 14) were female and 53.3% (n = 16) were male. The median age was 53.5 years (range, 34–69). Most patients had WHO grade 4 gliomas (66.7%, n = 20), followed by grade 3 (20.0%, n = 6) and grade 2 (13.3%, n = 4). Sixteen patients (52.8%) relapsed within 6 months of prior therapy, 10 (33.3%) within 6–12 months, and 4 (13.3%) after more than 12 months. By the last follow-up on April 26, 2023, 21 patients (70.0%) had died and 9 patients (30.0%) remained alive, with a median follow-up duration of 28.1 ± 5.07 months (95% CI: 18.20–38.06.20.06).

Case examples

Three patients with glioblastoma (GBM) had residual lesions following surgery or biopsy and subsequently received anlotinib plus TMZ. The first was a 58-year-old man who initiated treatment 3 months after completing the Stupp regimen; his PFS and OS were 8.5 and 11.1 months, respectively. The second was a 61-year-old man who began therapy 1 month after biopsy, achieving a PFS of 4.3 months and OS of 7.1 months. The third was a 66-year-old woman treated 1 month after biopsy, with PFS and OS of 1.6 and 3.3 months, respectively.

Survival outcomes

Kaplan-Meier survival curves are presented in Fig. 2. OS1 (from surgery): The median OS was 28.1 ± 5.07 months (95% CI: 18.20–38.06.20.06), with 1-, 2-, and 3-year OS rates of 80.0%, 56.7%, and 40.0%, respectively. OS2 (from recurrence): The median OS was 19.6 ± 1.51 months (95% CI: 16.62–22.52), with 6-, 12-, 18-, and 24-month OS rates of 88.0%, 66.7%, 60.0%, and 36.7%. PFS: The median PFS was 7.8 ± 0.82 months (95% CI: 6.22–9.44), with 6-month and 1-year PFS rates of 63.3% and 36.7%.

Univariate analysis

When we analyzed female and male patients, there was no significant difference in median overall survival (OS) (female vs. male: 18.70 ± 1.59 m vs. 12.43 ± 2.17 m, p = 0.501) and median progression-free survival (PFS) (female vs. male: 7.33 ± 5.68 m vs. 7.83 ± 0.67 m, p = 0.539). (Fig. 3A, B).

Univariate analysis of overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) stratified by patient characteristics. A-B OS and PFS according to gender. C-D OS and PFS according to age (≤ 55 vs. >55 years)

We used a median age 55-year as the cut-off age. It seemed that patients ≤ 55 had a longer median OS than those > 55 years old(19.90 ± 6.29 m vs. 10.53 ± 1.68 m), althouth this difference did not reach statistical difference(p = 0.106). In contrast, median PFS was significantly longer in the ≤ 55-year group (12.97 ± 1.71 m vs. 7.33 ± 1.95 m; p = 0.010) (Fig. 3C, D).

We found that patients with different WHO grade glioma had different median OS(WHO 3 vs. WHO 4: 28.47 ± 8.13 m vs. 15.80 ± 7.73 m, P = 0.036). Further pairwise analysis revealed that patients of WHO 2 group had a longer median OS compared with those of WHO 3 and 4 group(2 vs. 3: χ2 = 5.539, p = 0.019; 2 vs. 4, χ2 = 6.112, p = 0.013). There was no significant difference between WHO 3 and 4 group(χ2 = 0.0303, p = 0.582). The median PFS showed significant difference between the 3 WHO grade groups(2 vs. 3 vs. 4: --m vs. 11.17 ± 5.23 m vs. 7.20 ± 0.59 m, P = 0.035). Further pairwise analysis revealed that patients of WHO 2 group had a longer mPFS than that of WHO 3 and 4(2 vs. 3: χ2 = 4.16, p = 0.041; 2 vs. 4: χ2 = 6.15, p = 0.013). When we analyzed patients with WHO grade 3 and grade 4 gliomas, no significant difference was found(χ2 = 0.623, p = 0.430).(Fig. 4A, B).

In 1p/19q mutant related groups analysis, no significant differences were observed in median OS(17.8 ± 3.24 m vs. 28.47 ± 7.14 m vs. 12.4 ± 6.26 m, p = 0.478) and median PFS(7.3 ± 1.88 m vs. 12.9 ± 6.37 m vs. 8.4 ± 0.82 m, P = 0.514 m) among 1p/19q negative, positive, unknown groups.(Fig. 4C, D).

Similarly, in IDH mutant related groups analysis, although patients of IDH positive seemed to have longer median OS than that of IDH negative or IDH unknown, no significant differences were observed among the three groups(IDH negative vs. IDH negative vs. IDH unknown: 13.30 ± 3.70 m vs. -m vs. 12.43 ± 5.37 m, p = 0.175). Furthermore, there were no significant difference among patients with IDH negative, positive or unknown(7.33 ± 1.87 m vs. 12.97 ± 11.18 m vs. 8.40 ± 0.49 m, p = 0.804.) (Fig. 3E, F).

Multivariate analysis

Multivariate Cox regression analysis was performed with age, WHO grade, and IDH mutation status as covariates for OS and PFS (Table 2). None of these factors reached statistical significance, which may reflect the limited sample size of our cohort. Nevertheless, the hazard ratios and 95% confidence intervals are reported to illustrate the direction and magnitude of the associations. For example, older age (> 55 years) was associated with an increased risk of death (HR = 2.15, 95% CI: 0.67–6.91) and progression (HR = 2.53, 95% CI: 0.75–8.47), while IDH mutation showed a trend toward reduced risk of death (HR = 0.34, 95% CI: 0.08–1.46).

Adverse events

Treatment-related adverse events (AEs) are summarized in Table 3. The most frequent AEs were hand–foot syndrome (43.3%), leukopenia (40.0%), elevated transaminases (40.0%), hypertension (30.0%), thrombocytopenia (30.0%), proteinuria (26.7%), hyperbilirubinemia (26.7%), anemia (23.3%), neutropenia (26.7%), and gastrointestinal symptoms (30.0%). Grade 3 AEs included hand–foot syndrome (6.7%), thrombocytopenia (6.7%), and hyperbilirubinemia (3.3%). Four patients required dose reduction (two reduced from 12 mg to 10 mg, and two reduced sequentially from 12 mg to 10 mg to 8 mg). Toxicities improved after adjustment, and no treatment-related deaths occurred.

Discussion

The prognosis of patients with recurrent gliomas remains poor. According to the NCCN guidelines, bevacizumab is currently the preferred agent for the treatment of recurrent gliomas. Bevacizumab, a monoclonal antibody targeting VEGF, has been used as a later-line treatment for glioblastoma since its approval for recurrent disease in the United States in 2009, and it has since become a standard option in the NCCN guidelines [17]. Another recommended regimen is bevacizumab combined with chemotherapy (e.g., carmustine, lomustine, TMZ, or carboplatin). A meta-analysis demonstrated that bevacizumab combined with chemotherapy significantly improved progression-free survival and objective response rate but did not extend OS compared with bevacizumab monotherapy [18]. Thus, effective treatment options for recurrent glioma remain lacking, and molecularly targeted agents have therefore become the focus of research [19].

This retrospective study investigated the safety and efficacy of anlotinib combined with TMZ in patients with recurrent/residual glioma. Our data demonstrated that anlotinib combined with TMZ showed potential efficacy in recurrent/residual glioma. It was also well-tolerated in the evaluated patient population.

The present retrospective study evaluated the safety and efficacy of anlotinib combined with TMZ in patients with recurrent or residual glioma. Our findings indicate that this regimen provides potential clinical benefit and is generally well tolerated. Anlotinib is an oral multitarget tyrosine kinase inhibitor originally developed to inhibit VEGFR2/3, FGFR1–4, PDGFRα/β, and c-Kit, thereby exerting broad inhibitory effects on angiogenesis and tumor growth [14]. It has been reported as a promising option for glioblastoma with FGFR3–TACC3 fusion after bevacizumab failure [15], suggesting a potential therapeutic role in recurrent glioblastoma following TMZ or bevacizumab.

In our cohort, the median OS was 17.87 months, with 1-, 1.5-, and 2-year survival rates of 60.0%, 46.7%, and 36.7%, respectively. The median PFS was 7.83 months, with 6-month and 1-year PFS rates of 63.3% and 36.7%, respectively. In the subgroup of patients with recurrent glioblastoma, median OS reached 15.8 months and median PFS was 7.2 months. These outcomes compare favorably with previously reported salvage regimens for recurrent glioblastoma. For example, dose-dense TMZ monotherapy achieved a median OS of 6.9 months and PFS of 4.3 months [20], whereas bevacizumab monotherapy resulted in a median OS of 8 months and PFS of 3 months [21]. Combination regimens, such as apatinib with dose-dense TMZ, showed modest improvements (mOS 9 months, mPFS 6 months) in an uncontrolled study [22]. Notably, Xu et al. [23] reported a median OS of 16.9 months and PFS of 7.3 months with anlotinib plus TMZ in 21 recurrent glioblastoma patients, findings consistent with our results. By contrast, anlotinib monotherapy demonstrated more limited efficacy, with a median OS of 10 months and PFS of 7 months in recurrent grade 4 glioma [24]. Additional retrospective studies corroborate the limited survival benefit of anlotinib alone or in combination, reporting median OS of 7.7–13.6 months and PFS of 4.5–5.0 months [25–27]. In newly diagnosed glioblastoma, anlotinib combined with the standard Stupp regimen achieved a median OS of 17.4 months and PFS of 10.9 months [28], highlighting its potential role in frontline therapy.

When compared with existing evidence, our study shows that anlotinib combined with temozolomide (TMZ) achieves superior survival outcomes in recurrent glioblastoma, outperforming TMZ monotherapy, bevacizumab, and other anti-angiogenic regimens, and producing results comparable to or better than prior small-cohort reports of anlotinib plus TMZ. Our cohort reached a median OS of 15.8 months and a median PFS of 7.2 months, with the benefit supported by the longest follow-up period among comparable studies (median 28.1 ± 5.07 months, 95% CI: 18.20–38.06.20.06). These findings highlight the potential of anlotinib plus TMZ as a viable therapeutic strategy in a setting with few effective options and underscore its prognostic value in prolonging survival. By providing long-term, real-world evidence of meaningful clinical benefit, our study adds important context to the growing body of literature on anlotinib-based therapy and supports the need for larger, prospective trials to validate its role in recurrent glioblastoma. Given its oral administration, manageable toxicity profile, and encouraging efficacy, this regimen may represent a practical and accessible treatment option for appropriately selected patients in clinical practice.

In our study, anlotinib-related adverse reactions during the treatment of recurrent gliomas were favorably tolerated. Most patients experienced grade 1–2 side effects. Treatment-related grade 3 adverse effects included hand-foot syndrome (6.7%, 2/30), thrombocytopenia (6.7%, 2/30), and hyperbilirubinemia (3.3%, 1/30). Dose reduction occurred in four patients(two from 12 mg to 10 mg, the other two from 12 mg to 10 mg and then to 8 mg). The side effects were relieved after dose adjustment. These results are similar to those of previous studies [26–28], which demonstrated the favorable safety and tolerance profile of anlotinib combined with/without TMZ.

Although some basic and clinical progress has been made in anti-angiogenic therapy for glioma, the clinical effect in the real world is far from expected. Multitarget anti-angiogenic drugs, such as anlotinib, have some prospects. This study has several limitations. First, it was a retrospective analysis with a relatively small number of patients from a single institution. Second, although 1p/19q and IDH status were recorded, missing genetic and molecular data limited our ability to conduct a comprehensive evaluation and draw firm conclusions regarding molecular stratification. This limitation highlights the importance of incorporating complete molecular marker analyses in future studies to better define subgroups that may benefit from anlotinib treatment. Third, our cohort included four WHO grade 2 glioma cases, which represents a potential confounding factor; however, stratification analysis partially addressed this issue. Nevertheless, this study adds to the evidence on the efficacy and safety of anlotinib plus TMZ for recurrent/residual glioma, with a relatively large sample size and the longest follow-up period reported to date.

Conclusions

Anlotinib combined with TMZ exhibited encouraging efficacy for recurrent glioma, as reflected in observed OS and PFS outcomes, and demonstrated an acceptable tolerability profile in this patient cohort. Further randomized controlled clinical studies and application of molecular biomarkers are required to validate these findings and more definitively assess the efficacy of anlotinib combined with TMZ for the treatment of recurrent glioma.

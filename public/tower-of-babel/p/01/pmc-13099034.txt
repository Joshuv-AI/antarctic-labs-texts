Rising focus on neuropsychiatric risks: A half-century bibliometric analysis of sevoflurane literature
Authors: Jun Chao Dai, Min Shui, Chengyi Song, Yang Guo, Mingliang Xu, Jian Zhan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:An increasing number of articles have been published on sevoflurane. However, a quantitative evaluation of sevoflurane-related publications that are frequently cited has not been performed. This study aimed to determine how interest in sevoflurane has changed among the authors of academic journals over the past 50 years.Methods:Sevoflurane-related studies were retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection. The 100 most frequently cited original studies were identified via manual screening and analyzed. Document metrology software programs were used to perform visual analysis.Results:The citation frequency of the 10 most frequently cited articles ranged from 293 to 576, with an average citation frequency of 379.2. Five of the 10 most cited articles involved studies of sevoflurane-related neuropsychiatric complications. Among the 10 articles, the first three were related to neuropsychiatric complications. Only two articles on the effects of general anesthesia with sevoflurane were included. These articles originated from 17 countries, with more than half being from the United States and Japan. Harvard University was the most cited university. The three core journals were Anesthesiology, Anaesthesia and Analgesia, and the British Journal of Anaesthesia. Anesthesiology is the academic journal that publishes the most sevoflurane-related research. Fifteen of the 55 articles focused on neuropsychiatric complications, and 10 focused on the mechanisms of sevoflurane anesthesia.Conclusion:This study shows that the research focus has shifted from the anesthetic mechanisms of sevoflurane to its neuropsychiatric complications, highlighting the need for clinicians to carefully weigh its benefits against potential neurological risks, particularly in pediatric and elderly populations.

Introduction

General anesthesia can be divided into two categories: intravenous and inhalation general anesthesia.[1] General anesthetics function through two mechanisms:(1) enhancing inhibition via gamma-aminobutyrate receptors (e.g., benzodiazepines, propofol, etomidate, isoflurane, enflurane, and halothane)[2] and (2) attenuating excitatory effects via N-methyl-D-aspartate receptors (e.g., ketamine, nitrous oxide, and xenon).[3] Among the most commonly used inhalational anesthetics, such as sevoflurane, desflurane, enflurane, isoflurane, and nitrous oxide,[456] sevoflurane is one of the main drugs used to maintain general anesthesia and has sedative, analgesic, muscle relaxant, and bronchodilatory effects.[5] Sevoflurane has anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects and can protect organs from stress-induced damage.[7] Sevoflurane is a safe inhalational anesthetic with the advantage of inducing rapid recovery.[8] Since sevoflurane was synthesized in 1968, it has been studied extensively to determine its potential mechanism of inducing anesthesia and toxicological effects.[59] Sevoflurane can affect the functional connectivity of the brain in direct and indirect ways, leading to reversible loss and recovery of consciousness.[51011] An increasing number of studies have focused on the pharmacological and toxicological effects of sevoflurane and its effects on neurodevelopment, cognitive function, and organ protection in recent years.[5121314] In addition, the effects of sevoflurane on nerve cell apoptosis, mitochondrial function, and electroencephalograms have also become a focus of research.[151617] Many studies related to sevoflurane have been published over the years, increasing our understanding of this field.

Citation analysis is a widely accepted indicator for evaluating the influence of scientific articles.[1819] Frequently cited studies are often considered pioneering articles or hot research questions in the field. Thus, frequently cited publications in a field can provide valuable evidence and information regarding research trends and scientific progress.[20] Bibliometrics has been used to summarize the most-cited studies in professional fields, such as insomnia,[21] urological cancer,[22] neurorehabilitation,[23] Alzheimer’s disease and epilepsy,[24] and pain and depression.[25] However, no such analysis of the 100 most frequently cited studies on sevoflurane has been published. In this study, we analyzed the characteristics of the 100 most influential papers in the field of sevoflurane in recent decades to determine how interest in sevoflurane has changed among the authors of academic publications.

Materials and Methods

Search strategies

The 100 most frequently cited articles on sevoflurane were retrieved from the Web of Science’s Science Citation Index Expanded (SCIE) Core Collection (WoSCC; Clarivate Analytics, Philadelphia, PA, USA). The WoSCC is the world’s most influential multidisciplinary academic abstract index database. The most influential research usually has a high citation frequency, high citation impact, and high academic impact index and can arouse widespread public attention and discussion. This study chose the WoSCC as the database because of its advantages in terms of academic influence and recognition, comprehensive subject coverage, powerful retrieval function, convenient access, continuous update and maintenance, and support for interdisciplinary research. These advantages make the WoSCC an important tool for researchers to conduct academic research and literature searches. Data regarding the title (TI), abstract (AB), and author keywords (AK) were extracted via the following strategy: TI = (Sevoflurane) or AK = (Sevoflurane) or AB = (Sevoflurane). The period for publication ranged from January 1975 to September 2023. Articles published in any language were included.

Data extraction

The inclusion criterion was sevoflurane-related studies with original data. The records were arranged in descending order according to the frequency of citations in the WoSCC. Two researchers (Jun-chao Dai and Min Shui) independently screened the title, abstract, and type of reference of each eligible article. If necessary, the full text was read for a more detailed evaluation to confirm whether the study could be included. Review articles and meta-analyses without original data were excluded from the analysis. Disagreements between the two researchers were resolved through discussions until a consensus was reached under the scrutiny of senior experts. The 100 most frequently cited articles were downloaded and exported in plain text format for further analysis. The journal impact factor was derived from the Journal Citation Report 2023. The citation density of each article is defined as the average number of citations since publication, that is, the ratio of the total number of citations to the age of the article.[26]

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistical analyses and the generation of charts were performed via Microsoft Excel 2019. Author/journal cocitation analysis, country/institution coauthorship, and term co-occurrence analysis were performed via VOSviewer 1.6.19 (Leiden University, Netherlands). VOSviewer is a document knowledge visualization software program developed by van Eck and Waltman that can be used to build a document metrology network.[27] Different nodes represent different elements in the network visual map. The size of a node represents its frequency of occurrence, and if a node represents a country, the size represents the number of articles it has published. The connection between nodes represents a correlation between the two.

Results

Table 1 presents the 10 most frequently cited articles in the order of the total number of citations. The frequency of citations of the studies ranged from 293 to 576 (average: 379.2 times). Five of the 10 most cited articles were studies of sevoflurane-related neuropsychiatric complications. Among the 10 articles, the first three were related to neuropsychiatric complications. Only two articles on the effects of general anesthesia with sevoflurane were included. The highest number of high-impact articles were published in 2003 and 2004 (n = 8), followed by 2010 (n = 6), 2002, and 2005 (n = 5). Seventeen countries contributed to the 100 most frequently cited articles, with nine countries contributing more than three articles [Figure 1]. The United States ranks first, with 48 papers and 9635 citations [Figure 2]. Japan ranks second, with 12 papers and 2548 citations, followed by Germany (seven articles and 1259 citations) and Switzerland (five articles and 1051 citations).

Discussion

Bibliometric analysis is a feasible tool for the comprehensive quantitative and qualitative evaluation of research progress in a certain scientific field, and it can predict development trends and hot spots in a certain field through information visualization.[282930] Sevoflurane-associated neuropsychiatric complications and anesthetic mechanisms have remained the focus of research in the field. Since sevoflurane was synthesized in 1968, efforts have been made to elucidate the potential mechanisms of sevoflurane.[831] However, interest in research has changed markedly in the past few years. Owing to their groundbreaking contributions and reference value for further research, the most frequently cited studies in a particular field are generally considered landmark studies.

Our findings provide a comprehensive overview of the 100 most influential studies on sevoflurane. A relatively high number of high-impact articles were published in 2003, 2004, and 2010. The number of publications in this field has increased continuously since 1975; however, the years 2003 and 2004 can be considered inflection points for publication growth. Approximately half (48/100) of the 100 most cited articles in the field of sevoflurane were from the United States, which also accounts for the vast majority of total citations, indicating that the United States is the most influential country in the field. As the largest high-tech power source, the United States has pioneered several global research fields.[3233]

Although research on sevoflurane has been conducted worldwide, most eminent scholars are from the United States. This study revealed that nearly half of the most frequently cited authors were from the United States. In terms of national or institutional cooperation, the United States has worked closely with the United Kingdom. At the institutional level, the collaboration between Massachusetts General Hospital and Harvard University is the closest to conducting research on sevoflurane. This result may be attributed to several common research projects, among which studies conducted by Dong YL, Xie ZC, and Xu ZP are important bridges.[333435] The most frequently cited studies on sevoflurane are more likely to be published in high-impact journals such as Anesthesiology, Anaesthesia and Analgesia, the British Journal of Anaesthesia, Pediatric Anaesthesia, and Lancet, indicating that these journals are more likely to publish original research on sevoflurane. Most of these journals have high impact factor values, which supports the supposition that high-quality studies are usually published in journals with high impact factors.[36] As one of the most authoritative journals in the field of anesthesiology, Anesthesiology has published significantly more articles on sevoflurane-associated neuropsychiatric complications than on studies of anesthetic mechanisms in recent years. Thus, it may be concluded that research published in high-impact journals reflects the changing interest in sevoflurane-related research.

EI Eger II, an internationally respected anesthesiologist whose research interests include inhalation anesthesia, has contributed greatly to the establishment and development of research on sevoflurane. Among the 100 most influential articles, six mentioned his name. EI Eger II developed the concept of minimum alveolar concentrations for gaseous and volatile anesthetics[37] and identified the processes governing the onset, uptake, and distribution of inhaled anesthetics into tissues, as well as the duration required for their elimination at the end of anesthesia. Thus, EI Eger II is considered a star scientist with a high status in the field of inhalation anesthesia research, and his research has paved the path for research on anesthetic mechanisms and neuropsychiatric complications in this field.[3839] This phenomenon suggests that advances in medical science are, to some extent, reflections of the outstanding contributions of leading scientists.

In addition to analyzing its mechanism of action, the 100 most frequently cited articles also analyzed its effects on neurodevelopment, cognitive function, and organ protection,[35404142] indicating that these research directions are emerging as new targets for sevoflurane-related research. Davidson et al.[40] published a representative study on the effects of sevoflurane on neurodevelopment in The Lancet, which showed that, compared with awake regional anesthesia, less than 1 hour of sevoflurane anesthesia in infancy increases the risk of adverse neurodevelopmental outcomes at the age of 2 years. The activation of the Akt and ERK pathways and cognitive decline have been observed in mice following exposure to sevoflurane.[41] The protective effect of sevoflurane on the myocardium of patients undergoing off-pump coronary artery bypass surgery has been shown to be superior to that of propofol.[42] Moreover, sevoflurane has been shown to induce a decrease in postsynaptic density 95 and synaptic levels in fetal and offspring mice, indicating that it may be neurotoxic to the developing brain.[35] An increasing number of studies have confirmed the effects of sevoflurane on apoptosis and mitochondrial function. The apoptotic characteristics of neonatal neocortical neurons following the induction of sevoflurane anesthesia can reveal the patterns of death of neonatal neocortex neurons.[15] Sevoflurane exposure induces neurotoxicity via the regulation of the mitochondrial function of microglia due to nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide insufficiency.[16] In recent years, the study of sevoflurane-related neuropsychiatric complications has become the focus of scholars in this field.

Our study differs markedly from previous studies. While scholars such as Correll DJ and others have examined changes in research interest in intravenous general anesthetics and inhaled general anesthetics by the authors of scholarly publications over the past 50 years, in the present study, the changes in research interest in sevoflurane by the authors of scholarly publications over the past 50 years were specifically explored.[43] Scholars such as Vlassakov KV and others have used popularity indices, indices of change, and indices of ultimate success to assess progress in the field of anesthesia drugs such as sevoflurane over the past 30 years, whereas the present study has used the number of citations to articles, academic journals in which they were published, geographic regions of publication, and the relevance of publications to assess progress in the field of sevoflurane research over the past nearly half-century.[44] Gerónimo-Pardo M used clinical case analysis to study the frequency of dose notification of anti-infectives and only used sevoflurane as a comparison of the studies; our study took sevoflurane as the research object and analyzed sevoflurane-related studies as the main variable for the past 50 years.[45]

There were also several limitations to the present study. First, only the WoSCC database was used as the data source; this may have led to some citations being missed. However, different databases use different reference counting methods; thus, combining data from different databases may not be suitable. Moreover, the analysis of frequently cited articles in a certain field is most often performed via the WoSCC database.[3233] Second, earlier studies were cited more frequently than more recently published studies owing to the time factor. Consequently, some recently published breakthrough studies may have been omitted owing to the lack of sufficient time to accumulate citations. Third, although citations are widely used as reference indicators, their ability to reflect academic influence remains controversial.[46] The number of citations may not fully represent the true academic value; nevertheless, it can be used as a reference to assess the impact and value of a study comprehensively.

Conclusions

This study revealed that the research focus has shifted from the anesthetic mechanisms of sevoflurane to its neuropsychiatric complications, highlighting the need for clinicians to carefully weigh its benefits against potential neurological risks, particularly in pediatric and elderly populations.

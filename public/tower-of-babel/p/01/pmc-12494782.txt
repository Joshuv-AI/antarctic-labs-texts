Strong tracking dimensionality-reduction CKF algorithm for SINS initial alignment under large misalignment angle
Authors: Yong Yang, Donglin Li, Yujie Fu, Kaijun Xu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Initial alignment is the prerequisite and foundation for the normal operation of the Strapdown Inertial Navigation System (SINS). Kalman filtering and various evolution algorithms are commonly employed tools for achieving initial alignment. To address the high computational load issue of the Cubature Kalman Filter in the large azimuth misalignment angle initial alignment of SINS and the odometer increment errors caused by pulse count truncation, Strong Tracking Dimensionality-Reduction CKF (STDR-CKF) algorithm is proposed. By performing differential calculations between the SINS position information and the odometer-based navigation solution within a fixed mileage segment, and using the difference for feedback correction, the proposed method effectively suppresses the odometer increment errors. Furthermore, the dimensionality of state variable in proposed algorithm is separated into 6 nonlinear states and 12 linear states based on the “linear-nonlinear” state separation framework, thus the CKF sampling points number is greatly reduced, which significantly reducing the computational load. Additionally, the Strong Tracking Filter (STF) diminishing factor is incorporated into the dimensionality-reduced CKF(DR-CKF) to achieve real-time dynamic feedback correction of system model parameters, thus enhancing the robustness of the filter. Experimental results demonstrate that, compared to the conventional CKF algorithm, the proposed method ensures significantly stronger filtering stability, higher filtering accuracy, and shorter initial alignment time under large azimuth misalignment conditions.

Introduction

The Strapdown Inertial Navigation System (SINS) is a fully autonomous navigation system widely used in various motion platforms, including aircraft, vehicles, rockets, and ships1,2. By utilizing inertial sensors mounted on the moving platform, SINS calculates key navigation parameters such as the platform’s attitude, position, and velocity based on sensor measurements. It is characterized by its efficiency, stability, and independence from external signal support, making it widely applied in high-precision navigation tasks. Before performing navigation and positioning tasks, SINS must undergo initial alignment to ensure accurate calculation of the navigation parameters, including attitude, position, and velocity of the vehicle3–5. Initial alignment is a critical step in SINS, typically requiring high precision, strong reliability, and the ability to quickly adapt to various motion states. Currently, the issue of initial alignment in static base environments for SINS has been effectively resolved6,7. However, with the rapid increase in the number of vehicles and the complex, ever-changing operational environments, more and more application scenarios require SINS to perform initial alignment in dynamic base environments during vehicle movement8–10. Therefore, improving alignment accuracy in dynamic environments, while ensuring successful alignment, has emerged as a significant research hotspot. In contrast to initial alignment in static base environments, after rough alignment in dynamic base environments, the system often enters a state of large misalignment angles, and in some alignment cases without any prior information, the state equations exhibit significant nonlinear characteristics11. Moreover, initial alignment typically requires the assistance of external devices to provide partial motion information of the platform in order to complete the alignment process12,13. Currently, nonlinear Kalman filtering algorithms, including Extended Kalman Filter (EKF)14, Unscented Kalman Filter (UKF)15, Cubature Kalman Filter (CKF)16,17, Quadrature Cubature Kalman Filter (QCKF)18, and Particle Filter (PF)19, have been proposed successively and currently been extensively employed to address nonlinear estimation problems, in which CKF achieves a good balance between superior performance and complexity under strong nonlinear conditions .

Depending on the environment, commonly used external auxiliary devices include the Global Positioning System (GPS) and Odometers (OD), which provide supplementary information to assist in the alignment of SINS during its movement. However, for vehicle-mounted inertial navigation systems, GPS signals are prone to obstruction or interference in mountainous areas, tunnels, and urban canyons, and are unavailable during wartime, leading to poor system resistance to interference and limited autonomy, which makes it difficult to meet the operational requirements of vehicle-based systems. In contrast to GPS, which is susceptible to interference, the odometer, as an autonomous device, can be integrated with SINS to correct drift errors, thus achieving high-precision alignment in dynamic base environments. Thus, devices using OD assisted SINS can greatly enhance environmental adaptability and have attracted widespread attention.

In recent years, initial alignment with large azimuth misalignment angle has been becoming research hotspots in dynamic base environments and scenarios without effective auxiliary information, in which CKF is widely utilized and always showing superior performance on strongly nonlinear problems. Furthermore, various adaptive robust strategies coupled with CKF algorithm are proposed to address observation gross errors and inaccurate noise statistical characteristics in SINS/GPS initial alignment20,21; OD aided in-motion alignment method based on backtracking scheme for large misalignment angle is proposed, which greatly improves heading angle and position estimation accuracy22. However, the backtracking strategy somewhat limits the rate of initial alignment during movement; a fast two-position initial alignment method based on CKF is proposed to solve error parameters and improve the alignment accuracy23. However, the number of sampling points in the CKF algorithm is twice of the dimension of the nonlinear system state vector, resulting in a high computational load when applying the algorithm for initial alignment; A new in-motion alignment method for vehicle carried SINS aided by OD is proposed, in which a system measurement equation in the vehicle body coordinate system is derived, using the difference between the displacement increment outputs from the odometer and SINS as the observation24. However, the odometer pulse count truncation error still affects the actual alignment accuracy of SINS to some extent; In order to achieve higher alignment precision and lower computational cost, an improved dimensionality reduction CKF algorithm is derived to reduce the large calculation burden25, aiming to address the GPS aided SINS initial alignment26; CKF with strong tracking behavior is proposed to address the unknown or time-varying prior noise problem in nonlinear filtering system27,28.

Based on the inspirations provided and the aforementioned unresolved issues, Strong Tracking Dimensionality-Reduction Cubature Kalman Filter (STDR-CKF) algorithm is proposed. First, differential calculations are performed between the SINS position information and the odometer-based navigation solution within a fixed mileage segment, and the difference is used for feedback correction, effectively suppressing the odometer increment errors. Next, the dimensionality of state variable is separated into 6 nonlinear states and 12 linear ones based on the “linear-nonlinear” state separation framework, thus the CKF sampling points number is greatly reduced, which substantially reducing the computational load. Finally, the Strong Tracking Filter (STF) is combined with the dimensionality-reduced CKF, incorporating a diminishing factor to enhance the robustness.

Strong tracking dimensionality-reduction CKF

The above dimensionality reduction CKF algorithm assumes that the system noise is Gaussian white noise. However, in real operational conditions, the characteristics of the system noise are unknown. Under these conditions, using the dimensionality reduction CKF alone may lead to a deterioration in the system’s filtering performance, or even cause the filter to diverge. To alleviate the impact of outliers and non-Gaussian white noise to some extent and enhance the robustness of the filter, ensuring that it maintains good filtering performance and estimation accuracy even when the noise characteristics are unknown, and that it remains stable under sudden noise changes, the combination of Strong Tracking Filter (STF) and dimensionality reduction CKF is proposed. Theoretically, non-Gaussian white noise can lead to inaccurate system model, while STF can effectively curb the divergence of filtering by increasing the weight of fresh measurements and decreasing the functionality of outdated measurements. The combination of STF and CKF enables the system to maintain stable convergence in environments with model parameter changes and noise uncertainty, which can significantly improve the filter’s robustness while maintaining filtering accuracy29.

Conclusion

This paper proposes a Strong Tracking Dimensionality Reduction Cubature Kalman Filter (STDR-CKF) algorithm for dynamic base environments. The algorithm suppresses the odometer pulse count truncation errors by performing differential feedback correction between the SINS position information and the odometer-based navigation solution over a fixed mileage segment, effectively reducing error accumulation. Additionally, the algorithm can reduce the dimensionality of the CKF, which the state variables is separated into 6 nonlinear states and 12 linear states based on the “linear-nonlinear” state separation framework, which can decrease the computational load and save alignment time greatly. By combining Strong Tracking Filtering and dimensionality reduction CKF, the robustness of the filter is further improved. Experimental results show that, compared to the conventional CKF algorithm, the proposed algorithm improves alignment accuracy and shortens alignment time under large azimuth misalignment conditions. The proposed algorithm can efficiently and accurately complete the initial alignment task under vehicle-mounted dynamic base and motion conditions, offering high engineering practical value. The future research will focus on the numerical stability of proposed STDR-CKF algorithm under extreme dynamics including non-Gaussian noise environments, considering the combination of robust statistical methods and outlier handling strategies to further enhance the practicality and stability of the algorithm in complex environments; Meanwhile, as this algorithm can significantly reduce computational complexity while maintaining accuracy, further research can be conducted on real-time load analysis and porting the algorithm to embedded systems.

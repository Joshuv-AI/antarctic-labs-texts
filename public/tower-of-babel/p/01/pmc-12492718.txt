Frontline therapy combining with intrathecal dexamethasone effectively alleviate immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome in pediatric relapsed B-ALL receiving pCAR-19B-cases report
Authors: Lu Cao, Min Zhou, Zhequn Wei, Ruixin Zhu, Hongmei Chen, Libing Zhou, Shaoyan Hu, Zhengjiang Bai, Shuiyan Wu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundpCAR-19B is the first domestically approved CAR-T cell therapy for the treatment of children and adolescents targeting CD19. It has demonstrated efficacy and durability but is associated with significant side effects, particularly immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS). Currently, the frontline treatment for ICANS primarily involves intravenous corticosteroids and supportive care. However, some patients do not respond to this initial therapy, and there is a lack of treatment strategies for these non-responsive patients.Case presentationThis case is registered on Clinicaltrials.gov under the number NCT05334823. Table 1 outlines the patient characteristics. Two patients achieved complete remission post CAR-T therapy. In Case 1, neurological symptoms began on the 6th day at grade 2 and progressed to grade 4 on the 8th day, accompanied by respiratory depression necessitating endotracheal intubation and ventilator-assisted ventilation due to cerebral edema. Case 2 experienced grade 3 ICANS on the 9th day, which quickly escalated to grade 4 the following day. Both patients were treated with frontline therapy, including antiseizure medications, steroid infusions, and intrathecal (IT) dexamethasone. Their ICANS gradually improved, and levels of temperature, IL-6, CRP, and PCT decreased progressively. For Case 1, steroid therapy lasted 10 days, after which ICANS returned to grade 2. The cumulative steroids administered reached 348 mg, but no significant effect on CAR copy number amplification was observed. For Case 2, steroid therapy lasted 3 days, and ICANS returned to grade 2 by the 3rd day post-steroid treatment. The cumulative steroid dose was 22.9 mg, with no impact on CAR amplification. Both patients are currently alive following bone marrow remission bridging. Their progression-free survival (PFS) was 150 days for Case 1 and 290 days for Case 2. As of October 1, 2023, their overall survival (OS) was 234 days for Case 1 and 305 days for Case 2.ConclusionsA preliminary analysis suggests that the combination of frontline therapy and intrathecal dexamethasone is feasible, safe, and effective in these two patients receiving pCAR-19B for relapsed B-ALL. This approach may enhance the safety profile of pCAR-19B administration and broaden access to this treatment.Keywords: Relapsed B-ALL, Pediatric, CAR-T, Immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome, Intrathecal dexamethasone

Background

Chimeric antigen receptor T (CAR-T) as an epoch-making cancer therapy, its excellent efficacy has been fully demonstrated in some hematological tumors [1, 2]. Immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS) is a common side effect after CAR-T cell therapy, occurring in 20–60% of cases, with 12–30% having severe (grade ≥ 3) symptoms [3]. It often occurs during or after symptoms of cytokine release syndrome (CRS) [4]. Although most ICANS recover with treatment, they can be fatal for some patients [5]. Our center adopts the first humanized CD19-targeting CAR-T product (pCAR-19B) for children and adolescents in China to treat relapsed B-ALL. The CAR-T cells are currently being evaluated in phase II clinical trials (NCT05334823), with a current incidence of 48% (26/54) of phase II ICANS and 30% (16/54) of grade 3 and above ICANS. In the face of such a high incidence of ICANS, how to manage and treat the ICANS is an open question.

Treatment of ICANS include support care and use of immunosuppressants. Among them, intravenous corticosteroid is one of the commonly therapies, and although some reports suggest that frontline strategies with intravenous corticosteroid can significantly relieve the symptoms of ICANS, a large proportion of patients still have no or poor response to intravenous corticosteroid. At present, there are no clear guidelines for the treatment of this group of patients, and previous reports in adult lymphoma have observed that treatment with IT hydrocortisone, methotrexate, and cytarabine can rapidly relieve ICANS in adult who did not respond to intravenous corticosteroid [6–8], but only individual cases have been reported, and it is not clear whether different patients are equally applicable. In our study, we reported two children with CAR-T therapy who had severe ICANS achieved significant improvement after a series of treatments, hoping to bring new ideas to the treatment and management of severe ICANS after pCAR-19B therapy.

Case presentation

Case 1 was a 4 years 10 months boy diagnosed with relapsed B-ALL. The bone marrow smear showed active bone marrow cell hyperplasia, with blasts accounting for 8% and MRD monitoring being 3.6 × 10−4. Vincristine combined with prednisone chemotherapy was used to reduce tumor burden. Then he received autologous second-generation 4-1BB CD3ζanti-CD19 (pCAR-19B) after lymphocyte depletion with fludarabine and cyclophosphamide (FC). On the sixth day after CAR-T infusion, he occurred fever, the IL-6 and IL-10 of blood were increased simultaneously. At the same day, he showed ICANS presenting with intermittent mild tremor on both hands, poor response, and positive pap sign (grade 2 according to the Cornell Pediatric Delirium Assessment Scale) (Fig. 1A). On the seventh day, he received BM remission (3% blast in the BM smear, MRD was less than 1 × 10−4), but occurred seizure. Abnormal symmetry signaling was observed in bilateral lateral capsule region on cranial MRI (Fig. 2A). To address the CRS, the patient was administered an infusion of tocilizumab on day 7 (D7). Regrettably, concurrent with the tocilizumab administration, the patient also developed ICANS. In response to this complication, a multi - pronged treatment approach was promptly implemented. Mannitol was used to reduce intracranial pressure, and intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) was given. Levetiracetam was initiated from D7 to prevent seizures. Moreover, a single - dose infusion of methylprednisolone at a dosage of 10 mg/kg was carried out, and intrathecal dexamethasone at a dose of 5 mg per day was administered via intrathecal (IT) route on D7, D8, and D9. Serious cerebral oedema appeared on the 8th day with respiratory depression requiring endotracheal intubation and ventilator-assisted ventilation (grade 4). Then the patient was given additional intravenous methylprednisolone (D8-D13, 15 mg/kg/d, subsequent tapering) (Fig. 1A). Daily neurological evaluation showed gradual improvement, the ICNAS was gradually improved, and temperature, IL-6, CRP and PCT levels were gradually decreased (Fig. 1B-E). Although we used steroids therapy for ICANS at this stage, a decrease in CAR cell copy number wasn’t occurred (Fig. 2B). Cumulative dose of steroid (equivalent to dexamethasone) of him was 348 mg (16 kg), the duration of steroid was 10 days. Five months later, the patient relapsed again, he received a paternal haploid hematopoietic stem cell transplant. Due to the relapse, the PFS of this patient was only 150 days, but the OS was 234 days. It is now 2 months after the bone marrow transplant, bone marrow is still in remission, and no transplant-related complications have been observed.

Case 2, female, 6 years 6 months old, was diagnosed with B-ALL with EVI1 positive. She was accepted CCCG-ALL 2018 protocol (IR). The patient was not sensitive to chemotherapy, even didn’t received CR post 5 months with series of chemotherapy. After lymphocyte depletion with FC, she accepted autologous second-generation 4-1BB CD3ζanti-CD19 (pCAR-19B). Recurrent fever began to appear on the seventh day after CAR-T treatment, peripheral blood cytokines IL-6 was significantly elevated, CRS (grade 2) was considered, infection could not be excluded, tocilizumab, gamma globulin, anti-infection and other treatments were given. We also tested the BM, the results showed he received remission (1% blast in the BM smear, MRD was less than 1 × 10−4). On the day 9, the patients still had recurrent fever, tocilizumab was given again, convulsions began to appear at night (ICANS, grade 3), brain CT showed suspicious slightly low-density opacities in bilateral thalamic areas, ICANS was considered, intravenous methylprednisolone at a dosage of 1 mg/kg per day was administered. Meanwhile, dexamethasone (5 mg) was delivered via intrathecal (IT) injection. To alleviate intracranial pressure, mannitol was prescribed, and levetiracetam was used as an antiseizure medication. Significant change in mental status (grade 4) was observed on the 10th day, brain MRI showed slight swelling of the thalamus on both sides and cerebral feet, linear abnormal signals in the outer capsule region on both sides. The T1 signal of the bones at the base of the skull was reduced (Fig. 2C). Thus intravenous methylprednisolone (D10 and D11, 1 mg/kg/d) and intrathecal dexamethasone (D10, 5 mg) were keep giving. Cumulative dose of steroid (equivalent to dexamethasone) of him was 22.9 mg (23 kg), the duration of steroid was 3 days. Neurological evaluation was done daily, it showed that neurological symptoms were improved on the 14th day. Significant improvement in temperature and IL-6, CRP, and PCT levels after steroid therapy in this patient were observed either (Fig. 1G-J), but steroid therapy did not affect the amplification of CAR copy number (Fig. 2D). To reduce the incidence of recurrence, the children underwent allogeneic stem cell transplantation in the 6 months after CAR-T. At present, 4 months after bone marrow transplantation, it is in remission and has no adverse events. Until Oct 1,2023, the PFS of this patient was 290 days, the OS was 305 days.

Discussions and conclusion

The occurrence of ICANS is related to many factors, such as tumor type, tumor burden, lymphatic clearance status, presence or absence of CRS, age, and patient status. At present, it is believed that high tumor burden before CAR-T cell therapy, previous central nervous system injury or tumor involvement, infusion of high-dose CAR-T cells, CAR molecular structure, and use of CD28 co-stimulation molecules are associated with high incidence of ICANS [9, 10]. Six CAR-T cell therapies are currently approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and are undergoing clinical and preclinical studies [11, 12]. Our center adopts the first humanized CD19-targeting CAR-T product (pCAR-19B) for children and adolescents in China to treat relapsed B-ALL. pCAR-19B is a specific CAR-T targeting CD19 for recurrent B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (B-ALL), chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL) and B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma (B-NHL) in children, adolescents and adults independently developed by Chongqing Precision Biotechnology Co., Ltd. The CAR-T cells are currently being evaluated in phase II clinical trials (NCT05334823), with a current incidence of 48% (26/54) of phase II ICANS and 30% (16/54) of grade 3 and above ICANS.

ICANS is a common side effect after CAR-T cell therapy, occurring in 20–60% of cases, with 12–30% having severe (grade ≥ 3) symptoms [3]. For intensive ICANS, early intravenous corticosteroids and intensive care support are the mainstay of treatment. Most children with ICANS already have CRS in the early stage, and the development of the disease is also related to the level of cytokine IL-6. However, in terms of treatment, moderate to severe CRS with IL-6 inhibitors tocilizumab and glucocorticoids for immunosuppression has achieved good results. However, tocilizumab does not play a good role in the prevention and treatment of ICANS because it cannot effectively cross the blood-brain barrier [13]. In this study, it was observed that 2 children developed ICANS on the day of intravenous tocilizumab. Immediately, intrathecal dexamethasone was initiated. Post-treatment monitoring showed that the cytokine levels in the peripheral blood and cerebrospinal fluid decreased significantly, accompanied by a gradual alleviation of ICANS symptoms. Regarding the concern in clinical practice that intrathecal injection for the treatment of cerebral edema may induce brain herniation, the following measures were taken in this group of cases to effectively avoid risks: (1) Precise control of the drainage rate of cerebrospinal fluid; (2) Prophylactic use of mannitol to regulate intracranial pressure. The results indicated that this treatment did not induce severe complications such as brain herniation in the 2 children, confirming the safety of intrathecal injection under strict control.

In pediatric and young adult patients with relapsed/refractory B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL), intrathecal hydrocortisone has been reported to be effective in relieving hormonally insensitive ICANS [14]. However, it remains unclear which is more effective, dexamethasone or hydrocortisone, as there are no direct comparative studies between the two. At our medical center, the choice of dexamethasone for intrathecal injection is based on the following rationales. First and foremost, dexamethasone exhibits significantly stronger anti-inflammatory properties than hydrocortisone. This enhanced anti-inflammatory activity enables it to more effectively combat the underlying inflammatory processes associated with various conditions. Secondly, dexamethasone has a notably longer half-life. This pharmacokinetic characteristic allows it to exert a more consistent and sustained therapeutic effect over an extended period. As a result, patients can experience a more stable control of symptoms. Multiple research studies have demonstrated that intrathecal administration of dexamethasone is not only effective in alleviating the symptoms of neurotoxicity but also well-tolerated by patients. The combination of its superior anti-inflammatory action and long-lasting effect makes it a compelling option for intrathecal use in our clinical practice [15–17]. Therefore, we employed intrathecal dexamethasone to treat the ICANS in 2 children with 3 or higher grade in this study. It can be seen that the initial stage of ICANS in case 1 is grade 2, and the next day the disease progresses to grade 3, so the methylprednisolone infusion and intrathecal dexamethasone(5 mg) were given immediately when the ICANS was onset. The disease was stable and it returned to grade 2 ICNAS after 7 days. For this patient, the duration of steroid was 10 days, cumulative steroid was 348 mg. Unfortunately, he occurred 2nd relapse after 5 months (PFS: 150 days), and then hematopoietic stem cell transplantation was accepted. Previously some researches had been reported the high-dose steroid was associated with poor PFS [18]. This may partly explain our patients’ poor PFS. Case 2 had grade 3 ICANS at the beginning, and after treatment with IT dexamethasone and intravenous methylprednisolone on the day of symptom onset, the disease was quickly controlled, and the child returned to grade 2 ICANS after 3 days. The duration of steroid for 3 days, cumulative steroid was 22.9 mg. Although ICANS has a high incidence of first humanized CD19-targeting CAR-T product (pCAR-19B). But with our progress, all cases were actively and effectively treated, no deaths directly caused by neurotoxicity, and there were 2 patients who died after COVID-19 infection during the open of COVID-19.

Although the intrathecal administration of dexamethasone demonstrated promising efficacy in managing immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS) in the two pediatric cases, several limitations must be acknowledged. Most notably, both patients concurrently received intravenous methylprednisolone and tocilizumab for concomitant cytokine release syndrome (CRS), making it difficult to isolate the independent therapeutic contribution of intrathecal dexamethasone. Additionally, the small sample size and the lack of a control group preclude definitive conclusions regarding the superiority of intrathecal dexamethasone over alternative interventions. Future prospective, randomized studies with larger cohorts are warranted to delineate the specific role of intrathecal dexamethasone in ICANS managemen.

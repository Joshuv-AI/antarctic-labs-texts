Isomeric Imidazopyridine-Based NHC Ligands: Enhanced Catalytic Activity of an Electron-Donating Remote NHC in a PEPPSI-Type Palladium Complex
Authors: Yi-Cheng Chang, Jhen-Yi Lee, Chung-Fu Chiang, Tzu-Yun Chung, Hon Man Lee
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
We report the synthesis and characterization of a new series of imidazopyridine-based N-heterocyclic carbene (NHC) ligands, including three unique binding modes: less atom-stabilized (C5-bound), remote normal (C7-bound), and remote mesoionic (C8-bound) configurations. These distinct ligands were incorporated into palladium­(II) complexes of both mixed phosphine/NHC and PEPPSI-type structures to investigate the ligands’ electron-donating properties and their catalytic properties. Spectroscopic and computational analyses revealed that the C7-bound remote NHC ligand exhibits the highest electron-donating ability, with a unique balance of σ-donating and π-accepting characteristics that significantly enhance catalytic performance. The catalytic efficiency of the C7-bound NHC ligand, as demonstrated in the Mizoroki–Heck coupling of aryl chlorides, achieved near-quantitative yields and displayed rapid activation. This study underscores the potential of imidazopyridine-based NHC ligands for advancing catalyst design, especially for applications demanding high electron density and stability. These findings open new avenues to explore remote NHC ligands for challenging catalytic transformations, paving the way to improved reaction efficiency in synthetic organic chemistry.

Introduction

The chemistry of metal complexes with N-heterocyclic carbene (NHC) ligands has rapidly developed since the seminal work by Arduengo et al. in 1991. Due to their distinctive catalytic properties, NHCs are widely used in synthetic organic chemistry. Depending on the binding position, NHCs can adopt three classesnormal, abnormal, and remote NHCseach with distinct electron-donating characteristics that impact catalytic performance (Figure ). Among these, imidazole-based normal NHC ligands remain the most studied; , however, alternative binding modes, such as those exhibited by remote and abnormal NHCs, present an underexplored opportunity for enhancing catalytic properties.

Examples of different types of NHC complexes reported in the literature.

In 2001, Crabtree et al. showed that imidazole-based NHCs could exhibit an alternative binding mode via the C4/5 atom, instead of the commonly observed C2-bound mode. These variants of NHC ligands are commonly termed “abnormal NHCs”. They feature less heteroatom stabilization of the carbenic carbon and are more electron-rich than their normal NHCs. This electron-rich nature enhances the catalytic activities of their transition metal complexes. −,, According to the IUPAC definition, these abnormal NHCs should be termed mesoionic carbenes, as they contain no resonance form with all-neutral formal charges. Over the past decade, mesoionic and related less heteroatom-stabilized NHC complexes have attracted significant attention. Various heterocyclic compounds, such as 1,2,3-triazolylidene, 4-imidazolylidene, pyrazolylidene, pyridylidene, oxazolylidene, and thiazolylidene, have been successfully utilized in the construction of mesoionic NHC ligands, , though many other heterocycles hold potential for novel mesoionic NHCs.

In 2005, Herrmann et al. published the first palladium complex with a remote NHC ligand. Remote NHCs are defined by a carbenic carbon not directly adjacent to the stabilizing heteroatom. DFT calculations revealed that remote NHCs bind more strongly to metals than their normal NHCs. , Schneider et al. showed that a palladium complex with a remote NHC ligand had a strong metalcarbene bond due to electrostatic attraction and a high-energy σ-HOMO on the ligand. Catalytically, the remote NHC complex demonstrated significantly higher activity in certain C–C coupling reactions than normal NHC precatalysts. Since then, several intriguing palladium complexes with remote NHCs have been reported, including a benzoimidazole-based carbene palladium complex by Huynh et al.

The growing interest in mesoionic and remote NHC ligands stems from their enhanced electron-donating properties compared to traditional NHCs. However, there remains a notable gap in the exploration of metal complexes, particularly those featuring remote or mesoionic NHCs derived from imidazopyridine frameworks. In this study, we report a systematic exploration of isomeric imidazopyridine-based NHC ligands. The synthetic strategy employed, as illustrated in Scheme , involves the use of bromo-substituted imidazo­[1,2-a]­pyridine derivatives. Through the oxidative addition of the C–Br bonds to a palladium(0) precursor, we can efficiently generate a range of NHC ligands, including the less atom-stabilized normal NHC (C5-bound), the remote normal NHC (C7-bound), and the remote mesoionic NHC (C6- and C8-bound) ligands. Two series of palladium complexes were prepared for distinct purposes. The mixed/NHC palladium complex, trans-[Pd­(NHC)­(PPh3)2Br]­BF4, was prepared for the spectroscopic investigation of ligand basicity, while the PEPPSI-type complex, trans-Pd­(NHC)­pyBr2 (py = pyridine), was intended for catalytic applications. Detailed theoretical investigations were also conducted to better understand the electronic characteristics of these ligands and their resulting complexes. The catalytic performance of these complexes, specifically in the Mizoroki–Heck coupling reaction, was evaluated, showing promising efficiency. Our findings reveal that the C7-bound remote NHC ligand provides superior catalytic activity, opening new possibilities for designing highly efficient, thermally stable catalysts for complex organic transformations.

Results and Discussion

Syntheses of Ligand Precursors

A series of isomeric imidazopyridine-based NHC ligand precursors with distinct binding positions were prepared: C5-bound (less atom-stabilized), C7-bound (remote normal), and C8-bound (remote mesoionic) configurations. The synthetic route involved the reaction of commercially available bromo-substituted imidazo­[1,2-a]­pyridine derivatives with trimethyloxonium tetrafluoroborate ((CH3)3OBF4) under elevated temperature (80 °C) for 48 h (Scheme ). Using chloride-substituted analogs in place of bromides did not yield the desired products in subsequent complexation reactions, likely due to the lower reactivity of C–Cl bonds in oxidative addition reactions with palladium precursors. This highlights the importance of the bromo-substituted starting materials in accessing imidazopyridine-based NHC ligands efficiently. In this study, we focus on the synthesis and evaluation of palladium complexes derived from regioisomeric NHC ligands, including C5-, C7-, and C8-bound carbene variants. These were selected to span a range of steric and electronic environments. Although the C6-bound NHC isomer was not synthesized, preliminary DFT calculations revealed that its donor properties were intermediate between the C7- and C8-bound analogues, suggesting no clear advantage in catalytic performance. Given our aim to explore ligands with more pronounced contrasts in σ-donor strength, we prioritized those offering the most informative comparisons. This decision also reflects our efforts to reduce synthetic redundancy and chemical waste by avoiding targets with limited predicted impact.

Synthesis of Ligand Precursors

The synthesized ligand precursors exhibited characteristic 1H NMR signals in DMSO-d 6, with the methyl protons resonance observed at ca. 4.2 ppm, consistent with the formation of target compounds. The precursors were obtained with high purity and yields of 65% to 91%. This straightforward method provided clean, high-yield access to C5-, C7-, and C8-bound NHC ligand precursors, forming the basis for subsequent palladium complexation studies.

Syntheses of Palladium Complexes

The synthesized imidazopyridine-based NHC ligand precursors (1a–c) were used to prepare two types of palladium­(II) complexes: (i) mixed phosphine/NHC complexes, trans-[Pd­(NHC)­(PPh3)2Br]­BF4, and (ii) PEPPSI-type complexes, trans-[Pd­(NHC)­(py)­Br2] (py = pyridine) (Scheme ). The mixed phosphine/NHC complexes were synthesized primarily to facilitate spectroscopic evaluation of the electron-donating properties of each NHC ligand, while the PEPPSI-type complexes were designed for catalytic application studies.

Syntheses of Different Types of Palladium NHC Complexes

Mixed phosphine/NHC complexes 2a–c were prepared by the oxidative addition of C–Br bonds of the ligand precursors to Pd­(PPh3)4 in DMF at 80 °C. The pure complexes were obtained with modest yields of 47 to 49%. The yields of complexes 2a–c (47–49%) are modest, which we attribute to losses during the workup procedure rather than to instability of the ligands. The ligand precursors were stable under the reaction and purification conditions. However, removal of certain byproducts required treatment with a small amount of methanol, in which some of the product is also soluble, likely contributing to the reduced isolated yields. In each case, 31P­{1H} NMR and 13C­{1H} NMR spectra confirmed the presence of the carbene and phosphine ligands, with diagnostic chemical shifts. New PEPPSI-type palladium carbene complexes 3a–c were synthesized by the oxidative addition of the ligand precursors 1a–c with Pd­(dba)2 in the presence of NaBr in pyridine or 3-chloropyridine at an elevated temperature of 75 °C. These reactions proceeded smoothly, yielding the desired complexes in higher yields (52–78%) than the phosphine/NHC complexes. 1H and 13C­{1H} NMR analyses indicated the expected chemical shifts for pyridine and carbene environments, confirming successful complexation. The chemical shifts of the carbene carbons have been confirmed by 2D HMBC experiments (Figures S32–S34 in the Electronic Supporting Information).

Structural Descriptions

Single-crystal X-ray diffraction studies were conducted on representative complexes (2a, 2b, and 3a) to confirm structural details and examine how different NHC ligand binding modes affect the palladium coordination environment. Each complex adopts a distorted square planar geometry typical of Pd­(II), with variations in bond lengths and angles that reflect the distinct electronic properties of the C5-, and C7-bound NHC ligands.

Figures and show the molecular structure of 2a and 2b, respectively, with Pd­(II) coordinated to both phosphine ligands in a trans configuration. In both complexes, the Pd–C bond lengths are similar within experimental error, measuring 1.991(2) Å for C5-bound complex 2a and 1.994(5) Å for C7-bound complex 2b, indicating comparable metal–ligand bonding strength. These Pd–C bond lengths align with that observed in Huynh’s remote mesoionic NHC complex, which exhibited a Pd–C bond length of 1.986(10) Å. Distinctly, Pd–Br bond lengths differ, with C7-bound complex 2b showing an elongated Pd–Br bond of 2.5139(5) Å versus 2.4875(3) Å in C5-bound complex 2a. This elongation reflects the stronger σ-donating character of the C7-bound ligand, consistent with spectroscopic data suggesting its higher trans influence, and may enhance catalytic activity by facilitating substrate activation. Additional stabilization arises from π–π stacking interactions between the NHC ligand and the phenyl rings of the trans-PPh3 ligands. In complex 2a, the centroid-to-centroid distances between the heterocyclic ring and the two phenyl rings are 3.27 and 3.42 Å, which are typical distances for π–π stacking interactions.

Assessing Ligand Basicity via NMR Spectroscopy

Iglesias and Albrecht used 31P NMR spectroscopy to assess ligand basicity in a series of structurally related mesoionic NHC complexes, [Pd­(NHC)­(PPh3)2Cl]­[OTf]. Carbene ligands with higher basicity exhibit a more pronounced downfield shift of 31P NMR signals due to the increased population of the paramagnetic triplet state resulting from a lower HOMO–LUMO gap. The 31P NMR signals for 2a, 2b, and 2c are 22.1, 23.2, and 21.8 ppm, respectively. This means the C7-bound remote normal NHC ligand in 2b is the strongest donor, followed by the less atom-stabilized normal NHC ligand in 2a. The C8-bound remote mesoionic NHC ligand in 2c represents the weakest donor. A similar order is observed in the 13C NMR signals of the carbene carbons. Complex 2b shows the most downfield shift at 173.2 ppm, followed by 162.1 ppm in 2a, and 138.3 ppm in 2c. Complexes 3a–c exhibit a comparable trend with carbene carbon signals at 151.3 ppm in 3b, 149.7 ppm in 3a, and 122.2 ppm in 3c.

Ganter et al. proposed a straightforward method for evaluating the σ-donor properties of NHCs based on the Ccarbene–H coupling constant (1 J C–H). A larger coupling constant indicates weaker σ-donating property of the NHC ligand, suggesting an increased σ-orbital character of the C–H bond. Conversely, a smaller coupling constant correlates with a stronger σ-donating capability of the NHC ligand. Following this approach, we measured the 1 J C–H coupling constants coupling constants at C5 to C8 on the unsubstituted 1-methyl-1H-imidazo­[1,2-a]­pyridin-4-ium iodide from its proton-coupled 13C NMR spectrum (Figure S30). HSQC and HMBC were acquired to confirm the peak assignments of all the carbon and proton signals (Figures S35 and S36 in the Electronic Supporting Information). The observed order of J values is C7 < C6 < C8 < C5 (Figure ). This indicates that the σ-donating ability at the coordinated carbon sites follows the order C7 > C6 > C8 > C5. This sequence aligns perfectly with their HOMO energies, which will be discussed later. Notably, the trend predicted by the 1 J C–H coupling constants differs slightly from the predictions based on 31P and 13C NMR chemical shifts. These latter predictions suggest that the ligand bound at C5 is a stronger donor than the ligand bound at C8.

1-Methylimidazo­[1,2-a]­pyridine-1-ium iodide and its 1 J C–H coupling constants.

The distinct electron-donating characteristics of three different carbene ligands result in variations in electron density at the palladium centers. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) could theoretically determine Pd 3d binding energies in these complexes to infer electron-donating properties. However, as noted in a previous study, it faces limitations due to the subtle differences in Pd 3d binding energies among these complexes (Table S2 in the Electronic Supporting Information). Thus, NMR spectroscopy remains a robust and convenient approach for assessing the electron-donating properties of NHC ligands.

Computational Studies

In addition to structural and spectroscopic analyses, density functional theory (DFT) calculations were performed on the three NHC ligands in the new complexes using Gaussian 09. The calculation also includes an optimized structure for the C6-bound remote mesoionic NHC ligand, which does not form a complex in this study. The M06-2X functional and 6-31G* basis set were applied for both geometry optimization and single-point energy calculations. Typically, the σ-donor property of NHC ligands correlates with the energy of the σ symmetric carbene lone pair orbital, while π-acceptor capability is linked to the relevant π* orbital at the carbene carbon, which can overlap with the metal’s d orbitals.

In all ligands, the σ symmetric carbene lone pair orbitals corresponds to the HOMO, while the vacant π* orbital at the carbene carbon represents the LUMO (Figure ). HOMO energies follow this order: C7-bound (−5.85 eV) > C6-bound (−5.93 eV) > C8-bound (−6.29 eV) > C5-bound (−6.67 eV) ligands. This trend aligns perfectly with the σ-donating properties of the ligands, as indicated by the 1 J C–H coupling constants. The C7-bound ligand is the most electron-donating, and this strong electron-donating ability correlates with the superior catalytic performance of complex 3b among the PEPPSI-type complexes. Conversely, the C5-bound NHC ligand, which features a nitrogen atom adjacent to the carbene carbon, exhibits weaker electron donation, as the nitrogen withdraws electron density from the carbene carbon. This trend differs slightly from the predictions based on 31P NMR spectroscopy, which indicates that the C5-bound ligand is a stronger donor than the C8-bound ligand. It is worth noting that both the C6- and C8-bound ligands are remote mesoionic NHC ligands. The C6-bound ligand, with its carbene carbon farther from nitrogen atoms, experiences a reduced electron-withdrawing effect, resulting in higher σ-donating ability compared to the C8-bound ligand.

Molecular orbitals of the carbene σ-lone pairs (HOMO) and the unoccupied π* orbitals (LUMO) in the C5- to C8-bound NHC ligands at the M06-2X/6-31G* level of the theory with their calculated energies (isovalue = 0.02).

LUMO energies rank as follows: C8-bound (0.42 eV) > C7-bound (0.41 eV) > C5-bound (0.35 eV) > C6-bound (0.09 eV). Although the C6-bound ligand has the lowest LUMO energy, making it theoretically more π-accepting, it does not form a complex in this study. Among the new complexes, the C5-bound ligand exhibits the lowest LUMO energy, marking it as the strongest π-acceptor, while the C7-bound and C8-bound ligands have higher LUMO energies, indicating relatively lower π-accepting abilities.

Although the C6-bound ligand was not synthesized in this study, preliminary DFT analysis predicted that it would not surpass the C7-bound analogue in σ-donor ability. Its HOMO energy (−5.93 eV) lies between those of the C7- and C8-bound ligands, indicating intermediate donor strength. Given that our experimental focus was on probing the catalytic impact of ligands spanning the extremes of σ-donation, we prioritized synthesis of the C5-, C7-, and C8-bound ligands. Nonetheless, the C6-bound ligand remains of interest for future experimental validation.

While our ligands exhibit higher HOMO energies than benchmark NHCs like IMes (−7.64 eV) and IPr (−7.64 eV) (Figure S1 in the Electronic Supporting Information), we acknowledge that direct comparisons across different ligand scaffolds should be interpreted with caution. This observation is intended to provide a general context for donor strength, but our evaluations are primarily supported by a combination of spectroscopic (1 J C–H, 13C, 31P NMR) and computational metrics (CDA, ETS-NOCV, WBI) (vide infra), which together offer a more rigorous and internally consistent assessment of σ-donation across this ligand series.

All new complexes were optimized using the M06-L functional and the LanL2DZ basis set, while the Def2-TZVP basis set was employed for zero-point energy calculations. The HOMO–LUMO gap in complexes 2a–c falls within the range of 2.69 to 3.00 eV, which is higher than the range of 2.44 to 2.56 eV in complexes 3a–c. This indicates a generally higher stability of the mixed phosphine/carbene complexes. According to the second-order perturbation theory analysis using natural bond orbitals (NBOs), the P → Pd σ-donation in 2b exhibits a high stabilization energy of 88.21 kcal/mol. In contrast, the energy for the Br → Pd σ-donation in 3b is notably lower at 56.03 kcal/mol. This suggests that PPh3 ligands are less likely to dissociate compared to the bromide ligands. In addition, in complex 3b as an example, the HOMO involves only the bromide lone pairs and the dπ orbital of the palladium center along the Br–Pd–Br axis (Figure ). This indicates that the bromide ligands are more reactive compared to the carbene and pyridine moieties, and they are more likely to undergo substitution. All these are consistent with the catalytic results showing that the mixed carbene/phosphine complexes are ineffective due to the strong coordination of PPh3 ligands (vide infra). On the other hand, the LUMO in 3b is not only localized along the Br–Pd–Br axis but also extends over the frontier regions of the carbene and pyridine rings. This indicates the possibility π-backbonding from the d orbitals of the Pd center into the π* orbitals of the carbene and pyridine ligands.

HOMO (left) and LUMO (right) in complex 3b at the M06-L/Def2-TZVP//M06-L/LanL2DZ level of the theory.

To determine the tendency of the new complexes to accept electrons, we estimated the global electrophilicity index (ω) using the equation ω = μ2/2η, where η represents the chemical hardness and is calculated using the equation η = 12 (E LUMO – E HOMO), and μ represents the chemical potential and is calculated from the equation μ = 12 (E HOMO + E LUMO). The global reactivity descriptors of the new complexes, including ω, η, and μ, are listed in Table S3 in the Electronic Supporting Information. In the case of complexes 2a–c, the order of electrophilicity is observed as 2c (7.08 eV) > 2a (6.31 eV) > 2b (5.85 eV). For complexes 3a–c, 3a is the most electrophilic (6.87 eV), followed by 3c (6.70 eV), and the least electrophilic complex is 3b (6.06 eV). This trend confirms the C7-bound ligands as the most nucleophilic, consistent with their higher electron-donating ability and enhanced catalytic activity, particularly in 3b.

The Wiberg bond index (WBI) obtained from the NBO analysis provides a measure of the bond order by estimating the average electron pairs shared between atoms. For the Pd–C bonds in both mixed phosphine/carbene complexes (2a–c) and PEPPSI-type complexes (3a–c), the trend follows: C7-bound (b) > C5-bound (a) > C8-bound (c). In the mixed phosphine/carbene complexes, the WBI values are 2b (0.5949) > 2a (0.5778) > 2c (0.5570), indicating relatively weak covalency and partial ionic character due to the lower bond indices. In contrast, the PEPPSI-type complexes display significantly higher WBI values3b (0.8259) > 3a (0.7195) > 3c (0.6956)reflecting a stronger covalent character of the Pd–C bonds in this series. Importantly, the higher WBI observed for the C7-bound complexes is primarily a consequence of their superior σ-donor ability, which enhances the covalency of the Pd–C interaction. While π-backbonding can also contribute to bond order, its role is secondary here compared to σ-donation. The relative difference between the C5- and C8-bound ligands, however, can be attributed to stronger π-backbonding in the C5-bound case, which accounts for its higher WBI compared to the C8-bound analogue. Overall, these trends are consistent with the superior catalytic activity of the C7-bound complex 3b, where strong σ-donation correlates with the formation of the most covalent Pd–C bond (vide infra).

To compare the extent of π-backbonding from the filled dπ orbital of Pd into the π* orbital of the carbene carbon in the Pd–carbene bonds of the new complexes, a second-order perturbation theory analysis based on NBOs was conducted. This analysis calculated the stabilization energy of a filled Lewis-type NBO and an empty non-Lewis-type NBO. The NBOs involved are the lone pair of π-symmetry on the palladium center and the π* orbital on the carbene carbon, respectively. The calculated energies were 7.30, 7.55, and 6.16 kcal/mol for complexes 3a to 3c, respectively. A similar trend of stabilization energies for complexes 2a to 2c was also observed (Table S5 in the Electronic Supporting Information). These findings indicate that both C5-bound and C7-bound ligands have a significant π-acceptor property. In contrast, the C8-bound ligand has a relatively weak π-accepting capability and thus primarily functions as a σ-donor. Since the C5-bound ligand is an inferior σ-donor compared to the C7-bound ligand, it serves as a strong π-acceptor ligand with a relatively weak σ-donating property. Conversely, the C7-bound ligand acts as a very strong σ-donor with a moderate π-accepting property. The strong π-accepting property of the C5-bound ligand corresponds well with its lowest-lying LUMO, which involves accepting π electrons from the palladium center.

The palladium­(II) catalyst precatalyst is typically reduced to an active Pd(0) species in palladium-catalyzed reactions. When palladium complexes with bulky NHC ligands are used as catalyst precursors, the active species are accepted to be monoligated Pd0–NHC complexes. Therefore, an extended transition state-natural orbitals for chemical valence (ETS-NOCV) analysis was performed on monoligated Pd0–NHC complexes to assess the strength of σ-donation and π-backbonding in the Pd–carbene bonds (Figure ). The energies of σ-donor interactions (ΔE orb(σ)) and π-backbonding interactions (ΔE orb(π)) were computed. A more negative ΔE orb indicates stronger orbital interactions. The strength of the σ-donation for the Pd0–NHC complexes follows this order: C7-bound NHC ligand (−27.5 kcal/mol) > C6-bound NHC ligand (−26.6 kcal/mol) > C8-bound NHC ligand (−24.6 kcal/mol) > C5-bound NHC ligand (−20.6 kcal/mol). The π-back bonding interaction in these complexes decreases in this order: C5-bound NHC ligand (−15.3 kcal/mol) > C7-bound NHC ligand (−14.8 kcal/mol) > C8-bound NHC ligand (−12.1 kcal/mol) > C6-bound NHC ligand (−11.2 kcal/mol). In agreement with the aforementioned results, these findings suggest that the C7-bound NHC ligand acts as a strong σ-donor and can form a stable Pd–carbene bond, whereas the C5-bound NHC ligand is the strongest π-acceptor ligand. Interestingly, the C6-bound ligand, which does not form a complex in this study, is also a strong σ-donor with the weakest π-accepting capacity, despite having a low-lying LUMO (vide supra).

Isosurfaces of NOCV pairs depicting σ-donation and π-backbonding interactions between the C7-bound imidazopyridine-based NHC ligand and palladium(0) center in the optimized monoligated palladium(0) complex, along with corresponding interaction energies.

A charge-decomposition analysis (CDA) was performed on monoligated Pd0–NHC complexes (Table S4 in the Electronic Supporting Information). The CDA results align well with the findings from ETS-NOCV analysis, highlighting that the complex with the C7-bound remote NHC ligand exhibits the second-highest percentage of charge transfer through NHC → Pd π-donation, surpassed only by the C6-bound ligand. The C5-bound ligand is the weakest σ-donor. Furthermore, the complex with a C5-bound NHC ligand demonstrates the highest percentage of charge transfer through M → NHC π-back-donation, while the C6-bound ligand is the weakest π-acceptor. The electronic properties revealed by spectroscopic and computational studies are directly linked to the observed catalytic efficiency in the Mizoroki–Heck reaction, as explored below.

Summary of Methods for Evaluating σ-Donating and π-Accepting Properties

We applied various structural, spectroscopic, and computational methods to evaluate the σ-donating and π-accepting properties of imidazopyridine-based NHC ligands (Tables S6 and S7 in the Electronic Supporting Information). Most methods consistently identified the C7-bound ligand as the strongest σ-donor, with trans influence, HOMO energies, NMR shifts, and WBI supporting this finding. Minor variations were observed; for instance, while CDA highlighted the C6-bound ligand as the strongest σ-donor, ETS-NOCV analysis, and 1 J C–H coupling constants positioned it slightly lower. For π-accepting properties, most methods, including CDA and ETS-NOCV analysis, identified the C5-bound ligand as the strongest π-acceptor, though LUMO energies indicated the C6-bound ligand as a strong π-acceptor. Despite these minor discrepancies, the overall trends remain clear: the C7-bound ligand consistently stands out as a superior σ-donor, while the C5-bound ligand shows prominent π-acceptor strength, underscoring the impact of binding position on electronic properties.

Catalytic Reactions

We conducted a comprehensive investigation into the catalytic properties of the newly synthesized complexes using the benchmark Mizoroki–Heck coupling reaction of 4-chloroacetophenone and styrene in neat tetrabutylammonium bromide (TBAB) (Table ). Entries 1–3 clearly demonstrated that the PPh3-ligated complexes, trans-[Pd­(NHC)­(PPh3)2Br]­BF4, were ineffective as catalysts for this reaction, producing very low yields. This limited activity is likely attributed to a combination of strong coordination and significant steric bulk of the two PPh3 ligands, which together restrict access to the palladium center and hinder the formation of active catalytic species. Moreover, the ionic character of the Pd–C bonds in these complexes (vide supra) may contribute to their lower reactivity. Together, these factors diminish the ability of the complexes to participate efficiently in the catalytic cycle.

In contrast, the PEPPSI-type complexes trans-Pd­(NHC)­(py)­Br2 demonstrated markedly improved catalytic efficiency, as shown in entries 4–6. Among these, complex 3b, which features the electron-donating C7-bound remote normal NHC ligand, achieved a near-quantitative yield of 99%. This high yield correlates well with the findings from our 31P NMR and computational studies, which identified the C7-bound NHC as the strongest σ-donor among the synthesized ligands. The electron-donating capacity of the C7-bound ligand likely accelerates the formation of active LPd(0) species, facilitating the catalytic cycle. For benchmarking, Pd­(OAc)2 and Pd­(PPh3)4 were also evaluated under the same reaction conditions (Table , entries 9 and 10). These commercial Pd precursors afforded yields of 74% and 66%, respectively, which are significantly lower than the near-quantitative yield obtained with the C7-bound PEPPSI-type complex 3b (99–100%, entry 5). This difference underscores the superior catalytic efficiency of 3b under identical conditions. Moreover, unlike simple Pd salts, the designed NHC complexes provide valuable mechanistic insight: systematic correlations between ligand binding position, σ-donor strength, π-acceptor ability, and catalytic performance. These relationships highlight the advantage of rational ligand design in achieving both improved activity and a deeper understanding of catalyst behavior. Additionally, the 3-chloropyridine analogue of 3b was synthesized and evaluated, delivering a 68% yield (entry 11). This result highlights that the catalytic efficiency of PEPPSI-type complexes is highly sensitive to subtle changes in the electronic and steric properties of the pyridine ligand, and that donor strength alone does not determine activity. The blank experiment confirmed the absence of activity with the catalyst (entry 12).

Notably, although the final isolated yields of 3b and 3c under standard conditions are similar (99 vs 95%), their catalytic initiation behaviors differ markedly. Time–yield curves (Figure ) reveal that 3b reaches maximum yield within a significantly shorter activation period, while 3c and 3a require ∼30 min to reach similar levels. This rapid activation for 3b is attributed to the enhanced σ-donation of its C7-bound ligand, which accelerates Pd(0) formation and facilitates earlier engagement in the catalytic cycle.

Time–yield profiles for complexes 3a–c in catalyzing the Mizoroki–Heck coupling reaction between 4-chloroacetophenone and styrene.

Among the PEPPSI-type complexes, 3b exhibited the highest catalytic activity, followed closely by 3c and then 3a (3b ≥ 3c > 3a). While the difference between 3b and 3c is minor, this trend was reproducible across runs and is consistent with the observed electronic properties of the C7-bound ligand.

To further investigate the inhibitory effect of PPh3 ligands in the mixed phosphine/NHC complexes, we added one or two equivalents of PPh3 to the reaction mixture with complex 3b. This addition resulted in a significant reduction in product yields to 41% and 24%, respectively, corroborating the hypothesis that PPh3 ligands, by strongly coordinating to the Pd center, limit the active sites necessary for catalytic activity. Thus, the PEPPSI-based design with a C7-bound NHC provides an optimal balance, allowing effective electron donation while maintaining the accessibility of the palladium center for catalytic turnover.

To probe the nature of the active catalyst, mercury drop tests were performed for the Heck coupling reaction of 4-chloroacetophenone with styrene catalyzed by 3b under standard conditions. Upon addition of Hg(0) (Hg: Pd = 100:1), the yield dropped from 99% to 43%. The partial suppression of product formation in the presence of Hg(0) suggests that the reaction may proceed through a combination of homogeneous and heterogeneous pathways, involving both discrete Pd–NHC species and Pd clusters or nanoparticles. In our system, TBAB functions not only as the reaction medium, providing a polar ionic environment that enhances substrate solubility, but also as a stabilizing agent for Pd nanoparticles formed in situ, consistent with previous reports. Consistent with this interpretation, the sigmoidal time–yield profile observed for 3b (Figure ) may partly reflect nanoparticle nucleation and growth before reaching the active steady-state catalytic form. A proposed catalytic cycle illustrating both molecular and nanoparticle-mediated pathways is shown in Figure S6 in the Electronic Supporting Information. The active species involves a LPd(0) complex. In palladium-catalyzed coupling reactions, reduction of Pd­(II) precatalysts to Pd(0) is generally facilitated under the reaction conditions without requiring an external reductant.

The superior performance of complex 3b can be rationalized by its electron-rich Pd center, imparted by the strongly σ-donating C7-bound NHC ligand. In the PEPPSI-type framework, where Pd is initially coordinated to pyridine, this increased electron density facilitates pyridine dissociation and lowers the activation barrier for oxidative addition of the aryl halide, thereby accelerating the formation of the active Pd(0) species. This effect shortens the induction period, as evidenced by the time–yield profiles (Figure ), and promotes rapid entry into the catalytic cycle. Furthermore, the moderate π-accepting capacity of the C7-bound ligand helps stabilize Pd­(II) intermediates during migratory insertion without unduly retarding reductive elimination, leading to a balanced and efficient catalytic turnover sequence.

To examine whether the ligand-dependent activity trend extends to other catalytic reactions, we investigated the direct arylation of imidazo­[1,2-a]­pyridine with 4-chlorobenzonitrile using complexes 3a–c as catalysts (2 mol % loading) (Scheme ). Complexes 3a, 3b, and 3c delivered yields of 42%, 57%, and 45%, respectively. This order of activity (3b > 3c > 3a) is consistent with that observed for the Mizoroki–Heck coupling, reinforcing the conclusion that the stronger σ-donor ability of the C7-bound ligand in 3b translates into improved catalytic efficiency across different C–C coupling transformations.

Conclusion

This study developed and characterized a new series of isomeric imidazopyridine-based NHC ligand precursors, enabling the synthesis of palladium complexes with mixed phosphine/carbene and PEPPSI-type structures. Structural analysis, NMR spectroscopy, and DFT calculations consistently confirmed that the C7-bound remote NHC ligand is the strongest σ-donor among the isomers, enhancing electron density at the palladium center and, in turn, improving catalytic performance. The C5-bound, less atom-stabilized NHC ligand demonstrated strong π-acceptor characteristics, while the C8-bound remote mesoionic NHC ligand exhibited intermediate σ-donating properties and the weakest π-accepting ability.

The high electron-donating nature of the C7-bound ligand, combined with the robust Pd–C bond in the PEPPSI-type complex, was critical in enhancing catalytic efficiency, particularly in the Mizoroki–Heck coupling reaction. Complex 3b, featuring the C7-bound ligand, achieved high yields in challenging reactions, such as the coupling of aryl chlorides with styrene. In contrast, mixed phosphine/carbene complexes exhibited limited catalytic activity due to the strong coordination of PPh3 ligands, underscoring the importance of ligand design in catalyst accessibility.

These findings highlight the importance of NHC ligand electronic propertiesspecifically the balance between σ-donating and π-accepting characteristicsin enhancing catalytic efficiency. This study broadens the scope of accessible NHC ligands and underscores the unique advantages of remote NHCs derived from imidazopyridine frameworks, offering new pathways for designing next-generation catalysts. Further exploration of related remote mesoionic NHCs could provide deeper insights into σ-donating and π-accepting effects in catalysis. The structural and electronic versatility of these ligands lays a strong foundation for future advancements in high-performance catalyst design for organic transformations.

A General Procedure for Mizoroki–Heck Reaction

In a typical reaction, 4-chloroacetophenone (1 mmol), and styrene (1.4 mmol) were mixed with a base (1.1 equiv), a palladium precatalyst (0.1 mol %), and TBAB (2 g). The mixture was stirred at 140 °C for 2 h. After the reaction was cooled, the product was extracted with ethyl ether (3 × 10 mL), and the organic layer was dried using anhydrous MgSO4. The dried organic layer was then filtered, and the solvent was completely removed under high vacuum. The NMR yield was determined by using 1,3,5-trimethoxybenzene as an internal standard.

A General Procedure for Direct Arylation Reaction

A typical direct arylation reaction was carried out by mixing 4-chlorobenzonitrile (1.0 mmol), imidazo­[1,2-a]­pyridine (2.0 mmol), K2CO3 (2.0 mmol), PivOH (0.3 mmol), and a Pd precatalyst (2.0 mol %) in DMA (3 mL) under a nitrogen atmosphere. The reaction mixture was stirred at 140 °C in a preheated oil bath for 18 h. After cooling to room temperature, the reaction mixture was analyzed by gas chromatography (GC) using benzophenone as the internal standard.

Single-Crystal X-ray Diffraction Studies

Crystals suitable for X-ray structural studies were obtained by employing the solvent combination acetone/methanol and allowing for slow evaporation. The structural data were obtained using a Rigaku XtaLab Synerg DW X-ray diffractometer equipped with a MicroMax-007 HF microfocus rotating anode that generated Cu Kα radiation (λ = 1.54184 Å) and a HyPix-Arc 150° curved hybrid photon counting X-ray detector. The data acquisition and reduction were carried out using the CrysAlisPro 1.171.42.51a software. Empirical absorption correction was performed using spherical harmonics implemented in SCALE3 ABSPACK. The experiments were conducted at National Tsing Hua University, Hsinchu city, Taiwan. Direct methods were initially used to solve the crystal structures. The obtained solutions were further refined using full-matrix least-squares methods against F 2 with the SHELXL program. Refinement of the non-hydrogen atoms was carried out using anisotropic refinement methods, taking into account the anisotropy of thermal motion. The positions of the hydrogen atoms were calculated and fixed at their respective calculated positions. Riding refinements were then performed for the hydrogen atoms. CCDC file numbers are 2394058 (2a), 2394059 (2b), and 2394060 (3a).

Computational Studies

The calculations were conducted using the Gaussian 09W program. For the calculations, the M06-2X functional was used for the ligands, while the M06L functional was applied for all the metal complexes. Ligand structure optimization and zero-point energy calculations were conducted using the 6-31G* basis set, while the LANL2DZ basis set was used for structural optimization of the metal complexes. Zero-point energy calculations for all the metal complexes were performed using the Def2-TZVP basis set. To account for the solvent effects, the polarized continuum model (PCM) was employed to optimize all the geometries in N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF) solvent with a dielectric constant of ε = 37.219. The temperature and pressure were set at 298.15 K and 1 atm, respectively. The natural bond orbital (NBO) analysis was performed using the NBO 3.1 program within Gaussian 09W. Charge decomposition analysis (CDA) and energy decomposition analysis (ETS-NOCV) were carried out using the Multiwfn software.

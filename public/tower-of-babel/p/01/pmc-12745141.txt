The Impact of Different Antidiabetic Drugs on Fracture Risk in Patients With Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus: A Systematic Review and Network Meta-analysis of Randomized Controlled Trials With a Focus on SGLT2 Inhibitors
Authors: Bushra Admani, Fizza Zehra Raza, Fatima Siddiqui, Muhammad Talal Ashraf, Muhammad Khuzzaim Khan, Ifra Habib, Ayesha Usman, Bareeha Mansoor, Zaira Nadeem, Rana Jahanzeb Ghaffer, Muhammad Riyyan, Sawaira Sajid, Muhammad Hassan Ali Chania, Mahnoor Saleem, Saad Javaid, Nikhil Duseja, Hussam Al Hennawi, Sunita Lakhani
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractSGLT2 inhibitors are a new class of antidiabetic drugs that have shown cardiovascular benefits in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) and cardiovascular disease. However, their effects on fracture risk are unclear and may depend on the type and duration of treatment. This meta-analysis compares the fracture risk of different antidiabetic drugs, including SGLT2 inhibitors, based on data from randomized controlled trials (RCTs). We searched 4 databases for RCTs that reported fracture events in patients with T2DM who received different antidiabetic drugs. We included 117 RCTs that compared 9 types of antidiabetic drugs: SGLT2 inhibitors, DPP-4 inhibitors, α-glucosidase inhibitors, thiazolidinediones, insulin, GLP-1 receptor agonists, meglitinides, biguanides, and sulfonylureas. We used a statistical method called Frequentist meta-analysis to combine data from different studies and compare different treatments. The results showed that SGLT2 inhibitors were the only drug that significantly reduced the fracture risk compared to placebo and other drugs (OR 0.85; 95% CI, 0.74-0.98). The other antidiabetic drugs did not show any significant difference from placebo or from each other. The mechanisms behind the effects of SGLT2 inhibitors on bone health are not well understood and may involve changes in calcium, phosphate, sodium, and arginine vasopressin levels in the body. SGLT2 inhibitors demonstrated a favorable skeletal safety profile among antidiabetic drugs. More long-term studies focused on fracture as a primary outcome are needed to fully understand how SGLT2 inhibitors affect bone health and fracture risk in patients with T2DM.Keywords: Fracture risk, Meta-analysis, Sglt2 inhibitors, Type 2 diabetes mellitus

Introduction

Diabetes affects about 537 million people around the world, and most of them have type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM). This number is projected to increase to 783 million by 2045.1 People with diabetes have a much higher risk of heart failure (HF),2 which is 2 to 4 times more than normal. The American Diabetes Association (ADA)3,4 recommends that people with T2DM who have HF or chronic kidney disease (CKD) or who are at high risk of these conditions should use sodium-glucose co-transporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitors to prevent harmful outcomes. This new approach to treating T2DM is based on the results of several important clinical trials, such as EMPA-REG OUTCOME, CANVAS, LEADER, and SUSTAIN-6,5, 6, 7, 8, 9 which showed the benefits of these drugs.

However, in 2016, the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) warned that canagliflozin, one of the SGLT2 inhibitors, may increase the risk of fracture.10 This was after the CANVAS trial found more fractures in people who took this drug. Some researchers suggested that these drugs may affect how calcium and phosphate are used in the body, which may lead to compromised bone integrity and increase fractures risk.11

But later studies did not find the same problem. The CREDENCE trial in 2019 showed no difference in fracture risk between people who took canagliflozin or a placebo.12 The same was true for the EMPA-REG and DECLARE–TIMI 58 trials, which tested empagliflozin and dapagliflozin, respectively.13 Another trial, VERTIS-MET, even found that ertugliflozin, another SGLT2 inhibitor, reduced the risk of fractures.14

There are many different drugs for diabetes, but there has not been a study that compared them all together and looked at their effects on fracture risk.15, 16, 17 This is important because the new guidelines suggest using SGLT2 inhibitors more often, but their skeletal safety profile remains to be clearly established. To answer this question, we used a statistical method called Frequentist meta-analysis, which can combine data from different studies and compare different treatments. We reviewed all the clinical trials that reported fracture events in people with diabetes who used different drugs. Our goal was to evaluate the safety of these drugs for the bones. Our study may help doctors and patients make better decisions about diabetes treatment in the future.

Material and Methods

Strategy Search

We searched for randomized controlled trials (RCTs) that reported fracture events in patients with T2DM who received different antidiabetic drugs. We used the following keywords: “Anti-diabetic drug,” “Type 2 diabetes mellitus,” “sodium-glucose cotransporters,” “GLP-1 receptor agonists,” “sulfonylureas,” “insulin,” “DPP-4 inhibitors,” “metformin,” “bone fragility,” “bone mineral density,” and “fracture risk.” We searched the following databases: Medline, Google Scholar, ClinicalTrials.gov, and Cochrane CENTRAL. We limited our search to studies published from the inception of each database until August 2023.

Selection Criteria

We included only RCTs that compared the effects of sodium–glucose co-transporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitors with other antidiabetic drugs or placebo on fracture risk in patients with T2DM. The other antidiabetic drugs were sulfonylureas, dipeptidyl peptidase-4 (DPP-4) inhibitors, thiazolidinediones glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonists, or insulin. We also required that the studies reported fracture data, were written in English, and had a full-text article available. We excluded observational studies, single-arm studies, abstracts, case reports, conference presentations, editorials, and expert opinions.

Data Extraction and Quality Assessment

We searched the databases and imported the articles into EndNote Reference Library, where we removed the duplicates. We then screened the articles by reading their titles and abstracts and selected the ones that met our criteria. Full-text articles of potentially eligible studies were then assessed for inclusion. Two reviewers (BA and F-ZR) did this independently and resolved any disagreements with the help of a senior reviewer (FS). The data we extracted included the name of the first author, the year of publication, the number of participants, the average age, comorbidities, the duration of follow-up, the intervention and comparison groups, and the outcome of interest. We used the Cochrane risk-of-bias tool to assess the quality of the studies.18, 19

Statistical Analysis

Network meta-analysis was conducted based on the Frequentist model using the software Meta Insight V4.2.0. In our study, the risk of fractures was analyzed between SGLT2i versus 6 active comparators, namely dipeptidyl peptidase 4 inhibitors (DPP4i), glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP1RA), insulin, metformin, and sulfonylurea. Random-effects model was used to account for heterogeneity between clinical trials for Frequentist analysis model, odds ratios (ORs) with its 95% CI (CI) of antidiabetic drugs on bone fracture were evaluated, OR value < 1 favors “lower risk,” OR value > 1 favors a “higher risk.” Inconsistency was evaluated using through the unrelated mean effects (UME) consistency model,20 where inconsistency evidence was weighed against consistency. Correlation plots compared posterior mean deviations between consistency and inconsistency models for each data point.

Results

Study Characteristics and Quality Assessment

We followed the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions and the PRISMA statement to report the results of our meta-analyses. We found 47,869 articles from the databases and screened 812 of them for eligibility. We included 117 RCTs that compared 9 types of antidiabetic drugs: SGLT2 inhibitors, DPP-4 inhibitors, α-glucosidase inhibitors, thiazolidinediones, insulin, GLP-1 receptor agonists, meglitinides, biguanides, and sulfonylureas. Figure 1 shows how we selected the studies. Figure 2 shows the network of comparisons among the drugs. The methodological quality of all studies was evaluated to be high. Network Geometry

The network plot (Figure 2) includes 7 interventions: SGLT2 inhibitors, DPP-4 inhibitors, GLP-1 receptor agonists (GLP1RA), metformin, sulfonylureas, insulin, and placebo. A total of 6 direct comparisons between SGLT2 inhibitors and other treatments are represented, making it the most connected node in the network.

The most heavily studied comparison was between SGLT2 inhibitors and placebo (92 trials), followed by SGLT2i versus DPP-4 inhibitors (12 trials) and SGLT2i versus sulfonylureas (7 trials). Fewer trials were available for comparisons between SGLT2i and metformin (5 trials), GLP1RA (1 trial), and insulin (1 trial), indicating asymmetry in the evidence distribution.

Risk of Fractures

We analyzed the data from all the patients and RCTs to compare the fracture risk of different antidiabetic drugs. Figure 3 shows the results of this analysis in a forest plot. The only drug that showed a significant difference from placebo was SGLT2 inhibitors, which reduced the fracture risk by 15% (OR 0.85; 95% CI, 0.74-0.98). The other drugs, such as DDP-4 inhibitors, GLP-1 RA, insulin, metformin, and sulfonylureas, did not show any significant difference from placebo or from each other. This means that SGLT2 inhibitors are the safest option for the bones among all the antidiabetic drugs.

Discussion

The recent clinical trials of SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP-1 receptor agonists (including semaglutide) have shown that these drugs can lower the risk of cardiovascular events and death in patients with T2DM and cardiovascular disease.8,9,11 However, the effects of these drugs on fracture risk are not well understood.

A meta-analysis in 2016 compared the fracture risk of three SGLT2 inhibitors: canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, and empagliflozin. It found that none of them increased the fracture risk compared to placebo. However, it also found that the fracture risk was lower only during the first 52 weeks of treatment, and not after that.21, 22, 23

This meta-analysis suggested that SGLT2 inhibitors may have different effects on bone health over time. One possible explanation is that SGLT2 inhibitors may change the balance of calcium and phosphate in the body.24 These minerals play a critical role in bone mineralization and skeletal homeostasis. Some studies have shown that SGLT2 inhibitors may increase the amount of phosphate in the blood,25 which may stimulate hormones that break down bone.26,27 SGLT2 inhibitors may also cause weight loss,28,29 which may reduce bone density and increase bone turnover. These factors may reduce bone mineral density and increase fracture risk over time.

Another possible explanation is that SGLT2 inhibitors may affect the sodium levels in the bone. Bone acts as a sodium reservoir, and sodium release is regulated by endocrine mechanisms. Sodium release from bone depends on a hormone called arginine vasopressin,30 which also affects bone cells. Arginine vasopressin can inhibit bone formation and stimulate bone resorption, which may exacerbate osteoclastic activity and skeletal fragility. SGLT2 inhibitors may cause low sodium levels in the blood (hyponatremia), which may trigger more arginine vasopressin release and more bone loss.

However, these mechanisms are not well established and may vary among different SGLT2 inhibitors. Some studies have found that canagliflozin may increase markers of bone turnover and decrease bone density,31 whereas dapagliflozin had no significant effect on these measures.32 Ertugliflozin, another SGLT2 inhibitor, was even found to reduce the fracture risk in a recent trial. Therefore, more research is needed to clarify how SGLT2 inhibitors affect bone metabolism and fracture risk in patients with T2DM

Chronic hyperglycemia leads to the accumulation of advanced glycation end products (AGEs) in bone, which leads to the weakening of bone structure. SGLT2 inhibitors lower glucose levels, potentially reducing AGE formation.33 Furthermore, SGLT2 inhibitors are known to exert inflammatory effects and reduce oxidative stress, both of which contribute to bone resorption and fragility.34 According to a study conducted by Neal et al., SGLT2 inhibitors lead to moderate weight loss and better control over blood pressure, which improves physical function and reduces the fall risk, which is a major cause of fractures in T2DM patients.35 This suggests evidence related to the protective mechanisms of SGLT2 inhibitors.

This meta-analysis was based on RTCs, but it had some limitations that should be considered when interpreting the results. One limitation was the short duration of treatment and follow-up in the original studies. The effects of SGLT2 inhibitors on bone mineral density and fracture risk may not be evident for such a short time. Therefore, more long-term studies are needed to monitor the fracture risk of SGLT2 inhibitor users. Another limitation was the small number of fractures reported in the studies, especially for dapagliflozin and empagliflozin. This may affect the accuracy and reliability of effect estimates. The low number of fractures also raises the possibility of underreporting or missing fractures. Therefore, the results may not reflect the true fracture risk of SGLT2 inhibitors. A final limitation was that fracture was not the primary outcome of interest in most of the studies. This means that the studies may not have been designed or powered to detect differences in fracture risk among different treatments. Therefore, the results should be viewed with caution. More studies that focus on fracture as a primary outcome are needed to fully understand how SGLT2 inhibitors affect bone health.

Conclusions

This meta-analysis compared the effects of different antidiabetic drugs on fracture risk in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus. The results showed that only SGLT2 inhibitors significantly reduced the fracture risk compared to placebo and other antidiabetic drugs. The other antidiabetic drugs did not have any significant impact on fracture risk. The mechanisms underlying the effects of SGLT2 inhibitors on bone health are not clear and may involve changes in mineral and hormonal balance. More long-term studies are needed to evaluate the safety and efficacy of SGLT2 inhibitors for bone health in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Noninvasive tracking of myocardial fibrosis in pressure-overload heart failure with [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT
Authors: Shanshan Liu, Hui Wen, Bingqing Han, Fei Wang, Xiangyang Gao, Yi Luo, Hao Zhang, Xiaoyan Hao, Chao Fu, Zhaohui Xie, Xiaojun Zhang, Qiang Zeng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundMyocardial fibrosis is a major driver of heart failure progression, yet noninvasive tools to track its activity are limited. We evaluated fibroblast activation protein (FAP)-targeted [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 (FAPI = FAP inhibitor) positron emission tomography/computed tomography (PET/CT) in C57BL/6J mice subjected to transverse aortic constriction (TAC).ResultsPET/CT and echocardiography were performed at 2, 4, and 8 weeks post-surgery. Compared to the sham group, TAC mice exhibited significantly elevated myocardial [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 uptake at week 2, with progressive intensification at weeks 4 and 8. Although cardiac function remained preserved at week 2, pronounced systolic and diastolic dysfunction (decreased left ventricular ejection fraction [LVEF] and elevated E/A ratio) developed by week 4, deteriorating further by week 8. Correspondingly, histological (wheat germ agglutinin [WGA], Sirius Red, Masson’s) and autoradiographic analyses revealed progressive cardiomyocyte hypertrophy, accompanied by time-dependent increases in collagen deposition and FAP-specific signals. Importantly, a blocking experiment significantly suppressed myocardial uptake, confirming targeting specificity.Conclusions[68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT provides a specific, noninvasive method to track the progression of myocardial fibrosis. Crucially, increased FAPI uptake precedes the manifestation of cardiac dysfunction, highlighting its significant potential as a promising imaging tool for the early detection and longitudinal monitoring of heart failure.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13550-026-01461-5.

Background

Heart failure remains a leading cause of global morbidity and mortality. Myocardial fibrosis is a pivotal pathological driver that promotes the transition from compensated hypertrophy to decompensated failure [1, 2]. Consequently, early, dynamic, and accurate quantification of active fibrosis is essential for risk stratification and guiding individualized therapy. However, current diagnostic tools fall short. Although cardiac magnetic resonance can depict focal scar via late gadolinium enhancement, it has limited sensitivity for diffuse, early-stage fibrosis and cannot readily distinguish active fibrogenesis from established, quiescent scar [3].

Fibroblast activation protein (FAP) is an attractive molecular target for imaging active fibrosis. FAP is a type II transmembrane serine protease with negligible expression in healthy tissues but marked upregulation on activated fibroblasts during tissue remodeling [4]. Because FAP participates directly in extracellular matrix turnover, imaging its expression reflects ongoing pathophysiology rather than a static phenotype [5]. Positron emission tomography/computed tomography (PET/CT) using radiolabeled FAP inhibitors (FAPI) therefore offers a noninvasive means to quantify active fibrosis [6].

While FAPI PET/CT has transformed oncologic imaging [7–10], cardiovascular applications are still emerging. Preclinical cardiac studies have focused mainly on acute injury such as myocardial infarction (MI), where tracer uptake reflects a transient wound-healing response [11]. To investigate chronic disease progression, we utilized the transverse aortic constriction (TAC) model. Unlike toxic (e.g., doxorubicin) or acute neurohormonal (e.g., isoproterenol) models, TAC imposes sustained mechanical stress, accurately recapitulating the progressive shift from adaptive hypertrophy to maladaptive fibrosis seen in clinical hypertension and aortic stenosis [12, 13].

In this study, we evaluated the performance of [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT as a biomarker for pressure-overload heart failure using a murine TAC model. By integrating PET/CT signals alongside echocardiographic changes, autoradiography, and histological evidence, we investigated whether this modality can serve as a reliable, early, and specific molecular imaging tool for monitoring fibrosis progression.

Methods

TAC model

Eight-week-old male C57BL/6J mice (20–25 g; Vital River Laboratory Co., Ltd., Beijing, China) were housed under standard conditions (22–25 ℃; 12-h light/dark cycle) with ad libitum access to food and water. After acclimation, mice were randomly assigned to undergo transverse aortic constriction (TAC) or sham surgery. All animal procedures were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee and conducted in accordance with institutional guidelines. The detailed allocation scheme for the animal experiments is provided in Table S1.

For TAC, mice initially received buprenorphine (0.1 mg/kg, i.p.) and were anesthetized with isoflurane (induction: 3.0%; maintenance: 2.0%–3.5%) on a 37℃ heated platform. Through a midline cervical incision, the trachea was intubated under direct vision using a 24-gauge catheter. Mice were mechanically ventilated with 100% oxygen (tidal volume: 8–10 µL/g; rate: 150 breaths/min). Subsequently, the aortic arch was exposed and tied against a 26-gauge blunt needle with a 6 − 0 silk suture. The needle was immediately removed to create a defined stenosis. Sham mice underwent identical procedures without ligation. Postoperatively, mice recovered on a heating pad until ambulatory, and received buprenorphine (0.1 mg/kg, i.p.) every 8 h for 3 consecutive days for analgesia.

Echocardiographic measurements

Transthoracic echocardiography was performed at postoperative weeks 2, 4, and 8 using a high-resolution small-animal ultrasound system (Vevo 2100; VisualSonics, Toronto, Canada). Mice were anesthetized with 1–2% isoflurane; body temperature was maintained at approximately 37 ℃. M-mode tracings were used to measure left ventricular (LV) anterior/posterior wall thickness at end-systole and end-diastole (LVAWs/LVAWd; LVPWs/LVPWd) and LV internal diameter at end-systole and end-diastole (LVIDs/LVIDd), from which LV end-systolic and end-diastolic volumes (LVESV/LVEDV) were derived. Pulsed-wave Doppler at the aortic arch was used to measure peak flow velocity, and transmitral inflow was recorded to calculate the E/A ratio. All measurements were averaged over at least three consecutive cardiac cycles by an operator blinded to group allocation. Preparation of [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04

FAPI-04 was purchased from Huayi Technology (Suzhou, China). 68GaCl3 was eluted from a 68Ge/68Ga generator (ITM) and added to the lyophilized precursor. Sodium acetate was used to adjust pH to 4.0. The mixture was heated at 105 ℃ for 5 min in a sealed vial, purified using a C18 solid-phase extraction cartridge, and formulated in saline. Quality control was performed by thin-layer chromatography (mobile phase: methanol:0.1 M ammonium acetate = 1:1). Radio-HPLC confirmed that [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 had a radiochemical purity exceeding 98%.

[68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT Imaging

PET/CT imaging was performed using a small-animal IRIS PET/CT scanner (Inviscan, France) on two independent cohorts. A primary longitudinal cohort (n = 4 TAC, n = 4 sham) underwent serial scanning at 4 and 8 weeks post-surgery. To specifically capture early pathophysiological changes, a separate cross-sectional cohort (n = 4 TAC, n = 4 sham) was imaged at week 2 post-surgery. For all scans, mice received a tail-vein injection of [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 (4–5 MBq per animal) and were imaged 30 min post-injection. Identical TAC surgical procedures, tracer preparation protocols, imaging parameters, and analytical workflows were strictly applied to all animals to ensure data comparability across cohorts.

Non-gated small-animal PET/CT scans were performed in a single bed position (90-mm FOV). CT data (80 kV, continuous full-scan, 800 projections, 50 ms/exposure) were reconstructed to a 120-µm voxel size, with standard beam hardening and ring artifact pre-corrections applied. Subsequent PET data (600 s acquisition, 250–750 keV energy window) were reconstructed using a 3D-OSEM-MC algorithm (8 iterations, 8 subsets, unregularized). Standard corrections for decay, randoms, and dead-time were applied during PET reconstruction, whereas attenuation correction was omitted. For image analysis, volume of interest (VOI) was manually drawn to encompass the entire heart volume on the fused PET/CT images. A background VOI was placed in the right thigh skeletal muscle, taking care to exclude the femur and major blood vessels. Quantitative outcomes included the mean percentage of the injected dose per gram of tissue (%ID/g mean), myocardial SUVmax, and the target-to-background ratio (TBR). Specifically, the TBR was calculated by dividing the myocardial SUVmax by the SUVmean of the reference thigh skeletal muscle.

Blocking experiment

To evaluate the specificity of [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 uptake in the injured myocardium, a competitive in vivo blocking experiment was conducted at 8 weeks post-surgery. TAC mice were co-injected via the tail vein with [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 together with an excess dose of non-radiolabeled FAPI-04 (550 nmol) to competitively occupy FAP binding sites. PET/CT imaging was performed 30 min after injection, and attenuation of myocardial tracer uptake relative to unblocked conditions was taken as evidence of specific, receptor-mediated binding.

Autoradiography

Autoradiography was used to validate the spatial distribution of radiotracer uptake. Frozen sections were equilibrated in TBS for 30 min, incubated with [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 (740 kBq/mL in TBS) for 30 min at room temperature, washed with TBS (5 × 2 min), air-dried, and exposed to phosphor imaging plates for 2 h. Images were acquired using a Cyclone Plus Storage Phosphor System (PerkinElmer, USA). Adjacent sections underwent histology and FAP immunofluorescence for spatial co-registration.

Histological analysis

Following perfusion to remove blood, mouse hearts were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for at least 24 h. The trimmed, fixed tissues were dehydrated, embedded in OCT, and sectioned into 8-µm-thick cryosections. To evaluate tissue morphology and the extent of fibrosis, cryosections were stained with Hematoxylin and Eosin (H&E) (Servicebio, G1005), Sirius Red (Servicebio, G1078), and Masson’s trichrome (Servicebio, G1006), respectively. For immunofluorescence analysis, frozen heart sections were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde, subjected to antigen retrieval, and blocked with serum, followed by sequential incubation with an anti-FAP primary antibody (Invitrogen, PA5-99313) and a corresponding Alexa Fluor-conjugated secondary antibody. Nuclei were counterstained with 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI). Furthermore, to detect indicators of cardiac hypertrophy, a similar staining protocol was employed for wheat germ agglutinin (WGA) staining (Servicebio, G1730). All immunofluorescence images were captured using a laser scanning confocal microscope (Leica, Wetzlar, Germany) and analyzed with ImageJ software.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay

Blood samples were collected from the mice after they were sacrificed under anesthesia. The samples were allowed to stand at room temperature for 2 h and then centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 10 min at 4 ℃ to isolate the serum. Subsequently, the serum concentration of serum B-type natriuretic peptide (BNP) was measured using an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA, E-EL-M0166, Elabscience) in strict accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions.

Statistics

Data are presented as mean ± SD. Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 10.0. Differences in endpoint metrics between two groups were analyzed using Student’s t-test, while analysis of variance was used for comparisons among multiple groups. Data across multiple time points (e.g., echocardiography and PET/CT parameters) were analyzed using a mixed-effects model, followed by Sidak’s post hoc test for multiple comparisons. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Progressive cardiac dysfunction after TAC

A standardized TAC procedure established pressure overload as confirmed by Doppler echocardiography (peak aortic arch velocity ≥ 4000 mm/s; Fig. 1A). Three deaths occurred in the TAC group (two within 24 h postoperatively, one at week 8); no deaths occurred in shams. At the endpoint, indices of remodeling were increased: both heart weight/body weight (HW/BW) and heart weight/tibia length (HW/TL) were elevated (P < 0.0001), and serum BNP was significantly higher in TAC mice (P < 0.001; Fig. 1B–D). Serial echocardiography and quantitative analysis revealed that, compared to the Sham group, the TAC group exhibited time-dependent left ventricular dysfunction and structural remodeling (Fig. 2A–D). At 2 weeks post-surgery, there were no significant differences in LVEF and E/A ratio between the TAC and Sham groups. However, by 4 weeks, the TAC group demonstrated marked systolic and diastolic impairment, as evidenced by a significant decrease in LVEF and a concurrent significant increase in the E/A ratio. By week 8, LVEF further deteriorated, while the E/A ratio remained persistently elevated. Taken together, sustained pressure overload led to a progressive deterioration of both systolic and diastolic functions in mice.

[68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 uptake increases with heart failure progression

At 2 weeks, PET/CT already showed a mild increase in myocardial [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 uptake in TAC mice compared with shams, which became more pronounced at 4 weeks and further elevated at 8 weeks (Fig. 3). Quantitatively, myocardial %ID/g in the TAC group was 1.15 ± 0.06, higher than 0.96 ± 0.09 in the sham group (P < 0.05); at 4 weeks, 1.79 ± 0.11 vs. 0.98 ± 0.23 (P < 0.01); and at 8 weeks, 2.14 ± 0.25 vs. 1.09 ± 0.24 (P < 0.01). For SUVmax, the TAC group measured 0.33 ± 0.03 at 2 weeks vs. 0.27 ± 0.01 in shams (P < 0.05); at 4 weeks, 0.54 ± 0.03 vs. 0.28 ± 0.05 (P < 0.01); and at 8 weeks, 0.62 ± 0.06 vs. 0.31 ± 0.07 (P < 0.01). The TBR followed a consistent pattern: at 2 weeks, 1.83 ± 0.11 vs. 1.51 ± 0.12 (P < 0.05); at 4 weeks, 2.61 ± 0.20 vs. 1.64 ± 0.27 (P < 0.01); and at 8 weeks, 3.33 ± 0.37 vs. 2.02 ± 0.22 (P < 0.01). Longitudinal tracking of individual animals from 4 to 8 weeks confirmed this progressive signal amplification (Fig. S1), while hepatic uptake remained stable (Fig. S2). These findings indicate that myocardial FAPI signal increases progressively over time under pressure overload, with significant elevation as early as 2 weeks and continued amplification through 8 weeks.

Histopathology confirms hypertrophy and collagen deposition

Gross inspection and H&E staining revealed progressive ventricular dilation in mouse hearts after TAC (Fig. 4A–B). Autoradiography confirmed a markedly elevated [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 signal in TAC myocardium (Fig. 4C), and the temporal enhancement trend was completely consistent with the dynamic changes observed by in vivo [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT imaging. Sirius Red and Masson’s trichrome staining showed a time‑dependent increase in interstitial and perivascular collagen deposition from week 2 to week 8. WGA morphometry demonstrated a gradual increase in cardiomyocyte cross‑sectional area, which was most pronounced at 8 weeks. To validate the molecular basis of these changes, FAP immunofluorescence revealed a robust upregulation of FAP expression specifically localized within interstitial and fibrotic regions, peaking at 8 weeks (Fig. 5A–D and Fig. S3). The evolving trends observed in the above histological evaluations and ex vivo autoradiography signals were highly consistent with those of the concurrent [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT imaging results.

Blocking experiment

As shown in Fig. 6, at 8 weeks after surgery, TAC mice without blocking exhibited marked [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 accumulation in the myocardium, with enhanced myocardial signal observed on axial, coronal, and sagittal images. In contrast, co-injection with an excess of unlabeled FAPI-04 for competitive blocking markedly reduced tracer uptake in the myocardium, and physiological uptake in the muscle and skin was also decreased. These findings indicate that [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 uptake in the TAC myocardium is clearly competitively inhibitable, suggesting that it is mainly mediated by FAP-related specific binding.

Discussion

In this study, we systematically validated the capacity of [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT for the noninvasive, longitudinal, and quantitative assessment of active myocardial fibrosis within the classical TAC model of heart failure. We provide a multimodal chain of evidence linking molecular imaging with cardiac function and histopathology. The core findings demonstrate that myocardial FAPI uptake begins to increase at 2 weeks post-TAC and progressively intensifies up to 8 weeks. Importantly, these in vivo PET/CT results were strongly corroborated by ex vivo autoradiography, immunofluorescence, and histological staining. Together, these validations confirmed that the escalating radiotracer signals closely parallel the progression of myocardial fibrosis and the progressive upregulation of FAP expression.

A core contribution of this work is demonstrating that [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT can distinguish the kinetic phenotypes of different cardiac pathologies. In acute injury models such as myocardial infarction, multiple studies have confirmed a transient peak-and-decay pattern: the FAPI signal peaks around day 6–7 post-injury and then declines [11]. Biologically, this reflects a time-limited, repair-focused physiological process in which activated fibroblasts form a scar to maintain structural integrity and subsequently deactivate [14]. By contrast, our TAC model showed a sustained, monotonic increase in FAPI signal from weeks 2 to 8. These sustained progressive kinetics directly mirrors the model’s pathophysiological basis: prolonged mechanical stretch and neurohormonal activation prevent fibroblast deactivation, driving a chronic, maladaptive fibrotic process [15]. This kinetic divergence is clinically meaningful: FAPI-PET goes beyond detecting the mere presence of fibrosis to characterizing the disease process, distinguishing necessary, self-limited repair from pathological, progressive fibrosis.

While FAP expression can also be upregulated in inflammatory cells (e.g., macrophages) within fibroinflammatory microenvironments [16–18], the [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 signal in our TAC model appears primarily driven by active fibrogenesis rather than transient postsurgical inflammation. This conclusion is supported by two key observations. First, FAPI uptake increased progressively up to 8 weeks, a finding consistent with maladaptive remodeling, and it tightly colocalized with interstitial regions that were rich in collagen and highly positive for FAP on histology. Second, mice that underwent a sham operation exhibited substantially lower myocardial uptake despite undergoing an identical surgical thoracotomy, effectively ruling out acute surgical trauma as the primary source of the signal observed at later stages.

Interestingly, a prior study using a rat abdominal aortic constriction (AAC) model reported a gradual increase in hepatic FAPI accumulation during disease progression [19]. In contrast, extracardiac uptake in our TAC model, particularly in the liver, remained low and stable across all time points. This discrepancy likely stems from differences in the downstream hemodynamic consequences of the two surgical models.

Building on prior studies [20] and our current findings, quantitative FAPI PET/CT may serve as an earlier and more sensitive biomarker of fibrotic activity than conventional functional parameters. Notably, our data revealed that at 2 weeks post-modeling, even though echocardiography showed no significant differences in cardiac function compared to the control group, FAPI PET/CT had already confirmed a remarkable increase in myocardial FAPI uptake. The observed early sensitivity implies that FAPI PET/CT might hold potential for prognostic risk stratification, pending confirmation in future longitudinal studies. Furthermore, the present study offers preliminary experimental support for its potential translation into heart failure management. Such an approach could be especially beneficial for heart failure with preserved ejection fraction, a condition where diastolic dysfunction associated with diffuse myocardial fibrosis often poses difficulties for early conventional detection [21]. Finally, because active fibrosis creates an arrhythmogenic substrate, exploring the utility of FAPI PET/CT for arrhythmia risk assessment represents a highly promising avenue for future prospective investigations [22].

Several limitations point to clear avenues for subsequent research. First, our experiments involved only male mice; future work should include female animals to assess sex-related differences in cardiac remodeling. Second, we did not perform a direct, longitudinal comparison with advanced cardiac magnetic resonance quantitative indices (T1/extracellular volume mapping) in the same animals; such studies are essential to clarify the temporal relationships and complementarity of the two modalities [23]. Third, we treated activated fibroblasts as a relatively homogeneous population; however, single-cell RNA sequencing indicates substantial fibroblast heterogeneity, and future work should identify which specific subpopulations express FAP and dominate the signal in this model [24]. Finally, clinical translation is essential, and future research should include validation in human patient cohorts.

Conclusion

Our findings validate [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT as an effective, noninvasive modality for tracking the progressive course of myocardial fibrosis. With imaging signals tightly matching histological changes and preceding echocardiographic dysfunction, this specific technique holds high translational promise for the early assessment and longitudinal monitoring of fibrotic activity in heart failure.

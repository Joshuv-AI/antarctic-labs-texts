Chest wall reconstruction timing needs an admission-time denominator
Authors: Xiang Zhou, Hongyan Liu, Zhengdong Hua
Published: 2026

To the Editor,

Spering and colleagues [1] provide a clinically useful comparison of chest wall reconstruction (CWR) on the day of admission versus days 1 to 3. We agree with their cautious conclusion that the higher mortality after day-0 CWR should not be interpreted as harm from immediate surgery. Day-0 CWR therefore represents a distinct high-risk operative phenotype rather than a purely temporal exposure. The next reporting step therefore requires more than further adjustment within the treated cohort: it requires an admission-time denominator showing who was eligible to enter either timing strategy.

The study began with 37,646 patients with severe chest wall injury, excluded 2,987 deaths within 48 h, and then compared only patients who underwent CWR and had a known operation date. Patients treated on days 1 to 3 necessarily remained alive and operatively eligible through reassessment and survived beyond 48 h. Those treated on day 0 could include rescue CWR or fixation performed alongside another urgent operation. The two timing groups therefore differ not only by elapsed time, but also by the clinical route through which treatment became possible.

The same risk-set alignment matters when interpreting the RISC III benchmark. In the matched groups, observed mortality was 10.6% versus a prediction of 16.2% after day-0 CWR and 3.6% versus 10.7% after CWR on days 1 to 3. The authors appropriately note that observed mortality was lower because early deaths had been excluded. Once the cohort is conditioned on 48-h survival, receipt of CWR, and availability of the operation date, however, observed-to-predicted comparisons are most appropriately interpreted as descriptive benchmarking rather than evidence of treatment benefit. RISC III predicts hospital mortality in a trauma-registry population [2]; it does not estimate what mortality would have been in these selected survivors without CWR.

A future registry analysis could define eligibility at the first CWR decision after admission and retain mutually exclusive clinical pathways: immediate CWR for an urgent thoracic indication or as an adjunct to competing life-saving surgery; planned CWR after stabilisation; and deferred or non-operative management. Recording the indication, competing operations, physiological readiness, and reasons for deferral would make these pathways interpretable. Follow-up should then begin from the same clinical decision point for all eligible patients, consistent with target-trial principles [3], rather than from the operation date.

Could the authors clarify whether the TraumaRegister DGU can identify such an admission-time CWR-eligible risk set and distinguish rescue or adjunct fixation from planned stabilisation? This would allow future studies to separate patients requiring immediate rescue surgery from those eligible for planned early stabilisation, while using RISC III for case-mix description rather than as evidence of a treatment effect. The present data remain valuable for describing operative phenotypes; the admission-time denominator would make the timing comparison clinically actionable.

Correlation analysis of arterial blood lactate levels with major in-hospital adverse cardiovascular events after coronary intervention in patients with acute myocardial infarction
Authors: Zhang Juan, Ma Chunhua, Gu Lijiang Yikeran, Wang Fei, Wang Yong, Sheng shanshan, Wang Dong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo evaluate the relationship between percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) and major in-hospital adverse cardiovascular events (MACEs) in patients with acute myocardial infarction (PCI) after PCI by monitoring the level of arterial lactate at the time of admission.MethodsA total of 77 patients with acute myocardial infarction who received emergency PCI treatment in our hospital from June 2022 to December 2023 were enrolled, and they were divided according to the preoperative arterial blood lactate level after admission, the control group (40 cases) with normal lactate and the observation group (37 cases) with elevated lactate were enrolled. At the same time, the following major cardiovascular adverse events in the hospital were recorded during the course of the disease: cardiogenic death, cardiogenic shock, new-onset heart failure, angina/exacerbation of pre-existing heart failure, and malignant arrhythmia.ResultsThe incidence of major cardiovascular adverse events in the observation group was higher than that in the control group, and the difference was statistically significant(P < 0.05). Compared with the control group, the observation group had a higher peak cTnI (P < 0.05), a faster heart rate (P < 0.05), a lower systolic blood pressure (P < 0.05), a higher fasting blood glucose (P < 0.05), and a longer time from symptom to catheter (P < 0.05). With the increase of lactate level, the incidence of major cardiovascular adverse events in the hospital showed an overall upward trend. Binary logistic regression analysis showed that heart rate, systolic blood pressure, elevated lactate and fasting blood glucose were the main factors associated with in-hospital adverse cardiovascular events in patients with acute myocardial infarction after PCI.ConclusionsThe level of lactate in the arterial blood can be used as an effective early warning indicator to evaluate the occurrence of major adverse cardiovascular events in the hospital.Keywords: Blood lactate; Acute myocardial infarction; Percutaneous coronary intervention,; Major cardiovascular adverse events

Introduction

Acute myocardial infarction (AMI) is a rapid reduction or interruption of coronary blood flow caused by coronary artery disease, resulting in myocardial ischemia and necrosis, and is one of the leading causes of death and disability in patients with coronary heart disease [1]. According to the China Cardiovascular Health and Disease Report 2022, AMI mortality has increased rapidly since 2005, especially after 2012, when the mortality rate in rural areas was significantly higher than in urban areas. In 2020, the hospitalization expenses of cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases reached 270.901 billion yuan, of which 34.685 billion yuan were AMI expenses, which brought a heavy burden to the economy [2]. Currently, reperfusion therapy for AMI mainly includes percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI), intravenous thrombolysis, coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG), and emergency percutaneous transluminal coronary angioplasty (PTCA) [3]. Although these treatments significantly reduce mortality, major adverse cardiovascular events (MACEs) still occur in some patients in the acute phase, affecting prognosis. The study showed that the incidence of MACE within 30 days of PCI was 7.4% in patients with NSTEMI, while the incidence of MACE within 1 year was as high as 17.8% in patients with AMI who received PCI treatment. MACE includes cardiac death, recurrent myocardial infarction, angina, and heart failure [4]. At present, the model for predicting the risk of MACE in patients with AMI is mainly the GRACE score, covering 8 indicators such as age, systolic blood pressure, and heart rate. The MayoClinic PCI risk scoring system in foreign countries combines clinical and imaging indicators to assess risk [5]. Although existing systems contain biomarkers such as creatinine and cardiac enzymes, the lack of laboratory tests that reflect pathophysiology limits the development of personalized treatment. In recent years, the Intermountain Risk Score (IMRS) has also been demonstrated to hold significant prognostic value in patients with coronary artery disease. Its scoring system incorporates parameters related to acid-base balance, which align physiologically with lactate levels in reflecting tissue perfusion and metabolic status. Studies have shown that the IMRS can predict short- and long-term mortality in patients with ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction, as well as prognosis in those with cardiogenic shock [6]. Therefore, integrating the IMRS with lactate levels may offer a more comprehensive perspective for risk assessment in AMI patients following PCI.Lactate is a product of anaerobic digestion and reflects tissue perfusion and hypoxia [7]. In the ICU, lactate levels are used to assess the prognosis of critically ill patients. High lactate levels are associated with tissue hypoperfusion, severe infection, shock, and myocardial infarction. Studies have shown [8] that lactate monitoring can be used as an important indicator to assess the prognosis of patients with myocardial infarction, but there is no firm conclusion on the correlation of arterial blood lactate levels in MACE after PCI. Therefore, this study will investigate the relationship between blood lactate level and the occurrence of in-hospital MACE after PCI in patients with AMI.

Data and methods

General data

The subjects of the study were 77 patients with first-episode acute myocardial infarction who were admitted to our hospital for PCI treatment from June 2022 to December 2023. The patients with elevated arterial lactate level were divided into observation group (37 cases) and the patients with normal arterial blood lactate level were divided into control group (40 cases). After being reviewed by the ethics committee of our hospital, all research subjects agreed to sign the informed consent form.

Treatment methods

After admission, the patients in both groups were treated as follows in accordance with the “Guidelines for the Treatment of Acute Myocardial Infarction”:

All patients undergoing emergency PCI were given oral “one pack” (aspirin 300 mg and clopidogrel 300 mg or aspirin 300 mg plus ticagrelor 180 mg) before surgery.

All patients undergoing emergency PCI were given oral “one pack” (aspirin 300 mg and clopidogrel 300 mg or aspirin 300 mg plus ticagrelor 180 mg) before surgery.

PCI: All patients completed PCI according to the guidelines for emergency coronary artery stent implantation, and the success criteria for PCI were as follows: residual stenosis within 20% and TIMI grade 3 blood flow.

PCI: All patients completed PCI according to the guidelines for emergency coronary artery stent implantation, and the success criteria for PCI were as follows: residual stenosis within 20% and TIMI grade 3 blood flow.

All patients received secondary prophylaxis drugs for coronary heart disease after surgery. In addition, other drugs such as ACE inhibitors (angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors)/ARBs (angiotensin receptor blockers), β blockers, nitrates, and diuretics may be considered as appropriate according to the patient’s changing condition.

General information and main indicators

Collect general information of patients, including: name, gender, age, smoking history, previous history of hypertension, diabetes, cerebral infarction, etc.; Vital signs such as heart rate, blood pressure, etc. at the time of admission. The following major in-hospital adverse cardiovascular events (MACEs) were recorded during the course of the disease: (1) recurrent angina; (2) malignant arrhythmia; (3) cardiogenic shock; (4) exacerbation of pre-existing heart failure/new heart failure; (5) cardiac death, etc.; Serum troponin I (cTnI), fasting blood glucose, serum creatinine value, arterial blood lactate level, LVEF left ventricular ejection fraction at the time of admission, etc.

Statistical methods

SPSS26.0 medical statistics software was used for statistical analysis, and the measured data were expressed as mean plus or minus standard deviation (X ± S), while the counting data were expressed as frequency or percentage (n, %). When comparing ratios between different groups, the X2 test was used; For continuous data, a test for normality is first required, and if the assumption of normality is satisfied, an independent-samples t-test is used for comparison between groups. In addition, binary logistic regression analysis was used to compare the incidence of major in-hospital adverse cardiovascular events at different lactate levels by plotting a line chart, and the analysis of factors related to major in-hospital adverse cardiovascular events was used. Finally, the P value was less than 0.05 to determine whether the difference was statistically significant.

Discussion

Acute myocardial infarction (AMI) is a severe form of coronary heart disease with rapid onset and high mortality, mainly related to risk factors such as hypertension, dyslipidemia, diabetes, smoking, obesity, and family history. Since the 80s, the significance of restoring blood flow to the treatment of patients with AMI has been gradually recognized, which has led to the development of reperfusion therapy [9]. Percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) has become the preferred treatment due to its wide range of indications and significantly improving the survival and quality of life of patients with acute coronary syndromes [10].

Before clinical research, it is necessary to have a comprehensive understanding of the pathogenesis of the disease. The causes of elevated lactate in acute myocardial infarction include: (1) impaired cardiovascular function leads to reduced blood perfusion, which affects liver and kidney function, reduces lactate metabolism ability, and causes lactate accumulation; (2) Acute heart failure combined with cardiogenic shock and acute pulmonary edema, decreased cardiac output, resulting in hypoxemia and microcirculation disorders, and increased lactate; (3) NO synthesis decreases, endogenous oxygen radical scavenging function decreases, and lactate production is further increased [11]. Li Qin et al. [8] used the Lasso regression method to successfully screen out the variables that predicted the major adverse cardiovascular events after PCI in patients with AMI. Their study found that haemoglobin (Hb) levels had an effect on major long-term adverse cardiovascular events after PCI in patients with AMI, STEMI, and NSTEMI. The results of the study by Chen Xinsen [12] and his team [13] showed that high monocyte-to-lymphocyte ratio (MLR), mean platelet volume-to-lymphocyte ratio (MPVLR), and neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) could be used as independent factors in predicting multiple major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) in STEMI patients after PCI. It is important to note that there is no statistically significant difference in the predictive effect of these factors. However, studies have shown that a higher level of prediction can be achieved by combining MLR, MPVLR, and NLR [14]. This multifactorial approach is useful for more accurate risk stratification and prognostic assessment of patients with acute ST-elevation myocardial infarction. This finding provides important implications for clinicians that multiple independent biomarkers should be considered in order to improve the accuracy and precision of prediction when assessing the long-term prognosis of patients with STEMI after PCI. Jun Jiang et al. [15] conducted a study to investigate the value of eosinophil percentage and high-sensitivity C-reactive protein in predicting major adverse cardiac events (MACEs) in patients with STEMI undergoing PCI therapy. It was found [16] that the combination of eosinophil percentage and high-sensitivity C-reactive protein had significant predictive power in predicting cardiovascular adverse events in patients with STEMI after PCI. By combining indicators such as eosinophil percentage and high-sensitivity C-reactive protein, the cardiovascular risk of patients after PCI can be more accurately assessed, preventive measures can be taken in time, the incidence of adverse events can be reduced, and the prognosis of patients can be improved. The results of this study provide a useful reference for clinical practice and are expected to provide more effective support for the individualized treatment and care of patients with STEMI [17]. HbA1c and NT-proBNP also have independent predictive value in major adverse cardiovascular events in elderly patients with STEMI [18]. Sabafine et al. [19] found that C-reactive protein (CRP) was positively associated with recurrent major adverse cardiovascular events in patients with very low LDL-c, suggesting that inflammation may be an important precipitating factor for cardiovascular events. Chen Yu et al. [20] grouped STEMI patients after PCI according to the TIMI blood flow grade, and found that the lactate level in the microcirculation disorder group was significantly increased, and the in-hospital mortality rate and major cardiovascular event rates were higher than those in the control group. Wang Yanfei [21] showed that the incidence of MACE and all-cause mortality in the LA elevated group were significantly higher than those in the normal group. Shen Qingqing found that lactate and NT-proBNP levels were associated with the severity of disease and the risk of MACE in patients with AHF. Vermeulen [22] has shown that lactate levels are positively associated with in-hospital mortality and major cardiovascular events in patients with acute myocardial infarction. Raghavan [23] found that acidosis interfered with Na⁺-K⁺-ATPase activity increased the risk of arrhythmias, and that high lactate levels increased the risk of in-hospital mortality in patients with AMI and cardiogenic shock. Although studies have explored the association between lactate and major adverse cardiovascular events, there is no consensus. In this study, different types of AMI and a variety of adverse events were synthesized, and the results showed that heart rate, systolic blood pressure, elevated lactate, and fasting blood glucose were the relevant factors for adverse events after PCI in patients with AMI [24, 25].Furthermore, the elevated level of arterial lactate is directly linked to the increased incidence of adverse cardiac events in patients with cardiogenic shock (CS), a critical condition often complicating AMI. Studies have shown that in CS patients, elevated lactate serves as a key biomarker of tissue hypoperfusion and is strongly associated with higher in-hospital mortality and worse clinical outcomes [26, 27]. This aligns with our findings where lactate elevation was significantly associated with events like new-onset heart failure and cardiogenic shock, underscoring its prognostic value not only in unselected AMI populations but particularly in those with or at risk of hemodynamic compromise.

Limitations of this study

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting our findings. First, the relatively small sample size from a single center may limit the statistical power and generalizability of the results. Second, due to the sample constraints, this study could not comprehensively assess the association between arterial blood lactate levels and all known subtypes of adverse cardiovascular events; thus, our analysis should be regarded as exploratory in this regard. Third, the follow-up period was short, focusing only on in-hospital outcomes, which precludes any evaluation of the long-term prognostic value of lactate elevation in this patient population. Future studies with larger, multi-center cohorts and extended follow-up durations are warranted to validate and extend our observations.

Conclusions

In summary, arterial blood lactate levels were significantly associated with major in-hospital adverse cardiovascular events after PCI in patients with acute myocardial infarction, with stronger correlations observed for new-onset heart failure and cardiogenic shock. As arterial lactate increased, the incidence of these in-hospital events showed a corresponding upward trend. Heart rate, systolic blood pressure, elevated lactate, and fasting blood glucose were identified as key factors associated with in-hospital cardiovascular adverse outcomes following PCI in AMI patients.

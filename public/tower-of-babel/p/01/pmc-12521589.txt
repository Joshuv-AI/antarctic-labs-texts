Optimization allocation strategy of agricultural production resources based on SSA-BP algorithm
Authors: Chuang Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractInefficient agricultural resource allocation results in resource inefficiencies and reduced yields. Especially in the context of climate change and reduced arable land, how to balance maximizing yield, and improving ecological benefits is an urgent challenge for modern agriculture. The research proposes a hybrid optimization model based on Sparrow Search Algorithm (SSA) and Back-propagation Neural Network (BP). To solve the problems that traditional methods are prone to fall into local optimum, have slow convergence speed and insufficient prediction stability, and improve crop yield and optimize allocation, SSA explored the constrained resource allocation problem globally by simulating the foraging and alert behavior of sparrow populations, while BP fitted the nonlinear relationship between resources and yield through error back-propagation mechanism. The two work together to optimize the initial weights of the neural network and introduce differential evolution strategy as part of SSA to enhance robustness. The experiments showed that when the number of iterations of the research method reached 8 times, the average fitness dropped to 3. In the accuracy analysis of the calculation results, when the number of nodes in the hidden layer of the research method was 2, the accuracy remained above 98.5%. The resource cost-output ratio of the research method remained above 1.15, indicating cost-effectiveness. This study provides real-time decision-making tools for intelligent agricultural management platforms, supporting cross regional resource scheduling and extreme climate adaptation optimization. It can enhance resource utilization by adjusting the water and fertilizer ratio in real time, contributing to the dual optimization of agricultural economic and ecological benefits.Keywords: Production resources, Back-propagation neural network, Sparrow search algorithm, Yield prediction, Elite strategy, Differential evolutionSubject terms: Mathematics and computing, Applied mathematics, Computational science

Introduction

Under the dual pressure of global population growth (projected to reach 9.7 billion by 2050, a 19% increase from 2022 levels1 and climate change (global temperatures have risen by 1.09 °C since 1880, with agricultural losses due to extreme weather increasing by 34% over the past decade2, agricultural systems are facing unprecedented challenges. The Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations predicts that global food demand will increase by 60% by 2050. The reduced arable land resources, water scarcity, and frequent extreme weather have further intensified the food security crisis. How to efficiently allocate agricultural production resources through intelligent algorithms has become a key issue in ensuring food supply and promoting sustainable agricultural development. Optimizing agricultural resource allocation is achieved through artificial intelligence driven optimization models (mainly intelligent optimization algorithms) to seek the optimal combination strategy of production factors under conditions such as water, fertilizer, and labor, achieving a multi-objective balance of maximizing yield, minimizing costs, and synergistically improving ecological benefits (like the contradiction between increased production demand and excessive fertilizer leading to eutrophication of water bodies, intensive irrigation and regional water scarcity, and the dilemma of improving mechanical operating efficiency and increasing diesel carbon emissions)3. Traditional empirical resource allocation methods rely on qualitative analysis, making it difficult to accurately capture the nonlinear relationship between resource input and output, resulting in diminishing marginal benefits. Research has shown that intelligent optimization algorithm can increase food production by 10–15% in densely populated areas of East Asia while reducing resource waste by 20%−30%, which has significant strategic significance in alleviating regional grain supply pressure4,5. Resource allocation and sustainable agricultural practices are intrinsically coupled. Precision fertilization technology can reduce the pollution caused by nitrogen and phosphorus loss to water bodies. Smart irrigation systems can reduce water consumption in irrigated agriculture by 30–50%, and algorithm-based resource allocation can simultaneously optimize carbon emission intensity and soil health indicators. Therefore, modern optimization models need to move beyond single-goal optimization and construct a decision-making framework that synergizes economic, ecological, and social benefits. Although current research attempts to introduce intelligent algorithms such as genetic algorithms and ant colony optimization into the agricultural field, there are still significant limitations. Specifically, genetic algorithms are prone to fall into local optima and have a slow convergence speed. Ant colony algorithms have insufficient processing efficiency for high-dimensional data. Traditional Back-propagation Neural Networks (BP) have low prediction stability due to the vanishing gradient problem6,7. Prediction stability refers to the consistency of model outputs across multiple independent runs or datasets, typically quantified by the variance of prediction errors or the width of confidence intervals. Most of the existing methods focus on a single optimization objective and lack systematic integration of resource costs, environmental constraints, and climate adaptability. In response to the above problems, this research proposes a hybrid optimization model based on Sparrow Search Algorithm (SSA) and BP, namely Sparrow Search Algorithm-Back-propagation Neural Network (SSA-BP). SSA simulates the foraging and vigilance behaviors of sparrow populations and achieves a balance between global exploration and local development through the discoverer-follower mechanism. Its swarm intelligence characteristics can effectively avoid local extremum. BP is good at fitting the complex nonlinear relationship between resources and output through the error back-propagation mechanism. This research innovatively combines the global optimization ability of SSA with the local fine-tuning advantage of BP, utilizes SSA to optimize the initial weights and thresholds of BP, and enhances the algorithm robustness through differential evolution and elite strategies. Compared with existing methods, the research method has three contributions: (1) Establishing a multi-objective optimization function to simultaneously optimize output, resource cost, and carbon emission intensity; (2) Establishing a dynamic feedback mechanism to adapt to production scenarios in different climate zones and soil types; (3) Adopting a hybrid algorithm architecture to improve the convergence speed by more than 40%. It is expected that the research will provide decision-making tools with both theoretical innovation and practical value for smart agriculture, especially demonstrating unique potential in adaptive optimization in response to extreme climates.

Literature review

Some scholars have conducted research on allocation strategies of agricultural production resources. Aftab J et al. (2023) proposed a green human resource management to regulate agricultural production resources. The partial least squares structural equation model collected and analyzed production data. Environmental strategies and green innovation directions were adjusted. This approach effectively improved the effective utilization rate of production resources8. Melton F S et al. (2022) proposed a data visualization access tool for water resource management in agriculture. The research method took satellite system data as auxiliary data input to adjust the average absolute error to improve the accuracy of data calculation. This proposed method effectively improved the efficiency of water resource utilization9. Bedeke S B (2023) proposed a facility location modeling approach based on empirical data analysis to address the climate resource utilization in agricultural resource management. The construction location of agricultural facilities was analyzed. The relationship between facilities and producers was extracted. The impact of climate change was incorporated into the decision-making process. This proposed method enhanced the adaptability of producers to different climates10. Van Selm et al. (2022) proposed a resource allocation model to utilize animal production resources in agriculture and animal husbandry. The research method extracted the relationship between different nutrient supply ratios and corresponding yields. The comprehensive greenhouse gas emission data was the source data. This proposed method effectively improved the efficiency of agricultural and animal husbandry production11.

Some scholars have also carried out agricultural optimization using artificial intelligence technology. Shams M Y et al. (2024) proposed a Xai-Crop algorithm based on explainable artificial intelligence technology to address the lack of transparency in traditional crop recommendation systems in agricultural optimization. This study constructed an interpretable decision-making framework by integrating multi-source data such as soil characteristics, meteorological data, and historical yields using SHAP-based feature attribution technology. The experimental results showed that the determination coefficient of the proposed method reached 0.94152, which could explain 94.15% of the yield variation factors and successfully achieved high-precision and traceable agricultural optimization decision-making12. Akkem Y et al. (2024) aimed at the opaque decision-making in intelligent systems in agricultural optimization. They reconstructed the crop recommendation model by adopting interpretable artificial intelligence technology. The method integrated multi-dimensional agricultural data and developed visualization tools such as dependency graphs and waterfall graphs to analyze feature weights. The results showed that the proposed method improved the transparency of the recommendation logic in agricultural artificial intelligence systems by 82%13. Dhanke J A et al. (2025) developed an artificial intelligence-based precise irrigation framework for water resource waste and climate adaptation in agricultural optimization. The research collected soil moisture, meteorological data and crop growth parameters in real time through Internet of Things sensors and remote sensing devices. They constructed a deep long short-term memory neural network model that used a multi-step prediction mode to predict soil moisture content for the next 24 h. The input layer receives continuous data from t time steps in the past (soil moisture, meteorological parameters, and crop growth indicators), and the output layer generated predicted soil moisture values for the next 24 h, as well as dynamically programmed irrigation time and water volume. The results showed that the proposed method could precisely regulate the irrigation volume in both arid and rainy areas14. Li P et al. (2024) developed an artificial intelligence-based fish meal freshness detection system for feed quality control in agricultural optimization. The research collected volatile gas data through the self-developed electronic nose in a controlled laboratory environment using stored fishmeal feed samples, and combined the variational mode decomposition algorithm to decompose the total volatile basic nitrogen variation curve into seven frequency sub-sequences. The experimental results showed that the proposed method could provide an accurate intelligent monitoring scheme for feed quality for agricultural optimization15.

To sum up, previous studies have made remarkable progress in the field of agricultural resource allocation, but there is still a gap between theory and practice specifically manifested in: (1) The difficulty of single-objective optimization frameworks to balance economic benefits, ecological benefits, and social demands; (2) The lack of dynamic feedback mechanisms to adapt to variable climate and soil conditions; (3) Limited generalization ability due to reliance on idealized datasets that neglect noise data and extreme climate events in real-world scenarios. The advantages of the existing methods are reflected in multi-dimensional innovations. Green human resource management has enhanced resource utilization by optimizing labor allocation and operational decisions through environmental strategy adjustments. Data visualization tools combined with satellite data have optimized water resource management. Facility analysis technology enhances climate adaptability. Artificial intelligence technology has improved the prediction accuracy and resource regulation efficiency through transparent decision-making and real-time monitoring. However, these methods face many challenges in practical applications. The single-objective optimization framework is difficult to balance the complex relationship among economic benefits, ecological benefits, and social demands, and lacks a dynamic feedback mechanism to adapt to the changing climate and soil conditions. Furthermore, most research relies on idealized datasets and does not fully consider the noise data and extreme climate events in actual agricultural production, which limits the generalization ability. Based on previous research, the research proposes an agricultural production resource allocation strategy based on SSA-BP. The global exploration capability of SSA combined with the local fine-tuning advantage of BP neural network, and integrated with dynamic multi-objective optimization framework and extreme climate adaptation mechanism, has improved the economic and ecological synergy benefits of prediction stability and resource allocation. It is expected that the research method will achieve good performance in important indicators such as computational accuracy, the ratio of resource cost-to-output, and the average fitness convergence speed.

Methods and materials

Crop yield prediction method based on BP

When formulating strategies to allocate agricultural production resources, representative data support strategy formulation. The crop yield, as the most direct production data, can provide strong data support for optimizing the allocation strategy of agricultural production resources16,17. There are many parameters that have impacts on predicting crop yield. The impact of different parameters on yield prediction results varies, leading to computational complexity18,19. BP is an artificial neural network, which has strong adaptability in analyzing the characteristics of multi-parameter relationships. This research takes BP to construct a crop yield prediction method. The reasons for choosing BP instead of other models such as Support Vector Machine (SVM) or Random Forest are as follows. Specifically, compared to SVM, which relies heavily on kernel function selection and exhibits sensitivity to parameter tuning, BP’s multi-layer structure inherently captures complex nonlinear relationships without explicit kernel mapping. Meanwhile, although random forests are effective for classification tasks, they have low regression accuracy for continuous variables such as crop yield due to the piecewise constant prediction of their decision trees, and are difficult to model the temporal dependence in continuous agricultural data. Firstly, agricultural yield prediction involves multi-source nonlinear coupling factors such as climate, soil, and crop characteristics. BP can effectively fit the complex nonlinear mapping relationship between input features and output through multi-layer hidden nodes and back-propagation mechanism. However, when dealing with high-dimensional nonlinear problems, SVM relies on the selected kernel function, and the performance is easily limited by the type of kernel function and parameter sensitivity. This is particularly problematic in agricultural yield prediction because: (1) There is a lack of prior knowledge to determine the optimal kernel function for complex agricultural ecological interactions; (2) For time sensitive agricultural decisions, the computational cost of kernel experiments is high; (3) Insufficient fitting of threshold effects or overfitting of noisy field data. In addition, although random forests perform well in classification tasks, ensemble methods based on decision trees typically have lower regression prediction accuracy for continuous variables than neural networks. Optimization of crop yield prediction method based on SSA

When only using BP for calculation, there are problems such as small local poles and insufficient convergence speed, which leads to insufficient quality in solving crop yield20. SSA directly takes the error value of the objective function change as the fitness function value to perform iterative optimization. This can approximate the global optimum and effectively avoid the influence of BP being easily trapped in local optima. Figure 2 shows the SSA process.

The optimization process of SSA on a global scale.

In Fig. 2, the SSA achieves global optimization by simulating the foraging and alert behaviors of sparrow populations. Its operation process includes initialization stage, fitness evaluation, role division, and position update. Set the population size, the maximum number of iterations, and the individual dimension, and randomly generate the initial positions of sparrow individuals. Calculate the fitness value of each individual based on the error of the objective function, and record the current optimal individual position as the global optimal solution. Sort according to the fitness value, define the top 20% of individuals as “discoverers”, who are responsible for global exploration. The rest are “followers”, following the discoverer to carry out local development. The discoverer moves towards a better region while introducing random disturbances to avoid premature convergence. Followers approach the current optimal individual with a probability or randomly adjust their positions to enhance diversity. Randomly select some individuals as “alerters”. When potential threats are detected, quickly jump out of the current area to escape the local optimum. When reaching the maximum number of iterations, the final output is the global optimal solution as the initial weight and threshold of the BP. To improve the accuracy and stability of SSA, the research integrates differential evolution into the algorithm. Figure 3 shows the differential evolution process.

Differential evolution process.

In Fig. 3, it is also necessary to calculate the population fitness value during differential evolution. DE collaborates with SSA for optimization through three-stage operations of mutation, crossover and selection. In each generation iteration of SSA, when the positions of the discoverer, follower, and alert are updated, the current population is finely optimized through DE. Firstly, in the mutation stage, select three different individuals from the current population. Generate new mutant individuals by superimposed difference vectors. Mutation factors control the amplitude of disturbances to enhance population diversity. Subsequently, it enters the crossover stage, where the mutant individuals are probabilistically mixed with the original individuals by dimension to ensure that at least one information of the mutant dimension is retained, balancing exploration and development. Finally, in the selection stage, compare the fitness of individuals before and after mutation through the greedy strategy, and retain the better solution to enter the next generation population. The role of DE is mainly reflected in two aspects. Firstly, it breaks the local convergence trend that may be caused by the “discoverer-follower” mechanism in SSA through mutation perturbation. Secondly, crossover and selection operations refine and adjust the global search results of SSA locally to improve the quality of the solution. Furthermore, DE combines the elite strategy to ensure that the best individuals in history are directly retained for the next generation, avoiding the loss of high-quality solutions. In each iteration of SSA, after the positions of the discoverer, follower, and vigilante are updated, the DE algorithm is injected into the SSA process to perform mutation, crossover, and selection operations on the current entire population. The mathematical expression of the elite strategy is as follows: The updated population satisfies that the globally optimal individuals are in the next generation population. This mechanism forcibly preserves the historical optimal solution, ensuring that the algorithm does not lose the high-quality resource allocation scheme discovered when exploring new solution spaces. Optimization allocation strategy of production resources based on SSA-BP crop yield prediction

When using SSA-BP for crop yield prediction in practical scenarios, the research stipulates that climate conditions, soil conditions, crop characteristics, and historical yield data all have clear measurement values. The data preprocessing process includes the following steps. Firstly, clean up the raw data to eliminate duplicate records and outliers that deviate significantly from the actual situation. For the missing data, take time series linear interpolation method for filling to ensure data continuity. Secondly, for the dimensional difference problem of the input features, take the maximal-minimum normalization method to linearly map each feature value to the [0,1] interval, eliminate the interference of dimensional differences on the training of the neural network. Compared with Z-score standardization, maximum-minimum normalization preserves the original distribution of agricultural parameters within their known physical bounds, ensuring that all scaled values fall in [0,1] without generating negative or unbounded outputs that lack agronomic interpretability. Additionally, it aligns with the output range of activation functions to prevent gradient saturation, while the mean center scaling of Z-score may distort value semantics in situations where absolute range is crucial for decision-making. After normalization, randomly sort the data before being input into the model to reduce the potential bias in batch training. The random shuffling operation is executed when data is loaded, with a fixed batch size of 32. It is implemented using the tf. data. Results

Performance testing of agricultural production resource allocation strategy based on SSA-BP

In the practical application verification of the SSA-BP algorithm, the study selected two plain planting areas with distinct climatic conditions, namely Pedro in the temperate zone (mainly growing wheat) and Villa in the semi-arid zone (mainly growing corn), and both were located in China, each covering 100 hectares of cultivated land to simulate diverse agricultural production scenarios. The dataset is constructed based on the historical records provided by the local agricultural cooperative for five consecutive years from 2019 to 2023, covering a total of 12 variables in four major categories: climate, soil, management, and output. Specifically, climate includes daily temperature (°C), cumulative rainfall (mm), solar radiation (MJ/m2), and air humidity (%). Soil includes pH value, organic matter content (%), nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium nutrient content (kg/ha). Management includes planting density (plants/ha), fertilizer application amount (kg/ha), irrigation amount (m³/ha). The output is the per-season crop yield (tons/ha). The data collection frequency was once a day. The original records in a single region reached 3,650 (5 years ×730 days). After aggregation, 60 seasonal samples (2 growing seasons per year ×5 years) were formed. The study adopted a dual validation mechanism to divide the data. The Alpha dataset was divided in chronological order, with 48 seasonal samples from 2019 to 2022 serving as the training set and 12 samples from 2023 as the test set. The Bravo dataset is based on stratified sampling of climate characteristics to ensure that the training set (2019–2022) contains an equal proportion of dry/wet years as the test set. The sample sizes of the two datasets are the same (training set n = 48, test set n = 12), but the construction logic is complementary. Alpha tests temporal generalization, and Bravo verifies climatic robustness. During the training process, stratified 5-fold cross-validation is introduced. Each fold is strictly divided by year (that is, each fold contains the data of a complete year) to ensure that different climate fluctuation patterns are independently included in the validation set, thereby accurately evaluating the model robustness and avoiding overfitting. The verification process of the research method has three stages: prediction accuracy, resource cost efficiency, and field verification. After arranging these estimated values in ascending order, the 25th and 975th values were taken as the lower and upper limits of the confidence interval, respectively. The mutation and crossover operations of the differential evolution algorithm are explained. The diversity-enhancing perturbation of the SSA population has improved the solution space coverage of high-dimensional resource allocation problems and reduced prediction bias caused by parameter solidification.

The cost-to-output ratio is defined as the ratio of the total cost of all resources consumed in agricultural production to the total value of crop output. The specific calculation process is as follows. Firstly, the sum of the products of the usage of various resources and their unit costs is taken as the total input cost. Then, this total cost is divided by the product of the predicted crop yield and the market price per unit yield. When the ratio is greater than 1, it indicates that the input cost is higher than the output value. When it is less than 1, it indicates that the output value is higher than the input cost. In Fig. 11a, in Pedro, the resource cost-output ratio of the SSA-BP model fluctuated between 1.22 and 1.33. In Fig. 11b, in the Villa, the resource cost-to-output ratio of SSA-BP fluctuated between 1.15 and 1.33. The advantage of this research method is that SSA-BP avoids resource allocation imbalance caused by a single optimization by dynamically balancing the three objectives of maximizing output, minimizing resource costs, and controlling carbon emission intensity. During the SSA iteration process, the search space is dynamically adjusted in combination with the actual upper limit of farmland resources to avoid the decline in output caused by excessive cost compression, thereby maintaining the cost-to-output ratio at a reasonable high level.

Discussion and conclusion

The utilization efficiency of agricultural production resources determines the profit of agricultural production and crop yield. To improve the utilization efficiency of production resources, the research designs an optimal allocation method for agricultural production resources based on SSA-BP. The relationship among agricultural production resources is analyzed. Climatic conditions, soil conditions, crop characteristics, and historical yields are taken as calculation parameters, and the differential evolution algorithm is combined to improve the accuracy and stability of the algorithm. The elite strategy is adopted to obtain the random variables of the standard normal distribution. When the planting area reaches 12 hectares, the yield prediction deviation of the research method remains within 0.3t. In crop yield analysis, this research method increases the crop yield by 13.3% in the sixth season of a certain planting area. The SSA-BP performs well in temperate plains and semi-arid regions, and still maintains high prediction accuracy (MSE < 2.2 × 10−3) on the newly added tropical rainforest and alpine datasets, indicating its ability to generalize across climate zones.

The research method can effectively increase the crop yield and generate more accurate crop yield prediction results. The SSA-BP algorithm has extensive application potential in the actual agricultural environment. For instance, by accurately predicting crop yield and optimizing resource allocation, farmers can dynamically adjust the amount of water and fertilizer applied based on the soil characteristics and climatic conditions of different plots, significantly enhancing the resource utilization efficiency. In addition, this model can be integrated into the intelligent agricultural management platform to provide real-time decision support for agricultural cooperatives or large farms, helping them achieve dual optimization of economic and ecological benefits under changing market demands and climatic conditions. In arid areas, the system can allocate water resources to high-yield crops first, while in rainy areas, optimizing the drainage system can reduce flood risks. The research provides three core contributions to agricultural modernization. Firstly, the SSA-BP hybrid algorithm architecture achieves multi-objective dynamic optimization of resource allocation, promoting the intelligent agriculture from a single output oriented approach to coordinated economic and ecological development. Secondly, the constructed yield prediction system can provide real-time decision support for precision agriculture and reduce the trial-and-error costs of traditional experience-based planting. Thirdly, the proposed dynamic feedback mechanism provides an extensible framework for resource allocation in response to climate change, such as automatically adjusting irrigation plans during drought warnings, or optimizing fertilization schemes under carbon trading policies to reduce greenhouse gas emissions.

Although the SSA-BP model performs well under ideal conditions, its prediction accuracy may be affected by external uncontrollable factors. Extreme weather events may disrupt the steady-state assumptions of the model, leading to prediction deviations. In addition, pests and diseases directly affect the growth parameters of crops, and current models have not yet integrated real-time pest and disease monitoring data. Therefore, future research needs to combine meteorological early warning systems and plant protection sensors, and dynamically update the input variables to enhance the model robustness. Data collection is based on uniformly distributed soil and crop parameters, while significant spatial heterogeneity may exist in actual farmland. Furthermore, historical data do not cover the abrupt effects caused by technological innovations or policy adjustments, which may lead to long-term prediction biases. Future work needs to enhance the adaptability of the model to complex field conditions by introducing real-time monitoring data and high-resolution remote sensing images. Future research can continue to optimize the SSA-BP architecture to be compatible with multi-source data, support dynamic feature extension, and enhance adaptability to different crop types. It is also possible to compress the redundant steps and combine gradient pruning and early stopping strategies to shorten the training time. Future research can also introduce Dropout layers and L2 regularization to balance the complexity and generalization ability of the model. Bayesian optimization is also adopted for automatic parameter adjustment to replace manual trial and error.
